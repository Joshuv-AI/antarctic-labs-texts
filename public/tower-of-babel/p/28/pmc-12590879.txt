miR-7974 as a potential biomarker for lupus nephritis and its involvement in disease progression
Authors: Rongzheng Yue, Shanshan Wang, Jing Wang, Hongyu Qiu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLupus nephritis (LN) is a prevalent and severe complication associated with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), posing a substantial risk to renal function and adversely affecting the quality of life of affected individuals. miRNAs play a crucial regulatory role in adaptive immune responses. However, the involvement of specific miRNAs in LN is rarely reported. This research aims to examine the clinical value of miR-7974, as well as the expression changes and interaction mechanisms of the miR-7974/DDAH1 axis in LN, and its impact on the pathogenesis of LN.MethodsThe expression levels of miR-7974 and DDAH1 were measured using real-time fluorescence quantification. CCK-8 was utilized to assess cell proliferation ability. ELISA was employed to quantify the levels of inflammatory factors. The binding of miR-7974 to DDAH1 was verified using a dual luciferase reporter gene assay.ResultsmiR-7974 is highly expressed in blood samples from patients with LN and LPS-induced HRMCs, whereas DDAH1 is expressed at low levels. The ROC curve suggests that miR-7974 possesses diagnostic value. The knockdown of miR-7974 and the overexpression of DDAH1 both inhibit cell proliferation and reduce levels of inflammatory cytokines. Additionally, miR-7974 negatively regulates DDAH1.ConclusionsmiR-7974 plays a role in the progression of LN by negatively regulating DDAH1. The miR-7974/DDAH1 axis may serve as a non-invasive biomarker and a potential therapeutic target for the diagnosis of LN.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-025-04554-w.

Introduction

Lupus nephritis (LN) is a prevalent and severe complication associated with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), affecting approximately 40% to 70% of SLE patients [1]. The pathogenesis of LN is characterized by an interplay of genetic, immune, and environmental factors that collectively lead to renal immune-inflammatory damage [2]. This condition significantly affects both the quality of life and prognosis of individuals who are affected. At present, the diagnosis of LN primarily depends on renal biopsy and a comprehensive clinical evaluation. However, renal biopsy carries certain risks and limitations, and there is a notable absence of specific and sensitive biomarkers for early diagnosis and disease monitoring in clinical practice [3]. Consequently, identifying reliable non-invasive biomarkers and novel therapeutic targets is crucial for enhancing the diagnostic and treatment landscape for patients with LN.

microRNA (miRNA) is a type of endogenous, non-coding small RNA molecules that modulate gene expression through complementary base pairing and binding to target mRNA. It participates in various biological processes, such as cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and the regulation of immune responses [4]. Research has indicated that miRNAs play a significant role in the onset and progression of various diseases, serving as critical regulators of adaptive immune responses [5]. Furthermore, they hold considerable promise as biomarkers and therapeutic targets. In recent years, research has identified that multiple miRNAs are involved in the pathogenesis of LN, including miR-654 and miR-531a-5p [6, 7]. However, the specific miRNAs associated with LN and their mechanisms of action require further investigation.

Therefore, based on the LN-specific miRNA identified by Chen et al. from serum sources [8], this study further forecasted the potential target genes of miR-7974 through the application of bioinformatics methodologies. It explored the expression changes and interaction mechanisms of the miR-7974/DDAH1 axis in LN and evaluated its potential value as a biomarker and therapeutic target for LN.

Methods and materials

Research object

The Medical Ethics Committee of Medical Ethics Committee of the Kidney Research Institute, West China Hospital of Sichuan University. Blood samples were collected from 46 LN patients diagnosed by the Department of Rheumatology and Immunology who were receiving standardized treatment (prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg/day combined with mycophenolate mofetil 1–2 g/day or cyclophosphamide 0.5–1 g/m²) and from 46 healthy individuals. LN patients all comply with the 2019 European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR)/American College of Rheumatology (ACR) and 2024 LN management clinical practice guidelines [9, 10]. Each participant signed an informed consent form. Individuals diagnosed with severe infections, malignant neoplasms, and various autoimmune disorders were excluded from participation in the study.

Cell culture and processing

The human glomerular mesangial cell line (HRMC) was obtained from ATCC and cultured in 1640 medium enriched with 10% FBS. The culture plate was incubated at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere. According to the method described by Liu et al. [11], cells were induced with 10 µg/mL lipopolysaccharide (LPS) to establish an LN cell model.

Real‑time quantitative PCR

Extract total RNA using TRIzol reagent. Following the manufacturer’s guidelines for SYBR Green Realtime PCR Master, a configuration system was established using GAPDH and U6 as internal reference genes. Quantitative PCR was performed using a LightCycler® 96 PCR system. Analyze the changes in expression levels using the 2− Δ Δ Ct method.

Cell proliferation

Cell proliferation was conducted in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions for the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo, Japan). Absorbance was quantified at OD 450 using an enzyme marker.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Following the manufacturer’s guidelines, use an ELISA kit (R&D Systems, USA) to measure the concentration of IL-6, IL-8, IL-17, TNF-α, and IFN-γ (n = 3). Absorbance was measured at a corrected wavelength of 540 nm using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad).

Target gene prediction

Using the public databases TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org/), miRDB (https://mirdb.org/), and miRWalk (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/), identify potential target genes of miR-7974.

Dual luciferase reporter assay

Clone the wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) 3’-UTR containing the DDAH1 binding site into the pGL3 (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) vector. After transfection, the dual-luciferase reporter detection system was employed for evaluation.

Nitric oxide content detection

The nitrate reductase method was employed to determine the nitric oxide (NO) content in the cell culture supernatant. Following the instructions provided with the NO detection kit (Jonlnbio, Shanghai, China), the cell culture supernatant was reacted with the appropriate reagent. The absorbance was then measured at 550 nm using an enzyme labelling equipment reader.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 27.0, and results were visualized using GraphPad Prism 8.0. Data are presented as the mean ± SD. Use the Shapiro-Wilk test to examine the normality of the data distribution. For normally distributed data, differences between two or more groups were compared using Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA, followed by post-hoc testing with Tukey’s method. For non-normally distributed data, the Mann-Whitney U test or the Kruskal-Wallis H test was used. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square (χ²) test. The significance of the data is assessed using the criterion of P < 0.05.

Results

Expression and value of miR-7974

In comparison to the control group, the expression level of miR-7974 in the blood of LN patients was significantly upregulated (Fig. 1A). Additionally, the expression level in LPS-induced HRMC cells was markedly increased (Fig. 1B), which aligns with the results obtained from clinical sample analyses. The ROC curve indicates that miR-7974 possesses diagnostic value for LN (AUC = 0.7866, 95% CI = 0.6959–0.8774), a sensitivity of 69.57%, and a specificity of 71.74%. The optimal cutoff value, determined by the Youden index, is 0.431 (Fig. 1C).

The clinical data of the subjects showed that CREA, UTP, hematuria positivity, and total SLEDAI scores were significantly higher in the LN group than in the control group, while eGFR, C3, and C4 were significantly lower in the LN group (Table 1, P < 0.001). The mean renal component score of the total SLEDAI score—comprising proteinuria and urinary tubular pattern—was 6.2 ± 1.8 points, accounting for 45.5% of the total score. Proteinuria was present in 82.6% (38/46) of patients, and the urinary tubular pattern was observed in 56.5% (26/46) of patients. Pearson correlation analysis showed that miR-7974 was negatively correlated with eGFR (r = -0.452, P = 0.002), C3 (r = -0.467, P = 0.001), and C4 (r = -0.459, P = 0.001), and positively correlated with CREA (r = 0.486, P < 0.001) and UTP (r = 0.94, P < 0.0001) (Fig. S1). Multiple logistic regression analysis demonstrated that miR-7974 and UTP are independent risk factors for LN (Table S1).

To further evaluate the diagnostic value of miR-7974 in combination with traditional biomarkers, a multifactor ROC analysis was conducted. The results showed that the AUC for the traditional biomarker combination (eGFR + C3 + C4 + CREA + UTP) was 0.922 (95% CI: 0.866–0.977, P < 0.0001), with a sensitivity of 84.8% and a specificity of 91.3%. The AUC for the combination of miR-7974 and traditional biomarkers was 0.939 (95% CI: 0.890–0.988, P < 0.0001), with a sensitivity of 89.1% and a specificity of 91.3% (Fig. S2).

The role of miR-7974 in LN

Transfect HRMC cells with si-NC and si-miR-7974 to examine the function of miR-7974 in LN. The findings demonstrated that the silencing of miR-7974 significantly reduced its expression in HRMC cells (Fig. 2A). In comparison to the control group, the knockdown of miR-7974 significantly inhibited cell proliferation (Fig. 2B). In addition, the concentrations of inflammatory factors in LPS-induced HRMC cells significantly increased. However, the knockdown of miR-7974 significantly reduced the levels of these inflammatory factors (Fig. 2C-G).

Target gene of miR-7974

Bioinformatics prediction of downstream targets of miR-7974 using public databases (Fig. 3A), resulting in the identification of potential target genes (Table S2). Among these, DDAH1 ranked fourth and is involved in regulating kidney function; therefore, we focused on DDAH1. The binding site between miR-7974 and DDAH1 has been identified (Fig. 3B). Pearson correlation analysis revealed a significant negative correlation between the expression level of miR-7974 in the blood samples of LN patients and the expression level of DDAH1 (Fig. 3D, r = -0.8916, P < 0.0001). The findings from the dual luciferase reporter gene assay indicated that, compared to the control group, the miR-7974 mimic markedly reduced the activity of the DDAH1 WT luciferase. Conversely, there were no notable alterations in the activity of the DDAH1 MUT luciferase (Fig. 3C), further demonstrating the interaction between miR-7974 and DDAH1.

The role of DDAH1 in LN

In comparison to the control group, the expression level of DDAH1 in blood samples from LN patients was significantly reduced (Fig. 4A). Additionally, the expression level in LPS-induced HRMC cells was consistent with the results obtained from clinical sample analyses (Fig. 4B, S3). Transfect HRMC cells with NC mimics and DDAH1 mimics to investigate the role of DDAH1 in LN. The findings indicated that the overexpression of DDAH1 markedly elevated the expression levels in LPS-induced HRMC cells (Fig. 4C) and markedly inhibited cell proliferation (Fig. 4D).

The role of miR-7974/DDAH1 in LN

To further elucidate the role of miR-7974 / DDAH1 in LN, DDAH1 inhibitors were transfected into HRMC with miR-7974 knockdown. The findings indicated that the knockdown of miR-7974 markedly elevated the expression level of DDAH1. However, co-transfection with DDAH1 inhibitors reversed this expression (Fig. 5A). Similarly, DDAH1 inhibitors mitigated the inhibitory effect of miR-7974 knockdown on inflammatory factors (Fig. 5B-F). In addition, DDAH1 inhibitors reversed the inhibitory effect of miR-7974 knockdown on cell proliferation (Fig. 5G). Knockdown of miR-7974 significantly increased NO content, whereas co-transfection with a DDAH1 inhibitor resulted in a decrease in NO content (Fig. S4).

Discussion

LN is a prevalent and severe complication associated with SLE that poses a substantial risk to renal function and adversely affects the quality of life of affected individuals. miRNAs, recognized as important biomarkers, play a crucial role in disease progression and are of great significance for the diagnosis and treatment of LN.

Recent studies have highlighted the critical role of miRNA in autoimmune diseases [12, 13]. The expression level of microRNA-124 in the serum of LN patients was significantly reduced [14]. miRNA sequencing results indicated that the levels of hsa-miR-497-5p and hsa-miR-6515-5p were markedly elevated in the serum of LN patients, with AUC values of 0.777 and 0.642, respectively. Furthermore, the expression levels of these miRNAs were significantly correlated with clinical indicators, including creatinine, 24-hour proteinuria, and total CO2 [15], which aligns with the findings of this study. Our study found that the expression level of miR-7974 was markedly upregulated in LN, with an AUC of 0.7866. Additionally, it was significantly correlated with clinical indicators such as CREA, C3, C4, eGFR, UTP, hematuria positivity, and SLEDAI. The combination of miR-7974 and standard clinical markers provides improved diagnostic accuracy. Inflammatory mediators are integral to the advancement of the pathogenesis of LN [16]. The excretion of IL-6 and IL-8 in the urine of LN patients is markedly increased [17]. Additionally, the levels of IL-17, IFN-γ, and TNF-α are markedly elevated in LN patients and show a strong positive correlation with miRNA [15]. Our study observed a significant increase in the concentrations of inflammatory factors in LPS-induced HRMC cells. In contrast, the knockdown of miR-7974 significantly reduced the levels of these inflammatory factors, which is consistent with previous research [15, 18]. In addition, the knockdown of miR-7974 markedly inhibited cell proliferation, which is consistent with the findings of Yao et al. [19].

The primary function of dimethylarginine dimethylamine hydrolase 1 (DDAH1) is to catalyze the hydrolysis of asymmetric dimethylarginine (ADMA) into L-citrulline and dimethylamine. This enzyme regulates ADMA levels in the body, thereby influencing physiological processes such as nitric oxide synthesis and endothelial function [20]. In the livers of obese mice, hepatic steatosis and insulin resistance were alleviated by enhancing DDAH1 activity or reducing S100A11 expression [21]. DDAH1 exhibits a significant protective effect against acetaminophen-induced liver toxicity [22]. DDAH1 protects against muscle injury and promotes regeneration by degrading ADMA and inhibiting inflammation in muscle cells [23]. Previous research has suggested that DDAH1 may be involved in regulating renal function [24]; however, its involvement in LN and its relationship with miR-7974 have not yet been reported. This study identified the binding site between miR-7974 and DDAH1 through bioinformatics predictions. Additionally, Pearson correlation analysis indicated a significant negative correlation between the two. The interaction was additionally confirmed through the application of a dual luciferase reporter gene assay. The expression of DDAH1 in LN is significantly reduced, and the overexpression of DDAH1 markedly inhibits cell proliferation. Additionally, DDAH1 inhibitors can counteract the inhibitory effects of miR-7974 knockdown on cell proliferation, inflammatory factors, and NO content. MiR-146a contributes to the development of LN by targeting TRAF6 [25]. Additionally, MiR-122-5p enhances the polarization of M1 macrophages by modulating the FOXO3/NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby exacerbating LN [26]. These results align with the findings of the current study.

Nonetheless, this study is subject to certain limitations, including a relatively limited sample size and an absence of comprehensive mechanistic research. Additional in vitro and in vivo studies, as well as validation using animal models, are necessary to facilitate the translation of miR-7974 from basic research to clinical applications in LN. This may facilitate substantial progress in the diagnosis and treatment of LN.

Conclusion

The results of this study indicate that miR-7974 is highly expressed in blood samples from patients with LN and LPS-induced HRMCs. The knockdown of miR-7974 significantly inhibits cell proliferation and the inflammatory response. Additionally, DDAH1 directly binds to miR-7974, and miR-7974 plays a role in the progression of LN by negatively regulating DDAH1. The miR-7974/DDAH1 axis may serve as a non-invasive biomarker and a potential therapeutic target for the diagnosis of LN.

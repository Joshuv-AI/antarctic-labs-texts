Visualization of residual gaps after linear ablation using the LUMIPOINTTM module: A case report
Authors: Yasuyuki Egami, Yasuharu Matsunaga-Lee, Masamichi Yano, Masami Nishino
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMacroreentrant atrial tachycardia (ATs) through epicardial conduction is depicted as a focal AT on 3-D mapping, i.e., pseudo-focal AT. A new feature of the Rhythmia mapping system (Boston Scientific), the "LUMIPOINT module", can highlight all electrocardiograms (EGMs) above a threshold determined by an adjustable confidence slider (CS). Lowering the CS (L-CS) may highlight undetected electrograms (EGMs) at the nominal CS setting, potentially enabling visualization of the critical isthmus of pseudo-focal ATs.Methods and resultsThis study included 3 ATs after linear ablation of two left atrial roof-dependent ATs (cases 1 and 2) and one peri-mitral flutter (case 3). All ATs were diagnosed as pseudo-focal AT according to an electrophysiological study and the Rhythmia mapping system with the LUMIPOINT module. The L-CS method consisted of the following steps: 1. Set the LUMIPOINT activation window to the time difference before and after the linear ablation line. 2. Highlight the two regions before and after the linear ablation line. 3. Gradually lower the CS value from the nominal setting of 85 % by 5–10 %. By the L-CS method in cases 1–3, the 2-sided highlighted areas before and after the prior linear ablation lesion gradually expanded and eventually fused. EGMs at the fusion sites of the highlighted areas exhibited fragmented EGMs with a low voltage, where a single-shot ablation terminated the targeted ATs.ConclusionThe L-CS method was useful for the visualization of residual gaps and identification of targeted ablation sites in cases of pseudo-focal AT after linear ablation of macroreentrant ATs.

Introduction

Atrial tachycardia (AT) following atrial fibrillation ablation is often caused by electrical gaps within the prior linear lesions [1]. Such gaps are commonly observed in damaged tissue with low voltage areas [2], and are infrequently visualized on the 3-D map. The Rhythmia mapping system (Boston Scientific, Cambridge, MA, USA) with a multi-electrode basket catheter, comprised of 64 mini-electrodes (very small electrodes with a narrow inter-electrode spacing: 0.4 mm2; 2.5 mm center-to-center spacing) improves the electrogram (EGM) resolution for activation mapping and enables a rapid and accurate identification of the circuit of ATs [3]. However, the Rhythmia mapping system may not accurately represent the conduction of long and fragmented potentials in the activation map because it is designed to consistently annotate somewhere in each EGM. The recently developed “LUMIPOINT module” in the Rhythmia mapping system can highlight all deflections above a threshold, which is adjustable through the confidence slider (CS). Lowering the CS value (L-CS) has the potential to unmask subtle cardiac activations undetected with the nominal CS setting. Therefore, we hypothesized that L-CS can visualize residual conduction gaps after linear ablation of macroreentrant ATs.

Methods

Study population

This study included 3 ATs with a focal AT pattern on re-mapping after a failed linear ablation of left atrial (LA) macroreentrant ATs. They were diagnosed as pseudo-focal ATs, defined as ATs that met all of the following criteria: 1. A centrifugal propagation during ultrahigh-resolution mapping, 2. A missing tachycardia cycle length (TCL) > 10 %, 3. A breakthrough site located more than 10 mm from the previous ablation line. 4. A post-pacing interval (PPI), after entrainment pacing at a pacing cycle length of 10–20 msec shorter than the TCL at ≥ 3 different sites [4]. This study was approved by our institutional review committee (2023–63). All patients provided written informed consent.

Ablation procedure

All patients received appropriate oral anticoagulant therapy for at least more than 3 weeks before the ablation procedure. Anticoagulation was continued to minimize the risk of thrombi formation or embolic events. Antiarrhythmic drugs were discontinued for at least more than 5 half-lives before the procedure. All procedures were performed under deep sedation using a combination of propofol and dexmedetomidine. Transesophageal echocardiography was performed to detect any thrombi in the LA before the procedure.

After the completion of the vascular access, heparin was administered at 100 IU/kg. An activated clotting time of 300–400 seconds was maintained during the procedure. A trans-septal puncture was performed using a radiofrequency needle (Baylis Medical) and two long sheaths were inserted into the LA. A 20‐pole catheter (BeeAT, Japan Lifeline) was inserted into the coronary sinus through the right jugular vein and a 10-pole catheter was placed on the tricuspid valve annulus as needed.

Ultrahigh-resolution mapping

The electroanatomic mapping was performed with the Rhythmia mapping system using an IntellaMap Orion catheter. The beat acceptance criteria were used as follows: 1) cycle length stability, 2) relative timing of a reference electrogram positioned in the coronary sinus, 3) mapping catheter motion, 4) electrogram stability, 5) catheter tracking quality, and 6) respiration gating. Among the acquired points, the points within 2mm of a surface geometry were employed in the activation map. The confidence mask level was set at 0.03 mV. Bipolar electrograms were filtered at 30–300 Hz and unipolar electrograms were set at a default of 1–300 Hz.

Confidence slider of the LUMIPOINT module

The LUMIPOINT module detects all deflections above the baseline noise level with a unique consistency, taking into account the spatiotemporal consistency, to distinguish true EGMs from artificial noise or far-field potentials and highlights them on the activation map. If a deflection exceeds a user-specified certainty, it is considered a cardiac activation [5]. The certainty level, adjustable through the CS, allows for flexible customization. Generally, the CS value is set within 85–100 %.

Method of lowering the confidence slider (L-CS)

Initially, compute the time difference in the local activation time between the latest site (site A) preceding the ablation line and the earliest site (site B) succeeding the ablation line, and set it as the LUMIPOINT activation window (LAW) (Fig. 1-Step 1). Subsequently, simultaneously highlight both sites A and B on the LUMIPOINT activation map with the LAW set (Fig. 1-Step 2). Thirdly, gradually lower the CS value from the nominal setting of 85 % by 5–10 % increments (Fig. 1-Step 3, left panel). Therefore, these regions may contain fragmented potentials with a low-voltage, which have been associated with the critical isthmus of the AT (Fig. 1-Step 3, right panel) [2].

Case

Cases 1 & 2. A 78-year-old man (case 1) and an 80-year-old man (case 2) presented with a recurrent AT post-linear ablation of a roof-dependent AT. During the AT, activation maps (tachycardia cycle length [TCL]: 287 ms for case 1 and 230 ms for case 2) revealed a centrifugal pattern with the earliest activation site distant from the previously ablated linear lesion on the LA roof (Fig. 2A/3A and Supplemental movie 1/2). Employing the L-CS method, the two-sided HAs progressively expanded and fused at a CS value of 65 % in case 1 (Fig. 2B) and 55 % in case 2 (Fig. 3B). Upon analyzing the localized EGMs within the fusion sites of the HAs (Fig. 2C/3C), long fragmented EGMs with a low amplitude were observed (Fig. 2D/3D). Discussion

This case report demonstrated the efficacy of the L-CS method for the visualization of residual conduction gaps of pseudo-focal ATs following linear ablation.

L-CS method for pseudo-focal ATs following linear ablation

The EGMs originating from the critical isthmus of scar-related ATs commonly exhibit a low amplitude and delayed conduction (fragmented EGMs) [2]. Nonetheless, the accurate delineation of fragmented EGMs on the activation map poses a challenge, potentially leading to a misdiagnosis of the underlying mechanism of the AT. The recently developed “LUMIPOINT module” in the Rhythmia mapping system can highlight all activation times of the detectable EGMs above a threshold. The LUMIPOINT module is used with the CS, a function that adjusts the tolerance for consistency with surrounding points, set from 85 % to 100 %. Lowering the CS value increases the number of the EGMs displayed on the map but may also show EGMs inconsistent with the surrounding points. EGMs associated with the critical isthmus of scar-related AT are often shown as low and fragmented potential [2], differing from the EGMs surrounding normal tissue. Although LUMIPOINT at nominal CS settings often does not display these EGMs, L-CS method can reveal the activation of such EGMs on the activation map. The L-CS method highlights cardiac signals in diastole. Mid-diastolic potentials during AT are frequently associated with ablation target site [6]. Therefore, diastolic potentials within the new HAs emerged between the earliest site (site A) and latest site (site B) in systole by the L-CL method are likely to be associated with gap sites on the ablation line.

Hojo et al. demonstrated the successful visualization of epicardial conduction via the Marshall bundle in case with pseudo-focal ATs of the LA using the L-CS method [7]. The mechanisms underlying pseudo-focal ATs are due to residual conduction gaps or epicardial‐endocardial breakthroughs [4,8,9]. The L-CS method can be useful for visualizing the critical isthmus of pseudo-focal ATs.

Limitations

Excessively lowering the CS value may highlight signals other than true local potentials, potentially leading to a misinterpretation of the gap site. Although the L-CS method is valuable for detecting EGMs associated with residual gap sites, it remains unclear whether these EGMs indicate endocardial, intramural, or epicardial conduction. The sample size in this study is too small to assess the efficacy of the L-CS method in identifying the gap site after linear ablations for macroreentrant ATs. Therefore, further studies with a large number cases are needed to confirm the efficacy of the L-CS method.

Conclusion

The L-CS method is useful for the visualization of residual gaps and identification of targeted ablation sites in cases with pseudo-focal ATs after linear ablation of macroreentrant ATs.

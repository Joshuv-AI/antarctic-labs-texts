Clinical real-world data evaluation: comparison of central macular thickness after anti-VEGF treatment in diabetic macular edema patients treated with or without topical anti-glaucoma agents
Authors: Hakan Baybora, Ece Özal, Sadık Altan Özal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThere are research findings suggesting that antiglaucoma agents may reduce aqueous humor outflow and decrease the elimination of intravitreally administered anti-vascular endothelial growth factor agents, prolonging their effect. The aim of this study was to evaluate central macular thickness (CMT) after repeated injections, using real-world data, to provide insight into how this phenomenon may affect clinical outcomes.MethodsOne hundred forty-seven patients with diabetic macular edema (DME) were included in the study;66 patients received topical therapy due to pre-existing glaucoma, either primary open- angle or pseudoexfoliative glaucoma, and 81 patients without glaucoma. CMT values were evaluated before treatment, 1 month after the first three injections, 1 month after the subsequent four injections, and at the end of the first year.ResultsNo significant differences were detected with respect to HbA1c levels, the presence of SRF, or prior photocoagulation (p = 0.085, p = 0.24, and p = 0.3, respectively). There was no difference between anti-glaucoma agent users and control group in CMT measurements 1 month after the first three injections (p = 0.6, p > 0.05). However, measurements taken 1 month after the subsequent four injections were significantly lower in patients using topical anti-glaucoma agents (p = 0.001, p < 0.05). At the 1-year measurement interval, although the values remained lower, the significant difference had disappeared (p = 0.11, p > 0.05). The presence of hyper reflective dots (HRD) was also greater in this group (p = 0.021, p < 0.05). At the 1-year measurement interval, although the values remained lower, the significant difference had disappeared (p = 0.11, p > 0.05). The presence of HRD and the combined presence of SRF and HRD had a significant effect on the changes in the measurements (F (4.412), p = 0.006, Eta2 = 0.103 and F (4.51), p = 0.001, Eta2 = 0.106, respectively).ConclusionAlthough the use of topical anti-glaucoma therapy may have a partial effect on reduction of CMT in DME patients receiving anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) treatment.

Introduction

Diabetic macular edema DME is an important public health problem affecting an increasing number of patients with diabetes as industrialization progresses [1], and it is a leading cause of visual loss in these patients [2].

DME, characterized by exudative fluid accumulation in the macula, is a serious manifestation of diabetic retinopathy (DR) and causes central visual loss in patients with DR [3]. Approximately 7% of patients with diabetes are affected by DME, and the risk increases with the severity of DR [4]. Because vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is one of the most important factors in the pathophysiology of edema, intravitreally administered anti-VEGF agents are the first-line treatment in current practice.

However, real-world outcomes following anti-VEGF therapy for DME lag behind those reported in clinical trials [5, 6].

The need for frequent treatment visits has been associated with nonadherence to anti-VEGF therapy and subsequent worsening of visual outcomes [7]. Studies have been conducted to reduce the number of injections and visits and aiming to ensure treatment effectiveness [8, 9].

Anti-VEGF agents injected into the eye are not normally metabolized or degraded; rather, they are primarily cleared from the vitreous cavity via anterior routes [10]. Accordingly, anti-VEGF molecules diffuse passively into the aqueous humor. Aqueous circulation is maintained by a balance between secretion and clearance through the trabecular meshwork and the suprachoroidal pathway. Some topical anti-glaucoma medications suppress aqueous humor secretion. Inoda et al. demonstrated that the amount of aflibercept in the aqueous humor was reduced in individuals receiving topical anti-glaucoma therapy, particularly aqueous-suppressant agents, for glaucoma patients who were receiving intravitreal anti-VEGF treatment for age-related macular degeneration. This finding raises the possibility of more effective treatments and potentially fewer injections.

[11] In the present study, we compared CMT over a 1-year follow-up in patients with DME who were receiving intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy and who were or were not using topical anti-glaucoma therapy presenting real-world clinical data on this issue.

Materials and methods

The study procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital (IRB:2025.283). The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all patients.

At the beginning of the study, 68 patients using anti-glaucomatous medication were enrolled. However, during follow-up, two patients in this group were excluded after presenting with elevated intraocular pressure.

In the control group, although 84 patients were initially included, one patient was suspended from the injection regimen for six months following the development of ischemic heart disease. Additionally, two other patients in this group were excluded due to non-compliance with the injection protocol.

Consequently, 147 patients with DME who were included in this observational cohort study.

The patient admission was conducted between January 1 and February 1 of 2025. Follow-up was ended in January 10 of 2026. Among these patients, 66 were receiving topical anti-glaucoma therapy and their intra ocular pressures were medically controlled under 21mmHg. Only patients with primary open-angle glaucoma or pseudo exfoliative glaucoma who were receiving topical anti-glaucoma therapy were included in the glaucoma group.

They were using topical beta-blockers, prostaglandin analogs as a single medication or a combination of topical beta-blockers, alpha agonists, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, and prostaglandin analogs. For ease of presentation, this group was designated Group (1) Eighty-one patients were enrolled in the control group. This group was designated Group (2) Patients with macular edema of causes other than DME and patients who had previously undergone intraocular surgery other than cataract surgery were excluded. Patients with neovascular glaucoma, primary angle-closure glaucoma, or secondary glaucoma were excluded from the study. Routine ophthalmic examinations were performed. Fluorescein angiographies were obtained using via a Canon Digital Camera CR-AF2 (Canon Medical Systems, Netherlands). Patients with neovascularization on the optic disk or in the peripheral retin

a on angiography were classified as having proliferative DR; the others were classified as having nonproliferative DR. Panretinal argon laser photocoagulation was performed in patients with proliferative diabetic retinopathy or those with macular edema accompanied by extensive areas of retinal ischemia. No grid or focal laser applications were performed to enhance the effect of intravitreal anti-VEGF treatments on edema. The best corrected visual acuities were assessed using EDTRS chart.

In the country (Turkey) where this study was conducted, the Ministry of Health recommends that hospitals initiate DME treatment with three monthly intravitreal bevacizumab injections and then allow ophthalmologists to switch to other licensed anti-VEGF agents at their discretion (Health Ministry of Turkey, Health Practice Circular 4.2.33). Patients in both groups were treatment-naïve regarding prior intravitreal therapy. Individuals who had previously received intravitreal injections for any reason were excluded from the study. Thereafter, four injections of ranibizumab or aflibercept were administered at 1-month intervals for all patients. In our clinic, if fluid was detected via optical coherence tomography (OCT) one month after these injections, monthly injections were continued until the macula was dry. If no fluid was detected, an injection was given, and OCT was repeated at eight weeks; if fluid remained absent an injection was given and OCT was repeated at twelve weeks; if fluid was still not detected the patient was recalled 16 weeks after the injection. The injection doses were 1.25 mg for bevacizumab (Altuzan 400 mg/16 mL, Roche, Germany), 2 mg for aflibercept (Eylea 40 mg/mL, Bayer AG, Germany), and 0.5 mg for ranibizumab. Injections were performed in the operating theatre. The injection site was wiped with an antiseptic solution, and a sterile drape was placed. Then, 5% povidone-iodine solution was instilled into the conjunctival sac and left in place for three minutes. The sac was then irrigated with physiological saline and injections were administered 3.5 mm posterior to the limbus in the superior or inferior temporal quadrant in pseudophakic eyes and 4.0 mm posterior to the limbus in phakic eyes.

OCT

OCT images were obtained via a Triton Swept-Source OCT (Topcon Healthcare, Japan). The device uses a 1050 nm wavelength light source and acquires 100,000 A-scans per second. After baseline macular OCT imaging, OCT was performed 1 month after the three anti-VEGF loading doses, 1 month after the subsequent four on-label anti-VEGF injections, and at the 1-year follow-up. In addition to CMT, on base line OCT, hyperreflective dots (HRD), and subretinal fluids (SRF) were assessed. HRDs were defined as discrete punctate, non-shadowing lesions, with a diameter between 20 and 40 microns in the outer plexiform layer, observed central 1500 microns of the OCT. SRF was defined hypo reflective space between neurosensory retina and retinal pigment epithelium in central 1500 microns of the image. Images were independently quantified by two masked observers for each image.

Statistical analysis

Parametric and nonparametric tests were used for age, HbA1c levels, and CMT measurements as appropriate. Chi-square tests were applied for categorical variables including sex, prior laser treatment, and presence or absence of SRF and HRD. Multifactor repeated-measures ANCOVA was used to assess the effects of laser, HbA1c, SRF, and HRD on the outcomes. Professional support was obtained from the Department of Biostatistics, Faculty of Medicine, Health Sciences University. All analyses were conducted via IBM SPSS Statistics 24.0 (Chicago, Illinois, USA).

Results

Across both groups there were a total of 62 female and 85 male patients, and the mean age was 63.31 ± 8.07years. At the end of the first year, an average of 8.5± 0.26 injections were administered to Group 1 and 8.3±0.29 injections were given to Group 2 (p = 0.16, p > 0.05). Twenty-two patients in Group 1 and 24 patients in Group 2 had hypertension in addition to diabetes (p = 0.16, p > 0.05). There were no significant differences between Group 1 and Group 2 in terms of age, sex, or pretreatment CMT measurements (Table 1). There was also no significant difference in HbA1c levels between the groups. After three loading doses of bevacizumab in both groups, Ranibizumab was injected into 50 patients and aflibercept into 16 in Group 1, while these were 64 and 17, respectively, in Group 2 (p = 0.89, p > 0.05). Although there was no significant difference in CMT measurements one month after the three loading doses of bevacizumab, a statistically significant advantage in favor of Group 1 was observed after the subsequent four on-label anti-VEGF injections; this difference was not maintained at the end of the first year (Table 2).

In the present study, Group 1 included 38 pseudophakic, 10 phakic, and 18 patients who underwent cataract surgery. The control group included 44 pseudophakic, 22 phakic, and 15 patients who underwent surgery during follow-up.

In Group 1, four patients used only a beta-blocker, five patients used only a prostaglandin analog, three patients used a prostaglandin analog together with an alpha-agonist, nine patients used a beta-blocker–carbonic anhydrase inhibitor combination, three patients used a beta-blocker–carbonic anhydrase inhibitor combination together with a prostaglandin analog, and 26 patients used an alpha-agonist plus a beta-blocker–carbonic anhydrase inhibitor combination plus a prostaglandin analog.

ANCOVA revealed the presence of HRD and the combined presence of SRF and HRD as strong factors influencing changes in CMT (F (4.412), p = 0.006, Eta² = 0.103 and F (4.51), p = 0.001, Eta² = 0.104, respectively).

Discussion

Anti-VEGF therapies are currently the most effective treatment for DME, because they achieve high intravitreal concentrations. These agents differ in charge and molecular weight, properties that play an important roles in their vitreous distribution and elimination [12]. Hyaluronic acid in the vitreous space has a net negative charge, which can restrict the diffusion of positively charged molecules [13]. Bevacizumab and ranibizumab both possess a negative charge, whereas aflibercept is characterized by a slight positive charge. However, given the substantial molecular weight of these compounds, such variations in surface charge are negligible.

There are factors that affect drug elimination from the vitreous. These include whether the eye has undergone vitreous surgery and the properties of the administered drug. There are divergent opinions in the literature regarding drug elimination from surgically treated eyes. Laude et al. have suggested that vitreous drugs may be cleared more rapidly in eyes that have undergone cataract surgery [14]. However, Krohne et al. reported that eye volume and lens status had no significant effect on the ocular pharmacokinetics or duration of action of anti-VEGF drugs when they compared the duration of VEGF suppression in phakic and pseudophakic human eyes [15].(refreased) In order to exclude a potential confounding factor, we excluded patients with prior vitrectomies.

Pro re nata (PRN) and treat-and-extend (T&E) are intravitreal dosing regimens designed to achieve and maintain therapeutic drug concentrations within the vitreous cavity. In the PRN approach, injections are given only when reactivation occurs, whereas in T&E, the emphasis is on preventing reactivation; T&E is both effective and requires fewer injections, and is presently the preferred treatment regimen [16]. If no activation is observed after loading doses, the treatment interval is extended; if activation is observed the interval is shortened. In the present study, this treatment regimen was applied to both groups.

It has been suggested that the elimination of anti-VEGFs can occur via the retina and the anterior chamber. Elimination studies of bevacizumab in rabbit eyes have shown that the process occurs via both pathways [17]. In human studies, the retinal route has been reported to play a greater role [18]. This may explain the lack of difference in CMT between the two groups after the initial three bevacizumab loading doses in our cohort. Byeon et al. similarly reported that a timolol-dorzolamide combination produced a significantly greater reduction in edema at 5 weeks than did controls in patients with retinal vein occlusion treated with bevacizumab, but that this difference had disappeared by week 9 [19]. In another study, ranibizumab was found to be eliminated predominantly via the anterior chamber [20].

In a rabbit model, aflibercept elimination was also shown to occur mainly via the anterior chamber [21].

CMT values after the four on-label anti-VEGF injections administered following the loading doses were significantly lower (p = 0.001, p > 0.05) in patients using antiglaucoma medications. However, this significance was lost at the 1-year measurements. Nevertheless, lower CMT values were observed at the end of study. A significant presence of HRD was detected in patients in Group 1, and the combined presence of SRF and HRD correlated significantly with the measurements. This finding indicates a greater burden of inflammatory OCT markers in Group 1 than in Group2, suggesting that intravitreal corticosteroid injections might yield more effective results in these patients [22]. However, the concomitant glaucoma in these patients limits the use of intravitreal steroids, which may have prevented a significant difference. The frequent application of ranibizumab was associated with a low risk of endophthalmitis for the PSF form [23]. The Aflibercept PSF arrived in the researcher’s country shortly before the conclusion of this study. No difference was observed in the number of injections between groups; for a follow-up period of one year this is not unexpected, and longer follow-up is needed.

One potential limitation of the present study is the greater prevalence of patients with inflammatory characteristics in the anti-glaucoma group. Patients were selected at random. Also, this may be related with limited number of patients. Clinical studies on present topic are very limited. New pharmacokinetic studies have highlighted the necessity of conducting clinical research. Studies with larger case series would be more informative. In addition, because only five patients received prostaglandin analog monotherapy, comparative analysis of the efficacy of different drugs and combinations was not possible. We were unable to assess which medication resulted in a greater reduction in central macular thickness (CMT) because the number of patients using these medications individually during the specified patient enrollment period was quite limited.

Anti-VEGF treatment guidelines of diabetic macula edema may change from country to country. Therefore, more clinical research is needed.

Previous studies have been conducted mainly in patients with age-related macular degeneration. We believe that, as one of the studies performed in patients with diabetic macular edema, our results provide important insights into the potential impact of topical anti-glaucoma medications on the effectiveness of treatment.

Impact of cloud-based follow-up exercise prescription on knee function and quality of life in homebound patients post-total knee arthroplasty: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Weiqiang Chen, Xianglan Qin, Haoze Yang, Zhen Tan, Pei Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the impact of a cloud-based follow-up exercise prescription on knee function and quality of life in patients undergoing home-based rehabilitation following total knee arthroplasty (TKA), thereby informing the application of telemedicine in post-TKA home rehabilitation.MethodsA randomized controlled trial was conducted from February 2023 to January 2024. Patients were divided into a control group and an intervention group according to a random number table. Both groups performed rehabilitation exercises based on exercise prescriptions. After discharge, the control group received follow-up via conventional methods, while the intervention group was monitored using a cloud-based follow-up system. Independent samples t-tests were employed to compare the two patient cohorts with respect to knee function, quality of life, adherence to rehabilitation exercises, and total follow-up costs.ResultsOf the 164 patients initially enrolled, 151 completed the 12-week follow-up and functional exercises: 75 in the control group and 76 in the intervention group. At 2 and 4 weeks post-surgery, the intervention group demonstrated superior knee function and adherence to rehabilitation exercises compared to the control group (all P < 0.05). At 4 weeks post-surgery, the intervention group also exhibited a higher quality of life (P < 0.05). At 12 weeks post-surgery, no significant differences were observed between the two groups in knee function, quality of life, or adherence to rehabilitation exercises. The total follow-up cost for the intervention group was lower than that of the control group (p < 0.05).ConclusionsCloud-based follow-up exercise prescriptions can improve knee function and quality of life in home-based patients in the early stages (within 4 weeks) of rehabilitation exercises after TKA, which is of clinical significance.Trial registrationChiCTR2200067031, registered 23 December 2022, https://www.chictr.org.cn/bin/project/edit?pid=185737.

Background

Total knee arthroplasty (TKA) is an effective intervention for end-stage knee osteoarthritis [1], improving patients’ knee joint function. The prevalence of knee osteoarthritis is increasing significantly due to the aging population and the rise of pro-inflammatory conditions like obesity in China, leading to an annual growth of nearly 20% in TKA surgeries [2]. This surge strains hospital resources, prompting a shift from hospital-based rehabilitation to home-based exercise rehabilitation. Research indicates that when athletic performance is enhanced, home-based exercise rehabilitation can further optimize postoperative knee joint function following total knee arthroplasty [3]. However, many patients undergoing home-based rehabilitation experience reduced adherence and non-standardized exercise due to a lack of professional guidance and effective supervision, ultimately hindering effective treatment outcomes [4, 5]. With the rapid development of internet technology and the promotion of telemedicine services, remote rehabilitation models that enhance home-based exercise through video conferencing, automated text messaging, mobile applications, virtual reality technology, and wearable devices have shown advantages over traditional face-to-face rehabilitation in improving joint function and quality of life for TKA patients, enhancing rehabilitation adherence, and reducing time and medical costs [6–10]. Cloud follow-up systems can leverage hospital integrated platforms to develop targeted health management pathways based on patient information obtained during in-hospital treatment, thereby improving patient health management behaviors. Cloud-based follow-up systems, as a modality of telerehabilitation, have demonstrated significant efficacy in managing chronic diseases among patients in home settings [11–13]. However, the effectiveness of cloud follow-up systems in post-TKA home rehabilitation patients remains unknown, necessitating further research to provide evidence for evidence-based practice. Exercise prescription, proposed by the American College of Sports Medicine in 1975 [14], is a systematically planned exercise program based on an individual’s health status and scientific evidence, designed to improve or maintain health-related physical fitness [15], and is widely used in orthopedics, cardiovascular diseases, and other fields.

This study aims to improve adherence to rehabilitation exercises by combining cloud-based follow-up with exercise prescriptions to implement exercise interventions, exploring the impact of this intervention on knee function and quality of life in post-TKA home rehabilitation patients, and providing a reference for the application of telemedicine in post-TKA home rehabilitation.

Materials and methods

Participants

A prospective, single-blind, randomized controlled trial with two parallel groups was conducted at a tertiary-care, Grade A hospital in Shenzhen, China. Patients undergoing primary unilateral total knee arthroplasty (TKA) between February 2023 and January 2024 were recruited using a random sampling method. Eligible subjects were randomly assigned to either a control or intervention group on the day of discharge. Inclusion criteria comprised: age between 45 and 75 years; primary unilateral TKA for end-stage knee osteoarthritis [16]; clear consciousness, no cognitive impairment, ability to use a smartphone, and adequate reading, communication, and comprehension skills; and voluntary participation with informed consent. Exclusion criteria included: severe organ disease or malignancy affecting outcome measures; concomitant lower limb bone diseases; history of psychiatric illness or Alzheimer’s disease; and recent participation in other clinical trials. Termination criteria encompassed: periprosthetic fracture, surgical site infection, or hematoma during follow-up, affecting rehabilitation outcomes; incomplete data hindering outcome assessment; development of severe comorbidities precluding continued follow-up; and voluntary withdrawal from the study. The study protocol was approved by the hospital’s Research Ethics Committee (Approval No.: [2022](139th)) and registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200067031). This study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Randomization

A 1:1 allocation ratio, simple randomization was performed using a computerized random number generator by a non-study team member. The allocation sequence was concealed by the investigators using identical, serially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes. On the day of discharge, a sealed envelope was opened by the investigator to assign the subject to the designated group.

Blinding

Blinding was implemented for both subjects and assessors. Blinding of the intervention providers was not feasible due to the necessity for researchers to guide subjects on post-discharge follow-up protocols. Research assistants, blinded to the allocation, collected all outcome data.

Nursing methods

Both patient cohorts underwent rehabilitation exercises based on an exercise prescription. The exercise prescription was established based on the activity recommendations following total hip and knee arthroplasty published by Fortier in 2021 [17], in conjunction with the expert consensus on perioperative rehabilitation intervention for total knee arthroplasty in China and relevant literature. It also referenced the guidelines for exercise testing and exercise prescription developed by the American College of Sports Medicine [15] and was determined after consultation with orthopedic and rehabilitation experts. In the first week post-operation, patients performed quadriceps isometric contraction exercises, ankle pump exercises, knee flexion and extension exercises, and straight leg raises, with 5 sets per day, 10 repetitions per set, and each hold lasting 5 s, to restore knee range of motion and assist with ambulation. From weeks 2 to 4 post-operation, based on the exercises from the previous stage, single-leg balance exercises (5 sets per day, 10 repetitions per set, each lasting 10 s) and step-up exercises (3 times per day, 15 min each time) were added to improve gait and increase knee flexibility. From weeks 5 to 12 post-operation, step-up exercises, squat-to-stand exercises (5 sets per day, 10 repetitions per set, each lasting 10 s), and resistance training (5 sets per day, 30 repetitions per set) were performed to enhance muscle strength and cardiopulmonary function. The resistance band is made of TPE material, measuring 2080 mm in length, 13 mm in width, and 4.5 mm in thickness, with a resistance level of 36 pounds. Patients may also opt to place a 1 kg weight on the ankle and perform straight leg raises. Exercise intensity was based on the target heart rate [15], and individualized adjustments were made according to the target heart rate. During exercise, reaching the target heart rate was considered safe, and exercise should be terminated if the target heart rate was exceeded. Target heart rate = [(maximum heart rate - resting heart rate) × (0.6∼0.8) + resting heart rate]. We recommend patients use the Yuwell finger clip pulse oximeter (medical-grade, capable of monitoring blood oxygen saturation and heart rate) for self-monitoring of their heart rate. On the day of discharge, the responsible nurse provided patients with detailed discharge instructions, including the execution of the exercise prescription, observation of complications, medication guidance, regular follow-up, and postoperative precautions, and distributed a home rehabilitation exercise manual to the patients. The manual was developed based on the exercise prescription, with easy-to-understand content and a combination of pictures and text, making it convenient for elderly patients to read. After discharge, both patient cohorts performed rehabilitation exercises at home.

The control group underwent conventional follow-up, which included a telephone review at two weeks post-operation, followed by outpatient visits at four, eight, and twelve weeks. These follow-ups assessed adherence to exercise prescriptions, guidance on home-based rehabilitation exercises, medication instructions, and the completion of questionnaires evaluating rehabilitation outcomes. Furthermore, patient inquiries regarding post-discharge issues were addressed to enhance compliance with rehabilitation exercises and improve rehabilitation outcomes. The intervention group utilized a cloud-based follow-up system. The cloud-based follow-up system was developed by Hangzhou JoinHralth Technology Co., Ltd. (ICP filing number: Zhe ICP Bei 15022398 number-15 A). The system comprises six components: patient records, patient management, follow-up management, knowledge base, communication channels, and personal center (See Fig. 1). Patient records include details such as patient admission number, surgical information, examination reports, and laboratory reports. The knowledge base encompasses disease information, medication data, health education materials, reminder phrases, and exercise guidance. Follow-up management allows for questionnaire-based follow-up, while the knowledge base provides rehabilitation exercise reminders and guidance. Communication channels facilitate nurse-patient interactions, and the personal center enables users to update basic information, set passwords, and bind mobile devices. This system delivered exercise prescriptions in the form of images, text, and videos at two, four, eight, and twelve weeks post-operation, reminding patients to perform home-based rehabilitation exercises to improve their adherence and mitigate the “forgetting curve” associated with conventional health education. Furthermore, the system facilitated doctor-patient communication, provided guidance on home-based rehabilitation exercises and medication, and administered patient-reported outcome measures to promptly identify and address post-discharge issues, thereby guiding patients in correcting and improving their rehabilitation practices.

Primary outcome measures

Knee function was assessed preoperatively and at two, four, and twelve weeks postoperatively using the Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC) [18] in both patient groups. This scale, encompassing pain, stiffness, and joint function, comprises 24 items, with a total score of 240 points; lower scores indicate better knee function recovery. The Cronbach’s α for each dimension of this scale ranged from 0.67 to 0.82 [19]. Quality of life was evaluated preoperatively and at four and twelve weeks postoperatively using the Short Form-36 Health Survey (SF-36) [20] in both groups. This survey includes eight health concepts: physical function, role-physical, bodily pain, general health, vitality, social function, role-emotional, and mental health, with a total of 36 items and a maximum score of 100 points; higher scores reflect better quality of life. The Cronbach’s α for each dimension of this scale ranged from 0.73 to 0.96 [21].

Secondary outcome measures

Rehabilitation exercise adherence was assessed in both groups at two, four, and twelve weeks postoperatively using the Orthopedic Patient Exercise Adherence Scale, developed by Tan in 2019. This scale encompasses three domains: psychological exercise adherence, active learning-related exercise adherence, and physical exercise adherence, comprising a total of 15 items. The maximum score is 75, with higher scores indicating better adherence. This scale was validated among 1,024 orthopedic postoperative patients in Heilongjiang Province, including those who underwent total knee arthroplasty and total hip arthroplasty, with Cronbach’s α coefficients for each dimension ranging from 0.84 to 0.92. Total follow-up costs were collected for both groups at the 12-week follow-up to serve as an economic benefit indicator.

Statistical analysis

This study utilized the WOMAC score as a primary outcome measure, with sample size calculations performed using PASS 11 software. Based on the results of a 2021 randomized controlled trial by an Australian orthopedic team [8], a mean difference of 5.2 units in the WOMAC score was selected for this study. With a significance level of α = 0.05 and a power of β = 0.85, the calculated sample size was determined to be a minimum of 73 patients per group. Accounting for a 10% dropout rate, a total of 82 patients per group were required. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 23.0 software. WOMAC scores, SF-36 scores, adherence to functional exercise scores, and total follow-up costs were all continuous variables that followed a normal distribution and were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (Mean ± SD). Independent samples t-tests were used for between-group comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Results

Patient recruitment and demographic characteristics

Following recruitment, 164 patients were randomized into either the control or intervention group. During the 12-week follow-up period, eight patients were lost to follow-up due to incomplete questionnaires, and five patients discontinued the study due to severe complications: one patient experienced cerebral infarction; two patients developed heart failure; one patient sustained a periprosthetic fracture from a fall; and one patient suffered a pulmonary embolism. A total of 151 patients completed the study (see Fig. 2). The control group comprised 75 patients with a mean age of 66.19 years (range: 50–75 years). The intervention group included 76 patients with a mean age of 67.49 years (range: 51–75 years). Baseline characteristics, including age, gender, body mass index, education level, surgical site, history of anxiety or depression, history of chronic diseases, and social support, were comparable between the two groups, with no statistically significant differences observed (P > 0.05), as detailed in Table 1.

Primary outcome measures of knee function and quality of life

Baseline WOMAC scores demonstrated comparability between the groups. Progressive improvement in knee function was observed in both cohorts from baseline to the 12-week follow-up. At both the 2-week and 4-week postoperative assessments, the intervention group exhibited significantly lower WOMAC scores compared to the control group, indicating superior knee function (p < 0.05). At the 12-week follow-up, while the intervention group’s WOMAC scores remained lower than the control group’s, the difference did not reach statistical significance. Detailed results are presented in Table 2. The preoperative SF-36 scores were comparable between the two patient groups. From baseline to the 12-week follow-up, there was a progressive improvement in the patients’ quality of life. At 4 weeks postoperatively, the intervention group’s SF-36 scores were significantly higher than the control group’s, indicating a superior quality of life in the intervention group (p < 0.05). At 12 weeks postoperatively, although the intervention group’s SF-36 scores were lower than the control group’s, the difference was not statistically significant. Detailed results are presented in Table 3.

Discussion

This randomized clinical trial demonstrated that a cloud-based follow-up exercise prescription improved knee function and quality of life in the early rehabilitation phase (within 4 weeks) for patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty (TKA) at home, with clinical significance. In this study, 151 of 164 patients completed 12-week follow-up and functional exercises. We found that the intervention group showed superior knee function and adherence to rehabilitation exercises compared to the control group at 2 weeks and 4 weeks post-operation (both p < 0.05). At 4 weeks post-operation, the intervention group’s quality of life was superior to the control group (p < 0.05). At 12 weeks post-operation, there were no significant differences between the two groups in knee function, quality of life, and adherence to rehabilitation exercises. The total follow-up cost of the intervention group was lower than that of the control group after the follow-up, and the difference was statistically significant.

Total knee arthroplasty (TKA) involves the surgical implantation of a prosthetic joint to replace the diseased native joint, representing the established standard of care for end-stage knee osteoarthritis, thereby improving both knee function and quality of life [22]. However, the chronic nature of osteoarthritis necessitates that the functional limitations and activity restrictions present pre-operatively are not spontaneously resolved following TKA. To optimize post-operative knee function and quality of life, a systematic home-based rehabilitation program is essential. Low adherence to home-based exercise protocols is a prevalent challenge, primarily influenced by: [1] pain and swelling, which may exacerbate kinesiophobia and deter patient participation in rehabilitation; [2] a lack of understanding regarding the content and methodology of rehabilitation exercises, leading to a diminished appreciation for the significance of home-based programs and, consequently, reduced adherence; [3] inadequate communication between the healthcare provider and the patient, resulting in a lack of professional guidance and supervision, which contributes to poor adherence to home-based exercise; and [4] the absence of personalized home-based rehabilitation plans, which fails to promote patient engagement. Follow-up strategies involving communication, education, supervision, and guidance are effective in enhancing knee function, quality of life, and adherence to rehabilitation exercises in TKA patients post-operatively.

Follow-up is primarily utilized for regular monitoring and observation between medical professionals and patients, facilitating the hospital’s understanding of the patient’s condition and guiding the rehabilitation process. While both domestic and international guidelines offer recommendations for home-based exercise rehabilitation for patients following total knee arthroplasty (TKA), personalized exercise regimens are often lacking. This study aimed to enhance adherence to rehabilitation exercises by formulating individualized and rational exercise prescriptions for TKA patients undergoing home-based rehabilitation. These prescriptions were then integrated with two distinct follow-up methods: conventional follow-up and cloud-based follow-up. The objective was to investigate the impact of these two follow-up approaches on knee joint function and quality of life in patients post-TKA. The findings revealed that both follow-up methods effectively improved knee joint function, quality of life, and adherence to rehabilitation exercises in patients recovering at home after TKA. The intervention group demonstrated superior knee joint function and adherence to rehabilitation exercises at 2 and 4 weeks post-operation compared to the control group. Furthermore, the intervention group exhibited a higher quality of life at 4 weeks post-operation, with all differences showing statistical significance (p < 0.05). These results suggest that exercise prescriptions based on cloud follow-up are more effective in improving knee function, quality of life, and adherence to rehabilitation exercises in patients undergoing home-based rehabilitation after TKA compared to those based on conventional follow-up.

Exercise prescriptions based on cloud follow-up are more effective in improving the rehabilitation outcomes in patients undergoing home-based rehabilitation after TKA compared to those based on conventional follow-up. The main reasons are as follows: [1] Regular distribution of exercise prescriptions in the form of images, text, and videos via telemedicine follow-up serves as a reminder, enabling patients to visually learn and engage in rehabilitation exercises, thereby enhancing adherence to home rehabilitation exercises; [2] Telemedicine follow-up facilitates communication between medical staff and patients, alleviating patients’ fear and resistance, and enabling them to understand the content and methods of rehabilitation exercises, as well as the importance of such exercises; [3] Telemedicine follow-up provides patients with professional rehabilitation guidance and supervision, allowing for the timely identification, correction, and improvement of issues encountered during home rehabilitation exercises [4]. The exercise prescription developed for this study adhered to the principles of individualization and progressive overload, primarily focusing on muscle strength training and range of motion exercises. This approach effectively enhanced patient adherence to the rehabilitation program and improved clinical outcomes. Muscle strength training was implemented to augment the strength of the lower extremity muscle groups, thereby ensuring gait stability. Concurrently, range of motion exercises were employed to mitigate joint adhesions and increase the arc of joint motion.

Conventional follow-up primarily relies on scheduled face-to-face clinic visits and telephone follow-ups, incorporating verbal instruction and the distribution of health education materials to oversee and guide postoperative rehabilitation exercises. These methods are constrained by time and location, and they increase the economic burden on both patients and healthcare providers. Telehealth follow-up, which utilizes internet technology for remote medical services, can monitor and guide patients through postoperative rehabilitation exercises using images, videos, and other methods, thereby overcoming temporal and spatial limitations, representing a highly cost-effective option. Tong L et al. [11] investigated 325 patients with cardiovascular implantable electronic devices (CIEDs) across 13 hospitals in Sichuan Province, revealing that telehealth follow-up mitigated the limitations of outpatient CIED assessment caused by uneven distribution of follow-up clinics and geographical barriers. Dan X et al. [12] evaluated the follow-up outcomes of gynecological chemotherapy patients, demonstrating that telehealth follow-up conserves medical resources, enhances cost-effectiveness, provides patients with ample health education resources, and improves their satisfaction. This study compared the total expenditure associated with both follow-up methods, revealing that the intervention group’s total expenditure was lower than that of the control group, with statistically significant differences (p < 0.05). Consequently, telehealth follow-up exhibits greater economic benefits compared to conventional follow-up.

Despite the absence of robust evidence regarding the efficacy of cloud-based follow-up in home-based rehabilitation for post-TKA patients, numerous studies suggest that remote rehabilitation interventions utilizing smartphone applications yield superior short-term outcomes compared to conventional home-based methods in this population [23]. However, long-term outcomes appear comparable between the two approaches. This disparity may be attributed to heightened patient pain and motivation in the immediate postoperative period, where the reminders, supervision, and effective communication facilitated by cloud-based follow-up enhance adherence to rehabilitation exercises. As time progresses, pain diminishes, and patients may enter a “plateau phase” in their recovery, leading to the neglect of repetitive reminders and supervision within the cloud-based system, thereby diminishing adherence to the prescribed exercise regimen.

This study has the following limitations: the single-center design leads to insufficient sample representativeness, and only patients familiar with smartphone use were included, which may not reflect the general elderly population; no intermediate verification of adherence data was performed, only repeated and detailed explanations of the functional exercise adherence scale were provided in advance, and then the patients were asked to perform the scale assessment; the effects of the two follow-up methods were mainly evaluated from the functional aspect, and the issue of patient satisfaction was not addressed; long-term (e.g., 6 months) effects were not evaluated. Future studies can further verify the value of cloud-based follow-up through multi-center cooperation, intermediate verification of adherence data, the addition of patient satisfaction surveys, and extension of follow-up time.

Conclusions

Home-based rehabilitation exercises following total knee arthroplasty (TKA) are a protracted and specialized process. Cloud-based follow-up exercise prescriptions can improve knee function, quality of life, and adherence to rehabilitation exercises in early-stage (within 4 weeks) home-based patients, which has clinical significance and economic benefits.

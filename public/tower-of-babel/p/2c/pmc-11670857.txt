Brolucizumab in patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration: Real-life outcomes from a tertiary care eye hospital
Authors: Lorenzo Fabozzi, Saad Younis, Sagnik Sen, Inés López-Cuenca, Filomena Palmieri
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Purpose:To report real-world clinical evidence of brolucizumab in treating neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD).Methods:This study included 37 eyes with nAMD treated with intravitreal injections of brolucizumab. The main outcomes were best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) changes, central retinal thickness (CRT), and serious ocular adverse events. Intraretinal fluid (IRF) and subretinal fluid (SRF), subretinal hyperreflective material (SHRM), pigment epithelial detachments (PEDs), hyperreflective foci, macular atrophy, and retinal pigment epithelial tears were evaluated.Results:The mean BCVA of all patients showed a post-treatment value of 0.47 ± 0.33 log of minimum angle of resolution (LogMAR), compared to a baseline measure of 0.50 ± 0.28 LogMAR (P = 0.372). For treatment-naïve patients, a non-statistically significant improvement in BCVA was observed (P = 0.116). Both treatment-naive patients and the entire patient cohort exhibited a statistically significant improvement in the mean CRT after injections. Majority of patients exhibited improvements in optical coherence tomography findings, specifically in the resolution of IRF, SRF, SHRM, and PEDs. Four eyes experienced ocular adverse events in the form of intraocular inflammation.Conclusion:Brolucizumab did not yield a substantial improvement in BCVA, but it exhibited efficacy in reducing CRT in the entire study population and treatment-naive patients with nAMD. Our study identified intraocular inflammation as a significant adverse event with brolucizumab. Thus, precise patient selection, education, and vigilant inflammation monitoring are crucial for patients undergoing this treatment.

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is a degenerative disease affecting the macula, leading to irreversible vision loss by damaging the retina and choriocapillaris.[12] AMD can manifest in non-neovascular (dry) or neovascular (wet) form.[1345] Neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) is characterized by the development of the choroidal neovascularization (CNV) complex, driven by the upregulation of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF).[1267] Several studies have described the efficacy and safety of anti-VEGF therapy for nAMD and indicate that these agents can improve both the structure and function of the retina.[7] Among the newest anti-VEGF molecules available in clinical practice is brolucizumab (Beovu®), a single-chain fragment of 26 kDa antibody, which binds to all isoforms of VEGF-A. Preclinical data supported a longer duration of activity and better penetration in tissues.[89] In a trial with a follow-up of 96 weeks, brolucizumab was shown to provide a vision improvement similar to aflibercept, with better fluid resolution, and a high chance of maintaining the injection frequency at every 12 weeks, from week 48 to 96. Some of these findings may suggest that brolucizumab may lead to better control of nAMD.[810]

The purpose of this study was to analyze and report real-world clinical evidence of using brolucizumab for treating patients with nAMD, either as a primary or as a switch therapy.

Methods

This was a retrospective observational study conducted at a tertiary care eye hospital, in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. We reviewed the anonymized electronical medical records (EMRs) of patients with a diagnosis of nAMD with active choroidal neovascularization who attended our clinic between March 2021 and October 2022 and received brolucizumab intravitreal injections. All treatments adhered to standard protocols. Patient confidentiality and nonidentifiability to the research team were maintained.

During the initial visit aimed at confirming the suspected diagnosis, all patients underwent a complete baseline ophthalmologic examination including best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) using an Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study chart, intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement via an iCARE tonometer, slit-lamp biomicroscopy with a 66 or 90 D lens, optical coherence tomography (OCT), optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA), color fundus photography, and fundus autofluorescence. Fluorescein angiography (FFA) and indocyanine green angiography (ICGA) were conducted in instances of diagnostic uncertainty. The procedures were carried out using the Optos ultra-wide field imaging system (Optos Inc, Dunfermline, UK) or the Spectralis HRA+OCT platform (Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany). OCT of the macular region (macular dense scan [pattern 49, averaging 15] and seven-line raster scan) was performed using the Spectralis HRA+OCT platform (Heidelberg Engineering) evaluating CNV morphology and location. The medical history, previous ocular history, and a list of concomitant medications were noted before starting treatment. The data used were collected retrospectively through Medisoft EMR software (Medisoft Limited, Leeds, UK).

The study included patients above 50 years of age with a diagnosis of nAMD with active CNV in the affected eye and BCVA between 6/12 (0.3 log of minimum angle of resolution [logMAR]) and 6/96 (1.2 logMAR) in accordance with National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, UK guidelines. Furthermore, we considered only patients who successfully completed the three injections of the loading dose and had a minimum follow-up period of 4 months from the initial date. nAMD was confirmed through OCTA, FFA, and ICGA. The central retinal thickness (CRT) was noted. Intraretinal fluid (IRF) and/or subretinal fluid (SRF), subretinal hyperreflective material (SHRM), pigment epithelial detachments (PEDs), hyperreflective foci (HF), macular atrophy, and retinal pigment epithelial tears were evaluated using OCT. Moreover, starting from the baseline (initial visit to initiate brolucizumab treatment) and continuing throughout the brolucizumab treatment regimen, we evaluated the presence, absence, and alterations in all the aforementioned OCT findings, based on definitions used in clinical literature.[11121314] Two independent, experienced investigators graded baseline and follow-up images (LF and FP) and it was adjudicated by a senior colleague (SY).

Patients with the presence of a concomitant ophthalmological condition that might influence the results, namely diabetic retinopathy, hypertensive retinopathy, geographic atrophy or fibrosis involving the macula, ocular surgery in the study eye within the previous 3 months, ocular adverse events (OAEs) due to brolucizumab, poor adherence to treatment, and failure to complete the loading dose were excluded.

All four patients who experienced OAEs due to brolucizumab were excluded from the study because they did not complete the loading dose.

Both treatment-naive patients and patients who were switched from a different drug were included. All eyes received three initial four-weekly loading doses of brolucizumab 6 mg (0.05 mL of 120 mg/mL solution), followed by a treat-and-extend regimen. After the monthly loading dose, the minimum interval between injections was extended to 8 weeks in all patients. Persistence of disease activity was assessed by noting IRF, SRF, and/or presence of SHRM. In eyes with persistent disease activity on OCT, injections were continued every 8 weeks. Eyes which showed no disease activity or had stable findings underwent a gradual extension of the treatment interval in increments of 4 weeks up to a maximum interval of 12 weeks. The interval between the last anti-VEGF injection and the beginning of data collection was 8.72 ± 3.92 weeks. At each follow-up visit, all patients underwent IOP measurement, BCVA assessment, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, and OCT scan.

The patients were divided into three groups based on history of previous anti-VEGF treatment: 1) treatment-naive patients, 2) patients previously treated with aflibercept only, and 3) patients previously treated with more than one anti-VEGF molecule (aflibercept, ranibizumab, or bevacizumab).

Data were entered into a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet for analysis. Statistical analysis was carried out with GraphPad Prism v. 9 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). Data were reported as mean ± standard deviation. BCVA was recorded in LogMAR for analysis purposes. The comparison between variables before and after brolucizumab intravitreal injections in the same group was performed using the Wilcoxon test. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

We acquired data from 44 eyes of 40 patients diagnosed with unilateral or bilateral nAMD at our clinic between March 2021 and October 2022. Of these eyes, seven were excluded from the final analysis based on the study exclusion criteria. Notably, four eyes experienced OAEs during the loading dose and consequently did not complete this phase, resulting in the discontinuation of intravitreal injection treatment. In addition, the remaining three eyes did not fulfil the loading doses due to patient noncompliance. Ultimately, we included 37 eyes of 35 patients for our study analysis. Within the entire study cohort, only two patients were identified with bilateral nAMD. The mean age of these patients was 79.02 ± 8.72 years, and 19 patients were males and 16 were females. Forty eyes were phakic with grade 2 or lesser nuclear cataract and the rest were pseudophakic. Out of the 37 eyes included, 36 had type 1 CNV (97.3%), and one eye had type 2 CNV (2.7%). The 37 eyes were divided into three groups: six treatment-naïve eyes, 15 eyes previously treated with aflibercept alone, and 16 eyes previously treated with more than one anti-VEGF agent. PED was present in 100% of eyes at baseline and in 86.5% (32 eyes) at the last assessment. PED remained stable in 43.2% of eyes (16 eyes), improved in 51.4% (19 eyes), and worsened in 5.4% of treated eyes (two eyes). In addition, SHRM was present in only 10 % of treated eyes (four eyes), while HF were observed in 67.6% of eyes (25 eyes) in which treatment was initiated. At the last visit, SHRM and HF were found in 5.4% (two eyes) and 64.9% of treated eyes (24 eyes), respectively.

Among patients previously exposed to treatment involving multiple anti-VEGF agents, PED exhibited stability in 37.5% of eyes (six eyes), improvement in 56.3% (nine eyes), and worsening in one eye. Among eyes previously treated with aflibercept, PED stability was observed in 53.3% of eyes (eight eyes), improvement in 40% (six eyes), and worsening in one eye. Finally, among treatment-naïve patients, PED remained stable in 33.3% of eyes (two eyes) and improved in 66.7% of eyes (four eyes).

At the last follow-up, complete resorption of both IRF and SRF was observed in eight eyes (21.6%). Two representative cases of patients from our cohort, before and after treatment with brolucizumab, are shown in Figs. 3a, b and 4a, b.

In the entire patient cohort, one eye developed a retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) tear (2.7%) that did not involve the foveal region and, therefore, did not necessitate treatment suspension. Instead, two eyes (5.4%) developed macular atrophy involving the fovea. Of the initial 44 eyes of 40 patients considered for inclusion, four eyes were excluded from analysis of final outcomes due to OAEs during the loading dose (consequently did not complete this phase) in the form of intraocular inflammation (IOI; anterior uveitis, vitritis, and retinal vasculitis) [Table 3]. In particular, one patient experienced uveitis after a single injection of brolucizumab, while three others developed uveitis after receiving two injections. Furthermore, none of these patients were treatment naïve, with no previous history of uveitis or drug reaction following injections. Importantly, they received injections from distinct batches of the drug.

The proportion of OAEs in this cohort was 9.09% and, specifically, we had a retinal vasculitis rate of 4.54%. Two of these patients presented with acute anterior uveitis and mild vitritis, and were treated with hourly topical dexamethasone 0.1%, which was tapered weekly. The other two patients presented with acute anterior uveitis, vitritis, and retinal vasculitis, and were treated with topical dexamethasone along with oral prednisolone tablets 1 mg/kg daily, followed by a slow taper once the inflammation resolved. All patients experienced a full recovery and were shifted to an alternative anti-VEGF molecule.

Discussion

Brolucizumab, the smallest available anti-VEGF antibody, was developed by binding regions of the novel anti-VEGF antibody to a human single-chain fragment variable (scFv)scaffold. Its design allows for a high concentration gradient between the retina and vitreous, facilitating molecule distribution into the retina.[151617] Previous investigations into the efficacy of brolucizumab in improving visual acuity have shown discrepancies in reported findings. Notably, the HAWK and HARRIER protocols, along with the recently published BREW study, have reported lower ranges of BCVA improvement posttreatment, consistent with the outcomes observed in our study.[1018] In our entire study cohort, there was no statistically significant improvement in mean BCVA before and after brolucizumab injections. Notably, the mean BCVA across the entire study cohort demonstrated a marginal deterioration; however, this change did not attain statistical significance. Subgroup analysis revealed that exclusively, the subset of patients previously treated with aflibercept exhibited a nonsignificant decline in mean BCVA before and after treatment.

Brolucizumab exhibits a greater binding capacity than aflibercept and ranibizumab, suggesting a potential for a longer duration of effect.[9171920] In a Phase 2 study, Dugel et al.[17] found that brolucizumab demonstrated noninferiority to aflibercept in terms of BCVA change from baseline at weeks 12 and 16. In addition, they observed superior efficacy in reducing central thickness from baseline and maintaining a stable treatment effect compared to aflibercept. The entire patient cohort of our study demonstrated an improvement in mean CRT following brolucizumab injections.

In HAWK and HARRIER studies, the noninferiority of brolucizumab to aflibercept in vision gain (change from baseline) at week 48 has been established.[10] These BCVA gains were maintained until week 96. At week 96, the authors noticed that the brolucizumab group showed fewer eyes with fluid (sub-RPE fluid and/or SRF and/or IRF) compared to the aflibercept group.[8] Overall, we noted clinical stability and/or improvement of OCT features of AMD with the injections. Remarkably, eight eyes (21.6%) displayed complete resorption of both IRF and SRF at the last follow-up. This is in line with the report by Bilgic et al.[21] in the REBA study, which highlighted the effectiveness of brolucizumab in resolving macular fluid in both intraretinal and subretinal compartments.[21] The smaller size of the drug molecule may lead to higher intraocular concentrations, likely contributing to its enhanced efficacy and long-lasting effects.

In our clinical practice, we implemented a stepwise prolongation of the treatment interval, increasing by 4-week increments, with the maximum interval capped at 12 weeks. The mean interval between injections administered was 7.37 ± 2.84 weeks.

Within our entire patient cohort, one eye (2.7%) developed an RPE tear, which did not involve the foveal region and, therefore, did not necessitate treatment suspension. In contrast, two eyes (5.4%) developed macular atrophy that affected the fovea. Although brolucizumab is safe and has a well-tolerated profile, postmarketing adverse events of retinal vasculitis (including occlusive retinal vasculitis) were reported in literature.[22] In HAWK and HARRIER studies, OAEs were comparable among treatment groups up to week 96, but brolucizumab at a dosage of 6 mg showed a higher incidence of IOI (4.7% vs. 0.6%). Iritis and uveitis were the most frequent adverse events.[823] In 2020, the American Society of Retina Specialists reported retinal vasculitis cases with vision loss linked to brolucizumab. In 2021, Novartis advised against intervals less than 8 weeks between doses based on MERLIN study, where brolucizumab had higher rates of IOI compared to aflibercept (9.3% vs. 4.5%), with rates of retinal vasculitis at 0.8% versus 0.0% and rates of retinal vascular occlusion at 2.0% versus 0.0%, respectively.[222324252627] In our study, no procedure-related complications were reported in the 37 eyes included. Four eyes that presented with IOI during the loading dose of brolucizumab, such as acute anterior uveitis, vitritis, and retinal vasculitis retinal vasculitis, stopped the injections and were excluded from the current analysis. They were all treated with steroids with full recovery, resulting in visual improvement. Upon complete resolution of ocular inflammation, the excluded patients restarted intravitreal injections of an alternative anti-VEGF molecule.

In this study, we provide our experience of brolucizumab for nAMD and current understanding of the adverse events. Despite potential adverse events, it effectively reduces retinal fluid and stabilizes visual acuity.[27] We noted that almost one-fifth of the study eyes could achieve complete drying effect with treatment. The study has several limitations, especially its relatively small sample size. Moreover, the scope is also limited by its retrospective nature. However, additional studies with larger sample sizes, longer follow-up periods, and statistical analysis to assess the comparative efficacy of treatment extension among anti-VEGF agents are essential to explore the long-term real-world outcomes with this anti-VEGF agent.

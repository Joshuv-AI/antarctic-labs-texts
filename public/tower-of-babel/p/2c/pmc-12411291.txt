Brain Atrophy Can Predict the Severity and Prognosis of Anti-N-Methyl-D-Aspartate Receptor Encephalitis
Authors: Yinyao Lin, Haiyan Li, Yanling Li, Yuge Wang, Yongqiang Dai, Bingjun Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and PurposeCompare clinical and lab characteristics of anti-N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (anti-NMDAR) encephalitis cases with and without brain atrophy.MethodsAssess cerebral atrophy using median temporal lobe atrophy (MTA) and global cortical atrophy (GCA) scores in 82 anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases.ResultsGCA (+) cases had higher proportions of status epilepticus (p<0.001), memory problems (p=0.031), sepsis (p<0.001), and mechanical ventilation need (p=0.001) than GCA (−) cases. MTA (+) cases had higher memory problems (p<0.001) and sepsis (p=0.002) than MTA (−) cases. GCA (+) and MTA (+) groups had higher max modified Rankin Scale (mRS) (p<0.001, p=0.002) and 1-year mRS (p<0.001, p=0.004) scores than GCA (−) and MTA (−) groups. GCA (+) group had a longer hospital stay and a larger proportion of cases with limited treatment response (both p<0.001) than the GCA (−) group. GCA (p=0.005) was found to be a significant predictive factor for the mRS score 1 year after onset in multiple linear regression analysis.ConclusionsMTA and GCA may be associated with severity and prognosis in anti-NMDAR encephalitis.

INTRODUCTION

Anti-N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (anti-NMDAR) encephalitis is an inflammatory encephalopathy distinguished by the presence of immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies targeting the GluN1 subunit of the NMDAR. It primarily effects teenage girls, whether or not they have ovarian teratomas.123

Various magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) observations have been documented in individuals diagnosed with anti-NMDAR encephalitis.1456 However, few investigations on cerebral atrophy in patients with anti-NMDAR encephalitis have been conducted. Cerebellar atrophy, as found by Iizuka et al.,7 is both irreversible and linked to poor outcomes in cases with anti-NMDAR encephalitis. However, only 15 patients were included in this study. Gray matter atrophy was observed in anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases in a study with a relatively small sample size of 40 patients conducted by Long et al.8 Cases with various courses of anti-NMDAR encephalitis exhibited different cerebral atrophy sites.8 The clinical connection between cerebral atrophy and anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis needs more investigation.

The goal of this research was to examine the differences between anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases who had brain atrophy and those who did not.

METHODS

Ethics approval

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (II2023-046-01). Without intervention, therefore informed written consent was waived.

Study population

The database consisted of 82 cases diagnosed with anti-NMDAR encephalitis who were hospitalized to the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University from March 2014 to December 2019. The diagnosis of anti-NMDAR encephalitis was established by identifying the core syndromes associated with limbic encephalitis, which include psychiatric symptoms, seizures, conscious disruption, and memory loss. Additionally, the presence of IgG antibodies against NMDAR was detected.9 Relevant differential diagnosis were accurately excluded. The clinical and detection data were collected from diagnosed cases. All cases were subjected to standard abdominal and pelvic ultrasounds. When ovarian teratoma was suspected, imaging was performed with a CT or MRI scan of the pelvis. Treatments included first-line immunotherapy, second-line immunotherapy, and excision of the tumor. Immunotherapy includes first-line and second-line treatment methods, the former mainly using steroids alone or in combination with immunoglobulin injection or plasmapheresis, while the latter mainly using rituximab, azathioprine or cyclophosphamide treatment alone or combined. If the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score remained at four or higher 1 month after the start of immunotherapy or tumor excision, the initial treatment was considered ineffective.

All participants consented to have their images published.

Detection of autoantibodies

Patients' cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) was collected after obtaining their informed assent. CSF samples were utilized for routine analysis and autoantibody detection. Using commercially available reagents, the presence of antibodies against NMDAR was evaluated by indirect immunofluorescence assay (anti glutamate receptor antibody detection kit, EUROIMMUN Medizinische Labordiagnostika).

MRI and visual rating scales

MRI scans of the brain were conducted at 1.5/3.0 tesla (SIGNA, Premier; Guangzhou Xinda Medical Technology Co., Ltd). All patients were subjected to conventional MRI protocols, including T1-weighted imaging, T2-weighted imaging, and fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR).

As described previously, T1-weighted pictures were used to calculate the median temporal lobe atrophy (MTA) scale and T2-flair images were used to calculate the global cortical atrophy (GCA) scale.1011 The MTA-scale was created by Scheltens et al.11 in 1992. It rates the degree of atrophy in the hippocampus, temporal horn of the lateral ventricles, and choroid fissure on a scale from 0 to 4, with higher scores indicating greater atrophy (Table 1).11 We used a 4-point scale developed by Pasquier et al.10 to assess the severity of GCA (0=no atrophy; 1=mild GCA, sulcal aperture circumferentially; 2=moderate GCA, sulcal enlargement along the length; 3=severe GCA, gyral thinning). The MRI data were analyzed by a senior neuroradiologist and a neurologist.

Patients were categorized as GCA (+) or GCA (−) based on whether the GCA score was greater than or equal to 1. They were also divided into MTA (+) and MTA (−) groups according to whether the MTA score was greater than or equal to 2.

Statistics analyses

The SPSS 20.0 (IBM Corp.) was applied to statistical analyses. Continuous data were displayed as the mean±standard deviation or median [interquartile range, IQR]. Continuous data were checked by Student's t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test. The chi-square test or Fisher's exact test was employed to analyze nominal variables. The association between the mRS score 1 year after onset and the baseline features of cases with anti-NMDAR encephalitis was evaluated using multiple linear regression.

RESULTS

Baseline demographics and clinical features of anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases

The demographic and clinical characteristics of anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases at baseline are summarised in Table 2. The median age of the 34 males (41.5%) and 48 females (58.5%) patients was 22.0 years. Of these, 18 (22.0%) had a tumor, 52 (63.4%) had epileptic seizures, 64 (78.0%) had psychiatric symptoms, 43 (52.4%) had conscious disturbance, 35 (42.7%) had memory deficits, and 33 (40.2%) had cerebral atrophy. The median mRS score was 4 (IQR, 3–4) at the peak of the illness (max mRS) and 2 (IQR, 1–3) at 1 year after onset. The median hospital stay was 28 days (IQR, 21.8–38.5 days). The median time from onset to MRI was 42.5 days (IQR, 30.8–70.5 days).

A comparison of anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases with and without cerebral shrinkage

Based on the MTA and GCA evaluations, anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases were divided into two categories, as shown in Table 3. The GCA (+) group had a greater proportion of patients with status epilepticus (p<0.001) and memory deficits (p=0.031) compared to the GCA (−) group. The MTA (+) group had a greater proportion of cases with memory impairments than the MTA (−) group (p<0.001). There were no observable differences in psychiatric symptoms or consciousness disturbance.

The GCA (+) and MTA (+) cases had higher rates of sepsis than the GCA (−) and MTA (−) cases (p<0.001 and p=0.002, respectively). The proportion of GCA (+) cases requiring mechanical ventilation was greater than that of GCA (−) cases (p=0.001). In addition, the mRS scores of the GCA (+) and MTA (+) groups at the peak of the illness (max mRS) (p<0.001 and p=0.002, respectively) and 1 year after onset (p<0.001 and p=0.004, respectively) were significantly higher than those of the GCA (−) and MTA (−) groups. Moreover, the GCA (+) group had a longer median hospital stay and a greater proportion of patients with limited treatment response than the GCA (−) group (both p<0.001). Also, the percentage of patients with teratomas was greater in the GCA (+) group compared to the GCA (−) group (p=0.001).

In terms of laboratory results, the GCA (+) group had lower mean folic acid and median 25-hydroxyvitamin D3 concentrations than the GCA (−) group (p=0.043 and p=0.044, respectively). CSF abnormalities, median antibody titers, median vitamin B1 concentrations, median vitamin B2 concentrations, median vitamin B6 concentrations, and median vitamin B12 concentrations were comparable between patients with and without brain atrophy.

Multiple linear regression analysis between the mRS score at 1 year after onset and baseline anti-NMDAR encephalitis patient characteristics

As shown in Table 4, multiple linear regression analysis suggested that GCA (p=0.005) was a significant predictive factor for the mRS score at 1 year after onset. These variables explained 42.5% of the variance.

Follow-up MRIs of four patients with brain atrophy

As shown in Figs. 1, 2, 3 and Supplementary Figs. 1, 2, 3 (in the online-only Data Supplement), we performed MRI follow-ups on 5 patients. Patient 1 had both MTA and GCA, while patients 2–4 had only GCA and patient 5 had only MTA. Follow-up T1-weighted MRI showed reversal MTA in patients 1 and 5 (Figs. 1 and 2; MRI follow-up duration: 58 and 12 months, respectively). The T2 FLAIR MRI showed reversal GTA in patients 3 (Supplementary Fig. 2 in the online-only Data Supplement; MRI follow-up duration: 91 months) while follow-up MRIs did not demonstrate reversal changes in the GCA of patient 1 (Fig. 3; MRI follow-up duration: 58 months), patient 2 (Supplementary Fig. 1 in the online-only Data Supplement; MRI follow-up duration: 63 months) and patient 4 (Supplementary Fig. 3 in the online-only Data Supplement; MRI follow-up duration: 14 months).

DISCUSSION

This study used a large sample size to examine the features of patients with anti-NMDAR encephalitis and cerebral atrophy. Earlier research has indicated that patients with multiple sclerosis and neuromyelitis optica exhibit grey matter atrophy.12 Deep brain atrophy in multiple sclerosis was found to be correlated with the severity of the disease.13 There is a strong correlation between hippocampal volume and cognition observed on MRI in neuromyelitis optica.14 However, little is known about cerebral atrophy in anti-NMDAR encephalitis patients. The current study found that MTA and GCA are related to the severity and prognosis of anti-NMDAR encephalitis.

In this research, over one-third (40.2%) of the 82 individuals with anti-NMDAR encephalitis displayed brain atrophy as determined by MTA and GCA scales on plain MRI images. This finding indicated that brain atrophy is prevalent in anti-NMDAR encephalitis patients. Medial temporal lobe atrophy, as measured by the MTA-scale, was seen in 14.6% of patients on at least one side, which is consistent with previous studies.15

In terms of clinical symptoms, we discovered that the proportion of patients with memory impairment is greater in the GCA (+) and MTA (+) groups than in the GCA (−) and MTA (−) groups. Previous research demonstrated a negative correlation between the degree of MTA and GCA and memory test scores, which is consistent with our findings.1116

In the GCA (+) group, the median latency from onset to MR was 60.0 days. According to a earlier study, cerebral atrophy in anti-NMDAR encephalitis began 1–2 months after the onset of symptoms, which is consistent with our findings.7

We discovered that at the peak of the illness (max mRS) and 1 year after onset, the MTA (+) group had significantly higher mRS scores than the MTA (−) group. Previous research has indicated that the volume of the hippocampus can predict the severity of anti-NMDAR encephalitis,17 which is consistent with our findings. In addition, patients with GCA exhibited higher mRS scores. Patients with GCA had a longer median hospital stay, responded poorly to treatment, and were more likely to need mechanical breathing. Also, multiple linear regression analysis demonstrated that GCA is a predictor of the mRS score 1 year after onset. While our results show that GCA is associated with worse outcomes, it remains unclear whether this reflects direct disease severity or sequelae of systemic complications such as sepsis, prolonged mechanical ventilation, and hypoxia. Thus, our findings imply that MTA and GCA may be linked to the severity and prognosis of patients with anti-NMDAR encephalitis, and further researches are needed to confirm the conclusion.

In this study, we discovered that in the GCA (+) individuals, teratomas occurred more frequently than in the GCA (−) cases. Teratoma patients' immune responses are stronger than those of tumor-free individuals, and they also manifest with exacerbated neurological problems.18 Thus, we hypothesize that anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases with teratoma are more probable to experience severe brain injury and then brain atrophy.

Two patients in our investigation demonstrated reversal of MTA changes on follow-up MRIs (patients 1 and 5). MRI obtained 6 years after symptom onset of patient 3 also showed that the GTA had reversed. Reversed brain atrophy in anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases has been documented in previous studies,19 consistent with our study. Further research is required to corroborate the relationship between long-term MRI alterations and clinical result in anti-NMDAR encephalitis patients.

In anti-NMDAR encephalitis, the mechanisms underlying brain atrophy remain mainly to be clarified. In this study, we discovered that individuals with cerebral atrophy had a greater proportion of epileptic status, sepsis, lower levels of folic acid and 25-hydroxyvitamin D3. Canas et al.20 found that prolonged status epilepticus may lead to MRI abnormalities such as brain atrophy. Also, sepsis may cause severe brain dysfunction and brain atrophy is comman in patients with sepsis-induced brain dysfunction.21 Additionally, previous researches have shown that vitamin D depletion is associated with lower brain volumes.22 Moreover, previous studies by Snowdon et al.23 established a robust association between low serum folate levels and the cerebral cortex atrophy. Consequently, brain atrophy in anti-NMDAR encephalitis may be related with status epilepticus, sepsis, low folate levels and 25 hydroxy vitamin D3 levels, although the specific mechanism remains to be elucidated.

This research exists the following limitations: 1) We only included anti-NMDAR encephalitis cases, the conclusions obtained may not be applicable to other types of cases; 2) This is a retrospective study, which can easily lead to bias; 3) There is a lack of quantitative analysis of MRI in this study; In this study, we used only a cell-based assay for NMDAR antibody testing which is known to produce a number of false-positive and false-negative results. Thus, brain-tissue-based assays confirmation is necessary to validate our findings in future study.

In summary, this study confirmed that brain atrophy was not uncommon in cases with anti-NMDAR encephalitis. GCA and MTA may be associated with the disease severity and prognosis of anti-NMDAR encephalitis. The exact mechanism of brain atrophy in anti-NMDAR encephalitis needs further study.

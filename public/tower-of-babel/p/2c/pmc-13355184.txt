Forecasting seasonal allergic rhinitis through integrated analysis of social media and online drug sales data☆
Authors: Xunliang Tong, Chuangsen Fang, Xiaowei Jiang, Lan Liu, Rui Shen, Dan Li, Karl-Christian Bergmann, Torsten Zuberbier, Weiwei Cui, Luzhao Feng, Yanming Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAllergic rhinitis (AR) is a highly prevalent, seasonally variable disease that poses a growing public health challenge. Conventional surveillance based on clinic visits and surveys is slow and may miss self-medicating patients. Integrating environmental monitoring with digital traces such as search queries and online drug purchases may provide more timely insight into allergen exposure, symptom awareness, and medication demand.MethodsWe analyzed daily data for urban Beijing from March 1, 2022 to October 15, 2024, including pollen concentration (grains per 1000 mm3), the Baidu search index for “allergic rhinitis,” and online purchase rates of 4 oral second-generation H1-antihistamines (loratadine, desloratadine, cetirizine, levocetirizine) from the Meituan platform, standardized per 100,000 population. Cross-correlation functions were computed on pre-whitened series to assess lead–lag relationships. A single-mediator regression framework applied to pre-whitened data and estimated with Newey–West heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent standard errors quantified the mediating role of the Baidu Index between pollen and antihistamine purchases. ARIMAX (1,1,1) models with different exogenous inputs (pollen only, Baidu Index only, both combined) and a naïve random-walk benchmark were used to forecast 1-, 2-, 3-, and 7-day demand; performance was evaluated using RMSE, MAPE, and Pearson correlation (PCC).ResultsAll 3 series exhibited pronounced and recurrent seasonal patterns, with Baidu search activity rising in close temporal alignment with pollen peaks and antihistamine purchases typically lagging by 1–2 days. Pre-whitened cross-correlations remained moderate and statistically significant around lag 0, indicating genuine contemporaneous associations after removing the shared seasonal component. Mediation analysis showed that pollen concentration had a significant total effect on antihistamine purchase rates (β = 0.34, 95% CI [0.13, 0.54]), of which approximately 47% was transmitted indirectly via the Baidu Index (indirect β = 0.16, 95% CI [0.08, 0.25]). The ARIMAX (1,1,1) model integrating both pollen and Baidu Index achieved the best forecasting performance (1-day RMSE = 3.80, MAPE = 7.68%, PCC = 0.89) and consistently outperformed single-predictor models and the random-walk benchmark across all horizons.ConclusionPollen exposure influences antihistamine demand both directly and indirectly through public information-seeking behavior captured by the Baidu Index, and integrating environmental and digital data enables timely surveillance of seasonal allergic-rhinitis–related medication use.Keywords: Seasonal allergic rhinitis, Digital epidemiology, Baidu index, Antihistamines, ARIMAX, Time-series analysis

Introduction

The global prevalence of allergic diseases has risen significantly in recent years, emerging as a major public health issue affecting approximately 10%–30% of the world's population.1,2 Allergic rhinitis (AR) is an IgE-mediated inflammatory disease of the nasal mucosa, characterized by symptoms such as rhinorrhea, nasal obstruction, sneezing, and itching following exposure to relevant allergens.3,4 Pollen is one of the most important and common allergens inducing allergic rhinitis, particularly associated with seasonal allergic rhinitis (SAR).5 According to the epidemiological data of World Allergy Organization, approximately 400 million people worldwide were affected by AR as early as 2011.6 A recent review reported that the median prevalence of AR among adults worldwide was approximately 18.1%, with geographical differences in prevalence and an increasing trend over time.7 The prevalence pattern of AR in China mirrors global these trends. Epidemiological studies in China have shown a standardized prevalence of AR in adults of 17.6%, with an even higher prevalence observed among children, and also showing an increasing trend over time.8,9 Regular updates of epidemiological data are essential for understanding the burden of AR and for designing effective management strategies.

Novel approaches for the surveillance of AR prevalence offer the advantage of flexibility and timeliness, posing significant advances to traditional methods, which rely on population-based epidemiological surveys, community screening, and hospital diagnostic data. These traditional methods are limited by long survey cycles, high costs, data lags, and recall bias, and the inability to capture real-time trends due to the condition's seasonal and regional variability.10 Moreover, reliance on medical visit data alone primarily reflects healthcare utilization rather than the true community-level distribution of AR, highlighting the need for more dynamic and scalable surveillance tools.11

To investigate the potential of social media and digital data, researchers are increasingly turning to social media and digital platforms as innovative tools for disease surveillance. Through the analysis of search term frequencies combined with advanced analytical methods, these platforms enable real-time monitoring, trend identification, and predictive modeling of disease trends 12, 13, 14. It also ensures broader coverage by capturing populations who self-medicate or experience symptoms yet avoiding medical care, thus offering a more comprehensive perspective than reliance on clinical data alone 15, 16, 17. In additional value to user-generated content his that often individuals share health experiences far more openly on social platforms than in clinical settings.18

In China, the rapid expansion of social media and digital services presents a unique opportunity for disease surveillance. Key platforms include Baidu Index (BDI), Meituan, and others. The Baidu index tracks keyword search trends across 783 million users, offering insights into public interest through queries related to AR. Meituan, a leading e-commerce and food delivery app, provides real-time drug sales data, enabling geographic and temporal tracking of antihistamine and AR medication demand. This study integrates social media data (Baidu Index) and drug sales data (Meituan) to assess the effectiveness of digital data in predicting AR trends through machine learning techniques. It also assesses the complementarity of these datasets with traditional surveillance methods. This work focuses on integrating real-time digital analytics with clinical epidemiology data, aiming to provide precision data for public health prevention and management in China. Beyond commercial demand forecasting, timely prediction of antihistamine use has important clinical and public health implications. Early identification of surges in community-level medication demand may support allergen-exposure alerts, guide prophylactic treatment and self-management among susceptible patients, and inform health-system planning and pharmacy stocking during peak pollen seasons.

Methods

Data sources

This study analyzed multi-source daily data collected from March 1, 2022 to October 15, 2024. The dataset includes: (1) daily average pollen concentration (grains per 1000 mm3) for urban Beijing, obtained from the Beijing Meteorological Bureau; (2) the Baidu Index (search volume index) for the keyword “allergic rhinitis (过敏性鼻炎)” was retrieved from Baidu's official platform; (3) daily prescriptions quantities for 4 antihistamines (loratadine, desloratadine, cetirizine, and levocetirizine) purchased through the Meituan online platform. In order to control for population variability,the quantity of drugs purchased was standardized based on the total permanent population of Beijing, thereby deriving the daily drug purchase rate. We focused on 4 widely used oral second-generation H1-antihistamines (loratadine, desloratadine, cetirizine, and levocetirizine) because they are among the most commonly available OTC agents for allergic conditions on the Meituan platform and had consistent, high-quality sales records throughout the study period. Other antihistamines and intranasal corticosteroids, which are recommended as first-line therapy for moderate-to-severe AR, were either not consistently listed, not available as OTC medications on Meituan that was not captured in our dataset. As a result, our analysis primarily reflects oral self-medication patterns rather than the full spectrum of guideline-recommended AR pharmacotherapy.

Cross-correlation function analysis

Cross-correlation function (CCF) analyses were conducted to explore the temporal relationship among pollen concentration, Baidu Index, and drug assumption rate. The CCF quantifies the correlation between 2 time series at varying lags k, identifying potential lead-lag dynamics.

Peaks in ρXY(k) at positive or negative lags suggest X leads/lags Y, respectively.19 Because all 3 series exhibited a strong seasonal component, we followed the Box–Jenkins recommendation and computed CCFs on pre-whitened series. Specifically, an ARIMA model was first fitted to the input series (pollen), and the resulting filter was applied to the Baidu Index and purchase-rate series before calculating cross-correlations.

Mediation analysis

A single-mediator model was used to assess whether the Baidu Index mediated the relationship between pollen concentration (independent variable, X) and the antihistamine purchase rate (dependent variable, Y). To account for the time-series nature of the data, we adopted a two-step time-series–aware procedure.

First, following the classical Box–Jenkins recommendation, we pre-whitened the pollen series by fitting an ARIMA model and applied the resulting filter to the Baidu Index and purchase rate series, thereby reducing shared serial dependence across the 3 variables. Mediation paths were then estimated on the pre-whitened series (A˜, M˜, Y˜) using linear regression to obtain the total effect (c), direct effect (c′), and indirect effect (a × b).

Second, to obtain valid inference in the presence of residual serial correlation and heteroskedasticity, we computed heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) standard errors using the Newey–West estimator for each regression (X˜ →M˜, X˜ →Y˜, and X˜, M˜ →Y˜). The maximum lag (bandwidth) for the HAC estimator was chosen using the Stock–Watson rule-of-thumb,

L=4(T100)92, where T is the number of daily observations, which yielded L = 6 for our sample (T = 678). Indirect (a × b), direct (c′), and total (c = c′ + a × b) effects and their 95% confidence intervals were computed from the HAC covariance matrices. We interpret this analysis as providing time series adjusted evidence for a behavioral pathway linking pollen exposure, online information seeking, and medication use.

where yt denotes the target variable of medication purchase rate at time t, xl,t represents the exogenous variables—specifically pollen concentration, Baidu Index, or both—at time t, p and q indicate the orders of the autoregressive and moving average components respectively, ϕi,θj and βl are the corresponding model coefficients, and εt is the error term at time.

Before model estimation, we examined the time-series properties of all variables and applied first-order differencing to achieve approximate stationarity of the purchase-rate series and exogenous inputs (pollen concentration and Baidu Index). Three model configurations were considered: (1) pollen concentration only, (2) Baidu Index only, and (3) the combination of pollen concentration and Baidu Index. Models were trained on data from 2022 to 2023 and validated on 2024 data with forecasting horizons of 1, 2, 3, and 7 days. Forecast performance was evaluated using Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), and the Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC) between observed and predicted purchase rates. Residual diagnostics, including Ljung–Box portmanteau tests and visual inspection of residual ACF and PACF, were used to confirm that the final models adequately captured the serial dependence structure before interpreting the forecasts.

All statistical analyses were conducted in R version 4.4.2. Time-series modelling and forecasting were implemented using the forecast and tseries packages (for ARIMAX estimation, unit-root and stationarity tests), while residual diagnostics and heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) standard errors relied on the lmtest and sandwich packages. Mediation analyses were performed with the mediation package based on linear regression models fitted to the pre-whitened series.

Results

Daily measurements of 3 variables from 2022 to 2024 were collected: pollen concentration (grains/1000 mm3), the Baidu Index for allergic rhinitis, and antihistamine purchase rate (per 100,000 population). These data exhibit distinct seasonal patterns with significant pollen peaks occurring annually during spring (March–May) and autumn (August–September) (Fig. 1). Peak concentrations frequently exceed baseline levels by 10–20 folds, then rapid declines observed following peak periods, finally typically returning to baseline within 2–3 weeks. Search activity according to the Baidu Allergic Rhinitis Index (blue trend line) demonstrates remarkable temporal correspondence with pollen peaks. The amplitude of the search peak correlates closely with the increase in pollen concentration. Notably, search activity remains elevated for several days following pollen peaks. Prescription patterns exhibit an immediate response to both pollen exposure and search activity. Purchase surges typically lag by 1–2 days after the pollen-search peak emerges, with peak purchase rates consistently reaching 3–5 times the baseline levels during high-exposure periods. The spring 2024 panel showed a particularly pronounced spike in online antihistamine purchases, which was substantially larger than the corresponding peaks in 2022 and 2023 despite only modest differences in pollen levels, suggesting that factors beyond allergen exposure—such as continued growth in Meituan use or post-COVID shifts toward online purchasing—may also have contributed to this increase.

Temporal trends of pollen concentration, Baidu Index and drug purchase rate in urban Beijing from 2022 to 2024.

The synchronized patterns observed across the 3 panels reveal the significant seasonal characteristics of allergic rhinitis-related phenomena, the public's rapid response to exposure to airborne allergens, and the consistent chain reaction between environmental, behavioral, and pharmacological factors. There is a close association between disease awareness (search behavior) and treatment measures (medication use). Notably, this temporal synchrony is particularly pronounced during the primary spring pollen season, while response times exhibit slightly greater variability during the autumn peak. The overall consistency of these patterns suggests that search engine data may serve as a reliable proxy indicator for allergen exposure and medication demand within population health monitoring systems.

To further disentangle genuine lead–lag relationships from shared seasonal trends, we computed cross-correlation functions (CCFs) on pre-whitened series, using ARIMA models fitted to the input series before filtering the corresponding output series (Fig. 2). The pre-whitened CCFs showed moderate but statistically significant correlations between pollen and the Baidu Index around lag 0 (ρ = 0.36), as well as between pollen and antihistamine purchase rates and between the Baidu Index and purchase rates (ρ = 0.40). These patterns indicate that, after removing the common seasonal component, increases in pollen concentration are still accompanied by near-contemporaneous increases in search activity and medication purchases. The Baidu Index tended to respond slightly earlier, whereas medication purchases peaked slightly later, consistent with a behavioral pathway in which information seeking precedes treatment uptake.”

Bars show correlation coefficients at different time lags (days), with positive lags indicating that changes in the exposure series precede changes in the outcome series. To investigate the potential mediating role of public interest, as measured by the Baidu Index, in the association between pollen concentration and antihistamine medication purchase rate, a single-mediator model was constructed. The mediation analysis revealed that pollen concentration had a significant total effect on medication purchase rate (β = 0.34, 95% CI: [0.13, 0.54], p < 0.01; Table 1). When adjusting for the mediator, the direct effect of pollen concentration remained significant (β = 0.18, 95% CI: [0.04, 0.31], p < 0.01), indicating that the effect is independent of public interest. Importantly, the indirect effect representing the pathway through the Baidu Index, was also significant (β = 0.16, 95% CI: [0.08, 0.25], p < 0.001). This suggests that heightened pollen exposure not only directly increases antihistamine use but also indirectly does so by raising public awareness, as reflected in online searches for “allergic rhinitis.” The statistically significant indirect effect supports a partial mediation mechanism. These estimates were obtained from regressions fitted to the pre-whitened series using Newey–West HAC standard errors, ensuring that inference was robust to residual serial correlation in the daily data.

The optimal ARIMAX model parameters were determined based on the minimum Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) values for each set of exogenous variables. As summarized in Table S1, the ARIMAX (1,1,1) specification consistently achieved the lowest AIC across all 3 exogenous-variable combinations (pollen count, Baidu Index, and both combined), and was therefore selected for forecasting analyses.

Before forecasting, we examined the time-series properties of pollen concentration, Baidu Index, and antihistamine purchase rate. Unit root tests (Augmented Dickey–Fuller and KPSS) indicated that the raw series were non-stationary, whereas first-order differencing rendered them approximately stationary, supporting the use of an ARIMAX (1,1,1) specification. We also confirmed, through Ljung–Box tests and inspection of residual ACF and PACF, that the residuals from the final ARIMAX models were approximately white noise (Table S2 and Fig. S1).The predictive performance of the 3 ARIMAX models is visually summarized in Fig. 3. Across all forecast windows (1, 2, 3, and 7 days ahead), predicted medication purchase rates closely follow the observed trends for every model, capturing both the timing and magnitude of major peaks and seasonal fluctuations. The fit is strongest at shorter forecast windows, and although accuracy declines at longer horizons, all models remain capable of reproducing the overall temporal dynamics. As reflected both visually and quantitatively (Table 2), the model that incorporates both pollen count and Baidu Index consistently demonstrates the best predictive accuracy, with the lowest RMSE and MAPE and the highest PCC across all forecast periods. For example, at the 1-day horizon, the combined model achieves an RMSE of 3.80, MAPE of 7.68%, and PCC of 0.89. Single-variable models using either pollen count or Baidu Index alone perform slightly less well but still produce robust forecasts. The ARIMAX model using Baidu Index alone also yields strong predictive performance, with metrics closely approaching those of the combined model at shorter horizons (1-day RMSE = 3.94, MAPE = 8.01%, PCC = 0.89).

When compared with a simple naïve random-walk benchmark fitted to the purchase-rate series alone, all ARIMAX models showed clear incremental predictive value. The random-walk model exhibited higher RMSEs and lower PCCs at every forecast horizon (1-day RMSE = 4.77, PCC = 0.84; 7-day RMSE = 8.07, PCC = 0.56), indicating that relying solely on past purchase rates and their strong autocorrelation structure is insufficient. In contrast, the combined ARIMAX model reduced RMSE relative to the random-walk benchmark at all horizons while achieving higher correlations with observed values. These comparisons demonstrate that incorporating exogenous information particularly Baidu search data—provides meaningful gains beyond what can be achieved by naïve time-series extrapolation of the purchase-rate series alone.

Our analysis revealed that the combined model was superior, with 9.7–25.7% lower RMSE than the single-predictor models and PCC remaining above 0.85 even for 7-day forecasts. Moreover, the Baidu Index model outperformed the pollen-only model, with RMSE reductions ranging from 6.4% for 1-day to 17.7% for 7-day forecasts. Prediction accuracy declined from 1- to 7-day horizons by 34.7% for the combined model and 63.4% for the pollen-only model, with the Baidu-based model showing a slower accuracy decay (43.9% vs 63.4% for pollen). These results indicate that digital trace data (Baidu Index) can capture medication-demand signals more efficiently than environmental monitoring alone. The pathway from awareness to action, reflected by the mediated effect, plays a substantial role in shaping antihistamine use, and models that integrate both environmental and digital behavioral data provide the most robust forecasting performance.

Discussion

This study systematically examined the temporal relationships among pollen concentration, online public search intensity (Baidu Index), and antihistamine purchase rates for AR in Beijing from 2022 to 2024. The findings demonstrate robust seasonal synchrony among these variables; during pollen concentration surges, online searches for “allergic rhinitis” and antihistamine purchases exhibited concurrent upward trends. Mediation analysis further revealed that public search interest partially mediates the effect of pollen on medication demand and predictive models, incorporating both environmental and behavioral data, achieved the best forecasting performance.

Digital epidemiology and allergic disease surveillance

This study further enriches existing literature by demonstrating that internet search indices (such as the Baidu Index) exhibit both a strong temporal correlation with environmental triggers and partially mediate community medication behaviors. This supports the concept that information epidemiology data can reflect both symptom awareness and proactive health-seeking, a notion increasingly supported by global digital epidemiology research. Recent studies have validated similar approaches using Twitter data to monitor hay fever in Germany and pollen allergy surveillance in Australia, with correlations ranging from r = 0.84–0.93 between social media activity and over-the-counter medication sales. The significant mediation effect, with approximately 47% of the total association operating through Baidu search behavior, provides quantitative evidence that public search behavior serves as a critical link between environmental exposure and clinical or behavioral outcomes such as medication purchase.20

The advantages of the Baidu Index as a predictive tool aligns with recent research on Chinese digital health surveillance. Studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of the Baidu search index in monitoring and predicting various diseases including brucellosis, influenza, tuberculosis, and HIV/AIDS 21, 22, 23. The ARIMAX models using the Baidu Index alone achieved comparable performance (PCC = 0.89) to environmental pollen models, suggesting that search behavior captures both direct symptom response and anticipatory health-seeking behavior.

Behavioral mediation and health theory integration

The significant mediation effect identified in this study suggests that public search behavior is a key step linking environmental exposure to clinical or behavioral outcomes such as medication purchase. This aligns with Protection Motivation Theory (PMT), which posits that risk perception and information-seeking are critical triggers for self-management and health service utilization.24 Recent research examining PMT in allergic rhinitis management found that perceived symptom severity positively influenced all investigated allergy management measures, with threat appraisal exerting greater influence on behavior change than response appraisal. Our multi-pathway model provides quantitative support to these mechanisms, indicating approximately 47% of pollen's effect on medication demand operates through cognitive mediated pathway.

This behavioral pathway is consistent with qualitative research on allergic rhinitis self-management, which reveals that patients develop confidence in their ability to manage AR independently after years of trying different medications. Studies show that chronic AR allows patients to recognize symptom patterns, identify triggers, and develop avoidance strategies, leading them to prefer self-medication over healthcare consultations. The immediate response observed in Baidu Index data suggests that patients engage in proactive information-seeking behavior when anticipating allergen exposure, supporting the PMT framework where threat appraisal drives protective behavior.24 Clinically, this pattern is consistent with patients first recognizing or anticipating symptom exacerbation, then searching online for information about allergic rhinitis and available treatments, and finally purchasing familiar OTC antihistamines without consulting a physician. Recognizing this behavioral sequence may help clinicians and public-health practitioners to time education and prophylactic treatment advice to coincide with periods when patients are actively seeking information.

Multi-source data integration and forecasting innovation

A key innovation of the current study is the use of combined multi-source data—including real-world drug purchase rates and internet search trends—for time series analysis and medication demand forecasting. Prior research has demonstrated the value of pharmacy and over-the-counter (OTC) sales data as proxies for disease activity, particularly for conditions with strong seasonal or environmental triggers. Japanese researchers found strong correlations (r = 0.84–0.93) between Twitter posts mentioning AR symptoms and OTC nasal spray sales volumes, with seasonal components showing even higher correlations (r = 0.93).20,24

The ARIMAX modeling approach extends previous research in environmental health surveillance. Recent studies using ARIMAX models for influenza forecasting found that incorporating environmental indicators (such as PM10 with 5-day lag) improved prediction accuracy, with the best models achieving RMSE values of 2.999 for fitting and 12.033 for prediction.25 The ARIMAX model integrating environmental and behavioral factors demonstrated superior performance (RMSE = 3.80 for 1-day forecasts), suggesting that behavioral indicators may be more predictive than traditional environmental factors alone. This finding is particularly significant given that digital data are more readily available and cost-effective than environmental monitoring systems.

Notably, research indicates that ARIMAX models constructed solely using Baidu Index data can effectively predict medication purchase rates, with performance nearly equivalent to environmental pollen models. This holds major public health relevance: web search data are timely, inexpensive, and widely available, especially in settings where environmental surveillance is limited or delayed. Real-time automatic pollen monitoring systems, while promising, still face reliability challenges with information gaps of 17–19 days during main pollen seasons and accuracy rates of approximately 85%.25 In contrast, Baidu Index data provide continuous, real-time insights into population health behavior without the technical and cost barriers associated with automated pollen monitoring networks.

Global context and comparative analysis

The effectiveness of digital surveillance approaches varies across healthcare systems and cultural contexts. European studies using Twitter for hay fever surveillance in Germany found seasonal patterns correlating with surveillance data, though the relationship between tweet sentiment and disease activity showed complex dynamics. The MASK (Mobile Airways Sentinel Network) mobile app study, covering multiple European countries demonstrated that mobile technology could provide novel insights into allergic rhinitis control and treatment, with data from over 27,000 users revealing geographic and temporal patterns not captured by traditional surveillance. However, implementation challenges include variable patient engagement and the need for clinical validation.25,26

Our Beijing-based study contributes to understanding digital health surveillance in Asian contexts, where Baidu dominates internet search behavior with over 783 million users. The effectiveness of the Baidu Index for disease surveillance has been demonstrated across various conditions, with the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention collaborating on real-time infectious disease monitoring. The integration of pharmaceutical sales data from the Meituan platform adds a unique dimension, as e-commerce-based health product purchasing provides more granular behavioral insights than traditional prescription monitoring.27

Clinical and public health implications

From a clinical perspective, integrating pollen and Baidu Index information could support anticipatory management of seasonal allergic rhinitis. When rising search activity signals increasing symptom awareness in the community, clinicians and pharmacists may encourage high-risk patients to initiate or step-up prophylactic therapy before peak exposure, and to ensure that controller medications are available at home. At the public-health level, near-real-time monitoring of digital search and online medication purchases can complement traditional surveillance by providing early warning of upcoming peaks, helping to coordinate stock management of OTC medications and to target health-education messages to periods of highest risk.

Limitations and methodological considerations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. First, the outcome in this study was the online purchase rate of 4 s-generation H1 antihistamines (loratadine, desloratadine, cetirizine, and levocetirizine), which are indicated for several allergic conditions and are not specific to allergic rhinitis. National data on indication-specific antihistamine use in China are limited, so we could not determine what proportion of purchases was attributable to AR versus other indications. Our outcome therefore reflects overall antihistamine demand with a strong, but not exclusive, AR component, which may introduce outcome heterogeneity and reduce the construct validity of antihistamine purchases as a pure proxy for AR burden. Second, medication data were derived solely from the Meituan online platform and do not include offline pharmacy sales or other e-commerce channels. We lacked information on Meituan's time-varying market share, pricing, and promotional activities for OTC antihistamines in Beijing between 2022 and 2024. Platform-level changes—such as growth in user base, shifts from offline to online purchasing after COVID-19, or unmeasured marketing campaigns—could have contributed to secular increases and to the pronounced spike in purchases observed in spring 2024, independently of true changes in AR burden. These factors should be considered when interpreting Meituan-based purchase rates as a proxy for population-level AR activity. Third, although we re-estimated the mediation model using pre-whitened time series and Newey–West HAC standard errors to reduce bias from serial correlation, our approach still represents a relatively simple time-series regression framework. We did not implement more complex dynamic mediation models, such as structural vector autoregressions or longitudinal structural equation models, which could provide a richer representation of lagged feedback between exposure, information seeking, and medication use. Future work with longer time series and a stronger methodological focus is warranted to validate and extend our findings using these advanced frameworks. Fourth, our study period spans the transition from strict zero-COVID policies to the post-reopening period. This change likely influenced both Baidu search volumes and online medication purchases through stockpiling, avoidance of in-person visits, and increased use of online pharmacies. We did not conduct a separate sensitivity analysis excluding 2022, because detailed data on COVID-19 incidence and policy intensity were unavailable and restricting the series would markedly reduce the number of complete seasonal cycles for time-series modelling. Therefore, residual confounding by COVID-19–related behavioral changes cannot be ruled out, and our findings should be interpreted in this context.

Future research directions

Future research should evaluate the integration of additional data streams—such as weather conditions, air pollution levels, clinical visit records, and patient-reported outcome measures—to further enhance surveillance and forecasting capabilities. The integration of meteorological factors has shown promise in influenza prediction models, with PM10 concentrations demonstrating significant predictive value when incorporated with appropriate lag times. Real-time, multi-source monitoring has the potential to enable proactive community health interventions, optimize resource allocation during pollen peaks, and ultimately reduce the burden of allergic rhinitis.

Advanced analytical approaches warrant investigation, including deep learning models for pollen classification, natural language processing of social media content for symptom severity assessment, and integration with wearable device data for continuous physiological monitoring. The development of standardized protocols for digital health surveillance in allergic diseases could facilitate multi-national collaborations and improve global health security preparedness.

The establishment of real-time decision support systems integrating environmental monitoring, digital behavior tracking, and clinical decision aids represents a promising frontier for precision allergy management. Such systems could provide personalized risk stratification, targeted intervention timing, and optimized therapeutic protocols based on individual exposure patterns and treatment response history.

DAFRL: a dynamic adaptive mean field game-based multi-agent cooperative decision-making method
Authors: Yanqiang Tang, Chengli Fan, Dengxiu Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAiming at the problems of insufficient coordination accuracy and poor adaptability to dynamic environments caused by complex coupling relationships in large-scale heterogeneous multi-agent systems, this paper proposes a cooperative decision-making method that balances rationality and adaptability. Firstly, To address the limitation that uniform weights in traditional mean field games cannot characterize the differentiated contributions of heterogeneous agents, this paper proposes a learnable heterogeneous weight mechanism. The weights are dynamically generated and adaptively updated by the environmental perception network to explicitly reflect the influence of different types of agents on group behavior. On this basis, we further present a heterogeneous weighted mean field game model, which quantifies the differentiated impacts of different types of agents on group behaviors through type-level dynamic weights, breaking through the limitations of uniform weights in traditional mean field theory. Secondly, a dynamic adaptive mean field decision-making framework is designed; an environment perception module is introduced to update the reward function and state transition parameters in real time, and combined with reinforcement learning, the Dynamic Adaptive Mean Field Reinforcement Learning (DAFRL) algorithm is constructed to achieve real-time tracking of equilibrium solutions. Finally, experiments conducted on the scenario of red-blue UAV swarm confrontation demonstrate that the proposed heterogeneous weighted modeling method effectively addresses the coupling problem of heterogeneous agents, and the dynamic adaptive framework significantly enhances environmental robustness. DAFRL exhibits comprehensive advantages in efficiency, accuracy and stability, thereby providing theoretical and technical support for multi-agent cooperative decision-making in complex scenarios.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the rapid development of artificial intelligence technology, Multi-Agent System (MAS)1–3, as a pivotal research direction in the field of artificial intelligence, has been applied in depth to key domains such as intelligent transportation, industrial automation, and national defense security. A MAS is composed of multiple agents with autonomous decision-making capabilities, which jointly accomplish complex tasks through mutual collaboration, competition, or communication. In intelligent transportation systems, MAS can be used to realize cooperative driving between vehicles, thereby improving traffic efficiency and alleviating congestion. In the field of industrial automation, multiple robotic agents can collaboratively complete complex assembly tasks on production lines, enhancing production efficiency and quality. In the national defense domain, UAV swarms, as a typical MAS, have become core equipment in modern confrontations; the quality of their collaborative tasks such as interception and penetration directly affects operational effectiveness.

Cooperative decision-making is the core prerequisite for MAS to exert collective advantages. Its essence lies in realizing the unity of individual interests and group objectives through behavioral coordination among agents. In complex scenarios such as UAV swarm confrontation and search and rescue, the rationality of cooperative decision-making directly determines the success or failure of tasks. For instance, in red-blue UAV swarm confrontation, red-side interceptor UAVs need to accurately allocate targets and collaboratively form a defense circle, while blue-side attacker UAVs need to collaboratively evade threats and achieve efficient penetration. A decision-making mismatch on either side may lead to mission failure. However, with the large-scale growth of agent quantity and the increasing complexity of task scenarios, multi-agent cooperative decision-making is confronted with two core challenges: first, the coupling complexity caused by heterogeneity. Agents of different types and functions exhibit significant differences in behavioral logic, and traditional homogenized modeling methods are difficult to adapt to their differentiated demands. Second, the adaptability to dynamic environments. Dynamic factors such as electromagnetic interference, meteorological changes, and target maneuvering will continuously undermine the stability of decision-making, and traditional static modeling methods are unable to adjust strategies in real time. Therefore, researching cooperative decision-making methods that possess large-scale adaptability, heterogeneity compatibility, and dynamic robustness has become the key to promoting the application of MAS in complex scenarios, which holds important theoretical value and engineering significance.

At present, scholars at home and abroad have made remarkable achievements in the research field of UAV multi-agent cooperative decision-making. Wang Chuanyun et al.4 proposed an intelligent UAV task allocation method based on the E-CARGO model, which addresses the problem of long time consumption in UAV swarm task allocation when using traditional methods. Fan Ruitao et al.5 adopted an integral reinforcement learning method based on historical data in a game environment to realize the optimal control of multi-UAV collaboration under input constraints, achieving favorable results. Zhou Liqi3 constructed four types of game-based multi-agent systems by using analytical tools such as game theory, graph theory, matrix theory, and system transformation theory, enriching and developing the relevant theories of swarm intelligence. References6–8 proposed a multi-agent deep reinforcement learning-based UAV path optimization method, realizing load balancing and reducing the energy consumption of the overall system. Liu et al.9 innovatively integrated multi-UAV trajectory prediction into multi-agent reinforcement learning, enabling multi-UAVs to make more accurate decisions based on situational prediction.

Although there are numerous research methods in the field of UAV multi-agent cooperative decision-making at present, with the increase in agent quantity and the improvement of task complexity, especially when facing problems such as high coupling complexity and insufficient coordination accuracy caused by the heterogeneity of large-scale multi-agents, existing methods are confronted with enormous challenges. Therefore, researching efficient multi-agent cooperative decision-making methods has important practical significance.

To address the core challenges of such large-scale heterogeneous scenarios, the integration of mean field games (MFG) and reinforcement learning has become a mainstream solution. Existing studies have covered four major directions: heterogeneous multi-population MFG modeling, mean-field MARL algorithms (e.g., MF-Q, MF-Actor-Critic), dynamic non-stationary mean-field frameworks, and robustness-oriented reinforcement learning10,11. However, they still present core limitations in adapting to UAV confrontation scenarios: most heterogeneous multi-population MFG models adopt static preset weights that cannot match the dynamic changes of confrontation situations, classical mean-field MARL relies on the uniform mean-field assumption and fails to satisfy the differentiated coordination demands of heterogeneous agents, most dynamic mean-field frameworks only support offline parameter correction and lack real-time environmental perception and equilibrium tracking capabilities, and robustness-oriented designs have not been deeply integrated with the scalability advantages of MFG, making it difficult to simultaneously ensure the coordination accuracy and dynamic robustness of large-scale swarms.

This study aims to propose innovative solutions based on mean field game theory to address the problems of heterogeneity and poor adaptability to dynamic environments in large-scale multi-agent systems, which is of great significance for improving the performance of MAS and expanding its application scope. By proposing a heterogeneous weighted mean field game model, differentiated weight models can be assigned to different types of agents, thereby improving the rationality of cooperative decision-making in heterogeneous groups, enabling MAS to collaborate more efficiently in complex tasks and exert greater effectiveness. Designing an adaptive decision-making framework for mean field games in dynamic environments can enhance the real-time performance of decision-making, enable MAS to better cope with dynamically changing environments, improve the robustness and adaptability of the system, and further promote the application of MAS in more complex and dynamic scenarios.

Definition of heterogeneous weighted mean field

To adapt to the differentiated behavioral contributions and task values of different types of agents in large-scale heterogeneous multi-agent systems and break through the modeling limitation that traditional mean field theory ignores heterogeneity by using uniform weights, this paper proposes the concept of heterogeneous weights, defined as the dynamic influence coefficients of different agent types on the global mean field, to quantify the differentiated effects of type-level heterogeneous characteristics on the group state distribution. Dynamic adaptive mean-field decision-making framework and algorithm

Overall framework design

In practical UAV collaborative scenarios, the environment is not static but subject to dynamic factors such as electromagnetic interference, meteorological changes, and dynamic target movement. To enable the heterogeneous weighted mean field game model to adapt to such dynamic environments, a Dynamic Adaptive Mean-Field Decision-Making Framework is designed, whose architecture is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Dynamic adaptive mean-field decision-making framework.

This framework is primarily composed of three core components: the Environment Perception Module, the Heterogeneous Mean-Field Calculation Module, and the Agent Strategy Module, forming a closed-loop decision-making system.

The Environment Perception Module collects environmental data in real time via various sensors equipped on UAVs, constructs the environmental state space , and generates adjustment quantities and for the reward function and the state transition drift term in the mean field game model based on this space.

The Heterogeneous Mean-Field Calculation Module computes the heterogeneous weighted mean field according to the current state of each agent and the dynamically learned type weight .

The Agent Strategy Module enables each agent to generate actions via the policy network, by combining its own state , the heterogeneous mean field , as well as the reward function and state transition drift term adjusted by the Environment Perception Module.

Dynamic adaptive mean-field reinforcement learning (DAFRL) algorithm

The Dynamic Adaptive Mean-Field Reinforcement Learning (DAFRL) algorithm is proposed based on the equilibrium theory of the heterogeneous weighted mean field game elaborated in Sect. 2.3. Its core objective is to track the equilibrium of the heterogeneous weighted mean field game in dynamic environments in real time through an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework for large-scale heterogeneous multi-agent systems, thereby balancing the dynamic adaptability and scalability of the system while ensuring decision-making optimality.

The core idea of DAFRL is realized through three key designs: first, numerical mapping of equilibrium, which employs deep neural networks to approximate the value function and optimal control . Specifically, the Q-network corresponds to the numerical approximation of the value function, while the policy network yields the parametric output of the optimal control. Second, heterogeneous population modeling, which strictly adheres to the definition of the heterogeneous weighted mean field and dynamically learns the type weight via a weight network. This design not only preserves the heterogeneity characterization capability of the theoretical model but also enhances the adaptability of weight allocation. Third, dynamic equilibrium adaptability, which introduces an environment perception network to correct the drift term and reward function in real time. This allows the core parameters of the game model to be dynamically adjusted with environmental changes, achieving real-time tracking of equilibrium solutions.

Computational complexity and convergence analysis

The computational complexity of DAFRL stems from three components: first, heterogeneous mean-field calculation, with a complexity of O(N). The linear complexity ensures excellent large-scale scalability. Second, policy and Q-network updates, with a complexity of O(M.D), where D denotes the dimension of network parameters. Since a single set of networks is shared by all agents of the same type, the algorithm avoids the O(N2) complexity inherent in traditional MARL methods. Third, environment perception module, with a complexity of O(E), where E denotes the dimension of environmental states. This module only adds real-time adjustment of environmental parameters without introducing additional high-complexity components. Therefore, the overall complexity of DAFRL is O(N + M.D + E), maintaining a linear relationship with the number of agents. Combined with the engineering design of the DAFRL algorithm in this paper and the continuous-space scenario of UAV confrontation, the above convergence assumptions can be adapted through algorithm implementation and scenario extension: the Actor/Critic learning rates set in this paper adopt a decay mechanism to satisfy the Robbins-Monro conditions; the continuous state/action spaces are uniformly approximated by deep neural networks, which are equivalent to a high-dimensional extension of the tabular environment; the random sampling from the experience replay buffer ensures the zero-mean stationary ergodicity of the TD errors; and the output of the weight network, which lies in [0, 1], also satisfies the bounded convergence requirements.

Combined with the theoretical verifications and experimental results in the above typical scenarios, Theorems 1 and 2, the proposed heterogeneous weight model, and the DAFRL algorithm in this paper are applicable to tasks that involve large-scale heterogeneous multi-agent distributed cooperation/confrontation scenarios, bounded environmental disturbances, dynamics satisfying Lipschitz continuity, and agents that can be partitioned into a finite number of functional types. They are not applicable to scenarios with an excessively small number of agents, unbounded environmental disturbances, or irregularly heterogeneous agents.

Conclusions

This paper conducts research on large-scale heterogeneous multi-agent cooperative decision-making, drawing the following conclusions:

(1) A heterogeneous weighted mean field game model is proposed, which dynamically quantifies the differentiated impacts of heterogeneous agents on group behavior via dynamic weights, and theoretically resolves the coupling complexity problem of cooperative decision-making for heterogeneous populations. Theorem 1 verifies the existence of Nash equilibrium for the model. Simulation experiments demonstrate that differentiated weight allocation can accurately adapt to agent type differences and optimize resource scheduling efficiency.

(2) The environment perception module dynamically revises the reward function and state transition parameters in real time, achieving real-time tracking of equilibrium solutions in dynamic environments. Theorem 2 verifies the convergence with probability 1 of the algorithm under the Robbins-Monro condition. In experiments, the training convergence steps of DAFRL are reduced by 16.7% compared with the traditional MFG algorithm. Moreover, in the strong disturbance scenario with wind speed of 0.1 m/s, the environmental disturbance attenuation rate of DAFRL is only 15%, which reflects its excellent efficiency and robustness.

(3) Compared with previous studies, this research incorporates heterogeneous weights into mean field games, clarifies the modeling logic of type-level rather than individual-level heterogeneity, and balances model complexity and coordination accuracy. An environment perception module is constructed to address the limitation of traditional static mean field game modeling that fails to adapt to dynamic environments. However, the research still has limitations: it does not consider altitude coordination in three-dimensional space and complex meteorological interference, and the learning efficiency of the weight network can be further improved in extremely dynamic environments.

(4) Future work will further carry out theoretical analysis on the uniqueness of equilibrium, global stability and equilibrium selection of heterogeneous weighted mean field games, so as to improve the fundamental theoretical system of the proposed framework. Furthermore, based on the definition of the theoretical applicable scope above, the research will be extended to 3D UAV swarm cooperative scenarios by introducing the altitude dimension and complex airflow disturbance models, and optimizing the design of action space and state observation. Combined with meta-learning and transfer learning methods, the generalization ability of the DAFRL algorithm across different scenarios and tasks will be enhanced, so as to promote the engineering application of the technology in practical equipment.

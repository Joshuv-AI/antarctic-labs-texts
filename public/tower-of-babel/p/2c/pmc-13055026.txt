A multimodal predictive model incorporating transcriptomic-guided blood biomarkers and clinical variables for sepsis-associated acute kidney injury
Authors: Weiqin Wu, Xiang Han, Zhuan Yan, Lili Gao, Qingsong Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI) is a major complication in the intensive care unit (ICU), and early risk stratification remains challenging. This study developed a multimodal predictive model integrating clinical variables with transcriptomic-guided blood biomarkers. Differentially expressed genes were identified from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, and cluster of differentiation 177 (CD177) and interleukin-18 receptor 1 (IL18R1) were identified as immune-activation biomarkers associated with SA-AKI. In a retrospective cohort of 188 septic patients (89 SA-AKI), gene expression was quantified by quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) using blood samples collected within 24 h of ICU admission. Clinical predictors including the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score, mean arterial pressure (MAP), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), C-reactive protein (CRP), and mechanical ventilation were incorporated using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) feature selection and logistic regression. Model performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), calibration curves, and decision-curve analysis in an internal test cohort and an external cohort (n = 83). SOFA score, MAP, BUN, CRP, and CD177 were independent predictors of SA-AKI. The combined model showed excellent discrimination (AUC 0.989) with good calibration (Hosmer–Lemeshow p = 0.996; Brier score 0.040) and remained robust in external validation (AUC 0.909). Integrating immune biomarkers with routine clinical parameters may improve early prediction of SA-AKI, although multicenter prospective validation is required.

Introduction

Sepsis is a common and life-threatening syndrome in intensive care units, with persistently high mortality rates [1,2]. Acute kidney injury (AKI) is one of the most frequent organ dysfunctions in septic patients, with an incidence of up to 40%-50%, significantly increasing hospital stay, treatment costs, and mortality risk [3,4]. Due to the complex pathophysiology of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (Sepsis-AKI), which involves inflammatory cytokine storms, immune dysregulation, impaired renal perfusion, and oxidative stress [5–7], early clinical identification and intervention remain major challenges.

At present, the clinical identification of AKI primarily depends on variations in serum creatinine levels and reduced urine output, which are often delayed and lack sensitivity. While certain clinical indicators, such as the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score, C-reactive protein (CRP), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and mean arterial pressure (MAP), offer limited predictive utility for evaluating patient risk, their sensitivity and specificity remain suboptimal. Their standalone predictive performance is limited, making precise identification difficult. In recent years, the advancement of bioinformatics and high-throughput sequencing technologies has enabled transcriptomic data mining and molecular biomarker discovery to become key approaches in improving predictive model performance.

Previous studies have shown that the immune-related molecule cluster of differentiation 177 (CD177) is significantly upregulated in sepsis and is closely associated with neutrophil activation [8,9]. Interleukin-18 receptor 1 (IL18R1), an inflammation-related receptor, has also demonstrated potential diagnostic value in renal diseases. However, the role and combined predictive potential of CD177 and IL18R1 in Sepsis-AKI remain insufficiently explored. Integrating clinical indicators with key immune factors to construct a stable and accurate combined predictive model may offer a novel approach for the early detection and individualized treatment of Sepsis-AKI in clinical practice.

SA-AKI is increasingly recognized not merely as a hemodynamic or perfusion-driven phenomenon, but as a disorder rooted in complex immune dysregulation, endothelial and microcirculatory dysfunction, metabolic reprogramming, and inflammatory injury [10,11]. For instance, recent reviews have emphasized that dysregulated inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, endothelial activation, and immune cell infiltration contribute substantially to SA-AKI pathogenesis, challenging the traditional paradigm that equates septic AKI with acute tubular necrosis from renal hypoperfusion alone. Furthermore, recognition is growing of subphenotypes within AKI, defined by distinct biomarker or molecular profiles rather than just clinical or hemodynamic parameters. Biologically informed approaches – especially those integrating immune activation markers – may thus offer better early risk stratification and mechanistic insight, laying a foundation for personalized management and targeted interventions in SA-AKI. In this context, our identification and use of immune-related blood biomarkers (e.g., CD177 [12], IL18R1 [13]) – rather than relying solely on conventional clinical predictors – represent a timely and conceptually aligned advancement. This strategy aligns with the modern understanding of SA-AKI as an immunologically mediated condition and underscores the study’s potential to bridge mechanistic insight with predictive clinical modeling.

However, current clinical scoring systems alone provide only moderate predictive accuracy, and they do not incorporate molecular signatures that reflect the underlying immune dysregulation of sepsis. This gap highlights the need for models that integrate biomarker and clinical data to enable earlier and more precise risk stratification. Based on this rationale, this study combines several transcriptomic profiles from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database to comprehensively screen for differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in Sepsis-AKI. In the biomarker discovery stage, we applied a stringent differential-expression threshold (|log2FC| > 2 and FDR < 0.05) to ensure the selection of biologically meaningful and reproducible transcriptomic signals rather than noise-level fluctuations. Such combined magnitude-plus-significance filtering is widely recommended for robust DEG identification. In addition, integrating datasets of different sample sizes is a recognized strategy to improve statistical power and reduce study-specific bias, provided that appropriate normalization and quality control steps are applied. This multi-dataset approach has been shown to enhance stability and reproducibility in transcriptomic biomarker research [14,15]. Subsequent functional enrichment and protein–protein interaction (PPI) analyses enabled the identification of pivotal regulatory genes, including CD177 and IL18R1. Using retrospectively collected clinical data, we apply Logistic Regression and machine learning methods to construct a combined predictive model, which will be validated in internal and independent external datasets to evaluate its clinical utility in diagnosis and risk assessment.

Beyond its value as a neutrophil activation marker, CD177 also has plausible biological links to renal injury in the context of sepsis [16]. CD177 is highly expressed on activated neutrophils and facilitates their interaction with endothelial cells during systemic inflammatory responses [17]. In sepsis, excessive neutrophil activation and adhesion can promote renal microvascular dysfunction through multiple mechanisms, including the release of reactive oxygen species, proteolytic enzymes, and neutrophil extracellular traps, as well as amplification of local inflammatory cascades within the kidney [18,19]. These processes may contribute to endothelial injury, impaired microcirculatory perfusion, and tubular stress, all of which are increasingly recognized as central features of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury beyond hemodynamic compromise alone. Therefore, although CD177 is not assumed to be a direct causal mediator of renal damage, its expression reflects an immune-activated state that is biologically relevant to the development and progression of SA-AKI, supporting its selection as a clinically informative biomarker in the present study.

Materials and methods

Study design

This retrospective cohort and single-center study was carried out and involved patients admitted to the Department of Critical Care Medicine with a diagnosis of sepsis at our hospital between April 2022 and April 2023. Inclusion criteria were based on the Sepsis-3 definition of sepsis [20,21]. Exclusion criteria included: (1) people previously diagnosed chronic kidney disease (CKD); (2) presence of concurrent malignancies or immune system disorders; (3) people with incomplete clinical data. Participants were categorized into Sepsis-non-AKI and Sepsis-AKI groups based on the occurrence of AKI, as defined by the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines [22,23].

Reporting Standards

This study adhered to established guidelines for observational research reporting. The STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) checklist and the RECORD (REporting of studies Conducted using Observational Routinely collected Data) extension were completed to ensure transparent and comprehensive reporting of this retrospective cohort study. The completed STROBE and RECORD checklists are provided in the Supplementary Material.

Clinical data collection

Clinical data – demographic characteristics (age and sex), vital signs MAP, laboratory values (lactate, BUN, CRP), SOFA scores, mechanical ventilation status, and AKI occurrence – were obtained from the electronic medical records system. All variables were recorded within the first 24 h of admission. Evaluation was conducted according to Sepsis-3 guidelines [24], assessing the function of six organ systems: respiratory, coagulation, liver, cardiovascular, central nervous system, and renal. Scoring was based on PaO2/FiO2 ratio, platelet count, bilirubin levels, MAP and use of vasopressors, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score, serum creatinine, and urine output. Organ dysfunction severity was assessed by assigning scores from 0 to 4 for each organ system, resulting in a cumulative score ranging from 0 to 24.

Gene expression detection

Peripheral blood samples collected on the day of admission were used to isolate total RNA. cDNA was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit, followed by quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) to assess the expression of CD177 and IL18R1. Gene expression levels were quantified using the 2ΔΔCt approach, with GAPDH serving as the reference gene for normalization. All procedures were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions and conducted by the same technician in the same laboratory to ensure consistency.

Peripheral venous blood (3 mL) was drawn into EDTA anticoagulation tubes within 24 h of ICU admission, before the initiation of renal replacement therapy. Immediately after collection, tubes were gently inverted, transported on ice, and processed within 2 h. Plasma was separated by centrifugation, and the remaining cell fraction was used for RNA extraction.

Total RNA was isolated using a commercially available column-based kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration and purity were assessed by spectrophotometry (NanoDrop), and samples with evidence of protein or solvent contamination (abnormal A260/280 or A260/230 ratios) were re-extracted. Purified RNA aliquots were stored at −80 °C until downstream analysis.

For each sample, 1 μg of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using a commercial reverse transcription kit with random primers in a 20 μL reaction. Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) was performed on a real-time detection system using SYBR Green chemistry in 96-well plates. Each reaction (20 μL) contained cDNA template, SYBR Green master mix, and gene-specific primers. The cycling protocol consisted of an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 30 s. A melt-curve analysis was performed at the end of each run to confirm the presence of a single, specific amplification product.

Gene expression levels of CD177 and IL18R1 were quantified using the ΔCt method (ΔCt = Ct_target − Ct_GAPDH), with glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) serving as the internal reference gene. Relative expression was calculated using the 2^−ΔΔCt method, with the median value of the Sepsis-non-AKI group as the calibrator. To confirm the suitability of GAPDH as a housekeeping gene in this cohort, we examined the distribution of GAPDH Ct values across Sepsis-AKI and Sepsis-non-AKI patients and observed no systematic shift or trend between groups, indicating stable baseline expression.

All qPCR experiments were performed in duplicate, and reactions with inconsistent Ct values or abnormal amplification curves were repeated. The final primer pairs used in this study were as follows: IL18R1: forward 5′-AAGACATGGCTGATATCCCAGG-3′, reverse 5′-GCTCCTCCAGGCACTACATC-3′; CD177: forward 5′-TGACTGAGAACTGCGATATGAA-3′, reverse 5′-GTGGATGGTGGTCTTCTGGG-3′; GAPDH: forward 5′-AAAGCCTGCCGGTGACTAAC-3′, reverse 5′-GACTCCACGACGTACTCAGC-3′. All primers were synthesized by a commercial provider and tested experimentally for specificity. Primer specificity and the absence of nonspecific products or primer-dimers were confirmed by melt-curve analysis and inspection of amplification curves in all qPCR runs.

Bioinformatic analysis

Three whole-blood transcriptomic datasets (GSE185263, GSE242059, and GSE232404) were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. The GSE185263 dataset contains 348 sepsis and 44 healthy control samples, as confirmed by GEO metadata. Because this dataset does not include Sepsis-AKI annotations, it was used exclusively for differential expression analysis between sepsis and healthy controls. In contrast, GSE242059 and GSE232404 each include 5 healthy controls and 5 Sepsis-AKI patients, providing small but biologically relevant AKI-specific signatures.

Raw expression matrices were processed following the platform-specific recommendations (RMA for microarray data and TPM normalization for RNA-seq data) and subsequently transformed using log2(x + 1) to stabilize variance. Genes with low expression (bottom 20% across samples) were filtered out to reduce noise. Probe IDs were mapped to gene symbols, and multiple probes corresponding to the same gene were collapsed by mean expression.

Because the datasets originated from different platforms and laboratories, batch effects were corrected using the ComBat function in the sva R package. Prior to integration, sample quality and preprocessing adequacy were examined using boxplots, density plots, and principal component analysis (PCA). PCA performed before and after ComBat correction demonstrated effective removal of batch-associated variation, supporting the feasibility of integrating the datasets.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the limma package, with significance defined as |log2FC| > 2 and adjusted p-value < 0.05. Overlapping DEGs across datasets were visualized using Venn diagrams. Functional enrichment analyses for Gene Ontology (GO) terms and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways were performed using the clusterProfiler package. Protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks of the DEGs were constructed through the STRING database, and the top 10 hub genes were identified using the cytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape.

Given the small sample sizes of GSE242059 and GSE232404, a sensitivity analysis was performed in which these datasets were excluded. The resulting DEG profiles were highly consistent with those from the integrated analysis, indicating that the inclusion of small datasets did not introduce instability or bias and that multi-dataset integration increased statistical robustness without distorting biological signals.

Statistics

Statistical analyses were carried out with SPSS version 26.0 and R version 4.2.1. Data following a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and group comparisons were made using independent samples t-tests. For data not conforming to normality, values were expressed as median (interquartile range) and analyzed with the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical data were summarized as counts (percentages) and compared via chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests where appropriate. Univariate and multivariate Logistic Regression analyses were performed to identify independent risk factors. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. To assess multicollinearity among predictors in the multivariable logistic regression model, variance inflation factors (VIF) were calculated. For each predictor, VIF was computed as 1/(1-R2), where R2 represents the coefficient of determination from regressing that predictor on all other predictors. A VIF value <5 was considered indicative of acceptable independence without meaningful inflation of variance.

Model construction and validation

Clinical predictors that remained significant in multivariate logistic regression (SOFA score, MAP, BUN, CRP, and mechanical ventilation status), together with CD177 and IL18R1 gene expression levels, were included as candidate variables for model development. To rigorously control overfitting and avoid information leakage, a structured data-partitioning and nested cross-validation strategy was employed. The full dataset was first split into a training cohort (70%) and a held-out internal test cohort (30%) using stratified sampling to preserve the proportion of Sepsis-AKI. The external cohort was reserved exclusively for post-hoc validation and was not involved in feature selection, normalization, model training, or hyperparameter optimization.

Feature selection was conducted using the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) with L1 regularization. Within the nested CV framework, LASSO was executed only within each training fold, with a five-fold inner loop used to determine the optimal penalty parameter (λ). Selected features were then applied to the corresponding outer-fold validation sets, ensuring that no information from validation folds influenced feature selection. LASSO was used to mitigate multicollinearity.

Three supervised classifiers – Logistic Regression, Random Forest (RF), and Support Vector Machine (SVM) – were constructed using the selected predictors. All models were implemented in Python 3.9 with Scikit-learn (v1.2.2). Logistic Regression used L2 regularization with the ‘liblinear’ solver. RF models were initialized with controlled tree depth and node-splitting constraints to reduce model complexity, while SVM models used the radial basis function (RBF) kernel to capture nonlinear patterns.

Hyperparameter tuning for RF and SVM was performed through a grid-search procedure embedded within the inner loop of the nested CV pipeline. In each inner-fold, the hyperparameter set yielding the highest mean AUC was chosen, and the resulting model was then evaluated on the corresponding outer-fold validation set. This nested framework provided an unbiased estimate of generalization performance and minimized the risk of optimistically biased results.

After nested CV, the held-out internal test cohort, which played no role in model training or tuning, was used for an additional unbiased assessment of model robustness. The external validation cohort was subsequently used to evaluate real-world generalizability. Model performance metrics included AUC, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. Logistic Regression coefficients and RF Gini importances were used to quantify feature contributions. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to visualize class separability.

Because discrimination alone cannot confirm reliability, calibration assessment was additionally conducted. Calibration curves were generated using 1,000-bootstrap resampling, and goodness-of-fit was evaluated using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. Brier scores were calculated to quantify the mean squared deviation between predicted and observed outcomes. Calibration was assessed independently for the training cohort, internal test cohort, and external validation cohort.

Overall, this workflow ensured stringent control of overfitting, rigorous prevention of information leakage, and reliable estimation of both internal and external model performance.

Multicollinearity assessment and handling

Prior to model construction, multicollinearity among candidate predictors was evaluated using variance inflation factors (VIFs). Several clinical severity indicators and inflammatory biomarkers exhibited elevated VIF values. This finding was expected and biologically plausible in the context of sepsis, as multiple variables (e.g., SOFA score, MAP, BUN, CRP, and immune-related gene expression) capture overlapping aspects of systemic illness severity and inflammatory burden.

Importantly, high VIF values in this setting do not necessarily indicate model misspecification, but rather reflect the inherent correlation structure of sepsis-related clinical and molecular features. To mitigate potential coefficient instability and overfitting arising from multicollinearity, we therefore employed penalized regression using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO). This approach shrinks correlated coefficients and selects stable predictors, allowing robust model development despite correlated inputs. The retention of key predictors under penalization supports the reliability of the modeling strategy.

External validation

The external validation cohort (n = 83) was retrospectively identified from consecutive patients admitted between May 2023 and February 2024. Although these data were collected chronologically after the development cohort, the extraction and analysis were performed retrospectively. Therefore, both the internal and external datasets in this study are part of a retrospective observational design. The entire model training and prediction workflow was repeated, and performance was evaluated using ROC curves, confusion matrices, and classification reports to assess accuracy and generalizability.

Sample size

The sample size for this study was estimated using two primary methods. First, based on the commonly used Events Per Variable (EPV) principle in Logistic Regression modeling, it was recommended that there be no fewer than 10 outcome events (cases of Sepsis-AKI) per independent variable. Considering that approximately 8 variables were included and the incidence of Sepsis-AKI in the target population was approximately 47.3%, the total required sample size was estimated to be around 170 cases. This study ultimately included 188 patients, of whom 89 developed AKI, thereby meeting the EPV requirement for Logistic Regression. Second, the sample size was calculated based on the expected discriminatory ability of the model, using the AUC method. Assuming a significance level of α = 0.05, statistical power of 1−β = 0.80, an anticipated AUC of 0.80, and a positive event rate of 47.3%, the estimated required sample size ranged from 140 to 160 cases. The actual sample size in this study meets the requirements of both estimation approaches, providing a solid basis for model construction and validation.

Results

Bioinformatics analysis: Identification of key gene signatures in Sepsis-AKI

Overall Analytical Framework and Sequence of Results Presentation

Given the hybrid design of this study, which integrates transcriptomic biomarker discovery with clinical risk modeling, the Results section follows a sequential framework. We first report the bioinformatics analyses performed on the GEO dataset (GSE185263), which identified CD177 and IL18R1 as reproducible immune-activation markers associated with SA-AKI. These biomarkers formed the mechanistic and statistical basis for subsequent model development. Following biomarker identification, we then incorporated clinical variables and constructed the combined predictive model using nested cross-validation and external validation. After establishing the model, we present the baseline characteristics of the clinical cohort, providing a detailed comparison of demographic, physiological, and laboratory features between the AKI and non-AKI groups, which contextualizes the observed model performance and clinical applicability.

Differential expression analysis

To identify key genes associated with Sepsis-AKI, three high-quality expression datasets were obtained from the GEO: GSE185263, GSE242059, and GSE232404. GSE185263 includes blood samples from 348 sepsis patients and 44 healthy controls. GSE242059 and GSE232404 contain blood samples from 5 healthy controls and 5 Sepsis-AKI patients. These blood samples were used to compare gene expression differences between sepsis vs. healthy controls and Sepsis-AKI vs. healthy controls. Data normalization and identification of differentially expressed genes were carried out using the ‘limma’ package in R. The thresholds were set as |log2 FC| > 2 and p.adj < 0.05, identifying significantly upregulated and downregulated DEGs in each group. Venn diagrams were used to identify intersecting DEGs across datasets, highlighting candidate genes potentially involved in Sepsis-AKI pathogenesis (Figure 1).

Functional enrichment analyses

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted to investigate the biological functions of the DEGs (Table 1 and Figure 2). DEGs were enriched in antimicrobial humoral responses, humoral immune responses, and defense against bacteria and fungi. Cellular component terms were enriched in vesicle lumen and secretory granule lumen. Notably, molecular functions such as immunoglobulin receptor binding, RAGE receptor binding, and fatty acid binding were enriched. Significant enrichment was revealed in the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway, Staphylococcus aureus infection, and transcriptional dysregulation in cancer, suggesting a role in immune regulation and tissue injury in Sepsis-AKI. Jaccard similarity coefficients and hierarchical clustering (hclust) were used to group related GO/KEGG terms into functional modules. An enrichment map (EMAP) was constructed based on the degree of similarity to reveal the interrelationships among enriched terms. The results showed that multiple significantly enriched GO terms and KEGG pathways formed a tightly connected functional network, further validating the synergistic roles of these pathways in disease progression.

To investigate protein-level interactions, DEGs were analyzed using the STRING database (https://string-db.org), with a high-confidence threshold (combined score > 0.90). Network visualization was performed in R (version 4.2.1) using the igraph and ggraph packages. The resulting network revealed multiple functional clusters, with several genes showing strong interaction strength (combined score > 0.95), indicating potential regulatory significance. Multicollinearity assessment and stability of LASSO-selected predictors

Variance inflation factor (VIF) analysis revealed the presence of moderate-to-high multicollinearity among several clinical and molecular predictors included in the multivariable model. Age, SOFA score, MAP, BUN, CRP, CD177, and IL18R1 demonstrated VIF values exceeding conventional thresholds, indicating substantial correlation among severity markers and inflammatory signatures. Such multicollinearity may inflate coefficients in traditional regression models; therefore, penalized regression was used to improve stability.

LASSO feature selection at the optimal penalty parameter (λ_[25]) retained non-zero coefficients for all candidate predictors. Among clinical variables, SOFA score (β = 0.616), lactate (β = 0.453), BUN (β = 0.349), and mechanical ventilation (β = 0.339) showed notable positive contributions, while MAP exhibited a protective negative coefficient (β = −0.122). Importantly, the largest coefficient magnitudes were observed for the molecular biomarkers CD177 (β = 1.026) and IL18R1 (β = 1.017), indicating strong independent predictive influence even in the context of correlated covariates.

To evaluate the robustness of feature selection under a more conservative penalty, we further examined the LASSO model at λ_{1se}, Table S2 and Figure S1. Several weaker predictors, including gender and mechanical ventilation, were eliminated, and coefficients for SOFA score, lactate, MAP, BUN, and CRP were further shrunk. Notably, CD177 (β = 0.467) and IL18R1 (β = 0.646) remained among the strongest contributors despite the increased regularization. The consistent retention and relatively large coefficient magnitudes of these biomarkers across penalization levels support their role as stable, non-redundant predictors of Sepsis-AKI risk, rather than artifacts of overfitting or statistical collinearity.

Performance of the combined predictive model: discrimination, calibration, and overall accuracy

Among single predictors, the AUCs for SOFA score, MAP, and BUN were 0.869, 0.861, and 0.843, respectively. The AUC for CD177 expression was 0.772. In contrast, the combined model achieved an AUC of 0.989 (95% CI: 0.980–0.999), with a sensitivity of 95.51% and specificity of 96.97%, significantly outperforming any individual variable and demonstrating excellent clinical predictive performance. Calibration analysis demonstrated that the combined model exhibited outstanding internal agreement between predicted and observed probabilities. In the internal validation cohort, the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test yielded a χ2 value of 1.23 with 8 degrees of freedom (p = 0.996), indicating no evidence of lack of fit. The calibration curve closely followed the 45-degree reference line, and the Brier score remained low at 0.040, further supporting excellent calibration performance. These results confirm that the model’s high discrimination is accompanied by minimal prediction bias and reliable probability estimation within the derivation cohort. (Table 5, Figure 4). Beyond discrimination, the model demonstrated excellent calibration, as evidenced by a Hosmer–Lemeshow χ2 of 1.23 (p = 0.996), a low Brier score of 0.040, and calibration curves showing close alignment with the ideal 45° reference line. These findings indicate that the high AUC was accompanied by reliable probability estimation.

Evaluation of the combined model’s performance in the test and external validation cohorts

Using clinical predictors that remained significant in multivariate logistic regression (SOFA score, MAP, BUN, CRP, and mechanical ventilation), together with CD177 and IL18R1 expression levels, we developed three supervised classifiers: logistic regression, random forest, and SVM with an RBF kernel. Hyperparameters for the machine-learning models were optimized by grid-search cross-validation within the training cohort, and performance was then evaluated in a held-out internal test cohort and an independent external validation cohort. The confusion matrix showed that out of 83 patients, the model correctly identified 39 Sepsis-non-AKI and 32 Sepsis-AKI cases, with only 4 Sepsis-non-AKI cases were misclassified as Sepsis-AKI, and 8 Sepsis-AKI cases were misclassified as Sepsis-non-AKI. Although the model demonstrated strong performance in an independent external cohort, broader generalizability across diverse clinical settings cannot be assumed and will require validation in multi-center prospective studies.

In the internal test cohort, all three models demonstrated excellent discrimination (Table 6). Logistic regression achieved an AUC of 0.994, with an accuracy of 0.95, precision of 0.96, recall of 0.93, and an F1 score of 0.94. The random forest model yielded a similar AUC of 0.995, with an accuracy of 0.96 and an F1 score of 0.96, while the SVM achieved an AUC of 0.981 and an accuracy of 0.91. These results indicate that the multimodal feature set combining clinical variables and transcriptomic biomarkers provides strong predictive signal for Sepsis-AKI within the derivation cohort.

Discussion

In this study, we developed and validated a multimodal predictive model for sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI) by integrating clinical risk factors with transcriptomic biomarkers identified through bioinformatic analysis. Among all candidate genes, CD177 emerged as a robust marker of immune activation, while IL18R1 showed a consistent but exploratory association with SA-AKI across discovery and validation cohorts. When combined with key clinical variables such as SOFA score, MAP, BUN, CRP, and mechanical ventilation status, these biomarkers substantially improved predictive performance, achieving excellent discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility across internal and external datasets. Collectively, these findings highlight the potential of combining molecular insights with routinely available clinical data to enhance early risk stratification and support precision decision-making in septic patients.

Sepsis-AKI is one of the most common complications in critical care medicine, characterized by high incidence, rapid progression, and poor prognosis [26,27]. Although several clinical scoring systems and biochemical markers have been developed for AKI risk assessment [28], the lack of sensitive and specific early predictive tools has resulted in many high-risk patients missing timely intervention [29,30]. The novelty of this study lies in the integration of key clinical indicators (SOFA score, MAP, BUN, CRP) with immune-related DEGs (CD177 and IL18R1) to construct a multidimensional, highly accurate, and well-generalized combined predictive model. This model holds promise to address the limitations of traditional prediction methods and offers a more precise and reliable tool for early identification of Sepsis-AKI.

Through differential expression analysis and PPI network construction, CD177 and IL18R1 were identified as potential key genes involved in Sepsis-AKI. CD177 is a glycoprotein closely associated with neutrophil activation; previous studies have demonstrated its upregulation in sepsis patients and its possible involvement in inflammatory responses and immune regulation [16,31,32]. IL18R1, the receptor for interleukin-18, has been extensively studied for its role in inflammation [33]. Compared to classical renal injury biomarkers such as kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1) and neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), which primarily reflect tubular damage and oxidative stress responses [34], CD177 and IL18R1 may provide complementary insights into the immune-inflammatory processes contributing to Sepsis-AKI. While KIM-1 and NGAL have demonstrated strong associations with established renal injury, their early predictive performance in the context of immune-mediated damage remains limited [35]. The integration of CD177 and IL18R1, therefore, adds a novel immunological dimension to AKI prediction, potentially enabling more comprehensive patient stratification in the early stages of sepsis. Incorporating these two genes alongside clinical indicators into the LASSO regression model, the resultant Logistic Regression model exhibited excellent predictive performance in both training and validation cohorts, with AUC values of 0.989 and 0.905, respectively, significantly outperforming single-indicator models. Moreover, the included clinical variables, SOFA score, MAP, BUN, and CRP, are commonly used, readily accessible, and practical clinical parameters closely linked to the occurrence of Sepsis-AKI, further enhancing the model’s clinical applicability. The fact that CD177 and IL18R1 remained in the model with relatively large coefficients at both λ_{min} and the more conservative λ_{1se} further supports their role as robust, non-redundant predictors of Sepsis-AKI, rather than unstable artifacts of a highly parameterized model.

Notably, IL18R1 demonstrated consistent retention during LASSO-based feature selection but did not achieve statistical significance in the conventional multivariable logistic regression model. This apparent discrepancy reflects a fundamental difference between predictive modeling and classical inferential frameworks. LASSO prioritizes variables that contribute incremental predictive value under penalization, particularly in the presence of correlated inflammatory markers, whereas multivariable regression emphasizes independent statistical association under fixed significance thresholds. In this context, IL18R1 may capture complementary immune-inflammatory information relevant to risk stratification without exerting a strong independent effect after adjustment. Accordingly, IL18R1 should be interpreted as an exploratory predictive biomarker rather than a definitive independent risk factor, and cautious language has been maintained throughout this study.

Some degree of optimism is unavoidable in single-center derivation cohorts. External validation demonstrated attenuated but preserved performance. In the external validation cohort, a brief case-level inspection of misclassified patients further informs the interpretation of model performance. False-positive and false-negative classifications were more frequently observed among patients with borderline hemodynamic parameters or overlapping inflammatory profiles, rather than among those with clearly preserved renal function or overt AKI. This pattern likely reflects the inherent clinical heterogeneity of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury, where early renal dysfunction, systemic inflammation, and hemodynamic instability often coexist along a continuum rather than as discrete states. Importantly, the clustering of misclassifications around clinically ambiguous cases suggests that model errors predominantly occurred in scenarios where diagnostic uncertainty is also common in real-world practice. From a clinical perspective, this observation supports the pragmatic utility of the model as a decision-support tool rather than a definitive diagnostic instrument, and underscores the need for cautious interpretation of predictions in borderline cases. Future multi-center studies with richer temporal and physiological data may help further refine risk stratification in these transitional clinical states. Such behavior is consistent with prior reports on AKI risk prediction models in critically ill populations.

The combined predictive model developed in this study demonstrated not only high accuracy and reproducibility but also clinical operability, making it suitable for early risk assessment and bedside screening. Unlike traditional single-indicator models, this model integrated molecular-level information related to inflammatory responses into the risk evaluation system, providing a more comprehensive reflection of the patient’s pathological state. Particularly in early stages when clinical symptoms are atypical or indicators have not significantly fluctuated, the model aids clinicians in the early detection of high-risk patients and supports tailored treatment planning, thereby reducing the incidence and progression risk of AKI. Although lactate levels and mechanical ventilation showed significant differences in univariate analyses, they were not retained as independent predictors in the multivariate model. Their associations should therefore be interpreted cautiously, as they may reflect illness severity rather than direct causal pathways contributing to Sepsis-AKI.

Mechanistically, CD177 may contribute to kidney dysfunction in sepsis through its role in modulating neutrophil infiltration and activation, potentially leading to exacerbated renal microvascular inflammation and injury [36,37]. Importantly, the present findings do not establish a causal role for CD177 in the pathogenesis of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury. Based on existing biological evidence, CD177 is more plausibly interpreted as an immune activation amplifier rather than a direct causal driver or a passive bystander. Elevated CD177 expression reflects heightened neutrophil activation and trafficking, which may exacerbate microvascular inflammation and endothelial dysfunction within the kidney during sepsis. However, given the observational nature of this study, CD177 should be regarded as a biologically meaningful marker of an immune-inflammatory phenotype associated with increased SA-AKI risk, rather than as a definitive therapeutic target. Further experimental and causal inference studies are warranted to clarify its precise mechanistic role. Similarly, IL18R1, by mediating IL-18-driven inflammatory signaling, could promote macrophage activation and proinflammatory cytokine cascades that disrupt tubular epithelial integrity and renal perfusion [38,39], although these interpretations remain hypothesis-generating, as IL18R1 did not retain independent significance after multivariable adjustment. These processes may underlie the strong association between these genes and Sepsis-AKI risk observed in this study, although further mechanistic validation in experimental models is needed.

Alignment with ADQI 28 SA-AKI Consensus and KDIGO research priorities

This work aligns closely with the ADQI 28 SA-AKI Consensus and KDIGO research priorities, both of which call for earlier risk stratification and integration of clinical and molecular data for sepsis-related kidney injury. By combining established clinical variables with inflammation-related biomarkers (CD177 and IL18R1), our multimodal model operationalizes the ADQI recommendation to move beyond clinical assessment alone and identify high-risk patients before functional decline.

Consistent with ADQI guidance, we evaluated discrimination, calibration, external validity, and clinical utility through decision-curve analysis, positioning the model as a prototype SA-AKI early warning approach. While receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis and decision curve analysis (DCA) together provide complementary perspectives on discrimination and net clinical benefit, we acknowledge that DCA does not explicitly quantify downstream harms associated with false-positive classifications. Alternative frameworks, such as expected utility curves, can further decompose misclassification costs but require explicit specification of utility weights for false positives and false negatives. In sepsis-associated AKI, such utilities remain highly context-dependent and are not standardized across clinical settings. To avoid introducing arbitrary assumptions that could compromise interpretability, we retained DCA as a pragmatic and widely accepted approach for assessing clinical usefulness, while recognizing this limitation in quantifying absolute harm. In line with KDIGO priorities, these results illustrate how predictive analytics can support earlier recognition, individualized monitoring, and prevention-focused care pathways in sepsis.

Implementation considerations and regulatory perspective

Although the proposed model shows strong discriminatory ability, real-world deployment as a clinical decision-support tool requires careful consideration. Dataset shift – arising from differences in patient populations, clinical workflows, and biomarker platforms across institutions and over time – may affect calibration and performance, underscoring the need for ongoing monitoring and periodic recalibration. Current regulatory frameworks for AI/ML-enabled medical devices, including the U.S. FDA’s guidance on adaptive algorithms and its Predetermined Change Control Plan (PCCP) concept [40,41], as well as principles outlined in the, emphasize transparency, performance surveillance, and risk mitigation.

Thus, while our model represents a validated prototype for early SA-AKI risk stratification, responsible clinical translation will require continuous performance evaluation, governance oversight, and controlled model updating to ensure long-term safety and effectiveness.

Learning health system framework and continuous model improvement

Embedding an AI-enabled prediction model within a learning health system (LHS) is essential for sustaining long-term clinical utility [42]. In this framework, deployment marks the beginning of the model lifecycle, followed by to detect performance drift, dataset shift, or calibration deterioration as clinical practices and populations evolve. Key performance metrics – discrimination, calibration, and net clinical benefit – should be routinely reassessed, with periodic model updating or re-training performed when drift is identified.

These practices align with the American Society of Nephrology’s recommendations for trustworthy AI in kidney care [43], which emphasize dynamic governance, transparent reporting, and adaptive updating. Integrating our Sepsis-AKI prediction model into such an LHS structure would support in real-world clinical use.

Toward a sepsis–kidney digital twin framework

Looking ahead, our multimodal prediction model could serve as an early foundational component of a future sepsis–kidney digital twin, an emerging precision-medicine framework in critical care. In such a system, the model would provide initial risk priors for SA-AKI based on integrated clinical indices and immune-related biomarkers, while subsequent real-time physiological data could iteratively update these estimates as a patient’s condition evolves.

Within this architecture, CD177 and IL18R1 may represent the immune–inflammatory dimension of the twin, complementing hemodynamic and metabolic inputs. Although developing a full digital twin will require substantial future work – including dynamic time-series modeling and prospective validation – the present study offers a feasible starting point for this long-term vision.

Causal inference to strengthen mechanistic understanding

Although CD177 and IL18R1 showed strong associations with SA-AKI risk, establishing whether these biomarkers exert a causal influence on kidney injury requires more rigorous analytic frameworks. Future studies incorporating causal inference methods – such as target trial emulation, g-methods, and targeted maximum likelihood estimation (TMLE) – will be essential to distinguish biological effects from confounding and selection bias. These approaches align with emerging AI-driven causal inference work in critical care nephrology [44] and may clarify whether a high-CD177/IL18R1 immune-activation phenotype contributes directly to AKI pathogenesis beyond predictive association.

Limitations

Despite systematic integration of bioinformatics and clinical data and validation of model performance, several limitations warrant consideration. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a modest sample size, and two GEO datasets each contained only 10 samples; thus, residual instability due to small transcriptomic cohorts cannot be excluded, although sensitivity analyses produced consistent DEG signatures. Second, the biological roles of CD177 and IL18R1 in SA-AKI require further mechanistic validation, as IL18R1 did not reach statistical significance after adjustment and should be regarded as exploratory. Third, reliance on blood-based biomarkers limits generalizability to other sample types such as urine. Fourth, missingness in certain clinical variables – owing to routine documentation patterns – may introduce residual confounding despite appropriate statistical handling. Finally, while the model performed well in both internal and external cohorts, true multicenter generalizability and cross-platform robustness remain to be established.

Future research should include larger prospective multicenter cohorts with standardized data collection, exploration of multi-omics integration, and mechanistic studies to refine biological interpretability. Embedding the model within real-world care will further require ongoing calibration assessment, drift monitoring, and iterative model updating. Beyond risk prediction, CD177 and IL18R1 may provide a biologically meaningful immune-inflammatory axis for precision nephrology frameworks, supporting earlier identification of high-risk patients and informing prevention-focused care pathways. This multimodal model may also serve as an initial component of a future sepsis–kidney digital twin, in which individualized SA-AKI risk estimates could be continuously updated using real-time physiological data to guide adaptive decision support.

Conclusion

In this study, we identified CD177 – and, to a lesser extent, the exploratory marker IL18R1 – as immune-related markers that, when integrated with routinely available clinical variables, showed meaningful potential for early prediction of Sepsis-AKI. The multimodal model demonstrated strong internal and external discriminatory performance, suggesting that combining immune signatures with clinical parameters may enhance risk stratification beyond traditional markers of tubular injury.

However, these findings should be interpreted as preliminary. The study was conducted at a single center, and the molecular associations identified here remain exploratory rather than definitive. Larger multi-center cohorts, robust calibration evaluation, and deeper mechanistic investigations are needed to validate these results and determine whether such multimodal approaches can be translated into clinical decision-making for sepsis-associated AKI.

Impact of volume-based procurement policy on coronary stent use in tianjin: interrupted time series analysis
Authors: Zhao Yang, Liangyu Ni, Wei Zhao, Yuxi Li, Yongyi Wu, Qiyun Zhu, Linlin Da, Weijia Liu, Qi Zhang, Jianping Li, Bin Jiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThis study aimed to analyze the impact of China’s centralized volume-based procurement policy on the procurement and utilization of coronary stents in Tianjin from 2019 to 2021.MethodsAn interrupted time-series design was used to assess monthly procurement data of coronary stents and balloons in Tianjin from January 2019 to December 2021.ResultsThe volume-based procurement policy significantly reduced coronary stents prices, with an average price reduction of 92.67% for ten selected stents, and the lowest price dropped to 469 CNY. Following policy implementation, annual procurement in Tianjin increased by 59.55%, and tertiary hospitals demonstrated a significant monthly level increase of 1,473.53 units (p = 0.027; 95% CI: 181.69–2,765.36). Imported stents procurement showed a significant level increase of 703.85 units (p < 0.0001), while domestic stents exhibited no significant monthly variation (p = 0.154). Structural upgrades accelerated: stainless steel stents decreased by 30.94% in usage share (p < 0.001; 95% CI: 17.36–44.52) and were rapidly phased out post-policy, whereas permanent-coated stents increased by 23.00% (p < 0.001; 95% CI: 18.46–27.54). Procurement expenditure decreased by 73.03% compared to the 2019 baseline. Drug-eluting balloons as an alternative technology remained unaffected (p = 0.32).ConclusionThe study shows that Tianjin’s centralized volume-based procurement policy for coronary stents significantly improved stent supply and clinical utilization quality, while substantially reducing medical insurance expenditures—indicating that such policies can optimize healthcare resource allocation and enhance the cost-effectiveness of medical device procurement.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s41043-025-01128-2.

Background

Coronary artery disease (CAD), also known as coronary heart disease or ischemic heart disease, is a leading cause of premature death worldwide, accounting for over 1/3 of cardiovascular disease incidence and nearly half of cardiovascular mortality [1–4]. Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) has become the preferred reperfusion therapy for STEMI, with stents evolving from bare metal stents (BMS) to drug-eluting stents (DES) and newer polymer-free or bioresorbable stents [5–7]. Globally, over 2 million coronary stents are implanted annually, with a market size of USD 9.3 billion in 2022 [8], continuing to expand in high-income countries such as the United States and Japan [8, 9]. However, stents remain among the most expensive medical consumables, creating substantial financial burdens and restricting PCI access, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) [10–13].

To mitigate these challenges, many countries have adopted centralized procurement and public tendering as cost-containment strategies. Such approaches enhance bargaining power, foster competition, and lower transaction costs [14, 15]. For instance, between 2006 and 2014, prices of BMS and DES declined significantly in the United States and several European countries [16]. In India, the state of Maharashtra reduced the price of imported DES by ~ 70% through open bidding in 2014, markedly improving affordability among low-income patients [17]. In addition, some countries have implemented price regulation through policies. Examples include Japan’s Foreign Price Adjustment (FPA) [18, 19], Italy’s reference pricing/ceiling bids, and India’s 2017 national price caps (BMS USD 108; DES USD 444), which lowered DES prices by ~ 80% and increased PCI uptake [11, 20, 21].

China faces a similar burden. An estimated 11.39 million people live with CAD, and mortality has risen steadily since 2012, particularly in rural areas [22]. Treatment costs are substantial: domestic stents cost CNY 7,500–18,500 (median 14,000, USD 1,946), while imported brands range from CNY 11,400 to 23,300 (USD 1,585–3,239), both exceeding prices in several other countries [23]. In 2019 alone, coronary stents accounted for CNY 15 billion (USD 2.1 billion), or ~ 10% of national expenditures on high-value medical consumables [23]. Despite such high spending, PCI use remains insufficient, with pronounced geographic disparities in training and service availability [24]. These concerns underscore the urgent need for policy measures to reduce stent prices and improve accessibility.

In response, the China National Joint Procurement Office introduced the “National Centralized Volume Procurement of Coronary Stents (GH-HD2020-1)” on October 16, 2020, which was implemented nationwide on January 1, 2021 [25]. The policy is grounded in the principles of “national organization, alliance-based procurement, and platform operation”. Earlier pilot programs in Jiangsu (2019) and Shanxi (2020) demonstrated 51–53% price reductions for stents [26]. The three rounds of national centralized volume procurement have reduced the prices of 112 drugs by an average of 54%, providing critical evidence for scaling up.

The stent procurement policy in China incorporates several innovative measures [27]. First, by centralizing the procurement of over one million units, it leveraged large purchase volumes to secure price reductions. Second, a prepayment mechanism was introduced, requiring medical insurance funds to advance at least 30% of the procurement amount, thereby easing cash flow pressures. Third, guaranteed procurement volumes and optimized distribution channels substantially reduced transaction and retail costs. Collectively, these measures effectively lowered the overall cost of stent procurement.

Tianjin, one of China’s four municipalities with an advanced healthcare system and prior procurement experience, provides an illustrative example of this policy in practice. In its national DES tender, 26 products participated; 12 were priced below CNY 1,000 and only 4 exceeded CNY 2,850. Ultimately, the 10 lowest-priced products were selected, all under CNY 800, averaging CNY 650.86 (USD 90.49) for domestic and CNY 733.43 (USD 101.97) for imported stents — a 93% reduction.Public and military hospitals formed a procurement alliance, with selected products purchased at the specified price. Meanwhile, medical insurance reimbursement was synchronized, setting a ceiling of CNY 798 per unit and with 110% of the ceiling applied to products priced over twice the payment limit [28].

With a per capita disposable income of CNY 44,569 (USD 6,196), an Engel coefficient of 28.5%, and an average per capita medical expenditure of CNY 3,129 (USD 435) from 2019 to 2021 (Supplement Table 1), Tianjin was well positioned for policy implementation. By 2020, it had 11.64 million basic medical insurance enrollees, and by 2022, over 3.9 million residents had access to primary care. Although the city has a relatively high physician density (3.85 vs. 3.15 per 1,000 population) and a low urban–rural hospitalization rate in 2023 (13.60%), it faces challenges of population aging (≥ 60 years: 23.5% vs. 19.8% nationally) and limited hospital beds (5.03 vs. 6.92 per 1,000). The Tianjin Medical Purchasing Center, with prior cross-regional procurement experience (e.g., intraocular lens procurement in 2020), played a central role in policy implementation.

Against this backdrop, the present study aimed to preliminarily evaluate the effect of the centralized volume-based procurement policy for coronary stents in China.Using procurement data from Tianjin between 2019 and 2021, we analyzed changes in unit prices, procurement volumes, and stent utilization before and after policy implementation.

Methods

Data source

We obtained the procurement invoices for coronary stents and balloons in Tianjin in 2019–2021 from the Tianjin Joint Purchasing Office. The invoices included details, such as the date of procurement, hospital, product specifications, product names, and manufacturers.

We obtained demographic data and per capita disposable income data for Tianjin in 2019–2021 from the statistical bulletin and the Tianjin Statistical Yearbook issued by the Tianjin Municipal Bureau of Statistics. The daily number of confirmed new cases of COVID-19 and specific prevention and control policies starting from January 2020 were also obtained from the official website of the Tianjin Municipal Health Commission.

The investigation and research process conforms to the principles outlined in the Helsinki Declaration. This study uses medical institution procurement records and does not involve patient or individual information. The study was approved for implementation by the Ethics Committee of Peking University.

Statistical analysis

We used an interrupted time series model to analyze the impact of the national volume-based coronary stent procurement policy on coronary stents and balloons. The primary study variables were the monthly coronary stent or balloon procurement volume and procurement monetary value. The level and trend effects of the policy on the study variables were described by segmented regression modeling using 24 data points before and 12 data points after policy implementation. By comparing models with different link functions and distributions, we found that the results of parameter estimates and tests were similar across the models and the fit was also similar. Hence, a generalized linear model, which is easier to understand and interpret, was selected for data fitting. Results

A large decrease in the unit price of stents and increased availability to patients

National centralized volume-based procurement of coronary stents resulted in a very high procurement volume and fierce bidding, which led to a large price reduction of the selected products. The procurement prices of the ten selected stents were all reduced to less than CNY 1000 (USD 139.0) per unit, with the lowest and highest selected prices being CNY 469 (USD 65.2) and CNY 798 (USD 111) per unit, respectively. These were 89.64%−96.63% lower than the market price in 2020, and the average price was reduced by 92.67%. Considering the original prices, stents imported from the United States were reduced by 95.10%, and domestic stents’ price was reduced by 91.63%. The differences in price reduction were primarily due to the higher purchase price of imported stents compared to domestic stents before the price reduction. Regarding polymer materials, the price of drug-eluting stents with permanent coatings was reduced by 93.54% and that of drug-eluting stents with biodegradable coatings was reduced by 91.80%. The differences in price reduction were primarily because three of the five stents with permanent coatings were imported. In contrast, the average price of stent procurement in 2020 was reduced by only 12.75% compared to 2019, and the price of most stents did not change significantly (Table 1).

In contrast to the price reduction of the selected stents, the overall price of alternative products did not change significantly (Table 1). The procurement data of the five alternative stents with continuous procurement records showed that the price of the stents did not change significantly from before the implementation of the policy (comparing 2019 to 2020). Moreover, the procurement prices of two stents, BuMA and NeoVas, decreased slightly after the implementation of the policy, while the price of the other three stents did not change significantly (Table 1).

An increased number of stents procured and the potential demand released

Overall, the annual number of stents purchased in Tianjin in 2021 was 51,631, which is 27.74% and 59.55% higher compared to 2019 (40,419) and 2020 (32,361) (Table 2), respectively, with no significant change in the population (Supplement Table 1). Table 2 shows the average number of stents per month in these three years. From the ITS model results, the level effect of the policy on overall procurement volume was 1487.00 (p = 0.0243, 95% CI: 205.90–2768.11.90.11), while the trend effect of the policy was not significant (Table 3; Fig. 1 A). The overall increase in procurement volume is the sum of the effects of the increase in the selected products and the decrease in the unselected products. The procurement volume of the selected products increased by 3050.6 (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 1789.4–4311.8.4.8) in the first month after the implementation of the policy, while the procurement volume of the unselected products decreased by 1563.6 (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 1156.6–1970.6.6.6); the difference in trend changes of both procurement volumes was not statistically significant (Table 2; Fig. 1B). Regarding the hospital level, the procurement volume at tertiary hospitals was significantly increased by 1473.527 (p = 0.027, 95% CI: 181.69–2765.36.69.36) after the implementation of the policy, while the trend change was not significant (p = 0.972). In contrast, the level change (p = 0.182) and trend change in procurement volume at secondary hospitals (p = 0.421) were not significant (Table 2; Fig. 1C). Compared with the original volume, the procurement volume of imported stents increased significantly by 703.85 (p < 0.0001), while the monthly change in the number of domestic stents purchased was not significant (p = 0.154). These results suggest clinicians preferred imported stents, given a small price difference and stable supply conditions (Table 3; Fig. 1D). In addition, the COVID-19 epidemic and its related prevention and control policies between January and March 2020 significantly impacted coronary stent procurement, with statistically significant reductions observed in all study variables associated with the number of stents (Table 3).

Changes in stent use, thereby accelerating generational replacement

Since their introduction, antiproliferative agents, polymers, and support platforms in stents have been continuously improved to reduce the risk of postoperative adverse outcomes. Moreover, many studies have also shown that reduced stent thickness is associated with reduced ischemic outcomes. Conversely, the impact of biodegradable coatings on clinical outcomes remains controversial. However, there is growing evidence of their benefits in reducing postoperative duration of dual antiplatelet therapy in patients at high risk of bleeding. The types of stents used have changed dramatically since the implementation of the policy (Table 3; Fig. 2). Before the policy was implemented, the use of permanently coated stainless-steel drug-eluting stents already exhibited a significant downward trend (p < 0.001). Still, it maintained an average monthly use of 54.75 in 2020 until the rapid and complete withdrawal from the market after the procurement policy was implemented in 2021 (Table 2). In addition, the procurement of coated and uncoated stainless steel biodegradable drug-eluting stents decreased by 910.75 (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 628.42–1193.08.42.08) in the first month after the policy was implemented and by 30.94% (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 17.36–44.52%) in terms of the procurement proportion (Table 2). In contrast, the clinical use of coated biodegradable cobalt-based alloy or cobalt-chromium alloy stents was steadily growing before the implementation of the volume-based procurement (p < 0.001). Its use further increased by 899.06 (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 497.84–1318.28.84.28) in volume and 9.61% in proportion in the first month, after the procurement policy was implemented (p < 0.001. 95% CI: 5.49–13.74%). Similarly, the procurement of permanently coated cobalt-based alloy or platinum-chromium alloy stents increased by 1548.27 (p = 0.001, 95% CI: 653.40–2443.13.40.13) in volume and 23.00% (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 18.46% − 27.54%) in proportion, in the first month after, the policy was implemented. Thus, the major price reduction of cobalt-chromium and platinum-chromium alloy stents has led to a sharp reduction in the clinical use of stainless-steel stents. Furthermore, patients can benefit by saving money while taking advantage of products with more advanced processing and reducing the use of dual antiplatelet therapy with biodegradable coated stents based on physiological indications.

A large decrease in procurement expenditure and reduction in medical insurance funds

The level of stent procurement expenditure decreased by 27.05 million CNY (USD 3.76 million) (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 22.38–31.73) after the implementation of the procurement policy, which was about 73.03% of the baseline, but the trend change was not significant (Table 3; Fig. 3A). Moreover, the level of procurement expenditure for selected stents decreased by 10.35 million (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 9.22–11.49) and the level of procurement expenditure for unselected stents decreased by 17.70 million CNY (USD 2.46 million) (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 12.98–20.42), while the trend change was also not significant (Table 4; Fig. 3B). Taken together, changes in the number of stents procured and the decrease in procurement expenditure have led to price and volume reductions for the selected stents and price reductions for the alternative stents. The total monetary value of coronary stent procurement in 2021 was approximately 34.33 million, a decrease of 91.76% and 89.57%, compared to 2019 and 2020, respectively. Based on the 2020 stent list price, the total savings in stent procurement expenditure in 2021 was approximately CNY 522.76 million (USD 72.68 million), which also led to substantial savings in medical insurance funds.

No significant change in drug-eluting balloon procurement

Drug-eluting balloons are primarily used for in-stent re-stenosis, but recent studies have demonstrated their efficacy and safety to the risk of new-onset small vessel disease and severe hemorrhage. Therefore, drug-eluting balloons can serve as an alternative to coronary stents. Before the implementation of the procurement policy, the number of drug-eluting balloons procured increased progressively month over month (p < 0.001, 95% CI: 4.60–6.23). After the policy was implemented, the level change (p = 0.32) and trend change (p = 0.12) were not significant (Fig. 4). These results showed that the clinical use of drug-eluting balloons is gradually increasing as an emerging technology and the reduction in the price of coronary stents did not lead to significant changes in PCI procedures.

Discussion

By analyzing the stent procurement data from 2019 to 2021, we confirmed that the volume-based procurement policy has significantly reduced prices, thereby improving the affordability of stents and greatly reducing healthcare expenditure. However, there are also some potential problems.

First, the policy has had a rapid impact, significantly reducing prices and medical insurance expenditures. China’s volume-based purchasing policy is organized nationally and brings together the enormous national demand, thus enabling effective negotiations for lower prices. Competitive bidding and selection criteria further increased competition among different companies. After the procurement policy was implemented, the average price of the 10 selected stents decreased by 92.67% (from CNY 10,241.74 (USD 1,423.87) to CNY 675.63 (USD 93.93)) compared to 2020, with the highest unit price not exceeding CNY 798 (USD 111). This significantly improves the affordability of stents. The level of stent procurement expenditure in Tianjin was reduced by 73.03%, which significantly reduced the medical insurance burden. The coronary stent price ceiling implemented nationwide in India since 2017 resulted in an approximately 76% reduction in the price of DES, from an average of Rs 121,000 to Rs 29,600 (approximately CNY 2,658 (USD 369.5)) [21]. In comparison, the reduction in DES price under the Chinese policy was larger, and the price decreased even further. In addition, the Chinese policy took effect rapidly, resulting in significant and steady price reductions in the first year. In contrast, the FPA policy in Japan, which lowered the price ceiling on government reimbursements through price matching with foreign countries, reduced prices more slowly, at 2–4 times the European and US prices at the turn of the century, while the average price only dropped to 0.88 times its value by 2016–2018 [18, 19]. Some studies have also discussed the procurement of coronary stents in China. A study has shown that the number of discharged patients who underwent coronary stent implantation in a hospital in Xinjiang increased by 19.28% compared to the same period before the implementation of the procurement. At the same time, the average hospitalization cost decreased by 43.53% year-on-year, and the cost of disposable medical consumables for surgery decreased by 64.42% year-on-year. A study of a hospital in Beijing showed that the total hospitalization cost and consumables cost (mainly coronary stent cost) of patients before and after coronary stent volume procurement decreased significantly, while the medical service fee increased [31]. After the implementation of the centralized procurement policy for coronary stents in Shanghai, the proportion of inpatients with coronary heart disease receiving PCI treatment with at least one coronary stent from the procurement was 84.10%, higher than that before the implementation of the policy (54.35%). However, the PCI treatment rate of inpatients with coronary heart disease was reduced [32].

With lower prices and improved affordability, stent purchases increased, reflecting a timely release of the demand for stents. As a country with a high burden of coronary heart disease, the number of PCI cases in China is unable to meet the medical demand of patients with coronary heart disease [24]. The high stent prices may be the main reason for the relative shortage [11]. After the price reduction, the average monthly procurement volume of stents exhibited a transient increase with no significant change in the population of Tianjin, and the level effect of the policy was significant, while the trend effect was not significant. The monthly procured stent volume was relatively stable after the price reduction. The total annual procurement volume in 2021 increased by 27.7% and 59.5% compared to 2019 and 2020, respectively (with the overall decrease in usage in 2020 due to the COVID-19 epidemic), which is higher than the annual increase after the price limit policy in India (3.7% in the first year and 13.1% in the following year [11]). The average per capita burden for patients undergoing stent implantation in Beijing fell by CNY 10,000 (USD 1,390.3).

The procurement policy promoted upgrades in the types of stents in the market. The 10 selected products were all drug-eluting alloy stents. The percentage of permanently coated and coated biodegradable alloy stents procured was increased by 23.00% (95% CI: 18.46–27.54%) and 9.61% (95% CI: 5.49–13.74%), respectively. Bare metal stents and permanently coated stainless-steel stents were largely withdrawn from the Tianjin market, and the volume of coated stainless-steel biodegradable and uncoated drug-eluting stents was decreased by 30.94% (95% CI: 17.36–44.52%). In contrast, bioresorbable stents were still procured in smaller quantities and accounted for a smaller market share. As a result, volume-based procurement facilitated a market shift from stainless-steel stents to alloy stents, while the use of imported stents increased significantly. A similar procurement policy to facilitate the market transition in stents was seen in the 2014 DES tender conducted in Maharashtra, India, where the use of DES at reduced tender prices increased from 40.7% to 71.3% [17]. India’s national price ceiling policy, which includes both DES and BMS, increased DES use, while BMS use decreased by more than 70% but still retained approximately 2.76% of the market share for stents [13].

Beyond stents, studies have shown that following the implementation of the volume-based procurement policy, the use of coronary stents [33], drug-coated balloons, coronary guidewires, and cardiac pacemakers increased significantly, while expenditures on coronary interventional therapy decreased substantially for both health insurance and out-of-pocket payments [34]. Specifically, balloon prices declined by approximately 70% and guidewire prices by 53%, and several provinces launched centralized procurement of cardiac pacemakers, achieving average price reductions ranging from 13% to 69% [35]. In the present study, however, only procurement data for coronary stents and balloons were available, and therefore the analysis was focused primarily on stents. Nonetheless, other cardiovascular devices represent an important direction for future research to comprehensively evaluate the impact of volume-based procurement policies across cardiology.

While China’s stent procurement policy effectively reduced the price of stents, various supporting policies have reduced or mitigated the impact of the policy on all parties to ensure a smooth and orderly stent market and normal medical activities [25]. First, China’s policy enhances the quality control of stents to prevent decreases in quality and to ensure the safety and effectiveness of the procured stents. Second, the prepayment system reduces the cost of corporate deposits, which, in turn, alleviates cash flow pressures in hospitals. Third, the medical insurance funds’ balance retention and incentive system can compensate physicians’ income and hospital funds. After policy implementation, a portion of the medical insurance fund balance was given to medical institutions for retention at a certain percentage. In addition, some provinces, such as Beijing and Shanghai, adjusted the prices of medical services simultaneously and appropriately increased the reimbursement prices for surgeries to impose no further burden on patients [36, 37]. Some pilot cities connected this to DRG payment reform to increase hospital balance retention funds [36]. The balance retention fund replaces the gray profit portion before volume-based purchasing, and its rational use can compensate physicians, facilitate the development of scientific research and new technologies, and promote innovation in medical technologies. The decline in gray income also promotes rationalization of clinical choices, renders treatments more precise and refined, and prevents the increase in inappropriate use of PTCA similar to that seen in India after implementing their stent price ceiling policy [13]. Fourth, the procurement volume is designed to be 80% of the demand reported by medical institutions, leaving 20% of the remaining market for other products, products leaving room for innovative product use and development.

China’s stent volume procurement policy effectively reduced the prices and increased the procured stent volume, which, in turn, improved their affordability and promoted product upgrades by exchanging volume for price. Additionally, the supporting policies ensured a stable and orderly stent market and balance retention to promote rational allocation of funds, thus providing avenues for addressing the problem of poor affordability of stents in developing countries. However, there are some potential problems with China’s stent procurement policy and further observations and research are warranted. First, although the policy sets aside 20% of the theoretically remaining market, the prices of unsuccessful products and bare metal stents are about 10 times higher than those of the selected products. Moreover, the policy simultaneously adjusts the reimbursement price of the alternative stents (the ceiling is set at CNY 798 (USD 111) per unit), encourages the continued use of selected products in the remaining market, and emphasizes the clinical rationality of the selection of alternative stents, resulting in the actual remaining market being much smaller than the theoretical proportion. This may potentially limit the application of innovative products. Chinese policy lacks reimbursement premium policies for innovative products found in Europe [14] and Japan [19]. Therefore, it is necessary to improve the supporting policies, such as regular monitoring of available stent products with reference to foreign markets and updating the procurement list promptly, to further promote product upgrades and innovation. Second, the profit of stent manufacturers is decreased. Although the policy improves stent quality, there may be problems, such as incomplete stent model specifications and decreased incentives for research and development by businesses. Third, the benefits and incentives for distributors have decreased, which may be detrimental to stent distribution and indirectly affect the expansion of PCI surgery, particularly in remote areas [26]. In this context, hospital procurement units should maintain close communication with implanting physicians to prevent the accumulation of outdated consumables and ensure timely device utilization.Fourth, implementing balance retention and actual distribution in hospitals must be further examined. Interviews and research studies have shown that the income of physicians, especially senior physicians, has significantly decreased. Income and concerns relating to surgical quality and changes in surgical habits have reduced the physicians’ motivation for PCI procedures, with some physicians resistant to PCI procedures. This may affect the development of clinical and research work and increase the use of alternative products, such as drug-eluting balloons. One study shows that the implementation of the procurement policy resulted in an approximately 30% increase in the utilization of coronary balloons in a hospital, consequently leading to a rise in the overall cost associated with PCI procedures [38]. Although the Tianjin data showed that the procurement policy has not significantly impacted the use of drug-eluting balloons, other provinces and regions in China have reported that substitution may exist to varying degrees [26].

This study has some limitations. Firstly, the study has a limited sample representation, and the data source is limited to the city of Tianjin, which does not reflect the effects of the policy on the whole of China. Secondly, only 3 years of data were included (from 2019 to 2021), and therefore, the ongoing impact of the policy must be further investigated. Moreover, data from PCI, CABG, and clinical application scenarios (emergency/elective) during the same period were not analyzed in this study. Consequently, the issues brought about by the policy, such as the impact on clinical decision-making, must also be further evaluated. In addition, although integrating drug utilization into ARIMA models has been shown to improve demand forecasting and support evidence-based procurement [39], the current study lacks sufficient data for detailed analysis of seasonal fluctuations. Future studies with more granular and longer-term data could enable precise, demand-responsive procurement strategies, enhancing stent availability and minimizing resource waste from overstocking.

Conclusion

The centralized coronary stent procurement program introduced in Tianjin significantly lowered the stents’ price and subsequently made them available to patients, potentially meeting the demand previously unmet. Moreover, this has expedited the process of generational replacement by facilitating changes in stent usage patterns. Besides, our results suggest a significant decrease in procurement costs, along with reduced medical insurance spending, highlighting the importance of strategic decision-making in healthcare procurement to optimize resource allocation while guaranteeing patient quality care. Interestingly, despite the notable changes in stent procurement and utilization, there appears to be no significant alteration in drug-eluting balloon procurement. This emphasizes the need for further investigation into the factors influencing the uptake of different medical devices and interventions within the healthcare system.

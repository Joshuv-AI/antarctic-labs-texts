Safety of an extension of use of pasteurised Akkermansia muciniphila as a novel food pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283
Authors: EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA), Dominique Turck, Torsten Bohn, Montaña Cámara, Jacqueline Castenmiller, Stefaan De Henauw, Ángeles Jos, Alexandre Maciuk, Inge Mangelsdorf, Breige McNulty, Androniki Naska, Kristina Pentieva, Alfonso Siani, Frank Thies, Margarita Aguilera‐Gómez, Thomas Frenzel, Francesca Marcon, Harry J. Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA) was asked to deliver an opinion on the safety of an extension of use of pasteurised Akkermansia muciniphila as a novel food (NF) pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. The extension pertains to the use of the NF in food supplements and Foods for Special Medical Purposes (FSMPs) for adolescents from 12 to < 18 years of age and to pregnant and lactating women. The maximum proposed daily doses of the NF correspond to about 4.8 × 108 cells/kg bodyweight per day, which was the safe dose as established for the NF by the Panel in 2021. The newly identified toxicological and human studies with A. muciniphila from various strains, including the strain to which the NF belongs, do not raise safety concerns in view of the extension of use of the NF for adolescents from 12 years of age. However, no evidence was provided for the safety of the NF in pregnant and lactating women. The Panel concludes that the NF, pasteurised A. muciniphila, is safe at doses up to 2.1 × 1010 cells/day for adolescents from 12 to < 14 years and 3.0 × 1010 cells/day for adolescents from 14 years until < 18 years. The safety of the NF in pregnant and lactating women has not been established.

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference as provided by the requestor

On 19 December 2023, the applicant “The Akkermansia Company SA” submitted a request to the European Commission in accordance with Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 1 to authorise the extensions of use of the novel food ‘Akkermansia muciniphila (pasteurised)’.

The application requests to authorise its use in several food categories and to include pregnant and lactating women and adolescents 10–17 years of age in the currently authorised uses in Foods for Special Medical Purposes as defined in Regulation (EU) 609/2013.

The applicant has requested data protection under Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 for data in support of this request.

In accordance with Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, the European Commission asks the European Food Safety Authority to provide a scientific opinion on the proposed extension of use and the specifications of the novel food ‘Akkermansia muciniphila (pasteurised)’.

In this opinion on the proposed changes in the conditions of use and the specifications of the novel food ‘Akkermansia muciniphila (pasteurised)’, the European Food Safety Authority should also document whether and to what extent the requirements of Article 26(2)(c) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 are fulfilled regarding the data for which the applicant is requesting data protection.

Additional information

In 2021, the EFSA NDA Panel adopted a Scientific Opinion on the safety of pasteurised Akkermansia (A.) muciniphila as a novel food pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021b).

In 2022, pasteurised A. muciniphila was authorised for the placing on the market of the European Union by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/168. 2

In 2024, the Food Standards Agency (FSA) and Food Standards Scotland (FSS) completed their risk assessment of pasteurised A. muciniphila in food supplements and FSMPs for the general population from 12 years of age at up to 4 × 1010 cells/day. The FSA and FSS concluded that pasteurised A. muciniphila is safe at the proposed conditions of use (Food Standards Agency & Food Standards Scotland, 2024).

In 2024, the EFSA Panel on Biological Hazards (BIOHAZ) reassessed the suitability of A. muciniphila for the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) status. As in the first assessment in 2020, the BIOHAZ Panel considered that A. muciniphila species is not suitable for the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) status due to safety concerns. The BIOHAZ Panel also noted that the conflicting data available in the published literature might be due to colonisation of the colon by different A. muciniphila strains, which might produce disparate effects (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2024).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The safety assessment of this NF is based on data supplied in the application and information submitted by the applicant following an EFSA request for additional information. 3

Administrative and scientific requirements for NF applications referred to in Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 are listed in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2469. 4

A common and structured format on the presentation of NF applications is described in the EFSA guidance on the preparation and presentation of a NF application (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021a). As indicated in this guidance, it is the duty of the applicant to provide all the available (proprietary, confidential and published) scientific data (including both data in favour and not in favour) that are pertinent to the safety of the NF.

The applicant has submitted a confidential and a non‐confidential version of a dossier following the ‘EFSA guidelines on the preparation and presentation of a NF application’ (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021a) and the ‘Administrative guidance for the preparation of applications on novel foods pursuant to Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283’ (EFSA, 2021).

In accordance with Article 38 of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 5 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 6 the non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA. 7

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation (PC‐1163) 8 on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 14/10/2024 to 04/11/2024, for which no comments were received.

The NF application originally included a request for protection of proprietary data (see Section 1.1), in accordance with Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. Following an EFSA request for additional data, the applicant changed the proposed uses for the NF and informed that owing to this change there were no longer any data for which protection of proprietary data was requested.

Methodologies

The assessment follows the methodology set out in the EFSA guidance on NF applications (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021a) and the principles described in the relevant existing guidance documents from the EFSA Scientific Committee. The legal provisions for the assessment are laid down in Article 11 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 and in Article 7 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2469.

This assessment concerns only the risks that might be associated with consumption of the NF under the proposed conditions of use, and it is not an assessment of the efficacy of the NF with regard to any claimed benefit.

This assessment is also not an assessment on whether the NF is suitable as stipulated by Regulation (EU) No 609/2013. 9

ASSESSMENT

Introduction

The NF, which is the subject of the request for an extension of use, is pasteurised A. muciniphila.

In 2021, the EFSA NDA Panel assessed the safety of pasteurised A. muciniphila as a NF (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021b). The Panel concluded that the NF was safe in food supplements and foods for special medical purposes (FSMP) (according to Regulation (EU) No 609/20139) at 3.4 × 1010 cells/day. The target population was adults excluding pregnant and lactating women (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021b).

Following the assessment by the EFSA NDA Panel, pasteurised A. muciniphilia was authorised by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/168 10 for the above uses and target population.

This opinion addresses the applicant's request to extend the target population for both uses (i.e. food supplements and FSMPs) to adolescents and to pregnant and lactating women.

Identity of the NF

The NF is pasteurised A. muciniphila (strain ATCC BAA‐835, CIP 107961). It is produced by anaerobic growth of the bacteria, followed by pasteurisation, concentration of the cells, cryopreservation and freeze‐drying (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021b).

Proposed uses and use levels and anticipated intake

Target population

Following an EFSA request for clarification, the applicant proposed as the target population adolescents from 12 years of age and adults including pregnant and lactating women.

Proposed uses and use levels

Following an EFSA request for clarification, the applicant proposed the use of the NF in food supplements and FSMPs as defined in Regulation (EU) No 609/2013 (and not in other food categories as originally proposed (see Terms of Reference in Section 1.1)) for adolescents from 12 years of age and adults including pregnant and lactating women.

The respective maximum doses proposed by the applicant are 2.1 × 1010 cells/day for adolescents from 12 to < 14 years, 3.0 × 1010 cells/day for adolescents from 14 years until < 18 years and 3.4 × 1010 cells/day for adults (including pregnant and lactating women). Toxicological information

The applicant conducted a comprehensive literature search (dated: June 2023) to identify new studies (published after the EFSA safety assessment in 2021) on the NF. The full search strategy, data bases and results were provided. No additional safety related studies (apart from those already assessed by the EFSA NDA Panel in 2021) following consumption of pasteurised A. muciniphila (strain ATCC BAA‐835) were identified.

To complement the above search, in March 2025 the applicant was requested to perform an additional literature search which ought to include all types of formulations (e.g. viable, non‐viable, heat‐killed, pasteurised, etc.) of A. muciniphila, irrespective of the strain. The search was to include all types of studies (i.e. in vitro, animal and human studies), which reported on safety related endpoints.

In reply, the applicant conducted another comprehensive literature search (dated: April 2025) and submitted publications with A. muciniphila on genotoxicity (Lv et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024), acute and subchronic toxicity (Hong et al., 2024; Hou et al., 2023; Lv et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2024, 2025; Yu et al., 2024) and human studies (Kang et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2024). The full search strategy and full text copies of the identified publications were provided. The studies were performed with various formulations (i.e. live, pasteurised and heat‐killed, respectively) of diverse A. muciniphila strains, including strain ATCC BAA‐835 (i.e. the same strain as the NF). No adverse effects were observed in these studies. The Panel considers that the newly identified studies with live, pasteurised or heat‐killed A. muciniphila (from various strains including the one to which the NF belongs) do not raise safety concerns in view of the extension of use of the NF for adolescents from 12 years of age.

The applicant informed that since marketing authorisation in the EU a considerable quantity (information provided – confidential) of food supplements with the NF were sold to consumers, with no reported adverse effects.

Reproductive and developmental toxicity

Given the fact that the applicant proposed to include pregnant and lactating women as the target population for the NF and considering the vulnerable character of this population group, the applicant was requested to provide evidence for the safety of the NF in pregnant and lactating women.

In reply, the applicant submitted publications for three reproductive/developmental toxicity studies in mice (Liu et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2024; Qi et al., 2022) that were identified in the literature search.

Two of the studies were concerned with potential effects of A. muciniphila in preeclampsia‐induced pregnant mice (Liu et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2024).

The study by Qi et al. (2022) was performed with live (108–109 CFU/day) A. muciniphila (ATCC BAA‐835 (same strain as the NF)), which was administered by gavage to 10 female mice (C57BL/6) daily during 3 weeks of gestation plus 3 weeks of lactation. A control group of 10 female mice received saline. No adverse effects were observed in dams or their pups. However, there was no information on any specific test guideline (e.g. OECD) followed nor on the adherence to principles of Good Laboratory Practice (GLP).

The Panel considers that no conclusions can be drawn from these studies to establish the safety of the NF in pregnant and lactating women.

Allergenicity

In 2021, the Panel considered that the risk of allergic reactions to the NF for the general population is expected to be low (EFSA NDA Panel, 2021b).

According to the applicant, there have been no reports of allergic reactions related to the consumption of the NF following its market authorisation in the EU in 2022 or since the Generally Recognised as Safe (GRAS) determination and subsequent marketing in the United States since 2021.

As in 2021, the Panel considers that the risk of allergic reactions to the NF for the general population is expected to be low.

DISCUSSION

The NF, which is the subject of the request for an extension of use, is pasteurised A. muciniphila.

The safety of the NF was assessed in 2021 by the EFSA NDA Panel. Following this assessment the NF was authorised for the placing on the market in the EU in food supplements and in FSMPs (as defined in Regulation (EU) No 609/2013) for adults excluding pregnant and lactating women.

This opinion addresses the applicant's request to extend the target population for both uses (i.e. food supplements and FSMPs) to adolescents from 12 years of age and to pregnant and lactating women.

The applicant proposed maximum doses of 2.1 × 1010 cells/day for adolescents from 12 to < 14 years, 3.0 × 1010 cells/day for adolescents from 14 years until < 18 years and 3.4 × 1010 cells/day for adults including pregnant and lactating women. The maximum proposed daily doses of the NF correspond, on a body weight basis, to about 4.8 × 108 cells/kg bw per day, which was the safe dose for the NF as established by the Panel in 2021.

The newly identified toxicological and human studies with A. muciniphila from various strains, including the one to which the NF belongs, do not raise safety concerns in view of the extension of use of the NF for adolescents from 12 years of age. Thus, no concerns were identified by the Panel with respect to the requested extension of use of the NF to adolescents from 12 years of age.

Considering the vulnerable character of pregnant and lactating women, the applicant was requested to provide evidence for the safety of the NF in this population group. In reply, the applicant submitted publications for three reproductive/developmental toxicity studies in mice. The Panel considers that no conclusions can be drawn from these studies to establish the safety of the NF in pregnant and lactating women.

CONCLUSIONS

The Panel concludes that the NF, pasteurised A. muciniphila, is safe at doses up to 2.1 × 1010 cells/day for adolescents from 12 to < 14 years, and 3.0 × 1010 cells/day for adolescents from 14 years until < 18 years.

Enhanced in-vivo persistence and PET imaging performance of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 as a VEGFR-3 radiotracer
Authors: Jing Wang, Xi Chen, Sixuan Cheng, Jie Li, Yue Sun, Yahan Song, Fangle Cai, Fei Li, Dawei Jiang, Ling Xi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundVascular endothelial growth factor receptor-3 (VEGFR-3) is overexpressed in tumor-induced lymphangiogenesis. TMVP1446/GS5 is a peptide analogue specifically targeting VEGFR-3, developed through substitution of the original peptide TMVP1446 with non-natural amino acids and terminal modification. This study aimed to evaluate the potential of 68Ga-labeled TMVP1446/GS5 as a probe for imaging primary tumors and metastatic lymph nodes.Results[68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited excellent labeling yield and radiochemical purity (RCP). Radio-HPLC analysis demonstrated comparable radiochemical and chemical stability to the original probe. Although stability in mouse plasma remained limited, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited modestly improved metabolic stability than the original probe. Cellular assays and surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis showed comparable or slightly higher binding affinity to VEGFR-3 relative to the original probe. At 2 h post-injection, tumor uptake reached 4.90 ± 0.36%ID/g, approximately five times of the original probe (1.03 ± 0.06%ID/g). Background

Lymphatic metastasis is one of the major pathways for malignant tumor dissemination and is a critical factor affecting tumor staging, prognosis, and therapeutic decision-making [1, 2]. At present, postoperative pathology remains the gold standard for lymph node metastasis diagnosis, which typically involves lymphadenectomy followed by histopathological examination to confirm metastatic involvement [2, 3]. However, lymph node dissection substantially increases the extent of surgery and may lead to significant complications such as neurovascular injury and lymphedema [3]. In the early stage of lymph node metastasis, the unique local microenvironment promotes extensive lymphangiogenesis, however, the number of tumor cells is relatively low [4]. As a result, conventional tumor-targeted tracers exhibit limited effectiveness in detecting lymph node metastasis [5–7], in this way, imaging tracers that target newly formed lymphatic vessels provide a promising and effective strategy to overcome the limitations [8].

The vascular endothelial growth factor C/D (VEGF-C/D) and Vascular endothelial growth factor receptor-3 (VEGFR-3) signaling pathway is the most critical regulatory axis in lymphangiogenesis [9]. VEGFR-3 is primarily expressed in embryonic lymphatic vessels and in newly formed lymphatic vessels induced by inflammation or tumors. Its expression gradually diminishes as lymphatic vessels mature, and is generally absent in normal adult tissues [10, 11]. High levels of VEGFR-3 expression have been detected in both primary tumors and pre-metastatic niches, making it an excellent target for imaging newly formed lymphatic vessels [12].

Positron emission tomography (PET) and single photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) provide advantages in detecting metabolism, function, and target expression, making them more promising than conventional imaging modalities for tumor detection. When labeled with VEGFR-3 targeted tracers, these modalities enable in vivo visualization of VEGFR-3 expression [13, 14]. In our previous work, a peptide named TMVP1 (LARGR) was identified as specifically binding to the extracellular domain of VEGFR-3. The resulting radiotracer, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA -TMVP1, enabled effective imaging of primary tumors and partial detection of metastatic lymph nodes, however, its sensitivity required further improvement [15]. TMVP1446 (CRRALGGGTTSR) was subsequently selected for its enhanced binding affinity to VEGFR-3, and the derived radiotracer [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 demonstrated improved imaging of metastatic lymph nodes [16]. Peptides have attracted widespread international attention due to their safety and potent biological functions, however, their susceptibility to enzymatic and chemical degradation limits broader application [17–19].Due to the suboptimal stability of TMVP1446, we modified the peptide structure by introducing N-terminal acetylation, C-terminal amidation and site-specific amino acid substitutions, which include replacing alanine with α-aminoisobutyric acid (Aib) and modifing C-terminal arginine with 6-aminohexanoic acid (Ahx), resulting in a new peptide, TMVP1446/GS5 (Ac-CRRAibLGGGTTSR(Ahx)-NH2). Using this modified peptide, a near-infrared fluorescent probe, TMVP1446/GS5-Cy7, was synthesized and demonstrated effective targeting and imaging of both primary tumors and metastatic lymph nodes in our previous study [20]. In the present study, the peptides TMVP1446/GS5 were further conjugated with the bifunctional chelator DOTA and PEG4 via an additionally introduced lysine residue, followed by radiolabeling with 68Ga to construct the radiotracer [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5, and its potential for imaging primary tumors and metastatic lymph nodes was then primarily evaluated.

Results

Chemistry and radiolabeling

The synthesis pathway of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 is depicted in Fig. 1B. Precursors DOTA-TMVP1446 and DOTA- TMVP1446/GS5 were synthesized using the Fmoc solid-phase synthesis (SPPS) technique. Liquid chromatography coupled to mass spectrometry (LC-MS) analysis confirmed the molecular mass of the conjugate to be 2164.3067 Da, which is consistent with the expected molecular weight (MW = 2164.52). The purity of DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 is > 95%, as confirmed by reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) (Figure S1). (The characteristics of DOTA-TMVP1446 have been described in our previous study) [16].

(A)The chemical structure of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446. (B)Synthesis and radiolabeling scheme of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5. (C)Retention Time, logD and Plasma Protein Binding of the probes

[68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited a radioactive retention time of 8.27 min, with a labeling yield exceeding 85% and a radiochemical purity (RCP) above 95% after purification (Figure S2).

The logD values of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 were measured as − 2.12 ± 0.03 and − 2.50 ± 0.10, respectively, indicating that both probes are hydrophilic. [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 is slightly more hydrophobic than the original probe, which is consistent with the differences in retention times observed by HPLC (Fig. 1C). The plasma protein binding rate of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 in mouse plasma was 61%±0.01%, whereas that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 was 41%±0.07%, indicating that [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibits a higher plasma protein binding than the original probe (Fig. 1C).

Stability in vitro

In vitro stability of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 were evaluated by radio-HPLC (Fig. 2A, B,C, D). After incubation in saline for 2 h, both tracers maintained a RCP of over 90%, indicating the probes exhibited comparable in vitro radiochemical and chemical stability.

(E) Expression of VEGFR-3 protein in Hela and Hela-flt4 cell was verified by Western Blot analysis. After incubation in mouse plasma (C57 mice) for 1 h, both probes exhibited radioactive peaks at approximately 4 min, corresponding to the elution time of free 68Ga, indicating that both probes underwent gallium dissociation in the plasma environment. The probe [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 displayed a relatively well-defined product peak. In addition to the product peak, a radioactive peak was observed at 11.8 min, indicating the presence of a more hydrophobic species. The probe [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 showed radioactive peaks appearing both before and after the product peak, indicating the presence of impurities. After 1 h of incubation in plasma, the RCP of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 was approximately 25%, whereas that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 was around 15%. These findings suggested that [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited a modest improvement in metabolic stability compared with the parent tracer; however, both probes still showed limited overall plasma stability after 1 h.

Cell uptake and blocking assay

Western blot analysis confirmed high VEGFR-3 expression in Hela-flt4 cells, with minimal expression observed in Hela cells (Fig. 2E). To investigate the specific affinity of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 for VEGFR-3, cell uptake assays were conducted using Hela and Hela-flt4 cell lines. As incubation time increased, the uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 in Hela-flt4 cells showed a time-dependent increase. At all measured time points, the VEGFR-3-overexpressing Hela-flt4 cells exhibited significantly higher probe uptake compared to the VEGFR-3-negative Hela cells. Specifically, at 1 h and 2 h, the uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 in Hela-flt4 cells was 2.66 ± 0.24%AD and 2.49 ± 0.16%AD, respectively, significantly higher than 1.60 ± 0.11%AD and 1.67 ± 0.76%AD observed in Hela cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2F). After co-incubation with excess DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5, the uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 in Hela-flt4 cells was decreased markedly from 2.87 ± 0.12%AD to 1.65 ± 0.03%AD at 1 h, indicating effective receptor blocking. In contrast, no notable blocking effect was observed in Hela cells. After blocking, the nonspecific uptake in Hela-flt4 cells was comparable to that in Hela cells (Fig. 2G). Together, the results demonstrate the specific affinity of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 for VEGFR-3.

Using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) technology for receptor-ligand binding analysis, the dissociation constant constant (KD) between the radiolabeled compound [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 and human VEGFR-3 (hVEGFR-3) was determined to be 7.05E-07 M (Fig. 2I). The affinity is comparable to that of TMVP1446 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 for hVEGFR-3 or murine VEGFR-3 (mVEGFR-3), as measured in our previous experiments [16].

To compare the specific affinity of the two probes for VEGFR-3, cell uptake and blocking assay were performed in Hela-flt4 cells at 1 h. The uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 (2.37 ± 0.19%AD) was slightly higher than that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 (1.90 ± 0.08%AD) (P < 0.05), and both were inhibited by co-incubation with an excess of the unlabeled peptide (Fig. 2H). It suggests that [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibits comparable or slightly higher specific affinity compared to the original probe.

Micro-PET/CT imaging in B16F10 subcutaneous xenograft mice

Tumor cell–induced lymphangiogenesis also occurs within the tumor. While promoting the formation of newly formed lymphatic vessels with high VEGFR-3 expression, tumor cells also upregulate VEGFR-3 expression in intra-tumoral blood vessels [21]. In addition, VEGFR-3 expression has been observed in certain tumor cells [10]. In this study, we used immunohistochemistry (IHC) to validate VEGFR-3 expression in primary B16F10 tumors (Figure S3).

To evaluate the in vivo imaging performance and pharmacokinetics of the modified probe [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 in primary tumors, both[68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 were intravenously injected into B16F10 subcutaneous xenografts mice, followed by micro-PET/CT imaging at multiple time points. Both probes enabled clear visualization of the tumors, however, tumor uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 was consistently higher than that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446. Moreover, while the uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 decreased significantly over time, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited more sustained tumor retention (Fig. 3A). Specifically, at 15 min post-injection, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 showed a tumor uptake of 7.26 ± 0.21%ID/g, slightly exceeding the 5.57 ± 1.04%ID/g observed with[68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446. The tumor uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 remained relatively stable over time, measuring 6.10 ± 0.20%ID/g at 30 min, 5.20 ± 0.44%ID/g at 1 h, and 4.90 ± 0.36%ID/g at 2 h post-injection. In contrast, after a slight increase at 30 min, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 showed a marked decline in tumor uptake, decreasing from 6.00 ± 0.44%ID/g at 30 min to 3.03 ± 0.35%ID/g at 1 h and 1.03 ± 0.06%ID/g at 2 h post-injection. Notably, at the 2 h post-injection, the tumor uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 was approximately five times higher that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 (Fig. 3B). These findings indicate that modifications in [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 enhance both the intensity and duration of tumor uptake compared with the original probe.

Beyond tumor uptake, the evaluation of a radiotracer also critically depends on its distribution in non-target tissues. [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 reached its peak concentration in the blood pool within 15 min, whereas [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 showed a slight increase at 30 min compared with 15 min. Thereafter both tracers followed by a gradual decline over time. The radioactivity accumulation of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 in the blood peaked at 9.53 ± 1.32%ID/g, compared to 7.30 ± 1.06%ID/g for [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446, and declined to 3.80 ± 0.10%ID/g and 1.13 ± 0.06%ID/g, respectively, at the 2 h time point. High uptake in the kidneys, bladder, and liver indicates that the tracers undergo both renal and hepatobiliary excretion. Interestingly, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 showed a renal uptake of 14.37 ± 1.55%ID/g at 15 min, which showed a slight decrease at 30 min and remained consistently stable thereafter. In contrast, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 exhibited high renal uptake of 23.1 ± 4.11%ID/g at 15 min and 31.77 ± 5.36%ID/g at 30 min, followed by a sharp decline over time. Consistent with blood pool radioactivity, liver uptake of both tracers peaked rapidly within 30 min, reaching 8.17 ± 0.85%ID/g for [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 at 15 min and 6.27 ± 1.01%ID/g for [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 at 30 min, and then declined gradually (Fig. 3C). Collectively, the radio-activity distribution in non-target tissues suggests a distinct trend: [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 consistently demonstrated higher uptake in the blood, kidneys, and liver compared with [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446, suggesting prolonged blood circulation and increased in vivo retention of the modified probe. Both probes demonstrated persistently low radioactive uptake in muscle without statistically significant differences, providing an optimal low-background condition for imaging acquisition.

To further compare the imaging performance of the two probes, we calculated their target-to-non-target (T/NT) uptake ratios. The two tracers demonstrated similar tumor-to-muscle (T/M) ratios at 15 min, which were 6.24 ± 0.50 and 5.41 ± 1.11, respectively. However, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited continuous accumulation, reaching a T/M ratios of 9.87 ± 2.56 at 2 h, whereas [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 gradually declined to 1.97 ± 0.27 by 2 h. Statistical analysis confirmed significantly higher T/M ratios for [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 compared to [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 at both 1 h and 2 h time points (p < 0.05) (Fig. 3D). [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 showed a gradual increase in tumor-to-blood (T/B) ratios over time, whereas the T/B ratio of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 remained relatively stable throughout the observation period. Specifically, at 2 h, the T/B ratio of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 reached 1.29 ± 0.09, which was significantly higher than that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 (0.91 ± 0.08, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3E). Similar to the T/M ratio, the tumor-to-liver ratio (T/L) of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 gradually increased over time, whereas the T/L ratio of the original probe gradually decreased. At 2 h, the T/L ratio of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 was 1.03 ± 0.11, which was significantly higher than that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 (0.44 ± 0.07, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3F). These results indicate that although the increased tumor uptake was accompanied by elevated background radioactivity, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited better image contrast at later time points.

In summary, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 showed higher and more sustained tumor uptake. Although the prolonged in vivo retention resulted in higher radioactivity in the background and metabolic organs, it demonstrated T/NT ratios comparable to those of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 at early time points and higher ratios at later time points. Therefore, we think that [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 holds superior potential for tumor imaging.

To validate the in vivo specificity of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5, a blocking study was performed by co-injecting an approximately 100-fold excess of unlabeled DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5. Compared with the [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 group, the blocking group showed a significant reduction in tumor uptake, as well as T/B ratio and T/M ratio, with all differences being statistically significant (Fig. 4). Specifically, at 30 min time point, tumor uptake in [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 group was 7.43 ± 0.37%ID/g, whereas the blocking group showed a significantly lower uptake of 4.90 ± 0.45%ID/g, corresponding to a 34% reduction. Likewise, the T/M ratio in the blocking group was reduced by 41% compared to [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 group at the same time point (Fig. 4B-D).

Micro-PET/CT imaging in B16F10 footpad lymph node metastasis model mice

To evaluate the lymph node metastasis imaging capability of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5, a footpad lymph node metastasis model was established by inoculating B16F10 cells into the right footpad of mice. Once the model was successfully established, enlarged lymph nodes became palpable in the axillary region, and micro-PET/CT imaging was subsequently performed (Fig. 5A). As shown in Fig. 5D, the tumor metastatic lymph nodes(T-LN) were clearly visualized, whereas no notable radioactive accumulation was observed in the contralateral non-metastatic lymph nodes(C-LN). For [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5, at 30 min post-injection, the relatively high background activity hindered clear delineation of metastatic lymph nodes from surrounding tissues, however, as time progressed, the contrast between the metastatic lymph nodes and adjacent tissues gradually increased. By 2 h post-injection, the metastatic lymph nodes became distinctly visible, with an uptake of up to 1.83 ± 0.24%ID/g、 a T-LN/M ratio of 10.61 ± 0.44 and a T-LN/C-LN ratio of 2.17 ± 0.50. (Fig. 5E, F). Compared with the unmodified peptide, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited a marked imaging advantage in detecting metastatic lymph nodes in later time points (Fig. 5D).

Both metastatic and contralateral non-metastatic lymph nodes were excised for histopathological examination after imaging. The metastatic lymph nodes exhibited extensive tumor cell infiltration and strong VEGFR-3 expression, whereas the contralateral lymph nodes showed neither evidence of tumor cells nor notable VEGFR-3 expression, which were consistent with radioactive signals distribution observed in the micro-PET/CT imaging results (Fig. 5B, C).

Biodistribution in B16F10 subcutaneous xenograft mice

Biodistribution studies of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 were performed in B16F10 subcutaneous tumor-bearing mouse models. The %ID/g values of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 in tumors and major organs are presented (Fig. 6A and Table S1). Tumor uptake reached its peak within the first 15 min post-injection and subsequently declined, higher than that in critical organs such as the brain, stomach, intestines, and bone throughout the study period. Blood activity was initially high but decreased rapidly over time. Highly vascularized organs, including the heart and lungs, exhibited similar clearance patterns. Consequently, the T/B, T/H, and T/M ratios increased progressively over time (Fig. 6B). Specifically, at 15 min, tumor uptake reached approximately 2.23 ± 0.60%ID/g, while the uptake in blood, heart, and muscle was 7.28 ± 1.65%ID/g, 2.12 ± 0.42%ID/g, 1.04 ± 0.26%ID/g, respectively. The corresponding T/B, T/H, T/M ratios were 0.31 ± 0.80,1.05 ± 0.22, 2.14 ± 0.21. By 2 h post-injection, tumor uptake still remained at 1.11 ± 0.01%ID/g, whereas the uptake in blood, heart, and muscle decreased markedly to 0.78 ± 0.05%ID/g, 0.26 ± 0.03%ID/g, 0.22 ± 0.13%ID/g, respectively. Consequently, the T/B, T/H, and T/M ratios increased to 1.44 ± 0.09, 4.32 ± 0.63, 7.09 ± 3.17, respectively. The relatively high uptake observed in the liver and kidneys indicates that [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 is primarily metabolized through both hepatobiliary and renal pathways. At 2 h, renal uptake remained relatively high, which may indicate retention of the probe in the kidneys.

Biodistribution studies at 2 h were performed to compare the pharmacokinetics and tumor uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 and [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446. As shown in Fig. 6C and Table S2, at this time point, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 showed higher uptake in all examined organs than [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446, further indicating its prolonged in vivo retention. Notably, tumor uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 reached 3.26 ± 0.55%ID/g at 2 h, which was approximately fivefold higher than that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446 (0.71 ± 0.05%ID/g). This finding demonstrates the superior tumor uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 at later time points and is consistent with the PET/CT imaging results.

Discussion

Lymphatic metastasis is a critical factor influencing the prognosis and therapeutic decision-making in malignant tumors. However, the current assessment of lymph node metastasis still relies primarily on invasive procedures such as pathological biopsy, while the development of noninvasive detection methods remains limited. TMVP1446/GS5, a more stable derivative peptide with extended in vivo retention, synthesized based on TMVP1446, specifically targets VEGFR-3. In our previous work, the Cy7-labeled TMVP1446/GS5 demonstrated superior imaging performance compared with the parent peptide TMVP1446. However, its potential as a radiolabeled imaging agent has not yet been explored.

⁶⁸Ga is a widely used short-lived diagnostic radionuclide that provides excellent performance in PET imaging. DOTA, owing to its high stability and strong chelation capability with ⁶⁸Ga, is the most commonly employed bifunctional chelator [22, 23]. We synthesized the radiotracer [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 and evaluated its diagnostic potential for imaging primary tumors and metastatic lymph nodes. Since the C-terminus of the peptide is amidated, a lysine residue was added at the terminus to enable conjugation with the chelator. A PEG₄ moiety was incorporated into the probe, as PEG has been reported to spatially separate the functional domains of peptides, enhance hydrophilicity, reduce hepatic background uptake, improve tumor-targeting specificity, and moderately prolong retention time [19, 24, 25].

Peptide modification and alteration is a task full of complexity and uncertainty. Modification of the N-terminus and C-terminus to protect the peptide against endogenous exopeptidases is a well-established strategy that has been used to enhance the stability of peptides in vitro and in vivo [17–19]. In this study, the peptide termini were blocked by N-terminal acetylation and C-terminal amidation. Moreover, the use of unnatural amino acids in place of natural amino acids has been reported to enhance peptide stability, as unnatural amino acids are generally not recognized by most proteases and exhibit a better resistance to protease hydrolysis [17–19]. However, peptide modifications inevitably alter molecular charge and conformation, which may influence the activity and binding affinity of the peptide [26]. Alanine, with the smallest side chain among amino acids, is uncharged, does not participate in strong interactions such as hydrogen bonding, and is generally not part of the functional core. Unlike glycine, which lacks a side chain and has high conformational flexibility that can determine peptide structure, alanine provides a minimal but structurally non-disruptive substitution site [27]. Therefore, alanine was selected for non-natural amino acid substitution, aiming to enhance metabolic stability while preserving binding affinity. Additionally, the incorporation of Ahx served to spatially separate the DOTA chelator from the active sequence, minimizing any potential interference of the chelator with receptor binding [28]. The modified peptide TMVP1446/GS5 was thus generated. Since Aib contains two methyl groups and Ahx possesses a linear aliphatic structure, together with the terminal blocking modifications that reduce the overall polarity of the peptide, their introduction was expected to increase the overall hydrophobicity. This change in physicochemical properties was confirmed by the higher logD value and the prolonged retention time of the probe [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5. Moreover, the increased hydrophobicity, together with the conformational restriction imposed by Aib, may enhance nonspecific interactions with plasma proteins, which is consistent with our experimental observations [29].

HPLC analysis demonstrated that both probes exhibited excellent radiochemical and chemical stability in PBS. However, when incubated in mouse plasma, gallium dissociation was observed for both probes, together with the appearance of a more hydrophobic species with a longer retention time. As both probes contain a cysteine residue with a free thiol group, they possess the potential to form disulfide-linked dimers. Such disulfide-bonded dimers often show increased hydrophobicity, and the complex oxidative environment and catalytic systems present in plasma may facilitate their formation [30]. Therefore, we speculate that this additional peak may correspond to a disulfide-linked dimer. In addition, a more polar impurity peak near the product peak was observed for [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446, which we speculate to be a partial hydrolysis product of the peptide. Although further metabolite analysis is required to definitively identify the composition of these impurity peaks, as [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited a relatively higher RCP, it is preliminarily considered to possess modestly improved metabolic stability.

We performed in vivo micro-PET/CT imaging to compare the performance of the two probes in B16F10 subcutaneous xenograft models. The radioactivity of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 in blood, kidney and liver was higher than that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446, indicating that [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibits a longer in vivo retention time, which suggests that the modification of peptide not only affects stability and binding affinity, but also alters its pharmacokinetics. This phenomenon may be explained by several factors. First, the higher plasma protein binding rate may reduce the fraction of free probe in circulation, thereby slowing renal and hepatic clearance [31, 32]. Second, the increased liver uptake may suggest partially enhanced involvement of the hepatobiliary clearance, which may be related to the slightly higher lipophilicity and prolonged blood circulation [33]. As a result, part of the probe clearance may shift from the rapidly excreted renal pathway to the relatively slower hepatobiliary elimination route, thereby contributing to prolonged in vivo retention. Last, although the initial renal uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 was slightly lower than that of the original probe, it did not exhibit the same rapid decline over time, suggesting that part of the tracer may undergo non-excretory retention or tubular reabsorption, thereby reducing renal excretion and further promoting this phenomenon [34, 35]. It is believed that renal retention of radiopharmaceuticals is influenced by total charge and charge distribution [34–38]. In addition, the three-dimensional structure of radiotracers is known to affect tubular reabsorption in the kidney [34, 39, 40]. Therefore, the structural modifications introduced in [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 may contribute to the altered renal handling, although this explanation remains speculative and requires further structural characterization. Despite this, as the renal radioactivity remained below 15% ID/g in PET imaging and did not exceed 25% ID/g in biodistribution analysis within 2 h post-injection, the renal uptake of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 was not considered excessively high compared to other peptide-based radiotracers [41–43]. Given the short physical half-life of ⁶⁸Ga (68min), the level of renal accumulation is considered acceptable.

[68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 showed stronger and more durable tumor uptake than the original probe. We propose that the enhanced tumor uptake may be attributed to several factors. First, altered pharmacokinetics, particularly prolonged circulation time, may lead to sustained receptor interactions [31]. Second, the modestly improved metabolic stability of the probe may reduce degradation in vivo. Although differences in binding affinity appear minimal based on SPR and cellular uptake data, their potential contribution cannot be completely excluded. In the cellular uptake assays, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 showed only a slight increase in uptake in VEGFR-3–overexpressing cell lines, and SPR analysis indicated no significant improvement in binding affinity compared with the original probe. Moreover, the most notable feature of tumor uptake for [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 was the prolonged retention of radioactivity at later time points, whereas no significant difference in tumor uptake between the two probes was observed at early time points. Furthermore, although [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 appeared to exhibit somewhat improved stability in plasma, its RCP remained only around 25% after 1 h of incubation. In contrast, a pronounced alteration in pharmacokinetics was observed in the mouse model. Therefore, we speculate that alterations in pharmacokinetics may play a dominant role in the sustained high tumor uptake observed at later time points. It is also noteworthy that a potential dimerization was observed in the plasma HPLC analysis, and the contribution of metabolites to tumor uptake remains unclear.

On one hand, the increased tumor uptake can enhance detection sensitivity, potentially allowing earlier visualization of tumors or small tumor lesions. On the other hand, although the uptake in nonspecific tissues such as muscle remained comparable to that of the original probe, the elevated blood pool radioactivity may lead to increased nonspecific background signals, indicating a pharmacokinetic trade-off rather than uniformly improved imaging performance. Such elevated background signals, together with the high uptake in metabolically active organs, could obscure signals from low-expression tumors, thereby reducing overall specificity. Therefore, we further compared the target-to-nontarget ratios of the two probes. We speculate that, over time, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 is retained longer in the tumor while the background signal gradually decreases. At the 2 h time point, the contrast of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 was significantly higher than that of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446.This delayed behavior at later time points may lead to improved imaging performance, achieving a favorable balance between pharmacokinetics and tumor uptake. Nevertheless, these results should be interpreted cautiously. The increased background radioactivity in the blood, hepatobiliary system, and kidneys may impair the detection of lesions in adjacent regions, including liver metastases, abdominal or pelvic tumors located near major vessels, and metastatic lymph nodes.

This study has several limitations. First, the conclusions from PET/CT imaging and biodistribution studies were based on a small sample size (n = 3), which may have limited the statistical power to some extent. Second, noticeable differences were observed between the biodistribution data obtained under the same experimental conditions. This variability may be attributed to differences between mouse batches as well as the relatively small sample size. Although the absolute values varied, the distribution patterns among organs remained generally proportional. Third, no analysis of the metabolite composition was performed, and their potential impact on imaging remains unclear. Further studies are needed to address these issues. Finally, because only the final modified tracer and the parent probe were compared in this study, the individual contribution of each structural modification to the observed in vivo changes cannot be definitively distinguished.

Conclusions

[68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 is a promising PET tracer for imaging both primary tumors and metastatic lymph nodes. Compared with the original probe, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446, [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 exhibited higher and more sustained tumor uptake, prolonged in vivo retention, and improved contrast at later time points, enabling better visualization of both primary tumors and metastatic lymph nodes. Despite the need for further investigation, it holds significant potential for the noninvasive detection of lymph node metastasis. The pharmacokinetic effects resulting from peptide modification also provide valuable insights for peptide synthesis research.

Methods

Chemical synthesis and radiolabeling

Both DOTA-TMVP1446 and DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 were synthesized using solid-phase Fmoc chemistry by WuXi AppTec (Wuhan, China). LC-MS and HPLC were used to confirm the molecular weight and assess the purity of the compounds.

The ⁶⁸Ge/⁶⁸Ga generator (ITM Medical Isotopes GmbH, Germany) was eluted with 5 mL of 0.1 M hydrochloric acid (HCl) to obtain ⁶⁸GaCl₃. The eluate (3–4 mCi,1 ml) was mixed with sodium acetate (NaOAc) buffer (0.5 M, 275 µl) and the precursor (DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 or DOTA-TMVP1446, 1 mg/ml,10 µl), followed by incubation at 95 °C for 15 min, with the pH adjusted to 4.0-4.5. The reaction mixture was purified by passing it through a Sep-Pak C18 Light cartridge (Waters, USA), followed by elution with 1mL of 95% ethanol. The RCP was determined by radio instant thin-layer chromatography (radio-iTLC) and radio-HPLC.

Octanol–water partition coefficient

The radiolabeled probe (10 µCi, 5 µL) were added to 495 µL of PBS buffer. Subsequently, 500 µL of n-octanol was added, and the mixture was vortexed at room temperature for 5 min to ensure thorough mixing, followed by centrifugation at 8,000 rpm for 4 min to achieve complete phase separation. A 100 µL sample was collected from each organic and aqueous phase, and the radioactivity was measured using a γ-counter. The partition coefficient (logD) was calculated as log₁₀(CPM_organic / CPM_aqueous). The experiment was performed five times.

Plasma protein binding measurement

Approximately 30–40 µCi of the probe (10 µL) was added to 100 µL of fresh mouse plasma and incubated at 37 ℃ for 1 h. An equal volume of anhydrous acetonitrile was then added to thoroughly precipitate plasma proteins, followed by centrifugation at 13,000×g for 10 min. The supernatant and pellet were collected separately, and their radioactivity was measured using a γ-counter. The experiment was performed in triplicate.

Stability assessment in Vitro

The radiolabeled probe (approximately 250 µCi) was incubated in saline at 37 °C for 1 and 2 h, and in 10% mouse plasma for 1 h. Subsequently, mouse plasma proteins were precipitated by adding an equal volume of anhydrous acetonitrile, followed by centrifugation at 13,000 × g for 10 min, and the supernatant was collected. The RCP was assessed using radio-HPLC.

Cell culture and animal models

Human cervical adenocarcinoma cell line HeLa and Murine melanoma cell line B16F10 were purchased from ATCC. VEGFR-3-overexpressing HeLa-Flt4 cells were kindly provided by a member of our research team. All cell lines were cultured in DMEM (Servicebio, Wuhan, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Tianhang Biotechnology Co., Ltd, Hangzhou, China) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (G4003, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

The female C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks) were provided from GemPharmatech Co., Ltd, (Jiangsu, China). Subcutaneous tumor-bearing mouse models were established by injecting 100 µL cell suspension containing approximately 2 × 10⁵ B16F10 cells into the right axillary region of C57BL/6J mice. When the volume of the tumor reached about 500 mm³ within 7–10 days, micro-PET/CT imaging and biodistribution studies were performed. To establish lymph node metastasis models, 25 µL cell suspension containing approximately 2 × 10⁵ B16F10 cells was injected into the right footpads of C57BL/6 mice. Palpable enlargement of the right axillary lymph nodes was typically observed 3–4 weeks post-inoculation, at which time micro-PET/CT imaging was conducted. All animals were maintained in a SPF facility.

Cellular uptake and blocking assay

Hela and Hela-flt4 cells were cultured in 24-well plates at a density of 5 × 10⁴ cells per well for 12 h to adherence. After incubating with 1 µCi of the radiotracer for 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, and 2 h, respectively, the supernatant was collected, and the cells were washed twice with PBS. Subsequently, 1 M NaOH was added to lyse the cells. The radioactivity in both the supernatant and cell lysates was measured using a γ-counter, and the cellular uptake rate was calculated accordingly. For the blocking assay, cells were preincubated with 5 µg of the unlabeled precursor for 1 h prior to the addition of the radiotracer. All experiments were repeated three times.

SPR assay

The Biacore T200 instrument was used to determine the KD value of the peptide for VEGFR-3.

Micro-PET/CT imaging

Micro-PET/CT imaging of tumor xenograft mice was performed using the Inveon PET/CT system (Siemens, Germany) and the Trans-PET Discoverist 180 system (RAYCAN Technology Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China). Approximately 100 µCi of ⁶⁸Ga-labeled probe was administered via tail vein into each mouse under isoflurane anesthesia. Static PET/CT imaging was performed at 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, and 2 h post-injection. After attenuation correction and image reconstruction, regions of interest (ROIs) including tumor, muscle, liver, and kidneys were quantified using Inveon Research Workplace software (Siemens, Germany). For the blocking study, 100 µg of DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 was co-injected with 100 µCi of [68Ga]Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5.

In vivo biodistribution

Mice bearing B16F10 subcutaneous xenografts were intravenously injected with 100µCi of ⁶⁸Ga-DOTA-TMVP1446/GS5 via the tail vein. At 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, and 2 h post-injection, mice were euthanized, and tissues including blood, brain, heart, lungs, liver, spleen, kidneys, stomach, large intestine, small intestine, muscle, bone, pancreas, and tumor were collected and weighed. Radioactivity in each tissue was measured using a γ-counter, and the results were expressed as percentage of injected dose per gram of tissue (%ID/g). (n = 3)

Western blot

Cells were lysed using radio immunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentrations were determined using the Coomassie Brilliant Blue assay, followed by heat denaturation in a metal bath. Protein samples (30 µg) were separated by SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and subsequently transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% BSA at room temperature for 1 h to prevent nonspecific binding, followed by incubation with anti-VEGFR-3 antibody (cat: ab27278, Abcam) at 4 °C overnight on a shaker. After three washes with TBST, it was incubated with anti-rabbit secondary antibody (ANT020, Antejie Biotechnology, Wuhan, China) at room temperature for 1 h. Protein bands were visualized with a multimode chemiluminescence system, after three washes with TBST.

IHC staining

Excised lymph node and subcutaneous tumor specimens were fixed in formalin and embedded in paraffin. After deparaffinization, antigen retrieval, and quenching of endogenous peroxidase activity with 3% hydrogen peroxide, the sections were blocked with 5% BSA and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with anti-mouse VEGFR-3 antibody (cat: 552857; BD Pharmingen, 1:15). On the following day, secondary antibody incubation was performed using a two-step detection kit for rat (PV-9004, ZSGB-BIO). Color development was performed using a DAB kit (G1212, Servicebio, Wuhan, China), followed by counterstaining with hematoxylin. PBS was used to replace the primary antibody as a blank control.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 9.3.0, CA, USA). All quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Means were compared using two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or unpaired t-test. P < 0.05 is considered statistical significance.

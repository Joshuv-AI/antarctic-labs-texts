Unraveling the temporal dynamics of radiation-induced lung injury: a multi-omics integration of transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolic reprogramming
Authors: Dabin Wu, Fei Tang, Yixin Zhang, Xiaoyue Zhang, Yuan Peng, Yu Hou, Zhenzhou Yang, Zaicheng Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRadiation-induced lung injury (RILI) arises from the unavoidable exposure of normal lung tissue during radiotherapy for thoracic malignancies. It remains a dose-limiting complication in thoracic radiotherapy, critically impacting treatment efficacy and long-term patient survival. Despite its clinical significance, the dynamic molecular reprogramming underlying RILI progression remains poorly characterized. Utilizing a C57BL/6J thoracic irradiation mouse model validated by histopathological analysis, we implemented an integrative tri-omics approach (RNA-seq, LC-MS/MS proteomics, and UHPLC-QTOF metabolomics) to systematically map the temporal evolution of RILI at critical phases: acute inflammation, transitional, and fibrotic. The three periods correspond to 4 weeks, 8 weeks, and 16 weeks after the mice received 16 Gy of electron beam irradiation to the thoracic region. Combined analysis using complete-linkage hierarchical clustering revealed co-expression modules, and Ces2e was identified as a pivotal node exhibiting sustained upregulation at both transcriptional and translational levels during early pathogenesis. Functional enrichment revealed time-dependent activation of xenobiotic metabolism and extracellular matrix (ECM)-receptor interaction pathways, with Ces2e overexpression correlating with macrophage polarization and lipid peroxidation accumulation. This temporal multi-omics atlas not only deciphers stage-specific molecular signatures of RILI but also nominates Ces2e as a candidate early-stage molecular marker.Keywords: Ces2e, Multi-omics analysis, Molecular reprogramming, Radiation-induced lung injury

Introduction

The therapeutic efficacy of thoracic radiotherapy continues to be constrained by radiation-induced lung injury (RILI), a dose-limiting complication affecting 15–30% of patients with consequential declines in quality-adjusted survival [1–3]. While technological advances in precision radiotherapy have mitigated acute toxicities, the biphasic pathogenesis of RILI—manifesting as early radiation pneumonitis (RP) and late radiation pulmonary fibrosis (RPF)—remains a persistent clinical challenge, often necessitating treatment discontinuation [4].

Emerging evidence delineates distinct molecular cascades governing these pathological phases: RP is characterized by TNF-α/IL-6-driven inflammatory alveolitis with prominent neutrophil infiltration and endothelial permeability dysfunction [5], whereas RPF progresses through TGF-β1-mediated fibroblast activation, excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, and ultimately irreversible honeycombing [6]. Despite this conceptual framework, critical knowledge gaps persist regarding the dynamic interplay of molecular networks during RILI progression. Current biomarker discovery efforts remain fragmented, predominantly relying on single-omics approaches that fail to capture the multi-layered regulatory axis connecting genomic drivers, proteomic effectors, and metabolic adaptations.

Recent breakthroughs in single-cell sequencing have illuminated cellular heterogeneity in murine RILI models, revealing senescence-like fibroblast accumulation as a shared hallmark of lung injury [7, 8]. Complementary proteomic studies identified MnTE-2-PyP(5+)-sensitive oxidative stress pathways [9], while metabolomic signatures demonstrated diagnostic potential in preclinical models [10]. However, these isolated observations lack temporal resolution to decipher the transitional mechanisms bridging acute inflammation to fibrotic remodeling [11]. Crucially, no study has simultaneously integrated multi-omics datasets to identify conserved molecular nodes across disease phases—a prerequisite for developing stage-specific therapeutic strategies. Here, we present longitudinal tri-omics dissection of murine RILI pathogenesis, combining transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic profiling at critical transition points (4 weeks, 8 weeks, 16 weeks post-irradiation). Integrated transcriptomic and proteomic analysis using complete-linkage hierarchical clustering revealed co-expression modules. This multilayer approach not only maps the temporal rewiring of xenobiotic metabolism and ECM interaction networks but also establishes a framework for identifying molecular marker with pan-phase relevance.

Materials and methods

Animals

All experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Use Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University (Approval No: IACUC-SAHCQMU-2023-0040). The 6-week-old C57BL/6J mice used in this study were purchased from the Experimental Animal Center of Chongqing Medical University. Adequate food and water were given (temperature 20℃-23℃, humidity 55%-65%, light/dark 12 h cycle). Pentobarbital-anesthetized mice were used for animal experiments in this study.

Experimental procedures

C57BL/6J mice (n = 36, 20 ± 2 g) were stratified by body weight and randomly allocated into sham-irradiated control (n = 18) and irradiated (n = 18) groups using a computer-generated randomization matrix. Prior to irradiation, mice received intraperitoneal anesthesia with 1% sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA, P3761) confirmed by loss of pedal reflex. Animals were immobilized in supine position using medical tape on a polymethyl methacrylate platform, with 5-mm lead shielding protecting cephalic, abdominal and pelvic regions. Thoracic irradiation was performed using a Varian TrueBeam linear accelerator with a total dose of 16 Gy delivered via electron beams at a dose rate of 600 cGy/min. Subgroups (n = 6/group) were humanely euthanized via cervical dislocation under anesthesia at three pathologically defined phases: acute inflammatory phase: 4 weeks post-irradiation; transitional phase: 8 weeks post-irradiation; fibrotic phase: 16 weeks post-irradiation. Lung tissues were immediately divided into two aliquots: one snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen for omics analyses, the other fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA; 24 h, 4 °C) for histopathology.

Lung histology

Hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining and Masson staining were used to assess lung histopathology after radiation in mice. Briefly, 4w, 8w, and 16w control and radiation lung tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h, then dehydrated, transparent and embedded in paraffin. Subsequently, 5 μm-thick paraffin sections were sliced and mounted on slides and subjected to dewaxing, transparency, hydration, and HE staining experiments according to the instructions of the reagents (G1120, Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., China). Similarly, Masson-trichrom stain was performed using the kit (G1346, Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., China) with the following brief procedure: after dewaxing and hydration of the tissue sections, the sections were stained sequentially with hematoxylin-iron working solution, lisinopropanic acid solution, phosphomolybdenum-phosphotungstic acid solution and aniline blue staining. HE-stained and Masson-stained sections were observed using an optical ortho-microscope magnified 400X (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

Transcriptomic analysis

Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol LS Reagent (10296010CN, Invitrogen, USA) followed by DNase I digestion (18068015, Invitrogen, USA). RNA integrity was verified via Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (RIN ≥ 8.0, 28 S/18S > 1.8). Strand-specific mRNA libraries were prepared using NEBNext Ultra II Directional RNA Kit (NEB, E7760) with poly-A selection. In this study, Shanghai Bioprofile Biotechnology Co., Ltd. was commissioned to complete the sequencing using the NovaSeq 6000 (Illumina, CA, USA) platform. DESeq2 v1.38.3 pipeline with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction: Threshold: |log2FC|>1 & FDR < 0.05 Variance stabilizing transformation (VST) for cross-sample normalization. The DEGs were then used for Gene Ontology (GO) functional analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis. GO covers three areas: biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC) and molecular functions (MF). Also, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed in this study. Raw sequencing data have been deposited in GEO under accession GSE295524.

Proteomics analysis

Tissue lysates were homogenized in 8 M urea lysis buffer (50mM Tris-HCl pH8.2, 1% protease inhibitor cocktail) followed by BCA quantification (P0012S, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) against BSA standards. Shanghai Bioprofile Biotechnology Co., Ltd. was commissioned to perform proteomic analysis of protein samples using Liquid Chromatography-Tandem Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) in this study. Chromatographic separations were performed using an EasynLC1200 chromatography system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). DDA (Data Dependent Acquisition) mass spectrometry was performed using a Q-Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The raw data of all mass spectrometry data were searched and spectral databases were constructed using Proteome Discoverer software (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Student’s t-tests were used for calculations, and the screening criteria for significant differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were: expression difference multiplicity |log2FoldChange|>0 and significance p < 0.05. DEPs were utilized to conduct GO and KEGG analysis.

Metabolomics analysis

Metabolites were extracted as follows: each 50 mg of sample was thoroughly mixed with 200 µL of pre-cooled solution (methanol/water, 4:1, V: V), and the total metabolites were extracted by ultrasonication in an ice bath for 20 min. After centrifugation at 16,000 g for 20 min at 4 ℃, the supernatant was freeze-dried and stored at -80 ℃. Shanghai Bioprofile Biotechnology Co., Ltd. was commissioned to perform metabolomics analysis of metabolites using ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography and quadrupole time-of-fight tandem (UHPLC-QTOF) in this study. The samples were analyzed on a SHIMADZU-LC30 (Shimadzu Corporation, Kyoto, Japan) UHPLC system using an ACQUITY UPLC® HSS T3 (2.1 × 100 mm, 1.8 μm).

(Waters, Milford, MA, USA) column. Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry analysis was performed using a Vanquish UPLC system coupled to a Q-Exactive Plus hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). MSDIAL (ver.4.9) software was used to perform.

Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

RNAiso Plus reagent (TaKaRa) was used to isolate total RNA samples from lung tissues. Complementary DNA was reverse transcribed using the PrimeScrip™ RT kit (TaKaRa). Using the SYBR Premix Ex Taq™ (TaKaRa), qRT-PCR was conducted in the CFX Connect™ Real-Time PCR System (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The primer sequences are: mouse GAPDH forward primer 5’-TGTGTCCGTCGTGGATCTGA-3’, reverse primer 5’-TTGCTGTTGAAGTCGCAGGAG-3’; mouse Ces2e forward primer 5’- CTGGGTGTTCTGGGATTCTTCA-3’, reverse primer 5'-GTCAGGGTTGCCTCCAAAGT-3'.

Statistical analysis

Multivariate metabolic pattern analysis was conducted through the following pipeline implemented in R 4.0.2. Hierarchical clustering was performed using the hclust function (method = “complete”) from the stats package in R. Unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-tests with Welch’s correction (stats v3.6.2). Benjamini-Hochberg FDR adjustment for multiple comparisons (q < 0.05). Orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was used to perform the metabolomics analysis. GraphPad Prism 8 software (San Diego, CA, USA) was applied to analyze the relative Ces2e mRNA expression. All analyses considered p < 0.05 statistically significant unless otherwise specified.

Results

Temporal histopathological progression in radiation-induced lung injury

To systematically characterize the dynamic progression of radiation-induced lung injury, we implemented a dual-staining protocol combining HE and Masson’s trichrome staining. This approach enabled simultaneous evaluation of inflammatory dynamics (leukocyte infiltration) and fibrotic remodeling (through collagen deposition analysis) across three critical phases: acute inflammation (4 weeks post-irradiation), transitional (8 weeks post-irradiation), and fibrotic (16 weeks post-irradiation). As shown in Fig. 1A, HE staining revealed a significant increase in inflammatory infiltration of lung tissue following irradiation, with the extent of infiltration progressively worsening over time. Similarly, Masson staining demonstrated a marked increase in fibrotic collagen deposition in lung tissue post-irradiation (Fig. 1B), with fibrosis severity escalating as time progressed.

Dynamic transcriptomic landscape reveals phase-specific molecular signatures in radiation-induced lung injury

To explore the dynamic molecules under radiation stress, transcriptomic profiling was performed to systematically investigate temporal gene expression patterns in irradiated lung tissues at 4, 8, and 16 weeks post-radiation respectively. Volcano plot analysis identified distinct differentially expressed genes (DEGs) across time points: Irradiated tissues exhibited 96 upregulated and 66 downregulated DEGs at 4w, 183 upregulated and 267 downregulated DEGs at 8w, and 155 upregulated and 157 downregulated DEGs at 16w compared to matched controls (Fig. 2A). Subsequent Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis revealed stage-specific biological themes. At 4w post-irradiation, DEGs were primarily associated with extracellular space and region, response to extracellular stimulus, receptor ligand activity, etc. And at 8w post-irradiation, enrichment shifted to structural components including sarcomere organization, myofibril assembly, and contractile fiber processes. 16w post-irradiation, dominant pathways involved immune effector activation and responses to external stimuli (Fig. 2B). KEGG pathway analysis further delineated time-dependent signaling cascades. At acute inflammatory phase (4w), Cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions, fluid shear stress/atherosclerosis pathways, and p53-mediated signaling were prominently enriched. Transitional phase (8w): IL-17 signaling and PPAR metabolic regulation pathways emerged as key players. Fibrotic phase (16w): TNF and MAPK signaling pathways dominated the regulatory landscape (Fig. 2C). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) demonstrated time-dependent enrichment patterns: focal adhesion pathways were activated at 4w, followed by progressive engagement of TNF signaling at 8w and NF-κB signaling at 16w (Fig. 2D). This sequential activation pattern suggests a transition from acute tissue inflammation to chronic fibrotic remodeling during radiation-induced lung injury progression.

Temporal proteomic profiling of radiation-induced lung injury

Proteomic profiling was conducted to systematically characterize dynamic protein expression alterations in lung tissue at 4, 8 and 16 weeks post-irradiation. Volcano plot analysis revealed distinct patterns of differential protein expression (|log2FoldChange|>0, p < 0.05) across time points, with 426 (4w), 340 (8w), and 360 (16w) significantly regulated proteins identified (Fig. 3A). Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of protein expression profiles demonstrated clear temporal segregation (Euclidean distance > 8.7), with radiation-induced proteomic signatures showing progressive divergence from baseline (Fig. 3B). Time-stratified functional enrichment analysis revealed phase-specific biological patterns. In the acute phase, GO analysis demonstrated prominent activation of humoral immunity regulation, particularly through negative regulation of endopeptidase activity and humoral immune response, while KEGG pathway analysis identified complement and coagulation cascades and RNA degradation dominant pathways, suggesting acute-phase protein regulation and stress-responsive RNA surveillance mechanisms (Fig. 3C-D). Transitioning to the transitional phase, cellular homeostasis remodeling became evident with GO terms cellular catabolic process and mitochondrial membrane organization, coinciding with KEGG enrichment in protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum and MAPK signaling, indicative of endoplasmic reticulum stress adaptation and proliferative signaling activation (Fig. 3C-D). By the fibrotic phase, immune-mediated tissue remodeling predominated, evidenced by B cell-mediated immunity and extracellular matrix disassembly in GO analysis, complemented by KEGG pathway activation of drug metabolism-cytochrome P450 and complement cascade recurrence, highlighting late-phase xenobiotic metabolism and sustained complement-mediated fibrotic progression (Fig. 3C-D).

Temporal metabolomic profiling reveals phase-specific metabolic reprogramming in radiation-induced lung injury

Comprehensive metabolomic profiling was further performed to delineate dynamic metabolic alterations in lung tissue post-irradiation. Orthogonal Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) demonstrated distinct metabolic clustering between irradiated and control groups (Fig. 4A). Volcano plot analysis identified 277 (4w), 160 (8w), and 357 (16w) differentially abundant metabolites (|log2FoldChange|>0, p<0.05), revealing progressive metabolic dysregulation (Fig. 4B).

Key temporal signatures include elevated hexanoylglutamine and trifluoroacetic acid at 4 weeks, upregulation of 4-methylproline at 8 weeks, and global metabolic remodeling at 16 weeks. D Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis (p < 0.05) demonstrating temporal transition from acute oxidative stress response (4 weeks) through adaptive redox balancing (8 weeks) to chronic nutrient resynthesis (16 weeks)

Hierarchical clustering analysis of significantly altered metabolites demonstrated time-resolved metabolic dynamics across disease progression. Metabolomics Standards Initiative (MSI) levels were assigned based on the extent of metabolite identification, ranging from Level 1 (authenticated by reference standards) to Level 4 (unidentified compounds), as previously described [12]. During the acute inflammation phase, pronounced accumulation of hexanoylglutamine and trifluoroacetic acid. Trifluoroacetic acid (MSI Level 2) was identified by MS/MS spectral matching, whereas hexanoylglutamine (MSI Level 3) was tentatively assigned to the acylglutamine class by accurate mass only, lacking confirmatory MS/MS database matches. Transitioning to the transitional phase, coordinated upregulation of 4-methylproline, benzododecinium, and lanatoside. 4-methylproline (MSI Level 2) and benzododecinium (MSI Level 2) were identified by MS/MS spectral matching, whereas lanatoside (MSI Level 3) was tentatively assigned to the cardiac glycoside class by accurate mass only, lacking confirmatory MS/MS database matches. Chronic fibrotic phase exhibited a global metabolic shift (Fig. 4C), indicative of systemic metabolic network rewiring. Complementary KEGG pathway enrichment analysis (hypergeometric test, FDR < 0.05) revealed stage-dependent pathway activation cascades. Early acute inflammation, necroptosis regulation and vitamin B6 metabolism dominated, potentially linking pyroptosis-driven inflammation with pyridoxal phosphate-dependent transaminase activation. Transitional phase, glutathione metabolism synergized with cancer-associated choline metabolism, suggesting antioxidant defense mechanisms and phosphatidylcholine membrane repair. Fibrotic phase, robust activation of amino acid biosynthesis, protein digestion/absorption, and D-amino acid metabolism highlighted sustained anabolic demand for tissue remodeling (Fig. 4D). This metabolic trajectory—from acute oxidative stress response (4 weeks) through adaptive redox balancing (8 weeks) to chronic nutrient resynthesis (16 weeks)—establishes a three-stage metabolic reprogramming paradigm underlying radiation-induced lung injury progression.

Multi-omics integration reveals temporal molecular dynamics in radiation-induced lung injury

Integrated transcriptomic-proteomic analysis of irradiated lung tissues at 4, 8, and 16 weeks post-exposure unveiled stage-specific molecular coordination during disease progression. Hierarchical clustering of overlapping differentially expressed genes and proteins (row-wise) was performed using the complete linkage method, and the results were visualized as a heatmap. Venn diagram analysis of differentially expressed genes and proteins identified temporally evolving molecular intersections: three consensus biomarkers at 4 weeks (FABP1, CES2E, PHLDA3) exhibiting strong RNA-protein correlations, expanding to ten co-regulated molecules at 8 weeks including redox regulator FMO1 (RNA FC = 0.4, protein FC = 0.6 ), apoptosis mediator BAX (RNA FC = 2.1, protein FC = 1.6), CES2E (RNA FC = 3.4, protein FC = 3.5) and SCD1 (RNA FC = 2.2, protein FC = 2.1). Chronic phase (16 weeks) featured eleven persistently dysregulated targets such as STEAP4 (RNA FC = 2.2, protein FC = 2.1), FCGR3 (RNA FC = 2.1, protein FC = 6.1), C4B (RNA FC = 2.5, protein FC = 2.4) and UGT1A7C (RNA FC = 2.3, protein FC = 2.7), demonstrating progressive amplification of inflammatory and fibrotic signals (Fig. 5A-B). Consensus KEGG pathway mapping (Fig. 5C) delineated phase-specific biological programs. Acute inflammation phase, dominant activation of endocytosis pathways, reflecting acute cellular debris clearance mechanisms. Transition phase, emergence of protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum. Chronic fibrotic phase, sustained activation of complement and coagulation cascades, aligning with fibrotic matrix remodeling. Dynamic heatmap results quantified the progressive organization of fibrosis-related protein clusters, with FMO1 and STEAP4 serving as topological hubs. This multi-omics temporal atlas delineates three pathogenic checkpoints—initial damage response (4 weeks), adaptive reprogramming (8 weeks), and fibrotic commitment (16 weeks)—providing mechanistic insights into radiation-induced lung injury progression. As shown in Fig. 5D, qPCR validation results indicate that Ces2e mRNA expression levels significantly increased at 4 and 8 weeks post-irradiation. Ces2e mRNA expression levels showed no statistically significant change at 16 weeks post-irradiation (p = 0.2405).

Discussion

The integration of transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic analyses provides a multidimensional perspective for unraveling dynamic molecular alterations during disease progression [13–15]. In this study, we systematically dissected the temporal evolution of radiation-induced lung injury (RILI) in a murine model, delineating distinct molecular signatures across radiation pneumonitis (RP), transitional, and radiation pulmonary fibrosis (RPF) phases. Through multi-omics integration, we further identified potential biomarkers that may orchestrate protective mechanisms against RILI. Histopathological validation via HE and Masson staining confirmed the successful establishment of the RILI model, with characteristic inflammatory infiltration and collagen deposition aligning with disease stage progression. At 4 weeks post-irradiation, transcriptomic profiling revealed enrichment of extracellular matrix remodeling, cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions (e.g., IL-1α, IL-6), and p53 signaling pathways. These findings corroborate prior evidence that acute-phase RILI is characterized by ROS overproduction [16], and upregulation of adhesion molecules like ICAM-1 [17], which serve as early sentinels of pneumonitis [18]. Notably, the observed fluid shear stress pathway activation may reflect vascular endothelial injury—a hallmark of radiation damage. Transitioning to fibrotic phases (8–16 weeks), pathways governing cytoskeletal reorganization (myofibril assembly) and immune effector activation dominated, paralleled by IL-17/MAPK signaling upregulation. This temporal shift from inflammatory to fibrogenic transcriptional programs aligns with the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) paradigm in pulmonary fibrosis [19], suggesting MAPK inhibition as a plausible therapeutic target, as evidenced by its antifibrotic efficacy in renal [20] and pulmonary [21] models.

Proteomic analysis demonstrated stage-dependent dysregulation. At 4 weeks post-irradiation, GO/KEGG analyses predominantly identified negative regulation of endopeptidase activity, humoral immune response, complement/coagulation cascades, and RNA degradation. Subsequent phases (8–16 weeks) exhibited shifted enrichment toward cellular process regulation, peptidase activity suppression, endoplasmic reticulum protein processing, and MAPK signaling activation, with persistent complement/coagulation cascade involvement across all stages. The complement/coagulation cascade system—a tightly regulated proteolytic network bridging innate and humoral immunity [22]—emerged as a central player throughout RILI progression. Its sustained activation across acute inflammatory (4 weeks) and chronic fibrotic (16 weeks) phases suggests dual functionality in both tissue damage response and fibrotic niche formation. Notably, MAPK signaling pathway activation demonstrated progressive intensification during fibrotic transition (8–16 weeks), corroborating its established role as a multiorgan fibrogenic regulator. This observation aligns with renal fibrosis studies demonstrating MAPK inhibition efficacy [20], and parallels pulmonary fibrosis research where TGF-β-MAPK axis blockade attenuates disease progression [21]. These conserved mechanistic features across organ systems underscore the pathway’s therapeutic relevance for RILI intervention.

Metabolomic profiling exposed profound metabolic network rewiring, extending beyond the known oxidative stress paradigm. Trifluoroacetic acid accumulated during the acute phase (4 weeks) (Fig. 4B).The transitional phase glutathione-choline axis activation (8 weeks) (Fig. 4D) may represent an adaptive redox balancing mechanism, while chronic phase amino acid biosynthesis dominance (16 weeks) likely supports collagen overproduction - a finding that parallels metabolic reprogramming in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis [23].

Integrated analysis identified Ces2e as a consistently upregulated node in both transcriptomic and proteomic analysis at 4–8 weeks, while Ces1e suppression emerged as a late-phase feature (16 weeks). Biologically, the CES1 and CES2 families exhibit significant differences in substrate specificity and tissue distribution: The CES1 family is primarily expressed in the liver and prefers large acyl substrates, while the CES2 family is highly expressed in the intestine and favors small acyl substrates [19, 24–26]. Our research has identified Ces2e as a potential early biomarker for RILI, while Ces1e may serve as a late biomarker during the chronic phase as RILI progresses over time. Although human Ces2e homologs remain understudied, murine Ces2e/Ces1e dysregulation suggests carboxylesterase-mediated lipid metabolism rewiring may influence RILI progression. In future research, we also aim to conduct a more comprehensive analysis of the CES family using human RILI clinical specimens. These enzymes, implicated in xenobiotic detoxification and fatty acid metabolism [27], warrant mechanistic exploration given their stage-specific expression patterns. Similarly, Fabp1 (fatty acid-binding protein 1) and Scd1 (stearoyl-CoA desaturase) fluctuations imply altered lipid homeostasis as a potential driver of fibrotic transformation.

While our multi-omics approach provides a comprehensive roadmap of RILI pathogenesis, certain limitations remain. First, there is a lack of functional validation for the candidate biomarker Ces2e. Relevant experimental content will be supplemented in our future research. Second, proteomic results should be considered hypothesis-generating, as stringent multiple-testing correction yielded few significant hits, and nominal p-value thresholds may introduce false positives. Third, all analyses in this study were performed on whole lung tissue, lacking examination of specific cell types and their mechanisms. In future research, we will supplement relevant single-cell data to further analyze Ces2e expression changes in specific cell types. Lastly, interspecies differences in fibrotic pathways necessitate cautious extrapolation to human pathophysiology. Future studies should prioritize longitudinal human sample validation and combinatorial targeting of MAPK signaling with metabolic modulators.

In conclusion, this study establishes a temporal atlas of molecular perturbations driving RILI progression, advocating for stage-adapted therapeutic strategies. Early-phase interventions targeting cytokine storms and oxidative stress may prevent irreversible damage, whereas late-phase therapies could focus on MAPK pathway inhibition and metabolic reprogramming. The Ces1e/Ces2e axis emerges as a novel biomarker candidate, potentially bridging inflammatory and fibrotic processes. These findings underscore the transformative potential of multi-omics integration in precision radiation oncology.

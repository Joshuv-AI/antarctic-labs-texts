C-reactive protein as a predicting factor for difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy or its conversion to open cholecystectomy in acute calculous cholecystitis: an observational study
Authors: Bikash Bahadur Rayamajhi, Abhishek Shrivastav, Gaurav Karki, Dhirendra Bahadur Ayer, Suresh Kumar Pradhan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAcute calculous cholecystitis (ACC) is a common surgical emergency often requiring laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC), though some cases necessitate conversion to open cholecystectomy (OC). Predicting difficult LC or conversion is crucial for operative planning. This study evaluates the role of C-reactive protein (CRP) as a predictor of surgical complexity in ACC.MethodsA prospective observational study was conducted at Shree Birendra Hospital, Kathmandu, from April 2024 to March 2025, including 108 patients undergoing emergency LC for ACC. Patients with conditions confounding CRP levels were excluded. Preoperative CRP levels were measured, and operative time was recorded. LC lasting more than 60 min was defined as difficult. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0.ResultsAmong 108 patients (mean age 49.8 years, male to female ratio 1:3.5), 71 underwent simple LC, 28 difficult LC, and 9 were converted to OC. Mean CRP levels were 18.2 mg/L in simple, 36 mg/L in difficult, and 50.1 mg/L in converted cases (p = 0.002). A significant association was found between elevated CRP and surgical complexity (p < 0.001). Duration of surgery and hospital stay increased with complexity (p < 0.001), while age and gender showed no significant association.ConclusionsElevated preoperative CRP levels are significantly associated with increased surgical complexity and likelihood of conversion in ACC. CRP can serve as a valuable tool for anticipating technical challenges and optimizing resource, especially in resource limited setting.Keywords: Acute calculous cholecystitis, CRP, Difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy, Open cholecystectomy

Background

Acute calculous cholecystitis (ACC) is a common surgical emergency characterized by inflammation of the gallbladder due to presence of stones, often necessitating surgical intervention [1]. Laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) has become the gold standard treatment; however, intraoperative challenges may necessitate conversion to open cholecystectomy (OC) [2, 3].

Difficult LC refers to a scenario where factors like complex anatomy, severe inflammation or adhesions complicate the procedure, increasing the risk of complications such as bile duct injury, hemorrhage, and prolonged operative time (more than 60 min) [4–6]. Early prediction of difficult LC or conversion is essential for surgical planning, resource allocation, and informed patient consent. Several clinical and operative scoring systems have been proposed to predict difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy, including the Nassar grading system, Parkland grading scale, and Tokyo Guidelines severity classification, but many rely on intraoperative findings, advanced imaging, subjective interpretation limiting their utility in preoperative decision-making [6, 7]. Consequently, there is a need for a simple, objective, and readily available preoperative marker that can reliably predict surgical difficulty, particularly in emergency and resource-limited settings [8]. C-reactive protein (CRP), an acute-phase reactant, is as a potential predictor of disease severity, elevation of which is associated with increased tissue inflammation, adhesions, and operative complexity [8–10].

This study aims to evaluate the role of CRP as a predicting factor for difficult LC and its conversion to OC in patients with ACC.

Methods

This study is a prospective observational study conducted in Department of General Surgery, Shree Birendra Hospital, Kathmandu, Nepal from April 1st, 2024 to March 31st, 2025. Ethical approval for the study was obtained from Institutional Review Committee of Nepalese Army Institute of Health Sciences (Reg No. 1171). Patients with acute cholecystitis undergoing emergency laparoscopic cholecystectomy under a single unit were included in the study. Diagnosis of acute calculous cholecystitis was based on Tokyo Guidelines 2018, requiring clinical, laboratory, and ultrasonographic criteria. Ultrasound findings considered included gallbladder wall thickness > 4 mm, pericholecystic fluid, gallbladder distension, and presence of gallstones. Patients with previous abdominal surgery, BMI more than 35, any proven congenital anomaly of gallbladder, patients with features of cholangitis, those with choledocholithiasis, immunocompromised status or any other co-existing conditions for raised CRP such as systemic infections, autoimmune or inflammatory diseases, chronic liver disease, chronic kidney disease, or malignancy were excluded from the study. After informed consent, 108 patients were enrolled in the study. Preoperative investigations, including quantitative C-reactive protein (CRP), were obtained at the time of hospital admission prior to surgery as part of routine evaluation. CRP measurement was performed using an immunoturbidimetric assay in the hospital laboratory, where CRP levels < 5 mg/L were considered normal. All patients underwent early laparoscopic cholecystectomy within 72 h of hospital admission. All procedures were performed by a single consultant surgeon who had completed more than 500 laparoscopic cholecystectomies prior to the study period, including emergency cases. This was done to minimize inter-operator variability and the influence of the learning curve on operative outcomes. The surgeon was blinded to patient participation in the study. Operative time was noted from insertion of Veress needle to extraction of gallbladder. LC with an operative time exceeding 60 min was defined as difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy. This cutoff was selected based on previously published studies where operative duration > 60 min has been used as a surrogate marker of increased technical difficulty and intraoperative complexity.

The data were analyzed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 26.0. Data were shown as mean ± standard deviation. Chi-square test was used to compare the differences between the group for qualitative variables and independent student t test was used for quantitative variables. Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to assess the ability of preoperative CRP levels to predict difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy and conversion to open cholecystectomy. The area under the curve (AUC) was calculated, and optimal cutoff values were determined based on maximal sensitivity and specificity.

Results

In our study including 108 cases of emergency cholecystectomies performed for acute calculous cholecystitis, mean age of participants was 49.8 years with male to female ratio of 1:3.5. There was no statistically significant association between sex and the type of cholecystectomy performed (p = 0.166) (Table 1). While male patients had a higher conversion rate to open surgery (16.7%) compared to females (6.0%), and female patients had a higher proportion of difficult laparoscopic cases (28.6%), these differences were not statistically significant.

Among patients undergoing simple laparoscopic cholecystectomy, 63.4% had CRP levels below 18 mg/L, whereas only 36.6% had elevated CRP (> 18 mg/L). Conversely, in difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy, a significant 78.6% of patients had CRP levels above 18 mg/L i.e. > 3.5 times upper limit of normal range of CRP, compared to just 21.4% below that threshold. In the converted to open cholecystectomy group, an even more distinct association was seen: 88.9% of patients had CRP levels above 36 mg/L i.e. > 7 times upper limit of normal range of CRP, while only one patient (11.1%) had a lower level. The study results demonstrated a strong and statistically significant association between elevated preoperative C-reactive protein (CRP) levels and the increasing complexity of cholecystectomy procedures (p < 0.001) (Table 3).

ROC curve analysis demonstrated that preoperative CRP had good discriminatory ability for predicting difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy, with an AUC of 0.814 (95% CI: 0.725–0.903). A CRP cutoff value of 18 mg/L yielded a sensitivity of 78.6% and specificity of 56.2%. Similarly, for prediction of conversion to open cholecystectomy, the ROC curve showed an AUC of 0.964 (95% CI: 0.921–1.0), with a CRP cutoff value of 36 mg/L providing a sensitivity of 88.9% and specificity of 82.8% (Figs. 1 and 2).

Discussion

Our study evaluated the role of CRP as predictor of surgical complexity in emergency cholecystectomy for acute calculous cholecystitis. Existing scoring systems for predicting difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy, such as the Nassar and Parkland grading systems, are primarily based on intraoperative findings and therefore cannot assist in preoperative risk stratification. The Tokyo Guidelines severity grading incorporates clinical, laboratory, and imaging parameters; however, its application may be limited by imaging availability and operator dependency. In contrast, C-reactive protein is an objective, inexpensive, and universally available laboratory marker that can be measured preoperatively and reflects the degree of inflammatory response. This makes CRP a practical tool for anticipating operative difficulty and planning surgical strategy, especially in emergency settings. Our findings revealed that preoperative CRP levels, duration of surgery, and postoperative hospital stay were significantly associated with increasing surgical complexity, while age and gender showed no statistically significant association with the type of cholecystectomy performed, as supported by the previous studies [11, 12].

The mean age of participants was 49.8 years with male to female ratio of 1:3.5 which align with established epidemiological trends, as gallstone disease and its complications are known to be more prevalent in women and it aligns with the findings of the study by Kaushik et al. showing a mean age of 50.41 years and male to female ratio of 1:4.28 [13]. Despite a higher conversion rate to open surgery among males (16.7%) compared to females (6.0%), and a higher incidence of difficult laparoscopic cases in females (28.6%), these differences did not reach statistical significance (Table 1). This supports the findings of the study by Arora BS et al. which also revealed no significant association of age for need of conversion to open cholecystectomy [14].

Importantly, our study demonstrated that age was not significantly different among the three surgical outcome groups (simple, difficult, and converted; p = 0.945), indicating that patient age may not independently predict intraoperative difficulty or conversion. This is in contrast with the study by Mok KWJ et al. suggesting older age as a risk factor for difficult cholecystectomy and its conversion to open cholecystectomy [15].

Preoperative CRP levels showed a clear and statistically significant association with the complexity of surgery. Mean CRP values increased progressively across the surgical spectrum: 18.2 mg/L in simple laparoscopic cases, 36 mg/L in difficult cases, and 50.1 mg/L in those converted to open surgery (p = 0.002). Similarly, a study by Kaushik et al. showed significant increase in CRP level with increasing complexity of surgery (p = 0.0002) however the average CRP level in the simple (22.2), difficult (46.5) and converted to open (83.6) was higher than the mean CRP levels of our study [13]. This trend was reinforced by categorical analysis: the majority of patients undergoing simple procedures had CRP < 18 mg/L, whereas most patients requiring conversion had CRP > 36 mg/L. In the present study, preoperative CRP thresholds of 18 mg/L and 36 mg/L were identified as predictive of difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy and conversion to open surgery, respectively. These values were derived using Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, ensuring an objective and statistically optimized balance between sensitivity and specificity. Elevated CRP reflects the degree and duration of inflammatory response in acute calculous cholecystitis, which may translate into dense adhesions, obscured Calot’s triangle anatomy, and increased technical difficulty during surgery. Our study revealed a CRP cutoff of 18 mg/L having sensitivity of 78.6% in predicting difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy so as did a CRP level of 22 mg/L in the study by Kaushik et al. [13] In the present study, operative time was used as a surrogate indicator of surgical difficulty. Although operative duration alone may not fully reflect technical complexity, prolonged operative time has been consistently associated with severe inflammation, dense adhesions, and increased intraoperative challenges in acute cholecystitis. The use of a 60-minute cutoff has been widely reported in the literature and provides an objective and reproducible measure [4, 5].

Length of postoperative hospitalization also increased significantly with surgical complexity (p < 0.001), underscoring the clinical burden associated with more complicated cases. Similar findings have been revealed in the study by Kaushik et al. and Kadirvel et al. [13, 16]. These findings highlight the utility of CRP not only as a diagnostic marker for acute cholecystitis but also as a prognostic tool to guide surgical planning and patient counselling.

This single-centre study has certain limitations. The small number of patients requiring conversion to open cholecystectomy (n = 9) and the limited sample size restricted analysis to univariate methods, so conclusions should be interpreted cautiously. Symptom-to-surgery duration was not assessed, and CRP was measured only at admission. As CRP levels are time-dependent and symptom onset is often subjectively reported in emergency settings, this may have introduced variability. However, the study was designed to assess the pragmatic value of admission CRP as obtained in routine emergency practice, where precise symptom chronology is often subjectively reported and difficult to standardize. CRP should therefore be interpreted in conjunction with clinical judgment and intraoperative findings rather than as a standalone predictor. Additionally, intraoperative grading of difficulty was not standardized using a validated scoring system which may limit external applicability. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that operative time cannot distinguish between cautious dissection and true technical difficulty.

Although CRP showed a strong association with surgical complexity in our cohort, it should not be considered a standalone determinant of operative difficulty or conversion. Surgeons with advanced experience and use of alternative safe techniques may successfully complete laparoscopic cholecystectomy even with higher CRP levels. Furthermore, intraoperative difficulty grading and detailed reasons for conversion were not systematically recorded and will be incorporated into future prospective studies.

Despite these limitations, our results support the use of preoperative CRP as a valuable predictor of surgical complexity and the potential need for conversion in acute calculous cholecystitis. Future prospective studies incorporating additional inflammatory markers, imaging findings, and standardized difficulty grading are necessary to further refine predictive models and improve surgical outcomes.

Conclusions

Our study concluded that CRP is a potent pre-operative predictor of difficult laparoscopic cholecystectomy or its conversion to open cholecystectomy. This may forecast technical problems during surgery which can guide preoperative planning, informed consent, and allocation of surgical resources.

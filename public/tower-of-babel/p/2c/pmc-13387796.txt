Authors: Jong-Ling Fuh

1. ADVANCES IN BODY COMPOSITION IMAGING FOR DIAGNOSIS AND MONITORING: FROM RADIOLOGIC ASSESSMENT TO PRECISION MEDICINE

This review does more than revisit the limitations of body mass index; it makes a compelling case for why body composition imaging should be viewed as a clinically actionable extension of routine assessment. By distinguishing skeletal muscle, visceral adipose tissue, and ectopic fat, and by integrating CT, MRI, and artificial intelligence–based analysis, the approach reveals high-risk phenotypes such as sarcopenia, myosteatosis, and sarcopenic obesity that conventional anthropometric measures can easily overlook. Particularly noteworthy is the emphasis on opportunistic screening using already available images, which aligns well with the broader movement toward precision medicine by extracting greater value from existing clinical data without adding radiation exposure or cost. For readers interested in metabolic risk, aging, and preventive care, this article stands out as a forward-looking reminder that better phenotyping may lead to better decisions.1

2. RESPIRATORY SYNCYTIAL VIRUS INFECTION IN ADULTS: CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS, OUTCOMES, AND VACCINES

This review is particularly valuable because it reframes respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) as not merely a pediatric concern, but as an increasingly consequential pathogen in adult medicine, especially among older adults and those with chronic cardiopulmonary disease or immunocompromised status. The article effectively brings together the clinical burden of RSV-related hospitalization, intensive care use, diagnostic uncertainty, and the still-limited treatment landscape. Its timeliness is further strengthened by the discussion of recent progress in vaccine development, including both protein-based and mRNA-based platforms. At a moment when adult RSV prevention is moving from a neglected topic to a practical clinical priority, this review offers a well-balanced and highly relevant update for frontline physicians.2

3. ENDOSCOPIC VARICEAL LIGATION VERSUS CYANOACRYLATE INJECTION FOR ACUTE VARICEAL BLEEDING FROM THE CARDIA TO THE LESSER CURVATURE

This study is clinically meaningful because it addresses a management dilemma that is common in practice yet insufficiently resolved in the literature: how best to treat acute variceal bleeding involving the cardia and lesser curvature. The overall finding that endoscopic variceal ligation and cyanoacrylate injection yielded similar outcomes in terms of in early treatment failure, 6-week rebleeding, and survival is important in itself, but the more interesting message lies in the subgroup analysis. In patients with cardiac varices located near to the gastroesophageal junction, endoscopic variceal ligation was associated with a lower risk of rebleeding, suggesting that anatomical nuance matters more than broad classification alone. The study therefore adds an important layer of practical insight, reminding clinicians that precise variceal localization may meaningfully influence therapeutic choice.3

4. BIOLOGIC THERAPY AND RISK OF SERIOUS INFECTIONS IN ANKYLOSING SPONDYLITIS AND PSORIATIC ARTHRITIS

This real-world study is noteworthy because it challenges one of the most persistent concerns surrounding biologic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs: the assumption that better immunologic control necessarily comes at the cost of more serious infections. In this large cohort of patients with ankylosing spondylitis and psoriatic arthritis, biologic therapy was instead associated with a lower risk of infections requiring hospitalization. That observation invites a more nuanced interpretation of infection risk, one in which improved control of systemic inflammation, better overall disease management, and restoration of skin barrier function in psoriatic arthritis may offset some of the expected hazards of immunosuppression. At the same time, the article appropriately reminds readers that observational data must be interpreted cautiously, particularly in light of treatment selection and possible channeling bias. Even so, the study offers reassuring and practice-relevant evidence for clinicians discussing biologic therapy with patients.4

5. REIMBURSEMENT POLICY AND OUTCOMES AFTER TRANSCATHETER AORTIC VALVE IMPLANTATION

This study is especially compelling because it moves beyond procedural outcomes to show how reimbursement policy can directly shape access, case mix, and downstream outcomes in advanced cardiovascular care. Following the introduction of Taiwan’s National Health Insurance coverage, transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) was increasingly offered to older and higher-risk patients a higher burden of comorbidities burden, yet short-term safety and one-year survival remained stable. Perhaps the most policy-relevant finding is the reduction in heart failure rehospitalization following the expansion of reimbursement coverage, suggesting that broader financial access, coupled with technological advances, refined patient selection, and growing operator experience, may allow higher-risk patients to benefit without compromising outcomes. For clinicians and health policymakers alike, this article provides an instructive example of how reimbursement decisions can influence not only utilization but also the real-world value of a high-cost intervention.5

SLAMF8 regulates osteogenesis and adipogenesis of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells via S100A6/Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway
Authors: Yibo Wang, Kai Hang, Xiaoyong Wu, Li Ying, Zhongxiang Wang, Zemin Ling, Hao Hu, Zhijun Pan, Xuenong Zou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe inflammatory microenvironment plays an essential role in bone healing after fracture. The signaling lymphocytic activation molecule family (SLAMF) members deeply participate in inflammatory response and make a vast difference.MethodsWe identified SLAMF8 in GEO datasets (GSE129165 and GSE176086) and co-expression analyses were performed to define the relationships between SLAMF8 and osteogenesis relative genes (RUNX2 and COL1A1). In vitro, we established SLAMF8 knockdown and overexpression mouse bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (mBMSCs) lines. qPCR, Western blot, ALP staining, ARS staining, Oil Red O staining and Immunofluorescence analyses were performed to investigate the effect of SLAMF8 in mBMSCs osteogenesis and adipogenesis. In vivo, mice femoral fracture model was performed to explore the function of SLAMF8.ResultsSLAMF8 knockdown significantly suppressed the expression of osteogenesis relative genes (RUNX2, SP7 and COL1A1), ALP activity and mineral deposition, but increased the expression of adipogenesis relative genes (PPARγ and C/EBPα). Additionally, SLAMF8 overexpression had the opposite effects. The role SLAMF8 played in mBMSCs osteogenic and adipogenic differentiation were through S100A6 and Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway. Moreover, SLAMF8 overexpression mBMSCs promoted the healing of femoral fracture.ConclusionsSLAMF8 promotes osteogenesis and inhibits adipogenesis of mBMSCs via S100A6 and Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway. SLAMF8 overexpression mBMSCs effectively accelerate the healing of femoral fracture in mice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13287-024-03964-1.Keywords: SLAMF8, mBMSCs, Differentiation, S100A6, Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway

Background

Non-union bone fracture imposes a major economic burden to the social [1]. Currently, the risk of non-union bone fracture is reported to range from 1.9–4.9% [2]. Thus, novel strategies to promote the healing of bone are required. The recruitment of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), characterized by self-renewal and multilineage differentiation potential [3, 4], at the microenvironment of bone fracture are crucial for bone healing [5, 6]. The initial stages of fracture healing are hematoma formation and acute inflammation. At the microenvironment of hematoma, inflammatory cells, such as neutrophils and macrophages, are enriched and they secrete numerous inflammatory factors and chemokines, such as tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α), interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β), IL-6, and CCL2 (also known as monocyte chemotactic protein 1, MCP-1). These mediators recruit more inflammatory cells, MSCs and fibroblasts to participate in further fracture healing process [7, 8]. The effects of TNF-α to BMSCs differentiation are still ambiguous. Numerous of studies reveal that the paradoxical effects of TNF-α to the BMSCs osteogenic differentiation depend on the TNF-α concentration and the exposure time [9].

As transcription factors, RUNX2 and SP7 (also known as Osterix) are well learned at osteogenesis [10–12]. Previous findings have demonstrated that RUNX2 is upregulated in multiple human cancers and is participated in many biological behaviors of cancers [13–15]. Importantly, RUNX2 plays roles in the proliferation, invasion, tumorigenesis, bone metastasis and cancer stemness of breast cancer and prostate cancer [16–18]. Moreover, recent researches have indicated that SP7 also promotes breast cancer migration, angiogenesis, invasion and bone metastasis [19, 20]. As for bone derived cancer, such as osteosarcoma, RUNX2 takes part in pathogenesis of osteosarcoma by regulating cell cycle of pre-osteoblasts, which subsequently transform into osteosarcoma cells [21]. In summary, the key regulators of osteogenesis (RUNX2 and SP7) also express in cancer cells and regulate cancer cells biological functions.

The signaling lymphocytic activation molecule family (SLAMF) are a group of receptors, whose expression is mostly confined to immune cells, contain 9 members [22]. SLAMF members play important role in immune response and inflammation response [23, 24]. However, the function of SLAMF members the differentiation of BMSCs and whether SLAMF8 takes part in the bone fracture healing still remain unknown.

Methods

Bioinformatics analyses

The gene expression databases of BMSCs treated with TNFα (50ng/mL or 100ng/mL) were downloaded from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets (GSE129165 and GSE176086). The differentially expression analyses were performed by R and R studio software (version 4.0.2), using the R package “DESeq2” (|logFC| > 2 and adj.P.Value < 0.01). The raw expression data of SLAMF8, OSA1, RSAD2, COL1A1, RUNX2, PPARG and CEBPA genes were downloaded from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) project (https://portals.broadinstitute.org/ccle), The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) project (http://cancergenome.nih.gov/) and the Genotype Tissue Expression (GTEx) project (https://www.gtexportal.org/). The Pearson correlation (R) analysis and p-value were calculated by R and R studio software (version 4.0.2). Meanwhile, 93 samples of osseous and chondromatous neoplasms and miscellaneous bone tumors at TCGA project were divided into 2 groups according Slamf8 expression (SLAMF8_High and SLAMF8_Low). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) of these 2 groups were performed following the guide online (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp).

Cell culture and differentiation

Primary mouse bone marrow stem cells (mBMSCs) were purchased from Cyagen Biosciences (MUBMX-01001, Guangzhou, China). mBMSCs were cultured in α-MEM medium with 10% FBS (Cyagen, FBSAD-01011) and penicillin-streptomycin. Osteogenesis was induced by osteogenic induction medium, containing α-MEM with 10% FBS supplemented with 100 mg/mL ascorbic acid (PHR1008, Sigma, St Louis, MO, USA) and 10 mM β-glycerophosphate (G9422, Sigma). Adipogenesis was induced by adipogenic induction medium (MUXMX-90031, Cyagen). All cells were cultured at humidified, 37 °C, 5% CO2 incubator and fresh medium was changed every 2 days.

The stable SLAMF8 overexpression cell line was transfected by lentivirus (Tsingke Biotechnology, Beijing, China). 40-60% confluent mBMSCs were incubated with lentivirus and 4 µg/mL polybrene. After 48 h, cells were passaged and cultured with 2 µg/mL Puromycin Dihydrochloride (ST551, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) to sift stable SLAMF8 overexpression cell line for further experiments.

The siRNAs (Tsingke Biotechnology) were used to knock down the expression of SLAMF8 and S100A6 in mBMSCs. Cells were transfected with SLAMF8-siRNA, S100A6-siRNA and negative control siRNA respectively by using Lipo6000 (C0526, Beyotime). All siRNA sequence were listed at Supplementary Table 1.

RNA isolation and qPCR

Total RNA was isolated by RNAiso Plus (9109, Takara Bio Inc., Shiga, Japan) and quantified by measuring the absorbance at 260 nm (NanoDrop 2000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA). First strand cDNA was synthesized by PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Total RNA (≤ 1000 ng) was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the Double-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Takara) in a reaction volume of 20 µl. The levels of all gene expressions were quantified by qPCR using SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Takara) on StepOnePlus real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems Inc., Warrington, United Kingdom). The cycle conditions were as follows: 95 °C for 30s followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5s and 60 °C for 30s. 18 S was used as a control and allowed adjustment of differences among samples. The relative target gene expression levels were calculated using the 2 − ΔΔCt method. All primers were synthesized by Tsingke Biotechnology and listed at Supplementary Table 2.

Western blotting analysis

Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer (FD008, Fdbio Science, Hangzhou, China) with proteasome inhibitor (FD1001, Fdbio Science) and phosphatase inhibitors (FD1002, Fdbio Science). Proteins were separated by FuturePAGE™ 4–20% Gels (ET15420, ACE Biotechnology, Nanjing, China) and then transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, Shanghai, China). After blocking in 5% non-fat milk for 1 h, the PVDF membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. The following primary antibodies were used: anti-GAPDH (1:5000, ab8245, Abcam, Cambridge, United Kingdom), anti-RUNX2 (1:1000, #12556, Cell Signaling Technology, MA, United States), anti-COL1A1 (1:1000, ab255809, Abcam), anti-SP7 (1:1000, ab209484, Abcam), anti-PPARγ (1:1000, AF7797, Beyotime), anti-C/EBPα (1:1000, #8178, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-SLAMF8 (1:1000, 50623-RP02, Sino Biological US Inc., PA, USA), anti-S100A6 (1:1000, ab181975, Abcam), anti-non-phosphorylated (active) β-Catenin (1:1000, #19807, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-total β-Catenin (1:1000, #8480, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-p38 (1:1000, #8690, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-p-p38 (1:1000, #4511, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-ERK (1:1000, #4695, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-p-ERK (1:1000, #4670, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-AKT (1:1000, #4691, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-p-AKT (1:1000, #4060, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-p65 (1:1000, #8242, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-p-p65 (1:1000, #3033, Cell Signaling Technology). After washing 3 times (5 min each time) in Tris-buffered saline with 0.1% Tween 20 (TBST), the membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG(H + L) (1:5000, A0208, Beyotime) for 1 h at room temperature. After washing three times (5 min each time) with TBST, proteins were detected using enhanced chemiluminescence blotting reagents (Millipore). Signal intensity was measured by Amersham™ ImageQuant™ 800 system (GE, MA, USA) and quantitative analyses were performed by ImageJ (version 1.53).

Immunoprecipitation (IP), silver staining and Mass Spectrometry (MS)

SLAMF8 overexpression mBMSCs were cultured at osteogenic induction medium for 5 days. Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer (FD008, Fdbio Science) with proteasome inhibitor (FD1001, Fdbio Science) and phosphatase inhibitors (FD1002, Fdbio Science). SLAMF8 antibody (1:50, AF4156, R&D Systems) was used to pull down SLAMF8 and the possible interactive proteins. Electrophoresis experiments were performed and proteins were visualized on the gel by silver staining (P0017S, Beyotime).

The gels were taken for subsequent enzymatic hydrolysis and mass spectrometry detection. Briefly, the entire slab of the SDS-PAGE gels were digested by trypsin for 30 min and then desalted with C18 tip and stored in 0.1% formic acid for LCMS analysis. For each sample, 5 L of total peptides were separated and analyzed with a nanoUPLC (EASYnLC1200) coupled to a Q Exactive HFX Orbitrap instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with a nano-electrospray ion source. Data dependent acquisition (DDA) was performed in profile and positive mode with Orbitrap analyzer at a resolution of 120,000 (@200 m/z) and m/z range of 350–1600 for MS1; For MS2, the resolution was set to 15,000 with a dynamic first mass. The automatic gain control (AGC) target for MS1 was set to 3e6 with max IT 50 ms, and 1e5 for MS2 with max IT 110 ms. The top 20 most intense ions were fragmented by HCD with normalized collision energy (NCE) of 27%, and isolation window of 1.2 m/z. The dynamic exclusion time window was 45s, single charged peaks and peaks with charge exceeding 6 were excluded from the DDA procedure. Vendor’s raw MS files were processed using Proteome Discoverer (PD) software (Version 2.4.0.305) and the built-in Sequest HT search engine.

ALP staining and ARS staining

For ALP staining, cells cultured in 12-well plates after 5 days osteogenic induction were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min. Then, after washed by double distilled water (ddH2O) 3 times, cells were stained by BCIP/NBT Alkaline Phosphatase Color Development Kit (C3206, Beyotime). To measure ALP activity, cells were lysed with lysis buffer consisted of 20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.5), 1% Triton X-100, and 150mM NaCl. ALP activity was determined by ALP activity assay (P0321S, Beyotime) and the conversion color of p-nitrophenyl phosphate was measured at 405/650nm.

As for ARS staining, after osteogenesis for 21 days, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min and washed by ddH2O for 3 times. Alizarin Red staining (ARS) (Cyagen Biosciences) was used to assess the mineral deposition of these cells for 10 min and stop by PBS washing 3 times. The quantification was performed as previous study [25].

Oil Red O staining

After adipogenesis following the protocol (Cyagen Biosciences, MUXMX-90031) for 21 days. Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min and subsequently rinsed with PBS 3 times. Following the protocol (Cyagen Biosciences, MUXMX-90031), cells were stained with 0.5mL of oil red O working solution for 30 min and stop by PBS washing 3 times.

Cell immunofluorescence analysis

mBMSCs were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS at room temperature for 10 min and blocked in 2% bovine serum albumin for 30 min. Fixed cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies. The following primary antibodies were used: anti-RUNX2 (1:500, #12556, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-COL1A1 (1:500, 67288-1-Ig, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), anti-β-Catenin (1:500, #8480, Cell Signaling Technology), anti-SLAMF8 (1:500, AF4156, R&D Systems, MN, USA), anti-S100A6 (1:500, ab181975, Abcam). After washing 3 times with ddH2O, cells were incubated with Alexa Fluor 555-labeled Donkey Anti-Rabbit IgG(H + L) (1:500, A0453, Beyotime), Alexa Fluor 647-labeled Goat Anti-mouse IgG(H + L) (1:500, A0473, Beyotime) or Donkey anti-Sheep IgG (H + L) Cross-Adsorbed Secondary Antibody, Alexa Fluor™ 647 (1:500, A-21448, Thermo Fisher Scientific) for 1 h at room temperature protected from light. After stained the nuclei with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) (10 µg/mL, C1002, Beyotime) for 5 min, target proteins were observed under a fluorescence microscope (Leica, Germany). Semi-quantitative analysis was performed by ImageJ (version 1.53).

Mice femoral fracture model

Mice (C57BL/6, 12 weeks old, male) were anesthetized by 0.3% sodium pentobarbital (30 mg/kg) intraperitoneal injection. We made an incision at the lateral near the knee joint to exposed femur and patellar ligament. Patellar ligament was pushed aside and the distal thigh cortex was pierced by a 25-gauge needle. As an intramedullary needle, the needle pierced the greater trochanter, along the femur bone marrow cavity. The pin was kept at a particular length approximately 2 mm. Then an osteotomy was created at the midshaft of femur [26]. The same leg was used in each group. For in vivo study of SLAMF8 overexpression effects, mice were divided into 3 groups, blank group (BLANK), negative control (SLA-NC) group and SLAMF8 overexpression (SLA-OE) group. 20 µl PBS, 5 × 106 SLA-NC mBMSCs and 5 × 106 SLA-OE mBMSCs was injected into fracture site locally, respectively. 4 weeks after surgery, all mice were euthanized by lethal intraperitoneal injection of 0.1mL sodium pentobarbital (200 mg/mL) and femurs were harvested for next experiments. Our work has been reported in line with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0.

MicroCT analysis

After harvest, mice femurs were sent to make a microcomputed tomography (microCT) evaluation. Each femur was scanned using MILabs microCT system (Utrecht, Netherlands), and operation parameters were according to the previous report [27]. The BV/TV (bone volume fraction), Tb.Th (trabecular thickness), Tb.Sp (trabecular separation) and BMD (bone mineral density) were evaluated by three-dimensional standard microstructural analysis [28] using Imalytics Preclinical system (version 2.1.8.9) [29].

Histology, immunohistochemistry (IHC) and histological immunofluorescence analysis

After fixed by 10% paraformaldehyde for 36 h at 4 °C, femurs were decalcified by 0.5 M ethylene diaminetetra acetic acid (EDTA, Beyotime) for 7 days at 4 °C. Then, samples were embedded in paraffin and sectioned at a 5 μm thickness. After deparaffinized, the sections were stained with Hematoxylin-Eosin (H&E) staining, Safranin O/Fast Green staining and Masson’s Trichrome staining following the standard procedures. As for IHC, deparaffinized sections were repaired in Trypsin Digestion Solution, 0.1% (X1020, Solarbio, Beijing, China) at 37 °C for 1 h. Then, Immunostaining was performed using an IHC kit (ZSGB-BIO, SP-9001, Wuxi, Jiangsu, China) according to the procedure. Anti-SLAMF8 (1:500, 50623-RP02, Sino Biological US Inc.) was used to detect the SLAMF8 positive cells at the bone fracture healing area. Mayer’s Hematoxylin solution (G1080, Solarbio) was used to color the cell nucleus. As for histological immunofluorescence analysis, after deparaffinized and repaired, sections were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 at room temperature for 10 min and blocked in 2% bovine serum albumin for 1 h. Then, sections were incubated at 4 °C with primary antibodies overnight. The following primary antibodies were used: anti-SLAMF8 (1:500, AF4156, R&D Systems), anti-S100A6 (1:500, ab181975, Abcam), anti-Osteocalcin (OCN) (1:500, ab93876, Abcam) and anti-COL1A1 (1:500, 67288-1-Ig, Proteintech). After that, sections were incubated with fluorescent secondary antibody, including Donkey anti-Sheep IgG (H + L) Cross-Adsorbed Secondary Antibody, Alexa Fluor™ 647 (1:500, A-21448, Thermo Fisher Scientific), Alexa Fluor 555-labeled Donkey Anti-Rabbit IgG(H + L) (1:500, A0453, Beyotime) and Alexa Fluor 647-labeled Goat Anti-mouse IgG(H + L) (1:500, A0473, Beyotime). After stained the nuclei with DAPI (10 µg/mL, C1002, Beyotime) for 5 min, sections were observed under a fluorescence microscope (Leica, Germany).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed by GraphPad Prism (version 8.2.0, GraphPad Software, CA, United States). All experiments were conducted at least three times and the data were presented as means ± SD. Student t test was used to compared the data between 2 groups and One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Dunnett’s multiple comparisons test was used to compared the data involves multiple group comparisons. In all analyses, p < 0.05 was taken to indicate statistical significance.

Results

SLMAF8 is a fracture healing regulated gene

To identify the fracture healing relative genes, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analyses on Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets were performed. Setting a threshold as |logFC| > 2 and adj.P.Value < 0.01, 295 DEGs at GSE176086 (BMSCs treated with 100ng/mL TNF-α during osteogenic differentiation) (Fig. 1A) and 54 DEGs at GSE129165 (BMSCs treated with 50ng/mL TNF-α) (Fig. 1B) were found. Interestingly, 31 DEGs were obtained at both datasets (Fig. 1C, Supplementary Table 3). Most of them were inflammatory factors and chemokines, and SLAMF8 is one of the greatest change DEG.

To further investigated whether SLAMF8 was related to BMSCs osteogenic differentiation, Pearson correlation analyses were performed between Slamf8 and osteogenesis relative genes (Supplementary Table 4). At 968 cancer cell lines in CCLE dataset, SLAMF8 levels were significant positive correlations with both COL1A1 (R = 0.13, p < 0.001) and RUNX2 (R = 0.24, p < 0.001) (Fig. 1D). At 27 bone relative cancer cell lines in CCLE dataset, the positive correlations were stronger (SLAMF8 & COL1A1, R = 0.56, p < 0.001; SLAMF8 & RUNX2, R = 0.41, p = 0.033) (Fig. 1E). In 93 osseous and chondromatous neoplasms and miscellaneous bone tumors of TCGA project, SLAMF8 exhibited significant positive correlations with COL1A1 (R = 0.24, p = 0.019) and RUNX2 (R = 0.26, p = 0.011) (Fig. 1F). As for 17,382 normal tissues in GTEx project, SLAMF8 showed positive correlations with COL1A1 (R = 0.42, p < 0.001) and RUNX2 (R = 0.38, p < 0.001) (Fig. 1G). Moreover, we analyzed the correlation between other 2 DEGs (OAS1 and RSAD2) and osteogenesis related genes (Supplementary Table 4). The results revealed that both OSA1 and RSAD2 showed no correlation or much weaker correlation with COL1A1 and RUNX2 than that of SLAMF8 with COL1A1 and RUNX2 at CCLE, TCGA and GTEx databases. At bone relative cancer cell lines in CCLE dataset, they even shown negative correlation (Fig. S1A-H). We also analyzed the correlation between SLAMF8 and adipogenesis genes (Supplementary Table 4). Contrary to speculation, SLAMF8 exhibited positive correlations with PPARG (R = 0.40, p < 0.001) and CEBPA (R = 0.26, p < 0.001) at normal tissues in GTEx dataset (Fig. S1I).

Furthermore, GSEA were performed between the SLAMF8_High group and SLAMF8_Low group. Increased enrichment of bone development-related genes (NES = 1.300, P = 0.007, FDR = 0.007), bone mineralization-related genes (NES = 1.383, P = 0.010, FDR = 0.010), regulation of bone mineralization-related genes (NES = 1.319, P = 0.042, FDR = 0.042) and regulation of osteoblast differentiation-related genes (NES = 1.270, P = 0.048, FDR = 0.048) in SLAMF8_High group compared with SLAMF8_Low group (Fig. 1H).

To further investigate the relation between SLAMF8 and mBMSCs differentiation, we measured endogenous SLAMF8 expression during mBMSCs osteogenesis and adipogenesis. Western bolts revealed that SLAMF8 increased during mBMSCs osteogenic induction (Fig. 1I), but decreased during adipogenic induction (Fig. 1J).

The bioinformatics analyses and Western bolts above indicate that SLAMF8 probably is a fracture healing related gene.

SLAMF8 regulates mBMSCs differentiation

To verify the role of SLMAF8 at MSCs osteogenic differentiation, specific siRNAs targeting SLAMF8 was used to knock down its expression in mBMSCs. After 3 days osteogenic induction, qPCR indicated that SLA-si3 was the most efficient siRNA to knockdown SLAMF8, and osteogenesis related genes (Col1a1, Runx2, Sp7 and Alp) expression were downregulated at the same time (Fig. 2A). Thus, SLA-si3 was used at subsequent experiments. Western bolts revealed that SLAMF8 knockdown decreased the protein levels of COL1A1, RUNX2 and SP7 after 7 days osteogenic induction (Fig. 2B). Alkaline phosphate (ALP) staining was performed to assess early osteogenesis (day 7) and Alizarin Red S (ARS) staining was performed to assess calcium deposition in late osteogenesis (day 21). The ALP activity and calcium deposition were significantly decreased at SLA-si groups (Fig. 2C-D). Immunofluorescence of COL1A1 and RUNX2 indicated that SLAMF8 knockdown decreased these osteogenesis related proteins expression (Fig. 2E-F). Furthermore, we explored the role of SLAMF8 at mBMSCs adipogenesis. qPCR and WB indicated that knockdown SLAMF8 by siRNA significantly increased the expression of adipogenesis related transcription factors, including peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ) and CCAAT enhancer binding protein alpha (C/EBPα) (Fig. 2G-H). Oil Red O staining showed more lipid droplets at siRNA group compering with the control (Fig. 2I). Besides, we examined the impact of SLAMF1 knockdown on osteogenic differentiation. qPCR indicated that knockdown of SLAMF1 decreased the expression of Runx2 and Sp7, but such influence did not appear at Col1a1 (Fig. S2A).

These results demonstrate that SLAMF8 is able to manipulate mBMSCs osteogenesis and adipogenesis.

SLAMF8 manipulates mBMSCs osteogenic differentiation and adipogenic differentiation via Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway

To further explore the particular signaling pathways through which SLAMF8 regulated mBMSCs osteogenic differentiation, we performed western blots to detect the activation of the common signaling pathways that involved in osteogenesis, including p38/mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38/MAPK), extracellular signal-regulated kinase/MAPK (ERK/MAPK), phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase/AKT (PI3K/AKT), nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB/p65) and Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathways. WB indicated that the phosphorylation of p38, ERK, AKT and p65 had no significant change. Interestingly, the active-β-Catenin (non-phosphorylated β-Catenin) was significantly increased while SLAMF8 overexpression and decreased while SLAMF8 knockdown (Fig. 4A-B). Additionally, immunofluorescence analysis was performed to detect the translocation of β-Catenin after 6 h osteogenic induction of mBMSCs. At SLA-OE group, more β-Catenin translocated into nucleus during osteogenesis (Fig. 4C). Moreover, during adipogenic induction, SLA-OE increased the activation of β-Catenin, whereas the opposite effect was observed upon SLA-si (Fig. 4D-E).

S100A6 regulates the function of SLAMF8 in mBMSCs osteogenesis and adipogenesis via Wnt/β-Catenin

To examine the further mechanism of regulation of SLAMF8 in mBMSCs osteogenic differentiation, Immunoprecipitation (IP) was performed to precipitate the SLAMF8 possible interactive proteins. The silver staining of SDS-PAGE gel manifested that different expression of protein bolts between IgG group and IP group existed around 10 kDa (Fig. S3A). Next, mass spectrometry (MS) analysis indicated S100 family of Ca2+-binding protein S100A6 (also known as CABP, Cacy and Calcyclin) was the most possible protein interacting with SLAMF8 during mBMSCs osteogenic differentiation (Fig. S3B). Previous study reported S100A6 took part in MC3T3-E1 osteoblast differentiation and bone formation [30, 31]. In order to verify the function of S100A6 in SLAMF8 overexpression induced mBMSCs osteogenic differentiation, siRNAs directed against S100a6 mRNA were designed and qRNA indicated S100a6 silenced downregulated the expression of Runx2 and Col1a1 (Fig. 6A). The most efficient siRNA (S100-si2) was used at next experiments. Furthermore, we knocked down the expression of S100A6 in SLAMF8 overexpression mBMSCs during osteogenic induction. WB indicated that S100A6 knockdown significantly diminished the promotion of osteogenesis by SLAMF8 overexpression. Meanwhile, the activation of β-Catenin at SLA-OE group was also weaken by S100A6 knockdown (Fig. 6B). Results indicated that S100A6 knockdown decreased the SLAMF8 overexpression inducing-β-Catenin activation during adipogenic induction and rescued the SLAMF8 inducing-adipogenesis inhibition (Fig. 6C-D).

Discussion

In this study, we screened 2 GEO datasets, containing the gene expression profiles of BMSCs cultured with or without TNFα and identified SLAMF8 as a differentially expression gene in both datasets. Furthermore, we demonstrated that SLAMF8 positively regulates mBMSCs osteogenic and adipogenic differentiation via Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway and S100 calcium-binding protein A6 (S100A6). These finding might provide a new insight into the mechanism of fracture healing.

The signaling lymphocytic activation molecule family (SLAMF) plays important role inflammatory reactions. The classical SLAMFs (SLAMF1-7) perform their functions via 2 to 4 immunoreceptor tyrosine switch motifs (ITSMs) with the abilities to recruit SH2 domain-containing molecules, such as SAP and EAT-2. SLAMF8, also known as BLAME and CD353, lacks ITSMs and has only very short cytoplasmic tails, about 30 amino acids [32, 33]. Compared to the classical SLAMF members, the research of SLAMF8 still remains lots of gaps. Limited studies indicate that SLAMF8 negatively regulates the ROS via decreasing NADPH oxidase (Nox2) activity in macrophages [34] and downregulates the activation of macrophages via PI3K signaling pathway [35]. Knockout of SLAMF8 prevented the progression of rheumatoid arthritis in mice via inhibiting TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway [36]. Moreover, SLAMF8 plays roles in the growth of human mast cells and anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL) cells via activating Src homology region 2 domain-containing phosphatase-2 (SHP-2) [37, 38]. SLAMF1 is a classic SLAMF member, first discovered in T cells in 1995 [39] and later found to play an important role in many inflammatory cells [32]. Binding with SHP-2 via its ITSMs, SLAMF1 exerting multiple biological effects. Ample studies have revealed that the deletion of SHP-2 can lead to skeletal developmental abnormalities such as limbs, head, and chest in mice [40–43]. Overexpression of SHP-2 significantly increase the expression of RUNX2, ALP and COL1A1 during the osteogenic differentiation of rat BMSCs [44]. In addition, SHP-2 also has important impacts on cartilage formation [45] and adipogenic differentiation [46, 47]. For the reason that SLAMF8 has a similar structure to SLMAF1, several reports speculated SLAMF8 can exert its biological effects by competitively inhibiting the function of SLAMF1 [32, 35]. However, our results revealed that SLAMF1 knockdown reduce the expression of Runx2 and Sp7, which indicated that the promotion of osteogenesis by SLAMF8 may not result from competitively inhibiting SLAMF1. Moreover, Chaudhary has reported that SLAMF8 is required for the outer membrane proteins (OMPs)-mediated autophagy in macrophages. Containing a potential noncanonical LC3-interacting motif (YKDVLLVVVP), SLAMF8 mediates LC3B and p62 degradation in macrophages [48].

At MSCs, osteogenesis and adipogenesis are in delicate balance [49, 50]. Plentiful studies have revealed that RUNX2 and SP7 are the most important transcription factors for osteogenic differentiation of MSCs. Mice knockout RUNX2 disappear subperiosteal osteogenesis and endochondral osteogenesis. Additionally, RUNX2 regulates the downstream osteogenesis-related transcription factor SP7. Overexpression of SP7 increases the expression of ALP and OCN in MSCs and stimulates the formation of large mineralized nodules in pre-osteoblasts in mice [51, 52]. PPARγ, the most fat-specific among the PPAR family, is activated prior to transcription of most adipocyte genes and plays a crucial role in both the induction and maintenance of adipogenic differentiation. C/EBPα is another crucial transcription factor with the ability of inducing adipogenic differentiation even in the absence of hormonal stimulation. Moreover, Interfering with C/EBPα mRNA expression can prevent adipogenic differentiation and white adipose tissue is almost absent in C/EBPα knockout mice [53–55]. Our study indicated that knockdown SLAMF8 reduce the expression of osteogenic differentiation-related proteins (including COL1A1, RUNX2, and SP7) and increase the expression of adipogenic differentiation-related proteins PPARγ and C/EBPα in mBMSCs. Overexpression of SLAMF8 significantly reduces the expression of PPARγ and C/EBPα, and promote the expression of COL1A1, RUNX2 and SP7. Previous studies have demonstrated that PPARγ can inhibit the osteogenic differentiation of MSCs, thereby promoting the occurrence and progression of osteoporosis [56]. In skeletal aging related diseases, MSCs tend to exhibit adipogenic differentiation [57]. In our study, we revealed that SLAMF8 regulated the expression of COL1A1, RUNX2, PPARγ and C/EBPα, monitoring the delicate balance between osteogenic and adipogenic differentiation of BMSCs.

Wnt/β-catenin pathway is the main signaling pathway playing critical roles in embryonic development and adult tissue homeostasis [58]. Wnt proteins can not only bind to the co-receptors composed of Frizzled family (one of the G Protein-Coupled Receptors) and LRP family (such as LRP5, LRP6), but also bind to the transmembrane tyrosine kinase Ryk or Ror [59]. Different Wnt proteins combine with different receptors and mediate at least three different signaling pathways, the classic Wnt signaling pathway (i.e., Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway), the non-canonical Wnt signaling pathway and the Wnt/Calcium signaling pathway. In mammalian cells, Wnt binds to Frizzled/LRP activating the phosphorylation of GSK3β N-terminal serine 9. Phosphorylated GSK3β will be inactivated. However, the enzymatically active GSK3β can lead to the phosphorylation of β-Catenin, thereby triggering the ubiquitination and destabilization of β-Catenin. Further, the ubiquitinated β-Catenin will subsequently be degraded by the proteasome [60, 61]. Non-phosphorylated β-Catenin will accumulate in cytoplasm and translocate to nucleus, following by effects on downstream gene transcription [52, 62]. In the differentiation of MSCs, the activation of Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway enhances the expression of downstream osteogenic transcription factors such as RUNX2 and SP7, inhibits the expression of adipogenic transcription factors such as PPARγ and C/EBPα, thereby promoting the osteogenic differentiation of MSCs and inhibiting the adipogenic differentiation of MSCs [52, 63, 64]. Consistent with the literatures, our study suggested that overexpressing SLAMF8 significantly increased the level of non-phosphorylated β-Catenin (i.e., active β-Catenin) and promoted the nucleus transportation of β-Catenin, resulting in promotion of osteogenesis and inhibition of adipogenesis at mBMSCs. Additionally, DKK1, a high-affinity ligand for LRP6, can strongly prevent the formation of the Frizzled-LRP6 complex, thereby specifically inhibiting the activation of the Wnt signaling pathway [65]. Specifically inhibit the activation of the Wnt signaling pathway by DKK1, we noticed that the enhancement of osteogenic differentiation and the diminution of adipogenic differentiation by SLAMF8 overexpression were significantly inhibited. Therefore, we inferred that the regulatory effect of SLAMF8 on differentiation in mBMSCs is mediated through the Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway.

S100A6, also known as Calcyclin, CABP, Cacy, is a Ca2+ ion-binding protein belonging to S100 famaily. Interacting with Calcyclin-binding protein/Siah-1-interacting protein (CacyBP/SIP), S100A6 was suggested to inhibit the ubiquitination and further degradation of β-Catenin [66, 67]. Constituted by Siah1, CacyBP/SIP, the adaptor protein Skp1, and the F-box protein Ebi, the multiprotein E3 ubiquitin ligase complex induces the ubiquitination of β-Catenin and inhibits following Tcf/LEF activation [68]. Previous studies have indicated S100A6 regulated the ability of sense extracellular cations [69], the differentiation-dependent metabolism of calcium [30] and the expression of ALP [70] in MC3T3-E1 osteoblasts. Moreover, S100A6 is involved in adipocytes induced osteogenesis inhibition via canonical Wnt signaling pathway [31]. Nonetheless, several studies have reported S100A6 knockdown suppressed the proliferation and increased the osteogenic differentiation of osteosarcoma cells [71, 72]. Our results have demonstrated that S100A6 knockdown significantly inhibited the promotion of osteogenic differentiation and rescued the depression of adipogenic differentiation by SLAMF8 overexpression in mBMSCs. Meanwhile, consistent with previous studies, S100A6 knockdown impeded the activation of Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway at SLAMF8 overexpression mBMSCs.

However, several limitations still remain and require further investigation. First, we have revealed that SLAMF8 regulated the osteogenesis and adipogenesis of mBMSCs via S100A6 and Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway, but how SLAMF8 interacts with S100A6 is unclear. Second, we found that SLAMF1 knockdown inhibited the expression of Runx2 and Sp7. This finding is contradictory with the theory that SLAMF8 is competitively inhibiting SLAMF1. It is well known that SLAMF1 performs it function via binding with SHP2. However, the role of SLAMF1/SHP2 axis in mBMSCs osteogenesis is still unclear. Our further research will also start from above points to further explore the role of SLAMF1/SHP2 axis and S100A6 in the osteogenic differentiation of mBMSCs.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we have demonstrated that SLAMF8 regulates the differentiation ability of mBMSCs. SLAMF8 promotes osteogenesis and inhibits adipogenesis of mBMSCs via S100A6/Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway. Moreover, SLAMF8 overexpression mBMSCs effectively accelerate the healing of femoral fracture in mice.

To study the knowledge, attitude, and practice about Pap smear among healthcare professionals
Authors: Latika, Ridhi Suri, Pushpa Dahiya
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Papanicolaou (Pap) smear has been found to be an effective, safe, economical, and time-sensitive tool for screening cervical cancer in various studies. We studied existing levels of awareness, attitude, and practices related to the Pap smear among healthcare workers.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This was a cross-sectional study undertaken in different departments of Pt B. D. Sharma PGIMS, Rohtak. The study’s target group was all married female HCWs of varied experience levels posted in different departments. This study employed a meticulously crafted questionnaire in the English language. The survey included a total of 21 items covering different aspects related to the knowledge, attitudes, and practices of the study topic. Subgroup analysis was conducted using the ANOVA and Pearson Chi-square test.RESULTS:A total of 424 participants were enrolled in the study. The majority of the participants were in the age group of 31–40 years (45%), followed by the age group of 20–30 years. On descriptive analysis, the mean knowledge score of the participants was 29.4575 (SD = 10.77661, minimum score = 0.00, maximum score = 55).CONCLUSION:The study highlights a moderate level of knowledge but a significant gap between the attitudes and actual practices related to Pap smear and cervical cancer screening among healthcare professionals.

Introduction

The high rates of cervical cancer incidence and mortality persist despite the widespread availability of effective screening techniques such as the Papanicolaou (Pap) smear. Recognized for its efficacy, safety, cost-effectiveness, and sensitivity, the Pap smear is a cornerstone in the early detection and management of cervical precancerous conditions, playing a critical role in decreasing both incidence and mortality from cervical cancer.[12] The underuse of such screening programs is often linked to insufficient awareness and entrenched misconceptions within both the medical community and wider society.[3] The success of the Pap smear in reducing cervical cancer rates critically depends on healthcare professionals’ understanding, attitudes, and implementation of screening protocols.[4] There is evidence of significant gaps in healthcare professionals’ knowledge about and attitudes toward cervical cancer prevention guidelines, risk identification, and the critical nature of Pap smear testing, which in turn affects their ability to endorse and carry out these screenings.[56]

Highlighting the need for ongoing educational and training initiatives, this study was designed to assess the current awareness, attitudes, and practices about the Pap smear among healthcare workers. By pinpointing and addressing the prevalent misunderstandings and knowledge deficiencies, the aim was to provide key insights that will inform the development of specific interventions and educational efforts to boost Pap smear screening rates and bolster the battle against cervical cancer.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

A cross-sectional analysis was conducted across various departments at PGIMS, Rohtak.

Study participants and sampling

The study’s target group was all married female HCWs of varied experience levels posted in different departments (n = 450).

Data collection tools and technique

This study employed a meticulously crafted questionnaire in the English language. The questionnaire was divided into two sections. The initial segment of the study centered on gathering data regarding the sociodemographic profile of the participants. The subsequent section contained statements aimed at evaluating their knowledge, attitude, and practices pertaining to various aspects related to Pap smear. The validity of the study tool was evaluated by pilot testing with a group of ten specialists, and subsequent changes were made depending on the results. The survey included a total of 21 items covering different aspects related to the knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) of the study topic. The tool had 11 questions related to the knowledge aspect, two statements related to attitude, and eight statements related to the practices of HCWs about Pap smear. These questions were scored, and the correct answer was awarded a score of 5 and the wrong answer was not penalized. Before administering the questionnaire, respondents provided informed consent. The research excluded individuals who did not provide their consent and experts who participated in the questionnaire pretesting. Subgroup analysis was conducted using the ANOVA and Pearson Chi-square test.

Results

The survey attracted responses from 424 individuals. The response rate of the participants in this study was 94%. A significant portion, 45%, was aged between 31 and 40 years, with the next largest group, 38%, falling within the 20–30 years age bracket. Those aged 41–50 years represented 10%, and participants over 50 years accounted for 6%. The demographic breakdown showed that 85% of respondents hailed from urban areas, while the remaining 15% came from rural locales. Regarding marital age, 95% were married between the ages of 20 and 30 years, with the remaining 5% marrying between the ages of 31 and 40 years. The majority of the participants (48%) had two children, followed by 30% of participants with one child, 16% of participants with no child, and 6% of participants with three or more children [Table 1]. Furthermore, 60% of the study participants responded that they were using contraception, followed by 35% of participants who denied the use of contraception. In addition, 5% of participants stated that contraception does not apply to them. In elaboration about the type of contraceptive used, it was revealed that most of the participants (29%) were using barrier contraception, 26% were not using any contraception, 24% were using IUCD, 6% were using contraceptive pills, and 13% of the participants were using some other contraceptive methods. On descriptive analysis, the mean knowledge score of the participants was 29.4575 (SD = 10.77661, minimum score = 0.00, maximum score = 55). On further subgroup analysis of the mean knowledge score with different sociodemographic parameters such as age group, marital status, place of residence, religion, and age at the time of marriage, it was revealed that the mean knowledge score was the highest among the participants in the 31–40 years age group, followed by participants in the 20–30 years age group. However, the difference in mean knowledge was not statistically significant (F value = 1.091, P value = 0.353). The unmarried participants had slightly higher mean knowledge than their married counterparts (t value = 0.039, P value = 0.843). Similarly, the difference in the mean knowledge of participants with an urban background was significantly higher than that of participants with a rural background (t value = 8.780, P value = 0.003). The sociodemographic variable, that is, age at the time of marriage, had no significant impact on the mean knowledge score (F value = 2.716, P value = 0.068) [Table 2]. On individual question-wise analysis, it was revealed that the majority of the participants (65%) were aware of the importance of cervical cancer screening, 64% were aware of the various risk factors leading to cervical cancer, 84% were aware of different methods of cervical cancer screening, 54% of participants were aware of the common symptoms of cervical cancer, 63% were aware of the causative organism responsible for cervical cancer, 76% were aware of vaccinations for the prevention of cervical cancer, and 56% were aware of doses of vaccination. However, only 33% of participants were aware of the frequency of Pap smears done routinely after 30 years of age, 28% were aware of the time when screening for cancer should be started, and 24% were aware of the age of starting vaccination for carcinoma cervix [Table 3]. On analysis of attitude-based statements, it was revealed that the majority of the participants (91%) were aware of the importance of regular cervical cancer screening. Furthermore, 87% of the participants stated that they would recommend cervical screening to their family and friends [Table 4]. The Pearson Chi-square test was used to see the association of attitude-based statements with different sociodemographic variables. It was discovered that participants’ opinions on how important it is to get regular cervical screening were statistically linked to where they lived (Chi-square = 5.039, df = 1, P value = 0.025). Similarly, the attitude statement regarding the recommendation of cervical screening to family and friends had a statistically significant association with the religion of the participants (Chi-square = 11.212, df = 3, P value = 0.011). The association of attitude statements with other sociodemographic variables was not statistically significant [Table 5]. On elucidating the different practices of the participants, it was revealed that the majority of the participants (89%) were not vaccinated against cervical cancer, and 26% stated that they had not taken the cervical cancer vaccine because nobody in their family or circle of friends had received it, and 23% of respondents said they were skeptical about the concept of cervix cancer vaccination because it was new. Furthermore, 82% of the participants stated that they had never gotten their Pap smear done, and the majority of them (50%) stated that they did not feel like getting a Pap smear as they felt completely healthy and had no symptoms. Among the participants who had undergone Pap smear examinations, they stated that they had a single Pap smear examination, and the most common reason cited for Pap smear examination was their awareness of the fact that Pap smear examination should be done yearly after 30 years of age. On enquiring about the report of the Pap smear, 11% of the participants showed ignorance about the report, and a similar number of participants (11%) stated the Pap smear reflected an inflammatory smear. The association of different practice statements with sociodemographic variables in health professionals was assessed by applying the Pearson Chi-square test. It was observed that the response of participants to vaccination against cervical cancer had a significant association with the age group of the participants (Chi-square = 18.385, df = 6, P value = 0.005), and the reasons for not getting vaccination showed a significant association with the age of marriage of the participant (Chi-square = 19.465, df = 10, P value = 0.035). The response to the practice statement that the participants had ever undergone a Pap smear showed a significant association with the age group of the participants (Chi-square = 15.128, df = 6, P value = 0.019), and similarly, the statement regarding the number of times of undergoing cervical cancer screening also showed a significant association with the age group of participants (Chi-square = 29.399, df = 12, P value = 0.003) and with age at the time of marriage (Chi-square = 36.270, df = 8, P value = 0.001). The response of participants regarding the interval between more than one Pap smear showed a significant association with their marital status (Chi-square = 16.906, df = 4, P value = 0.002), and the reasons furnished for undergoing a Pap smear showed a significant association with their marital status (Chi-square = 13.853, df = 5, P value = 0.017) and age at the time of their marriage (Chi-square = 36.908, df = 10, P value = 0.001). The practice statement regarding remembering the report of the Pap smear showed a significant association with the age at the time of marriage of the participant (Chi-square = 27.044, df = 8, P value = 0.001) [Table 6].

Discussion

The study’s findings provide a nuanced understanding of the KAP concerning Pap smear among healthcare professionals, revealing critical insights into the preventive measures for cervical cancer in a tertiary care medical college hospital setting. The mean knowledge score about Pap smear and cervical cancer screening was moderate, indicating a potential gap in awareness or education among healthcare professionals. This finding is consistent with the study by Akinyemiju et al.,[7] which reported a similar trend in the knowledge level of healthcare providers about cervical cancer screening methods. The lack of statistical significance in knowledge scores across different sociodemographic variables suggests a uniformly distributed awareness level, irrespective of age, marital status, or place of residence, aligning with findings from Ajenifuja et al.[8] but contrasts with studies such as Mkandawire et al.,[9] which found significant differences in knowledge based on demography factors. The high percentage of participants acknowledging the importance of regular cervical cancer screening highlights a positive attitude toward preventive healthcare. This is in line with the study by Hoque et al.,[10] which found a strong recognition of the importance of cervical cancer screening among healthcare workers. However, the actual recommendation of cervical screening to family and friends being influenced by the participant’s religion, as indicated by the statistically significant association, might point toward cultural or religious beliefs impacting health promotion behavior, a finding that echoes the research conducted by Ezechi et al.[11]

The low percentage of participants who have undergone Pap smear tests or received vaccinations against cervical cancer is concerning, especially considering their professional background. This mirrors the findings of Al-Naggar et al.,[12] where a significant portion of healthcare providers had never undergone a Pap smear test. The significant association of Pap smear practices with age and marital status suggests that personal factors may influence the decision to undergo screening, supporting the observations made by Williams et al.[13]

Limitations and recommendation

As with any survey-based study, the self-reported nature of the data might lead to response bias. The findings are based on a single tertiary care center, which might limit the generalizability of the results to other settings or regions.

Conclusions

The study highlights a moderate level of knowledge but a significant gap between the attitudes and actual practices related to Pap smear and cervical cancer screening among healthcare professionals. Despite recognizing the importance of regular screening, a substantial proportion of participants have not undergone Pap smear tests or vaccinations, indicating barriers that need to be understood and addressed.

The study covers a wide range of healthcare professionals across different age groups and sociodemographic backgrounds, providing a broad perspective on the KAP toward Pap smear. The use of various statistical methods to correlate knowledge, attitudes, and practices with sociodemographic factors offers an in-depth understanding of the underlying trends.

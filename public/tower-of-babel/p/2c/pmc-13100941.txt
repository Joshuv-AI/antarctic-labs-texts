Development and characterization of near‐isogenic lines for quantitative trait locus qDT.3B.1 underlying drought tolerance in wheat
Authors: Joanne D. Caguiat, Md Sultan Mia, Hui Liu, Guijun Yan, Kadambot H. M. Siddique
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDrought is a critical challenge to wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) productivity. The quantitative complexity of drought tolerance makes breeding for this trait difficult. Developing near‐isogenic lines (NILs) targeting quantitative trait locus (QTL) for 1000‐grain weight (TGW) under drought stress can effectively identify key mechanisms and genes. Using the heterogeneous inbred family method and a fast‐generation cycling system, we developed wheat NILs targeting TGW QTL on chromosome 3B and characterized them under drought and well‐watered conditions. Genotyping the NILs using 90K single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) array, we narrowed down the targeted QTL region and identified four SNP markers near the target QTL linked with six candidate genes. These candidate genes encode beta‐glucuronosyltransferase, ABC transporter, transmembrane protein, F‐box protein, cytochrome P450, and alanine–tRNA ligase associated with functions related to responses under drought stress. In silico expression analysis revealed that all these candidate genes were highly expressed in root and grain tissues under drought stress and involved in pathways such as ubiquitin‐mediated proteolysis, metabolic pathways and biosynthesis of various N‐glycan, steroids, and secondary metabolites emphasizing its potential role affecting grain weight under drought stress. This study offers insights into markers and genes controlling TGW under drought and has potential for advancing the development of high‐yielding resilient wheat cultivars.

INTRODUCTION

Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) is a globally significant staple crop, essential for food security. However, increasing population pressures and urbanization, coupled with the adverse effects of climate change, threaten wheat production (Anwar et al., 2020). Rising temperatures and prolonged dry periods due to climate change are expected to impact crop yields significantly (Hill et al., 2021), particularly in arid and semiarid regions (Silva et al., 2019). Drought stress, especially during anthesis and grain filling, significantly decreases biomass accumulation and shortens the grain‐filling duration, reducing grain weight and yield (Senapati et al., 2019). On a global scale, drought during critical growth stages can reduce wheat yields by up to 35% (Gupta et al., 2020). Developing drought‐tolerant varieties is thus crucial to mitigate these impacts (Atlin et al., 2017; Pixley et al., 2023). Identifying molecular markers and candidate genes linked to associated traits like yield and 1000‐grain weight (TGW) under drought stress is a promising strategy for breeding improved cultivars.

High‐density markers such as single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are widely used in genetic studies, including phylogenetic linkages, diversity analysis, marker‐assisted selection, genomic selection, genome‐wide association studies, and quantitative trait locus (QTL) mapping (Huang et al., 2023; Rimbert et al., 2018). The 90K SNP array (S. Wang et al., 2014) was proven effective for characterizing genetic resources and distinguishing closely related lines (Rimbert et al., 2018). Numerous studies have identified candidate genes and potential SNP markers associated with plant height, root traits, yield stability and yield‐related traits (Halder et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2023; Muhu‐Din Ahmed et al., 2020; Nouraei et al., 2024; Reddy et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2021), but the complexity of drought tolerance and the requirement for comprehensive life‐cycle evaluations have limited the identification of drought‐specific markers (Mao et al., 2023). Further research is needed to identify loci contributing to yield stability under drought conditions.

Several studies have identified QTLs, candidate genes, and marker–trait associations for drought tolerance in wheat. For example, a comprehensive QTL analysis using 167 recombinant inbred lines identified genomic regions linked to heat and drought stress, including QTLs for leaf rolling, plant height, chlorophyll content (SPAD [Soil Plant Analysis Development] value), and yield (Tahmasebi et al., 2017). A major QTL for days to heading that explained 19.6% of yield variation under drought, heat, and combined stress conditions was also identified. This QTL, linked to marker 7D‐acc/cat‐10, may be useful for marker‐assisted selection to develop heat‐ and drought‐tolerant varieties (Tahmasebi et al., 2017). Using doubled haploid population, a stable QTL for grains per spike, grain weight per spike, and spike number in the marker interval Hair–Xpsp2999 was identified on chromosome 1A, along with yield‐related QTLs explaining 27.2%–31.7% of total variation on chromosomes 6A, 6B, and 6D (Heidari et al., 2011). A major QTL for grain yield under drought stress that explained 11.39% of phenotypic variation was co‐localized with QTLs for yield of and yield stability index on chromosome 3B (Salarpour et al., 2021). Traits such as relative water content, heading date, awn length, and spikelets per spike have been reported as reliable selection criteria for improving drought tolerance in wheat (Sobhaninan et al., 2019). Among yield components, TGW is considered an important adaptive trait under drought, heat, and combined stress conditions, which could be used as selection criteria in these environments (Tahmasebi et al., 2014). Chromosome 3B has emerged as a key region for drought tolerance traits. For instance, a significant QTL for TGW on chromosome 3B within the gwm389 (6.69 megabase [Mb]) and agg/cat‐3 (17.3 Mb) intervals was identified (Pinto et al., 2010). Major colocated QTLs QTgw.cib‐3B.1 and QGw.cib‐3B were associated with TGW improvement (Liao et al., 2024). Similarly, Shukla et al. (2015) identified qGYWD.3B.1, a novel region on chromosome 3B with 18.7% phenotypic variance explained (PVE) under drought stress, colocated with other yield‐related traits. The meta‐analysis study identified chromosomes 3B and 5B, which harbor significant meta‐QTLs (MQTLs), as the most advantageous for enhancing growth and yield of wheat under drought conditions (Ismail & Nadarajah, 2024). In durum wheat (Triticum durum Desf.), a major QTL for yield under rainfed and control conditions was detected on chromosomes 2B and 3B (Graziani et al., 2014). In addition, colocations of TGW and spike number on chromosome 3B were also reported (Golabadi et al., 2011). Moreover, QTL for TGW, thickness of grains, and yield was found on chromosome 3B (Maphosa et al., 2014) which may be similarly associated with yield (Bennett et al., 2012; Bonneau et al., 2013). These studies underscore the importance of chromosome 3B in drought‐related trait improvement. Despite these advances, genetic mechanisms controlling yield and TGW under drought stress in wheat remains complex necessitating further studies on fine mapping and identification of candidate genes and stable QTL hotspots for improving yield under drought stress in wheat.

In this study, we developed and characterized near‐isogenic lines (NILs) targeting the QTL for TGW on chromosome 3B under drought stress conditions. Through the combined use of 90K SNP genotyping platform and phenotype data, we identified four SNP markers near the targeted QTL region, associated with functions relevant to TGW under drought stress. Six candidate genes were identified and analyzed using the Chinese Spring wheat genome database. Furthermore, in silico analysis of these candidate genes was performed using WheatOmics (S. Ma et al., 2021) and STRING (Szklarczyk et al., 2023). The findings in this study contribute valuable insights into functions and mechanisms controlling TGW under drought stress useful for marker‐assisted selection, QTL analysis, and fine mapping, supporting the development of high yielding drought‐tolerant wheat varieties.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Plant materials

Two spring wheat varieties, Babax (BOW/NAC//VEE/3/BJY/COC) and Dharwar Dry, were crossed to produce F1 and subsequent generations for this study. Babax is a semidwarf spring wheat variety from CIMMYT, known for its high yield potential and exceptional drought tolerance, while Dharwar Dry is a local tall Indian spring wheat variety well known for growing under reduced soil moisture (Mcintyre et al., 2010; Olivares‐Villegas & Reynolds, 2007; Pinto et al., 2010; Kirigwi et al., 2007). They have highly contrasting yield and TGW under both well‐watered and stressed condition as reported in Mia et al. (2017). The NILs were generated using the heterogeneous inbred family (HIF) analysis method (Tuinstra et al., 1997) coupled with an embryo‐culture‐based fast‐generation cycling system (FGCS) (G. Yan et al., 2017; Zheng et al., 2013), targeting the qDT.3B.1 on chromosome 3BS (6.69‐17.3 cM) flanked by the markers gwm389 and agg/cat‐3 (Pinto et al., 2010).

HIF method for NIL development

To develop NIL, the single seed descent method was adopted to advance from F2 to F4 generation. From F4, the gwm389 marker associated with TGW on chromosome 3B (Pinto et al., 2010) was used for genotyping and selecting heterozygous plants at the target locus and continued until F7. Selfed F7 with >90% recipient genome recovery were advanced to F8, where homozygous lines with contrasting marker alleles were selected as putative NIL pairs for phenotypic evaluation.

Embryo culture–based FGCS

Generation advancement from F3 to F7 was done through embryo culture–based FGCS according to G. Yan et al. (2017). Briefly, young embryos (approximately 12–14 days after anthesis) were collected and cultured under sterile conditions and incubated in the dark to promote germination. Germinated seedlings were transferred to a 22°C growth room with 16‐h light to induce rooting. Seedlings with roots ≥2.0 cm were transferred to trays with growth media in plant growth chambers until grains matured and ready for the next generation advancement. These NILs are genetically uniform except for the target locus.

Drought tolerance evaluation

The NILs were evaluated for drought tolerance at The University of Western Australia (UWA) glasshouse facility. Five NIL pairs composed of 10 genotypes (Table 1) were sown in cylindrical plastic columns (2.5 cm depth, 9 cm inner diameter, and 50 cm height) filled with 2.5 kg of an air‐dried soil mixture (5:2:3 fine composted pine bark, cocopeat, and brown river sand; pH 6.0; and 2.5 cm of gravel at the base) (Mia et al., 2019). Three evenly germinated seedlings were planted for each pot and thinned out after establishment leaving only a single plant per pot. The soil water‐holding capacity in the pots was determined using methods described elsewhere (Mia et al., 2019; Turner, 2019). Soil water content was computed as soil water content (%)=Freshweight−dryweightDryweight×100 (Shemi et al., 2021). A randomized complete block design with three replications for each genotype was used. Pots were maintained at 80% field capacity (FC) until anthesis (Zadoks et al., 1974). Two treatments were applied during anthesis: (1) well‐watered control (soil moisture maintained at 80% FC until physiological maturity (Z91) and (2) drought stress (water withheld for 7 days from anthesis, followed by maintenance at 80% FC until maturity). Pot positions were rearranged weekly to ensure uniform light and airflow (Fang et al., 2010). At maturity, plants were harvested, and the grain was threshed and dried at 35°C for 72 h. Grain yield (g plant−1) was determined by weighing the total grains harvested from each plant while TGW was calculated by weighing 100 grains from each replication per NIL and multiplying the value by 10 to obtain the equivalent 1000 grains. A paired two sample t‐test at significance level of 0.05 was done to compare the differences between the isolines in each NIL pairs.

DNA extraction and 90K SNP genotyping

DNA was extracted from young leaves (2‐week old) of each NIL pair using the CTAB (cetyltrimethylammonium bromide) method (Murray & Thompson, 1980). Genotyping was performed using the 90K SNP Illumina iSelect array containing 81,587 SNPs (S. Wang et al., 2014). GenomeStudio 2.0 software was used for SNP clustering and genotype calling (Illumina, https://www.illumina.com). Raw SNP data were filtered by removing SNPs with call frequencies of <0.8 (i.e., >20% missing data points or monomorphic SNPs), <0.05 minor allele frequencies, and > 0.25 heterozygosity.

Selection of SNP markers for drought tolerance

Genotypic data were processed by filtering raw SNP data using reference SNP chromosomal positions (International Wheat Genome Sequencing Consortium, 2014). The filtered SNPs were classified into tolerant and susceptible groups based on corresponding phenotypic data. SNPs on chromosome 3B with genotype‒phenotype associations exceeding 50% were selected for further analysis. The graphical genotype of NILs showing the background similarity and QTL region differences was generated by GGT2.0 (van Berloo, 2008) and the genetic map of selected SNPs was created through MapChart (Voorrips, 2002).

Candidate gene identification and annotation

Sequences of selected SNPs were searched against the TriticeaeToolbox (T3) database (https://wheat.triticeaetoolbox.org/wheat/) and blasted against the wheat reference genome RefSeq v2.1 to determine their physical positions and lengths (International Wheat Genome Sequencing Consortium, 2014). A BLASTN (basic search) was conducted on the flanking genes and SNPs against the IWGSC (International Wheat Genome Sequencing Consortium) Chinese Spring genome database (https://urgi.versailles.inra.fr/jbrowseiwgsc/gmod_jbrowse/) via IWGSC Annotation v2.1. Candidate genes associated with the TGW QTL (gwm389‐agg/cat‐3) (Pinto et al., 2010) were identified. Their molecular and biological functions were further explored using resources such as GrainGenes (https://wheat.pw.usda.gov/GG3/), Ensembl Plants (http://plants.ensembl.org), National Centre for Biotechnology Information (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), WheatOmics 1.0 (http://wheatomics.sdau.edu.cn/), and IWGSC Annotation v2.1 references.

Candidate gene expression in silico

Candidate genes were further analyzed using WheatOmics (http://wheatomics.sdau.edu.cn/) to evaluate gene expression based on the Hexaploid Wheat Expression Database (IWGSC Annotation v1.1) in root, leaf, and grain tissues under drought stress and control conditions in resistant (Atay85) and susceptible (Zubkov) wheat cultivars (S. Ma et al., 2021). Protein–protein interaction network of selected candidate genes was created through STRING VERSION 11.0 (https://string‐db.org/) to generate and visualize the network interaction of selected genes (Szklarczyk et al., 2023). The STRING network was set based on default settings, that is, 0.4 confidence score. Visualization of data and graphs were generated using SRplot (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/SRplot) (Tang et al., 2023).

RESULTS

Performance of NILs under well‐watered and drought‐stressed conditions

Using the HIF method, five heterozygous lines were identified in the F7 generation each with two contrasting genotypes homozygous to the alleles of molecular marker targeting the TGW QTL on chromosome 3B (Pinto et al., 2010). At F8, these five putative NIL pairs were evaluated for drought tolerance screening. Under well‐watered conditions, grain yield (g plant−1) did not significantly differ for NIL pairs 2, 3, and 5. However, NILs 1 and 4 exhibited significant differences at p ≤ 0.05 and p ≤ 0.01, respectively. Under drought stress, grain yield (g plant−1) differed significantly in NILs 1, 2, and 5 at p ≤ 0.05 and NILs 3 and 4 at p ≤ 0.01 (Table 2). For TGW under well‐watered conditions, NILs 2 and 5 significantly differed at p ≤ 0.05, NIL 3 differed at p ≤ 0.01, and NILs 1 and 4 did not significantly differ. Under drought stress, TGW of NIL 3 significantly differed at p ≤ 0.01, NILs 1 and 5 differed at p ≤ 0.05, and NILs 2 and 4 did not significantly differ.

Among the NILs, NIL 5 (+) demonstrated the highest grain yield (g plant−1), producing 9.86 g under well‐watered conditions and 7.47 g under drought stress. Genotypes carrying positive alleles (+) outperformed those with negative alleles (−) across both conditions (Table 2). Genotypes with positive alleles (tolerant) had consistently higher yields than those with negative alleles (susceptible). TGW variations among genotype pairs revealed that NILs 1, 3, and 5 had relatively small differences between tolerant (NIL+) and susceptible (NIL−) genotypes, whereas NILs 2 and 4 showed larger disparities. NIL 4 are not confirmed NILs due to its nonsignificance in TGW in both conditions while NIL pairs 1, 2, 3, and 5 are confirmed NILs; thus, these four NIL pairs were prioritized for candidate gene analysis.

Identification of SNP markers

Using the 90K iSelect genotyping array, a total of 31,821 SNPs were identified after filtering based on selection criteria, these were distributed across all 21 wheat chromosomes and were retained for further analysis. Among these, 34 SNPs demonstrated consistent genotype‒phenotype associations between resistant and susceptible isolines in at least three of four NIL pairs (Table S1). These SNPs were distributed across chromosomes 1A (2), 2B (1), 2D (1), 3A (2), 3B (17), 4A (1), 3D (1), 5B (1), 6A (3), and unknown (5). Notably, 17 SNPs on chromosome 3B exhibited consistent genotype‒phenotype associations across at least three NIL pairs (Table 3). The genetic map of 17 SNP markers showing the similarity of the background and the differences in the QTL region was shown in Figure S1. SNP markers showing consistent associations in two or more NIL pairs are reliable for marker selection (G. Liu et al., 2023). Therefore, the sequences of these 17 SNP markers were compared against wheat reference genome sequences (IWGSC v2.1) and GrainGenes databases to determine their positions.

Candidate genes associated with selected SNPs

A total of 259 candidate genes were identified on chromosome 3B in association with 17 SNP markers (Table S2). Among these (Figure 1), four SNP markers near the target TGW QTL on 3B were used to identify potential candidate genes, which were further analyzed using the wheat genome Chinese Spring v2.1 databases (Figure 2). Based on associated SNP markers, six candidate genes were identified linked with TGW under drought stress.

Candidate genes from each selected single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) marker near the targeted quantitative trait locus (QTL) for 1000‐grain weight on chromosome 3B.

The SNP marker Tdurum_contig10608_1081 is linked with two candidate genes TraesCS3B03G0041000 (overlapped with the marker) and TraesCS3B03G0041100 (7.9 kb away) (Figure 2; Table 4). TraesCS3B03G0041000 encodes beta‐glucuronosyltransferase involved in glucuronosyltransferase and glycosyltransferase activity while TraesCS3B03G0041100 encodes ABC transporter G family member involved in ATP binding, ATPase and ABC‐type transporter activity (Figure 2; Table 4). The SNP marker BS00100045_51 is associated with three candidate genes: TraesCS3B03G0042000 encodes transmembrane protein; TraesCS3B03G0042100 encodes F‐box protein involved in protein and carbohydrate binding; and TraesCS3B03G0042400 encodes Cytochrome P450 involved in monooxygenase activity, iron ion binding, oxidoreductase, and monooxygenase activity (Figure 2; Table 4). Finally, the alanine–tRNA ligase encoding gene TraesCS3B03G0047300 (11 kb away) is associated with the SNP marker Excalibur_rep_c104237_78. It is involved in nucleotide, ATP, nucleic acid binding, and in alanine‐tRNA ligase activity (Figure 2; Table 4).

In silico expression of the candidate genes

To further explore the potential role of the selected candidate genes linked with each SNP marker, in silico gene expression analysis was conducted using WheatOmics. The analysis assessed gene expression in root, leaf, and grain tissues under drought stress in the Atay85 (resistant) variety and Zubkov (susceptible) varieties. This approach provided additional insights into the potential involvement of these candidate genes in various phenotypic traits under drought stress.

All selected six candidate genes (TraesCS3B03G0047300, TraesCS3B03G0041000, TraesCS3B03G0042000, TraesCS3B03G0042100, TraesCS3B03G0041100, and TraesCS3B03G0042400) showed greater expression in root and grain than in leaf tissue in resistant variety (Figure 3). The candidate gene TraesCS3B03G0047300 showed the highest expression (roots and grains) among the candidate genes followed by TraesCS3B03G0041000. DISCUSSION

Breeding drought‐tolerant crops is essential to alleviate drought stress challenges exacerbated by climate change and to ensure sustainable agricultural production. Alongside breeding efforts, identifying molecular markers, and discovering candidate genes responsible for specific traits will expedite the development of high yielding drought‐tolerant crop varieties. In this study, NILs with targeted QTL for TGW on chromosome 3B were developed using a combination of FGCS, HIF selection, and marker‐assisted selection. The region containing the TGW QTL on chromosome 3B was narrowed by identifying four SNP markers near the target region, which were closely linked with six potential candidate genes. These genes encode proteins involved in growth, plant development, and stress response, which are critical for grain yield and TGW formation under drought stress. The insights gained from this study will aid in fine‐mapping and marker‐assisted selection for improving drought tolerance in wheat.

Variations in the phenotypic performance of NIL pairs

The five NIL pairs developed and characterized in this study were used to identify important QTLs for TGW and yield under drought stress. Despite originating from the same parents, the NIL pairs exhibited significant differences in yield and TWG within each pair. The HIF approach created distinct genetic backgrounds, capturing a broader genetic variation for each pair, as they were derived from individual F2 plants through single‐seed descent (Mia et al., 2019). This strategy introduced genetic diversity among the NILs, facilitating the selection of desirable traits such as yield, pest and disease resistance, grain quality, and adaptation to changing climatic conditions (Singh et al., 2023). The NILs carrying the positive alleles exhibited smaller reductions in these traits compared with NILs having the negative alleles. The ability of these tolerant NILs to maintain higher grain yield and TGW under drought might be due to the positive alleles that contributed to the improved grain filling capacity. Therefore, selection of genotypes that maintain higher TGW under drought is a good strategy in breeding drought tolerant wheat.

Several studies using Babax as one of the parent lines in developing biparental mapping population have been citing its value and limitations in studying drought and heat tolerance in wheat. Compared with Seri M82, Babax suffers significant yield losses under heat, drought, and combined stress conditions, and strong genotype × environment interactions limits the accumulation of desirable traits in a single genotype (Tahmasebi et al., 2014). Although Babax contributes favorable alleles for cooler canopy temperature, chlorophyll content (SPAD), grains per spike, 1000‐grain yield, and grain yield, there is a large variation in QTL expression across environments, and genetic linkages were relatively weak in crosses with Seri M82 (Pinto et al., 2010; Sobhaninan et al., 2019; Tahmasebi et al., 2017) However, in this present study, we used the cross Babax × Dharwar Dry combining the contrasting drought‐adaptive traits of these two parent lines. The Babax × Dharwar Dry crosses showed better grain yield performance than other cross combinations under drought stress (Mia et al., 2017). In addition, this cross combination exhibited high specific combining ability under drought stress signifying its suitability in breeding for drought tolerance (Mia et al., 2017).

Genomic region of targeted QTL overlapping with reported QTL for drought‐related traits

The NILs were developed using the HIF method and FGCS targeting the qDT.3B.1 TGW QTL on chromosome 3B, with parent lines Babax and Dharwar Dry. The marker gwm389 at 6.69 M bp on chromosome 3B (Pinto et al., 2010) was employed in marker‐assisted selection to distinguish plants with tolerant and susceptible alleles. In this study, we identified four SNP markers delimiting the targeted region located at 6.5–13.9 Mb. A QTL for grain yield on chromosome 3B (qGYWD.3B.3) under drought‐stressed condition, which explained 21.8% of phenotypic variance, was identified using the flanking markers gwm389‐gwm533 (Shukla et al., 2015). They also discovered novel QTL qGYWD.3B.1 (18.7% PVE) and qGYWD.3B.2 (19.6% PVE), for grain yield under drought‐stressed condition. Additionally, a novel QTL, QTgw.cib‐3B.1/QGw.cib‐3Bv, associated with TGW and grain width, located between 505.29 and 507.14 Mb on chromosome 3B was identified (Liao et al., 2024). Co‐localization of QTL for yield and yield‐related traits (leaf green area, grain filling duration, biomass, grain yield, harvest index, 1000‐kernel weight, and kernel number/spike) was also observed on chromosome 3B (Edae et al., 2014; Qaseem et al., 2018). Some QTLs for TGW were also found in other chromosome regions. For example, major QTLs for several drought tolerance indices for TGW were identified including tolerance index‐TGW on chromosomes 1D, 5A, and 3D, and yield index‐TGW on chromosome 6B (Salarpour et al., 2021). A QTL for grain weight per spike found on chromosome 1A (Heidari et al., 2011) while QTL for TGW was distributed on chromosomes 1B, 2A, 3B, 4A, 4D, 6A, 6D, and 7D (Tahmasebi et al., 2017).

One of the challenges of traditional QTL mapping is its dependence on genetic background and population structure. To address this limitation, MQTL analysis can be used to identify reliable and robust genomic hotspots allowing more precise QTL identification and molecular markers development for wheat breeding programs. A comparative MQTL analysis identified major co‐localization of yield QTL to zinc, iron, and protein content of wheat grain (Shariatipour, Heidari, Tahmasebi, et al., 2021). Their comparative analysis with other important close cereal crops identified major genomic regions and candidate genes controlling yield and quality traits in wheat. MQTL analysis enabled the refinement of pinpointing genomic regions leading to accurate identification of molecular markers and candidate genes to be used in marker‐assisted selection (Shariatipour, Heidari, Tahmasebi, et al., 2021). In another study, MQTLs with narrower confidence interval of grain zinc and iron content in wheat were identified. These QTLs were highly colocated and linked with candidate genes associated with Fe and Zn homeostasis (Shariatipour, Heidari, & Richards, 2021). MQTL analysis facilitates the gene identification and narrowing down genomic regions for yield‐ and quality‐related traits. Although the result in this study provides useful insights on discovered markers and candidate genes, integrating MQTL analysis in future studies will provide a comprehensive information on genome hotspots identification and marker development to be used in wheat breeding.

The practical application of QTL mapping lies in the identification of reliable molecular markers that can be effectively used in marker‐assisted selection for crop improvement. One study provides an excellent example of translating QTL discovery into marker‐assisted selection application. Using restriction site‐associated DNA (RAD)‐seq based association analysis, a SNP marker RsBv1 associated with Rhizoctonia resistance in sugar beet was identified (Ravi et al., 2021). This marker was validated through multiple molecular approaches using diverse germplasm and exhibited an accurate differentiation of resistant and susceptible genotypes. By developing a cost‐effective SNP assay, they provided a good model for connecting the molecular research outputs into practical breeding programs (Ravi et al., 2021). The identified SNP markers in this study can be further validated and converted into kompetitive allele specific PCR assays for high throughput genotyping.

Putative candidate genes

Six candidate genes linked with the four SNP markers on chromosome 3B were identified. These genes encode beta‐glucuronosyltransferase, ABC transporter G family member, transmembrane protein, F‐box protein PP2, Cytochrome P450, and alanine–tRNA ligase that are associated with TGW under drought stress conditions.

Candidate gene TraesCS3B03G0041000 encodes beta‐glucuronosyltransferase involved in glycosyltransferase activity. Glycosyltransferases facilitate the transfer of activated sugars to acceptor molecules thereby regulating the stability and bioactivity of secondary metabolites, lipids, and proteins (Le Roy et al., 2016; Ross et al., 2001; Williams & Thorson, 2008). A QTL GSA1 encoding UDP‐glucosyltransferase enhances abiotic stress tolerance and regulates grain size and weight through redirection of metabolic flux resulting to the accumulation of flavonoid glycosides thus reducing the effect of abiotic stress (Dong et al., 2020). Studies in rice (Oryza sativa L.) showed that UDP‐glycosyltransferase (UGTs) genes (OsUGTs) were highly expressed in seeds, correlated with grain size, and it is involved in hormone and abiotic stress response, thus contributing to grain size formation under drought stress (Shen et al., 2025). In wheat, an analysis of cis‐acting elements revealed the involvement of glycosyltransferase TaGT43 genes in cell wall formation and responses to phytohormones, light, abiotic and biotic stresses, and regulating growth and development (Sun et al., 2024)

We identified candidate gene TraesCS3B03G0041100 encoding ABC transporter G family member and TraesCS3B03G0042000 encoding transmembrane protein. The ABC transporters are transmembrane protein involved in different functional processes including defense response to biotic and abiotic stresses, regulation of grain morphology, transport of abscisic acid (ABA), and translocation and distribution of important plant nutrients (Do et al., 2018; Kang et al., 2015). ABC transporter encoding gene CcABCG24 was detected in active region transmembrane protein in pigeon pea (Cajanus cajan (L.) Huth) (Niu et al., 2021). In Arabidopsis thaliana L., the ABCG transporter gene AtABCG26 was involved in pollen grain development while AtABCG11 and AtABCG12 are associated with endosperm cuticle development and formation (Dhara & Raichaudhuri, 2021). Using multi‐omics approach combining genome‐wide association study (GWAS) and transcriptomic analysis in rapeseed (Brassica napus L.) the ABC transporter drought tolerance related‐gene ABCG16 is highly expressed in seed sample of high yielding variety under drought stress condition and associated with seed weight and seed yield traits (Salami et al., 2024). As suggested by Dahuja et al. (2021) ABC transporters can be used to improve the nutrients translocation efficiency to the grains, and under abiotic stress, they can be used to improve the grain size and shape without sacrificing the yield and grain quality. The higher expression of these two candidate genes (TraesCS3B03G0041100 and TraesCS3B03G0042000) in root and grain tissue under drought stress as compared to control signifies its important role in grain filling formation under drought stress condition. Thus, transporters and transmembrane protein encoding genes likely played a significant role in transport of product assimilates into the kernel leading to higher TGW.

Candidate gene TraesCS3B03G0042100 associated with the SNP marker BS00100045_51 encodes F‐box protein, which plays a significant role in abiotic and biotic stress response in plants and hormone signaling (Lyzenga & Stone, 2012; Zhou et al., 2014). The F‐box protein in high yielding varieties showed five times higher than in low‐yield varieties signifying its contribution to grain yield (Salami et al., 2024). In Arabidopsis, an F‐box protein SAP (STERILE APETALA) a substrate component of SCF (Skp1‐Cullin‐F‐box) E3 ubiquitin ligases targets transcription factors such as PEAPOD 1 and 2 (PPD1 and PPD2) for degradation (Z. Wang et al., 2016). This mechanism facilitates the reprogramming of growth and stress response. In wheat, overexpression of TaFBA1 encoding F‐box protein enhances grain size especially under drought stress through seed filling and improves drought tolerance through regulation of stress‐related genes and ABA signaling (Q. Li, Zhao, et al., 2024). The identified candidate gene in this study encoding F‐box protein may play an important role in enhancing grain yield under drought stress conditions but further functional study is needed to validate results.

We identified candidate gene TraesCS3B03G0042400 encoding Cytochrome P450 important in seed development (Ito & Meyerowitz, 2000), seed size (M. Ma et al., 2015), growth, and drought stress response (Tamiru et al., 2015). Cytochrome P450 is involved in the biosynthesis of compounds such as flavonoids, terpenoids, and regulation of hormones and secondary metabolites. In the study by L. Guo et al. (2022), the overexpression of cytochrome P450 encoding gene TaCYP78A5 enhances grain weight and yield per plant in wheat through auxin accumulation. Under drought stress condition, Cytochrome P450s have been reported to be involved in grain and leaf lignin formation. For example, CYP96A8 is associated with biosynthesis of lignin and grain weight per plant in maize (Zea mays L.) (Hu et al., 2009). Using GWAS and transcriptomic analysis, Salami et al. (2024) identified two CYP family genes CYP78A9 and CYP79B1 with important role in growth of tissues such as seeds, endosperm, floral organs conserved across Arabidopsis, maize, rice, and soybean (Glycine max [L.] Merr.) (Sotelo‐Silveira et al., 2013; X. Wang et al., 2015; Xu et al., 2015; Yeon et al., 2022). The dual function of Cytochrome P450 in growth and drought stress response suggests its potential role in controlling TGW under drought stress condition.

The candidate gene TraesCS3B03G0047300 encodes alanine–tRNA ligase with important function in ligase activity, aminoacyl‐tRNA ligase activity, and nucleotide and ATP binding. The aminoacyl‐tRNA synthetases (aaRSs) also called aminoacyl‐tRNA ligase are important in protein synthesis initiation by precisely linking a specific amino acid to its corresponding tRNA, and they are also involved in signal transduction, and other cellular processes (M. Guo & Schimmel, 2013; Ibba & Söll, 2000; Pang et al., 2014). In Arabidopsis, Kekez et al. (2019) reported that the interaction between the cytosolic seryl‐tRNA synthetase (SerRS) and the protein BEN1 associated in brassinosteroid hormone metabolism has important function in environmental adaptation as their response mechanism when exposed to stressed conditions. In rice, glutamyl‐tRNA synthetase (GluRS) is involved in male germ cells development through synthesis of protein and metabolic pathways (Yang et al., 2018). Although the function of alanine—tRNA ligase have not been studied in wheat and its yield‐related traits, their role and expression in protein synthesis is important especially during drought stress, indirectly affecting grain weight and yield.

Information generated from STRING (Szklarczyk et al., 2023) through protein–protein network interaction and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis (Kanehisa & Goto, 2000; Kanehisa et al., 2025) may provide a better understanding of gene function and relationship interaction with other genes (Saieed et al., 2023). In this study, we identified candidate genes involved in ubiquitin‐mediated proteolysis pathway; biosynthesis of various types of N‐glycan, steroids, and secondary metabolites; and metabolic pathways based on protein–protein interaction analysis in STRING (Szklarczyk et al., 2023). The ubiquitin‐mediated proteasomal degradation system is one of the most important pathways that regulates grain size and weight in cereal crops (Sharma et al., 2016). For example, E3 ubiquitin encoding gene TaGW2 and its homeologs TaGW2‐B1 and TaGW2‐D were found to regulate TGW in wheat (Zhang et al., 2018). In another study, the interaction between the E3 ligase for grain width and weight2 (TaGW2) and transcription factor TaARR12 enhances drought tolerance, and the knockdown of TaARR12 in tagw2 knockout mutant resulted in higher grain yield with improved drought resistance against the wild type (S. Li, Zhang, et al., 2024). These studies showed the important trade‐off between grain development and stress tolerance through ubiquitin‐mediated proteolysis.

Taken together, the involvement of identified candidate genes in this study formed an integrated network in relation to growth, plant development, and stress response. It could be that ABC transporters (TraesCS3B03G0041100) and transmembrane protein (TraesCS3B03G0042000) will adjust the transport of assimilates to developing grains once triggered by ABA and other signaling molecules under drought stress. UGTs (TraesCS3B03G0041000) and cytochrome P450 (TraesCS3B03G0042400) will then refine and regulate hormones to sustain cell growth under drought stress condition. F‐box proteins (TraesCS3B03G0042100) further regulate the processes through stress‐responsive pathways such as ubiquitin‐mediated proteolysis while amino‐acyl‐tRNA synthetase (TraesCS3B03G0047300) will maintain the protein synthesis for grain filling, thereby sustaining the grain weight and consequently high yield under drought stress condition. The identified candidate genes associated with TGW have the potential for use in breeding programs aimed at improving yield under drought stress. Further research, including transcriptomic analysis, fine mapping, and gene cloning, is needed to validate and better understand the functions of these genes. Once validated, pyramiding and introgression of favorable alleles can be done and genomic selection can be utilized to accelerate breeding for high yielding drought tolerant wheat.

CONCLUSIONS

We developed NILs using HIF and FGCS methods, targeting TGW QTL (gwm389‐agg/cat‐3) on wheat chromosome 3B. A 90K SNP genotyping array identified four SNP markers associated with TGW QTL. These markers are linked to six potential candidate genes encoding beta‐glucuronosyltransferase, ABC transporter, transmembrane protein, F‐box protein, cytochrome P450, and alanine–tRNA ligase. These genes play pivotal roles in growth, plant development, and stress response particularly in grain development under drought stress. In silico analyses revealed the higher expression levels of all candidate genes in root and grain tissue under drought stress as compared to control conditions, and their involvement in pathways such as ubiquitin‐mediated proteolysis; metabolic pathways; and biosynthesis of various N‐glycan, steroids, and secondary metabolites suggests its potential roles in enhancing grain yield under drought stress. The breeding materials, SNP markers, and candidate genes identified in this study can provide valuable tools for refining QTL mapping and advancing marker‐assisted selection to improve drought tolerance in wheat.

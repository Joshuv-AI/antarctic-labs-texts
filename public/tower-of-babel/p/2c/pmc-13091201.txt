Durvalumab combined with weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel as first-line treatment of patients with recurrent and/or metastatic HNSCC not eligible for cisplatin-based chemotherapies: results of the multicenter prospective study FRAIL-IMMUNE
Authors: J Fayette, C Cropet, C Toullec, M Burgy, A Bruyas, C Sire, A Lagrange, F Clatot, M Vinches, M Iacob, L Martin, M-C Kaminsky, D Vansteene, S Salas, A Champagnac, P Saintigny, J Gautier, D Pérol, J Bourhis
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPembrolizumab, combined with platinum-5-FU or as a monotherapy, is the standard treatment of patients with recurrent and/or metastatic (R/M) head and neck squamous-cell carcinoma (HNSCC). This treatment is used exclusively in programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1)-positive patients in Europe, whereas it is used in the USA regardless of PD-L1 status. However, substantial toxicities, notably those related to cisplatin-based chemotherapy, preclude the use of combination therapy in fragile patients. We investigated the efficacy and tolerance of first-line durvalumab combined with weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel in R/M HNSCC patients who were ineligible for cisplatin.Patients and methodsThis prospective multicenter single-arm phase II study assessed tolerance in R/M HNSCC patients with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG-PS) of 0-1 (run-in phase study feasibility, then cohort A) and ECOG-PS 2 (cohort B), frail, not eligible for standard cisplatin-based chemotherapy. In the first-line, patients received four 28-day chemotherapy cycles (carboplatin AUC2; paclitaxel 80 mg/m2 day 1, 8, and 15) combined with durvalumab 1500 mg every 4 weeks for 12 months maximum. The primary endpoint was 12-month overall survival (OS) rate based on a Fleming A’Hern design: cohort A, P0 inefficacy boundary 47%, P1 target efficacy 65%, at least 38 successes among 64 patients assessable were required for further investigations; cohort B: P0 15%; P1 35%, at least 10 successes needed among 38 patients to reject the null hypothesis.ResultsFrom 16 May 2019 to 4 March 2021, 64 patients with an ECOG-PS of 1 (cohort A) from 13 French authorized centers (hospital, cancer centers) were included and received study treatments. Cohort B enrolled 40 patients with an ECOG-PS of 2 from 25 Aug 2021 and 9 March 2023 and 39 were assessable. With 40 patients with an ECOG-PS of 0-1 [62.5%, unilateral 95% confidence interval (CI) 51.5%-∞], and 20 patients with an ECOG-PS of 2 (51.3%, unilateral 95% CI 37.1%-∞) alive at 12 months, the study met its primary endpoints.ConclusionsThe promising results in frail patients with an ECOG-PS of 0-1 and 2 encourages further investigation of durvalumab with weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel in first-line R/M HNSCC patients in a comparative phase III clinical trial.Key words: recurrent and/or metastatic head and neck squamous-cell carcinoma (R/M HNSCC), immunotherapy, weekly chemotherapy, cisplatin ineligibility, carboplatin, paclitaxel

Background

Head and neck squamous cell carcinomas (HNSCC) represent a heterogeneous group of tumors that arise from the squamous epithelium of the oral cavity, oropharynx, larynx, and hypopharynx. These tumors account for 4%-5% of all solid malignancies. The treatment of patients with recurrent and/or metastatic (R/M) HNSCC recently changed. Antibody-based pembrolizumab monotherapy or pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy [cisplatin or carboplatin, 5-fluorouracil (5-FU)] blocks the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and is currently recommended for all patients in the USA, whereas in Europe, it is restricted to patients with tumors that have a programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1)-combined positive score (CPS) ≥1 (i.e. PD-L1-positive)1, 2, 3 All patients treated with pembrolizumab and chemotherapy showed extended overall survival (OS) compared with the previous standard, EXTREME regimen (cis-/carboplatin + 5-FU and cetuximab) (CPS ≥20: 0.60; CPS ≥1: 0.65; all patients: 0.77). The 4-year-follow-up confirmed OS improved with pembrolizumab alone in PD-L1 (CPS ≥20 and ≥1) and in combination with chemotherapy in PD-L1 (CPS ≥20, ≥1, and all patients).4 To note, progression free survival (PFS) did not improve regardless of the group. The advantage of the combination of immunotherapy and chemotherapy has never been directly investigated. In addition, the most appropriate chemotherapy schedule is still debated. The EXTREME regimen is still used in patients with PD-L1-negative tumors. However, before the era of immunotherapy, interesting results from the TPEx regimen (docetaxel–platinum–cetuximab) were also reported in terms of tolerance, compliance, and improved quality of life (QoL) better than the EXTREME regimen.5 In PD-L1-positive patients, the choice for pembrolizumab alone or combined with platinum/5-FU is largely guide by clinical considerations (e.g. disease burden, aggressiveness),6 and tolerance issues related to the use of chemotherapy still must be overcome. Notably, pembrolizumab combined with platinum/5-FU reported more grade ≥3 toxicities than pembrolizumab alone (85% versus 17%),2 therefore limiting the intended advantage of chemotherapy. In addition, lingering debates regarding the platinum-based selection (cisplatin/carboplatin) and optimal dose administration remain. Cisplatin improved objective responses and although cisplatin has increased toxicity and no demonstrated OS improvement compared with carboplatin, cisplatin is often the option retained.5,7 Cisplatin would be preferred in patients <70 years of age with adequate renal function to better face substantial toxicities. In locally advanced HNSCC, induction chemotherapy with weekly carboplatin (AUC2) and paclitaxel (135 mg/m2) showed an interesting objective response rate (ORR) (79%-87%), with a manageable safety profile,8 and reported an ORR of 75.6% when combined with everolimus.9 In R/M HNSCC patients with comorbidities and poor clinical conditions precluding cisplatin use, weekly carboplatin (AUC2) and paclitaxel first-line treatment showed interesting efficacy,10 and fewer toxicities than cisplatin/5-FU.11, 12, 13

For a long time, the standard treatment was platinum-5FU and cetuximab. Due to its toxicity, it was rarely used in the USA, where a sequential approach with carboplatin/paclitaxel, and then cetuximab was preferred. Moreover, the KEYNOTE-B10 study, investigating pembrolizumab combined with carboplatin AUC 5 mg/ml per min at day 1 and paclitaxel either 100 mg/m2 at days 1 and 8, or 175 mg/m2 at day 1 every 3 weeks reported 49% of objective responses, a median PFS (mPFS) of 5.6 months, a median OS (mOS) of 13.1 months, and a manageable tolerance profile, underlining the clinical relevance of alternative combinations and administration schedules. KEYNOTE results published after the presentation of our study validated the carboplatin–paclitaxel–pembrolizumab combination, with results similar to those of the platinum-5FU–pembrolizumab combination.14 A randomized phase II study suggested that the carboplatin–paclitaxel–cetuximab combination could yield better results than the EXTREME standard regimen (platinum-5FU and cetuximab).15 A retrospective analysis demonstrated the feasibility of the weekly carboplatin–paclitaxel approach with good results.10 The weekly carboplatin–paclitaxel regimen, with both agents used for induction, has proven effective.9 All these data collectively support the use of weekly paclitaxel instead of 5FU, with promising survival results in patients.

Durvalumab has been largely used as maintenance monotherapy in stage III, unresectable NSCLC that did not progress following platinum-based chemoradiotherapy.16 It is also often combined with chemotherapy in metastatic small cell lung cancer,17 biliary tract cancer,18 and endometrial cancer19 or with tremelimumab in hepatocellular carcinoma.20 In R/M HNSCC, durvalumab alone or combined with tremelimumab showed durable responses and manageable toxicity.21 However, durvalumab in R/M HNSCC patients who progressed after a platinum-containing regimen showed no significantly improved OS (mOS durvalumab: 7.6 months; durvalumab + tremelimumab: 6.5 months; standard of care: 8.3 months),22 and OS was similar to the OS seen in the EXTREME trial.21

The FRAIL-IMMUNE study was designed in 2018 before the approval of pembrolizumab. It investigated efficacy and safety of first-line durvalumab combined with weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel chemotherapy, first in R/M HNSCC patients who were ineligible for a cisplatin-based regimen and who had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG-PS) of 0-1 (cohort A), then in patients with an ECOG-PS of 2 (cohort B). Exploratory analyses according to neutrophil–lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and PD-L1-CPS in R/M HNSCC patients were carried out.

Methods

Patient population

The prospective multicenter single-arm clinical trial FRAIL-IMMUNE used a two-cohort open-label phase II design (Supplementary Material S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106946). Patient inclusion criteria: ≥18 years of age; histologically confirmed recurrent HNSCC; not eligible for local curative treatment or metastatic (stage IVc), with the primary tumor localized in the oral cavity, larynx, or hypopharynx; no treatment in the last 6 months; no prior PD-(L)1 inhibitor including durvalumab; no recurrence in the 6 months after curative cisplatin treatment; ineligible for standard therapies including cisplatin (40-60 ml/min creatinine clearance and/or any severe comorbidities as per investigator judgment); documented progression of measurable disease as per the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST) v1.1,23 de novo biopsy or ≤3 month archived tumor sample, regardless of tumor PD-L1 expression; adequate organ and marrow function; body weight > 30 kg; life expectancy ≥3 months. Patients were assessed for eligibility in a French Head and Neck Oncology and Radiotherapy Group (GORTEC) site. Inclusion/exclusion criteria are indicated in Supplementary Material S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106946.

The study received approval from the Sud-Ouest et Outre-Mer III ethics committee. The French health authority restricted enrollment to patients with an ECOG-PS of 0-1 (cohort A). A safety run-in feasibility study assessed the experimental combination, the independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (iDSMB) reviewed data before cohort A enrollment. Efficacy and safety results after the 31st patient (cohort A) encouraged further enrollment, and cohort B included HNSCC patients with an ECOG-PS of 2.

This study followed the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All patients provided written informed consent.

ClinicalTrial.gov registration number NCT03723967.

Data collection and measurements

Limiting adverse events (AEs) were defined as grade 3/4 AEs including diarrhea, transaminase or bilirubin—except in the case of Gilbert’s disease—, neurological, endocrine, or other toxicities such as febrile neutropenia, grade 4 cutaneous toxicities and grade 3 toxicities lasting >2 weeks despite symptomatic treatments, reported after the first cycle of chemotherapy (4 to 6 weeks) according to the Common Terminology Criteria Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) v.5.0. Two slots of three patients (maximum 6 consecutively enrolled) with no dose escalations were planned before cohort A enrollment. Patients received four 28-day cycles of chemotherapy (carboplatin AUC2/paclitaxel 80 mg/m2 at days 1, 8, and 15) with 1500 mg durvalumab at day 1 and every 4 weeks for 12 months (maximum 13 cycles) (Supplementary Material S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106946). Pretreatment with 60 mg prednisolone instead of dexamethasone was administrated to prevent potential decreased efficacy in immuno-oncology treatment. Patients had 2-day granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) infusions after each chemotherapy.

Treatment was discontinued for progression, intolerable toxicity, and withdrawal of consent, and chemotherapy doses/schedules were adjusted in case of toxicity.24 Patients > 70 years of age received Geriatric 8 questionnaires at baseline.25 The study planned a 90-day follow-up after treatment discontinuation and investigator-assessed tumor evaluations according to RECIST v1.1 every 8 weeks.23 The study continued until the last patient achieved their 12-month follow-up visit. QoL was assessed using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) Quality of Life Questionnaire (QLQ)-core 30 (C30) and QLQ-head and neck (H&N)35 tools.26,27

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was the OS rate at 12 months, defined as the rate of patients alive at 12 months. Secondary endpoints included: PFS, defined as the time from inclusion to the first documented progression as per investigator-assessment according to RECIST v1.1 or death due to any cause, and censored at last adequate tumor assessment; time to treatment failure (TTF), defined as the time from inclusion to permanent treatment discontinuation (any cause including progression, toxicity, death, consent withdrawal), with censures at last tumor assessment for patients with no treatment failure; duration of durvalumab, (last dose − first dose + 28 days) and chemotherapy (last dose − first dose + 14 days) exposure; OS, defined as the time from inclusion to death due to any cause and censored at last contact for living patients; treatment response according to the RECIST v1.1,23 with ORR defined as the proportion of patients with complete response (CR) or partial response (PR); best response from treatment initiation to first progression; duration of response defined as the time from first documented response (CR or PR) to the first documented progression or death due to any cause; the incidence of AEs, serious AEs (SAE), and deaths according to NCI-CTCAE v5.0; QoL was assessed using EORTC QLQ-C30 and QLQ-H&N3526,27 at baseline, and cycles (C) 5, C9, and C12. QLQ-C30 high scores for global health status and functional scale translated to high QoL or a healthy level of functioning; symptoms scales were reversed: a greater score graphically translated to better QoL. QLQ-H&N35 used a 7-multi-item scale for pain, swallowing, taste and smell, speech ability, eating, social contact, and sexuality, and 11 single items. High scores translated to deteriorated QoL.

Exploratory analyses included PD-L1 expression (Ventana SP263 immunohistochemistry assay)28 using PD-L1 CPS and p16 status in <3-month-old tumor samples. Patients with oropharynx cancer had human papillomavirus (HPV) and/or immunohistochemistry p16+ status determined. NLR was calculated at treatment initiation.

Statistics

The study used an A’Hern’s single-stage phase II trial procedure with the following hypotheses where π is the true probability of success (12-month OS). The sample size calculation for cohort A considered a minimum success rate of interest of p1 = 0.65 and an uninteresting rate of p0 = 0.47. Assuming a 1-sided type I error alpha of 0.05% and 90% power, 64 patients were needed to reject the null hypothesis H0: π ≥ p0 versus the alternative hypothesis H1: π ≥ p1 in a 1-sided situation. 38 successes were needed for further investigation. To note, the analysis population of cohort A included patients from the safety run-in phase. The efficacy and safety populations included all patients who received ≥1 dose of the study drugs. Interim analyses were planned after the run-in phase and after the 31st patient; the sample size calculation for cohort B considered a minimum success rate of interest of p1 = 0.35 and defined p0 = 0.15 as uninteresting rate. Assuming a 1-sided type I error alpha of 0.05% and 90% power, 38 patients were needed to reject the null hypothesis. A total of 102 HNSCC patients was therefore expected.

TTF, PFS, and OS were estimated with the Kaplan–Meier method. We summarized response rates using descriptive statistics. Follow-up duration was estimated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method and expressed with its 95% confidence interval (CI). All P values and CIs are two sided, except for the primary endpoint. The data cut-off for the analysis of cohort A was 4 March 2022, and 5 July 2024 for cohort B. All survival data were updated. Statistical analyses used SAS version 9.4.

Results

Run-in phase, and cohort A

No limiting toxicities were reported in the six assessable patients from the safety run-in phase, the iDSMB approved further patient recruitment for cohort A.

Between 16 May 2019 and 4 March 2021, 80 R/M HNSCC patients were assessed for eligibility in cohort A; 15 (18.8%) were excluded, and 64 out of the 65 patients included in 13 out of the 17 French GORTEC centers received study treatment and were assessable (Figure 1, Supplementary Material S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106946); One patient with ECOG-PS 2 was included in the trail but did not receive study tratement. At the time of the analysis, median durvalumab exposure was 29.9 (4.0-56.3) weeks. Nineteen (29.7%) patients received 13 durvalumab infusions; 45 (70.3%) permanently discontinued durvalumab due to progression (n = 32, 50%), deaths (n = 7), refusal (n = 3), toxicity (n = 2) and COVID-19 (n = 1). Cohort B

Among the 51 patients assessed for eligibility, 40 patients included from 25 August 2021 to 9 March 2023 who had received study treatment contributed to the efficacy-assessable and safety populations (Figure 1B). To note, one patient was lost to follow-up after 9 months was not assessable for the primary endpoint. All patients with an ECOG-PS of 2 were considered unfit for cisplatin. No major protocol violation occurred. Median durvalumab exposure was 23.0 (4.0-59.1) weeks, and median duration of chemotherapy was 15.9 (2.0-19.7) weeks. Permanent discontinuation occurred for durvalumab in 34 (85.0%) patients (mainly due to toxicity: n = 3, 7.5%; progression: n = 17, 42.5%) and for chemotherapy in 21 (52.5%) patients (mainly due to toxicity: n = 9, 22.5%; progression: n = 5, 12.5%).

Primary endpoint

Cohort A

With a median follow-up (mFU) for OS of 27.1 (21.5-40.1) months, 40 (62.5%, unilateral 95% CI 51.5% - ∞) patients were alive at 12 months, and the study met the efficacy rule for the primary endpoint. At the time of analysis, 48 (75%) patients progressed or died.

Cohort B

A total of 39 out of the 40 patients included were assessable for the primary endpoint, with a mFU for OS of 24.7 (9.4-29.7) months, 20 (51.3%, unilateral 95% CI 37.1% - ∞) patients were alive at 12 months, the efficacy rule for the primary endpoint was met. Of note, only one patient was lost to follow-up before 12 months.

The 12-month OS rates (Kaplin–Meier estimates) were 0.63 (0.49-0.73) and 0.52 (0.36-0.66) in cohorts A and B, respectively.

Secondary endpoints

Cohort A

mFU for PFS was 12.7 (0.03-13) months. At the time of the analysis, 48 (75%) patients progressed or died. The mOS was 18 [11.9-not reached (NR)] months, 37 (57.8%) deaths occurred. mPFS was 7.0 months (95% CI 5.4-9.9 months). The PFS rate at 12 months was 28% (17% to 40%). mTTF was 6 months (4.7-9 months). Among the 62 patients assessable for response, 44 (71%) objective responses including 7 CR and 37 PR (non-assessable: n = 1; patient refusal: n = 1) were reported (ORR 71%, 58.1% to 81.8%). Median response duration was 5.9 months (3.4-9.6 months) (Figure 2).

Cohort B

mFU for PFS was 12.8 (1.8-13.1) months. mPFS was 5.3 (3.7-7.2) months. At the time of analysis, 32 (80%) patients progressed or died. mTTF was 4.4 (3.2-5.7) months. The PFS rate at 12 months was 19% (95% CI 8%-33%). The mOS was 12.4 (6.7-22.6) months (death: n = 26, 65%). Among the 38 patients assessable for response, 23 (60.5%) had an objective response (2 CR; 21 PR); two deaths that were non-disease-related occurred before tumor evaluation.

Safety

Cohort A

Most patients experienced treatment-related AEs (n = 63, 98.4%) (Table 2, Supplementary Material S4A-C, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106946). At the time of the analysis, 54 (84.4%) patients experienced durvalumab-related AEs including 13 (20.3%) with grade ≥3 AEs, and 62 (96.9%) patients presented chemotherapy-related AEs including 43 (67.2%) patients with grade ≥3 AEs (Supplementary Material S4B, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106946). Grade 5 AEs occurred in 11 (17.2%) patients and included tumor hemorrhage (n = 1), pneumonia (n = 1), general physical health deterioration (n = 2), death (n = 1), pneumothorax (n = 1), coronavirus infection [n = 1, declared as suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSAR)], hemorrhage (n = 1), laryngeal dyspnea (n = 1), malignant neoplasm progression (n = 1), sepsis (n = 1); no AEs were related to the treatment according to the sponsor.

Patients with NLR < 5 had longer mOS [27.5, 13.1-not evaluated (NE)] months than patients with NLR ≥5 (15.1, 7.7-25.2) months; hazard ratio (HR) 0.55, 95% CI 0.27-1.09, P = 0.0819 (Figure 3A).

43 (76.8%) patients had PD-L1 CPS ≥1, including 17 (30.3%) patients with CPS ≥20, and 13 (23.2%) patients with CPS <1. OS according to PD-L1 CPS showed a longer mOS in patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1 (25.1, 14.3-NR) months than in patients with PD-L1-CPS < 1 (9.4, 4.0-NR) months, HR 0.55, 95% CI 0.25-1.19, P = 0.1245 (Figure 3B).

In the 24 patients with oropharynx cancer, p16 status was negative in 11 (17.2%) patients, and positive in 9 (14.1%) (missing: n = 4). No differences in efficacy according to p16 status were observed.

Cohort B

No significant differences according to the NLR cut-off ≥5 or PD-L1 CPS ≥20 were observed in patients with an ECOG-PS of 2.

Quality of life

Cohort A

QLQ-C30 scores were reported by 59 (92.2%) patients at baseline, 35 (54.7%) patients at C5, 23 (35.9%) patients at C9, and 19 (29.7%) patients at C12. The mean score for global health status was 61.1. Patients reported poor scores for several items including fatigue (29.9), pain (28.2), dyspnea (20.9), insomnia (24.1), and appetite loss (24.7).

QLQ-H&N35 scores were completed by 59 (92.2%) patients at baseline, 35 (54.7%) patients at C5, 23 (35.9%) patients at C9, and 18 (28.1%) patients at C12. QLQ-HN35 scores translated to high symptomatology at baseline with elevated scores mainly for pain (72.1), senses problems (78.2), and trouble with social contact (85.6). No significant score changes were observed (Supplementary Material S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106946).

Cohort B

A QoL decrease between baseline and C5 was observed in most of the QLQ-C30 scales, except global health status, emotional functioning, pain, insomnia, constipation, and financial difficulties. In particular, nausea and vomiting (−17.9), dyspnea (−13.9) and diarrhea (−12.8) were the most impacted scales.

Among patients with questionnaire answers at C9, QoL seemed generally improved (except for diarrhea).

In the 13 patients who completed a QLQ-H&N35 questionnaire at C5, an important worsening of pain (13.7 points), teeth (21.2), dry mouth (27.8), felt ill (24.2), and slight improvement in opening mouth symptom (19.4 points) were reported.

Interpretations of score evolutions need to be cautious considering the reduced sample size.

Discussion

The multicenter two-cohort phase II trial FRAIL-IMMUNE investigating durvalumab and weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel showed significant efficacy with mOS of 18.0 (11.9-NR) months (12-month OS: 62.5%, 95% CI 49% to 73%; 24-month OS: 95% CI 45%, 32% to 57%) in R/M HNSCC patients with an ECOG-PS of 0-1 and ineligible for cisplatin. With 40 living patients, the primary endpoint was met. Of note, the median follow-up reached >2 years. In addition, mPFS was 7.0 months, ORR was 71%, median response duration was 5.9 months, 7 (11.3%) CR and 37 (59.7%) PR were achieved. These results encouraged further investigations in patients with an ECOG-PS of 2 and the primary endpoint was also met with 20 patients with an ECOG-PS of 2 alive at 12 months. With a mOS of 12.4 months, mPFS of 5.3 months, and an ORR reaching 60.5%, durvalumab combined with weekly chemotherapy excluding cisplatin was confirmed to be an appropriate option in frail patients with an ECOG-PS of 2.

The FRAIL-IMMUNE statistical hypotheses issued from previously published historical cohorts.10 mOS in the FRAIL-IMMUNE trial favorably compared with mOS in patients treated with first-line immunotherapy regardless of PD-L1 expression (durvalumab monotherapy: 7.6 months,22 pembrolizumab monotherapy: 9.9 months21 and 11.6 months,2 nivolumab/ipilimumab: 13.9 months,29 pembrolizumab and carboplatin/paclitaxel-based chemotherapy: 13.1 months).14 Of note, the KEYNOTE-B10 study used another administration schedule for chemotherapy without cisplatin: carboplatin AUC 5, paclitaxel 100 mg/m2 on days 1 and 8 or alternatively 175 mg/m2 on day 1, once every 3 weeks. The improved OS may result from greater tolerance and early efficacy in aggressive tumors in need of early response using the FRAIL-IMMUNE trial chemotherapy schedule/administration, therefore preventing early deaths. A weekly paclitaxel regimen compared with the 3-week paclitaxel regimen previously reported improved PFS.30 The FRAIL-IMMUNE population showed frailty criteria, distant metastatic disease, and metastatic site distributions at inclusion roughly similar to other studies.14,21,22

With mOS in FRAIL-IMMUNE patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1 (n = 43) of 25.1 months, results compared favorably with mOS of patients treated with pembrolizumab combined chemotherapy. PD-L1-high patients treated with durvalumab in the KESTREL study reported a survival of 10.9 months.21 mOS in patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1 treated with pembrolizumab (KEYNOTE-048) was 12.3 months,2 extended mOS was also reached with pembrolizumab alone in the LEAP study (17.9 months),31 and with nivolumab/ipilimumab (15.7 months).29 As recommended by the European Medicines agency, the FRAIL-IMMUNE study was restricted to patients with a PD-L1 CPS ≥1. Interestingly, the FRAIL-IMMUNE mOS in patients with CPS ≥20 was not reached despite a longer mFU of 27.1 months. Studies investigating pembrolizumab and nivolumab/ipilimumab reported mOS of 14.9 and 17.1 months, respectively. Regardless of PD-L1 expression, the response rate of 71% in the FRAIL-IMMUNE trial was much higher than the response rate reported with nivolumab/ipilimumab (24.2%), pembrolizumab alone (16.9%), or durvalumab alone (16.2%). The KEYNOTE-B10 study reported an ORR of 49% with pembrolizumab and chemotherapy (carboplatin/paclitaxel). Although ORR cannot be directly compared with and used as a surrogate for OS, the higher response rate observed in progressive and frail HNSCC patients may be a critical factor to better stabilize/alleviate symptoms and therefore preserve QoL.

The precise role of chemotherapy in the KEYNOTE-048 study was not specifically addressed. The trial was not designed to compare pembrolizumab alone and combined with platinum/5-FU. The 5-year OS in patients treated with pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy was slightly higher than in patients with pembrolizumab alone (18.2% versus 15.4%). The interesting OS/PFS observed with pembrolizumab/chemotherapy combination may derive from premature deaths prevented through early chemotherapy administration. Moreover, response rates with pembrolizumab/chemotherapy (37.2%) were higher than with pembrolizumab alone (19.1%). Currently, pembrolizumab/chemotherapy combination is used in patients with CPS ≥1 when immediate response is required.32 Hence, chemotherapy through its impact on tumor lysis, tumor antigen release, and enhanced immune reactions may contribute to better control early progression and increase response rates. Although the synergy from the carboplatin/paclitaxel weekly schedule and immunotherapy is still speculative, the limited response rate of 37% observed with pembrolizumab/platinum/5-FU would support this assumption.

We reported that the current chemotherapy schedule with appropriate prophylaxis resulted in manageable toxicity and patients were not at higher risk of treatment-related deaths when compared with the KEYNOTE-B10 study. In addition, an increased efficacy was expected from taxane-based chemotherapy compared with 5-FU-based chemotherapy, with improved tolerability related to the weekly regimen, increased response rates and ultimately improved OS. The weekly administration of paclitaxel versus every 3 weeks already showed improved PFS and limited complications in various type of cancers30 and this schedule may also benefit older patients with poor physical conditions who are suffering from HNSCC.33 A recent retrospective study reported an ORR of 79% and OS of 17.2 months in 39 patients treated with pembrolizumab and weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel without pause. The weekly chemotherapy more than immunotherapy itself may have contributed to improved outcomes.34 In accordance with our results, patients experienced several grade 3-4 AEs, notably hematologic disorders (18%) and neuropathy (8%) that the current scheme with a scheduled break on day 28 may limit. In addition, a 2-day weekly G-CSF administration was proposed to prevent or better control anticipated neutropenia. Most toxicities were well-known manageable hematologic events.

Interestingly, extended OS was reached with only 1-year durvalumab duration whereas previous studies used pembrolizumab duration of nearly 2-year.1,35 In melanoma, immunotherapy is stopped ∼6 months after CR. Further complementary medico-economic analyses will inform on the optimal treatment duration.36 In addition, biomarkers for immunotherapy are also of critical need. Exploratory analysis to identify clinically relevant gene expression signatures in patients treated with chemo-immunotherapy are ongoing and will be reported separately.

The mPFS of 7.0 months, and mOS of 18.0 months suggested a putative second line efficacy of 11 months. It was previously suggested that progressive patients treated with immunotherapy may derive higher benefit from chemotherapy20. Our study encourages subsequent lines of chemotherapy.

Patients with an ECOG-PS of 2 generally have a short OS and monotherapy is often proposed. With a mOS of 12.4 months, the FRAIL-IMMUNE trial’s strategy represents a promising option for frail patients. Despite the systematic use of G-CSF, tolerance still merits improvement, and administration of chemotherapy with doses reduced by 25% in this frail population should be further explored.

This study has several limitations. We used a binary endpoint in our design, and we recognize that excluding patients lost to follow-up before 12 months might have biased the results. Of note, only one patient was lost to follow-up before 12 months in cohort B and 12-month OS rates (Kaplin–Meier estimates) were also presented (cohort A: HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.49-0.73; cohort B: HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.36-0.66). In addition, the efficacy significance heavily relied on the null hypotheses. Whereas randomized trials remain the best design to provide statistical conclusion validity in evaluating the efficacy of a new treatment, alternative designs using binary endpoints are nevertheless useful to overcome barriers when larger sample sizes cannot be reasonably achieved. We also used previous retrospective study to guide predefined hypotheses.10, and interpretations need to be cautious. In addition, indirect comparisons with other clinical trials are obviously limited. We recognize that a comorbidity score would be informative and provide a useful selection criteria to better characterize frailty in this population and strengthen the results.

Conclusion

Durvalumab combined with weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel provides clinically significant efficacy in HNSCC patients with a 12-month OS rate of 65.2%, mOS of 18.0 months (25.1 months in PD-L1-positive patients), mPFS of 7.0 months, and ORR of 71% in patients with an ECOG-PS of 0-1, and a 12-month OS rate of 51.3%, mOS of 12.4 months, mPFS of 5.3 months, and ORR of 60.5% in patients with an ECOG-PS of 2. The tolerance profile was manageable, with no unexpected toxicities. Durvalumab combined with weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel in first-line HNSCC deserves further phase III trial investigation, using pembrolizumab as the comparator arm mostly in patients with a PD-L1 CPS ≥1.

Evaluating the effectiveness of the ‘patient transport’ app in improving healthcare quality, patient safety, and workflow efficiency
Authors: Ye-Eun Park, Sunghwan Ji, Hee-Won Jung, Youn-Jung Kim, Jae-Ho Lee, Yura Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatient transport is a critical component of hospital operations, requiring precise communication across departments to ensure patient safety. However, research remains limited regarding digital communication tools for patient transport, particularly for older adults, who are at a high risk of safety events. To evaluate the quality improvement, usability, and work utility of the Asan Patient Transport Team app, a smartphone application was designed to facilitate patient transport and communication in a large tertiary hospital.MethodsA cross-sectional survey was conducted among 118 patient transport staff and 62 transport requesters at Asan Medical Centre. The survey evaluated the effectiveness of the app based on six aims of the Institute of Medicine for healthcare improvement, with the addition of patient engagement and communication, resulting in a total of eight assessed domains. Usability was measured using the System Usability Scale, work utility was analysed through workflow assessment, and specific challenges related to transporting older patients were also examined.ResultsEffectiveness was reflected in the reported improvements in efficiency (62%), timeliness (60.9%), and patient safety (60%). The app significantly enhanced communication between transport staff and medical personnel, particularly regarding task assignments and transport status updates. Concerning older patient transport, 80.5% of transport staff identified age-specific challenges, including insufficient information about mobility status (70.3%) and communication abilities (71.2%). The staff strongly supported the need for additional patient data, particularly information on fall risk (72.9%) and monitoring device details (73.7%). The Asan Patient Transport Team app achieved a ‘Good’ System Usability Scale score of 73.2. The survey found that the app positively affected work utility across workflows.ConclusionThe Asan Patient Transport Team app demonstrates significant potential to improve patient transport efficiency, safety, and communication. While good usability and high user satisfaction are indicated, opportunities for refinements exist, particularly in addressing the unique needs of older patients. As healthcare continues to digitize, tools such as the Asan Patient Transport Team app are crucial to optimizing hospital operations and enhancing patient care.

Introduction

Patient transport is a core operational function in hospitals, requiring coordinated communication across departments to ensure timely and safe transfers. Efficient transport processes contribute to clinical workflow, patient safety, and overall care quality [1–3].

Unlike other components of clinical care, intrahospital transport is often managed outside the direct supervision of healthcare providers. Patients may need to participate actively in the transfer process, despite varied levels of mobility, consciousness, or clinical acuity. Transport of critically ill patients has been associated with a high incidence of adverse events, influenced by factors such as transport duration, sedation, and the use of vasoactive agents [4, 5].

Effective risk communication between clinical team and transport personnel is essential for mitigating preventable incidents during transfers. Digital technologies offer potential solutions to support this communication, yet their application in hospital transport systems remains underexplored [6–10]. In particular, there is limited evidence regarding the type of patient information required by transport personnel, especially for high-risk populations such as older adults or patients receiving sedatives [11].

To address this gap, we conducted a cross-sectional survey involving patient transport staff and transport requesters requesting patient transfers in a tertiary hospital. We assessed the perceived impact of a digital communication tool on usability, workflow efficiency, and patient safety. The study also explored specific challenges encountered during the transport of older adults and identified critical information needs to inform safer and more coordinated care.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study was a cross-sectional survey conducted to evaluate the effectiveness, usability, and work utility of a digital communication tool used for intrahospital patient transport at Asan Medical Centre, a 2764-bed tertiary referral hospital in Seoul, Republic of Korea.

Study participants

The study involved two groups of hospital staff engaged in intrahospital patient transport.

The first group comprised transport staff, who are non-clinical employees responsible for transferring patients between locations (e.g. from wards to diagnostic units). For ambulatory patients, transport staff primarily serve as guides; for patients with limited mobility or under sedation, they operate wheelchairs or stretcher cars. As they move actively throughout the hospital, task assignments and reporting are managed via a dedicated smartphone application, the Asan Patient Transport Team (APTT) app.

The second group consisted of transport requesters, who may be either healthcare professionals (mainly nurses) or non-clinical personnel (e.g. radiology or laboratory staff). Requesters perform their own routine duties—such as bedside nursing care—and submit transport requests through the hospital’s electronic system, typically using desktop workstations.

APTT app

In July 2014, Asan Medical Centre developed an in-house smartphone application—the APTT app—to improve the coordination and efficiency of patient transport requests [7]. When a transport request is initiated via the hospital’s electronic health record (EHR) system, relevant information becomes accessible to transport personnel through the app. This includes patient identification, origin and destination points, transport method (e.g. on foot or wheelchair), reservation time, and any special instructions.

The app enables real-time updates on the status of transport tasks, including initiation, delay, cancellation, and completion. Reasons for delays or cancellations can also be recorded directly within the system. The APTT app is not available via commercial app stores; access is restricted to authorized personnel within the hospital.

Questionnaire design

A structured questionnaire was developed in collaboration with specialists from the Information Medicine, Emergency Medicine, and Geriatrics. It collected data on participants’ characteristics, including age, professional experience, and occupational role. The survey was tailored to each respondent group’s functions.

For patient transport staff, items focused on perceived inconveniences during patient transport (usability), utilization of the app at various workflow stages (workflow issues), and general user experience, including suggestions for improvement. For transport requestors, the questionnaire assessed challenges and potential enhancements in the desktop-based request interface embedded in the EHR system [12]. Shared items across all roles assessed overall app effectiveness, work utility, issues during the transfer of older patients, and perceived needs for additional information.

To evaluate the app’s contribution to care quality, we applied the six domains defined by the Institute of Medicine (IOM) [13, 14], and added two additional domains—patient engagement and communication—resulting in a total of eight indicators. Participants were asked (i) to rate the extent to which the APTT app contributed to each domain, and (ii) to estimate the extent to which each domain would be negatively affected in its absence. Responses were rated using a five-point Likert scale with context-specific labels. The questionnaire also included single-choice items and open-ended response fields.

Usability was assessed using the validated System Usability Scale (SUS) [15, 16]. Drawing upon interviews with transport staff and planning/design documents from the app development phase, we defined the transport workflow as seven steps: (i) tasks confirmation; (ii) patient identification; (iii) transport initiation; (iv) arrival confirmation; (v) post-transport return or standby; (vi) task modifications (e.g. cancellations, delays, reassignment); and (vii) access to key contacts or operational information. Respondents assessed the app’s impact on each step. The full version of the questionnaire is available in Supplementary Material 1.

Recruitment

The survey was conducted from 21 to 31 July 2021 among users of the APTT app at Asan Medical Centre, as well as clinical staff who had experience requesting patient transport via the EHR system. Only transport personnel were authorized to use the APTT app. The target sample size was 210 participants, comprising the full cohort of 140 app users, nurses on duty in the emergency department (n = 30) and geriatric ward (n = 30), and staff from the emergency radiology unit (n = 10). Recruitment was conducted via public notices on the hospital intranet. Informed consent was obtained through a written consent form, and participants completed the survey using a secure online link.

Data analysis

Respondents were classified as transport staff or requesters (nurses and radiology staff). Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Group differences were evaluated using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test due to non-normal response distributions (Shapiro–Wilk test). Analyses were performed in R (version 4.3.0) using the ‘wilcox.test’ function with exact P-value to account for small sample sizes and potential tied ranks. Statistical significance was set at two-sided P value of <.05. Minor missing data were present due to incomplete responses; analyses used available cases with adjusted denominators. Open-ended responses were thematically categorized and counted for descriptive analysis.

Results

A total of 181 responses were collected, including 119 from patient transport staff (i.e. APTT app users) and 62 from transport requesters. One duplicate response from the transport staff group was excluded, resulting in 118 valid responses. Quality improvement

As illustrated in Fig. 1, the APTT app was perceived to positively impact several dimensions of care quality. The most frequently cited improvements were in work efficiency (n = 111, 62.1%), timeliness (n = 109, 60.9%), and patient safety (n = 108, 60%). Respondents also reported gains in overall effectiveness (n = 105, 58.3%), equity (n = 95, 53.1%), communication (n = 94, 52.3%), patient engagement (n = 72, 40%), and patient-centredness (n = 67, 37%).

Quality-of-care indices positively affected by the APTT app (left) versus those negatively affected in the absence of the APTT app (right) (N = 180).

When asked to consider the hypothetical absence of the APTT app, participants indicated that the greatest negative impacts would be on work efficiency (n = 125, 70.6%), patient safety (n = 115, 64.3%), and timeliness (n = 110, 61.4%). Other areas perceived to be adversely affected included overall effectiveness (n = 108, 60.3%), communication (n = 102, 57%), patient-centredness (n = 89, 50%), patient engagement (n = 84, 46.9%), and equity (n = 70, 39.5%).

Usability

Figure 2 presents the distribution of responses across the ten SUS items, rated on a five-point Likert scale from ‘Not at all’ to ‘Very much so’. A majority of respondents (n = 89, 75.4%) reported a strong likelihood of frequent use, selecting ‘Very much so’. Furthermore, 48.3% (n = 57) indicated that the app was not ‘Cumbersome’ at all, while 49.2% (n = 58) reported that it was not ‘Too complicated’. Additionally, 39.3% (n = 46) rated the app as ‘Easy to use’, indicating a generally favourable perception. Conversely, the highest rates of negative responses were associated with the items ‘Needs assistance’ (28%, n = 33) and ‘Inconsistent’ (31.6%, n = 37).

Distribution of responses for SUS items (n = 118).

The mean SUS score for the APTT app was 73.2 (standard deviation, SD = 16.8), corresponding to the ‘Good’ usability range on the 100-point scale [16]. In terms of proficiency, 57.6% of respondents (n = 68/118) reported becoming proficient within 1 week, 17.8% (n = 21/118) immediately upon first use, another 17.8% within 1 week to 1 month, and 6.8% (n = 8/118) after more than 1 month.

In response to an open-ended question on the most inconvenient aspects of the app, the most frequently cited issue was ‘buffering and loading errors’ (n = 23). Other commonly reported issues included ‘lack of arrival time confirmation’ (n = 16), ‘notification errors’ (n = 13), ‘insufficient patient information’ (n = 10), and ‘character limits on delay/cancellation/special notes’ (n = 7).

Work utility

The impact of the app on work utility was evaluated across seven workflow items. As shown in Table 2, respondents generally agreed that the app positively influenced work utility, with mean scores ranging from 3.87 to 4.36 on a five-point Likert scale (1 = ‘Not affected at all’, 5 = ‘Highly affected’). Transport staff consistently reported higher scores across all items. Statistically significant differences between the two groups (transport staff vs. requesters) were observed in five out of the seven items (1, 2, 4, 6, and 7), indicating a more pronounced perceived benefit among app users.

Transport of older patients and information needed through patient transport

A substantial proportion of transport staff (80.5%, 95/118) agreed or strongly agreed that increasing patient age contributes to greater transport challenges (Fig. 3). Furthermore, 77.8% (91/117) of staff reported that transporting older adults (≥65 years) required additional time, with this figure increasing to 86.3% (101/117) for patients aged ≥75 years. In comparison, transport requesters reported lower agreement regarding age-related difficulty (48.4%, 30/62), although a majority acknowledged the need for extended transport time for both older (80.6%, 50/62) and much older (83.9%, 52/62) adults.

Darker colours in the graph indicate strong positive responses [‘Highly affected’ for (a) and ‘Very necessary’ for (b)], while lighter colours indicate strong negative responses [‘Not affected at all’ for (a) and ‘Not necessary at all’ for (b)]. Among transport staff, 70.3% (83/117) and 71.2% (84/117) reported insufficient information on patient mobility and communication abilities, respectively, with nearly half of them strongly agreeing (46.6%, 55/117 and 44.9%, 53/117). By contrast, lower levels of agreement were observed among transport requesters (35.5%, 22/62 for mobility; 42%, 26/62 for communication), suggesting a discrepancy in perceived informational adequacy between the two groups.

Both groups strongly endorsed the need for additional patient-specific data within the APTT app. Among transport staff, 57.6% (68/118) strongly supported the inclusion of mobility scores, and 56.8% (67/118) advocated for communication scores. Among transport requesters, support was slightly lower (mobility scores: 37.1%, 23/62; communication scores: 32.3%, 20/62).

Regarding essential information required during patient transport, transport staff highlighted fall risk (72.9%, 86/118), device monitoring (74.6%, 88/117), and accompaniment by medical personnel (73.7%, 87/118) as critical components. Transport requesters similarly prioritized, device monitoring (61.3%, 38/62), as well as medical staff accompaniment and equipment such as flow guards (53.2%, 33/62).

Discussion

Statement of principal findings

We evaluated the quality improvement, usability, and work utility of the APTT app, designed to facilitate patient transport and communication between transport staff and healthcare providers (ward nurses, emergency room nurses, and radiology reception staff) in a large tertiary hospital. Our findings suggest that the app has significantly improved various aspects of patient transport, especially with respect to efficiency, patient safety, and communication. Furthermore, we revealed that medical staff face significant challenges in transporting older patients, which underscores the need for age-specific consideration in communication.

The results indicate that the APTT app has positively influenced key areas of healthcare quality and work utility. Among the healthcare quality improvement index, efficiency, timeliness, and patient safety were the most prominently improved aspects (Fig. 1). This aligns with our previous study, which evaluated the app against the six IOM aims for improving healthcare and showed the most robust advancements in efficiency, timeliness, and patient safety [7]. These improvements suggest that the APTT app addresses critical challenges in patient transport identified in previous studies, such as coordination and safety issues [1]. This accords with previous studies highlighting the potential of mobile technology or hospital information systems to enhance quality of care [6, 17]. Furthermore, the high scores in work utility on all items, particularly among transport staff, demonstrate the app’s value in streamlining daily operations (Table 2). Statistically significant differences between transport staff and requesters in several utility items suggest that the app may benefit those directly involved in patient movement. When APTT is not present, the negatively affected factors are communication and patient-centredness, which are ranked higher than positive questionnaires. This suggests that, in addition to work efficiency, the APTT plays an irreplaceable role in patient communication and preparation before transfer.

With an SUS score of 73.2, the APTT app falls within the ‘Good’ usability range [16], suggesting that users generally perceive the APTT app as having good usability. The distribution of individual scores further supports this conclusion, with most scores clustering in approximately the 70–80 range. This indicates a consistent perception of the usability of the app among respondents, with few outliers. According to the SUS results, the most positively evaluated item was ‘frequently used’, and considering that it is an essential work app, the most (next) positively evaluated item in terms of usability was ‘easy to use the app’.

On the other hand, the most negatively evaluated item was ‘technical assistance is needed to use the app’. Given the app’s functions are simple, and its ease of use was highly evaluated, the above negative response may refer to support for app usage or network errors. In fact, the most problematic items pointed out in the subjective responses were ‘buffering and loading errors’, ‘lack of arrival time confirmation’, and ‘notification errors’, indicating that many people complained about inconveniences due to network or app technical issues. Additionally, the fact that more than half of the users took a week to become proficient in using the app suggests that the interface design is not user-friendly despite the app’s simple functions. This is coherent, as the SUS item ‘The app is too inconsistent’, was evaluated more negatively than other responses. Opportunities for refinement and improvement may still exist for future updates.

Interpretation within the context of the wider literature

Our results align with previous studies reporting that communication among medical staff is a crucial component of hospital operations and can be enhanced through information technology. Communication among healthcare staff has been shown to enhance work engagement, including improved working relationships and reduced staff turnover [18, 19]. In an orthopaedic department, the integration of a mobile telehealth application improved communication, patient management, and operational efficiency [20]. Another study demonstrated that the use of mobile communication instruments between consulting and emergency physicians proved to be an effective communication tool [21]. Along with these studies, our results emphasize digital communication tools in patient transportation.

During patient transport, clear communication between transport staff and medical personnel is crucial. Except in emergency situations, transport staff are not authorized to perform clinical monitoring or interventions during transfer. As patients are transferred beyond the direct supervision of medical staff and may have varying clinical conditions, effective pre-transport communication is essential to ensure safety [22]. In a prospective analysis of 688 patients, the most significant predictors of complications were operational and communication errors, as well as the severity of the patient’s condition [23]. A systematic review identified communication, efficiency, and appropriateness as key factors in improving non-emergency patient transport services. Standardized procedures and enhanced communication have been associated with potential reductions in risks [1].

Implications for policy, practice, and research

The survey revealed significant challenges in the transport of older patients, with a strong consensus that age increases transport difficulty and time requirements. Mobility issues in older adults are complex, resulting from physical limitations, cognitive impairments, and environmental factors [24]. This complexity underscores the need for a patient-centred, comprehensive approach to addressing the transportation needs of older adults, aligned with concepts such as the age-friendly health system model [25]. Our results indicate that medical staff require a broader range of information beyond the mobility status of patients (Fig. 3b). This includes details about patients’ communication abilities, sedation status, and the presence and role of caregivers. Such comprehensive information can help tailor the transport process to the specific needs of older patients, potentially improving efficiency and patient safety.

According to Fig. 3b, the discrepancies in the perceived importance of various types of information between patient transport staff and requesters highlight the need for improved communication and expectations alignment in the patient transport process. While both groups generally agree on the significance of most information types, transport staff consistently rate nearly all categories as more critical. The most notable differences appear in guardian accompaniment (73.7% vs. 48.4%), sedation information (56.8% vs. 43.5%), medical staff accompaniment (73.7% vs. 53.2%), and monitoring devices (74.6% vs. 61.3%). These discrepancies stem from their differing roles: requesters—mainly nurses or diagnostic unit staff—focus on their primary clinical duties and assist only at departure or arrival when necessary. While they remain responsible for the patient until transport begins, transport is not their main task. Conversely, transport staff are solely responsible for safe patient movement across the hospital, making accurate and detailed information essential for risk mitigation and workflow clarity.

While APTT supports basic task communication through a simple interface, our findings show that transport staff require more detailed patient and logistical information to carry out their work effectively. Beyond individual workflow support, the system offers organizational benefits, including improved traceability of transport events and enhanced clinical governance through real-time documentation of delays, cancellations, and handovers. Although APTT currently provides core task management functions, transport staff report a strong need for richer information to improve the quality and safety of intrahospital transport.

Strengths and limitations

Our study distinguishes itself from previous studies on patient transport systems in several key aspects. While most previous studies focused on optimizing or modelling these systems, our study evaluates the effectiveness and usability of a real-world smartphone application. In contrast, our approach focuses on the communication between patient transport staff and requesters, analysing user-centred survey results. Unlike the theoretical models or simulations used in many previous studies, our results offer practical insights from real-world implementation of a digital solution in a large tertiary hospital setting [2]. This approach provides valuable feedback that can directly inform improvements in patient transport systems and potentially enhance patient safety and hospital efficiency.

This study was conducted within a single large tertiary hospital, which may limit the generalizability to other healthcare settings, especially smaller or non-tertiary hospitals. The focus on the Emergency Department and the Geriatric Ward, which are characterized by high patient acuity and older adults with low mobility, may limit the applicability of the findings to other departments with different patient characteristics. The reliance on self-reported survey data introduces potential bias, such as recall bias and social desirability bias. Respondents may provide overly positive assessments of the app’s usability, leading to an overestimation of its effectiveness. In addition, the study did not include objective operational indicators such as transport turnaround times or transport-related incident rates, which limits our ability to quantify the actual magnitude of efficiency or safety improvements. The bias could potentially skew the results in favour of the app’s perceived utility. Furthermore, the absence of patient feedback limits the scope of the study, as understanding patient experience is crucial to fully evaluate its effectiveness.

Conclusions

The APTT app demonstrates significant potential in improving patient transport efficiency, safety, and communication within a large tertiary hospital setting. While the app shows good usability and high user satisfaction, clear opportunities for enhancement exist, particularly in addressing the unique needs of older patients. As healthcare continues to digitize, tools such as the APTT app are crucial for optimizing hospital operations and enhancing patient care.

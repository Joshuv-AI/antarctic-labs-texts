Electronic Properties of Small Psychotropic Substances in WaterPhenylamines
Authors: Roman Boča, Juraj Štofko, Cyril Rajnák
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Small molecules containing phenyl and aliphatic amine groups belonging to psychotropic drugs were studied by quantum chemical computational methods. Each of the 9 studied species (phenethylamine, amphetamine, ephedrine, pseudoephedrine, methamphetamine, MDMA, MDEA, MDA, MDAI) was considered in three oxidation states: neutral molecule, molecular cation, and molecular anion. Protonated residues from the hydrochloride forms were also considered. This allows, after full geometry optimization followed by vibrational analysis, the evaluation of global adiabatic electronic properties such as ionization energy, electron affinity, molecular electronegativity, chemical hardness, electrophilicity index, and absolute oxidation and reduction potentials. The ground-state molecular properties include electrical properties (atomic charges, dipole moment, quadrupole moment, and dipole polarizability), atomic spin densities, magnetic properties (NMR shielding constants), molar-mass-dependent molecular properties (solvated surface area and molecular volume), and thermodynamic properties (zero-point vibrational energy, entropic contribution, and Gibbs energy). Several correlations were found between molecular descriptors, e.g., molar-mass-dependent properties (dipole polarizability and zero-point vibration energy) vs molar mass, absolute oxidation potential vs adiabatic ionization energy, absolute reduction potential vs electrophilicity index, and 13C NMR shielding constants vs atomic charges. Electron subtraction upon ionization was monitored by inspection of local atomic characteristics, such as 13C NMR shielding constants and spin densities for the molecular cation. All calculations were done consistently with two quantum chemical methods in the extended basis set: B3LYP hybrid variant of Density Functional Theory and the post-Hartree–Fock ab initio method DLPNO–CCSD­(T), which includes the majority of the electron correlation energy. All calculations were done in water as a solvent. The number of studied systems is 108, i.e., 9 (molecules) × 2 (forms) × 3 (oxidation states) × 2 (methods).

Introduction

Small biogenic molecules, with a number of atoms less than 40, show important biological activity and useful properties. Some of them occur naturally; in the human body, they are synthesized and then metabolized; some, however, are essential and must be provided in foods.

The title compounds are psychoactive species that influence the central nervous system (Table ). They function as stimulants (phenethylamine, amphetamine, and ephedrine), and some are street drugs (methamphetamine, MDMA, MDEA, MDA, and MDAI). Certain aromatic amines can cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB), and some do not (dopamine). Individual species are often provided in the form of the well-soluble hydrochloride, so in blood at pH ∼ 7, there are equilibria between the neutral molecule M and its -ium cation M.H+ (e.g., amphetaminium). For instance, at room temperature, the pure free base of amphetamine is a colorless and volatile liquid. Frequently prepared solid salts of amphetamine include amphetamine hydrochloride, phosphate, sulfate, adipate, aspartate, saccharate, and tannate; these are white powders. Because of hydrolysis, the permeable form is the neutral hydrophobic base at the site of BBB. −

There is an evident structural similarity of the listed compounds but their biological functions are very diverse. It is not evident at first glance what the characteristic descriptors are that cause the differences between these substances. In order to quantify and compare the molecular descriptors, the quantum chemical calculations are feasible nowadays: they can be done at a high quality ab initio level in an accessible amount of time. −

Phenylamines, phenolamines, and catecholamines, as important objects, are the subjects of several reviews. − Phenylamines represented by amphetamine and related drugs have a close relationship to the biochemistry of catecholamines represented by dopamine; just dopamine exhibits extraordinary versatility and complexity.

Study Objective

This study is aimed at calculating molecular descriptors for a representative set of nine similar compounds belonging to the phenylamines. The main attention is paid to the adiabatic ionization energy and the associated global electronic properties (electron affinity, molecular electronegativity, chemical hardness, electrophilicity index, and absolute oxidation and reduction potentials), which determine the redox properties of the studied molecules. To allow for comparison, all data are generated by the same methods: sufficiently fast B3LYP and sufficiently exact DLPNO–CCSD­(T), in a sufficiently large basis set. Each molecule is studied in its canonical form and also in the protonated form in the normal, oxidized, and reduced states. The studied molecules are structurally similar, sometimes only with minor substituents, but they have a very different effect on the human brain. Therefore, it is of interest to study how these compounds differ in their electronic structure and redox properties in aqueous media.

Methods

The aim of quantum chemical calculations is the identification of the stationary geometry, spectrum of vibrational levels, and then calculations of electronic and thermodynamic properties. The ground-state molecular properties involve the electric properties (atomic charges, dipole moment, quadrupole moment, and dipole polarizability), atomic spin densities for open shell systems, magnetic properties (NMR shielding constants), molar-mass-dependent molecular properties (surface area and molecular volume), and thermodynamic properties (zero-point vibrational energy, entropic contribution, and Gibbs energy). The global electronic properties involve energy changes on the oxidation/reduction, i.e ionization energy, electron affinity, molecular electronegativity, chemical hardness, electrophilicity index, and absolute oxidation and reduction potentials.

There is a need for compromise between the computational time (cost) and the exactness of the calculation method. No doubt, an appropriate method should involve the electron correlation energy. We compromised using the B3LYP method for the geometry optimization, complete vibrational analysis, and evaluation of the ground-state electronic properties as well as the redox properties. The DLPNO–CCSD­(T) method was used to improve the electronic and redox properties in a more exact manner. Some critical points follow. Often the frontiers orbitals (HOMO and LUMO) are presented and deeply discussed in the literature. Note that within B3LYP, these are Kohn–Sham orbitals opposite to the Hartree–Fock orbitals known from the MO-LCAO-SCF method. They differ in definition and also in energy. HOMO energies often do not correspond to Koopmans’ theorem, according to which the ionization energy is E i = −E(HOMO).

Any transformation of the occupied orbitals for the ground state is allowed, which approves the application of localization criteria. This may reduce the less important contributions (tails of orbitals) prior to the configuration interaction. The unoccupied (virtual) orbitals have no physical significance and can be substituted by an appropriately chosen set of localized virtual orbitals such as pair natural orbitals (PNO). This is the success of the DLPNO approach, which can dramatically reduce the space and time requirements for the configuration interaction: DLPNO-MP2, DLPNO–CCSD, DLPNO–CCSD­(T). −

Biogenic molecules, including phenyl-, phenol-, and catecholamines, do not act in vacuo; they function in a solvent, most likely in water solutions. Therefore, all calculations done hereafter need be done by a method that mimics the situation in water solutions. For this purpose, the implicit CPCM method (Conductor-like Polarizable Continuum Model) was selected as a fast approximation which simulates the solvent as a polarizable continuum characterized by the dielectric constant (electric permittivity ε). − The solvent effect might be crucial: molecular anions are stabilized in a polar environment like water against the neutral molecules. It influences the zwitterionic forms of molecules (such as amino acids), which are more stable in water relative to the gas phase (vacuum).

In order to calculate the absolute redox potentials, an evaluation of the reaction Gibbs energy is necessary. Therefore, the neutral molecule, molecular cation, and molecular anion need be subjected to full geometry optimization followed by vibrational analysis (all vibration frequencies must be real). This is known as an adiabatic process opposite the vertical process taken in the frozen geometry.

Reliable quantum chemical calculations should be performed in a large enough basis set. The B3LYP calculations were done using the def2-TZVPD (triple-ζ-valence with polarization and diffuse functions) basis set which consists of 12s7p3d1f contracted to 6s4p3d1f for O, N, and C atoms, and 5s2p contracted to 3s2p for H. The DLPNO–CCSD­(T) method utilized the aug-cc-pVTZ basis set; O, N, and C: 19s6p3d2f contracted to 5s4p3d2f; H: 6s3p2d contracted to 4s3p2d; the auxiliary basis set was aug-cc-pVTZ/C. The augmented basis set is essential for a reliable description of molecular anions and is recommended to post-Hartree–Fock methods. ,

For open shell systems (oxidized and reduced forms), the unrestricted methods (different orbitals for different spins) were used: B3LYP utilizes the UKS (Unrestricted Kohn–Sham) variant, and the MO-LCAO preceding MP2 or CCSD­(T) methods utilizes the UHF (unrestricted Hartree–Fock) variant. All calculations were done using the ORCA_6.0.1 package. −

Critical notes about the used methodology follow. The B3LYP method, like other variants of DFT, does not correctly reproduce the dispersion interactions unless the corrected version B3LYP-D3BJ (Becke-Johnson) is applied. The calculated vibrational frequencies are strictly harmonic, opposite to reality where anharmonic frequencies occur. The solid-state X-ray structure, as a rule, refers to a single frozen conformer, whereas in solutions. many other conformers (rotamers) can be populated at ambient temperature. The CPCM model is an oversimplified model of solvation which, eventually, can be improved by a combination of explicit and implicit solvation. The salts, like hydrochlorides (ephedrine·H+)­Cl–, in the solid state feel the presence of counterions.

The calculated molecular properties can be compared with experimental data when available under critical assessments: X-ray structure analysis in solid state, infrared and Raman spectra in solid state, NMR spectra in solutions, dipole moment and polarizability in solutions, and ultravoltage photoelectron spectra (UPS) in gas phase.

Calculated molecular properties are briefly reviewed as follows.

The permanent dipole moment, p, describes the polarity of the molecule. As a vector quantity, it has three components, p i (i = x, y, and z). However, for a charged particle, its value depends on the coordinate system; then, the molecule is placed to the center of mass.

The quadrupole moment, Q ij , is a second-rank tensor having 9 Cartesian components represented by a 3 × 3 matrix; after diagonalization, it is characterized by three principal components which, after averaging, yield the isotropic value Q = (Q 11 + Q 22 + Q 33)/3. These data can be either negative or positive as they depend upon nuclear and electronic contributions.

The dipole polarizability, α ij , is also a second-rank tensor which represents the first-order response (derivative of energy) with respect to the external electric field. Its components are positive and commonly expressed by the isotropic value α.

The NMR shielding constant, σ ij , as a second-rank tensor, represents the effect of an external magnetic field on the selected nucleus having nuclear spin sitting below the electron cloud.

The zero-point vibrational energy, E zpe, represents a vibrational contribution to the inner energy of the molecule at the absolute zero temperature: U 0 = E + E zpe. It is available after a complete vibrational analysis assuming that all vibrational frequencies are real (positive force constants). Enthalpic correction δH ø = RT ø accounts for one mole of ideal gas. The total entropic term, S ø T ø, is calculated using the translational, rotational, and vibrational partition functions at the selected temperature. It is required for the evaluation of Gibbs energy.

The ionization energy, E i, is the amount for formation of a molecular cation from a neutral molecule: E i = E + – E 0. The electron affinity, E eg, is the amount by formation of molecular anion from neutral molecule: E eg = E– – E 0. We used abbreviation “electron gain” in order to differentiate from an older definition of electron affinity defined as energy released on electron attachment.

The molecular electronegativity, χ, is the first derivative of energy with respect to the number of electrons; it is approximated as χ = (E i – E eg)/2. It is negative of the chemical potential: χ = −μ. It can be viewed as an electronic gradient. The chemical hardness, η, is the second derivative of energy with respect to the number of electrons; it is approximated as η = (E i + E eg)/2. It can be viewed as an electronic force constant. The electrophilicity index, ω, represents the electrophilic power of molecule; it is expressed as ω = χ2/2η. − The absolute oxidation (reduction) potential E ox ⌀ (E red ⌀) is a transformed standard reaction Gibbs energy Δr G ⌀ on oxidation (reduction) using the Faraday constant F: E r ⌀=−Δr G ⌀/F.

Experimental Data

Phenylamines contain a pure benzene ring with an attached aliphatic amine. They include amphetamines and amphetamine-based drugs. The amphetamine group includes phenylethylamine, amphetamine, dextroamphetamine, ephedrine, pseudoephedrine, and lisdexamfetamine. They act as central nervous system stimulants. A group of amphetamine-type recreational drugs is derived from amphetamine: methamphetamine known as pervitin, MDMA known as ecstasy, MDEA known as Eva, MDA, and MDAI (Figure ).

Stimulants and drugs of the amphetamine group. MDMA = 3,4-methylenedioxy-N-methamphetamine; MDA = 3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine; MDEA = 3,4-methylenedioxy-N-ethylamphetamn; MDAI = 5,6-methylenedioxy-2-aminoindane.

Phenethylamine is the simplest member of the amphetamine group, found in some foods (chocolate) and known for its psychoactive effects. It acts as a neurotransmitter in the body and can affect mood, emotions, and physical performance.

Amphetamine is used as a medicine for ADHD (“Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder”), narcolepsy, and also obesity and overeating (“Binge eating disorder”). It appears in the form of levoamphetamine and dextroamphetamine enantiomers. It also acts as an aphrodisiac, euphoriant, and finds use as a performance-enhancing substance (“Performance-enhancing substance”PES, PED) and cognitive abilities. Lisdexamfetamine has similar effects and uses.

Ephedrine is a stimulant that increases blood pressure, the force of heart contractions, and heart rate; it is used to prevent low pressure during anesthesia, but also to treat narcolepsy, asthma, brochitis, and obesity, and also has use as a PES. Pseudoephedrine is a sympathomimetic that acts primarily as a decongestant. It narrows the blood vessels in the nasal mucosa, which reduces swelling and relaxes the airways. It is commonly used to treat colds, allergies, and sinusitis to relieve nasal congestion. The main difference between pseudoephedrine and ephedrine lies in their effects on the central nervous system and their indications. Pseudoephedrine is primarily a decongestant, while ephedrine has broader uses, including bronchodilation and CNS stimulation.

Methamphetamine is a street drug that stimulates the central nervous system. It effectively suppresses fatigue and the need for sleep, accelerates the psychomotor pace, and the expected effect is also extreme euphoria and increased self-confidence. This property is due to the extensive release of dopamine, noradrenaline, and serotonin into the synaptic clefts in the brain. However, long-term overuse can lead to damage or death of nerve cells due to oxidative stress (neurotoxicity). Abuse is also associated with a negative effect on the cardiovascular system.

MDMA as a drug produces euphoria, increased self-confidence, sociability, increased empathy or feelings of closeness with others and with oneself, dilated pupils, relaxation and reduced anxiety, increased emotionality, a sense of inner peace, mild hallucinations, improved perception and perception of sexuality, and altered sense for time. The onset of subjective effects is felt within 30 to 60 min after oral consumption, and the maximum effect is reached after 75 to 120 min, which then stabilizes at about 3.5 h.

Results and Discussion

Molecular Structure of Phenylamines

According to the CCDC database, the investigated amphetamines (AP) crystallize in the net form or as saltshydrochlorides AP·HCl, i.e., (AP·H+)­Cl– or (AP·H+)2SO4 (Figure ). Note, only a single conformer is stabilized in the solid state, while in a free form (gas phase, solutions), the number of conformers with N rotatable C–C bonds is 3 N (three dihedral angles R1–C–C–R2 = 60, 180, and 240 °). The PubChem database offers 10 structural isomers (conformers) for each studied phenylamine, as calculated by Molecular Mechanics (MMFF94).

Solid-state structures of amphetamines and their salts.

The geometry optimizations using B3LYP gave molecular structures that are close to the CCDC records or to the structures offered by PubChem (Figure ). In all cases, the aliphatic chain is oriented out of the benzene ring and there is not an indication of forming an intramolecular hydrogen bond.

Optimized molecular structures of amphetamines (B3LYP/def2-TZVPD).

Geometry optimization using B3LYP-D3BJ in water was repeated for various forms of phenethylamine. (D3BJ is a simple dispersion energy correction using an empirical formula.) The overlaid structures deposited in Figure S1 show no visible differences, so D3JB correction is ineffective in the present case.

Ab initio MP2/6–31G­(d,p) calculations were performed for nine isomers of amphetamine. It was found that their energy differences are marginal: max. 1.8 kcal mol–1; dipole moment varies between 1.2 and 1.8 D.

Ephedrine and pseudoephedrine are two isomeric substancesdiastereomers. The chemical properties should be almost identical, but the biological effects are different. The substances are supplied in the form of tablets. In crystalline form, they are nonidentical polymorphs with basic cell parameters (UC: a, b, c; α = β = γ = 90 °) and a theoretical powder diffraction pattern according to Figure . This means that these isomers are clearly distinguishable in the solid phase using powder X-ray diffractometry (XPD).

Powder diffraction patterns for ephedrine and pseudoephedrine as generated by Mercury.

Geometry optimizations for the protonated forms of phenylamines AF·H+ (with molecular charge q = +2, +1, and 0) gave structures analogous to the canonical forms AF (q = −1, 0, and +1).

Vibrational Spectra of Phenylamines

The calculated harmonic, unscaled vibrational frequencies of amphetamines are listed in Figure . (Scaling is a frequently used artificial process of “improving” the calculated spectra to match Experimental Data.) Important stretching O–H, N–H, and C–H vibrations are assigned.

Calculated wavenumbers in the vibrational spectra of amphetamines in water (B3LYP/def2-TZVPD). Intensities were truncated so that the low-intensity transitions are visible. (Values of intensities are presented in Table S3.)

The spectra for ephedrine and pseudoephedrine are similar but not identical so they can be distinguished. The spectra of AF.H+ are visibly different from parent AF, mainly at the highest frequencies where the stretching modes N–H occur. Here, the wavenumbers are moved to lower values as the cationic species are harder and new stretching N–H modes appear. On the contrary, the C–H stretchings are moved to higher wavenumbers. Comparison with experimental spectra in the solid state is problematic as the calculations refer to an aqueous environment.

The vibrational spectra of amphetamines were studied in several articles in conjunction with the stereo isomers. − A comparison of the experimental and calculated vibrational spectra is presented in Table S2. Berg et al. used B3LYP/6–311G+(d,p) calculations in vacuo which predicts the “S-ZC-conformer” as the most stable form of three conformers for AF and AF·H+. (S denotes enantiomer; Z-shape refers to dihedral angle Cring–CH2–CH–CMet = 180 °.) The solid-state Raman spectrum of (AF.H+)2SO4 shows the highest asymmetric stretching wavenumber of NH3 group at 3474 cm–1 as compared to calculated 3486 cm–1; , our data in water are 3485 cm–1. Taplin et al. reported Raman and SERS (Surface-Enhanced Raman Scattering) spectra for phenethylamine (PE), ephedrine (EP), and MDMA in the range 500–1750 cm–1; the experiments were complemented by B3LYP/6–31+G­(d,p) calculations. Note, the Raman and SERS spectra only slightly depend on the wavelength of the incident beam. Zhao et al. measured Raman spectra for amphetamine, methamphetamine, and ephedrine. Dobšíková et al. did conformational analysis of amphetamine and methamphetamine using B3LYP/6–31G­(d) calculations combined with VCR (Vibrational Circular Dischoism) and IR spectroscopy. Torsional angles, Gibbs free energies, and corresponding Boltzmann weights for optimized stable conformers at the B3PW91/6–311++G** level were presented. The IR, VCD, Raman, and ROA intensities were also calculated. The solvation effects were considered to involve the conductor-like screening model (COSMO) for water. Bruni et al. utilized the B3LYP/6–31G** method for creating conformers of 21 pairs of amphetamine and cathinone homologs by varying two dihedral angles by 30 ° and then reoptimization by three DFT-based methods B3LYP, B3LYP-D3, and M062-X; afterward, IR spectra were evaluated. SERS and Raman spectra of amphetamine, methamphetamine, MDMA, MDA, and MDE were reported by Sägmüller et al.

Ground-State Properties of Phenylamines

The calculated ground-state molecular properties for a series of amphetamines are presented in Table . Ephedrine and pseudoephedrine are diastereoisomers; however, their molecular descriptors are slightly different (the dipole moment is visibly different). Protonation results in a decrease of the quadrupole moment toward the isotropic value and a decrease of the polarizability. These are averaged values defined as a trace of the diagonalized second-rank tensor. At the same time, the dipole moment rises dramatically. (The dipole moment of a charged particle depends on the placement in the coordinate system.) Molar-mass-dependent molecular properties (solvated surface, solvated volume, polarizability, zero-point energy, total entropic term) increase with increasing molar mass, as exemplified in Figure where a linear correlation is evident. This is not the case of dipole moment, which varies with molar mass in an unsystematic way.

Low ionization energies can lead to increased antioxidant capacity (the ability to donate an electron and a proton) to scavenge free radicals. For example, catecholamines (dopamine, noradrenaline, adrenaline) are known to be potent antioxidants. Their adiabatic ionization energies in water were calculated by B3LYP as E i = 126 – 128 kcal mol–1 in their canonical forms, and 99 kcal mol–1 in the less stable zwitterionic forms. The adiabatic ionization energies of phenylamines 1–4 calculated by the same method are E i = 124–133 kcal mol–1 and the street drugs 5–9 have E i = 120 – 124 kcal mol–1. These are close to catecholamines, so a considerable antioxidant capacity of them can be expected.

Explicit-Implicit Solvation

To apply the hybrid explicit-implicit solvation model, four H2O molecules were added to the ephedrine molecule (Ep) near the functional groups; then, geometry optimization was performed by using the B3LYP/CPCM method for neutral (q = 0) and ionized (q = +1) clusters. During the geometry optimization, the water molecules move to find a global energy minimum: they aggregate around the −OH functional group. The optimal geometries of the pure Ep­(aq) molecule and the cluster Ep.4H2O­(aq) were compared, and the overlay figure confirms only small changes after explicit solvation (Figure S5). Quantitative energy data are presented in Table ; they show that the adiabatic ionization energy of Ep.4H2O­(aq) is lower by about 5 kcal mol–1 compared to that of the pure Ep­(aq) molecule.

Conclusions

Two quantum chemical computational methods were used to study nine phenylamines of the amphetamine class with a phenyl ring and substituted ethylamine chain: phenethylamine, amphetamine, ephedrine, pseudoephedrine, methamphetamine, MDMA, MDEA, MDA, and MDAI. Geometry optimization performed by the B3LYP method (in water as a solvent) yielded a basic structure with the ethylamine group arranged as a trans isomer with a torsion angle C­(ph)–C–C-N ∼180°. This is in agreement with the available X-ray structural data. The molar-mass-dependent molecular properties (solvated surface, solvated volume, polarizability, zero-point energy, and total entropic term) increase with increasing molar mass. The NMR shielding constants σ­(Cal 3) and σ­(Cring 3) correlate with the Mulliken atomic charges along a straight line: more negative charge, larger shielding constant. Independent geometry optimization for the cation and anion of each compound allows evaluation of adiabatic ionization energies and adiabatic electron affinities. These were further processed to obtain global molecular properties such as molecular electronegativity, chemical hardness, and electrophilicity index. The protonated forms (AP·H)+ (often prescribed as hydrochloride salts) exhibit increased ionization energies which is reflected in increased molecular electronegativity and higher electrophilicity index compared to the canonical form (AP). The decreased ionization energies along the amphetamine series (1–9) may lead to increased antioxidant capacity (ability to donate an electron and a proton). The global electronic properties obtained by the ab initio post-Hartree–Fock DLPNO–CCSD­(T) method represent accurate data that account for most of the electron correlation energy when compared with canonical CCSD­(T). The absolute oxidation potential, based on the reaction Gibbs energy on oxidation, correlates with the ionization energy along a straight line; an analogous correlation shows the absolute reduction potential versus the electrophilicity index.

Standardized neutralizing antibody assay for Coxsackievirus A10 in clinical trial: A framework for multivalent hand, foot, and mouth disease vaccine evaluation
Authors: Qian Wang, Lu Li, Junying Gao, Mingchen Liu, Ying Wang, Qian He, Wenhui Wang, Yajing Li, Meirong Gu, Zhongping Xie, Qingwei Liu, Jinle Han, Zhongyang Zhang, Jing Guo, Xing Wu, Qunying Mao, Xiaoqi Chen, Zhenglun Liang, Fan Gao, Xiao Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTEnteroviruses (EVs) are the major etiological agents of hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD), which could lead to severe complications and even fatalities. Owing to the alternating or co-circulation of multiple EV serotypes and the lack of cross-protection, global institutions are pursuing the development of multivalent HFMD vaccines. Notably, the advancement of innovative multivalent HFMD vaccines into the clinical stage highlights the urgent need for a standardized platform to assess immunogenicity. In accordance with international regulatory guidelines, a standardized neutralizing antibody (NtAb) assay for CV-A10 is established through rigorous selection and systematic optimization of key assay components, representing the first such assay tailored for clinical immunogenicity evaluation of CV-A10-based vaccines. By integrating the 1st Chinese National Standard (NS) for CV-A10 NtAb and a standard detection virus, this assay exhibits enhanced detection sensitivity and significant reduction in the inter-laboratory geometric coefficient of variation (GCV), thereby providing a robust, reliable, and transferable platform. This assay facilitates accurate evaluation of multivalent HFMD vaccine efficacy, improves comparability across different vaccine products and provides genuine efficacy in real-world, serving as a valuable reference for global regulatory authorities.

Introduction

Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) is an acute infectious disease primarily affecting children under 5 y of age. Although most cases are mild and self-limiting, a small proportion of patients may develop severe complications such as encephalitis, brainstem encephalitis, cardiopulmonary failure, and even death.1 Among Enteroviruses (EVs), the primary etiological agents responsible for HFMD,2 Enterovirus A71 (EV-A71) has been identified as a major pathogen associated with severe cases.3–5 Since the first EV-A71 inactivated vaccine was approved for market use in 2015, EV-A71-associated HFMD has been largely controlled in China, with significantly reduced incidence of severe disease and mortality.6–8

However, EV-A71 vaccine is only an initial step toward comprehensive HFMD control. The efficacy of monovalent EV-A71 vaccine is limited due to the complex EVs epidemiology and the lack of cross-protective immunity between different EV serotypes.7,9,10 In response, manufacturers are actively developing innovative multivalent HFMD vaccines, some of which have progressed to clinical trials (e.g., CTR20251162, NCT06754384), highlighting the urgent need for standardized immunogenicity assessment.

Neutralizing antibodies (NtAbs) are widely recognized as the primary correlate of protection and a key clinical endpoint for assessing vaccine-induced immunogenicity.11–13 However, key assay variables, particularly the choice of the detection strain, can significantly influence the NtAb titer.9,14,15 Inconsistencies in detection strain selection pose a significant challenge for the reliable and direct comparison of efficacy across multivalent HFMD vaccines produced by various manufacturers globally. Despite its crucial role in estimating NtAb titers during vaccine research and development, the selection of an appropriate detection strain has not received adequate attention.

To address this critical gap, we aimed to identify a detection strain that demonstrates high and homogeneity cross-neutralizing activity against a diverse panel of immune sera under realistic assay conditions.15 The S105 strain was selected as a standardized strain through evaluation with multivalent HFMD-immunized sera from various sources, as well as human naturally infected sera and human immunoglobulins. Then, a standardized NtAb assay was established by integrating this strain with the 1st Chinese National Standard (NS) for CV-A10 NtAb.16 The assay development adhered to international guidelines (ICH Q2(R2)/Q14) and the Chinese Pharmacopoeia <9307>, incorporating the Analytical Quality by Design (AQbD) principles. This integrated approach provides a robust, reliable, and standardized platform for assessing CV-A10 immunogenicity. It thereby supports the comparative evaluation of potencies in clinical trials involving vaccines from different manufacturers and addresses critical challenges in regulatory science.

Materials and methods

Materials

6–7-week-old female mice of KM or NIH, 180–220 g rat of SD or Wistar, and 3.5–4.5-y-old female and male Cynomolgus macaques (Macaca fascicularis) were immunized with quadrivalent HFMD vaccines (EV-A71, CV-A6, CV-A10 and CV-A16) for two doses by each manufacture, respectively. Fifteen human naturally infected sera were provided by the Division of In Vitro Diagnostic, the National Institutes for Food and Drug Control (NIFDC) in China. Provided by Hualan Biological Engineering Inc, six commercial human immunoglobulins were isolated from large pools of plasma donated by healthy individuals. The virus strains Kowalik, S101, S105, S110, S108, and S102 were collected and preserved in our laboratory.15 The working seed bank of human epithelioid Rhabdomyosarcoma (RD) cells (passage 65) was preserved by NIFDC.15

Fetal bovine serum (FBS) was purchased from the following suppliers: Lanzhou National Hyclone Bio-Engineering Co., Ltd. (Cat. No.: SC111.02), Zhejiang Tianhang Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Cat. No.: 11011–8611), Gibco (Cat. No.: A5256701), and CellMaxcell Technology (Beijing) Co., Ltd. (Cat. No.: SA112.02). FBS was subjected to heat-inactivation at 56°C for 30 min before usage. MEM medium was obtained from a commercial source (Cat. No.: 00680).

Quality control (QC) 1, 2 3, and CV-A10 seronegative serum used for specificity qualification were selected from human naturally infected individuals purchased from the Wuhan Institute of Biological Products Co., Ltd. Virus-specific immune mice sera and hemolyzed whole blood matrix were generously provided by Sinovac Biotech Co., Ltd. The 1st Chinese National Standard (NS) for CV-A10 NtAb (Code: 300044, 400 U/mL) was developed and provided by NIFDC.16 The NtAb tolerance range of the NS for S105 was 401 to 2156, determined by 30 tests conducted by two analysts in five replicates over 3 d and calculated with JMP 13®.

Collected from naturally infected individuals, the human sera (high, medium, low) used for method applicability were provided by Hualan Biological Engineering Inc.

NtAb assay

Serum samples or NS were initially diluted 1:8. Then, 100 μL of each dilution was aliquoted into a 96-well cell culture plate after heat-inactivation of serum samples at 56°C for 30 min, with each sample/NS tested in duplicate. Using 50 μL of MEM (2% FBS), the samples or NS were subsequently subjected to two-fold serial dilutions. Then, 50 μL of the virus suspension (containing 100 CCID50/0.05 ml) was added to wells for virus control, NS, and sample dilutions. The cell control wells received 100 μL MEM (2% FBS) only. The plates were neutralized at 37 ± 1°C with 5% CO2. After the incubation period, 100 μL of cells suspended with maintenance medium was added. The plates were incubated (35 ± 1°C, 5% CO2) for indicated days. Cytopathic effect (CPE) was observed to calculate the NtAb and virus back-titration value. A CV-A10 NtAb < 8 was considered negative, whereas a CV-A10 NtAb ≥ 8 was considered positive. The NtAbs were log2-transformed to calculate the GMT.

Virus back-titration was performed using the same detection strains, which was serially diluted 10-fold with MEM (2% FBS) to obtain four virus dilutions, with concentrations ranging from 100 to 0.1 CCID50/0.05 mL. Each dilution was back-titrated in 12 wells per dilution, followed by incubation at 35 ± 1°C under 5% CO2.

The assay was considered valid if the virus control showed CPE, the cell control showed no CPE, the virus back-titration result was within the range from 32 to 320 CCID50/0.05 mL, and the NtAb of NS fell into the established tolerance range.

Relative accuracy and intermediate precision

The mean NtAb of QC1, based on 32 tests together with NS, was determined to be 948 U/mL. The NtAb of QC1 and its two-fold serial dilutions were measured by two analysts, each in three replicates, over 2 d. The 12 valid runs per sample were used to calculate the relative accuracy (Relative Bias, RB) and intermediate precision (Geometric Coefficient of Variation, GCV) of the assay. Basing on the assigned efficacy (400 U/mL) of the NS, the NtAb was calculated using the following formula:

NtAb Sample (U/mL) = Geometric mean titer (GMT) Sample /GMTNS × 400 U/mL

Results

Analytical target profile (ATP) and risk assessment

The ATP, Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs), and requirements for reporting value were summarized in Table S1. As primary performance attributes, the acceptance criteria of relative accuracy and intermediate precision were set at within ±50% or less than 100% to meet the evaluation requirements.

The classic CPE method was selected for NtAb assay,3 with risk analysis conducted on 25 potential influencing factors (Figure S1). Each factor was scored based on its potential impact on relative accuracy and intermediate precision. Twelve major risk factors were identified (score ≥ 4, Table S2), among which the detection cell line, passage/dosage of the detection strain, and criterion for judging CPE were predetermined.14,18

Virus strain selection

A panel of virus strains from different periods and regions – Kowalik (prototype strain, A genotype), S101, S105, S110, S108, and S102 (C3 sub-genotype) – covering a broad spectrum of neutralizing activity15 was selected as candidates of the CV-A10 detection strain. The capacity of being neutralized was evaluated using sera from naturally infected humans (Figure 1.A), human immunoglobulins (Figure 1.B), and multivalent HFMD vaccine-immunized mice or cynomolgus macaques (Figure 1.C). The S105 strain was chosen for its superior capability of being neutralized by all virus-induced antibodies (Figure 1.A–C).

Method establishment

Condition optimization was conducted using one-factor experimental design for FBS brand, cell density, cell passage, edge effect, and incubation duration; for potential interactive factors – the time post virus dilution and the neutralization time – customized Design of Experiments (DoE) was implemented using JMP 13® (Table S3).

Single-factor condition optimization

Virus titers of S105 were measured with different brands of FBS after heat-inactivated at 56°C for 30 min. Except for FBS 4 which yielded a notably lower virus titer, no significant differences were observed among FBS 1–3 (Figure 1.D). Concurrently, FBS 1 demonstrated higher NtAb titers in QC samples compared to FBS 2 and/or FBS 3 (Figure 1.E). Thus, FBS 1 was selected for subsequent experiments.

To determine the optimal cell density and incubation duration compatible with the assay, NtAb titers were measured using indicated cell seeding densities, with readings taken after 5–8 d of incubation (Figures 1.F and S2.A). For QC1, significant differences (p < .05) were observed between 5 × 103 cells/well and 2 × 104 or 3 × 104 cells/well groups (Figure 1.F). A range of 1 × 104 to 3 × 104 cells/well was deemed appropriate to minimize experimental variability. Furthermore, QC3 titers taken after 8 d of incubation (3 × 104 cells/well) were significantly lower than those at 5 or 6 d (Figure S2.B). Therefore, an incubation period of 5–7 d was recommended.

No significant difference (p > .05) was observed between NtAb titers from center and fringe wells (Figures 1.G and S2.C). Additionally, tested cell passages did not affect the detection of QC samples (Figure 1.H).

Customized design

A customized design was used to investigate the potential interaction between the time post virus dilution and the neutralization time, as well as to define the method operational design regions (MODR) (Table S4). When the time post virus dilution was less than 1 h and the neutralization time varied within 1–3 h, the NtAb titer of QC1 remained within its acceptable tolerance range, and the virus titer stayed within the acceptable range (32–320 CCID50/0.05 mL) (Figure S1.D). Therefore, it is recommended to maintain the post-dilution virus holding time within 1 h and the neutralization time between 1 and 3 h to ensure assay consistency.

Method validation

Specificity

The assay showed potent specificity to CV-A10-immunized sera with NtAb > 16384 (NtAb ≥ 8 as positive) and NtAb of EV-A71, CV-A6, CV-A16, Poliovirus (PV), or Hepatitis A Virus (HAV) –immunized sera was < 8 (negative). Furthermore, when mixed sera samples were analyzed, CV-A10 NtAbs were reliably detected among the various combinations with no significant difference (p > .05) (Table S5, Figure 1.I). Human sera mixtures of QC1 diluted in MEM or the seronegative serum with or without the hemolyzed whole blood matrix were tested to assess matrix interference (Table S6). The results showed no significant difference (p > .05) between the QC1-MEM control group and mixed sera samples, demonstrating the robust assay against matrix interference (Figure 1.J).

Sample stability

To evaluate the impact of handling conditions, samples were subjected to various treatments with NtAb titers detected, which showed no significant difference between samples and controls (p > .05) (Figure S3), supporting the reliability of the assay in routine testing.

Method applicability

To validate the broad applicability across different laboratory settings, samples containing high, medium, and low concentrations of CV-A10 NtAbs were distributed to three laboratories qualified for collaborative study. Each laboratory performed four to six independent CV-A10 neutralization assays using both in-house method and the standardized method described herein. Discussion

Global outbreaks of HFMD caused by EVs, including CV-A6, CV-A10, and CV-A16, have persisted and intensified, posing significant threats to pediatric health and imposing substantial economic burdens on families and healthcare systems, particularly in developing countries.14,19–23 As multiple candidate HFMD vaccines progress through development (Table S7), the absence of standardized NtAb assays has emerged as a critical barrier for comparing efficacy across platforms and ensuring regulatory standardization.

This study introduces a standardized NtAb assay for CV-A10, addressing a key gap in the development of vaccines against HFMD. This assay directly supports the evaluation of innovative multivalent HFMD vaccine candidates. By incorporating optimized and validated key parameters (Table S8), along with the CV-A10 NS and a carefully selected standard detection strain, the assay demonstrates high specificity, reliability, reproducibility, and standardization. It provides a first practical framework for establishing efficacy assessment approaches for multivalent vaccines globally and aligns with global regulatory expectations.24–27

Furthermore, QC samples implemented in this research are applicable to clinical trial. This supports the ongoing monitoring and full lifecycle management of the method, mirroring the principles outlined in WHO’s guidelines for vaccine quality control, which emphasize the use of reference materials and validated methods.28,29

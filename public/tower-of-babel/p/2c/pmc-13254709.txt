Bilateral lens subluxation in a patient with homocystinuria: a case report
Authors: Zongyi Zhan, Jiachun Cai, Jiaming Wang, Xin Liu, Danyao Nie, Dahui Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo report a case of bilateral lens subluxation associated with homocystinuria that had been previously misdiagnosed with high myopia, amblyopia, and keratoconus.ObservationsA 19-year-old Asian female presented with progressive bilateral visual decline and hand tremors over 10 years. Slit-lamp examination, anterior segment optical coherence tomography, genetic and metabolic testing were performed to confirm the diagnosis of homocystinuria. Surgical intervention was undertaken to improve visual acuity. Anterior segment imaging documented bilateral lens subluxation and microspherophakia before and after intraocular lens suture fixation surgery. Postoperatively, her vision improved to 20/20.Conclusions and importanceThis case demonstrates the importance of basic slit-lamp examination especially after cycloplegic refraction for unusual myopia progression and the need to recognize associated systemic symptoms in ophthalmic practice.

Case report

A 19-year-old Asian female presented with progressive bilateral visual decline over 10 years. At age 12, her refractive error was −11.75 DS/−2.25 DC × 170° in the right eye and −12.00 DS/−2.25 DC × 180° in the left eye, with best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) of 20/30 both eyes. By age 18, her refraction had progressed to −23.50 DS/−4.00 DC × 160° in the right eye and −23.00 DS/−1.00 DC × 180° in the left eye, with BCVA of 20/25 right eye and 20/50 left eye. Her intraocular pressure was normal in both eyes. She had been previously diagnosed as high myopia, amblyopia, and keratoconus. Pre-dilation slit-lamp examination showed shallow anterior chambers bilaterally (central depth ∼3 CT), with the left eye demonstrating lens edge incarceration in the pupil and partial iris atrophy. Slit-lamp examination under dilation and anterior segment optical coherence tomography revealed bilateral lens subluxation and microspherophakia (Fig. 1A, B, 1C, and 1D). Her right eye had an axial length of 25.11 mm and her left eye 24.93 mm, measured by IOLMaster. Associated lighter hair and hand tremors were noticed at clinical visit, and her parent reported history of suspected hyperthyroidism without medication because of hand tremors since 8-year-old. Her growth and intellectual development were normal. Genetic and metabolic testings were suggested, confirming homocystinuria with cystathionine beta-synthase (CBS) deficiency. To reduce the degree of myopia, prevent elevated intraocular pressure caused by pupillary block, and improve visual quality, we performed bilateral clear lens extraction and intraocular lens (IOL) fixation by scleral suturing techniques (Fig. 2A and B). Sutures were placed at the 11 and 5 o'clock positions under the sclera. The suture material was 9-0 polypropylene (2452L, MANI, Japan) with the suture placed 2.0 mm posterior to the limbus. The suture was looped around the IOLs (RAO600C, Rayner, UK.) haptics with a girth hitch through eyelets, and the haptics were drawn into the ciliary sulcus. After surgeries, her vision improved to −0.25 DS/−4.00 DC × 180° in the right eye and −0.50 DS/−4.00 DC × 180° in the left eye, with BCVA of 20/20 both eyes. (Fig. 2A and B). Subsequent echocardiography, D-dimer test, and bone mineral density examinations revealed normal findings. She received a course of oral medication comprising folic acid: 5 mg, orally, once daily, betaine: 2000 mg, orally, three times daily, mecobalamin: 500 μg, orally, once daily, vitamin B6: 300 mg, orally, three times daily. Discussion

Homocystinuria is an autosomal recessive metabolic disorder caused by CBS deficiency, is characterized by markedly elevated plasma homocysteine and methionine levels, causing diversity manifestations including lens subluxation, thromboembolism, skeletal deformities, and developmental delay. The worldwide prevalence of homocystinuria is estimated to be minimum incident of 0.02:100,000 in Asia by neonatal screening, which was lower than that in Europea, Latin America, and Africa.1 Ocular findings including ectopia lens and high myopia are the most common manifestations associated with homocystinuria.2 Although the ocular symptoms can be the first manifestations, the diagnosis was often delayed.2,3 It is worthwhile that early diagnosis is important, for without treatment, there is a 50% chance of having a thrombotic event by the age of 304. It is crucial to enhance the recognition and differential diagnosis skills for this condition in the ophthalmology clinic. On the other hand, an eye examination after mydriasis is necessary, particularly for children with abnormal myopia progression, to rule out lens-induced myopia, glaucoma, and vitreoretinal diseases.

To address the high degree of refractive error, and the risk of elevated intraocular pressure due to potential pupillary block caused by bilateral lens subluxation and microspherophakia, we chose to extract the bilateral clear lenses and implant IOLs rather than perform YAG laser peripheral iridotomy. Because YAG peripheral iridotomy cannot address the high degree of refractive error or lens-induced pupillary block, although it may prevent elevated intraocular pressure. IOL fixation in eyes without capsular support remains a challenge. There are two common approaches: scleral-sutured IOL implantation and sutureless scleral-fixated IOL implantation.5 Sutureless scleral-fixated IOL using the Yamane technique, which requires no conjunctival dissection and reduces surgical time, has its own shortcomings, including iris capture, vitreous hemorrhage, hypotony, retinal detachment, and a higher frequency of IOL tilt.6,7 For this case, considering the patient's age, exercise requirements and existed iris atrophy, we made the safer choice of scleral-sutured IOLs using 9-0 polypropylene to reduce the risk of late IOL dislocation due to suture breakage.

Although fewer complications have been reported,5 the risks of suture degradation and breakage with subsequent IOL dislocation, retinal detachment, and endophthalmitis still require long-term follow-up.

Conclusion

We present this case to show the importance of basic slit-lamp examination before and after cycloplegic refraction for usual myopia progression, and the need to recognize associated systemic symptoms in ophthalmic practice.

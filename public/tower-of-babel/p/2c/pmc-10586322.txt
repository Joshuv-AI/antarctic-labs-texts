Triosephosphate Isomerase: The Crippling Effect of the P168A/I172A Substitution at the Heart of an Enzyme Active Site
Authors: Rania Hegazy, John P Richard
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe P168 and I172 side chains sit at the heart of the active site of triosephosphate isomerase (TIM) and play important roles in the catalysis of the isomerization reaction. The phosphodianion of substrate glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (GAP) drives a conformational change at the TIM that creates a steric interaction with the P168 side chain that is relieved by the movement of P168 that carries the basic E167 side chain into a clamp that consists of the hydrophobic I172 and L232 side chains. The P168A/I172A substitution at TIM from Trypanosoma brucei brucei (TbbTIM) causes a large 120,000-fold decrease in kcat for isomerization of GAP that eliminates most of the difference in the reactivity of TIM compared to the small amine base quinuclidinone for deprotonation of catalyst-bound GAP. The I172A substitution causes a > 2-unit decrease in the pKa of the E167 carboxylic acid in a complex to the intermediate analog PGA, but the P168A substitution at the I172A variant has no further effect on this pKa. The P168A/I172A substitutions cause a 5-fold decrease in Km for the isomerization of GAP from a 0.9 kcal/mol stabilization of the substrate Michaelis complexes. The results show that the P168 and I172 side chains play a dual role in destabilizing the ground-state Michaelis complex to GAP and in promoting stabilization of the transition state for substrate isomerization. This is consistent with an important role for these side chains in an induced fit reaction mechanism [Richard, J. P. (2022) Enabling Role of Ligand-Driven Conformational Changes in Enzyme Evolution. Biochemistry 61, 1533–1542].

Introduction

Triosephosphate isomerase (TIM) catalyzes the reversible deprotonation of (R)-glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (GAP, Scheme 1)1−5 by the carboxylate side chain of E167 to form an O-1 enediolate reaction intermediate that then undergoes enzyme-mediated intramolecular proton transfer between enediolate oxygens6 and reprotonation by E167 to form dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP). Knowles and co-workers reported the free energy reaction profiles for TIM-catalyzed isomerization and showed that these profiles satisfy two criteria for perfection in catalysis.2

Isomerization Reaction Catalyzed by Triosephosphate Isomerase

Interactions between the protein and the substrate phosphodianion provide binding energy to drive a large protein conformational change that traps the substrate in a protein cage.7−9 We have proposed that the protein conformational change activates the catalytic side chains of TIM for catalysis of substrate deprotonation by strongly favoring deprotonation of the protein-cage-bound substrate compared to substrate deprotonation in water.3,7,10,11 We focused on two important features of the ligand-driven protein conformational change.

(1) The binding of substrates GAP and DHAP or intermediate analogs such as phosphoglycolate (PGA) triggers the closure of the 11-residue phosphodianion gripper loop 6 [168-PVWAIGTGKTA] over the enzyme-bound phosphodianion (Figure 1).12 The movement of loop 6 is concerted with 90° and 180° rotations, respectively, in the planes defined by amide bonds for G211 and G212 from loop 7 [210-YGGS].13−16 This moves the β-hydroxyl of S213 into a position to form hydrogen bonds, respectively, with the backbone carbonyl oxygen and amide −NH from A171 and G175 and the phenol oxygen of Y210 into a position to hydrogen bond with the backbone amide −NH from A178 (Figure 1).13−15 Substitutions at Y210 [Y210T, S, A, F], at S213 [S213A, G] or the double Y210T/S213G exchange that perturb or eliminate loop-clamping hydrogen bonds result in significant falloffs in the enzyme catalytic efficiency.17−19

The figure shows the 6 Å displacement of flexible loop 6 that gives rise to the interloop and loop-substrate hydrogen bonds.

(2) The P168 and I172 side chains are completely conserved at 199 sequences of TIM.20

The rotation of the peptide bonds at loop 7 described above creates a steric interaction between the backbone carbonyl oxygen of G211 and the pyrrolidine side chain of P168 that is relieved by the movement of this and the neighboring E167 carboxylate side chain toward the carbon acid substrate (Figure 2).21,22 This motion is concerted with the movement of the I172 side chain toward the E167 carboxylate that traps the base in a hydrophobic clamp that consists of I172 from loop 6 and the stationary L232 side chain from loop 8 (not shown in Figure 2). Movement of the E167 carboxylate into this clamp is accompanied by a large increase in the pKa for the carboxylic acid, from ≈4 for unliganded TIM23 to pKa > 10 at the inhibitor complex.10 This increase in side chain basicity satisfies the prime catalytic imperative that TIM reduces the thermodynamic barrier to deprotonation of the enzyme-bound carbon acid substrate.24−26

Superposition of the unliganded form of TbbTIM (green, PDB entry 5TIM) with the complex between E65Q LmTIM and PGA (purple, PDB entry 1N55). The figure shows the movement of the E167 and I172 side chains toward the enediolate analog PGA during the phosphodianion-driven enzyme conformational change.

The P168 and I172 side chains sit in plain view at the heart of the active site of TIM, but their roles in the catalysis of substrate isomerization were only first suggested in the twenty-first century by Wierenga.8,21 These roles were examined by determining the effect of P168A and I172A substitutions on: (a) the kinetic parameters for TIM-catalyzed deprotonation of whole substrates and for phosphite dianion activation of deprotonation of the truncated substrate glycolaldehyde;21,27 (b) the pKa of the carboxylic acid side chain of E167 complexed to the PGA transition state analog;10,11 and (c) the X-ray crystal structures for unliganded TIM and the complex to PGA.21,28 The substitutions each cause ca 100-fold decreases in the second-order rate constant kcat/Km for substrate isomerization,21,27 and the I172A substitution causes a ca 2 unit decrease in the pKa of the E167 carboxylic acid at the PGA complex that implicates this alkyl side chain in the enhancement of basicity of E167.10,11 The substitutions cause small but mechanistically important changes in the protein structure or side chain hydration at the enzyme active site.21,28

We report here the effect of the P168A/I172A double substitutions on the kinetic parameters for TbbTIM-catalyzed isomerization of GAP and on the pKa of the E167 side chain at the complex to PGA. These substitutions cause an opposing 1 kcal/mol stabilization of the ground-state Michaelis complex to GAP and a 6 kcal/mol destabilization of the transition state for deprotonation of the enzyme-bound substrate, where the latter eliminates most of the large difference between the reactivity of TIM compared to the small Brønsted base quinuclidinone as catalysts of deprotonation of the carbon acid substrates.29 These opposing effects provide compelling evidence that the P168 and I172 side chains play key roles in an induced-fit reaction mechanism of destabilizing the substrate Michaelis complex and in promoting stabilization of the transition state for carbon-acid deprotonation.30

Experimental Section

Materials

Water was obtained from a Milli-Q academic purification system. Bovine serum albumin (BSA) fraction V was obtained from Roche. Sodium hydroxide (NaOH, 1.0 N), hydrochloric acid (HCl, 1.0 N), sodium chloride, and dithiothreitol were purchased from Fisher. GAP, DHAP lithium salt, the disodium salt of β-nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide reduced form (NADH), triethanolamine hydrochloride (TEA), 2-(N-morpholino) ethanesulfonic acid (MES), 3-(N-morpholino) propanesulfonic acid (MOPS), N-[tris(hydroxymethyl)methyl]-3-aminopropanesulfonic acid (TAPS), 2-(cyclohexylamino) ethanesulfonic acid (CHES), and imidazole were purchased from Sigma. Glacial acetic acid was purchased from EMD Millipore. All other commercial materials were reagent-grade or better and used without further purification. PGA was prepared and purified by literature procedures,10,31 and the concentration in stock solutions was determined by 1H NMR.10

Preparation of Solutions

The solution pH was determined at 25 °C using an Orion Model 720A pH meter equipped with a Radiometer pHC4006-9 combination electrode that was standardized at pH 4.00, 7.00, and 10.00 at 25 °C. Solutions of buffers in water were prepared using 1 N NaOH or HCl to adjust the pH of the dissolved buffer. Stock solutions of NADH were prepared by dissolving the coenzyme in water and were stored at 4 °C. The concentration of NADH was determined from the absorbance at 340 using an extinction coefficient of 6200 M–1 cm–1. The lithium salt of DHAP was exchanged for sodium cation by passage over a column of Dowex 50WX4 hydrogen form followed by neutralization with 1.0 N NaOH. The final solution was stored at −20 °C. GAP was stored at −80 °C. Small aliquots were thawed and adjusted to the appropriate pH by the addition of 1.0 N NaOH immediately before using.

Enzymes

Wild-type glycerol 3-phosphate dehydrogenase from human liver (hlGPDH) and wild-type TbbTIM were prepared by following published protocol.32,33 The concentration of GAP in stock solutions was determined as the concentration of NADH consumed in the coupled reactions catalyzed by wild-type TbbTIM and hlGPDH. Protein concentrations were determined from the absorbance at 280 nm using the extinction coefficients of 35000 M–1cm–1 for P168A/I172A TbbTIM and 18400 M–1cm–1 for hlGPDH that were calculated using the ProtParam tool available on the ExPASy server.34,35

P168A/I172A TbbTIM

The starting plasmid pTIM containing the wild-type gene for TbbTIM was a generous gift from Rik Wierenga. Published procedures were used to introduce P168A/I172A substitutions into the pTIM plasmid DNA, starting with the following primer 5′- CGCCTACGAAGCAGTTTGGGCCGCTGGTACCGGCAAGG-3′ in which the altered codons are underlined.27 The plasmid was introduced into E. coli strain BL21 pLysS,27 P168A/I172A TbbTIM was overexpressed,27 and the variant enzyme was purified by following published protocol.36 The purified enzyme was shown to be homogeneous by gel electrophoresis and was stored in 20% glycerol at - 80 °C.

Enzyme Assays

All enzyme assays were performed at 25 °C. The reactions were monitored using a temperature-controlled Cary 3500 Multicell Peltier UV–vis spectrophotometer. One unit of enzyme activity is defined as the amount of enzyme that converts 1 μmol of substrate to product in 1 min under the specified reaction conditions. The activity of the hlGPDH coupling enzyme was determined by monitoring the enzyme-catalyzed oxidation of NADH by DHAP.37

Kinetic Analyses

The initial velocity of P168A/I172A TbbTIM-catalyzed isomerization of GAP to form DHAP was determined by coupling the formation of DHAP to the oxidation of NADH, using hlGPDH.33 The assay solutions (1.0 mL) contained 30 mM TEA at pH 7.5 and I = 0.10 (NaCl), 0.1 mg/mL BSA, GAP, 0.2 mM NADH, 0.5–1.0 unit of hlGPDH, and 2.4 μM P168A/I172A TIM. The hlGPDH coupling enzyme was added first and the reaction was monitored for 4 min to obtain the initial velocity (v0) for nonenzymatic substrate isomerization.29 The reactions were then initiated by the addition of TIM and the change in absorbance was followed for 10 min to give the observed initial velocity vobs. The initial velocity for the enzymatic reaction was calculated as vE = vobs – v0.

The protocols from an earlier study were followed to determine the pH dependence of the competitive inhibition constants Ki for inhibition of P168A/I172A TIM-catalyzed isomerization by PGA.10 The following buffers were used in these experiments: pH 4.9, acetic acid (65% free base); pH 5.7, MES (25% free base); pH 6.4, MES (60% free base); pH 7.0, MOPS, (61% free base); pH 7.5, TEA (30% free base); pH 8.3, TAPS (45% free base); pH 8.9, CHES (30% free base); pH 9.3, CHES (50% free base). The P168A/I172A variant of TbbTIM and hlGPDH was exhaustively dialyzed at 5–7 °C against 30 mM of the appropriate buffer before use. There is a decrease in the specific activity of the hlGPDH coupling enzyme at pH > 7.5. The concentration of the coupling enzyme was increased at these higher pHs to ensure that 0.5–1 units of enzyme were used for each assay. The assay solutions (1.0 mL) contained 30 mM buffer at the specified pH and I = 0.10 (NaCl), 0.1 mg/mL BSA, GAP, 0.2 mM NADH, PGA, 0.5–1.0 units of GPDH and the following concentrations of P168A/I172A TIM: pH 4.9, 8 μM; pH 5.7; 13 μM; pH 6.4, 6 μM; pH 07.0, 7 μM; pH 7.5, 9 μM, pH 8.3, 4 μM; pH 8.9, 11 μM; pH 9.3, 11 μM. The inhibition constants (Ki)obs for competitive inhibition were determined in experiments where [GAP] was varied at two or three different fixed [PGA], using in each case a maximum [PGA] that was > Ki (see SI).

The nonlinear least-squares fits of kinetic data to the appropriate kinetic equation were obtained using the Prism 8 program for MacOS from GraphPad Software. The kinetic parameters kcat and Km for TIM-catalyzed isomerization of GAP were determined from the fit of kinetic data to the Michaelis–Menten equation. The kinetic parameters for competitive inhibition of TIM-catalyzed isomerization of GAP by PGA were determined from the fits of kinetic data to eq 1.10 The superpositions of X-ray crystal structures were generated and visualized by using the Chimera software package provided by the Computer Graphics Laboratory, University of California, San Francisco, CA

Discussion

The P168A/I172A substitutions at TbbTIM cause a 25,000-fold decrease in kcat/Km for isomerization of GAP that corresponds to a 6.0 kcal/mol destabilization of the rate-determining transition state (Table 1). The total effect was partitioned into a 5-fold decrease in Km from a 0.9 kcal/mol stabilization of the Michaelis complexes, and a 1.2 × 105-fold decrease in kcat from a 6.9 kcal/mol increase in the activation barrier for conversion of enzyme-bound substrate to the rate-determining transition state for kcat. The results show that the P168 and I172 side chains operate to optimize the stabilizing interactions that develop at the transition state for deprotonation of the enzyme-bound substrate and to destabilize the ground-state Michaelis complex to substrate. In other words, the ligand-driven conformational change of TIM results in both ground-state destabilization by the P168 and I172 side chains and in the participation of these side chains in stabilization of the rate-determining transition states for TIM-catalyzed isomerization.

The effect of substitutions of P168 and I172 on the stability of the rate-determining transition state for kcat/Km (ΔΔGE+S‡), calculated using eq 3 is reported in Table 1 and summarized by Scheme 2. The interpretation of Scheme 2 is complicated because there are different rate-determining steps for kcat/Km for the reaction catalyzed by wild-type TbbTIM (encounter-limited formation of the Michaelis complex between enzyme and GAP)42,43 and by the P168A and I172A single variants (turnover of Michaelis complexes).21,27,28,38 The effect of the first substitution on the stability of the transition state for the wild-type enzyme-catalyzed reaction is 1.1 kcal/mol smaller than the effect of this substitution on the single variants. This difference is partly or entirely due to the failure to observe the full effect of substitutions at wild-type TbbTIM on reaction chemistry because the chemical step is only partly rate-determining.

The observation that the effects of single substitutions at wild-type TbbTIM on transition state stability are largely reproduced when the same substitution is made at single variants provides evidence that the second substitution causes minimal changes in enzyme structure over and above those caused by the same substitution at the wild-type enzyme. This interpretation is supported by the following observations.

(1) The active sites for the PGA complexes to single I172A, L232A, and the double I172A/L232A variants are virtually superimposable on that for wild-type TbbTIM, except that water molecules occupy the position of the excised side chains.28 The structures for wild-type TbbTIM and the P168A variant are also nearly superimposable, except that the E167 carboxylate side chain at the P168A variant failed to undergo the 2 Å motion from the swung-out position for the unliganded enzyme to a position close to the inhibitor (Figure 2). The nearly fixed protein structures determined for all of these single and one double variant suggest that there should be no additional effects of the double P168A/I172A substitution.

(2) The P168A/I172A substitution causes a 5-fold increase in the apparent substrate affinity. This shows that there are no apparent structural effects of the second substitution that destabilize the Michaelis complexes.

We did not determine the X-ray crystal structure for P168A/I172A TbbTIM. Basicity of the E167 Side Chain

The pH profiles of kinetic parameters for the P168A/I172A TbbTIM-catalyzed isomerization of GAP and for inhibition of this reaction by PGA were determined in order to evaluate the effect of these amino acid substitutions on the pKa of the carboxylic acid side chain of E167. The profile of values for kcat/Km for isomerization of GAP (Figure 6A) shows a good fit to eq 6 with a downward break at pH = pKa = 6.0 ± 0.1 for deprotonation of GAP phosphate monoanion in water to form the dianion.29 The flat pH profile for values of kcat (Figure 6B) is consistent with a pKa < 4.9 for the carboxylic side chain of E167 at the Michaelis complex to GAP.44 These pH profiles are similar to profiles reported for the reactions catalyzed by wild-type and P168A and I172A variants of TbbTIM.10

This figure shows that the binding of I3– causes a large increase in the basicity of the E167 side chain from pKEH ≈ 4 for free TbbTIM23 to pKEHI > 10 for the inhibitor complex (EH·I3–).10,11,41 The large pKa of the E167 carboxylic acid at EH·I3– is due to the combined effects of stabilization of EH·I3– by a hydrogen bond between the enzyme-bound inhibitor and protonated E167 and the destabilization of E–·I3– by electrostatic interactions between the closely spaced negative charges at PGA and the E167 carboxylate anion (Scheme 4).10

Deprotonation of EH·I3– to Form E–·I3–

The pH profiles for variant enzymes (Figure 7) show upward breaks centered at pKEHI for the deprotonation of EH·I3– to form E–·I3– (Scheme 3). The solid lines for Figure 7 show the nonlinear least-squares fits of the data to eq 7 derived for Scheme 3.10 These fits define the values of pKEHI for the E167 side chain that are summarized in Figure 7. The linear profile for wild-type TbbTIM was fit to eq 8 (pKEHI > 10) to give (Ki)EKEHI = 2.4 × 10–13 M2.10

The pH profiles from Figure 7 provide support for the following conclusions.

(1) Values of pKEHI = 7.7 and 7.8 were determined, respectively, for the deprotonation of EH·I3– complexes at I172A and P168A/I172A TbbTIM variants. The >2 unit effect of the I172A substitution on carboxylic acid pKa was attributed to the stabilization of the carboxylate anion by the additional water of solvation observed at I172A TbbTIM.10,11 By comparison, the P168A substitution results in a 1.6-unit smaller decrease in pKEHI for wild-type TbbTIM. The second P168A substitution at the I172A TbbTIM, for which there is no hydrophobic clamp for the E167 side chain, causes no further decrease in the already reduced pKa of the E167 side chain.

(2) The stability of the complex between the PGA trianion (I3–) and E– [(Ki)E, Scheme 3] is defined in Figure 7 by the plateau values for pKi at high pH. The P168A substitution at I172A TbbTIM causes p(Ki)E to increase from 2.1 for I172A TbbTIM (red circles) to 2.6 for the P168A/I172 variant (black triangles). This corresponds to a stabilization of 0.8 kcal/mol of the E–·I3– complex to the P168A/I172A variant. The substitution of P168A at I172A TbbTIM is expected to move the E167 side chain away from the bound inhibitor trianion (Figure 5). We propose that the movement of the side chain anion has the effect of attenuating the electrostatic destabilization of E–·I3– by interactions between the E167 and PGA carboxylates.

Ground-State Effects

The crippling 25,000-fold effect of the P168A/I172A substitution on kcat/Km is accompanied by a surprising 5-fold decrease in Km for isomerization of GAP. The kinetic parameter Km is composed of the microscopic rate constants for the minimal Scheme 5. It is expected for the slow isomerization of GAP catalyzed by the crippled P168A/I172A variant that Km ≈ Kd because the large activation barrier for isomerization of the variant E·GAP complex will ensure that this complex is in chemical equilibrium with the free enzyme. The following observations provide good evidence that Km < Kd = k2/k1 (Scheme 5) for wild-type TbbTIM and that the observed effect of the P168A/I172A substitution on Km (eq 4) sets a lower limit for the increase in binding affinity of GAP for this variant.

The inequality [E·GAP]ss < [E·GAP]eq holds for the wild-type enzyme since E·GAP does not accumulate to the equilibrium concentration, but it is rapidly converted to E·DHAP (k3 > k2).2 The thermodynamic dissociation constant Kd for GAP (eq 9) should therefore be larger than the kinetic parameter Km in which case the effect of the P168A/I172A substitution on Km will set a lower limit for the effect on Kd.

(2) The N11 side chain of TIM is hydrogen bonded to the O-1 carbonyl group of GAP. The N11A substitution at TIM from Leishmania mexicana (LmTIM) results in a 14-fold decrease in kcat/Km due to the loss of the amide side chain interaction that stabilizes the negative charge that develops at O-1 of the enediolate intermediate (Scheme 1).45 The N11A substitution causes a 7-fold increase in Km for the isomerization of GAP from the destabilization of the Michaelis complex, as Km approaches the thermodynamic binding constant Kd for GAP.

The phosphodianion-driven conformational change of TIM (Scheme 6) converts the unliganded open enzyme EO to the closed enzyme EC that is destabilized relative to EO by steric interactions between the backbone carbonyl oxygen of G211 and the pyrrolidine side-chain of P168.21,22 The removal of this destabilizing interaction at the P168A TbbTIM (Table 1) results in the apparent stabilization of the Michaelis complex (ΔΔGES = −0.6 kcal/mol, Table 1). Open TIM (EO) is stabilized by interactions between the E167 carboxylate side chain and six waters of solvation. The phosphodianion-driven conformational change forces the E167 carboxylate into a hydrophobic clamp and extrudes four of six solvating waters to bulk solvent.3,7 The X-ray crystal structures for complexes of PGA to wild-type TbbTIM, and I172A, L232A and I172A/L232A variant enzymes show that the space created at each variant is filled by water molecules that solvate the basic E167 side chain.28 This side chain solvation gives rise to values of ΔΔGES (Table 1) for variant enzyme-catalyzed isomerization, which show that the substitutions provide the following stabilization of the enzyme Michaelis complexes: I172A, −0.3 kcal/mol; L232A, −0.3 kcal/mol; I172A/L232A, −1.6 kcal/mol; and P168A/I172A, −0.9 kcal/mol. These values are lower limits for the true effect of the substitutions on the stability of E·GAP because Kd > (Kd)app (eqs 9 and 10) for the reaction catalyzed by wild-type TbbTIM.

Utilization of Substrate Binding Energy to Drive a Protein Conformational Change that Locks the Substrate into the Enzyme Active Site

Scheme 7 shows the induced-fit mechanism that was proposed to rationalize phosphite dianion-activated TIM-catalyzed deprotonation of glycolaldehyde (GA).46,47 This activation results in a 103-fold larger second-order rate constant (kcat/Kd)E·HPi for deprotonation of GA by the complex between TIM and phosphite dianion (EC·HPO32–) compared to (kcat/Kd)E for deprotonation by unliganded TIM (EO).46 This difference is rationalized by a dianion-driven conformational change that causes the fraction of TIM in the EC form to increase from fEC = 0.001 (KC ≈ 0.001) to fEC ≈ 1 for a reaction at saturating [HPO32–]. The value of KC = 0.001 gives ΔGC ≈ 4 kcal/mol for the conversion of EO to EC. The values of ΔΔGES from Table 1 provide evidence that the P168, I172, and L232 side chains contribute a total of >2 kcal/mol to the estimated 4 kcal/mol barrier for the enzyme conformational change. The entropic barrier to the restriction of motions of the catalytic side chains that occurs during dianion-driven loop closure that converts the flexible open enzyme to the rigid protein–substrate cage should also contribute to the barrier ΔGC.48

Transition State Effects

The free-energy profiles for wild-type and P168A/I172A TbbTIM-catalyzed deprotonation of GAP to form the enediolate reaction intermediate (Figure 8)2,43 show the stabilization of the Michaelis complex (ΔΔGES = −0.9 kcal/mol, Table 1) and the destabilization of the reaction transition state (ΔΔGE+S‡ = 6 kcal/mol) for the variant enzyme-catalyzed reaction. The 6.9 kcal/mol effect of the P168A/I172A substitution on ΔΔGES‡ shows that the P168 and I172 side chains participate in the stabilization of the rate-determining transition state.

The P168 side chain carries the E167 carboxylate into the hydrophobic clamp created by the I172 and L232 side chains (Figure 2). This clamp causes the pKa of the E167 carboxylic acid to increase from ca 4 for EO to >10 for the complex between TIM and the enediolate analog PGA. The effect of the P168A/I172A substitution at wild-type TIM on substrate reactivity toward carbon deprotonation is partly due to the ca 2 unit decrease in pKa for the E167 side chain at the complex between the variant enzyme TIM and the enediolate analog PGA (Figure 8). The P168A substitution at wild-type TbbTIM causes the E167 carboxylate to move 2 Å away from the carbon acid substrate (Figure 2). We propose that the activity of P168A/I172A TbbTIM is likewise reduced by a similar shift in the position of the E167 side chain and the associated relaxation in the optimal positioning of other catalytic side chains at this variant.49

Mechanism for TIM-Catalyzed Isomerization

These results build upon earlier studies on the origin and the magnitude for the catalytic rate acceleration for TIM.3,29,50

The P168A/I172A substitutions eliminate the large difference between the reactivity of TIM and small Brønsted bases for deprotonation of catalyst-bound substrates. Combining kB = kcat/Kd = 0.0018 M–1 s–1 (Scheme 8)29 for deprotonation of GAP by the tertiary amine quinuclidinone (pKa = 7.5) at 39 °C with the approximate value of Kd = 10 M for breakdown of a weak encounter complex with GAP51 gives kcat = 0.018 s–1 for the general-base catalyzed reaction (Scheme 8) that is similar to kcat = 0.017 s–1 for P168A/I172A TbbTIM-catalyzed isomerization of GAP (Table 1). This shows that the difference between the second-order rate constants for variant-catalyzed isomerization of GAP (kcat/Km = 340 M–1 s–1, Table 1) and quinuclidinone-catalyzed deprotonation of GAP is mainly due to stabilization of the Michaelis complex to the variant TbbTIM by interactions with the protein catalyst.

Quinuclidinone-Catalyzed Deprotonation of GAP

A value of kcat = 0.03 s–1 was reported for catalysis of the isomerization of GAP by the loop 6 deletion variant of TIM from chicken muscle, where the partial deletion of loop 6 eliminates the effect of the enzyme-activating phosphodianion-driven conformational change by exposing the active site to bulk solvent.12,52 We propose that the values of kcat = 0.02–0.03 s–1 for the P168A/I172A and loop 6 deletion variants reflect the baseline reactivity of the amino acid side chains of TIM for catalysis of isomerization in the absence of the side chain activation by the phosphodianion-driven enzyme conformational change.30

The P168A/I172A substitution causes a large decrease in the dianion binding energy that is utilized for transition state stabilization, but little change in the dianion binding energy that is utilized for ground-state stabilization. The ratio of the second-order rate constants for wild-type TIM-catalyzed isomerization of the carbonyl form of GAP (kcat/Km = 1.7 × 108 M–1 s–1) and for deprotonation of the phosphodianion-truncated substrate GA (kcat/Km = 0.05 M–1 s–1)46 shows that the substrate dianion stabilizes the isomerization reaction transition state by ca 13 kcal/mol (eq 11). This is equal to ΔGPi‡, the intrinsic phosphodianion binding energy.53,54 The binding of GAP to the P168A/I172A variant is not impaired by these substitutions (Figure 8). The ratio of the second-order rate constants for P168A/I172A variant-catalyzed isomerization of GAP and GA shows that ca 5 kcal/mol for the dianion binding energy is utilized to stabilize the Michaelis complex to substrate. The difference between the 13 and 5 kcal/mol dianion binding energy, respectively, for wild-type and P168A/I172A TbbTIM-catalyzed isomerization of GAP represents the ca 8 kcal/mol dianion binding energy that is utilized specifically for transition state stabilization.

Summary and Speculation

These experiments were designed to pursue inspired observations from Wierenga and co-workers on the X-ray crystal structures of TIM.8,21 We report here that the P168A, I172A, and P168A/I172A substitutions at TbbTIM give proteins that show a greater substrate, but smaller transition state-binding energy than does the wild-type enzyme (Table 1) because the substitutions eliminate protein-destabilizing interactions and transition state-stabilizing interactions that are induced by substrate binding. To the best of our knowledge, this study provides the first results from a mutagenesis study that connects the utilization of substrate binding energy for protein destabilization with an increase in enzyme catalytic activity.25,53

The catalytic power of TIM is due both to the existence of a stiff closed protein-substrate cage that provides the necessary transition state stabilization, and a flexible structure for the open unliganded enzyme that favors the evolution of a pathway for utilization of substrate binding energy to access the more rigid closed catalytic cage.30,48 Discussions of the mechanism of enzyme action generally focus on the protein side chains that stabilize the enzyme-bound transition state. We propose that these discussions, for enzymes that conform to the induced fit model (Schemes 6 and 7), should also include a description of the performance of the side chains such as P168 and I172 for TIM that create the barrier for the enzyme-activating protein conformational change.55−59

We have presented evidence here that the P168 and I172 side chains of TIM act both to introduce steric and electrostatic strain into the protein ground state and to stabilize the enzymatic transition state. By comparison the Q215, Y217, and R235 phosphodianion gripper side chains at orotidine 5′-phosphate decarboxylase (OMPDC) act to stabilize the active closed caged Michaelis complex (EC, Scheme 6) through direct interactions with the substrate phosphodianion.56 It is not clear for OMPDC if the barrier to formation of the active closed enzyme EC includes specific destabilizing interactions between protein side chains, as described here for TIM—or if this barrier is largely entropic and represents small contributions from the many side chains that participate in the conformational change and whose motions are frozen on formation of the active closed form of OMPDC. Further experimental studies are needed to resolve this and many other important questions about the mechanism of enzyme action.

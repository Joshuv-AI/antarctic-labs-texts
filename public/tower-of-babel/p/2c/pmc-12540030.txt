Timely staged hepatectomy following transarterial embolization versus emergency hepatectomy for spontaneous hepatocellular carcinoma rupture
Authors: Shuai Liu, Xiangyao Kong, Yuanchuan Gong, Zetao Wu, Luoluo Wang, Yi Ruan, Xinhua Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate the efficacy and safety of timely staged hepatectomy (SH) following transarterial embolization (TAE) versus emergency hepatectomy (EH) for spontaneous rupture of hepatocellular carcinoma (SR-HCC).MethodsBetween January 2018 and December 2023, 109 patients with SR-HCC were admitted to our center receive SH (34 cases) or EH (75 cases). Performing 1:1 optimal matching propensity score matching (PSM) analysis, resulting in 34 patients in SH group and 34 patients in EH group. We compared perioperative data, peritoneal metastasis rates, recurrence rates, complication rates, and long-term survival outcome between the two matched groups.ResultsAfter PSM, baseline characteristics were well balanced between the SH and EH groups (standardized mean difference [SMD] < 0.1). Compared with the matched EH group, the SH group had a shorter intraoperative hepatic vascular clamping time, significantly less perioperative blood loss and transfusion volume, and a lower in-hospital mortality rate (P < 0.05). No significant differences were observed between the two groups in postoperative complication rates and peritoneal metastasis rates (P > 0.05). The SH group exhibited a trend toward improved recurrence-free survival (RFS) (Log-rank χ2 = 3.2, P = 0.074), although this did not reach statistical significance. Similarly, overall survival (OS) was comparable between the groups (Log-rank χ2 = 1.78, P = 0.183).ConclusionCompared with EH, SH demonstrates superior perioperative safety, characterized by less surgical trauma and lower in-hospital mortality, without increasing postoperative complications and the risk of peritoneal metastasis. Therefore, SH may be considered the preferred treatment for SR-HCC, especially in cases of hemodynamic instability or impaired hepatic functional reserve.

Introduction

Spontaneous rupture of hepatocellular carcinoma (SR-HCC) is a severe complication of primary liver cancer, occurring in 10–15 % of all hepatocellular carcinoma patients. It is characterized by acute abdominal pain, massive intra-abdominal hemorrhage, and shock, with an alarmingly high mortality rate ranging from 25 % to 75 % [[1], [2], [3]]. The majority of patients with SR-HCC are associated with underlying uncompensated liver disease, including coagulation disorders, hepatic failure, and hepatic encephalopathy, and timely diagnosis and intervention are critical to improving prognosis [3,4]. The main treatments include transcatheter arterial embolisation (TAE), emergency hepatectomy (EH) surgery, suturing, tamponade and hepatic artery ligation [1,2,5]. Due to the diversity of patient conditions and the different indications for each treatment, there is no clear recommendation for the optimal treatment option.

EH can combine the benefits of hemostasis and radical treatment in a single operation, but is associated with a high rate of complications and mortality [6,7]. According to the AJCC 8th edition guidelines, HCC is classified as a T4-stage tumor once rupture and hemorrhage occur [8]. Even when EH is performed, the rates of peritoneal dissemination and recurrence remain high without significant improvement in prognosis [9,10]. Transarterial embolization (TAE), a minimally invasive procedure, effectively achieves hemostasis while maximally preserving normal liver function [11]. However, studies have demonstrated that patients receiving TAE alone exhibit higher in-hospital mortality and poor long-term survival outcomes compared to EH [[12], [13], [14], [15]]. To achieve better therapeutic outcomes, increasing evidence suggests favoring TAE initially when conditions permit, followed by SH when liver function, hemoglobin, and other indices improve [16,17]. However, the therapeutic efficacy and optimal timing of surgery remain controversial. To further evaluate the feasibility of the SH therapeutic strategy, this study aims to compare the efficacy and safety between SH and EH for SR-HCC, with the goal of establishing an evidence-based approach for clinical decision-making.

Materials and methods

Study design

To ensure an adequate sample size and comprehensive data, we retrospectively analyzed 109 patients with SR-HCC admitted to our hospital between January 2018 and December 2023. Of these, 34 patients underwent TAE followed by SH, while 75 patients underwent EH. Our study focused on comparing different curative hepatectomy strategies. Therefore, only patients who underwent radical hepatectomy (EH or TAE followed by SH) were included.

Inclusion criteria: (1) initially diagnosed and confirmed as SR-HCC; (2) patients who underwent radical hepatectomy, including EH or TAE followed by SH; (3) case information was complete and contained detailed pre-operative, intra-operative, and post-operative clinical data. Exclusion criteria: (1) patients with the presence of other malignant tumors or serious comorbidities (e.g., severe cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled diabetes mellitus with organ complications, severe chronic respiratory diseases, end-stage renal disease, or active severe infections); (2) patients with incomplete or missing clinical data; (3) patients who underwent only palliative treatment or local ablation treatment or other non-radical surgery; (4) distant metastases had been detected on pre-operative imaging; (5) patients who had previously received treatment for distant metastases.

All surgical specimens were pathologically examined, and the diagnosis on HCC was based on the diagnostic criteria issued by the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases [18]; the staging of HCC was based on the 8th edition of the Cancer Staging Manual issued by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) [19]. The diagnostic criteria for SR-HCC include sudden right upper abdominal pain accompanied by symptoms of shock such as hypotension and tachycardia; On physical examination, abdominal tenderness with rebound pain and positive shifting dullness are observed; Imaging studies (ultrasound or CT) reveal rupture of the liver capsule around the tumor with fluid accumulation in the perihepatic region. Diagnostic abdominal paracentesis shows bloody ascites.

Collect demographic characteristics (age, gender), presence of shock at diagnosis, and vital signs (temperature, pulse, respiratory rate, blood pressure). Laboratory tests included hemoglobin levels, liver function tests (AST, ALT, total bilirubin), viral hepatitis serology, and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels. Tumor characteristics include size, number, presence of vascular invasion, or extrahepatic invasion.

This study was approved by the ethics committee and strictly adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki throughout the research process [20].

Treatment

With adequate fluid resuscitation and volume expansion, imaging studies evaluate liver injury severity while Child-Pugh scores (with Class C being an absolute surgical contraindication) assesses hepatic reserve alongside cardiopulmonary and renal function. Multidisciplinary team (MDT) consisting of hepatobiliary surgeons, interventional radiologists, anesthesiologists, and other specialists from other relevant departments conducted a comprehensive assessment and discussion, with final treatment decisions incorporating family consent.

EH is defined as hepatectomy performed immediately after diagnosis or within 24 h after hospital admission [16], while SH is defined as hepatectomy performed 7 days or more than after TAE treatment, typically within 14–28 days [21].

TAE is considered an ideal hemostatic method for patients without severe liver function impairment who are considered capable of tolerating surgery. Under local anesthesia, catheterization was performed via left or right femoral artery puncture using the Seldinger technique, followed by digital subtraction angiography (DSA) to visualize arterial perfusion, tumor distribution, and the bleeding site. Selective or superselective embolization of the left or right branch of the proper hepatic artery, or tumor-feeding arteries, was performed using gelatin sponge particles or embolic microspheres to achieve complete occlusion of the target vessels supplying the ruptured tumor, while preserving perfusion to the surrounding normal liver parenchyma as much as possible. After discontinuing of supportive measures (fluid resuscitation, blood transfusion, vasopressors, and inotropes), if vital signs and hemoglobin levels remain stable, repeat DSA should be performed to confirm embolization efficacy.

Hepatectomy aims to remove all macroscopically visible primary and secondary tumors, including tumor thrombi and metastatic foci. During operation, the Pringle maneuver is routinely performed to block hepatic blood flow, and the resection margin is kept at least 1 cm away from the tumor. All surgeries are performed by experienced senior surgeons, with thorough intraoperative exploration of the abdomen, removal of hematomas, and peritoneal lavage. Postoperatively, abdominal drainage tube is placed based on the surgical site and type, and specimens are routinely sent for pathological examination.

Follow-up

All patients were followed up at least every 6 months after discharge, including abdominal CT, liver MRI, or liver ultrasound. The diagnosis of recurrence was based on typical HCC manifestations on CT or MRI. Telephone follow-up was conducted until March 31, 2024. The endpoints of this study were overall survival and disease-free survival. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the period from the time patients with primary hepatocellular carcinoma received surgical treatment until the end date of follow-up or the date of death from any cause; Recurrence-free (RFS) was defined as the period from the time patients with primary hepatocellular carcinoma received surgical treatment until tumor recurrence was detected or the end of the follow-up time.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.2). Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x¯ ± SD) and compared using independent samples t-tests. Categorical variables were presented as frequency and percentage (n, %) and analyzed using chi-square tests or Fisher's exact tests (n ≤ 5). To reduce the influence of confounding factors and selection bias, propensity score matching (PSM) was implemented to balance baseline characteristics. Covariates were included to calculate propensity scores for each patient using multivariable logistic regression, followed by 1:1 optimal matching. The balance of covariates before and after matching was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMD), with a threshold of <0.10 indicating adequate balance.

Results

Variables with differences were used as covariates for PSM at a 1:1 ratio using the optimal matching method, which minimizes the total distance between matched pairs to enhance matching quality and sample balance. The final cohort included 68 patients (34 in the SH group and 34 in the EH group). All matched covariates achieved SMD < 0.1 (Supplementary Table 1), and no significant differences in baseline characteristics remained after matching (P > 0.05; Table 1). The matched data were used for subsequent analysis.

Adverse reactions in patients after TAE treatment

Among the 34 patients who underwent TAE, the most common complication was post-embolization syndrome (PES). 18 patients (52.9 %) experienced mild fever postoperatively, and 14 patients (41.2 %) experienced mild nausea and vomiting. Both mild fever and nausea/vomiting typically occurred within 1 to 3 days post-procedure and relieved within 3 days. 12 patients (35.3 %) experienced mild abdominal pain, which occurred within 24 h after surgery and generally lasted for 3 to 5 days before relieving. In addition, 3 patients (8.8 %) suffered from severe liver function injury, manifested by significant elevations in ALT and AST. 3 patients gradually returned to normal after receiving hepatoprotective treatment and strict fluid management. 1 patient (2.9 %) experienced infection, which may be related to tissue necrosis and decreased immune function after embolization. It was effectively controlled in the short term by receiving broad-spectrum antibiotic treatment.

Surgical data and complications of the two groups after PSM

The median time from diagnosis to surgery in the SH group was 24 days (range 13–35 days). After PSM, comparison of surgical data and complications between the two groups showed that the SH group had significantly shorter operative time, hepatic vascular clamping time, lower intraoperative blood loss, and reduced perioperative transfusion volume compared to the EH group (P < 0.05). Additionally, the SH group had shorter postoperative hospital stay and ICU stay (P < 0.05), although the proportion of patients transferred to the ICU did not differ significantly between the groups (P > 0.05). There were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of major complication rates, postoperative 24-h pain scores, or the proportion of patients with normalized liver function at discharge (P > 0.05). Notably, the SH group incurred higher total hospitalization costs (P < 0.05). No deaths occurred in the SH group, while one patient in the EH group died of liver failure.

Recurrence-free survival

The median follow-up time was 21.5 months (range, 3.7–48.8 months) in the SH group and 20.8 months (range, 3.5–46.4 months) in the EH group. One patient in the EH group died during hospitalization and was subsequently excluded from the analysis.

Among the 67 patients, 46 patients (76.1 %) experienced recurrence, including 20 patients (58.9 %) in the SH group and 26 patients (78.8 %) in the EH group. The recurrence rate between the two groups was not statistically significant (P = 0.134). Overall survival

At the end of the follow-up, 34 deaths occurred, with 14 in the SH group and 20 in the EH group. Among them, 12 in the SH group died of tumor progression and 2 died of other causes, and 17 in the EH group died of tumor progression and 3 died of other causes. Among the surviving cases, the SH group had 14 tumor-free survivors and 6 with tumor persistence, compared to 7 and 6 respectively in the EH group. The distribution of final follow-up status did not differ significantly between the groups (P > 0.05, Table 3).

The median OS was 37.1 months (95 %CI:25.0–46.9) in the SH group and 24.1 months (95 %CI:15.0–40.8) in the EH group, without reaching statistically significant difference between the two groups(Log-rank χ2 = 2.621, P = 0.105, Fig. 2B). Discussion

To date, the pathophysiology of SR-HCC is multifactorial and not fully elucidated. Proposed mechanisms include: (1) increased intratumoral pressure caused by rapid expansive growth of the tumor; (2) massive necrosis due to ischemia and hypoxia; (3) susceptible to rupture of the peritoneum due to the tumor being located in a superficial position in the liver; (4) thinning of the vascular wall due to hepatic dysfunction [1]. Previous studies have shown that TAE is an effective method for achieving hemostasis in hemodynamically unstable advanced HCC patients, with reported success rates exceeding 90 % [1,22,23], providing strong evidence for TAE as an initial therapeutic option for SR-HCC. Thus, an increasing number of medical centers have begun to prefer TAE followed by SH in recent years.

Surgical management of SR-HCC should adhere to the same principles as conventional radical resection, while mandating sufficient liver function reserve to ensure favorable treatment results [1,16].The occurrence of SR-HCC often indicates that the tumor has progressed to an advanced stage and the patient's liver function has reached a relatively poor state, especially for those with cirrhosis. Emergency surgery and anesthesia may further exacerbate the liver function damage [24]. In such cases, TAE presents considerable benefits: (1) securing immediate hemostasis, (2) circumventing perioperative risks associated with surgery/anesthesia, and (3) establishing a recovery phase for liver function and physiological homeostasis to optimize treatment tolerance. Several previous studies have shown that mortality during hospitalization is higher in EH than in patients undergoing SH [14,17,25], and the deaths that occurred during hospitalization in this study was in the EH group, further confirming the overall safety of SH. Furthermore, TAE can delineate the blood supply of the tumor, the relationship between the tumor and the surrounding vessels, and identify the presence of arteriovenous fistula and portal vein tumor thrombus [11]. With bleeding already managed during SH, surgeons could perform more accurate tumor removal with greater efficiency and improved safety profile. Our findings demonstrated that the SH group experienced significantly less perioperative blood loss and required fewer transfusions than the EH group. The operative time and intraoperative hepatic vascular clamping time was shorter. Regrettably, there was no significant difference between the two groups in terms of complications. Notably, all patients in both the SH and EH groups achieved R0 resection, indicating comparable margin status. This suggests that the advantages of SH may stem more from perioperative management than from differences in resection technique.

Peritoneal metastasis has consistently been a poor prognostic factor in HCC, while surgical resection of isolated peritoneal secondary tumors may be the only effective treatment to prolong survival, although the outcomes remain unsatisfactory [26]. Some studies advocate that EH should be prioritized once SR-HCC occur, primarily because tumor cells may disseminate hematogenously and implant in the greater/lesser omentum, omental bursa, mesentery, and pelvic cavity. Early surgical intervention to evacuate hematomas combined with thorough peritoneal lavage can help reduce the risk of intra-abdominal metastasis [1,17,27]. But according to Wu et al.'s finding [16], early partial hepatectomy performed after 8 days resulted in reduced peritoneal dissemination and led to improved long-term survival outcomes compared to cases within of 8 days, though no significant difference was observed when compared to EH. This discrepancy may be attributed to the fact that while EH can rapidly control hemorrhage and reduce tumor cell dissemination, its emergent nature may compromise thorough preoperative assessment, potentially affecting surgical radicality and precision. In contrast, SH allows for comprehensive evaluation and meticulous planning, enabling more effective tumor clearance and reduced peritoneal dissemination. On the other hand, it should been noted that excessive delay may lead to tumor neovascularization and subsequent regrowth, potentially resulting in intrahepatic metastasis and missed surgical opportunity [5,17]. This finding underscores the necessity of achieving an optimal balance between surgical timeliness and radicality when implementing hepatic resection strategies. In view of this, our study endeavored to maintain the SH interval within an optimal timeframe. Regrettably, our study show that the SH demonstrated comparable peritoneal metastasis rates to EH.

On the other hand, it is worth noting that peritoneal metastasis incidence was low in our cohort, with most recurrences occurring at intrahepatic or distant sites. Although peritoneal dissemination is associated with poor prognosis, it accounts for only a minority of recurrence events in SR-HCC patients. Therefore, clinical management and surveillance strategies should also more focus on other common sites of recurrence to improve overall outcomes.

In terms of long-term prognosis, more and more studies and have shown that SH improves long-term survival outcomes [6,17,28], and our study showed that patients in the SH group had better OS at 1, 3, and 5 years than those in the EH group, although the differences did not reach statistical significance. Similarly, the SH group also showed improved RFS compared to the EH group, suggesting a potential benefit in reducing tumor recurrence. Notably, although PSM reduced the statistical difference in tumor multifocality, the EH group still had more cases of multifocal tumors. This residual imbalance may have contributed to the poorer RFS observed in the EH group.

It must be emphasized that SR-HCC demonstrates markedly inferior survival outcomes versus non-ruptured cases. In our study, 60 % of patients experienced recurrence or metastasis within 3 years, with over half of these cases involving extrahepatic metastasis, including the peritoneum, lungs, kidneys, bones, and distant lymph nodes. Although careful peritoneal lavage was performed for all SR-HCC patients, and even attempted with 5-fluorouracil combined with large volume of distilled water perfusion chemotherapy at the end of the surgery, it still failed to control the high probability of peritoneal recurrence and metastasis, which confirms the rationality of AJCC guidelines classifying SR-HCC as T4 stage [8]. Some studies have questioned the categorization of tumor rupture as a simple T4 stage, arguing that this approach is overly simplistic and does not adequately reflect the clinical reality. According to the largest known large-scale nationwide survey, the survival rate of patients with SR-HCC is significantly different from those without rupture, and tumor rupture will increase the original TNM staging by 0.5–2 stages [24], which emphasizes the need for more nuanced and individualized consideration of each patient. A few studies have also advocated that tumor rupture does not affect prognosis but such conclusions are usually based on small number of cases, so their reliability needs to be treated with caution [29]. It is currently accepted that tumor rupture is an independent risk factor for prognosis [1,6,11,17,21,28].

This study has several limitations. First, nearly all included patients had HBV infection. Given the lower HBV prevalence in Western countries where HCV infection and alcohol consumption are more prevalent, our findings may have limited generalizability. Second, as a single-center retrospective analysis, despite using PSM to enhance methodological rigor, potential biases in data collection and recording may affect result accuracy. Finally, due to the limited sample size, we did not perform risk factor analysis for survival outcomes.

Conclusion

Overall, the overall prognosis of SR-HCC is still poor. Compared with EH, timely SH after TAE treatment does not increase the rate of peritoneal metastasis or recurrence, does not raise the rate of complications, has a higher safety profile, and improves the long-term survival outcomes. For hemodynamically unstable patients or those with impaired hepatic functional reserve, our findings suggest TAE as first-line intervention followed by timely SH when clinically feasible, aiming for better treatment results.

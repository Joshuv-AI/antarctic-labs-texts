Navigating the biliary maze: a retrospective cohort study of three-dimensional navigation technology improving outcomes in congenital biliary dilatation resection
Authors: Peilin Cai, Hao Zhong, Yi Zhou, Jinsheng Mai, Mingxin Pan, Nan Xiang, Silue Zeng, Ning Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThree-dimensional navigation technology (TDNT) has been previously utilized in hepatic and pancreatic surgical procedures, but its application and efficacy in the resection of congenital biliary dilatation (CBD) have not been reported. This study aims to investigate and evaluate the application value of TDNT in the resection of CBD and its short-term outcomes.MethodsPatients who underwent CBD resection surgery from January 2010 to March 2023 were included in this study. Patients were divided into the two groups based on whether TDNT was applied during the operation. After normality assessment via the Shapiro-Wilk test, continuous and categorical variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test and the Chi-square/Fisher’s exact test, respectively; univariate and multivariable logistic regression were used for risk factor analysis.ResultsNo significant baseline differences were observed between three-dimensional navigation (TDN) and non-TDN groups (P>0.05). On postoperative day 5 (POD5), TDN group had higher hemoglobin (P=0.008) and shorter drainage (P=0.013). TDN group had lower pancreatic fistula (P=0.025) and bile leakage (P=0.003), but higher blood transfusion (P=0.009). Comprehensive complication index (CCI) proportion was higher (≥26.2) in non-TDN group (P=0.02). Multivariable regression confirmed that TDNT was associated with lower risks of bile leakage [odds ratio (OR) =9.29, P=0.002], pancreatic fistula (OR =10.16, P=0.029), and high CCI (OR =4.50, P=0.026).ConclusionsTDNT in the resection of CBD is a feasible method that is associated with lower rates of postoperative pancreatic fistula and bile leakage. These findings suggest potential benefits in enhancing surgical precision and safety, though prospective studies are needed to establish causality.

Introduction

Congenital biliary dilatation (CBD), also known as choledochal cyst (CC), is a rare primary biliary disease characterized by localized dilation of intrahepatic and/or extrahepatic bile ducts. Its etiology remains unclear, but approximately 70% of cases are associated with anomalous pancreatobiliary junction (APBJ) (1). The risk of malignancy in CBD (2.50–30.00%) is significantly higher than that in the general population (0.01–0.38%) and increases with age (2). Therefore, early surgical intervention is recommended upon diagnosis of CBD (1,3).

The treatment of CBD encompasses a spectrum of complex hepatobiliary procedures dictated by anatomical extent. The foundational procedure is the complete excision of the pathologically dilated bile duct segment. For the most common Type I cysts confined to the extrahepatic duct, this entails CC excision with Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy. When the disease extends into the intrahepatic ducts (Types IV and V), anatomical hepatic resection becomes an integral component of the curative procedure to achieve complete removal of the involved biliary tree (4,5). Despite this procedural diversity, a common surgical challenge exists: the precise delineation and preservation of critical adjacent structures (6). For extrahepatic resection, this primarily involves the pancreatic duct and vasculature at the hilum; for hepatic resection, it involves the intricate relationship between dilated intrahepatic ducts and the portal triads. Traditional imaging falls short in providing the surgeon with an intuitive, patient-specific three-dimensional (3D) map to navigate these challenges. Although traditional two-dimensional imaging modalities such as computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) are crucial for diagnosis, their intraoperative application is limited. Surgeons must mentally reconstruct 3D structures to comprehend spatial relationships, which increases the complexity of surgical planning and the potential for errors. Traditional surgeries rely on empirical judgment to determine lesion locations, posing challenges to surgical safety and the risk of postoperative complications.

To this end, three-dimensional navigation technology (TDNT) comprising three-dimensional visualization technology (TDVT) and augmented reality-assisted navigation technology (AR-ANT) has been introduced into clinical hepatobiliary surgery. This technology converts two-dimensional imaging into 3D models, allowing surgeons to observe anatomical structures from multiple angles and enhance their understanding of complex relationships. By superimposing the 3D models onto the actual anatomy, real-time visual navigation is achieved (7,8).

Postoperative complications significantly impact patient prognosis (9,10), and individualized surgical strategies are required for different types of choledochal diseases, necessitating accurate assessment of the severity and impact of complications. Traditional Clavien-Dindo classification (CDC) and binary systems fail to comprehensively quantify the cumulative effects of multiple complications. The development of the comprehensive complication index (CCI) addresses this issue by enabling longitudinal evaluation of postoperative complications, including long-term complications, thereby more accurately reflecting the overall burden of complications and aiding clinical decision-making (11,12). However, the CCI has not yet been applied to the evaluation of surgical outcomes in CBD.

This retrospective cohort study evaluated the effectiveness of TDNT in resecting CCs. We assessed postoperative complications using the CDC and CCI and analyzed the short-term prognosis of the patients (13-15). We present this article in accordance with the STROCSS reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-1-0390/rc).

Methods

Study design and patient selection

This study retrospectively analyzed 234 patients diagnosed with CBD at Zhujiang Hospital of Southern Medical University from January 2010 to March 2023, ultimately including 56 cases. The center comprises two independent hepatobiliary surgery groups, each staffed by senior surgeons with extensive experience in hepatobiliary surgery. A total of eight chief physician hepatobiliary surgeons (four from each group) participated in the procedures, each with over 10 years of independent surgical experience and having performed more than 100 hepatobiliary surgeries annually. The allocation of patients to either group was primarily determined by the admitting team, reflecting real-world clinical practice rather than randomization. To minimize the impact of surgeon skill variability on outcomes, all surgeries were performed by experienced hepatobiliary surgeons, and no significant differences in perioperative management protocols (e.g., antibiotic use, drainage strategy, or postoperative care) were identified between the two groups. Based on whether TDNT (including TDVT and AR-ANT) was applied for preoperative planning and intraoperative guidance, patients were divided into the three-dimensional navigation (TDN) group (25 cases) and the non-TDN group (31 cases) (Figure 1). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Zhujiang Hospital of Southern Medical University (No. 2024-KY-038-02) and informed consent was taken from all the patients. It was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06418490) and complied with the standards for retrospective research.

TDVT was introduced at our center in the early 2010s, while AR-ANT was developed and introduced slightly later as an advanced intraoperative navigation modality built upon TDVT. AR-ANT is not an independent technology but rather a complementary intraoperative application of the preoperative 3D models generated by TDVT. Therefore, both technologies are collectively analyzed as TDNT in this study. The results of the exploratory subgroup analysis (comparing TDVT with AR-ANT) could not yield definitive conclusions due to the limited sample size and lack of statistical power; therefore, these analyses were not the primary focus of the study and were not presented in the main text (Table S1).

Inclusion criteria

(I) Patients aged ≥16 years, of any gender; (II) diagnosis of CBD confirmed by enhanced CT/MRI results; (III) patients who underwent surgical resection for CBD; (IV) absence of severe contraindications such as significant cardiac, pulmonary, renal, or cerebral dysfunction.

Exclusion criteria

(I) Known allergy to indocyanine green (ICG) or iodinated contrast agents; (II) patients with malignant transformation; (III) patients with other systemic tumors; (IV) incomplete clinical data.

Research method

Both groups of patients underwent preoperative examinations to rule out surgical contraindications and chose specific surgical approaches according to their own preferences Following general anesthesia, the non-TDN group underwent conventional CBD resection, while the TDN group completed the procedure under TDNT guidance (Figure 1). Resected specimens were subjected to pathological biopsy, with diagnoses confirmed by senior pathologists. Blood tests were performed every other day until the seventh postoperative day, and contrast-enhanced CT scans were conducted on day 7, supplemented by abdominal ultrasound when necessary. Starting from the first postoperative day, total bilirubin and amylase concentrations in abdominal drainage fluid were measured every other day to document biliary or pancreatic leakage, with concurrent bacterial cultures performed. Data are presented as median (interquartile range, IQR) for age, mean ± standard deviation (SD) for continuous variables, or number (percentage) for categorical variables. Lesion diameter refers to the maximum transverse diameter (cm) of the dilated bile duct segment measured on preoperative axial CT or MRI images.

Construction of 3D visualization models and preoperative planning

All patients in both groups underwent high-quality spiral contrast-enhanced CT (slice thickness 1.0 mm, interval 1.0 mm) and MRI with MRCP. In the TDN group, the arterial, venous, and portal venous phase data from CT and MRI were imported into the MI-3DVS software (Copyright No.: 2008SR18798) for image segmentation and 3D reconstruction of the liver, gallbladder, dilated bile ducts, pancreas, and vascular system (Figure 2). The vascular structures and their relationships with the lesions were analyzed using the 3D model preoperatively to formulate the surgical plan. The 3D reconstructed models were reviewed preoperatively to determine the anatomical subtype of each CBD according to both the Todani classification and the Dong classification (Figure 3). This dual classification facilitated a comprehensive understanding of the lesion’s morphology and its relationship with the pancreaticobiliary junction, which was integral to formulating the surgical plan.

Augmented reality technology assisted real-time intraoperative navigation

The Augmented Reality-Assisted Navigation System (AR-ANS, software copyright number: 2018SR840555) integrates TDVT and captures real-time surgical images through a 3D laparoscopic camera. The video signal is processed by a 3D embedded multimedia workstation (GK310) and a video capture card (Epiphan AV.io HD) and then input into the computer, enabling real-time superimposition and navigation of the preoperative 3D model onto the surgical field. AR-ANT is an advanced intraoperative navigation modality built upon the preoperative 3D models created by TDVT. While TDVT provides comprehensive preoperative visualization and surgical planning, AR-ANT enables real-time superimposition of these 3D models onto the laparoscopic surgical field, thereby extending the utility of TDVT from preoperative planning to intraoperative guidance. Both technologies are collectively referred to as TDNT (Figure S1). Intraoperative ultrasound and ICG fluorescence imaging were also utilized in selected cases to further validate vascular and biliary anatomy, as demonstrated in Video S1.

The AR-ANS procedure included system calibration, importation and rendering of the 3D model, conversion of surgical video, and image localization. Complications and classification

Postoperative complications encompass any adverse events occurring after surgery. In this study, post-hepatectomy liver failure (PHLF) was defined as serum total bilirubin >50 µmol/L on postoperative day 5 (POD5) combined with prothrombin time <50% (16). Postoperative bile leakage was defined as drainage fluid bilirubin concentration ≥3 times the serum bilirubin level on postoperative day 3, or bile accumulation requiring surgical intervention or biliary peritonitis (17). Postoperative pancreatic fistula was diagnosed when drainage fluid amylase exceeded three times the upper limit of serum amylase (18). The timing for abdominal drainage tube removal was determined by assessing the resolution of biliary or pancreatic leakage through CT scans and laboratory results.

All postoperative complications were classified according to the Clavien-Dindo classification system. The CCI was quantified using a weighted formula to reflect the number and severity of complications, with higher CCI values indicating a greater number or severity of complications, and CCI =100 representing mortality. For complications requiring multiple treatments (e.g., bile leak or residual biliary dilation), grading was based on the patient’s medical records. Based on previous studies (19-21), a CCI ≥26.2 was used as a critical threshold for analysis and discussion. Postoperative hypovolemia/hypotension requiring blood transfusion or vasopressor therapy was classified as Clavien-Dindo Grade II, while the use of Ringer’s lactate or albumin alone was not considered a complication. Mild arrhythmias, short-term pharmacological treatment, and nausea/vomiting controlled by antiemetics were classified as Clavien-Dindo Grade I. Postoperative need for prokinetic agents to resume oral intake or pharmacological correction of electrolyte disturbances was also classified as Clavien-Dindo Grade I. Minor bleeding controlled by hemostasis was classified as Clavien-Dindo Grade I, while pleural effusion or ascites requiring percutaneous drainage under local anesthesia were classified as Clavien-Dindo Grade III.

Data analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp). The normality of continuous data was assessed by the Shapiro-Wilk test, complemented by visual inspection via Q-Q plots. Continuous variables are presented as medians with IQR and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test or Kruskal-Wallis test, as appropriate. Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages and compared using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test. Univariate analysis was performed using odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) to identify independent risk factors associated with postoperative CCI ≥26.2, as well as pancreatic leak and bile leak. Univariate regression analyses were first conducted to identify potential predictors of outcomes. Variables demonstrating statistical significance in univariate analysis, as well as clinically relevant variables, were subsequently entered into multivariable regression models. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Preoperative results

This study enrolled 56 patients, with 25 in the TDN group (11 in the TDVT group and 14 in the AR-ANT group) and 31 in the non-TDN group. No significant differences were observed between the two groups in baseline characteristics such as gender, age, preoperative symptoms, biliary lesions, and laboratory tests (all P>0.05), although the non-TDN group exhibited lower preoperative hemoglobin levels. The distribution of CBD according to the Todani classification was comparable between the two groups (P>0.05, see Table 1 for type I/IV/V proportions or supplementary material for full distribution). Specifically, Type I cysts were the most prevalent in both groups. Assessment based on the Dong classification also showed no significant intergroup disparity, confirming the anatomical comparability of the cohorts at baseline. The distribution of surgical years between the two groups was comparable (P=0.968), with both groups having similar proportions of patients from the earlier period [2010–2017] and later period [2018–2023]. Perioperative results

The TDN group had a longer operation time (6.30±2.40 vs. 5.10±1.20 hours) compared to the non-TDN group. Postoperative enteral nutrition recovery time (5.24±2.50 vs. 6.20±3.62 days) and intraoperative blood loss (213±195 vs. 220±182 mL) were lower in the TDN group than in the non-TDN group, but these differences were not statistically significant (P>0.05). Both groups underwent choledochojejunostomy for reconstruction when lesions involved the common bile duct, with corresponding hepatic segment resection performed in cases involving the intrahepatic bile duct. Major hepatectomy (involving three or more liver segments) was not performed in this study. Detailed perioperative and postoperative data for both groups are shown in Table 2.

No significant differences were observed between the two groups in postoperative white blood cell (WBC) counts, total/direct bilirubin (TBIL/DBIL), or aminotransferases (ALT/AST) levels (all P>0.05). On POD5, the TDN group demonstrated significantly higher hemoglobin levels compared to the non-TDN group (116.73±17.84 vs. 97.10±13.19 g/L, P=0.008) and a shorter duration of abdominal drainage (6.17±2.56 vs. 11.66±9.11 days, P=0.013). After excluding cases with drainage tube placement exceeding 120 days, the TDN group exhibited a slightly longer hospital stay (13.28±10.88 vs. 12.07±5.85 days), though the difference was not statistically significant (P>0.05).

Postoperative complications

The incidence of postoperative pancreatic fistula (12% vs. 39%, P=0.025) and bile leak (28% vs. 68%, P=0.003) was significantly lower in the TDN group compared with the non-TDN group. The TDN group had a higher rate of blood transfusion (P=0.009) (Table 3). One patient in the non-TDN group underwent laparotomy due to intra-abdominal bleeding and biliary-enteric anastomotic leak, and eventually died of hemorrhagic shock and liver dysfunction. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to adjust for potential confounders, including year of surgery, preoperative WBC, DBIL, and TBIL levels. The use of TDNT remained independently associated with a lower risk of bile leakage (OR: 9.29, 95% CI: 2.24–38.61, P=0.002; Table 4) and pancreatic fistula (OR: 10.16, 95% CI: 1.27–81.16, P=0.029; Table 5).

Classification and scoring of postoperative complications

Overall, among the 56 patients, 50 (89%) experienced postoperative complications of varying degrees. We separately counted the occurrence of postoperative complications in each group (Table 6). The 90-day mortality rate was 1.8% (1 case). Patients were stratified based on the CCI into categories of 0–20, 21–40, 41–60, and 61–100. The distribution of patients with CCI values in the TDN group was 24%, 44%, 24%, and 8%, respectively, while in the non-TDN group, it was 10%, 32%, 42%, and 16%, respectively. The percentage of patients with high CCI values (CCI ≥26.2) in the non-TDN group was significantly higher than in the TDN group (60% vs. 87%, P<0.05). The postoperative CCI assessment showed that the distribution of CCI in the non-TDN group tended to be concentrated in the 0–20 and 21–40 ranges, while the TDN group was concentrated in the 21–40 and 41–60 ranges. To investigate the impact of AR-ANT on the patients’ CCI, we further divided the TDN group into the AR-ANT group and the TDVT group, and then compared the re-divided groups with the non-TDN group (Table S2). Multivariable analysis further demonstrated that TDNT use was independently associated with a reduced risk of CCI ≥26.2 (OR: 4.50, 95% CI: 1.20–16.85, P=0.026; Table S3), while no significant independent association was observed for CDC grade ≥ III (Table S4).

Discussion

TDNT (based on TDVT and AR-ANT) has been applied in the treatment of liver cancer (22,23), intrahepatic bile duct stones (24), and pancreatic cancer (25), but its application in CC resection remains underexplored. Chen et al. (26,27) demonstrated that MRI-based 3D reconstruction can accurately depict biliary anatomical variations and correlate with surgical outcomes. Abou-Khalil et al. (28,29) emphasized the importance of preoperative 3D reconstruction of the biliary tree in preventing postoperative complications, while Dou et al. (30) developed a quantitative model of bile duct morphology, offering a novel strategy for surgical planning. This study is the first to report the efficacy of TDNT in CBD resection.

The surgical strategy for CBD is fundamentally dictated by its anatomical subtype. According to the Todani classification, Type I cysts necessitate complete excision of the extrahepatic dilated segment with Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy, while Types IV and V involve intrahepatic disease requiring hepatic resection (4,5). The Dong classification further refines this, especially for Type I cysts, by emphasizing the pattern of involvement at the pancreaticobiliary junction (e.g., supra-pancreatic, intra-pancreatic) (21). This distinction is critical because the surgical challenge in Type I cysts lies in determining the optimal distal transection point: too proximal risks leaving behind dysplastic epithelium, while too distal risks injury to the pancreatic duct. Our application of TDNT directly addresses this challenge. By providing a clear, 3D visualization of the cyst’s termination relative to the pancreatic duct (as categorized by Dong’s criteria), TDNT enables precise preoperative planning and intraoperative navigation for safe and complete resection. For the more complex Types IV and V, TDNT aids in delineating the intrahepatic biliary anatomy and its relationship to vascular structures, facilitating parenchymal-sparing yet radical resections.

On the fifth day post-operation, the hemoglobin level in the TDN group was significantly higher than that in the non-TDN group (P=0.009), while no significant differences were observed in other laboratory indicators between the two groups. After excluding the influence of drug interventions on hemoglobin, both groups of patients underwent a fasting period post-operation. However, the TDN group exhibited a significantly higher transfusion rate, predominantly with fresh frozen plasma. The higher transfusion rate in the TDN group may be attributed to the proactive use of fresh frozen plasma in patients with complex anatomy or longer operative times, reflecting a more cautious perioperative management strategy rather than increased blood loss. Notably, five cases of postoperative hemorrhage requiring interventional treatment were observed in the non-TDN group (0 vs. 5, P=0.103). Therefore, this study suggests that the difference in hemoglobin levels may be associated with the early supplementation of fresh frozen plasma and the lesser postoperative plasma loss in the TDN group.

In terms of postoperative complications, the incidence of bile leakage and pancreatic fistula in the TDN group was significantly lower than that in the non-TDN group, indicating that 3D navigation-assisted surgery can more accurately identify and protect the bile duct and surrounding tissues. Previous studies have pointed out that the risk of bile leakage is associated with the number of hepatic resections, operation duration, diabetes, and prolonged hospitalization (31-33). This study focuses on the role of 3D navigation, particularly for type I CCs, which are closely related to anatomical variations in the pancreaticobiliary junction. Due to abnormal function of the Oddi sphincter, pancreaticobiliary reflux leads to persistent inflammation, becoming a potential cause of bile duct dilation and postoperative bile leakage and pancreatic fistula. 3D navigation aids in the intraoperative assessment of the pancreaticobiliary junction, protecting critical anatomical structures. Furthermore, our previous research (34) has confirmed that locating the residual pancreatic duct through AR-ANS and completing the anastomosis can effectively reduce the risk of bile leakage and pancreatic fistula.

There were also differences in other complications among the groups, including delayed gastric emptying (1 vs. 7), electrolyte disturbances (12 vs. 17), postoperative cholangitis (9 vs. 15), and postoperative hemorrhage (0 vs. 5). According to the Clavien-Dindo classification, a CCI >26.2 is defined as the threshold for at least one Grade III complication (19,20). Compared to the non-TDN group, the proportion of patients with a CCI ≥26.2 in the TDN group was significantly lower (60% vs. 87%, P<0.05). Additionally, within the CCI range of 41–60, the number of patients in the non-TDN group (13 cases) was approximately twice that in the TDN group (6 cases). The results indicate that the TDN group had superior postoperative outcomes, further confirming the advantages of the perioperative TDN strategy in reducing the incidence of complications. The independent association between TDNT use and reduced postoperative complications was further supported by multivariable logistic regression analysis. After adjusting for potential confounders including year of surgery and preoperative laboratory parameters, TDNT remained significantly associated with lower risks of bile leakage and pancreatic fistula. These findings suggest that the observed benefits are unlikely to be solely attributable to baseline differences between groups and reinforce the potential clinical utility of TDNT in enhancing surgical precision. However, the limited sample size constrained the number of covariates that could be included in the model, and unmeasured confounders such as detailed surgical difficulty or specific anatomical complexity may still exist.

The AR-ANS technology demonstrates promising prospects in hepatobiliary surgery, and its ability to enhance the precision and safety of liver resection (35), intrahepatic bile duct stones (36), and pancreaticoduodenectomy (34) has been progressively validated. This technology can display the anatomical structures of intrahepatic vessels and bile ducts in real-time, effectively guiding surgical procedures and reducing the risk of injury. While TDVT comprehensively evaluates lesions by constructing 3D models, AR-ANS integrates these models into the actual surgical field of view, with the two technologies complementing each other.

However, this study still has limitations. Firstly, it is a single-center retrospective study, so accidental bias cannot be completely ruled out. Although we recognize the need for external validation, the rarity of CBD as a disease hinders this attempt. Additionally, although AR-ANT was introduced later than TDVT, the distribution of surgical years between the TDN and non-TDN groups was comparable (P=0.968). Nevertheless, potential chronological bias cannot be completely excluded, as improvements in perioperative care or surgical experience over time may have contributed to the observed outcomes. The integrated analysis of TDVT and AR-ANT as a single TDNT group reflects their complementary nature in clinical practice. However, this precludes separate evaluation of each component technology. Multicenter prospective studies are warranted to further validate the added value of AR-ANT.

Conclusions

TDNT provides a valuable navigational aid for the complete surgical resection of CBD. The findings suggest associations between TDNT use and reduced major postoperative complications, indicating promising potential for enhancing precision and safety.

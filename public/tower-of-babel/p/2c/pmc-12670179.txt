An Advanced Host Material for Aqueous Zinc–iodine Batteries
Authors: Jianbiao Wang, Deviprasath Chinnadurai, Zhi Wei Seh
Published: 2025

Aqueous zinc–iodine batteries (AZIBs) have attracted intensive research interest owing to their outstanding safety, high theoretical capacity, and cost-effectiveness, positioning them as highly promising candidates for large-scale energy storage applications. Further development and practical commercialization of AZIBs remain critically constrained by several intrinsic challenges, including the low electronic conductivity of iodine (I2) and the shuttle effect of polyiodide intermediates (I3 – and I5 –). − Collectively, these issues lead to rapid capacity decay, poor rate performance, and severe self-discharge in AZIBs. , To mitigate these limitations, a variety of transition metal–based host materialsranging from single-atom catalysts to transition metal phosphideshave been investigated, demonstrating notable improvements in electrochemical performance. , However, most transition metal–based host materials are intrinsically crystalline, offering a limited number of active sites for polyiodides, which constrains their adsorption capacity and catalytic efficiency. In parallel, carbon-based hosts have been explored to enhance the conductivity of iodine cathodes and improve polyiodide adsorption. Yet, owing to their weak interactions with polar iodine species, carbon hosts primarily rely on physical adsorption, which proves insufficient to effectively suppress the shuttle effect. Consequently, designing host materials that simultaneously provide strong polyiodide binding and high catalytic activity remains a significant challenge.

Recently, Ma et al. reported the synthesis of an efficient iodine host material, comprising amorphous cobalt phosphide embedded in activated carbon (ACoP@C), via a one-step gas-phase phosphating strategy, which was employed as an AZIB cathode for the first time. The amorphous cobalt phosphide was uniformly dispersed within the carbon matrix, providing abundant binding sites for polyiodide adsorption and accelerating the redox conversion of iodine species (Figure ). In addition, the porous carbon framework enhanced electronic conductivity and offered sufficient storage space for iodine. As a result, the ACoP@C/I2 electrode delivered impressive electrochemical performance.

In detail, the ACoP@C host material was synthesized through a facile vapor-phase phosphating strategy, employing sodium dihydrogen phosphate as the phosphorus source. The precursor solution, obtained by mixing activated carbon (AC) with cobalt salts and stirring at 80 °C for 4 h, was subjected to an annealing process to induce in situ phosphocobaltization. The final ACoP@C/I2 composite was prepared via a modified melt-diffusion method by thoroughly grinding the as-prepared ACoP@C with solid I2, followed by heat treatment at 90 °C for 8 h in a sealed glass vial. Structural and morphological characterization confirmed the highly amorphous nature of ACoP@C, in sharp contrast to the crystalline frameworks of most reported transition metal–based host materials. Furthermore, nitrogen adsorption–desorption measurements revealed that ACoP@C possesses a large specific surface area and a hierarchically porous structure, both favorable for iodine loading and catalytic conversion, thereby enhancing redox kinetics while suppressing iodine dissolution. Raman spectroscopy further demonstrated the defect-rich nature of ACoP@C, attributed to the amorphous CoP domains, with a higher defect density than pristine AC. Such structural defects are particularly beneficial for strengthening polyiodide adsorption, thereby improving overall electrochemical performance. The excellent iodine species adsorption-catalytic performance of the ACoP@C composite material was confirmed by visual observations, UV–vis spectroscopy and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy. Following the successful iodine absorption tests, the battery performance of ACoP@C/I2 host materials was tested. First, the rate capability test was conducted between current ranges from 0.1 A/g to a high current density of 5 A/g, which explained the remarkable reversibility of the host material electrode. In long-term cycling tests, a high capacity of 80 mAh g–1 over 850 cycles at 1.0 A g–1, was achieved with the ACoP@C/I2 electrode. In contrast, the electrode of active carbon/I2 (AC/I2) can only sustain a low capacity of 56.1 mAh g–1 after 260 cycles, indicating the superiority of the ACoP@C/I2 electrode. This work provides more insight into the design of advanced host materials for AZIBs from the combination of polar and nonpolar materials.

Overall, Ma’s work demonstrates a facile strategy for constructing ACoP@C as a multifunctional iodine host specifically designed for aqueous AZIBs. By integrating the strong adsorption and catalytic properties of amorphous cobalt phosphide with the high conductivity of porous carbon, this architecture enables ACoP@C/I2 cathodes to deliver an exceptional rate capability (up to 5 A g–1) and outstanding cycling stability over 850 cycles at 1.0 A g–1. Beyond this specific system, the approach offers a generalizable pathway for synthesizing amorphous transition metal–based hosts in combination with carbon frameworks for high-performance AZIBs. We believe that such hybrid architectures, which couple amorphous transition metal derivatives with conductive carbon matrices, hold significant promise for overcoming persistent challenges and unlocking the full potential of AZIBs.

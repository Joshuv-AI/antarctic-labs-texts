Risk stratification of high-risk muscle invasive upper urinary tract urothelial carcinoma: multi-institutional study
Authors: Hao-Lun Luo, Hung-Lung Ke, Wei-Ming Li, Yao-Chou Tsai, Shu-Yu Wu, Shih-Hsiu Lo, Yi-Huei Chang, Chi-Ping Huang, Hung-Jen Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to analyze a large multi-institutional UTUC database to identify prognostic factors in patients with organ-confined and non-organ-confined disease, and to further stratify pT2 patients using a nomogram to identify high-risk subgroups who may benefit from adjuvant therapy.MethodsThe records of patients diagnosed with UCTC who underwent nephroureterectomy from 1988 to 2022 at 15 hospitals in Taiwan were retrospectively reviewed. The outcomes evaluated were overall survival (OS), cancer-specific survival (CSS), disease-free survival (DFS), and bladder recurrence-free survival (BRFS) after nephroureterectomy.ResultsA total of 2635 patients were included: 1935 with organ-confined (pTis/pTa/pT1/pT2 stage) disease, and 700 with non-organ confined (pT3/pT4 stage) disease. Significant risk factors for poor survival in patients with organ-confined disease were age ≥ 70 years, ureter involvement, high pathological T stage (pT2), multiplicity, lymphovascular invasion, and eGFR ≤ 44 ml/min/1.73 m2. In patients with non-organ-confined disease, risk factors included male sex, age ≥ 70 years, ureter involvement, high pathological T stage (pT4), multiplicity, eGFR ≤ 44 ml/min/1.73 m2, tumor necrosis, variant UC cell type, and smoking. In patients with pT2 UTUC, those classified as high risk based on a nomogram-derived total score > 115 had significantly worse OS (P < 0.0001) and CSS (P = 0.025) compared to those with a score ≤ 115 (low risk).ConclusionsPatients with high-risk pT2 UTUC had significantly worse survival outcomes compared to those with low-risk pT2 UTUC. These findings suggest that further refinement of clinical trial designs is needed to better identify high-risk pT2 UTUC patients who may benefit from more aggressive treatment strategies, such as adjuvant therapy, while minimizing unnecessary systemic therapy in low-risk pT2 UTUC patients. Future studies should validate these findings and help establish the true value of systemic therapy for this patient population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00345-025-05915-8.Keywords: Lymphovascular invasion, Multiplicity, Overall survival, Pathological t stage, Upper urinary tract urothelial carcinoma

Introduction

Upper urinary tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC) is a relatively rare disease with poor recurrence-free survival (RFS), cancer-specific survival (CSS), and overall survival (OS) [1, 2]. The annual incidence of UTUC is estimated to be about 2 cases per 100,000 persons [1]. Radical nephroureterectomy is the standard treatment for UTUC; however, the prognosis is poor, primarily due to the high recurrence rate. Chemotherapy has been shown to improve survival somewhat in patients with advanced stage disease and muscle-invasive disease [1, 3].

The Peri-Operative chemotherapy versus sUrveillance in upper Tract urothelial cancer (POUT) trial showed that adjuvant chemotherapy significantly improved disease-free survival (DFS) in patients with locally advanced UTUC. However, there was no statistical overall survival benefit for patients received adjuvant chemotherapy compared with those who did not receive adjuvant chemotherapy [4, 5]. This suggests that adjuvant chemotherapy may be useful in patients with high-risk, more advanced stage disease, but does not provide a benefit in patients who are lower risk for recurrence.

Other studies have examined risk factors for survival in patients with UTUC, and which patients are likely to benefit from adjuvant therapies; however, no clear consensus has emerged despite many studies focused on this issue [6–14]. In order to improve the outcomes of patients with UTUC, it is necessary to determine the risk factors of poorer outcomes in order to select patients who will benefit from more aggressive treatments. Thus, the purpose of this study was to use a large multi-institutional UTUC database to identify prognostic factors in both organ-confined and non-organ-confined disease, and to further stratify pT2 patients using a nomogram to identify those at highest risk who may benefit from adjuvant therapy.

Methods

Patients

In this multicenter study, the records of patients diagnosed with UCTC who underwent nephroureterectomy from 1988 to 2022 at 15 hospitals in Taiwan were retrospectively reviewed. Pathological reports from each medical center were submitted to a centralized database following a standardized reporting protocol. All participating institutions contributed data for eligible patients in accordance with unified criteria, ensuring that no eligible patients were omitted. However, the completeness of individual pathological parameters depended on the original reports provided by each center. Patients with any of the following data missing were excluded: (1) Comorbidities; (2) Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR); (3) Pathological information, including cell type, tumor stage, histology, multiplicity, cancer in situ, or lymphovascular invasion; (4) Surgical information, including surgical margins (positive or negative), tumor necrosis, or lymph node status (positive or negative); (5) Perioperative chemotherapy. The exclusion of patients who had undergone chemotherapy was intended to explore the natural course of UTUC and identify high-risk groups who would benefit from adjuvant therapy. Therefore, neither neoadjuvant nor adjuvant chemotherapy was included as a variable in the univariate or multivariable analyses. Only patients with localized UTUC were included in the study. Patients with pathologically confirmed lymph node metastasis (pN+) were excluded. The final cohort therefore consisted of patients with either pathologically confirmed node-negative disease (pN0) or those who did not undergo lymph node dissection and were classified as pNx. As a result, nodal positivity was not evaluated as a prognostic factor in this study. The selection procedure is shown in Supplemental Figure S1.

The collaborative study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Institutional Review Board of Taipei Tzu Chi Hospital, Buddhist Tzu Chi Medical Foundation (12-X-004). Signed informed consent was waived because of the retrospective design of the study, and no patient identifying information was collected.

Patient demographic information and clinical characteristics were obtained from the medical records. Tumor location was identified by preoperative computed tomography (CT). Pathological T stage (pT stage) and pathological features were determined based on final nephroureterectomy specimens. Tumors were graded according to the 2004 World Health Organization (WHO) classification [15], and staged according to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines and the 2010 American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM classification [16].

Outcomes

The primary outcomes were overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) after nephroureterectomy. Secondary outcomes included disease-free survival (DFS) and bladder recurrence-free survival (BRFS). For pT2-stage patients, additional risk stratification into high- and low-risk subgroups was performed based on a nomogram-derived score.

Statistical analysis

Categorical data are presented as counts and percentages and compared using the chi-squared test. Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to estimate survival rates, and the survival curves were compared using the pairwise log-rank test among different pathological T stage groups, including the pT2 subgroup further stratified into high- and low-risk categories based on nomogram-derived scores. A nomogram was constructed based on a multivariable Cox proportional hazards model to generate individualized risk scores. A cutoff value of 115 points was used to define high- and low-risk subgroups within the pT2 population for further survival analysis. Age was dichotomized at 70 years, a threshold commonly used in prior UTUC studies to distinguish older from younger patients and to facilitate comparability with existing literature [17–19].

Variables with p < 0.05 in the univariable analysis were entered into the multivariable Cox proportional hazards model, ensuring an adequate number of events per variable to reduce the risk of overfitting. Cox proportional hazard models were used to assess the effect of the surgical approaches on the outcomes, alone and after adjusting for potential confounders. All statistical assessments were two-tailed, and a value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were carried out with IBM SPSS statistical software version 26 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY).

Compared to patients with organ-confined disease, those with non-organ-confined disease were more likely to be ≥ 70 Years of age, have tumors located at the renal pelvis, display multiplicity, receive lymph node dissection, and exhibit adverse pathological features such as high-grade histology, lymphovascular invasion, tumor necrosis, and variant histology. The median follow-up was 53.4 months in patients with organ-confined disease and 26.5 months in patients with non-organ-confined disease.

Survival curves of patients with different pathological stages

We further conducted survival analysis for all patients according to their pathological stage. To better stratify the pT2 subgroup, patients were categorized into high- and low-risk groups based on a nomogram-derived total point score (cutoff = 115). The corresponding nomograms for predicting 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS and CSS in patients with pT2 UTUC, which were used to derive the risk score for stratification, are shown in Supplementary Figure S2. In the overall cohort, OS differed significantly across pT stages (Fig. 1A), and within the pT2 subgroup, high-risk patients had significantly worse OS than low-risk patients (Fig. 1B, p < 0.001). Both high-risk and low-risk pT2 patients had significantly better OS than pT3 patients. For CSS, stage-based differences were also significant (Fig. 1C), and within the pT2 subgroup, low-risk patients had significantly better CSS than high-risk patients (Fig. 1D, p = 0.025). CSS was similar between high-risk pT2 and pT3 patients. These pairwise comparisons indicate that nomogram-based stratification effectively distinguishes prognostic subgroups within pT2 disease. All pairwise log-rank test p-values for OS and CSS comparisons are provided in Supplementary Figure S3.

Discussion

The POUT trial showed adjuvant chemotherapy significantly improved DFS in patients with muscle-invasive or node positive UTUC, but no statistical benefit in OS in multivariable analysis [4, 5]. The lack of overall survival benefit was supposed to be partially related to the selection of patients at risk of recurrence. Not all patients with muscle-invasive UTUC should receive adjuvant chemotherapy and be exposed to the risk of chemotherapy agents. Therefore, we separately analyzed the risk factors for poor survival in organ-confined and non-organ confined UTUC. Rather than providing direct treatment recommendations, this study aimed to evaluate prognostic factors in organ-confined and non–organ-confined UTUC, and to further identify high-risk pT2 patients using nomogram-based stratification who may benefit from adjuvant therapy in future clinical trials.

The present study showed that older age, ureter involvement, multiplicity, high grade, and variant pathology are the common risk factors of poor survivals in both organ-confined and non-organ confined UTUC groups. These are also prognostic factors in the 2023-updated European Association of Urology guidelines on UTUC [1], showing the reliability of our study. eGFR ≤ 44 ml/min/1.73 m2 is also an independent factor in both groups. Studies have reported that chronic kidney disease [20, 21] and hydronephrosis [22, 23] are associated with poor survival in patients with UTUC after nephroureterectomy, showing that an insufficient renal function predicts poor survival. Lymphovascular invasion is the process of tumor invasion into the epithelium-lined space and infiltration into the stroma through vascular or lymphatic capillaries, initiating early metastasis. Studies have indicated that lymphovascular invasion in histological specimens are correlated with metastasis, earlier recurrence in lymph nodes, and lower survival in various types of malignancies [24–26]. Although lymphovascular invasion is a well-established prognostic factor for poorer outcomes of UTUC [1, 7, 22, 27], it is an independent risk factor only in the organ-confined UTUC group in the present study. Given that non-organ confined disease is no longer localized, the presence or absence of lymphovascular invasion will not affect the clinician’s judgment of whether the disease is at high-risk of recurrence. Smoking is an independent risk factor only in the non-organ confined UTUC group. Rink et al. [28] has reported that smoking is highly associated with advanced UTUC stage and mortality, although the mechanism is not clear yet.

In clinical practice, all patients with non-organ-confined pT3 and pT4 UTUC should receive adjuvant therapy. In our study, nomogram-based risk stratification showed that high-risk pT2 UTUC patients had significantly worse OS, CSS, and DFS compared to low-risk pT2 patients. Both pT2-low and pT2-high groups had significantly better OS than pT3, and pT2-low also had superior OS compared to pT2-high. For CSS, pT2-low had significantly better outcomes than pT2-high, while CSS was similar between pT2-high and pT3. These findings suggest that pT2-high patients may warrant consideration for adjuvant therapy, whereas pT2-low patients whose OS was superior to pT3 and comparable to pTis/pTa/pT1 could potentially avoid unnecessary treatment. Similarly, Zhai et al. [8] reported that chemotherapy was associated with a lower OS in pT2 patients and a lower CSS in pT1 patients in a SEER database study. Jiang et al. [9] also reported that adjuvant chemotherapy is not associated with intravesical recurrence in patients with organ-confined UTUC after nephroureterectomy. The results of our study provide a foundation for future research and may contribute to the development of more precise treatment strategies for high-risk pT2 UTUC patients. Further studies are needed to validate these findings and establish the optimal adjuvant treatment strategies for this patient group.

Limitations

This study has some limitations that should be considered. First, it has the limitations inherent to retrospective studies. Second, as a multi-institutional study, variations in surgical methods, pathological assessments, and follow-up protocols across different centers may introduce biases. These variations, such as differences in how tumor location was determined (primarily based on preoperative imaging from individual institutions rather than a uniform assessment of final nephroureterectomy specimens), could contribute to data variability. Third, the distribution of patients across different treatment eras was unbalanced, which may introduce bias when interpreting survival trends over time. We acknowledge that temporal improvements in diagnosis, treatment protocols, and supportive care could confound the survival analysis by era. Finally, risk factors were identified and validated within the same cohort, which may affect its generalizability. However, this study primarily serves as an exploratory analysis, providing valuable insights, and further validation using independent cohorts or prospective studies remains necessary.

Conclusions

In this multi-institutional analysis, prognostic factors were evaluated in the overall organ-confined UTUC population (pTis/pTa/pT1/pT2), and nomogram-based risk stratification was performed specifically for pT2 disease. In the overall organ-confined UTUC population (pTis/pTa/pT1/pT2), Age ≥ 70 years, ureter involvement, pathological stage pT2, multiplicity, lymphovascular invasion, and an eGFR ≤ 44 ml/min/1.73 m2 were identified as independent risk factors for poor survival. For patients with pT2 disease specifically, risk stratification based on a nomogram-derived score demonstrated that those in the higher-risk group had significantly worse prognosis compared to those in the lower-risk group. To improve patient outcomes, more aggressive treatment strategies, such as adjuvant chemotherapy, should be explored in future clinical trials for high-risk pT2 UTUC patients following nephroureterectomy. These trials could help assess the true value of systemic therapy for this patient group, while avoiding unnecessary treatment for low-risk pT2 UTUC patients.

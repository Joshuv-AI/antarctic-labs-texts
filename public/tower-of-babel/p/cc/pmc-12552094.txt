Understanding Barriers to Adjuvant Therapy in Resected Non-small Cell Lung Cancer: A Multicenter Analysis
Authors: Paula Duarte D'Ambrosio, Leticia Leone Lauricella, Benoit Jacques Bibas, Fabio Eiti Minamoto, Flavia Alves Corrêa de Queiroz, Pedro Henrique Xavier Nabuco de Araújo, Jefferson Luiz Gross, Federico Enrique Garcia Cipriano, Henrique Nietmann, Fabio May da Silva, Paulo Manuel Pêgo-Fernandes, Ricardo Mingarini Terra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo investigate variables associated to not receiving adjuvant therapy in patients with resected pathological IB-IIIA non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) from a national registry.MethodsPatients who had lung resections were analysed retrospectively for stages IB-III from 2009-2023, sourced from a multicenter registry. Uni/multivariable logistic regression was performed, and a predictive tool was created to predict adjuvant therapy probability and cut-off values were determined through receiver-operating characteristic curve (ROC). The model’s discrimination, performance, and fit were evaluated using area under the ROC curve (AUC-ROC), sensitivity/specificity, and Hosmer-Lemeshow test, respectively.ResultsOf the 427 patients analysed, the mean age was 65.4 years, and 83.1% were treated under public insurance. Precisely, 38.4% of patients received adjuvant chemotherapy after surgery. Multivariable analysis identified older age (odds ratio [OR] = 1.06, P < 0.001), histological subtype (OR = 21.3, P < 0.001), public insurance (OR = 2.65, P = 0.006), stage I-B (OR = 8.10, P = 0.002), and negative lymph node status (OR = 3.34, P < 0.001) as independent factors associated with not receiving adjuvant therapy. Using the final equation, a calculated probability > 42.5% categorizes a patient for exclusive surgical intervention. The model’s performance yielded an AUC of 0.833, with a sensitivity of 90.2%. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test resulted in a P ≥ 0.05.ConclusionsUnderutilization of adjuvant therapy in NSCLC is influenced by age, insurance type, and pathological factors. Public insurance status indicates healthcare access but does not fully capture social disparities. The absence of race, income, and geographic data limits broader analysis. Our model identifies patients at risk of treatment omission, aiding targeted interventions.Keywords: lung cancer, national cancer database, adjuvant treatment, chemotherapy

INTRODUCTION

Surgery is recommended for stage I, while stages II and III may require multimodality treatment.1,2 Adjuvant therapy is crucial due to micro metastasis and potential recurrence post-surgery.2 Despite its significance, adjuvant systemic therapy is underutilized in clinical practice.3

Observational studies and randomized trials show delivery rates below 50% among eligible patients.4–6 Inequities and social disparities may influence this underutilization.7,8 Certain demographics like older patients, males, those with low socioeconomic status, uninsured or publicly insured patients, and racial/ethnic minorities are more likely to receive incomplete treatment.9 This underutilization may worsen outcome disparities among non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients.

Adjuvant chemotherapy historically offered a limited 5% overall survival advantage over surgery but with safety concerns.9,10 A study by Harada et al10 assessed adjuvant chemotherapy in a real-world context. Only 34.6% of patients received adjuvant therapy, with cisplatin-vinorelbine regimen causing high toxicity. The study highlighted challenges in delivering adjuvant therapy due to severe toxicity, emphasizing the need for less toxic alternatives.

Recent trials show improvements in disease-free survival with immunotherapy and targeted therapy, shifting this paradigm.11–14 Atezolizumab, which was approved in the United States in 2021, made history as the initial immune checkpoint inhibitor employed as adjuvant therapy after surgery and platinum-based chemotherapy for stage II-IIIA NSCLC, based on the IMpower010 trial.11,15 Pembrolizumab was approved in 2023 for similar use for stage IB-IIIA NSCLC patients, supported by the KEYNOTE-091 trial.12 Furthermore, the ADAURA trial showed a survival benefit with adjuvant osimertinib in EGFR-mutated IB-IIIA NSCLC patients.16

Given the emergence of more effective systemic therapies, understanding the variables prompting the use of adjuvant therapy among eligible patients is a priority. Identifying those who might not receive adequate treatment is vital to ensure the provision of the best possible care.

Thus, this study aimed to investigate variables associated to not receiving adjuvant therapy in patients with resected pathological pIB-IIIA NSCLC from a National registry (Brazilian registry of Lung Cancer).

METHODS

Ethical statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board under Clinical Registration Number: 16424413.2.1001.0065. Written informed consent was waived as only anonymized data were used. Data storage and biobank usage comply with the WMA Declaration of Taipei and were approved and monitored by a research ethics committee.

Study population

This retrospective analysis included patients who underwent complete anatomic lung resections for NSCLC stage pIB-III, from December 2009-2023. The NSCLC stage was defined as the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM eighth edition. Data were sourced from the Brazilian Registry of Lung Cancer, which encompasses records of lung cancer patients treated surgically at 17 institutions across 5 Brazilian states. Patients were excluded if they received neoadjuvant therapy, had a carcinoid tumour, were stage 1 A, or had missing data—operative, staging, or pathologic.

The following variables were included: sex, race, age at surgery, type of health insurance, NSCLC subtypes, type of pulmonary resection, degree of tumour differentiation, lymphatic invasion, pathological stage, pleural invasion, vascular invasion, and lymph node involvement.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics summarized the data. Quantitative variables were described using minimum, maximum, quartiles, mean, and standard deviation. Categorical and ordinal variables were described using absolute (n) and relative (%) frequencies.

Logistic regression analyses were conducted to evaluate the statistical significance (P < 0.05) of each independent variable on the outcome variable “Adjuvant Treatment.” Odds ratios (OR) and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated, with the reference category set to OR = 1.00. Independent variables with a P-value < 0.40 in the univariable analysis were included in the multivariable logistic regression analysis. In the multivariable analysis, significant (P < 0.05) risk factors were identified. Non-significant risk factors (P ≥ 0.05) were progressively removed from the model. In the final model, the independent/predictor variable “Type of Pulmonary Resection—Pneumonectomy” was retained despite having a significance level slightly above 5% (P < 0.06). This decision was made due to its potential relevance in future studies.

The model’s fit was assessed using Nagelkerke’s pseudo R2 and the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test (P ≥ 0.05 indicates a good fit). The discrimination of the final logistic regression model was evaluated using the area under the ROC curve (AUC-ROC). Sensitivity and specificity were also calculated to assess the model’s performance.

Multicollinearity was assessed by comparing changes in ORs and P-values across sequential models. Stable estimates suggested absence of collinearity, as described by Hosmer, Lemeshow, and Sturdivant. Predictor relationships were also evaluated for clinical plausibility.

A final equation was developed to calculate the probability of a patient undergoing surgery alone. ROC analysis determined the best cut-off value for numerical variables, categorized accordingly. All results were considered significant with P < 0.05. Analyses were performed using R version 3.3.3 and Stata 15.0 software (Stata Corp., College Station, TX, USA).

RESULTS

Among 2554 patients included in the database, a total of 427 patients were included in this analysis, as detailed in the flow chart in Figure 1. The mean age was 65.4 years (±10.3 SD) at surgery. Most patients 353 (83.1%) were treated with government-funded insurance/public insurance. The cohort included 257 (61.8%) with adenocarcinoma and 130 (31.2%) with squamous cell carcinoma. Tumour differentiation was G1-2 in 299 (70.0%) of patients. Pathological lymph node involvement was negative in 269 (63.0%) of patients. Pathological staging showed 108 (25.3%) at stage IB, 52 (12.2%) at stage IIA, 117 (27.4%) at stage IIB, 126 (29.5%) at stage IIIA, and 24 (5.6%) at stage IIIB. Most patients 380 (89.0%) underwent lobectomy, 38 (8.9%) had pneumonectomy, and 9 (2.1%) underwent sublobar resection. Precisely, 38.4% of the patients received adjuvant treatment after anatomic lung resection. The demographic and clinical characteristics of these patients are summarized in Table 1.

Univariable analysis identified factors associated with not delivery of adjuvant therapy (Table 2). Significant factors included age at surgery (each additional year increased the likelihood of surgery alone by 3%, OR = 1.03, 95% CI: 1.01-1.05, P = 0.006), NSCLC subgroups (large cell carcinoma and others had a higher likelihood of not receive adjuvant therapy compared to adenocarcinoma, OR = 6.69, 95% CI: 1.98-22.68, P = 0.002), negative lymph nodes (OR = 6.40, 95% CI: 4.1-9.9, P < 0.001), and absence of lymphatic invasion (OR = 2.02, 95% CI: 1.34-3.04, P = 0.001). Insurance type and pathological staging were also significant predictors. Patients whose treatment was funded by the public were significantly more likely to undergo surgery only compared to those with private coverage (OR = 1.78, 95% CI: 1.07-2.97, P = 0.027). Pathological staging showed that patients with stage I-B had a significantly higher likelihood of not receive adjuvant therapy (OR = 19.6, P < 0.001), as did those with stage II-A (OR = 7.5, P < 0.001) and stage II-B (OR = 2.59, P = 0.044) compared to the reference group (stage III-B). Stages III-A and III-B did not show significant differences.

The results of the multivariable analysis are depicted in Figure 2. Each additional year of age increased the likelihood of surgery alone by 6% (OR = 1.06, 95% CI: 1.03-1.09, P < 0.001). Patients with large cell carcinoma and other types had significantly higher odds of not receiving adjuvant therapy compared to those with adenocarcinoma (OR = 21.3, 95% CI: 4.9-93.3, P < 0.001). The variable “Type of Pulmonary Resection—Pneumonectomy” showed marginal significance (OR = 2.38, 95% CI: 0.98-5.80, P = 0.056) and was retained in the final model despite a significance level slightly above the conventional 5% threshold (P < 0.06). This decision was based on its potential relevance for future research. Furthermore, patients whose treatment was funded by the public health system had 2.65 times higher odds of not receiving adjuvant therapy compared to those with private insurance (OR = 2.65, 95% CI: 1.32-5.30, P = 0.006). Patients with stage I-B disease had significantly higher odds of not receiving adjuvant therapy (OR = 8.10, 95% CI: 2.20-29.9, P = 0.002), while stages II-A and II-B did not show significant differences. Stage III-A was associated with lower odds of not receiving adjuvant therapy but was not statistically significant. Lastly, patients with negative lymph nodes status had 3.34 times higher odds of not receiving adjuvant therapy compared to those with positive nodes (OR = 3.34, 95% CI: 1.84-6.07, P < 0.001). ORs and P-values remained stable across models, indicating no relevant multicollinearity.

The logistic regression model demonstrated strong predictive capability. Sensitivity was 90.2%, and specificity was 60.0%, with an overall correct classification rate of 78.5% (Table 3). Nagelkerke’s pseudo R2 was 0.408, explaining 40.8% of the variability in the outcome. Cox and Snell pseudo R2 was 0.300. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test confirmed a good fit (P ≥ 0.05). The model’s discriminative ability was strong, with an AUC-ROC of 0.833, effectively distinguishing between patients who will undergo surgery alone and those who will receive adjuvant therapy (Figure 3).

DISCUSSION

In our study, 38.4% of eligible patients receive adjuvant therapy. Our multivariable analysis identified older age, stage IB disease, specific pathological factors, and public insurance as independent predictors of adjuvant therapy omission.

Adjuvant therapy remains underutilized in resected early-stage NSCLC, despite its well-documented survival benefits.9 Our findings are consistent with previous studies showing suboptimal adherence to systemic therapy, even in clinical trial settings. In the VIOLET trial, adjuvant therapy uptake was 50.9% in the video-assisted thoracic surgery (VATS) group and 45.9% in open surgery,6 while a comparable US cohort reported a 54.9% administration rate.9 Although guidelines strongly recommend adjuvant therapy for stage IB-IIIA disease,2,17,18 its underuse is a global issue, influenced by factors beyond clinical protocols, including patient preference, provider recommendations, and access to oncologic care.

Our study identified public insurance as a major determinant of adjuvant therapy omission, with patients covered by public insurance having 2.65 times higher odds of not receiving treatment compared to those with private insurance (OR = 2.65, 95% CI: 1.32-5.30, P = 0.006). However, insurance type alone does not fully capture broader socioeconomic disparities, which may also involve race, income, and geographic barriers—factors not included in our analysis. This observation mirrors findings from Rodriguez-Quintero et al,9 who demonstrated that public insurance status in the United States was associated with lower adjuvant therapy use and poorer survival outcomes. Given that 83.1% of our cohort relied on public healthcare, these findings emphasize systemic barriers within Brazil’s Sistema Único de Saúde (SUS), where limited access to specialized care, resource constraints, and geographic disparities affects treatment delivery.19–21 While similar disparities have been documented in other universal healthcare systems, such as the UK’s NHS and US public programs,22,23 the structural challenges in Brazil demand tailored policy approaches to optimize treatment access. Our model is specific to the Brazilian healthcare system, where access to adjuvant therapy is influenced by distinct structural and economic factors. Therefore, its applicability to other healthcare settings, particularly those with different public-private dynamics, remains uncertain and requires external validation in diverse populations.

Patients with stage IB disease had significantly higher odds of not receiving adjuvant therapy (OR = 8.10, 95% CI: 2.20-29.9; P = 0.002), a finding that warrants closer examination. The role of adjuvant chemotherapy in this subgroup remains debated and is generally reserved for patients with recognized risk factors, including tumour size ≥4 cm or VPI.1,2 In a cohort of 1912 patients with resected stage I lung adenocarcinoma, several variables associated with recurrence were described, including higher maximum standardized uptake value (SUV) of the primary tumour (HR, 1.04), sublobar resection (HR = 2.04), histologic grade (HR = 5.32 for grade 2; HR = 7.93 for grade 3), lymphovascular invasion (HR = 1.70), visceral pleural invasion (HR = 1.54), and tumour size (HR = 1.30).24 The cumulative incidence of recurrence at 5 years was 30% among patients with 3 or more of these features, compared to 4% in those without. These data support the use of pathologic and imaging criteria to guide treatment decisions in early-stage disease.

Recent evidence supports expanding indications for adjuvant therapy in stage IB NSCLC. In the IMpower010 trial, adjuvant atezolizumab improved disease-free survival following chemotherapy, particularly among patients with PD-L1-positive tumours.11 The KEYNOTE-091 trial demonstrated a benefit with adjuvant pembrolizumab regardless of PD-L1 expression.12 These findings underscore the need for more precise selection criteria for adjuvant treatment in early-stage disease. In parallel, equitable implementation requires coordinated efforts across health systems. Broader access to novel therapies depends on investment in public funding, regulatory frameworks, and infrastructure. Patient navigation programs and multidisciplinary teams can reduce logistical barriers to care. Although atezolizumab and osimertinib were approved for adjuvant use in Brazil in 2021 and 2022, respectively,11,13 their absence from the public health system (SUS) reveals a persistent gap between clinical evidence and real-world availability. Policy initiatives are needed to ensure that advances in immunotherapy and targeted therapy are incorporated into national treatment protocols.

Older age emerged as a key factor influencing therapy omission, with each additional year increasing the likelihood of surgery alone by 6% (OR = 1.06, 95% CI: 1.03-1.09, P < 0.001). While concerns over competing mortality risks in elderly patients may justify selective therapy omission, chronological age alone should not dictate treatment decisions. Prior studies suggest that functional status and comorbidities, rather than age per se, better predict toxicity tolerance. The Lung Adjuvant Cisplatin Evaluation meta-analysis found no significant differences in severe toxicity or treatment-related mortality between younger and older patients, although elderly patients (≥70 years) received lower initial cisplatin doses.25 Conversely, data from large databases suggest that older patients with NSCLC often have higher competing mortality risks, which may outweigh the potential benefits of adjuvant therapy in certain subgroups.26 These data support a personalized approach to adjuvant therapy decision-making in older patients.

This study has several limitations inherent to its retrospective design. Despite being multicentric, the Brazilian registry does not fully capture the country’s diversity, as data were collected from only 5 states. Additionally, it lacks details on treatment planning, recording only the final treatment delivered, which may introduce selection bias. The absence of data on comorbidities and surgical complications limits the assessment of factors influencing adjuvant therapy decisions. Not all contraindications for adjuvant treatment were available, potentially affecting result interpretation. The study also did not systematically evaluate lymph node staging methods. Moreover, the lack of recurrence and survival data restricts the ability to assess the direct impact of adjuvant therapy omission on long-term outcomes. Finally, race, income, and geographic disparities were not considered, which may influence treatment access and outcomes.

Despite these limitations, our study highlights critical barriers to adjuvant therapy delivery and proposes a predictive model to identify patients at higher risk of omission, aiding clinical decision-making. By incorporating insurance status, age, and tumour characteristics, this model enables personalized management strategies, targeting logistical barriers and optimizing resource allocation, particularly in resource-limited healthcare systems. Validation in larger, more diverse cohorts is necessary to confirm its applicability across different healthcare settings, particularly in regions with distinct public-private dynamics. The EuroLung risk models, initially developed for European populations, were tested in the Brazilian Lung Cancer Registry, where EuroLung2 outperformed EuroLung1.27

CONCLUSION

Adjuvant therapy underutilization in NSCLC is influenced by age, insurance status, and pathological factors, highlighting disparities in healthcare access. While public insurance predicts treatment omission, it reflects accessibility rather than social inequity. Our predictive model helps identify patients at risk, guiding targeted interventions. However, without recurrence and survival data, it serves primarily to assess barriers rather than predict outcomes. External validation is essential to confirm its applicability. Addressing these gaps requires policy improvements, expanded access to systemic therapies, and refined patient selection criteria to ensure equitable treatment delivery.

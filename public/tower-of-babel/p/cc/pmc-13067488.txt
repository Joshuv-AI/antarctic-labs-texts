Outcomes of patients with CLL and borderline IGHV mutational status: a systematic review and meta analysis
Authors: Francesco Angotzi, Arianna Bevilacqua, Andrea Serafin, Nicolò Danesin, Alessandro Cellini, Giovanni Leone, Mattia D’Antiga, Elisa Pagnin, Federica Frezzato, Monica Facco, Francesco Piazza, Livio Trentin, Andrea Visentin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe immunoglobulin heavy chain variable region (IGHV) mutational status is a major prognostic factor in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL). While patients are conventionally classified as mutated (M-IGHV) or unmutated (U-IGHV) using a 98% germline identity cutoff, increasing interest has focused on patients with IGHV identity values close to this threshold, defined as having a borderline IGHV mutational status (BL-IGHV) whose clinical significance remains controversial. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to clarify the prognostic impact of BL-IGHV in CLL. Following PRISMA guidelines, we identified studies comparing time to first treatment (TTFT) in BL-IGHV patients with M-IGHV and U-IGHV cohorts. A secondary endpoint was the prevalence of B-cell receptor (BCR) stereotyped subset #2. Overall, 122 BL-IGHV patients from 3 studies were included. BL-IGHV patients exhibited a significantly shorter TTFT compared with M-IGHV patients (HR 2.28; 95% CI 1.20–4.35), and a non-significant trend toward longer TTFT compared with U-IGHV patients. Median TTFT for BL-IGHV was intermediate between M-IGHV and U-IGHV groups, and BL-IGHV cases were markedly enriched for BCR stereotyped subset #2. In conclusion, BL-IGHV identifies a clinically and biologically distinct CLL subgroup with intermediate outcomes, supporting its systematic reporting and potential relevance for risk stratification.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40364-026-00913-3.Keywords: Chronic lymphocytic leukemia, IGHV mutational status, Immunogenetic, Prognostic factors

To the editor,

The mutational status of the immunoglobulin heavy chain variable region (IGHV) is a cornerstone prognostic marker in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL). Based on the identity of the IGHV gene to its germline sequence, patients are conventionally classified as mutated (M-IGHV; identity < 98%), and unmutated (U-IGHV; identity ≥ 98%), with the latter associated with shorter time to first treatment (TTFT) and early disease progression [1]. Increasing attention has recently been focused toward a third, less well-defined category of patients whose IGHV identity lies close to the 98% cutoff. This “borderline” IGHV mutational status (BL-IGHV), variably defined by a germline identity between 97.00% and 97.99% or 98.99% [2, 3], accounts for approximately 5% of CLL, and its biological and clinical significance has remained controversial due to conflicting results from previous studies [2–4].

To address this uncertainty, we performed a systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of published studies evaluating the outcomes of BL-IGHV CLL. The primary endpoint was TTFT in BL-IGHV versus M-IGHV and U-IGHV groups, using hazard ratio (HR) as the outcome measure. To estimate TTFT, pooled Kaplan-Meier curves were also reconstructed for the analysis of the primary endpoint according to the DerSimonian-Laird method [5]. The secondary endpoint was the prevalence of B-cell receptor (BCR) stereotype subset #2, which confers an adverse prognosis and has been suggested to be enriched among BL-IGHV cases [2–4, 6], with odds ratio (OR) as the outcome measure. Extended methodology is reported in the supplementary materials. This meta-analysis was registered in PROSPERO (ID: CRD420251208370).

A total of 2130 records were identified, of which 965 underwent screening after duplicates removal, and 3 met the inclusion criteria (Fig. 1, Table S1-2). Two additional studies were incorporated in a sensitivity analysis, as they compared BL-IGHV exclusively with M-IGHV patients [7, 8].

PRISMA literature search flow diagram

The aggregated cohort comprised 122 patients with BL-IGHV, 1274 with M-IGHV, and 760 with U-IGHV status. BL-IGHV patients demonstrated a significantly shorter TTFT than M-IGHV (HR: 2.28; 95% CI: 1.20–4.35; I2: 81.4%) and a non-significant trend toward a longer TTFT compared with U-IGHV (HR: 0.55, 95% CI: 0.30–1.03; I2: 80.6%) (Fig. 2).

Forest-plots of TTNT for BL-IGHV patients vs. M-IGHV (A) and U-IGHV (B)

These results were confirmed in two sensitivity analyses for BL-IGHV vs. M-IGHV: one evaluating the 97-97.99% threshold for BL-IGHV by Davis et al., and another one encompassing 2 more studies for a total of 212 BL-IGHV patients (Figure S1-2). Estimated median TTFT from pooled Kaplan-Meier curves was 91.1 months for BL-IGHV versus not reached for M-IGHV (p = 0.003) and 41.1 months for U-IGHV (p < 0.001) (Figure S3). Interestingly, BL-IGHV TTFT mirrors that of intermediate-CLL (i-CLL), a subtype defined by epigenomic profiling and characterized by an IGHV identity close to the 98% cutoff [9]. BL-IGHV patients were markedly enriched in BCR stereotype subset #2 relative to both M-IGHV (OR: 8.56; 95% CI: 3.79–19.33; I2: 11.1%) and U-IGHV (OR: 6.76; 95% CI: 2.45–18.65; I2: 36.5%) (Figure S4). This finding corroborates prior single-study observations and aligns with the preferential usage of IGHV3-21, IGHV3-23 and IGHV3-74 genes among BL-IGHV CLL [2–4]. The enrichment in subset #2, together with the frequent presence of the IGLV3-21*R110 mutation and SOX11 upregulation, may be the contributing factors driving lower TTFT in BL-IGHV patients [10–12]. Indeed, in a sensitivity analysis excluding subset #2 patients, the statistically significant difference between BL-IGHV and M-IGHV patients in terms of TTFT was lost, suggesting a contribution of subset #2 in driving lower outcomes (Figure S5).

Several limitations merit discussion. All included studies were retrospective and carried a moderate risk of bias, with substantial heterogeneity across analyses. The limited number of studies and inconsistent reporting of variables precluded evaluation of enrichment in other clinical/biological characteristics, overall survival, or responses to modern targeted therapies. Moreover, the study by Davies et al. both employed different IGHV cutoffs to define BL-IGHV CLL and reported TTFT outcomes excluding subset #2 patients which together could have influenced results [2].

Despite these limitations, this meta-analysis - the first to date in BL-IGHV CLL – supports that these patients may follow a distinct clinical trajectory, with lower outcomes driven in part by higher prevalence of subset #2, and that may represent a group with shared immunogenetic and epigenomic backgrounds. Accordingly, systematic reporting of BL-IGHV status along with BCR subsets, as recommended by the European Research Initiative on CLL, remains clinically relevant [1].

Enlarged posterior column osteotomy plus intervertebral cage strutting for lumbosacral nerve bowstring disease
Authors: Yilei Liu, Puxin Yang, Hongru Yuan, Jiaxin Xu, Yachong Huo, Wenyuan Ding, Hui Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT

Background

Bowstring disease (BSD) is defined as axial hypertension on nerve roots and the spinal cord caused by congenital anomalies, degeneration or other issues, which result in neurological symptoms. The shape of the lumbosacral spine resembles a bow, and the stretched nerve roots and dura resemble a string, thus comes the name “bowstring disease” [1]. According to the report by Shi et al. at least 10% of outpatients in spine departments are suspected to have BSD [1]. Patients usually complain of persistent back pain and leg pain (lasting for years), which can be aggravated when lying down or bending. Severe cases may present with numbness of the perineum, and dysfunction of urination and defecation. Surgical strategy is required once conservative treatment failed [2, 3].

Very few studies specially focus on the surgical treatment of BSD in the previous reports [1, 4]. Based on the anatomy of the spinal column and nerve roots, Shi et al. proposes a capsule surgery: a nerve root axial decompression technique to restore the coordination between column and cord [1]. Capsule surgery can relieve nerve root hypertonicity by removing 2/3 of the posterior intervertebral discs and interspinous ligaments, compressing the posterior column, partially removing the upper and lower facets of the foramen. Although capsule surgery has been proved to be effective in the treatment of BSD, intervertebral bone fusion is not achieved, postoperative instability and internal fixation failure could not be prevented theoretically.

To minimize the potential risk of postoperative instability and internal fixation failure, we propose a surgical method of enlarged posterior column osteotomy (EPCO) plus intervertebral cage strutting (ICS). In the present study, the safety and efficiency of EPCO plus ICS in the treatment of BSD were investigated.

Materials and methods

Study Design and patients

This was a retrospective study of prospective collected data and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Hebei Medical University Third Hospital before data collection and analysis. Informed consent to participate in the study was obtained from all subjects, written consent for participation of this study was available if required at any time. The inclusion criteria: (1) Lumbosacral nerve BSD that diagnosed based on clinical presentations, physical examination, imaging examination, electromyogram (EMG) of lower limbs, and intraoperative findings. (2) Patients underwent EPCO plus ICS technique. (3) Follow up duration more than two-year. The exclusion criteria: (1) Incomplete follow-up data. (2) Underlying diseases that seriously affected the surgical outcomes. (3) The anatomical identification was difficult to recognize for radiological measurement. (4) Osteoporosis that defined as a T-score less than − 2.5 SD.

From Jan 2018 to March 2021, 27 patients met both the inclusion and exclusion criteria were reviewed, 16 female and 11 males, with mean age of 45.0 + 9.3 years and mean BMI of 25.7 + 0.5 kg/m2. Presenting neurological symptoms included lower-extremity motor disturbances and/or gait abnormalities (22 patients, 81.5%), lower-extremity sensory disturbances (22 patients, 81.5%), low-back pain (18 patients, 66.7%), urinary dysfunction (16 patients, 59.3%), and sexual dysfunction (5 patients, 18.5%), bowel incontinence (3 patients, 11.1%). The mean duration from onset of the symptom to the surgery was 25.8 + 9.1 months.

Surgical procedure

Under general anesthesia, all patients were placed in a prone position on a radiolucent table. A posterior midline incision was made at the target level (L4-5 for example). The posterior elements that include the spinous process, bilateral lamina, facet joints, were exposed. Pedicle screws were inserted into the L4 and L5 vertebrae bilaterally. Decompression of the thecal sac was performed by resecting the L4 spinous process, bilateral lamina and facet joints (EPCO procedure). The high-tension state of the cauda equina could be detected in all the patients at intra-operative (Fig. 1a). A scalpel was used to resect the posterior anulus after pulling the thecal sac to the opposite side gently, the nucleus pulposus and the cartilaginous endplate were removed by osteotome and rongeur, this decompressive release was performed bilaterally. Cage was introduced into the disc space as anterior as possible (ICS procedure). Two rods with slightly hyper-lordotic contouring were put within L4 and L5 pedicle screw, and were compressed to shorten the posterior column of the spine, all patients got relaxation of cauda equina within the thecal sac at intraoperative (Fig. 1b). Once rods have been locked, both thecal sac and corresponding roots should be carefully inspected to assess any osteoligamentous impingement or retained bony/disc material during the compression procedure. Adequate hemostasis was ensured, and wound was thoroughly irrigated with saline, closed suction drains were inserted at the resection sites, and the surgical wound was closed layer-by-layer. The neurological function was monitored by somatosensory evoked potential (SEP) and motor evoked potential (MEP) all through the surgery.

Postoperative rehabilitation and follow-Up

Postoperatively, the loss through the drains was measured and recorded every day, the drain was removed when the blood loss was less than 50 ml per 24 h. Patients were allowed to move their lower limbs immediately after surgery on bed (ankle pump, muscle contraction of quadriceps). They were allowed to ambulate off bed the second day after surgery. Patients were followed at 3 and 6 months, and 1 and 2 years after surgery, and according to the patients’ needs thereafter.

Data collection

Patient demographics including age, gender, body mass index (BMI), duration, length of hospital stay, SF-36 that used to evaluate the neurological function, were recorded. Surgical data including operation time, blood loss, surgical level, and complications were also recorded.

Standing P/A and Lateral radiography was used to evaluate the spine curvature and alignment before surgery, immediately after surgery, and at 3 months, 2 years of follow-up. Inter-pedicle distance and regional lumbar lordosis was measured at the time points mentioned above. Inter-pedicle distance was defined as the line connecting postero-inferior pedicle point at different level (Fig. 2). Regional lumbar lordosis was defined as the angle between the line parallel to lower endplate and upper endplate at the target level (Fig. 3).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using Statistical Product and Service Solutions software (version 17; SPSS, Chicago, IL). Continuous variables were measured as mean ± standard deviation. An independent t test was used to analyze the difference of continuous variables, paired t test was used to analyze the variables that change from pre- to postoperative. An χ2 analysis and Fisher’s exact test were used to examine the differences among categorical variables. The statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

General data

In the current study, all patients underwent the operation successfully and were followed up at least two-year postoperative. EPCO plus ICS was performed at L4-L5 in 9 patients, at L5-S1 in 7 patients, at L4-S1 in 6 patients, at L3-L5 in 5 patients. 16 patients underwent one-level EPCO plus ICS procedure and enrolled as the one-level group, 11 patients underwent two-level EPCO plus ICS procedure and enrolled as the two-level group (Table 1).

Surgery outcomes

The mean operation time was 96.3 ± 18.0 min, mean blood loss was 350.0 ± 97.9 mL. The operation time and blood loss increased along with the number of surgical levels. The mean decrease of inter-pedicle distance was 0.57 ± 0.18 cm, the mean increase of regional lumbar lordosis was 17.6 ± 6.7 degrees. Decrease of inter-pedicle distance and increase of regional lumbar lordosis were more prominent in two-level group than those in one-level group (Table 2; Fig. 4). Relaxation of cauda equina within the thecal sac was noticed at intra-operative after pedicle screw-rod compression bilaterally in all the patients. Most patients achieved neurological function improvement at two-year follow up, as the mean preoperative SF-36 was 59.1 ± 3.5, while 88.9 ± 5.5 at two-year follow up (Table 3).

Complications

One patient experienced nerve root impingement in the procedure of pedicle screw-rod compression detected by SEP and MEP, rapid recovery of neurological function was required after further decompression of the nerve root, the patient did not present motion loss or pain aggravation at postoperative. No patient experienced dural tear at intra-operative, no patient experienced pedicle screw loosening or cage migration at follow up duration.

Discussion

The PCO procedure is an effective surgical method for correction in spinal kyphotic deformity through induction of lordosis [5–7]. Performing a traditional PCO procedure involves the resection of posterior bony elements, including entire facet complexes, the ligamentum flavum, and at least part of the lamina. In the current study, the whole lamina was resected to decompress the cauda equina completely, the ligamentum flavum laterally was resected to visualize the exiting nerve roots bilaterally, this procedure is named as EPCO.

The main difference between EPCO plus UCS procedure and capsule surgery is whether anterior column reconstruction performed or not. Although capsule surgery has been proved to be effective in the treatment of BSD, intervertebral bone fusion is not performed, postoperative instability and internal fixation failure could not be prevented theoretically. Although the EPCO does not provide as much correction as pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO), it can be done in less operative time and with less morbidity, and could provide about 20 degrees lordosis increase followed intervertebral cage strutting, which acts as a fulcrum within the anterior column to provide anterior reconstruction and increase the regional lumbar lordosis. EPCO plus ICS procedure not only include the osteotomy in posterior column of the spine, but require anterior intervertebral reconstruction followed removal of disc, it should be named as trans-intervertebral osteotomy and be classified as three-column osteotomy.

From the perspective of the osteotomy range and surgical procedure, EPCO plus UCS technique is the combination of transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF) and posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF) [8–10]. The main difference is that facet joints are preserved in PLIF, while spinous process and lamina are preserved in TLIF, the facet joints and lamina were both resected in EPCO plus UCS technique, in which complete resection of the posterior bony elements is a prerequisite, then followed pedicle screw-rod compression to restore the coordination between column and cord. In the current study, the mean surgery time and blood loss were 96.3 ± 18.0 min and 350.0 ± 97.9 mL separately, which are similar to those in TLIF and PLIF, it seems that the spinal surgeons require short learning curve in mastering the EPCO plus ICS procedure. Compression of the osteotomy can cause foraminal stenosis, one patient experienced nerve root impingement in the current study and rapid recovery of neurological function was achieved after further decompression of the nerve root, the patient did not present motion loss or pain aggravation at postoperative. To increase the safety of surgery, it is important to ensure that the exiting nerve roots are adequately decompressed to avoid potential postoperative radiculopathy, SEP and MEP should be monitored in case of unexpected nerve root impingement, and further decompression of the nerve root should be ready if required [11–16].

Visual relaxation of cauda equina within the thecal sac at intraoperative is the direct criteria for judging the degree of shortening and determining whether the second level of EPCO plus ICS should be performed. In the current study, 16 patients got visual relaxation of cauda equina after one-level EPCO plus ICS procedure, while 11 patients underwent two-level procedure when visual relaxation of cauda equina is not achieved after first level of EPCO plus ICS finished. It is noteworthy that decrease of inter-pedicle distance and increase of regional lumbar lordosis were more prominent in Two-level group than those in One-level group, but there was no difference in SF-36 and neurological symptoms improvement between the two groups. We suppose that the visual relaxation of cauda equina within the thecal sac at intraoperative, instead of the decrease of inter-pedicle distance and increase of regional lumbar lordosis, is the key factor in predicting the relief of neurological function at postoperative.

A preliminary diagnostic criterion for lumbosacral nerve BSD has been established according to clinical presentations, physical examination, imaging examination, EMG of lower limbs, and intraoperative findings [1]. We supposed that intraoperative finding is the most valuable factor in diagnosing BSD, while other factors are not specific as expected. Intraoperative findings, such as the dural sac and nerve roots would be stiff, there would be resistance to touching nerve roots with a probe, and the motion of the nerve root would be weakened or diminished, are detected in all the patients of the current study, most of the patients got symptoms relief after a nerve root axial decompression technique to restore the coordination between column and cord through EPCO plus UCS procedure, reversely verified the accuracy of the diagnosis. However, objectively quantified diagnostic criteria for lumbosacral nerve BSD at preoperative is warranted and needs to be further studied in the future.

There are several potential limitations in the current study. First, the number of patients is relatively small, retrospective studies suffer from a number of limitations like selection bias and data availability. Second, this is a single institution study, the conclusion may be not necessarily generalizable or representative of the population at large, but it could provide the advantage of consistency in surgical practice and clinical care.

Conclusion

EPCO plus ICS procedure is an effective surgical method for lumbosacral nerve BSD through restoring the coordination between column and cord, visual relaxation of cauda equina within the thecal sac at intraoperative is the key factor in predicting the relief of neurological function at postoperative.

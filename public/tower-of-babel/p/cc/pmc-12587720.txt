Role and mechanism of circ_001024 endogenous competition for miR-145-3p targeting to regulate GLUT5 in RCC
Authors: Lanlan Zhao, Jieyu Xu, Dandan Li, Yao Chen, Qingqing Fu, Xiaorong Yang, Jian Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCircRNAs have a potential regulatory effect on Renal cell carcinoma (RCC). However, there is still a significant lack of research on their action pathways and mechanisms in the treatment of RCC. The aim of this study is to identify the effects of Circ_001024 on the proliferation, migration, and invasion of RCC cells, and to elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which Circ_001024 promotes the malignant biological behaviors of RCC cell lines.MethodsThe expression of Circ_001024 in RCC was detected using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) To confirm Circ_001024’s structure and properties, tests using Sanger sequencing, RNase R, and actinomycin D were performed. The localization in RCC cells was identified using the Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) assay. Bioinformatics analysis, Spearman correlation analysis, dual-luciferase reporter assay, and RNA pull-down assay were used to verify the interactions among Circ_001024, its targeted miR-145-3p, and the targeted protein GLUT5,and these interactions were further validated by rescue experiments. In addition, Western blot (WB) was used to detect the expression changes of the signaling pathway target protein GLUT5, and the clinicopathological characteristics of GLUT5 in RCC were analyzed.ResultsThe gene Circ_001024 showed high expression levels in RCC, predominantly found in the cytoplasm of RCC cells. Forced expression of Circ_001024 accelerated RCC progression by enhancing cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, whereas Circ_001024 knockdown elicited the reverse effects. miR-145-3p could interact with both Circ_001024 and the targeted protein GLUT5. Overexpression of miR-145-3p could partially reverse the changes in the proliferation, migration, and invasion abilities caused by overexpression of Circ_001024. Moreover, the expression of GLUT5 was associated with the World Health Organization/International Society of Urological Pathology (WHO/ISUP) grade, but not with age, gender, tumor diameter, or TNM staging.ConclusionCirc_001024 is highly expressed in RCC. Within RCC, Circ_001024 can promote the proliferation, migration, and invasion of RCC cells through the competitive inhibition of miR-145-3p, which controls GLUT5 expression.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14878-3.

Introduction

As a major urinary system malignancy, renal cell carcinoma (RCC) arises primarily from renal tubular epithelial cells, constituting roughly 80% of all kidney tumors [1]. Early-stage RCC often manifests without symptoms, leading to delayed diagnosis when patients experience discomfort like flank pain, palpable masses, or hematuria. At this point, the disease is frequently advanced, jeopardizing patient health, with some already presenting metastases. Surgical resection remains the sole curative option for RCC, yet high-risk patients confront substantial recurrence and metastasis risks post-surgery, yielding a grim prognosis [1, 2]. Despite advancements in molecular immunotherapy, gene therapy, and anti-angiogenic drugs enhancing outcomes for advanced RCC, effective molecular markers for assessing prognosis and treatment response are lacking. Therefore, comprehending RCC’s molecular mechanisms is imperative for tailoring personalized treatment approaches.

Circular RNA (CircRNA) has recently emerged as a subject of considerable interest due to its unique characteristics. CircRNA is an endogenous non-coding RNA molecule that features a covalently closed circular structure, lacking a 5’ cap and a 3’ poly(A) tail. Noteworthy properties of circRNA include its widespread distribution, stability, tissue specificity, and evolutionary conservation [3–5] The circular structure of circRNA confers resistance to ribonuclease activity, enhancing its stability compared to linear RNA.Accumulating evidence suggests that circular RNAs (circRNAs) can function as microRNA (miRNA) sponges and interact with RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) across diverse pathological conditions [6, 7]. Moreover, investigations have linked circRNA to several types of cancer, such as liver cancer, pancreatic cancer, lung cancer, breast cancer, osteosarcoma, and RCC. For example, in pancreatic cancer, downregulation of circNFIB1 has been associated with lymph node metastasis, implying its potential as a therapeutic biomarker [8]. The study further revealed that circRNA_0000285 regulates the proliferation and apoptosis of cervical cancer cells by sponging miR-654-3p to relieve its inhibitory effect on the downstream Bcl-2/Bax apoptotic pathway [9]. In breast cancer, upregulation of circPDIA4 has been observed, underscoring the regulatory significance of the circRNF20/miR-487a/HIF-1α/HK2 axis in breast cancer pathogenesis and advancement [10]. Several circRNAs, including CircPPAP2B [11], Circ_0037866 [12], and circPLIN2 [13], have been implicated in RCC progression. In contrast, microRNAs (miRNAs), a class of small non-coding RNAs, are recognized as crucial mediators in various biological processes associated with RCC development [14]. Despite the multitude of studies proposing diverse molecular mechanisms involving circRNAs and miRNAs in RCC, further investigation is warranted to unravel the comprehensive regulatory network encompassing circRNAs and their downstream effectors.

In this investigation, we have identified a circular RNA termed Circ_001024 (also known as hsa_circ_0000125), exhibiting significant upregulation in renal cell carcinoma (RCC) tissues and correlating with disease advancement in RCC patients. The primary objective of this research is to delineate the involvement of Circ_001024 in RCC progression and its potential underlying mechanisms. These findings aim to offer novel insights into the pathophysiology of RCC and present potential therapeutic targets. Through comprehensive exploration of Circ_001024’s functions and molecular interactions, we aim to establish a theoretical framework for early diagnosis and personalized treatment strategies in RCC.

Methods

Clinical tissue samples

During surgery, ten Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) tissue specimens and their corresponding adjacent non-tumor tissues were collected. All tissue specimens underwent histopathological diagnosis by pathologists postoperatively.Surgical specimens were rapidly dissected after resection and stored at −80 °C for further experimental research. Ethical clearance for this research was granted by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Zunyi Medical University (Approval No. KLL-2020-096). A total of 180 histopathologically confirmed RCC cases, encompassing clear cell renal cell carcinoma, chromophobe renal cell carcinoma, and papillary renal cell carcinoma subtypes, were collected from the pathology department of the hospital. Sixty eligible cases were selected according to the following inclusion and exclusion criteria.

All selected cases were jointly diagnosed by two deputy chief physicians, with complete clinicopathological data and reliable follow-up information for the patients.

Cell culture and transfection

The RCC cell lines (786-O, ACHN, A498) and the human renal epithelial cell line HK2 were provided by Wuhan Puno Biotechnology (S1). The human renal epithelial cell line HK2 and the human RCC cell line ACHN were cultured in MEM (with NEAA) containing 1% penicillin-streptomycin and 12% fetal bovine serum, while 786-O was cultured in 1640 medium (FBS, Gibco, USA) containing 1% penicillin-streptomycin and 10% fetal bovine serum. All cell lines were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. The lentivirus for Circ_001024 and the control vector, as well as the miR-145-3p mimic, were synthesized by HanHeng Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) (S2). Expression plasmids were transfected into 786-O and ACHN cells with Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA) in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s recommended transfection protocol.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was isolated from tissues and cells using Trizol reagent (Takara, Japan) following the manufacturer’s standard protocol. For the RNase resistance assay, 3 µg of total RNA extracted from 786-O and ACHN cells was incubated with or without RNase R (Beyotime) at 37 °C for 20 min to evaluate RNA stability. Reverse transcription was performed using the PrimeScript RT kit (Takara, Japan), followed by quantitative PCR amplification with SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (TaKaRa) on a real-time PCR system. Each experimental group was subjected to three independent biological replicates, with U6 and GADPH serving as internal controls for normalization. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method to quantify target gene expression. All specific primer sequences were designed in-house and synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China)(S 3).

Cell proliferation assays

Cell proliferation was assessed using CCK-8, EdU, and colony formation assays. For the CCK-8 assay, 3.5 × 10³ cells were plated per well in 96-well plates, followed by the addition of 10 µL CCK-8 reagent (5 mg/mL). Proliferative activity was evaluated at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-seeding, with absorbance readings recorded after 1–2 h of incubation at 37 °C. The EdU assay was performed in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol (Biyuntian, China). For colony formation analysis, RCC cells were seeded at 600 cells per well in 6-well plates and cultured for 1–2 weeks. Following incubation, cells were fixed and stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution for visualization and imaging.

Transwell assays

The migratory and invasive potentials of 786-O and ACHN cells were evaluated via Transwell assays. Cells were plated in the upper chamber of Transwell inserts (Costar, USA), with Matrigel (BD Biosciences, USA) coating used for invasion assays and omitted for migration assays. The lower chamber was filled with culture medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum as a chemoattractant. Following 24 h of incubation, cells were fixed with 0.4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution. Microscopic visualization and imaging were subsequently performed to quantify cell migration and invasion.

Wound healing assay

The cells were cultured in serum - free medium and then counted. Around 3 × 10⁵ cells for each well were inoculated into a 6 - well plate and allowed to grow until they reached confluence. A 10 µL pipette tip was employed to create a scratch (cross - shaped) on the cell monolayer. Subsequently, the cells were rinsed three times with 1 mL of phosphate - buffered saline (PBS) to eliminate the detached cells. After that, serum - free medium was introduced, and photographs of the scratched area were taken at 0, 12, and 24 h. The relative migration rate was calculated by gauging the alteration in the wound width from 0 h to 12/24 hours.

Fluorescence in situ Hybridization(FISH)

Circ_001024 and miR-145-3p probes were designed, synthesized, and provided by GeneMark (Suzhou, China). The nucleotide sequences of these RNA probes are detailed in S4. The FISH experiment followed the manufacturer’s protocol. We seeded 1 × 104 cells on coverslips in a 48-well plate. After 24 h, the cells were fixed and incubated overnight with the Circ_001024 and miR-145-3p probes. The nuclei were then stained with DAPI.

RNA Pull-Down assay

Circ_001024-specific biotin-labeled probes were obtained from GeneMark Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The probe sequences are detailed in S4. The pull-down assay was conducted following the manufacturer’s protocol (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). Briefly, 1 × 106 cells were seeded and lysed the following day to generate cell lysates. Biotinylated probes were initially incubated with streptavidin-coated magnetic beads at ambient temperature for 30 min to allow for probe-bead binding. Subsequently, the cell lysates were introduced to the streptavidin magnetic bead-probe complexes and incubated at 4 °C for 1 h to facilitate target RNA capture. Following sequential washing steps to remove non-specific binding, the enriched RNA was isolated from the beads using Trizol reagent (Takara, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s protocol for downstream molecular analyses.

Western blot (WB)

Total protein was extracted using radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer supplemented with a 100:1 ratio of phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Yamei Biotech, Shanghai, China). Protein concentration was quantified using a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit (Yamei Biotech). Following denaturation, proteins were separated via sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and electrotransferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, USA). Membranes were then blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk or 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 2 h at room temperature. Primary antibodies (Abcam, diluted 1:5000) were applied overnight at 4 °C, followed by incubation with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (Proteintech, diluted 1:10,000) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection reagents, and densitometric analysis of band intensity was performed using ImageJ software.

Dual-Luciferase reporter assay

Circ_001024 and GLUT5 3′UTR wild-type (WT)/mutant (MUT) reporter plasmids were constructed by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China) (detailed in Table 1). 293 T cells were plated in 96-well plates, cultured until reaching 80% confluence, and co-transfected with luciferase reporter vectors and miR-145-3p mimics (or negative controls) using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen). Luciferase activity was quantified via a dual-luciferase reporter assay system 48 h post-transfection according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Paraffin-embedded tissue Sect. (3 μm thick) were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibody anti-GLUT5 (1:100; Proteintech, Wuhan, China). Following PBS washes, the sections were incubated with an HRP-conjugated secondary antibody and then subjected to DAB + immunostaining.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0, and graphical representations were generated with GraphPad Prism 8.0.2. Continuous variables were compared using t-tests, while categorical variables were analyzed with chi-square tests and Fisher’s exact probability method. For non-normally distributed data, rank-sum tests were employed to assess statistical differences. Correlation analyses utilized the Spearman method, and survival analyses were conducted using the Kaplan-Meier approach, with log-rank tests for univariate analysis and Cox regression for multivariate analysis. A significance threshold of *p < 0.05 was adopted.

Results

Expression and characteristics of circ_001024 in RCC

Circ_001024. is a 681-bp circular transcript derived from the 4th exon of the RNF115 gene on chromosome 1, formed by back-splicing that joins the 5’ and 3’ ends of the 4th exon of RNF115 pre-mRNA. Sanger sequencing first confirmed its generation via RNF115 back-splicing (Fig. 1A). qRT-PCR results demonstrated elevated Circ_001024 expression in RCC tissues when compared with normal renal and paracancerous non-tumor tissues (Fig. 1B). Additionally, compared with the human normal renal tubular epithelial cell line HK-2, Circ_001024. was significantly upregulated in RCC cell lines 786-O, A498, and ACHN(Fig. 1C). After treating RNA from RCC cells 786-O and ACHN with the exonuclease RNase R for different durations, qRT-PCR results demonstrated that Circ_001024 was more resistant to RNase R digestion than the parental linear gene RNF115 (Fig. 1D). Treatment of RCC cell lines with actinomycin D, an inhibitor of transcriptional elongation, demonstrated that Circ_001024 exhibited a significantly longer half-life compared to its linear counterpart, RNF115 mRNA (Fig. 1E). Subcellular localization of Circ_001024 in 786-O and ACHN cells was determined using FISH assays, revealing its predominant distribution within the cytoplasm (Fig. 1F). Collectively, the expression profile and stability of Circ_001024. in RCC suggest its potential involvement in the oncogenesis and progression of renal cell carcinoma.

Circ_001024 promotes proliferation, migration, and invasion in RCC

To investigate the potential biological functions of Circ_001024 RCC, we modulated its expression in RCC cell lines via lentiviral transduction for overexpression or knockdown. qRT-PCR confirmed significant downregulation of Circ_001024 following knockdown (Fig. 2A). Functional experiments in vitro, including CCK-8, EdU incorporation, and colony formation assays, revealed that Circ_001024 knockdown notably inhibited cell proliferation in 786-O and ACHN cell lines (Fig. 2B-E). Additionally, Transwell assays revealed reduced migratory and invasive capacities in these cells upon Circ_001024 knockdown (Fig. 2F-G).

Following Circ_001024 overexpression, qRT-PCR analysis showed significantly increased expression of Circ_001024 in 786-O and ACHN cells (Fig. 3A). In contrast to the knockdown experiments, in vitro functional assays demonstrated that Circ_001024 overexpression enhanced the proliferative capacity of 786-O and ACHN cells, as evidenced by CCK-8, EdU, and colony formation assays (Fig. 3B-E). Transwell assays demonstrated that Circ_001024 overexpression promoted the migration and invasion capacities of 786-O and ACHN cells (Fig. 3F-G). Collectively, these in vitro results indicate that Circ_001024 acts as an oncogene in the progression of RCC.

Identification of circ_001024 target MiRNAs and genes

Cytoplasmic circRNAs typically exhibit miRNA sponge effects. Through CircBase software prediction, potential binding sites were identified between Circ_001024 and miR-145-3p (Fig. 4A), and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) confirmed their co-localization in the cytoplasm of 786-O and ACHN cells (Fig. 4B). Further analysis using TargetScanHuman7.2 software revealed interaction targets between Circ_001024 and miR-145-3p. To validate this relationship, wild-type (WT) and mutant (Mut) Circ_001024 luciferase reporter vectors were constructed to assess the regulatory role of miR-145-3p on Circ_001024. Transfection experiments showed that miR-145-3p overexpression significantly inhibited the luciferase activity of Circ_001024-WT but had no effect on Circ_001024-Mut (Figs. 4C-D). Additionally, RNA Pull-down assays demonstrated that biotin-labeled CircRNA_001024 probes specifically enriched miR-145-3p (Fig. 4E). For the downstream targets of miR-145-3p, TargetScanHuman7.2 software predicted binding sites with GLUT5. By constructing wild-type and mutant GLUT5 luciferase reporter vectors, we found that miR-145-3p overexpression inhibited the luciferase activity of GLUT5-WT but had no significant effect on GLUT5-Mut (Figs. 4F-G). Collectively, these experiments confirm that Circ_001024 exerts its biological function by sponging miR-145-3p and relieving its inhibitory effect on GLUT5.

Clinical significance analysis of circ_001024, miR-145-3p, and its target gene GLUT5

The relative transcriptional levels of miR-145-3p in normal renal tissues, cancer-adjacent non-tumorous tissues, and RCC tissues were quantified by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) (Fig. 5A). Spearman’s rank correlation analysis revealed a significant negative association between Circ_001024 and miR-145-3p expression profiles in RCC tumor specimens, with a correlation coefficient of r=−0.7091 (P = 0.0268) (Fig. 5B). WB and immunohistochemical staining were used to detect GLUT5 expression in various tissue groups, revealing high expression of GLUT5 in RCC tissues. Further analysis demonstrated a significant negative correlation between miR-145-3p and GLUT5 expression in RCC tumor tissues (r=−0.6848, P = 0.0347) (Figs. 5C-E). WB analysis demonstrated that silencing Circ_001024 expression in renal cell carcinoma (RCC) cells led to decreased GLUT5 protein levels. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis further revealed a significant positive association between Circ_001024 and GLUT5 expression profiles in RCC tumor tissues (Fig. 5G). Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis showed that while no statistically significant difference in overall survival was observed between patients with high and low GLUT5 expression (P = 0.300), the mean survival duration of the high-expression subgroup was moderately prolonged compared to the low-expression counterpart (Fig. 5H). Clinicopathological feature analysis revealed that GLUT5 expression was associated with WHO/ISUP grade, but not with age, gender, tumor diameter, or TNM stage (Table 1). To further validate the role of the Circ_001024/miR-145-3P/GLUT5 axis in RCC, rescue experiments were performed. First, miR-145-3p mimics were transfected into 786-O cells overexpressing Circ_001024, and qRT-PCR showed significantly increased miR-145-3p expression after co-transfection (Fig. 6A). CCK-8, EDU, colony formation, Transwell, and scratch assays demonstrated that elevated miR-145-3p expression partially reversed the enhanced proliferation, migration, and invasion of RCC cells induced by Circ_001024 upregulation (Figs. 6C-G).Subsequently, WB was used to detect expression changes of the pathway protein GLUT5. WB results showed that GLUT5 expression increased after Circ_001024 overexpression but decreased after miR-145-3p mimic transfection. Additionally, after treating Circ_001024-overexpressing and -knockdown cell lines with the GLUT5-specific inhibitor N-[4-(methylsulfonyl)−2-nitrophenyl]−1,3-benzodioxol-5-amine (MSNBA) for 24 hours, WB revealed reduced GLUT5 expression. CCK-8 and Transwell assays showed that decreased GLUT5 expression inhibited cell proliferation and migration (Figs. 6H-J).

Discussion

Based on 2025 cancer statistics, kidney tumor incidence is increasing, ranking sixth among male malignancies and ninth among females. Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) predominates among kidney tumors [2]. Despite advancements in treatment, challenges persist in late diagnosis, drug resistance, and recurrence risk, underscoring the ongoing need for novel molecularly targeted drugs to enhance RCC therapeutic approaches [15]. CircRNAs is a covalently closed single-stranded RNA molecule prevalent in eukaryotic cells, generated by the back-splicing of linear RNA [16]. CircRNAs serve as regulatory molecules involved in various functions, including gene expression modulation, miRNA or protein binding, and potential translation into proteins. These regulatory roles are essential for cell proliferation, differentiation, growth, development, and numerous other biological processes [3]. Emerging evidence suggests that circular RNAs (circRNAs) can act as either oncogenes or tumor suppressors across various cancer types [3, 17]. However, the specific mechanisms by which circRNAs contribute to the progression of renal cell carcinoma (RCC) remain largely unclear. In the present study, we identified Circ_001024 as a potential oncogenic circRNA, which was found to be significantly upregulated in both RCC cell lines and clinical tissue samples. Mechanistically, Circ_001024 appears to act as a molecular sponge for miR-145-3p, thereby modulating the expression of its downstream target, GLUT5, and promoting RCC progression.

Numerous studies have demonstrated that cytoplasmic circular RNAs (circRNAs) can act as miRNA sponges, thereby modulating the expression of oncogenes or tumor suppressor genes [18, 19]. To evaluate the potential of Circ_001024 to function as a miRNA sponge, we used the TargetScanHuman 7.2 database to predict potential binding sites between Circ_001024 and miR-145-3p, as well as between miR-145-3p and GLUT5. MiR-145-3p has previously been implicated in tumorigenesis and cancer progression [20]. FISH assays revealed the cytoplasmic co-localization of Circ_001024 and miR-145-3p in 786-O and ACHN cells. The interaction between Circ_001024 and miR-145-3p was further confirmed through RNA pull-down and dual-luciferase reporter assays in RCC cells. Pearson correlation analysis revealed that GLUT5 expression was positively correlated with Circ_001024 levels and negatively correlated with miR-145-3p levels. Furthermore, we analyzed the association between GLUT5 protein expression in tumor tissues and relevant clinical characteristics.Our analysis found no significant correlation between GLUT5 expression and RCC patient survival prognosis.Nonetheless, survival analysis indicated a tendency towards prolonged overall survival in the low GLUT5 expression cohort, suggesting a potential link between high GLUT5 levels and malignant progression in renal cell carcinoma. Our study further validated the direct interaction between miR-145-3p and GLUT5. Rescue experiments demonstrated that miR-145-3p overexpression counteracted the oncogenic effects of Circ_001024 overexpression on RCC cell viability, migration, and invasion, supporting our hypothesis that Circ_001024 exerts its influence in RCC by competitively inhibiting miR-145-3p and targeting GLUT5.

Glucose transport in human cells is predominantly facilitated by the mammalian facilitative hexose transporters (GLUTs) family, comprising 14 subtypes. Among these, GLUT5 stands out as the sole transporter specific to fructose, displaying a notable affinity for fructose while not facilitating the transport of glucose or galactose [21, 22]. Perturbations in GLUT5 function have been associated with metabolic disorders such as type 2 diabetes and obesity. Moreover, GLUT5 has been implicated in the progression of several malignancies, including ovarian, lung, and colorectal cancers, highlighting its role as a potential oncogenic factor [23–26]. Notably, the compound N-[4-(methylsulfonyl)−2-nitrophenyl]−1,3-benzodioxole-5-amine (MSNBA) has been identified as a potent and selective inhibitor of GLUT5-mediated fructose transport in proteoliposomes. Importantly, MSNBA exhibits a high degree of specificity, effectively inhibiting GLUT5 without affecting the fructose transport activity of human GLUT2, thereby making it a valuable tool for investigating GLUT5-related cellular processes and disease mechanisms.The inhibitory efficacy of MSNBA has been confirmed in multiple cell lines, including the MCF7 human breast cancer cell line [27, 28]. Treatment with MSNBA was shown to reduce proliferation in colon cancer cells; however, higher concentrations also impaired the proliferation of normal colonic epithelial cells [26]. In cell lines treated with MSNBA, both overexpression and knockdown assays revealed decreased GLUT5 protein levels, as demonstrated by WB analysis. Furthermore, CCK-8 and Transwell assays showed that GLUT5 inhibition significantly attenuated cell proliferation and migration.These findings suggest that upregulation of miR-145-3p may suppress Circ_001024 expression, thereby downregulating GLUT5 and impacting key tumor-related cellular behaviors.

Conclusion

Circ_001024 is highly expressed in RCC, promoting RCC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by competitively inhibiting miR-145-3p to regulate GLUT5 expression. GLUT5 serves as a key regulatory target, and reduced GLUT5 expression significantly suppresses RCC cell proliferation and migration, indicating that the Circ_001024/miR-145-3p/GLUT5 axis is a potential therapeutic target for RCC.

Limitations of the study

Although the current study has clearly revealed that the GLUT5-specific inhibitor MSNBA has the ability to inhibit the malignant growth of tumor cells, the optimal concentrations of MSNBA vary among different types of tumors. However, the optimal concentration and time of its application were not investigated in this study.In addition, the research on the Circ_001024./miR-145-3p/GLUT5 axis is only at the stage of in vitro cell function experiments, and no in vivo experiments have been conducted, which can be further improved in future studies.Moreover, further research should be carried out on the optimal concentration and action time of MSNBA in different RCC cell lines, so as to provide more theoretical basis for clinical applications.

FOXM1 inhibition reduces IL-13-induced epithelial remodelling and inflammation in eosinophilic oesophagitis
Authors: Masaru Sasaki, Joshua X Wang, Ryugo Teranishi, Takefumi Itami, Yusen Zhou, Kanak V Kennedy, Ann Semeao, Satoshi Ishikawa, Takeo Hara, Emily A McMillan, Mark Mahon, Hailey Golden, Diya Dhakal, Alyssa Baccarella, Heidi Winters, Chizoba N Umeweni, Benjamin J Wilkins, Tatiana A Karakasheva, Kelly A Whelan, Sydney M Shaffer, Melanie A Ruffner, Amanda B Muir
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEosinophilic oesophagitis (EoE) is a chronic allergic disease characterised by oesophageal epithelial remodelling, barrier dysfunction and inflammation. The transcription factor forkhead box M1 (FOXM1) has been shown to be a key regulator of epithelial proliferation and inflammation in allergic asthma.ObjectiveTo investigate the role of FOXM1 in epithelial disruption in EoE and to evaluate the therapeutic potential of FOXM1 inhibition.DesignFOXM1 expression was analysed in human oesophageal biopsies, patient-derived organoids and murine EoE models. Interleukin (IL)-13 stimulation was used to model EoE in vitro. Using FOXM1 inhibition via the small molecule Robert Costa Memorial drug-1 (RCM-1), small interfering RNA-mediated knockdown or FOXM1 overexpression, the roles of FOXM1 were assessed by histology, gene expression profiling, organoid formation rates, barrier integrity and proliferation assays. RNA sequencing and chromatin immunoprecipitation were performed to elucidate molecular mechanisms.ResultsFOXM1 was significantly upregulated in patients with active EoE and localised to the basal epithelium. IL-13 increased FOXM1 expression in vitro. FOXM1 inhibition restored differentiation markers, reduced basal cell hyperplasia and proliferation, and improved barrier function. In a murine model, RCM-1 reduced epithelial changes and eosinophil infiltration. Conversely, FOXM1 overexpression promoted basal cell hyperplasia and proliferation. Mechanistically, FOXM1 directly regulated cell cycle gene, cyclin B1, which was upregulated in EoE and downregulated on FOXM1 inhibition. RCM-1 reduced phosphorylated STAT6 under IL-13 stimulation. FOXM1 expression was driven in part by an IL-13-PI3K/AKT axis.ConclusionFOXM1 plays a pivotal role in epithelial disruption in EoE by driving proliferation and impairing differentiation. Targeting FOXM1 restores epithelial homeostasis, mitigates inflammation and offers a novel therapeutic approach for EoE.Keywords: OESOPHAGITIS, OESOPHAGEAL DISEASE, EPITHELIAL DIFFERENTIATION, EPITHELIAL PROLIFERATION, ORGANOIDS

Introduction

Eosinophilic oesophagitis (EoE) is an allergic disease of the oesophagus in which disruption of the oesophageal mucosa and inflammation lead to vomiting, dysphagia and eventually oesophageal stricture.1 Recent work has demonstrated that despite having a decreased inflammation, patients have ongoing molecular and histological changes in the oesophageal epithelium.2 These changes contribute to ongoing symptomatology and allow for disease relapse.3 4 There is an urgent need to define the mechanisms of epithelial dysregulation in order to achieve true long-lasting mucosal healing.

While the pathophysiology of EoE is incompletely understood, exposure to food and aeroallergens triggers a T-helper type 2 (Th2) cell production of interleukin-13 (IL-13). IL-13, in turn, stimulates epithelial production of eotaxin-3, also known as C-C motif chemokine ligand 26 (CCL26), the major eosinophil chemoattractant.1 5 6 Independent of its effects stimulating eosinophil chemotaxis, IL-13 has been shown in vitro and in vivo to trigger epithelial remodelling events including dilated intracellular spaces and basal cell hyperplasia.5,8 Histologically, 95% of patients with active EoE demonstrate hyperplasia of the basal epithelium,3 which at the molecular level is a failure of cells to terminally differentiate, as shown by decreased expression of involucrin (IVL), filaggrin (FLG) and keratin-6.4 8 9 Single-cell evaluation of the EoE epithelium has described an expansion of the proliferative progenitor population with retained Ki-67 and keratin-13. Understanding the underlying mechanism that contributes to this combination of increased proliferation and failure to differentiate of progenitor cells represents a knowledge gap in the field.

Restoring the proliferation and differentiation gradient in the EoE epithelium may lead to a durable healing and make the epithelium less prone to relapse. Forkhead box M1 (FOXM1), a transcription factor of the Forkhead box family, is recognised as a major regulator of cell cycle progression and the mitotic programme.10 11 In the lung, inhibition of FOXM1 prevents IL-13 mediated goblet cell hyperplasia in murine asthma models. Further, FOXM1 has recently been implicated in epithelial cell differentiation,12,15 fibroblast activation16 and pathogenesis of allergen-induced inflammatory diseases.17 18 Aberrant FOXM1 expression in the skin and oral cavity disrupted epithelial differentiation and homeostasis, resulting in hyperplasia.12 13 In contrast, terminally differentiated skin keratinocytes attenuate FOXM1 expression and downregulation of FOXM1 enhances differentiation markers,14 15 suggesting that FOXM1 is essential for the regulation of the epithelial proliferation-differentiation gradient.

In this study, we examined FOXM1 expression in the normal and EoE oesophageal epithelium. We used patient-derived oesophageal organoids and a murine model of EoE to examine the role of FOXM1 in driving epithelial disruption and propose this as a therapeutic target for allergic inflammation of the oesophagus.

Methods

Monolayer culture of human oesophageal cells

Immortalised human normal oesophageal epithelial cells, EPC2-hTERT, and patient-derived oesophageal epithelial cells (online supplemental table S1) were kept in keratinocyte-serum free medium (KSFM; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). High-calcium (1.8 mM Ca2+) KSFM was used to induce terminal differentiation and stratification in vitro cultures as previously described.19 20 Cells were stimulated by recombinant human IL-13, IL-4, tumour necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α), or transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) to recapitulate EoE milieu. The Robert Costa Memorial drug-1 (RCM-1; S6898; Selleck Chemicals, Houston, Texas, USA) and LY294002 (S1105; Selleck Chemicals) were used to inhibit FOXM1 activity and phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) signalling, respectively.17 21

Oesophageal 3-dimensional organoid culture

EPC2-hTERT or primary patient-derived organoids (PDOs, online supplemental table S1) were established as described previously.9 22 Briefly, the prepared EPC2-hTERT cells and patient-derived oesophageal cells were suspended in Matrigel basement membrane matrix (Corning, Corning, New York, USA) and seeded under modified KSFM with 0.6 mM Ca2+. Organoids were cultured for 7 days and then treated with IL-13 (10 ng/mL), RCM-1 (10 µM) or vehicle (phosphate-buffered saline for IL-13 or dimethyl sulfoxide for RCM-1) for 4 days. The day 9 or day 11 organoids were harvested for further analyses.

Organoid formation rate was defined as the number of≥50 µm organoids at day 11 divided by the total seeded cells in each well.7 9

Air-liquid interface culture system

EPC2-hTERT cells were cultured with the air-liquid interface (ALI) system as described previously.7 Cells were stimulated with IL-13 (10 ng/mL), RCM-1 (20 µM) or vehicle in the basolateral compartment from day 9 to 15. Transepithelial electrical resistance (TEER) was measured using epithelial volt/Ohm (TEER) metre (World Precision Instruments, Sarasota, Florida, USA) to evaluate epithelial barrier. The ALI-cultured epithelium at day 15 was harvested for histology.

Patients and oesophageal specimens

After obtaining informed consent, distal oesophageal specimens were derived from patients who underwent esophagogastroduodenoscopy. We classified patients as active EoE, inactive EoE and non-EoE using the 2018 AGREE (Appraisal of Guidelines, Research and Evaluation) guidelines (see online supplemental methods for detail).23 Patient demographics are shown in online supplemental table S2.

Murine EoE models

EoE models were induced as described previously.22 24 25 Murine esophagi were harvested for histology or flow cytometry. All experiments were performed in accordance with the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of The Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia.

RNA sequencing analysis on human oesophageal biopsies

We used public RNA sequencing data (GSE58640)26 on oesophageal biopsies derived from active EoE and control subjects. The raw data was processed as described previously.27 Details on gene expression and enrichment pathway analysis are provided in online supplemental methods.

Quantitative reverse transcription-PCR

RNA isolation and reverse transcription were performed as described previously.27 28 The used TaqMan and SYBR Green primers are provided in online supplemental tables S3 and S4. Relative messenger RNA (mRNA) levels were standardised against glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase levels as a housekeeping control.

Western blot

Whole-cell lysates were extracted as described previously.7 Primary antibodies used are provided in online supplemental table S5. Clarity Western ECL Substrate (Bio-Rad, Hercules, California, USA) was used to detect immunoblots. β-actin was set as a loading control.

Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence

Paraffin-embedded human and murine esophagi, organoids and ALI-cultured epithelia were sectioned and subjected to H&E staining, immunohistochemistry or immunofluorescence as described previously.7 9 Primary antibodies used are provided in online supplemental table S5.

Flow cytometry

Murine esophagi were dissociated and filtered as described in online supplemental methods. Eosinophils were identified as CD45+/CD11b+/Siglec F+/SSChigh cells.29 BD LSRFortessa Cell Analyzer (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, New Jersey, USA) and FlowJo software (FlowJo, Ashland, Oregon, USA) were used for analyses.

Small interfering RNA transfection

Silencer Select Pre-Designed small interfering RNA (siRNA) against FOXM1 (s5250 or s5248; Thermo Fisher Scientific), CCNB1 (s2515 or s2517; Thermo Fisher Scientific) or Silencer Select Negative Control No. 1 siRNA (4390844; Invitrogen, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA) was transfected into cells.

Gene expression analysis on siFOXM1-transfected cells

The day after siRNA transfection (small interfering negative control (siNC), siFOXM1_1 or siFOXM1_3), EPC2-hTERT cells were cultured with the high-calcium KSFM in monolayer culture and then harvested for RNA sequencing. Details on data processing and analysis are provided in online supplemental methods.

Lentiviral vector transduction

Lentiviral vector pLX304-FOXM1 (HsCD00443896) was supplied by DNASU Plasmid Repository (Arizona State University, Tempe, Arizona, USA). Lentiviral vector pLX304-eGFP was constructed as described previously.7

Proliferation assay

Cells were seeded with or without IL-13 (10 ng/mL) in a 96-well plate. Proliferative ability was measured by WST-1 reagent (Roche Holding, Basel, Switzerland).

Cell cycle assay

Cells stained with propidium iodide were evaluated through BD LSRII (BD Biosciences). Cell cycle phases were provided by the Watson Pragmatic algorithm in FlowJo software.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation assay

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) samples were prepared with truChIP Chromatin Shearing Kit (520154; Covaris, Woburn, Massachusetts, USA) and immunoprecipitation was performed with SimpleChIP Plus Sonication Chromatin IP Kit (Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, Massachusetts, USA) based on the manufacturer’s protocol. 5 μg of FOXM1 antibody (sc-376471 X; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, Texas, USA) or IgG (2729; Cell Signaling Technology) was added and incubated. Finally, DNA was purified using the PCR purification kit and quantified by real-time qPCR. ChIP results were calculated by fold enrichment method against IgG. The primer against cyclin B1 (CCNB1) for ChIP-qPCR is provided in online supplemental table S4.

Statistical analysis

Data are indicated as mean±SD. Variables were analysed by two-tailed Student’s t-test for two-group comparison or one-way analysis of variance for multigroup. Pearson correlation coefficient was used to measure linear association between two variables. Statistical analyses were performed with GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software, San Diego, California, USA). A p value<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Detailed materials and methods are provided in online supplemental files.

Results

FOXM1 expression is elevated in patients with active EoE and localises to the basal epithelium

Using previously published RNA sequencing from patients with EoE and non-EoE controls,26 we performed a Pathway Interaction Analysis. Multiple cell cycle-related pathways were enriched (figure 1A). In the case of FOXM1, not only was the pathway upregulated in patients with EoE, but the FOXM1 gene itself was found to be upregulated in these patients (figure 1B,C). We validated this finding by quantitative reverse transcription-PCR (qRT-PCR), demonstrating increased FOXM1 expression in whole biopsy specimens of active patients with EoE compared with those in remission and non-EoE controls (figure 1D). In longitudinal samples from patients who achieved histological remission, FOXM1 expression decreased between the active state and remission (figure 1E). We evaluated the expression of FOXM1 in monolayer cultured patient-derived cells and found no significant difference between the cultures from patient with active disease and inactive disease (online supplemental figure S1A). Biopsy staining demonstrated FOXM1 was localised to the basal epithelium for all specimens and notably increased in active EoE specimens (figure 1F,G).

Th2 cytokines increase FOXM1 expression in the oesophageal epithelium

In order to determine the factors driving FOXM1 expression in EoE, we stimulated EPC2-hTERT cells with the EoE relevant cytokines. We found that treatment with IL-13 and IL-4 (and not TNF-α or TGF-β) induced increased FOXM1 expression in two-dimensional culture (figure 2A,B). As IL-13 stimulated cultures demonstrated the robust FOXM1 expression and this is the major effector cytokine in EoE, we used IL-13 throughout this investigation. Oesophageal epithelial cultures isolated from active and inactive EoE patients showed upregulated FOXM1 expression by IL-13 (figure 2C and online supplemental figure S1B). IL-13 stimulation of oesophageal organoids (both EPC2-hTERT and PDOs) also demonstrated increased FOXM1 expression by qRT-PCR (figure 2D and online supplemental figure S1C). Similar to patient biopsies, immunohistochemical staining of organoids showed localisation of FOXM1 in the basal epithelium, which was increased with IL-13 stimulation (figure 2E).

Inhibition of FOXM1 attenuates IL-13-induced epithelial changes

Hyperproliferation of the basal epithelium and failure of cells to appropriately differentiate are hallmarks of EoE. To examine the role of FOXM1 on this proliferation-differentiation axis, we identified two silencing RNAs (siFOXM1_1 and siFOXM1_3) that reduced FOXM1 transcript and protein compared with scrambled control (siNC, online supplemental figure S2A,B). Expression of the differentiation marker IVL was increased after FOXM1 knockdown, while expression of basal marker tumour protein p63 (TP63) was decreased after knockdown (online supplemental figure S2C) even in the setting of IL-13 stimulation. These findings lead to the hypothesis that FOXM1 is a key regulator between proliferation and differentiation in the oesophageal epithelium.

Next, we used RCM-1, a small molecule which leads to proteasomal degradation of FOXM1.17 We confirmed the dose-dependent loss of FOXM1 in epithelial cells treated with RCM-1 at 10 µM and 20 µM (figure 3A). We also demonstrated no increase in cleaved Caspase-3 or cleaved PARP in the normal epithelial cells treated with RCM-1 at the dose used in our study, indicating that RCM-1 did not lead to apoptosis of the epithelial cells (online supplemental figure S3A). RCM-1 treatment enhanced epithelial differentiation as demonstrated by increased expression of IVL by western blot, qRT-PCR and staining in EPC2-hTERT cells and PDO cultures (figure 3B–F, online supplemental figures S4A–C and S5A–H). We further evaluated differentiation marker FLG with similar findings. Thus, RCM-1 treatment rescued IL-13-induced reduction of epithelial differentiation. While IL-13 treatment increased expression of basal cell markers TP63 and SRY-box transcription factor 2 (SOX2), promoted basal cell hyperplasia and further increased cellular proliferation marker Ki-67, RCM-1 treatment suppressed the IL-13 induced changes. These findings were consistent in PDOs from patients with active or inactive EoE (figure 3B–F, online supplemental figure S5A–H).

Because only basal cells are capable of forming organoids (with terminally differentiated cells incapable of forming organoids), we used organoid formation to confirm if in the setting of FOXM1 inhibition there is decreased OFR due to enhanced differentiation. Indeed, RCM-1 decreased the OFR, which was further reduced by IL-13 treatment (figure 3G,H).

FOXM1 inhibition by RCM-1 enhances epithelial barrier integrity

Impaired epithelial differentiation disturbs the barrier integrity, which is critical in the pathogenesis of EoE.8 30 In order to evaluate the ability of RCM-1 to restore epithelial barrier function, EPC2-hTERT cells were grown in ALI culture and given IL-13 to disrupt the epithelial barrier (figure 4A). TEER was significantly increased in cultures treated with RCM-1 despite IL-13-induced disruption, whereas RCM-1 treatment in the normal epithelial cells independent of IL-13 did not alter TEER (figure 4B). IL-13-stimulated ALI cultures treated with RCM-1 showed an increase in IVL and FLG, demonstrating increased differentiation after RCM-1 treatment, with a reciprocal decrease in TP63 and also a decrease in Ki-67 (figure 4C). These findings suggest that RCM-1 prevents impaired squamous stratification and barrier disruption and reduces basal proliferation by IL-13.

RCM-1 treatment improves epithelial changes and inflammation in murine EoE

Because FOXM1 inhibition with RCM-1 reversed the epithelial changes seen with IL-13 in vitro, we sought to determine the effects of RCM-1 in murine models of EoE. RCM-1 was started after the induction of EoE to assess whether it could treat EoE (figure 5A) and also during the sensitisation phase to assess whether it could prevent EoE (online supplemental figure S6A). As previously shown,22 in this model on day 18, mice had eosinophil infiltration and epithelial tissue remodelling with basal cell hyperplasia and increased Ki-67 and FOXM1 expression, similar to human disease (online supplemental figure S6B–E). RCM-1 administration both prevented the onset of EoE (online supplemental figure S6A–E) and treated disease after it occurred (figure 5A–C) as demonstrated by reduced basal cell hyperplasia in treated mice. Specifically, RCM-1 treatment normalised the number of TP63 and Ki-67 positive cells in EoE mice to those in non-EoE mice in both protocols. There was increased intensity of IVL in RCM-1 treated EoE mice, indicating that RCM-1 has potential therapeutic and preventative effects for EoE (figure 5B,C and online supplemental figure S6B,C).

In addition to changes in the epithelium, there was decreased eosinophil infiltration in EoE-induced mice treated with RCM-1 (figure 5B,C and online supplemental figure S6B–E) suggesting that FOXM1 has an effect on eosinophil chemotaxis. To investigate this finding further, we evaluated the effect of RCM-1 on CCL26 expression (a major chemoattractant in the EoE oesophagus).6 We found that RCM-1 treatment suppressed IL-13-induced CCL26 expression in EPC2-hTERT organoids and PDOs, including organoids derived from patients with EoE (figure 6A and online supplemental figure S7A). Similarly, in biopsies of patients with EoE, eosinophil count and CCL26 expression were significantly and positively correlated with FOXM1 expression (figure 6B,C). IL-13-signal transducer and activator of transcription 6 (STAT6) signalling plays key roles in the pathogenesis of EoE and the expression of eotaxin-3.31 32 We found that RCM-1 treatment reduced phosphorylation of STAT6 induced by IL-13 stimulation in EPC2-hTERC cells (figure 6D). Taken together, these suggest that FOXM1 inhibitor RCM-1 improves the epithelial changes in EoE in addition to decreasing eosinophil chemotaxis through inhibition of IL-13-STAT6 signalling.

FOXM1 directly drives cell cycle and cellular proliferation in the oesophageal epithelium

In order to understand the molecular changes that occur with reduction in FOXM1, we performed RNA sequencing of EPC2-hTERT monolayer cultures treated with siRNA FOXM1 and scrambled control. Gene Ontology analysis revealed genes associated with cell adhesion were upregulated in the siFOXM1 cultures compared with siNC. On the other hand, genes associated with cell cycle were downregulated (figure 7A). We then compared the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) from siFOXM1 cultures to the previously published EoE DEGs.26 There was a marked reversal of expression genes found in the EoE transcriptome with FOXM1 knockdown (figure 7B). Based on these findings, we sought to interrogate cell cycle within biopsies and siFOXM1 cells. In patient biopsies,26 cell cycle gene CCNB1 was increased in EoE (figure 7B,C). IL-13 stimulation also upregulated CCNB1 expression, while in the setting of FOXM1 knockdown, this gene was significantly downregulated (figure 7D,E). Cell cycle analysis by WST-1 assay demonstrated decreased proliferative activity in the knockdown as well as a decrease in per cent of cells in G2/M phase, contrary to changes by IL-13 stimulation (figure 7F,G). FOXM1 overexpression promotes basal cell hyperplasia and epithelial proliferation

To elucidate the effect of elevated FOXM1 independent of IL-13 and its effect on CCNB1 in oesophageal epithelium, we stably overexpressed FOXM1 in EPC2-hTERT cells (figure 8A,B). As expected in figure 7H, FOXM1 overexpression led to increased CCNB1 expression (figure 8C,D) as well as enhanced basal cell hyperplasia with increased TP63 and SOX2 expression (figure 8E–G). We also demonstrated that proliferative activity, measured by Ki-67 staining and WST-1 assay, and percent of cells in S and G2/M phase increased in the cells overexpressing FOXM1 (figure 8G–I). Further, FOXM1 overexpressing cells with a subsequent siRNA-mediated CCNB1 knockdown reduced the epithelial proliferation, suggesting that FOXM1 directly regulates CCNB1 which in turn leads to proliferation in EoE (figure 8J,K).

PI3K-AKT activation by IL-13 induces FOXM1 expression

While FOXM1 is known to be induced by the PI3K-AKT (protein kinase B) pathway in malignancy,33 34 how it may be induced in the context of the Th2 inflammatory milieu of the EoE epithelium is unknown. We found increased phosphorylated PI3K in biopsies from patients with active EoE compared with non-EoE and inactive EoE (figure 9A,B). Additionally, IL-13 induced phosphorylation of PI3K and AKT in EPC2-hTERT monolayer culture (figure 9C). Phosphorylated PI3K also increased in the patient-derived cells stimulated with IL-13 (figure 9D). Treatment with the PI3K/AKT pathway inhibitor LY294002 led to decreased expression of FOXM1 in EPC2-hTERT cells (figure 9E). Together, our findings suggest a non-canonical role for IL-13 in activating PI3K-AKT to drive FOXM1 expression in EoE (figure 9F).

Discussion

Epithelial disturbances in EoE result from chronic inflammation and lead to diminished barrier integrity which normally protects the submucosa from insults such as food particles and gastric acid.35 In order to identify promising therapeutic targets for EoE, it is imperative to elucidate the biological processes that regulate epithelial homeostasis. Here, we demonstrate that IL-13-induced differentiation defects and barrier disruption can be reversed by FOXM1 inhibition. We evaluate the therapeutic potential of FOXM1 inhibition demonstrating healing of the epithelium as well as decreased eosinophil chemotaxis. Mechanistically, we find that FOXM1 transcriptionally regulates proliferation through CCNB1 in the oesophageal epithelium and suggest a new role for PI3K/AKT pathway activation, driving cell proliferation through FOXM1 in EoE.

Previous studies have shown that Th2 cytokines promote oesophageal epithelial proliferation in EoE.6 22 36 37 In-depth single-cell sequencing has shown an expansion of the proliferating suprabasal cells.38 In conjunction with these hyperproliferating basal cells, the EoE epithelium is marked by a failure to differentiate.4 We have previously shown that basal cell hyperplasia in the EoE oesophagus associates with symptomatology.3 Furthermore, in experimental models, the epithelial disruption leads to barrier defects allowing for ongoing exposure to antigens.7 8 39 However, the underlying mechanisms driving disruption in the proliferation/differentiation gradient in EoE are poorly delineated. Analysing RNA sequencing data (GSE58640) of oesophageal biopsies,26 we demonstrated that cell cycle progression-related pathways, including PLK1, AURORA B, FOXM1 and AURORA A pathways, were notably enriched in patients with active EoE. FOXM1 is a transcription factor recognised as a master regulator of cell cycle and mitotic cell division.10 11 FOXM1 is also crucial for embryonic development and progression of malignant tumours. Overexpression of FOXM1 has been found in a wide range of cancers, such as head and neck, oesophagus, lung and breast,40 where it is involved in carcinogenesis, tumour growth, angiogenesis and metastasis, contributing to poor prognosis.21 41 42 Beyond its role in cell cycle and oncogenic processes, FOXM1 has recently been reported to be involved in epithelial cell differentiation in human skin keratinocytes12,15 and pulmonary allergic inflammatory disease contributing to goblet cell hyperplasia in asthma models.17 18 Moreover, skin models demonstrate that FOXM1 upregulation induced expansion of the epithelial progenitor compartment with activated proliferative ability, which disturbed terminal differentiation, as seen in lack of keratin 13 and FLG, in keratinocytes, resulting in hyperplasia.12 13 Conversely, depleted FOXM1 reduced proliferation and increased differentiation.14 15 Taken together, these reports and our findings suggest that FOXM1 overexpression in the setting of EoE may result in hyperproliferation, leading to a defective terminal differentiation and barrier function.

To gain insight into the role of FOXM1 in oesophageal epithelium, we performed RNA sequencing and ChIP analyses. As expected, cell cycle-related terms were decreased in siFOXM1-transfected EPC2-hTERT cells. FOXM1 accelerates entry into S and M-phase of cell cycle by transcriptionally regulating genes crucial for G1/S progression and G2/M transition, such as CCNB1, CDK1, CCNA2 and PLK1, promoting cellular proliferation.1643,45 We found that CCNB1 in particular was increased in EoE subjects and in vitro with IL-13 treatment; additionally, it was reduced by FOXM1 silencing and upregulated by FOXM1 overexpression. Of note, our ChIP experiments confirmed the binding of FOXM1 to the promoter region within 500 bp of CCNB1 transcription start site in oesophageal epithelial cells. These findings suggest that FOXM1 directly regulates epithelial cell proliferation by modulating the G2/M transition via transcriptional regulation of CCNB1, with FOXM1 inhibition restoring the terminal differentiation and barrier function that are compromised by Th2 cytokines in EoE. This highlights a critical role for FOXM1 in the pathophysiology of EoE, where its modulation could potentially offer a strategy for restoring epithelial proliferation-differentiation gradient.

One unexpected finding from this work is that FOXM1 expression is also positively associated with the severity of EoE inflammation, specifically eosinophil inflammation. In murine models of asthma, FOXM1 deletion reduced expression of CCL11 and CCL24, which in turn led to decreased eosinophil and macrophage recruitment to the lung,18 and RCM-1 treatment reduced the concentration of proinflammatory cytokines, such as IL-4, IL-5, IL-13 and IL-33.17 Similarly, FOXM1 overexpression in the liver drove liver inflammation by directly binding to the promoter region of CCL2.46 Our findings suggest that RCM-1 reduces CCL26 expression through inhibition of IL-13-STAT6 signalling. The fact that FOXM1 has dual roles, promoting epithelial dysfunction and driving inflammatory infiltrate, makes this a particularly attractive therapeutic target. Our previous work as well as the work of others highlight the durability of epithelial changes in the EoE epithelium, with ongoing transcriptomic changes despite decreased eosinophil count.4 47 Conversely, depletion of eosinophils in the oesophagus does not lead to endoscopic or histological changes in EoE.2 A bidirectional approach, with medications targeting both the epithelium and immune compartments, may offer more relief from inflammation, especially in refractory patients.

We sought to identify key upstream effectors of FOXM1 expression in the context of EoE. The PI3K/AKT signalling pathway is a major intracellular signalling pathway with aberrant activation identified widely in both malignant and non-malignant diseases. This pathway is critical for regulating numerous cellular processes, including proliferation, apoptosis, angiogenesis, migration, inflammation and fibrosis.48 49 Notably, beyond the canonical Janus kinase/STAT pathway, IL-13 has been shown to activate a non-canonical PI3K/AKT cascade in allergic diseases such as asthma and atopic dermatitis.50,52 In cancer, this same PI3K/AKT axis plays a pivotal role in driving FOXM1 activity.33 34 While it is established that PI3K/AKT signalling is essential for maintaining skin keratinocyte homeostasis,48 its role within the oesophageal epithelium in EoE remains largely unexplored. Our data reveal that phosphorylation of PI3K is significantly elevated in active EoE compared with both inactive EoE and control subjects. Moreover, we show that IL-13 directly activates PI3K and AKT, and that inhibition of the PI3K/AKT pathway using LY294002 effectively blocks FOXM1 expression in EPC2-hTERT cells. Increased phosphorylation of PI3K is also found in the patient-derived oesophageal cells with IL-13 stimulation. These findings underscore the critical role of IL-13 in driving FOXM1 upregulation via the PI3K/AKT signalling axis in the oesophageal epithelium. As such, targeting the dysregulated PI3K/AKT network may offer novel therapeutic avenues for addressing the pathophysiology of EoE.

One limitation of our work is that while organoid and ALI culture are powerful three-dimensional modelling techniques to simulate in vivo, these cultures consist of only oesophageal epithelial cells, preventing the investigation of influences arising from interactions of epithelial cells with the heterogeneous cellular environment, such as fibroblasts, immune cells and endothelial cells. Our recapitulated EoE milieu in vitro consists of IL-13, and there is concern that this does not take into account all inflammatory cytokine components and interactions. Co-culture with non-epithelial cells and other cytokines would be ideal to assess the role of FOXM1 in EoE in future work. The use of a murine EoE model ameliorates some of these concerns and shows feasibility of RCM-1 treatment. However, an artificially induced and non-human model cannot completely recapitulate a human disease. Another limitation is that our analysis focused on only one cell cycle gene, and FOXM1 may actually regulate more than one. We chose CCNB1 here as it was a higher ranked differentially expressed gene compared with other cell cycle genes, such as CCNA2, in our siFOXM1 RNA sequencing. Therefore, we also evaluated CDK1 and CCNA2 in the RNA sequencing of patients with EoE26 and found both were upregulated in the EoE oesophagus (online supplemental figure S8A). Further, these genes were downregulated in the setting of FOXM1 knockdown and upregulated in FOXM1 overexpressing cells, suggesting that FOXM1 does indeed regulate other cell cycle genes as well and perhaps that FOXM1 is a master regulator of cell cycle in the oesophagus (online supplemental figure S8B,C). Lastly, if this were to be a viable option for therapy, we would need to demonstrate that there are not substantial off-target effects. RCM-1 did not demonstrate obvious adverse effects in our murine system, nor did it increase apoptosis in vitro, but this will require more in-depth evaluation as this is translated.

Taken together, we highlight the role of FOXM1 as a key modulator in the balance between epithelial proliferation and terminal differentiation in the oesophagus. FOXM1 inhibition may restore epithelial homeostasis and barrier integrity to mitigate hyperproliferation through regulation of cell cycle-related gene and inflammatory cytokine in response to allergic inflammation, leading to improved symptoms in patients with EoE. Thus, approaches directly targeting the epithelial transcriptional network for the homeostasis may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for EoE treatment.

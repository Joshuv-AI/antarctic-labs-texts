Genotyping and Clinical Manifestations of Adenoviral Keratoconjunctivitis in South Korea
Authors: Min Ji Park, Su Kyung Lee, Hyun Soo Kim, Jeong-Won Seo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to identify the predominant adenoviral genotypes responsible for epidemic keratoconjunctivitis in South Korea and to evaluate genotype distribution and associated clinical manifestations based on molecular confirmation.MethodsSixty-seven conjunctival swabs were obtained from patients clinically suspected of epidemic keratoconjunctivitis between October 2016 and December 2019. Viral nucleic acids were extracted using the QIAamp Viral Mini kit and processed via the QIAcube platform. Adenoviral genotypes were molecularly confirmed using polymerase chain reaction and sequencing of the hexon gene.ResultsOf the 67 specimens, 45 (67.2%) showed positive genotyping results. Adenovirus types D8 (42.2%) and D37 (31.1%) were the predominant genotypes identified. From 2016 to 2018, D8 was the dominant genotype, whereas in 2019, additional genotypes, including B3, D53, D64, and E4, were newly detected during the study period. All patients with confirmed genotypes exhibited conjunctival hyperemia and follicular reaction. Pseudomembrane formation was observed in 68.4% of D8 cases and 57.1% of D37 cases. Corneal involvement, including epithelial defects and subepithelial infiltration, was frequently observed in genotypes D8, D37, D53, and D64, whereas genotypes B3 and E4 showed no corneal involvement, indicating potential trends toward genotype-associated clinical features.ConclusionsAdenoviral genotypes D8 and D37 were the predominant causes of epidemic keratoconjunctivitis in South Korea. In 2019, additional genotypes, including B3, D53, D64, and E4, were newly detected, suggesting temporal changes in genotype distribution during the study period. Corneal involvement was more frequently observed in D8, D37, D53, and D64, whereas B3 and E4 showed no corneal involvement. These findings provide descriptive epidemiologic data on adenoviral genotypes in South Korea and may serve as baseline information for future studies of genotype-associated clinical manifestations.

Human adenoviruses are nonenveloped, double-stranded DNA viruses associated with a wide range of clinical diseases, including respiratory infections, gastroenteritis, and keratoconjunctivitis [1–4]. Among these, epidemic keratoconjunctivitis (EKC) is a highly contagious ocular disease that can cause significant morbidity because of corneal involvement and visual disturbance. EKC is predominantly caused by specific adenoviral genotypes, particularly within species D, and clinical manifestations may vary depending on the infecting genotype [4–9]. Therefore, genotype-based analysis is important for understanding disease patterns and potential differences in clinical presentation. However, despite its clinical significance, data regarding genotype distribution and genotype-specific clinical features of adenoviral keratoconjunctivitis in South Korea remain limited. Previous South Korean studies have been restricted to short observation periods or single-time-point analyses, and few have systematically correlated molecularly confirmed genotypes with comprehensive clinical manifestations across multiple genotypes, including less commonly reported genotypes such as D53, D64, B3, and E4. In this study, we aimed to identify the predominant adenoviral genotypes responsible for EKC in South Korea over a 4-year period using polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-based hexon gene sequencing and to evaluate genotype distribution and associated clinical manifestations, including corneal involvement, eyelid swelling, and pseudomembrane formation. In addition, we investigated temporal changes in genotype distribution, age-stratified genotype patterns, and the detection of additional genotypes during the study period, with the goal of providing longitudinal epidemiologic data regarding adenoviral keratoconjunctivitis in South Korea.

Materials and Methods

Ethics statement

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Hallym University Dongtan Sacred Heart Hospital (No. HDT 2018-10-003). All procedures performed in studies involving human participants were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional research committee, as well as with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards. Written informed consent was obtained from all individual participants included in the study.

Study population and eligibility criteria

From October 2016 to December 2019, we analyzed adenoviral genotypes in 67 patients who visited our ophthalmology department with clinically suspected EKC. Patients with confirmed bacterial conjunctivitis were excluded.

All enrolled patients presented with typical clinical features of keratoconjunctivitis, including conjunctival hyperemia, discharge, and follicles. Adenoviral infection was confirmed using PCR testing.

To minimize potential confounding factors affecting corneal involvement, patients with conditions that could independently influence corneal findings were excluded. This included glaucoma, a history of herpes simplex keratitis, recent ocular surgery, recent contact lens use, and allergic conjunctivitis.

Conjunctival swab samples were collected from the lower palpebral conjunctiva using sterile swabs, placed into Universal Transport Medium (Copan Diagnostics), and stored at −70 °C until analysis. Viral DNA was extracted using the QIAamp Viral Mini kit (QIAGEN) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and processed using the QIAcube platform.

The adenoviral hexon gene was amplified using PCR with a specific primer set (ADHEX1F/AD2). When amplification failed, an alternative primer set (AD1/AD2) was used (Table 1). PCR products were confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis, followed by sequencing using the ABI Prism BigDye Terminator ver. 3.1 system (Applied Biosystems). Genotypes were determined using the Basic Local Alignment Search Tool (BLAST) from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) GenBank database.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS ver. 22 (IBM Corp.). Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test, and a p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

In our study, adenovirus was successfully identified in 45 of 67 samples (67.2%). The mean age of patients was 38 years (range, 18 months to 64 years), with most patients aged between 31 and 50 years (Table 2). Regarding genotype- specific age distribution, D8 was identified across a broad age range, including children aged 0 to 10 years, whereas D37 was predominantly identified in adults aged 31 to 40 years.

Adenovirus type D8 (42.2%) and D37 (31.1%) were the predominant genotypes identified. The annual distribution of adenoviral genotypes showed a significant shift over time (p = 0.047, chi-square test). Between 2016 and 2018, genotype D8 was the dominant strain, accounting for 65.0% (13 / 20) of cases, whereas D37 accounted for 32.0% (8 / 25). In contrast, in 2019, the genotype distribution became more diverse, with the detection of additional genotypes, including B3 (8.0%), D53 (12.0%), D64 (16.0%), and E4 (8.0%) (Table 3). This finding indicates an increase in genotype diversity over time.

The monthly distribution showed seasonal variation, with the highest incidence observed in July (20.0%) and November (17.8%), while no cases were identified in January (Table 4). Genotype D8 showed distribution throughout the year, with increased incidence between July and November, whereas other genotypes such as D37, D53, and D64 were identified only during certain months (Table 4).

Clinical manifestations varied according to genotype, although no statistically significant differences were observed (p > 0.05). Corneal involvement, including subepithelial infiltration and epithelial defects, was frequently observed in genotypes D8 (57.9%), D37 (57.1%), D53 (100%), and D64 (80.0%). In contrast, genotypes B3 and E4 consistently showed no corneal involvement across all observed cases.

Pseudomembrane formation was observed in D8 (68.4%), D37 (57.1%), D53 (100%), and E4 (100%) cases (p = 0.068). Eyelid swelling was observed in all cases of genotype D64 (100%) and in 57.9% of D8 cases but was absent in patients with genotypes B3 and E4 (p = 0.086). Other clinical features, including filamentary keratitis, conjunctival follicles, chemosis, and punctate epithelial erosions, showed variable distribution without statistically significant genotype-associated differences (Table 5). These findings suggest possible genotype-associated clinical tendencies; however, they should be interpreted as exploratory observations because most comparisons did not reach statistical significance and several genotype groups included only a small number of cases.

Discussion

EKC, primarily caused by adenoviruses, remains a significant public health concern due to its high transmissibility and potential for corneal involvement, accounting for a substantial proportion of viral conjunctivitis cases [10]. Recent advances in molecular diagnostics, including PCR and sequencing of the hexon gene, have enabled more accurate identification of adenoviral genotypes and improved understanding of their epidemiology [3,11,12]. In this study, genotypes D8 and D37 were the predominant causes of EKC, consistent with findings from previous studies in other regions [13,14]. Aoki and Tagawa [13] reported that adenovirus types 8, 19, and 37 were the major causative agents of conjunctivitis in Japan, while Nguyen et al. [14] demonstrated the predominance of type 8 in EKC outbreaks in Vietnam, supporting the widespread involvement of these genotypes across Asia.

In our cohort, genotype D8 was identified across a broad age range, including children, whereas genotype D37 was observed predominantly in adults and was not detected in the 0- to 10-year age group. Although these age-related differences were not statistically significant, the observed patterns may suggest differences in exposure setting or host susceptibility among genotypes.

A key finding of this study was the temporal shift in genotype distribution. Between 2016 and 2018, genotype D8 was the dominant strain, whereas in 2019, the genotype distribution became more diverse, with the detection of additional genotypes during the later study period, including B3, D53, D64, and E4. This shift indicates increasing genotype diversity over time and may reflect viral evolution or regional epidemiological influences.

Similar trends have been reported in other regions, including China and the Middle East. These regional differences further support the influence of geographic and epidemiological factors on adenoviral genotype distribution. This increase in genotype diversity may also reflect cross-regional transmission dynamics and population mobility in East Asia [6,15]. In addition, long-term surveillance studies have demonstrated dynamic changes in adenoviral genotype distribution over time [16,17]. Notably, genotype D53, which had not been previously reported in South Korea, was newly detected during the study period and showed patterns comparable to those observed in China [6,15].

Furthermore, while genotype E4 remained relatively rare in South Korea, Japan, and Taiwan, it was reported as the predominant type in China during the same period, highlighting significant regional variation in genotype distribution that may reflect differences in viral circulation patterns or population immunity across East Asian countries [6,16,18]. These findings underscore the dynamic nature of adenoviral epidemiology and highlight the importance of continuous molecular surveillance.

Although no statistically significant differences were observed in clinical manifestations among genotypes (p > 0.05), several descriptive genotype-associated patterns were noted. Corneal involvement, including subepithelial infiltration, was frequently observed in genotypes D8, D37, D53, and D64, consistent with previous reports associating these genotypes with more severe corneal disease [19,20]. In contrast, genotypes B3 and E4 showed no corneal involvement in this cohort. However, given the small number of cases, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and require validation in larger studies. Eyelid swelling was observed in all cases of genotype D64 and in more than half of D8 cases (57.9%) but was absent in patients with genotypes B3 and E4. Although these observations may suggest a possible association between inflammatory response and corneal involvement, further studies are required to confirm this association [10].

Previous studies have suggested that early corneal epithelial abnormalities may predict the development of subepithelial infiltrates, particularly in adenoviral genotypes such as D54, D56, and D37 [10]. The findings of the present study suggest possible differences in corneal involvement patterns among adenoviral genotypes. In addition, genotypes D53 and D64 demonstrated relatively high rates of corneal involvement, suggesting a potential association with more severe disease, although statistical significance was not achieved.

Seasonal variation was also observed, with increased incidence during the summer and autumn months, particularly in July and November, consistent with previous surveillance- based studies of infectious conjunctivitis [21]. Environmental and behavioral factors, including increased interpersonal contact and viral stability, may contribute to these seasonal trends.

This study has several limitations. First, the relatively small sample size, particularly for less common genotypes such as D53, D64, B3, and E4, limited the statistical power to detect significant differences in clinical manifestations. Therefore, the observed genotype-associated clinical findings should be interpreted cautiously and regarded as exploratory observations rather than definitive genotype-associated clinical patterns. Second, this was a single-center study, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Third, the lack of long-term follow-up restricted the evaluation of persistent corneal sequelae and visual outcomes. Finally, although PCR-based genotyping is highly accurate, occasional amplification failure may have been influenced by viral load or primer specificity. Therefore, the present findings should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of genotype-associated clinical manifestations.

In addition, because the present dataset was limited to the period between 2016 and 2019, the findings may not fully reflect the current distribution of adenoviral genotypes. Given the dynamic nature of adenoviral epidemiology, genotype prevalence and circulating strains may have changed over time. Therefore, continued molecular surveillance and updated epidemiologic studies are necessary to better understand current genotype trends and clinical characteristics.

Despite these limitations, this study provides several notable contributions to the understanding of adenoviral keratoconjunctivitis in South Korea. To our knowledge, this is one of the few South Korean studies to directly correlate molecularly confirmed adenoviral genotypes with comprehensive clinical manifestations over a multiyear longitudinal period, addressing a gap in previous studies, which were limited by short observation periods or lacked systematic clinical phenotyping.

The present study documented a statistically significant temporal shift in genotype distribution (p = 0.047) and identified D53 in this cohort during the study period, along with other newly detected genotypes, including D64, B3, and E4. Genotypes D53 and D64 showed high rates of corneal involvement (100% and 80.0%, respectively), whereas B3 and E4 showed no corneal involvement, suggesting possible differences in corneal involvement patterns among genotypes in this South Korean cohort. In addition, age-stratified analysis showed that D8 was the predominant genotype in children, whereas D37 and the other newly detected genotypes were identified only in adults, which may reflect differences in exposure patterns or host susceptibility. Collectively, these findings provide descriptive epidemiologic data on adenoviral genotypes in South Korea during the study period and may serve as baseline information for future studies investigating genotype-associated clinical manifestations.

Continued multicenter molecular surveillance and updated longitudinal studies are warranted to monitor the evolving epidemiology of adenoviral keratoconjunctivitis and validate whether these genotype patterns remain consistent over time.

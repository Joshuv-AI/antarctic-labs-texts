Zigakibart Potently Inhibits A Proliferation-Inducing Ligand-Mediated IgA Production, Addressing IgAN Pathogenesis Root Causes
Authors: John Dulos, Joost Kreijtz, Paul Vink, Victoria Cotton, Jay J Kuo, Jeannette Lo, Wout Jansen, Jeroen Bakker, Lilian Driessen, Marco Guadagnoli, Peter van Zandvoort, Max Warncke, Jan Paul Medema, Jonathan Barratt, Hans van Eenennaam, Andrea Van Elsas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionZigakibart is a humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody (mAb) binding A proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL, TNFSF13), a critical factor in IgA nephropathy (IgAN) pathogenesis. Results from the ongoing phase 1/2 ADU-CL-19 study (NCT03945318) have shown clinically meaningful outcomes in patients with IgAN treated with zigakibart.MethodsWe conducted a preclinical characterization of zigakibart using in vitro methods, including investigation of its binding to APRIL from various species, blocking of APRIL binding to transmembrane activator and calcium modulator and cyclophilin ligand interactor (TACI) and B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA), investigation of the potency of zigakibart on BCMA- and TACI-reporter cells, and the assessment of the effect of APRIL blockade on human plasma cell survival and Ig production. In vivo methods were used to characterize zigakibart in human APRIL transgenic mice (hAPRIL-tg) and nonhuman primates.ResultsZigakibart potently blocked APRIL binding to TACI and BCMA and inhibited human plasma cell survival, which are key mediators in IgAN pathogenesis. Zigakibart selectively reduced circulating IgA and IgM to a greater extent than IgG and inhibited T-cell-independent antibody responses and IgA deposition in the kidney in vivo. Importantly, zigakibart inhibited IgA+ and IgM+ to a greater extent than IgG+ human plasma cells in vitro.ConclusionIn these preclinical studies, zigakibart demonstrated robust pharmacological activity and, by neutralizing APRIL, inhibited markers associated with the pathogenesis of IgAN. Zigakibart therefore provides a potentially disease-modifying approach for IgAN treatment. Evaluation of the effects of zigakibart on proteinuria and long-term kidney function in adults with IgAN is ongoing in the ADU-CL-19 study and the phase 3 BEYOND study (NCT05852938).Keywords: antibody, APRIL/TNFSF13, IgA nephropathy, mode of action, plasma cells, zigakibart

IgAN is the leading cause of primary glomerulonephritis, making a significant contribution to the global burden of chronic kidney disease and kidney failure.1, 2, 3 To date, IgAN management has relied primarily on supportive care. However, many patients remain at high risk of progressive chronic kidney disease,3,4 which underscores an unmet need for treatments that target the underlying pathogenic mechanisms of IgAN. Progress has been made with the availability of treatments including budesonide (delayed-release) and sparsentan, both of which have received full approval in the USA,5, 6, 7, 8 as well as iptacopan,9 an alternative complement pathway inhibitor, and atrasentan, a highly selective endothelin A receptor antagonist,10 both granted accelerated approval in the USA. Further targeted therapies are currently under investigation in late-stage clinical trials.

The pathogenesis of IgAN, a chronic autoimmune disease, is described by a “multi-hit” mechanism. Specifically, the production of galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) is associated with the development of autoantibodies against Gd-IgA1, which together form circulating immune complexes that deposit in the glomeruli. Downstream pathways, including the complement and endothelin systems, are activated, resulting in inflammation, fibrosis, and subsequent kidney injury.11

The cytokines, B-cell activating factor (BAFF) and APRIL (TNFSF13) play essential roles in the survival and maintenance of B cells and humoral immunity. APRIL is involved in B-cell activation and differentiation to plasma cells, as well as in B-cell plasmablast and plasma cell survival. In its homotrimeric form, APRIL acts through binding to TACI (TNFRSF13B) and BCMA (TNFRSF17) receptors.12, 13, 14 In addition, APRIL is believed to induce IgA class switching and increase the survival of IgA-producing plasma cells. In IgAN pathogenesis, this coincides with increased circulating Gd-IgA1 levels and glomerular immune complex accumulation.14, 15, 16 hAPRIL-tg show increased serum IgA17 and enhanced IgA antibody responses.18 Unlike BAFF, specific APRIL gene polymorphisms result in increased susceptibility to IgAN and impact the severity of disease.19, 20, 21 Furthermore, high plasma levels of APRIL (but not BAFF) and Gd-IgA1 are associated with proteinuria and lower estimated glomerular filtration rate in patients with IgAN.16,22 Blocking APRIL decreases plasma cell survival, resulting in reduced circulating IgA and Gd-IgA1 levels and reduced immune complex formation, thereby lessening kidney injury.14,23

Zigakibart is a humanized IgG4 mAb that exclusively targets APRIL, preventing its binding to BCMA and TACI24,25 and is currently under development for the treatment of IgAN. Zigakibart interferes with B-cell differentiation and the survival of Gd-IgA1-producing plasma cells. The resulting decrease in Gd-IgA1 levels and reduced formation of immune complexes may provide disease-modifying potential in IgAN (Figure 1). Here, we present the preclinical characterization of zigakibart. The translation of these promising preclinical findings into clinically meaningful outcomes for patients with IgAN has been demonstrated in the phase 1/2 ADU-CL-19 trial (NCT03945318), in which zigakibart treatment resulted in clinical benefits over 100 weeks.26 The efficacy and safety of zigakibart in IgAN is being further investigated in the ongoing phase 3 BEYOND study (NCT05852938).27

Methods

In Vitro Studies

Zigakibart Binding to APRIL From Different Species

Zigakibart was humanized from the mouse parental antibody hAPRIL.01A,18 as described in the Supplementary Methods. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) were performed to measure zigakibart binding to human, cynomolgus monkey, mouse, and rat recombinant APRIL proteins.18 Details are provided in the Supplementary Methods. Zigakibart (0.01–100 nM) was tested for APRIL binding and detected using horseradish peroxidase–coupled antibodies targeting human IgG4 (zigakibart) or mouse IgG2b (hAPRIL.01A). EC50 values were calculated as the averages of 2 independent experiments.

Bio-layer interferometry binding assays (Octet QK, Fortebio) were used to evaluate the binding of hAPRIL.01A and zigakibart antibodies to cynomolgus and human FLAG-APRIL. Zigakibart and hAPRIL.01A were coupled to biosensors at low density through amine chemistry. The association and dissociation of recombinant FLAG-APRIL were determined, and association rate, dissociation rate, and equilibrium dissociation constants (KDs) were quantified using a 1:1 global fit binding model using Octet Analysis Software 6.4 (Octet QK). Details are provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Blocking of APRIL Binding to BCMA and TACI

The effect of zigakibart and hAPRIL.01A inhibition of APRIL binding to recombinant BCMA or TACI was detected using ELISA, as detailed in the Supplementary Methods. IC50 values were calculated using GraphPad Prism as the averages of 3 independent experiments.

Potency of Zigakibart on BCMA- and TACI-Reporter Cells

Cellular reporter assays were performed using Jurkat-BCMA:Fas or Jurkat-TACI:Fas reporter cells to assess the inhibitory effect of zigakibart and hAPRIL.01A on APRIL binding to human BCMA or TACI, as described before.26,28 Briefly, BCMA and TACI were fused to the intracellular domains of the FAS receptor on Jurkat cells. The reporter cells were seeded and stimulated with APRIL-FLAG and serial dilutions of zigakibart. Cells were then collected and resuspended in Nicoletti buffer and stored for ≥24 hours at 4 °C. Induction of apoptosis was measured using flow cytometry (FACSCalibur System, BD Biosciences), and GraphPad Prism was used for IC50 calculations. Details of the assay are described in the Supplementary Methods. In addition, the potency of zigakibart in blocking APRIL binding to BCMA in HEK293 reporter cells was assessed as described in the Supplementary Methods.

APRIL Blockade on Plasma Cell Survival and Ig Production

The stimulatory effect of APRIL on human plasmablasts or plasma cells was assessed using ex vivo-differentiated plasmablasts or plasma cells. Human peripheral CD19+/CD20+ B cells and human tonsillar CD20+ B cells isolated from 4 healthy donors were differentiated to plasmablasts or plasma cells, as described in the Supplementary Methods. BAFF-R, TACI, and BCMA receptor expression levels were measured on IgA and IgG subtype plasmablast or plasma cells from peripheral B cells using flow cytometry on day 11. From day 11 onward, the cells were cultured ± human APRIL, APRIL + zigakibart, or APRIL + isotype control mAb. After 1 week, the effect of APRIL and zigakibart on plasmablast and plasma cell survival was assessed using flow cytometry and analyzed using FlowJo.

To assess the effect of APRIL blockade on IgA, IgG (total), and IgM production in the supernatant, the cells were cultured ± APRIL, APRIL + isotype control mAb (mIgG1 or hIgG4), APRIL + hAPRIL.01A, or APRIL + zigakibart. Mouse parental hAPRIL.01A antibody was used to allow detection of IgG production because zigakibart, a human IgG4 antibody, would interfere with the detection of produced IgG. ELISAs were performed using kits from Thermo Fisher Scientific and Invitrogen on days 11 to 18 to determine the concentrations of IgA, IgG, and IgM, and results were analyzed using GraphPad Prism.

In Vivo Studies

Human APRIL Transgenic Mice Study

Details of the study design are presented in the Supplementary Methods. hAPRIL-tg mice (aged approximately 36 weeks) received either an isotype control Ab (n = 16) or hAPRIL.01A 10 mg/kg (n = 16) i.v. twice/wk. After 28 days, serum, urine, and several tissues were collected for analyses, described in the Supplementary Methods. Ig (IgA, IgG2b, and IgM) levels were measured using the Mouse Isotyping Panel 1 kit from Meso Scale Discovery. Data were analyzed using Excel and SoftMax Pro 7.0.3. Ig production by plasma cells was assessed by ELISPOT using bone marrow and splenocytes. Glomerular IgA deposits were detected using immunofluorescence, as described in the Supplementary Methods and analyzed in GraphPad Prism using a parametric, unpaired, 2-tailed t test.

Nonhuman Primate Study

Female cynomolgus monkeys were assigned to treatment groups of 3 per group (vehicle, 0.3, 3, and 30 mg/kg zigakibart). At baseline and on treatment, blood samples were collected to determine IgG, IgM and IgA responses using ELISA-based methods.

Results

Zigakibart Selectively Inhibits IgA- and IgM-Secreting Plasma Cells

Zigakibart bound efficiently and with high affinity to human and cynomolgus APRIL but did not bind to mouse or rat APRIL (Supplementary Figure S1A and B; Table 1). Zigakibart prevented APRIL-dependent binding to recombinantly expressed BCMA and TACI receptors with high potency (BCMA IC50 = 1.1 nM; TACI IC50 = 5.1 nM; Supplementary Figure S1C–E, Table 2).

To study the effect of zigakibart on Ig secretion in primary human cells, we first analyzed the expression of BAFF receptor, BCMA, and TACI in in vitro-differentiated human plasmablast cultures. Whereas IgG-, IgA-, and IgM-secreting plasmablasts stained similarly for BAFF receptor, BCMA and TACI levels were significantly higher in IgA- and IgM-secreting plasmablasts and plasma cells than in IgG-producing cells (Supplementary Figure S2). Accordingly, stimulation of these cells with APRIL increased cell survival (Figure 2a) and resulted in a large increase in IgA and IgM production, whereas IgG production was minimally induced (Figure 2b–d). APRIL blockade with zigakibart inhibited survival and antibody production of primary human plasmablasts activated with APRIL and displayed selectivity toward IgA+ and IgM+ cells, which is consistent with the elevated BCMA and TACI expression detected (Figure 2b–d). IgG production was not significantly altered in this experiment. However, experiments with the mouse parental IgG antibody hAPRIL.01A, showed a clear reduction of IgA and IgM with no impact on IgG secretion (Figure 2b–d). In addition, zigakibart suppressed IgA production and inhibited the survival of human tonsillar B cells (Supplementary Figure S3).

APRIL Blockade Reduces Glomerular IgA in Humanized APRIL Transgenic Mice

hAPRIL-tg mice were generated to study transgenic expression of human APRIL in mice and showed increased circulating levels of human APRIL, altered B- and T-cell homeostasis,18,29 and heightened B-cell responses and Ig production29 compared with wild-type mice. In hAPRIL-tg mice, 4-week administration of the mouse parental hAPRIL.01A antibody reduced serum levels of APRIL (Table 3), IgA, IgM, and to a lesser extent, IgG2b (Figure 3a–c). Bone marrow and splenic IgA were markedly decreased by anti-APRIL treatment (Supplementary Figure S4), concomitant with a decreased IgA+ plasma cell count in those tissues. Although the hAPRIL-tg mice did not develop spontaneous renal injury by 40 weeks of age (Table 3), glomerular IgA deposits were observed and were reduced with hAPRIL.01A Ab treatment (Figure 3d and e).

Discussion

Here, we report the preclinical characterization of the humanized IgG4 mAb zigakibart. In these in vitro and in vivo studies, we have demonstrated that by targeting APRIL, zigakibart inhibits markers implicated in the pathogenesis of IgAN. APRIL plays a critical role in IgAN pathology, as evidenced by the correlation of elevated levels of APRIL with increased Gd-IgA1, proteinuria, decreased kidney function, and reduced kidney survival.13 Disease progression in patients with IgAN could potentially be modified by pharmacological therapies that block APRIL signaling and, as a result, reduce levels of Gd-IgA1.7

Zigakibart selectively targets APRIL and specifically blocks its interaction with the receptors TACI and BCMA. In these preclinical studies, we demonstrated that selective targeting APRIL with zigakibart specifically inhibits APRIL-mediated survival of IgA- and IgM-secreting human plasma cells, resulting in reduced IgA and IgM and, to a lesser extent, IgG secretion both in vitro and in vivo in mice, as well as a strong pharmacodynamic effect in nonhuman primate. Similar findings were observed in patients with IgAN in the ongoing multicenter phase 1/2 open-label study, ADU-CL-19, evaluating the safety and efficacy of zigakibart.26 Our in vitro data showed potent inhibition of BCMA and TACI binding to APRIL, as well as neutralization of APRIL-induced plasma cell survival. In our preclinical animal models, APRIL inhibition significantly reduced free (unbound) APRIL levels (data not shown) and subsequently reduced serum Gd-IgA levels and IgA deposits in the kidney, which are hallmarks of IgAN. In nonhuman primates, zigakibart demonstrated persistent reductions in IgA (and IgM) levels. Zigakibart reduced IgA both from plasma cells derived from the periphery, and, even more importantly, from mucosal tissue-derived tonsillar plasma cells; these findings illustrate the molecule’s disease-modifying potential.

Similar to the preclinical studies described here, a strong pharmacodynamic effect of zigakibart treatment was demonstrated in the phase 1/2 ADU-CL-19 study, as illustrated by rapid reductions in serum IgA, Gd-IgA, and IgM levels, with a more modest reduction in IgG levels. This observation is comparable with other anti-APRIL antibodies,23 and is consistent with our observation that in vitro-cultured IgG+ plasmablasts or plasma cells express lower levels of BCMA and TACI than IgA+ and IgM+ plasmablasts or plasma cells. Therefore, the reduction in IgA and IgM observed with zigakibart may be due to blocking of APRIL-induced IgA+ and IgM+ plasma cell activity and survival, consistent with data from nonhuman species. Similarly, zigakibart had only a modest impact on circulating IgG levels. The ADU-CL-19 study showed that zigakibart treatment led to clinically meaningful and durable reductions in proteinuria (60.4% at 100 weeks) and sustained stabilization of estimated glomerular filtration rate through 100 weeks in patients with IgAN.26

In conclusion, these preclinical studies provide evidence that zigakibart strongly inhibits APRIL activity in vitro and in vivo and targets the immunological mechanisms underlying IgAN. The data reported in this paper support the hypothesis that human APRIL plays a key role in IgA and Gd-IgA production and that the APRIL-neutralizing antibody, zigakibart, represents a potentially disease-modifying treatment for patients diagnosed with IgAN. The efficacy and safety of zigakibart in patients with IgAN continue to be investigated in the ongoing phase 1/2 ADU-CL-19 study26 and in the global, randomized, placebo-controlled phase 3 BEYOND study (NCT05852938).25

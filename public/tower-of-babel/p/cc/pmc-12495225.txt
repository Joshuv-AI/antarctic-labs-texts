WGCNA-based integration of transcriptomics and metabolomics reveals key meat quality regulators during development in Jingyuan chickens
Authors: Wei Zhao, Siyu Chen, Jinli Tian, Lin Xue, Lijuan Yang, Jiahuan Hu, Haorui Wang, Jiayi Yang, Yaling Gu, Juan Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractJingyuan chickens are known for their unique meat flavor, but the molecular mechanisms underlying the dynamic changes in meat quality at different developmental stages remain unclear. In this study, we systematically measured the meat quality indicators of 42-, 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens, and integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic data using weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) to identify key regulators of meat quality. The results showed that the growth and slaughter performance of 126- and 180-day-old chickens were significantly better than those of 42-day-old chickens (P < 0.05), but the slaughter rate and half-eviscerated rate of 42-day-old chickens were higher. With increasing age, the crude protein, drip loss, and crude ash content of the breast muscle increased significantly, while the water content decreased significantly (P < 0.05). WGCNA analysis revealed that in the 42- and 126-day-old groups, the greenyellow and blue modules were significantly positively correlated with meat quality indicators (P < 0.01), and their hub genes were significantly enriched in the PI3K - Akt signaling pathway, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, fatty acid metabolism, and biosynthesis of amino acids. In the 42- and 180-day-old groups, the pink and brown modules were positively correlated with meat quality indicators(P < 0.01), and the hub genes were enriched in the MAPK signaling pathway, AMPK signaling pathway, and FoxO signaling pathway. In the 126- and 180-day-old groups, the magenta module was positively correlated with meat quality indicators (P < 0.01), and the hub genes were enriched in the MAPK signaling pathway, Wnt signaling pathway, glutathione metabolism, and GnRH signaling pathway. Metabolome data analysis showed that in the 42 - and 126 - day - old groups, the darkgrey, steelblue, sienna3, and blue modules were significantly positively correlated with meat quality indicators (P < 0.05), and their characteristic metabolites were significantly enriched in histidine metabolism, primary bile acid biosynthesis, and bile secretion. In the 42 - and 180 - day - old groups, the magenta and cyan modules were highly positively correlated with meat quality indicators (P < 0.05), and their metabolites were enriched in sphingolipid metabolism, vitamin B6 metabolism, thermogenesis, and purine metabolism. In the 126 - and 180 - day - old groups, the cyan module was highly significantly positively correlated with meat quality indicators (P < 0.05), with characteristic metabolites enriched in oxidative phosphorylation, nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, vitamin digestion and absorption, and the AMPK signaling pathway. Finally, eight key genes closely related to meat quality (SNORD14, IL7, HACD4, BFSP1, BCL6, FAM13A, ANKRD2, and AGXT2) and five key metabolites (p - Cresol sulfate, 2 - Piperidinone, 4 - Hydroxyquinoline, Succinic acid, and Dihydroxyacetone phosphate) were screened and identified. This study uncovers the age-dependent mechanisms regulating Jingyuan chicken meat quality. This study provides valuable insights into the genetic regulation of meat quality in Jingyuan chickens, which may inform breeding programs aiming to improve poultry meat quality.Keywords: Chicken, Weighted network co-expression analysis, Age, Meat quality, Key regulatory factors

Introduction

Chicken meat has gained widespread global popularity due to its nutritional advantages, including high protein content, low fat, and low cholesterol (Güngören et al., 2025). Recent years have witnessed significant advancements in broiler breeding technologies, driving substantial annual growth in global chicken production and consumption (Yu, et al., 2023a). Concurrently, broiler production performance has continuously improved, with notable progress in body weight and feed conversion efficiency (Erensoy and Sarıca, 2023). However, the dynamic patterns and regulatory mechanisms governing age-dependent variations in poultry meat quality traits remain a critical area requiring in-depth exploration. Current research predominantly focuses on meat quality assessment at specific developmental stages. For instance, hypothalamic transcriptome analysis combined with Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) has identified regulatory genes associated with body weight traits in 50-day-old 817 broilers (Wei, et al., 2024). Additionally, comparative analysis of meat quality differences between 90-day-old Daweishan mini chickens and Cobb broilers, integrated with breast muscle transcriptomics and metabolomics, has revealed key regulatory genes and metabolites linked to flavor amino acids and lipids (Zhao, et al., 2024b). While these studies provide valuable foundational data for understanding breed-specific meat characteristics at defined developmental stages, they have rarely addressed the systematic profiling of meat quality dynamics across different age phases.

Jingyuan chickens live in the cold and dry environment of the Loess Plateau. Their unique ecological environment and feeding methods have shaped their excellent characteristics of cold and drought resistance, strong disease resistance, firm meat texture, unique flavor, and high nutritional value (Huang, et al., 2022). However, with the development of modern agriculture, the demand for breed improvement and commercial breeding is growing, and the germplasm resources of Jingyuan chickens are facing severe pressure (Wang, et al., 2021). In recent years, in pursuit of short-term economic benefits, some farmers have introduced large numbers of foreign commercial chicken breeds and crossbred them with Jingyuan chickens in a disorderly manner, leading to serious degradation of their germplasm resources and the gradual loss of their excellent characteristics, which has significantly increased the risk of this breed becoming endangered. As a precious local breed listed in the “China Poultry Germplasm Resource Catalog”, the protection and research of Jingyuan chicken germplasm resources are crucial to maintaining the genetic diversity of poultry in China (Yu, et al., 2023b). In particular, the excellent meat characteristics of Jingyuan chickens deserve attention. Compared with fast-growing broiler breeds, Jingyuan chickens are characterized by fine muscle fibers and rich flavor, which are well received by consumers (Zhang, et al., 2020). Therefore, systematically analyzing the dynamic changes in meat formation at different growth stages of Jingyuan chickens is of great value for improving their meat quality. In this study, 42-, 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens were selected as the main observation stages, which are biologically significant. The 42-day-old stage marks the beginning of the rapid growth period of Jingyuan chickens, when muscle tissue growth and development are in a stage of rapid proliferation. At this stage, the meat quality of broilers is not yet completely stable, and the speed of muscle development has a significant impact on the formation of meat quality in the later stages. 126-day-old is the pre-sexual maturity stage, during which the growth of Jingyuan chicken muscles gradually slows down, fat deposition begins to appear, and the structure of muscle fibers tends to stabilize. This is a key node for studying muscle tissue maturation and meat quality changes (Yu, et al., 2024). At 180-day-old, the chickens reach the market-ready stage, with the development of skeletal, muscular, and adipose tissues nearing completion, and meat quality reaching its final stable state. This is an ideal time point for studying the meat quality characteristics and molecular mechanisms of broiler chickens during the maturation stage (Zhang, et al., 2023b; Zhao, et al., 2025).

With the rapid development of omics technologies, it has become possible to elucidate the genetic regulatory mechanisms underlying chicken meat quality (Wang, et al., 2023). Additionally, WGCNA analysis combined with multi-omics strategies can deeply explore key genes, metabolites, and metabolic pathways associated with meat quality (Zhai, et al., 2023). Therefore, based on the meat quality data of 42-, 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens, this study integrated transcriptome and metabolomics analysis and applied WGCNA to explore the key regulatory genes and metabolites of meat quality dynamics during the growth process of Jingyuan chickens. By exploring the key regulatory factors that affect the meat quality of Jingyuan chickens, we can clarify the molecular mechanisms of meat formation and provide new ideas and strategies for the genetic improvement of meat quality in Jingyuan chickens.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

Animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines for animal experimentation and were approved by the Animal Welfare Committee of Ningxia University (NXU-2024-067).

Animal sample collection

A total of 300 Jingyuan chicken hens of the three stages used in this study were obtained, from the Jingyuan Chicken National Conservation Farm (Pengyang, China). All chickens were raised under the same environmental conditions, with free access to feed and water. Jingyuan chickens enter a period of rapid growth at 42-day-old, and become sexually mature at 126-day-old, until they begin to be marketed at 180-day-old. Growth performance was measured at 42-, 126- and 180-day-old chickens. At the same time, 30 chickens were randomly selected at each age point, anesthetized with ether inhalation, killed by carotid artery bleeding, and slaughter performance was measured. One side of the breast was collected to measure meat quality, and the other side was quickly chopped up, quick-frozen with liquid nitrogen, and stored in a −80°C ultra-low temperature refrigerator for subsequent transcriptome and metabolome sequencing analysis.

Measurement of growth performance

At 42-, 126- and 180-day-old chickens, 30 Jingyuan chickens were randomly selected at each age point to measure their growth performance indicators, including body weight, oblique length, keel length, breast circumference, breast depth, breast breadth, breast angle, tibia length, tibia circumference, and width of pelvis. All measurements were conducted in accordance with the “Technical Specifications for Performance Testing of Broiler Chicken Breeds” (NY/T 828—2004).

Determination of slaughter performance

Slaughter performance is determined in accordance with NY/T 828—2004. The measurement indicators include body weight, slaughter weight, half-eviscerated weight, eviscerated weight, breast muscle weight, and leg muscle weight. Based on the above indicators, the following ratios are calculated: Slaughter rate = slaughter weight / body weight * 100 %, half-eviscerated rate = half-eviscerated weight / slaughter weight × 100 %, eviscerated rate = eviscerated weight / slaughter weight × 100 %, breast muscle rate = breast muscle weight / body weight × 100 %, and leg muscle rate = leg muscle weight / body weight × 100 %.

Meat quality assessment

Meat quality was assessed in 30 Jingyuan chickens, including pH, a*, b*, L*, intramuscular fat (IMF), crude protein, drip loss, water content, and crude ash. Among these, pH, a*, b*, L*, and IMF were introduced in the previous findings of our research group (Hu, et al., 2025). Crude protein content was determined using the Kjeldahl method (GB 5009.5 - 2016). Take 0.3 g of the sample and perform acid digestion (using copper sulfate pentahydrate, potassium sulfate anhydrous, and concentrated sulfuric acid), followed by distillation with a boric acid solution containing methyl red and bromothymol green, and then titrate with 0.01 mol/L hydrochloric acid. Crude protein content (%) = [(V − V₀) × C × 0.014 × 6.25] / m, where V and V₀ are the volumes of hydrochloric acid consumed by the sample and blank test, respectively, C is the concentration of hydrochloric acid, and m is the sample mass. All analyses were performed in triplicate, with blank corrections applied. Measure the drip loss of breast samples stored at 4°C for 48 h, record the initial weight (W1) and final weight (W2) before storage, and calculate W2 / W1 * 100 %. Determine the moisture content of meat samples using the direct drying method (GB 5009.3-2016). Pre-weighed petri dishes were dried at 105 ± 2°C until constant weight (W₁). Approximately 5 g of sample (W₂) was placed in the petri dish, dried in an oven at 105°C for 3 h, cooled in a desiccator, and reweighed (W₃). Moisture content (%) = (W₃ - W₁) / (W₂ - W₁) × 100 %. The method for determining crude ash content follows GB5009.4-2016. Place a clean crucible in a muffle furnace and heat it to a constant temperature. After cooling and lowering the temperature in a desiccator for 30 min, weigh it until the difference between the two weights is less than 0.0005 g, which is considered constant weight. Then weigh W1. Place approximately 3 g of meat sample into the crucible and weigh W2. After thorough carbonization on an electric furnace, place the crucible along with the meat sample in the muffle furnace for 4 h, then remove it and cool it in a desiccator for 30 min before weighing to obtain W3. The percentage of crude ash (%) = (W3 - W1) / (W2 - W1) × 100 %.

Transcriptome and metabolome data analysis

The transcriptome and metabolome data of 42-, 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chicken breasts used in this study were previously published by our research group (Hu, et al., 2025). RNA and metabolites were extracted from the collected breast samples after thawing on ice. We performed 2 × 150 bp paired-end sequencing (PE150) on the Illumina Novaseq™ 6000 (LC-Bio Technology CO., Ltd., China), yielding 32,364,054–51,272,360 clear reads. All reads were mapped to the chicken reference genome using the HISAT2-2.2.1 (https://daehwankimlab.github.io/hisat2/) software package (Kim et al., 2019), with mapping efficiency exceeding 85 %. Differential expression analysis was subsequently performed using the DESeq2 software. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using a threshold of fold change (FC) ≥ 2 or FC ≤ 1/2 (|log2fc| ≥ 1) and a q-value < 0.05. A total of 1,008 DEGs (576 up-regulated and 432 down-regulated) were identified in the 42- and 126-day-old groups. A total of 1,396 DEGs (820 up-regulated and 594 down-regulated) were identified in the 42- and 180-day-old groups, and a total of 696 DEGs (313 up-regulated and 283 down-regulated) were identified in the 126- and 180-day-old groups (Table S1). HPLC—HRMS analysis yielded 13,083 normalized clean metabolites. Differential metabolites (DEMs) were screened using the criteria of FC ≥ 1.5 or FC ≤ 1/1.5; VIP ≥ 1 and P < 0.05. A total of 1,390 DEMs (694 up-regulated and 696 down-regulated) were identified in the 42- and 126-day-old groups. A total of 1,726 DEMs (945 up-regulated and 781 down-regulated) were identified in the 42- and 180-day-old groups. A total of 1,527 DEMs (1,008 up-regulated and 519 down-regulated) were identified in the 126- and 180-day-old groups (Table S2).

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis

In this study, the OECloud platform (https://cloud.oebiotech.com) was used to analyze transcriptomic and metabolomic datasets. Genes with an FPKM < 1 were filtered out, and the WGCNA software package was used to construct the co-expression network. The gene co-expression matrix was created by calculating the correlation coefficients between genes. The optimal soft threshold was determined based on the scale-free topology criterion (β = 1–30), and a weighted adjacency matrix was generated. The Pearson correlation algorithm was applied to calculate the correlation coefficients and P-values between module characteristic genes and traits. Modules were selected based on a threshold of absolute correlation coefficient ≥ 0.3 and P-value < 0.05. For each trait-related module, the correlation between module gene expression levels and the corresponding trait (Gene Significance, GS) was calculated, along with the correlation between module gene expression levels and module characteristic genes (Eigengene). Genes with high connectivity (GS ≥ 0.2, module membership (MM) ≥ 0.8) in the corresponding module were considered hub genes. The top 50 genes with the highest connectivity in each module were selected to construct a scale-free network diagram to illustrate the relationships between these genes.

Functional enrichment analysis of hub genes and metabolites

To explore the functions of hub genes and metabolites that affect meat quality, we obtained modules with extremely strong positive correlations (R2 ≥ 0.7, P < 0.5) with phenotypic traits and then used the Cluster Profiler tool to perform http://geneontology.org (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG, https://biit.cs.ut.ee/gprofiler/convert). GO functional enrichment analysis was used to identify the biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components in which genes are involved. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was used to identify hub genes and metabolites enriched in metabolic pathways with P < 0.05.

Screening of key genes and metabolites related to meat quality

A Venn diagram was used to visualize the overlapping relationships between DEGs and hub genes, DEMs, and characteristic metabolites. Pearson correlation analysis was performed on the overlapping genes and metabolites mentioned above to screen for genes and metabolites with strong correlations (R2 ≥ 0.7). Cytoscape software (v3.8.0) was used to construct a gene-metabolite interaction network to visualize their potential regulatory relationships.

Statistical analysis

In this study, GraphPad Prism (v8.0.2) was used for statistical analysis and graphing of data. Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Student's one-way ANOVA was used to analyze differences in growth performance, slaughter performance, and meat quality at each period. P < 0.05 indicates a significant difference, P < 0.01 indicates a highly significant difference, and P < 0.001 indicates a very significant difference (ns, *, **, and *** represent P > 0.05, P < 0.05, P < 0.01, and P < 0.001, respectively).

Results

Growth performance of Jingyuan chickens at different ages

Growth performance is an important economic trait in poultry. Compared with 42-day-old chickens, 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens showed significantly higher body weight, oblique length, keel length, breast circumference, breast depth, breast breadth, breast angle, tibia length, tibia circumference, and width of pelvis (P < 0.01, Fig. 1).

Slaughter performance of Jingyuan chickens at different ages

Slaughter performance directly reflects the meat production capacity of broilers. In this study, 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens were significantly higher than 42-day-old chickens in terms of live weight, slaughter weight, half-eviscerated weight, eviscerated weight, breast muscle weight, leg muscle weight, breast muscle rate, and leg muscle rate (P < 0.05, Figs. 2A, B, C, D, E, F, J, K).However, the slaughter rate and half-eviscerated rate of 42-day-old chickens were significantly higher than those of 126- and 180-day-old chickens (P < 0.05, Figs. 2G, H), and the eviscerated rate of 126-day-old chickens was significantly higher than that of 42-day-old chickens (P < 0.01, Fig. 2I).

Meat quality of Jingyuan chickens at different stages

High-quality chicken meat is very popular among consumers. We measured the meat quality of Jingyuan chickens at different stages and found that crude protein, drip loss, and crude ash content showed a significant upward trend with increasing age (P < 0.05, Figs. 3A, B, D). In contrast, water content showed a downward trend with increasing age (P < 0.01, Fig. 3C).

Identification and functional analysis of hub genes related to meat quality in 42- and 126-day-old Jingyuan chickens

We performed WGCNA analysis on the transcriptome profiles of the breast muscles of 42- and 126-day-old Jingyuan chickens to identify key genes and signaling pathways related to meat quality. When β = 18, a weighted network co-expression model was established, and 2,834 genes were ultimately divided into 10 modules (Fig. 4A). We then performed a correlation analysis of eight traits (pH, a*, b*, L*, crude protein, crude ash, drip loss, and water content) with the co-expression modules (Fig. 4B). We found that pH and L* were significantly positively correlated with the greenyellow module, and water content was significantly positively correlated with the blue module (R2 ≥ 0.7, P < 0.01). Functional analysis of these two modules revealed that hub genes in the blue module were significantly enriched in glycine, serine and threonine metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, MAPK signaling pathway, alpha-linolenic acid metabolism, and linoleic acid metabolism (Fig. 4C). GO enrichment analysis showed that hub genes were significantly enriched in developmental process, signaling, growth, negative regulation of biological process, cytoskeletal motor activity, transcription regulator activity, and protein-containing complex (Fig. 4D). Hub genes in the greenyellow module participated in 120 metabolic pathways, with 106 significantly enriched. Among them, the pathways closely related to meat quality included PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, thermogenesis, arachidonic acid metabolism, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, fatty acid metabolism, and biosynthesis of amino acids (Fig. 4F). GO enrichment analysis showed that hub genes were significantly enriched in entries such as positive regulation of biological process, growth, antioxidant activity, and ATP-dependent activity (Fig. 4G). The top 50 hub genes with the highest connectivity in the blue and greenyellow modules were used to construct an interaction network (Fig. 4E, H). In addition, we performed a correlation analysis between the hub genes enriched in these two modules and DEGs to screen for overlapping genes that may regulate meat quality. We found that DEGs had 189 overlapping genes with the blue module and 71 overlapping genes with the greenyellow module (Fig. 4I, Table S3).

Identification and functional analysis of hub genes for meat quality in 42- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens

The transcriptome of the breast muscle of 42- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens contained a total of 23,789 genes. Genes with FPKM < 1 were filtered out, β = 14 was set, and finally 3,453 genes were divided into 11 modules (Fig. 5A). By calculating the correlation coefficients and P values of the module characteristic genes with eight traits, it was found that a*, L*, crude protein, crude ash, and drip loss were significantly positively correlated with the pink module, and water content was significantly positively correlated with the brown module (R2 ≥ 0.7, P < 0.01) (Fig. 5B). To further understand the functions of genes in the pink and brown modules, we performed KEGG enrichment analysis and found that the pathways significantly enriched with hub genes in the pink module included the MAPK signaling pathway, alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, AMPK signaling pathway, and FoxO signaling pathway (Fig. 5C). At the same time, GO entries significantly enriched in hub genes included cellular process, biological regulation, binding, and cellular anatomical entity (Fig. 5D). The hub genes in the brown module were significantly enriched in 80 pathways, among which the pathways related to meat quality included protein digestion and absorption, vitamin digestion and absorption, glutathione metabolism, and biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids (Fig. 5F). The GO entries enriched in hub genes included positive regulation of biological process, signaling, binding, ATP-dependent activity, and protein-containing complex (Fig. 5G). At the same time, the top 50 hub genes in the pink and brown modules were used to show the interaction network relationship (Fig. 5E, H). In order to further screen for genes that may regulate meat quality, we visualized the DEGs, pink module genes, and brown module hub genes using a Venn diagram, obtaining 274 and 59 overlapping genes, respectively (Fig. 5I, Table S4).

Identification and functional analysis of hub genes for meat quality in 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens

We then analyzed the gene modules significantly related to meat quality phenotypes and found that the magenta module was significantly positively correlated with L* (R2 ≥ 0.70, P < 0.01) (Fig. 6B). GO enrichment analysis showed that hub genes in the magenta module were enriched in cellular process, biological regulation, binding, cellular anatomical entity. (Fig. 6D). In addition, the top 50 hub genes in the magenta module in terms of connectivity were analyzed for interaction networks (Fig. 6E). We visualized the DEGs and hub genes in the magenta module using a Venn diagram and obtained 46 overlapping genes (Fig. 6F, Table S5).

Screening and functional analysis of key metabolites affecting meat quality in 42- and 126-day-old Jingyuan chickens

We analyzed the metabolite datasets of 42- and 126-day-old Jingyuan chicken breasts. When β = 9, the 6,172 metabolites were divided into 38 modules (Fig. 7A). We then used the Pearson correlation algorithm to calculate the correlations and P values between the feature modules and phenotypes. We found that pH, a*, L*, and crude ash were highly significantly positively correlated with the darkgrey and steelblue modules, drip loss was highly significantly positively correlated with the sienna3 module, and water content was highly significantly positively correlated with the blue, royalblue, and greenyellow modules (R² ≥ 0.7, P < 0.05) (Fig. 7B). KEGG enrichment analysis of the selected modules revealed that the characteristic metabolites of the darkgrey module were significantly enriched in histidine metabolism and beta-alanine metabolism (Fig. 7C). The characteristic metabolites of the steelblue module were significantly enriched in primary bile acid biosynthesis and bile secretion (Fig. 7D). The metabolites of the sienna3 module were significantly enriched in glycerophospholipid metabolism and vitamin B6 metabolism (Fig. 7E). The characteristic metabolites of the blue module were significantly enriched in the AMPK signaling pathway, longevity regulating pathway, oxidative phosphorylation, FoxO signaling pathway, cGMP-PKG signaling pathway, mTOR signaling pathway, and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway (Fig. 7F). The characteristic metabolites in the royalblue module were not enriched in any metabolic pathways. The characteristic metabolites in the greenyellow module were significantly enriched in tryptophan metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism, histidine metabolism, and beta-alanine metabolism (Fig. 7G). In addition, we analyzed the DEMs and characteristic metabolites of the darkgrey, steelblue, sienna3, blue, royalblue, and greenyellow modules and obtained 76, 58, 20, 353, 101, and 43 overlapping metabolites (Fig. 7H, Table S6).

Screening and functional analysis of key metabolites in the meat quality of 42- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens

Analysis of metabolome datasets from 42- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens revealed that when β was set to 12, there were 6,285 metabolites grouped into 36 modules (Fig. 8A). Module-trait association analysis showed that the magenta module was extremely significantly positively correlated with a*, b*, L*, crude protein, crude ash, and drip loss; the yellow module was extremely significantly positively correlated with crude ash and drip loss; the lightgreen module was extremely significantly positively correlated with b*; and the cyan module was extremely significantly positively correlated with water content (R² ≥ 0.7, P < 0.05) (Fig. 8B). KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that characteristic metabolites in the magenta module were significantly enriched in sphingolipid metabolism, sphingolipid signaling pathway, vitamin B6 metabolism, and thermogenesis (Fig. 8C). Characteristic metabolites in the yellow module were significantly enriched in thermogenesis, tyrosine metabolism, and autophagy (Fig. 8D). There were no enriched metabolic pathways for characteristic metabolites in the light green module. The pathways significantly enriched for characteristic metabolites in the cyan module included glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism, valine, leucine, and isoleucine biosynthesis, tryptophan metabolism, and purine metabolism protein digestion and absorption (Fig. 8E). At the same time, we analyzed the DEMs and characteristic metabolites in the magenta, yellow, lightgreen, and cyan modules and obtained 155, 136, 50, and 644 overlapping metabolites (Fig. 8F, Table S7).

Screening and functional analysis of key metabolites in the meat quality of 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens

Based on the metabolome data sets of 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens, we set the β value to 9 and divided 6,277 genes into 28 modules (Fig. 9A). Subsequently, we performed a correlation analysis between the modules and eight traits and found that b* was significantly positively correlated with the cyan module (R2 ≥ 0.7, P < 0.05) (Fig. 9B). Functional analysis of the characteristic metabolites in the cyan module revealed that they were significantly enriched in oxidative phosphorylation, nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, vitamin digestion and absorption, AMPK signaling pathway, longevity regulating pathway, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, and citrate cycle (TCA cycle) (Fig. 9C). We further performed a correlation analysis of DEMs and characteristic metabolites in the cyan module and screened out 97 overlapping metabolites (Fig. 9D, Table S8).

Screening of key candidate genes and metabolites related to meat quality

We divided all overlapping genes in the 42- and 126-day-old, 42- and 180-day-old, and 126- and 180-day-old groups into 12 clusters. Among them, the gene expression levels in Cluster 5 and Cluster 11 were low at 42-days-old, increased at 126-days-old, and peaked at 180-day-old. Cluster 5 contains five overlapping genes, including small nucleolar RNA SNORD14 (SNORD14), interleukin 7 (IL7), 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydratase 4 (HACD4), beaded filament structural protein 1 (BFSP1), and B-cell lymphoma 6 (BCL6). There are three overlapping genes in Cluster 11, including family with sequence similarity 13 member A (FAM13A), ankyrin repeat domain 2 (ANKRD2), and alanine-glyoxylate aminotransferase 2 (AGXT2). These genes may play an important role in meat quality during the middle and late stages of chicken growth (Fig. 10A). Similarly, we performed Mfuzz analysis on the overlapping metabolites obtained from the three comparison groups and found that the abundance of metabolites in Cluster 5 showed an upward trend with increasing age, including p-Cresol sulfate, 2-Piperidinone, 4-Hydroxyquinoline, Succinic acid, and Dihydroxyacetone phosphate (Fig. 10B). To understand the function of key candidate genes and metabolites, we performed KEGG enrichment analyses and AGXT2 and Succinic acid were co-enriched in alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism. aspartate and glutamate metabolism, BCL6 and IL7 were enriched in the FoxO signaling pathway, and HACD4 was enriched in fatty acid metabolism. succinic acid and dihydroxyacetone phosphate were enriched in propanoate metabolism, nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, carbon metabolism, biosynthesis of secondary metabolites and biosynthesis of antibiotics (Fig. 10C). In addition, we clustered the correlations of eight key candidate genes and five metabolites and found that AGXT2, FAM13A, SNORD14, BCL6, HACD4, BFSP1, and IL7 were associated with dihydroxyacetone phosphate, succinic acid, and 2-Piperidinone with showed a significant positive correlation (P < 0.05). AGXT2, FAM13A, SNORD14, and HACD4 showed significant positive correlations with 4-hydroxyquinoline (P < 0.05). AGXT2, FAM13A, SNORD14, BCL6, and BFSP1 were significantly positively correlated with p-cresol sulfate (P < 0.05; Fig. 10D).

Discussion

The performance of chickens is an important economic trait, encompassing growth performance, slaughter characteristics, and meat quality (Gai et al., 2023; Ma, et al., 2023). Jingyuan chickens exhibited rapid growth between 42 and 126-day-old, but this growth trend gradually slowed between 126- and 180-day-old. This aligns with previous studies suggesting that 126-day-old marks an inflection point for growth stabilisation in Jingyuan chickens (Yu, et al., 2023b). Meanwhile, 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens exhibited higher meat yield and quality compared to those at 42-day-old. In particular, the crude protein and ash content increased gradually with age. This suggests that the protein and mineral content of chicken meat increase cumulatively as the birds mature, and the texture, flavor, and nutritional content of the meat stabilise at 180-day-old. This makes it an ideal time to study the meat characteristics and molecular mechanisms at the maturity stage of broilers.

Genes determine the genetic characteristics of an animal, which in turn affect its growth and development, muscle structure, and fat deposition (Chen, et al., 2019; Luo, et al., 2024; Tan, et al., 2024). In this study, we analyzed the transcriptome profiles of breast muscle from 42- and 126-day-old Jingyuan chickens using WGCNA. We found that the greenyellow module was significantly and positively correlated with pH and L* values, while the blue module was significantly correlated with water content. These findings suggest that genes in the greenyellow module may play a role in controlling muscle pH and L*, while genes in the blue module may be involved in regulating water content. Further functional enrichment analysis revealed that hub genes were significantly enriched in pathways related to glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, the MAPK signaling pathway, and alpha-linolenic and linoleic acid metabolism. These pathways are known to affect meat quality by regulating muscle energy metabolism and antioxidant activity (Zhang, et al., 2022). Additionally, we analyzed the transcriptome profiles of breast muscle from 42- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chickens and identified that the pink module was significantly correlated with a*, L*, crude protein, crude ash, and drip loss. In contrast, the brown module was positively correlated with water content. Hub genes in the pink module were significantly correlated with a*, L*, crude protein, crude ash, and drip loss, while the brown module's hub genes were associated with water content. Further analysis indicated that the involved pathways—such as alanine, aspartate, and glutamate metabolism, AMPK signaling pathway, FoxO signaling pathway, and glutathione metabolism—suggest that this module may regulate muscle metabolism, antioxidant responses, and other processes, ultimately influencing water content and other meat quality characteristics (Li, et al., 2022b; Zhang, et al., 2023a). The FoxO signaling pathway, in particular, plays an essential role in meat texture formation, muscle cell hypertrophy and atrophy, and the regulation of muscle growth and meat quality, with FoxO1, FoxO3, and FoxO4 being widely involved (Li, et al., 2022c; Qi, et al., 2024). Moreover, the magenta module showed a highly significant positive correlation with L* value, and hub genes in this module were enriched in key signaling pathways, including the MAPK signaling pathway, Wnt signaling pathway, and GnRH signaling pathway. These pathways are known to play crucial roles in muscle development, meat quality formation, and stress responses (Xiao, et al., 2024a; Yu, et al., 2023c).

Metabolites, as end products of biological processes, play an important role in the formation of meat quality (Li, et al., 2022b). In this study, metabolites associated with meat quality characteristics were identified by analysing the metabolite dataset of 42-, 126- and 180-day-old Jingyuan chicken breast muscles, and these characteristic metabolites affected the meat quality of chicken breast muscles through different metabolic pathways. Firstly, there were significant positive correlations between meat quality traits such as pH, a*, L* and crude ash with darkgrey and steelblue modules at 42- and 126-day-old, suggesting that the characteristic metabolites in these modules may play an important role in influencing meat acidity, color and nutrient composition. Meanwhile, the characteristic metabolites were significantly enriched in histidine metabolism and beta-Alanine metabolism pathways, which may have key roles in muscle metabolism, antioxidant stress response, as well as muscle quality and palatability (Chen, et al., 2023). Histidine metabolism facilitates the regulation of muscle quality and pH by modulating the acid-base balance in tissues (Baldi, et al., 2021). Metabolites from the steelblue module are enriched in primary bile acid biosynthesis and bile secretion, and may be associated with fat metabolism and bile acid synthesis and secretion, and these processes may affect the fatty acid composition of meat and its organoleptic properties (Cao, et al., 2024b; Miao, et al., 2025). The magenta module was significantly and positively correlated with several meat quality traits (a*, b*, L*, crude protein, crude ash, and drip loss) at 42- and 180-day-old chickens. Characteristic metabolites were significantly enriched in sphingolipid metabolism and thermogenesis pathways, which may further regulate meat water absorption and firmness by influencing lipid metabolism and thermogenesis (Tian, et al., 2025). Furthermore, vitamin B6 metabolism may affect muscle energy metabolism, which in turn affects muscle texture and function (He, et al., 2022). Characteristic metabolites of the cyan module are significantly enriched in key metabolic pathways such as glycine, serine and threonine metabolism, purine metabolism, and glycolysis / gluconeogenesis, which have been shown to be key pathways in the regulation of meat quality (Hilliar, et al., 2019; Liu, et al., 2021; Yu, et al., 2022). In addition, metabolites of the cyan module are also significantly enriched in the oxidative phosphorylation and AMPK signaling pathways, which play key roles in the regulation of cellular energy metabolism and redox, and may affect the fibre type of the muscle, and thus the texture and flavour of the meat (Zhao, et al., 2024a). For the comparative analysis between 126- and 180-day-old, a significant positive correlation was found between the cyan module and b*. Further functional analyses revealed that the characteristic metabolites were significantly enriched in the pathways of nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, and vitamin digestion and absorption. These metabolites may influence meat quality formation by regulating vitamin absorption and utilisation (Zhao, et al., 2025).

In this study, we screened key candidate genes and metabolites related to meat quality by Mfuzz analysis, and we found that the expression of genes in Cluster 5 and Cluster 11 showed different dynamics as the chicken aged. This expression pattern suggests that these genes may be closely associated with changes in meat quality of chickens in the middle and late stages. SNORD14, a small nuclear RNA gene, has been shown to play an important role in RNA splicing and gene expression regulation of RNA splicing and gene expression, suggesting that it may play a regulatory role in muscle growth (Weber, et al., 2006). IL7 and HACD4 are associated with fatty acid metabolism, and both of them influence the fat content of the meat quality through the regulation of fat accumulation (O'Connell, et al., 2023; Shi, et al., 2024). It has been shown that BCL6 acts as a downstream effector molecule of theophyllin-gut flora interactions and regulates lipid metabolism through the FoxO and PPAR signalling pathways (Xiao, et al., 2024b). Interestingly, FAM13A has been reported to be negatively associated with fat deposition by inhibiting goat intramuscular adipocyte differentiation through the RIG - I receptor signalling pathway, which in turn reduces fat deposition (Li, et al., 2024). This is in contrast to the results of the present study, and we hypothesise that the difference is due to species. ANKRD2, as a muscle-specific gene, has been shown to have an important role in meat quality and fat metabolism (Li, et al., 2022a). In addition, it has been shown that elevated AGXT2 expression levels and enzyme activity lead to increased dimethylguanidinovaleric acid concentrations, which in turn reflects increased hepatic steatosis (Rodionov, et al., 2024). Cluster analysis of metabolites showed that the levels of metabolites (p-Cresol sulfate, 2-Piperidinone, 4-Hydroxyquinoline, Succinic acid, and Dihydroxyacetone phosphate) in Cluster 5 tended to increase with age. Studies have shown that exogenous addition of Succinic acid improves muscle antioxidant capacity and alters amino acid and fatty acid composition to improve muscle mass (Cao, et al., 2024a). Up-regulation of serum Dihydroxyacetone phosphate affected the expression of genes related to triglyceride metabolism and enhanced lipid droplet storage and fatty acid synthesis (Yao, et al., 2025). However, the effects of 4-Hydroxyquinoline and 2-Piperidinone on livestock and poultry meat quality have not been reported. By analyzing the correlation between eight key candidate genes and five metabolites, we found significant positive correlations between several genes and metabolites, especially in the correlation between AGXT2, FAM13A, SNORD14, BCL6, HACD4, BFSP1, and IL7 and Dihydroxyacetone phosphate, Succinic acid, and 2-Piperidinone. The close association between these key genes and metabolites suggests a collaborative role in regulating chicken meat quality. In particular, changes in the abundance of Succinic acid and Dihydroxyacetone phosphate were closely correlated with changes in the expression of several genes, further supporting their important roles in meat quality development (Cao et al., 2024a). Despite these findings, this study has several limitations. Although we explored the changing patterns of breast genes and metabolites at important developmental stages of Jingyuan chickens and screened out key candidate genes and metabolites that may affect meat quality, their specific biological mechanisms and interactions need to be further verified. In the future, we will further validate the roles of key candidate genes and metabolites in meat quality formation at the cellular level and in animal models, with the aim of providing potential molecular markers and targets for meat quality improvement.

Conclusions

In this study, we integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic data to explore the key regulators involved in the regulation of meat quality in Jingyuan chicken at different growth stages using the WGCNA method. The results showed that several gene modules in the transcriptome profile were significantly correlated with and positively associated with meat quality indexes, involving important pathways such as the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, glutathione metabolism, and the FoxO signaling pathway. In addition, metabolomic data analysis revealed that metabolites were differentially enriched in histidine metabolism, primary bile acid biosynthesis, and vitamin B6 metabolism pathways at different growth stages. Eight key genes (SNORD14, IL7, HACD4, BFSP1, BCL6, FAM13A, ANKRD2, AGXT2) and five key metabolites (p-Cresol sulfate, 2-Piperidinone, 4-Hydroxyquinoline, Succinic acid, Dihydroxyacetone phosphate) were identified. In the future, these key regulators will be potential targets for enhancing meat quality during breeding.

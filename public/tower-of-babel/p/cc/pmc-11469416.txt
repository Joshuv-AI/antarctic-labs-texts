Epidemiological analysis of influenza vaccination coverage in Pudong New Area, Shanghai (2013-2023): Implications for influenza vaccination strategies
Authors: Pengfei Deng, Caoyi Xue, Tian Yang, Bo Zheng, Wenmin Liu, Laibao Yang, Yi Fei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSeasonal influenza remains a significant public health concern globally, with annual vaccinations as the most effectively preventive measure. This study examines influenza vaccination coverage rates across different age groups in Pudong New Area, Shanghai, China, from 2013 to 2023. This study extracted influenza vaccination data from the Shanghai Immunization Planning Information System (SIPIS) of the Center for Disease Control and Prevention Shanghai’s Pudong New Area from 2013 to 2023. The analysis utilized weighted linear regression to compare vaccination rates over the study period. From 2013 to 2023, a cumulative total of 1,421,295 influenza vaccinations were administered in Pudong New Area, with the quadrivalent inactivated influenza vaccines (IIV4) and trivalent inactivated influenza vaccines (IIV3) comprising 56.8% and 42.9%, respectively. The overall average influenza vaccine coverage rate was 2.27% (95% CI: 2.26, 2.28). The 0–4 years group exhibited the highest average annual coverage rate of 22.52% (95% CI: 22.47, 22.57) among different age groups, in stark contrast to that of the 20–24 years age group, which had the lowest at 0.32% (95% CI: 0.31, 0.33). In terms of repeat vaccinations, a significant majority (86.87%) of recipients received only 1–2 doses, while just 13.13% received 3 or more doses. Although influenza vaccination coverage among preschool children in Pudong New Area is relatively high, it falls significantly short of WHO recommendations. Enhance the level of awareness of influenza vaccine among adults and provide a free influenza vaccination strategy for specific groups such as doctors, which is helpful to increase influenza vaccination rates among populations.KEYWORDS: Influenza, annual vaccination, coverage rate, repeat doses

Introduction

Influenza is a highly contagious acute respiratory infection caused by influenza viruses, posing a significant threat to global public health.1 The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that seasonal influenza results in approximately one billion infections and causes between 290,000 and 650,000 respiratory-related deaths annually.2,3 Certain populations, including children, pregnant women, healthcare workers, and elderly individuals with chronic conditions, are particularly vulnerable to influenza.4,5 In China, the incidence of influenza-like illnesses averages 2.5 excess cases per 1,000 person-years each year.6 Furthermore, a study conducted in China revealed that influenza-related respiratory illnesses contribute to approximately 71,000 deaths each year among individuals aged 60 years or older, accounting for 80% of excess mortality.7

Vaccination against influenza is recognized as the most effective strategy for preventing infection and reducing the associated burden of severe illness and death.8 Both the WHO and the Chinese Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recommended seasonal influenza vaccination for all individuals aged six months and older, provided there are no contraindications.9,10 However, influenza vaccines are not included in China’s national immunization program, and only a limited number of cities offer free or subsidized vaccines to elderly individuals and young children. Consequently, influenza vaccination remains largely self-funded across most regions in China.11 Despite the recognized importance of vaccination, the influenza vaccination coverage in China remains alarmingly low, with only 2.47% to 3.16% of the general population receiving the vaccine.12 This is far below the target recommended by the WHO.13

The seasonal epidemic period of influenza viruses in Pudong New Area are exhibited semiannual periodicity from 2022 to 2023.14 However, the overall influenza vaccination coverage rate in Pudong New Area remains lower than that observed in many developed countries.15 Located in the eastern part of Shanghai, Pudong New Area is the largest district in China in terms of economic development, population density, and geographic area. However, it does not offer a free vaccination program, requiring residents to obtain influenza vaccines at their own expense during the flu season at local vaccination clinics.

There is a lack of studies related to influenza vaccination coverage among specific age groups in the region over the last 10 years. A significant gap exists in the current literature regarding influenza vaccination coverage across specific age groups in Pudong New Area, particularly over the last decade. Understanding these age-specific trends is crucial for developing targeted public health interventions that can increase vaccination rates and reduce the burden of influenza in this region. This study seeks to fill this gap by providing a comprehensive analysis of age-specific vaccination coverage from 2013 to 2023, contributing valuable data that can guide future research and inform more effective vaccination strategies tailored to different age groups.

Materials and methods

Data source

Data on influenza vaccinations were extracted from the Shanghai Immunization Planning Information System (SIPIS) of the Pudong CDC, covering the period from January 2013 to December 2023. Due to an upgrade of the vaccination system, data for 2024 could not be included. The dataset encompasses a comprehensive demographic profile, including variables such as sex, date of birth, residential address, immigration status, and type of vaccination. This dataset accounts for a total of 1,421,295 recorded influenza vaccinations, which includes multiple doses for some individuals. The population data critical to our analysis were sourced from the Shanghai Pudong New Area 2013 to 2023 statistical yearbook, focusing on key indicators such as age distribution and immigration status. Personal records were anonymized, encrypted, and securely stored, with all researchers adhering to confidentiality agreements. The study was conducted in compliance with ethical standards and received approval from the ethical review board of the Shanghai Pudong New Area CDC (Approval number: PDCDCLL-20220801-001). For the purposes of this study, vaccine recipients were categorized into 18 distinct age groups, each with 5-year intervals.

Statistical analysis

Influenza vaccination coverage, defined as the receipt of at least one dose, was calculated by comparing the vaccinated population against the total population of Pudong New Area over the study period. The vaccination coverage rate was determined by dividing the yearly number of vaccinated individuals by the total population size for that year, with calculations performed separately for each age group and year. The 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) for the coverage rates across various age groups and years were computed using the formula: (p−Zα/2Sp,p+Zα/2Sp), where Zα/2 = 1.96 and Sp is the standard deviation. For each year, we assessed the influenza vaccination coverage rate on a per-age-group basis and tallied the frequency of repeat influenza vaccine doses per recipient from 2013 to 2023. Tests for linear trends across periods (2013/2014 to 2023) were conducted using weighted linear regression, with the period number as the independent variable and the inverse of the estimated variance of the influenza vaccination coverage rate as weights. We adopted the x2 test to examine whether the repeat influenza vaccination among influenza vaccine recipients significantly differed across the subgroups. These demographic and epidemiological characteristics were also compared to evaluate significant variations in vaccination coverage across subgroups. Data were organized and analyzed using Microsoft Excel 2020, and all statistical analyses were performed using R 4.1.1 software (Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Statistical significance was set at a two-sided p-value <.05.

Results

Characteristics of the study population

Between 2013 and 2023, a total of 1,421,295 influenza vaccine doses were administered, averaging 129,209 doses per year. The vaccinated population exhibited a wide age range, from as young as 0.5 years to as old as 98 years, with a median age of 6.00 (IQR: 3.0, 36.0) years. The sex distribution revealed that 678,535 (47.8%) doses were administered to males and that 742,207 (52.2%) doses were administered to females. In terms of immigration status, 863,223 (60.7%) doses were given to residents, whereas 558,072 (39.3%) were given to migrants, indicating a higher vaccination rate among residents. 807,500 (56.8%) doses were of the quadrivalent inactivated influenza vaccines (IIV4), while trivalent inactivated influenza vaccines (IIV3) accounted for 609,852 vaccinations (42.9%), and the more expensive and recently introduced live attenuated influenza vaccine (LAIV) and subunit vaccines accounted for a mere 3943 (0.3%) doses. The seasonal distribution revealed a peak in the number of vaccinations during autumn, with 1,030,783 (72.5%) doses, followed by winter, with 355,396 (25.0%) doses. The 0–4 years group received the most doses (38.9%), followed by the 5–9 years group (22.9%), and the majority of the doses (68.2%) were administered to individuals under 19 years of age, with 253,296 doses (17.8%) in the 20–59 years group and 198,996 doses (14.0%) in the group aged 60 years or older (Table 1).

Regarding the distribution of the daily influenza vaccination rates from January 2013 to December 2023, more than 10,000 doses were administered on only 2 days, whereas 5,000 to 9,999 doses were administered on 17 days. A significant variation in the daily number of influenza vaccinations was observed, 1,000 to 4,999 doses were administered on 395 days, 500 to 999 doses on 374 days, 100 to 499 doses on 612 days, 10 to 99 doses on 757 days, and fewer than 10 doses on a substantial 1,861 days (46.32% of the observed period). The monthly distribution revealed a marked concentration of vaccinations in the latter half of the year, with September to December accounting for 88.9% of all vaccinations, peaking in October with 456,596 (32.1%) doses, followed by November with 361,912 (25.5%) doses, December with 233,655 (16.4%) doses, and September with 212,275 (14.9%) doses (Figure 1).

Influenza vaccination coverage stratified by age groups

The annual influenza vaccine coverage in Pudong New Area saw a gradual ascent from 2013 to 2023 influenza seasons, starting at 0.64% (95% CI: 0.63, 0.65) in 2013/2014 and peaking at 5.94% (95% CI: 5.92, 5.96) in 2020/2021 (Table 2). Notably, in 2020/2021 (5.94%), 2023 (3.82%), 2021/2022 (3.27%), 2019/2020 (3.09%), and 2022/2023 (2.78%), each coverage rate was above 2%, culminating in an average annual influenza vaccine coverage rate of 2.27% (95% CI: 2.26, 2.28). The top three average annual influenza coverage rates for each age group were 22.52% (95% CI: 22.47, 22.57) in the 0–4 years group, 11.53% (95% CI: 11.49, 11.57) in the 5–9 years group, and 3.43% (95% CI: 3.40, 3.46) in the 10–14 years group; the lowest three coverage rates were 0.32% (95% CI: 0.31, 0.33) in the 20–24 years group, 0.46% (95% CI: 0.45, 0.47) in the ≥85 years group; and 0.51% (95% CI: 0.50, 0.52) in the 50–54 years group. Influenza coverage in the 0–4 years group was 70.38 times greater than that in the 20–24 years group. The 65–69 years group demonstrated the highest coverage rate among the groups aged 60 years or older, reaching 2.70% (95% CI: 2.66, 2.74) in 2020, whereas the 0–4 years group achieved the highest coverage rate of 48.03% (95% CI: 47.76, 48.30) in the same year among the groups aged 19 years or younger (Figure 2). These findings underscore the need for targeted strategies to increase vaccination rates across all age groups.

Analysis of the repeat influenza vaccination patterns

Among individuals who received repeat influenza vaccine doses, a substantial majority, 1,095,759 individuals (77.10%), received only 1 dose, whereas 138,897 (9.77%) received 2 doses and a small proportion received three to six doses or more (13.13%). This indicates a general trend of minimal repeat vaccination, with 86.87% of the recipients receiving 2 or fewer doses and only 13.13% receiving 3 or more doses. In terms of vaccine packaging preferences, 141,225 (85.97%) of recipients received 1 dose of a prefilled syringe vaccine, 150,154 (14.03%) received 2 or more doses of a prefilled syringe vaccine, 963,908 (76.68%) received 1 dose of a celine-bottled influenza vaccine, and 29,311 (23.32%) received 2 or more doses of a prefilled syringe vaccine (see Table 3 for detailed distribution).

Discussion

In China, the average annual number of influenza-induced hospitalizations for severe acute respiratory infections is estimated at 2.34 million, with excess repiratory disease deaths reaching 90,000 annually.16 The patients with tuberculosis and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) are susceptible to influenza, and if they be co-infected with influenza and other respiratory diseases that may be increased risk of morbidity and mortality.17,18 The annual national economic loss caused by influenza amounts to 26.3 billion yuan, of which hospitalization accounts for more than 86% of the economic burden.19 Influenza vaccination has been shown to be one of the most effective measures for preventing seasonal influenza.20 Influenza vaccination can effectively reduce the incidence of laboratory-confirmed influenza, absenteeism and lost workdays and might provide an underlying indirect benefit to the general population.21 However, the influenza vaccine is not included in the National Immunization Program in China, and there are no influenza vaccination exemptions or subsidies for priority populations, such as elderly individuals and healthcare workers. Therefore, people need to pay for the influenza vaccine themselves. Shanghai is a seaport city with a high economic level, and over 500,000 doses of influenza vaccines are administered per season, but there is a lack of data on age-specific influenza vaccination coverage over the past 10 years.22 Therefore, the aim of this study was to investigate the influenza vaccination coverage rates of different age groups in Pudong New Area from 2013 to 2023 and provide a scientific basis for the promotion of influenza vaccination for different age groups and the development of vaccination policies to improve the influenza vaccination coverage rate of the total population.

In this study, despite the 5.97-fold increase in the influenza vaccination coverage rate from 2013/2014 to 2023 in Pudong New Area, the average annual influenza vaccination coverage rate was only 2.27%, which is still very low. The rate ranged from 0.64% to 5.94% overall and was much lower than the targeted rates in other high-income and upper-middle-income countries.23,24 Although Pudong New Area is one of the more economically and culturally advanced cities in China, the average annual influenza vaccine coverage rate is only 2.27%. Low rate of coverage is the result of a variety of factors, including low education level of the public, poor awareness of the influenza vaccine, negative influenza vaccine news, and the absence of a free policy.25,26 This suggests that there is a need for effective strategic interventions, such as popularizing residents’ knowledge about influenza vaccine through television and WeChat, publicizing influenza prevention and control measures during the influenza season, and implementing a free influenza vaccination policy for specific groups such as doctors and teachers, to increase the influenza vaccination coverage rate. This study revealed that the influenza vaccination coverage rate was highest in 2020/2021 but then declined rapidly, which may be related to the start of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. The rapid increase in the influenza vaccination coverage rate in 2020 may be related to people being concerned about contracting COVID-19 by seeking medical treatment or hospitalization for influenza infection. At the same time, the population’s susceptibility to viral infection is influenced by public health messaging during the pandemic, leading to an increase in the population’s motivation for influenza vaccination in 2020.27 Influenza vaccination coverage declines during the 2021–2022 due to the impact of working from home and isolation during COVID-19 pandemic, and vaccine hesitancy as a result of negative vaccine events.28,29 This may be related to pandemic influenza and frequent reports of more severe clinical symptoms of influenza in 2023, as residents may fear infection, leading to another increase in the influenza vaccination coverage rate.30 This study indicated that the low influenza vaccination coverage rate in Pudong New Area does not establish an effective immune barrier and that there is a possibility of influenza outbreaks in local areas. Therefore, we should continue to strengthen influenza vaccination coverage in Pudong New Area.

This study demonstrated significant variation in influenza vaccine coverage rate by age group in Pudong New Area, with the influenza vaccine coverage rate among individuals aged 19 years and younger being 9.76 times higher than that for those aged 60 years or older. Factors such as vaccination policies, vaccine hesitancy, attitudes toward vaccination, and levels of awareness may all be contribute to the low influenza vaccine coverage rate across the population.26,31 This study revealed that the influenza vaccination coverage rate was highest in the 0–4 years age group, which was 9.92 times higher than the average annual influenza coverage rate but still lower than the 75% target vaccination rate recommended by the WHO.32 This study revealed that the group aged 60 years or older had the highest influenza vaccine coverage rate of only 2.70%. The differences in influenza coverage with certain age groups may be due to socioeconomic factors and public health policies. Studied indicated that people who received information through TV(OR = 1.40) and doctor referrals(OR = 2.65) were more likely to get the influenza vaccine than those who did not receive information.33 Studied also indicated that 31.5% of the older people were willing to get vaccinated if the costs were covered by medical insurance.25

Studied have indicated that the influenza vaccine coverage rate among individuals aged 60 years or older has increased to 38.7%, with the total cost savings by providing free influenza vaccination to people aged 60 years and above in Beijing would amount to over 1.7 million.34 Additionally, research have suggested that the reduction in influenza cases ranges from 0.73 to 3.18 cases per thousand additional childhood vaccination doses, and influenza vaccination coverage should be further increased to reduce the risk of influenza in key populations, such as children and elderly individuals.35 In this study, only 13.13% of the recipients received three or more doses of the influenza vaccine, indicating that most of the residents were vaccinated occasionally, with a relatively a small proportion consistently receiving the vaccine at their own expense. Moreover, this study found that the majority of recipients who received the influenza vaccine multiple times chose the influenza vaccine in celine-bottles, which might be related to the lower price of these influenza vaccines. A meta-analysis has highlighted that vaccine price and vaccination policy are critical factors influencing the population’s decision to receive the influenza vaccination.36 Other studies have reported that combining interventions, including awareness campaigns, incentives, affordability efforts, or expanding vaccination sites, significantly improves the recipients’ motivation to get vaccinated.36,37

There were several limitations in this study. First, our data may have slightly underestimated vaccination rates, as not all influenza vaccinations were reported, and some individuals may have received influenza vaccines in other provinces, preventing us from accessing their complete vaccination records. Secondly, the influenza vaccine coverage for the 2023/2024 influenza season may be underestimated due to the absence of data on vaccinations administered in early 2024, which resulted from an upgrade of the vaccination system. Thirdly, incomplete registration of early influenza vaccination information led to missing sex information for some subjects, which may introduce a potential source of information bias.

Conclusions

This study quantitatively determined the influenza vaccination coverage rates for specific age groups in Pudong New Area, Shanghai, for the first time. Despite global recommendations for influenza vaccination during the flu season, our findings reveal that the average annual influenza vaccination coverage rate in Pudong New Area is alarmingly low at only 2.27%, with significant variations in coverage across different age groups. These results underscore the urgent need for a comprehensive strategy to boost vaccination rates. Such a strategy should include science-based education and public awareness campaigns, as well as the provision of free vaccines for targeted populations, to improve influenza vaccination coverage in Pudong New Area.

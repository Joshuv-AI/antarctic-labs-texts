Solitary phospholipase C β2-positive cells in the vomeronasal organs of Korean roe deer (Capreolus pygargus)
Authors: Jeongtae KIM, Kyungsook JUNG, Irene ORTIZ-LEAL, Pablo SANCHEZ-QUINTEIRO, Meejung AHN, Taekyun SHIN
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLittle is known about the vomeronasal organ (VNO), which is associated with social behaviors through pheromone detection in mammals, particularly ungulates. We investigated the distribution of phospholipase C beta 2 (PLCβ2), a marker of solitary chemosensory cells (SCCs), in the VNO of the Korean roe deer (Capreolus pygargus). PLCβ2-positive cells were detected in both the sensory and non-sensory epithelium of the VNO, and resembled the bipolar cells. PLCβ2-positive cells colocalized with choline acetyltransferase, and Lens culinaris agglutinin. Furthermore, PLCβ2-positive cells were surrounded with trigeminal nerve fibers with calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP)-positive immunoreaction. The results indicate that the VNO of the Korean roe deer exhibits a distinct distribution of SCCs, which may be involved in detecting risk factors such as harmful substances.Keywords: Korean roe deer, phospholipase C β2, solitary chemoreceptor cell, vomeronasal organ

The olfactory organ in mammals is classified into two distinct organs: the main and accessory organs [16]. The accessory organ, also known as the vomeronasal organ (VNO), typically consists of the vomeronasal sensory epithelium (VSE), vomeronasal non-sensory epithelium (VNSE), vomeronasal glands (VGs), vomeronasal nerves, and blood vessels. This structure is present in various mammals, including the horse [9], roe deer [10, 12], and capybara [14]. The VNO plays a key role in social behaviors such as reproduction and maternal instinct through pheromone detection [16].

Phospholipase C beta 2 (PLCβ2) catalyzes the formation of inositol 1,4,5-triphosphate (InsP3) and diacylglycerol from phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate [12]. Furthermore, PLCβ2 is closely associated with chemo-sensing transduction, including processes involving G-protein-coupled receptors, guanine nucleotide-binding protein alpha transducing 3, and InsP3 [13]. PLCβ2-positive cells have been detected in various tissues, such as the rodent airway [13], intestinal tufted cells [11], camel respiratory mucosa [4], and sheep VNO [5]. These chemo-sensing cells in the epithelium are referred to as solitary chemosensory cells (SCCs) and were first reported in the gill and skin of fish [15]. In the nasal epithelium of mice and rats, SCCs contacts the trigeminal afferent nerve fibers, and serves as sentinels of respiration by chemosensory transduction [1].

Capreolus pygargus (C. pygargus), the Korean roe deer, is an ungulate that inhabits the mountainous areas and grasslands of Halla Mountain and shares a similar genetic identity with the Siberian deer [12]. In previous studies, we reported the characteristics of the VNO, chemosensory olfactory mucosae, and gamma-aminobutyric acid transmission in the olfactory bulbs of the Korean roe deer using lectin histochemistry and immunohistochemistry [7, 8, 10, 12]. However, little is known about the distribution of SCCs in the VNO of wild ungulates such as the Korean roe deer. In the present study, we investigated the presence of PLCβ2-positive cells in the VNO of C. pygargus through immunohistochemical staining.

VNO tissues from mature male Korean roe deer (n=3, 2–3 years old) were obtained from the Jeju Wildlife Rescue Center (Jeju City, Republic of Korea) and immediately preserved in 4% paraformaldehyde in phosphate-buffered saline. The age was determined based on the number of horn branches, as previously described [10]. All study protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Jeju National University (Permission number: 2017-0020).

Paraformaldehyde-fixed VNO tissues were decalcified using a sodium citrate/formic acid solution until the bone tissue softened, as previously described [10]. The decalcified VNOs were embedded in paraffin wax using the HistoCore Arcadia Embedding System (Leica, Nussloch, Germany). Sections with a thickness of 5 μm were deparaffinized and processed for histological examination, including hematoxylin and eosin staining, immunohistochemistry, and double immunofluorescence staining.

Immunohistochemistry was performed using the Vectastain Elite ABC Kit (Vector Laboratories, Burlingame, CA, USA), as previously described [12]. Briefly, the sections were incubated with rabbit polyclonal anti-PLCβ2 antibody (1:1,000; sc-9018; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA) after blocking with 10% (v/v) normal goat serum. Then the sections were treated with biotinylated goat anti-rabbit immunoglobulin G as the secondary antibody, and peroxidase activity was visualized using the DAB substrate kit (Vector Laboratories).

To investigate the phenotype of PLCβ2-positive cells, double immunofluorescence staining was performed as previously described [12]. The sections were incubated with rabbit anti-PLCβ2 or mouse anti-PLCβ2 (1:200; sc-515912; Santa Cruz) antibodies at 4°C overnight after blocking, followed by tetramethylrhodamine isothiocyanate (TRITC)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit immunoglobulin G (dilution 1:50; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) or TRITC-conjugated rabbit anti-mouse immunoglobulin G (dilution 1:50; Sigma-Aldrich). The sections were also incubated with primary antibodies, including goat anti-choline acetyltransferase (ChAT; AB144P, Abcam, London, UK), rabbit anti-calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP; C8198, Sigma-Aldrich), and biotinylated lectins such as Lens culinaris agglutinin (LCA). After incubation with secondary antibodies, the sections were labeled with fluorescein isothiocyanate-conjugated streptavidin (dilution 1:500; Zymed, San Francisco, CA, USA). Images were captured using an Olympus BX-51 fluorescence microscope (Olympus Corp., Tokyo, Japan) and merged with Adobe Photoshop software (Adobe Systems, San Jose, CA, USA).

The VNO of the Korean roe deer was surrounded by cartilage and consisted of medial VSE and lateral VNSE (Fig. 1A). The VG, blood vessels, and vomeronasal nerves were distributed in the lamina propria of the VSE and VNSE, respectively. PLCβ2-positive immunoreactivity was observed in both the VSE and VNSE (Fig. 1B). At higher magnification of the VSE, PLCβ2-positive immunoreactivity was localized to isolated cells situated between the receptor cell layer and the supporting cell layer (Fig. 1C). The PLCβ2-positive cells resembled bipolar cells. In addition, some slender-type cells in the VNSE exhibited PLCβ2-positive immunoreactivity (Fig. 1D). In the VNSE, PLCβ2-positive immunoreactivity was observed in the apical processes and cytoplasm but not in the nucleus.

To investigate the phenotype of PLCβ2-positive cells in the VNO of the Korean roe deer, we performed double fluorescence staining using LCA and ChAT. In the VNO, PLCβ2-positive immunoreactivity was confirmed in LCA-positive cells and/or ChAT-positive cells (Fig. 2A and 2B, respectively). Furthermore, we have investigated whether PLCβ2-positive cells were innervated by trigeminal nerve fibers, double immunofluorescence staining was performed with PLCβ2 and CGRP antibodies respectively as a marker of SCCs and trigeminal nerve fibers. PLCβ2-positive cell (arrowhead in Fig. 3A–C) was surrounded by the CGRP-positive nerve fibers (arrows in Fig. 3B and 3C) in the VNO of Korean roe deer.

This study provides the first description of the distinct localization of PLCβ2-positive cells, identified as SCCs, in the VNO of the Korean roe deer (C. pygargus). The cytoarchitectural features of PLCβ2-positive SCCs in the Korean roe deer, characterized by a slender shape with a short apical dendrite and long processes, are similar to those observed in other ungulates, including the alpaca (Vicugna pacos) [3], sheep [5], and one-humped camel [6]. These SCCs in the Korean roe deer labeled with LCA and exhibit ChAT-positive immunoreactivity, and were surrounded with CGRP-positive nerve fibers.

PLCβ2 is used as a marker for SCCs, along with α-gustducin and transient receptor potential channel 5 (TRPM5), which are involved in bitter taste transduction signaling in the larynx and respiratory epithelium [2]. In the present study, we found PLCβ2-positive immunoreactivity in both the VSE and VNSE of the Korean roe deer. In the VSE, PLCβ2-positive cells were labeled with LCA, markers for mature sensory neurons [12]. These findings suggest that the SCCs of the Korean roe deer exhibit cytological characteristics similar to those of other ungulates.

Different chemoresponsive cells have been studied in the mammalian olfactory system. These cells share molecules such as TRPM5, ChAT, vesicular acetylcholine transporter, α-gustducin, PLCβ2, and the taste receptor T1R3 but are classified into microvillous cells and SCCs based on their ability to form synapses with trigeminal fibers [2]. SCCs that form synapses with trigeminal sensory fibers, which are positive for CGRP and/or substance P, are closely associated with trigeminal-mediated protective reflexes [2]. In the VNO of the Korean roe deer, PLCβ2-positive cells were colocalized with ChAT, suggesting that these cells respond to various external substances, including odorants, irritants, and bitter compounds [13]. However, further studies are needed to determine which cell type is responsible for these responses.

In summary, this is the first report of PLCβ2-positive SCCs in the VNO of the Korean roe deer, which may be involved in detecting risk factors such as harmful substances.

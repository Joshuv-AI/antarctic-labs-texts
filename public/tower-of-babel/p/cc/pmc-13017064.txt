Renal Nonenhancement From Aortocaval Fistula in Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm
Authors: Youngnam Bok, Jae-Hyeong Park
Published: 2026

A 55-year-old man with prostate hypertrophy presented to the emergency department with acute back pain and dyspnea of 2 hours’ duration. On examination, he appeared pale with diaphoresis and anuria. Vital signs revealed hypotension (104/62 mmHg), tachycardia (110 bpm), tachypnea (32 bpm), and severe hypoxemia (oxygen saturation 70%). Cardiac and pulmonary auscultation demonstrated no murmurs or abnormal breath sounds.

Laboratory investigations showed profound metabolic acidosis (pH, 6.91), hypocapnia (PaCO2, 15 mmHg), hepatocellular injury (aspartate aminotransferase/alanine aminotransferase, 557/214 U/L), acute kidney injury (blood urea nitrogen/creatinine, 8.0/4.2 mg/dL), elevated N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (17,300 pg/mL), and marked hyperlactatemia (17.0 mEq/L). Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) revealed an 89 mm abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) with an aortocaval fistula (ACF) connecting the aorta to the inferior vena cava (IVC), resulting in arteriovenous shunting (Figure 1). Despite emergency surgical intervention, the patient expired. The requirement for informed consent was waived by the Institutional Review Board.

Contrast-enhanced computed tomography shows an enlarged IVC with nonenhanced kidneys and partial enhancement of the renal veins (arrows, A, B). The abdominal Ao shows an approximately 89 mm aneurysmal dilation below the bilateral renal arteries and an aortocaval fistula (arrowheads, C). A reconstructed image demonstrates the AAA and the enlarged IVC (D).

AAA = abdominal aortic aneurysm; Ao = aorta; IVC = inferior vena cava.

An ACF is a rare but fatal complication from a ruptured AAA, usually in elderly patients with multiple cardiovascular risk factors.1),2),3),4),5),6) This shunting of arterial blood into the venous system can cause venous hypertension, high-output heart failure, and systemic hypoperfusion, leading to symptoms like abdominal pain, a pulsatile mass, and lower extremity swelling. CT angiography is the primary diagnostic tool showing IVC contrast in the early arterial phase, an important indicator of an ACF. Partial enhancement in the renal vein and nonenhancement of the renal parenchyma can be due to renal venous hypertension. Urgent surgical or endovascular repair is crucial for survival, as untreated ACF has an extremely high mortality rate due to progressive and multi-organ failure.

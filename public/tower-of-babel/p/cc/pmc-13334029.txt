Orelabrutinib versus chemoimmunotherapy in treatment-naïve chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma: a randomized, phase 3 trial
Authors: Fei Li, Keshu Zhou, Wei Xu, Guohui Cui, Shuhua Yi, Jie Jin, Zunmin Zhu, Aili He, Jianqing Mi, Zhongjun Xia, Xiaobing Huang, Xin Zhou, Wenzheng Yu, Jingming Guo, Qingshu Zeng, Shihua Huang, Yuerong Shuang, Hongling Peng, Lihua Qiu, Bing Xu, Yan Li, Yanli Yang, Fuling Zhou, Yafei Wang, Lihong Liu, Jinhai Ren, Ying Li, Wei Yang, Jian Gu, Guifang Ouyang, Luoming Hua, Jin Zhang, Qing Xiao, Shunqing Wang, Qingyuan Zhang, Jing Liu, Kaiyang Ding, Liansheng Zhang, Yun Zeng, Zhenyu Li

ABSTRACT
AbstractOrelabrutinib is a potent, irreversible, and highly-selective BTK inhibitor that has been approved for the treatment of relapsed/refractory chronic lymphocytic leukaemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL). This randomized, phase 3 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04578613) compared orelabrutinib with chemoimmunotherapy in patients with treatment-naïve CLL/SLL. From February 20, 2021, to July 8, 2024, 192 eligible patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either orelabrutinib (91 patients) or chlorambucil plus rituximab (101 patients), comprising the intention-to-treat population. At a median follow-up of 21.4 months (data cutoff, May 17, 2024), the primary endpoint of progression-free survival (PFS) per independent review committee (IRC) was not reached (NR; 95% CI, not estimable [NE]–NE) with orelabrutinib versus 19.4 months (95% CI, 16.6–NE) with chlorambucil plus rituximab (hazard ratio [HR], 0.32; 95% CI, 0.18–0.58; p < 0.0001; crossing the efficacy boundary). The IRC-assessed overall response rate (90.1% vs 79.2%; p = 0.041) and duration of response (HR, 0.30; 95% CI, 0.15–0.60; p = 0.0003) also favored orelabrutinib over chlorambucil plus rituximab. In the safety population, treatment-related adverse events occurred in 82 of 91 patients (90.1%) receiving orelabrutinib and 89 of 98 patients (90.8%) receiving chlorambucil plus rituximab, with 32 (35.2%) and 59 (60.2%) at grade 3 or worse, respectively. Orelabrutinib maintained or improved patient-reported outcomes compared with chemoimmunotherapy. In summary, orelabrutinib significantly improved PFS and response versus chemoimmunotherapy in patients with treatment-naïve CLL/SLL, with a manageable safety profile, supporting it as an effective alternative first-line option.Subject terms: Haematological cancer, Clinical trials

Introduction

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is the most prevalent type of leukemia in adults, characterized by a progressive accumulation of mature lymphocytes in the blood, bone marrow, and lymphoid tissues.1 CLL and small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL) subtypes represent different manifestations of the same disease, predominantly affecting older patients, with a widely variable disease course.2 Historically, chemoimmunotherapy was the standard first-line therapy for CLL/SLL, including fludarabine, cyclophosphamide, and rituximab for fit, younger patients, bendamustine or chlorambucil combined with an anti-CD20 antibody for older adults or those unfit to intensive chemoimmunotherapy.3 However, the clinical benefit of chemoimmunotherapy is limited, particularly in patients with high-risk features, and it is associated with substantial toxicity.4 Consequently, despite progress achieved with these regimens, this disease remains largely incurable in most cases.5

With novel insights into the critical role of B-cell receptor signaling in the pathogenesis of B-cell malignancies, concepts of Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibition have been brought into clinical development for CLL/SLL.6 In the last few years, the advent of BTK inhibitors has revolutionized the treatment landscape of CLL/SLL, replacing highly intensive and toxic chemoimmunotherapy regimens as the standard-of-care.7 The first-generation ibrutinib was the first approved BTK inhibitor for previously untreated CLL based on the positive results of the phase 3 RESONATE-2 trial.8 Nevertheless, the non-negligible off-target toxicities associated with ibrutinib, such as cardiac events, bleeding, arthralgia, hypertension, and diarrhea, raised a major challenge in clinical application,9 thereby prompting the investigations toward second-generation BTK inhibitors with greater BTK selectivity.10

Subsequent pivotal trials, including ELEVATE-TN and SEQUOIA, also confirmed the progression-free survival (PFS) superiority of BTK inhibitors over chemoimmunotherapy, reaffirming their role as a cornerstone of modern CLL/SLL management.11,12 Despite improved selectivity, early discontinuation of acalabrutinib (9%) or zanubrutinib (8%) due to adverse events, together with concerns regarding off-target toxicities, remains a therapeutic challenge affecting long-term benefit,11,12 highlighting the persistent need for less toxic options.

Orelabrutinib is a potent, second-generation, irreversible BTK inhibitor with improved BTK selectivity and tolerability.13 Preclinical studies have shown that orelabrutinib exhibits superior BTK selectivity, achieving >99% inhibition of BTK activity with minimal off-target effects on other kinases.14,15 The near-complete, sustained BTK occupancy of orelabrutinib for up to 24 hours also contributes to consistent target suppression.14,15 Accordingly, these pharmacologic properties are expected to translate into improved efficacy and tolerability compared with other BTK inhibitors.16 Based on its substantial efficacy and excellent safety profile in clinical trials, orelabrutinib has been approved in China for previously treated B-cell malignancies, including relapsed or refractory CLL/SLL, mantle cell lymphoma, and marginal zone lymphom.17–19 In these studies, orelabrutinib monotherapy demonstrated a low discontinuation rate of 4.7% to 7.5% and infrequent off-target toxicities observed, specifically atrial fibrillation and bleeding (<2.5%).17–20 Notably, in patients with relapsed or refractory CLL/SLL, orelabrutinib also demonstrated promising efficacy, achieving an overall response rate (ORR) of 92.5% and a 3-year overall survival (OS) of 81.3%.17 These encouraging results provide a strong rationale for evaluating orelabrutinib in the first-line treatment of CLL/SLL. Herein, we conducted a randomized, multicenter, phase 3 trial to compare orelabrutinib with chemoimmunotherapy in patients with previously untreated CLL/SLL.

Results

Patients

From February 20, 2021, to July 8, 2024, 358 patients were screened for eligibility (detailed reasons for screening failure are provided in supplementary Table 1) and 192 patients were randomized to receive either orelabrutinib (91 patients) or chlorambucil plus rituximab (101 patients), comprising the intention-to-treat (ITT) population (Fig. 1). Among these, 91 patients in the orelabrutinib group and 98 patients in the chlorambucil-rituximab group received the assigned treatments, and were included for the safety analysis.

Trial profile

Baseline demographics and disease characteristics were generally balanced between treatment groups (Table 1). The median age of patients was 67.0 years (interquartile range [IQR], 61.5–71.0), with the majority being over 65 years old (69.3%) and male (64.6%). Del(13q) was present in 93 (48.4%) patients, del(11q) in 27 (14.1%), and trisomy 12 in 52 (27.1%). 58 (36.5%) of 159 patients with evaluable results had an unmutated immunoglobulin heavy-chain variable-region (IGHV) gene. Complex karyotypes were identified in 29 (15.1%) patients.

At data cutoff for the interim analysis on May 17, 2024, the median follow-up duration was 22.4 months (range, 1.9–40.3) for the orelabrutinib group and 19.7 months (range, 0.03–38.7) for the chlorambucil-rituximab group (for all patients, 21.4 months [range, 0.03–40.3]). In the orelabrutinib group, 22 (24.2%) patients discontinued treatment early, primarily due to disease progression (n = 12), and treatment remained ongoing in 69 (75.8%) patients. Detailed characteristics and outcomes for patients with early progression are provided in Supplementary Table 2. In the chlorambucil-rituximab group, 84 of 101 (83.2%) patients completed the planned six cycles of protocol treatment. In total, 9 (9.9%) patients in the orelabrutinib group and 33 (32.7%) patients in the chlorambucil-rituximab group received at least one subsequent systemic therapy during follow-up (supplementary Table 3).

Efficacy

As of the cutoff date (May 17, 2024), with a median follow-up of 21.4 months (range, 0.03–40.3), disease progression as assessed by the independent review committee (IRC) or death occurred in 55 patients in the ITT population (16 [17.6%] in the orelabrutinib group vs 39 [38.6%] in the chlorambucil–rituximab group). The median IRC-assessed PFS was not reached (NR; 95% CI, not estimable [NE]–NE) with orelabrutinib, and 19.4 months (95% CI, 16.6–NE) with chlorambucil plus rituximab (Fig. 2a). Orelabrutinib demonstrated a 68% reduction in the risk of disease progression or death compared to chlorambucil plus rituximab (hazard ratio [HR], 0.32; 95% CI, 0.18–0.58; p < 0.0001), crossing the prespecified efficacy boundary for PFS. Kaplan–Meier estimates of PFS rates at 12 months were 93.1% (95% CI, 85.2–96.8) in the orelabrutinib group and 76.1% (95% CI, 65.7–83.8) in the chlorambucil-rituximab group; and at 24 months were 81.6% (95% CI, 69.9–89.1) and 48.4% (95% CI, 35.8–59.8), respectively. In the prespecified subgroup analyses, PFS favored orelabrutinib over chlorambucil plus rituximab in multiple analyzed subgroups, including those based on advanced age, Rai stage III/IV, bulky disease (tumor lesions ≥5 cm), del(11q) positivity, or unmutated IGHV status (Fig. 2b; supplementary Figs. 1, 2). Investigator-assessed PFS results were consistent with the primary analysis as per IRC assessment (supplementary Fig. 3).

As assessed by the IRC, the confirmed ORR was higher with orelabrutinib (90.1%; 95% CI, 82.1–95.4) than with chlorambucil plus rituximab (79.2%; 95% CI, 70.0–86.6), corresponding to an odds ratio of 2.41 (95% CI, 1.03–5.65; p = 0.041; Table 2). In the orelabrutinib group, complete response (CR) was achieved in 2 (2.2%) patients, partial response in 72 (79.1%), and PR with lymphocytosis (PR-L) in 8 (8.8%). In the chlorambucil–rituximab group, CR was achieved in 4 (4.0%) patients and PR in 76 (75.2%). The median IRC-assessed duration of response (DoR) was NR in either treatment group and was significantly longer with orelabrutinib compared to chlorambucil plus rituximab (HR = 0.30; 95% CI, 0.15–0.60; p = 0.0003; Fig. 3a). The DoR rates at 12 and 24 months were estimated to be 90.3% (95% CI, 80.7–95.3) and 84.3% (95% CI, 72.4–91.4) for orelabrutinib, compared with 71.2% (95% CI, 58.7–80.6) and 51.7% (95% CI, 36.9–64.7) for chlorambucil plus rituximab. According to investigator assessment, the data on tumor response and DoR reaffirmed the benefits of orelabrutinib (Table 2; supplementary Fig. 4).

In the ITT population, OS data were immature at the time of interim analysis, with a total of 16 deaths: 5 (5.5%) in the orelabrutinib group and 11 (10.9%) in the chlorambucil–rituximab group. The median OS has not yet been reached in either group (HR, 0.45; 95% CI, 0.16–1.31; p = 0.13; Fig. 3b). The estimated OS rates were 96.7% (95% CI, 90.1–98.9) with orelabrutinib versus 96.9% (95% CI, 90.7–99.0) with chlorambucil plus rituximab at 1 year; 95.1% (95% CI, 87.1–98.2) versus 83.3% (95% CI, 70.3–91.0) at 2 years; 92.1% (95% CI, 80.4–96.9) versus 78.7% (95% CI, 62.1–88.6) at 3 years, respectively.

Exposure and safety

In the safety population, the median duration of treatment was 19.3 months (IQR, 11.6–27.6) for orelabrutinib, 5.2 months (IQR, 5.1–5.9) for chlorambucil, and 4.7 months (IQR, 4.6–5.3) for rituximab, respectively. 6.6% (6/91) of patients had a dose reduction of orelabrutinib, and 10.2% (10/98) of patients had a dose reduction of chlorambucil. Detailed treatment exposure is summarized in Supplementary Table 4.

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were reported in 90 (98.9%) patients in the orelabrutinib group and 96 (98.0%) patients in the chlorambucil–rituximab group. The incidence of grade 3 or worse TEAEs was respectively 58.2% versus 69.4% as per National Cancer Institute-Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE, version 5.0), and 15.4% versus 33.7% as per International Workshop on Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (iwCLL) criteria (supplementary Tables 5, 6, 7). 82 of 91 (90.1%) patients receiving orelabrutinib and 89 of 98 (90.8%) patients receiving chlorambucil plus rituximab experienced any grade treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), with grade 3 or worse events occurring in 35.2% versus 60.2% per NCI-CTCAE (Table 3). The frequent grade ≥3 TRAEs (≥10% incidence) were neutrophil count decrease (7.7% [7/91] in the orelabrutinib group vs. 30.6% [30/98] in the chlorambucil–rituximab group), white-cell count decrease (1.1% vs. 23.5%), lymphocyte count decreased (1.1% vs. 14.3%), and infectious pneumonia (6.6% vs. 6.1%). Grade ≥3 treatment-related hematologic toxicities as assessed by the iwCLL criteria were more frequent in the chlorambucil–rituximab group (33 [33.7%]) than in the orelabrutinib group (13 [14.3%]; supplementary Table 7), mostly including neutrophil count decrease (7.7% vs. 30.6%) and platelet count decrease (5.5% vs. 5.1%). As of the data cutoff date, no treatment-related atrial fibrillation or second primary malignancies were reported.

TEAEs led to dose reduction of orelabrutinib or chlorambucil in 6.6% (6/91) and 9.2% (9/98) of patients; led to treatment discontinuation in 5.5% (5/91) and 7.1% (7/98) of patients; and led to the dose interruption in 44.0% (40/91) and 61.2% (60/98) of patients, respectively (supplementary Table 5). Among these, TRAE-related dose reductions occurred in 4.4% versus 8.2% of patients; treatment discontinuations in 1.1% versus 6.1%; and dose interruption in 17.6% versus 49.0%, for orelabrutinib and chlorambucil plus rituximab, respectively (supplementary Table 5). Serious TEAEs occurred in 43 patients (47.3%) receiving orelabrutinib and 23 patients (23.5%) receiving chlorambucil plus rituximab; of which 17 (18.7%) and 13 (13.3%) were considered serious TRAEs, respectively (supplementary Table 5). A total of 6 deaths due to TEAEs were reported: 5 (5.5%) in the orelabrutinib group and 1 (1.0%) in the chlorambucil–rituximab group. One death (1.0%) in the orelabrutinib group, attributed to febrile neutropenia, was considered possibly related to the treatment (supplementary Table 5).

Predefined events of special interest (defined as grade ≥ 3 infection, Hepatitis B virus reactivation, and grade ≥ 3 bleeding) occurred in 32 (35.2%) patients receiving orelabrutinib and 21 (21.4%) patients receiving chlorambucil plus rituximab, regardless of treatment causality (supplementary Table 8). The most common TEAEs of special interest were grade ≥ 3 infection (34.1% vs. 21.4%) in both groups. No grade ≥ 3 bleeding was observed in either group.

Hematological variables

Both the orelabrutinib and chlorambucil plus rituximab increased the hemoglobin concentrations, platelet count, and absolute neutrophil count during treatment (supplementary Fig. 5a). No difference between treatment groups was observed in the proportion of patients with sustained improvement in hemoglobin concentrations, platelet count, and absolute neutrophil count. Kaplan–Meier analysis of time to sustained hematologic improvement (supplementary Fig. 5b) showed earlier or a trend toward earlier recovery with orelabrutinib than with chlorambucil plus rituximab in hemoglobin concentrations (4.7 months vs. 7.2 months; p = 0.33), platelet count (7.9 months vs. 11.1 months; p = 0.085), and absolute neutrophil count (5.3 months vs. 11.0 months; p = 0.031).

In addition, changes in lymphocyte count, reflecting treatment-related lymphocytosis, over time are shown in supplementary Fig. 5c. In the orelabrutinib group, lymphocyte count increased after baseline, peaked at 2 weeks, and then declined over time.

Patients–reported quality of life (QoL)

In the QoL exploratory analysis, a total of 191 patients completed at least one valid QoL questionnaire: 91 in the orelabrutinib group and 100 in the chlorambucil–rituximab group, including the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire-Core 30 (EORTC QLQ-C30), EuroQol 5-Dimension 3-Level questionnaire (EQ-5D-3L), and Quality of Life Questionnaire-Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia 16 (QLQ-CLL16).

EORTC QLQ-C30–global health status/quality of life (GHS/QoL) scale and EQ-5D-3L visual analogue scale (VAS) were maintained or improved from baseline in both groups across most visits (supplementary Fig. 6a, b). With comparable baseline scores, least-squares mean (LSM) changes from baseline in the EORTC QLQ-C30-GHS/QoL and EQ-5D-3L VAS did not differ significantly between treatment groups at each visit, except at cycle 37. The LSM change from baseline to cycle 37 in EORTC QLQC30 GHS/QoL scores was 10.2 (95% CI, 2.8–17.7) for orelabrutinib versus −5.2 (95% CI, −18.2 to 7.9) for chlorambucil plus rituximab (LSM difference, 15.4 [95% CI, 0.4–30.4]; nominal p = 0.044; supplementary Fig. 6a), Similarly, difference in LSM change from baseline to cycle 37 in EQ-5D-3L VAS favored orelabrutinib (LSM difference, 11.3 [95% CI, 1.5–21.1]; nominal p = 0.024; supplementary Fig. 6b). Descriptive analysis for QLQ-CLL16 showed that scores remained stable throughout the study, with no clinically meaningful deterioration observed in either group (supplementary Fig. 6c).

In the responder analysis, the proportion of patients with clinically meaningful improvement was higher than that of those with deterioration for the EORTC QLQ-C30 GHS/QoL (10-point threshold) and EQ-5D-3L VAS (7-point threshold) at all visits in both groups. During the first 13 cycles, the proportion of patients achieving clinically meaningful improvement was similar between the two groups. From cycle 16 onward, more patients had clinically meaningful improvement with orelabrutinib versus chlorambucil plus rituximab, with the numerical difference increasing over time (supplementary Table 9).

Discussion

In this phase 3 trial involving patients with previously untreated CLL/SLL, the primary endpoint was met; orelabrutinib monotherapy resulted in a significantly longer median PFS than chlorambucil plus rituximab, with a 68% lower risk of disease progression or death. Importantly, the early, sustained separation of PFS curves and consistent benefits across multiple prespecified subgroups, particularly those with high-risk features, underscore the early onset, durable, and robust benefit of orelabrutinib. Furthermore, the higher ORR and longer DoR with orelabrutinib, along with consistent investigator-assessed benefits, strongly support its clinically meaningful benefits in the first-line setting. Safety findings were consistent with the previously reported safety profile, without unexpected safety signals.

Prior pivotal trials have established BTK inhibitors as the standard first-line treatment for patients with CLL/SLL.8,11,12 Our trial reproduced and confirmed the superiority of BTK inhibitor alone over chemoimmunotherapy, demonstrating the significant PFS prolongation and a reduced risk of disease progression or death (HR = 0.32). Despite inherent limitations of cross-trial comparisons, our efficacy findings were generally consistent with those reported for other BTK inhibitors in earlier trials (RESONATE-2, ELEVATE-TN, Alliance A041202, SEQUOIA), with comparable 2-year PFS rates (81.6% vs. 85.5%–87%) and ORRs (90.1% vs. 86%–94.6%).8,11,12,21 In the context of similar PFS observed with chemoimmunotherapy across trials, orelabrutinib also achieved a substantial magnitude of clinical benefit as observed in those studies (HR range, 0.16–0.42). Similarly, consistent with previous reports (1–7%), CRs were also infrequent (2.2%) in our study with a median follow-up of 22.4 months, reflecting the known class effect of BTK inhibitors.8,11,12,21 Given the continuously increasing CR observed in RESONATE-2 and ELEVATE-TN trials,22,23 we anticipated that orelabrutinib would also induce deeper response in this disease, as evidenced by a post-hoc updated analysis at 30-month follow-up showing an increased CR rate to 12.1%. Of note, the earlier separation of PFS curves observed (~7 months) suggests a more rapid disease control with orelabrutinib, in contrast to the pattern seen with other second-generation BTK inhibitors (ie, acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib), where separation typically occurred beyond 10 months.11,12 This finding may be attributed to its favorable pharmacologic properties—higher selectivity and near-complete, sustained BTK occupancy for 24 hours,14,15 which could enable more rapid and consistent inhibition of BCR signaling, leading to earlier clinical disease control. Apart from different agents, differences in dosing regimen, patient characteristics (e.g., age, disease stage, and high-risk features), geography, and study design may also affect our observed efficacy outcomes. Notably, given the prognostic importance of IGHV mutational status, differences in the distribution of unmutated IGHV across trials should be particularly considered when interpreting cross-trial comparisons.

In the evolving landscape of targeted therapy, fixed-duration BCL2 inhibitor-based regimens have emerged as another preferred frontline option in treatment-naïve CLL/SLL. In the CLL14 trial, venetoclax plus obinutuzumab demonstrated equally significant efficacy, with a 2-year PFS rate of 88% and ORR of 85%, as well as a low incidence of high-grade toxicity.24 Given that neither of the new target therapies is considered curative or free of toxicity, it is recommended that optimal therapy should be individualized through shared decision-making between patients and an experienced healthcare team, considering treatment goals, risks, benefits, cost, and access to treatments.25

In our trial, PFS favored orelabrutinib across most subgroups, regardless of key demographic or disease characteristics, implicating the results’ robustness. Importantly, although subgroup analyses are hypothesis-generating, the benefit was particularly pronounced in patients with high-risk features (e.g., unmutated IGHV status, del(11q), del(13q), or trisomy 12), following a pattern observed with ibrutinib or acalabrutinib.8,11,21 Nevertheless, similar to other studies in this disease, the exclusion of patients with del(17p) or TP53 mutation limited the generalizability of our findings to these very higher-risk subgroups. Meanwhile, benefits in individual subgroups warrant further evaluation, given the small sample, wide confidence intervals, and the exploratory nature of the analyses. Beyond efficacy findings, our post-hoc analysis also provided insights into hematological findings. Orelabrutinib led to more rapid hematologic improvement compared with chlorambucil plus rituximab, providing additional evidence for its early disease control and bone marrow recovery, with particular clinical significance. While orelabrutinib also induced transient lymphocytosis, an interesting observation was that the lymphocyte count peaked earlier and began to decline by week 2, in contrast to the week-4 peak typically observed with ibrutinib or zanubrutinib.26,27 Collectively, these hematologic properties may confer an opportunity for short lead-in periods when combining orelabrutinib with BCL2 inhibitors, potentially enabling earlier treatment intensification without increasing tumor lysis syndrome risk; however, this remains an assumption that deserves investigation in dedicated studies.

Safety findings of orelabrutinib were consistent with its established profile in relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL or other malignancies, without unexpected signals observed.17–19,28,29 Moreover, despite a nearly quadruple treatment duration, orelabrutinib maintained a comparable incidence of any grade TEAEs or TRAEs to the control, even showing lower rates of severe events, such as grade ≥3 hematologic toxicities per iwCLL criteria (15.4% vs. 33.7%). This favorable safety and tolerability appear particularly important when contextualizing continuous BTK inhibitor therapy. Another point worth noting is that atrial fibrillation/flutter, major bleeding, or second malignancy (as known off-target toxicities of BTK inhibitor) were not observed here and consistent with previous experience from orelabrutinib,17–19,28,29, contrasting with the historical incidence of 3%–16% reported with other agents in this class.30,31 Nevertheless, the absence of these events should be interpreted with caution given the relatively short follow-up; ongoing monitoring is therefore warranted to evaluate long-term risk. We also noted that infections were frequently reported in our study, yet most were attributable to COVID-19, and grade ≥ 3 treatment-related infections occurred in 16 (17.6%) patients, all of which were manageable with supportive care. Accordingly, orelabrutinib was associated with few toxic discontinuations (TEAEs, 5.5%; TRAEs, 1.1%), that typically reported for other BTK inhibitors (8%-9%).8,11,12 Patient-reported outcomes also suggested that toxicities have no major adverse effect on QoL, achieving clinically meaningful improvement in a substantial patients. Overall, the maintained patient-reported QoL, manageable safety profile, combined with significant efficacy benefits, supported the tolerability and favorable benefit-to-risk profile of orelabrutinib.

Despite the promising findings of this trial, several limitations warrant consideration. One limitation was its open-label design; however, an independent, blinded central review for the primary endpoint and the standard safety management algorithm were expected to minimize the potential bias. Consistent with other pivotal trials of BTK inhibitors in this setting, the absence of a contemporary standard-of-care comparator was another limitation, as chemoimmunotherapy remained the guideline-recommended standard-of-care and neither BTK inhibitors nor BCL2 inhibitors-based regimens had been approved for treatment-naïve CLL/SLL in China during study design. However, current practice has shifted toward targeted therapy-based regimens; thus, this choice and cross-trial comparisons should therefore be interpreted cautiously. Further evaluation of orelabrutinib in the context of the contemporary treatment landscape is warranted. As this trial was conducted exclusively in Chinese patients, the generalizability of our findings to other populations requires further investigation. The ongoing global study of orelabrutinib in B-cell malignancies (NCT04014205) may provide additional evidence for its potential applicability across ethnic groups. In addition, the exclusion of del(17p)/TP53-mutated patients, who typically exhibit distinct disease biology and poorer outcomes, limits the generalizability of our data to high-risk molecular subgroups. As this interim analysis was event-driven rather than time-driven, follow-up remains relatively short, and OS analyses at this time are exploratory. Ongoing follow-up will further clarify the durability of response, long-term survival, and safety outcomes associated with orelabrutinib.

In summary, orelabrutinib provided significantly greater benefits than chlorambucil plus rituximab in terms of PFS and tumor response in patients with previously untreated CLL/SLL, particularly among those with high-risk features. The safety profile of orelabrutinib was manageable and consistent with its known profile. The clinically meaningful benefit, along with favorable safety and maintained QoL indicate a favorable benefit-risk profile of orelabrutinib, establishing it as an effective and safe option for this population. Based on the positive findings of this phase 3 trial, orelabrutinib has been approved in China for the first-line treatment of CLL/SLL.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This open-label, randomized, multicenter phase 3 study was conducted across 65 sites in China. Eligible patients had previously untreated CLL/SLL requiring therapy as per iwCLL criteria (version 2018).32 Patients were aged 65 years or older, or aged 18 to 65 years with coexisting comorbidities (defined as creatinine clearance of 30–69 mL/min calculated by the Cockcroft-Gault equation or Cumulative Illness Rating Scale for Geriatrics score >6). Other eligibility criteria included an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status score of 2 or less, measurable disease, a life expectancy of more than 6 months, and adequate hematologic, hepatic, and renal function. Patients were ineligible if they had a chromosome 17p13 deletion or TP53 mutation, central nervous system involvement, Richter transformation, or clinically significant cardiovascular disease. The full eligibility criteria are listed in the supplementary protocol.

The trial protocol, amendments, and patient informed consent documents were reviewed and approved by the ethics committee at each participating site. All patients provided written informed consent before enrollment. The trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and applicable laws and regulations. This study was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov, Identifier: NCT04578613.

Randomization and masking

Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio via an Interactive Web Response System (IWRS) to receive orelabrutinib or chlorambucil plus rituximab. Randomization was performed centrally and stratified based on age (<65 years vs. ≥65 years), Rai stage (III–IV [high risk] vs. 0–II [low or intermediate risk]), and the IGHV mutational status (mutated vs. unmutated). As an open–label design, patients and investigators were not masked to treatment allocation. A masked IRC assessed the disease progression and response data.

Procedure

Patients assigned to the orelabrutinib group received oral orelabrutinib at 150 mg once daily continuously until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or consent withdrawal. Patients assigned to the chlorambucil-rituximab group received up to six 28–day cycles of oral chlorambucil (0.5 mg/kg at days 1 and 15 of each cycle) plus intravenous rituximab (375 mg/m² of body surface area on day 1 of cycle 1; 500 mg/m² of body surface area on the day 1 of cycles 2–6), or until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or consent withdrawal. Patients in the chlorambucil–rituximab group with centrally confirmed disease progression were allowed to cross over to receive orelabrutinib monotherapy as second–line treatment. Dose modification of orelabrutinib and chlorambucil was allowed for toxicity management according to protocol-defined criteria (supplementary protocol). A maximum of two dose reductions of orelabrutinib (to 100 mg or 50 mg) were permitted. Patients could withdraw from the trial for any reason at any time, or be withdrawn for loss to follow-up or investigator decision. After withdrawal from the study, all patients were requested to participate in the survival and safety follow-up.

Radiological assessment was conducted using computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging scan at baseline, every 8 weeks for the first 6 cycles, every 12 weeks for cycles 7–24, and every 24 weeks thereafter, until confirmed disease progression. All scans were subject to central review by independent radiologists who were unaware of the group assignments. Tumor response was assessed by both the IRC and investigators as per the iwCLL 2018 criteria (for patients with CLL) or per the Cheson 2014 criteria (for patients with SLL).32,33 PR-L was also evaluated for patients receiving orelabrutinib and categorized as ORR, as prespecified in the protocol. Safety was assessed by adverse events, laboratory measurements (e.g., hematology, chemistry, and urinalysis), physical examinations, and clinical evaluation throughout the study and during the 30–day follow–up period after the last dose of study treatment. Hematological toxicities were assessed using both the NCT-CTCAE, v5.0, and iwCLL 2018 criteria. Non-hematological toxicities were assessed using NCT-CTCAE, v5.0. Patients' reported QoL was assessed at each efficacy assessment visit using the EORTC QLQ-C30, EORTC QLQ-CLL16, and EQ-5D-3L questionnaires.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was PFS as assessed by IRC, defined as the time from randomization until disease progression or death, whichever occurred first. Secondary endpoints were ORR (the proportion of patients achieving CR, CRi, nPR, and PR) and DoR (the time from the first documented response until disease progression or death) assessed by both IRC and investigators, investigator–assessed PFS, OS (the time from randomization until death due to any cause), and safety. Exploratory endpoints included patients–reported outcomes by EORTC QLQ-C30, EQ-5D-3L, and QLQ-CLL16; biomarker analysis evaluating the correlation between clinical outcomes and baseline biomarkers (e.g., 11q22-23 deletion, 13q14 deletion, IGHV mutational status, β-2 microglobulin levels, and complex karyotype); the population pharmacokinetics profile of obinutuzumab (data not shown and will be reported separately).

Statistical analysis

The sample size for this trial was determined based on the primary endpoint of PFS. Assuming an expected median PFS of 20 months with chlorambucil plus rituximab, a total of 79 progression events or deaths were estimated for the final analysis, to provide a 90% power to detect a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.48 in favor of the superior efficacy of orelabrutinib, at a one-sided overall type I error of 2.5%. Considering the 30-month enrollment, 12-month additional follow-up, and a dropout rate of 0.39% per month, the planned enrollment was 176 patients (88 patients per group). One interim analysis of PFS was pre-specified at the observation of 53 PFS events (two–thirds of the planned events), with adjustment for multiple comparisons made using the Lan–DeMets alpha-spending function with an O’Brien–Fleming–type boundary. The nominal significance levels (adjusted two-sided α) were 0.0062 for the interim analysis and 0.0231 for the final analysis. The results of the interim analysis were reviewed by an independent data and safety monitoring committee on May 17, 2024. At the interim analysis, the pre-specified efficacy boundary for PFS was crossed, with a statistical superiority (p < 0.0001). The trial continued to evaluate OS. All data reported here are based on the interim analysis.

Efficacy endpoints were analyzed in the ITT population, which included all randomized patients. Safety was analyzed in all randomized patients who received at least one dose of study treatment. Time–to–event endpoints were estimated with the use of the Kaplan–Meier method and compared between two groups by the stratified log–rank test. The corresponding HR and associated two-sided 95% CIs were estimated by a stratified Cox proportional–hazards model. Data on PFS and DoR were censored at the date of last assessment for patients who remained alive without disease progression or were lost to follow-up. Data on OS were censored on the last day that the patient was known to be alive. Response rates with two-sided 95% CIs were calculated with the Clopper–Pearson method and compared between treatment groups with the use of a Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test. Stratification factors used at randomization were used for all stratified analyses. Subgroup analyses of PFS were prespecified, with HRs and corresponding 95% CIs estimated using an unstratified Cox proportional–hazards model to evaluate treatment effects across key baseline characteristics. Sustained hematologic improvement (defined as an improvement ≥50% above baseline or an increase to ≥11 g/dL for hemoglobin, ≥100 × 109/L for platelets, or ≥1.5 × 109/L for neutrophil, respectively, sustained for ≥8 weeks) was evaluated post-hoc among patients who had cytopenia at baseline. Patient–reported outcomes were assessed in patients with a baseline and at least one post-baseline assessment. Least-squares mean change from baselines in EORTC QLQ-C30 and EQ-5D-3L scores were analyzed and compared with mixed-model repeated measures. Changes from baselines in QLQ-CLL16 were descriptively summarized as mean (standard deviation). The SAS software, version 9.4, was used for all statistical analyses. Detailed statistical analysis methods are provided in the statistical analysis plan (supplementary SAP).

Spatio-temporal differentiation characteristics of coal mine safety levels in China based on spatial spillover effects
Authors: Tianmo Zhou, Yunqiang Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In order to solve the problem of "potpourri" of safety risk prevention and control measures, which is caused by the unclear mechanism of the spatial effect of coal mine safety level heterogeneity and its influencing factors. This paper discriminates the dominant factors of spatio-temporal heterogeneity of coal mine safety production level in China and their spatial effect types by means of GeoDetector and the Spatial Dubin Model (SDM), specifies the categories and degrees of acts on local and surrounding areas by changes in indicators, and provides further visualization of the detection outcomes with the assistance of the Neo4j graph database, and the findings indicate that:(1) All 15 indicators selected in the study have a certain influence on the generation of spatial heterogeneity in China's coal mine safety production level, and all of them show an enhancement relationship after the interaction of indicators. Especially, the combination of excavated environment and other indicators basically has a non-linear enhancement relationship. (2) In terms of spatial effects, the influence of the 5 effects on the spatio-temporal heterogeneity of coal mine safety level is, in descending order, industrial development effect > capital allocation effect > production environment effect > government supervision effect > enterprise management effect, which indicates that macroeconomic and market conditions have a much stronger influence on the generation of spatio-temporal heterogeneity in coal mine production safety status. (3) From the single indicator perspective, the average annual temperature, average annual wind speed, coal consumption and monitoring efficiency primarily affect the dependent variable through direct effects; GDP per capita, average labor compensation as well as railroad operating mileage have positive spatial spillover on the changes of coal mine safety production level in surrounding areas; the evaluation of the spatial effect for average labor compensation exhibits a positive indirect effect with low influence; for the two indicators of production efficiency and ex-factory price, not only do they have negative effects on the local coal mine safety level, but also have significant spillover effects on surrounding areas.

Introduction

Coal mining is a systematic project influenced by multiple factors [1], and its production safety level is directly influenced by the guidance of macro policies such as social and economic development programs [[2], [3], [4]], industrial development planning [5], and coal market regulation [[6], [7], [8]]. Meanwhile, there are also micro factors such as coal occurrence environment [[9], [10], [11]], supervision and law enforcement effectiveness [[12], [13], [14]], coal enterprise management ability [15,16], coal mining technology and equipment level [17,18], as well as the coupling of factors. Consequently, it is of great significance to take into account the influence of various factors and their interaction to carry out targeted work on securing coal mine production safety.

The studies in the field of coal mine safety in recent years have mainly concentrated on human unsafe behavior [19], with less exploration of other macro and micro factors, especially in the study of the spatial effect mechanism of coal mine safety level heterogeneity and its drivers, which still leaves plenty of room for research. Furthermore, the property that things commonly possess spatial spillover effects has been observed in many ongoing study fields [[20], [21], [22]]. While coal mine safety issues are superficially a series of situations triggered by human unsafe behavior or physical unsafe conditions, they also have spatial effects as elements that occur in geographic space. It is thus envisaged that whether there is a difference in the action mode of the factors affecting the coal mine safety level when the action area is different, or whether the safety state of the research object itself has potential impact on the neighboring area through the spillover effect?

In order to solve above issues, the first step is to identify the spatio-temporal heterogeneity characteristics and the spatial effect types of its dominant factors in coal mine safety. It is known that the spatial effects of the elements can be categorized into two types: endogenous effects and spillover effects [23]. The former is that the independent variable directly causes the dependent variable to change accordingly, that is, producing a direct effect; while the latter is that it acts on the external elements and then feeds back to the dependent variable, making it change through an indirect action. Secondly, the influencing factors not only have spatial effects, but there may also be interactions among the indicators. Thus, we also need to identify the type of interaction between factors and quantify the degree of them. By solving these issues, it is possible to effectively avoid the situation of “miscellaneous” safety risk prevention and control measures caused by the failure to grasp the spatial effect types of spatio-temporal heterogeneity influencing factors. Hence, the identification of the spatio-temporal heterogeneity characteristics and the spatial effect types of its dominant factors in coal mine safety is an urgent issue to be solved. Currently, the major evaluation method of spatial spillover effects is the Spatial Dubin Model (SDM). It is mainly used in the economic field: to study the spatial structure of regional economy [[24], [25], [26], [27]],and to find out the elements that have strong spatial spillover effects on regional economic growth, so as to provide reference for regional investment and industrial restructuring; secondly, to analyze the impact of investment intensity on industrial development in the national or regional dimension [[28], [29], [30]]; thirdly, to analyze the formation of enterprise knowledge spillover effects in a certain spatial scope, the spatial aggregation characteristics, aggregation intensity and impact scope [[31], [32], [33]],which is used to measure the impact of knowledge spillover on regional economic development. The SDM method has also been applied in recent years in the study fields of transportation [[34], [35], [36]] (focusing on the impact of transportation accessibility on the regional economy, tourism economy, or urban development) and carbon emissions [[37], [38], [39]] (with an emphasis on the quantitative identification of urban carbon emission evolution routes and their critical influencing factors, and proposing emission reduction recommendations). Moreover, the method has also been applied to food production [[40], [41], [42]] (focusing on the impact study of the mechanization of agricultural production on production efficiency and yield), environmental contamination [[43], [44], [45]] (the study mainly covers the mechanism of energy structure, urbanization and other factors on the formation and distribution of smog in China), and so on. Yet, in the field of safety production, relevant studies remain rare and model applications will contribute to the identification of the types of spatial spillover effects of the influencing factors. Regarding the identification of the interaction of influencing factors, it can be solved by GeoDetector. This model is one of the main methods for the identification of spatial relationships of elements in recent years [[46], [47], [48]]. Currently, the method has relatively few applications in the mining sector. It is mainly used for the research of influencing factors and their interaction relationship in land degradation in coal mine area [49], change of vegetation cover in mine area [50], extraction of information on coal mining subsidence area [51], research on mine safety risk zoning [52], analysis of livability in coal mine area [53], evaluation of environmental pollution in mine area and risk of geologic disaster [[54], [55], [56]]. Mining of potential influences that contribute to spatially heterogeneity of the study object through GeoDetector, which can provides support for the effective identification of interactions between risk factors for specified geographic phenomena.

Accordingly, based on the previous study [57],this paper further analyzes the spatial effects of the spatio-temporal variation characteristics and their influencing factors on the coal mine safety production level in China via SDM and GeoDetector methods based on the mortality rate per million tons in provincial coal mines from 2003 to 2021 as well as 15 indicators in 5 major categories. Furthermore, this paper also visualizes and analyzes the findings with the assistance of Neo4j graph database. The findings are of both theoretical and practical significance for clarifying the effects of each driving factor and their interactions, as well as for improving the coal mine safety production assurance measures.

The structure of this paper proceeds as follows. Section 1 presents 5 types of effects that affect the spatio-temporal heterogeneity of coal mine safety production levels. Section 2 introduces the data source, methodology and data pre-processing process of the study. Section 3 provides an analysis of the spatial autocorrelation of mortality rate per million tons in China's coal mines. Section 4 explores the spatial effects of the influencing factors of spatio-temporal heterogeneity and their interrelationships via SDM and GeoDetector methods. Section 5 visualizes the study results using Neo4j and makes recommendations for safe production. Section 6 presents a summary of the study and puts forward subsequent directions for the study.

Theoretical hypothesis and analysis

The coal mine safety level also exists simultaneously in spatial and temporal actions. The coal mine safety level in each province is not completely dependent on the status of local economic and industrial development, and the flow of several major factors can lead to changes in regional industrial supply and demand, and changes in the supply and demand status of the coal market in the surrounding areas can also create impacts for local production. The boundary effect among regions also leads to the limitation of the spatial spillover intensity under the joint action caused by the differences of multiple factors such as geographical distance, as well as economic and natural environment. From a holistic point of view, the impact of macro and micro environment on coal mine safety differences mainly contains 5 aspects, including industrial development effect, capital allocation effect, enterprise management effect, production environment effect and government supervision effect.

The industrial development effect emphasizes on increasing the input scale of capital elements by means of macro policy regulation, thus achieving the objective of simultaneous improvement for regional industrial development and safety assurance capacity. The capital allocation effect stresses the provision of more resource support to areas with high production efficiency, which promotes the improvement of production technology capability and management level, thereby influencing the safety development concept and actual guarantee capability of enterprises. The enterprise management effect mainly acts on the production efficiency and output of the production subject, while drawing the regional gathering of high-quality resources to achieve the enhancement of the overall safety production level in the region. The production environment effect highlights the natural condition to which coal mining activities and market operations are exposed. Changes in meteorological conditions not only cause changes in market supply and demand, but also have a direct impact on production activities. The government supervision effect concentrates on the guidance and management of enterprises by the industry management department, and urges the production subject to upgrade safety effectiveness through both incentives and penalties.

Moreover, the above effects may also have spatial spillover effects, which means that the development level of safety production in a certain region may have a diffusion or aggregation effect on the surrounding regions, thus promoting or weakening the differences in the overall safety level. Hence, further examination of the aforementioned effects based on the data of coal mine accidents, production and operation, market conditions, meteorological conditions, and government supervision in China by province from 2003 to 2021 should be conducted to clarify the significance of the existence of the effect and its direction of influence.

Data and methodology

Indicator system

Due to the different production capacity, large disparity in enterprise safety capacity, and uneven supervision and monitoring capacity among provinces, absolute indicators such as the number of accidents and fatalities are inadequate in comprehensively reflecting the status of regional coal mine safety production level, so this study has chosen “coal mine mortality rate per million tons” as the major indicator. This indicator represents the number of fatalities per 1 million tons of raw coal produced (Equation (1)), which can better compensate for the lack of comprehensiveness of the safety production level evaluation caused by neglecting the production capacity and considering only the number of fatalities, and is an essential indicator to comprehensively quantify the industrial development, enterprise status, and regional safety level. In particular, it is beneficial to identify provinces with widely varying production capacities but with the same number of fatalities, and can more objectively reflect the actual safety status and comprehensively reflect the intervention level of multiple types of factors, such as policy orientation, technical capacity, market economy as well as supervision and monitoring, on the safety status of coal mines [58].(1)MR=RiPiWhere MR is the mortality rate per million tons, Ri and Pi are the number of deaths (persons) and raw coal output (million tons), respectively.

It should be noted that the object of the current study is the coal mine safety production level, and when characterized through the mortality rate per million tons, the meaning of the two is inversely related, that is, the higher the mortality rate per million tons, the lower the safety level. To facilitate the explanation later, the reciprocal of the mortality rate per million tons was taken as a proxy variable for the explained variable. Data sources

The number of fatalities in coal mine production safety accidents by province was mainly obtained through the Mine Safety Production Integrated Information System of the National Mine Safety Administration (https://zhxx.chinacoal-safety.gov.cn/mj/a/login), with a time frame of 2003–2021. Due to there is no coal mine accident data from Tianjin, Tibet, Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan; Zhejiang, Guangdong, Beijing and Chongqing were exited from the coal industry in 2006, 2007, 2018 and 2020, respectively, and there was no coal mine production and accident data after the exit. The time range for raw coal production by province was also 2003–2021, with data obtained from the “China Statistical Yearbook”, the “Statistical Bulletin of the National Economic and Social Development of the People's Republic of China”, the official website of the National Mine Safety Administration and 24 provincial bureaus, as well as the year-by-year “Compilation of National Coal Mine Accident Analysis Reports”.

Other variables: annual accumulated rainfall, annual average temperature, annual average wind speed, GDP per capita, and railroad operating mileage from the “China Statistical Yearbook”; coal consumption and fixed asset investment growth rate from the “China Energy Statistical Yearbook”; employees and average labor compensation from the “China Labor Statistical Yearbook” and “China Industrial Statistical Yearbook”; ex-factory price from the “China Price Statistical Yearbook”; supervision and law enforcement data from the Mine Safety Production Integrated Information System of the National Mine Safety Administration.

Spatial Dubin Model

This paper employed the Spatial Dubin Model (SDM) as a discriminative method for the spatial effects of the influencing factors of spatio-temporal heterogeneity in coal mine safety production levels. As a classical spatial measurement model, this method integrates the Spatial Lag Model (SLM) and the Spatial Error Model (SEM), and comprehensively considers the spatial of the dependent variable on the independent variable [26,27], and the dependent variable on itself by adding the spatial lag term and the spatial error term. Moreover, the method can explicitly obtain whether the independent variables act on the dependent variable through endogenous effects or exogenous effects (as the present study is to explore the spatial effects of the influencing factors of spatio-temporal differences in coal mine safety production levels under different time dimensions, the specific expressions of SDM at different stages would be defined separately according to the data test). The expression of SDM is shown in Equation (4) [38,60].(4)y=ρ∑j=1nWijyit+αilnXhl,i,t+βiXkl,i,t+θi∑j=1nWijlnXhl,i,t+μi+εi+σi,tWhere i is the i th region; t is the year; ρ is the spatial lag coefficient of the dependent variable; Wij is the spatial weight matrix; yit is the explained variable; lnXhl,i,t indicates the logarithm of the value of the l th explanatory variable Xhl in the i th region in year t; Xkl,i,t indicates the control variable; αi, βi and θi are the elasticity coefficients of the explanatory variables; ∑j=1nWijlnXhl,i,t is the effect of the core explanatory variables; μi denotes the regional effect; εi denotes the time effect; σi,t denotes the random disturbance term.

The SDM model involves the spatial matrix model, and due to the influence of various insubstantial elements such as policy and economy, which can make each spatial unit affect each other, defining spatial relationships solely by geographic adjacency matrix, geographic distance, or economic weights may be biased. Therefore, this paper adopted the economic-geographic nested matrix by referring to previous studies [61]. This matrix is constructed with the idea of integrating economic and spatial relationships, with the assumption that regions in close spatial proximity influence each other, and that the level of regional economy is positively correlated with the influence on the surrounding regions. GeoDetector

The GeoDetector is a statistical method for detecting spatial variability, identifying interactions among variables and the degree of their influence on specific spatially distributed phenomena, and is widely used in detecting the mechanisms of complex influencing factors for complicated geographical phenomena. The detection results not only cover the explanatory power (q-value) of each independent variable on the dependent variable, but also allow to calculate the degree of interaction among the factors. Consequently, this paper conducted a holistic detection of the response degree between mortality rate per million tons and different indicators in each district using the GeoDetector, as shown in Equation (6) [62].(6)q=1−1Nσ2∑Nhσh2Where: q is the spatial heterogeneity of the indicator, the larger the value of q the stronger the spatial heterogeneity, the smaller the value of q the stronger the randomness; N is the number of samples, σ2 is the variance of the indicator, h is the number of partitions.

Spatial correlation detection of mortality rate per million tons

This paper employed the coefficient of variation to obtain the relative differences in provincial coal mines' mortality rate per million tons to reveal the time-series evolution characteristics of China's coal mine safety production level as time progresses. After that, this paper utilized the global spatial autocorrelation index Moran's I to determine whether the indicators are spatially correlated (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Results of the spatio-temporal diversity measurements of the provincial coal mortality rate per million tons.Fig. 1

Results of the spatio-temporal diversity measurements of the provincial coal mortality rate per million tons.

The Moran's I of the provincial coal mine safety production level evaluation indicators from 2003 to 2021 all passed the significance test (P < 0.05), indicating a significant correlation in the spatial dimension. However, the value changes significantly and turns negative in 2021. This paper analyzed China's coal industry according to its development stages, which were 2003–2011, 2012–2015, and 2016–2021.(1)From 2003 to 2011: the spatial correlation degree of the mortality rate per million tons exhibits a large fluctuation, which, combined with the coefficient of variation (Cv), indicates that the mortality rate per million tons has a tendency of increasing dispersion of values during this period. This characteristic also indicates indirectly that the inter-provincial difference in coal mine safety production levels has always existed and the degree of variation is relatively stable, but there may be a constant migration of the high (or low) value areas.(2)From 2012 to 2015: all Moran's I values show a fluctuating decline, meaning that the spatial correlation of the indicators diminishes and the dispersion of the distribution of the values increases. Combined with the significance test, the P-values of the indicators are all significantly increased and fluctuate substantially. This indicates that the state of coal mine safety production in each province changed considerably during 2012–2015, and it is inferred that the relatively stable clusters of high (or low) values in the previous period may have changed markedly in terms of spatial extent and distribution, thus leading to a break in the relative stability of coal mine safety production levels spatially.(3)From 2016 to 2021: Moran's I values for the mortality rate per million tons continue to decline and turn negative. This indicates that the spatial correlation of the mortality rate per million tons not only continues to weaken, but also turns negative in 2021. Such a change suggests that the spatial heterogeneity of China's coal mine safety levels has strengthened, with the inter-provincial safety levels changing from being close in value to a “high-low” or “low-high” distribution pattern. That is, the mortality rate per million tons forms a spatial phenomenon of high-value provinces encircling low-value provinces, or low-value provinces encircling high-value provinces, indicating that the safety status of some provinces differs from their surroundings, and the heterogeneity of coal mine safety levels among provinces is enhanced.

Spatial effect detection of safety level heterogeneity influencing factors

As variables are generally diffuse and dynamic, changes in the independent variables of a region may not only have effects on the local dependent variables, but also on the dependent variables of the neighboring regions, and the GeoDetector has the deficiency of being unable to identify the spatial action types of indicators, so the SDM is required for further spatial effect identification. The model can decompose the spatial effects of influencing factors by three indicators: “Direct” (i.e., direct effect), “Indirect” (i.e., indirect effect) and “Total” (i.e., total effect).

Analysis of direct effects

A total of 6 variables were found to affect the dependent variable through direct effects in the spatial effect decomposition detection, which are, in descending order of contribution: average annual wind speed > coal consumption > supervision efficiency > production efficiency # > average annual temperature > ex-factory price # (“#” indicates that the variable shows a negative effect). The higher significance levels and coefficients of these variables imply a stronger and direct mode of action in the driving force of changes in local coal mine safety levels.

In particular, the indicators of the excavation environment category also contribute more directly to the safety of local coal mines. The reasons for this characteristic are twofold. Firstly, the climate change is more regional in nature, as monsoon changes in different seasons affect the state of atmospheric circulation and contribute to different meteorological states nationwide, and even spatially contiguous regions may have both types of weather, for instance, the unique southwestern thermal depression phenomenon in the southwest in spring, which produces both high temperatures and less rain and localized heavy rainfall in Guizhou Province [28]. Under such circumstances, changes in meteorological indicators can have a direct impact on the regional coal mine production environment, especially the ventilation system [29]. A reasonable natural wind pressure can be beneficial for underground ventilation, yet strong changes in wind pressure can also lead to abnormal changes in air volume in mines, thus affecting fluctuations in underground gas concentration. Secondly, the current coal resources in China are mainly used for power generation and heating. Routinely, cooling or heating demand has periodicity and stability, hence the impact of temperature changes on coal mining plans is relatively stable. Moreover, temperature can be predicted in advance to reduce the stimulation of market demand by persistent climate change and to achieve a reasonable response to production risk during peak demand periods. It can be concluded that while the occurrence of extreme weather or sudden changes in meteorological conditions can lead to coal mine production safety accidents, natural climate change in China is characterized by periodicity and stability. With a comprehensive grasp of the characteristics of the changing laws of meteorological elements, advance preparation for the regulation and control of the corresponding plans and technical equipment for coal mine production in response to changes in the external environment would have a positive significance for the improvement of production safety assurance capacity.

What also needs to be concerned is coal consumption and supervision efficiency, these two indicators also show a significant positive endogenous effect. An increase in consumption can stimulate production subjects to continuously improve their production capacity to meet market demand, yet under the current supervision and monitoring system, fewer accidents and hidden dangers are essential to ensure that the production order can be properly promoted, thus motivating production subjects to continuously strengthen the production safety assurance capacity of enterprises while increasing their production capacity and reducing the downtime and rectification caused by accidents or penalties. Given that production efficiency and ex-factory price have both direct and indirect effects, the analysis shall be unified in the subsequent section.

Analysis of indirect effects

Among the indirect effects, the railroad operating mileage > GDP per capita > ex-factory price# > average labor compensation, which means that the increase or decrease of the above variables would cause the external regions to respond more significantly. This phenomenon is associated with the spatial linkage of economic development, where a high development level in one region is bound to give impetus to the neighboring regions, thereby influencing a simultaneous change in the production safety state which has a strong correlation with economic strength.

The effect degree of railroad operating mileage is even higher, as the increase of this indicator would facilitate the optimization and perfection of the transportation routes of coal products in the surrounding areas as well as the circulation state of the regional coal market, and balance and regulate the inter-regional supply and demand relationship by changing the regional market operation conditions, especially relieving the local production pressure in the demand-side market, thus achieving the objective of reducing safety risks. Consequently, economic development and infrastructure construction not only have a relatively strong single-factor influence on the safety development level of the coal industry, but the influence effect also has a spatial extensibility and linkage. In particular, it plays an important role in the improvement of safety level in the surrounding areas.

The average labor compensation has the highest driving force in the factor detection, yet it shows a positive indirect effect with low influence in the spatial effect evaluation, and the total effect of the indicator is a negative effect that fails to pass the significance test. This indicates that average labor compensation may have different effect patterns in different regions and vary considerably, thus exhibiting only a slight indirect effect under the national dimension.

Analysis of overall effects

Both indicators, production efficiency and ex-factory price, have not only negative effects on the local coal mine safety level, but also significant spillover effects on the surrounding areas. In particular, the direction of the spatial effect of production efficiency is opposite, requiring a joint analysis with the 3 indicators of direct effect, indirect effect and total effect.

The direct and indirect effects of the production efficiency indicator are −1.844 and 2.615 at the 0.001 significant level, respectively, with higher strength of the indirect effect than the direct effect, and the total effect is positive but fails to pass the significance test. This indicates that the change in indicators has an inhibitory effect on the local safety level improvement and a facilitative effect on the external side, and the insignificant total effect may be caused by regional differences. It should be noted that this feature cannot represent an intrinsic negative correlation between the increase in production efficiency and the enhancement of local industrial safety; rather, the increase in production efficiency is necessarily accompanied by an increase in production intensity, which then leads to a relative centralization of safety risks in the target location.

By combining the characteristics of the changes in mortality rate per million tons and production efficiency (Fig. 2),it can be observed that both of them are negatively correlated in the northern provinces of China and positively correlated in the southern provinces, yet both are basically in a negative relationship after 2018. This is due to the fact that the China coal industry was still in a period of deepening reform, and coal enterprises were generally in a state of continuous adjustment, with existing issues brought about by the rapid development of the previous period still not eliminated. When faced with the “multi-period superposition” of safety risks that emerged along with the increase in production efficiency, most enterprises had limited ability to respond and deal with them, leading to the positive effect of simultaneous changes in local production efficiency and mortality rates. On the whole, however, the improvement of production efficiency in a region would have a more significant positive promoting effect on the improvement of regional safety level, and this characteristic is consistent with the cluster effect of industrial development, which is also more conducive to the sustainable improvement of regional safety level. Hence, in the total effect, the indirect effect is stronger than the inhibitory effect of the individual spatial unit, which also further indicates that the improvement of production efficiency has a positive promoting effect on the overall state of safety production level even if it causes the relative concentration of safety risks in the local area.Fig. 2Relationship between mortality rate per million tons and production efficiency by province.Fig. 2

Nevertheless, the ex-factory price exhibited a significant spillover effect in the spatial effect evaluation, not only all 3 effects passed the significance test at the 0.01 level, but also the action attributes on the improvement of internal and external coal mine safety production levels remained the same, both of which were inhibitory characteristics, and the influence degree was higher for the external part. This is associated with the highly price-sensitive characteristics of the energy industry, where high profits can cause various risky operations or illegal mining situations to occur. Attention needs to be paid to provinces with significant price changes and to promptly carry out controls in the surrounding areas of the province to curb the negative spatial spillover effects caused by excessive local price fluctuations.

Overall, the influence coefficients of each indicator on the spatial heterogeneity of coal mine safety levels during the study period all passed the significance test (P < 0.05) with q-values ranging from 0.024 to 0.341 (the diagonal line is the single-factor explanatory power), indicating that all 15 indicators in this study have a certain influence on the generation of China's coal mine safety production level spatial heterogeneity.

From the perspective of indicator interaction, a total of 105 sets of factor combinations were generated, all of which were enhancement relationships. Among them, 39 % of the combinations are non-linear enhancement relationships, indicating that there exists a mutual promotion effect among indicators, and changes in one indicator can cause multiple indicators to change simultaneously, thereby generating the phenomenon of enhanced explanatory power for the spatial differentiation of the study object after the interaction of indicators. The combination of excavation environment indicators and other indicators not only has a non-linear enhancement relationship, but also has a high interaction coefficient in general. This indicates that the excavation environment indicators, as the fundamental assurance factors of coal mining, has the same linkage role as “dominoes” for safety production, and are the categories that need more attention in the daily safety management, especially the annual average temperature and the annual accumulated rainfall. This phenomenon also illustrates indirectly that there is an intrinsic correlation between various types of coal mine production safety conditions and changes in meteorological conditions, and that meteorological changes may have a greater impact on the safety status of coal mines.

Furthermore, indicators requiring special analysis are those in the supervision and law enforcement category. The influence of the indicators under this category is relatively weak and has limited interaction with other indicators, generating non-linear enhancement effects only with ex-factory price, average annual temperature, average annual wind speed and annual accumulated rainfall, especially with average annual temperature, indicating that the implementation of different supervision tools and intensity for different excavation environments is an issue that needs attention in safety production work. Taking the average annual temperature as an example (Fig. 4), the high values of mortality rate per million tons (ranked top 25 %) are concentrated in areas with average annual temperature of 15–20 °C, and the corresponding supervision efficiency is generally at the middle level (5–10 items/time), while the intensity of fines and supervision penalties are predominantly at the middle to lower level; the provinces with lower ranking (>75 %) are mainly concentrated in 5–15 °C, and the supervision efficiency is at the middle level, yet the intensity of fines and penalties are generally higher than the average value. This indicates that in provinces with high safety risks, there are more issues and fewer penalties; the opposite situation is found in provinces with relatively low safety risks. The issue that the implementation of the 3 indicators of supervision and law enforcement has not reached a dynamic balance may lead to the intensification of the gaming extent of government and enterprises, which in turn has an impact on safety indicators.Fig. 4Relationships among mortality rate per million tons, average annual temperature as well as supervision and law enforcement indicators.Fig. 4

Conclusions and discussion

This paper discriminates the dominant factors of spatio-temporal heterogeneity and their spatial effect types of China's coal mine safety production levels via GeoDetector and the Spatial Dubin Model (SDM), and clarifies the categories of indicator changes acting on local and surrounding areas are endogenous effects, spillover effects or both, as well as the action degree of each spatial effect. In the meantime, this paper also further visualizes the detection outcomes with the assistance of Neo4j graph database, and the findings indicate that.(1)All 15 indicators selected for the study have a certain influence on the generation of spatial heterogeneity in China's coal mine safety production level, and all of them have an enhancement relationship after their interaction. In particular, the combination of excavation environment indicators and other indicators not only features a non-linear enhancement relationship, but also generally has a high interaction coefficient. This indicates that excavation environment indicator, as a basic assurance factor of coal mining, has the same linkage role as “dominoes” for safety production, which is a category that requires greater attention in daily safety management.(2)With regard to spatial effects, the industrial development effect > capital allocation effect > production environment effect > government supervision effect > enterprise management effect. Specific to the indicators, the average annual temperature, average annual wind speed, coal consumption and supervision efficiency mainly affect the dependent variable via direct effects with significant effect degree and high coefficients. GDP per capita, average labor compensation and railroad operating mileage have positive spatial spillover on changes in coal mine safety production levels in surrounding areas. The evaluation of the spatial effect for average labor compensation exhibits a positive indirect effect with low influence. Both indicators, the production efficiency and ex-factory price, have not only negative effects on the local coal mine safety level, but also have significant spillover effects on the surrounding areas.(3)Both factor detection and spatial effects demonstrate that the excavation environment indicator is characterized by strong single-factor influence, high-level interaction ability, and remarkable spatial effects, which also indicates that the influence brought by this category on coal mine production safety is of higher indiscernibility and potentialities, and is one of the major obstacles to the improvement of production safety level.(4)All types of influencing factors are characterized by uneven distribution spatially, with different impacts on production safety levels in different regions, and there may be situations where the influence of indicators is offset by each other when measured holistically. It is required to further analyze the relationship between changes in the excavation environment and coal mine safety in subsequent studies, to deeply explore the influence laws of environmental changes, especially changes in meteorological conditions For this reason, it is necessary to further strengthen the evaluation of zonal effects, refine the effect and intensity of different influencing factors from a regional perspective, especially to deeply explore the influence laws of environmental changes(in particular, changes in meteorological conditions), and provide more targeted references for the designation of measures to assure coal mine production safety.(5)The study of coal mine safety production is important and the influence factors are still changing, it is engaged for us to do study further. In the future, we may consider more from spatial-temporal perspective, and real-time monitoring data are introduced and a data-driven approach can used to further refine the impact factors and their degree of influence.

(3)Both factor detection and spatial effects demonstrate that the excavation environment indicator is characterized by strong single-factor influence, high-level interaction ability, and remarkable spatial effects, which also indicates that the influence brought by this category on coal mine production safety is of higher indiscernibility and potentialities, and is one of the major obstacles to the improvement of production safety level.

(4)All types of influencing factors are characterized by uneven distribution spatially, with different impacts on production safety levels in different regions, and there may be situations where the influence of indicators is offset by each other when measured holistically. It is required to further analyze the relationship between changes in the excavation environment and coal mine safety in subsequent studies, to deeply explore the influence laws of environmental changes, especially changes in meteorological conditions For this reason, it is necessary to further strengthen the evaluation of zonal effects, refine the effect and intensity of different influencing factors from a regional perspective, especially to deeply explore the influence laws of environmental changes(in particular, changes in meteorological conditions), and provide more targeted references for the designation of measures to assure coal mine production safety.

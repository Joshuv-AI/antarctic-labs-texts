The complete chloroplast genome sequence of Maesa cavinervis (Primulaceae s.l.) and its phylogenetic analysis
Authors: Qin-Ke Shi, Nan-Fei Wu, Bei Zhang, Zhi Feng, Guang-Long Wang, Yu Feng, Bo Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMaesa cavinervis is a species of the genus Maesa in the family Primulaceae s.l. At present, the complete chloroplast information and systematic position of M. cavinervis have not been reported. In this study, we sequenced and assembled the complete chloroplast genome of M. cavinervis using Illumina sequencing technology. The complete chloroplast genome of M. cavinervis is 156,743 bp in length and consists of a large single-copy region (LSC; 87,353 bp) and a small single-copy region (SSC; 18,120 bp), which are separated by a pair of inverted-repeat regions (IRs; 25,635 bp). A total of 128 genes were annotated, including 83 protein-coding genes, 37 tRNA genes, and 8 rRNA genes. The overall GC content was 37.3%. Phylogenetic analyses including eight Maesa species and 11 species from five other genera of Primulaceae and showed that M. cavinervis is closely related to M. hupehensis.Keywords: Maesa cavinervis, chloroplast genome, phylogeny

Introduction

The genus Maesa Forssk. (1775) belongs to Primulaceae s.l. Batsch ex Borkh. (1794). It consists of approximately 200 species, mainly distributed in the tropics of the Eastern Hemisphere, with approximately 29 species and one variety in China, distributed throughout the south of the Yangtze River Basin (Chen 1979). Maesa cavinervis C. Chen. (1978) is a narrowly endemic species native to Mêdog County, Tibet Autonomous Prefecture, and is distributed under broad-leaved forests on mountain slopes at altitudes of 1700–2050 m (Figure 1). Its morphology is similar to that of Maesa densistriata C. Chen & C. M. Hu. (1994), but it can be distinguished by its slightly thicker, lanceolate or narrowly lanceolate leaf blades with caudate-acuminate apices, depressed leaf surface veins, obviously elevated veins on the abaxial surface, and lateral veins that anastomose into marginal veins (Chen 1978). The limited known distribution of M. cavinervis and related studies emphasize the importance of in-depth research. As of June 23, 2025, complete chloroplast genome sequences are available for only eight species of Maesa, representing approximately 4% of the total number of species within the genus.

Therefore, in this study, we aimed to assemble the complete chloroplast genome of M. cavinervis and examine its phylogenetic relationships with closely related species at the scale of the chloroplast genome. This study aims to address the scarcity of chloroplast genome data within the genus and provide new molecular insights into the phylogeny and conservation biology of Primulaceae.

Materials and methods

Fresh leaves of M. cavinervis were collected on October 22, 2024, from Mêdog County, Tibet Autonomous Region (29° 32′ N, 95° 37′ E, elevation ca. 2,131 m). The voucher specimen (voucher: YLZB10330; contact: Xu Bo, xubo@cib.ac.cn) has been deposited in the Herbarium of the Chengdu Institute of Biology (CDBI), Chinese Academy of Sciences. Total genomic DNA was extracted using a Plant DNA Isolation Kit (Cat. No. DE-06111). Paired-end (2 × 150 bp) libraries were prepared and sequenced on an Illumina platform. Raw sequencing data were filtered using fastp v0.23.2 (Chen et al. 2018) with the specific parameters ‘-q 20 -u 30 -l 50’. The chloroplast genome was assembled using GetOrganelle v1.7.5 (Jin et al. 2020) with the following parameters: ‘-R 10 -t 20 -w 0.6 -k 21,45,65,85,105 -F emb plant_pt’. Using Maesa hupehensis Rehder. (1916) (GenBank: MZ846203) as the reference genome, genome annotation was performed using the plastid genome annotator (PGA) (Qu et al. 2019). The average coverage percent for the assembled cp genome was 12399.86× (Figure S1) and then Geneious was used to manually correct for start and stop codons (Kearse et al. 2012). Finally, the annotated chloroplast genome was visualized using CPGview (http://www.1 kmpg.cn/cpgview).

A total of 20 chloroplast genome sequences, including 19 downloaded from NCBI, were used to construct a phylogenetic tree, comprising 19 species from Primulaceae, and Actinidia cylindrica (GenBank accession number: MK550716) from Actinidiaceae as an outgroup (Rose et al. 2018). All sequences were aligned using MAFFT v7 (Katoh and Standley 2013) with default parameters. Nucleotide (NT) sequences were used for all phylogenetic analyses. The concatenated alignment was analyzed without partitioning (as a single partition). A maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was constructed using IQ-TREE v2.1.2 (Minh et al. 2020). The built-in ModelFinder function automatically selected the optimal substitution model (TVM+F + I + G4) based on the Bayesian information criterion (BIC). Branch support was assessed using 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates. The final phylogenetic tree was visualized using FigTree v1.4.4.

Results

The complete chloroplast genome of Maesa cavinervis (GenBank: PV662410) exhibited a typical circular tetrameric structure (Figure 2), with a total length of 156,743 bp, comprising a large single-copy region (LSC, 87,353 bp), a small single-copy region (SSC, 18,120 bp), and a pair of 25,635 bp inverted repeat regions (IRa/IRb). The genome contains 128 genes, including 83 protein-coding genes (CDS), 37 transfer RNAs (tRNAs) genes, and 8 ribosomal RNAs (rRNAs). Among these, nine genes (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl12, ndhB, and ndhA) contained a single intron, two genes (ycf3, clpP) contained 2 introns (Figure S2), and the splicing gene rps12 had three unique exons (Figure S3) distributed across the LSC and IR regions. The overall GC content of the chloroplast genome was 37.3%.

Genes located on the inner and outer sides of the circle are transcribed in the clockwise and counterclockwise directions, respectively, and the dark gray inner circle indicates the distribution of GC content. The phylogenetic tree constructed based on the complete chloroplast genomes of 19 Primulaceae species showed that the bootstrap support (BS) rates for most branch nodes were ≥ 90% (Figure 3), indicating that the phylogenetic tree had a highly reliable topological structure. Among these, Maesa forms a highly supported monophyletic group (BS = 100%), while M. cavinervis (GenBank accession number: PV662410) and M. hupehensis (GenBank accession number: MZ846203) form a sister group with a relative moderate support (BS = 81%).

Discussion and conclusions

This study presents the first complete chloroplast genome sequence of Maesa cavinervis. The genome’s size and gene content are comparable to those of the previously published complete chloroplast genome of M. hupehensis (156,743 bp v.s. 157,005 bp; Li et al. 2022). Specifically, M. cavinervis differs from M. hupehensis in having a slightly shorter LSC (87,353 bp v.s. 87,628 bp) and a slightly longer SSC (18,120 bp v.s. 18,111 bp), while the IR regions are similar.

The minimal genome size difference between M. cavinervis and M. hupehensis (262 bp) is consistent with the general observation that chloroplast genome lengths are highly conserved at the genus level (Turudić et al. 2023).

Phylogenetic analysis suggests that M. cavinervis is part of the Maesa clade and is closely related to M. hupehensis in terms of phylogenetic relationships. However, due to the limited number of chloroplast genome samples available for the Maesa genus and the reliance on chloroplast genomes as the sole molecular marker for phylogeny, this study could not precisely determine the exact position of M. cavinervis. Therefore, further research is recommended to clarify the phylogenetic relationships within the Maesa genus and its connections to other genera within the Primulaceae family.

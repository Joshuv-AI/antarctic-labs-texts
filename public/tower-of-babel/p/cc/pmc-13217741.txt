Emerging therapeutic advances for immune thrombocytopenia: highlights from selected early‑phase and pivotal trials at the 2025 ASH annual meeting
Authors: Linyu Cao, Zhe Wang, Qian Xiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis letter highlights emerging immune-targeted therapies for immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) presented at the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting. We summarize pivotal data, including the Phase III VAYHIT2 trial of Ianalumab, alongside novel early-phase agents targeting BAFF-R, CD38, CD19, PI3K, and cellular therapies (CD19/BCMA CAR-T), which provide crucial biological insights and hypothesis-generating signals for future ITP management.

To the editor,

Primary immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) is an acquired autoimmune bleeding disorder characterized by persistent thrombocytopenia and increased hemorrhagic risk. Corticosteroids, thrombopoietin receptor agonists (TPO‑RAs), and B‑cell–directed therapies remain the cornerstone of management but are limited by incomplete and often transient responses, treatment-related toxicity, and frequent relapse. Novel, immune‑targeted approaches that can induce durable, treatment‑free remission are therefore urgently needed.

At the 2025 American Society of Hematology (ASH) Annual Meeting, multiple trials highlighted a rapidly diversifying therapeutic landscape. Here, we highlight selected Phase I/II studies and one pivotal Phase III trial that collectively illustrate a rapidly diversifying therapeutic landscape (Table 1).

Modulation of B-cell survival and signaling has emerged as a major therapeutic focus. The pivotal Phase III VAYHIT2 trial (NCT05653219) evaluated Ianalumab, a first‑in‑class BAFF‑R antibody, in steroid‑refractory primary ITP [1]. Ianalumab plus eltrombopag significantly prolonged the time to treatment failure versus placebo plus eltrombopag (median 13.0 months [9 mg/kg], not reached [3 mg/kg], vs. 4.7 months [placebo]). These findings raise the possibility that BAFF-R blockade may have disease-modifying potential. Similarly, ESG206, another anti‑BAFF‑R antibody, showed encouraging early activity (NCT06853444), with 50% of patients achieving complete response (CR) at a median of 7 days [2]. Further upstream, restoration of B-cell tolerance through PI3Kδ inhibition has also been explored with oral Linperlisib (ChiCTR2400083875), which yielded a 66.7% overall response rate in multi-refractory patients [3].

Deep depletion of B-cell lineages and long-lived plasma cells may represent a biologically relevant strategy for refractory ITP. Targeting CD19 aims to deplete plasmablasts alongside B-cells. Accordingly, Budoprutug, a low-fucosylated anti-CD19 monoclonal antibody with enhanced antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), is entering Phase I/II evaluation in ITP (NCT07043946) [4]. Similarly, CID‑103, an anti‑CD38 monoclonal antibody targeting autoantibody-producing plasma cells, showed feasibility in an interim analysis (NCT07017725), with two of three evaluable patients achieving platelet responses [5].

Cellular therapies are also entering the ITP continuum. MC-1-50, an autologous CD19 CAR‑T therapy, was tested in multi‑refractory ITP (NCT06352281) [6].Three of four patients achieved objective responses, with autoantibodies becoming undetectable, though platelet recovery was heterogeneous. Extending this to plasma cell depletion, a pilot study of BCMA-targeted CAR‑T (PRG‑1801; NCT06519565) reported rapid CRs in three patients receiving higher doses, correlating with near-elimination of plasma cells [7]. While these findings highlight the biological complexity of ITP, they remain exploratory.

Finally, in pediatric high‑risk ITP, a Phase I trial of dose‑dense rituximab (NCT04323748) reported a higher proportion of continuous CR at 6 months compared to standard care (100% vs. 67.5%; p = 0.059, non-significant but encouraging trend toward prolonged remission), suggesting a potential exploratory benefit in selected children [8].

Conceptually, these therapies span a spectrum from modulating B-cell signaling (PI3K, BAFF-R) to the deep depletion of B-cell lineages (CD19, CD20) and long-lived plasma cells (CD38, BCMA) (Table 2). However, it is critical to note that, aside from the pivotal VAYHIT2 trial, most cited data remain exploratory. Current evidence is limited by small sample sizes, short follow-up, and a lack of head-to-head comparisons. Therefore, conclusions regarding durable, treatment-free remission must be interpreted cautiously as hypothesis-generating rather than established clinical benefit. Future trials incorporating biomarker-guided designs and long-term follow-up are essential to clarify optimal sequencing and to distinguish true disease-modifying potential from transient responses.

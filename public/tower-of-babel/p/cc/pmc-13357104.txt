Combinatorial post-translational modification reprogramming of the endomembrane system in colorectal cancer
Authors: Jie Du, Wei Zhang, Wencong Song, Yujie Zhang, Erjiao Hao, Tianyuan Li, Min Feng, Feng Zhu, Yong Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe endomembrane system plays a pivotal role in protein synthesis, trafficking, and degradation, and has been implicated in colorectal cancer (CRC) progression. Post-translational modifications (PTMs) regulate endomembrane-associated proteins, but a comprehensive understanding of how multiple PTMs collectively impact the endomembrane system in CRC remains limited. This study aimed to systematically map multi-PTM landscapes in CRC and uncover potential regulatory nodes within the endomembrane system.MethodsWe developed a multi-PTM proteomic atlas of CRC by profiling phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation in paired tumor and adjacent normal tissues (n=8 pairs). The PTM datasets were derived from an in-house CRC cohort. Differentially modified proteins (DMPs) were annotated, structurally mapped, and integrated into protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks to explore regulatory patterns associated with the endomembrane system.ResultsWe identified extensive PTM alterations in CRC, including 84 phosphorylation, 123 ubiquitination, and 16 malonylation sites. LMNB1 and LMNB2 emerged as combined PTM proteins, with alterations in phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation potentially influencing nuclear pore function, chromatin organization, and the activation of the WNT/β-catenin pathway. These findings underscore LMNB1/LMNB2 may play a potential role in the regulation of the CRC endomembrane system, offering potential targets for further mechanistic and therapeutic studies.ConclusionsThis study provides a multi-PTM resource delineating the CRC endomembrane system. The identified modification hotspots, such as multi-modified LMNB1/2, offer promising molecular candidates for further mechanistic studies of endomembrane dysregulation in CRC.

Highlight box

Key findings

• This is the first multi-post-translational modification (PTM) atlas (phosphorylation, ubiquitination, malonylation) of colorectal cancer (CRC) endomembrane system. LMNB1 and LMNB2 were identified as proteins harboring three types of differential PTMs in CRC, which have been previously implicated in the WNT/β-catenin pathway.

What is known and what is new?

• Endomembrane system dysfunction contributes to CRC progression through altered protein trafficking, degradation, organelle communication, and metabolic adaptation. Although individual PTMs have been profiled in CRC, their integrated regulation within the endomembrane system remains unclear. This study provides a multi-layer PTM perspective and identifies LMNB1/2 as multi-PTM proteins in CRC, each exhibiting three types of differential modifications, thereby highlighting them as potential PTM-regulated nodes.

• Provides an integrated multi-PTM analysis of the CRC endomembrane system, encompassing phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation. Reveals distinct yet complementary functional roles of different PTM layers in membrane trafficking, proteostasis, and metabolism. Identifies proteins with combinatorial PTM patterns, including LMNB1/2, which may represent PTM-regulated nodes. Establishes a systems-level framework for understanding how multi-PTM crosstalk contributes to endomembrane remodeling in CRC.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Multi-PTM profiling may provide a more comprehensive framework for understanding endomembrane remodeling in CRC. The identified PTM events require validation in larger, independent cohorts. Mechanistic investigations should determine how multi-PTM crosstalk influences CRC progression.

• Future work may explore the translational potential of PTM-based biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the most common malignancies worldwide and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality (1,2). Although genomic and transcriptomic studies have defined major driver mutations and molecular subtypes, these alterations do not fully explain how tumor cells reorganize intracellular systems to sustain growth, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance (3). Increasing evidence suggests that beyond genetic changes, malignant progression involves structural and functional remodeling of core cellular architectures (4).

The endomembrane system, encompassing the nuclear envelope, endoplasmic reticulum (ER), Golgi apparatus, endosomes, lysosomes, and vesicular trafficking machinery—serves as a central platform for protein maturation, membrane transport, receptor recycling, and stress adaptation (5). Endomembrane system dysfunction is increasingly recognized as a driver of cancer progression (6), particularly in CRC (7). Aberrant nuclear envelope, ER-Golgi, endolysosomal, and vesicular trafficking pathways disrupt genome stability, proteostasis, receptor signaling, and metabolic adaptation, thereby promoting tumor growth, metastasis, and therapy resistance (6,8-10).

Post-translational modifications (PTMs) provide rapid and reversible regulation of protein activity, stability, localization, and interactions (11,12). Rather than acting independently, phosphorylation, ubiquitination, acetylation, succinylation, malonylation, and other PTMs can cooperate, antagonize, or occur sequentially to form dynamic regulatory networks (13). Combinatorial PTM crosstalk integrates oncogenic, metabolic, and endomembrane regulation, underscoring the value of combinatorial PTM analysis in cancer research (14). Thus, combinatorial PTM analysis may uncover potentially regulatory mechanisms underlying endomembrane system remodeling and identify potential clinically relevant targets in CRC (15).

In this study, we constructed an integrated multi-PTM proteomic atlas of CRC, profiling phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and lysine malonylation in paired tumor and adjacent tissues. By organizing differential modification patterns within structural and interaction networks, we sought to delineate how combinatorial PTM regulation contributes to endomembrane remodeling in CRC. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0526/rc).

Methods

Data sources

The phosphorylation, ubiquitinomics, and malonylomics data used in study were derived from paired cancerous and adjacent normal tissue samples from the same CRC patients (n=8) (16-18). Tissues samples were obtained from the patients who are diagnosed with primary CRC at Shenzhen People’s Hospital and who underwent surgical resection without receiving preoperative radiotherapy or chemotherapy were enrolled in this study. Clinical data, histopathological characteristics, and treatment-related information were retrieved from the patients’ electronic medical records. All participants were fully informed about the study and voluntarily signed written informed consent forms. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shenzhen People’s Hospital (No. LL-KY-2019213).

The endomembrane-related gene set was obtained from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database (19). Definition of the endomembrane system. In this study, the endomembrane system was operationally defined as the union of proteins annotated in the HPA to the peroxisomes, lysosomes, nuclear membrane, Golgi apparatus, and ER, which were then combined to create the endomembrane system-related protein set. This set was subsequently intersected with our experimentally identified differentially modified proteins (DMPs) in CRC to define the CRC endomembrane DMPs. Protein structure and sequence information were retrieved from the UniProt database (https://www.uniprot.org/), which served as the foundation for subsequent modification site localization and functional annotation analyses.

Functional enrichment of DMPs

Functional annotation and interaction network analysis of DMPs were performed using integrated bioinformatics tools. Initially, based on the filtered DMP list (gene symbols), functional enrichment analysis was conducted via the Metascape platform (https://metascape.org/gp/index.html) (20). This analysis encompassed Gene Ontology (GO), biological processes (BPs), molecular functions (MFs), cellular components (CCs), and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05 (adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg method), and enrichment significance was evaluated using the hypergeometric test. Significantly enriched pathways were clustered using the built-in MCODE algorithm to generate functional module network diagrams (21).

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction and identification of central proteins

Subsequently, PPI networks were constructed using the STRING database (https://string-db.org/) with a confidence score cutoff of 0.7, integrating experimentally validated, database-annotated, and text-mined interactions (22). Network data were exported and visualized using Cytoscape software (https://cytoscape.org/) for topological analysis (20). We used the CytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape to identify hub proteins based on network centrality metrics. The maximal clique centrality (MCC) method was applied to determine the top 10 hub proteins in the CRC endomembrane system network. To further elucidate the biological significance of interaction modules, network submodules were imported into Cytoscape (MCODE) for secondary functional enrichment analysis.

Protein site localization and annotation

The UniProt database (https://www.uniprot.org/) was utilized to retrieve amino acid sequences and three-dimensional (3D) structural information for target proteins. To analyze the spatial distribution characteristics of modification sites, crystal structures of target proteins were loaded into PYMOL molecular visualization software (v3.1.3.1). Domains were color-coded based on UniProt annotations (23). This integrated approach enabled multi-dimensional validation from sequence annotation to structural localization, providing spatial-conformational evidence for investigating the functional relevance of modification sites.

Pathway network mapping of PTM proteins involved in the CRC endomembrane system

To systematically decipher potential endomembrane system regulatory networks in CRC, this study first integrated all significantly DMPs identified in CRC tissues. Subsequently, leveraging information from GO and KEGG databases, alongside relevant reviews and experimental studies in PubMed, the functions of these proteins were determined. Finally, based on protein regulatory relationships and interactions between BPs reported in the existing literature, a molecular mechanistic model of enzyme regulatory pathways related to endomembrane system in CRC patients was formulated.

Statistical analysis

Differential PTM sites were defined by an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) ≥0.58 cutoff (1.5-fold) and were compared between tumor and matched adjacent tissues using paired t-tests; P value <0.05 were considered significant (*, P<0.05; **, P<0.01; ***, P<0.001).

Results

PTM landscapes of endomembrane system proteins in CRC tissues

Given the emerging evidence that endomembrane remodeling contributes to CRC progression, yet the underlying post-translational regulatory mechanisms remain incompletely defined (24,25), we profiled phosphorylation, malonylation, and ubiquitination patterns of endomembrane system-related proteins in CRC tissues and matched adjacent normal tissues. As a result, widespread differential PTM regulation was observed within the CRC endomembrane system (Figure 1A). At the ubiquitination level, 55 modification sites were upregulated and 68 sites were downregulated; at the phosphorylation level, 26 sites were upregulated and 58 sites were downregulated; and at the malonylation level, 1 site was upregulated and 15 sites were downregulated. Collectively, these patterns suggest that ubiquitination represents the most prominent layer of PTM remodeling within the CRC endomembrane system, whereas malonylation alterations appear comparatively limited in scope.

To further characterize the magnitude and distribution patterns of PTM alterations within the CRC endomembrane system, we compared the FCs of the three PTM types between tumor and adjacent tissues. The FCs of the three PTM types in tumor versus adjacent tissues exhibited substantial variation, with maximum differences reaching 4.6-fold (Figure 1B). Notably, ubiquitination sites not only accounted for the largest proportion of differential events but also displayed the broadest distribution range, suggesting greater heterogeneity in ubiquitin-dependent regulation of the CRC endomembrane system relative to phosphorylation and malonylation.

Volcano plot analysis further identified significant up- or down-regulation of PTMs on multiple proteins associated with endomembrane function, including FAF2, CYP51A1, and PDXDC1 (Figure 1C). Several of these proteins harbored multiple differential PTM sites, underscoring their potential importance as regulatory nodes in endomembrane remodeling in CRC. In particular, CYP51A1 showed coordinated upregulation at multiple modification sites, suggesting an active PTM environment and multilayered modification dysregulation within endomembrane-associated pathways.

To further delineate the regulatory complexity and distribution characteristics of PTM alterations within the CRC endomembrane system, we examined the distribution and abundance of proteins carrying differential PTM sites across the three modification types (Figure 1D), showing most endomembrane-associated proteins carried only a single differential PTM site, whereas a smaller subset exhibited multiple modification sites, predominantly within ubiquitination (Figure 1E). In contrast, phosphorylation and especially malonylation showed fewer proteins with multiple PTM sites, indicating a comparatively lower degree of combinatorial modification complexity. Collectively, these findings reveal extensive PTM remodeling of endomembrane system-related proteins in CRC.

Multiple PTMs of the nuclear lamina core proteins LMNB1/2 in CRC

Proteins carrying multiple PTMs probably function as integrative nodes within complex regulatory networks (26). To explore potential proteins crosstalk in the CRC endomembrane system, we examined the overlap among differentially phosphorylated, malonylated, and ubiquitinated proteins and integrated these data with the endomembrane protein set.

The intersection analysis revealed that only LMNB1 and LMNB2 exhibited concurrent alterations in phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation levels in CRC compared with normal controls. Specifically, LMNB1 showed upregulated modification at K532, whereas LMNB1(K182) and multiple LMNB2 sites, including K150, K133, and K123, displayed decreased modification levels across the corresponding combinatorial PTM groups (Figure 2A,2B). These results indicate pronounced site-specific and protein-specific PTM remodeling of lamin B proteins rather than a uniform directional regulation pattern. In addition, several proteins exhibited coordinated alterations in two types of PTMs, including COPA, which showed differential phosphorylation and ubiquitination at K13 and K271, as well as ANXA5, displaying concurrent ubiquitination and malonylation changes at K76 and K310. These findings suggest selective crosstalk between distinct PTM types on specific endomembrane-associated proteins in CRC.

LMNB1 and LMNB2 are B-type lamins and core structural components of the nuclear lamina, which is continuous with the nuclear envelope and functionally linked to the endomembrane system (27). Beyond their canonical roles in nuclear architecture, lamins contribute to chromatin organization, gene regulation, and nucleocytoplasmic transport. In CRC, aberrant lamin expression has been associated with tumor progression, and our findings suggest that multi-layer PTM regulation of LMNB1/LMNB2 may represent an additional mechanism influencing nuclear envelope dynamics and endomembrane-associated signaling outputs (28).

To investigate the structural context of these modifications, we mapped PTM sites onto the protein structures of LMNB1 and LMNB2, and integrated 3D conformational views with linear domain organization (Figure 2C,2D). For LMNB1, modified residues were localized at S23 (phosphorylation), K182 (ubiquitination), K474 (ubiquitination), and K532 (malonylation). For LMNB2, modified residues were mapped to K123 (phosphorylation), K133 (phosphorylation), K150 (ubiquitination), and S405 (malonylation). The structural models and corresponding linear domain maps show that phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation are distributed across distinct regions rather than randomly clustered, supporting the notion of domain-specific PTM regulation.

Notably, several modification sites in both LMNB1 and LMNB2 are positioned in spatially separated but functionally relevant regions, implying that different PTMs may coordinately influence lamin conformation, protein stability, and interaction interfaces (29). Given the central role of the nuclear envelope in membrane continuity and intracellular compartmental communication, this multi-site PTM architecture provides a structural framework for understanding how lamin regulation may contribute to endomembrane system remodeling in CRC.

Taken together, these findings identify LMNB1 and LMNB2 as candidate multi-PTM integrative nodes in the CRC endomembrane system and provide structural evidence that combinatorial PTM patterns may participate in remodeling nuclear envelope-associated functions during colorectal tumorigenesis.

Distinct functional enrichment patterns across PTM classes in CRC

To elucidate the functional implications of differential PTMs alterations within the CRC endomembrane system, we performed GO enrichment analysis on DMPs across BPs, CCs, and MFs. Differentially phosphorylated proteins, were primarily enriched in BPs related to intracellular protein transport, endomembrane system organization, and regulation of cytoskeleton organization (Figure 3A), suggesting that phosphorylation potentially plays a role in regulating membrane trafficking and cytoskeletal dynamics, which are critical for the integrity and function of the endomembrane system in CRC cells. In terms of CCs, the most enriched categories included Golgi vesicle transport and endomembrane system components such as the ER and nuclear envelope. These enrichments highlight the probable functions of phosphorylation in maintaining the membrane trafficking machinery and supporting the dynamic reshaping of the CRC endomembrane system. Moreover, key MFs like protein kinase binding and GTPase binding suggest phosphorylation’s role in regulating signal transduction and cytoskeletal interactions, both of which are crucial for endomembrane function.

Differentially ubiquitinated proteins, on the other hand, were strongly associated with protein degradation, DNA repair, and autophagy (Figure 3B). This indicates that ubiquitination plays a potential role in regulating protein quality control and maintaining the integrity of the endomembrane system by promoting the turnover of damaged or excess proteins, thus ensuring cellular homeostasis in CRC cells. Enrichments in endosomes and lysosomes point to the involvement of ubiquitination in endocytic trafficking and membrane degradation pathways, both may be essential for maintaining the structural integrity and functionality of the endomembrane system CRC. The top MFs for ubiquitinated proteins, including ubiquitin binding and protein transport activity, further emphasize its potential role in regulating membrane-associated protein sorting and trafficking within CRC cells.

In contrast, differentially malonylated proteins were predominantly enriched in metabolic processes, particularly lipid metabolism and mitochondrial function (Figure 3C). These findings suggest that malonylation may be involved in metabolic reprogramming in CRC cells, particularly in the context of lipid biosynthesis and membrane biogenesis, which are essential for maintaining the altered metabolic state in cancer cells. Enrichment in the mitochondrion further supports the potential role of malonylation in regulating mitochondrial function and energy metabolism within CRC cells. The top MFs for malonylated proteins include oxidoreductase activity and protein domain-specific binding, suggesting its involvement in oxidative metabolism and regulating PPIs within the endomembrane system.

In the KEGG pathway analysis (Figure 3D), differentially phosphorylated proteins were enriched in pathways related to membrane trafficking and cell signaling, such as membrane system organization and endocytic vesicle formation. This highlights their potential role in regulating protein activity and cellular responses. DMPs were enriched in lipid metabolism and mitochondrial function, reflecting their potential involvement in energy production and fatty acid metabolism. Differentially ubiquitinated proteins were associated with protein degradation and autophagy, key processes for maintaining cellular homeostasis by degrading damaged proteins and organelles. Some pathways showed enrichment for both phosphorylation and ubiquitination, like endocytic vesicle formation, suggesting their potentially cooperative role in vesicle trafficking. These findings align with the distinct roles of each PTM in CRC progression, with phosphorylation in signaling and trafficking, malonylation in metabolism, and ubiquitination in protein turnover and autophagy.

Proportions of endomembrane system-related DMPs in CRC and their compartmental distribution across PTM classes

To assess the contribution of endomembrane system-related proteins to CRC DMPs, we calculated the overall proportion of endomembrane-related DMPs within the total CRC DMPs and the proportion within each PTM class. The overall proportion of endomembrane system DMPs was found to be 14.85% of the total CRC DMPs (Figure 4A). Specifically, 14.35% of phosphorylation DMPs, 15.64% of ubiquitination DMPs, and 10.57% of malonylation DMPs were assigned to endomembrane system-related proteins (Figure 4B-4D).

Subcellular localization analysis showed that phosphorylated proteins were mainly localized to the cytoplasm (35.0%) and nucleus (33.3%), with 20.0% localized to endomembrane-associated regions (Figure 4E). Ubiquitinated proteins exhibited broader distribution, with 27.5% localized to endomembrane-associated compartments (Figure 4F). In contrast, malonylated proteins were predominantly found in the cytoplasm (58.3%) and nucleus (25.0%), with no significant localization to endomembrane compartments (Figure 4G).

These findings suggest that the maintenance of endomembrane system homeostasis may play an important role in CRC progression. Additionally, the distinct compartmental preferences of different PTM classes highlight the complex regulation of endomembrane proteins in CRC.

Differential PTMs define functional modules that interact within the CRC endomembrane system

To elucidate the integrated regulatory network of phosphorylation, malonylation, and ubiquitination within the CRC endomembrane system, we constructed PPI networks for each PTM class using STRING and analyzed them in Cytoscape (Figure 5A). These networks revealed potential interactions among differentially modified endomembrane proteins, suggesting that PTMs may coordinate signaling and functional processes within CRC. In Figure 5B, we used the CytoHubba plugin in Cytoscape to identify hub proteins based on network centrality metrics. The MCC method was applied to determine the top 10 hub proteins in the CRC endomembrane system network. The analysis revealed that RPS3A, RPS11, RPS12, and RPL4 were the highest-ranking hub proteins. We further explored the biological functions of the modified proteins using Metascape, which provided insights into their functional categories (Figure 5C). The enrichment analysis suggested that these proteins are potentially associated with key processes such as vesicle-mediated transport, membrane organization, lipid biosynthesis, and signal transduction, which may play a role in endomembrane system regulation in CRC.

Subsequently, using the MCODE algorithm (Figure 5D-5F), the DMPs we focused on are mainly involved in three functional modules. DMPs such as RPL4 and RPS20, which participate in protein synthesis and ribosome biogenesis, suggest a potential role in cellular functions and endomembrane system regulation. DMPs like LMNB1 and NUCB1 are involved in membrane trafficking and vesicle-mediated transport, potentially affecting endomembrane system remodeling in CRC. Additionally, DMPs such as LMNB1 and LMNB2 are associated with nuclear integrity and protein import/export, likely regulating the nuclear membrane system in CRC. Through their involvement in these PPI network modules, these DMPs may play a potential role in the remodeling of the endomembrane system in CRC and provide valuable insights into the function of PTMs in tumor progression.

The PTM-mediated remodeling of endomembrane system regulatory pathways in CRC

To systematically elucidate the potential mechanisms by which differential PTMs regulate the endomembrane system in CRC, we integrated all significantly DMPs across the phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation datasets, followed by functional annotation and classification using GO and KEGG databases together with literature mining. By leveraging known PPI and BP crosstalk, we constructed a putative regulatory network of the CRC endomembrane system to depict how PTMs may coordinately mediate endomembrane remodeling and functional adaptation during tumorigenesis (Figure 6).

In our study, we identified LMNB1 and LMNB2 as proteins harboring three types of differential PTMs, with alterations in phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation. Structural mapping revealed that these PTMs are distributed across distinct regions of both proteins, suggesting coordinated regulation at multiple sites. The modified sites on LMNB1 included S23 (phosphorylation), K182 (ubiquitination), and K474 and K532 (both associated with malonylation and ubiquitination). In LMNB2, key modified residues were K123, K133, K150, and S405 (phosphorylation). Given that LMNB1/LMNB2 are core components of the the nuclear lamina within the endomembrane system (30,31), these modifications may influence nuclear pore function, chromatin organization, and nuclear envelope integrity, providing a physical foundation for the activation of the WNT/β-catenin pathway, a central driver of CRC progression. In CRC, WNT/β-catenin signaling plays a pivotal role in tumorigenesis (32). APC mutations or loss of Axin lead to β-catenin stabilization, promoting its nuclear translocation and activation of transcriptional programs that drive cell proliferation, migration, and survival (33). Our findings suggest that LMNB1/LMNB2 modifications within the endomembrane system may interact with β-catenin, regulating its nuclear accumulation and transcriptional activity, thereby facilitating CRC progression.

Previous studies have shown that LMNB2 promotes the transcription of p21 (28), which is a key cell cycle inhibitor downstream of p53 (34). The p21-p53 axis plays a crucial role in regulating CRC cell proliferation, particularly by inhibiting the transition from G1 phase to S phase (35). The p53 pathway, as a key tumor suppressor in CRC, regulates DNA repair, cell cycle arrest, and apoptosis (36). Mutations in p53 lead to genomic instability and cell cycle dysregulation, thereby promoting tumor progression (37).

Additionally, RPL4, a ribosomal protein essential for protein synthesis and ribosomal function (38), was identified as a DMP, with upregulation in phosphorylation at S295. Simultaneously, previous studies have shown that mitochondrial dysfunction and metabolic stress can perturb cytosolic translation machinery and ribosome-associated regulation, suggesting that RPL4 may be affected in this context, although this possibility was not examined in the present study (39). Moreover, RPL4 has been reported to interact with MDM2 and promote MDM2-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of p53, thereby linking RPL4 to p53-dependent regulation of cell proliferation and survival (40). In previous work, we identified RPL4 as one of the hub proteins through hub analysis of the PPI network. Together, these findings support RPL4 as a candidate PTM-regulated node in CRC that warrants further mechanistic investigation.

Taken together, these findings reveal that LMNB1/LMNB2 and RPL4 modifications may play a potential role in CRC progression through their interactions with the WNT/β-catenin and p53 signaling pathways. PTMs on LMNB1/LMNB2 may influence nuclear envelope integrity and β-catenin activity, driving tumor progression. Modifications in RPL4, particularly phosphorylation, impact ribosomal function and p53 regulation, linking it to cell cycle control and genomic stability. These proteins, particularly through their PTMs, represent promising therapeutic targets for controlling CRC progression through modulation of the endomembrane system.

Discussion

Aberrant endomembrane system function and dysregulated PTMs have been implicated in cancer progression, influencing protein trafficking, organelle dynamics, and tumor adaptation (41-44). In the present study, we constructed an integrated multi-PTM proteomic atlas focusing on the endomembrane system in CRC (Figure 7). By jointly profiling phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and lysine malonylation in paired CRC tissues, we reveal that endomembrane remodeling in CRC is not governed by isolated post-translational events but instead involves coordinated, multilayered PTM reprogramming. These findings extend current models of CRC progression beyond genomic and transcriptomic alterations and highlight PTM-mediated regulation as a structural dimension of tumor cell adaptation.

While previous studies have characterized individual PTM landscapes in CRC, most have examined phosphorylation, ubiquitination, or acylation in isolation (45). Our integrative analysis demonstrates that different PTM classes exhibit distinct yet complementary functional enrichments. Phosphorylation preferentially regulates membrane trafficking and cytoskeletal organization (46); ubiquitination is strongly associated with protein quality control, endocytic transport, and autophagy (47); and malonylation appears linked to metabolic and mitochondrial processes (48). This compartmental and functional differentiation suggests that PTM layers operate in distinct but interconnected regulatory networks within the CRC endomembrane system.

Importantly, ubiquitination displayed the broadest distribution and greatest heterogeneity among PTM classes, implying that ubiquitin-dependent regulation may represent a dominant remodeling axis in CRC endomembrane dynamics. Given the central role of ubiquitination in cargo sorting, vesicle trafficking, and lysosomal degradation, our findings support a model in which CRC cells rely heavily on proteostatic rewiring to sustain endomembrane flexibility under oncogenic stress.

A key finding of this study is the identification of LMNB1 and LMNB2 as proteins concurrently modified by phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation. Unlike proteins exhibiting single-layer PTM alterations, lamins displayed combinatorial and site-specific modification patterns distributed across structurally distinct domains. Structural mapping revealed that these sites are spatially organized rather than randomly distributed, supporting the concept of domain-specific PTM regulation.

The regulatory pathway model constructed for the CRC endomembrane system highlights the potential involvement of LMNB1/LMNB2 and RPL4 in CRC progression through their putative connections with WNT/β-catenin, p53, and p21 signaling. As nuclear lamina components associated with the endomembrane system, LMNB1 and LMNB2 may influence nuclear architecture, β-catenin nuclear accumulation, and transcriptional activity, thereby contributing to tumor cell proliferation (49-51). RPL4, a ribosomal protein involved in protein synthesis, may be influenced by mitochondrial dysfunction, as previous studies have shown that metabolic stress can affect cytosolic translation machinery and ribosome-associated regulation (39). Although this potential upstream link was not examined in the present study, RPL4 has been reported to interact with MDM2 and participate in p53 regulation, suggesting a possible connection between RPL4 and the p53-p21 axis in CRC (52). These findings suggest that LMNB1/LMNB2 and RPL4, probly through their PTMs, form a feedback loop with the p53-p21 axis, representing promising therapeutic targets for controlling CRC progression via modulation of the endomembrane system.

By integrating PTM datasets with PPI networks and functional modules, we propose a regulatory framework in which phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation converge on three major endomembrane axes: ribosomal/protein synthesis networks, vesicle trafficking machinery, and nuclear envelope-associated transport systems. PTMs act coordinately across these layers, enabling CRC cells to simultaneously adjust protein synthesis, trafficking, and nuclear integrity. This multi-layer architecture shifts the view of PTMs from isolated marks to combinatorial regulators of cellular organization. Notably, endomembrane system-associated PTMs, including combined PTMs in LMNB1/2, highlight potentially candidate biomarkers and therapeutic targets, whose dynamic and reversible nature may provide more sensitive, real-time indicators than genomic or transcriptomic markers.

Despite the valuable insights gained, our study has several limitations. The relatively small sample size highlights the need for validation in larger cohorts. Moreover, our study is based mainly on samples from a single center, indicating the necessity for multicenter validation. While our multi-omics analysis has uncovered potential regulatory roles for novel PTM sites, the causal relationship between these modifications and the endomembrane system in CRC still requires experimental confirmation. Looking ahead, we aim to address several challenges in applying this approach to broader and more diverse CRC patient populations. Future work will aim to translate these findings into clinical applications by developing robust, scalable methods for PTM detection, such as targeted mass spectrometry and PTM-specific antibodies, and by evaluating their diagnostic, prognostic, and functional relevance in prospective studies. To address patient-specific variation and tumor heterogeneity, we will implement standardized multicenter protocols and perform time-course analyses using organoid and clinical models, with the goal of elucidating how PTM alterations influence endomembrane dynamics and CRC progression.

Conclusions

This study provides a discovery-level combined PTM proteomic atlas of the CRC endomembrane system, profiling phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and malonylation alterations in paired tumor and adjacent tissues. It reveals how PTMs coordinately regulate key molecular pathways within the cancer-associated endomembrane system. These findings underscore the potential role of PTMs in proteins such as LMNB1/LMNB2 in regulating endomembrane homeostasis and tumor microenvironment adaptation. They also provide a theoretical basis for the development of endomembrane-targeted therapeutic strategies and CRC biomarkers.

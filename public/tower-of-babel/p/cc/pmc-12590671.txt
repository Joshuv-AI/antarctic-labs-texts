VP1 region-based molecular characterization of foot-and-mouth disease virus serotype O from clinical cases in Haryana, India
Authors: Parminder Dalal, Swati Dahiya, Anshul Lather, Deepika Sheoran, Punesh Sangwan, Neelam Rani, Ankit Pannu, Parveen Kumar, Pawan Kumar, Saravanan Subramaniam, Rabindra Prasad Singh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFoot-and-mouth disease (FMD) is a highly transmissible viral infection affecting cloven-hoofed animals, significantly impacting livestock productivity and trade in India. The causative agent, FMD virus (FMDV), exhibits considerable genetic and antigenic diversity, with serotype O being most prevalent. Haryana, a northern Indian state with a large susceptible livestock population, has reported sporadic FMD cases despite ongoing vaccination efforts. This study aims to detect, type, and characterize FMDV from suspected animals in Haryana during 2024, with a focus on genetic variations in the VP1 region compared to the vaccine strain and contemporary isolates.ResultsClinical samples from suspected cases were analyzed using RT-multiplex PCR, confirming the presence of FMDV O in five buffaloes from two districts. The partial P1 region was amplified and sequenced from five samples using 1C/2B gene-specific primers. Phylogenetic analysis classified all five samples (GenBank Accession Nos. PV068827–PV068830 & PV288326) within the Middle East-South Asia (ME-SA) topotype based on 1D gene sequences (639 nt). Four samples (PV068827–PV068830) exhibited 12.3–12.8% nucleotide divergence from the Indian vaccine strain FMDV O/IND/R2/1975 and clustered under the O/ME-SA/SA-2018 lineage, while one sample (PV288326), showing 14.3% divergence, belonged to the O/ME-SA/Ind2001e lineage. Amino acid analysis revealed 10–11 variations, including four common mutations. Antigenic sites remained conserved, except for an I144V substitution, which is common in all Indian serotype O isolates reported earlier.ConclusionThese findings underscore the need for targeted interventions, such as enhanced biosecurity at livestock fairs and markets, restrictions on animal movement from endemic to disease-free zones, wildlife surveillance, and the inclusion of the role of less-monitored species, like small ruminants and wildlife in virus maintenance. Future studies should include antigenic relationship (r-value) tests to assess vaccine efficacy against circulating strains.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12917-025-05112-0.

Introduction

Foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) is one of the diseases listed by the World Organization for Animal Health (WOAH, formerly OIE) as affecting terrestrial animals [1]. It is a highly contagious viral infection affecting domestic and wild cloven-hoofed animals, including cattle, buffalo, pigs, sheep, goats, deer, wild boar, and bison. FMD virus (FMDV), responsible for causing FMD, belongs to the genus Aphthovirus under the family Picornaviridae. Its genome consists of a single-stranded RNA approximately 8.4 kb in length, which contains an open reading frame (around 7.0 kb) encoding a polyprotein precursor (L-P1-P2-P3). This polyprotein undergoes processing by both viral and host proteases to yield four structural proteins (SPs), viz. VP1-VP4 from the P1 region and 10 non-structural proteins, namely L, 2A, 2B, 2C, 3A, 3B1-3, 3C, and 3D from the P2 and P3 segments [2].

FMD is considered the most significant transboundary disease among farm animals in India due to its adverse effects on livestock productivity and the imposition of trade restrictions on animals and their derivatives [3–5]. Out of the six globally prevalent antigenically distinct serotypes (O, A, Asia-1, SAT-1–3), only three (O, A, and Asia-1) are currently prevalent in India, with most FMD outbreaks attributed to serotype O [4, 6]. These serotypes display considerable genetic variability, with new genotypes and lineages emerging periodically [7–10]. Antigenic diversity among FMDV strains mainly arises from amino acid changes in structural proteins, particularly VP1, which is encoded by the 1D gene [11, 12]. The surface-exposed G-H loop of the VP1 structural protein of FMDV plays an important role in inducing neutralizing antibodies, generation of antigenic variants, and virus attachment [13–15]. Some regions exhibit high variability in the VP1 gene [16, 17]. Hence, the coding sequence of VP1 has been utilized to examine the genetic relatedness of FMDV field strains and trace the movement of virus strains among susceptible livestock populations [18–20].

The world’s largest livestock population (approximately 536 million) is susceptible to FMD in India [21], and the prevalence of three serotypes, numerous genetic groups, and variants has made laboratory detection and treatment of the disease more challenging [22]. In India, 32 Network Units, including six Regional Research Centres (RRC) in different states and Union Territories, are working under the aegis of the Indian Council of Agricultural Research-National Institute on FMD (ICAR-NIFMD), Bhubaneswar, Odisha, with the support of ICAR and Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying (DAHD), Government of India, for FMD surveillance and vaccine monitoring. Haryana, situated in northern India, is a prominent agricultural state where animal husbandry plays a significant role in the rural economy. This sector supports income generation, provides draft power, and aids in the socio-economic development of rural communities. According to the 20th Livestock Census of 2019, Haryana had a total livestock population of 7.12 million, comprising 4.37 million buffaloes, 1.93 million cattle, 0.28 million sheep, 0.33 million goats, 0.10 million pigs, and other animals [23]. The state’s protected area network comprises two national parks, eight wildlife sanctuaries, and two conservation reserves, collectively covering approximately 0.75% of its total geographical area, where no FMDV vaccination is carried out.

Historically, FMD has appeared sporadically across the state. FMDV serotypes Asia-1 and A have not been documented in Haryana since 2009 and 2010, respectively [24]. No FMD outbreak was reported in Haryana from April 2019 onwards, except for one incidence in migratory goat from an adjoining state in 2021 [10]. In 2024, FMD outbreaks were reported in seven villages of Haryana within a four-month period. Therefore, it is imperative to study the genetic variations arising in the FMDV reported from these outbreaks in comparison to the vaccine strain used in the state under the Livestock Health and Disease Control Programme, run by the Government of India, to control FMD. Keeping this in view, the present work has been planned to detect and type the FMDVs from clinically suspected animals in Haryana, followed by their molecular characterization based on the VP1 region.

Materials and methods

Study area

Haryana (latitude 27°39’N to 30°55’N and longitude 74°27’E to 77°36’E) has an area of approximately 44,212 sq. km, which constitutes 1.34% of the country’s geographical area (Fig. 1). The state’s climate ranges from moist sub-tropical conditions in the north, near Himachal Pradesh, to arid zones in the south adjoining Rajasthan. Annual rainfall across the state ranges from approximately 200 to 1,400 mm, and temperatures typically vary between 1 °C and 45 °C, creating conditions that favour FMDV survival and transmission during certain months [25, 26].Fig. 1Geographical location of Haryana (shown in pink), India, along with the districts Hisar (shown in orange) and Jind (shown in green) from where clinical samples of suspected animals were collected [Source: https://d-maps.com/carte.php?num_car=4185&lang=en and https://d-maps.com/carte.php?num_car=8646&lang=en#google_vignette]

Clinical samples

The clinical samples (vesicular tissue/gum/tongue epithelium/saliva) were collected by the disease investigation team of the university using a convenience (purposive) sampling design from FMD-suspected animals in the rural cohort of Haryana, India, during 2024 (Fig. 1). This study employed a descriptive observational design without any experimental infection or interventions. In January, vesicular tissue (Sample ID 10/24) was collected from a buffalo exhibiting clinical signs of FMD in the village Sarsod, district Hisar, while in February, saliva (ID 13/24) from a buffalo of village Chamar Khera, district Hisar; and gum epithelium of a buffalo (ID 17/24) of village Lalit Khera, district Jind were collected. In March and April, vesicular tissue (gums) (ID 18/24) and tongue epithelium (ID 20/24) from buffaloes in the villages of Sabarwas and Khedar, respectively, in the district of Hisar, were collected. These samples were placed in media containing TRIzol LS reagent (Invitrogen™) and transported to the RRC on FMD, Hisar, under cold chain as per biosecurity protocols for further processing.

RNA extraction

For the detection and typing of FMDV, RNA was extracted from all clinical samples (n = 5) using TRIzol LS reagent (Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions [27]. Firstly, a 10% phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) suspension of the tissue samples (vesicular tissue/gum/tongue epithelium) was prepared using a pestle and mortar, followed by centrifugation at 2000 rpm for 10 min. at 4 °C. The clear supernatant was transferred to a fresh vial and stored at −20 °C for further use. Saliva samples were used directly for RNA extraction. FMDV serotypes O, A, and Asia-1 (inactivated and lyophilized viruses received from ICAR-NIFMD, Bhubaneswar, India) were used as positive controls. The nucleic acid pellet was dissolved in 30 µl nuclease-free water (NFW), followed by quantification and storage at −20 °C till further use.

Molecular detection and typing of FMDV using reverse transcription- multiplex polymerase chain reaction

Reverse Transcription- Multiplex Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-mPCR) was used to rapidly detect and type FMDV serotypes using the OneStep RT-PCR Kit (Qiagen) with minor modifications. The primers used for the study contained a universal NK61 primer (reverse sense, 2B gene) and serotype-specific (genome sense, 1D gene) primers: DHP13, DHP15, and DHP9 for serotypes O, A, and Asia-1, respectively, to yield an expected product size of 249 bp, 376 bp, and 537 bp, respectively [24, 28]. A single-step RT-mPCR was performed in a 12.5 µl reaction mixture containing 5.7 µl nuclease free water (NFW), 2.5 µl of 5x RT-PCR buffer (Qiagen), 0.2 µl of each of the four primers (10 µM), 0.5 µl rRNasin® (Promega), 0.5 µl one step RT-PCR Enzyme Mix (Qiagen), 0.5 µl dNTP Mix (10 mM) and 2.0 µl RNA template. The reaction was carried out in a thermocycler (BioRad) using the conditions: 50 ℃ for 30 min, 95 ℃ for 15 min, followed by 35 cycles at 94 ℃ for 30 s, 60 ℃ for 30 s, 72 ℃ for 30 s, and final extension at 72 ℃ for 2 min. The amplified products were subjected to 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis (AGE).

RT-PCR for partial P1 region amplification of FMDV serotype O from clinical samples

For molecular characterization of FMDV detected in the clinical samples, a partial P1 region-based RT-PCR assay was carried out using 1C/2B gene-specific primers to yield an expected product size of 1165 bp [19] using SuperScript Ⅳ One-Step RT-PCR System with ezDNase™ enzyme (Invitrogen) and other reagents mentioned in Table 1. The reaction was carried out in a 0.2 mL PCR tube, where the ingredients (S. Nos. 1–4) were added, gently mixed, and incubated at 37 °C for 5 min. The remaining ingredients (S. Nos. 5–9) were added to the same tube, gently mixed, and then spun. The reaction was carried out in a thermocycler (BioRad) using the conditions: 52 ℃ for 10 min, 98 ℃ for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles at 98 ℃ for 15 s, 60 ℃ for 60 s, 72 ℃ for 90 s, and final extension at 72 ℃ for 5 min. The amplified PCR products were subjected to 1% AGE.

Gel extraction and purification of PCR products for nucleotide sequencing

The PCR products thus generated were gel-purified using a PCR Clean-up Gel Extraction kit (Wizard SV Gel Clean-up System; Promega) as per the manufacturer’s protocol. The purified PCR products were then checked in a 1% agarose gel and stored at −20 °C until further use.

Nucleotide sequencing, multiple sequence alignment, and phylogenetic analysis

The gel-purified PCR products were submitted to Eurofins Genomics India Pvt. Ltd. for nucleotide sequencing using the Sanger sequencing method. The raw sequence data, obtained in ABI file format for the forward and reverse primers, were analyzed and trimmed using the SnapGene tool (https://www.snapgene.com). The full-length 1D gene (639 nt) and deduced VP1 amino acid (213 aa) sequences of five FMDV serotype O samples were submitted to GenBank. The sequences were aligned with the nucleotide and amino acid sequence of an Indian vaccine strain FMDV O/IND/R2/1975 using ClustalW Multiple Alignment in the BioEdit Sequence Alignment Editor [29, 30]. A phylogenetic tree was constructed to evaluate the evolutionary relationships among representative Indian isolates of FMDV O reported after 2018 from different parts of the country, available in GenBank, including the samples sequenced in this study and the Indian FMDV O/IND/R2/1975 vaccine strain based on the 1D region. The phylogenetic tree was constructed using the maximum likelihood (ML) method in MEGA v.11 software [31], which supports parallel computing (up to five threads) and bootstrap analysis with 1,000 replicates. The best-fit model, Kimura 2-parameter with gamma-distributed rate variation, was selected.

Results

Molecular detection and typing of FMDV

RNA extracted from all clinical samples (n = 5) exhibited high purity, as indicated by an A260/A280 ratio of ~ 2.0. The positive controls for FMDV serotypes O, A, and Asia-1 yielded the expected product sizes of 249 bp, 376 bp, and 537 bp, respectively. All the clinical samples (10/24, 13/24, 17/24, 18/24, and 20/24) exhibited a 249 bp PCR product, which indicated that FMDV serotype O was responsible for the outbreaks in Haryana.

RT-PCR for partial P1 region amplification and gel purification

All five clinical samples found positive for FMDV serotype O in RT-mPCR yielded an expected product size of 1165 bp when subjected to partial P1 region-based RT-PCR (Fig. 2). The 1165 bp PCR products thus generated were gel-purified and confirmed in 1% AGE before submission for nucleotide sequencing.

Nucleotide sequencing and multiple sequence alignment

The electropherograms obtained using the Sanger sequencing method for each sample demonstrated the high quality of the nucleotide sequencing data. The sequences thus obtained were aligned and trimmed manually to obtain 639 nt full-length 1D gene sequences for all five clinical samples. The GenBank accession numbers received for each submission, i.e., sample ID 10/24 (Village Sarsod, Hisar), 13/24 (Village Chamar Khera, Hisar), 17/24 (Village Lalit Khera, Jind), 18/24 (Village Sabarwas, Hisar), and 20/24 (Village Khedar, Hisar), were PV068827, PV068828, PV068829, PV068830, and PV288326, respectively, which were aligned with the 639 nt sequence of an Indian FMDV O/IND/R2/1975 vaccine strain to find out the variations (Fig. 3).

Genetic diversity and phylogenetic analysis

Table 2 presents the nucleotide sequence identity matrix among the five FMDV O samples and the Indian vaccine strain, based on the full-length 1D gene. The full-length 1D gene sequences of representative Indian isolates of FMDV serotype O reported after 2018 from different parts of the country were used to evaluate the evolutionary relationships. The samples sequenced in this study (accession numbers PV068827−PV068830 and PV288326) and the Indian FMDV O/IND/R2/1975 vaccine strain were used for phylogenetic tree construction (Fig. 4). All five FMDV O samples reported from Haryana in this study were classified within the Middle East-South Asia (ME-SA) topotype based on nucleotide sequence homology. Specifically, the four samples 10/24, 13/24, 17/24, and 18/24 from villages Sarod (Hisar), Chamar Khera (Hisar), Lalit Khera (Jind) and Sabarwas (Hisar), respectively, were grouped with the O/ME-SA/SA-2018 lineage, whereas the remaining sample 20/24 from village Khedar (Hisar) clustered within the O/ME-SA/Ind2001e lineage. The Haryana isolates of the O/ME-SA/SA-2018 lineage displayed close genetic relationships with isolates collected from Maharashtra, Odisha, and Jharkhand during 2021 and 2022, showing nucleotide divergences of 2.4–3.1%.

Discussion

Sample collection and diagnostic findings

All five clinical samples from FMD-suspected buffaloes in Haryana (January–April 2024) were tested positive for FMDV serotype O by RT-mPCR. None of the five animals found positive for FMDV exhibited protective antibody titres of ≥ 1.65 log10 against FMDV O, A, and Asia-1 when their serum samples, collected during the outbreak, were tested by Solid Phase Competitive ELISA (Data not shown here). These outbreaks may be caused by waning immunity, as the previous round of FMD + HS combined vaccination was carried out in the state from May to June 2023. Three of the five animals sampled (13/24, 17/24, and 20/24) were ≤ 18 months old at the time of the outbreak, suggesting they may have received only a single vaccine dose about 8–9 months earlier. The other adult milch animal (18/24) was reported by the owner to have not been vaccinated in the previous round. Outbreaks were likely driven by waning immunity in young animals and vaccination gaps in adults.

Molecular characterization and genetic diversity of FMDV

Molecular techniques, such as RT-PCR and nucleotide sequencing, can be used to classify FMDV isolated or detected from clinical cases in the field into topotypes based on geographical location. Bioinformatics tools can establish the genetic relationships with vaccine strains. Genetic variation studies in FMDV are crucial because changes to the genes encoding capsid proteins result in antigenic variation, which affects the effectiveness of vaccination programs [18].

In the present study, the P1 region-based molecular characterization was carried out by amplifying the partial P1 region (partial 1C gene, full-length 1D and 2 A genes, and partial 2B gene) of FMDV serotype O in clinical samples. The 1165 bp gel-purified PCR products from all five samples were sequenced, and full-length 1D gene sequences (639 nt) were aligned. The four FMDV serotype O samples (PV068827-PV068830) showed a nucleotide divergence of 12.3–12.8% compared to the Indian FMDV O/IND/R2/1975 vaccine strain. These four samples exhibited 0.9–1.4% nucleotide divergence amongst themselves, with the highest divergence (1.4%) observed between samples 10/24 (Sarsod, Hisar) and 18/24 (Sabarwas, Hisar) and between 13/24 (Chamar Khera, Hisar) and 18/24 (Sabarwas, Hisar) and the lowest (0.5%) between 10/24 (Sarsod, Hisar) and 17/24 (Lalit Khera, Jind). These findings indicate genetic drift from the vaccine strain. The FMDV serotype O sample (ID 20/24; accession no. PV288326) from village Khedar, Hisar, exhibited 14.3% divergence from the vaccine strain and 12.5–13.3% from the other four samples (PV068827-PV068830) collected from bovines of Haryana within a period of one to three months. Hossain et al. [32]. reported the emergence of a novel MYMBD21 sublineage within the SA-2018 lineage of FMDV serotype O in Bangladesh, exhibiting 12–13% VP1 divergence from existing vaccine strains, highlighting potential vaccine escape and the need for updated FMD control strategies.

Grazioli et al. [33]. reported that knowledge of FMDV’s antigenic structure is relevant in developing diagnostic assays, evaluating antigenic variability, and selecting appropriate vaccine strains. Rweyemamu et al. [34]. recognized the anomalies in the system of subtyping FMDV, and both recommended establishing a list of FMD reference vaccine strains with which to compare new field isolates. Haydon et al. [35]. reported that owing to the rapid mutation tendency of FMDV, even short outbreaks could result in substantial antigenic variability. Chitray et al. [36]. reported that FMDV naturally diversifies as it replicates in infected animals, leading to the swift emergence of variants that facilitate continued transmission, produce new strains rapidly, and contribute to disease persistence. They observed genetic heterogeneity in the leader and P1-coding regions of FMDV serotypes A and O in Africa. The intertypic nucleotide and amino acid variation of all the isolates supported the previous findings that the externally located 1D was the most variable, whilst the internally located 1A was the most conserved, which likely reflects the selective pressures on these proteins.

Phylogenetic analysis and lineage distribution

Based on 1D phylogeny, FMD serotype O viruses were divided into three groups based on their origin: Asia, Europe-South America, and the Far East [18, 37, 38]. Eleven global topotypes have been identified within serotype O of FMDV, including Europe-South America (EURO-SA), Middle East-South Asia (ME-SA), South East Asia (SEA), Cathay, Indonesia (ISA)−1, ISA-2, East Africa (EA)−1, EA-2, EA-3, EA-4, and West Africa (WA) [7, 18]. In India, serotype O isolates predominantly belong to the ME-SA topotype, further classified into various phylogenetic groups. The two major genetic lineages circulating in the country were O/ME-SA/PanAsia and O/ME-SA/Ind2001 [9].

In this study, phylogenetic analysis using the full-length 1D gene classified all five FMDV O samples from Haryana buffaloes within the ME-SA topotype, with four samples (10/24, 13/24, 17/24, 18/24) in the O/ME-SA/SA-2018 lineage and one (20/24) in O/ME-SA/Ind2001e. The co-circulation of these distinct lineages with unique antigenic profiles may challenge the efficacy of a single vaccine strain. This highlights the need for additional vaccine matching studies using virus neutralization tests to assess the protective efficacy of existing vaccines against these co-circulating lineages, as reported previously [9, 10, 39].

The O/ME-SA/Ind2001 lineage, first reported in 2001, has diversified into at least five sub-lineages: Ind2001a, b, c, d, and e [40]. Among these, the O/ME-SA/Ind2001e lineage emerged in 2015, initially causing sporadic cases (2015–2017) before triggering widespread outbreaks in 2018. Previously, the O/ME-SA/Ind2001d lineage had been the predominant cause of FMD outbreaks since its emergence in 2008. However, the rise of O/ME-SA/Ind2001e led to a decline in O/ME-SA/Ind2001d circulation, with both lineages co-circulating between 2015 and 2017 before O/ME-SA/Ind2001d was largely replaced in 2018 [39]. The O/ME-SA/SA-2018 lineage was first detected in India in 2018, during a few outbreaks, and exhibited an increasing trend in circulation from 2021 to 2022. The FMD epidemic of 2021 in India involved both O/ME-SA/Ind2001e and O/ME-SA/SA-2018 lineages. Of the recorded outbreaks, 60% were caused by the O/ME-SA/Ind2001e lineage, while the remaining 40% were attributed to the O/ME-SA/SA-2018 lineage [10].

Amino acid variability and antigenic consequences

Amino acid changes can alter hydrophobicity, charge, size, or polarity, which are crucial for protein folding, stability, and function. Non-conservative substitutions were projected onto the known structural models of capsids from serotypes O (O1BFS) [41] and A (A10/HOL/61) [42], and mutations potentially contributing to antigenic variation were recognized. The G-H loop of the VP1 protein in FMDV typically spans amino acid residues 135 to 160 [17, 43]. Highly variable regions, defined as 10-residue stretches with over half the positions varied, can impact phenotype; even single VP1 substitutions near the highly conserved RGD motif (commonly at positions 145–147) may alter antigenicity and receptor binding, potentially enabling vaccine escape under intense immunization pressure [12, 43].

In the present study, comparison of VP1 amino acid sequences from four samples (O/ME-SA/SA-2018; PV068827–PV068830) with the vaccine strain O/IND/R2/1975 revealed 11 consensus variations and a few additional sample-specific substitutions. Change at P4T, i.e., proline (rigid, nonpolar) to threonine (polar) and S140H, i.e., serine (small, polar) to histidine (larger, positively charged at physiological pH), can alter local conformation or epitope accessibility. Substitutions like G139S (glycine to serine) could introduce steric hindrance or additional H-bonding, possibly impacting receptor interactions or virus-host attachment. The consensus-level variations indicate consistent evolutionary drift among the isolates, while sample-specific changes (e.g., T43A, A152T) may represent intra-cluster diversity or quasi-species behaviour.

VP1 sequence of the sample (O/ME-SA/Ind2001e; PV288326) showed 10 consensus variations from the vaccine strain O/IND/R2/1975. The analysis of amino acid substitutions revealed distinct shifts in hydrophobicity that may have implications for protein structure and function. Notably, the S140A mutation represents the most significant change, with an increase in hydrophobicity (+ 2.6), resulting from the substitution of a polar serine residue with a nonpolar alanine. This alteration within the G-H loop may influence structural flexibility or epitope accessibility. Similarly, mutations such as P4T (+ 0.9) and K45Q (+ 0.4) exhibit increased hydrophobic character, potentially affecting protein surface properties. In contrast, the A13T substitution shows a marked decrease in hydrophobicity (− 2.5), indicating the replacement of a nonpolar alanine with a polar threonine, which could affect local conformational stability or interactions with solvent molecules. Minor hydrophilic shifts were also observed in I144V (− 0.3) and T142S (− 0.1). Mutations such as D46N, D138E, N197E, and E198Q do not significantly alter hydrophobicity. These changes indicate a modest overall trend toward increased hydrophobicity in certain surface-exposed regions, which may influence antigenicity and immune recognition.

Amino acid positions critical for antigenic sites (VP1: 43, 44, 144, 148, 149, 154, and 208) were fully conserved across all the samples from Haryana, except for an I144V replacement. This replacement has been consistently observed across all Indian serotype O isolates, regardless of lineage [9]. The strong conservation observed at these immunologically important sites is likely driven by functional and structural constraints, which may contribute to the high antigenic similarity between the vaccine strain and circulating field isolates.

Generally, evaluation of the full-length capsid protein gene sequence enhanced confidence in tree topology compared to analysis based solely on the 1D gene [36]. The present study employed partial sequencing of the P1 region, with a particular focus on the full-length VP1 gene (1D), to classify topotype/lineage and assess drift from the O/IND/R2/1975 vaccine strain. Future whole-genome approaches will provide deeper insights into FMDV evolution.

Implications for vaccine efficacy and disease control

Despite implementing an intensive FMD Control Programme and biannual vaccination strategy, the reemergence of FMD outbreaks in Haryana in 2024 necessitates a closer examination of underlying epidemiological and immunological dynamics. Although the official vaccination coverage across the state is reported to be above 90%, the outbreaks suggest potential gaps in herd immunity. These gaps may arise from uneven vaccine administration, suboptimal vaccine storage or delivery, waning immunity over time, delay in vaccination, or the presence of naïve or unvaccinated animal subpopulations, particularly among transient or newly introduced livestock. Another concern is the potential reservoir role of wild and domestic animal species, particularly small ruminants, in maintaining FMDV circulation. Such factors compromise herd-level protection, creating niches for virus circulation and resurgence.

Haryana shares extensive borders with several states, facilitating the interstate movement of livestock and potentially allowing the transmission of FMDV across regions. Wildlife species such as wild boar, deer, or semi-feral pigs, along with FMDV carrier domestic cattle, buffaloes, and small ruminants, may serve as reservoirs, playing a pivotal role in seeding fresh outbreaks [1, 44]. Continuous virus circulation within these populations poses a hidden threat to fully susceptible or inadequately vaccinated herds. The present investigation offers valuable insights into the genetic diversity of FMDV serotype O in Haryana, highlighting notable yet conserved variations in antigenic sites. These findings underscore the necessity of targeted interventions, such as enhanced biosecurity at livestock fairs and markets, restriction of animal movement from endemic to free zones, wildlife surveillance, and the inclusion of the role of less-monitored species, like small ruminants and wildlife, in virus maintenance.

Conclusion

FMDV serotype O was detected in all five FMD-suspected buffaloes from five villages of Haryana, northern India, during 2024. The exclusive presence of serotype O in all recent clinical cases, combined with the absence of serotypes Asia-1 and A since 2009 and 2010, respectively, suggests a regional shift in serotype dominance. This restriction to serotype O may reflect the long-term impact of intensive vaccination campaigns, with serotype O persisting due to its evolutionary fitness and continued circulation. Based on full-length 1D gene sequences, all five clinical samples were classified within the ME-SA topotype. Notably, amino acid positions critical for antigenic sites were fully conserved across all the samples from Haryana, except for an I144V replacement, which is consistently observed across all Indian serotype O isolates. This study highlights waning immunity and the co-circulation of distinct lineages, alongside evidence of antigenic drift. Together, these factors carry significant implications for the long-term efficacy of existing FMD vaccines. Future research should focus on conducting antigenic relationship (r-value) and neutralization tests to assess how effectively the current vaccine neutralizes circulating strains and to better understand the significance of amino acid substitutions, as this was beyond the scope of the present study. Strengthening the integration of field epidemiology with molecular surveillance, including whole-genome sequencing and predictive bioinformatics, will be instrumental in anticipating and mitigating future outbreaks.

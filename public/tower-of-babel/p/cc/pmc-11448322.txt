Immunogenicity and immune persistence of Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen of rabies vaccine in Chinese healthy individuals: A randomized, parallel-controlled of homologous vaccine with different immune procedure study
Authors: Li Li, Jingyi Xu, Jiao Zhang, Fang Wang, Jianlin Cai, Liqing Yang, Zhenggang Zhu, Yunhua Bai, Bin Jia, Jianxin Ma, Nianmin Shi, Shuping Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study was a randomized, parallel-controlled of homologous vaccines with different immune procedure research to evaluate the immunogenicity and immune persistence of Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen of rabies vaccine in Chinese healthy individuals. 240 subjects aged ≤ 20、21-50、≥51 y were randomly divided into 2 groups (1:1), Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen receivers as experimental group and Essen 5 regimen receivers as controlled group. Researchers collected venous blood of each subject before vaccine injection and on the day 7, 14, 42, 180, 365 after first dose. The immunogenicity and immune persistence was assessed by neutralizing antibody. The positive rate of neutralizing antibody in experimental group was 14.53% on the 7th day, and raised to 100% on the 14th day. It showed no significant difference between experimental and controlled group (P>0.05). Either in experimental or controlled group, GMC of neutralizing antibody was up to the peak on the 14th day, and it showed no significant difference between two groups (P>0.05). On the 42nd day, the antibody positive rate remained 100% with both Zagreb 2-1-1 and Essen 5 regimens, and the GMC of antibodies also remained high level. Then, on the 180th and 365th day with both regimens, the GMC of antibodies dropped dramatically, although it remained above the protective level of 0.5 IU/ml, the positive rates dropped to 84.40% and 84.11% (on the 180th day), and 61.29% and 58.62% (on the 365th day). Rabies vaccine injected by Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen can produce neutralizing antibody fastly and perdurably.Registration: ClinicalTrials.gov #NCT01821911and NCT01827917KEYWORDS: Rabies vaccine, zegreb 2-1-1 regimen, immunogenicity, immune persistence

Introduction

Rabies is a viral zoonosis caused by the infection of rabies virus, which is a member of the genus Lyssavirus of the Rhabdoviridae family.1 It also remains a serious public health problem with a mortality rate nearly 100%.2,3 Rabies is endemic in more than 150 countries and territories worldwide. Although rabies is basically extinct among industrialized countries, it is conservatively estimated to cause 60,000 deaths every year worldwide, especially in Asia and Africa.4–7 It is estimated that about 327,000 persons die of rabies in Asia and Africa each year if prophylactic vaccination is not given after exposure.8 China is one of the countries threaten severely by rabies, almost 40 million persons were bitten by animals each year.9 And rabies once was the top one death report of all infectious diseases in China, which severely threaten the health of Chinese people.10 Three major epidemics of rabies in China were reported between 1950 and 2007.11 The last one was in 2007 with 3300 cases after a rapid increase of pet dog population in urban areas.11,12 Because of the rational use of rabies vaccines/passive immune products and the proper management of dogs, the morbidity of rabies has been declined in recent years in China.12–14

Rabies is a vaccine-preventable disease; timely and effectively rabies vaccination could prevent rabies even after exposure.2,15 At present, WHO recommends two intramuscular post-exposure prophylaxis regimens: Essen 5, a 5 doses regimen on days 0, 3, 7, 14 and 28; Zagreb 2-1-1, a 4 doses regimen that two sites IM on day 0, one site IM on days 7 and 21.15 In China, Essen 5 is a traditional regimen which was formally approved and recommended in 1980. Zagreb 2-1-1 was approved to be used in the purified vero cell rabies vaccine (SPEEDA, manufactured by Cheng Da Co., Ltd.) by the Chinese State Food and Drug Administration (SFDA) in 2010 for the first time. The most significant advantages of Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen are reduced vaccination doses, shortened treatment cycles, reduced medical expenses and increased adherence of vaccine recipients. Previous researches showed that Zagreb 2-1-1 and Essen 5 regimens were equally safe and immunogenic.16–20 However, no other studies on immune persistence of Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen have been reported worldwide, except for the result which was reported in 2017 from our research team. Since 2012, our research team has conducted series of studies on Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen, aiming to investigate its safety, immunogenicity, efficacy and immune persistence. And lots of results has been generated guiding the practice of rabies vaccination in China.

This study reported in the paper is a randomized, parallel-controlled of homologous vaccine with different immune procedure research, the objective of which is to evaluate the immunogenicity and immune persistence of Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen of rabies vaccine.

Materials and methods

Study design

A randomized, parallel-controlled design was used in this study. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was performed in accordance with Good Clinical Practice (GCP). Prior to study initiation, the protocol, informed consent form and other study-related documents were approved by the ethics committee of Chaoyang District Center for Disease Control and Prevention (the IRB approval number was CYCDPCIRB-20130826-1).

Sample size

The sample size of the study was calculated in accordance with GCP and “Technical Guidelines for vaccine clinical trials” of China. Differential hypothesis testing was used to calculate the sample size of the study (α = 0.05, β = 0.1). Statisticians calculated the sample size of each regimen group was 95, and considering the loss rate (20%), the sample size of each regimen group was finally determined to be 120.

Study subject

According to the study protocol, researchers developed recruitment inspiration. Recruitment inspiration was then issued in the adult vaccination clinic of Wuhan Center for Disease Control and Prevention. And then the post-exposure individuals who came for inoculating of rabies vaccines became the target of recruitment.

Based on the principle of informed consent and voluntary participation, also inclusion and exclusion criteria of the study, researchers selected 240 eligible volunteers as study subject. There were 80 eligible volunteers in each age group (≤20, 21–50 and ≥51 years old). Then eligible volunteers were randomly and equally assigned to experimental and controlled groups by ratio of 1:1 in each age group. Blocked randomization was used for allocation of the study subjects, each block included 10 subjects. The randomly codes are generated by SAS software.

The exclusion criteria included rabies vaccine, rabies immunoglobulin and rabies antiserum injection history; allergy to vaccine component; fever higher than 37°C (axillary); acute diseases; serious chronic diseases; treatment with immunosuppressive or immune enhancer drugs; eclampsia, epilepsy, mental and nervous system diseases; blood or blood products transfusion (within 3 months); pregnancy, planned pregnancy during the study; other conditions not compliable with the study protocol. Written informed consents were obtained from all volunteers before enrollment.

Vaccines

Rabies Vaccine (Vero Cell) for Human Use (freeze-dried, titer ≥ 2.5IU, 0.5 ml/dose, Lot No. 201204117) was manufactured by Liaoning Chengda Biological Co., Ltd, Changchun, China. The storage Conditions was 2 ~ 8°C.

Experimental group received vaccine injection by Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen (4 doses), which was two sites (deltoid muscle of each arm) on day 0, one site on days 7 and 21. Controlled group received vaccine injection by Essen 5 regimen (5 doses), which was on days 0, 3, 7, 14 and 28. Vaccines were administered by IM, left and right arm alternately injected.

Immunogenicity and immune persistence assessment

3.0 ml venous blood of each subject was collected before vaccination and the 7th, 14th, 42nd, 180th and 365th day after first dose injection. The serum was isolated from venous blood, which was centrifuged at 3000 RPM. Neutralizing antibody was detected by NIFDC with the method of RFFIT. The titer of neutralizing antibody ≥ 0.5IU/ml was considered as have protective effect.

Assessment indicators

Indicators for primary immunogenicity included the positive rate of neutralizing antibody on days 0, 7, 14, 42, 180 and 365. Indicators for secondary immunogenicity included geometric mean concentration (GMC) of neutralizing antibody on days 0, 7, 14, 42, 180 and 365.

Statistical analyses

SPSS 20.0 software was used for statistical analysis. Continuous variables were described by mean and standard deviation. The categorical and rating data were described by frequency and percentage. Statistical significance was considered if p ≤ .05.

Chi-square test was conducted by professional statistician to compare the difference in the positive rate of neutralizing antibody between experimental and controlled group.

Antibody titer data of antibody were log10 transformed, then analysis of covariance (ANOCVA) was used to test the difference in GMC among groups.

In this study, a full analysis set (FAS) was used for immunogenicity and immune persistence analysis, including all subjects who were randomized into groups, they had at least one dose of vaccine record and underwent an evaluation of the effectiveness after vaccination.

Blinding

In the study, the blood sample collectors, laboratory testers and statisticians were blind, while the inoculators and study subjects were not.

Immunogenicity and immune persistence

Positive rate of neutralizing antibody

Positive rates of neutralizing antibody before vaccination in experimental and controlled group were 1.67% and 2.50%, respectively.

And in experimental group, the rate was 14.53% on the 7th day after the first dose of vaccination, 100.00% on the 14th day, and dropped to 84.40% on the 180th day, 61.29% on the 365th day. Meanwhile, in controlled group the rate peaked at 100% on the 42nd day, and dropped to 84.11% on the 180th day, 58.62% on the 365th day. There were no statistically significant differences in positive rate of neutralizing antibody at all time point of blood sampling between experimental and controlled group, as showed in Table 3 (P > 0.05).

GMC of neutralizing antibody

GMC of neutralizing antibody before vaccination in experimental and controlled group was both lower than 0.5IU/ml.

On the 14th day after the first dose of vaccination, GMC of neutralizing antibody peaked at 28.63 ± 46.1IU/ml and 25.27 ± 32.7IU/ml in experimental and controlled group, respectively. And then, it decreased slightly on the 42nd day. On the 365th day, it was still higher than 0.5IU/ml (1.05 ± 1.6IU/ml and 1.03 ± 1.3IU/ml in experimental and controlled group). There was no statistically significant difference in GMC of neutralizing antibody at all time point of blood sampling between experimental and controlled group (P > 0.05) (Table 4, Figure 2).

Discussion

The 2018 WHO position paper suggests that for pre-exposure prophylaxis, three doses of human rabies vaccine can be administered at different times at the same site, while for post-exposure prophylaxis, either Essen 5 or Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen can be used.15

Essen 5 regimen gives reliable post-exposure immunization against rabies, and has been widely used in developing and developed countries for several decades. Compared to traditional Essen 5, Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen has some advantages. First of all, Zagreb 2-1-1 is a 4 dose regimen of rabies vaccine, it needs 1 less dose injection compared to Essen 5. Therefore, it can reduce the costs of vaccination and alleviate the economic burden on vaccine recipients. Many patients especially in developing countries still could not afford the cost of vaccination. And low economic status is one of the three leading factors of delayed rabies vaccination in India.21 Secondly, cost of vaccination directly affects the adherence of vaccine recipients. On the other hand, Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen can reduce vaccination doses, shorten treatment cycle and then increase adherence of vaccine recipients. Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen shortens the vaccination schedule from 28 days to 21 days, which can enhance adherence rate of vaccine recipients by about 20%.22 Thirdly, may be the most importantly, Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen doubles the initial vaccine dose and simultaneously stimulates both sides of lymphatic systems, stimulating antibody production faster and higher. On day 14, the antibody positive rate reaches 100%, providing better protection for the exposed population.23 In this study, the antibody positive rate was 14.53% on day 7 of Zagreb 2-1-1 regimen after the first dose and reached 100.00% on day 14, with no statistically significant differences compared to Essen 5 regimen. The findings are consistent with reports from other researchers and previous studies of our research team.9 The GMC of antibodies peaked on day 14 after the first dose under both regimens, and there had no statistical difference between them. It suggests that Zagreb2-1-1 regimen can stimulate antibody produced fastly to generate protective effect.

On day 42, the antibody positive rate remained 100% under both Zagreb 2-1-1 and Essen 5 regimens, and the GMC of antibodies also remained high level. Then, on days 180 and 365 with both regimens, the GMC of antibodies dropped dramatically, although it remained above the protective level of 0.5 IU/ml, the positive rates dropped to 84.40% and 84.11% (on day 180), and 61.29% and 58.62% (on day 365). It suggests that if individuals are bitten by risk animal again after 42 days of full dose and regimen immunization of rabies vaccine, they still need to be immunized for strengthen.

Conclusions and future directions

The neutralizing antibody can be produced fastly after rabies vaccine injected by Zegreb 2-1-1 regimen. If individuals are bitten by risk animal again after 42 days of full dose or regimen immunization of rabies vaccine, they still need to be immunized for strengthen.

This study has several limitations. First, the sample size was small, and the immunogenicity and immune persistence cannot be fully evaluated. Second, we collected venous blood of each subject on the day 7, 14, 42, 180, 365 after first dose, so we could not obtain the data of antibody positive rate and GMC of antibodies on day 42–180. Therefore, we could not analysis exactly when the antibody positive rate slided lower than 100%. More studies should be performed for further evaluation.

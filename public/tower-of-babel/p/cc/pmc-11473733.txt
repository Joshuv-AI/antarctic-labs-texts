An adaptive hexagonal deployment model for resilient wireless sensor networks in precision agriculture
Authors: Zhang Yinjun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presents an innovative hexagonal deployment model designed specifically for wireless sensor networks (WSNs) with a primary application in precision agriculture. The proposed protocol integrates advanced features, notably an adaptive frequency-hopping spread spectrum (AFHSS) mechanism and a decentralized real-time adaptation strategy to optimize data transmission in dynamic agricultural environments. The simulation study, conducted in diverse terrains with realistic sensor node distributions, meticulously evaluates the protocol’s performance using comprehensive Quality of Service (QoS) metrics. The hexagonal deployment model operates by strategically positioning sensor nodes in a hexagonal grid pattern, ensuring uniform coverage of the agricultural field. The AFHSS mechanism dynamically adjusts frequency channels, mitigating interference and fortifying the network’s robustness against external disruptions. Complementing this, the decentralized real-time adaptation empowers individual nodes to autonomously respond to the ever-changing environmental conditions, optimizing data transmission efficiency. Quantitative results from the simulations exhibit outstanding performance metrics. The protocol achieves an average latency of 50 milliseconds, a packet loss rate below 2%, a success rate exceeding 95%, and highly efficient obstacle management, with adjusted nodes accounting for less than 5%. These compelling outcomes underscore the protocol’s exceptional ability to deliver responsive and reliable data transmission, positioning it as a promising solution for enhancing environmental monitoring in precision agriculture. This study provides quantitative evidence of the protocol’s prowess and delves into the nuanced working mechanisms, offering a deeper understanding of its potential impact. The findings contribute significant insights to the field, serving as a robust foundation for researchers and practitioners engaged in designing and implementing resilient WSNs tailored for precision agriculture applications.Keywords: Hexagonal Deployment, Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs), Precision Agriculture, Adaptive frequency-hopping spread spectrum (AFHSS), Quality of service (QoS), Environmental monitoringSubject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering, Mechanical engineering

Sensor node failure and tolerance

In any WSN, node failures present a big problem to the reliability as well as the performance of the entire system. Failure in sensor nodes can be in various forms such as Hardware failure, Battery failure, and Communication failure. Physical breakdown, which can stem from damage to the nodes as well as to their components leads to hardware failures, while the ability of batteries to allow nodes to perform non-stop operations deplete. Another type of interference in communication processes is interrupted signal transmission which can affect the communication process. These failures may result in poor coverage in the network, poor collection of data, and low efficiency of the entire system. To overcome these problems, a strong fault tolerance mechanism is required. One of them is redundancy where extra sensor nodes are used in addition to the number that is sufficient to cover the network area. Failure of nodes in the network is tolerated by having redundant nodes that are on standby and are activated in the event of node failure. The second one is Self-healing mechanisms which enable the network to automatically reconfigure itself to bypass failed nodes and maintain communication paths. For instance, the dynamic path adjustment feature of the obstacle management algorithm can be extended to detect and reroute around failed nodes, ensuring uninterrupted data transmission. The third one is Data replication used for safeguarding against data loss. Critical information collected by sensor nodes is replicated across multiple nodes or storage systems so that the failure of one node does not result in the loss of valuable data.

Challenges and drawbacks

In this section, the major hexagonal deployment challenges of WSNs in agricultural fields are explained. These problems are difficult in real-time management of obstacles, more load on the CPU and delayed process as well as increasing energy consumption in sensor nodes. Also, non-regular terrains made it difficult to place nodes in the right location and resulted in some areas having coverage gaps or more than doubled areas. Signal interference is on a high level, as WSNs communicate in a wireless manner. However, AFHSS adaptivity is still not powerful enough for dense networks and needs further optimizations to improve reliability. Additionally, the system incurs high energy consumption of sensor nodes by keeping them all monitored and dynamic pathfinding, it is not therefore suitable for being nested in a remote environment scenario with only a limited battery lifetime. Similarly, it is easier to guarantee reliable coverage and communication paths in smaller fields than in large fields due to scalability that increases the size of the network. Moreover, latency and data synchronization problems are noticed that can influence real-time data gathering which is essential in precision farming.

Conclusion and future research plan

The Hexagonal Deployment Model (HDM) revealed much potential in the field of WSNs, particularly used for environmental observation with precision agriculture. The research included a number of aspects related to the HDM, ranging from hexagonal grid construction and operation, obstacle handling capabilities, performance with Adaptive Frequency-Hopping Spread Spectrum (AFHSS), as well as application-related criteria like disease reporting rate. Extensive Analysis of the crucial Performance Metrics such as Network coverage, Connectivity, Energy Consumption Meter over Distance (HDM), Average Throughput, and Resilience have unveiled operational Efficiency and adaptability with Efficient performance compared to the Literature Protocol. The findings revealed the promise of using Hexagonal Deployment Model polygons in spatial node distribution optimization, obstacle handling, and data transmission dependency also exploring some challenges for transmitting reliable data under changing agricultural field conditions. Future research work would focus on its scalability and robustness, especially in wide agricultural fields under varied environmental conditions. Moreover, the incorporation of machine learning algorithms and sensor node optimization along with real-time data fusion methods were proposed successfully as well as addressing security/privacy issues. Further studies should investigate the HDM behavior in different environments and larger agricultural fields to assess its scalability and robustness. Areas such as energy-efficient communication protocols, machine learning integration, sensor node optimization, and real-time data fusion are a few to mention in this direction which need further exploration along with security aspects. We propose these guidelines to increase the efficiency of the implementation of HDM, which would in turn constitute more sustainable use and less waste for precision agriculture.

High expression of polo-like kinase 4 predicts worse progression-free survival in diffuse large B cell lymphoma patients treated with R-CHOP
Authors: Dehong Xu, Qijie Ran, Biao Xu, Qi Wang, Tao Geng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPolo-like kinase 4 (PLK4) has been shown to be a tumor-promoting factor with multiple impacts on cancer growth, invasiveness, and treatment sensitivity. The current study aimed to investigate the expression of PLK4 in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) versus control tissues and its relationship with disease features and prognosis in DLBCL patients undergoing R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone) treatment.MethodsNinety-seven DLBCL patients who received R-CHOP treatment were retrospectively included, and the lesion tissue was retrieved and subjected to quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) for PLK expression determination. Another 30 control tissues (reactive lymphoid tissue) were also subjected to qPCR.ResultsPLK4 expression was greater in DLBCL tissue than in reactive lymphoid tissue (nearly 3-fold change, P < 0.001). PLK4 in DLBCL was correlated with increased Ann Arbor stage (P = 0.038), lactate dehydrogenase abnormality (P = 0.031), international prognostic index (IPI) score (P = 0.002) and IPI risk stratification (P = 0.001). After cutoff by quartiles, PLK4 ≥ 50% quantile (P = 0.040) and PLK4 ≥ 75% quantile (P = 0.021) were correlated with shorter progression-free survival (PFS), but PLK4 ≥ 25% quantile was not (P = 0.236); PLK4 ≥ 25% quantile (P = 0.349), PLK4 ≥ 50% quantile (P = 0.126), and PLK4 ≥ 75% quantile (P = 0.090) were not correlated with overall survival (OS). Moreover, threshold by best cutoff value (3.755), PLK4 > 3.755 was correlated with both worse PFS (P = 0.002) and OS (P = 0.036). After adjustment via multivariate Cox regression, PLK4 > 3.755 was independently correlated with PFS (hazard ratio = 3.900, P = 0.023).ConclusionsHigh PLK4 expression can reflect severe disease conditions and predict poor PFS in DLBCL patients undergoing R-CHOP treatment. This study sheds a light on the potency of PLK4 as a prognostic biomarker for DLBCL management. However, more large-sample-sized and multiple regional studies are needed for verification in the future.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-025-04017-4.Keywords: Diffuse large b-cell lymphoma, Polo-like kinase 4, R-CHOP, Disease features, Prognosis

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), one of the major types of non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (NHL) [1], comprises nearly 1/3 NHL cases that differ by continent and region (20–50% in different areas) [2, 3]. The prognosis of DLBCL has progressed substantially in the past decade, and over 60% of patients are able to be cured by R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone)-based therapy [1, 4]. However, some DLBCL patients benefit less from R-CHOP-based therapy, and some patients fail to respond to this therapy; therefore, research on prognostic markers for R-CHOP therapy has attracted great attention in the past, present and future [5–10].

Polo-like kinase 4 (PLK4) is a member of the polo-like kinase (PLK) family, the latter consisting of PLK1, 2, 3, 4, and 5, which have diverse and even opposite biological functions [11, 12]. PLK4 has been shown to be a tumor-promoting factor with multiple impacts on cancer growth, invasiveness, and treatment sensitivity, and targeting PLK4 or its inhibitors (such as centrinone and CFI-400945) can effectively repress cancer progression [13–16]. Moreover, the quantification of PLK4 has efficient prognostic value in several cancers [17–19].

PLK4 is also implicated in the pathogenesis of hematological malignancies [20–25]. PLK4 is highly expressed in leukemia, especially in AML with TP53 mutations, where its inhibition can induce DNA damage, cell apoptosis, and reduce proliferation, exerting an anti-leukemia effect; at the same time, PLK4 inhibition can remodel histone methylation, activate the cGAS-STING immune pathway, and work synergistically with anti-CD47 antibodies to enhance macrophage phagocytosis [20–22]. PLK4 is also able to synergize with bortezomib to treat multiple myeloma [23]. With respect to the involvement of PLK4 in lymphoma, PLK4 is amplified in classical Hodgkin’s lymphoma [24], and its inhibition has an antitumor effect on DLBCL by facilitating mitotic defects and DNA damage; furthermore, PLK4 inhibitor synergizes with doxorubicin to treat DLBCL [25]. Therefore, this study hypothesized a clinical implication of PLK4 in DLBCL patients.

Therefore, the current study aimed to investigate the expression of PLK4 in DLBCL versus control tissues and its relationship with patient properties and survival profiles in DLBCL patients undergoing R-CHOP treatment.

Materials and methods

Participants

This retrospective study analyzed 97 DLBCL patients who received R-CHOP treatment between Apr. 2019 and Oct. 2022. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (i) diagnosed with primary DLBCL; (ii) aged ≥ 18 years; (iii) had accessible frozen fresh tumor tissue that could be used for a quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assay; (iv) had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) score ranging from 0 to 2 before treatment; (v) received R-CHOP treatment for at least 6 cycles; and (vi) agreed to participate. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (i) previously received systemic treatment for DLBCL; (ii) had other solid tumors or malignant hematologic diseases; and (iii) had no follow-up data. This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of General Hospital of Central Theater Command. All the participants or their families provided informed consent forms.

The retrieval of data regarding patient characteristics

The clinical characteristics of the DLBCL patients included age, sex, molecular subtype, B-cell CLL/lymphoma 2 (BCL-2)/V-myc avian myelocytomatosis viral oncogene homolog (MYC) double expression, ECOG PS, Ann Arbor stage, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level, extranodal involvement, the international prognostic index (IPI) and IPI risk stratification. The germinal center B-cell-like (GCB) or non-GCB subtype was determined by immunohistochemistry according to the Hans algorithm. The IPI assessment was based on five individual risk factors: (i) age > 60 years; (ii) LDH above the upper normal value; (iii) ECOG PS score from 2 to 4; (iv) Ann Arbor stage III or IV; and (v) extranodal involvement > 1. Each individual risk factor was given 1 point. All individual risk factors were summed to obtain the final IPI for the patient (the total score ranged from 0 to 5). The DLBCL patients were categorized into three risk stratifications on the basis of IPI score: low risk (0–1 point), intermediate risk (2–3 points; low-intermediate risk for 2 points and high-intermediate risk for 3 points), and high risk (4–5 points).

The retrieval of data regarding prognostic outcomes

The follow-up data of the patients were retrieved, and progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were calculated. Five patients had no follow-up data and were excluded, remaining 97 patients included in the analysis. In general, according to standard and clinical experience, patients are followed up for efficacy evaluation every 2–4 cycles during treatment, every 3 months after treatment ends for 2 years, and then every 3–6 months to 5 years.

Treatment information

Since the study was retrospective in design, the treatment did not involve intervention but rather was decided on the basis of the guidelines, physicians’ experience, and patients’ willingness. To reduce potential bias, the patients who received R-CHOP were screened in this study. Generally, the detailed R-CHOP regimen was set on the basis of the patients’ age and IPI. For patients who were aged less than 60 years and were at a low or low-intermediate risk of IPI, rituximab was used for 6 cycles, and CHOP was used for 6 cycles, with 21 days as a cycle. For patients who were aged less than 60 years and were at a high-intermediate risk or high risk of IPI, rituximab was used for 8 cycles, and CHOP was used for 6 cycles, with 21 days as a cycle. For patients who were older than 60 years of age, rituximab was used for 8 cycles, and CHOP was used for 6–8 cycles, with 14 days as a cycle. Moreover, doxorubicin was replaced by liposomal doxorubicin in patients who had cardiac insufficiency.

Detection of relative PLK4 expression

The relative PLK4 expression in DLBCL tissues was detected via qPCR: total RNA was obtained from the tissues via TRIzol (Beyotime, China); cDNA was obtained via BeyoRT™ II (Beyotime, China); and quantification was performed via BeyoFast™ SYBR Green qPCR Mix (Beyotime, China). The thermal cycles for qPCR were as follows: 95℃ for 2 min, 1 cycle; 95℃ for 15 s, 61℃ for 30 s, 40 cycles. In addition, 30 lymph node reactive hyperplasia tissues (reactive lymphoid tissues) were applied as control samples and were also used for PLK4 detection. The results were calculated via the 2−∆∆Ct method. GAPDH was used as the internal reference. The sequences of primers used are presented in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 27.0 statistical software (IBM Corp., USA) was used for data analysis. Comparative analyses were performed via the Mann‒Whitney U test and the Kruskal‒Wallis H test. The correlation analyses were performed via Spearman’s test. The ability of PLK4 to distinguish DLBCL versus (vs.) reactive lymphoid tissues was determined via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. The relative expression of PLK4 was cut off by quartiles (25% quantile value, 50% quantile value, and 75% quantile value) for prognostic analysis referring to the method in previous studies [26–28]. Moreover, ROC curve was also performed to analyze the best cutoff value of PLK4 for progression/death risk. The correlation between relative PLK4 expression and PFS or OS was analyzed through the log-rank test and presented as Kaplan‒Meier curves. PLK4 expression along with all baseline characteristics were included in the stepwise backward multivariate Cox regression analysis to screen independent factors that influenced PFS or OS. P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical information

The median (range) age of the DLBCL patients was 52.0 (22.0–74.0) years (Table 1). There were 48 (49%) female patients and 49 (51%) male patients. A total of 40 (41%) patients were categorized as GCB patients, and the other 57 (59%) patients were recognized as non-GCB patients. Twenty-four (25%) patients had double BCL-2/MYC expression. In addition, the ECOG PS scores were 0, 1, and 2 for 32 (33%), 51 (53%), and 14 (14%) patients, respectively. Fifty-nine (61%) patients were Ann Arbor stage I or II, and the other 38 (39%) patients were Ann Arbor stage III-IV. Moreover, 17 (18%) and 25 (26%) patients had extranodal involvement sites ≥ 2 and abnormal LDH, respectively. With respect to the IPI risk stratification, 64 (65%) patients were at low risk, 26 (28%) patients were at intermediate risk, and 7 (7%) patients were at high risk.

PLK4 in DLBCL tissues and reactive lymphoid tissues

PLK4 expression was greater in DLBCL tissue than in reactive lymphoid tissue (nearly 3-fold change, P < 0.001) (Fig. 1A). Specifically, the median (range) PLK4 level was 3.01 (0.70-10.13) in DLBCL tissue compared with 1.04 (0.22–3.19) in reactive lymphoid tissue. Moreover, PLK4 showed a good ability to differentiate DLBCL tissue from reactive lymphoid tissue (area under the curve: 0.880, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.817–0.942) (Fig. 1B).

PLK4 expression between DLBCL tissue and control tissue. PLK4 expression in DLBCL tissue vs. control tissue (reactive lymphoid tissue) (A) and the ability of PLK4 expression to distinguish DLBCL tissue from control tissue by ROC curve analysis (B)

Correlation of PLK4 with disease features in DLBCL patients

Increased PLK4 was correlated with Ann Arbor stages III-IV (P = 0.038) and abnormal LDH levels (P = 0.031) in DLBCL patients (Table 2). PLK4 was also positively associated with the IPI score (P = 0.002) and IPI risk stratification (P = 0.001). In addition, PLK4 tended to be correlated with age > 60 years (P = 0.082) and extranodal involvement (P = 0.089) but did not reach statistical significance. However, PLK4 was not associated with other clinical characteristics, including age, sex, molecular subtype, presence of BCL-2/MYC double expression, ECOG PS, or extranodal involvement, in DLBCL patients.

Correlation of PLK4 quartiles with PFS in DLBCL patients

The 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year cumulative PFS rates were 85%, 79%, and 79%, respectively, in the DLBCL patients who receiving R-CHOP treatment (Fig. 2A). The PLK4 ≥ 25% quantile was not related to PFS (P = 0.236) (Fig. 2B). Importantly, PLK4 ≥ 50% quantile (P = 0.040) (Fig. 2C) and PLK4 ≥ 75% quantile (P = 0.021) (Fig. 2D) were correlated with shorter PFS.

Correlation of PLK4 quartiles with OS in DLBCL patients

The 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year OS rates were 93%, 86%, and 86%, respectively, in the DLBCL patients who receiving R-CHOP treatment (Fig. 3A). PLK4 ≥ 25% quantile (P = 0.349) (Fig. 3B) and PLK4 ≥ 50% quantile (P = 0.126) (Fig. 3C) were not correlated with OS. Notably, although not statistically significant, PLK4 ≥ 75% quantile (P = 0.090) (Fig. 3D) tended to be associated with worse OS.

Correlation of PLK4 cutoff by best threshold with PFS and OS in DLBCL patients

ROC curve analysis revealed that the best cutoff value of PLK4 for progression or death risk was 3.755 (Fig. 4A). Then PLK4 was cut off by this threshold for prognostic analysis, which revealed that PLK4 > 3.755 was correlated with both worse PFS (P = 0.002) (Fig. 4B) and OS (P = 0.036) (Fig. 4C).

Independent factors predicting PFS or OS in DLBCL patients

PLK4 > best cutoff value (3.755) (hazard ratio (HR): 3.900, 95% CI: 1.202–12.654, P = 0.023), age > 60 years (HR: 10.613, 95% CI: 2.817–39.990, P < 0.001), BCL-2/MYC double expression (HR: 6.836 95% CI: 1.951–23.952, P = 0.003), higher ECOG PS (HR: 2.516, 95% CI: 1.152–5.494, P = 0.021), and extranodal involvement ≥ 2 (HR: 3.885, 95% CI: 1.056–14.298, P = 0.041) independently predicted shorter PFS (Fig. 5A). However, PLK4 was not independently correlated with OS, but age > 60 years (HR: 15.211, 95% CI: 2.398–96.502, P = 0.004), BCL-2/MYC double expression (HR: 15.350 95% CI: 2.449–96.212, P = 0.004), and higher IPI risk (HR: 9.035 95% CI: 1.918–42.558, P = 0.005) could independently predict worse OS (Fig. 5B). Since extreme HRs were observed regarding age > 60 years and BCL-2/MYC double expression in the OS multivariate analysis, K-M curves based on this were performed, which revealed that age > 60 years (P = 0.011) and BCL-2/MYC double expression (P = 0.006) were both correlated with OS (Supplementary Fig. 1A-B).

Discussion

PLK4 has been reported to be dysregulated in several cancers, including hematological malignancies [29–31]. In line with these previous studies, our current study revealed that PLK4 was 3-fold higher in DLBCL tissue than in control tissue, and the ROC curve revealed a high AUC (0.880) for distinguishing this gene. We consider the following explanations: (i) PLK4 is positively related to cell viability and motility in cancers [32, 33], and compared with control tissues, DLBCL cells have greater viability and motility; thus, DLBCL tissues present higher PLK4 expression. (ii) PLK4 is correlated with fewer mitotic defects and less DNA damage in DLBCL [25]; therefore, PLK4 expression is greater in DLBCL tissue.

In addition, the present study revealed that PLK4 was associated with increased disease stage (Ann Arbor stage), LDH abnormality, and elevated IPI risk stratification in DLBCL patients. We consider the following explanations: (i) PLK4 can promote invasiveness and is related to lymph node metastasis [19, 33, 34]; the latter is essential for evaluating the Ann Arbor stage; therefore, PLK4 is correlated with a higher Ann Arbor stage. (ii) PLK4 is related to tumor burden due to its effect on cancer progression [32–34], and LDH can be reflected by and is related to tumor burden; therefore, PLK4 is correlated with LDH abnormalities. (iii) PLK4 is correlated with increased Ann Arbor stage and LDH abnormalities and tends to be correlated with extranodal involvement (although not statistically significant), as shown in the present study. These 3 factors are involved in the assessment of IPI risk stratification; therefore, PLK4 is correlated with elevated IPI risk stratification.

Moreover, the current study revealed that PLK4 was correlated with poor PFS in DLBCL patients undergoing R-CHOP treatment independently, but it was not associated with OS independently. The findings remained the same after adjustment via multivariate regression. We consider the following explanations: (i) PLK4 is related to severe disease conditions, as revealed by its correlation with higher disease stage (Ann Arbor stage), LDH abnormality, and elevated IPI risk stratification in DLBCL patients (as shown in our former results); therefore, it predicts worse PFS in DLBCL patients. (ii) Reduced PLK4 can synergize with doxorubicin for DLBCL treatment [25]; therefore, its high expression predicts worse PFS than R-CHOP (involving doxorubicin) therapy in DLBCL patients. (iii) OS can be affected by many factors, such as subsequent treatment options following disease progression and physical conditions; therefore, OS has difficulty showing a significant relationship with a single index, PLK4, in DLBCL patients. (iv) Deaths are not frequent during the relatively short follow-up duration, leading to decreased statistical power for OS analysis; therefore, PLK4 is not correlated with OS in DLBCL patients. Moreover, this study found that PLK4 ≥ 75th percentile associated with worse PFS but not OS, which might be due to the impact of salvage treatment after progression and the limited death events in this study.

The limitations of the current study include the following: (i) The study findings are restricted to specific DLBCL patients who receive R-CHOP treatment; therefore, the findings are not appropriate for DLBCL patients who receive other treatments. (ii) This was a retrospective study in which complete data were retrieved, and patients without complete data were excluded, which could lead to potential selection bias. (iii) PLK4 is detected via one method (qPCR), and further validation via another method is needed. (iv) The follow-up duration is relatively short enough to comprehensively reflect the long-term prognosis analysis, which could be lengthened in future studies. (v) The sample sizes between DLBCL tissues and reactive lymphoid tissues are not balanced, which may affect the finding. (vi) The number of high-risk IPI cases is small, which may limit the reliability of multivariate analysis. (vii) The number of death events is limited in this study, leading to extreme HRs in the OS analysis.

Conclusions

In conclusion, PLK4 is upregulated in DLBCL tissue compared with control tissue, and its high expression reflects severe disease conditions (higher Ann Arbor stage, LDH abnormality, and elevated IPI risk stratification) and predicts worse PFS in DLBCL patients undergoing R-CHOP treatment. This study provides some evidence for the potential of PLK4 as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target; however, prospective, multi-center studies with longer follow-up and multimodal validation are still needed in the future.

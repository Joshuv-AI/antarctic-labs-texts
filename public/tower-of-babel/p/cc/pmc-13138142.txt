Results of the American Venous Forum research retreat on C2 disease confirms that the biology of varicose veins including etiology, progression, and novel therapeutics, as well as disparities, are top research priorities
Authors: Ulka Sachdev-Ost, Andrea Obi, Eri Fukaya, Cassius Ochoa Chaar, Scott Robinson, Limael Rodriquez, Khan Nguyen, Peter Henke, Andre Van Rij, Benjamin Jacobs
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveFunding for research in varicose vein disease has traditionally been poor, despite its negative impact and high prevalence, affecting millions of people. To identify high-yield topics that would benefit from dedicated research and funding, the American Venous Forum (AVF) Research Committee hosted a Research Priorities Retreat for C2 disease. We hypothesized that an open forum of brief presentations, open panel discussions, and forced rank surveys would identify scientific priorities in C2 disease and provide a foundation for designing future research efforts.MethodsThe initial list of topics for discussion and potential speakers was reviewed by members of the 2024 AVF Research Committee based on recently published guidelines on C2 disease and on scientific experience. All participants were required to participate synchronously during the virtual event. The format prioritized high-yield topics, opportunities for robust discussion, and transparency for registered AVF members. A force rank survey was administered before and after the retreat to committee members, panel speakers, and discussions. In addition, registrants who attended were also given the opportunity to rank topics after discussion.ResultsThe speakers, discussants, and committee members (n = 12) who ranked topics immediately before and after the retreat represented a multidisciplinary team from vascular surgery, interventional radiology, vascular medicine, and phlebology, with clinical and scientific doctorate experience. Understanding the underlying etiologies and the biological mechanisms and identifying new medical therapies were considered the highest research priorities, followed by ways to achieve health care equity for patients with C2 disease.ConclusionsThis study represents a multidisciplinary effort by the AVF Research Committee to identify research priorities in C2 disease. The retreat was carefully planned and transparently executed, providing a mechanism to inform future research efforts of the AVF. Future biologic therapies to target and improve the treatment of varicose veins and health care equity were the top priorities in post event ranking. The resulting document may provide guidance for investigators hoping to pursue research in C2 varicose vein disease.Keywords: American Venous Forum Research Committee, Research priorities, Varicose veins

Chronic venous disease (CVD) is a common and potentially morbid condition that is understudied, despite its predominance and costs to the health care system.1,2 It is estimated that 2% of health care budgets are spent on the management of CVD,3 yet available scientific literature on CVD dwarfs in comparison to literature on coronary artery or peripheral arterial disease. CVD represents a spectrum of disease that includes not only varicose veins, but also venous leg ulcers, chronic pelvic venous disease, edema with and without skin changes, and thrombosis. Each can result in negative impacts on quality of life and should be well-understood to help improve patient outcomes.4, 5, 6

Varicose veins characterize clinical class 2 of 6 in the CEAP classification system7 of CVD and represent one of the most common presentations of disease. It is estimated that 25 million adults in the United States suffer from varicose veins.8 Varicose veins cause more than cosmetic complaints. Rather, they are a source of significant symptoms including aching, fatigue, and heaviness, and can increase the risk of thrombosis.9 Minimally invasive and surgical treatments for varicosities are accessible and commonly performed by a diverse group of practitioners. They are also well-supported by current multidisciplinary guidelines that define standards of care and result in improved outcomes for many people.1

Despite the high prevalence of varicose veins, research on the disease is significantly underfunded. To help address this funding disparity, the research committee of the American Venous Forum (AVF) initiated a research priorities initiative to define issues in C2 varicose vein disease that would greatly benefit from funding support. Our goal was to develop a transparent process by which research priorities could be presented and ranked based on expertise of an international, multidisciplinary, and multi-institutional group. We hypothesized that an open forum of brief presentations, open panel discussions, and ranking surveys would identify scientific priorities in C2 disease and provide a foundation to design future research projects. This report summarizes the process and outcomes of the conference and is intended to complement—not replace—alternative efforts,1 while helping to focus future research on the highest priorities for C2 disease.

Methods

The 2024 research committee of the AVF represented members from academic institutions within and outside of the United States who had been selected or volunteered for the position. The idea and preliminary design of the research priorities conference had been initiated in years prior but were refined and executed during the 2024 term. Other goals of this conference were to enhance transparency and encourage widespread participation among the venous disease community of clinicians and scientists including AVF members.

A list of topics was created using a process fueled by recurrent committee meetings to refine the categories, as well as by referencing published literature. The final list of topics is shown in Table I. Speakers were invited based on expertise as evidenced by publications and invited talks as well as on their availability to attend remotely. Discussants for each topic were chosen from among the committee members. Each topic was presented by a 10-minute talk, a 3-minute analysis by a discussant, and designated time for a question-and-answer session. The total time of the conference was approximately 3 hours. The conference was open to AVF members who were permitted to participate remotely. A forced-rank survey format was used to define priorities of highest to lowest importance and was completed both before and after the seminar.

Results

The AVF Research Priorities in C2 Disease “retreat” was virtually held in late August 2024, was open to the AVF community, and was hosted with support from AVF leadership. Speakers and discussants (n = 10) (Table I) represented a multidisciplinary team from vascular surgery, interventional radiology, vascular medicine, and phlebology and had either clinical and/or scientific doctorate experience. The forced rank survey was administered immediately before and after the seminar. Because a “no-response” option was not available, all topics were ranked. The results of the ranking before and after the talks is shown in Table II. Genetic, biologic, and etiologic causes of varicose veins, as well as equity in management, were the highest priorities based on ranking scores.

Discussion

The conference represented a multidisciplinary effort by the AVF Research Committee to identify research priorities in C2 disease. This retreat had the intention of optimizing transparency and maximizing participation among AVF members and leaders and provided an efficient mechanism to focus and inform future research efforts of the AVF. All topics were found to be important for advancing knowledge in the field, although biologic causes, etiology, and disparities in care were consistent themes thought to warrant further research. The resulting document may provide guidance for investigators hoping to pursue research in C2 varicose vein disease.

Committee work in national and international academic medical societies is largely performed by unpaid volunteers with limited tenures. Thus, achieving meaningful results during a committee term is often challenging. In this report, we demonstrate how leveraging group input, utilizing virtual platforms that improve access to members, employing ranking systems, and amassing group knowledge could be used to create a list of useful research priorities in a transparent, collaborative, and expeditious manner. Our model can be used by other groups to achieve similar short- and long-term goals at a relatively low cost. Furthermore, this type of conference provides important content that can be leveraged by funding agencies to define community needs.

Strengths of this effort included the process to design the conference. The topics that were decided on for presentation were chosen during multiple meetings of the research committee and based on published literature.1 Input was encouraged by all members, and the final list was agreed upon by AVF leadership. Speakers were suggested by the group based on their national and international reputation and expertise; committee members were discussants and community, and member engagement was encouraged throughout the entire process. Furthermore, the AVF community was specifically invited to the conference and had opportunities to submit questions.

Although this process was successful, there are also clear limitations with this approach. First, the time constraints of a committee term limit the ability to advertise widely beyond the AVF membership. In the future, efforts such as this may be planned over a longer term to maximize engagement and allow for timely speaker invitations. Additionally, patient input was not included in this forum. In other organizations such as the James Lind Alliance, patient input is paramount to creating research partnerships that marry the ideas of clinicians and researchers with needs of patients.10 In future efforts, patient representatives and advocates may improve the quality and credibility of this type of conference and should be integral to our research goals. Finally, our ranking system only had the input from the speakers and the committee members. Although we had attempted to make surveys available to the AVF membership who attended, response rates were not robust enough to add to this data. Timely advertising and alternative outreach methods such as social media outlets may help to improve participant engagement.

Conclusions

To conclude, research priorities in C2 varicose vein disease appear to center around biological bases that can drive therapeutics and elucidate etiologic mechanisms, as well as health care disparities. Similar strategies can be performed to identify topics deserving of funding in other areas of venous disease.

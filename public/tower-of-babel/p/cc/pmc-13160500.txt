Preventing Cardiac Surgery-Associated Acute Kidney Injury with the 4-B Approach: Biomarkers, Bundles, Blood Purification, and Blockage of the Complement Cascade
Authors: Maria Gabriela Guimarães, Thiago Reis, Irene L. Noronha, Paulo Novis Rocha, Claudio Ronco
Published: 2026

Cardiac surgery-associated acute kidney injury (CSA-AKI) represents a major clinical challenge in the intensive care unit (ICU) setting, with reported incidence ranging from 15% to 50% [1]. It results from patient factors, preexisting pathologies, and surgical stressors (such as surgical complexity, prolonged cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) and aortic cross-clamp duration, intraoperative hemodynamic instability, bleeding, and transfusion requirements), compounded by postoperative complications. CSA-AKI is not only a marker of kidney dysfunction but also a predictor of major adverse kidney event and higher mortality rates during both early recovery and long-term follow-up [2]. Despite heterogeneous outcomes to date, effective preventive strategies are emerging, underscoring that their success depends not only on the intervention itself but also critically on the timing of their application.

Patients may present with established risk factors; however, the onset of major pathophysiological injury in CSA-AKI typically coincides with exposure to CPB (Fig. 1). CPB initiates a complex renal injury cascade that begins the moment blood meets the artificial circuit [3]. Hemodilution, ischemia from nonpulsatile flow, repeated shifts in perfusion pressure, and reperfusion injury contribute to complement activation, leukocyte adhesion, endothelial swelling, and tubular metabolic stress. These mechanisms converge toward mitochondrial dysfunction, microvascular obstruction, and loss of autoregulation. In the context of cardiac surgery, mitochondrial injury in the highly energy-dependent myocardium impairs adenosine triphosphate generation and contractile reserve, amplifying hemodynamic instability and further compromising renal perfusion [4]. Understanding these effector pathways is central not only to interpreting the clinical expression of injury but also to identifying therapeutic windows. The strategies discussed later in this review map directly onto these pathogenic nodes: early identification, perfusion support, inflammatory modulation, and complement inhibition.

More than 2 decades ago, early investigations demonstrated that urinary neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL) measured 2 h after CPB predicted subsequent acute kidney injury (AKI) with remarkable accuracy [5, 6]. Although these observations were initially seen as physiological insights with uncertain clinical use, they hinted at a future where early signs of tubular stress could inform real-time decisions. However, at that time, early detection of AKI was not paired with any intervention to prevent it.

The PrevAKI trial addressed this challenge by identifying high-risk individuals through urinary TIMP-2 and IGFBP7 (NephroCheck test, Biomérieux, France) within 4 h of the procedure and implementing the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) bundle of care (hemodynamic optimization; avoidance of hydroxyethyl starch, gelatine, and chloride-rich solutions; discontinuation of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor and angiotensin-II receptor blocker; avoidance of nephrotoxic agents; and glucose control) [7, 8]. The primary outcome was achieved, with higher adherence to the bundle than with standard care. Although overall CSA-AKI incidence was similar (46.3% vs. 41.5%, p = 0.423), moderate-to-severe cases were fewer within the bundle group (14.0% vs. 23.9%, p = 0.034).

This outlook paved the way for further studies incorporating biological predictive enrichment into perioperative care. The BigpAK-2 trial enrolled patients at increased risk for AKI, defined by at least one clinical factor (age ≥75 years, postoperative vasopressor and/or mechanical ventilation use, preexisting chronic kidney disease [CKD] stage 3, or intraoperative radiocontrast exposure]) in combination with an elevated urinary TIMP-2 × IGFBP7 concentration (≥0.3) measured 4–18 h after surgery [9]. Participants were randomized to usual care or to the protocolized KDIGO bundle. The primary outcome was achieved, with the KDIGO bundle reducing the incidence of moderate-to-severe AKI within 72 h (14.4% vs. 22.3%, p = 0.0002). This benefit was similarly observed in the cardiac surgery subgroup, where the intervention remained statistically superior to standard care. These findings emphasize that early biomarker-guided detection and structured care bundles are effective, non-pharmacological strategies for preventing CSA-AKI, ideally initiated during the transition from the operating room to the ICU.

During CPB, inflammatory mediators like C3a, C5a, histamine, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α trigger cellular activation, causing systemic inflammation, increased vascular resistance, endothelial permeability, and a prothrombotic state, contributing to CSA-AKI [10, 11]. The use of extracorporeal blood purification (EBP) devices alongside CPB has been explored to mitigate systemic inflammation and organ dysfunction, as seen in a recent randomized controlled trial (RCT) using a polystyrene-divinylbenzene polymer hemoadsorption cartridge (HA380, Jafron Biomedical, China) that showed not only reduced levels of IL-6, IL-8, and IL-10 compared to the control group but also a statistically significant decrease in mechanical ventilation duration, ICU stay, and vasopressor use demand in the intervention group [12]. The REMOVE trial, a randomized controlled study evaluating a hemoadsorption cartridge with polystyrene-divinylbenzene polymer beads (CytoSorb, CytoSorbents, Germany), assessed changes in the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment score up to postoperative day 9. The study found no significant differences in either the primary endpoint or the occurrence of AKI (57% vs. 55.6%, p = 0.91) [13]. Conversely, a propensity score-matched cohort of patients with baseline CKD (KDIGO G2/A2) undergoing elective coronary artery bypass grafting with prolonged CPB demonstrated favorable outcomes with hemoadsorption. At 3 months, these patients experienced less acute kidney disease (p = 0.04) and required renal replacement therapy (RRT) less frequently (p = 0.02) [14].

The acrylonitrile-sodium methallylsulfonate/polyethylenimine (AN69-PEI) filter (Oxiris®, Vantive, France) has also been evaluated as an adjunctive EBP therapy during cardiac surgery. In a multicenter RCT of 343 patients, the intervention group received CPB with the Prismaflex system incorporating the AN69-PEI filter, whereas the control group underwent CPB with a polysulfone filter limited to ultrafiltration [15]. The use of the AN69-PEI filter significantly reduced the incidence of CSA-AKI at 7 days, with an adjusted difference of 10.4% (95% CI, 2.3–18.5; p = 0.01). Notably, most CSA-AKI events occurred within the first 48 h and were transient, with EBP demonstrating the greatest efficacy in attenuating mild stages of injury. Patients with advanced CKD (stages 4–5) were excluded, although this subgroup remains among those most vulnerable to CSA-AKI.

Another preventive approach is modulation of renal perfusion with amino acid infusion. The PROTECTION trial, a large double-blinded, multinational RCT, enrolled 3,511 patients undergoing cardiac surgery with CPB across 22 centers [16]. Participants were assigned to receive either an intravenous infusion of a balanced amino acid solution (2 g/kg/day, up to 3 days) or a placebo. The intervention significantly reduced the incidence of CSA-AKI as defined by KDIGO criteria (26.9% vs. 31.7%; RR 0.85; 95% CI 0.77–0.94). The occurrence of severe AKI (KDIGO stage 3) was also lower in the amino acid group (1.6% vs. 3.0%; RR 0.56; 95% CI 0.35–0.87), with a nonsignificant trend toward reduced use of RRT. These findings highlight amino acid infusion as one of the most pivotal pharmacological interventions evaluated to date, offering a safe and biologically plausible approach that targets kidney hypoperfusion and recruits its functional reserve during the perioperative period.

Even in a condition with a well-defined injury onset linked to CPB initiation, uncertainties persist regarding when interventions should be initiated, how long they should last, and which patients derive the most significant benefit. The effectiveness of such interventions may be influenced by their timing within the surgical course (and after it) and by patient-specific risk profiles. Furthermore, the value of applying resource-intensive strategies, such as those found in the SIRAKI02 study, to prevent transient or mild AKI remains unclear [15]. These challenges underscore the value of predictive enrichment in CSA-AKI trials, a strategy that selects participants most likely to respond based on clinical features and/or biomarker profiling [17].

The ARTEMIS trial exemplifies predictive enrichment in the design of CSA-AKI prevention studies [18]. This ongoing phase 3 trial includes the enrollment of patients with CKD stages 3–4, a population with heightened susceptibility to postoperative kidney injury due to preexisting endothelial dysfunction and amplified complement activation during CPB. By focusing on this high-risk cohort, the trial increases the probability of observing a treatment effect. The intervention consists of a single preoperative dose of ravulizumab, a long-acting monoclonal antibody targeting complement component C5, administered to achieve sustained inhibition throughout surgery and the early reperfusion phase. The primary endpoint is the occurrence of CSA-AKI and major adverse kidney event within 90 days. Its design, similar to the use of biomarkers from the PrevAKI and BigpAK-2 studies, exemplifies how selecting a clinically enriched population may enhance the efficiency of preventive studies, potentially setting a precedent for future methodological approaches in this field. Additionally, ongoing RCTs, involving participants undergoing complex heart surgeries and/or procedures with long CBP times who are randomized to either hemoadsorption or placebo, may also refine the indication for precision medicine in cardiac surgery care [19, 20].

In day-to-day practice outside the research setting, application of the 4-B framework should be driven by a pragmatic, risk-based, and cost-conscious multidisciplinary strategy. Timely detection of kidney stress through clinical surveillance and biomarkers is essential as early identification enables personalized care by balancing interventions with higher costs and identifying patients who would benefit from them. Patients at risk should receive prompt preventive measures tailored to their condition. Importantly, this stratification must acknowledge not only the economic gradient between interventions but also the substantial costs of CSA-AKI and its consequences, including prolonged ICU stays, RRT, mechanical ventilation, early readmissions, and CKD progression [21]. Such nuanced allocation of resources is best achieved within a multidisciplinary framework, through close collaboration between cardiac surgeons, intensivists, anesthesiologists, and nephrologists, thereby ensuring that interventions are biologically sound, delivered at the right time, and proportionate to individual risk and overall cost to patients and health care systems.

Looking ahead, the landscape of CSA-AKI prevention is shifting from supportive care alone to a broader spectrum of targeted interventions. Trials of EBP, amino acid infusion, and complement inhibition are paving the way for strategies that not only attenuate AKI but may also mitigate its long-term consequences. As CSA-AKI arises from a complex interplay of patient-related factors, surgical stress, and systemic inflammatory mechanisms, the greatest advances will likely emerge from approaches that integrate precision in timing with thoughtful selection of at-risk populations. While challenges remain, the field is entering a promising era in which earlier recognition, biologically directed interventions, and personalized preventive strategies may finally alter the trajectory of this common and severe complication.

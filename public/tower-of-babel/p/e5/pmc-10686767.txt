Extracellular polysaccharide of Lactobacillus plantarum enhance immune efficacy of oprH gene recombinant subunit vaccine from Pseudomonas aeruginosa
Authors: Jiayu TIAN, Qiang GONG, Shiji ZHU, Yajing LI
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo evaluate the immune enhancement effect of the extracellular polysaccharide of Lactobacillus plantarum on oprH recombinant subunit vaccine from Pseudomonas aeruginosa, a recombinant subunit vaccine of oprH (rOprH vaccine) was developed. The EP-rOprH vaccine was prepared with the extracellular polysaccharide of L. plantarum as an adjuvant. Mice were vaccinated with the rOprH and EP-rOprH vaccines, and the outer membrane protein (OMP) and inactivated vaccines were used as controls. The levels of serum antibody, interferon-γ (IFN-γ), interleukin (IL-2), and IL-4 were determined after vaccination. Finally, the protective efficacy of the vaccine was evaluated after challenge with virulent P. aeruginosa. Following vaccination, the serum antibody levels were significantly higher in mice vaccinated with the EP-rOprH vaccine than in those vaccinated with the rOprH vaccine (P<0.05). Moreover, the serum antibody levels detected in the EP-rOprH vaccine group were similar to those detected in the OMP vaccine group when P. aeruginosa suspension was used as the coating antigen. However, the levels in the EP-rOprH vaccine group were higher than those in the OMP vaccine and inactivated vaccine groups when the purified rOprH protein was used as the coating antigen (P<0.05). The level of IFN-γ, IL-2, and IL-4 in mice vaccinated with the EP-rOprH vaccine was significantly higher than that in mice vaccinated with the rOprH vaccine (P<0.05) and comparable to that in mice vaccinated with the OMP vaccine. The protective rates were 65%, 80%, 80%, and 95% with the rOprH, EP-rOprH, OMP, and inactivated vaccines, respectively. Thus, the extracellular polysaccharide of L. plantarum significantly enhanced the immune response and protection provided by the recombinant subunit vaccine of oprH.Keywords: extracellular polysaccharide, Immune efficacy, oprH, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Recombinant subunit vaccine

Pseudomonas aeruginosa is a gram-negative nonsporing bacillus that lives in the water, air, normal human skin, respiratory tract, intestinal tract, and other environments. The bacterium is a common conditional pathogen and is one of the main pathogens of nosocomial infection. Patients with metabolic diseases, hematological diseases, and malignant tumors as well as those who have undergone surgery or have weakened immunity can be easily infected with this bacterium. It can cause bedsore, abscess, suppurative otitis media, bronchitis, pneumonia, and other diseases [4]. In addition, the pathogen can infect pigs, poultry, mink, and other animals, suggesting that it is a typical zoonotic pathogen [2, 11, 19]. Antibiotics play an important role in the treatment of bacterial infections; however, with the long-term application of antibiotics, P. aeruginosa has developed serious resistance to various antibiotics, leading to increasing treatment difficulties [1, 5, 20]. Some new antibiotics, including the fourth generation cephalosporins and carbapenems, have been developed to combat resistant P. aeruginosa infection. Among them, carbapenems, including imipenem, have strong antibacterial activity and broad antibacterial spectrum and have been widely used in clinical treatment of P. aeruginosa diseases. However, with the continuous application of such drugs, the pathogen has been gradually mutating, leading to the emergence of drug-resistant strains and gradually reducing the treatment effect of these new drugs. In addition to developing new antibiotics, other effective measures, such as immunoprophylaxis, should be taken to control this infectious bacterium. However, no vaccine exists for controlling this bacterium. Therefore, it is of great significance to develop an effective vaccine to control this pathogen.

In the present study, a recombinant subunit vaccine was prepared using the outer membrane protein (OMP)-coding gene oprH of P. aeruginosa. The immune enhancement effect of the extracellular polysaccharide of Lactobacillus plantarum on this vaccine was evaluated via animal experiments. This study aimed to provide a reference for the study of vaccines against P. aeruginosa.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Bacterial strain and experimental animals

P. aeruginosa was purchased from the Chinese Institute of Veterinary Drug Control (IVDC, Beijing, China), and L. plantarum was isolated and maintained by a laboratory of Microbiological Genetic Engineering, Henan University of Science and Technology, Luoyang, China. Healthy 6-week-old BALB/c mice (n=100) were obtained from Luoyang Frontier Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Luoyang, China. The study protocol was approved by the Animal Monitoring Committee of Henan University of Science and Technology (Permit Number 2023-0006; February 7, 2023).

Construction of recombinant plasmid

Primers were designed according to the nucleotide sequences of opH of P. aeruginosa (GenBank accession number AE004091.2). The primer sequences used are as follows:

PU: 5′-CGGGGTACCATGAAAGCACTCAAGACT-3′,

PL: 5′-CCGCTCGAGTTAGAACTTGTAGTTGGC-3′.

An oprH fragment was amplified via PCR using P. aeruginosa genomic DNA as the template. Then, the target fragment was cloned into the pMD™19-T vector (Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). Subsequently, the target gene fragment was digested with KpnI/XhoI from the pMD™19-T vector, ligated into the prokaryotic expression vector pET32a, and transformed into Escherichia coli DH5α competent cells. The plasmids were extracted and identified using KpnI and XhoI, and the positive plasmids were named pET32a-oprH.

Expression and purification of recombinant protein

The recombinant plasmid pET32a-oprH was transformed into the competent E. coli BL21 (DE3) strain and incubated at 37°C until OD600 reached 0.6. Then, isopropyl-beta -D-thiogalactopyranoside was added to a final concentration of 1 mM for 5 hr to induce the expression of recombinant oprH (rOprH) protein. The recombinant protein was purified using a Ni-IDA His-tagged protein purification kit (Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd.). The expression and purification of rOprH protein were detected using sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) electrophoresis. The rOprH protein level was determined via the Bradford method.

Extraction of extracellular polysaccharide of L. plantarum

L. plantarum was inoculated in de Man Rogosa Sharpe liquid media and cultured at 37°C for 24 hr. Then, 80% trichloroacetic acid (m/v) was added to a final concentration of 4% and stirred for 2 hr at room temperature, followed by centrifugation under 4,000 × g for 30 min at 4°C. The supernatant was collected and anhydrous ethanol (three times the volume of the supernatant) was added and mixed thoroughly. Then, the mixture was kept at 4°C for 16 hr. Subsequently, the solution was concentrated in a spinning evaporator at 50°C. The concentrate was centrifuged under 10,000 × g for 40 min at 4°C. After centrifugation, the precipitate was collected, dissolved in a small amount of ddH2O, and dialyzed for 48 hr. After dialysis, the polysaccharide was obtained via vacuum freeze-drying. The concentration of the polysaccharide was determined using the phenol–sulfuric acid method, and it was stored at −80°C until further use.

Preparation of recombinant subunit vaccine, inactivated vaccine, and OMP vaccine

Tween-80 (6% of the total volume) was added to the purified rOprH protein and thoroughly mixed in an aqueous phase. An oil phase comprising 94% white oil and 6% span-80 and an aqueous phase were mixed in a ratio of 2:1 to produce the rOprH vaccine. The rOprH protein concentration was 1 mg/mL in this vaccine. Another batch of rOprH vaccine was prepared, in which the concentration of rOprH protein was 2 mg/mL and an equal proportion of L. acidophilus extracellular polysaccharide (2 mg/mL) was added. This vaccine was labeled the extracellular polysaccharide-rOprH (EP-rOprH) vaccine. The protein concentration in this vaccine was also 1 mg/mL. Finally, an inactivated vaccine (containing 5 × 109 CFU/mL of P. aeruginosa) and OMP vaccine (containing 1 µg/µL OMP of P. aeruginosa) were prepared according to a previous method [8].

Immunization of mice

Healthy 6-week-old mice (n=100) were reared in a purpose-built animal house with controlled environmental light, temperature, and humidity. The mice had ad libitum access to water and nonmedicated food. After adaptation to the new environment, the mice (7-week-old) were randomly assigned to five groups: rOprH vaccine, EP-rOprH vaccine, OMP vaccine, inactivated vaccine, and negative control. Mice in the four vaccine groups were subcutaneous injected with 100 µL of the corresponding vaccines, respectively. Mice from the negative control group were injected with 100 µL normal saline. The mice from all groups were immunized thrice at 2-week intervals. After each vaccination, the mice were closely observed for any adverse reactions. Any mice that presented with depression, lack of appetite, or other clinical signs of illness were isolated to a quiet feeding environment where they had access to more palatable food until they recovered.

Measurement of serum antibody levels

Following vaccination, blood samples from the mice were collected weekly for 6 weeks prior to the challenge. Serum antibody levels were detected using indirect enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) with 2 × 109 CFU/mL of P. aeruginosa suspension or 20 µg/mL purified rOprH protein as the coating antigens and horseradish peroxidase-labeled goat anti-mouse IgG (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) as the secondary antibody, according to a previous method [7]. Three replicates were set for each sample, and the average value was considered.

Determination of serum cytokine levels

Two weeks after each vaccination, blood samples were collected from the vaccinated mice and serum was isolated. The interferon (IFN)-γ, interleukin (IL)-2, and IL-4 levels in the serum were determined using a commercial ELISA kit (Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd.) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Three replicates were set for each sample, and the mean value was calculated.

Challenge experiment

Two weeks after the third vaccination, a P. aeruginosa strain CAU0792 suspension was prepared and adjusted to 1.25 × 109 CFU/mL (5 LD50 per mouse). All of the mice were injected intraperitoneally with 100 µL P. aeruginosa suspension and reared for 15 days. After challenge, the mice were closely observed every day for any clinical signs of illness. Mice showing signs of depression and/or lack of appetite were isolated and placed under close observation. Mice exhibiting the symptoms of chills, pneumonia, or other abnormal signs were withdrawn from the study and euthanized with an intravenous injection of pentobarbital sodium. At the end of the study, the survival and protection rates were calculated for each group.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SAS software (Version 9.4; SAS Institute). The one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Fisher’s least significant difference (LSD) test were used to determine significant differences in the means among the experimental groups. Differences were considered significant at P<0.05.

RESULTS

Identification of recombinant plasmid pET32a-oprH

OprH was ligated to pET32a to construct the recombinant plasmid pET32a-oprH. The plasmid was extracted and digested with the restriction enzyme KpnI/XhoI, and the products were identified via agarose gel electrophoresis (Fig. 1). The presence of the DNA fragments of 610 bp indicated the successful construction of the recombinant plasmid pET32a-oprH.

Agarose gel electrophoresis analysis of KpnI/XhoI digestion of recombinant plasmid pET32a-oprH. Lane M: DL2000 DNA marker; Lane 1, 2, 3: pET32a-oprH digested with restriction enzymes.

Expression and purification of rOprH

The recombinant plasmid pET32a-oprI was transformed into E. coli BL21 (DE3) for expression. The target recombinant protein was purified using a protein purification kit. As expected, the results showed that the molecular weight of the target protein was approximately 38 kD (Fig. 2A). After purification, a single protein was obtained, indicating the successful expression and purification of rOprH.

Sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) analysis of expression (A) and purification (B) of rOprH protein. Lane M: Protein molecular weight marker; A. Lane 1: Expression of plasmid pET32; Lane 2: Expression of rOprH protein; B. Lane 1: rOprH protein that was purified once; Lane 2: rOprH protein that was purified twice.

Serum antibody levels

The serum antibody levels were detected using indirect ELISA. As shown in Fig. 3, serum antibody levels in the negative control group remained low throughout the experiment. The serum antibody levels increased in the groups vaccinated with the four vaccines and significantly exceeded the values detected in the negative control group (P<0.01). When the P. aeruginosa suspension was adopted as the coating antigen, no differences were observed in the antibody levels among the four DNA vaccine groups 1 week after the first vaccination (P>0.05). Following 2 weeks of the first vaccination, antibody levels detected in the inactivated vaccine group were higher than those in the other three vaccine groups (P<0.05). Four weeks after the first vaccination, antibody levels in the OMP and EP-rOprH vaccine groups were higher than those in the rOprH vaccine group (P<0.05), and no significant difference was observed in the antibody levels between the EP-rOprH and OMP vaccine groups (P>0.05; Fig. 3A).

Dynamic changes of antibody levels in serum of vaccinated mice. Following the first vaccination, serum antibody levels were determined by indirect ELISA weekly for 6 weeks with Pseudomonas aeruginosa suspension (A) or purified rOprH protein (B) as coating antigen. Mice were vaccinated with rOprH vaccine (♦), EP-rOprH vaccine (●), OMP vaccine (▲), inactivated vaccine (■), and negative control (saline; ×).

When the purified rOprH protein was adopted as the coating antigen, no differences were found in the antibody levels among the four DNA vaccine groups after the first vaccination (P>0.05). After the second vaccination, antibody levels in the rOprH and EP-rOprH vaccine groups were significantly higher than those in the OMP and inactivated vaccine groups (P<0.05). After the third vaccination, antibody levels in the EP-rOprH vaccine group were higher than those in the rOprH vaccine group (P<0.05; Fig. 3B).

Cytokine levels

After vaccination, the level of IFN-γ, IL-2, and IL-4 in the serum of immunized mice was determined (Fig. 4). After each vaccination, the levels of the three cytokines in the negative control group were all significantly lower than the four vaccine groups (P<0.01). Following the first vaccination, no significant differences were observed in the level of the three cytokines among the four vaccine groups (P>0.05). After the second and third vaccinations, however, the level of IFN-γ and IL-2 in the inactivated vaccine group was higher than that in the rOprH, EP-rOprH, and OMP vaccine groups (P<0.05). Moreover, the level of IFN-γ and IL-2 in the EP-rOprH and OMP vaccine groups was higher than that in the rOprH vaccine group (P<0.05), and no significant difference was found between the two former groups (P>0.05; Fig. 4A, 4B). The level of IL-4 in the rOprH vaccine group was significantly lower than that in the EP-rOprH, OMP, and inactivated vaccine groups after the second and third vaccinations (P<0.05). Following the second vaccination, no significant difference was found in the level of IL-4 among the EP-rOprH, OMP, and inactivated vaccine groups (P>0.05). However, the level of IL-4 in the inactivated vaccine group was higher than that in the EP-rOprH and OMP vaccine groups after the third vaccination (P<0.05).

Concentrations of interferon (IFN)-γ (A), interleukin (IL)-2 (B) and IL-4 (C) in serum of vaccinated mice. Different lowercase letters indicate significant difference (P<0.05); the same letter indicates no significant difference (P>0.05).

Challenge experiments

The mice were challenged with live virulent P. aeruginosa 15 days after the third vaccination. The surviving mice were enumerated, and the survival curve was plotted (Fig. 5). Mice in the negative control group began to die on the day of challenge, and none of the mice survived for >5 days. Mice from other groups began to die on the second day. Until 15 days after the challenge, the survival numbers in the rOprH, EP-rOprH, and OMP vaccine groups were 13, 16, and 16, respectively. Only one mouse in the inactivated vaccine group died on the second day after challenge. Thus, the protection rates were 65%, 80%, 80%, and 95% for the rOprH, EP-rOprH, OMP, and inactivated vaccine groups, respectively.

DISCUSSION

Owing to the increasing severity of drug resistance, the treatment of P. aeruginosa infection in clinical practice is becoming increasingly difficult. This bacterium has become one of the most important pathogenic microorganisms that seriously threaten human and animal health. Therefore, the development of an effective P. aeruginosa vaccine is clinically significant for the control of this pathogen. At present, few studies have described P. aeruginosa vaccines such as subunit and chimeric vaccines [10, 13]. Previous studies developed DNA and recombinant subunit vaccines for P. aeruginosa using oprL, oprF, flgE, and oprD [6, 9]. However, the protective rates of these vaccines were inferior to that of the inactivated vaccine. Thus, additional research is needed to enhance the immune efficacy of these vaccines.

In the present study, a recombinant subunit vaccine of P. aeruginosa based on oprH was developed, and its immune efficacy was evaluated. The protein encoded by oprH is one of the major OMPs of P. aeruginosa and the smallest channel-forming protein. OMPs play important roles in the exchange of internal and external substances, drug resistance, and their pathogenicity and immunogenicity during P. aeruginosa growth. Although this bacterium contains >20 major OMPs, there is a high degree of conservation among the different serotypes of P. aeruginosa. Therefore, the genes encoding the OMPs of P. aeruginosa are important candidate antigen molecules for novel vaccine research. Among these, OprH protein is the smallest channel forming protein on the outer membrane of P. aeruginosa and is related to the stability of bacterial outer membrane and antibiotic resistance. Studies have shown that OprH exhibited good immunogenicity [3, 12]. Moreover, OprH antibody is universal in patients with P. aeruginosa infection [15]. Based on these studies, oprH was selected to prepare a recombinant subunit vaccine in the present study. To further improve the immune efficacy of the rOprH vaccine, the EP-rOprH vaccine was prepared using the extracellular polysaccharide of L. plantarum as an adjuvant. Extracellular polysaccharide is a metabolite secreted outside the cell by a microorganism and can improve body immunity, control endotoxin, induce IFN and antibody production, and activate macrophages [16, 18, 21]. Studies have shown that extracellular polysaccharides from lactic acid bacteria have immunomodulatory effects and can be used as vaccine adjuvant [14, 17]. L. plantarum belongs to lactic acid bacteria, and based on this, the extracellular polysaccharide of L. plantarum was chose as adjuvant of rOprH vaccine.

Serum antibody and cytokine levels are important indicators reflecting the immune efficacy of a vaccine. In the present study, the levels of antibodies induced by four vaccines were assessed. The results showed that the antibody levels induced by the EP-rOprH vaccine were higher than those of the rOprH vaccine, regardless of the coating antigen used (P. aeruginosa suspension or purified rOprH protein). These results indicated that the ability of the EP-rOprH vaccine to stimulate a humoral immune response was superior to that of the rOprH vaccine. Serum cytokine assays showed that the EP-rOprH vaccine could induce higher levels of the Th1 cytokines IL-2 and IFN-γ and the Th2 cytokine IL-4 than the rOprH vaccine but equivalent to the OMP vaccine. Thus, the EP-rOprH vaccine in this study could induce stronger Th1 and Th2 responses than the rOprH vaccine. A challenge experiment is an important index used to evaluate the protective efficacy of vaccines. The challenge experiment performed in the present study demonstrated that the protective efficiencies achieved by the EP-rOprH vaccine, although inferior to that achieved by the inactivated vaccine, were superior to the rOprH vaccine and similar to the OMP vaccine. Compared to OMP vaccine, EP-rOprH vaccine has advantages such as single component, easy preparation, and low cost. Therefore, it has more application prospects than OMP vaccine. Taken together, the extracellular polysaccharide of L. plantarum could enhance the abilities of the recombinant subunit vaccine based on P. aeruginosa oprH to induce immune responses and protective efficacy.

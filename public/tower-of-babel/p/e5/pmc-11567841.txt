REAL AD—Validation of a realistic screening approach for early Alzheimer's disease
Authors: Antoine Leuzy, Fiona Heeman, Iris Bosch, Frida Lenér, Maria Dottori, Kajsa Quitz, Alexis Moscoso, Silke Kern, Henrik Zetterberg, Kaj Blennow, Michael Schöll
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEarly diagnosis is crucial to treatment success. This is especially relevant for Alzheimer's disease (AD), with its protracted preclinical phase. Most health care systems do not have the resources to conduct large‐scale AD screenings in middle‐aged individuals in need of novel AD treatment options and early, accurate diagnosis. Recent developments in blood‐based biomarkers and remote cognitive testing offer novel, cost‐effective, and scalable methods to detect cognitive and biomarker changes that may indicate early AD. In research cohorts, promising results have been reported, but these modalities have not been validated in population‐based settings. The validation of a realistic screening approach for early Alzheimer's disease (REAL AD) study aims to validate the diagnostic and prognostic performance of the combined use of blood‐based biomarkers and remote cognitive testing as a screening approach for early AD employing an existing health care infrastructure (the Swedish Västra Götaland Region Primary Healthcare). REAL AD aims to provide a concrete, individualized diagnostic framework, which could significantly improve AD prognosis.Highlights In Sweden, most Alzheimer's disease (AD) diagnoses are made in primary care, where access to AD biomarkers is almost non‐existent.Most health care systems have limited resources for the screening of middle‐aged adults for early evidence of AD pathology.Blood‐based biomarkers and remote cognitive testing offer novel, cost‐effective, and scalable methods for detecting cognitive and biomarker changes that may indicate early AD.The REAL AD study aims to validate the diagnostic and prognostic performance of blood‐based biomarkers and remote cognitive testing as a screening approach for early AD in an existing primary health care infrastructure in the Västra Götaland Region in Sweden.Studies such as REAL AD will play a vital role in helping to move the field toward concrete implementation of biomarkers in AD diagnostic workup at all care levels, eventually providing more comprehensive treatments options for the large and growing AD population, and for those at risk. BACKGROUND

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a progressive, irreversible, and ultimately fatal neurodegenerative disease, and is the leading cause of dementia. The estimated global number of patients with dementia now exceeds 50 million, costing more than a trillion U.S. dollars per year. 1 Historically diagnosed based on overt clinical symptoms, 2 AD has been redefined by a recent research framework as a biological construct that can be diagnosed in vivo by abnormalities on biomarkers of the core features that define it as a unique neurodegenerative disease, namely, amyloid beta (Aβ) plaques and neurofibrillary tau tangles. 3 , 4 Within this framework, AD is proposed to be a continuum, beginning with the appearance of Aβ pathology in asymptomatic individuals (preclinical AD), and then progressing through stages of increasing pathologic burden until clinical symptoms eventually appear (mild cognitive impairment [MCI]) and progress, leading to dementia. 3 Although this influential biological definition of AD is not universally accepted, alternative diagnostic guidelines led by the International Working Group (IWG) that accommodate clinical heterogeneity also use biomarkers in a roughly similar manner, differing mostly in that they are considered complementary tools for establishing a clinical diagnosis rather than standalone disease classifiers. 5 , 6 In addition, whereas the biological framework considers the presence of Aβ abnormalities in cognitively asymptomatic individuals to be “preclinical AD,” the IWG consider these individuals to be “asymptomatic at risk.” Despite this important difference in terminology, the notion of early pathological changes being deleterious and imposing a higher risk for disease progression is conveyed by both working groups, stressing the importance that biomarkers are progressively gaining in the diagnostic workup of AD.

Novel anti‐Aβ monoclonal antibodies (e.g., lecanemab, donanemab) have yielded successful phase 3 clinical trials at early symptomatic stages of AD (MCI or mild AD dementia), leading to their approval by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and elsewhere. 7 , 8 , 9 Slowing effects on clinical progression have been modest, however, potentially because disease‐modifying agents are introduced too late (i.e., when downstream effects of Aβ have resulted in substantial and irreversible neurodegeneration). 9 , 10 This notion, combined with the reported slow rate of Aβ accumulation 11 in AD, that Aβ starts to aggregate and accumulate in cortical extracellular plaques ≈10 to 30 years before dementia onset, 12 , 13 , 14 has led to the hypothesis that treatments targeting Aβ will likely be most effective during the preclinical phase of AD, 15 and has informed the establishment of secondary prevention trials testing anti‐Aβ therapies in this population. 16 , 17 , 18

Current health care systems and especially primary care providers, however, are neither prepared nor equipped for screening relevant age groups for signs of preclinical AD, mostly because the state‐of‐the‐art diagnostic modalities for detecting early AD pathophysiology, 19 such as Aβ and tau positron emission tomography (PET) or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) biomarker assessment, as well as advanced neuroimaging facilities for magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and neuropsychological testing, are restricted to highly specialized care units. As such, there is an increasing focus on widely available, accessible, and affordable screening tools for AD.

Recent advances in digital technology have facilitated the use of remote cognitive testing on mobile platforms. 20 , 21 Although these platforms still require broader validation against reference measurements from neuropsychological test batteries, PET, and CSF examinations, they are viewed to offer increased sensitivity, reliability, and accessibility—including frequent testing of people's cognitive function from their home environment—compared to paper‐and‐pencil measures, and carry potential as screening tools at the population level. 22 The need for more accessible disease‐specific markers for AD and technical innovations in analytical methods has also led to the development of blood‐based biomarkers (BBMs) for Aβ and several isoforms of hyperphosphorylated tau (p‐tau). Numerous studies have reported high AD specificity, as well as high diagnostic and prognostic sensitivity for these BBMs, whereas certain technical and practical issues remain to be solved. 23 , 24 , 25 , 26 Furthermore, recent studies have highlighted the ability of several BBMs to accurately differentiate individuals with AD neuropathologic changes from those without and to predict future cognitive decline, even in pre‐dementia stages. 26 , 27 , 28

Herein we describe the REAL AD study, a longitudinal prospective study on screening for early AD combining several of the latest developments in remote cognitive testing with novel, sensitive, and AD‐specific BBMs. Inspired by the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI)—with its focus on the implementation of AD blood biomarkers and methodological improvements to clinical trials and AD research 29 —REAL AD is also part of the broader AD‐RIDDLE study, 30 an Innovative Health Initiative (IHI)–funded consortium involving 24 academic and industry partners, health care providers, regulatory bodies, and patient groups, with a goal to develop, test, and deploy early detection, prevention, and treatment of AD. REAL AD is also part of the PROMINENT consortium, another IHI public–private partnership project focused on cost‐effective care and improved health outcomes through the use of innovative health technologies. 31

In REAL AD, participants will be recruited from the general population of the Västra Götaland County (“Västra Götalandsregionen,” VGR; inclusion criteria detailed below in Methods), on the west coast of Sweden, a catchment area comprising ≈17% of Sweden's population (i.e., ≈1.6 million inhabitants, with ≈730,000 in the target age group). After answering an array of relevant questionnaires on, for example, AD risk factors, and completing digital cognitive tests remotely, participants are offered the option to donate a blood sample via the VGR public primary care system (VGR “Närhälsan”). The blood samples are then centrally analyzed at the Neurochemistry Laboratory of the University of Gothenburg (UGOT) and the Sahlgrenska University Hospital (SU). A stratified sub‐cohort will then be invited to undergo the established clinical and biomarker assessments to retroactively assess the prognostic and diagnostic potential of the screening approach.

METHODS

Screening study

A targeted recruitment campaign was launched on April 22, 2024, in collaboration with a public relations agency. The campaign involves dissemination of study information through targeted social and traditional media advertising, as well as placing study information at all VGR primary care centers and utilizing the website www.realad.se as a central study information and communication hub with the goal of recruiting a minimum of 3000 participants (with resources available for a maximum of 10,000 participants). To ensure simulation of a realistic population screening scenario for early, including preclinical, AD, inclusion criteria are kept minimal as follows: (1) age 50–80 years; (2) no clinical dementia diagnosis; (3) ability to read and understand any of the offered languages (see below) and to independently operate a mobile device and/or a computer; and (4) access to a smartphone, tablet device (Android or iOS), or computer with internet access. An overview of the screening study design is provided in Figure 1. To increase diversity and maximize representation of the VGR and thus the Swedish population, all study content is offered in Swedish, Finnish, English, and Arabic (which were chosen because they are the four most common languages spoken in VGR) and dedicated “Health Guides” will arrange information meetings in VGR areas with large non‐Swedish populations.

Overview of the screening study design. Each of the four steps in the screening study encompasses multiple data collection points: baseline, 18‐, 27‐, and 36‐month follow‐up for steps 1 (questionnaires about basic health, demographic, and lifestyle information) and 2 (remote cognitive testing), as well as the optional questionnaires on lifestyle factors, baseline and 18 months for step 3 (blood sampling).

After registering through the study website using a Swedish electronic identification system (BankID), individuals fulfilling the inclusion criteria provide informed consent and are assigned a unique study ID. Participants are required to fill in a questionnaire for acquiring basic health, demographic, first‐degree family history of AD or other dementias, and lifestyle information, as well as information about subjective cognitive impairment and the Quick Dementia Rating System (QDRS). 32 In addition to these, several optional in‐depth questionnaires are available to the participant (Table 1), covering diet, 33 sleep, 34 social network, 35 depression, anxiety and stress, 36 alcohol and drug use, 37 , 38 personality, 39 physical activity, 40 and subjective cognitive complaints. 41 National and regional registries will also be accessed for relevant socioeconomic and health data. Following registration and completion of the screener, participants will initiate remote cognitive testing (see Section 2.1.1) using either a mobile app (neotiv) or, if the participant is not able to or does not want to use an app‐based platform, a website‐based alternative (Cognitron). After conclusion of the remotely administered cognitive tests after ≈3 months, participants will be invited to donate blood samples at the closest available VGR primary care center. Within the VGR, all 111 primary care centers are enrolled and allow for people to donate their blood samples at the centers (Figure 2).

Remote cognitive testing

The mobile app neotiv was developed as a spin‐off from research conducted at the German Centre for Neurodegenerative Diseases (DZNE). The mobile app encompasses a cognitive test battery tailored to detect earliest AD‐related cognitive changes, including object and scene mnemonic discrimination, complex scene recognition, and Objects‐in‐Room Recall. These tasks have been demonstrated to rely on key brain memory networks and medial temporal lobe subregions that are affected by early AD pathology. 42 , 43 The neotiv app has been implemented successfully in several research cohorts, including the German Longitudinal Cognitive Impairment and Dementia Study (DELCODE), the Swedish Biomarkers For Identifying Neurodegenerative Disorders Early and Reliably (BioFINDER), the Gothenburg H70 studies, and the Wisconsin Registry for Alzheimer's Prevention (WRAP; Wisconsin, USA) study. Study participants will be asked to complete testing on at least one and a maximum of nine dedicated testing and one to nine recall occasions within a 3‐month period, followed by the same regimen after 18, 27, and 36 months, with each testing session taking 10–15 min. The alternative to neotiv is a website‐based platform, Cognitron, which is based on traditional neuropsychological and intelligence tests. Cognitron consists of nine sub‐tests that assess different aspects of cognition such as object recognition, attention, concentration, spatial planning, and verbal analogies. 44 , 45 It has been used, among other studies, as part of “The Great British Intelligence Test,” a large‐scale survey of cognition and to study cognitive deficits post severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS‐CoV‐2) infection. 46 , 47

Blood sample acquisition and analysis

At baseline, 4 mL of whole blood will be collected, and at both baseline and 18 months of follow‐up, three 7 mL samples of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) plasma will be collected. Whole blood samples will be analyzed for genetic AD risk factors; the initial analyses will focus on the main genetic risk factor for AD, apolipoprotein E (APOE) genotype, whereas future analyses will also include genome‐wide association studies (GWAS). All plasma samples will be centrifuged, aliquoted at the individual primary care sites within VGR, and then shipped via standard clinical routine to the clinical neurochemistry laboratory at the Sahlgrenska University Hospital, Mölndal, Sweden, where they will be stored until all samples can be analyzed in batches to maximize analytic validity. For the initial screening of AD‐related brain changes, a panel of recently established BBMs for typical aspects of AD pathophysiology will be evaluated 3 : Aβ (A), tau (T), and neurodegeneration (N). For Aβ, plasma Aβ42/Aβ40 will be measured, as it shows a decrease in Aβ‐positive asymptomatic individuals compared with Aβ‐negative controls. 48 For p‐tau (T), an array of plasma p‐tau markers (phosphorylated at threonine 181, 217, or 231) will be assessed. For neurodegeneration (N), plasma neurofilament light (NfL) will be analyzed for assessing neuronal damage downstream of Aβ and tau. 27 , 49 In addition, we will analyze the blood samples for glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) because recent evidence has demonstrated strong associations with early amyloidosis in preclinical AD. 50 Although our groups have contested the utility of plasma Aβ with regard to its vulnerability to long‐term analytical variations, 51 , 52 , 53 single‐batch analyses of this analyte are still important to further elucidate confounding factors that make its interpretation even more challenging, as described recently for a commonly used cardiovascular drug that may lead to misclassification of Aβ blood tests in patients taking it. 54 All analyses will be performed at the clinical neurochemistry laboratory using, initially, the Roche Elecsys and the Quanterix Simoa platforms. Most samples will be stored for future analyses with upcoming novel analysis techniques and for novel biomarker candidates.

Access of relevant national databases

In addition to the collected cognition and biomarker data, the included lifestyle and demographic information, as well as genetic risk factor data, we aim to access the following Swedish national and regional register for relevant data on socioeconomic and health data:

The Swedish Dementia Registry (SveDem), the H70‐study, the National Patient Register, the National Prescribed Drug Register, Cause‐of‐death Register, National Register of Municipal Support and Service for Persons with Certain Functional Impairment (SOL), and the Longitudinal integrated database for health insurance and labour market studies (LISA).

Validation study

A stratified subsample of a minimum of 425 participants from the baseline screening study will be invited to participate in the validation study. This sub‐study aims to validate the diagnostic and prognostic performance of the modalities from the screening study against established measures (see Section 3.2 for its objectives), including CSF‐based biomarkers, MRI, and tau PET on two occasions over the course of 48 months. After the baseline screening phase has been completed (top panel), participants will be recruited to the validation sub‐study (bottom panel) and undergo the following examinations at baseline and on a follow‐up occasion 48 months later: Neuropsychological testing, blood and CSF sampling, clinical assessment, structural and functional MRI, as well as tau PET using [18F]RO948.

CSF biomarkers

Lumbar punctures will be carried out at the SU Memory Clinic by an experienced physician. CSF will be analyzed initially for Aβ42/40, p‐tau (e.g., 181, 217, and 231), NfL, and GFAP at the neurochemistry laboratory at UGOT/SU according to established routine protocols using the NeuroToolKit on Cobas e601 (Roche Diagnostics). 55

Neuropsychological assessment

On the occasion of lumbar puncture, traditional neuropsychological testing will be performed at the SU Memory Clinic, including, among others, the Preclinical Alzheimer cognitive composite (PACC) test battery. 56 The PACC is designed to be sensitive to cognitive change in preclinical AD. 57 , 58 Furthermore, participants will be asked to perform a digitalized onsite cognitive test battery developed by the Swedish BioFinder team (BioCog), including 10‐Word List Recall, Temporal Orientation, a digitalized version of the Symbol Digit Modalities Test, Picture Story Report, Reaction Time, Trail Making A and B, and a Paired‐Associates Learning Test. Finally, activities of daily living will be assessed using the Amsterdam IADL Questionnaire. 59

Tau PET and MRI

On a separate occasion, temporally close to the visit at the SU Memory Clinic, [18F]RO948 tau PET will be performed at the SU Imaging and Intervention Centre, 70 to 90 min after tracer injection, in parallel to MRI on a GE Signa combined PET/MRI scanner. PET data will be processed and analyzed using a previously established processing pipeline to compute standardized uptake values (SUVRs) using the inferior cerebellar cortex as reference region. 60 Target regions of interest (ROIs) will cover the full spectrum of tau aggregation in AD: early (entorhinal cortex), intermediate (temporal composite), and late (neocortical composite). 61

T1, T2‐FLAIR (fluid‐attenuated inversion recover), and diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) sequences will be acquired in parallel with the PET scans. MRI scans will be analyzed for cortical volume and thickness, white matter lesions, vascular pathology, and tract integrity and visually assessed by a trained radiologist to exclude co‐pathologies and possible incidental findings.

Expected impact of REAL AD

REAL AD assesses the applicability of a realistic population‐based screening approach to identify individuals with evidence of early AD using a fully digitalized approach that is deeply rooted in the existing regional infrastructure. The study will provide information about the prevalence of cognitive and risk factor profiles in relation to AD biomarkers in a relevant age group. As part of the broader AD‐RIDDLE and PROMINENT studies, REAL AD will inform the development of regional and European strategies for funneling at‐risk individuals into existing health care routines and facilitating future treatment options. 30

Recent work has shown the feasibility of fully remote and unsupervised cognitive assessments using the neotiv digital platform in large community‐based cohorts, 62 , 63 highlighting its potential in large‐scale population‐based applications, with other platforms also showing similar results in cohorts with available CSF biomarkers. 64 Because digital cognitive metrics can capture subtle signs of impairment that cannot be captured by standard clinical tests performed once or twice during routine assessment, they might be particularly valuable in early phases of the disease when cognitive impairment is at subthreshold levels on current scales. 65 Screening for AD, recruitment, and longitudinal follow‐up in clinical trials could all be transformed if cognitive testing were to be conducted robustly, remotely, and frequently. 66 REAL AD will provide further usability testing of this approach in a representative, naturalistic sample of users. This is critical given the availability of disease‐modifying treatments for AD and the need for sensitive cognitive tests to stratify patients who might benefit from early interventions. 9 , 67 Given previous validation efforts of remote cognitive assessment with the proposed instrument in similar but smaller populations, we expect that digital cognitive testing will also prove itself useful and feasible in REAL AD. In the eventuality that this does not show itself to be the case, our results may indicate the need for relevant adjustments and improvement of remote cognitive testing tools.

BBMs can provide a non‐invasive, cost‐effective and easily available method to detect AD pathophysiology. 19 Although the development of BBMs has been hindered previously by insufficient analytical sensitivities, recent studies suggest promising results using easily accessible and potentially scalable tests. 19 , 68 For example, primarily in specialized memory clinics, blood‐based AD biomarkers have been shown to differentiate AD dementia from dementia caused by other neurodegenerative disorders with accuracies non‐inferior to CSF and PET biomarkers, and to predict future development of AD dementia in non‐demented patients with cognitive complaints. 19 As a result of the implementation of CSF AD biomarkers and amyloid PET in many countries, validation of relevant BBMs in memory clinics and clinical trials has accelerated. Their use in primary care, however, is likely to take longer as there are few prospective research studies on BBMs related to AD in this setting. 69 The study design of REAL AD—including its use of relevant and accurate reference standards (CSF, PET) in the validation component—will help to develop and validate the use of BBMs in primary care. 70 It is important to note that recent guidelines on minimally acceptable criteria for the performance of BBMs have been published by the Global CEO Initiative on AD, and REAL AD will also aim to evaluate whether the components of our BBM panel are aligned with these performance requirements. 71

The success of, for example, lecanemab and donanemab highlights the critical need to better define appropriate trial populations in order to provide the foundation for additional productive drug development programs. 72 It is notable that REAL AD will raise awareness in Sweden among clinicians and policy makers and contribute to the international research community by making acquired data available to collaborators. Experiences and considerations will also be compiled into a whitepaper to communicate practical recommendations to policy makers, patient support groups, the public, and the scientific community. Studies such as REAL AD will play a vital role in helping to move the field toward more comprehensive diagnostic and eventually treatment options for the large and growing AD population and for those at risk.

A simple, inexpensive, and rapid method for measuring calcium concentrations in colostrum to predict postpartum milk fever in cows using a commercially available water quality test device
Authors: Nao KONDO, Masaaki MAEDA, Ryo MUROTSUKI, Tatsuya RYOHA, Marina OTSUKA, Kenji TSUKANO, Hiroaki KOZUTSUMI, Keiko IKEDA, Kana KAMITANI, Minoru OKAMOTO, Haruyuki HIRATA, Kazuyuki SUZUKI
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo diagnose milk fever (MF) in dairy cows under clinical practice, measurement of ionized calcium concentrations in blood using point-of-care (POC) devices is required. However, blood sampling poses a significant financial burden on farmers and increases the workload of veterinarians who must visit farms to perform the examinations. The present study aimed to evaluate whether the onset of MF could be predicted using colostrum samples, which can be collected by farmers themselves. Total calcium (tCa) concentrations in colostrum were measured using a commercially available water quality test device. The results showed that cows treated for MF had significantly higher tCa concentrations in colostrum (2,141.1 ± 623.7 mg/L) compared to cows without MF (1,546.3 ± 494.4 mg/L) (P=0.006). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis suggested that measuring tCa concentration in colostrum could be used to predict the onset of MF. When the cut-off value was set at >2,000 mg/L, the sensitivity and specificity were 66.7% and 88.9%, respectively. Results from these findings, measuring tCa concentration in colostrum using a water quality test device offers a simple, inexpensive, and rapid method for predicting the onset of MF in dairy cows.Keywords: calcium, colostrum, dairy cow, milk fever, water quality test device

INTRODUCTION

Postpartum dairy cows are physiologically predisposed to declines in blood calcium concentrations due to the transfer of calcium into colostrum. Therefore, they may develop recumbency accompanied by impaired thermoregulation, characterized primarily by decreased consciousness and flaccid paralysis of the skeletal muscles [17, 21]. This condition is commonly referred to as milk fever (MF), and is one of the main periparturient diseases encountered by veterinarians who provide clinical care to lactating cows. The incidence of MF ranges between 2 and 5% in calved cows [7] and is higher in multiparous cows and high-producing dairy cows [19, 20]. The standard treatment generally involves the oral, subcutaneous, or intravenous administration of calcium borogluconate preparations [1, 3, 8, 25].

Calcium in blood exists in three forms: ionized calcium (iCa), complexed calcium (cCa), and protein-bound calcium (pCa) [18]. Martinez et al. [16] defined subclinical hypocalcemia as a state in which the blood concentration of iCa is <1.0 mM and the cow does not exhibit rumination. In clinical practice, veterinarians typically estimate the iCa status by measuring total calcium (tCa). However, in acidemia, iCa dissociates from cCa and pCa, increasing its concentration, whereas in alkalemia, iCa binds to other molecules, resulting in elevated cCa and pCa concentrations and a decreased iCa concentration. Therefore, the blood concentration of iCa fluctuates with the changes in the physiological acid-base balance [5].

Even when tCa concentrations fall within the normal range, actual iCa concentrations may be abnormal due to fluctuations in cCa and pCa [23]. Hence, an evaluation of iCa, the physiologically active form of calcium, is considered to be more important than that of tCa for diagnosing MF [18]. However, blood iCa measurements using Point-of-Care (POC) devices, such as i-STAT 1, require expensive cartridges, which poses a significant financial burden on farmers. Furthermore, blood sampling is necessary for these measurements, and veterinarians must visit farm to establish whether a postpartum cow requires treatment, which involves substantial labor.

Previous studies suggested a relationship between the onset of MF and colostrum production, with an increased risk of MF being attributed to a higher colostrum yield [26]. It has reported that mastecomized cows in mid-gestation do not exhibit MF, thereby eliminating lactation-associated metabolism [11]. However, predicting the onset of MF based on measurements of calcium concentrations in colostrum has yet to be reported. So it was investigated whether measuring iCa and tCa concentrations in colostrum, a sample that is easily collected by farmers themselves as an alternative to blood samples, represents a simple, inexpensive, and rapid method for predicting the onset of MF. In other words, the aim of this study was to investigate whether measuring the calcium concentration in colostrum on the day of calving could serve as a highly specific screening test for the early detection of cows at high risk of developing MF. We focused on the use of LAQUAtwin Ca-11 (Horiba Advanced Techno, Co., Ltd., Kyoto, Japan), an iCa meter originally designed for water quality testing, to measure calcium concentrations in colostrum. This device measures iCa concentrations in liquid samples in approximately 10 sec and is highly portable, weighing only 50 g. These devices are generally used for quality control of milk and dairy beverages, and there have also been reports of their application on dairy farms for measuring calcium concentrations in the milk of cows with mastitis [12]. If the onset of MF may be predicted by measuring iCa or tCa concentrations in colostrum using LAQUAtwin Ca-11 as a substitute for blood iCa concentrations, the financial burden on farmers and the workload on veterinarians required for farm visits may both be reduced. However, in general, blood iCa and colostral iCa or tCa are considered physiologically distinct, and it has been reported that blood calcium concentrations do not influence colostral calcium concentrations [13]. On the other hand, as parathyroid hormone-related protein (PTHrP), produced in the mammary gland, is suggested to be involved in the transport of calcium from blood to milk [15], there are also reports suggesting a relationship between blood and milk calcium concentrations. Therefore, the present study (1) confirmed the pretreatment conditions required for measuring calcium concentrations in colostrum and validated the accuracy of a calcium ion meter originally designed for water quality testing, and then (2) investigated whether the measurement of calcium concentrations in colostrum using LAQUAtwin may be used to predict the onset of MF.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

All procedures were performed in accordance with the Good for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of the School of Veterinary Medicine at Rakuno Gakuen University (Approved #: VH24C9). This series of studies consisted of two parts: (1) laboratory-level precision and accuracy control tests for an iCa measuring device for water quality testing, and (2) clinical trials performed as the Cow-Side Test under typical Japanese dairy farm conditions.

Laboratory-level precision and accuracy control tests

To measure total iCa or pCa concentrations in colostrum using an iCa measuring device, ideally all of the calcium present in colostrum needs to be ionized. Prior to the initiation of this study, it was necessary to confirm whether the majority of calcium in milk was ionized when the pH of colostrum was around 4, and also to check the accuracy of the iCa measuring device for measuring calcium concentrations in colostrum. To achieve these objectives, six colostrum samples were collected within 3 days after parturition from adult cows that were raised and managed at the Rakuno Gakuen University Farm. The relationship between changes in pH and iCa concentrations measured in colostrum was evaluated by sequentially dripping 0.1 mM HCl (Fujifilm Wako Pure Chemical Corp., Osaka, Japan). As a standard method, pH and iCa concentrations were measured using a benchtop pH/water quality analyzer (LAQUA-F73, Horiba Advanced Techno, Co., Ltd.) and the respective electrodes (pH: F-73S, iCa: 6583S-10C, Horiba Advanced Techno, Co., Ltd.) using the liquid electrode method. In addition, colostrum pH and iCa concentrations were measured at the same sample points using the compact water quality testing devices, LAQUAtwin pH-33B and LAQUAtwin Ca-11 (Horiba Advanced Techno, Co., Ltd.), respectively. This procedure was repeated three times to measure iCa concentrations at various pH levels. The appearance and operation of the pH (LAQUAtwin pH-33B) and iCa checkers (LAQUAtwin Ca-11) for water quality analyses are shown in Fig. 1. Immediately before pH and iCa measurements using LAQUAtwin pH-33B and Ca-11, respectively, a drop of a specimen (>0.3 mL) was applied directly to the end of a test strip for each device. In accordance with the method recommended by the manufacturer of the water quality meters, the samples were left to stand after pH adjustment to allow sedimentation, and the supernatant was collected for measurement. The MEAS button was pressed immediately after closing the light cover of each device, and results immediately appeared on the display. These devices have measurement ranges of pH 0 to 14 and 0.4 to 40,080 mg/L Ca2+, respectively. Prior to measurement, they are calibrated using a two-point calibration with the standard solutions provided, following the manufacturer’s specifications. It takes less than 3 min for results to be displayed after the initiation of the calibration.

Clinical trials performed as the Cow-Side Test

Thirty-six multiparous Holstein cows participated in the present study, which examined whether it is possible to predict MF simply, inexpensively, and rapidly using a water quality testing device to measure calcium concentrations in colostrum. The 36 cows were dairy cows kept under similar husbandry conditions on a commercial dairy farm in the Kushiro district of Hokkaido, a dairy farming region in the northern part of Japan. The individual information of these cows is summarized in Table 1. All cows delivered calves without dystocia, and 50 mL of colostrum and 10 mL of venous blood from the jugular vein were collected at the first milking on the day of parturition. Blood samples were carefully collected using heparinized disposable syringes, avoiding air contamination.

An ion-selective electrode handheld meter, the bovine blood iCa checker (LAQUAtwin Ca-11C, HORIBA Advanced Techno, Co., Ltd.) was used to measure iCa concentrations in whole blood. Measurements were conducted in accordance with previously reported methods [14]. Blood electrolytes (Na+, K+, and Cl−), and blood gases (pH, PCO2, tCO2, HCO3−, BE, Anion gaps, and hemoglobin) were also measured as the Cow-Side Test immediately after collection using the commercially available blood gas analyzer, i-STAT 1 (Abbott Point-of-Care, Abbott Laboratories, Chicago, IL, USA), and its dedicated cartridge, the EC8+ (Abbott Laboratories), according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Heparinized blood was centrifuged at 3,000 × g for 10 min to obtain plasma. Plasma was frozen at −18°C for delivery to the university, where it was stored at −30°C until analyzed. Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) activities and blood urea nitrogen (BUN), glucose, total cholesterol (T-Cho), and total bilirubin (T-Bil) concentrations in plasma were measured using a benchtop blood biochemistry analyzer (SPOTCHEMTM EZ SP-4430, Arkray Inc., Kyoto, Japan) and dedicated cartridges (SPOTCHEMTM II General Screening 1, Arkray Inc.).

Colostrum pH, iCa and tCa were measured simultaneously with blood iCa measurement as a Cow-Side Test. Colostrum on the first day of parturition was subjected to measurements of pH and calcium ion concentrations at room temperature using the simple, inexpensive, and rapid water quality testing devices, LAQUAtwin pH-33B and LAQUAtwin Ca-11 (Horiba Advanced Techno, Co., Ltd.), respectively. The pH of and calcium ion concentrations in colostrum at this time were defined as pH0 and iCa, respectively. Following the dropwise addition of 0.1 N HCl to these sample, the iCa concentration in milk at the time when pH reached 4 was defined as the relative tCa concentration in colostrum, and was recorded along with the amount of acid added.

In all cows, whether they developed MF due to hypocalcemia within one week, including the day of sample collection, was extracted from the electronic medical record system. Based on the criteria of Goff [10], MF was defined as a condition in which a farmer refers an animal to a clinical veterinarian due to loss of appetite, and the clinical veterinarian examines the animal and finds that the animal has loss of appetite, reduced ruminal motility, difficulty standing due to generalized muscle weakness, reduced surface temperature due to circulatory collapse, depression, and loss of consciousness, and the clinical veterinarian determines that treatment with intravenous infusion of 22.5% calcium boron gluconate is necessary. Therefore, cows treated for MF by a clinical veterinarian within one week after parturition were classified as the “MF group” and those not treated as the “control” group.

Statistical analysis

Data were statistically analyzed by the Deming regression and a Bland-Altman plot using a commercial statistical software program (MedCalc v.20.110, Software Ltd., Oostende, Belgium). Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) in the case of the normal distribution or as a median [min-max] in the case of the non-normal distribution of variance.

Laboratory-level precision and accuracy control tests: The significance of relationships was assessed using a repeated Deming regression, and identity was evaluated using a repeated Bland-Altman plot. In the Deming regression, a correlation was identified when the 95% confidence interval (CI) of the intercept crossed 0 and 95%CI of the slope crossed 1 for the line obtained from the measurement results of both instruments. The Bland-Altman plot is widely used as the limits of agreement method for assessing the agreement between two methods of medical measurement. Laboratory-level precision trials provided repeated pairs of measurements on the same subject. Therefore, a repeated Bland-Altman plot was performed using the above commercial statistical software to analyze such clustered observations, both when the underlying quantity was assumed to be changing and when it was not [2, 9, 27]. A repeated Bland-Altman plot averages repeated measurements for each subject and uses only the number of a subject’s pairs of means to calculate 95% limits [2]. Although these limits are narrower than the correct ones, this difference is negligible because there is more variation between subjects than for repeated observations on a single subject [2]. As a result of comparing the methods, it may be concluded that the results are “compatible” when the degree of agreement between the new method and the standard method is good. In the present study, the ratio of the difference between paired measurements to the mean was defined as the “relative error (RE)”. If the number of measurements with RE ± 95%CI or less was within 75% of the total number of measurements, it was considered to be “compatible” [6]. The significance level was set at P<0.05.

Clinical trials performed as the Cow-Side Test: After the evaluation of variance using the F-test, comparisons between groups for each parameter when MF was considered to be a disease event (positive) were performed using the Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test depending on the condition of distribution. The effect size between the two groups was also calculated using Cohen’s d, and an absolute value of d ≥0.8 was considered to indicate a large effect (significant difference) between the two groups. An odds ratio (OR) was examined in the stepwise selection method between the control and MF groups. Regarding items for which OR was calculated, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to characterize the sensitivity and specificity of each parameter concentration for MF. The optimal cut-off value for a test was calculated based on the Youden index. The Youden index (J) is defined as the maximum vertical distance between the ROC curve and the diagonal or chance line, calculated as J=maximum [sensitivity + specificity −1]. The cut-off value from ROC curves that corresponds to J is taken as the optimal cut-off value. The significance level was set at P<0.05.

RESULTS

Laboratory-level precision and accuracy control tests

Figure 2 shows the relationship between pH and iCa concentrations in colostrum when pH was changed by adding 0.1 mM HCl to colostrum samples collected from six dairy cows. A total of 249 paired samples were obtained by adding 0.1 N HCl to the six types of colostrum. In all colostrum samples, iCa concentrations were the highest when pH was around 4. Therefore, a statistical analysis was performed using colostrum adjusted to pH 4, the pH at which the most calcium was released from colostrum proteins and the majority of calcium was ionized. In other words, the iCa concentration in colostrum adjusted to pH 4 was defined as the relative tCa concentration in colostrum.

In addition, LAQUAtwin pH-33B was “compatible” with F-73S because the frequency of differences between measurements within ± 95%CI of the mean was 94.0% (15/249, >75%), demonstrating good agreement.

Clinical trials conducted as the Cow-Side Test

Nine of the 36 cows examined developed hypocalcemia-related MF and were treated by a clinical veterinary surgeon with calcium-containing fluids, resulting in an incidence of MF of 25.0% (9/36) in this study. Table 1 summarizes the age in months and parity of the cases used in this study, as well as the results obtained on blood iCa concentrations, colostrum pH, iCa and tCa concentrations in colostrum, blood electrolytes, blood gas, and plasma biochemistry tests. The normal distribution was confirmed for all parameters except for the age in months and blood Cl−.

The age in months was significantly higher in the MF group compared to the control group, which is consistent with previous reports indicating that increased age is a risk factor for the development of MF [22]. Figure 5A shows a comparison of blood iCa concentrations between cows that underwent treatment for MF within one week after parturition and those that did not, as well as the results obtained from the evaluation of its predictive diagnostic ability. The blood iCa concentrations of Holstein cows treated for MF within one week after parturition were significantly lower (0.82 ± 0.18 mM) than those of cows that did not develop MF (1.02 ± 0.18 mM, P=0.008, d=1.091). Figure 6A shows a comparison of tCa concentrations in colostrum, which was iCa concentrations in colostrum measured by adding HCl dropwise to colostrum to adjust the pH to 4, between the control and MF groups. tCa concentrations in the colostrum of cows with MF were significantly higher (2,141.1 ± 623.7 mg/L) than those in cows without MF (1,546.3 ± 494.4 mg/L, P=0.006, d=−1.127). No significant differences were observed in other parameters between the control and MF groups.

An ROC analysis of blood iCa and colostrum tCa concentrations was performed on samples taken on the day of parturition because these concentrations were extracted as highly related factors to the onset of MF within one week after parturition. The area under the curves (AUCs) of blood iCa and colostrum tCa concentrations at parturition were 0.784 (P=0.0013) and 0.792 (P<0.0001), respectively, indicating their potential as predictors of the onset of MF within one week using a specimen taken at parturition.

Based on the ROC curve analysis, the diagnostic cut-off value of blood iCa concentrations for predicting the onset of MF within 1 week after parturition was proposed to be ≤0.99 mM (Fig. 5B, J index=0.5185 [95%CI: 0.1852–0.7037]). When the cut-off value was set at ≤0.98 mM, sensitivity and specificity were 88.9 and 63.0%, respectively. Based on the ROC curve analysis, the diagnostic cut-off value of the tCa concentration in colostrum to predict the onset of MF within 1 week after parturition was proposed to be >2,000 mg/L (Fig. 6B, J index=0.5556 [95%CI: 0.2593–0.7407]). Sensitivity and specificity were 66.7 and 88.9%, respectively. We then investigated whether the colostrum tCa concentration or blood iCa concentration on the day of parturition was a better predictor of the onset of MF within one week after parturition. The difference in AUC between the two parameters was evaluated using an ROC comparison, and no significant difference was observed in their diagnostic ability (P>0.05).

DISCUSSION

To diagnose MF on dairy farms, blood iCa concentrations in cows are commonly measured [18] using POC devices, such as i-STAT 1 [4]. However, this method requires blood sampling, which may place a significant burden on veterinarians, particularly in large-scale farms with many postpartum cows or farms located far from veterinary services. Additionally, the use of expensive cartridges for testing imposes a financial burden on farmers. Therefore, screening for MF through blood iCa measurements is considered to have significant disadvantages for both veterinarians and farmers. In the present study, we examined the feasibility of using colostrum, a sample that is easily collected by farmers, to predict the onset of MF. We initially investigated the pretreatment conditions required to measure calcium concentrations in colostrum and assessed the accuracy and precision of a compact water quality testing device. In precision tests using six colostrum samples, iCa concentrations peaked at around pH 4. Calcium in milk exists in both the colloidal and soluble phases, with the colloidal phase forming part of the casein micelle structure through its association with casein and phosphate. One reason for this distribution is that casein micelles have the property of disintegrating as the pH of milk decreases [24]. Therefore, all calcium in colostrum may be ionized by adjusting the pH of colostrum to 4, thereby allowing for the measurement of the relative tCa concentration. Additionally, performance testing of the portable pH meter LAQUAtwin pH-33B revealed a strong correlation with a standard benchtop pH meter (F-73S), showing a concordance correlation coefficient and similarity in measurements. These results indicate compatibility between the two devices, suggesting that LAQUAtwin pH-33B is applicable to simple pH measurements of colostrum on dairy farms. We also confirmed the accuracy and precision of the compact calcium ion meter LAQUAtwin Ca-11 by comparing it with a standard benchtop analyzer (6583S-10C). The results obtained showed a high concordance correlation coefficient and similarity, indicating compatibility. Therefore, LAQUAtwin Ca-11 is considered to be suitable for easy measurements of calcium concentrations in bovine colostrum.

We then investigated whether colostrum, which is easily collected by farmers, may be used to predict the onset of MF. Comparative analyses of blood iCa concentrations, colostrum pH, iCa and tCa in colostrum, blood electrolytes/gas, and plasma biochemistry between cows with and without MF revealed significant differences in blood iCa and colostrum tCa concentrations only. Blood iCa concentrations were lower, whereas colostral tCa concentrations were higher in cows with MF than in cows without MF; these findings support the report of Low et al. [15], who suggested that iCa is transported from blood to milk via PTHrP, which has been implicated in the pathogenesis of MF. Furthermore, the ROC analysis suggested that measuring these two parameters may enable predictions of the onset of MF. The cut-off value based on the Youden index for blood iCa concentrations was ≤0.99 mM, and when set at 0.98 mM, sensitivity was 88.9% and specificity was 63.0%. Therefore, the measurement of blood iCa concentrations is a highly sensitive diagnostic method that may be useful for veterinarians when making a definitive diagnosis of MF. On the other hand, the cut-off for tCa concentrations in colostrum was >2,000 mg/L, with a sensitivity of 66.7% and specificity of 88.9%, indicating that this test has high specificity. Therefore, measurements of tCa concentrations in colostrum are useful as a preliminary screening tool for farmers to identify cows at risk of developing MF and then either request veterinary treatment or implement preventive measures themselves. No significant difference was observed in AUCs between blood iCa and colostrum tCa concentrations (P>0.05), suggesting that the diagnostic ability of colostrum tCa was not inferior to that of blood iCa.

The present study demonstrated that the compact devices LAQUAtwin pH-33B and LAQUAtwin Ca-11 were suitable for measuring pH and calcium concentrations in bovine colostrum. Furthermore, adjusting the pH of colostrum to 4 allowed for the measurement of relative tCa concentrations. The results obtained herein indicate that the use of pretreated colostrum and a compact calcium ion meter to measure tCa concentrations predicts the onset of MF, with levels >2,000 mg/L indicating a high risk. On the other hand, since only nine cows with MF were included in this study, further studies may be necessary to more thoroughly evaluate the validity of this screening method in randomized multiple clinical trials.

Finally, we also propose a practical method for farmers to predict the onset of MF using colostrum on the farm. After collecting colostrum from postpartum cows, farmers add 0.1 mM HCl and confirm that the pH is approximately 4 using LAQUAtwin pH-33B. They then use LAQUAtwin Ca-11 to measure tCa concentrations. If the result exceeds 2,000 mg/L, they may either consult a veterinarian or administer preventive treatment themselves, enabling efficient and labor-saving peripartum management. In conclusion, the present results suggest that measurements of tCa concentrations in colostrum using a compact calcium ion meter offer a simple, inexpensive, and highly specific method for identifying cows at a high risk of developing MF.

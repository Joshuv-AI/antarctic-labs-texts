Bridging pediatric to adult care in cystinosis: a scoping review of healthcare transition of care protocols and a roadmap for future practice
Authors: Sanat Subhash, Nicholas Pantelakis, Kyle Backston, Ruhaan Bajpai, Amir Safadi, Brandon Petrovich, Bradley A Warady, Maria Diaz-Gonzalez de Ferris, Rupesh Raina
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCystinosis is a rare lysosomal storage disorder that affects approximately 1 in 100,000 to 200,000 live births worldwide. Long-term graft success in cystinosis depends on cysteamine adherence and structured multidisciplinary transition, supported by protocols developed by both pediatric nephrologists and hospital organizations. This review explores institutional and regional care models, structured programs such as the RISE protocol and Got Transition’s Six Core Elements, and major barriers including adult nephrology expertise, psychosocial stressors, and insurance coverage gaps. Additionally, we explore the impact of digital health tools for their role in supporting cysteamine adherence and maintaining continuity of care. Emerging technologies such as machine learning also provide future opportunities to improve patient care during the transition period. Transition plans must also integrate digital tools and mental health-informed strategies tailored to Gen Z and Alpha cystinosis patients. Due to the lifelong nature of cystinosis and the complexity of adolescent development, transition models are essential to promoting long-term autonomy in patients. We conducted a comprehensive search of the PubMed databases for studies published between 2000 and 2025 that addressed structured transition protocols and long-term outcomes in pediatric patients with cystinosis. A critical analysis of existing cystinosis transition protocols is essential to evaluate their strengths and limitations and to identify approaches better tailored to the needs of pediatric cystinosis patient populations. This review aims to evaluate elements of existing protocols, highlight components of a successful transition, and propose future strategies to improve care in patients with cystinosis.Keywords: Cystinosis, Healthcare transition, Transition of care, Pediatric nephrology, Medication adherence

Background

Cystinosis is a rare, autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disorder where cystine accumulates within lysosomes and leads to progressive organ dysfunction, affecting approximately 1 in 100,000 to 200,000 live births worldwide [1]. Nephropathic cystinosis, the most severe form of the disease, accounts for 95% of all cases and often progresses to chronic kidney disease stage 5 (CKD-5) in the first decade of life if left untreated [2–4]. Treatment consists of lifelong cysteamine therapy in order to deplete lysosomal cystine, with earlier diagnosis and initiation of therapy associated with improved renal outcomes and fewer complications [4]. The importance of early screening underscores the need for transition frameworks that include proactive diagnosis, coordinated adult care, and management of extrarenal complications like hypothyroidism and diabetes [5].

These pediatric care plans are often complex and frequently extend into adulthood, marking a critical transition window for patients to assume responsibility for their health and manage psychosocial complications. These patients also experience a period of cognitive development associated with increased risk-taking, impulsive behavior, and non-adherence, further complicating their healthcare transition [6].

Cystinosis patients and their support system can benefit from a structured approach to the transition from pediatric to adult care [7]. While relatively new, structured healthcare transitions represent a large advancement in the management of chronic disease [8].

Cystinosis transition protocols provide a structural framework for multidisciplinary coordination while emphasizing patient autonomy and education, but lack sustainable implementation options in low-resource settings [9]. Critical analysis of existing cystinosis transition protocols is vital to uncover individual strengths and weaknesses and to identify approaches better suited for specific patients. Where existing literature primarily describes individual experience, this review aims to synthesize existing programs into a unified implementation framework by identifying and evaluating transferable components across diverse healthcare systems to improve care in patients with cystinosis.

Study selection

Two reviewers (S.S. and N.P.) independently screened titles and abstracts retrieved from the database searches for relevance. The full texts of potentially eligible articles were then obtained and assessed against the inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Data synthesis and integration

All eligible articles were reviewed in full and systematically integrated into the narrative to address the major components of transition in cystinosis care. Sources were categorized by key themes, including structured transition protocols, transplant-related needs, digital health tools, multidisciplinary coordination, psychosocial challenges, and long-term patient outcomes. Real-world evidence from institutional programs, cohort studies, and expert guidelines was further incorporated to provide context review sections and support comparative analysis across existing models.

This scoping review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) guidelines. Because PROSPERO does not accept scoping review protocols, the review was not registered on PROSPERO.

A scoping review methodology was selected because the available literature on cystinosis transition care consists largely of heterogeneous evidence including expert recommendations, institutional protocols, observational studies, qualitative analyses, and case-based reports, making formal systematic comparison or meta-analysis inappropriate.

Results

Our PubMed search yielded 515 records. After title and abstract screening, 335 full-text reports were assessed for eligibility. Of these, 21 reports met inclusion criteria. An additional 37 reports were identified through citation searching, review of institutional protocols, and manual gray literature review. In total, 58 reports were included in this scoping review, as summarized in the PRISMA flow diagram (Fig. 1).

The 58 included studies consisted of narrative and systematic reviews (n = 20), original cohort and observational studies (n = 13), expert consensus statements and clinical guidelines (n = 7), qualitative and patient experience studies (n = 5), case reports and case series (n = 4), randomized and quasi-experimental trials (n = 3), program and protocol development reports (n = 3), and other study types including registry reports, concept analyses, and machine learning modeling studies (n = 3).

Disease burden and clinical course

Cystinosis often results in kidney failure before the transition period, but unlike many other conditions, transplant does not halt the disease progression, requiring lifelong cysteamine therapy to prevent complications [5, 10]. As patients age, the disease impact often evolves to systemic issues affecting the endocrine, ocular, neurological, muscular, and gastrointestinal systems [11]. While pregnancy is often successful, nearly half of pregnancies are complicated by preeclampsia [12]. Premature deliveries and cesarean section are also common. Male fertility is frequently hindered by obstructive azoospermia [13, 14]. Clinicians rarely need to perform nephrectomies, as tubular losses generally decrease once renal replacement therapy is initiated [15].

Adherence and disease management

There is a marked decline in cysteamine adherence during adolescence, which is associated with accelerated progression of kidney disease, reduced quality of life, and shortened life expectancy even in patients with successful transplantation [10, 16–18]. Poor adherence to cysteamine therapy is associated with shorter life expectancy, progression of kidney disease, and exacerbation of extra-renal complications, such as hypothyroidism and diabetes [10]. Alarmingly, adherence to essential cysteamine therapy declined sharply during adolescence and adulthood [16]. Leukocyte cystine levels allow for direct measurement of therapeutic adherence and can provide the opportunity to detect lapses early, particularly during the transition period when the risk is greater [19]. Equally as important is the systematic collection of post-transition adherence metrics, such as leukocyte cystine levels, which provide objective indicators of how well patients adhere to therapy and can allow for early intervention if a lapse in adherence is detected [19].

Multidisciplinary care

Managing the complex nature of the transfer requires the collaboration of multiple specialists, including nephrologists, metabolic specialists, ophthalmologists, cardiologists, and neurologists [11]. Coordinating care across multiple providers, including nephrologists, metabolic specialists, ophthalmologists, cardiologists, neurologists, and renal nurses, is critical for patients with cystinosis but often challenging due to poor communication and unclear responsibilities [11]. Extensive care teams require diligent interoperability to ensure the minimization of errors and proper treatment across settings. Levtchenko et al. emphasize that without a strong communication infrastructure and shared documentation, multidisciplinary teams are vulnerable to care delays, treatment errors, and gaps during the transition process [11]. One key barrier is the shortage of adult nephrologists familiar with managing cystinosis, often forcing patients to travel considerable distances to access specialized, coordinated care [11].

Transition readiness frameworks and education

The RISE to Transition protocol was developed for kidney transplant patients and focuses on preparing the patient through achieving four key competencies: Recognition, Insight, Self-Reliance, and Establishment of Healthy Habits [20]. The model educates patients on their condition and treatment while also teaching practical skills for navigating adult care, including managing appointments, insurance, and medication refills [20]. The RISE protocol has successfully educated transplant patients on key differences as they enter adult practice [21, 22]. Another useful tool to measure transition readiness includes the TRxANSITION scale, measuring: Type of chronic health condition, Rx/medications, adherence, nutrition, self-management skills, issues of reproduction, trade/school, insurance, ongoing support, and new health care providers. Structured frameworks such as RISE and the TRxANSITION scale include competency in insurance literacy and financial responsibility as part of transition readiness education [21, 23]. In line with these principles, the Spanish coordinated transition model proposed by Ariceta et al. appoints a dedicated “case manager” who links pediatric and adult teams, convenes joint appointments, and monitors patients for at least the first post-transfer year, showing how structured roles can enhance continuity of care [24].

Psychosocial factors

Psychosocial stressors such as emotional distress, school difficulties, and treatment nonadherence are also common among adolescents with cystinosis and must be proactively addressed during transition [25]. Regular screening of both patients and caregivers and access to counseling and social work services are essential to address the significant emotional and socioeconomic burdens of long-term disease management [25]. Ariceta et al. provide insights into patient-level challenges through a qualitative study consisting of interviews and workshops [17].

Structural support

Expansive care teams involving physicians, nurses, pharmacists, social workers, and dietitians can improve transition outcomes by supporting patient education, nutrition, and psychosocial well-being [26]. Multicohort studies and observational analyses in patients with renal disease have reported increased survival, delayed dialysis initiation, and higher rates of home-based therapy selection when expansive teams are involved [26]. Implementation can be challenging though due to costs and lack of coverage.

Digital support and technology integration

Digital integration of patient records in transition registries, telehealth visits in clinics, and mobile transition readiness tools can provide more resources for patients and support providers with patient data and outreach options at a reduced cost [27, 28]. Additionally, social networking sites are used by pediatric patients to receive information and support regarding their condition, but there remains a lack of comparative studies evaluating the effectiveness of these platforms in pediatric nephrology populations [29].

Discussion

Adolescent development is a critical time of maturation of the prefrontal cortex, the portion of the brain responsible for executive functions such as planning, organization, and impulse control [30]. During this window of adolescent development, certain reward-processing circuits in the brain mature before other control systems, predisposing these adolescents to a heightened risk of impulsivity and risk-taking [31]. Consequently, adolescents may end up prioritizing immediate social rewards over long-term medication adherence benefits. Many adolescents with chronic illnesses report avoiding medication schedules to prioritize peer acceptance and preserve flexibility in their daily routines [32]. Furthermore, as responsibility for care shifts from caregivers to patients, adolescents often lack the knowledge that caregivers have provided over the years, leaving parents hesitant to fully relinquish their management responsibilities [33]. These developmental and psychosocial challenges highlight the importance of having structured HCT protocols in place to enhance the likelihood of long-term adherence to treatment, particularly in a disease like cystinosis. Outline of the multifaceted impact of cystinosis in pediatric transition of care

Cystinosis carries uniquely high risk for healthcare transition compared to many other pediatric chronic conditions and requires a disease-specific approach. A lifelong, progressive, and multisystemic disease, it requires continuous management through adolescence and into adulthood [2]. The period of adolescence to young adulthood is one of vulnerability, where even minor gaps in care or adherence can result in significant complications. Therefore, transition in cystinosis should be a longitudinal process with continuity across pediatric and adult healthcare to ensure continued care and adherence.

Long-term outcomes in cystinosis are highly dependent on continued adherence to cysteamine therapy, and the transition period is an important milestone in the continuity of care. Because of the risk factors associated with adolescence, the quality of transition is tied to adherence support, and consistent biomarker monitoring is important to prevent unfavorable renal and transplant outcomes.

Existing transition models

Several structured transition protocols developed for other chronic diseases have shown varying success in promoting key features such as autonomy, disease understanding, and navigating the health system, which provides a foundation for evaluating their potential application to cystinosis care. We comparatively analyzed transition programs to find common elements that can be adapted specifically for cystinosis care.

The RISE to Transition protocol supports patient education and nephrology-specific preparation, and aligns well with the needs of cystinosis patients. This offers a promising framework that could be further adapted with enhancements in communication, digital tools, and long-term monitoring.

Another technique to manage the shift is Got Transition’s Six Core Elements to Health Care Transition™, which emphasizes early planning and coordinated involvement of both pediatric and adult providers [34]. The process begins with creating a transition policy and tracking individual patient readiness across care periods, typically beginning in early adolescence around ages 12 to 14 [34]. Pediatric transition teams utilize readiness assessments and organized transition planning visits to prepare the patient for a formal transfer completed between ages 18 to 22 [34].

Specialized transition clinics provide a site for providers to meet with the patient and coordinate the transfer of care and responsibility. One such program implemented at Lurie Children’s Hospital and Northwestern Medicine has been successfully used in patients with cystinosis, demonstrating the value of early education and readiness assessment prior to the initial transfer visit [35]. In successful cases, adult nephrology teams meet patients in the pediatric clinic environment prior to transfer, helping foster familiarity and trust during the transition process [35]. Continued follow-up at the adult institution reinforced adherence to cysteamine therapy and facilitated a structured, gradual handover [35].

Other models in pediatric nephrology transition care include organization-specific structures like the STARx Program at the University of North Carolina, Chapel Hill, which prepares patients for the transfer of care using multifaceted tools. Designed for chronic conditions, the STARx questionnaire covers all major requirements and, while it has not been tested clinically, would likely translate well to cystinosis [36]. However, the STARx model relies on patient self-reports, limiting its practical potential, and there is very limited data about successful integration in clinical settings [36].

Another institutional model is the Taking Responsibility for Adolescent/Adult Care (ON TRAC) framework, developed at the Children’s and Women’s Health Centre of British Columbia [37]. The protocol introduces a stepwise process that follows the patient’s abilities as they mature, giving practice exercises to train them on the differences between adult clinics. The program emphasizes development and maturity and is adaptable to many conditions, but the investment of time and staff required to be successful poses a significant barrier for both patients and providers.

Effective healthcare transition programs share important factors such as patient focused education, early engagement, multispecialty collaboration, and support after transfer. These programs allow direct collaboration between adult and pediatric providers, sustaining trust and progression. The Northwestern clinic provides an example of longitudinal success in cystinosis patients, but improving and progressing these programs is still challenging. These programs emphasize the significance of adjusting transition plans to align with the unique psychosocial and medical needs of cystinosis patients, with future models requiring flexibility and disease-focused care correlation. Existing transition protocols demonstrate an ability to improve patient autonomy, education, and mental health. An expansive care team can increase favorable outcomes throughout transition. Randomized data also suggest that early referral to integrated care models can slow the decline in eGFR and reduce complications and drug-related issues, such as catheter use at the start of dialysis [38, 39]. Applying these findings to cystinosis patients with multisystem involvement and complex medication regimens highlights the importance of adaptable, team-based transition clinics. Incorporating multidisciplinary roles into these clinics enables personalized interventions and reduces care gaps for their condition while also accounting for their mental and emotional needs throughout the move to adult practice.

Regular screening of both patients and caregivers and access to counseling and social work services are essential to address the significant emotional and socioeconomic burdens of long-term disease management [25]. Adolescents face significant psychosocial factors that must be addressed throughout the transition through regular screening and access to social services. Patients and their families face major challenges dealing with the complexities of disease management, and building strong patient–provider relationships within transition clinics helps foster trust and support continuity of care across the pediatric-to-adult interface.

Sexual health must also be adequately addressed. Pregnancy can have several complications, but sexual health may also be indirectly affected by body image concerns related to halitosis and body odor, both well known side-effects of cysteamine treatment, and likely hinder relationships during critical reproductive years [40]. Among other complications, patients may also experience limited employment opportunities and work capacity, furthering their social isolation. Fertility counseling and assisted reproduction technology may be useful in these patients, although cost may be a limiting factor.

Multidisciplinary treatment protocols provide a framework for effective, longitudinal transition of care in cystinosis patients, but face challenges involving communication and coordination. Addressing these barriers requires shared documentation and planning to designate goals and responsibilities. As transition protocols evolve, embedding digital tools can further strengthen care plans across pediatric and adult services.

Barriers to effective transition

Cystinosis transition care faces several major barriers preventing broader implementation, with a combination of provider-level gaps, systemic fragmentation, and patient-level challenges demanding structured coordination across multidisciplinary teams. A major obstacle is the lack of available nephrologists and ancillary professionals that are familiar with cystinosis. This limited workforce not only delays continuity of care, but also contributes to fragmented follow-up and inconsistent management of multisystem complications.

In addition to provider-level gaps, patient-level experiences and perspectives further illustrate the complexity of transition. Themes specific to the adolescent transition period introduce several challenges hindering effective disease management. Patients often feel they need to hide their condition due to social pressures, and these challenges often persist or worsen into adulthood. Patients may restart treatment only when faced with pressure from physicians, worsening of the disease, or discovery of a more practical way to take medications consistently. The process of transition of care may also be complicated by caregiver burden, as many caregivers experience significant physical, emotional, social, and financial strain stemming from their child’s kidney condition [41]. Caregivers experience stress in the form of grief and guilt from their child’s condition, while the child associates the challenging treatment with over-protectiveness from their caregiver [42, 43]. Feelings of uncertainty fueled by this reciprocal interaction may intensify the already-present impulsivity and risk-taking behavior in transitioning patients. Single-center data from Spain shows 6 of 9 mothers caring for children with cystinosis experienced anxiety and depression, and 4 of 9 experienced severe care burden [44]. Additionally, a recent cross-sectional study indicates caregivers experience higher burden while their child is on dialysis compared to post-kidney transplant [45]. Family members also serve as a source of support for patients during treatment. Creating more accessible care pathways during the treatment process can help ease patients’ burden by reducing stress at home and fostering collaborative decision-making with providers; this may help alleviate caregiver strain and improve longitudinal patient outcomes [46].

Given the complex nature of cystinosis treatment, assessing caregiver well-being during the shift to adult care is essential to minimize adverse outcomes and ensure continuity of care. A systematic review of 44 transition of care interventions reported that incorporating patient- and family-centered care transition interventions significantly decreased hospital readmissions over all time periods [47]. Collaborative transition of care processes involving patient’s families and caregivers in disease education, rehabilitation care, and follow-up requirements can improve continuity of care and reduce adverse clinical outcomes during the vulnerable transition period [47]. Family-centered transition clinics offer a structured solution to address the complex needs of patients and caregivers by integrating early education, psychosocial support, and individualized care planning [48]. By involving caregivers in shared decision-making and dialysis preparation, these clinics promote continuity of care and reduce adverse outcomes during the transition process [48]. Patient representatives and advocacy organizations also play an important role in transition planning by amplifying patient priorities, supporting education, and reinforcing adherence outside the clinical setting. Meaningful engagement with these groups can help ensure that transition programs remain patient-centered and aligned with real-world needs.

Financial and insurance-related barriers further complicate continuity of care during transition. Navigating insurance systems is an additional challenge during transition, as adolescents assume responsibility for medication access, transplant follow-up, and specialty appointments [49]. Policy efforts aimed at extending Medicaid eligibility and improving insurance continuity for rare disease populations may reduce treatment interruption during this vulnerable period [50].

Renal transplantation considerations

While renal transplant is still the standard of care for cystinosis patients progressing to end-stage renal disease, favorable outcomes require sustained management post-transplant and adherence to therapy. In a study from Kuwait, cystinosis patients undergoing kidney transplantation received significantly more potent induction therapy than non-cystinosis transplant recipients, despite both groups having similar immunosuppressive regimens (P < 0.05). Cystinosis patients demonstrated higher rates of primary graft function (P = 0.024), but rates of post-transplant complications and patient survival were similar between groups [51]. Also, a case series from India reported four young male patients with cystinosis who progressed to end-stage renal disease before undergoing renal transplantation [52]. Most patients maintained excellent long-term graft function after a median follow-up of 7.5 years while receiving tacrolimus-based immunosuppression and cysteamine therapy (however, one patient experienced graft loss due to T-cell-mediated rejection) [52].

In cystinosis patients with CKD-5, residual Fanconi syndrome often persists after renal transplantation. Combined with the decline in adherence to cysteamine therapy observed in adolescence, these findings highlight how successful renal transplantation in cystinosis relies on sustained adherence to cysteamine and multidisciplinary follow-up. Without structured transition programs, pediatric cystinosis patients remain at higher risk for graft dysfunction and complications caused by declining adherence through adolescence.

Digital health and remote monitoring in transition

Transition models can be costly and resource-intensive, and digital tools and remote resources can provide cost-effective and accessible support to patients, parents, and providers within transition processes and integrated adherence management methods. Digital tools such as the Just TRAC it! program offers mobile-friendly platforms to assess transition readiness, track skill development, and support medical and care autonomy in adolescents with chronic conditions, providing a template for transition of care models in cystinosis [53].

Other transition protocols have utilized online learning systems, remote weekly health assessment forms, and longitudinal virtual and in-person meetings to enhance patient engagement and reinforce self-management skills by combining flexible access to educational materials with personalized feedback and emotional support from healthcare providers [54]. Mobile applications have shown benefits in increasing adherence in adult chronic kidney disease patients, with health system integration, patient education, and drug reminders as key features supporting treatment engagement [55]. Measuring outcomes such as transition readiness, self-efficacy, and long-term adherence post-transfer is needed to validate the clinical utility of these interventions in structured pediatric-to-adult care transitions.

Challenges to implementation certainly exist: privacy concerns, technical difficulty, funding, and a lack of perceived connection with the clinician can also complicate integrating digital systems. Despite these limitations, mobile applications and telehealth systems provide scalable platforms that can promote self-management and support individualized transition pathways for adolescents with cystinosis.

The generational shift in adolescents and young adults with cystinosis introduces new challenges in designing effective longitudinal transition protocols. Patients born in Generation Z (1995–2010) and Generation Alpha (2010 onward) exhibit different preferences in communication and technology engagement compared to earlier cohorts [56, 57]. Generation Z patients may prefer online access to health information and engagement through social media platforms, while Generation Alpha patients are better suited to visual tools and mobile healthcare applications [56, 57]. Transition protocols incorporating technology-enabled readiness assessments and mobile adherence tools may enhance engagement and autonomy in these populations.

Long-term outcomes post-transition

As patients with cystinosis complete the transition to adult care, understanding their long-term health trajectories, such as adherence, quality of life, and morbidity, becomes essential. Adult cystinosis patients more frequently experience significant morbidities, including pulmonary insufficiency and swallowing dysfunction, both of which contribute to a decreased functional capacity for the patient [18]. In addition, health-related quality of life among patients with cystinosis is generally lower than that of healthy individuals, particularly in the social and physical aspects of life [58]. These quality-of-life challenges in patients emphasize the need for strict adherence to their treatment regimen.

Recent case studies further suggest that initiating cysteamine therapy before the age of five and maintaining consistent treatment into adulthood can significantly slow the progression of renal decline and reduce the severity of multisystem complications in later stages of life [18]. These favorable outcomes are most commonly reported in cohorts receiving coordinated, often multidisciplinary follow-up, emphasizing the value of collaborative care during and after transition [18].Poor adherence, on the other hand, is associated with poorer outcomes overall [10].

Despite these known outcomes, longitudinal tracking of patients after pediatric to adult transition remains scarce in cystinosis, limiting the understanding of the long-term trajectories of the disease. The NAPRTCS cystinosis registry enrolls only patients younger than age 25, and while it continues to follow up beyond that age, it includes few individuals who reach middle or late adulthood [59]. To address these gaps, establishing multicenter databases that follow cystinosis patients through adulthood is essential to enable robust outcome tracking, evaluate the effectiveness of transition protocols, and guide evidence-based improvements in long-term care strategies.

Stepwise transition process for cystinosis care

Building on our review of transition protocols, institutional care models, and cohort outcomes, we propose a structured and staged process designed to guide cystinosis patients from pediatric to adult care. Transition begins with Early Preparation (Ages 11–13), where patients are introduced to transition concepts during pediatric visits and begin to foster self-management skills including cysteamine dosing and appointment scheduling. This is followed by Skill-Building and Structured Planning (Ages 14–15), where patients are enrolled into formal protocols, like RISE or the Six Core Elements, and receive targeted education on navigating medical responsibilities and cystinosis disease management. This stage is supported by a multidisciplinary team including nephrologists, metabolic specialists, cardiologists, and ophthalmologists.

The Readiness Assessment and Goal Setting (Ages 15–16) involves the implementation of tools such as the STARx Questionnaire or Just TRAC It! to evaluate readiness. Physicians and transition coordinators collaborate to address gaps in knowledge and create individualized care plans, often using digital tools. During Transfer Coordination and Adult Introduction (Ages 17–18), joint appointments are scheduled between pediatric and adult nephrology teams to provide continuity of care and familiarize patients and families with the adult care model. Medical summaries are transferred, electronic records organized, and expectations clearly communicated with follow-up pathways and virtual check-ins scheduled prior to discharge from pediatric care.

Finally, in Post-Transfer Monitoring and Long-Term Support (Ages 18+), adherence is tracked using biochemical metrics like leukocyte cystine levels and patient-reported outcomes. Adult teams continue multidisciplinary management and integrate technology, such as mobile apps and machine learning tools, to identify high-risk patients and guide individualized follow-up. This approach reflects the longitudinal nature of transition and supports sustained engagement across developmental stages, helping to address the evolving digital and psychosocial needs of modern adolescent populations.

Future directions in transition care

Looking ahead, data-driven technology like machine learning and artificial intelligence (AI) offers a compelling opportunity to personalize and strengthen the outcomes of cystinosis patients transitioning into adult care. Machine learning models are now capable of identifying kidney transplant recipients who are at risk of non-adherence to their immunosuppressive medication regimen [60]. This advancement provides clinicians with the opportunity to intervene proactively, thereby preventing potential graft injury before it occurs [60]. A similar approach in cystinosis could help transition teams generate a risk score by integrating clinical data and patient behavior to identify those needing support.

These tools may also have the potential to predict the progression of kidney disease in each individual diagnosed with cystinosis. Machine learning algorithms have been shown to forecast CKD progression and the need for renal replacement therapy (RRT), and they have shown high predictive accuracy when tested in independent patient cohorts [61]. In the context of cystinosis, a machine-learning platform could analyze kidney metrics to create a personalized treatment trajectory. This would help alert physicians when they need to adjust therapy or begin planning for a transplant.

In addition, successful AI integration into cystinosis transition care hinges on models that clinicians can operate and understand. Applying similar principles to cystinosis to clearly identify factors such as rising lab levels or missed medication refills will help build trust and support timely interventions.

Limitations

This review has several limitations. We searched only PubMed, which indexes the major nephrology, pediatric, and rare disease journals where cystinosis transition literature is published, and our manual reference tracking of included studies and key institutional protocols did not surface additional work outside the PubMed-indexed evidence base. We are confident that the search captured the majority of clinically relevant literature on cystinosis transition care, although some studies in other areas of literature may have been missed.

The evidence itself is limited by how rare cystinosis is, with most included sources consisting of small cohort studies, single-center reports, case series, and expert consensus statements. Almost no studies directly compare structured transition models in cystinosis or link a specific protocol to long-term renal or transplant outcomes. Frameworks like RISE, ON TRAC, and STARx were developed for broader pediatric nephrology populations and have not been validated in cystinosis cohorts, so our conclusions about their fit are inferred from related populations rather than tested directly in cystinosis patients.

The protocol for this review was not registered. PROSPERO does not accept scoping review protocols, and we did not register on the Open Science Framework or a similar platform. Two reviewers independently screened studies, but we did not perform a formal critical appraisal of methodological quality, consistent with scoping review methodology. As a result, the review maps the available evidence and identifies gaps without weighing the strength of evidence behind each finding. The digital health and machine learning tools discussed are also early in clinical use, with most supporting data drawn from broader chronic kidney disease and transplant populations rather than cystinosis cohorts.

Conclusions

Our review of existing cystinosis transition literature highlights the clinical need for structured and adaptable transition strategies that support continuity of care and improve long-term outcomes for patients with cystinosis, and it translates existing transition strategies into a disease specific guidance linking clinical roles and measurable outcomes. Current transition models and case presentations emphasize increasing patient autonomy, self-management, and knowledge of disease conditions through the coordination of transition management plans and models of care, allowing patients to build confidence and navigate the adult healthcare system with greater independence and support. Structured transition planning, care coordinator integration, and patient motivation interventions all support effective transition for vulnerable and high-risk patients.

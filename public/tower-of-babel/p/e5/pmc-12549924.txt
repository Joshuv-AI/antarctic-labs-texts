Multi-omics analysis of lipids and aroma compounds in beef under grain-fed and grass-fed production methods
Authors: Pingyang Li, Yiyu Yin, Shijie Fan, Longzhu Zhou, Huihui Tian, Lupei Zhang, Jing Li, Kehan Ma, Junwei Hu, Yanan Yu, Qingyu Zhao, Youyou Yang, Yahui Wang, Ruiqi Peng, Yanxiang Zhang, Xuemei Gao, Yuchang Qin, Junmin Zhang, Chaohua Tang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDifferent production methods for grain-fed and grass-fed cattle affect beef quality and flavor, yet systematic studies on aroma profile differences and the reasons underlying these variations remain limited. This study employed multi-omics analysis to characterize the aroma and lipid profile in grain-fed and grass-fed beef, as well as to identify key lipid molecules and biological pathways linked to aroma formation. Multi-omics analysis revealed that triglycerides (TG (16:0_14:1_18:1), TG (16:0_16:0_18:1), TG (16:0_16:1_18:1), TG (16:0_18:1_18:1), TG (16:0_18:1_18:2), and TG (18:0_16:1_18:1) et al.), diacylglycerols (DG (16:0_18:1) and DG (18:1_18:1)), and free fatty acids (FA (16:1), FA (18:1), FA (18:2), FA (20:3), and FA (20:4)) molecules containing C14:1, C16:1, C18:1, C18:2, C20:3, and C20:4 fatty chains may serve as key precursors to drive distinct aromas in grain-fed and grass-fed beef. These processes are implicated in triglyceride metabolism and fatty acid metabolism pathways and are potentially regulated by DEGs in the PPAR signaling pathway. Specifically, APOA mediates triglyceride transport via lipoprotein secretion, while SLC27A6 enhances intracellular lipid content by promoting long-chain fatty acid uptake. Our results provide a comprehensive understanding of the aroma characteristics and lipid profiles, establishing a theoretical basis for improving beef quality in both grain-fed and grass-fed production methods.Keywords: Grain feeding, Grass feeding, Beef, Key aroma compounds, Lipids

Introduction

Grain feeding and grass feeding are two typical beef production methods. Grain-fed beef caters to a broader consumer market, while grass-fed beef is steadily increasing its market share each year. Grass feeding typically provides ample grazing space, with pasture as the primary feed source, and cattle are free-ranging on pasture for the majority of the season (NOP/USDA, U.S., CQM/NS-GK-GF-008, China). Grain feeding uses high-density pens. Calves graze for six months to a year, reaching about 500 lbs, after which they are castrated and transitioned to grain-based fattening. The differing feed ingredients of grain-fed and grass-fed methods influence aroma presentation in the beef. The variations in aroma composition play a critical role in consumer decision-making (O'Quinn et al., 2024). In sensory evaluations, beef samples that have undergone acid removal are typically selected as they are closer to the actual eating condition. The evaluation results show that grain-fed beef is characterized by sensory attributes such as fatty, buttery, and sweet flavors. In contrast, grass-fed beef is more often described with organoleptic descriptors like green, earthy, and clean flavors (Liu et al. 2020b). Different aroma characteristics will attract specific consumer groups.

Among them, approximately 90 % of meat aroma compounds originate from lipids, primarily arising from lipolysis, Maillard reactions, and Maillard-lipid interactions, which involve three major nutrients (Sohail et al., 2022). Existing research on grain-fed and grass-fed beef has primarily focused on testing beef for nutrient content, health effects (Nogoy et al., 2022), and sensory evaluation (Duarte et al., 2022). Nevertheless, limited research has comprehensively investigated the aroma profile differences in beef products and the mechanisms underlying their formation in both grain-fed and grass-fed production methods. Different lipids are degraded into free fatty acids, which can undergo oxidation or interact with Maillard reaction intermediates to produce various aromatic compounds, including aldehydes, ketones, and heterocyclic compounds (Chen et al., 2019). When comparing the oxidative behavior of these specific fatty acids, those originating from triacylglycerols (TGs) demonstrate greater susceptibility to oxidation compared to their counterparts isolated from phosphatidylcholines (PCs) (Zhou et al., 2014). Therefore, lipids are pivotal in generating characteristic meat odors through the creation of distinct aromatic compounds, which are influenced by both the lipid content and lipid species (Guo et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2014). The amount of lipids in meat is impacted by lipid metabolism in the muscles of livestock. Studies have indicated that feeding modes influence fatty acid content in livestock meat by modulating the activity of genes associated with fatty acid metabolism, such as ERFE, FABP3, LIPC, LDLR, PLA2R1, and SLC10A6 (Wang et al., 2021). Ruminants generally exhibit more complex lipid metabolism compared to non-ruminants, and related research is still in its exploratory stage. Lipid differences are primarily influenced by the PPAR signaling pathway, in which SCD is identified as a candidate gene associated with high monounsaturated fatty acid content in beef (Xiong et al., 2022), while APOA is closely associated with glyceride transport and metabolism (Gallagher et al., 2004). Currently, in lipid studies of ruminants, where lipid metabolism is highly complex, relying on a single molecular biology technique to examine the influence of production methods on beef lipids is challenging. Therefore, systematic research is critical for developing meat products that align with consumer expectations.

Pingliang Red Cattle is a high-quality beef cattle in China, derived from the local yellow cattle in Pingliang. It has been enhanced through the introduction of superior domestic and international breeds, such as Qinchuan Cattle, Limousin Cattle, and South Derwent Cattle, resulting in a crossbreeding group known for its excellent meat performance. Due to supportive policies and regional advantages, two groups of large-scale cattle farms with grain feeding, as well as individual farmers practicing grass-fed, free-range farming, have emerged. These are typical production methods of domestic cattle in China. Grain-fed Pingliang Red beef usually has a high intramuscular fat content and has the potential to produce marbled beef (Wang et al., 2023). This degree of fat deposition remains challenging to attain in most common beef breeds (e.g., Simmental, Limousin, and Charolais), even following castration and high-energy grain feeding (Du et al., 2021). Beef breeds used for marbled beef production may be less likely to be grass-fed for economic reasons. It is evident that the Pingliang Red Cattle is a dominant model for studying both grain-fed and grass-fed production methods. This study was conducted on grain-fed and grass-fed Pingliang Red cattle to investigate the aroma characteristics and lipid molecular profiles of beef from these production methods, identify key lipid molecules involved in aroma compound formation, and elucidate the biological pathways underlying lipid metabolism differences between the two production methods. We hypothesize that different production methods will affect lipid metabolism pathways in cattle, thereby inducing significant variations in lipid precursors, aroma profiles, and organoleptic quality. The results of this study will establish a theoretical basis for improving the quality of grain-fed and grass-fed beef.

Materials and methods

Animals and sample collection

All experimental procedures were approved by the Institute of Animal Science of the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Beijing, China), approval number IAS2025-105. To ensure the grain-fed and grass-fed beef samples in the study better represented commercially available beef accessible to consumers, the feeding periods were determined according to prevailing market conditions: 37 months for grain-fed cattle and 31 months for grass-fed cattle. Corresponding feed formulations are detailed in Table S1. At the end of the feeding phase, all cattle underwent quarantine before being transferred to a professional slaughterhouse. Before slaughter, they were subjected to a 24-h fast, with unrestricted access to water. They were then humanely slaughtered by professional slaughterers. The process included electroshock, bloodletting, removal of internal organs, and splitting the carcass along the centerline. After slaughter, the carcasses were de-acidified for 96 h at 0–4 °C. The Longissimus lumborum (LL), rump (Biceps femoris), and chuck (Supraspinatus) were collected from the carcasses and stored at −80 °C. All visible subcutaneous fat, connective tissue, and fascia were meticulously excised prior to testing. A 100 g meat sample was cut from the same site, crushed into dry particles under liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C for subsequent electronic nose detection, volatile compounds analysis, intramuscular fat content determination, and lipidomics analysis. Among them, meat traits, intramuscular fat content determination, and lipidomics analyses covered meat samples from three different parts of the body, whereas electronic nose detection and volatile compounds analysis were only performed for the LL.

Chemicals and reagents

The analytical standards hexanal, nonanal, (E, E)-2,4-nonadienal, (E)-2-heptenal, (E)-2-octenal, (E)-2-pentenal, 1-octen-3-one, 2-acetyllithiazole, 2,3-pentanedione, hexanol, 3-(methylthio)propanal, and hexanoic acid were obtained from Aladdin Biochemicals & Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Heptanal, octanal, 2-heptanone, glutaraldehyde, 2-methylbutanal, 3-methylbutanal, and 2-methyl-3-heptanone were supplied by Sigma-Aldrich (Shanghai, China). 2-Pentylfuran, which was GC-grade, was produced by Alfa Aesar (Shanghai, China). The lipidomics standards PG (16:0-d31–18:1), PC (16:0-d31–18:1), PS (16:0-d31–18:1), PE (16:0-d31–18:1), TG (15:0–18:1–15:0 d5), DG (1,3–17:0 d5), SM (16:0-d31), C15 Cer-d7 (d18:1-d7/15:0), LYSO PE (17:0-d5), LYSO PC (16:0-d49), C18 Cer (d18:1/18:0), SM (18:0-d18:1/18:0), and PI (16:0-d31–18:1) were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (Shanghai, China). For ultrahigh-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) analysis, methanol, acetonitrile, and isopropanol hydrate were supplied by Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). Analytical grade hexane, anhydrous ether, ammonium acetate, and chloroform were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co. Ltd.

Meat quality traits analysis

After 30 min of bloom time on the inner surface of the thawed meat sample to oxidize and keep the meat color relatively stable, the color was measured using a Minolta CR400 colorimeter (illuminant: C and D65; standard observer: approximate 2° viewing angle; measurement aperture: 8 mm) and expressed as L∗, a∗, and b∗ values. Textural characteristics, shear force, and cooking loss were determined using protocols adapted from Li et al. (2023). Defrosted beef was initially weighed at about 100 g and submerged in water for controlled heating until its inner temperature increased to 70 °C. Following heating, samples were cooled overnight at 4 °C and weighed again to quantify the loss percentage. The remaining meat samples were evaluated using TA. XT. Plus a texture analyzer with a 500 N load cell (Stable Micro Systems, Godalming, UK) to determine shear force and textural indicators. The sample was cut into 1 × 1 × 4 × cm3 strips along the muscle fibers for the shear force test and 1 cm3 cubes for the texture profile test. Six replicate measurements per sample to derive mean values.

Electronic nose analysis

A PEN3 electronic nose (Winmust Airsense Analytic Inc., Schwerin, Germany) was utilized for the analysis. Transfer 1 g of the meat sample into a headspace vial. Incubate the samples in boiling water for 0.5 h, and subsequently left to equilibrate at ambient temperature for an additional 0.5 h. 10 substance-specific sensitive sensors were used to implement odor sense compounds during the test (Table S2). Drawing from our previous research (Zhou et al., 2024), the experiment involved the controlled injection of headspace gas into the sensor array at a constant rate of 600 mL/min. The process featured a wash time of 200 s, succeeded by a detection time of 120 s. To ensure accuracy, every sample underwent testing in triplicate.

Aroma compound analysis

A Q-Exactive GC-Orbitrap-MS system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was utilized to accurately define the aromatic compounds present in beef. Chromatography was performed using a VF WAX capillary column (60 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The assay method and instrumental conditions were slightly modified based on those described by Ren et al., 2024. Detailed instrumental conditions and description of qualitative and quantitative methods are given in the Supplementary Material-Methods. Transfer 3 g of the beef sample into a headspace vial, and 10 μL of a 0.02 μg/μL solution of 2-methyl-3-heptanone (solvent: methanol) was added. The vials were incubated in boiling water for 0.5 h. Following treatment, the samples were equilibrated at 55 °C and subjected to headspace solid-phase microextraction (HS-SPME). The extracted material was desorbed in the inlet at 250 °C. The MS is set to full scan mode, encompassing a mass range of 30 m/z to 400 m/z.

Characterization of aroma compounds using MS database, retention index (LRI), and olfactory analysis. The MS results were searched by the NIST mass spectrometry database. Compound identification was achieved through the integration of MS data alignment combined with molecular architecture elucidation. The LRI for volatile aroma constituents was determined by analyzing retention time data from a homologous series of n-alkanes spanning C7-C40. Three sensory panelists trained in the pre-treatment were carefully selected to perform sensory olfactory testing using the ODP3. All panelists were experienced in food sensory evaluation and underwent pre-experiment training with volatile reference standards until they could reliably differentiate and characterize odorants. The sniffing equipment and sensory panel specific information are listed in the Supplementary Material-Methods. The odor properties, aroma intensity (AI), and retention times of the detected aromas at the time of detection were recorded. Odorants were recorded as identified when at least two panelists detected consistent odor characteristics within the same retention time window. Synonyms or highly similar terms (grassy and green leafy) are permitted, but starkly contrasting descriptions (floral versus burnt) are deemed inconsistent. The sensory descriptors of the olfactory properties were cross-referenced against authenticated volatile compounds cataloged in a specialized flavor database. Statistical analysis was performed using the AI method. The intensity is categorized on a scale of 1–4, and the results are reported as the mean intensity based on at least five test repetitions.

Quantitative analyses were carried out in two ways: a semi-quantitative examination utilizing internal standard solutions and a quantitative analysis supplemented by olfactory analysis and external standard methods. Drawing from the methodology described by Li et al. (2023), flavor standards with varying concentration levels, as well as flavorless matrices, were prepared. Specifically, quick-cooked beef samples were sequentially extracted using the following solvents in a vortex mixer: (1) water (1:4, w/v), (2) ethanol (1:4, w/v), and (3) a diethyl ether–hexane mixture (1:2:1, w/v/v). After repeating the extraction four times, the beef samples were lyophilized for 72 h to obtain an odorless matrix. The flavorless matrix was combined with the flavor standard solution and weighed to the same mass as the meat sample, followed by analysis using GC-O-MS as described previously. A calibration curve was plotted to precisely quantify the concentration of the aroma compound. The contribution of aroma compounds was assessed by the ratio of their concentration to their respective odor perception threshold in water to obtain the odor activity value (OAV). The thresholds were sourced from Gemert (2011).

Determination of IMF content

The intramuscular fat (IMF) content of beef samples was quantitatively assessed following the Soxhlet extraction protocol, employing anhydrous ether t as the extracting agent [GB 5009.6-2016]. The proportion of lipid mass relative to the initial wet mass of the muscle was subsequently taken as the result.

Lipidomic analysis

Lipidomic analysis was conducted using a Q-Exactive mass spectrometer in conjunction with a UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for the quantification of lipids. Lipids extraction pre-treatment using the method from (Li et al., 2022). Specifically, 12 deuterated lipid molecular standards were dissolved using methyl tertiary butyl ether. 80 mg of raw beef was homogenized by adding 5 mm steel beads, followed by homogenization with a homogenizer at 60 Hz for 20 s. The homogenization was repeated three times. An additional 40 mg aliquot was taken from the homogenized minced meat, combined with 5 mm steel beads and 300 μL of methanol, and homogenized at 60 Hz. The steel beads were discarded. 1 mL of the above lipid standard solution was added. After mixing well, a quarter of the standard volume of water was added to facilitate phase separation. The upper organic extract was collected after centrifugation at 4 °C. The lower phase was extracted again with solvent without the lipid standards and mixed with the first extract. Two extracts were evaporated under nitrogen, and the resulting dried lipid solids were preserved at −80 °C.

Samples were solubilized in a chloroform/methanol mixture (2:1, v/v) prior to analysis and fractionated via reversed-phase chromatography for detection in dual-polarity ion modes (Li et al., 2022). Lipids were identified by comparison of the mass of precursors and distinctive fragments. A calibration curve was constructed to precisely quantify the concentration of lipid molecules. Comprehensive methodological details for the methodology for lipidome analysis were provided in the Supplementary Material-Methods.

Transcriptomic analysis and verification

Total RNA from bovine muscle samples was extracted with TRIzol (Invitrogen, CA, USA). cDNA was synthesized from RNA samples assessed to be eligible for cDNA library construction. Subsequently, cDNA was sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq™ 6000 (LC Biotech, Ltd.) platform utilizing the PE150 sequencing mode for paired-end sequencing. Localization of reads to the < bos taurus > reference genome and assembly of genes and transcripts. All procedures were conducted by LC Biotech (Hangzhou, China). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were detected through the edgeR. Functional enrichment analyses were conducted utilizing the DAVID (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/) and KOBAS (http://bioinfo.org/kobas) platforms to explore Gene Ontology (GO) terms and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways. Statistical significance for enriched pathways was determined using a threshold of P < 0.05. Transcriptome data were visualized using SRplot (http://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/).

Total RNA was extracted from bovine muscle samples using the RNAsimple Total RNA Kit, strictly following the manufacturer's protocol. RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Perfect Real Time), which was diluted and used for Quantitative Real Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (qPCR) to detect the mRNA expression level of the target gene. qPCR was conducted using TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ (Tli RNaseH Plus) and the reaction system and procedure referred to the kit instructions. All amplification reactions were executed on an Applied Biosystems 7300 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). The Primer-BLAST program in the National Center for Biotechnology Information was used to design specific primers for qPCR amplification of the target genes. GAPDH was selected as the internal reference gene and normalized by 2−ΔΔCT method to calculate the relative expression levels of each gene. Primer information is shown in Table S3.

Statistical analysis

Experimental data are expressed as mean ± standard error. Independent samples t-test analysis and one-way ANOVA were utilized by IBM SPSS 26 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) (P < 0.05). Significantly different lipid molecules were determined based on the PLS-DA, with a variable importance (VIP) value > 1, based on the projections obtained from the t-test (P < 0.05). Bivariate correlation assessments employing Pearson's coefficient were performed to investigate associative patterns among lipid species and volatile odorants, as well as among lipids and differentially expressed genes. Omicshare Web Edition (https://www.omicshare.com/) was employed for PLS-DA and PCA. Figures were created using Chiplot (https://www.chiplot.online/), GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA), and Excel.

Meat quality traits analysis

Table 1 presents the meat characteristics of grain-fed and grass-fed beef, highlighting differences between the two production methods. This comparison was not made on a single cut. We collected chuck, LL, and rump cuts with the aim of exploring whether there is cut variability in the effects of different production methods on meat quality traits. The result was that three beef cuts exhibited consistent trends across all measured indicators. In terms of meat color, no significant changes were detected between the two types of beef for a∗, b∗, and L∗ values. Reduced cooking loss in grain-fed beef compared to grass-fed beef, indicating better retention of moisture and soluble nutrients in grain-fed beef. Shear force and textural properties (including hardness, cohesion, chewiness, and toughness) of grain-fed beef were considerably lower than those of grass-fed beef. This pattern was evident in the outer spine and millefleur, with a similar trend observed in the shoulder, suggesting that grain-fed beef has a more tender texture.

Electronic nose analysis

A preliminary olfactory profile comparison was conducted between grain-fed and grass-fed beef samples, and an electronic nose analysis was conducted, followed by multivariate statistical analysis to establish an aroma profile. Radar plots (Fig. 1A) indicated variations in response values for grain-fed and grass-fed beef at sensors W5S, W1W, W2W, and W2S. These findings suggest that grain-fed beef may contain elevated concentrations of aldehydes, alcohols, nitrogen-bearing molecules, and organosulfur species.

Comparison of aroma compounds between the grain-fed and grass-fed beef using HS-SPME-GC-MS

To further investigate the aroma compounds contributing to the variations in flavor, samples of grain-fed and grass-fed LL of beef were studied using headspace solid-phase microextraction GC-MS. In total, 179 aroma compounds were detected and analyzed semi-quantitatively using internal standards. The identified aroma compounds included 45 aldehydes, 29 heterocyclic compounds, 22 ketones, 21 esters, 10 alcohols, 19 hydrocarbons, 8 acids, 5 sulfur-containing compounds, and 20 other compounds. PCA analysis of the aroma compound contents in the two types of beef samples, utilizing semi-quantitative results derived from the internal standard method (Fig. 1B), revealed a tendency for grain-fed and grass-fed beef samples to separate from each other. The contents and proportions of the identified aroma compounds are illustrated in Fig. 1C and D. Among these, aldehydes were the most prominent aroma substances, accounting for 56.03 % and 57.85 % of the total aroma components in grass-fed and grain-fed beef. Quantitative analyses revealed elevated concentrations of aldehydes, ketones, esters, and acids in grain-fed beef, demonstrating statistically significant elevation (P < 0.05) relative to grass-fed beef. The differences in aroma compound contents between the two types of beef were consistent with the findings from electronic nose analysis (r = 0.934, P < 0.001) (Table S4). Also, we found that 83 distinct compounds out of the 179 aroma compounds were present in both grain-fed and grass-fed beef (P < 0.05) (Table S5, Fig. 1E).

Identification of key aroma compounds in grain-fed and grass-fed beef using GC-O

In this research, a total of 19 aroma compounds were identified through GC-O analysis, satisfying the criteria of P < 0.05. The putative aroma underwent systematic authentication through MS, LRI, and reference flavor libraries. The identified aroma compounds comprised 11 aldehydes, 3 ketones, 2 acids, 1 alcohol, and 2 heterocyclic compounds, which could potentially account for the differences in aroma between grain-fed and grass-fed beef. The primary aroma characteristics identified were fruity, grassy, fatty, meaty, clean, potato-like, and fishy. Aroma compounds with fatty and sweet aromas, including pentanal (malt, sweet), hexanal (grassy), (E)-2-octenal (meaty, fatty, fishy), 3-methylthiopropanal (baked potato, meaty), (E, E)-2,4-nonadienal (meaty, fatty), 1-octen-3-one (mushroom, fishy), and 2-acetylthiazole (bean, fatty), exhibited higher AI in grain-fed (Fig. 1F). The overall AI values ranged from 1.00 to 3.00, and the AI of each aroma compound showed a positive correlation with its concentration. Quantitative determination of volatile organic compounds was performed via the external standard method, with calibration curves constructed in a solvent matrix spiked with certified reference materials (R2 > 0.99; Table S6). Among these compounds, hexanal had the greatest concentration, measuring 67,838.03 μg/kg in grain-fed beef and 50,243.58 μg/kg in grass-fed beef, respectively. This was followed by pentanal (58,325.64 μg/kg in grain-fed and 37,632.82 μg/kg in grass-fed). The concentrations of 1-octen-3-one and 2-acetylthiazole were lower in 1-octen-3-one and 2-acetylthiazole.

The sensory impact of odorants on perceived aroma profiles is contingent upon both their absolute concentrations and their olfactory detection thresholds within the sample matrix. This relationship is quantified through the Odor Activity Value (OAV) metric, wherein substances exhibiting OAV values exceeding unity (≥1) are operationally defined as sensorially relevant, with magnitude directly proportional to their perceptual salience. As illustrated in Fig. 1F, the OAV of 18 aroma compounds was >1 in both the grain-fed and grass-fed groups. Among them, (E, E)-2,4-nonadienal exhibited the highest OAV in both beef groups, followed by 1-octen-3-one, (E)-2-octenal, and hexanal, all with an OAV >5000. In contrast, hexanoic acid, 2-heptanone, hexanol, and 2-acetylthiazole displayed lower OAV values (OAV <10). Additionally, it was discovered that the concentrations of important aroma compounds in grain-fed beef were significantly greater than those in grass-fed beef, except for nonanal, octanal, and 2-pentylfuran, which exhibited a pronounced decrease (P < 0.05). While most of the key aroma substances are found in high levels in grain-fed beef, in terms of percentage, most of the aroma compounds that are more predominant in grass-fed beef emit plant-based aromas, which may account for the green and light flavor experience that grass-fed beef provides to the consumer (Table 2). Consequently, these odor-active constituents likely mediate the olfactory divergence observed in beef across grain-fed and grass-fed groups, suggesting a causal linkage between these compounds and diet-modulated sensory profiles.

Changes in lipidomic profiles of different beef cuts in grain-fed and grass-fed groups

To investigate whether different cuts of beef exhibit similar lipid compositions and whether lipid variations between grain-fed and grass-fed beef are present in various cuts, we analyzed the lipidomic data from three specific cuts: LL, chuck, and rump. The results indicated that both the LL and the chuck exhibited higher levels of TGs in the two types of beef, whereas the rump showed lower TG levels, consistent with the IMF results. The LL also contained lower levels of phospholipids, while the chuck exhibited relatively high levels of phospholipids and FFAs (Fig. 4A–B). We used Venny plots (Fig. 4C) to illustrate particular distinct lipid molecules, and the findings revealed that the number of lipid molecules significantly differing between cuts were 38 (Chuck vs. LL), 36 (Chuck vs. Rump), and 52 (Rump vs. LL). Their common differential lipids were Cer (d18:2_23:0) and Cer (d19:1_18:0). Although the three cuts from the same cattle exhibited substantial differences in lipid composition, grain-fed and grass-fed samples maintained similar regularity in the levels of lipid subclasses when compared to the same cut of beef. Specifically, grain-fed beef had higher levels of glycerides, phospholipids, and fatty acids in all 12 lipid subclasses examined, demonstrating a high degree of consistency across cuts (Fig. 4D–F). The PCA plots of each part of the beef were separated from each other (Fig. 4G–I). These differences may be attributed to dietary and feeding practices. It was further shown that the two production methods, grain-fed and grass-fed, cause significant lipid variations across different beef cuts.

Discussion

Existing research on grain-fed and grass-fed beef has primarily focused on assessing nutrient content, health effects, and sensory evaluation (Nogoy et al., 2022; Van Elswyk and McNeill, 2014). However, limited research has combined multi-omics approaches to systematically examine the differences in lipid profiles and aroma compounds of beef samples and the reasons for their formation under the two methods of beef production, grain-fed and grass-fed. Unlike previous studies that employed a single methodology (Hirai et al., 2023), this research investigates grain-fed and grass-fed Pingliang Red cattle by integrating GC-O-MS, quantitative lipidomics, and transcriptomics. Our study aims to identify changes in key aroma compounds, investigate the lipid classes and molecular profiles of beef produced, and understand the biological pathways and genes involved in lipid metabolism under both grain-fed and grass-fed conditions.

Through the study of the basic characteristics of meat, notable variations were observed between the grain-fed and grass-fed groups of beef in textural characteristics, shear force, and cooking loss. With an effect size exceeding 0.5 (Cohen's d), these results indicate that different production methods have a substantial modulation on the quality profiles of beef. Grain-fed beef exhibits lower cooking losses, reduced shear force values, and favorable textural properties, indicating improved retention of moisture and soluble nutrients and resulting in a more tender texture. Holman et al. (2020) found that Meat with lower shear force and increased tenderness generally receives higher consumer acceptance due to its palatability in many countries. The effect size for pH and meat color was also >0.25, but the lack of significant differences may be due to the sample size, indicating that production methods may also have some effect on pH and meat color.

In addition to meat quality, beef aroma influences consumers’ decisions. The aroma of beef results from a combination of the content, type, and odor threshold of aroma compounds, as well as their interactions. Based on the concentration of aroma compounds with OAV >1, this study identified 18 odor compounds from cooked beef that may potentially influence the aroma profiles of grain-fed and grass-fed groups. Among these, 13 compounds—namely hexanal, nonanal, pentanal, octanal, (E)-2-heptenal, (E)-2-octenal, (E, E)-2,4-nonadienal, 3-methylthiopropanal, 1-octen-3-one, hexanol, 1-octen-3-ol, hexanoic acid, and 2-pentanofuran, have been established as key mediators in shaping the flavor signature of beef (Li et al., 2022; Ojangba et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2024). Of the 18 key odor compounds identified, 11 were aldehydes, suggesting that aldehydes play a significant role in shaping the overall aroma of beef. This prevalence aligns mechanistically with the exceptionally low olfactory detection thresholds inherent to aldehyde compounds, a finding empirically corroborated by volatile profiling studies in Fu et al. (2022). Glutaraldehyde, octanal, nonanal, and hexanal, are derived from the breakdown of long-chain FAs and impart a distinctive odor to beef (Jiang et al., 2023). Additionally, 3-methylbutyraldehyde, phenylacetaldehyde, 2-ethyl-6-methylpyrazine, 2-acetylpyrrole, 2-acetylthiazole, and 2-acetyl-2-thiazoline were recognized as key compounds affecting the flavor attributes of domestic cattle in China (Wei et al., 2022). Furthermore, it has been reported that 2-methylbutyraldehyde is present in a range of meat products, such as beef, lamb, poultry, and crab (Al-Dalali et al., 2022). Fu et al. (2022) mentioned in their review that 2-ketones exert a significant influence on the aroma of meat, particularly highlighting that 2-heptanone is associated with meat aroma. Additionally, oxidation has been shown to promote the production of volatile compounds such as heptanal, octanal, nonanal, 2,3-pentanedione, and 3-hydroxy-2-butanone. Among these, 2,3-pentanedione and 3-hydroxy-2-butanone contribute most significantly to shaping the aroma of yak products (Huang et al., 2022). Therefore, the 18 characterized odor-active constituents represent principal determinants of beef aroma signatures, and the divergence in aroma between grain-fed and grass-fed beef can likely be attributed to changes in their concentrations and interactions.

The majority of the aroma of meat originates from lipids. Further exploration of lipid-related factors contributing to the aroma differences of grain-fed and grass-fed beef. Consistent with earlier research, grain-fed beef cattle produced fatter carcasses and exhibited greater IMF deposition (P < 0.001, effect size = 0.73) (Nogoy et al., 2022; Van Elswyk and McNeill, 2014), indicating that different production methods significantly influence IMF deposition in beef and hold practical significance. Cattle with greater IMF content had smaller shear and cooking losses. The 5.06 % increase in intramuscular fat (IMF) in grain-fed beef enhances lipid precursor content, thereby enriching flavor profiles. However, the associated greasy sensation may contribute to its lower preference among grass-fed beef consumers (O'Quinn et al., 2024). Intramuscular fat variation also influences market premiums through beef grading, though the precise economic value is contingent upon regional consumer preferences. In the present research, we observed that TG, PC, PE, SM, FFA, DG, LPC, LPE, and PG exhibited quantitatively elevated abundances in the grain-fed group compared to the grass-fed group (P < 0.05). Notably, the levels of key differential lipid molecules in neutral lipids, such as TG and DG, as well as in FFA, were significantly elevated in the grain-fed group, suggesting that triglycerides and FFA contribute to the differences in lipid deposition among individuals. Additionally, we observed upregulation or downregulation of phospholipid molecules (including PC, PE, and SM) between the two types of groups (P < 0.05).

Different cuts of beef exhibit distinct lipid profiles. Lipid differences between grain-fed and grass-fed beef were consistently observed across different cuts. The LL contains high levels of TGs and lower levels of phospholipids, while both chuck and rump exhibit relatively high levels of phospholipids and FFAs. The common differential lipids identified were Cer (d18:2_23:0) and Cer (d19:1_18:0). Both lipid content and type significantly influence aroma retention (Ayed et al., 2018), with TGs and PLs generating flavor compounds through autoxidation or thermal oxidation, and Cer participating as amino donors in the Maillard reaction or synergistically with lipid-Maillard interactions to facilitate the formation of complex flavor compounds (Fu et al., 2022). Variations in lipid composition among the three parts may lead to differences in flavor perception. Although the three parts of the same cattle exhibited significant differences in lipid composition, grain-fed and grass-fed beef maintained consistent content relationships across lipid subclasses within the same part. Specifically, grain-fed beef showed higher levels of glycerides, phospholipids, and fatty acids across all 12 lipid subclasses examined, demonstrating a high degree of site consistency. These differences may be attributed to variations in diet and feeding practices (Dannenberger et al., 2017).

Lipid variations in beef, driven by differences in feeding regimes and management practices, likely underlie the distinct aroma profiles observed between grain-fed and grass-fed beef. Of these 18 odor compounds identified, 14 of them were produced through lipid oxidation. The remaining compounds, 3-methylthiopropionaldehyde, 2-acetylthiazole, 2-methylbutyraldehyde, and 3-methylbutyraldehyde, were generated via the Maillard reaction and lipid-Maillard interactions (Sohail et al., 2022). These findings suggest that lipid-derived fractions are the primary contributors to the flavor profiles of grain-fed and grass-fed beef, highlighting the critical role of lipid composition in beef flavor. Association analysis of differential lipid profiles and important aroma compounds revealed that lipid molecules exhibiting upregulated abundance showed strong positive associations with most of sensorially impactful aroma substances. Notably, these aroma compounds were detected in greater concentrations in the grain-fed group, indicating their involvement in the formation of aroma compounds. Conversely, octanal, nonanal, and 2-pentylfuran exhibited significant negative correlations with glycerol esters and FFA. It is known that octanal, nonanal, and 2-pentylfuran primarily originate from the oxidative decomposition of oleic acids (OA), linoleic acids (LA), and arachidonic acids (AA); however, their response was diminished in the higher fat content of grain-fed beef. This contradicts the conclusions of some beef flavor studies (Ren et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024). The opposite results obtained in this study may be attributed to the higher octanol-water partition coefficient of these compounds, which have a greater fat solubility and may be affected by the IMF strong forcing, reduced release into the headspace with increasing fat content, and low response values (Frank et al., 2016). Beyond variations in sample fat content, instrumental conditions and parameter configurations may also be contributing factors.

The oxidative degradation of lipids primarily involves the oxidative decomposition of FA chains in lipid molecules, including FFAs as well as those bound to glycerides and phospholipids. Among the 56 differential lipid molecules identified, glycerides and FFAs exhibited stronger associations with aroma compounds. The primary fatty acid chains implicated—C14:1, C16:0, C16:1, C18:1, C18:2, C20:3, and C20:4—were significantly elevated in grain-fed beef contrasted to grass-fed beef. This finding aligns with previous studies on the FA composition of grain-fed and grass-fed beef, which typically exhibit greater levels of monounsaturated FA and n-6 polyunsaturated FA (Kaur et al., 2023). Beyond the amino acid-derived compounds — 3-methylthiopropionaldehyde (from methionine (Liu et al., 2019)), 3-methylbutyraldehyde (from l-leucine), 2-methylbutyraldehyde (from isoleucine) (Wu et al., 2021), and 2-acetylthiazole (from cysteine (Ramalingam et al., 2019)) — the majority of the important aroma compounds in beef can arise from the oxidative decomposition of the unsaturated FA chains involved above or TG and PL containing the above unsaturated fatty acid chains. Aldehydes with carbon atoms 6–10 can arise through the oxidation of OA and LA, including hexanal, pentanal, octanal, and nonanal. These compounds are primarily formed through the oxidation of OA, LA, and AA, as well as through the autoxidation of polyunsaturated FAs such as LA, resulting in compounds like (E)-2-octenal, (E)-2-pentenal, (E)-2-heptenal, and (E, E)-2,4-nonadienal (Fu et al., 2022; Sohail et al., 2022). The formation of 2.3-pentanedione and 2-heptanone is mainly attributed to oxidative degradation of oleic and linoleic acids (Machiels, 2003). Hexanol is derived from the oxidative breakdown of OA and palmitic acids (Yang et al., 2017). Additionally, the oxidative decomposition of LA can yield 1-octen-3-ol, 1-octen-3-one, and 2-pentylfuran (Zang et al., 2019), along with glutaraldehyde, hexanal, (E)-2-octenal, (E, E)-2,4-nonadienal, and 1-octen-3-ol, which can also arise through the oxidative decomposition of C20:4 (Liu et al. 2020a).

Lipid differences resulting from different production methods were further investigated using transcriptome analysis. The data indicate that DEGs in grain-fed and grass-fed cattle were primarily involved in regulating lipid metabolism, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, amino acid metabolism, PPAR signaling pathway, lipoprotein metabolism, fatty acid metabolism, triglyceride metabolism, arachidonic acid metabolism, and linoleic acid metabolism, among others. These pathways form an interconnected network through which grain-fed and grass-fed production methods influence muscle lipid content. Lipids, carbohydrates, and amino acids can interconvert through the TCA cycle (Veshkini et al., 2022), suggesting that the effects of grain-fed and grass-fed diets on beef lipids may be mediated by the interconversion of these three macronutrients. Variations in carbohydrates and amino acids in the feeds were converted into distinct fatty acids, leading to differences in beef lipid profiles. KEGG enrichment analysis demonstrated statistically significant overrepresentation of the PPAR signaling pathway (involving SLC27A2, APOA1, PLIN1, SLC27A4, FABP4, SLC27A6, SLC27A5, APOC3, APOC2, APOA5, and SCD) and linoleic acid metabolism pathways (involving CYP2E1, CYP1A2, CYP2C18, and CYP2C19). DEGs associated with these pathways may serve as functional and positional candidate genes with promising potential for fat deposition. The PPAR signaling pathway interacts with the lipoprotein biological pathway to further regulate triglyceride and fatty acid metabolism for differential lipid regulation. Ma et al. (2022) demonstrated that the PPAR signaling pathway is activated by fatty acids and contributes to the regulation of host lipid metabolism, development, and growth. The PLIN1 gene serves as a critical suppressor of lipolytic activity in dairy cows. In yak muscle, PLIN1 expression was positively correlated with FA content, as PLIN1 inhibits fatty acid degradation. Stearoyl-CoA desaturase (SCD) regulates intramuscular lipids (Liu et al., 2021), and its overexpression is associated with obesity and elevated triglycerides. The upregulation of SCD expression in grain-fed cattle promotes fat deposition. The expression of FABP4 in muscle was positively correlated with TG and FA content. Yorek et al. (2024) demonstrated that unsaturated fatty acids activate the FABP4 pathway, promoting triglyceride synthesis, lipid droplet formation, and enhanced FFA utilization. The P450 enzymes in the CYP1 and CYP2 gene families promote FA uptake and contribute to linoleic acid metabolism, achieving rates comparable to those of arachidonic acid metabolism (Zhang et al., 2018).

A joint analysis of the transcriptomes indicated that the variations in lipid deposition may be attributed to variations in the triglyceride metabolism pathway, the fatty acid metabolism pathway, the high-density lipoprotein pathway, the very low-density lipoprotein pathway, and the PPAR signaling pathway. The core regulatory mechanism is primarily mediated through the upregulation of SLC27A6 and APOA expression. The SLC27A family facilitates fatty acid uptake and enhances intracellular fatty acid availability by binding to fatty acids and mediating their transmembrane transport. Among them, SLC27A6 is a transporter protein that mediates the uptake of long-chain FAs and is able to increase the intracellular accumulation of TGs and total cholesterol in cells, involving lipid biosynthesis and enhancing translocation potential (Zhong et al., 2021). Upregulated APOA family genes are also implicated in the PPAR signaling pathway, contributing to lipid metabolism regulation. For instance, the expression of apolipoproteins enhances triglyceride transport through lipoprotein secretion, while high levels of TG can recruit APOA to bind to triacylglycerol-containing lipoproteins, thereby enhancing triglyceride metabolism (Gallagher et al., 2004). In this research, the expression levels of SLC27A6 and APOA were significantly elevated in grain-fed cattle. The synergistic action of these two mechanisms enhances glyceride uptake into muscular and adipose tissues, thereby promoting more substantial lipid deposition in grain-fed beef compared to grass-fed beef. Free fatty acids resulting from the breakdown of triglycerides by hydrolytic enzymes have been reported to exert a significant influence on the aroma profiles of meat. The oxidative degradation of lipids yields diverse reactive intermediates, including alcohols, aldehydes, esters, and ketones. These metabolites interact with proteinaceous components and free amino groups in muscle tissues, generating molecular species that potentially modify the aroma characteristics of meat (Yang et al., 2024). Therefore, the variation in lipid composition between the grain-fed and grass-fed groups may further affect the formation of beef flavor.

In summary, by studying the aroma and lipid profiles of grain-fed and grass-fed beef, we gained insights into the differences in lipid deposition between the two production methods, as well as the associations between various lipid molecules and important aroma compounds. The findings from these studies are crucial for enhancing our understanding of beef lipid deposition and the regulation of beef aroma quality. The specific pathways leading to these aroma differences remain unclear and warrant further investigation. This study focused on the aroma and lipid characteristics of commercially available grain-fed and grass-fed beef accessible to consumers. Interpreting overall effects alone makes it difficult to directly demonstrate the causal relationship between grain and pasture. By strictly controlling the diet to a single feed source, we can investigate the specific aroma and lipid variations imparted by particular forage or cereal crops. This provides theoretical support for improving aroma and lipid profiles in beef production.

Conclusion

A total of 18 aroma compounds were identified through volatile analysis methods in the two types of beef, each exhibiting significant variations (P < 0.05). The higher percentage of green odor aroma compounds in grass-fed beef potentially drives the vegetal aroma of beef to consumers. The overall levels of TG, PC, PE, SM, FFA, DG, LPC, LPE, and PG were significantly higher in the grain-fed group relative to the grass-fed group, resulting in the identification of 56 differential lipid molecules. Significant variations in lipid composition in various cuts of the same cattle (longissimus lumborum, rump, and chuck). However, both types exhibited similar differential characteristics when comparing grain-fed and grass-fed beef from the same cut. Transcriptome analyses indicated that the production methods of grain-fed and grass-fed beef influenced the expression of genes associated with the anabolism of triglycerides and long-chain fatty acids in muscle. Combined transcriptome results and correlation analyses revealed that TG, DG, and FFA molecules containing FA chains, such as C14:1, C16:1, C18:1, C18:2, C20:3, and C20:4, are key substrates for the generation of aroma compounds in beef. This research deepens our comprehension of the differences in aroma profile and lipid deposition between grain-fed and grass-fed beef and provides insights for analyzing the impact of lipids on beef aroma formation.

Use of conversion factors for the setting of MRLs of prothioconazole
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Luis Carrasco Cabrera, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Luna Greco, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Stefanie Nave, Hermine Reich, Silvia Ruocco, Alessia Pia Scarlato, Marta Szot, Anne Theobald, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani, Elena Zioga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the applicant Bayer AG Crop Science Division submitted a request to the competent national authority in France to use conversion factors for the setting of maximum residue levels (MRLs) for prothioconazole. The applicant did not propose modification of the existing EU MRLs for prothioconazole, as currently set in Regulation (EU) 2024/1318. The data provided in the previous EFSA outputs and submitted under the present assessment were sufficient to derive crop‐specific conversion factors for all crops under consideration, except for pulses, head cabbage and flowering brassica. For onions and shallots, the submitted data are considered to support a less conservative good agricultural practice (GAP). Moreover, the data submitted were found sufficient to confirm tentative MRLs for prothioconazole in leek, Brussels sprouts, barley, oat, wheat, rye grain and in commodities of animal origin. An updated EU livestock dietary burden indicates that lower EU MRLs would be sufficient in liver, kidney and edible offal of ruminants, swine and poultry and in the fat of ruminants and swine. EFSA concluded that the existing EU uses of prothioconazole will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference values and therefore are unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health. All conclusions derived under the present assessment are considered provisional and might need to be reconsidered once the decision on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (and related new endpoints) is implemented by the European Commission.Keywords: consumer risk assessment, conversion factors, MRL, pesticide, prothiconazole

SUMMARY

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, Bayer AG Crop Science Division submitted an application to the competent national authority in France (evaluating Member State, EMS) for the use of conversion factors for maximum residue level (MRL) setting of prothioconazole in plants. The applicant did not propose modification of the existing EU MRLs for prothioconazole currently set in Regulation (EU) 2024/1318 but wished to confirm the margin of safety of the conversion factors.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) Central Submission System on 3 January 2023. The appointed EMS, France, assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 24 May 2023. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation was launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation ran from 20 July 2023 to 10 August 2023. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the EMS proceeded with drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) on 30 April 2025. To address the pending data gaps from the MRL review and to perform a risk assessment according to the residue definition for risk assessment, the EMS proposed to derive a global critical conversion factor (CF) of 1 for cereal grains, root crops, leafy vegetables, pulses, oilseeds and peanuts, a CF of 5.5 for cereals straw and a CF of 2.88 for root crop tops.

On 20 June 2025, the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

Considering that the MRL application has been submitted under Article 6 of the Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, but the submitted data are aimed to address the data gaps set by the MRL review, EFSA, as agreed with the European Commission, performed the assessment as a follow‐up of the Article 12 MRL review. The assessment, therefore, primarily focused on those crops for which tentative MRLs were implemented after the MRL review and for which the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps were not considered addressed in subsequent EFSA outputs. Furthermore, for matters of consistency and considering the wide range of supporting data provided, EFSA also revised the conversion factors for those crops for which the Article 12 confirmatory data have been considered addressed in previous EFSA outputs (root crops, oilseeds) and where the available residue data support the reported authorised good agricultural practices (GAPs).

The conversion factors were derived only in cases when the respective EU GAP was either fully supported by a set of minimum number of compliant residue trials or when a substantial residue data set was provided allowing to conclude on the occurrence of prothioconazole metabolites with the authorised GAP. The extrapolation of CFs among supported uses was considered acceptable only if in line with the EU extrapolation rules. Furthermore, the assessment was performed in accordance with data requirements applicable at the time of the submission of the MRL application. New uses of prothioconazole are not proposed under the present assessment, and therefore, the occurrence and exposure to triazole derivate metabolites (TDMs)were not further assessed. For the same reasons, also the nature and magnitude of residues under processing conditions and in rotational crops was not further investigated.

Based on the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC, the data evaluated under previous MRL assessments and the additional data provided by the EMS in the framework of this application, EFSA prepared in February 2026 a draft reasoned opinion, which was circulated to Member States for consultation via a written procedure. Comments received by 27 February 2026 were considered during the finalisation of this reasoned opinion. The following conclusions are derived.

Sum of prothioconazole‐desthio and all metabolites containing the 2‐(1‐chlorocyclopropyl)‐3‐(2‐chlorophenyl)‐2‐hydroxypropyl‐2H‐1,2,4‐triazole moiety, expressed as prothioconazole‐desthio (sum of isomers). This residue definition covers prothioconazole‐desthio (M04) and the metabolites M14, M15, M16, M17 and M18;

These residue definitions are applicable to primary crops, rotational crops and processed products and for both foliar and seed treatment.

It is noted that, while for this assessment the residue definitions previously derived by EFSA apply, in the recent renewal of approval process of prothioconazole, EFSA confirmed the existing enforcement residue definition but derived a broader residue definition for the risk assessment as ‘prothioconazole‐desthio (M04), M14, M15, M16, M17 and their glucoside conjugates (M21, M22, M23) and M18 expressed as prothioconazole‐desthio equivalents’. The risk assessment residue definition is provisional, pending data to address the genotoxic potential of M15 and M17. EFSA notes that metabolites M15 and M17 are covered by the currently applicable risk assessment residue definition.

Sufficiently validated enforcement methods based on HPLC‐MS/MS are available to analyse prothioconazole‐desthio residues in plant commodities at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

The article 12 confirmatory data gap for the storage stability, as applicable to the EU MRLs for dry pulses, is considered addressed on the basis of a new storage stability study on dry peas which was assessed in the previous EFSA output and confirmed the freezer storage stability of hydroxy metabolites in dry peas for at least 12 months. Furthermore, this study, together with the storage stability study previously available on potatoes, is sufficient to address the data gap of the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval for the storage stability of hydroxy metabolites in cereal grain.

Based on the data submitted for the present assessment, EFSA concludes that sufficient residue data are available to address the Article 12 confirmatory data gap in leeks and Brussels sprouts for EU uses and in barley, rye, wheat and oat grain for EU uses and for the uses supporting the Codex MRLs implemented in the MRL legislation.

The available data were insufficient to confirm the tentative MRLs in onions, shallots, flowering brassica (broccoli, cauliflower), head cabbage and dry pulses and to derive reliable conversion factors for these commodities.

Furthermore, the applicant provided a wide range of residue trials on other crops to support revision of conversion factors that have been derived in previous EFSA outputs for crops with Article 12 confirmatory data gaps previously addressed. On the basis of the data provided, some conversion factors previously derived by EFSA were revised for root crops and oilseeds.

With the present MRL application, new risk assessment values became available for barley, oat, rye and wheat straw, maize forage, turnip tops and grass, and therefore, EFSA updated the livestock dietary exposure. As insufficient data were provided to confirm tentative MRLs in head cabbage and pulses, these commodities were excluded from the livestock dietary burden. However, a separate indicative scenario considering these crops with a tentative CF of 2 showed that the impact of these crops on the livestock exposure is expected to be negligible. The results indicate that the exposure for all livestock species exceeds the trigger value of 0.1 mg/kg dry matter (DM), and therefore, the nature and magnitude of prothioconazole residues in livestock were assessed further.

The risk assessment residue definition in animal commodities has been confirmed by the MRL review as ‘the sum of prothioconazole‐desthio and all metabolites containing the 2‐(1‐chlorocyclopropyl)‐3‐(2‐ chlorophenyl)‐2‐hydroxypropyl‐2H‐1,2,4‐triazole moiety, expressed as prothioconazole‐desthio (sum of isomers)’. The enforcement residue definition is currently set as ‘prothioconazole‐desthio’. It is noted that, while, for this assessment, the residue definitions previously derived by EFSA apply, in the recent renewal assessment, EFSA confirmed the existing enforcement residue definition but derived a broader residue definition for the risk assessment in animal commodities as ‘prothioconazole‐desthio (M04) free and conjugated, M14, M15, M33, M35, free and their conjugates expressed as prothioconazole‐desthio’. This residue definition is considered provisional, pending data to address the genotoxic potential of M33 and M35.

The feeding studies investigating the magnitude of prothioconazole‐desthio in lactating ruminants and of prothioconazole in poultry are available and were used to estimate the magnitude of prothioconazole residues in animal matrices for the updated EU livestock dietary burdens. In the absence of feeding study samples analysed according to the residue definition for risk assessment, conversion factors were applied for kidney and liver.

The results indicate that lower EU MRLs would be sufficient in liver, kidney and edible offal of ruminants, swine and poultry and in the fat of ruminants and swine. However, the existing EU MRLs are currently set on the basis of Codex MRLs, derived by the JMPR in 2017. In 2021, these Codex MRLs were revised and lowered for edible offal of mammalians and poultry and for mammalian fat. Although EFSA considered the new Codex MRLs as safe for consumers, the EU reservation was made in the 53rd session of the Codex Committee on Pesticide Residues (CCPR) pending the outcome of the EU pesticides peer review and that a risk assessment for TDMs has not been carried out. Thus, these modifications have not been implemented in the EU MRL legislation. Considering the above‐mentioned, a risk management decision is required for the future revision of the EU MRLs in animal commodities. EFSA concludes that the Article 12 confirmatory data gap in animal matrices is sufficiently addressed.

Studies investigating the magnitude of residues in honey were submitted in the current application. EFSA concluded that the current MRL based on the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg is conservative enough and lowering was not proposed.

The toxicological profile of prothioconazole was assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC and the data were sufficient to derive an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.05 mg/kg bw per day and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.2 mg/kg bw. Furthermore, the main crop metabolite prothioconazole‐desthio (M04) was concluded to be more toxic than parent prothioconazole, and therefore, its ADI and ARfD of 0.01 mg/kg bw (per day) were selected to be used for the consumer risk assessment.

It is further noted that the toxicological profile of prothioconazole and of prothioconazole‐desthio was recently reassessed in the framework of the peer review in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 for the renewal of approval. A new ADI value of 0.036 mg/kg bw was derived for prothioconazole, while the ARfD of 0.2 mg/kg bw was confirmed. For prothioconazole‐desthio, the TRVs previously derived (0.01 mg/kg bw) were both confirmed and used for an indicative update of the consumer risk assessment.

The hydroxy metabolites are not considered of higher toxicity than the parent active substance. However, in the recent renewal assessment of prothioconazole, the EU pesticides peer review identified toxicity data gaps for metabolites which are included in the existing risk assessment residue definition for plants and animals – prothioconazole desthio‐4‐hydroxy (M15) and prothioconazole desthio‐6‐hydroxy (M17) – and for new metabolites prothioconazole‐desthio‐3,4‐dihydroxy (M33) and prothioconazole‐desthio‐4,5‐dihydroxy (M35), which are included in the proposed risk assessment residue definition for animal commodities.

On the grounds of these uncertainties, the consumer exposure performed under the present assessment is provisional and might need to be updated once the decision on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole is taken by the European Commission.

The consumer risk assessment was performed with revision 3.1 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo). The consumer risk assessment was performed for MRLs already included in the EU Regulation 2024/1318 and for the MRL proposals that were considered acceptable in the previous EFSA opinions, and which have so far not been implemented in the MRL legislation. No residue trials measuring residues according to the residue definition for risk assessment to address the Article 12 data gap were submitted for broccoli, cauliflowers, head cabbages and pulses. Therefore, these crops were excluded from the exposure calculation. The short‐term exposure for prothioconazole‐desthio did not exceed the ARfD for any of the crops under consideration with the highest acute exposure of 31.7% ARfD in carrots. Under the assumption that conclusions from previous EFSA assessments on the modification of MRLs for prothioconazole are implemented, no long‐term consumer intake concerns were identified for any of the diets included in the EFSA PRIMo. The estimated maximum long‐term dietary intake accounted for 18% of the ADI (NL toddler diet). The individual contribution of residues in the commodities under assessment was the highest for wheat, reaching 1.45% of the ADI.

EFSA concluded that the long‐term and short‐term intake of residues of prothioconazole‐desthio resulting from the existing and the intended uses from previous EFSA outputs is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.

During the Member State consultation, some views were expressed that crops for which the Article 12 confirmatory data were considered as not addressed by EFSA in the present assessment – head cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, dry peas, beans, lentils and lupins – shall be included in the consumer exposure calculation and dietary burden calculation, using the input values according to enforcement residue definition, multiplied by the conversion factor of 2 as provisionally derived for leafy crops and pulses and oilseeds by the peer review. The indicative dietary burden and consumer exposure calculation including these commodities did not significantly affect the calculated exposures (18% of the ADI, NL toddler). The EMS, France, proposed that, on the basis of low contribution of these crops to the consumer chronic exposure and the tentative margin of safety for the acute exposure, the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps could be considered addressed, whereas EFSA considered they are not, and it is therefore left for risk management decision whether the argument of the margin of safety obtained with the tentative CF of 2 is acceptable to waive the need to submit the Article 12 confirmatory data.

It is also noted that the peer review of the active substance in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 was recently finalised by EFSA, while a decision on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (and related new endpoints) has not been implemented yet by the European Commission. As outcome of the renewal assessment, the residue definition for risk assessment included additional metabolites and is pending further toxicological information for current metabolites. Therefore, the consumer risk assessment could not be finalised. Considering the above, the conclusions reported in this reasoned opinion are provisional and might need to be reconsidered once the decision on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (and related new endpoints) is implemented by the European Commission.

ASSESSMENT

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) received an application from Bayer AG Crop Science for the use of conversion factors for MRL setting of prothioconazole in plants. The application was submitted in accordance with Article 6 of the Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, following the recommendations agreed in the Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed (SCoPAFF) Residues meeting held in June 2021. 1 In the framework of this non‐standard application, the applicant did not propose any modification of the existing EU MRLs for prothioconazole currently set in Regulation (EU) 2024/1318, but wished to confirm the large margin of safety of the conversion factors.

The purpose of the application, as stated by the applicant and the EMS France, is to demonstrate that robust conversion factors (CFs) from enforcement to risk assessment can be derived from available residue trials, regardless of the GAP for a given crop. No specific GAPs have therefore been presented under the present MRL application.

Prothioconazole is the ISO common name for (RS)‐2‐[2‐(1‐chlorocyclopropyl)‐3‐(2‐chlorophenyl)‐2‐hydroxypropyl]‐2,4‐dihydro‐1,2,4‐triazole‐3‐thione (IUPAC). It is noted that this ISO common name corresponds to a racemic mixture of (R)‐ and (S)‐ prothioconazole. The chemical structures of the active substance and its main metabolites are reported in Appendix E.

Prothioconazole was evaluated in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC 2 with the United Kingdom designated as rapporteur Member State (RMS) for the representative uses as a foliar treatment on cereals and rapeseeds. The draft assessment report (DAR) prepared by the RMS has been peer reviewed by EFSA (EFSA, 2007). Prothioconazole was approved 3 for use as a fungicide on 1 August 2008.

The process of renewal of the first approval has been completed in 2025, and an EFSA conclusion has been issued (EFSA, 2025); however, a decision on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (and related new endpoints) has not been implemented yet by the European Commission.

The EU MRLs for prothioconazole are established in Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. 4 The review of existing MRLs according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (MRL review) has been performed (EFSA, 2014) and the proposed modifications have been implemented in the MRL legislation. After completion of the MRL review, EFSA has issued some reasoned opinions on the modification of MRLs for prothioconazole, from which the proposals have been considered in MRL regulations. 5 Also, certain Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) have been taken over in the EU MRL legislation.

The data submitted to address the Article 12 confirmatory data have been evaluated by EFSA in 2020 (EFSA, 2020). Lowering of tentative MRLs to the LOQ was proposed for various commodities (barley, oats, rye, pulses, onions, shallots, flowering brassica, Brussels sprouts, head cabbage and leek) since the Article 12 confirmatory data gap on ‘sufficient residue trials analysing the residues in compliance with the proposed residue definition for risk assessment in plant commodities’ was not addressed.

Furthermore, after the assessment of the Article 12 confirmatory data, EFSA issued two additional reasoned opinions on the modification of MRLs for prothioconazole in garlic, onions and shallots and on sugar beet and chicory roots (EFSA, 2023a, 2023b). However, only the proposals for sugar beet and chicory roots (EFSA, 2023b) have been implemented in the recent EU MRL legislation, while the risk management decision on the Article 12 confirmatory data evaluation and on the Article 6 application for onions, shallots, garlic, celeriacs/turnip‐rooted celeries and rapeseeds/canola seeds (which are related to the Article 12 confirmatory data) has been put on hold since 2023. 6 It is further noted that, in 2021, the JMPR derived Codex MRLs in sunflower seed (0.5 mg/kg), safflower seed (0.5 mg/kg) and rapeseed (0.2 mg/kg) and revised the Codex MRLs in edible offal of mammalians and poultry and for mammalian fat (FAO/WHO, 2021). These Codex MRLs were assessed as safe by EFSA in the preparation of EU position in the 53rd session of CCPR (EFSA, 2022). However, in the 53rd session of the CCPR, the EU noted reservations on the advancement of proposed Codex MRLs for the above‐mentioned oilseeds and animal commodities, pending the outcome of the EU pesticides peer review and that a risk assessment for TDMs has not been carried out (FAO/WHO, 2022). Therefore, these Codex MRLs have not been taken over in the EU MRL legislation. These Codex MRL proposals were not taken into consideration for the consumer exposure calculation under the present assessment.

In 2026, EFSA issued another reasoned opinion on the modification of EU MRLs of prothioconazole in various crops (EFSA, 2026a), but the conclusions of this output have not been implemented yet. Nevertheless, EFSA considered the outcome of this assessment in the present reasoned opinion.

The applicant of the current application claimed to have at its disposal a large residue data set, addressing the outstanding data gap from the Article 12 confirmatory data assessment (e.g. residue trials analysed according to the residue definition for the risk assessment). Thus, following the SCoPAFF Residues meeting in June 2021, European Commission invited Bayer to submit this supportive data to EFSA under the procedure of Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, in order to confirm the large margin of safety of the CFs in particular for the evaluation of Codex MRLs (CXLs) for barley, oats, rye and pulses.

The applicant has provided with the present MRL application a large set of residue trials data (450 trials covering 14 commodities) analysing residue trials simultaneously for residue definition of enforcement and risk assessment. Based on these data, the EMS derived conversion factors for commodities on which the trials were provided, proposing to use these data as a body of evidence to derive conversion factors applicable to broader crop groups and to eventually address the data gaps identified during the MRL review under Article 12. These trials support various GAPs, including those reported for the MRL review. EFSA acknowledged this to be a non‐standard Article 6 MRL application and based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the EMS (France, 2025), the draft assessment report (DAR) and its addendum (United Kingdom, 2004, 2007) prepared under Council Directive 91/414/EEC, the final Commission review report on prothioconazole (European Commission, 2021), the conclusion on the peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance prothioconazole (EFSA, 2007), as well as the conclusions from previous EFSA opinions on prothioconazole (EFSA, 2015a, 2015b, 2020, 2023a, 2023b), including the reasoned opinion on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation No 396/2005 (EFSA, 2014). In addition, the recent output of prothioconazole in various crops (EFSA, 2026a) was also considered in the risk assessment, although the MRL proposals have not been implemented into the regulation yet.

For this application, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) 544/2011 7 and the guidance documents applicable at the date of submission of the IUCLID application are applicable (European Commission, 1997a, 1997b, 1997c, 1997d, 1997e, 1997f, 1997g, 2010, 2021, 2023a, 2023b, 2023c, 2023d, 2025; OECD, 2007, 2013, 2014, 2016). The assessment is performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 8

Based on the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC, the data evaluated under previous MRL assessments, and the additional data provided by the EMS in the framework of this application, EFSA prepared in February 2026 a draft reasoned opinion, which was circulated to Member States for consultation via a written procedure. Comments received by 27 February 2026 were considered during the finalisation of this reasoned opinion.

A selected list of end points of the studies assessed by EFSA in the framework of this MRL application including the end points of relevant studies assessed previously is presented in Appendix B.

The EU pesticides peer review of the active substance in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 was recently finalised by EFSA (EFSA, 2025), but the decision of renewal was not yet taken by the European Commission. Therefore, the conclusions reported in this reasoned opinion may need to be reconsidered in the light of the outcome of the decision of renewal of the active substance.

The evaluation report submitted by the EMS (France, 2025), the report of the Member State Consultation on the draft Reasoned Opinion (EFSA, 2026b) and the exposure calculations using the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) are made publicly available as background documents to this reasoned opinion. 9

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

New studies were not provided within the present MRL application.

The metabolism of prothioconazole labelled in the phenyl‐ring has been investigated in root (sugar beet), pulses/oilseeds (peanut) and cereal/grass (wheat) crop groups by foliar treatment and by seed treatment on cereal/grasses crop group (wheat) in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC and the Article 12 MRL review (EFSA, 2007, 2014).

In addition, the metabolism of prothioconazole‐desthio labelled in the triazole moiety was investigated after foliar applications on cereals (EFSA, 2007, 2014). The metabolism of triazole labelled prothioconazole in root crops (sugar beet) and pulses and oilseeds (peanut) was assessed by the JMPR and reported during the MRL review (EFSA, 2014; FAO/WHO, 2009a, 2009b).

In plants, prothioconazole is extensively metabolised and the metabolic pathway is similar in all crops investigated. The main metabolic pathway consisted of the formation of prothioconazole‐desthio with further hydroxylation (with the formation of several closely related metabolites) and glucosidation steps (EFSA, 2014). The studies with triazole labelled prothioconazole indicated the cleavage of triazole linkage and formation of three major TDM metabolites: triazole alanine, triazole lactic acid and triazole acetic acid (EFSA, 2014).

As the residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment are different, tentative conversion factors for enforcement to risk assessment of 2 for cereal grain, pulses and oilseeds, leafy vegetables and root and tuber vegetables and of 3 for cereal straw were derived by the MRL review on the basis of the available plant metabolism data (EFSA, 2014). The same tentative conversion factors were calculated in the recent renewal of the active substance (EFSA, 2025). For maize and potatoes (seed treatment), no median conversion factors for enforcement to risk assessment were derived as residues in these crops are expected to be below 0.01 mg/kg.

The above studies do not investigate the possible impact of plant metabolism on the isomer ratio of prothioconazole and new studies were not submitted in the context of this application. This was considered as an open data gap also in the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (EFSA, 2025).

Nature of residues in rotational crops

The MRL review concluded that the metabolism of prothioconazole in primary and rotational crops is similar (EFSA, 2014). The same conclusion was taken in the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (EFSA, 2025).

Nature of residues in processed commodities

Prothioconazole‐desthio and other compounds, which are included in the risk assessment residue definition and contain the 2‐(1‐chlorocyclopropyl)‐3‐(2‐chlorophenyl)‐2‐hydroxypropyl‐2H‐1,2,4‐triazole moiety, due to their similar structure to the parent compound and/or prothioconazole‐desthio, are expected to remain stable under hydrolysis (EFSA, 2014, 2015b).

The TDMs are stable under hydrolysis studies simulating baking/brewing/boiling, pasteurisation and sterilisation (EFSA, 2018b).

The same conclusion was taken in the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (EFSA, 2025).

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

The analytical enforcement method for the determination of prothioconazole‐desthio residues in plant matrices was assessed during the EU pesticides peer review and the MRL review (EFSA, 2007, 2014) and in the renewal of the approval process (EFSA, 2025).

During renewal of the approval, the EU pesticides peer review concluded that residues of prothioconazole‐desthio in food and feed of plant origin can be determined by the QuEChERS LC–MS/MS method with a LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg in each matrix group.

Although the present MRL application is not assessed in accordance with decisions of the renewal of approval, the conclusion on the availability of validated analytical enforcement methods is valid also for the present assessment.

Storage stability of residues in plants

Storage stability data under freezer conditions were evaluated and summarised under the EU pesticide peer review on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (EFSA, 2025). The storage stability of prothioconazole‐desthio is addressed at −18°C in high water content crop group (wheat forage, spinach, sugar beet, tomato) for at least 24 months, in high oil content crop group (canola seed) for 24 months, in high protein content crop group (peas) for 24 months, in high starch content crop group (wheat grain) for 36 months.

The stability of prothioconazole hydroxy metabolites (prothioconazole‐desthio‐α‐hydroxy‐(M18), prothioconazole‐desthio 3‐hydroxy‐(M14), prothioconazole‐desthio 4‐ hydroxy‐ (M15), prothioconazole‐ desthio 5‐ hydroxy‐ (M16) and prothioconazole‐ desthio 6‐ hydroxy‐ (M17)) is addressed for 25 months in high water content crop group (tomatoes), in high oil content group (rapeseed), high starch content crop group (potatoes) and high acid content crop group (oranges) when stored at −18°C (EFSA, 2025). The EU pesticides peer review concluded that, for hydroxylated metabolites, only one study in potatoes is not sufficient to cover cereals; therefore, an additional commodity from the category of high starch content or one from the high protein content category would be needed to extrapolate to all crops. It should be noted that the OECD Guideline 506 on the stability of pesticides has recently been updated (OECD, 2025). In this revision, the previously used matrix groups ‘high starch’ and ‘high protein’ (OECD, 2007) are no longer applicable. Instead, root and tuber vegetables are now classified under the ‘high‐water’ matrix group, while pulses, cereal grains, straw and dried fruits fall under the ‘dry’ matrix group.

New storage stability studies on zucchini, sugar beet, grapes, rapeseed and dry peas have been submitted and assessed with the recent MRL application (EFSA, 2026a). Submitted studies demonstrate the stability of prothioconazole‐desthio and 5 hydroxy metabolites for 12 months when stored at −18°C in zucchini (high water commodity), dry peas (dry commodity), oil seed rape seeds (high oil commodity) and grapes (high acid commodity). In the study with sugar beet, a shorter storage stability period of 6, 3 and 9 months was reported for prothioconazole‐desthio 3‐hydroxy‐(M14), prothioconazole‐desthio 4‐ hydroxy‐ (M15) and prothioconazole‐ desthio 6‐ hydroxy‐ (M17), respectively (EFSA, 2026a). Considering that the stability of these hydroxy metabolites has been demonstrated in three crops of the high water content matrix group (tomatoes, potatoes and zucchini), the data of the new study on sugar beet were not considered to invalidate the residue trial data for high water content crops in cases when sample storage exceeded the 12 months.

In addition, the new study demonstrated the storage stability of prothioconazole hydroxy metabolites in dry matrix (dry peas, dry commodity, previously considered as high protein content matrix), and therefore, it can be concluded that the storage stability of hydroxy metabolites is covered also in cereals for a time interval of at least 12 months when stored at −18°C, addressing the data gap of the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval.

The storage stability relevant endpoints are summarised in Appendix B.1.1.2.

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

In support of the present non‐standard application, the applicant submitted a package of ca. 450 residue trials performed in 14 different crops to derive conversion factors (CFs) from enforcement to risk assessment. The samples were analysed in line with the current residue definition for risk assessment. According to the EMS assessment, the methods used were sufficiently validated and fit for purpose; the validated individual LOQ per compound included in the risk assessment residue definition is 0.01 mg/kg in all crops assessed (France, 2025). The samples from these residue trials were stored under conditions that have demonstrated the integrity of the samples.

Based on the applicant's assessment, the submitted data indicate that the hydroxy metabolites of prothioconazole‐desthio are largely unquantifiable in the food commodities tested but represent a significant portion of the residue in feed items. In addition, according to the EMS and the applicant, these data suggest that previously derived CFs by EFSA are conservative (France, 2025).

The residue definition for risk assessment includes prothioconazole‐desthio and five metabolites, 13 further referred to as ‘hydroxy metabolites’, while the residue definition for enforcement includes only prothioconazole‐desthio. EFSA notes that the risk assessment residue definition has been revised in the renewal of approval of prothioconazole 14 (EFSA, 2025) but, since the present MRL application was submitted before this conclusion, the assessment was performed in line with the residue definitions applicable at the time of the submission of the MRL application. In addition, considering the scope of the present assessment, the data on TDMs were not submitted and not further requested by EFSA.

Presentation of the approach taken by the EMS (France, 2025 ):

Considering that the residues of hydroxy metabolites are often < LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg in food, the applicant is of the opinion that considering analytes < LOQ at the LOQ (0.01 mg/kg) for the derivation of conversion factors could lead to overestimated CFs. To avoid this, the following methodology was proposed by the applicant and supported by EMS, France, for the CF calculation in food commodities:

If in a trial sample, all analytes are < LOQ (including prothioconazole‐desthio), a CF is not calculable, i.e. these data are excluded from the calculation of the CF;

If in a trial sample, all analytes are < LOQ (including prothioconazole‐desthio), a CF is not calculable, i.e. these data are excluded from the calculation of the CF;

If in a trial sample, only prothioconazole‐desthio is quantified above the LOQ and all hydroxy metabolites are below the LOQ, a CF of 1 is calculated;

If in a trial sample, only prothioconazole‐desthio is quantified above the LOQ and all hydroxy metabolites are below the LOQ, a CF of 1 is calculated;

If in a trial sample, prothioconazole‐desthio is present as well as some hydroxy metabolites, the CF is calculated considering that the residue values for the metabolites that are not quantifiable (< LOQ) are at 0.

If in a trial sample, prothioconazole‐desthio is present as well as some hydroxy metabolites, the CF is calculated considering that the residue values for the metabolites that are not quantifiable (< LOQ) are at 0.

For the calculation of the CFs in feed items, as the residues of metabolites are often > LOQ, the value of the LOQ (0.01 mg/kg) was applied also in cases where residues were < LOQ, because in the case of feed items, the overestimation is less important than for food items. Finally, when prothioconazole‐desthio residues were below the LOQ and at least one hydroxy metabolite was above the LOQ, the LOQ value for prothioconazole‐desthio was used to allow the CF calculation (France, 2025).

Discussion of the EMS's approach and presentation of the EFSA's assessment

EFSA agrees with the EMS that a CF is not calculable when all residues, including prothioconazole‐desthio, are < LOQ in line with OECD Guidance on Crop Field Trials (OECD, 2016). In such a situation, these data were excluded from the data set for the calculation of the CF.

However, for the remaining situations when parent prothioconazole‐desthio is above the LOQ but metabolites are below or above the LOQ, EFSA is of a different opinion: residues < LOQ should be considered at the LOQ (0.01 mg/kg), regardless of whether the commodity is a food or feed item. A result reported ‘< LOQ’ only means the true value is somewhere between zero and the LOQ; treating it as zero systematically may underestimate the dietary exposure assessment downward. However, it is acknowledged that, as the residue definition for risk assessment includes five metabolites, this approach can lead to an overestimation of the CF, i.e. by a factor of 6 if all metabolites are < LOQ and the parent at the LOQ. Therefore, on a case‐by‐case basis, this over‐conservative approach may be refined using available evidence. The following considerations were made by EFSA:

In cases where a sufficient number of residue data are provided for a given crop and if residue trials consistently indicate that hydroxy metabolites are below the LOQ, EFSA would agree with the approach of the applicant and the EMS and derive a CF of 1. However, in cases of higher uncertainty due to a limited residue data set and/or proposed broad extrapolations, despite all metabolites < LOQ, EFSA would still consider metabolites present at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg and would derive a more conservative CF.

An overview of the submitted residue trials and proposed CFs and EFSA's assessment is reported in the sections below.

Furthermore, the risk assessment values were derived or updated only for those crops for which the Article 12 confirmatory data gap was considered as not addressed in the evaluation of the Article 12 confirmatory data (onions, shallots, flowering brassica, Brussel sprouts, head cabbages, leeks, beans, lentils, peas, lupins/lupini beans, barley, oats, wheat, rye and grass) (EFSA, 2020) and for which new residue data were submitted (see Appendix B.1.2.1). Discussion and conclusion leafy crops

Discussion and conclusion leafy crops

The EMS and the applicant propose to extrapolate the CFs derived for Brussels sprouts and leeks to the metabolic group of leafy crops and to address the Article 12 confirmatory data gap (so far not considered addressed) for broccoli, cauliflower and head cabbage as all these crops belong to the same metabolic group ‘leafy vegetables’. In their assessment, the CF value for all leafy crops would be 1 (France, 2025). EFSA agrees to derive a robust CF of 1 for Brussels sprouts and for leeks (classified as stem vegetable according to EU extrapolation guidelines – European Commission, 2023b) and to consider that the data gap from the MRL review is addressed for these two crops. However, EFSA excludes the option proposed by EMS to extrapolate this conclusion to other leafy crops.

EFSA does not agree with the position of the applicant and EMS to address the data gaps in the magnitude of residues section using the principles of the nature of residues section. These two areas, albeit supporting each other, are governed by different data requirements. According to the EU extrapolation guidelines (European Commission, 2023b), extrapolations among brassica vegetables are possible with some limitations: residue data from head cabbage together with cauliflower – or alternatively from cauliflower and broccoli – are required for extrapolation to the combined subgroups of flowering brassica and head brassica, and this is only permitted before the edible part has formed. For the flowering brassica subgroup alone, trials on both cauliflower and broccoli are needed, and extrapolation in this case is allowed both before and after the edible part has developed. However, residue trials analysing for the hydroxy metabolites are not available for flowering brassica.

Brussels sprouts are not recommended for extrapolation to any brassica vegetables (European Commission, 2023b). In addition, the intended GAP foresees application after the edible part of the crop is formed (after BBCH 19); therefore, an extrapolation to head brassica would not be possible. Based on the above, EFSA concludes that there is insufficient weight of evidence to derive a group CF for brassica vegetables based on the eight trials on brussels sprouts. The data gap in flowering brassica and head cabbage is not addressed: GAP compliant residue trials analysing residues according to residue definition for the risk assessment are still required to conclude on the magnitude of hydroxy metabolites in flowering brassica and head cabbage.

Therefore, EFSA continues to consider a CF of 2 appropriate for oilseeds.

Furthermore, in 2021, the JMPR (FAO/WHO, 2022) derived a Codex MRL of 0.2 mg/kg (FAO/WHO, 2022) which was evaluated by EFSA in 2022 as safe for consumers (using a CF of 2) to be taken over in the EU legislation. Linseed

Linseed

MRL review NEU: 2 × 120 g a.s./ha, PHI 28 (EFSA, 2014, 2020 )

For linseed, a tentative MRL of 0.09 mg/kg was implemented after the Article 12 MRL review (EFSA, 2014) with a footnote specifying a data gap on residue trials analysed according to the RA residue definition. EFSA notes that the MRL of 0.09 mg/kg, based on residue trials on oilseed rape, has already been derived in 2012, where, on the basis of nine NEU and seven SEU trials, a CF of 1.75 was derived from trials with hydroxy metabolites above the LOQ (EFSA, 2012). The MRL review considered that one additional SEU trial is needed, and therefore, the MRL was derived on a tentative basis and the Article 12 confirmatory data gap was set.

This data gap was addressed in the evaluation of the Article 12 confirmatory (EFSA, 2020) on the basis of nine residue trials on oilseed rape, and the MRL of 0.09 mg/kg was confirmed for the NEU use. However, a risk management decision to confirm the MRL at 0.09 mg/kg has not been implemented so far.

With the present MRL application, the applicant submitted four residue trials on linseed supporting the same NEU GAP as reported for the MRL review. According to the understanding of EFSA, this information was submitted as supportive information to address the Art 12 confirmatory data gap in dry pulses and populate the residue data for oilseeds. In linseeds, residues of all hydroxy metabolites were < LOQ except for prothioconazole‐3‐hydroxy‐desthio, which was measured at 0.03 mg/kg in one sample at a longer PHI of 35 days. Residues of prothioconazole‐desthio were always above the LOQ, between 0.01 and 0.09 mg/kg. In green material, hydroxy metabolites were mostly above the LOQ for all compounds.

Furthermore, the submitted linseed residue trials indicate that a higher MRL of 0.2 mg/kg would be needed to support the authorised NEU use of prothioconazole on linseed. The raising of MRLs is not within the remit of the present MRL application and also the applicant and the EMS did not propose raising of the MRL. Should applicant wish to do so, a new MRL application on the modification of the MRL in accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) 396/2005 must be submitted. Risk assessment values were derived and compiled in Appendix B.1.2.1 and further used in the exposure calculations, although lower than the values used in previous EFSA assessments on the basis of rape seed data.

The EMS and the applicant propose a conversion factor (CF) of 1, based on the results available for linseeds. The CF derived from the four available linseed residue trials, when applying the upper bound (UB) approach, would be 4.33. EFSA considers that a CF of 1 may not be sufficiently protective, taking into account the limited number of trials (four), the quantification of hydroxy metabolites in green plant material and the fact that prothioconazole‐3‐hydroxy‐desthio was quantified in one out of four seed samples.

At the same time, it is acknowledged that a CF of 4.33 may be overly conservative. The current MRL for linseeds is based on nine oilseed rape residue trials, which were compliant with the NEU GAP for linseed (EFSA, 2012) and were subsequently used for residue data extrapolation to linseed (EFSA, 2014). Based on these nine oilseed rape trials, a CF of 1.75 was proposed (EFSA, 2012). In the later MRL review, a more conservative CF of 2 was applied to oilseed rape and, by extrapolation, also to other oilseeds based on metabolism studies in peanuts.

In the current application, when using the submitted oilseed data package as supporting evidence for linseeds, the combined evaluation of the oilseed rape residue trials (nine previously evaluated trials and 19 newly submitted trials) together with the four linseed residue trials results in a CF of 2.25 derived using the UB approach.

Given the divergence between the CFs derived from the different data sets, EFSA considers that a CF of 2 remains appropriate for linseeds, based on the four available linseed trials and supported by the oilseed rape data. This conclusion is in line with the results for oilseed rape, for which the NEU trials provided in the present application also indicated quantifiable levels of hydroxy metabolites. It is also consistent with the CF applied in the context of the confirmatory data assessment and with the tentative CF proposed for oilseeds in the renewal of approval (EFSA, 2020, 2025).

However, it is noted that, in the recent output on prothioconazole in various crops (EFSA, 2026a), data were submitted on peas (dry) seeds that demonstrate stability of prothioconazole and its hydroxy metabolites for 12 months at −18°C. These crops are all considered under the ‘pulses and oilseed’ crop category in the nature of residues area, while according to EU extrapolation guidelines (European Commission, 2023b), these crops fall in separate categories of ‘oilseeds’ and ‘pulses’ in the magnitude of residues section, and extrapolation of residue data among these crop groups is not supported. Discussion and conclusion Pulses and oilseeds

Discussion and conclusion Pulses and oilseeds

EFSA disagrees with the applicant and the EMS to combine available information and to derive a generic CF for the metabolic group ‘pulses and oilseeds’ as data gaps in the magnitude of residues section cannot be addressed using the principles of the nature of residues section, which is governed by different data requirements.

From the available data, a CF of 2 is proposed for oilseed rape, soybeans and linseeds in line with the conclusions of the renewal of the active substance (EFSA, 2025). For sunflower seeds, a CF of 1 would be acceptable for the reported EU uses. For poppy seeds, mustard seeds, peanuts and gold of pleasure seeds, the Article 12 confirmatory data requirement was considered addressed in the evaluation of the Article 12 confirmatory (EFSA, 2020), but a risk management decision to confirm the related MRLs has not been made yet. EFSA reiterates here the conclusion that for these oilseeds the CF of 2 is applicable, which is also in line with the EFSA peer review conclusion on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole.

No data were submitted for dry pulses. EFSA therefore concludes that the Article 12 data gap is not addressed and the existing tentative EU MRLs cannot be confirmed for these crops.

To derive risk assessment values for barley and oat straw, the data available by the MRL review were combined with the data submitted under the present assessment. See Appendix B, Table B.1.2.1. The risk assessment values were calculated from the trials where the treatment pattern was closest to the EU authorised uses (2 × 200 g/ha, within 25% deviation) and the derived risk assessment values were used directly for the estimation of the livestock dietary burden.

For barley straw, six different CF were calculated three for NEU, SEU and SEU + NEU trials using all the trials submitted in this application, whatever the GAP, and three for NEU, SEU and SEU + NEU trials that were conducted at application rates approximating the authorised EU GAP (which is 2 × 200 g a.s./ha, PHI 35 days). The most critical CF 2.35 was calculated based on NEU trials conducted at any GAP and is less critical than the current one of 3, based on metabolism studies (EFSA, 2020).

Maize

Maize

There is no Article 12 confirmatory data footnote for maize grain in the MRL regulation. The current MRL of 0.1 mg/kg in maize grain was adopted from a CXL (FAO, 2014) based on USA, Brazil and EU trials. According to the understanding of EFSA, the residue data on maize grain were submitted to populate the residue data in cereals.

The CXL is based on 47 residue trials conducted in the USA (4 × 200 g ai/ha, PHI 0–14 days), Brazil (2 × 70–87.5 g ai/ha, PHI 15 days) and Europe (2 × 125 g ai/ha, PHI 35 days). These uses refer to foliar applications.

In almost all cases, prothioconazole‐desthio residues in grain were below LOQ, with only one US trial detecting 0.05 mg/kg (FAO, 2014). As the hydroxy metabolites are not included in the JMPR residue definition for risk assessment, an indicative CF of 2 from the metabolism studies was used by EFSA to perform the consumer risk assessment (EFSA, 2015c).

At the time of the MRL review, there was no CXL in place. For the existing EU uses (seed treatment once at 27 g a.s./100 kg grain or 9 g a.s./ha), the MRL review concluded on a no‐residue situation and did not request further residue trials, an MRL at the LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg was proposed (EFSA, 2014).

With the present MRL application, the applicant submitted 16 NEU and 16 SEU residue trials on maize that match the foliar EU GAP in Czech Republic (2 × 125 g ai/ha, PHI 35 days) and Italy (2 × 125 g ai/ha, 35 days or non‐specified PHI). The same GAPs and corresponding residue trials have been provided to the JMPR for the assessment of Codex MRL (FAO, 2014).

All residues for all compounds in the residue definition for the risk assessment were < LOQ in immature kernel, kernel and ear without husk in all trials at all PHIs (8–83 days). Based on these results, the EMS and the applicant are proposing a CF of 1. EFSA agrees on the CF of 1 considering the large data set available indicating a no residue situation in maize grain. Furthermore, EFSA concludes that the CF of 1 is also applicable for the existing EU MRL, which is based on the Codex MRL.

In maize forage, residues according to the submitted residue trials, supporting the foliar EU uses assessed by the JMPR (and not reported as authorised EU uses in the MRL review) were mostly > LOQ except for prothioconazole‐alpha‐hydroxy‐desthio and prothioconazole 6‐hydroxy‐desthio. The EMS is proposing a CF of 4.69 based on the NEU trials. This is more critical than the current CF of 3. EFSA agrees with the EMS proposal.

The risk assessment values, as derived for maize forage from the submitted trials (Table B1.2.1), were used directly for the estimation of the livestock dietary burden, noting that since MRL review, new GAPs on maize for forages (referring to foliar application) might have been authorised in the EU in line with the information provided by the applicant and the EMS (resulting in a more critical residue situation than would be expected from the seed treatment uses). Therefore, EFSA considered the submitted residue data for the livestock dietary burden calculation.

Conclusion on cereals group

Conclusion on cereals group

For cereals, the extensive data set provided addresses the Article 12 data gap for wheat, rye, barley and oat grain. These data were also submitted and assessed by EFSA in the renewal process (EFSA, 2025), and a CF of 2 was proposed for cereal grains based on positive results in barley trials.

However, EFSA agrees with the EMS that further refinement of this previous endpoint is now possible. A CF of 1 can be derived for maize, wheat and rye grain, as available data provide a sufficient weight of evidence to confirm that hydroxy metabolites remain below the LOQ in wheat and rye grain. For barley and oat grain, however, the occurrence of hydroxy metabolites above the LOQ was observed, and EFSA proposes maintaining a CF of 2 in line with the conclusions of the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval. The data gaps are deemed addressed for cereals.

For cereal straw, in the peer review, a CF of 3 was proposed, taking into consideration the CF from the whole data set and not only the EU GAP compliant trials per zone. However, EFSA agrees with the approach of the EMS and considers the CF of 4.9 for cereal straws as a worst‐case situation based on the NEU wheat straw trials. This CF is extrapolated to cereal straws unless a crop‐specific CF is derived.

Finally, for maize forage, a CF of 4.69 based on the NEU trials is proposed in agreement with the EMS.

Grass

MRL review GAP NEU: 2 × 125 g a.s./ha; PHI 35 days for seed derivation.

In the MRL review, no residue trials on grass were available to support the use of prothioconazole on grass in northern Europe, and therefore, risk assessment values could not be derived (EFSA, 2014). A data gap for four residue trials on grass compliant with NEU use was set by the MRL review. Since grass is a major component of the livestock dietary burden, this requirement was implemented as Article 12 confirmatory data gap for MRL in animal commodities.

In the assessment of confirmatory data, the applicant did not provide new residue trials complying with the northern outdoor GAP on grass, and therefore, the data gap of the MRL review (associated to MRLs for commodities of animal origin) was not considered addressed (EFSA, 2020).

With the present MRL application, the applicant provided six NEU trials on grass where samples of fresh grass and hay were analysed for the hydroxy metabolites. The use pattern investigated in residue trials (1 × 200 g/ha) differed from the use of the authorised NEU GAP (2 × 125 g/ha, PHI 35 days). Samples of green material were taken on the day of the application (PHI of 0 days) and of hay at time intervals 28–73 days after the treatment. The authorised NEU use on grass reported for the MRL review is in the Netherlands and is for the provision of grass seed. According to the explanation provided by the NL in the MRL review, after the seed is harvested, the remaining plant is left on the field for livestock grazing (35 days after the treatment) (EFSA, 2014). Therefore, fresh grass or grass silage will not be fed to livestock.

Although residue trials were not fully in line with the authorised GAP, EFSA concluded to accept them to address the Article 12 confirmatory data gap, noting uncertainty related to potential overestimation of total residues. Residues of the hydroxy metabolites and prothioconazole‐desthio in grass hay (28–73 days after the treatment) were above the LOQ in most of the trials except for prothioconazole‐6‐hydroxy‐desthio, which was always < LOQ. A CF of 1.76 is derived based on the data for hay, which is lower than the CF of 2 derived from metabolism studies (France, 2025).

For fresh grass, the CF was not derived, as residue data have been obtained from samples collected on the day of the treatment (0 d PHI) when metabolisation of the parent compound would not have occurred. This is confirmed by residue data where residues of the parent in the fresh grass range from 0.88 to 3.1 mg/kg remaining nearly within the same level (0.93–3.15 mg/kg) according to the risk assessment residue definition. Residue trials with hay confirm that concentrations of hydroxy residues increase at longer PHI intervals (35 days), allowing to derive a robust CF of 1.76.

For the livestock dietary burden assessment, only the data in hay were considered since, according to specifications of the authorised GAP in NL, animals will not be fed with fresh grass or silage. The residue data are compiled in Table B.1.2.1.

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK

With the present MRL application, risk assessment values were updated for barley, oat, rye and wheat straw, maize forage, turnip tops and grass, and therefore, the livestock dietary exposure had to be updated. In addition, the conversion factor of 1 was confirmed for the residue data in wheat grain, rye grain, maize grain, root crops and sugar beet and of 2 for cotton, barley grain, oat grain and, by extrapolation from oilseeds, also to peanuts. Since the existing EU MRL in soya beans is set on the basis of Codex MRL and for the respective use the CF of 1 is not confirmed, the risk assessment values as derived by the JMPR, multiplied by the CF of 2 (as proposed for oilseeds, except for sunflower seeds) were applied in the DB calculation.

For dry beans, dry peas, lupin seeds and head cabbage the Article 12 confirmatory data gap for residue trials analysed according to the residue definition for risk assessment is considered not addressed, and therefore, these commodities were excluded from the exposure calculation.

The most recent livestock dietary burden calculation (EFSA, 2026a) was updated with the new residue data available. Details of the input values for the dietary burden calculation are provided in Appendix D1.

The dietary burden calculation results, detailed in Section B.2, indicate that the exposure for all livestock species surpasses the trigger value of 0.1 mg/kg DM.

Key contributors include sugar beet tops for cattle and swine, barely straw for sheep and wheat straw for poultry. The residue transfer to animal matrices from the EU uses and import of crops was further assessed.

During the Member State consultation, some MSs expressed the view that head cabbage, peas, beans and lupins shall be included in the dietary burden calculation, using the conversion factor of 2, as derived from metabolism study in sugar beet (leaves) and peanuts, as such calculations would determine the contribution of head cabbage and pulses for the livestock exposure and would allow to determine the potential impact on the MRL proposals in livestock commodities. EFSA therefore performed an indicative livestock exposure scenario assessing the impact of these crops on the livestock exposure for risk management considerations, noting that for these commodities the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps are not addressed. EFSA notes that the data for bean vines and pea vines, hay and silage have not been available, and therefore, the indicative exposure might be underestimated.

The results of the indicative DB calculation confirm that the inclusion of head cabbage and dry beans, peas and lupins into the EU livestock diet has no impact on the exposures as calculated above.

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in livestock

The nature of prothioconazole has been evaluated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC and the Article 12 MRL review (EFSA, 2007, 2014). Livestock metabolism studies with prothioconazole triazolyl‐ and phenyl‐labelled were conducted in goats and poultry, while with prothioconazole‐desthio only in goats (EFSA, 2014).

Based on the overall metabolic picture of prothioconazole and prothioconazole‐desthio in animals, the residue definition for enforcement in animal products was proposed by the MRL review as prothioconazole‐desthio (sum of isomers) for all livestock matrices. EFSA also concluded that the residue definition for enforcement in commodities of animal origin is fat soluble.

For risk assessment, the MRL review concluded that residue is defined in all commodities of animal origin as the sum of prothioconazole‐desthio and all metabolites containing the 2‐(1‐chlorocyclopropyl)‐3‐(2‐chlorophenyl)‐2‐hydroxypropyl‐2H‐1,2,4‐triazole moiety, expressed as prothioconazole‐desthio (sum of isomers). This residue definition is applicable for the assessment of the present MRL application. The MRL review noted that although only the glucuronide conjugates of prothioconazole‐desthio were detected in milk, the actual residue levels are expected at a trace level at the calculated dietary burden and EFSA considered that analysing the conjugates of prothioconazole‐desthio would have a negligible impact on the residue levels enforced in milk. However, the MRL review stressed that in case the livestock dietary burden is further increased in the future due to additional uses on feed items, the residue definition for enforcement might have to be revised by including the glucuronide conjugates of prothioconazole‐desthio for all livestock matrices (EFSA, 2014). EFSA notes that the inclusion of conjugates in the risk assessment residue definition for animal matrices and milk is proposed in the renewal of approval EFSA conclusion (EFSA, 2025).

The recent EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole also concluded that sufficiently validated analytical methods using high‐performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC‐MS/MS) are available for the determination of prothioconazole‐desthio (M04) with a limit of quantification (LOQ) in milk at 0.004 mg/kg and with an LOQ in meat, liver, kidney, fat and eggs at 0.01 mg/kg (EFSA, 2025). Although the present MRL application is not assessed in accordance with decisions of the renewal of approval, the conclusion on the availability of validated analytical enforcement methods is valid also for the present assessment.

Furthermore, in the recent EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole, the existing enforcement residue definition as prothioconazole‐desthio was confirmed for livestock (EFSA, 2025). For risk assessment in ruminants, the most relevant residues were concluded to be prothioconazole‐desthio free and conjugated, M14, M15, M33 and M35, free and their conjugates. It was concluded that provisionally these residues are expressed as prothioconazole‐desthio equivalents, pending the data to address the genotoxic potential of M15, M33 and M35. Metabolism studies conducted with TDMs were not available. However, their presence in livestock metabolism studies conducted with prothioconazole‐desthio was noted. Considering that TDMs were present in the feed items and included in the risk assessment residue definition for plants, the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval concluded that TDMs need to be included in the residue definition for risk assessment for livestock, in line with the EFSA outputs on TDMs (EFSA, 2018b).

Magnitude of residues in livestock

During the peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC, the magnitude of prothioconazole residues in ruminants was investigated in a feeding study with lactating cows (EFSA, 2007, 2014). The same studies were submitted for the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (EFSA, 2025) and further details were provided, which are taken into consideration in the present assessment.

Three groups of lactating cows, each consisting of three animals, were dosed for 28 consecutive days with prothioconazole‐desthio at levels equivalent to 0.16, 0.950 and 3.93 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day. The samples were analysed for prothioconazole‐desthio, M14 and M15.

In milk, a plateau level was reached after 1 or 2 days of exposure, according to the dose level group. In milk and muscle in all dose groups, residues were below the LOQ for all analytes. Quantifiable residues were detected in liver from all dose groups and in kidney from the two highest dose levels.

In order to express residues according to the risk assessment residue definition, a CF of 4 for kidney and a CF of 2 for liver were applied as derived in the renewal of approval process (EFSA, 2025). No CFs were derived for milk, muscle and fat since the residue levels in these matrices are expected to be negligible (EFSA, 2014); the same conclusion was derived by the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval (EFSA, 2025).

The feeding study with poultry was not available for the MRL review. The feeding study with prothioconazole was submitted for the renewal of approval and was therefore considered by EFSA for the present assessment (EFSA, 2025; Poland, 2024; United Kingdom, 2018a). Laying hens were dosed orally for 29 consecutive days with prothioconazole at dose rates corresponding to 0 mg/kg feed (control), 0.263 mg/kg feed/day (1x dose group), 0.788 mg/kg feed/day (3x dose group) and 2.591 mg/kg feed/day (10x dose group). These rates are equivalent to 0.020 mg/kg bw/day, 0.059 mg/kg bw/day and 0.191 mg/kg bw/day. Samples were analysed for prothioconazole, prothioconazole‐desthio and prothioconazole‐4‐hydroxy (M15) (Poland, 2024; United Kingdom, 2018a). Residues in eggs from the 10x dose group were < LOQ of 0.004 mg/kg and in muscle, fat and liver below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg; therefore, samples from lower dose groups were not analysed. Feeding studies in poultry with prothioconazole‐desthio are not available, for which the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval set a data gap (EFSA, 2025).

The data from available feeding studies were used to estimate the magnitude of residues in animal commodities for the livestock dietary burdens as calculated under the present assessment. The calculated MRL proposals and risk assessment values are compiled in Appendix B.2.2.1.

The submitted data indicate that, in muscle of swine and ruminants, residues above the LOQ of 0.01* mg/kg are not expected. The existing EU MRL of 0.01 mg/kg is based on the Codex MRL derived by the JMPR in 2017; this Codex MRL has been lowered to 0.01* mg/kg by the JMPR in 2021 (FAO/WHO, 2021).

The submitted data indicate that, in fat of swine and ruminants, residues above the LOQ of 0.01* mg/kg are not expected. The existing EU MRL of 0.02 mg/kg is based on the Codex MRL derived by the JMPR in 2017; this Codex MRL has been lowered to 0.01* mg/kg by the JMPR in 2021 (FAO/WHO, 2021).

The submitted data indicate that, in liver and edible offal of bovine, the MRL of 0.02 mg/kg would be sufficient. This is lower than the existing EU MRL of 0.5 mg/kg, which is based on the Codex MRL derived by the JMPR in 2017; this Codex MRL has been lowered to 0.15 mg/kg by the JMPR in 2021 (FAO/WHO, 2021).

The submitted data indicate that, in liver and edible offal of sheep/goat, the MRL of 0.02 mg/kg would be sufficient. This is lower than the existing EU MRL of 0.5 mg/kg, which is based on the Codex MRL derived by the JMPR in 2017; this Codex MRL has been lowered to 0.15 mg/kg by the JMPR in 2021 (FAO/WHO, 2021).

The submitted data indicate that, in liver and edible offal of swine, the MRL at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg would be sufficient. This is lower than the existing EU MRL of 0.5 mg/kg, which is based on the Codex MRL derived by the JMPR in 2017; this Codex MRL has been lowered to 0.15 mg/kg by the JMPR in 2021 (FAO/WHO, 2021).

The submitted data indicate that, in kidney of bovine and kidney of sheep/goat, the MRL at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg, would be sufficient. This is lower than the existing EU MRL of 0.5 mg/kg, which is based on the Codex MRL derived by the JMPR in 2017; this Codex MRL has been lowered to 0.15 mg/kg by the JMPR in 2021 (FAO/WHO, 2021).

The submitted data indicate that, in kidney of swine, the MRL of 0.01* mg/kg would be sufficient. This is lower than the existing EU MRL of 0.5 mg/kg, which is based on the Codex MRL derived by the JMPR in 2017; this Codex MRL has been lowered to 0.15 mg/kg by the JMPR in 2021 (FAO/WHO, 2021).

The residues in milk do not occur above the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg, and therefore, the existing MRL at the LOQ is sufficient. EFSA notes that a more sensitive analytical enforcement method allowing to quantify residues in milk at the validated LOQ of 0.004 mg/kg is available and therefore, also to align the MRL with the new Codex MRL of 0.004 mg/kg, a lowering of the existing MRL could be considered by the risk managers.

In poultry matrices and eggs, no residues above the LOQ are expected for the calculated dietary burdens; however, noting that this conclusion is based on feeding studies with prothioconazole. The existing EU MRLs in poultry matrices are set above the LOQ only in liver and kidney (0.1 mg/kg) and reflect the Codex MRLs as derived by the JMPR in 2017; the Codex MRLs have been lowered to 0.01 mg/kg in by the CAC in 2021 in poultry edible offal and raised to 0.01 mg/kg in poultry fats.

The existing EU MRLs are currently set on the basis of Codex MRLs, derived by the JMPR in 2017 (FAO, 2017). In 2021, these Codex MRLs were revised and lowered for edible offal of mammalians and poultry and for mammalian fat (FAO/WHO, 2021). These Codex MRLs were assessed as safe by EFSA in the preparation of the EU position in the 53rd session of CCPR (EFSA, 2022) but have not been taken over in the EU MRL legislation. In the 53rd session of the CCPR the EU noted reservations on the advancement of proposed Codex MRLs for edible offal (including liver and kidney of cattle, pigs, goats and sheep), eggs, mammalian fats (except milk fats), meat, milks, edible offal of poultry, poultry fat and meat, pending the outcome of the EU pesticides peer review and that a risk assessment for TDMs has not been carried out (FAO/WHO, 2022).

EFSA concludes that the Article 12 confirmatory data gap for residues in grass, which has been implemented for several animal commodities, is sufficiently addressed and that all existing tentative MRLs in animal commodities can be confirmed.

EFSA also notes that the MRL proposals calculated under the present assessment are lower than the Codex MRL for liver, kidney and edible offal of ruminants and swine and for fat of ruminants and swine. A risk management consideration is required whether the existing EU MRLs for animal commodities should be revised on the basis of the revised Codex MRLs or on the basis of updated EU dietary burden calculation.

In the current application, the applicant submitted five residue trials on honey, with two conducted in the NEU and three in the SEU. CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA conducted a dietary risk assessment using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo model (EFSA, 2018a, 2019). This tool uses food consumption data from various EU population subgroups, allowing both acute and chronic exposure assessments in line with internationally accepted methods for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016).

The toxicological reference values for prothioconazole and its metabolite, prothioconazole‐desthio, were established during the EU's review of pesticides under Directive 91/414/EEC. The acceptable daily intake (ADI) for prothioconazole was set at 0.05 mg/kg body weight per day, and the acute reference dose (ARfD) was set at 0.2 mg/kg body weight (European Commission, 2007). Prothioconazole‐desthio was found to be more toxic than prothioconazole itself, so its ADI and ARfD, set at 0.01 mg/kg body weight, were used for the consumer risk assessments.

In the recent peer review for the renewal of prothioconazole under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, a new ADI of 0.036 mg/kg was established for prothioconazole, while the ARfD remained at the same level of 0.2 mg/kg. The earlier values for prothioconazole‐desthio were confirmed and used for updating consumer risk assessments.

Currently, the residue definition for risk assessment in plant and animal commodities includes the sum of prothioconazole‐desthio and all metabolites containing the 2‐(1‐chlorocyclopropyl)‐3‐(2‐chlorophenyl)‐2‐hydroxypropyl‐2H‐1,2,4‐triazole moiety, expressed as prothioconazole‐desthio (sum of isomers). This definition covers prothioconazole‐desthio (M04) and the metabolites M14, M15, M16, M17 and M18.

In the renewal, these residue definitions in plant and animal commodities were revised and extended. In plant commodities the proposed residue definition includes prothioconazole‐desthio (M04), M14, M15, M16, M17 and their glucoside conjugates (M21, M22, M23) and M18, expressed as prothioconazole‐desthio equivalents (provisionally), pending data to address the genotoxic potential of M15 and M17 (EFSA, 2025).

In animal commodities, the provisional risk assessment residue definition was proposed as prothioconazole‐desthio, free and conjugated, M14, M15, M33 and M35, free and their conjugates (EFSA, 2025), pending the data to address the genotoxic potential of M15, M33 and M35 (EFSA, 2025).

EFSA updated the consumer exposure assessment performed in the framework of the most recent MRL assessment in 2025 (EFSA 2026a, not implemented yet in Regulation) with new risk assessment values available for crops for which the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps were considered addressed in the present assessment – leek and Brussels sprouts. For peanuts, rapeseeds, linseeds, poppy seeds, gold of pleasure seeds and mustard seeds, the risk assessment values for enforcement residue definition were multiplied by a robust CF of 2 and were used as input values.

For barley, oat, rye and wheat grain, the Article 12 confirmatory data gap was considered addressed, but the tentative MRL is based on Codex MRL, and therefore, the CFs as confirmed under the present assessment (CF of 1 for rye and wheat grain and a CF of 2 for barley and oat grain (see Section 1.2.1.5)) were applied to the STMR values corresponding to the Codex MRLs.

For onions and shallots, the Article 12 confirmatory data were considered not addressed, and therefore for these commodities, risk assessment values corresponding to a lower MRL as proposed in previous EFSA output (EFSA, 2023a) were used in the risk assessment.

Broccoli, cauliflowers, head cabbages and dry pulses were excluded from the exposure assessment in the absence of residue data according to the risk assessment residue definition (Article 12 confirmatory data gap not addressed) or a robust CF.

For rapeseeds and sunflower seeds, the input values were as derived in the previous EFSA outputs for rapeseeds (EFSA, 2020) and sunflower seeds (EFSA, 2015a). For sunflower seeds, data according to the risk assessment residue definition were not available, and therefore, a CF of 1 was applied to the STMR value derived according to the enforcement residue definition. For rapeseed, the STMR value as derived for the risk assessment residue definition was available and was used as an input value.

For animal commodities, the risk assessment values supporting the existing EU MRLs and derived by the JMPR (FAO/WHO, 2010, 2018) were used as input values, in liver and kidney multiplied by a conversion factor of 2 and 4, respectively, to account for the presence of conjugates (see Section 2.2.) EFSA notes that, in 2021, the JMPR proposed to lower some animal commodity Codex MRLs, but these values have not been taken over in the EU MRL legislation due to reservations expressed by the EU in the 53rd session of the CCPR (FAO/WHO, 2022). EFSA notes that the risk assessment values derived for the EU livestock dietary burden with the present assessment are lower.

Crops for which no uses were reported in the MRL review or subsequent assessments were excluded from the calculation. The summary of the input values is provided in Appendix D.2.

The short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment did not exceed the ARfD for any of the crops on which prothioconazole uses are authorised or intended with the previous EFSA outputs. Under assumptions that conclusions from previous EFSA assessments on the modification of MRLs for prothioconazole are implemented, the highest estimated exposure for raw agricultural commodities under consideration accounted for 31.7% of the ARfD for carrots. The highest estimated exposure for processed commodities (from the crops under consideration) accounted for 25% of the ARfD for boiled turnips and boiled parsnips (see Appendix B4).

Regarding the chronic dietary risk assessment, no long‐term consumer intake concerns were identified for any of the diets included in the EFSA PRIMo rev 3.1. This conclusion is valid under assumptions that conclusions from previous EFSA assessments on the modification of MRLs for prothioconazole are implemented. The estimated maximum long‐term dietary intake accounted for 18% of the ADI (NL toddler diet). The highest individual contribution of residues in the commodities under assessment was generally low with the highest contribution calculated for wheat with a contribution of 1.45% of the ADI (GEMS/Food G06 diet). For the remaining crops under assessment, the contribution was below 1% of the ADI. No intake concerns were identified in relation to residues in animal commodities.

EFSA concluded that the long‐term and short‐term intake of residues of prothioconazole‐desthio resulting from the existing uses or intended uses with previous EFSA outputs is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health. It is, however, noted that for two metabolites included in the existing risk assessment residue definitions (prothioconazole‐desthio‐4‐hydroxy (M15) and prothioconazole‐desthio‐6‐hydroxy (M17), the genotoxicity potential remains not addressed according to the latest EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval (EFSA, 2025), and therefore, the exposure calculation is considered provisional.

The results of the calculations are summarised in Appendix D.2. For further details on the exposure calculations, screenshots of the Report sheet of the PRIMo are presented in Appendix C.

Indicative exposure assessment

During the Member State consultation, some views were expressed that crops for which the Article 12 confirmatory data were considered as not addressed by EFSA in the present assessment ‐ head cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, dry peas, beans and lupins – shall be included in the consumer exposure calculation, using the STMR and HR value expressed according to the residue definition for enforcement, multiplied by the conversion factor of 2. It was argued that such calculation would be sufficiently conservative to determine the contribution of residues in these crops to the acute and chronic exposure.

EFSA therefore performed an indicative consumer exposure scenario where, in addition to considerations outlined in the section above, head cabbage, dry pulses, cauliflower and broccoli were included. For these crops, the input values were as derived for enforcement residue definition in the MRL review (EFSA, 2014) or JMPR (FAO/WHO, 2010) and multiplied by a CF of 2 (as provisionally derived by the peer review on the renewal of approval) (EFSA, 2025). The indicative exposure calculation did not indicate significant changes to the chronic exposure, which accounts for 18% of the ADI (NL toddler diet). The highest acute exposure from residues in the crops under consideration was calculated for children's diets and was at 53.1% of ARfD for head cabbages, being lower for other crops.

The EMS, France, proposed that on the basis of low contribution of these crops to the chronic consumer exposure and the tentative margin of safety for the acute exposure, the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps could be considered addressed. EFSA is of the contrasting opinion, and it is therefore left for risk management decision whether the argument of the indicative margin of safety obtained with the tentative CF of 2 is acceptable to waive the need to submit the Article 12 confirmatory data.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The current assessment was carried out as a follow‐up to the confirmatory data evaluation performed after the Article 12 MRL review. Accordingly, the assessment focused on crops with tentative MRLs established after the Article 12 MRL review, for which the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps were not considered addressed in subsequent EFSA outputs.

The conversion factors were derived only in cases when the respective EU GAP was either fully supported by a set of minimum number of compliant residue trials or when a substantial residue data set was provided allowing to conclude on the occurrence of prothioconazole metabolites for the authorised GAP. The extrapolation of CFs among supported uses was considered acceptable only if in line with the EU extrapolation rules.

Furthermore, for reasons of completeness and considering the wide range of supporting data provided, EFSA also revised conversion factors for those crops for which the Article 12 confirmatory data have been considered addressed in previous EFSA outputs (root crops, oilseeds) and where the available residue data support the reported authorised GAPs.

The evaluation followed the data requirements applicable at the time of the original MRL application and did not consider new uses of prothioconazole; therefore, TDM occurrence, processing studies and residues in rotational crops were not reassessed. Residue trials submitted for other crops were considered supportive only, as they could not address Article 12 data gaps in the absence of GAP‐compliant trials on the respective crop or acceptable extrapolation.

The data submitted in support of this MRL application were found sufficient to confirm the tentative MRLs and address the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps in leek, Brussels sprouts, barley, oat, wheat and rye grain and to confirm the tentative MRLs in commodities of animal origin (associated to a data gap on grass).

No data were submitted to address the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps in dry pulses (beans, peas, lentils and lupins/lupini seeds), head cabbage and flowering brassica or to derive reliable conversion factors. Therefore, for these commodities, the tentative MRLs cannot be confirmed, and risk managers may consider lowering these MRLs to the enforcement LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

The indicative dietary burden and consumer exposure calculation including these commodities, as requested by some Member States during the MS consultation, did not affect the calculated exposures when residues expressed for enforcement residue definition together with a tentative CF of 2 were used. The EMS France proposes that, on the basis of the indicative exposure results for these crops, the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps could be considered addressed. EFSA is of the contrasting opinion, and it is therefore left for risk management decision whether the argument of the margin of safety obtained with the tentative CF of 2 is acceptable to waive the need to submit the Article 12 confirmatory data.

No data were submitted to address the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps in onions and shallots since the residue trials submitted in this application support a less critical GAP. However, since a less critical GAP, fully supported by residue trials, has been assessed by EFSA in 2023 – with an MRL proposal of 0.02 mg/kg – the risk managers may consider lowering the existing EU MRL of 0.05 mg/kg to 0.02 mg/kg.

The EU livestock dietary burden was updated with new residue data available for barley, oat, rye and wheat straw, maize forage, turnip tops and grass. Results indicate that lower EU MRLs would be sufficient in liver, kidney and edible offal of ruminants, swine and poultry and in the fat of ruminants and swine. However, the existing EU MRLs are currently set on the basis of Codex MRLs, derived by the JMPR in 2017; these Codex MRLs have been further revised by JMPR in 2021, but not implemented in EU MRL legislation due to EU reservations noted for advancement of these MRLs in the 53rd Session of the CCPR, pending the outcome of the EU pesticides peer review and that a risk assessment for TDMs has not been carried out (FAO/WHO, 2022). Thus, a risk management consideration is required whether the existing EU MRLs for animal commodities should be revised on the basis of updated EU dietary burden calculation.

Consumer exposure calculation was updated with the data available under the present assessment. EFSA concluded that the existing EU uses of prothioconazole or intended uses from previous EFSA outputs will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference values and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health.

EFSA further notes that all conclusions derived under the present assessment are considered provisional and might need to be reconsidered in the light of the outcome of the EU pesticides peer review on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole, once the decision on the renewal of approval of prothioconazole (and related new endpoints) is implemented by the European Commission and pending further data to address the genotoxic potential of M15, M17, M33 and M35.

Finally, it should be noted that the derived conversion factors are not to be used to waive a need for a full set of GAP compliant residue trials with samples analysed according to the residue definition for the risk assessment. The CFs which are derived under the present assessment support specific use patterns on crops concerned and might be considered for application in enforcement situations.

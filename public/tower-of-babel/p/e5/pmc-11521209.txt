Nongenetic Risk Factors of Severe Hypospadias: A Case–Control Study
Authors: Vita Indriasari, Rizki Diposarosa, Yoni Fuadah Syukriani, Dedi Rachmadi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Various risk factors were contributed to hypospadias and may be varied by region and hypospadias severity. The study aims to analyze the nongenetic risk factors associated with severe hypospadias in a provincial referral hospital in West Java, Indonesia.Methods:A case–control study was conducted in hypospadias patients and boys with normal penis treated in our institution during 6-month period. Risk factors of hypospadias were compared between severe and mild hypospadias and the control group and analyzed with Chi-square/Fisher exact test and multivariate logistic regression analysis (P < 0.05 = significant).Results:We studied 84 subjects consist of 46 hypospadias patients (severe: 30 and mild: 16) and 38 control groups. Maternal workplace, primipara, age >35 years old, overweight, the absence of nausea, hypertension, bleeding, alcohol, cigarette smoking, medication, and vegetarian during pregnancy, as well as prematurity, were not significantly different between groups. The use of hormonal contraception and low birth weight infants were significantly higher in severe hypospadias (P = 0.033; P = 0.023; respectively). Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that hormonal contraception, primipara, and age >35 years old were the risk factors of severe hypospadias (P = 0.008; P = 0.003, P = 0.049, respectively).Conclusions:Hormonal contraception, primipara, and maternal age >35 years old are among the nongenetic risk factors of severe hypospadias in West Java.

INTRODUCTION

Hypospadias results from the failure of the urethral closure of the male fetus which causes a ventral urethral opening. Exogenous endocrine-disrupting chemicals, estrogen-like compounds, contraceptives, diet, lifestyle, and maternal environment are known as nongenetic factors that play a role in this process.[12]

Risk factors of hypospadias may be different by region, ethnic characteristics, and severity of hypospadias. In our institution, most patients are Sundanese and live in urban and agricultural areas. Patients’ characteristics are also different from the Western studies, in which most of them had more severe types of hypospadias.[3] The aim of this study is to analyze the nongenetic risk factors associated with severe hypospadias.

METHODS

This study was conducted in a tertiary hospital in West Java Province, Indonesia. Pediatric patients diagnosed with hypospadias undergoing surgical repair during January to June 2023 were included in the study. The control group consists of boys with normal penises who were planned for circumcision. We did consecutive sampling of hypospadias patients and control group. Sample size calculation for logistic regression analysis was obtained, with a minimal sample size of 55 with around 80% power of analysis.

A case–control design was used to evaluate the nongenetic risk factors of severe hypospadias. Severe hypospadias was assessed by physical examination with Glans–Meatus–Shaft Score of more than 6.[4] Nongenetic risk factors were recorded, including maternal workplace associated with pesticides or chemical exposure, hormonal contraception, primipara, maternal age >35 years old, overweight mother (body mass index >25 kg/m2), the absence of nausea in early pregnancy, hypertension, bleeding, alcohol, cigarette smoking, medication (valproate, loperamide, and paroxetine), vegetarian during pregnancy, prematurity, and low birth weight (LBW) infant.

Association between the risk factors and hypospadias severity and the incidence of severe hypospadias was analyzed using Chi-square or alternatively Fisher’s exact test. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was also used to test the risk factors of severe hypospadias, P < 0.05 was considered significant.

RESULTS

During the study period, 84 patients were evaluated. Of these, 46 were hypospadias patients classified as severe (30 cases) and mild hypospadias (16 cases); and 38 were boys with normal penis.

Table 1 shows the results of bivariate analysis of the association between nongenetic risk factors and various degrees of hypospadias and normal penis. Maternal use of hormonal contraception was more frequent in severe hypospadias compared to mild cases and controls (60% vs. 25% and 34.2%, P = 0.033). The history of LBW infants was also higher in the severe type (33.3% vs. 31.3% vs. 7.9%, P = 0.023).

The results showed that maternal hormonal contraception (P = 0.008), primipara (P = 0.003), and maternal age >35 years old (P = 0.049) were significant risks of severe hypospadias.

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, this is one of the few studies specifically evaluating the relationship between nongenetic risk factors and the severity of hypospadias, especially in Indonesia. In this study period, the number of severe hypospadias patients in our institution was more than the mild type. This is in line with previous study at the same hospital, where the characteristics of our hypospadias patients had a worse penile biometry profile with a more severe degree of hypospadias.[3]

The process of urethral closure is known to be responsive to the nongenetic risk factor. Agricultural pesticides may act as endocrine disruptors in developing fetuses, thus results in hypospadias.[2] Previous studies found a significant link between the occurrence of hypospadias and the residence of pregnant mothers close to vineyards.[5] However, in this study, only 2 mothers of hypospadias patients worked in the agricultural field. This signifies that this factor was not associated with the severity of hypospadias.

Hormonal contraceptives are high in estrogen.[6] In this study, its use was higher and was a significant risk for severe hypospadias. This finding differs from the results of a study by Nørgaard et al., in which oral contraceptives were not associated with an increased risk of hypospadias.[7] Medication of valproate,[8] loperamide,[9] and paroxetine[10] may confer an increased risk of hypospadias. However, in this study, we did not find any of this drug consumption by the mothers.

In this study, primiparity and age >35 years old were not significantly different between severe and mild hypospadias and control groups. However, further multivariate analysis to control the confounding effect of variables showed that primipara and maternal age >35 years old were the significant risk factors for severe hypospadias. Maternal primiparity and age older than 35 years are known to be associated with an increased risk of hypospadias among offspring, especially when combined in presentation. Increased risk of hypospadias in primiparity may be related to a disturbed androgen/estrogen balance. Older mothers would potentially have longer exposure to endocrine disruptors than younger mothers and thus a greater risk of hypospadias.[2] A population-based study has noted an increase in organochlorine pesticide concentration in adipose tissue with increasing age.[11] In contrast to the results of this study, studies by Wang et al. found that multiple pregnancies increase the risk of hypospadias due to placental insufficiency which plays a role in the pathogenesis of this disorder.[12]

Maternal lifestyle during pregnancy may affect the risk of having son with hypospadias. Maternal overweight and obesity may cause high concentrations of circulating estrogen and increased the risk of hypospadias in male infants. Due to its anti-estrogenic effect, smoking during pregnancy was associated lower occurrence of hypospadias. However, this finding is still debatable. Vegetarian diet in pregnant women has been associated with a higher risk of hypospadias, perhaps due to greater exposure to phytoestrogens or ingestion of pesticides and herbicides.[2] Our data were not supporting the association of these factors with the occurrence of severe hypospadias. Cigarette smoking, drinking alcohol, and vegetarian are not the common lifestyles of mothers live in West Java. However, we did not evaluate the maternal habit of eating raw vegetables specifically, as this practice is common in West Java people.

Maternal hypertension during pregnancy, preterm delivery, and LBW infants are also associated with hypospadias.[13] This has been attributes to in utero growth retardation. Placental dysfunction may lower chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) production and impair testicular Leydig cells to produce testosterone.[14] This will affect the external genitalia differentiation. Nausea in early pregnancy is believed to be caused by the early surge of hCG. First-trimester nausea is considered to be a healthy sign of pregnancy and is associated with lower risks of miscarriage and premature birth. In contrast, the absence of nausea may reflect in low hCG level. Some studies reported the absence of nausea in early pregnancy which is associated with increased hypospadias risk. In this study, LBW infants were more frequently found in severe hypospadias. This finding supports Giordano et al. study about the strong association of LBW and proximal hypospadias.[15]

This study has several limitations. It is difficult to establish direct association between nongenetic risk factors and severe hypospadias as the effect of genetics may interfere. In this study, the proportion of severe and mild hypospadias was not equal. This was due to a lower incidence of mild hypospadias in our patients. Data regarding the risk factors were collected from medical records and obtained at the time of hospital admission for surgery, not during the neonatal period, and may increase the possibility of parents forget about the perinatal history. Certain data such as assessments of placenta function in the early stage of pregnancy, paternal factors, and endocrine disruptor exposure at the mothers’ residence were not available. Further study may require a more comprehensive design to provide deep insight into the complex etiology of hypospadias.

CONCLUSIONS

Maternal use of hormonal contraception, primipara, and maternal age >35 years old are among the nongenetic risk factors of severe hypospadias in West Java.

The application effect of the segmented teaching method in training medical students on clinical practice skills
Authors: Chao Liu, Mei Ren, Chaoen Luo, Khan Asfandyar, Huijie Dai, Jinxin Yang, Zhengwen Lei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe surgical clinical practice skill training is an important part of medical undergraduate education. Surgical skills are complex and difficult to master. However, the traditional teaching method has some disadvantages, for example, low student participation, weak learning atmosphere, inadequate mastery of the subject matter. We innovatively put forward the segmented teaching method in the field of clinical practice skills training. The segmented teaching method refers to dividing a specific teaching content into several relatively independent parts and providing segmented practice for each part. This approach enhances students’ learning outcomes.The aim of this controlled study is to investigate the effects of the segmented teaching methods on clinical practice skills training in medical students.Methods31 medical students participating in clinical practice skill training at The First Affiliated Hospital of University of South China from March to April 2024 were randomly assigned to either the experimental group (n = 16) receiving segmented teaching method or the control group (n = 15) receiving traditional teaching method. After completion of practical exercises, both groups underwent operational assessments, theoretical assessments, Mini-Clinical Evaluation Exercise (Mini-CEX), and Student Evaluation of Educational Quality (SEEQ) for teaching quality evaluation. A comparative analysis of the results between the two groups was conducted.ResultsAfter implementing the program, there were no statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) between the experimental and control groups in theoretical assessment scores, Mini-CEX clinical judgment scores, and SEEQ teaching quality evaluation scores. However, compared to the control group, the experimental group demonstrated better operational assessment scores, higher Mini-CEX scores in history taking, physical examination, professionalism, doctor-patient communication, organizational efficiency, and comprehensive abilities, with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05).ConclusionThe application of segmented teaching method in clinical practice skill training for medical students yields favorable training outcomes.Keywords: Segmented teaching method, Teaching approach, Medical students, Surgical clinical practice skill training, Application effect

Background

Clinical practice ability refers to the capability of clinical healthcare professionals to apply their specialised knowledge and skills in diagnosing, treating, and providing humane care for patients in a clinical setting.Current medical education demands that medical students not only possess a solid theoretical foundation but also demonstrate proficient clinical practice abilities to adapt to the practical nature of medicine. In recent years, due to advancements and shifts in medical technology and practices, medical universities have increasingly emphasized the cultivation and enhancement of clinical practice abilities among medical students [1]. Teaching clinical skills is an important component of educational programs for medical undergraduates [2]. Surgical skills are an important part of clinical practice skills, medical schools are responsible for teaching and ensuring proficiency of basic surgical skills [3]. However, the transition from learning theoretical medical knowledge to mastering clinical practice abilities often requires a structured process. Thus, the training of medical students’ clinical practice abilities is crucial. Various training methods have emerged, but their reported effectiveness varies [4–7].

Traditional teaching methods refer to a teacher-centred approach that involves unidirectional knowledge transmission in the classroom through lectures and slide presentations. Although discussions and question-and-answer sessions may sometimes be included, students passively receive information during the teaching process, lacking opportunities for interaction and practical application. This is a general term for one of the most common Chinese teaching methods. In this teaching model, students primarily rely on textbooks and class notes to learn, which allows them to grasp theoretical knowledge to some extent; however, they often lack experience and confidence in practical operations and clinical decision-making, making it difficult to apply theoretical knowledge in real clinical practice. It is necessary to design better and more efficient teaching methods. For example, some scholars designed integrated problem-based learning (PBL) combined with lecture-based classroom (LBC) teaching in undergraduate medical clinical skills education [1]. Kweki AG et al.have applied simulation training-based teaching methods to cardiovascular medicine skill training, further improving students’ operational skills and clinical decision-making abilities [8]. In addition, Yang B et al. have proposed practice-based learning and improvement (PBLI) methods for clinical skills training of general practitioners, aiming to enhance students’ practical abilities through practical operations [9].

Although these teaching methods have improved educational effectiveness to some extent, they still have certain limitations. Problem-Based Learning (PBL) is time-consuming and resource-intensive, requires high levels of professional competence from teachers, and suffers from uneven student participation. Simulation-based teaching methods face issues of high costs and a lack of real-life scenarios. Additionally, Practice-Based Learning and Improvement (PBLI) is constrained by limited opportunities for practice, significant individual differences, and challenges in assessment. Overall, current educational innovations continue to face challenges regarding resources, costs, practical opportunities, and evaluation.

To address these issues, we propose a segmented teaching method that combines phased theoretical and practical training, aiming to overcome the shortcomings of current approaches and enhance the clinical skills of medical students.Segmented teaching method is a teaching strategy that divides a specific teaching content and process into several stages or parts. This method focuses on gradually advancing student learning by breaking down larger teaching objectives into smaller, more manageable units. Each unit or stage has its specific learning objectives, content, activities, and evaluations. This decomposition aims to make the learning process clearer and more systematic, while also facilitating teachers to monitor and guide students’ learning progress.Surgical practice operation process is relatively complex, the steps are relatively cumbersome, the basic surgery requirements are high, more urgent need for innovative teaching methods is required. However, there are not a lot of reports on the segmented teaching methods of surgical clinical practice skills training, each training modality expressed unique strengths and limitations, with limited data reported on the educational impact connected to each training tool [10].We innovatively propose segmented teaching method it is a teaching strategy that divides a specific teaching content and process into several stages or parts. This method focuses on gradually advancing student learning by breaking down larger teaching objectives into smaller, more manageable units. Each unit or stage has its specific learning objectives, content, activities, and evaluations. This decomposition aims to make the learning process clearer and more systematic, while also facilitating teachers to monitor and guide students’ learning progress.

The difference between segmented teaching method and traditional teaching method: Segmented teaching method is student-centered, which involves theoretical segmentation, operational segmentation, segmented timing, segmented evaluation, and integrated reinforcement of a certain operational project. It is gradual and emphasizes key points, making it easy for students to understand and master knowledge. Traditional teaching methods, on the other hand, are teacher centered, rigid, straightforward, and lack emphasis, making it difficult for students to understand and master knowledge.

The author applies segmented teaching to medical students’ clinical skills training to enhance their clinical practice abilities.

Methods

Study subjects

31 medical students from the First Affiliated Hospital of University of South China participating in clinical practice skill training in March-April 2024 were selected as research subjects. Among them were 31 fifth-year clinical medicine students from the 2020 cohort (10 males, 21 females). They were randomly divided into an experimental group and a control group. The research team consisted of four members, including one teaching instructor (a senior attending physician in orthopedics), one assessment instructor (a senior attending physician in surgery), and two assessment assistants (master’s students in the hospital). Ethical approval for this project was obtained from the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of University of South China.

Textbooks

The textbooks “Chinese Medical Student Clinical Skills Operation Guidelines (3rd Edition)” (People’s Health Publishing House) and “Surgery (9th Edition)” (People’s Health Publishing House) were selected as the unified teaching materials. The above textbooks are instructional materials, and theoretical and operational knowledge are taught according to the contents of the textbooks.

Teaching methods

Both groups of students underwent pre-training operational and theoretical assessments after receiving 12 h of “general surgery operation” training during pre-internship reinforcement training. We give students pre-training operational assessment (hundred-mark system) and theoretical assessment (hundred-mark system). General surgery operation training entails basic surgical procedures, including surgical asepsis, sterilization and draping, dressing changes and suture removal, debridement and suturing, as well as ex vivo small intestine resection and anastomosis.

The operation of “debridement and suturing” follows a set process, including cleaning, suturing, and other basic surgical tasks like using sterile items, disinfecting, laying sterile sheets, anesthetizing, and suturing. This common and typical surgical procedure represents broader surgical skills and is essential in clinical practice. We used the segmented teaching method to initially train medical students in these clinical practice skills. A training framework and flowchart were developed (Fig. 1). Both groups of medical students received multimedia lectures and model operation demonstrations from the same surgical instructor (a senior attending physician in the hospital) for two sessions, followed by guided practice sessions for six sessions. The control group received traditional teaching methods: the theory and operation steps were explained according to the content of the textbook, and the operation demonstration and subsequent operation exercises were guided according to the steps.In practice, the overall time from the start to the end of the operation and the comprehensive feedback at the end of the operation were recorded.While the experimental group received segmented teaching methods. In the segmented teaching method, the theoretical knowledge and operation steps were divided into three stages: pre-operation, during operation, and post-operation. Pre operation stage: refers to the stage from the beginning of preparation of items to the start of surgery after determining that the patient needs to undergo debridement and suturing surgery, including doctor-patient communication, informed consent, privacy protection, preparation of medical personnel (wearing masks, hats, washing hands), and preparation of equipment and items. In operation stage: refers to the general term for the stage from the beginning of surgery to the completion of suturing and covering with dressings, including positioning, disinfection, laying sheets, anesthesia, wound cleaning, debridement, exploration, suturing, etc.The wound referred to in this text is a small debridement and suture wound with a 4 cm incision designed on the model, requiring four stitches. The suturing method used is simple interrupted suturing.Post operation stage: refers to the stage from covering with dressings to the end of all operations, including restoring the patient’s position, explaining precautions (measuring blood pressure, anti-inflammatory, anti-tetanus, changing dressing, removing stitches, and restoring function), and sorting and disposing of medical waste. Then the theoretical knowledge was conducted in stages, the surgery demonstration was conducted in stages, and the follow-up surgery practice was guided. The operation time of each stage was recorded during practice, and the feedbacks were made in stages, and the corresponding strengthening was carried out in regards to the weak stage. Only after passing the process and operation time can the next stage of operational practice be entered. After each stage meets the standards, integrated reinforcement training will be conducted.Both groups received the same teaching resources, including textbooks, instructors, hours of instruction, and theoretical and surgical assessment questions.

Assessment methods

After completing the practice sessions, both groups of medical students underwent post-training operational assessments (percentage-based), post-training theoretical assessments (percentage-based), and Mini-Clinical Evaluation Exercise (Mini-CEX) assessments of their practical operation abilities. Mini-CEX assessments utilized a 7-item scale to evaluate aspects such as history taking, physical examination, professionalism, clinical judgment, doctor-patient communication, organizational efficiency, and comprehensive abilities. Scores were based on a 3-level, 9-point scoring system. A score of 1–3 is below expectations, 4–6 is in line with expectations, and 7–9 is beyond expectations [11]. Additionally, both groups were given the Student Evaluation of Educational Quality (SEEQ) questionnaire for satisfaction surveys, with a 100% response rate. Statistical analysis was conducted on the operational and theoretical assessment scores, Mini-CEX scores, and SEEQ scores between the two groups to evaluate training effectiveness [12, 13].

Statistical methods

Data were analyzed using SPSS 26 statistical software. Continuous data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (X ± S). For normally distributed data with homogeneity of variance, paired-sample t-tests were used for within-group comparisons, and independent-sample t-tests were used for between-group comparisons. For data with inhomogeneity of variance, corrected t-tests were employed. Categorical data were presented as percentages (%), and inter-group comparisons were conducted using chi-square tests, corrected chi-square tests, or Fisher’s exact probability tests. A significance level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of groups in age, gender, and University grade point average(GPA)

The experimental group comprised 16 students (7 males, 9 females) with an age range of 20 to 22 years and an average age of (21.31 ± 0.60) years. Their university GPA ranged from 2.86 to 4.11, with an average of (3.80 ± 0.28). The control group consisted of 15 students (3 males, 12 females) with an age range of 21 to 22 years and an average age of (21.40 ± 0.51) years. Their academic GPA ranged from 3.66 to 4.18, with an average of (3.87 ± 0.14). There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in terms of age, gender, or academic GPA (P > 0.05).

Comparison of pre-training theoretical and operational assessments

The experimental group scored (45.22 ± 13.60) points in the pre-training operational assessment, while the control group scored (52.77 ± 9.50) points. The difference in pre-training operational assessment scores between the two groups was not statistically significant (P = 0.086). The experimental group scored (79.28 ± 10.62) points in the pre-training theoretical assessment, while the control group scored (73.50 ± 11.44) points. The difference in pre-training theoretical assessment scores between the two groups was not statistically significant (P = 0.155) (Table 1). Both groups showed comparable performance in theoretical and operational assessment scores. Discussions

Medical education aims to cultivate physicians who contribute to the overall health of the population, making it a top priority both domestically and internationally. Medicine is a highly practical discipline, requiring students to possess the ability to identify and solve problems. The acquisition of clinical skills by medical students is an essential part of medical education. Clinical skills training is an effective means of acquiring these skills, and the quality of such training plays a crucial role in the future clinical practice of medical students [14]. Clinical practice skills training for medical students can further consolidate the medical knowledge learned in the classroom, help to transform the role of students to doctors, and comprehensively cultivate the independent thinking ability and clinical thinking ability of medical students [1].

The selection of appropriate training methods is very important to improve the clinical skills of medical students, and the student’s demand for clinical practice skills teaching is also increasing [6].Innovative teaching methods, such as problem-based learning (PBL) and simulation-based teaching (SBT) [2, 15], are used in clinical practice teaching. Gong J et al. applied team-based learning (TBL) to the bedside clinical practice teaching of pediatrics of traditional Chinese medicine, which not only improved the clinical practice skills of medical students, but also improved the clinical thinking and consulting abilities of medical students [14]. Team-Based Learning (TBL) is a new active learning paradigm based on problem-solving, introduced in 2002, that preserves the educational advantages of Problem-Based Learning (PBL) in a more efficient manner. Both TBL and the segmented teaching method share a student-centered focus, emphasize the mastery of practical skills by students, and prioritize student feedback. These pre-existing methods have inspired and guided our proposal for segmented teaching.

In our study, both groups of medical students underwent intensive pre-internship training and received training in “General Surgery Operations” before the practice, and pre-training assessments were conducted to evaluate both theoretical and practical competencies. In the assessment of operation steps, fluency and clarity of thought, the number of students who did not meet the standards was large. It was observed that traditional teaching methods faced challenges such as students’ lack of proficiency in operational steps, lack of fluency in procedures, and unclear overall understanding, hindering their mastery of practical skills. Hence, there is a pressing need to design better and more efficient teaching methods [9]. To address the aforementioned challenges, we propose a segmented teaching approach.

The segmented teaching method can be used in the teaching of other subjects: Huang P and other scholars combined the targeted teaching method and the stepped assessment method in the open tennis class of colleges and universities, which can improve the basic tennis skills of students, as well as their learning interest and motivation [16]. Wong H M et al. proposed a phased teaching ward round and pointed out that an effective bedside teaching ward round should be divided into three stages: preparation, teaching and evaluation [17]. Some scholars also pointed out that four-stage teaching is used in practical skill teaching, which divides the skill teaching process into four stages: demonstration, deconstruction, conception and performance. However, it is too complicated, cost-effective and time-efficient [18]. We simplified it into three stages: before operation, during operation and after operation, which were easy to understand and implement. We applied it to clinical practice skills training for the first time. It has the characteristics of strong pertinence, prominent focus, concentrated on key problems, and students are good at understanding and mastering. The segmented teaching method trains medical students through five parts: theoretical segmentation, segmented training, timed segments, segmented reviews, and integrated reinforcement. The assessment results are satisfactory, and students are very fond of this new teaching method. This aligns with the goal of fostering active learning, long-term memory, and analytical thinking in medical students, enhancing their understanding of theoretical and practical knowledge, promoting student initiative and participation, and facilitating reflection and understanding of knowledge.

Debridement and suturing is an important technique for surgeons, and it is also a basic surgical procedure.Emphasizing debridement can reduce wound infections and even eliminate the need for antibiotics; Emphasizing suture techniques can reduce scar formation and alleviate post-traumatic sequelae. By learning and practicing this basic surgical operation, medical students can master correct operating skills, tool usage, and basic wound management abilities. This not only provides necessary basic training at the technical level, but also has profound significance and influence in team cooperation, patient safety, professional development, and educational evaluation.

In this study, the scores of “debridement and suture” in the experimental group were higher than those in the control group, and the difference was statistically significant (P < 0.05), indicating that the segmented method in clinical practice skills training is easier for medical students to understand and master the relevant skills, and the training effect is better. However, because the difficulties of debridement and suture mainly involved in the operation part, there was no significant difference in the theoretical examination scores between the two groups before and after the training (P > 0.05), and there was no significant difference in the theoretical examination scores between the two groups after the training (P > 0.05). At the same time, Mini-cex is introduced into clinical teaching evaluation as a formative evaluation tool, which can give feedback on students’ performance in clinical practice and ultimately promote and develop their clinical skills[11].In terms of Mini-cex score, the score of Mini-cex in the experimental group was higher than that in the control group in the six aspects of history collection, physical examination, professional quality, doctor-patient communication, organizational effectiveness and comprehensive ability, and the differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05), indicating that the segmented teaching method in clinical practice skills training can make the overall process of operation more fluent and more skilled. The operation assessment mainly involved the overall operation process and operation proficiency, and involved relatively little clinical thinking. Therefore, there was no statistically significant difference in the scores of Mini-cex in the aspect of clinical judgment between the two groups (P > 0.05). There was no significant difference in the scores of the teaching quality assessment scale for teachers and training content between the two groups (P > 0.05), indicating that the two groups received the same teaching resources, including textbooks, teachers, class hours and theoretical and operational examination questions, which were comparable.

Segmented teaching method is a teaching strategy that divides a specific teaching content and process into several stages or parts. This method focuses on gradually advancing student learning by breaking down larger teaching objectives into smaller, more manageable and manageable units. Each unit or stage has its specific learning objectives, content, activities, and evaluations. This decomposition aims to make the learning process clearer and more systematic, while also facilitating teachers to monitor and guide students’ learning progress. In the operational training, the specific implementation of the segmented teaching method is reflected in the following aspects: (1) Theoretical segmentation: Before operation: Indications and contraindications for operation; Patient preparation, doctor preparation, item preparation, and environment preparation. In operation: surgical procedure for debridement and suturing; Key points for debridement of different wounds; Exploration of tendons, blood vessels, nerves, and repair of bones; Intraoperative precautions (such as handling anesthesia accidents, allergic shock, and massive bleeding). After operation: patient’s humanistic care, body position recovery, postoperative precautions (blood pressure measurement, anti-inflammatory, anti-tetanus, dressing change, suture removal and functional recovery, medical waste sorting, etc.), observation and treatment of complications (wound infection and treatment). (2) Segmented training: The pre operation stage includes: doctor-patient communication, informed consent, privacy protection, preparation of medical personnel (wearing masks, hats, washing hands), and preparation of equipment and items. During the operation phase: including positioning, disinfection, single placement, anesthesia, wound cleaning, debridement, exploration, suturing, etc. Post operation stage: including restoring the patient’s position, explaining precautions (measuring blood pressure, anti-inflammatory, anti-tetanus, changing dressing, removing stitches, and restoring function), and sorting and disposing of medical waste. (3) Segmented timing: Timing the operations of three stages separately. (4) Segmented review: Time the operations of the three stages separately. (5) Integrated reinforcement: Through segmented training, segmented timing, and segmented evaluation results, identify the weak links of the students in the three stages for reinforcement training, and finally conduct complete fusion training. Using the segmented instructional method, both teachers and students enjoyed a harmonious and relaxed atmosphere during the one-month clinical skills training. The students gained a lot from the experience. We will continue to follow up on the long-term training outcomes.

However, our study has several limitations, such as a small sample size, single-center design, and the exclusion of internal medicine procedures. Future research should consider these limitations and further explore the impact of segmented teaching methods on subjective outcomes such as students’ confidence, attitudes, and communication skills.

Conclusion

The research demonstrates that applying a segmented teaching approach to the training process of medical students in the clinical practice skill of “debridement and suture” can enhance their clinical practice abilities and achieve favorable training outcomes. This indicates that segmented teaching holds significant practical value in this particular clinical skills training. Future research should aim to explore the optimal conditions and strategies for implementing segmented teaching methods in medical education and investigate their long-term outcomes and impact on resident physicians in society.

Optimization of hinge domain enhances antitumor efficacy of globo H-targeted CAR-T cells in solid tumor models
Authors: Shance Li, Huimin Xie, Yuge Zhu, Jiaxin Tu, Xinyu Li, Guanyu Zhang, You He, Liying Xing, Zheming Lu, Hongchao Xiong, Chaoting Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryChimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy has shown remarkable efficacy in hematologic malignancies but remains limited in solid tumors. Globo-H, a glycosphingolipid overexpressed in epithelial cancers with restricted normal expression, is an attractive target. Globo-H-targeted vaccines and antibody-drug conjugates have demonstrated safety and antitumor activity. Although Globo-H CAR-T cells have been tested with different costimulatory domains, the impact of hinge configuration on CAR-T function remains largely unexplored. Here, we compare CD8α, CD28, and IgG4 hinges within a 4-1BB-based CAR framework. CD8α-hinge CAR-T cells exhibited superior activation, cytokine release, and cytotoxicity against Globo-H+ tumor cells in vitro. In xenograft models of renal and ovarian cancer, CD8α-hinge CAR-T cells significantly inhibited tumor growth, with enhanced expansion and infiltration into blood and tumor tissues. These findings support Globo-H as a viable target for CAR-T therapy in solid tumors and highlight the CD8α hinge as an optimal component for Globo-H CAR design.Keywords: globo-H, CAR-T, solid tumor

Introduction

Chimeric antigen receptor T cell (CAR-T) therapy has shown significant success in hematological cancers.1 However, its application in solid tumors remains limited due to challenges such as antigen heterogeneity, immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments, and on-target off-tumor toxicities.2,3 Thus, identifying suitable tumor-associated antigens that are selectively expressed on cancer cells is critical for advancing CAR-T therapy for solid tumors.4,5

Globo-H, a glycosphingolipid antigen, is overexpressed in a variety of epithelial cancers, including breast, lung, ovarian, and prostate cancers, while demonstrating minimal expression in normal tissues.6 This selective expression has made Globo-H an attractive target for cancer immunotherapy. Previous studies with Globo-H-targeted vaccines and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) have demonstrated promising safety profiles and antitumor activity,7,8,9 indicating the feasibility of targeting this antigen for cancer treatment. CAR-T cells targeting Globo-H might provide more durable and robust anti-tumor efficacy compared with vaccines or ADCs with shorter half-life or potential drug resistance.10 Preclinical studies have evaluated Globo-H-targeted CAR-T cells, such as the anti-Globo-H CARs described by Putti et al. (ESMO Open, 2025)11 and the OBI-R007 candidate reported at AACR 2023,12 which focused primarily on costimulatory domain selection (CD28 vs. 4-1BB) and demonstrated antigen-specific cytotoxicity and in vivo antitumor efficacy. However, the role of hinge domains in modulating CAR-T function, including proliferation, memory differentiation, and signaling dynamics, remains largely unexplored. Because hinge architecture can influence synapse formation and CAR signaling, empirical comparison is essential. Accordingly, in this study, we systematically evaluate three commonly used hinge domains—CD8α, CD28, and IgG4—within a 4-1BB-based CAR framework targeting Globo-H and found its appreciable antitumor efficacy through extensive in vitro and in vivo experiments. These findings support Globo-H as a candidate antigen to target with CAR-T cells for the treatment of solid tumors.

Results

To identify the most efficacious Globo-H-targeting CAR construct for potential clinical application, we generated three second-generation CARs differing solely in their hinge regions: CD8α hinge (CD8H), CD28 hinge (CD28H), and IgG4 hinge (IgG4H), while keeping all other domains—including the scFv, CD28 transmembrane region, and 4-1BB/CD3ζ intracellular domains—identical (Figure 1A; Figure S1).

We first evaluated the in vitro functional properties of each CAR-T cell construct using two Globo-H-positive human tumor cell lines: A498 (renal carcinoma) and OVCAR-3 (ovarian carcinoma). CAR-T cells were co-cultured with these tumor cells, and multiple functional readouts were assessed to compare their activation and cytotoxic profiles. Upon stimulation with either target cell line, CD8H CAR-T cells produced significantly higher levels of interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) than CD28H and IgG4H CAR-T cells, as measured by ELISA (Figures 1B and 1C). In addition, flow cytometry analysis demonstrated that CD8H CAR-T cells expressed markedly elevated levels of the early activation marker CD69 and the co-stimulatory receptor CD137 compared to the other constructs, indicating stronger and more sustained activation (Figures 1D–1G).

To assess degranulation, we measured the surface expression of CD107a on CAR-T cells following tumor cell co-culture. CD8H CAR-T cells displayed significantly greater CD107a expression relative to the CD28H and IgG4H counterparts, suggesting enhanced cytotoxic granule release capacity (Figures 1H and 1I). We next performed flow cytometry-based cytotoxicity assays, which confirmed that CD8H CAR-T cells exhibited the highest target cell killing efficiency against both A498 and OVCAR-3 cells. In contrast, mock-transduced T cells had negligible cytotoxic activity (Figures 1J and 1K). Taken together, these results indicate that the CD8H hinge enhances CAR expression and functionality, leading to superior effector activity in vitro.

Given the superior in vitro performance of CD8H CAR-T cells, we proceeded to evaluate their antitumor efficacy in vivo using subcutaneous xenograft models of A498 and OVCAR-3 in NSG mice. Tumor-bearing mice received a single intravenous injection of 3–5 × 106 CD8H CAR-T cells or mock T cells three days after tumor inoculation (Figures 2A and 2B). In the A498 model, CD8H CAR-T cells significantly inhibited tumor progression, as demonstrated by reduced tumor volumes over time and lower tumor weights at endpoint compared to the mock T group (Figures 2C and 2D).

In the OVCAR-3 model, CD8H CAR-T treatment also led to marked tumor growth suppression (Figures 2E and 2F). To further assess in vivo CAR-T cell dynamics and functionality, we analyzed peripheral blood and tumor tissues by flow cytometry. In peripheral blood, mice treated with CD8H CAR-T cells exhibited a significantly higher proportion of T cells, along with increased frequencies of CD137+ and CD107a+ subsets, indicating robust systemic expansion and activation (Figures 2G–2I). In tumor tissues, CD8H CAR-T cells also accumulated at substantially higher levels than in controls. Notably, these intratumoral CAR-T cells retained functional activity, as evidenced by elevated expression of CD137 and CD107a compared to mock T cells (Figures 2J–2L). These results demonstrate that CD8H CAR-T cells not only effectively trafficked to the tumor site but also maintained an activated, cytotoxic phenotype within the tumor microenvironment.

Consistent with these findings, immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis confirmed markedly increased T cell infiltration in tumors treated with CD8H CAR-T cells. Compared to tumors from mock T cell-treated mice, which showed sparse T cell presence, CD8H CAR-T-treated tumors exhibited dense infiltration of CD3+ T cells throughout the tumor parenchyma (Figures 2M–2O). This histological evidence further supports the superior intratumoral accumulation and activity of CD8H CAR-T cells.

To explore the underlying mechanisms, we performed repeated antigen stimulation assays to model chronic antigen exposure, a key challenge in solid tumor settings. CAR-T cells were sequentially co-cultured with target tumor cells on days 0, 5, and 10, followed by functional and phenotypic analyses after each stimulation. As shown in Figure S3B, CD8 hinge CAR-T cells consistently maintained a significantly higher proportion of stem cell memory T cells (Tscm) after each round of stimulation compared with CD28 and IgG4 hinge variants. Preservation of the Tscm compartment is closely associated with enhanced self-renewal capacity and long-term functional durability. In parallel, analysis of proliferative capacity revealed that, following the third antigen encounter, CD8 hinge CAR-T cells exhibited significantly higher Ki-67 expression relative to the other hinge configurations, indicating superior proliferative potential under sustained antigen challenge. Functionally, CD8 hinge CAR-T cells demonstrated consistently enhanced cytotoxic activity against target tumor cells after each stimulation cycle, aligning with the observed advantages in proliferative and memory phenotypes. Finally, exhaustion marker analysis showed that after three rounds of stimulation, CD8 hinge CAR-T cells expressed the lowest levels of PD-1 and TIM-3 among all groups, suggesting reduced functional exhaustion during chronic antigen exposure (Figure S3).

Collectively, these results demonstrate that hinge engineering, particularly using the CD8α hinge, enhances the antitumor potency of Globo-H-specific CAR-T cells by promoting stronger activation, greater cytotoxic function, and improved in vivo persistence and infiltration.

Discussion

CAR-T cell therapy has achieved remarkable clinical success in hematological malignancies; however, its application to solid tumors remains challenging due to the lack of ideal tumor-associated antigens, limited T cell persistence, and suboptimal CAR signaling within the complex tumor microenvironment.13 Identifying tumor-selective antigens and optimizing CAR architecture are therefore critical steps toward improving CAR-T efficacy in solid tumors. Globo-H, a tumor-associated glycosphingolipid abundantly expressed in multiple epithelial cancers but minimally detected in normal adult tissues, has emerged as an attractive immunotherapeutic target.6 Previous clinical studies using Globo-H-targeted vaccines and antibody-based therapies have demonstrated favorable safety profiles, supporting its translational potential.7,8,9

In this study, we developed Globo-H-specific CAR-T cells and systematically investigated how hinge domain selection influences CAR-T cell function, while keeping the antigen-binding domain, costimulatory domain (4-1BB), and CD3ζ signaling module constant. Our central hypothesis was that hinge architecture represents a critical yet underexplored determinant of CAR-T performance in solid tumors, particularly for glycolipid antigens such as Globo-H, where epitope accessibility and synapse geometry may differ from protein antigens. The data presented here strongly support this hypothesis.

Our results demonstrate that CAR-T cells incorporating a CD8α hinge consistently outperformed CD28 and IgG4 hinge variants across multiple functional dimensions, including antigen-specific cytotoxicity, cytokine secretion, activation marker expression, and in vivo tumor control. Importantly, this advantage was not limited to short-term assays. Through repeated antigen stimulation experiments designed to model chronic antigen exposure—a key feature of solid tumors—we found that CD8α hinge CAR-T cells maintained superior cytotoxic capacity across successive stimulations. These cells also exhibited enhanced proliferative potential, as evidenced by increased Ki-67 expression, and preserved a higher proportion of stem cell memory T cells (Tscm), a population closely associated with self-renewal, long-term persistence, and durable antitumor responses.

These findings align with and extend prior studies showing that CAR structural elements beyond the scFv and costimulatory domains can profoundly shape CAR-T fate and function. While the influence of costimulatory domains such as CD28 and 4-1BB on CAR-T differentiation and persistence has been extensively characterized, the role of hinge domains has remained comparatively less defined and appears to be highly context dependent. Emerging evidence suggests that hinge length and flexibility can modulate immunological synapse formation, signaling strength, and exhaustion kinetics. Our study adds to this body of work by demonstrating that, in the specific context of Globo-H targeting, the CD8α hinge provides a favorable balance that supports sustained signaling without promoting premature differentiation or exhaustion.

Notably, although the overall transduction efficiency of our CAR constructs was modest, robust antigen-specific cytotoxicity and in vivo tumor control were consistently observed. This observation is consistent with prior reports indicating that CAR-T populations with relatively low CAR expression can still mediate potent antitumor effects, particularly when CAR signaling dynamics favor durability over rapid terminal differentiation.14 Future optimization of vector design, promoter selection, or manufacturing conditions may further enhance transduction efficiency without altering the intrinsic functional advantages conferred by hinge architecture.

In summary, this work provides a systematic evaluation of hinge domain selection in Globo-H-targeted CAR-T cells and identifies the CD8α hinge as a superior structural component for enhancing CAR-T function and durability. By demonstrating that hinge architecture critically influences CAR-T performance under repeated antigen challenge and in vivo conditions, our study underscores the importance of empirical CAR design optimization for solid tumor immunotherapy. These findings support further preclinical development of Globo-H-directed CAR-T cells and provide a rational framework for refining CAR structural elements to improve therapeutic efficacy in solid tumors.

Limitations of the study

Several limitations of this study warrant discussion. First, while our in vivo experiments demonstrate superior antitumor activity of CD8α hinge CAR-T cells across multiple xenograft models, these systems do not fully recapitulate the complexity of human solid tumors. Establishment of orthotopic or immunocompetent models will be important to further assess CAR-T trafficking, persistence, and safety in more physiologically relevant settings. Second, although our phenotypic analyses support a model in which the CD8α hinge promotes favorable CAR-T differentiation and proliferative capacity, future studies incorporating direct measurements of CAR signaling intensity, synapse organization, and metabolic fitness will be valuable to further dissect the underlying mechanisms.

Experimental model and study participant details

Cell culture

The human renal cell carcinoma cell line A498 and ovarian adenocarcinoma cell line OVCAR-3 were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). A498 cells were maintained in high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), while OVCAR-3 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 (Sigma-Aldrich) supplemented with 10% FBS and 0.01 mg/mL insulin. HEK293FT cells (ATCC), used for lentivirus production, were cultured in DMEM (Sigma-Aldrich) supplemented with 10% FBS, GlutaMAX, sodium pyruvate, and MEM non-essential amino acids. All cell lines were maintained at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. All cell lines were authenticated by their respective providers using short tandem repeat (STR) profiling and tested for mycoplasma within the past three years. Additionally, prior to each use, cells were routinely tested for mycoplasma and re-authenticated by STR profiling in our laboratory to avoid contamination.

Xenogeneic mouse models

All animal studies were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Beijing Cancer Hospital (Beijing, China). Female NOD/SCID mice (6–8 weeks old; HFK Bioscience, China) were housed under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions. Xenograft tumors were established by subcutaneous (s.c.) injection of 1 × 106 A498 or 2.5×106 OVCAR-3 cells in 200 μL PBS: Matrigel (1:1) suspension. Mice were randomized into treatment groups on day 3 post-inoculation and intravenously injected with 3×106 or 5×106 CAR-T or mock T cells. Tumor size was measured using digital calipers, and tumor volume was calculated as (length × width2)/2. Peripheral blood was collected via retro-orbital bleeding and processed for flow cytometry to quantify circulating T cells on day 8 post-infusion. At study endpoint, tumors were harvested, enzymatically dissociated into single-cell suspensions, and analyzed for tumor-infiltrating CAR-T cells via flow cytometry.

Method details

Generation of anti-Globo H CAR constructs

Lentiviral vectors were constructed by inserting a single-chain variable fragment (scFv) derived from the anti–Globo H monoclonal antibody OBI-8887 into the pCDH vector. The scFv was fused with one of three hinge regions (CD8α, CD28, or IgG4), followed by a CD28 transmembrane domain and intracellular signaling domains from 4-1BB and CD3ζ. All CAR constructs were cloned into the pCDH lentiviral backbone and sequence-verified as previously described.

Lentivirus packaging and T cell transduction

Primary T cells were isolated from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and activated for 48 h in T cell culture medium containing 50 ng/mL anti-CD3 (OKT3) and 1 μg/mL anti-CD28 antibodies. Lentiviral particles were generated by co-transfecting HEK293FT cells with the CAR construct and packaging plasmids using PEI MAX 40,000 (Polysciences, USA). Supernatants were harvested at 48- and 72-h post-transfection, filtered through a 0.45 μm membrane (Millipore), and concentrated by ultracentrifugation, as previously delineated. Activated T cells were transduced with concentrated lentivirus in the presence of 8 μg/mL polybrene. Transduction efficiency was assessed 48 h later via flow cytometric analysis of FLAG expression.

Flow cytometry and cell staining

Single-cell suspensions were washed with cold PBS containing 1% BSA and stained with fluorophore-conjugated antibodies for 30 min at 4°C in the dark. The following antibodies were used: anti-CD3-BV510 (UCHT1) and anti-FLAG-FITC (M2) for transduction efficiency; anti-CD69-APC (FN50) and anti-CD137-BV421 (4B4-1) for activation status (all from BD Horizon, Franklin Lakes, NJ). For CD107a degranulation assays, 1×105 CAR-T cells were co-cultured with A498 or OVCAR-3 target cells at an effector-to-target (E:T) ratio of 2:1 in a 96-well U-bottom plate for 12 h without exogenous cytokines. PE-labeled anti-CD107a (H4A3) was added at the beginning of co-culture, followed by the addition of BD GolgiPlug (10 μg/mL) after 1 h. After an additional 11 h, cells were stained with anti-CD3 and anti-FLAG antibodies and analyzed using a BD FACSAria II flow cytometer. Data were processed with FlowJo v10 software.

Normalization of CAR expression for functional assays

To ensure fair functional comparisons among CAR-T cells with different hinge designs, CAR expression levels were normalized prior to all in vitro and in vivo functional assays. After determining CAR positivity by flow cytometry using FLAG staining, CAR-T cell products with higher transduction efficiencies were diluted with mock-transduced T cells to achieve comparable percentages of CAR+cells across different constructs. All functional assays were subsequently performed using these normalized CAR-T populations, with effector-to-target ratios calculated based on total T cell numbers.

IFN-γ enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

CAR-T and target tumor cells were co-cultured at an E:T ratio of 2:1 (2×105:1×105) for 18–24 h. Supernatants were collected and analyzed for human IFN-γ using a commercial ELISA kit (R&D Systems) following the manufacturer’s protocol.

Cytotoxicity assay

Target tumor cells were labeled with CFSE (BD Biosciences) at 37°C for 15 min, followed by co-culture with CAR-T cells at E:T ratios ranging from 0.5:1 to 4:1 for 4–6 h. After co-culture, propidium iodide (PI, 1 μg/mL; BD Biosciences) was added to assess cell death. Cells were analyzed on a BD Accuri C6 flow cytometer. Cytotoxicity was calculated as the percentage of CFSE+PI+ cells among total CFSE+ target cells.

Histological and immunohistochemical analyses

At study endpoint, tumor tissues were fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin, dehydrated in graded ethanol, cleared in xylene, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 5 μm thickness. Sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated, followed by standard hematoxylin and eosin (H&E; Servicebio) or immunohistochemical (IHC; ZSGB-BIO) staining procedures. Representative images were captured using an Olympus light microscope.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SEM from at least three independent experiments unless otherwise stated. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.5.0, FlowJo v10, and Adobe Illustrator 2020. Group comparisons were made using unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-tests or one-way/two-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test. Statistical significance was defined as follows: p < 0.05 (∗), p < 0.01 (∗∗), p < 0.001 (∗∗∗), and not significant (ns).

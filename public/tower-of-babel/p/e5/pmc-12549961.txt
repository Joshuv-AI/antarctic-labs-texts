Enhancing pumping unit diagnosis with similarity splicing data augmentation and wavelet denoising
Authors: Xinlong Tan, Shaohua Wang, Hongqiang Wu, Fei Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Dynamometer card-based fault diagnosis confronts critical challenges including imbalanced data distributions, noise contamination, and constrained generalization capacity. This paper proposes a novel diagnostic framework integrating similarity-guided data augmentation with wavelet-optimized lightweight neural networks to address these limitations. The methodology employs kinematic feature recombination for minority class expansion, resolving data imbalance through physically consistent sample generation. Concurrently, vertical load disparities, temporal load variations, and auxiliary operational parameters are fused into standardized dynamometer card representations, enhancing feature discriminability without architectural modifications. We integrate Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) layers into stride-2 inverted residual modules ofMobileNet-V2, strategically embedding spectral noise suppression during feature downsampling while preserving diagnostically critical low-frequency patterns. Experimental validation confirms the framework’s superior accuracy and noise robustness, achieving significant performance improvements over conventional approaches while maintaining computational efficiency. The proposed solution establishes a deployable framework for industrial fault diagnosis, balancing diagnostic precision with operational practicality in oilfield monitoring applications.

Introduction

Pumping units and oil wells are susceptible to a variety of common faults that have historically required manual identification through the integration of disparate data sources. This conventional approach not only demands substantial human labor but also necessitates extensive domain-specific expertise. The ground dynamometer card (DC), a graphical representation of the load-displacement relationship during the reciprocating motion of oil pumps, serves as an effective diagnostic tool for monitoring pump working conditions and oil well operational status1. Although DC-based fault diagnosis in pumping units is conventionally framed as a pattern recognition problem2, the accurate and efficient identification of DC patterns continues to present significant challenges for the oil and gas industry.

Recent advancements in deep learning have facilitated the proliferation of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in DC-based fault detection applications3,4. Pioneering work by Cheng et al.5 demonstrated the effectiveness of transfer learning with AlexNet6 combined with support vector machines (SVM) for automated feature extraction from DCs. Zhang et al.7 achieved notable improvements in model generalization and diagnostic accuracy through customized activation function optimization applied across five distinct neural architectures.

Contemporary research increasingly focuses on sophisticated network architectures, including DWR-VGG8,9 and modified ResNet10 structures, to enhance contour feature extraction capabilities. Yang et al.11 implemented architectural improvements to ResNet34 through depthwise separable convolution, significantly reducing parameter complexity while maintaining computational efficiency. Sreenivasan and Krishna12 developed a hybrid framework combining GoogleNet13 features with error-correcting output coding (ECOC) supervised learning, thereby improving classification robustness. Bing et al.14 proposed TTSSA-CNN, an optimized CNN architecture employing tunable tandem sparrow search algorithm (TTSSA) for hyperparameter optimization, which enhances both training efficiency and diagnostic precision.

The diagnostic performance of CNN-based models is particularly vulnerable to data quality degradation in noisy environments, where their sensitivity to input variations becomes notably pronounced. Three primary challenges hinder practical implementation: First, the inherent imbalance in fault occurrence probabilities results in skewed training data distributions. Second, significant pattern similarity across different fault categories complicates precise classification. Third, the inclusion of cross-well DC data introduces operation-characteristic-related noise, creating substantial domain shifts between training and target datasets. These factors collectively explain the observed performance discrepancy: models achieve superior accuracy when trained and tested on homogeneous data from single machines, but exhibit markedly reduced error tolerance when applied to heterogeneous data sources. To enable reliable field deployment, substantial optimization efforts remain necessary to enhance model generalization capabilities across diverse operational datasets.

Current research efforts have explored traditional image-flipping techniques to alleviate data imbalance challenges15–17. Nevertheless, the inherent physical-geometric correlations in DCs render conventional horizontal/vertical flipping operations counterproductive, as they distort intrinsic information patterns, introduce artificial artifacts, and ultimately compromise model generalization through overfitting. In response, Lv et al.18 established static apparent stiffness features with matched extraction algorithms to generate distribution-aligned training samples. A complementary approach by Wang19 presented a distribution-aware cost-sensitive active learning framework specifically designed for multi-class imbalance scenarios in long-tail datasets.

Domain-specific augmentation frameworks employing conditional generative adversarial networks (cGANs)20 have emerged to address cross-field distribution discrepancies and class imbalance simultaneously. Liu et al.21 pioneered a self-attention enhanced conditional DCGAN architecture for DC synthesis, while Hao et al.22 enhanced Wasserstein GAN-GP through Gaussian mixture noise injection to boost sample diversity. Despite these advancements, the prevailing reliance on complex generative architectures reveals persistent limitations in training stability and deployment feasibility. Even state-of-the-art diffusion-based methods like ParaDC23 remain constrained by their dependence on idealized theoretical assumptions during wave equation simulations, thereby limiting practical scalability.

Robust feature extraction constitutes a fundamental requirement for processing noisy DCs24–27. He et al.28 engineered four-dimensional time-frequency representations from displacement-load signals via a convolutional shrinking architecture (CSNN) that effectively suppresses low-frequency interference. Li et al.29 devised a dual-backbone fusion model integrating Fourier transform features with DC characteristics, surpassing conventional single-input approaches in classification performance. The recent emergence of Vision Transformers (ViTs) has inspired cross-modal frameworks30,31 that combine temporal-spectral information through dual-branch architectures with attention-based fusion mechanisms. Notwithstanding these achievements, current multi-branch implementations typically require computationally intensive parallel processing of heterogeneous features, resulting in parameter-intensive implementations with limited practical viability.

Recent studies have further explored multimodal and time-frequency representations for fault diagnosis. For example, a dual-input CNN combining acoustic emission and vibration signals32 demonstrated enhanced multimodal classification. Similarly, wavelet coherent analysis and S-transform scalograms integrated with CNN-KAN33 achieved strong performance in centrifugal pump diagnosis. While these methods highlight the benefits of multimodal and time-frequency approaches, our work differs by focusing on DC-specific augmentation and lightweight deployment.

Three critical limitations persist in existing methodologies: (1) Conventional augmentation techniques compromise DCs’ physical-geometric integrity, while GAN-based solutions suffer from inherent instability; (2) Current architectures lack adaptive mechanisms for noise suppression during feature extraction; 3) Accuracy gains from multi-branch designs come at prohibitive computational costs that hinder real-world deployment.

Similar challenges regarding noise robustness and computational efficiency are actively addressed in related industrial fault diagnosis domains, such as pipeline leak detection. Recent advancements demonstrate the efficacy of tailored signal processing combined with efficient deep learning architectures. For instance, researchers have employed enhanced time-frequency representations (scalograms) processed through Gaussian/Laplacian filters alongside CNN/autoencoder fusion for improved leak identification under noise34, while others have leveraged adaptive wavelet decomposition (EWT) integrated with customized lightweight DenseNet variants for highly accurate and computationally efficient leak detection and sizing35. These approaches highlight the potential of integrating signal enhancement techniques with streamlined network designs to tackle noise sensitivity and deployment constraints.

However, directly translating such methods to DC-based pumping unit diagnosis faces unique obstacles. The distinct physical-geometric correlations inherent in DCs impose stricter constraints on augmentation and feature manipulation than pipeline AE signals. Furthermore, the complex pattern similarities and domain shifts specific to oil well operations necessitate specialized solutions. To overcome these limitations while drawing inspiration from the success of noise-robust, efficient frameworks in related fields, this research introduces three key innovations:

Physics-Consistent Similarity Splicing: A geometrically invariant augmentation technique that swaps homologous stroke segments to preserve physical correlations while enhancing data diversity.

Unified Motion-Feature Fusion: An integrated preprocessing framework converting raw DCs into standardized motion-feature maps through single-image transformation, drastically reducing model complexity.

Wavelet-Enhanced MobileNet-V2: A lightweight hybrid architecture combining wavelet decomposition with depthwise separable convolutions to enable noise-robust feature learning from low-frequency components.

Compared with prior industrial fault diagnosis studies that primarily address vibration or acoustic modalities, our work specifically tackles the unique challenges of dynamometer card (DC) data, where stroke-phase morphology and physical consistency are critical. We introduce a similarity-guided splicing augmentation method tailored for DC continuity, a motion-feature fusion embedding adapted to DC-specific operational parameters, and a lightweight WMobileNet-V2 framework integrated with wavelet denoising. This combination addresses the dual constraints of small-sample imbalance and field-deployable efficiency, differentiating our approach from existing CNN- or Transformer-based fault diagnosis studies.

Theoretical dynamometer card analysis

The theoretical dynamometer card (DC) presents a characteristic closed-loop geometry resembling a quasi-parallelogram configuration (green trace in Fig. 1), representing the pumping unit’s idealized operational profile. However, under actual operating conditions, the DC undergoes characteristic deformations due to non-ideal mechanical interactions including frictional losses, inertial effects, and gas interference (red trace in Fig. 1). The complete pumping cycle comprises two distinct phases: upstroke (ABC) and downstroke (CDA), each containing sub-operational stages with unique mechanical signatures.

Initiated by motor-driven rotation transmitted through the gearbox-crank mechanism, the upstroke phase (ABC sequence) features tow mechanical regimes, loading regime (AB) and stabilization regime (BC). an unloading stage (BC). In the loading regime, polished rod ascension induces sucker rod elongation and plunger elevation, concurrently decreasing pump barrel pressure to activate standing valve opening for fluid intake. This process manifests as progressive load escalation on the DC. Upon achieving maximum rod load, the system maintains constant tension during continued ascension, producing a quasi-horizontal trajectory in the DC until stroke reversal.Fig. 1Theoretical dynamometer card.

Theoretical dynamometer card.

The downstroke also consists of an unloading regime (CD) and a re-equilibration regime (DA). In the unloading regime, gravity-assisted rod descent coupled with motor drive initiates plunger retraction, concurrently increasing pump barrel pressure to trigger traveling valve activation for fluid discharge. This transition appears as gradual load reduction on the DC. Minimum load conditions stabilize during sustained descent, generating a second quasi-horizontal DC segment preceding cycle repetition.

While load-displacement measurements alone prove insufficient for distinguishing loading/unloading transitions, displacement extrema provide reliable indicators of phase reversal points. To optimize feature extraction, this study adopts a simplified two-phase DC representation (ABC upstroke vs. CDA downstroke), effectively capturing essential mechanical states while reducing computational complexity.

Dataset

The benchmark dataset, derived from the nationwide industrial benchmark dataset of the National Industrial Internet Innovation Competition, contains 11-dimensional operational parameters: (1) ID; (2) Timestamp; (3) Displacement; (4) Load; (5) Daily liquid yield; (6) Daily gas production; (7) Maximum casing pressure; (8) Maximum tubing pressure; (9) Back pressure; (10) Stroke count; (11) Fault type. Displacement-load pairs form DC trajectories for motion feature extraction, while production parameters (items 5-9) provide supplementary system-level context. The training set comprises 9,880 samples with comprehensive coverage of operational scenarios: normal operations, sensor faults, 15 single-fault modes, and 6 compound-failure patterns. An independent test set contains 3,213 samples maintaining equivalent category distributions but collected from divergent pumping units.Fig. 2Distribution of fault types in training and testing sets.

Distribution of fault types in training and testing sets.

As shown in Fig. 2, three critical data challenges emerge: (1) Severe class imbalance stemming from divergent fault occurrence frequencies; (2) Cross-machine domain shifts due to heterogeneous data sources; (3) Significant distribution discrepancy between training and test sets. Conventional evaluation protocols that randomly split homogeneous machine data achieve inflated accuracy metrics but fail to reflect real-world applicability across heterogeneous field deployments.

Dynamometer card standardization

Raw DCs exhibit parameter-induced geometric variance across pumping units as visualized in Fig. 3. Two normalization challenges are identified: 1) Intra-class positional shifts: Divergent displacement-load ranges within identical fault categories cause trajectory translation in feature space; 2) Inter-class load inconsistency: Distinctive load amplitude patterns across different fault types create feature space misalignment.

Model design

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) demonstrate pronounced sensitivity to input perturbations, where minor image distortions—common in dynamometer card diagnostics—can critically degrade model performance. Two primary noise sources compromise diagnostic reliability in this context: interpolation artifacts generated during displacement-load data conversion to images introduce contour distortions, while domain shifts between training and testing data distributions inject inference-phase noise, collectively undermining the model’s generalization capacity.

To address these challenges while balancing accuracy and computational efficiency, this work adopts the lightweight MobileNet-V2 architecture as the foundational framework. Its inherent design advantages, including linear bottleneck layers and inverted residual modules, enable efficient depth scaling for real-time inference. However, the baseline architecture’s susceptibility to high-frequency noise necessitates enhancement for robust DC analysis.

The experimental outcomes confirm three pivotal advancements: First, the similarity-guided augmentation strategy effectively mitigates class imbalance through physically consistent sample synthesis, as demonstrated by the 8.52% accuracy boost in WResNet18. Second, the unified feature fusion framework enhances diagnostic specificity through coordinated learning of geometric patterns and operational parameters, evidenced by DenseNet121’s 6.78% improvement. Third, wavelet-enhanced architectures achieve optimal noise immunity by prioritizing low-frequency energy components during feature extraction, with WMobileNet-V2 achieving 86.33% accuracy despite operating on noisy field data.

The proposed WMobileNet-V2 demonstrates distinct advantages over conventional architectures: Its spectral filtering mechanism reduces high-frequency noise susceptibility by 63% compared to baseline models, while the optimized MobileNet-V2 backbone enables 22% faster inference speeds than ResNet18 counterparts. These characteristics position it as an optimal solution for edge-deployed oilfield diagnostics, where accuracy, computational efficiency, and noise robustness must be simultaneously addressed.

Conclusions

This study presents an integrated framework for pumping unit fault diagnosis, combining similarity-based data augmentation, physics-informed feature fusion, and wavelet-enhanced lightweight neural networks. The methodology addresses critical challenges in industrial diagnostics–specifically, imbalanced fault distributions and noise-contaminated dynamometer card data–through three synergistic innovations.

The similarity-guided augmentation strategy resolves data imbalance by generating synthetic samples through controlled recombination of kinematically analogous DC segments, expanding minority fault representations while preserving physical consistency. Concurrently, the feature fusion architecture enriches diagnostic information by embedding motion-derived parameters (vertical load differences, load variations, acceleration) and operational metrics (production rates, pressure extremes) into standardized DC images. This dual-channel encoding preserves native geometric signatures while supplementing contextual system-level data, creating a comprehensive input representation for neural networks.

The wavelet-optimized WMobilenet-V2 architecture introduces Discrete Wavelet Transform layers into MobileNet-V2’s inverted residual modules, achieving spectral noise suppression during downsampling. By decomposing feature maps into frequency components and retaining diagnostically critical low-frequency elements, the network develops enhanced resistance to interpolation artifacts and domain-shift noise. Experimental validation confirms these improvements, with WMobilenet-V2 achieving 86.33% test accuracy—a 6.16% enhancement over baseline performance—while maintaining computational efficiency through its compact 3.4M parameter footprint.

Future directions emerge from current limitations and industrial requirements. First, temporal feature integration could enhance diagnostic precision by capturing dynamic fault evolution patterns across operational cycles. Second, architectural adaptations could extend the wavelet denoising framework to other convolutional networks, potentially unlocking deeper performance optimizations. Third, rigorous field validation under diverse production scenarios remains essential to verify model robustness against real-world variabilities, including multiphase flow effects and sensor drift.

In practical terms, the proposed methodology establishes a new paradigm for oilfield equipment monitoring, balancing diagnostic accuracy with edge-deployment feasibility. By maintaining native DC interpretability while leveraging data-driven pattern recognition, it bridges the gap between conventional engineering analysis and modern machine learning, offering a viable pathway for intelligent maintenance systems in the energy sector.

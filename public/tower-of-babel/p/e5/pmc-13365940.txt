IFPHOR0031 Working with Community Settings in North Somerset to Promote Oral Health
Authors: Catherine Wheatley
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundReduced oral health has implications for a person’s overall health and mental wellbeing. In England it is the responsibility of local authorities to improve the oral health of their populations. North Somerset Council began workforce training four years ago with early years professionals and has expanded into other sectors since.Aimto establish a programme that embeds oral health promotion into day-to-day work of key community settings.MethodsUsing a Health in All Policies approach to connect with, train, and support settings, to help them embed small changes that create oral-health-supportive environments, integrating oral health strategies into policies and practices across multiple sectors.ResultsTraining has been delivered to over 600 professionals from around 70 settings, including Family Hubs, libraries, Youth Justice Service, refugee resettlement, smoking cessation team, foster carers, care-leaver support and homelessness support. As the learning turns into action, settings have been supported to integrate oral health into their policies and routine practices. Reflecting on previous successful oral health promotion strategies has been shown to provide valuable insights into shaping future policy and practice. Strategies have included: writing oral health modules into induction and ongoing staff training schedules; ensuring oral health messaging is a regular part of service delivery; including prompts about oral health in support planning; creating oral-healthy food policy; and self-funding supervised toothbrushing.ConclusionThe North Somerset oral health promotion programme demonstrates the potential for workforce-wide training to support community settings to deliver sustainable improvements in population oral health.

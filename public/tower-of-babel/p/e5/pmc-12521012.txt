Anthracycline selection in drug-eluting-beads-transarterial chemoembolization (DEB-TACE) for hepatocellular carcinoma: emerging evidence for idarubicin
Authors: Mina S. Makary
Published: 2025

The management of intermediate-stage hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) continues to evolve, yet transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) remains a cornerstone of therapy for patients with Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage B disease (1,2). Despite its widespread adoption, the optimal choice of chemotherapeutic agent in TACE—especially with drug-eluting bead (DEB) platforms—has long remained controversial (3,4). The recent phase IV randomized controlled trial by Liu et al. provides pivotal evidence comparing idarubicin and epirubicin in DEB-TACE, positioning idarubicin as a potentially superior agent in terms of efficacy without compromising safety (5).

This multicenter study randomized 239 patients with BCLC-B HCC across four centers in China to receive DEB-TACE with either idarubicin or epirubicin. The results showed a significantly longer progression-free survival (PFS) in the idarubicin group (10.8 vs. 8.7 months; hazard ratio: 0.61, P=0.002) and improved overall survival (OS) at 24 months (71.8% vs. 54.0%) (5). Notably, the objective response rate (ORR) was also higher with idarubicin (70.8% vs. 57.1%, P=0.03), without an associated increase in treatment-related adverse events, which remained comparable between groups (5). These results not only underscore the therapeutic potential of idarubicin in DEB-TACE but also challenge the current dominance of epirubicin as the anthracycline of choice.

From a methodological standpoint, the study by Liu et al. demonstrates commendable rigor, but a detailed critical appraisal reveals both strengths and areas for caution. The inclusion criteria were appropriately defined: patients had to have BCLC stage B HCC, be ineligible for curative treatment, and maintain adequate hepatic reserve (Child-Pugh A or B7) with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–1. Exclusion criteria included prior systemic therapy or TACE, extrahepatic spread, and major vascular invasion. Randomization was computer-generated in a 1:1 ratio using a stratified block design, and although the allocation process was centralized, the study was open-label due to the visual differences in idarubicin and epirubicin, precluding blinding of both operators and patients. The lack of blinding introduces a risk of performance and detection bias, particularly in subjective endpoints, although this was mitigated by independent, blinded central imaging review for tumor response assessment. The sample size (N=239) was determined a priori with adequate power to detect a significant difference in the primary endpoint—PFS—and was sufficient to support the trial’s main conclusions. The statistical methods, including Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, Cox proportional hazards modeling, and subgroup stratification, were appropriate and transparently reported.

Historically, the use of anthracyclines in TACE has been driven more by availability and toxicity profiles than by robust comparative data. Epirubicin, a stereoisomer of doxorubicin, is widely favored for its more tolerable side-effect profile, though its anti-HCC efficacy remains modest (6-8). Moreover, doxorubicin itself has not been shown to significantly improve OS in randomized trials (9). In contrast, idarubicin—a synthetic anthracycline originally developed for hematologic malignancies—has recently emerged as a promising alternative due to its superior lipophilicity, sustained half-life (through its active metabolite idarubicinol), and ability to overcome P-glycoprotein–mediated drug resistance (10-12). Preclinical and early-phase studies have suggested improved tumoricidal activity when idarubicin is incorporated into TACE regimens (13,14), and the present trial marks the first prospective head-to-head clinical validation of these hypotheses in a randomized setting.

The superior efficacy of idarubicin in DEB-TACE may be explained by several pharmacodynamic advantages over other anthracyclines. First, idarubicin exhibits significantly higher lipophilicity than epirubicin and doxorubicin, which facilitates its diffusion through cellular membranes and enhances tissue penetration, leading to more effective drug accumulation within tumor cells (10,11). Second, its active metabolite, idarubicinol, has a longer plasma half-life and persists within hepatic tissue, maintaining cytotoxic pressure on tumor cells for extended periods (11,12). Third, idarubicin has been shown to evade P-glycoprotein–mediated efflux pumps, a mechanism commonly implicated in chemoresistance among HCC cells—thereby sustaining intracellular drug concentrations and therapeutic activity even in resistant tumors (12,13). In preclinical studies, idarubicin demonstrated greater cytotoxicity against HCC cell lines compared to doxorubicin and epirubicin, particularly under hypoxic and acidic conditions characteristic of tumor microenvironments (10,13). Additionally, early-phase clinical trials such as IDASPHERE I and II have shown that idarubicin-loaded microspheres yield favorable pharmacokinetics and potent local tumor responses in unresectable HCC (14,15). These findings suggest that the physicochemical properties and metabolic profile of idarubicin may underlie its superior efficacy, and they support its use as a rational alternative to conventional anthracyclines in TACE platforms.

Liu et al.’s trial demonstrated excellent scientific rigor. Central radiologic review using modified RECIST (mRECIST) criteria, intention-to-treat efficacy analysis, and appropriate powering for primary endpoint detection reflect high trial quality. The study’s consistency with existing DEB-TACE data also enhances external validity. The median PFS in the epirubicin arm (8.7 months) aligns with prior DEB-TACE benchmarks reported in the TACE 2 (7.8 months) and PRECISION ITALIA (9.0 months) trials (15,16), both of which utilized doxorubicin, supporting the reliability of the comparator arm and reinforcing the credibility of the observed efficacy advantage for idarubicin.

Additional studies further corroborate the therapeutic potential of idarubicin in HCC. Preclinical models have consistently demonstrated greater cytotoxicity of idarubicin over other anthracyclines, supported by enhanced lipophilicity and its ability to bypass P-glycoprotein-mediated drug resistance mechanisms (10,12). Early-phase trials using idarubicin-loaded microspheres have reported favorable pharmacokinetics and local drug retention with minimal systemic exposure, aligning with observed clinical efficacy (14). Notably, a recent real-world cohort study comparing DEB-TACE with idarubicin versus epirubicin found improved PFS and ORRs with idarubicin, without increased toxicity—reinforcing the findings of Liu et al. in a non-trial setting (17). Collectively, these findings support a growing consensus that idarubicin may offer pharmacologic and clinical advantages over traditional anthracyclines in TACE for HCC.

From a safety perspective, both agents were well tolerated, without treatment-related mortality and a low incidence of grade ≥3 adverse events. The side-effect profile—largely consisting of postembolization syndrome and mild hematologic abnormalities—did not significantly differ between the groups. Importantly, the idarubicin dose used in this trial was considerably lower than systemic leukemia regimens, minimizing the risk of cardiac and myelosuppressive toxicity (5,18). This finding suggests that idarubicin retains its antitumor potency even at reduced systemic exposure, likely due to favorable tumor pharmacokinetics and lipophilic accumulation in neoplastic tissue.

Notably, subgroup analyses demonstrated a consistent PFS benefit with idarubicin across key variables—tumor size, nodularity, α-fetoprotein levels, and liver function—supporting its broad therapeutic applicability. However, as the trial cohort was predominantly hepatitis B virus (HBV)–infected, caution is warranted in extrapolating these findings to populations where hepatitis C virus (HCV) or nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)/nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) are more prevalent (19). These etiologies differ in tumor biology, immune microenvironment, and susceptibility to TACE-related liver injury, potentially influencing treatment response. As such, future studies are needed to validate idarubicin’s efficacy across diverse patient populations and etiologic backgrounds.

Several implications stem from this work. First, idarubicin now warrants serious consideration as a preferred anthracycline in DEB-TACE protocols for BCLC-B HCC. Second, the pharmacologic properties that differentiate idarubicin from its counterparts—such as prolonged tumor exposure, high intratumoral concentration, and resistance to drug efflux—may render it a superior backbone for future combination therapies involving systemic agents. For example, combining idarubicin DEB-TACE with immune checkpoint inhibitors or anti-VEGF therapies could enhance locoregional tumor control while amplifying systemic immunologic responses, as suggested in emerging combination paradigms for HCC (20,21).

The study does have limitations. The open-label design introduces potential for procedural bias, and the inability to blind operators due to color differences in the agents is a known barrier in interventional oncology trials. Moreover, while the investigators selected DEB-TACE for standardization, results may not be generalizable to conventional TACE with iodized oil, which is still commonly employed in many regions. Although prior real-world studies have demonstrated idarubicin’s promise in both DEB and conventional platforms (17,22), confirmatory trials across modalities are needed. Additional limitations include the potential for geographic and etiologic bias due to a HBV-dominant Chinese cohort, lack of quality-of-life assessment, and exclusion of patients with advanced liver dysfunction (Child-Pugh B8 or higher), which limits generalizability. Lastly, the cost implications of switching from epirubicin to idarubicin in TACE are also relevant and currently unknown. Although the clinical benefit is evident, pharmacoeconomic analyses would be valuable in determining broader healthcare system feasibility, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where HCC burden is highest.

In conclusion, Liu et al. have delivered a significant contribution by providing the first randomized evidence that idarubicin outperforms epirubicin in DEB-TACE for intermediate-stage HCC. The study’s findings validate earlier pharmacologic and observational data and may influence clinical decision-making and guideline recommendations. Future studies should aim to replicate these findings across diverse patient populations, explore synergistic systemic combinations, and expand our understanding of the mechanisms by which idarubicin exerts its therapeutic advantage. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., atezolizumab or durvalumab) and anti-angiogenic agents (e.g., bevacizumab or lenvatinib) are logical partners for combination therapy, given that TACE induces immunogenic cell death and transient hypoxia-driven angiogenesis—both of which can be modulated by these agents (20,21). As the HCC treatment landscape rapidly evolves, optimizing the efficacy of locoregional therapies remains a critical priority—and idarubicin may well be the next cornerstone in this journey.

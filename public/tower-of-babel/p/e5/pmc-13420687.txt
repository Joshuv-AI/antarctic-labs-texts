Multimodality Cardiac Imaging and Multidisciplinary Collaboration in Monitoring Intracardiac Sarcoma Response to Immunotherapy
Authors: Christopher Kocx, Lucy Geraghty, Zara Rolfe, Hayden McColl, Cameron Holloway, Vivek Bhadri, Sameer Karve, Jia Liu, Imre Hunyor
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntracardiac metastases causing right ventricular outflow tract obstruction are rare and carry a dismal prognosis. History of Presentation

A 40-year-old man was referred to the cardiology service in January 2023 with progressive exertional dyspnea and a new pericardial effusion on staging computed tomography (CT). He had a complex oncological history significant for alveolar soft part sarcoma (ASPS) diagnosed in 2004, with subsequent pulmonary and cerebral metastases managed with surgery, systemic chemotherapy, and cranial radiation therapy. Restaging CT revealed intracardiac masses and a pericardial effusion, prompting cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging (Videos 1 and 2).

CMR confirmed 3 discrete intracardiac masses: the largest (72 × 50 × 43 mm) within the right ventricular (RV) outflow tract (RVOT), invading through the anterior RV wall; a second RV apical mass (60 × 37 × 29 mm) traversing the myocardium into the pericardial space; and a third intramyocardial mass (40 × 32 × 30 mm) in the basal-to-mid anterior left ventricular (LV) wall. Tissue characterization demonstrated a viable, vascularized tumor: isointense signal with heterogeneous hyperintensity on steady-state free precession imaging, mildly increased T2-weighted signal, perfusion hyperenhancement, and heterogeneous late gadolinium enhancement (LGE), distinguishing metastatic disease from thrombus without endomyocardial biopsy. The RV was severely dilated (indexed right ventricular end diastolic volume 151 mL/m2) with severely impaired systolic function (RV ejection fraction 19%), and there was a moderate circumferential pericardial effusion. Given hemodynamically significant RVOT obstruction, his prognosis was estimated at months.

After pericardiocentesis, palliative radiotherapy, and systemic therapy with a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, he presented 2 months later with symptomatic cardiac tamponade. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) confirmed a large mass adherent to the RV apex extending to obstruct the RVOT, a moderate pericardial effusion with tamponade physiology, preserved LV systolic function, and qualitatively poor RV function (Videos 3 and 4).

Past Medical History

Stage II ASPS of the left gluteal region was diagnosed in April 2004 and surgically resected. The patient received first-line doxorubicin/ifosfamide and second-line carboplatin/docetaxel for progressive pulmonary disease in 2005. He developed a left parietal lobe brain metastasis in 2016, managed with craniotomy, cranial radiation, and bilateral lung metastasectomy. Disease remained stable until January 2023 when restaging imaging revealed chest wall, pulmonary, and intracardiac progression. He was a former construction worker and lived regionally.

Differential Diagnosis

The differential diagnoses for multiple intracardiac masses included metastatic disease, primary cardiac tumor, thrombus, and infective vegetation. The clinical context of known metastatic ASPS, multiplicity of lesions, and RVOT involvement strongly favored metastatic disease. Perfusion hyperenhancement and heterogeneous LGE confirmed vascularized tissue, excluding thrombus. Combined with prior histopathological confirmation of Transcription Factor E3-positive ASPS from the cerebral metastasectomy specimen, CMR enabled a confident noninvasive diagnosis, obviating biopsy where procedural risk was prohibitive.

Management

Initial management prioritized palliation of RVOT obstruction and pericardial effusion. Pericardiocentesis was performed in January 2023 and palliative radiation delivered to the intracardiac masses (25 Gy in 15 fractions). Pazopanib was commenced as third-line systemic therapy in February 2023 but discontinued after 1 month when the patient was hospitalized with cardiac tamponade requiring repeat pericardiocentesis and management of rapid atrial fibrillation.

ASPS is increasingly recognized as an immunotherapy-sensitive histology; however, at the time of presentation, there was no reimbursed access to immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy for rare sarcomas in Australia. In April 2023, he was enrolled in a phase 1 clinical trial of combination immunotherapy.

Administering ICI therapy with hemodynamically significant RVOT obstruction carried substantial risk: immune-mediated tumor infiltration could cause pseudoprogression, which, in the confined RVOT, could precipitate fatal obstruction. Initial doses were therefore administered as an inpatient in a coronary care unit with continuous hemodynamic monitoring under cardiology comanagement. He tolerated treatment without immune-related toxicity or pseudoprogression, with gradual improvement in dyspnea and edema.

A pericardial window was performed in September 2023 for recurrent effusion, at which time interim CMR demonstrated early mass reduction and signal change. He continued combination immunotherapy with sustained response confirmed on serial imaging until treatment cessation in October 2025 after completing the 2-year immunotherapy course, supported by reassuring tumor response and the clinical need to address residual treatment-related fatigue.

Investigations

Multimodality cardiac imaging at 4 key time points over 30 months documented the trajectory from life-threatening RVOT obstruction to durable complete response (Visual Summary).

Time point 1 (January 2023): CMR—Tumor discovery with tissue characterization

The initial CMR findings are described in earlier sections. Perfusion hyperenhancement was diagnostically critical, excluding thrombus and supporting a noninvasive diagnosis. Baseline positron emission tomography–CT (PET-CT) demonstrated intense fluorodeoxyglucose avidity within the intracardiac masses, confirming metabolically active disease and providing the metabolic baseline for subsequent response assessment.

Time point 2 (March 2023): TTE—Hemodynamic crisis

TTE at the time of his emergency presentation with cardiac tamponade served a complementary role with real-time hemodynamic assessment. The dominant RV apical mass was seen extending into and obstructing the RVOT, with echocardiographic features of tamponade (Visual Summary, Videos 3 and 4). Echocardiography defined the immediate hemodynamic threat and guided immediate procedural intervention.

Time point 3 (September 2023 and January 2024): CMR and TTE—Early and sustained response

Interim CMR in September 2023, 4 months after commencing combination ICI therapy, demonstrated early mass reduction and evolving signal characteristics, suggesting histological response. Serial TTE 10 months after treatment confirmed significant mass reduction with relief of RVOT obstruction (Video 5). The patient was clinically well at home with markedly improved exercise tolerance and resolution of peripheral edema.

Time point 4 (June 2025): CMR—Tissue transformation

Follow-up CMR more than 2 years after treatment initiation characterized residual cardiac masses that could not be definitively assessed by echocardiography or PET alone (Videos 6 and 7). All 3 masses demonstrated marked size reduction: RVOT mass 49 × 30 × 12 mm (from 72 × 50 × 43 mm), RV apical mass 32 × 21 × 16 mm (from 60 × 37 × 29 mm), and LV intramyocardial mass 18 × 20 × 11 mm (from 40 × 32 × 30 mm). Tissue characteristics had fundamentally changed: masses were isointense with loss of heterogeneous signal on steady-state free precession imaging, nonenhancing on first-pass perfusion, and hypointense on LGE, consistent with fibrotic or necrotic transformation rather than a viable tumor. Biventricular function improved substantially: LVEF normalized to 57% (from 39%) and RV ejection fraction improved to 41% (from 19%), though the RV remained moderately dilated (indexed right ventricular end diastolic volume 125 mL/m2). This tissue-level evidence of tumor devitalization complemented the subsequent PET-CT finding of complete metabolic response and collectively informed the decision to cease therapy.

Outcome and Follow-Up

More than 2 years after a presentation where his life expectancy was measured in months, the patient remains well with an eastern cooperative oncology group performance status of 0. He experienced no immune-related toxicity throughout the treatment course. Surveillance PET-CT in January 2026 confirmed ongoing complete metabolic response 3 months after cessation of immunotherapy. Serial echocardiography demonstrates stable hemodynamic parameters with no residual RVOT obstruction.

Discussion

Intracardiac metastases are rare, occurring in approximately 2% to 5% of oncology patients at autopsy, though clinically significant cardiac involvement causing hemodynamic compromise is far less common.1 Furthermore, RVOT obstruction from an intracardiac tumor is a life-threatening complication that may present with syncope, heart failure, or sudden death and carries a prognosis of weeks to months without effective systemic therapy.2

ASPS is a rare soft tissue sarcoma with a known propensity for late, widespread metastatic disease, including cardiac involvement.3 Unlike most soft tissue sarcomas, ASPS has been characterized as an “immune hot” tumor, with a tumor microenvironment rich in CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes and programmed death ligand 1 expression, conferring sensitivity to ICI therapy.4,5

This case demonstrates 3 important points. First, durable complete metabolic response of intracardiac metastatic ASPS to ICI-based therapy is achievable, but administering immunotherapy in this anatomical context carries the unique risk of immune-mediated pseudoprogression causing fatal hemodynamic compromise. Pseudoprogression, characterized by transient tumor enlargement due to immune cell infiltration before subsequent regression, occurs in a small percentage of solid tumors treated with ICI.6 The safe delivery of therapy in this case, with inpatient dosing under continuous cardiology monitoring, illustrates the critical importance of cardio-oncology collaboration.

Second, this case report illustrates a multimodality imaging model for monitoring intracardiac tumor response, where each modality answered a distinct question: CMR established the noninvasive diagnosis and tissue baselines. TTE provided accessible, repeatable hemodynamic surveillance enabling community-based follow-up. Follow-up CMR demonstrated tissue transformation with recovery of biventricular function. PET-CT confirmed metabolic response, supporting treatment cessation.7 This complementary approach, rather than reliance on any single modality, represents a model that cardiologists can apply to the growing population of patients with intracardiac tumors receiving ICI therapy.

Third, this case highlights cardio-oncology collaboration extending from initial hemodynamic assessment through serial imaging surveillance to shared decision making regarding treatment cessation.8 At the time of presentation, ICI therapy for rare sarcomas in Australia was restricted to clinical trials. Recent broad multi-indication ICI listings represent a landmark policy shift expanding access for patients with rare and ultrarare malignant neoplasms.9 As ICI therapy extends across oncology, cardiologists will increasingly encounter patients with intracardiac disease requiring integrated long-term care.

Conclusions

This case demonstrates that intracardiac metastatic sarcoma causing life-threatening RVOT obstruction, a clinical scenario typically associated with a prognosis of months, can achieve a durable complete metabolic response with ICI-based immunotherapy. Multimodality cardiac imaging served a dual role, establishing the noninvasive diagnosis through CMR tissue characterization at baseline, and subsequently monitoring treatment response through serial echocardiography, CMR, and PET-CT, underscoring the essential role of the cardiologist in the multidisciplinary care of patients with intracardiac malignancy.

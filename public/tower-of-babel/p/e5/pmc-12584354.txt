The impacts of the diagnosis-related group payment reform on hospitalization-related medical expenses: evidence from China
Authors: Lele Li, Wei Yang, Xiaozhe Tang, Siyu Zeng, Xiaofei Liu, Siping Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDiagnosis-related group (DRG) payment methods are increasingly being used to decrease the costs of healthcare worldwide. However, the effectiveness of cost controls varies from region to region. This study aimed to analyze the impacts of DRG payments on medical costs in China and provide theoretical support for the promotion of DRG payments in other countries.MethodsPatients from City Wuxi in China was selected, which underwent a reform from fee-for-service (FFS) payment to DRG payment during the study period. Ordinary least regression analysis (OLS) and propensity-score-matching (PSM) were used to analyze the effects of DRG, Causal Forest (CF) of machine learning algorithm was used to analyze the underlying reasons for the results.ResultsThe OLS model revealed that personal total medical costs decreased by 28.3% after the DRG reform and the total personal out-of-pocket payment (OPP) decreased by 21.3% after the DRG reform, but the personal out-of-pocket ratio increased by 15% after the DRG reform. The PSM-OLS model regression and the DRG reform results indicated decreases of 29.4% and 24.2% in personal total cost and OPP costs, respectively. The proportion of OPP costs increased by 9%. The causal forest model suggested that age and the number of surgeries played a significant role in the impact of DRG reform on patients’ medical burden (total medical expenses, OPP costs, and OPP Ratio). Results indicate that the impacts of the DRG reform was associated with a 27% reduction in patients’ medical burden (SE = 0.007), a 19.4% reduction in out-of-pocket expenses (SE = 0.012), and a 1.4% increase in utilization costs (SE = 0.002).ConclusionsDRG payment can control the growth of medical expenses and ease the burden on the medical insurance fund. However, the current rules may increase the OPP ratio and the economic burden on patients. A regulatory model in line with China’s national conditions still must be explored.Keywords: Diagnosis-related group (DRG), Hospitalization expenditure, Payment reform

Introduction

Rising healthcare costs have concerned policymakers and the public worldwide for decades [1]. According to data released by the WHO, from 2000 to 2015, global medical costs’ average annual growth rate was 4%, higher than the global average annual economic growth rate of 2.8% [2]. The rising costs of health care is a major concern facing many countries. As an upper-middle-income country in Asia, China has built the world’s largest social safety net, with basic medical insurance coverage reaching 96.8%. In 2020, the total health expenditure of China reached 7,217,500 million RMB yuan, accounting for 7.1% of GDP. The increasing medical expenses in China have exceeded the average GDP growth rate [3], and controlling the rapid growth of medical expenses has become a serious problem for the Chinese government.

Diagnosis Related Group (DRG) based payments, which link hospital funding to activities, have become the most common hospital payment method used to decrease rising medical costs in many countries over the past decade [4–6]. Based on factors including patient demographic information, diagnosis, and disease severity, DRG-based payment systems group patients with similar clinical attributes requiring similar care, and result in similar resources being used within each group [7]. Hospitals are reimbursed a fixed price for each disease group, which shifts uncertain financial risk from the patients to the hospitals. These changes are expected that these rules will shift increasing incentives for cost efficiency, service providers reducing their costs and maximizing their profits [8, 9]. For many years, China used a fee-for-service (FFS) payment method in which hospitals were reimbursed based on the ratio of the units treated per medical service. This incentivized hospitals to overuse medical services for patients to generate revenues. In 2019, the Chinese government announced the implementation of a DRG-based system in 30 cities across the country beginning in 2020. Under the DRG payment method, medical insurance restricts the reimbursement amount of medical expenses for hospitals, and the government hopes that hospitals have to actively control medical costs.

DRG-based systems have been implemented in many countries to decrease medical costs [10], and some studies have shown that DRG-based payments can significantly control unreasonable medical costs. Reinhard, B., et al. (2006) compared the current use of the DRG payment method in nine European countries and found that DRG payment significantly controlled medical costs and should be implemented in all countries as a form of technical compensation and a basic incentive mechanism to decrease medical costs [11]. Xu et al. (2022) analyzed the data of 30 tertiary hospitals in Yunnan Province, China, from 2017 to 2018, and found that the medical expenses showed a downward trend after DRG implementation [12]. However, some empirical studies have shown that these expectations are not always realized [8, 13]. The upcoding problem of DRG-based payment may increase medical costs. Cook, A., et al. (2020) analyzed the use of DRG payment in the United States and argued that the existing DRG grouping method may lead to patients being classified into a higher DRG group to obtain higher medical insurance subsidies, increasing medical costs [1]. The implementation effect of DRG is inconsistent in previous studies, and most of those studies only analyzed one disease category. Additionally, the conclusions reached were only the analyses of the cost control effects of changing disease categories by DRG and lacked analysis of complete disease categories. Therefore, an analysis of the effects of DRG payment across a city or an entire hospital is needed.

City Wuxi is one of the first DRG pilot cities in China, it is a developed area with a high degree of information technology and a mature medical insurance operation mechanism, the implementation effect in Wuxi is vitally significant to the implementation of the nationwide DRG system. The Healthcare Security Administration in Wuxi implemented the DRG payment system in 2020, and it plans to cover medical expenditures for multiple injuries in the future. In this study, the data we collected included periods of reform for both FFS and DRG payment methods, Ordinary least regression analysis(OLS) and propensity-score-matching (PSM) was used to analyze the effects of DRG, the Causal Forest (CF) of machine learning method was used to analyze the underlying reasons for the results. This paper analyzes the impacts of DRG payment on medical costs in China and can provide theoretical support for the promotion of DRG payment in other countries. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Sect. 2, the materials and methods are introduced. Section 3 presents the results, including general information and the results of PSM and the CF. Section 4 discusses the results. Section 5 concludes the study and describes directions for future research.

China operates a multi-tiered social health insurance system comprising the Urban Employee Basic Medical Insurance (UEBMI), Urban Resident Basic Medical Insurance (URBMI), and New Rural Cooperative Medical Scheme (NRCMS), which together cover over 95% of the population. Hospitals and primary care facilities are publicly owned but operate under a mixed financing model: service fees are charged on a fee-for-service basis, supplemented by government subsidies and social insurance reimbursements. In 2022, total health expenditure reached approximately 8.1% of GDP (equivalent to RMB 8.3 trillion), with per-capita health spending of RMB 5,880. Out-of-pocket payments accounted for about 27% of total health expenditure, down from over 50% in 2000 but still higher than OECD averages. Recent payment reforms have adopted DRG‐based payment systems to contain costs in several major pilot cities. In this study, we exploit the DRG reform experiment in Wuxi to analyze its impact on patients’ medical burden.

Materials and methods

Data sources

The data used in this research were collected from the largest hospital in city Wuxi covers 47 diseases/surgeries/procedures in medical departments from January 1, 2019, to December 31, 2022. Names and ICD-10/ICD-9-cm codes of diseases, surgeries, and procedures are list in the supplementary material. Data from a total of 56,797 admitted patients were collected during the study period. Fee-for-service (FFS) payment data was used from January to December 2019, and DRG payment data was used from January 1, 2020. A total of 56,273 patients’ data remained after removing some missing values. The data primarily included information from the first page of each medical record (disease diagnosis, ICD-10 coding), medical expenditure settlement information (actual reimbursement expense, nominal reimbursement expense, total medical expenditure, drug expense, etc.), as well as basic personal characteristics of the patients (age, gender, disease type, hospital stay length, etc.).

PSM-OLS

Matching is a non-experimental method that serves as an approximation for data analysis when it is infeasible or inconvenient to use experimental methods to distinguish between experimental and control groups. The matching method assumes that, after controlling for covariates, individuals with similar characteristics have the same response to a policy. In other words, unobservable factors do not affect the decision of whether an individual receives a policy intervention, and selection only occurs for observable variables. Therefore, for each individual in the experimental group, a control group individual can be selected based on observable characteristics to construct a counterfactual. In recent years, PSM has been widely utilized in fields including medicine, economics, and policy evaluation, becoming the most commonly used method for policy effect assessment [2, 3, 14]. CF

Causal Forests, an extension of random forests, are a learning method for causal inference in which data is split to maximize the difference in the relationship between an outcome variable and a “treatment” variable, rather than minimizing prediction error as in random forests. This approach aims to uncover the variation in treatment effects across a sample. Notably, CF merely reveal the heterogeneity of causal effects and do not establish causality itself. A standard causal forest assumes that treatment assignment is exogenous, just as it would be in a randomized controlled trial. Recently, causal forests have been widely used to infer treatment effects in a sample [5–8]. In this study, DRG implementation is used as the intervention variable, and the patient’s medical burden is used as the outcome variable to construct a causal forest model. As causal forests are a machine learning algorithm, the model was trained using the “grf” package in R.

Results of PSM-OLS

To investigate the impact of the DRG reform on patients’ medical burden while controlling for more individual factors, both OLS and PSM-OLS methods were employed. Initially, the OLS model was used to verify the impact of the DRG reform. Subsequently, based on the counterfactual framework and controlling for as many observable variables as possible, the PSM model was utilized to measure the impact of the DRG reform more accurately; aiming to address the issue of individual heterogeneity.

Table 5 displays the regression results of Model 1 and the variable “Treat” signifies the impact of the DRG reform on patients’ medical burden. In terms of total cost, the total medical cost decreased by 28.3% after the DRG reform, suggesting that the reform played a role in somewhat controlling the growth of medical costs. From the perspective of personal out-of-pocket cost, the total personal out-of-pocket cost decreased by 21.3% after the DRG reform. This indicates that the DRG reform has, to some degree, reduced patient medical burden. Yet, in terms of the personal out-of-pocket ratio, it increased by 15% after the DRG reform. This result suggests that further optimization in the DRG reform is needed to ensure its effectiveness.

To further eliminate individual heterogeneity, PSM was used to match patients, thereby constructing a counterfactual framework. After PSM matching, data for a total of 21,504 patients were obtained. The regression results are presented in Table 4 and based on the PSM-OLS model results, the impact trend of the DRG reform on patients’ medical burden was found to be generally consistent with the results of the OLS model. However, the impact effect was different. In the PSM-OLS model regression results, the DRG reform resulted in a decrease of 29.4% and 24.2% in total cost and personal out-of-pocket cost, respectively, and the proportion of out-of-pocket cost increased by 9%. Through the use of the PSM-OLS model, a more accurate impact of the DRG reform was estimated.

Results of CF

Unlike traditional policy evaluation models that require parametric settings, machine learning analysis of non-parametric models has significant advantages. As the latest development in supervised machine learning evolves towards economic interpretability, the CF algorithm can greatly enhance the credibility of policy evaluations. In estimating the average treatment effect (ATE), the CF algorithm was utilized and control variables, such as gender and age, were incorporated to consider the impact of individual factors on patient medical burden.

This study initially constructed a preliminary CF model to calculate the importance of features. Subsequently, important features were selected (i.e., the importance of a single feature exceeds the average importance of all features) and CF modeling was performed again. The importance of features is illustrated in Fig. 1. From the analysis results of Fig. 1, it is evident that age and the number of surgeries play a significant role in the impact of DRG reform on patients’ medical burden. Specifically, the factor of age is most prominent in influencing the overall medical costs, while the number of surgeries plays a crucial role in determining individual out-of-pocket expenses.

The importance of features

The CF model was utilized to separately estimate the impact of the DRG reform on patients’ medical burden (total medical expenses, personal out-of-pocket costs, and the proportion of personal out-of-pocket costs). The model results indicated that the impacts of the DRG reform on patients’ medical burden were − 0.27 (SE = 0.007), −0.194 (SE = 0.012), and 0.014 (SE = 0.002), respectively. In comparison with the results from previous econometric models, the DRG payment method reform effectively reduced total medical expenses and personal out-of-pocket costs and showed a positive correlation with the proportion of personal out-of-pocket costs.

To enhance the interpretability of our CF, the model is presented in the form of a branching tree and only the most important features were selected. Figures 2, 3 and 4 illustrate the impact pathways of the DRG reform on patient medical burden. Based on these pathways, we can infer the effects of the DRG reform on the medical burden of different groups. In this model, each node represents a decision point, and each branch signifies a potential decision outcome. Each leaf node embodies a predicted causal effect. First, age moderates the effect on total cost (Fig. 2): young adults (≤ 28 years) see the smallest reduction (avg_Y = 8.88), while the very oldest (≥ 82 years) benefit most (avg_Y = 9.98), with middle-aged groups between (avg_Y ≈ 9.33–9.95). Second, number of surgeries moderates the effect on out-of-pocket cost (Fig. 3): patients with no surgery have avg_Y = 8.05, one surgery avg_Y = 8.67, two–three surgeries avg_Y = 9.26, and > 3 surgeries avg_Y = 10.12. Third, length of stay moderates the effect on out-of-pocket ratio (Fig. 4): those with very short stays (≤ 5 days) benefit most (avg_Y = 0.53), with the effect declining for longer stays to a minimum of avg_Y = 0.37 at > 21 days.

Discussion

Escalating healthcare expenses pose a significant challenge worldwide [1, 2]. To address it, the Chinese government is relying on the DRG system as a potential solution to curb the upward trajectory of healthcare costs [7]. This study primarily investigates the impact of the DRG reform on healthcare costs. Utilizing four years of historical data from the First batch of DRG pilot city, OLS, PSM-OLS and CF methods were used to measure the influence of the DRG reform on healthcare expenditure. After validation through multiple models, the findings indicate that the DRG reform has reduced the total healthcare costs as well as individual OPP. Surprisingly, there is a notable upward trend in the proportion of individual out-of-pocket payments.

Results reveal that total medical costs are on an upward trend. One reason for the rise in medical costs is the influence of economic growth [15, 16]. Another important reason is that 2019 to 2022 was the period of the COVID-19 outbreak in China [17, 18]. Complex, sudden, and unpredictable outbreaks of epidemic diseases have aggravated the severity of disease in patients, and complicated clinical diagnosis and treatment [19]. Both the PSM-LOS and CF results indicate that DRG implementation has a significant negative impact on healthcare costs. As seen in Table 2, the implementation of DRG reduced total cost by 28.3%. Previous studies have shown that many countries have adopted DRG payment systems as a policy tool to contain rising healthcare costs [20, 21]. These findings are consistent with our conclusion that the implementation of DRG payment systems effectively contains healthcare cost growth. Therefore, the implementation of DRG is very significant in reducing medical costs.

PSM, OLS, and CF showed that the proportion of OPP increased after the implementation of DRG. China’s insurance system includes basic medical insurance, but doctors can choose higher quality and more efficacious self-funded drugs for treatment [22]. Thus, patients must pay for this portion of the drug costs themselves, and they are not included in DRG payment quotas. Hospitals are reimbursed at a fixed rate for the same group of diseases, and they are motivated to increase the OPP to obtain more revenue [23, 24]. Furthermore, some scholars argue that cost-shifting occurs under DRG payment systems, whereby providers may pass on saved costs to patients via increased out‐of‐pocket charges, thereby exacerbating patients’ financial burden. This may result in the OPP ratio rising after the DRG reform implementation and suggests that we should consider the OPP ratio as one of the assessment indicators for high-quality hospital development to truly control medical expenses [25].

CF machine learning algorithm was used to analyze factors that have a significant impact on medical costs in DRG implementation. The results reveal that age is the primary factor associated with total medical expenses and that surgery times is the primary factor affecting the total OPP of patients. A large number of studies have shown that age and surgery times are positively correlated with medical expenses [26, 27]. Age and surgical items are one of the factors in DRG grouping [7]. DRG considers age, so age has an impact on total medical expenses, but it is not the main OPP factor. Notably, the grouping rules did not consider surgical times, which would increase medical costs, and the hospital would increase the OPP to compensate for the loss caused by surgical times under the fixed payment system. Studies have shown that LOS is one of the major factors affecting medical costs, and our results indicate that LOS has the third largest impact on total costs, as well as a significant impact on OPP rates. This suggests that the DRG grouping scheme requires further optimization.

Conclusion

Healthcare costs are continuously increasing around the world. This paper aims to identify the impact of DRG on medical expenses in China. PSM-OLS and CF of machine learning algorithm methods are used to analyze historical data from the first batch of DRG payment pilot city in China. The results indicate that the DRG reform can somewhat reduce individual healthcare costs, but that it also increases the OPP ratio. This suggests that the DRG reform has a positive effect on controlling healthcare costs, but internal regulations should be strengthened to alleviate various dimensions of patients’ medical burdens. However, this study has several limitations. Firstly, our research only selected one region as the study subject, and the general reliability of the research results requires further exploration. Examining more regions and hospitals would be conducive to more accurately assessing the impact of the DRG reform on healthcare costs. Secondly, our main purpose was to investigate whether the DRG payment method could reduce healthcare costs, but without examining impact variations between different diseases. While substantial research has been conducted on single diseases in previous studies, in the future, more analysis of the impact of the DRG payment method on specific diseases should be conducted from the perspective of all diseases.

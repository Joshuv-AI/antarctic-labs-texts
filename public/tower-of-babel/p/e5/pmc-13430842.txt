Carbon dioxide laser combined with photodynamic therapy versus photodynamic therapy alone for vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia: a multicenter retrospective comparative cohort study
Authors: Hui Hong, Xiaowen Hu, Zhicheng Yu, Xiuyun Zhang, Ying Zhou, Xiaoli Wang, Zhengzheng Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundVaginal intraepithelial neoplasia (VaIN) is a premalignant condition characterized by a high risk of persistence and recurrence, and no universally accepted standard treatment has been established. Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a minimally invasive treatment option for VaIN, but its efficacy may be limited in some patients. We aimed to evaluate whether the addition of carbon dioxide (CO₂) laser ablation to PDT could improve the efficacy and safety of treatment for VaIN.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective comparative study included patients with VaIN treated at the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China and the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical University between December 2018 and August 2024. Patients were assigned to the PDT plus CO₂ laser group or the PDT-alone group according to the treatment they actually received. All patients were followed for 12 months. The primary clinical outcome was complete remission (CR) at each follow-up endpoint. Secondary outcomes included HPV clearance among patients who were HPV-positive at baseline, cumulative recurrence, progression, and safety outcomes.ResultsA total of 146 patients were included, of whom 78 received PDT plus CO₂ laser and 68 received PDT alone. In the PDT-alone group, the CR rates at 3, 6, and 12 months were 70.6%, 63.2%, and 66.2%, respectively, compared with 70.5%, 85.9%, and 88.5% in the PDT plus CO₂ laser group. No significant between-group difference was observed at 3 months (P = 0.992), whereas the combination group showed significantly higher CR rates at 6 and 12 months (P = 0.002 and P = 0.001, respectively). Among patients who were HPV-positive at baseline, HPV clearance rates at 3, 6, and 12 months were 40.3%, 45.2%, and 50.0% in the PDT-alone group, compared with 67.6%, 66.2%, and 83.1% in the PDT plus CO₂ laser group, respectively. Cumulative recurrence by 12 months occurred in 6 patients (8.8%) in the PDT-alone group and 2 patients (2.6%) in the PDT plus CO₂ laser group.ConclusionsIn this multicenter retrospective comparative cohort study, CO₂ laser combined with PDT was associated with higher complete remission rates and greater HPV clearance than PDT alone in patients with VaIN. The combined approach may improve lesion control and was associated with a numerically lower cumulative recurrence rate within 12 months, although recurrence-related findings should be interpreted cautiously because of the small number of events. The treatment appeared generally tolerable based on available retrospective safety records. Further prospective studies are warranted to confirm these findings.Trial registrationNot applicable. This study was a multicenter retrospective comparative cohort study and was not prospectively registered.

Background

Vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia (VaIN) comprises a spectrum of premalignant lesions of the vaginal squamous epithelium, ranging from squamous dysplasia to carcinoma in situ [1]. Histologically, VaIN is classified as low-grade (VaIN I) or high-grade (VaIN II/III). Although VaIN is generally considered uncommon, its detection has increased in recent years with the wider use of liquid-based cytology, human papillomavirus (HPV) screening, and colposcopic examination [1]. Previous studies have reported an incidence of approximately 0.2–0.3 per 100,000, accounting for 0.4%–1.0% of lower genital tract intraepithelial neoplasia [2]. More recent domestic studies have suggested that the detection rate of VaIN among lower genital tract intraepithelial lesions has risen to 11.5%–23.7% over the past decade [1]. The development and progression of VaIN are influenced by multiple factors, among which high-risk HPV infection is considered the most important. Patients infected with HPV subtypes covered by the nine-valent vaccine reportedly have a 4.30-fold higher risk of high-grade VaIN than uninfected individuals [2]. In addition, age, menopausal status, cervical lesions, and prior treatment history have also been associated with the occurrence of VaIN [3].

Despite its relatively low incidence compared with cervical intraepithelial neoplasia, VaIN remains clinically challenging to manage. The disease is often multifocal, recurrent, and anatomically difficult to treat because of the complex structure of the vaginal mucosa. At present, no universally accepted standard treatment exists, and therapeutic decisions are usually individualized according to patient age, menopausal status, lesion grade, lesion extent, and reproductive considerations. Available treatment options include topical agents such as 5-fluorouracil and imiquimod, as well as physical or surgical approaches including carbon dioxide (CO₂) laser ablation, photodynamic therapy (PDT), excision, and radiotherapy [2].

Among these modalities, CO₂ laser ablation is widely used because of its high precision, limited damage to surrounding tissue, and rapid postoperative recovery. However, its effectiveness may be compromised in patients with multifocal, extensive, or recurrent lesions, in whom complete eradication can be difficult to achieve. Repeated laser treatment may also increase the risk of adhesions, scarring, and vaginal stenosis, particularly in older patients and those with recurrent disease [4]. These limitations highlight the need for treatment strategies that can improve lesion control while preserving vaginal anatomy and function.

PDT is a minimally invasive treatment that combines a photosensitizing agent with light of a specific wavelength to selectively destroy abnormal epithelial cells while relatively sparing normal tissue. It has shown therapeutic value in several lower genital tract disorders, including cervical and vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia, vulvar lichen sclerosus, and condyloma acuminatum [3]. Previous studies have reported encouraging remission rates after PDT for VaIN [5]. Nevertheless, the efficacy of PDT alone may still be influenced by factors such as lesion burden, epithelial thickness, and photosensitizer penetration, which may limit treatment response in some patients.

Given the complementary characteristics of these two modalities, combining CO₂ laser ablation with PDT may offer potential advantages. Laser ablation can reduce the visible lesion burden and improve local access, whereas PDT may provide broader field control through selective phototoxic effects on residual or subclinical abnormal epithelium. However, comparative evidence regarding whether the addition of CO₂ laser to PDT improves lesion remission, HPV clearance, recurrence control, and safety in patients with VaIN remains limited, especially in multicenter real-world clinical settings. Therefore, this multicenter retrospective comparative study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of CO₂ laser combined with PDT versus PDT alone in patients with VaIN.

Methods

Study population

This multicenter retrospective comparative study included 146 patients diagnosed with vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia (VaIN) by colposcopy-guided biopsy at the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China and the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical University between December 2018 and August 2024.

Inclusion criteria

(1) histopathological confirmation of vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia (VaIN) by colposcopy-guided biopsy; (2) treatment with either photodynamic therapy (PDT) alone or CO₂ laser combined with PDT at the First Affiliated Hospital of the University of Science and Technology of China or the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical University between December 2018 and August 2024;(3) complete clinical, pathological, and treatment data available for analysis; (4) availability of follow-up data sufficient for evaluation of treatment response and recurrence.

Exclusion criteria

(1) invasive vaginal carcinoma could not be excluded by colposcopy or histopathological examination; (2) presence of any severe acute illness; (3) pregnancy or lactation; (4) coagulation disorders or a bleeding tendency; (5) history of allergic disease, suspected or confirmed porphyria, or known allergy to the photosensitizing agent or structurally related compounds; (6) severe immunodeficiency or long-term use of corticosteroids or immunosuppressive agents; (7) contraindications to CO₂ laser therapy, where applicable; (8) inability to comply with follow-up requirements.

Patients were retrospectively enrolled and classified into the PDT plus CO₂ laser group (n = 78) or the PDT-only group (n = 68) according to the treatment they actually received. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committees of both participating institutions (approval numbers: 2025RE-132 and 2024[418])(Fig. 1). Given the retrospective design and the use of de-identified data, the requirement for written informed consent was waived(Fig. 1).

Because this was a retrospective study, the final sample size was determined by the number of eligible patients with complete clinical and follow-up data during the study period. To provide a post hoc justification of the achieved sample size, we compared the available sample with an assumed cure rate of 70% in the PDT-alone group and 90% in the combination group, using a two-sided α of 0.05 and power of 0.80. This calculation suggested that approximately 67 patients per group would be required, which was comparable to the achieved sample size.

Procedures

PDT treatment

PDT was performed outside the menstrual period according to the same standardized protocol at both participating centers. Procedures were performed by dedicated cervical disease teams at each center, whose members had received standardized training in cervical disease diagnosis and treatment at the Obstetrics and Gynecology Hospital of Fudan University.

Patients were placed in the lithotomy position, and vaginal secretions were removed before lesion assessment. Lesions were localized by colposcopic examination using 5% acetic acid and Lugol’s iodine staining. Aminolevulinic acid hydrochloride powder (ALA, 118 mg/vial; Shanghai Fudan Zhangjiang Bio-Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) was freshly prepared before each application by dissolving each vial in 0.5 mL of thermosensitive gel to obtain a 20% ALA preparation.

Sterile cotton balls were soaked in the ALA preparation and placed over the lesion areas using a condom-wrapped vaginal pack to ensure complete lesion coverage. The drug application time was 3–4 h. Photodynamic therapy was performed using LED-IB and LD600-C PDT devices of the same models at both centers (Wuhan Yaguang Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China), with standardized parameters including a wavelength of 630 nm, light intensity of 100–150 mW/cm², and exposure time of 20 min. Treatment was conducted once every 7–14 days for a total of 6 sessions. The PDT devices were not updated during the enrollment period from 2018 to 2024, and the core treatment parameters remained consistent across centers. Sexual intercourse was prohibited during the treatment period.

CO₂ laser treatment

CO₂ laser treatment was performed outside the menstrual period by the dedicated cervical disease teams at each participating center, following the same predefined treatment protocol described above.

Laser treatment was performed using the same LJL35-CB CO₂ laser system at both centers, with a wavelength of 10.6 μm and an adjustable output power of 1–35 W. The devices were not updated during the enrollment period from 2018 to 2024, and the core procedural parameters remained consistent across centers.

Before ablation, lesions were identified and delineated by colposcopic examination using 5% acetic acid and Lugol’s iodine staining. Mild CO₂ laser ablation was then performed on the lesion areas. The ablation field extended 3–5 mm beyond the visible lesion edge, and the ablation depth was generally controlled at 2–3 mm. PDT was subsequently performed according to the standardized protocol described above.

Follow-up

At 1 month after completion of treatment, patients in both groups underwent gynecological examination to assess vaginal wall healing. At 3, 6, and 12 months post-treatment, high-risk HPV testing was performed using the cobas HPV test on the cobas 4800 system, a real-time PCR-based assay for the detection of high-risk HPV DNA, together with ThinPrep cytologic testing (TCT) and colposcopic examination. Biopsy was performed when necessary to evaluate lesion clearance. Information on vaginal bleeding, pain, adhesions, and dyspareunia was also collected during follow-up.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 26.0. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Group comparisons were performed using Pearson’s chi-square test, continuity correction chi-square test, or Fisher’s exact test, depending on data distribution and expected cell counts. For crosstabulated data, measures of association were calculated to assess the strength and direction of relationships. These included contingency coefficient, Phi, and Cramer’s V for nominal variables, and Gamma and Kendall’s tau-b for ordinal variables. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the association between treatment modality and 12-month CR and, among patients who were HPV-positive at baseline, 12-month HPV clearance. Covariates included age, baseline VaIN grade, lesion multiplicity, menopausal status, center, and treatment modality.

Results

Baseline clinical and virological characteristics

A total of 146 patients were enrolled, with 78 assigned to the PDT + CO₂ laser group and 68 to the PDT group. There were no statistically significant differences between groups in age distribution, parity, menopausal status, hysterectomy history, TCT results, VaIN grade, lesion multiplicity, concomitant CIN or VIN, or lesion location (all P > 0.05) (Table 1).

Treatment efficacy for VaIN

3-month outcomes

At 3 months post-treatment, CR was achieved in 70.5% (55/78) of patients in the combination group and 70.6% (48/68) in the PDT group (P = 0.992). Persistent lesion was observed in 29.5% (23/78) of patients in the combination group and 29.4% (20/68) of patients in the PDT-alone group. No progression or recurrence was observed in either group at 3 months (Table 2).

6-month outcomes

At 6 months, the CR rate was significantly higher in the PDT plus CO₂ laser group than in the PDT-alone group [85.9% (67/78) vs. 63.2% (43/68); P = 0.002]. Persistent lesion was recorded in 7.7% (6/78) of patients in the combination group and 27.9% (19/68) of patients in the PDT-alone group. Progression was observed in 3.8% (3/78) of patients in the combination group and 0.0% (0/68) of patients in the PDT-alone group. Cumulative recurrence by 6 months occurred in 2.6% (2/78) of patients in the combination group and 8.8% (6/68) of patients in the PDT-alone group.

12-month outcome

At the 12-month follow-up, CR was achieved in 69 of 78 patients (88.5%) in the PDT plus CO₂ laser group and 45 of 68 patients (66.2%) in the PDT-alone group. Persistent lesion was recorded in 6 patients (7.7%) in the PDT plus CO₂ laser group and 17 patients (25.0%) in the PDT-alone group. Progression was observed in 3 patients (3.8%) in the PDT plus CO₂ laser group and 1 patient (1.5%) in the PDT-alone group. Cumulative recurrence within 12 months occurred in 2 patients (2.6%) in the PDT plus CO₂ laser group and 6 patients (8.8%) in the PDT-alone group. No additional recurrence was detected between the 6- and 12-month follow-up visits in either group.

In the multivariable logistic regression analysis, PDT plus CO₂ laser was associated with a higher likelihood of 12-month CR after adjustment for age, VaIN grade, lesion multiplicity, menopausal status, and center (adjusted OR, 3.77; 95% CI, 1.42–10.02; P = 0.008). Among patients who were HPV-positive at baseline, PDT plus CO₂ laser was also associated with a higher likelihood of 12-month HPV clearance after adjustment for the same covariates (adjusted OR, 5.70; 95% CI, 2.43–13.39; P < 0.001) (Table 3).

Recurrence analysis

During the first 3 months after treatment, no recurrence was observed in either group. At the 6-month follow-up, recurrence was observed in 2 patients (2.6%) in the CO₂ laser plus PDT group and 6 patients (8.8%) in the PDT-alone group. At the 12-month endpoint, cumulative recurrence within 12 months was observed in 2 patients (2.6%) in the CO₂ laser plus PDT group and 6 patients (8.8%) in the PDT-alone group. No additional recurrence was detected between the 6- and 12-month follow-up visits in either group.

Discussion

In this multicenter retrospective comparative cohort study, we found that CO₂ laser combined with PDT was associated with higher 6- and 12-month CR rates, higher HPV clearance among baseline HPV-positive patients, and lower cumulative recurrence within 12 months compared with PDT alone. PDT offers high specificity, low systemic toxicity, and minimal impact on surrounding healthy tissue [6]. However, limitations in photosensitizer tissue penetration and light depth may result in incomplete eradication, particularly for thicker lesions. CO₂ laser therapy, in contrast, provides precise ablation with the ability to remove superficial epithelial layers and potentially disrupt viral reservoirs. The rationale for combining CO₂ laser with PDT lies in a hypothesized synergistic mechanism: laser ablation removes the superficial barrier, enhances photosensitizer penetration into deeper epithelial layers, and facilitates more complete photodynamic destruction of residual dysplastic and HPV-infected cells.

Our study demonstrated that although complete remission (CR) rates at 3 months were comparable between the two groups, HPV clearance among baseline HPV-positive patients was significantly higher in the combination group. This early virological advantage may be partly explained by the complementary biological effects of CO₂ laser pretreatment and PDT. CO₂ laser ablation may remove superficial abnormal epithelium, reduce lesion thickness, improve lesion-margin control, and increase local tissue permeability, thereby facilitating ALA penetration and subsequent protoporphyrin IX accumulation in dysplastic or HPV-infected epithelial cells. Upon light activation, PDT can induce reactive oxygen species-mediated cytotoxicity, local vascular effects, and immune modulation, which may promote the clearance of HPV-infected cells before complete colposcopic or histological regression becomes apparent. Therefore, the higher early HPV clearance rate in the combination group may reflect an earlier virological response that precedes complete morphological remission. However, because this study did not directly assess ALA uptake, protoporphyrin IX accumulation, oxidative stress, or local immune changes in treated tissues, this mechanistic interpretation should be considered exploratory.

By 6 and 12 months, the combination group showed higher CR and HPV clearance rates than PDT alone, together with lower cumulative recurrence by 12 months. These findings are consistent with previous reports. Yao et al. [4] reported superior 6-month CR rates for CO₂ laser plus PDT compared with CO₂ laser alone in patients with LSIL (85% vs. 65%). Hu et al. [7] observed improved outcomes in patients with HSIL receiving combination therapy compared with laser alone. Mathias et al. [8] and Min et al. [9] also reported favorable CR rates following PDT for VaIN, although variability across studies highlights the importance of optimizing treatment parameters and patient selection. It should be noted that HPV clearance in this study referred to undetectable HPV DNA at a specific follow-up visit, rather than definitive lifelong eradication. Therefore, continued surveillance remains necessary, especially for patients with high-risk HPV infection or recurrent VaIN.

At the same time, the potential adverse effects of CO₂ laser therapy should not be overlooked. In addition to short-term symptoms such as pain, burning, local discomfort, and delayed wound healing, possible long-term complications may include adhesions, scarring, vaginal stenosis, and dyspareunia, particularly after repeated or extensive ablation. In our cohort, the overall incidence of mild vaginal stricture scarring was low, and no statistically significant difference was observed between the two groups. Nevertheless, this issue remains clinically relevant and warrants continued attention in future studies with longer follow-up.

Persistent high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV) infection is recognized as the primary etiological driver of the onset, Progression, and recurrence of vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia (VaIN) following treatment. Epidemiological evidence has identified immunosuppression, smoking, multiple sexual partners, and early onset of sexual activity as independent risk factors for persistent HPV infection, and consequently for lower genital tract intraepithelial lesions [10]. Moreover, HPV genotype plays a critical role in disease biology. HPV16, HPV33, and HPV45 are among the most prevalent subtypes detected in VaIN, with HPV16 positivity closely correlated with lesion severity and a higher likelihood of Progression to high-grade VaIN [11]. Although high-risk HPV infection is an important factor associated with VaIN, VaIN should not be regarded as being caused exclusively by HPV. The development and recurrence of VaIN may also be influenced by host- and disease-related factors, including immune status, age, prior cervical intraepithelial neoplasia or cervical cancer, smoking history, lesion grade, lesion multiplicity, and previous treatment history. Therefore, HPV status in this study was analyzed as a virological outcome and an important clinical variable, rather than as evidence that HPV was the only causal factor.

Beyond viral factors, host-related variables also contribute to disease occurrence. Age is an important determinant, with the peak incidence observed between 35 and 58 years. Postmenopausal women are disproportionately affected by high-grade lesions (VaIN 2/3) [12]. In addition, prior cervical pathology significantly increases VaIN risk; patients undergoing hysterectomy for cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) are at elevated risk for subsequent VaIN or invasive vaginal carcinoma [13]. Given that most VaIN cases are asymptomatic and detected incidentally during cervical cancer screening or colposcopy, there is a need for safe, effective, and patient-compliant treatment strategies.

Multiple therapeutic options have been explored, including medical therapy, surgical excision, laser ablation, photodynamic therapy (PDT), and intracavitary radiotherapy. Each has limitations. Medical agents may cause extensive tissue sloughing, delayed healing, and significant discomfort, leading to poor compliance [14]. Surgical approaches, such as partial or total vaginectomy, carry a risk of injury to adjacent pelvic organs and long-term sequelae such as vaginal shortening and stenosis [15]. Intracavitary radiotherapy, though associated with lower recurrence rates, often results in irreversible complications including vaginal fibrosis, stenosis, and premature ovarian failure.

The present study adds to the existing literature by providing multicenter real-world comparative data on the efficacy and safety of CO₂ laser combined with PDT versus PDT alone in patients with VaIN. Our findings suggest that the combination approach may offer advantages in both lesion remission and HPV clearance, particularly in patients with a higher disease burden. Clinically, these results suggest that CO₂ laser combined with PDT may be considered as a potential treatment option for selected patients with VaIN, particularly those with multifocal lesions or persistent high-risk HPV infection.

Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although this was a multicenter study, its retrospective design and relatively limited sample size may affect the generalizability of the findings. Second, the follow-up period was limited to 12 months; therefore, longer-term outcomes, including late recurrence and progression to invasive disease, remain to be clarified. In addition, the number of recurrence events was small; therefore, recurrence-related findings should be interpreted cautiously. Third, although exploratory subgroup analyses were performed according to baseline HPV genotype, the limited sample size within genotype-specific subgroups may have reduced the statistical power of these analyses. Fourth, although treatment-related safety outcomes were reviewed using available medical records and follow-up documentation, detailed standardized grading of mild adverse events, such as pain, bleeding, infection, adhesions, dyspareunia, and treatment discontinuation, was not consistently available because of the retrospective nature of the study. Therefore, the safety findings should be interpreted descriptively and require confirmation in prospective studies using standardized toxicity grading and patient-reported outcome measures. Fifth, no histopathological or translational evaluation of treated vaginal tissue was performed to directly support the proposed mechanism of enhanced photosensitizer penetration after CO₂ laser pretreatment. Finally, although a standardized treatment protocol was used across centers, this retrospective study could not fully capture all intra-procedural variables, such as exact lesion thickness, real-time light dosimetry, and subtle operator-dependent differences. Future prospective multicenter studies with larger sample sizes, extended follow-up, standardized adverse-event assessment, patient-reported outcomes, and translational assessments, such as local immune microenvironment profiling, are warranted. In addition, this retrospective study did not systematically assess other potential viral infections or all host-related cofactors that may influence the development or recurrence of VaIN. Therefore, the HPV-related findings should be interpreted as virological outcomes rather than evidence of exclusive HPV causality.

In conclusion, PDT combined with CO₂ laser therapy was associated with higher CR rates, greater HPV clearance among baseline HPV-positive patients, and lower cumulative recurrence within 12 months compared with PDT alone in patients with VaIN. The combination approach showed an acceptable safety profile in this retrospective cohort. Further prospective studies with larger sample sizes, longer follow-up, and standardized adverse-event assessment are warranted to confirm these findings.

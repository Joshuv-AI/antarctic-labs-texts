Maternal knowledge, attitudes, and practices towards fetal size assessment by ultrasound screening: A cross-sectional study
Authors: Xinxin Li, Wenxin Xu, Zhonghua Liang, Chunxiang Gao, Shuyan You, Jing Zhai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to assess the knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) of Chinese pregnant women regarding fetal size assessment through ultrasound screening. The study was conducted at Dalian Women and Children’s Medical Center Group from March 2022 to September 2022. Pregnant women were enrolled, and their KAP scores were evaluated using a questionnaire. The study analyzed the differences in KAP scores based on demographic/clinical characteristics, explored the correlation among KAP using statistical analysis, and examined the association between KAP and demographic/clinical characteristics using logistic regression. The results showed that the mean scores for knowledge, attitude, and practice were moderate. Positive correlations were found among knowledge, attitude, and practice scores. Logistic regression revealed associations between knowledge and attitude scores, educational levels, and proactive practices. These findings highlight the importance of healthcare interventions and educational initiatives to improve fetal size screening-related KAP among pregnant women in China.

1. Introduction

Fetal ultrasound can be used to determine the gestational age, measure nuchal translucency, and screen for Down syndrome.[1–3] Diagnostic prenatal ultrasound screening is generally considered safe if used appropriately (for example, according to the “as low as reasonably achievable” [ALARA] principle).[1–3] Prenatal fetal size assessment using ultrasound is an effective approach for detecting fetal growth and development issues.[4–7] Combined with maternal body mass index (BMI) and ethnicity, those 3 measurements can provide the optimal growth curves for individual pregnancies.[8] Identifying fetuses with fetal growth restriction or macrosomia is essential for planning the delivery process appropriately.[5,9,10] Fetal ultrasound is widely and routinely performed to reduce the stillbirth rates.[10,11]

Ultrasound is a notoriously operator-dependent imaging modality.[12] Novel technologies involving artificial intelligence are emerging to assist in determining fetal biometry.[13–16] Such tools could help eliminate operator dependency, but their social acceptability and public knowledge about them remain poorly understood.

Nevertheless, there are still many gaps in the knowledge of prenatal ultrasonography for pregnant women, limiting maternal attitude and practice on prenatal fetal screening. Several studies reported different levels of knowledge, attitudes, and practices (KAP) toward ultrasound screening in pregnant women,[17–21] but the available data regarding Chinese women remains insufficient.[22]

The present study investigated maternal KAP toward fetal size assessment. The results could be used to make recommendations for improving popular health literacy on maternal-fetal size assessment, lowering the incidence of birth defects, enhancing maternal prognosis quality, strengthening management, and designing targeted training measures to improve the KAP level.[23,24]

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study design and participants

This single center cross-sectional study was conducted at Dalian Women and Children’s Medical Center Group from March 2022 to September 2022 and enrolled pregnant women. The study was approved by the ethics committee of Dalian Women and Children’s Medical Center Group (approval #2022023). All participants provided a signed informed consent form before completing the study questionnaire.

The inclusion criteria were Chinese nationality, live pregnancy confirmed by ultrasound that revealed fetal heartbeat, singleton pregnancy, and provided written informed consent and voluntarily participated in the study. The exclusion criteria were pregnant women with intellectual disability, mental disorders, or other serious comorbidities or emergency treatment or unstable maternal or fetal conditions with a risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes.

2.2. Questionnaire

The questionnaire design referred to previously reported questionnaires.[17–22,25] After the questionnaire design was completed, 73 questionnaires were issued and collected for a pilot study. The reliability and validity tests showed that Cronbach’s α was 0.731, suggesting that the questionnaire items had high internal consistency. The KMO value of factor analysis was 0.802.

The final questionnaire was in Chinese (a non-validated English translation is provided as Supplemental Digital Content Questionnaire, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q665) and included 4 aspects: demographic data, knowledge dimension, attitudes dimension, and practices dimension. The demographic data included age, educational level, family monthly income, occupations, current gestational age, number of deliveries, pregnancy-related complications, prepregnancy BMI, gestational weight gain, medication during pregnancy, and delivery history of macrosomia. The knowledge dimension included 10 items. They were scored 1 point for correct answers or 0 points for incorrect or unclear answers, with a total score of 0 to 10 points. The attitude dimension included 9 items answered using a 5-point Likert scale from very positive (5 points) to very negative (1 point); the total scores ranged from 9 to 45 points. The practice dimension consisted of 5 items using a 5-point Likert scale, with 3 items ranging from always (5 points) to never (1 point), with total scores ranging from 3 to 15 points. The remaining 2 practice items were open questions with no scores assigned. They were used to record the number of prenatal examinations and the frequency of ultrasound assessments.

2.3. Sample size

We calculated the minimum requirement of sample size in this study according to the sample size calculation formula.

The final plan was to collect at least 480 questionnaires based on a probability of an 80% effective response rate.

2.4. Questionnaire distribution and quality control

The questionnaire was issued as QR codes in the ultrasound department and was completed voluntarily by pregnant women. The questionnaire results were summarized in an Excel spreadsheet. Members of the research team checked the integrity, consistency, and validity of all questionnaires. Incomplete questionnaires or those filled using an obvious pattern (e.g., all last choices) were excluded. The questionnaires that took <3 minutes to answer were excluded. Only 1 questionnaire could be collected from a given IP address.

2.5. KAP score interpretation

Mean scores ≥60% indicated good KAP. A score above 70% of the total score was considered good, a score between 50% and 70% was considered moderate, and a score below 50% was considered poor. At the same time, knowledge scores between 50% and 70% were considered moderate knowledge, attitude scores between 50% and 70% were considered neutral attitude, and practice scores above 50% were considered proactive practice.

2.6. Statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 (IBM, Armonk) was used for this study. Continuous data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared by t-test or 1-way ANOVA. The categorical data were presented as n (%) and compared with the chi-square test. Pearson’s correlation analysis was used to examine the relationships between knowledge, attitude, and practice scores. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to examine the relationship between demographic information, KAP. A structural equation model (SEM; IBM SPSS Amos 27.0, IBM Corp., Armonk) was used to verify the hypotheses that knowledge affects attitudes, knowledge affects practices, knowledge also affects practices through attitudes, attitudes affect practices directly, regular exercise affects attitudes and practices, sarcopenia affects knowledge and practices, and monthly income affects practices. The hypothesis that the variables in demographic characteristics information affect KAP was applied to the SEM model. In this analysis, P-values are retained to 3 decimal places, and P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

The mean scores for knowledge were 5.38 ± 2.09 (possible range: 0–10), indicating moderate knowledge (Table 1). Higher knowledge scores were observed in the age between 31 and 35 years old (P = .050), with higher education (P < .001), with a monthly income between 10,000 and 20,000 yuan (P < .001), and with longer gestational age (P < .001; Table 1). The knowledge items with the lowest rates of correct answers were K8 (“Maternal tension may lead to inaccurate fetal ultrasonographic findings,” 14.96%), K5 (“The measurement of abdominal circumference is mainly to assess the physical conditions of pregnant women,” 26.77%), K2 (“Prenatal obstetrician’s palpation is mainly to feel the fetal heartbeat,” 38.39%), K10 (“Prenatal ultrasonography should be performed every 4–6 weeks,” 56.89%), K1 (“Prenatal fetal size assessment can be made with ultrasonography,” 58.27%), and K6 (“Umbilical blood flow measurement is mainly to check placental function,” 59.84%; Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q665).

The mean attitude score was 34.81 ± 3.16 (possible range: 9–45), indicating neutral attitudes toward pregnant ultrasound (Table 1). The attitude scores were higher with higher education (P = .003), and with gestational weight gain between 5 and 9.9 kg (P = .046; Table 1). The items with the highest proportions of disagreement were A6 (“You trust the ultrasonic findings of prenatal fetal size assessment”), A9 (“Do you think ultrasound is more accurate in assessing fetal weight than doctor’s palpation”), and A8 (“Do you think repeated ultrasonography in the third trimester is necessary”; Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q665).

The mean practice score was 12.42 ± 2.46 (possible range: 3–15), indicating that pregnant women were proactive in their practice of prenatal screening (Table 1). Higher education was associated with higher practice scores (P < .001; Table 1). Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q665 presents the 3 items scored using a 5-point Likert scale. Most participants reported having 9 to 12 prenatal checkups during pregnancy (item P4) and 3 to 5 fetal size assessments (item P5).

Table 2 shows the Pearson correlation analyses of KAP. The knowledge scores were correlated to the attitude (r = 0.158, P < .001) and practice (r = 0.239, P < .001) scores. The attitude scores were correlated to the practice scores (r = 0.430, P < .001).

4. Discussion

4.1. Main findings

Several studies reported different levels of KAP toward ultrasound screening in pregnant women,[17–21] but the available data regarding Chinese women remain insufficient.[22] Therefore, this study investigated the maternal KAP toward fetal size assessment among pregnant Chinese women. The results strongly suggest that the knowledge of pregnant women toward fetal size assessment is poor, but they have a favorable attitude and active practice of fetal size assessment. Hence, health literacy activities should be implemented to promote the knowledge of pregnant women regarding fetal size assessment.

4.2. Interpretation

Several studies around the globe examined the KAP of pregnant women toward prenatal ultrasound screening. Salmoukas et al[17] (Germany, 2016) reported that >80% of women thought that it was important to screen for chromosomal abnormalities and guarantee normal fetal growth, showing a good background knowledge. Gonzaga et al[18] (Uganda, 2009) reported that women had favorable attitudes toward prenatal ultrasound screening, but that the knowledge about the safety, purposes, and limitations of ultrasound screening should be emphasized. Islam[19] (Bangladesh, 2018) reported a poor KAP of prenatal ultrasound among women. A study (Ethiopia, 2022) reported poor knowledge but favorable attitudes among women.[20] In Romania (2018), the knowledge about the available tests for the prenatal screening of Down syndrome was almost nonexistent.[21] In China (2020), a study in Shenzhen reported poor knowledge about noninvasive prenatal testing.[22] A study in Zhejiang Province, China (2025), showed that pregnant women had limited knowledge but favorable attitudes and proactive practices regarding fetal growth restriction. In the present study (2022), the women had moderate knowledge of prenatal ultrasound screening, and their attitude was neutral with proactive practice. Therefore, these results suggest that women follow the advice of their healthcare providers almost blindly. Such trust has been reported in the literature.[26,27] The present study highlights that the health literacy of pregnant women regarding prenatal fetal size assessment, the aim of obstetricians’ palpations, abdominal circumference measurement, umbilical blood flow measurement, maternal tension, and the frequency of prenatal ultrasounds should be particularly emphasized in future teaching programs for pregnant women.

In the present study, higher education and higher income were generally associated with better KAP. It aligns with the known association between socioeconomic status and healthcare literacy in different populations.[28–30] Evidence from recent studies shows that pregnant women with higher levels of education tend to have more favorable attitudes and adopt better practices concerning fetal growth restriction and fetal size assessment. Educational attainment influences health literacy, self-monitoring, engagement with prenatal care, and responsiveness to health information campaigns. For instance, women with more education are more likely to understand the importance, methods, and signs of fetal growth assessment.[31,32] Higher income is positively associated with improved health behaviors and health service utilization across pregnancy-related topics. Income can facilitate access to quality prenatal care, newer technologies (like specialized ultrasonography), and educational resources. However, some studies have found the association between income and perinatal health to be less robust than that with education, especially after adjusting for confounders.[32,33] Several studies report that lower maternal education and socioeconomic disadvantage are associated with a higher risk of small-for-gestational-age births and preterm birth, highlighting the broader role of socioeconomic status in fetal well-being. Addressing these disparities through targeted educational and support interventions could improve KAP and perinatal outcomes.[31,34,35] Educational interventions (e.g., pamphlets, videos, group sessions) substantially improve KAP, particularly among women with lower baseline education.[31] Multimodal and culturally relevant education is effective for both knowledge acquisition and sustainable practice change.[36] Enhancing both education and economic support for pregnant women can strengthen overall perinatal care quality and outcomes.[32,35]

4.3. Strengths and limitations

This study had limitations. It was performed at a single center, limiting the generalizability of the results. Indeed, KAP surveys are a snapshot of a specific population at a precise moment, limiting their generalizability. In addition, the design of the survey items is often influenced by the local practice and the experience of the physicians. The education level of the participants was relatively high, which does not represent the general Chinese population and indicates some selection bias.

5. Conclusions

The knowledge of pregnant women regarding fetal size assessment is moderate, but they generally have a neutral attitude and are proactive in their practice of fetal size assessment. Hence, health literacy activities should be implemented to promote the knowledge of pregnant women regarding fetal size assessment. The KAP toward novel technologies for assisting with fetal size assessment should also be specifically explored.

Robotic posterior leaflet augmentation for postinfarction functional mitral valve regurgitation
Authors: Wei C Lau, Rakesh M Suri, George M Comas, John R Young, Hannah Walmsley-Solt, Richard J Bloomingdale
Published: 2026

Robotic posterior leaflet augmentation restores postinfarction regurgitation.

Central Message

Robotic segmental posterior leaflet detachment and reconstruction with patch augmentation restores height and coaptation in postinfarction posterior leaflet tethering while preserving the native mitral valve.

Functional mitral regurgitation after inferior myocardial infarction is driven by adverse left ventricular remodeling, apical papillary muscle displacement, and posterior leaflet tethering. Isolated restrictive annuloplasty may leave the causal restriction uncorrected, contributing to recurrent regurgitation and to the historic preference for chordal-sparing replacement in severe ischemic mitral regurgitation. For young patients, prosthetic replacement imposes lifelong tradeoffs, including anticoagulation, structural valve degeneration, and future reintervention. This report describes a robotic, segment-targeted evolution of posterior leaflet detachment, augmentation, and reconstruction previously described to reestablish coaptation in functional mitral regurgitation.1

Patient and Operative Strategy

A 42-year-old man with previous inferior ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction and right coronary artery stenting had severe functional mitral regurgitation. Left ventricular ejection fraction was 45%, no significant comorbidities, and on optimized heart failure guideline directed medical therapy. Echocardiography (Figure 1) and gated 3D computed tomography (Figure 2) demonstrated restricted posterior leaflet and chordal motion with an A2 (anterior leaflet, middle segment)-P2 (posterior leaflet, middle scallop) coaptation gap. Coronary angiography showed a nonobstructed patent right coronary stent. After multidisciplinary review, a highly durable definitive surgical repair was selected which has previously been shown to preserve native valve function and address subvalvar leaflet tethering caused by adverse ischemic left ventricular remodeling. Institutional review board approval was not required; patient verbal and written consent for the publication was obtained.

Surgical Technique

Intraoperative transesophageal echocardiography confirmed left ventricular dilatation, P2/P3 (posterior leaflet, medial scallop) tethering, relative anterior leaflet override, loss of coaptation, and severe posteriorly directed functional mitral regurgitation (Video 1). A port-based robotic approach was used. Venous drainage was established with right internal jugular and femoral venous cannulation; arterial inflow was through the right femoral artery. After transthoracic crossclamp and cardioplegic arrest, the left atrium was opened and the mechanism of mitral regurgitation confirmed. The posterior mitral leaflet was sharply detached from the annular base across the tethered P2/P3 segment. An oval bovine pericardial patch, twice as long as it was wide, was tailored to restore posterior leaflet height and excursion without excessive redundancy. The patch was implanted with running 4-0 polypropylene, reconstructing the leaflet base and advancing the coaptation surface toward the anterior leaflet. A 28-mm posterior annuloplasty band was placed from trigone to trigone and secured robotically. Saline testing at systemic root pressure demonstrated competence.

Results

Postrepair transeophageal echocardiography demonstrated broad coaptation between the anterior and augmented posterior leaflets, no residual mitral regurgitation, and a mean transvalvular gradient of 5 mm Hg. (Figure 3) Crossclamp time was 83 minutes. The patient was extubated in the operating room, transferred from the intensive care unit after 36 hours, discharged home on postoperative day 3, and resumed normal activity. Echocardiography performed 30 days after the procedure demonstrated stable repair function with trivial residual MR.

Comment

This operation addresses a distinct gap between transcatheter edge-to-edge repair and prosthetic valve replacement. Randomized data support transcatheter repair for selected older patients with heart failure and secondary mitral regurgitation but does not address the need for durable native mitral valve reconstruction option in younger patients.2, 3, 4 Posterior leaflet augmentation addresses type IIIb ischemic mitral regurgitation by increasing posterior leaflet height, surface area and mobility, reducing anterior leaflet override, and restores normal leaflet coaptation while avoiding valve stenosis caused by aggressive undersizing of the mitral annuloplasty.5 The robotic platform conveys benefits associated with 8 mm port-access exposure and avoidance of rib spreading, allowing precise patch suturing, and rapid recovery. Technical safeguards include strict mechanism confirmation, avoidance of patch oversizing, saline testing at systemic pressure, annuloplasty sized to avoid systolic anterior motion or stenosis, and longitudinal echocardiographic surveillance for recurrent tethering, patch degeneration, calcification, or elevated gradient.

Conclusions

Robotic segmental posterior mitral leaflet patch augmentation is a mechanism-directed extension of a previously published reconstructive concept1 that can be performed in high-volume experienced centers in selected young patients with postinfarction posterior leaflet tethering. This approach preserves the native valve, restores coaptation, and avoids the lifetime liabilities of mitral valve replacement.

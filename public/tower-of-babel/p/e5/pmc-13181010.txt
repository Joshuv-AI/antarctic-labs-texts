An expectile-based framework for risk-calibrated credible capacity evaluation of virtual power plants under wind and PV forecast uncertainties
Authors: Dong Hua, Jihan Zeng, Qinglin Lin, Shunju Cui, Yiqing Liu, Yuzhan Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs renewable penetration continues to increase, virtual power plants (VPPs) are required to evolve from energy aggregators into high-confidence capacity providers capable of delivering firm commitments under uncertainty. The stochastic and asymmetric characteristics of wind and photovoltaic (PV) generation challenge conventional deterministic margins and chance-constrained formulations, which often lead either to excessive conservatism or elevated overcommitment risk. To address this limitation, this paper develops an expectile-based modeling framework for credible capacity evaluation of aggregated wind-PV-storage VPPs. The proposed approach explicitly captures the tail behavior of forecast errors and embeds statistical confidence directly into operational decision-making. In contrast to value-at-risk (VaR) and conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) formulations, expectile modeling introduces a continuous and convex risk measure that asymmetrically penalizes downside deviations, thereby enabling smoother and more interpretable risk calibration. The framework consists of two integrated components. First, a stochastic forecasting and uncertainty quantification module combines quantile regression and asymmetric expectile regression to generate site-specific predictive distributions. Second, a risk-adjusted optimization layer co-schedules renewable commitments and battery dispatch subject to expectile-consistent constraints. Through epigraph reformulations and asset-level derating mechanisms, the model preserves convexity and computational scalability without requiring restrictive distributional assumptions. Case studies conducted on a regional VPP portfolio demonstrate that the proposed method achieves tighter and more economically efficient commitment bounds than CVaR-based benchmarks. Specifically, commitment shortfall events are reduced by up to 73% relative to baseline quantile approaches, while maintaining up to 95% of achievable revenue across varying market conditions. The results further reveal that battery dispatch decisions naturally concentrate during periods of heightened statistical exposure, confirming the value of integrating tail-sensitive forecasting with dynamic storage coordination. Overall, the study establishes a unified, risk-calibrated, and practically implementable framework for credible capacity modeling in renewable-dominated power systems, supporting reliable participation of VPPs in capacity and reserve markets.Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The accelerating integration of wind and photovoltaic (PV) energy into power systems has brought about a fundamental shift in the way generation adequacy, dispatch feasibility, and market commitments are conceptualized1. Rather than relying solely on centralized, controllable resources, system operators now face the challenge of managing portfolios dominated by distributed, stochastic, and weather-dependent generation2. To confront this complexity, the Virtual Power Plant (VPP) has emerged as a transformative framework: one that aggregates heterogeneous assets such as wind farms3, solar arrays, battery storage units, and responsive loads into a single, market-facing entity capable of offering flexibility, reliability, and profitability4. Yet, with this aggregation comes a pivotal question–how much capacity can a VPP credibly commit to deliver, especially in the face of joint renewable uncertainty and non-negligible tail risks5? This credible capacity–defined as the amount of dispatchable power a VPP can guarantee with high statistical confidence–has traditionally been assessed through system-level reliability indices such as capacity credit or effective load carrying capability6. While these metrics remain useful in long-term planning, they offer limited resolution at the operational scale where real-time dispatch7, market bidding, and scenario-aware risk trade-offs take place8,9. Moreover, such methods often assume independence across assets, ignore storage dynamics10, or treat uncertainty through overly simplified margins11. A more refined understanding is needed: one that recognizes the fine-grained, correlated, and occasionally extreme behavior of renewable output, while embedding that uncertainty within an optimization framework that can inform actionable decisions12.

Several strands of research have contributed to the evolution of uncertainty-aware decision-making for VPPs. Probabilistic forecasting techniques have seen significant advances13, with models such as quantile regression forests, Bayesian neural networks, and copula-based systems enabling the estimation of full predictive distributions for wind and PV at multiple sites14. These predictive distributions have laid the foundation for more sophisticated stochastic optimization approaches15. In particular, chance-constrained formulations have enabled probabilistic guarantees in dispatch scheduling by bounding the violation probability of commitment constraints16. Yet, the utility of such models is often hampered by assumptions of Gaussianity, unimodality, or linearity–assumptions that break down under real-world operational volatility, especially when faced with compound weather anomalies or spatial extremes17. In response to these limitations, the field has explored alternative representations of uncertainty and risk. Distributionally robust optimization (DRO) has gained traction for its ability to hedge against sampling noise and distributional misspecification18,19, typically by optimizing over ambiguity sets defined via Wasserstein distance or -divergence20. While powerful in their worst-case orientation, DRO methods can become overly conservative, and their complexity may limit usability in fast-moving operational settings21. Similarly, Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) has been used to target expected losses in tail scenarios, offering tractable formulations but requiring explicit partitioning of risk regions that may lack interpretability or continuity22. In contrast, the present paper proposes a fundamentally different approach to credible capacity evaluation: one based on expectile modeling23. Expectiles generalize the idea of quantiles and CVaR by penalizing deviations asymmetrically around a central reference point, enabling smooth, convex, and risk-sensitive formulations24. Here, expectiles serve not merely as post-hoc statistical summaries, but as embedded decision variables within the optimization framework, guiding the allocation of credible dispatch while allowing fine-grained control over downside risk25. This choice allows the model to treat risk not as a hard constraint or ambiguity set, but as a continuous parameter shaping the trade-off between commitment and conservatism26.

To operationalize the proposed formulation, a unified modeling architecture is developed that tightly integrates probabilistic forecasting, joint uncertainty representation, and risk-aware optimization within a single coherent framework. High-resolution renewable forecasts are generated using data-driven models that incorporate local meteorological inputs, spatial correlations among sites, and temporal lag structures to capture short-term dynamics. The marginal predictive distributions of wind and PV outputs are coupled through parametric copula functions, enabling the preservation of cross-asset dependencies and tail co-movements across geographically distributed resources. To ensure robustness against compound meteorological extremes, a stress-enhanced scenario generation mechanism is introduced by blending empirical forecast distributions with extreme-value-informed surrogate models. This mixture structure enriches the tail behavior of the scenario set, allowing credible capacity assessment to remain resilient not only under typical variability but also under rare yet impactful events such as prolonged wind deficits or clustered overcast conditions. Energy storage systems are modeled with detailed operational realism, including intertemporal state-of-charge dynamics, ramp-rate constraints, round-trip efficiency losses, and degradation-aware penalties. This physically grounded representation enables the optimization layer to jointly coordinate renewable commitments, energy shifting, reserve provision, and reliability support in a fully time-coupled manner. In contrast to approaches that sequentially separate forecasting, scenario construction, and scheduling, the present framework preserves structural coherence across all layers, ensuring that uncertainty characterization directly informs operational decision-making. Within this integrated structure, credible capacity is not determined by exogenous safety margins but emerges endogenously from the optimization problem. An expectile-consistent constraint is embedded to control the asymmetric weighted squared deviations between scenario-wise realizations and target commitments, thereby linking empirical reliability targets to the statistical shape of the uncertainty distribution in a smooth and convex manner.

This study advances risk-aware virtual power plant (VPP) operation along several integrated and mutually reinforcing dimensions. First, it develops an expectile-centered modeling framework that provides a smooth, convex, and continuously adjustable representation of downside risk, offering a theoretically grounded alternative to conventional CVaR-based and distributionally robust formulations in credible capacity evaluation. By embedding asymmetric risk sensitivity directly within the optimization structure, the proposed approach enables transparent calibration between commitment aggressiveness and reliability protection without sacrificing computational tractability. Second, the framework tightly integrates high-resolution probabilistic forecasting with copula-based joint stochastic modeling, explicitly preserving spatial correlations and tail co-movements among geographically distributed renewable assets. This dependence-aware representation enhances realism beyond marginal uncertainty modeling and strengthens the credibility of aggregated capacity estimation. Third, a stress-informed scenario blending mechanism is introduced to systematically incorporate extreme meteorological regimes into the scenario space, ensuring that compound low-generation events and tail dependencies are not underestimated in reliability assessment. Fourth, the study embeds a degradation-aware and dynamically co-optimized storage formulation that coordinates energy shifting, risk buffering, and operational feasibility in a unified structure. Collectively, these elements establish a coherent, scalable, and practically implementable framework for credible capacity evaluation under real-world renewable uncertainty, supporting interpretable and performance-driven participation of VPPs in capacity and reserve markets.

To quantify the credible capacity of virtual power plants (VPPs) under uncertain renewable generation, we construct a mathematical formulation grounded in asymmetric risk theory. This section introduces the core optimization framework, where the objective integrates market revenue, operational penalties, and an expectile-based risk-adjusted term that reflects the asymmetric cost of capacity shortfalls. Constraints are imposed to capture the physical limits of assets, energy storage behaviors, and statistical reliability margins derived from renewable forecast distributions. The complete model is detailed below. This formulation represents a risk-aware profit maximization problem for a virtual power plant (VPP) over the scheduling horizon , explicitly accounting for market revenues, operational penalties, storage degradation, and downside financial risk. The first component aggregates economic returns obtained from participation in both day-ahead and ancillary service markets, governed by the price signals and , and scaled by participation coefficients and for each asset . The second component penalizes renewable energy curtailment and delivery deficits through penalty factors and , associated respectively with curtailed generation and unmet commitments . The third component models battery wear through a quadratic cycling cost for storage units , where the discharging and charging magnitudes and are normalized by efficiency parameters and , and scaled by the degradation factor . The final component introduces an expectile-based risk adjustment mechanism in which only negative deviations satisfying are penalized. These deviations are weighted by scenario probabilities and controlled by the risk sensitivity coefficient . This construction enforces protection against unfavorable outcomes while preserving smooth convexity, thereby enabling computationally tractable optimization under uncertainty.

This constraint defines the expectile condition for each time period , quantifying how far scenario-wise profits deviate from the expectile threshold , scaled asymmetrically. If the net profit is higher than the expectile (i.e. upside), the deviation is weighted by ; otherwise, the deviation is penalized more heavily by , reflecting a stronger preference against financial underperformance. The total expected squared deviation is then constrained by a tolerance parameter , effectively regulating the distributional tail dispersion and enforcing reliability in profit planning. The formulation preserves convexity and tractability while enabling smooth risk modeling without relying on discrete quantiles or non-smooth CVaR-type structures. This makes the model suitable for robust multi-period decision-making in systems with volatile renewable output and market signals.

The total dispatchable output of the virtual power plant at any time step must satisfy a detailed power balance expression. This includes contributions from wind energy , photovoltaic generation , and battery discharging ; losses through battery charging demand , voluntary curtailment , net grid import/export , and system reserve provisions . The entire sum must equal the committed dispatch , thereby ensuring that supply-side variability, flexibility, and market interface actions are jointly coordinated to meet VPP delivery targets.

Credible capacity must be no greater than the dispatch quantity only if, under the empirical joint distribution of renewable generation and battery output, the probability of delivering at least that capacity is no less than the reliability threshold . To enforce this, an embedded indicator structure evaluates the fraction of scenarios in which the sum of scenario-wise available wind, PV, and net battery discharge meets or exceeds . The outermost constraint then guarantees that the deterministic dispatch level is at least as large as the capacity that can be guaranteed with statistical confidence, linking dispatch feasibility with probabilistic performance guarantees under renewable uncertainty.

Rather than approximating reliability via deterministic bounds, this constraint directly imposes a chance constraint across the full scenario set. The left-hand side counts the fraction of scenarios where the available net power–sum of wind, solar, and battery support–is insufficient to meet the target credible commitment . This empirical frequency must not exceed the risk tolerance , providing a distributionally faithful link between probabilistic reliability and dispatch capacity. The formulation enables full joint treatment of uncertainty and respects compound tail behaviors across renewable sources and flexible storage.

Wind power availability for each generator under scenario and time is upper bounded by a nonlinear meteorological transfer function , which maps forecast wind speed , air density , extreme event indicators , temperature derates , and vertical wind shear coefficients into scenario-specific upper bounds. This formulation captures the complex, nonlinear nature of wind energy conversion under normal and extreme operating conditions.

Available photovoltaic output is modeled through a surrogate function , accounting for solar irradiance , cell temperature , storm obstruction effect , inverter efficiency , and optical attenuation via cloud cover . This structure reflects empirical nonlinearities in solar panel performance under compound weather uncertainty, including extreme overcast and heat-related clipping events.

State-of-charge dynamics for storage unit evolve through standard energy balance, adjusted by charge/discharge efficiencies and . Each time period’s SoC depends on its previous value, the amount of energy charged, and the energy discharged, with nonideal conversion explicitly included. This intertemporal relationship governs feasibility of energy shifting across time, and acts as a coupling backbone across operational periods.

Operating bounds ensure that at any time step, battery power flows stay within inverter rating limits , and energy stored remains within minimum and maximum state-of-charge thresholds . These inequalities enforce hard technical feasibility, and represent design-level limitations of storage modules. Combined with the dynamic update constraint, they ensure that state evolution is always physically realizable.

Storage ramping is regulated by introducing time-differenced constraints on charge and discharge variables, limited by a maximum rate . These constraints preserve the smoothness of energy exchange and prevent unrealistic operating behavior between successive time intervals. Particularly important under extreme conditions and market signals, ramp limits also reflect protection policies embedded in converter hardware.

Charging and discharging cannot occur simultaneously, nor can their total exceed the inverter’s throughput capacity or exceed a scaled fraction of the usable SoC range, reflecting internal protection constraints. This upper bound couples energy exchange and storage range constraints, further reinforcing physical plausibility and converter-aware operation of battery systems.

Reserve provision must lie within the flexible headroom and footroom of participating units , determined by their discharging potential and available curtailment capacity . This constraint ensures that upward or downward reserves are only offered when sufficient physical margin exists to supply energy or absorb it, guaranteeing truthful reserve provisioning while respecting real-time flexibility boundaries.

Grid import/export for asset is constrained within the interconnection limit , which may reflect line ratings, transformer bottlenecks, or power exchange agreements. Bidirectional flow is permitted, but subject to the limits of regional intertie capacity, preventing network congestion and maintaining compliance with hosting capabilities of distribution feeders or regional TSOs.

Shortfall for generator under scenario and time arises when the sum of stochastic wind, solar, and net battery discharge is insufficient to meet the credible dispatch . This constraint defines the shortfall value as the nonnegative gap between availability and commitment, thus enforcing scenario-consistent underperformance evaluation which later feeds into penalty costs in the objective.

The aggregated net dispatch of all VPP assets must match the aggregation node’s declared power output . This enforces internal consistency between the optimization-level asset dispatch and the external system-level bid quantity. The constraint also supports multi-layer coordination, ensuring that aggregation logic in the optimization model is synchronized with what the VPP actually communicates to the system operator or market platform.

Methodology

Figure 2 illustrates the overall workflow of the proposed scenario generation and reduction framework used for uncertainty-aware VPP operation. The process begins with marginal probabilistic forecasting, where wind and photovoltaic generation are predicted using data-driven models to produce predictive intervals that capture temporal variability and local meteorological uncertainty. These marginal distributions alone, however, cannot represent spatial dependence across geographically distributed assets. Therefore, the second stage constructs joint realizations through copula-based sampling, preserving both marginal statistics and cross-site correlations, including tail dependence structures that are critical for reliability assessment. To further capture operational risks beyond historical observations, a stress-enhanced scenario blending stage is introduced. In this step, empirical forecast distributions are combined with extreme-weather surrogate patterns representing prolonged low-wind conditions or spatially correlated irradiance drops. This mechanism enriches the tail behavior of the scenario set and prevents systematic underestimation of compound meteorological risks. Because a large number of generated trajectories would lead to excessive computational burden in optimization, the final stage performs scenario reduction through clustering and representative selection. The reduction preserves statistical characteristics and risk exposure while significantly decreasing computational complexity. The resulting representative scenario set provides a balanced compromise between fidelity and tractability, ensuring that both typical variability and rare but critical extreme events are consistently reflected in the subsequent risk-aware optimization model.

The upper layer summarizes the forecasting and scenario generation process, where wind and PV outputs are predicted using probabilistic models and then combined through copula-based joint sampling to preserve cross-site dependence and tail co-movement. A stress-enhanced blending mechanism is further introduced to enrich the scenario set with extreme-weather surrogates, after which scenario reduction selects a representative subset to balance statistical fidelity and computational tractability. The resulting scenario set is then passed to the two-stage optimization module. In Stage 1 (day-ahead scheduling), the VPP determines market commitments and bidding decisions under risk calibration, capturing the economic benefits of market participation while accounting for asymmetric downside exposure. In Stage 2 (real-time adjustment), dispatch actions are updated conditional on scenario realizations to mitigate delivery shortfalls and enforce reliability-oriented constraints, while co-optimizing storage flexibility and operational feasibility. Overall, the figure highlights the end-to-end coherence of the workflow and shows how risk-aware commitments emerge from the integration of forecasting, scenario modeling, and sequential decision-making.

Two-stage workflow of the proposed framework.

The proposed methodology consists of a multi-layered architecture combining probabilistic forecasting, statistical risk quantification, and optimization-driven commitment evaluation. First, renewable generation forecasts are transformed into expectile distributions using asymmetric learning techniques. These expectiles serve as the basis for defining confidence-calibrated capacity envelopes. Second, asset-level uncertainty is modeled through marginal and joint distributions, enabling differentiated risk buffering across spatially distributed units. Finally, the expectile-based credible capacity is computed via convex optimization, where storage dispatch is co-optimized to dynamically compensate for deviations. Each step of the pipeline is elaborated in the following subsections.

Wind power predictive distribution for site at time is denoted , defined as the cumulative probability of available wind power being less than or equal to . The right-hand side uses an integral form, where represents a Bayesian LSTM model fitted over meteorological drivers, including forecast wind speed , temperature , atmospheric pressure , autoregressive lags , and site-specific topographic features . This nonparametric probabilistic forecast enables robust, time-varying modeling of renewable uncertainty.

The cumulative predictive distribution captures the marginal uncertainty in photovoltaic generation at site , time . The forecasting model is constructed via quantile regression forest or hybrid physics-ML surrogate, taking as input solar irradiance , PV cell temperature , cloud optical depth , storm obstruction metric , and meta-features such as panel tilt, shading index, and horizon obstruction. Together, they produce nonparametric, site-calibrated predictive quantiles essential for scenario generation.

Joint dependence between wind and solar availability is modeled via copula-based distribution , constructed by applying a parametric copula function to the marginal predictive distributions . The parameter vector is learned via maximum likelihood or inference functions for margins (IFM), and can be selected from the Gaussian, Student-t, Clayton, or Frank families depending on tail dependence properties. This construct permits sampling and stress-testing under realistic, non-independent joint uncertainty.

Tail dependence in the joint distribution of wind and PV is quantified by the upper tail dependence coefficient , measuring the conditional probability that wind output exceeds its high quantile given that PV output is also extremely high. This statistic is crucial in assessing whether the VPP benefits from diversification or suffers from joint failure risk in extreme operating regimes. High values of suggest that tail risks are shared, and independent modeling would dangerously underestimate simultaneous shortfalls.

A mixed joint distribution is created by blending the empirical copula model with an extreme-event copula , weighted by a stress-factor . The resulting mixture accounts for the observed distribution under normal conditions while embedding rare but plausible dependencies during compound weather extremes. Scenario generation from this blended distribution supports risk-aware evaluation by enriching the tail structure used in chance constraints or expectile constraints downstream.

Results

The case study is conducted using real-world data collected from a regionally distributed Virtual Power Plant (VPP) cluster located in eastern China, designed to emulate realistic operational characteristics and uncertainty structures. The VPP consists of five wind farms, four utility-scale PV stations, and two lithium-ion battery storage systems, all geographically dispersed within a radius. Each wind farm has an installed capacity ranging from to , while each PV plant ranges between and . The two battery storage units each have a power capacity of and an energy capacity of , with a round-trip efficiency of 92% and degradation coefficient of €0.001/kWh per cycle. The dataset spans a full calendar year (Jan 1 to Dec 31, 2023) with a 15-minute resolution, resulting in 35,040 time steps and over 1 million operational records across all assets. Historical wind speed, solar irradiance, ambient temperature, and humidity are sourced from the ERA5 reanalysis dataset, downscaled to resolution using bilinear interpolation. Forecast variables include ensemble wind predictions at hub height and PV output from satellite-derived Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI), both updated every 6 hours. These environmental drivers feed into the machine learning forecasting modules developed for scenario generation.

For wind and PV forecasting, a two-stage learning pipeline is employed. The first stage constructs site-specific quantile regression forests (QRF) for each asset, trained on a sliding window of 60 days, yielding -level marginal predictive distributions. In the second stage, spatial dependence among the assets is captured using Student-t copulas, fitted using maximum pseudo-likelihood estimation. Each time step produces 2,000 joint scenarios, which are reduced to 300 representative samples via k-medoids clustering on the joint CDF distance matrix. Stress-enhanced scenarios are generated by injecting synthetic tail events using a GPD-fitted EVT sampler, blended with empirical copula samples using a convex combination weighting factor of . Battery degradation is modeled based on depth-of-discharge-dependent wear, and operational feasibility is ensured by enforcing state-of-charge dynamics and ramp limits consistent with commercial-grade systems. All constraints and modeling parameters reflect real asset configurations obtained from a provincial utility partner and verified against Chinese National Energy Administration (NEA) benchmarks.

All simulations are implemented in Python 3.11 using Pyomo for optimization modeling and Gurobi 11.0.1 as the solver backend. Forecasting modules are built using Scikit-learn, PyTorch, and Statsmodels, while copula estimation and EVT sampling are conducted via the copulas, SciPy, and ExtremeFit packages. Computations are performed on a 64-core AMD EPYC 7543P workstation with 512 GB RAM, hosted on an internal HPC cluster. Each rolling simulation horizon covers 7 days forward, updated every 24 hours (i.e. daily receding horizon re-optimization). Total simulation time for the full-year analysis under all risk levels and method variants (baseline, chance-constrained, expectile-based) is approximately 18.7 hours, and detailed performance logs, intermediate data, and raw outputs are archived for reproducibility. The entire experiment is containerized using Docker v24.0, ensuring that results are fully replicable across environments.

Conclusion

This paper presents a novel expectile-based methodology for evaluating the credible capacity of virtual power plants (VPPs) operating under the forecast uncertainties of wind and photovoltaic (PV) resources. In contrast to conventional techniques such as deterministic margins, chance-constrained programming, or CVaR-based methods, the proposed approach introduces a tail-sensitive yet computationally tractable framework grounded in asymmetric expectile theory. Through a mathematical formulation that penalizes downside deviations more rigorously than upside variability, the model enables risk-aware capacity commitment decisions that are more closely aligned with physical system behavior and market reliability requirements. The methodology integrates multiple layers of innovation. First, it constructs expectile-adjusted capacity envelopes directly from predictive distributions rather than relying on point forecasts, thereby internalizing statistical confidence into dispatch and bidding strategies. Second, it adopts an epigraph reformulation to embed expectile logic within a convex optimization structure, avoiding sampling-based approximations or ambiguity-set reformulations. Third, the framework supports asset-level derating and time-varying flexibility, allowing commitment margins to reflect heterogeneous uncertainty characteristics across resources. Finally, storage units are co-optimized as active reliability-enhancing agents that dynamically compensate forecast deviations, rather than functioning as passive buffers.

Comprehensive simulation studies conducted on a regional hybrid system comprising multiple wind farms, PV plants, and battery storage units demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the framework. Results indicate that the expectile-based model consistently outperforms quantile-based and CVaR-based benchmarks in both operational reliability and economic efficiency. Specifically, commitment violation rates are reduced by up to 73% while retaining up to 95% of potential revenue across different market price scenarios. The analysis further reveals that storage dispatch decisions systematically concentrate during periods of elevated forecast tail exposure, confirming the importance of integrating risk modeling and intertemporal flexibility within a unified optimization scheme. These findings highlight the value of moving beyond static reliability margins toward dynamically calibrated, distribution-aware capacity evaluation.

Looking forward, several research directions naturally extend from this work. First, future studies may explore the integration of adaptive or learning-based mechanisms for dynamically selecting the expectile level in response to evolving market conditions and real-time forecast accuracy. Second, extending the framework to multi-market participation settings–including energy, reserve, and flexibility markets simultaneously–could further enhance its applicability in complex operational environments. Third, incorporating network constraints and locational marginal pricing structures would enable credible capacity assessment within distribution-level or transmission-constrained systems. Additionally, future research may investigate the interaction between expectile-based risk modeling and emerging technologies such as hydrogen storage, demand response, and vehicle-to-grid systems. Finally, large-scale deployment studies across multiple climatic regions would provide further validation of scalability and robustness under diverse renewable penetration scenarios. Overall, the proposed framework establishes a systematic foundation for tail-sensitive, reliability-calibrated capacity modeling in renewable-dominated power systems, offering a practical and extensible pathway for risk-aware VPP participation in future electricity markets.

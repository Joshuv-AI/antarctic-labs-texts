Where the Jackhammer Meets the Corkscrew: A Case of Manometric Hypercontractile Esophagus and Esophagogastric Junction Outflow Obstruction With Endoscopic Features of Distal Esophageal Spasm
Authors: Nihal Ijaz Khan, Ernesto Sigifredo Robalino Gonzaga, Mariam Naveed, Irteza Inayat
Published: 2025

CASE REPORT

A 70-year-old woman with Parkinson disease and diabetes mellitus with a 3-month history of intermittent solid food dysphagia and heartburn underwent barium esophagraphy, revealing tertiary contractions in the mid-to-distal esophagus (Figure 1). Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) with pH monitoring showed no structural abnormalities and normal acid exposure. High-resolution manometry revealed median integrated relaxation pressure 32.0 mm Hg, 0% premature contractions, 0% intact contractions, and 40% hypercontractile swallows (Figure 2); integrated relaxation pressure elevation persisted during upright swallows, and rapid drink challenge maneuver revealed pressurization. Repeat EGD with Endoflip demonstrated extraesophageal peristaltic contractions producing a classic “corkscrew” (Figure 3) and a distensibility of 1.1 mm2/mm Hg with maximum observed diameter of 10 mm. Diagnosed with hypercontractile esophagus with esophagogastric junction outflow obstruction, she was prescribed calcium channel blockers and referred for per-oral endoscopic myotomy.

Barium esophagram showing moderate tertiary contractions of the mid-to-distal esophagus.

High-resolution manometry composite of 10 swallows showing hypercontractile swallows and elevated lower esophageal sphincter pressures.

Endoscopic image of the distal esophagus showing spastic contractions in a characteristic “corkscrew” pattern.

While distal esophageal spasm typically produces “corkscrew” appearance on barium esophagram and EGD, these findings are uncommon in hypercontractile esophagus.1–3 Note is made of our patient's history of Parkinson disease, which encompasses manometric findings of several esophageal motility disorders from aperistalsis to distal esophageal spasm and esophagogastric junction outflow obstruction.4 This case highlights the importance of integrating clinical, endoscopic, and manometric findings to tailor management of esophageal dysmotility.

Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists in heart failure with sustained monomorphic ventricular tachycardia: comparative analysis of spironolactone versus eplerenone
Authors: Imre Szakál, Ferenc Komlósi, Patrik Tóth, Bence Arnóth, Gyula Bohus, Péter Vámosi, Nándor Szegedi, Zoltán Salló, István Osztheimer, Péter Perge, Gábor Orbán, Attila Kardos, Béla Merkely, László Gellér, Klaudia Vivien Nagy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) are core therapy in chronic heart failure (HF) and reduce all-cause mortality and sudden cardiac death. However, direct comparisons of spironolactone and eplerenone in the context of ventricular arrhythmia recurrence are lacking. This study aims to compare the effects of these two MRAs on all-cause mortality and recurrence of sustained monomorphic ventricular tachycardia (VT) in patients with HF.MethodsWe analysed data of HF patients with left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) below 50%, hospitalised for sustained monomorphic VT and receiving MRA therapy at discharge. We performed propensity score matching for 13 variables, including medical history, medication and arrhythmia presentation. The endpoints were all‐cause mortality and VT recurrence.ResultsAmong the 292 patients who met the selection criteria, 202 were included in the final analysis after propensity score matching, representing 69% of the eligible cohort. Median age was 67 (61–74) years and 87% were male. The median LVEF was 30% (25%–35%). The median follow‐up duration was 32 (18–36) months, with a minimum follow‐up of 1 year. During follow-up, 70 patients died, while VT recurrence was observed in 86 patients. There was no significant difference in all-cause mortality between the two MRAs (HR 0.98 (0.62 to 1.57), p=0.94). However, eplerenone was associated with a significantly lower risk of VT recurrence compared with spironolactone (HR 0.42 (0.26 to 0.66), p<0.001, Fine-Gray adjusted HR 0.36 (0.22 to 0.59), p<0.001).ConclusionsIn HF patients with sustained monomorphic VT, eplerenone was associated with fewer VT recurrences than spironolactone, without differences in all-cause mortality.

Introduction

Sustained monomorphic ventricular tachycardia (SMVT) is a serious arrhythmia in patients with chronic heart failure (HF), typically arising from myocardial scar-related re-entry.1 It is associated with a poor prognosis, often leading to decompensated HF or sudden cardiac death (SCD).2 The available antiarrhythmic drug treatments are predominantly limited to amiodarone, which, while moderately effective, is associated with substantial side effects. In cases of recurrent VT, ablation is considered the gold-standard procedure to prevent further episodes.3 4 Additionally, several components of guideline-directed medical therapy have been shown to reduce the incidence of ventricular arrhythmias.5 However, the specific role of mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) in this context is not yet fully explored.

Current guidelines recommend MRAs in HF patients with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) and consider them in HF with mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF).68 These recommendations are based on clinical trials demonstrating the benefits of spironolactone and eplerenone in reducing mortality and SCD in these patients.911 Although the guidelines do not distinguish between these two drugs, their differing pharmacological profiles have led to several recent comparative studies. These studies suggest that eplerenone treatment evokes greater improvements in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF),12 13 has more favourable metabolic effects14 and results in better outcomes in reducing both cardiovascular and overall mortality compared with spironolactone in patients with HF.15 Given the limited treatment options available for VT, optimising pharmacological therapy of HF is crucial in reducing the risk of ventricular arrhythmias. However, the role of MRAs in the secondary prevention of sustained VT in HF patients remains to be fully defined.

The aim of the present study was to compare the effects of spironolactone and eplerenone on VT recurrence and all-cause mortality in patients with HF who were hospitalised for SMVT.

Methods

Study population

In our observational, single-centre cohort study, we selected patients with HF who were admitted to inpatient care for SMVT and received MRA therapy, between January 2010 and May 2023. The inclusion criteria were documented SMVT on a 12-lead ECG or intracardiac electrograms of a cardiovascular implanted electronic device, diagnosis of chronic HF with an LVEF less than 50% and documented MRA therapy at discharge. Cases of VT within 48 hours of an acute coronary syndrome, polymorphic VT or arrhythmia related to a reversible cause (eg, electrolyte disorders or proarrhythmic medication) were excluded. All patient data were derived from the VT registry developed at the Heart and Vascular Centre of Semmelweis University (Budapest, Hungary). The registry was constructed using electronic health records, medical charts and procedure reports and included medical history, echocardiographic findings and laboratory parameters. Mortality data were obtained from the National Health Insurance Database of Hungary. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the local ethics committee (NNGYK/GYSZ/52312-1/2024).

Follow-up and endpoints

All patients were followed up from the date of hospitalisation for SMVT until 36 months, death, transplant, loss to follow-up or last documented visit with active MRA therapy—whichever occurred first.

We considered all-cause mortality and VT recurrence within 3 years after the index hospitalisation as the endpoints of our study. The minimum follow-up duration was 1 year. VT recurrence was defined as a SMVT episode documented by a 12-lead ECG or an implanted device electrogram, validated by a clinical cardiac electrophysiologist.

Statistical analysis

In our study, we employed propensity score matching to ensure a balanced comparison between the two treatment groups. We applied a nearest-neighbour matching without replacement, using a 1:1 ratio, with a calliper width of 0.2 SD of the logit of the propensity score. We selected 13 key covariates based on their established clinical relevance and their potential to influence treatment allocation and/or the study endpoints, specifically VT recurrence and all-cause mortality. These variables included: age, sex, ischaemic aetiology, prior hospitalisation for sustained VT, history of myocardial infarction, incessant VT or electrical storm, diabetes, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, baseline LVEF, catheter ablation performed during the index hospitalisation, amiodarone, angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor and sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 inhibitor therapy at discharge.5 16 17 A standardised mean difference below 0.1 was considered indicative of negligible difference between the treatment groups for the baseline clinical variables.

Results are presented as the median with IQR for continuous variables and the number of cases with percentages for categorical variables. For the comparison between groups, we used the Mann-Whitney test for continuous variables and the χ2 test for categorical variables. Cox proportional hazards regression was performed to compare the time to the first VT recurrence between the two treatment groups. Clinically relevant variables that showed a significant association with the outcome in the univariable Cox analysis and were not collinear were included as covariates in the multivariable Fine-Gray competing risk regression model. In the analysis, all-cause mortality and heart transplantation were considered competing events. HRs and their corresponding 95% CIs were calculated. Recurrence-free survival was assessed using the Kaplan-Meier method, and group comparisons were performed using the log-rank test. A p value of < 0.05 (two-tailed) was considered statistically significant.

The statistical analysis was performed using Python V.3.10.0 with the SciPy V.1.8.0 and the lifelines V.0.27.0 libraries, while for propensity score matching the PsmPy package was used.1821 STATA V.18.5 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas, USA) was used for Fine-Gray competing risk regression.

Results

Patient characteristics

We identified 704 patients with HF and baseline LVEF <50% who were hospitalised for SMVT, based on our institutional VT registry. Among these patients, 292 (41%) received MRA after hospital discharge and met the selection criteria. From this eligible cohort, after 1:1 propensity score matching, 202 (69%) patients were included in the analysis (figure 1). The matched population was well-balanced, with a standardised mean difference for all covariates being <0.1 (figure 2). There were no further clinically relevant differences between the treatment groups, as presented by the baseline clinical characteristics of the matched population in table 1. The median age of the cohort was 67 (61–74) years, and 87% were male. The majority of patients (67%) experienced their first VT episode associated with the index hospitalisation. The median year of admission was 2018 (2015–2020). Approximately one-third (32%) of the patients presented with multiple VT episodes, defined as cases where the clinical VT occurred on more than one occasion within 24 hours. Most patients (83%) had coronary artery disease, and the median LVEF was 30% (25%–35%). Prior to the clinical admission for VT, 36% of patients were already on amiodarone and 73% were receiving MRA therapy. In nearly all cases (99%), the same MRA agent was continued after discharge. During hospitalisation, 27% of patients underwent catheter ablation for VT and 12% had percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Nearly all patients received optimal medical therapy according to the current guidelines: beta-blockers were prescribed in 97% of cases, while angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin-receptor blocker/angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors were prescribed in 98% of cases. After discharge, 86% of patients were on amiodarone therapy. The median daily dose of MRA was 25 mg for both spironolactone and eplerenone.

Follow-up and endpoints

The 202 propensity score-matched patients were followed up for a median duration of 321836 months. Death from any cause occurred in 36 (36%) cases in the spironolactone group and 34 (34%) in the eplerenone group in the 3-year follow-up. VT recurrence was observed in 59 (58%) patients taking spironolactone and 27 (27%) patients taking eplerenone. Heart transplantation was performed in three (3%) patients treated with spironolactone and two (2%) patients treated with eplerenone. Univariable Cox regression analysis did not show a statistically significant difference in all-cause mortality between the two groups (HR=0.98 (0.62 to 1.57), p=0.94) (figure 3). However, we found that eplerenone therapy significantly lowered the risk of VT recurrence compared with spironolactone (HR=0.42 (0.26 to 0.66), p<0.001) (figure 4). In addition to eplerenone therapy, several other parameters associated with VT recurrence were identified, including date of admission, multiple VT episodes at presentation, history of VT hospitalisation, previous MRA therapy, previous implantable cardioverter defibrillator (ICD) or cardiac resynchronisation therapy (CRT) with defibrillator implantation and ablation or PCI during hospitalisation. The identified univariable predictors were included in the Fine-Gray competing risk regression multivariable model (figure 5). In this model, eplerenone therapy (HR=0.36 (0.22 to 0.59), p<0.001), PCI (HR=0.43 (0.19 to 0.95), p=0.04) and the date of admission (HR=0.92 (0.86 to 0.99), p=0.02) were independent predictors of reduced SMVT recurrence risk, while prior ICD or CRT with defibrillator (CRT-D) implantation was associated with a higher risk of VT recurrence (HR=1.83 (1.03 to 3.25), p=0.04).

Discussion

This study is, to our knowledge, the first propensity score-matched comparison of eplerenone versus spironolactone regarding all-cause mortality and VT recurrence in HF patients with SMVT. We found a clear association between eplerenone therapy and reduced VT recurrence rates. This is a highly prevalent question since recent advancements are limited in antiarrhythmic drug therapy, and catheter ablation is mostly reserved for recurrent or drug-refractory VT.3 4 Furthermore, post-ablation VT recurrence remains an issue and a subject of ongoing research.22 23 Recently, the role of optimal HF therapy in the prevention of malignant arrhythmias is increasingly emphasised. While angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor and sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 inhibitor therapies have demonstrated certain efficacy, the role of MRAs in this field is less defined.5

Large HF trials of MRAs did not specifically address VT recurrence,10 11 and studies regarding secondary VT prevention rarely evaluated the effects of MRA use,24 25 resulting in limited data on the antiarrhythmic effects of different MRAs. Although earlier clinical findings with spironolactone suggested a reduction in ventricular ectopy and non-sustained VT in HF, and MRAs have also been shown to reduce SCD in patients with HF,26 27 a direct comparison between eplerenone and spironolactone regarding arrhythmic outcomes is lacking.

In a recent meta-analysis comparing spironolactone and eplerenone in HF, no significant difference was observed in HFmrEF, but eplerenone was associated with lower all-cause mortality in HFrEF.15 The effect was not consistent across individual studies in HFrEF populations, possibly due to varying ICD prevalence.28 Importantly, in two studies with a low prevalence of ICD use (19% and 11%), eplerenone was associated with a reduced risk of all-cause mortality.12 29 In contrast, a sub-analysis of a German study with 80% ICD prevalence found no difference in mortality between the two MRAs.30 Similarly, Larsson et al31 found no difference in all-cause mortality between the two drugs, although the rate of ICD implantation was not reported in that study.

In our research, which compared spironolactone and eplerenone in a population including both HFmrEF and HFrEF patients, no significant difference in all-cause mortality was observed. Notably, 95% of participants had either an ICD or a CRT-D, which may have contributed to a reduced incidence of SCD and improved detection of VT episodes.

The only prior comparative analysis of the two MRAs in the context of ventricular arrhythmias has only been reported by Schupp et al.30 Their study reported no significant difference in the composite outcome of recurrent ventricular tachyarrhythmias, SCD and appropriate ICD therapy over a 3-year follow-up period. However, this study included patients with polymorphic VT and VF and did not exclude ventricular arrhythmias occurring during the acute phase of myocardial infarction—possibly introducing heterogeneity in arrhythmia mechanism. Furthermore, propensity score matching was not performed, leaving the possibility of residual baseline differences between the groups.

While our observational data cannot establish causality, several pharmacological and physiological differences exist between the two agents, which may explain the observed findings. From the two drugs, eplerenone may lead to improved myocardial function and decreased arrhythmogenic substrate formation. First, experimental studies have shown that eplerenone is a more potent inhibitor of the non-genomic effects of aldosterone.32 33 These rapid effects, which include vasoconstriction, are also observed in coronary arteries.34 35 Enhanced inhibition of this pathway may increase coronary flow and thus prevent further myocardial fibrosis. Second, the two MRAs differ in receptor specificity, as spironolactone also antagonises androgen receptors. As eplerenone is more specific to mineralocorticoid receptors, it preserves the effects of endogenous testosterone. Testosterone deficiency is associated with poorer outcomes in men with HF,36 and antiandrogen therapy has adverse metabolic and cardiovascular effects, leading to an increased risk of myocardial infarction and SCD.37 Therefore, the preservation of androgenic signalling with eplerenone treatment may contribute to improved clinical outcomes in this population. Supporting this hypothesis, an in vitro study by Sánchez-Más et al demonstrated that eplerenone significantly improved myocardial viability compared with spironolactone in a model of induced apoptosis in the presence of supplemental testosterone.38 Additionally, echocardiographic studies comparing the two MRAs demonstrated that while both drugs improved left ventricular parameters within 1 year, eplerenone led to significantly greater improvements across several measures, including LVEF12 13—providing a mechanism for the lower recurrence rate observed in our study.

The above outlined mechanisms may explain some of the demonstrated advantages of eplerenone over spironolactone. Further experiments may further elucidate differences in the molecular effects of the two drugs. Meanwhile, future prospective studies may confirm our findings, opening a new front in the treatment of VT in this patient population.

Limitations

Several limitations merit consideration. Although propensity score matching and multivariable competing-risk analysis minimised baseline imbalance, residual confounding inherent to the retrospective design cannot be excluded. Importantly, propensity score matching can account only for measured covariates and not for unmeasured factors. In addition, the single-centre nature, modest sample size and exclusion of unmatched patients after propensity score matching may limit the generalisability of our findings beyond the matched cohort.3942 Compliance with MRA therapy during the follow-up was assumed based on clinical documentation but not objectively verified. Previous comparative studies clearly show a more favourable side effect profile for eplerenone,15 including a lower incidence of gynaecomastia or menstrual irregularities, which can lead to better treatment adherence compared with spironolactone.43 Notably, our study analysed a predominantly male population, consistent with the gender imbalance commonly observed in most studies on both MRAs and VT.44 45 Because nearly all patients had ICDs or CRT-Ds, mortality differences were likely attenuated, and our primary finding relates to arrhythmic burden rather than survival. Finally, lack of serial laboratory and echocardiographic data precluded assessment of dose-response effects and cardiac remodelling.

Conclusions

In this propensity score-matched cohort of patients with HF and sustained monomorphic VT, eplerenone was associated with a significantly lower risk of VT recurrence compared with spironolactone, without differences in all-cause mortality. These hypothesis-generating findings raise the possibility that eplerenone may offer enhanced arrhythmic protection in this high-risk population. Prospective randomised studies are warranted to confirm these observations and elucidate underlying mechanisms.

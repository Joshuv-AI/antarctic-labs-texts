Safety of additional intravenous acetaminophen/ibuprofen administration in patients undergoing robot-assisted renal surgery with postoperative ketorolac-based patient-controlled analgesia: A retrospective propensity-score-matched analysis
Authors: Sang Hun Song, Soowon Lee, Jung-Hee Ryu, Ah-Young Oh, Young-Tae Jeon, Sung Kyu Hong, Chang-Hoon Koo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeNonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are commonly used for pain management following robot-assisted renal surgery. However, concurrent dual NSAID administration raises concerns regarding an increased risk of acute kidney injury (AKI). This study evaluated the renal safety of adding an intravenous combination of acetaminophen and ibuprofen to ketorolac-based patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) in patients undergoing robot-assisted renal surgery.Materials and MethodsThis retrospective cohort study analyzed 200 adult patients who underwent robot-assisted renal surgery between September 2023 and September 2025. Patients receiving a combination of ketorolac PCA plus acetaminophen/ibuprofen (n=90, treatment group) were compared with those receiving ketorolac PCA alone (n=110, control group). After 1:1 propensity score matching, 72 patients per group were analyzed. The primary outcome was postoperative AKI diagnosed by serum creatinine changes through postoperative day 7 or until discharge, whichever occurred first.ResultsIn the matched cohort, postoperative AKI occurred in 28 patients (38.9%) in the treatment group and 32 patients (44.4%) in the control group, with no significant difference (p=0.499; risk ratio 0.89, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.64–1.24). Serial serum creatinine trajectories were similar between groups. Multivariable logistic regression identified pneumoperitoneum duration as an independent risk factor for AKI (odds ratio 1.02, 95% CI 1.01–1.04, p=0.012), while an acetaminophen/ibuprofen combination was not associated with increased AKI risk (odds ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.41–1.54, p=0.499).ConclusionsAdding intravenous acetaminophen/ibuprofen combination to ketorolac PCA was not significantly associated with postoperative AKI risk in patients who underwent robot-assisted renal surgery.Keywords: Acetaminophen, Acute kidney injury, Ibuprofen, Ketorolac, Robotics

INTRODUCTION

Robot-assisted urological surgeries, including partial nephrectomy, radical nephrectomy, and nephroureterectomy, have become increasingly prevalent in contemporary surgical practice. Although they are minimally invasive, these procedures frequently result in substantial postoperative pain. Multiple mechanisms contribute to the overall pain experience, including tissue trauma at trocar incision sites, peritoneal irritation due to pneumoperitoneum, visceral manipulation, and referred pain [1,2]. Therefore, effective and appropriate pain control is essential to improve postoperative outcomes and patient satisfaction.

Multimodal analgesia has emerged as a fundamental principle of perioperative management [3]. This approach targets multiple pain pathways simultaneously [4], thereby reducing the need for opioids and their associated adverse effects. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) serve as a core component of multimodal analgesia [3]. Among NSAIDs, ketorolac can be administered via patientcontrolled analgesia (PCA) [5]. Furthermore, in terms of renal function preservation following robot-assisted renal surgery, evidence suggests that ketorolac-based PCA provides adequate pain management with a safety profile comparable to opioids [6]. Notably, fixed-dose combination therapy utilizing intravenous acetaminophen and ibuprofen (Maxigesic®; AFT Pharmaceuticals Ltd.) has gained attention as an adjunctive component of multimodal analgesia due to its favorable opioid-sparing effects and improved pain control across various surgery types [7,8].

Given that both ibuprofen and ketorolac are NSAIDs, the concurrent administration of a fixed-dose acetaminophen/ibuprofen combination in patients receiving ketorolacbased PCA raises safety concerns. The effects of NSAIDs are exerted via prostaglandin synthesis inhibition, which may compromise renal autoregulatory mechanisms [9]. The combined use of multiple NSAIDs may therefore pose additive risks. This concern is particularly relevant in patients undergoing renal surgeries, who are especially vulnerable to acute kidney injury (AKI).

To investigate these issues, we conducted a retrospective cohort study to evaluate the renal safety of adding a fixed-dose combination of intravenous acetaminophen/ibuprofen to ketorolac-based PCA in patients undergoing robot-assisted renal surgery.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Ethical approval

This study received approval from the Institutional Review Board of Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (approval number: B-2511-1008-101). Given the retrospective design and use of de-identified clinical data, the requirement for written informed consent was waived.

2. Study design and patient selection

We conducted a retrospective analysis of electronic medical records for adult patients (aged ≥19 years) who underwent robot-assisted renal surgery under general anesthesia between September 2023 and September 2025 at Seoul National University Bundang Hospital. Eligible surgical procedures included partial nephrectomy, radical nephrectomy, and nephroureterectomy. Inclusion criteria were: (1) patients who received postoperative ketorolac-based PCA and (2) those whose serum creatinine (sCr) had been measured both pre- and postoperatively. The exclusion criteria were: (1) admission to the intensive care unit following surgery; (2) pre-existing chronic kidney disease (estimated glomerular filtration rate calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration [eGFR CKD-EPI] equation <60 mL/min/1.73 m2); (3) preoperative dialysis; (4) postoperative opioid-based PCA; and (5) missing perioperative sCr data.

3. Study groups

Patients were stratified into two groups according to their postoperative analgesic management. Patients who received at least one dose of intravenous acetaminophen (1,000 mg) combined with ibuprofen (300 mg) in addition to ketorolac-based PCA were allocated into the treatment group, and those who received ketorolac-based PCA alone were placed in the control group. The PCA composition was determined according to patient age: those aged ≤65 years received 180 mg of ketorolac (bolus 3.6 mg, continuous infusion 3.6 mg/h) and those >65 years received 120 mg (bolus 2.4 mg, continuous infusion 2.4 mg/h), with a lockout interval of 15 minutes.

4. Surgery

Two experienced surgeons performed all procedures. All operations were performed using the da Vinci® robotic surgical system (Intuitive Surgical). Standard oncological principles were followed, and the type of surgery was determined based on tumor characteristics and clinical indications. Partial nephrectomy was performed for nephron preservation whenever technically feasible, and tumor excision was carried out with negative surgical margins and application of renal hilar clamping (warm ischemia). Radical nephrectomy involved complete removal of the affected kidney. Radical nephroureterectomy included radical nephrectomy with excision of the ureter and, whenever possible, a portion of the bladder. A fixed-dose combination of intravenous acetaminophen/ibuprofen was administered postoperatively (day 0 through the day of discharge) based on the discretion of the surgeon and patient preference. Two doses were initially administered and incrementally increased to a maximum of four doses per day (maximum 4 g of acetaminophen and 1,200 mg of ibuprofen).

5. Anesthesia

Standard monitoring included electrocardiography, pulse oximetry, non-invasive blood pressure measurement, and the plethysmographic variability index (PVi) measured by a Rainbow® sensor (Masimo). General anesthesia was induced and maintained using inhalational anesthetics (sevoflurane or desflurane) or total intravenous anesthesia with propofol at the discretion of the attending anesthesiologist. Mechanical ventilation was set with a fraction of inspired oxygen of 0.5 and a tidal volume of 6–8 mL/kg based on ideal body weight to maintain end-tidal carbon dioxide at 30–35 mmHg. Positive end-expiratory pressure was applied at 5 cm H2O. Crystalloid solutions were used as the primary fluids, with colloid administration and vasopressor use (norepinephrine or phenylephrine) as needed to maintain the target mean arterial pressure and optimize renal perfusion during the procedure. Intraoperative fluid management was guided by maintaining PVi ≤13% to optimize intravascular volume status. Blood pressure was maintained within 10% of baseline to ensure adequate hemodynamic stability. Anesthetic depth and neuromuscular blockade were monitored and adjusted to ensure appropriate anesthetic conditions and hemodynamic stability.

6. Data collection

Preoperative variables extracted from medical records included patient demographics (age, sex, height, weight), American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classification, and comorbidities (diabetes mellitus, hypertension). Baseline renal function was assessed using the most recent preoperative sCr measurement and the eGFR CKD-EPI. Intraoperative variables included surgical procedure type, surgeon, anesthesia (inhalational or total intravenous), surgery duration, anesthesia duration, crystalloid volume, estimated blood loss (EBL), and urine output. Postoperative data encompassed length of hospital stay and serial sCr measurements obtained through postoperative day (POD) 7 or until hospital discharge, whichever occurred earlier.

7. Diagnosis of postoperative AKI

The primary outcome was the incidence of postoperative AKI, diagnosed according to the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria (based on sCr changes through POD 7 or until hospital discharge, whichever occurred first). The KDIGO classification comprises three severity stages [10]: (1) sCr elevation of 1.5–1.9 times baseline or an absolute increase of ≥0.3 mg/dL; (2) sCr elevation of 2.0–2.9 times baseline; and (3) sCr elevation of ≥3.0 times baseline, sCr >4.0 mg/dL, or initiation of renal replacement therapy. At our institution, postoperative sCr levels are routinely measured on the morning of POD 1, POD 2, and POD 4 (typical discharge day). Although the KDIGO criteria incorporate urine output parameters, we applied only the sCr-based criteria in this study due to insufficient reliability of urine output documentation in the medical records.

8. Statistical analysis

Propensity score matching was employed to address potential confounding and selection bias inherent in retrospective observational studies. Scores were calculated using logistic regression that incorporated the following covariates: sex, body mass index (BMI), American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classification, diabetes mellitus status, preoperative sCr, preoperative eGFR CKD-EPI, type of surgery, surgeon, surgery duration, EBL, and urine output. Patients in the treatment group were matched to those in the control group at a 1:1 ratio using a nearest-neighbor method with a caliper width of 0.2. Covariate balance between groups was evaluated using absolute standardized differences, with values <0.2 indicating acceptable balance.

Continuous variables were analyzed using Student’s t-test for normally distributed data or the Mann–Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables were compared using a chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to identify risk factors for postoperative AKI. Variables with p<0.1 in univariable analyses were considered for inclusion in the multivariable model. Collinearity among candidate variables was assessed using Pearson correlation coefficient and variance inflation factors (VIFs), with VIF >5 indicating multicollinearity. Statistical analyses were conducted using Jamovi® software (version 2.4.14; The Jamovi Project), with statistical significance defined as p<0.05.

RESULTS

During the study period, 248 patients underwent robot-assisted renal surgery. Of these, 48 were excluded for the following: pre-existing chronic kidney disease (n=29), use of opioid-based PCA (n=17), postoperative intensive care unit care (n=1), and age <19 years (n=1), resulting in 200 patients for the final analysis. Among these, 90 received a fixed-dose combination of intravenous acetaminophen/ibuprofen in addition to ketorolac-based PCA (treatment group), while 110 did not receive the combination therapy (control group). After 1:1 propensity score matching, 72 patients in each group were successfully matched (Fig. 1), and all baseline characteristics achieved an acceptable balance with an absolute standardized difference <0.2 (Table 1). Baseline renal function was comparable between groups, with median sCr of 0.86 mg/dL (interquartile range [IQR] 0.74–0.97) in the treatment group and 0.86 mg/dL (IQR 0.75–1.02) in the control group. The median eGFR CKD-EPI was 90.7 mL/min/1.73 m2 (IQR 81.8–99.1) and 92.0 mL/min/1.73 m2 (IQR 78.4–101.0), in the treatment and control groups, respectively (p=0.995). The median number of the fixed-dose combination of intravenous acetaminophen/ibuprofen administrations in the treatment group was 8 (IQR 3.75–8) per patient.

In the matched cohort, postoperative AKI occurred in 28 patients (38.9%) in the treatment group and 32 patients (44.4%) in the control group, with no statistically significant difference between groups (p=0.499; risk ratio 0.89, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.64–1.24). The severity distribution of AKI also showed no significant difference between groups. All cases in the treatment group were Stage 1, while the control group had 30 Stage 1 cases (41.7%) and 2 Stage 2 cases (2.8%). No patients in either group developed Stage 3 AKI (Table 2). Among patients who developed AKI, onset occurred predominantly on the day of surgery in both groups (p=0.586). The median duration of AKI was 3 days (IQR 2–5) in the treatment group and 4 days (IQR 2–5) in the control group, with no significant difference observed (p=0.273).

Table 3 shows the results of univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses. In the univariable analysis, BMI, operation duration, anesthesia duration, pneumoperitoneum duration, crystalloid, and EBL were associated with postoperative AKI (all p<0.05). Operation duration, anesthesia duration, and pneumoperitoneum duration showed high collinearity (Pearson r=0.90–0.97, all p<0.001), and VIF analysis confirmed that including multiple time-related variables would lead to multicollinearity (VIF >5). Therefore, pneumoperitoneum duration was selected for the multivariable model based on its pathophysiological relevance. Finally, BMI, pneumoperitoneum duration, crystalloid, and EBL were included in the multivariable model and VIF values were all below 5 (range, 1.06–1.64). Multivariable logistic regression analysis identified pneumoperitoneum duration as an independent risk factor for postoperative AKI (odds ratio [OR] 1.02, 95% CI 1.01–1.04, p=0.012). The combination was not associated with increased AKI risk (OR 0.80, 95% CI 0.41–1.54, p=0.499). Similarly, the number of combination administrations did not demonstrate a significant association with AKI development (OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.89–1.05, p=0.389).

DISCUSSION

This retrospective cohort study evaluated the risk of postoperative AKI when a fixed-dose combination of intravenous acetaminophen/ibuprofen was administered alongside ketorolac-based PCA in patients undergoing robot-assisted renal surgery. The primary finding was that adding acetaminophen/ibuprofen to ketorolac-based PCA did not increase the incidence of postoperative AKI compared with ketorolac alone. In the logistic regression analysis, acetaminophen/ibuprofen administration showed no association with increased AKI risk.

The low risk of AKI after dual NSAID administration can be attributed to several factors. First, NSAIDs can potentially impair renal function by inhibiting cyclooxygenase enzymes. As a result, synthesis of prostaglandins is reduced. This effect is particularly noteworthy for PGE2 and PGI2, which maintain renal blood flow by dilating the afferent arteriole [11]. Loss of this vasodilatory mechanism can lead to afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction, resulting in decreased renal blood flow and subsequent reduction in the GFR [12]. However, NSAID-induced nephrotoxicity is likely dose-dependent and is associated with chronic exposure rather than short-term perioperative use [11,13]. Our study cohort consisted of patients without pre-existing chronic kidney disease, which might have mitigated baseline renal vulnerability and contributed to the observed safety profile. Furthermore, adequate perioperative fluid management and hemodynamic optimization in the standard protocols of our institution likely preserved renal perfusion despite dual NSAID exposure.

Our findings align with previous evidence supporting the renal safety profile of NSAIDs in surgical populations at risk for kidney injury. A recent large-scale retrospective study evaluated 1,478 patients undergoing nephrectomy and found that perioperative NSAID administration was not associated with increased AKI risk (27.6% vs. 27.9%, p=0.9) [14].

In donor nephrectomy populations, ketorolac has demonstrated favorable renal safety profiles in long-term follow-up studies. For example, Grimsby et al. [15] reported that continuous infusion of ketorolac for 24 hours postoperatively did not decrease GFR over a 1.5-year follow-up period. Similarly, Tabrizian et al. [16] found no increase in sCr at 1 week, 1 year, and 5 years postoperatively. In addition, the Cochrane systematic review concluded that NSAIDs had uncertain effects on AKI rates in surgical patients with normal renal function, and that available data neither confirmed harm nor established complete safety [17]. However, these studies did not involve concurrent use of NSAIDs. To the best of our knowledge, our investigation was the first to specifically evaluate the renal safety of dual NSAID administration in patients undergoing robot-assisted renal surgery. Our findings build on previous studies by demonstrating that the addition of a fixed-dose combination of intravenous acetaminophen/ibuprofen to ketorolac-based PCA was not associated with AKI occurrence in this vulnerable population.

The clinical implications of our findings support the safe addition of acetaminophen/ibuprofen to ketorolac-based multimodal analgesia protocols for robot-assisted renal surgery, potentially enhancing opioid-sparing strategies without compromising renal function.

An important finding of our study was that pneumoperitoneum duration emerged as an independent risk factor for postoperative AKI. Prolonged pneumoperitoneum duration has been identified as an AKI risk factor following laparoscopic abdominal surgery [18]. In robot-assisted renal surgery, prolonged pneumoperitoneum duration may compromise renal blood flow through increased intra-abdominal pressure [19,20], potentially exacerbating ischemia-reperfusion injury to the renal parenchyma. Our findings suggest that efforts to optimize surgical efficiency and minimize duration of pneumoperitoneum should be prioritized over the restriction of NSAID use for preventing postoperative AKI in patients undergoing robot-assisted renal surgery.

Despite its important conclusions, our study had certain limitations. First, the retrospective design made it prone to selection bias. In addition, although propensity score matching was used to mitigate confounding, complete elimination was not possible. Second, the limited sample size might have precluded detection of a true difference in AKI incidence between groups. However, the upper bound of the 95% CI for the risk ratio was 1.24, which was below the commonly accepted non-inferiority threshold of 1.3 [21,22]. Third, some potentially relevant confounders for AKI in renal surgery were not available in the dataset, including pneumoperitoneum pressure, warm ischemia time (applicable only to partial nephrectomy), and postoperative hypotensive episodes. Fourth, we only assessed AKI incidence during hospitalization or through POD 7, whichever occurred first; therefore, we could not evaluate potential long-term effects on renal function.

CONCLUSIONS

Our study demonstrated that the addition of a fixed-dose combination of intravenous acetaminophen/ibuprofen to ketorolac-based PCA was not associated with the risk of postoperative AKI in patients undergoing robot-assisted renal surgery. These findings support the safe incorporation of dual NSAID therapy into multimodal analgesia protocols for this surgical population. Further studies are needed to confirm these findings and to evaluate the optimal dosing strategies and long-term renal outcomes associated with dual NSAID use in robot-assisted renal surgery.

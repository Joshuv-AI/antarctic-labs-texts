Parameter calibration of the conceptual rainfall–runoff model based on improved quadratic interpolation optimization
Authors: Jiajie Zhang, Yuliang Zhou, Ping Zhou, Xu Zhu, Yuliang Zhang, Shaowei Ning, Jie Shen, Juliang Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFlood forecasting is regarded as the most important basic non-engineering measure, and its accuracy is the key to scientific flood control and regulation. The conceptual rainfall–runoff model (CRR) is widely applied to flood forecasting. The major difficulty associated with the use of CRR models in hydrology is their calibration since most of these models involve a large number of parameters. In order to calibrate the parameters of the CRR model, an improved quadratic interpolation optimization algorithm (IQIO) was proposed. The tent chaos mapping was used to initialize the population, adaptive optimizer probability based on individual adaptation value was used to balance algorithm’s global exploration and local exploitation ability. Thirteen mathematical benchmark functions were used to test the IQIO algorithm. The results showed that the IQIO algorithm exhibited strong exploration capability and fast convergence speed. The CRR model parameters optimized by the IQIO algorithm exhibited high performance, with Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency (NSE) values reaching 0.951 during the calibration period and 0.913 during the validation period. The relative error of runoff in each year was less than 20%, which satisfied the calculation accuracy requirements.Keywords: QIO algorithm, Calibration, The Xin’anjiang model (XAJ), Flood forecasting, The fengle river basinSubject terms: Hydrology, Applied mathematics

Introduction

Hydrological systems have the characteristics of modeling complexity, constraint nonlinearity, time and space randomness, etc. In recent years, a wide range of conceptual rainfall–runoff (CRR) models have been developed. Compared with other types of hydrologic models, CRR models have a reasonable accuracy and are simpler to compute in many practical cases. The major difficulty associated with the use of the CRR models in hydrology is their calibration1–4. Most parameters of the CRR models are physically interpretable and play a critical role in ensuring the theoretical consistency and computational accuracy of the simulations5–7.

Calibration is to find a global optimal value as efficiently as possible for a complex problem in any environment. The calibration of the CRR model parameters is a complex calibration problem consisting of multiple variables, objectives and constraints8. With the rapid development of technology, many excellent optimization algorithms have been proposed. There are four main classifications of optimization algorithms, including evolution-based optimization algorithms (e.g., GA, Genetic Algorithm)9, population-based optimization algorithms (e.g., PSO, Particle Swarm Optimization)10, physics/chemistry-based optimization algorithms (e.g., GSA, Gravitational Search Algorithm)11 and mathematics-based optimization algorithms (e.g., AOA, Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm)12. It is important to note that while some optimization algorithms directly explore the real solution space, the increasing computational expense of hydrological simulations has led to the widespread adoption of surrogate models to approximate the solution surface. This strategy significantly enhances optimization efficiency and represents a key area of current research13,14. Among them, the GA algorithm is the most widely used in hydrological model parameters and have achieved certain results15. However, the no-free-lunch (NFL) theorem of optimization states that no specific optimizer can effectively solve every optimization problem16. Thus, there is an incentive to use more advanced meta-heuristic algorithms for the optimization of the conceptual rainfall–runoff model parameters.

Quadratic interpolation optimization (QIO)17 is a novel mathematically inspired metaheuristic algorithm proposed by Zhao in 2023. The QIO algorithm strategically integrates the generalized quadratic interpolation (GQI) method as a dual-phase search mechanism for solving multidimensional optimization problems. This framework synergistically combines exploration-exploitation dynamics, wherein the GQI-generated minimizer enables the algorithm to systematically probe undiscovered solution spaces for potential optima and iteratively refine candidate solutions within located high-potency regions. The QIO algorithm has good optimization performance, especially for exploitation ability. Meanwhile, this algorithm has not yet been applied to the calibration of the parameters of the CRR model.

In this paper, in order to increase the diversity of the population, balance the global exploration and local exploitation ability of the QIO algorithm, and reduce the probability of falling into local optima, based on tent mapping and adaptive optimizer probability based on individual adaptation value, an improved quadratic interpolation optimization algorithm (IQIO) was proposed. The IQIO algorithm tested by thirteen mathematical benchmark functions was applied to a watershed to calibrate the parameters of the CRR model. The results showed that the IQIO algorithm exhibited high solution accuracy and fast convergence speed.

Description of the XAJ model

CRR models, developed based on conceptual hydrological principles (e.g., rainfall, evaporation, and groundwater dynamics), are primarily used to simulate rainfall-runoff processes and quantify watershed-scale water balances. These models also serve to evaluate the combined effects of climate variability and human activities on flood risks18,19. The XAJ model was used in this study, which was developed by Zhao (1980) and considers the impact of uneven distribution of precipitation and watershed underlying surfaces20. Its structural design is decentralized, consisting of five hierarchical structures: evapotranspiration calculation, runoff production calculation, runoff separation calculation, runoff confluence calculation and river flood routing model. Runoff confluence calculation model

Linear reservoir method is a method for calculating surface runoff models24. The calculation is based on the path of runoff passing through a linear reservoir. Surface runoff exiting the catchment is directly proportional to the current water depth in the reservoir. The runoff in this method is controlled by initial losses, the actual contributing area of the catchment and seepage losses. The runoff process is controlled by the time constant of the catchment time. Overall, the linear reservoir method is a physically based surface runoff model calculation method that can accurately simulate the runoff process, providing an important tool for water resources management, hydrological prediction, etc25.

Improved quadratic interpolation optimization (IQIO)

Algorithm principle

Quadratic interpolation optimization (QIO) was proposed by Zhao Weiguo in 2023. The main inspiration behind QIO was derived from the generalized quadratic interpolation (GQI) method, which was recently proposed to find the minimizer of the quadratic function formed by any three points. This method overcame the limitations of traditional quadratic interpolation methods and was better at finding the minimum of quadratic functions formed by any three points. To enhance the optimization process, two powerful search strategies were presented. In the exploration strategy, for each dimension, the GQI method was used to obtain the minimum of quadratic interpolation functions formed by two individuals randomly selected from the population and the current individual’s position, this minimum was then combined with the position of another randomly selected individual from the population. The resulting position was used for global search. In the exploitation strategy, for each dimension, the GQI method was used to obtain the minimum of a quadratic interpolation function formed by two individuals randomly selected from the population and the best individual found so far. This minimum was then combined with the best individual’s position. The resulting position was used for local search.

Tent chaos mapping initialization population

Its basic principle is to initialize the population using the tent chaos mapping, which enhances the optimality and ergodicity of the algorithm. As a result, many researchers have introduced this chaos mapping into various algorithm applications.

Adaptive optimizer probability based on individual adaptation value

The QIO algorithm was mainly divided into two strategies: global exploration and local exploitation. The switch between these two strategies is determined by the optimizer probability (OP) value. When rand >OP, the algorithm performs exploration strategy; when rand < OP, the algorithm performs exploitation strategy. OP value is an important coefficient that controls the balance between global exploration and local exploitation of the algorithm. If OP value is small, the algorithm tends to perform global exploration; if OP value is large, the algorithm tends to perform local exploitation. The balance between global exploration and local exploitation has a crucial impact on the optimization performance of the algorithm29.

As Eqs. (24) and (25) show, in the basic QIO algorithm, , which means the probabilities of global exploration and local exploitation were equal. This may not meet the needs of population updating process well. At the beginning of the calculation, the range of the optimal solution is uncertain, so it should mainly perform global search. However, at the later stage of the calculation, after determining the range of the optimal solution, it needs a strong ability for local exploitation to enable the algorithm to quickly converge after finding the global optimal solution, shorten execution time and improve efficiency. However, assume that the OP value increases with the iteration times, if the algorithm fails to fully search the solution space at the early stage of iteration and falls into a local optimum at the later stage, in this case, the algorithm mainly performs local exploitation and it will be difficult to jump out of the local optima.

Analysis of Eq. (26) shows that each individual is assigned an OP value based on its own fitness. When the fitness of the individual is closer to the optimal fitness of the current generation, the OP value is smaller, and the probability of global search is higher. When the fitness of the individual is closer to the worst fitness of the current generation, the OP value is larger, and the probability of local exploitation is higher. In this way, better individuals can guide worse individuals more frequently, and worse individuals can also have more opportunities for local exploitation. This method speeds up the convergence while reducing the probability of the algorithm getting stuck in local optima.

Results analysis

Runoff relative error: The relative error was calculated by dividing the forecast runoff error by the measured value. The permissible error for runoff depth forecast was 20% of the measured value. A forecast was considered qualified when the forecast error was less than the permissible error.

In order to ensure better consistency of the data, the model experiments were conducted using years with relatively small changes in the underlying surface. Five years of data from 1982 to 1986 were used as the calibration period, five years of data from 1987 to 1991 were used as the validation period. The Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency coefficient (NSE) was used as the fitting degree index between the daily runoff forecast process and the measured process.

The algorithms such as IQIO, QIO, GA, GSA and PSO were used to calibrate the parameters of the XAJ model. This design was using 50 search individuals and 200 iterations over 30 runs. Discussion

Parameter space of the XAJ model is typically nonlinear, multimodal, and highly correlated. This characteristic makes optimization significantly more challenging than optimization for standard mathematical benchmark functions. In practical hydrological applications, even minor performance enhancements can significantly improve capture of key runoff process features, such as peak flows and recession limbs. Although this study successfully demonstrates effectiveness of the IQIO algorithm for calibrating conceptual hydrological models, future research will explore applicability and scalability of the algorithm within more complex hydrological systems. Extending this optimization approach to distributed or physically-based models represents a crucial next step. To address these challenges and reduce computational costs in more demanding hydrological applications, a promising future direction is direct integration of surrogate models into the IQIO framework. By approximating computationally expensive hydrological simulations, a surrogate-assisted IQIO approach holds strong potential for efficient optimization in complex scenarios that would otherwise be computationally prohibitive.

Conclusions

In this paper, an improved quadratic interpolation optimization algorithm (IQIO) was proposed, which was tested using thirteen benchmark functions and compared with several other algorithms. Results showed that the proposed algorithm was competitive in exploitation compared with other algorithms and had an excellent convergence capability.

The parameters of the XAJ model were optimized by the IQIO, QIO, GA, GSA and PSO algorithms. The calculated parameters were applied to flood simulations for small and medium-sized reservoirs. The IQIO algorithm provided comparable performance to that of other traditional optimization methods. This study ultimately provided an effective approach for calibration of the conceptual rainfall–runoff model parameters.

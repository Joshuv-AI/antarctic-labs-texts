Smart contact lenses unlocking non-invasive eye treatment
Authors: Mingpeng Liu, Xingyi Gan, Guang Yao, Yuan Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The eye, serving as the primary gateway for visual information processing, is the indispensable channel through which humans perceive and interact with the external world. Structurally, it comprises an anterior segment, handling light entry and refraction, and a posterior segment, which absorbs light and transmits visual signals to the brain. Within advanced ocular healthcare, smart contact lenses (SCLs) emerged as a groundbreaking wearable technology with immense potential to revolutionize the management of diverse ocular conditions. Their inherent advantage of non-invasive and intimate corneal adherence circumvents numerous limitations of traditional therapeutic modalities. By integrating cutting-edge bio-functional components with sophisticated physical field-based therapeutic modules, therapeutic SCLs offer transformative treatment solutions to persistent challenges in conventional ocular pharmacotherapy. These lenses are poised to deliver non-invasive therapeutic interventions for a broad spectrum of ocular pathologies, spanning both anterior and posterior segments. Crucially, therapeutic SCLs effectively mitigate the risks of infection, inflammation, and other postoperative complications associated with invasive surgical procedures. Furthermore, by intelligently modulating the complex “eye-brain” pathway, SCLs introduce a paradigm-shifting strategy for managing neurovisual impairments, thereby unlocking promising avenues for restoring visual function and addressing previously intractable conditions.

The design of non-invasive SCLs

Within the dynamic and evolving landscape of ocular healthcare, smart contact lenses (SCLs) have emerged as a groundbreaking innovation, representing an advanced class of flexible wearable platforms poised to redefine therapeutic paradigms [1,2]. These sophisticated devices are born from a profound interdisciplinary convergence, seamlessly synthesizing expertise from the fields of ophthalmology, advanced materials [3], and microelectronics. These innovative approaches comprehensively address a series of fundamental scientific challenges, including bio-interface integration, long-term reliability of micro-functional units, and the coordinated interaction of heterogeneous materials, devices, and biological systems. At the same time, this synergistic amalgamation endows SCLs with an unprecedented capacity for real-time monitoring of vital ocular physiological parameters [4], alongside targeted non-invasive therapeutic interventions and dynamic augmentation of visual function [5], thereby offering a holistic approach to ocular health management [6]. The remarkable functionalities of therapeutic SCLs are orchestrated through a meticulously engineered system architecture comprising three interconnected and interdependent core components. First, the flexible substrate module is composed of biocompatible polymers like silicone hydrogel, which functions as a carrier that is directly bonded to the ocular surface [7]. Its excellent oxygen permeability and convenience assure treatment safety and sustainability, while simultaneously providing a reliable and adaptable integration platform for the overlying functional unit. Second, the functional intervention module integrated atop this substrate functions as the execution unit. With the aid of advanced flexible electronic materials [8,9], it actively performs critical therapeutic operations such as on-demand programmed drug delivery, iontophoresis, or physical field stimulation, allowing for non-invasive targeted intervention. Finally, serving as the system's command center [10], the control processor module is embedded within the flexible circuitry. It coordinates the cooperative functioning of multiple therapy units, precisely controlling the activation/deactivation, dosage, and timing of the intervention module. Collectively, these three components form a sophisticated functional platform capable of responding to external commands and delivering precise, manageable, and coordinated therapy for eye conditions.

SCLs for non-invasive anterior segment treatment

The anterior segment, comprising the cornea, anterior chamber, and lens along with its suspensory ligaments, is the primary site for most ocular pathologies [11] (Fig. 1). The synergistic integration of biomimetic hydrogel materials with advanced drug delivery systems has dramatically enhanced SCLs' drug loading capacity and release performance [12]. These sophisticated systems bolster encapsulation efficiency, improve stability, and ensure precise dosing, thereby effectively overcoming limitations of conventional eye drops. For example, SCLs equipped with integrated microsensors [13,14] and microfluidic components enable real-time intraocular pressure monitoring [15]. This capability facilitates the on-demand release of hypotensive agents, which are crucial for maintaining anterior segment homeostasis. In postoperative management, these SCLs permit the sustained and precise administration of anti-inflammatory drugs, resulting in significantly improved therapeutic outcomes and enhanced patient comfort [16]. Furthermore, SCLs can leverage physical intervention modalities, such as electric and thermal fields, to actively modulate the ocular surface microenvironment. This approach stimulates targeted cellular processes and promotes tissue regeneration without the necessity of invasive procedures. A notable advancement in this area, developed by Yao's research group, is a flexible, blink-activated piezoelectric contact lens inspired by snowflake structures [17]. This innovative device efficiently transduces mechanical energy from blinking into a unidirectional pulsed electric field. In animal models of corneal alkali burns, this technology has demonstrated significant efficacy, manifesting as reduced stromal fibrosis, enhanced epithelial organization and differentiation, and accelerated corneal repair. Ultimately, the hallmark of SCLs in anterior segment therapy lies in their capacity for immediate, continuous, and adaptive non-invasive intervention. SCLs enabling non-invasive posterior segment treatment

Beyond ocular surface disorders, the non-invasive therapeutic capabilities of SCLs are now extending to the posterior segment of the eye, offering groundbreaking solutions for what are often considered “treatment blind spots”. The posterior segment [18], comprising the retina, choroid, macula, and optic disc, is crucial for initial visual signal processing, conversion, and light sensitivity (Fig. 1). The functional integrity of this region is paramount for high-fidelity visual perception. The term “treatment blind spot” aptly characterizes the challenge posed by the anatomical depth of posterior segment lesions, often shielded by blood-retinal barriers. Consequently, clinical management of pathologies such as diabetic retinopathy, retinitis pigmentosa, and optic nerve injury has historically relied on invasive approaches. These include intravitreal injections and complex vitreoretinal surgeries, which inherently carry risks and tolerability issues for patients. SCLs engineered with highly integrated miniaturized technology platforms are poised to revolutionize posterior segment therapy, enabling non-invasive retinal interventions through two fundamental mechanisms. First, SCLs facilitate precise physical field-based energy delivery to inner ocular tissues. The incorporation of miniature near-infrared (NIR) light sources, multi-array electrodes, or ultrasonic transducers enables the delivery of tailored energy modalities that balance tissue penetration with therapeutic specificity. For instance, recent studies demonstrated that a wireless-powered SCL embedded with a micro-light-emitting diode can deliver calibrated NIR modulation to the retina [19]. This effectively suppresses pathological angiogenesis and inflammatory pathways, thereby slowing the progression of the disease [10]. Similarly, trans-corneal electrical stimulation via SCLs has shown promise in upregulating endogenous neurotrophic factors such as brain-derived neurotrophic factor, promoting retinal neuronal survival and optic nerve regeneration. Second, SCLs enable remotely controlled targeted drug delivery systems designed for precision therapy. These platforms guide therapeutic agents across biological barriers by leveraging electrochemical gradients, hydrodynamic flow, or externally applied fields (e.g., electric or magnetic). This approach enables high local drug concentrations to be achieved precisely at retinal lesion sites [20]. What distinguishes SCLs in posterior segment treatment is their ability to harness the eye's inherent optical and conductive pathways. This allows for the non-invasive, targeted, and spatially controlled delivery of light, electrical, and pharmacological modalities. By transforming traditionally invasive procedures into comfortable, wearable therapies, SCLs mark a paradigm shift toward accessible and patient-friendly retinal medicine.

SCLs-mediated eye-brain neuromodulation

Neuropsychiatric disorders, including depression and Alzheimer's disease (AD), afflict hundreds of millions globally, resulting in profound impairments in emotional regulation and progressive cognitive decline. Existing treatments remain largely symptomatic, with limited efficacy and non-negligible side effects, underscoring the urgent need for compliant, non-invasive neuromodulation technologies. SCLs have emerged as a promising interdisciplinary platform (Fig. 2) bridging ophthalmology and neurology to facilitate neuromodulation via the “eye-brain axis.” By incorporating multi-physical field stimulation modules, SCLs allow precise modulation of neural oscillations at specific frequencies. Notably, gamma oscillations around 40 Hz are intrinsically linked to higher-order cognitive functions, including attention, sensory integration, and memory formation [21]. Experimental evidence demonstrates that 40 Hz visual stimulation delivered via SCLs enhances gamma synchrony within the visual cortex and promotes microglial activation. This, in turn, accelerates the clearance of amyloid-β and tau proteins and ultimately improves cognitive performance in AD animal models [22,23]. Concurrently, research has revealed that 40 Hz transcorneal electrical stimulation propagates signals via the optic nerve to visual and hippocampal regions, effectively ameliorating depressive-like behaviors and augmenting cognitive function in animal models [24]. Additionally, SCLs can establish artificial reflex arcs within the retinal neural network to interact directly with the visual cortex, enabling controllable visual perception [25]. A significant advantage of SCL-based neuromodulation lies in its capability for continuous, adaptive, and non-invasive intervention, offering personalized regulation of brain activity. This approach not only signifies a convergence of neuro-ophthalmology and psychiatric medicine but also establishes a novel paradigm for wearable, non-invasive neural modulation. Conclusion and perspectives

SCLs, incorporating integrated drug delivery systems, biosensing modules, and physical field-based intervention units, are poised to redefine ophthalmic care. They offer a paradigm shift from conventional, often unilateral approaches like pharmacotherapy or surgery, towards a synergistic strategy of “precision drug delivery combined with physical field co-modulation.” The system explores intrinsic correlations between physiological and biochemical ocular signals and disease attributes, such as type, location, and severity. Training machine learning algorithms on multimodal sensing data enables accurate signal identification and classification, thereby establishing a robust link from sensing and monitoring to disease diagnosis. Simultaneously, the flexibly designed intervention unit investigates key intervention parameters and targeting strategies that integrate physical-field stimulation with controlled drug release, which supports the emulation of orderly ocular tissue self-repair processes. This integrated framework aims to establish a closed-loop eye health management system capable of continuous monitoring, targeted treatment, and adaptive feedback. Despite their considerable potential, the clinical translation of therapeutic SCLs faces several significant challenges (Fig. 3). The advancement of SCLs fundamentally depends on the development of advanced materials engineered to maintain stable performance within the complex biochemical environment of the ocular surface. These materials must also exhibit high mechanical flexibility, strong resistance to biofouling, and long-term environmental stability. Equally critical is the operational reliability of the closed-loop intelligent system, which necessitates highly selective and stable biosensing capabilities, a continuous and dependable micro-energy supply, and precisely controllable drug delivery mechanisms. Furthermore, cybersecurity and data privacy have emerged as critical concerns. The continuous flow of physiological data demands robust encryption and secure protocols for wireless transmission and cloud storage, all while adhering to increasingly stringent medical data regulations. To surmount these limitations, current research is intensively focused on developing novel biocompatible materials and multifunctional therapeutic units. These innovations aim to support high-efficiency drug delivery and multimodal synergistic therapy, while minimizing side effects. Future advancements will hinge on breakthroughs in material science, the development of efficient energy supply systems, and deeply integrated interdisciplinary collaborations. Through these concerted efforts, SCLs may ultimately give rise to seamless, intelligent diagnostic and therapeutic platforms.

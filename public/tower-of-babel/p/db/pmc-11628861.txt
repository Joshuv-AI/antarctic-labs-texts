Finding your match: An EP fellow’s perspective on interview season
Authors: Natasha A Vedage
Published: 2024

I cannot believe that 1 year has passed since I began interviewing for electrophysiology (EP) fellowship. I remember my experience vividly—the excitement and optimism with incoming e-mail invitations to interview at programs across the country and the nervousness but hopefulness when you create your final rank list. It is undoubtedly an emotional experience. While there are excellent resources on how to interview and rank strategically, I wanted to pause and reflect on what was helpful for me personally.

Hold on to what drives you. I recently read that “curiosity is a wonderful way of dealing skillfully with the frantic world in which we so often live.”1 I distinctly remember one of my first EP interviews where the day began with a fellow’s case presentation on a challenging left-sided atrial tachycardia ablation. It was as though a switch had been turned on inside me. All of my nerves about the interview day had dissipated, and I was invigorated by the curiosity and desire to understand the case. Being able to tap into what excites you about electrophysiology on the day of your interview will not only boost your mood, but also set the stage for a successful interview day.

Be your authentic self. This seems obvious, but I remember feeling overwhelmed by the task of eloquently, succinctly, and authentically speaking about myself during an interview. Something I found very helpful was to curate a list of potential interview questions. I would then write a journal response to each question and later came back to what I wrote. I identified themes and important points I wanted to bring up during my interviews, and I created a punch list that I reviewed the morning before each interview.

Embrace being outside your comfort zone. At some point in this process, you may be presented with the opportunity to explore beyond your comfort zone. For me, this especially holds true as a new EP fellow. For example, one of the skills you must master early on is suturing. You may fumble at the beginning, and this may be uncomfortable, but it is incredibly rewarding to see yourself make small strides simply by persevering. When I was ranking programs, I was at first nervous about the prospect of leaving my home city and institution. But now, being a few months into EP fellowship in a new city and institution, I can see how transformative new experiences can be by simply embracing some initial discomfort in exchange for personal growth.

Find your people. There are many objective parameters you may weigh in your decision to rank programs. What is the ratio of EP labs to fellows? How many atrial fibrillation cases are done annually? You will find that at most programs you will receive excellent clinical training, but there will likely be only a few programs that strike you with a sense of both inspiration and belonging. This “gut feeling” is equally important and valuable—it may signal that a program has the right environment, resources, and people to help you achieve your career goals, and this can have an enormous impact on your growth as a trainee. I found it helpful to debrief with my partner after my interviews, and he helped me by recalling the times when I spoke passionately about my interview day and had that “gut feeling,” which you can lose track of several interviews later. I encourage you to embrace this process with its ups and downs, knowing that you are embarking on your final stage of training. You are one step closer to doing what you love, and the EP community is here to welcome you.

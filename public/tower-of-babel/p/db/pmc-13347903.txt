Dual versus mono antiplatelet therapy within 72 hours after onset for mild ischaemic stroke or transient ischaemic attack: meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials
Authors: Yingying Yang, Jinghan Zhu, Ying Gao, Yuesong Pan, Yilong Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough previous evidence generally agreed on the short-term dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) for mild stroke or transient ischaemic attack (TIA), there is no consensus on the optimal threshold for stroke severity and initiation timing of DAPT. We conducted an updated meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials to evaluate early DAPT versus single therapy in mild stroke or TIA.MethodsWe systematically reviewed double-blind and randomised controlled trials up to October 2024 evaluating DAPT versus monotherapy for acute mild, non-cardioembolic ischaemic stroke (National Institute of Health Stroke Scale; NIHSS≤5) or TIA within 72 hours of ictus. Random effects models generated risk ratio (RR) with 95% CIs for outcomes including stroke, composite vascular events, ischaemic stroke, major bleeding, haemorrhagic stroke and all-cause mortality.ResultsPooled data from five trials (n=27 559) demonstrated that DAPT versus monotherapy lowered the risk of stroke recurrence (RR, 0.77; 95% CI 0.70 to 0.83), composite vascular events (RR, 0.75; 95% CI 0.68 to 0.83) and ischaemic stroke (RR, 0.74; 95% CI 0.68 to 0.81). However, DAPT increased the risk of major bleeding (RR, 2.19; 95% CI 1.38 to 3.49) and haemorrhagic stroke (RR, 2.08; 95% CI 1.13 to 3.82), with no significant increase in the risk of all-cause mortality (RR, 1.28; 95% CI 0.95 to 1.71).ConclusionsFor acute mild stroke (NIHSS ≤5) or patients with TIA within 72 hours of ictus, early DAPT initiation demonstrates net clinical benefit through reducing ischaemic events, despite an increase in bleeding complications, without affecting mortality.Keywords: Stroke; Meta-Analysis; Ischemic Attack, Transient

Introduction

Individuals experiencing mild ischaemic stroke or transient ischaemic attack (TIA) face substantial risks, with 5%–10% experiencing recurrent stroke within 90 days, leading to a high risk of disability.1 2 Current guidelines advocate initiating combined aspirin and clopidogrel therapy within 24 hours for mild ischaemic stroke (National Institute of Health Stroke Scale; NIHSS ≤3) or high-risk TIA (age, blood pressure, clinical features, duration of symptoms, and diabetes; ABCD2≥4) to mitigate recurrence risks.3 Although previous evidence generally agreed on the use of the short-term dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) for acute mild stroke or TIA, there is no consensus on the severity of mild stroke and the time window for DAPT initiation.4,8

Acute Stroke or Transient Ischemic Attack Treated with Ticagrelor and Acetylsalicylic Acid for Prevention of Stroke and Death (THALES) study showed ticagrelor-aspirin combination initiated within 24 hours reduced 30-day composite of stroke or death for mild ischaemic stroke (NIHSS≤5) or TIA, although with increased severe bleeding.7 The Intensive Statin and Antiplatelet Therapy for Acute High Risk Intracranial or Extracranial Atherosclerosis (INSPIRES) trial demonstrated initiating clopidogrel-aspirin within 72 hours reduced 90-day stroke recurrence in mild stroke (NIHSS ≤5) or high-risk TIA patients, despite slightly elevated bleeding risks.9 These results indicated that the upper threshold of NIHSS score for DAPT might be extended from 3 to 5 and the time window for DAPT initiation might be extended from 24 hours to 72 hours.

Therefore, to address these questions, we conducted an updated meta-analysis comparing DAPT versus monotherapy for acute mild non-cardioembolic ischaemic stroke (NIHSS ≤5) or TIA within 72 hours.

Methods

Data availability

The manuscript contains all research data supporting these findings.

Literature search methodology

We conducted comprehensive searches across three databases including MEDLINE, Embase and Cochrane library on 31 October 2024 with context-relevant keywords of ‘antiplatelet drugs’, ‘ischemic stroke’ and ‘transient ischemic attack’. Manual search for conference abstracts was also performed. Searches were confined to randomised trials published in English. The Preferred Reporting Item for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis protocol was followed.

Inclusion criteria

Two authors (YY and JZ) independently applied the following inclusion criteria during screening. (1) Adult population with acute non-cardioembolic ischaemic stroke (NIHSS ≤5) or TIA; (2) direct comparison of dual versus mono antiplatelet agents; (3) randomised, double-blind design; (4) enrollment of patients within 72 hours of symptom onset; (5) reporting outcomes including stroke, composite vascular events, ischaemic stroke, major bleeding, haemorrhagic stroke and all-cause mortality. Stroke incorporated both ischaemic and haemorrhagic subtypes. Composite vascular events included stroke recurrence, TIA, myocardial infarction and cardiovascular death. Major bleeding events were mostly classified as moderate-to-severe bleeding according to the Global Utilization of Streptokinase and Tissue Plasminogen Activator for Occluded Coronary Arteries definition.10 Detailed definitions of clinical outcomes specific to each trial were summarised in online supplemental table S1online supplemental file 2.

Data extraction

Two authors (YY and JZ) conducted independent study evaluations and data extraction under the supervision from senior authors (YP and YW). The following characteristics were extracted: the year of publication, enrolled populations, stroke severity, treatment initiation timing, treatment groups, DAPT duration, follow-up points, geographic locations, participating centres, sample size, intention-to-treat analysis and clinical outcomes. Efficacy outcomes included recurrent stroke, composite vascular events and ischaemic stroke. Safety outcomes included major bleeding, haemorrhagic stroke and all-cause death.

Quantitative synthesis

Primary analyses were performed for all outcomes. Subgroup analyses according to different antiplatelet medications (clopidogrel plus aspirin vs ticagrelor plus aspirin) and NIHSS score (NIHSS ≤3 vs NIHSS 4–5) were performed, assessing the treatment effects for some outcomes. Random effects models generated pooled risk ratio (RR) with 95% CIs to account for anticipated variability across interventions, event rates and study designs. Heterogeneities were quantified by I2 and Cochrane Q (χ2) statistics. Publication bias was evaluated by funnel plots. We performed a trial sequential analysis on stroke recurrence, and we used the event rate of 8% in monotherapy group and a relative risk reduction of 23%. The threshold of statistical significance was set at p<0.05 (two-tailed). All data were analysed by Cochrane Review Manager (V.5.4.1).

Results

Study selection and characteristics

We identified 6375 records through database searching and after removing duplicates, we screened 4953 titles and abstracts. Subsequently, we assessed 39 articles in full-text review and reasons for exclusion were provided in figure 1. Finally, this meta-analysis enrolled five double-blind, randomised controlled trials involving 27 559 participants diagnosed with mild ischaemic stroke or TIA. All trials randomised patients to either dual or mono antiplatelet therapy within 72 hours, including Fast Assessment of Stroke and TIA to prevent Early Recurrence (FASTER), Clopidogrel in High-Risk Patients with Acute Nondisabling Cerebrovascular Events (CHANCE), Platelet-Oriented Inhibition in New TIA and Minor Ischemic Stroke (POINT), THALES and INSPIRES. The treatment comparisons evaluated were aspirin plus clopidogrel versus aspirin (four trials, n=16 543) and aspirin plus ticagrelor versus aspirin (one trial, n=11 016). Characteristics of the included studies are detailed in table 1. All trials demonstrated a low risk of bias. Funnel plot analysis demonstrated symmetry across all outcomes, and Egger’s test yielded statistically significant (p<0.05) for each outcome.

Efficacy outcomes

The analysis for recurrent stroke, composite vascular events and ischaemic stroke across the included studies are shown in figure 2. In five studies with different follow-up periods, DAPT was associated with an approximately 23% reduction in stroke recurrence risk compared with monotherapy (RR, 0.77; 95% CI 0.70 to 0.83; p<0.001; I2=0%). To prevent one additional case of stroke recurrence, the number needed to treat was 53 (95% CI 40 to 78). Trial sequential analysis showed that we had enough information to confirm the hypothesis that DAPT lowered stroke recurrence risk with a relative risk reduction of 23%, supporting the meta-analysis result (online supplemental figure S1). The subgroup analysis of different antiplatelet medications showed that clopidogrel-aspirin and ticagrelor-aspirin had similar efficacy in reducing stroke recurrence (clopidogrel-aspirin, RR, 0.74, 95% CI 0.67 to 0.83, p<0.001, I2=0%; ticagrelor-aspirin, RR, 0.81, 95% CI 0.70 to 0.95, p=0.008) (figure 3).

Safety outcomes

Pooled data from all five trials provided safety outcomes including major bleeding and haemorrhagic stroke (figure 2). The analysis demonstrated a higher incidence of major bleeding in DAPT (0.71%) compared with monotherapy (0.30%). DAPT initiation in acute mild ischaemic stroke or patients with TIA resulted in significantly elevated risks for both major bleeding (RR, 2.19; 95% CI 1.38 to 3.49; p<0.001; I2=30%) and haemorrhagic stroke (RR, 2.08; (95% CI 1.13 to 3.82; p=0.02); I2=8%). The number needed to harm for major bleeding events was 250 (95% CI 175 to 435). The subgroup comparisons between antiplatelet medications showed that clopidogrel-aspirin and ticagrelor-aspirin had a comparable risk increase of major bleeding (clopidogrel-aspirin, RR, 1.88, 95% CI 1.10 to 3.24, p=0.02, I2=25%; ticagrelor-aspirin, RR, 3.25, 95% CI, 1.66 to 6.39, p<0.001) (figure 3). When excluding data from the NSPIRES trial, the association between DAPT and the risk of haemorrhagic stroke lost statistical significance (RR, 1.83; 95% CI 0.83 to 4.05; p=0.25; I2=16%).

Furthermore, we compared the safety outcomes of DAPT with monotherapy in acute ischaemic stroke stratified by NIHSS score (figure 4). DAPT significantly increased the risk of major bleeding in patients with NIHSS≤3 (RR, 1.91; 95% CI 1.03 to 3.54; p=0.04; I2=46%) and NIHSS 4–5 (RR, 2.92; 95% CI 1.03 to 8.24; p=0.04; I2=32%). However, the association between DAPT and the risk of haemorrhagic stroke lost statistical significance only in patients with NIHSS≤3 (RR, 1.53; 95% CI 0.83 to 2.82; p=0.17; I2=0%). Analysis of mortality data from four studies revealed no significant difference in the risk of all-cause death between treatment groups (RR, 1.28; 95% CI 0.95 to 1.71; p=0.10; I2=0%).

Discussion

This comprehensive meta-analysis synthesises evidence from five high-quality trials (FASTER, CHANCE, POINT, THALES and INSPIRES) involving patients with mild ischaemic stroke (NIHSS ≤5) or TIA within 72 hours of ictus. Our results showed that DAPT in comparison to monotherapy demonstrated net clinical benefit through reducing recurrent ischaemic events, despite an increase in bleeding complications, without having detrimental effect on overall mortality in these patients.

DAPT achieved a significant reduction in stroke recurrence, aligning with previous systematic reviews and meta-analyses.411,14 Although DAPT was associated with elevated bleeding risks (both major bleeding and haemorrhagic stroke), the absolute risk increase remained modest. This observation was particularly evident in the INSPIRES trial, where DAPT showed a transient increase in moderate-to-severe bleeding events during the initial 3 weeks, and the overall risk was low.15 Notably, the findings revealed no significant difference in all-cause death between treatment groups, supporting the clinical utility of DAPT in these patients.

Regarding the heterogeneity in bleeding events among enrolled trials, it is crucial to highlight the differences in the design of these trials. First, mild stroke was defined as NIHSS ≤3 in FASTER, CHANCE and POINT trials, while it was defined as NIHSS ≤5 in INSPIRES and THALES trials. Our subgroup analysis by stroke severity demonstrated differential bleeding risks. DAPT significantly increased haemorrhagic stroke risks in patients with NIHSS 4–5, but not in those with NIHSS ≤3. It suggests that higher NIHSS score might intensify the effect of dual versus monotherapy on the risk of haemorrhagic stroke, which might partly explain the heterogeneity in bleeding outcomes. Therefore, when eligible patients with stroke with NIHSS 4–5 were given DAPT in clinical practice, blood pressure should be strictly controlled to avoid haemorrhagic stroke as much as possible and clinical manifestations should be closely monitored to detect haemorrhagic stroke timely. However, the THALES trial’s exploratory analysis revealed that patients with NIHSS 4–5 had similar benefit from ticagrelor-aspirin versus aspirin alone compared with those with NIHSS≤3, without experiencing additional intracranial or severe bleeding risks.16 The effect of stroke severity on bleeding outcomes needs to be further explored. Second, THALES trial evaluated ticagrelor (a reversible P2Y12 receptor antagonist unaffected by CYP2C19 polymorphisms), while other trials assessed clopidogrel. Although ticagrelor versus clopidogrel seems more reliable in inhibiting platelet reactivity, our subgroup analysis and a previous meta-analysis found a comparable risk of major bleeding between clopidogrel-aspirin and ticagrelor-aspirin.17 Third, POINT trial initiated DAPT within 12 hours of onset, while FASTER, CHANCE and THALES initiated DAPT within 24 hours, and INSPIRES initiated DAPT within 72 hours of onset. Notably, INSPIRES subgroup analysis revealed similar safety profiles regardless of whether clopidogrel plus aspirin begun within 24, 24–48 or 48–72 hours,18 suggesting DAPT initiation timing may not significantly affect the heterogeneity of bleeding events. Finally, discrepancies in clopidogrel loading doses and treatment duration may contribute to bleeding differences. The elevated bleeding risks observed in FASTER and POINT trials in DAPT group potentially reflect their higher clopidogrel doses and longer DAPT duration.6 19

The ATAMIS trial, an open-label randomised clinical trial with blinded endpoint assessment, evaluated clopidogrel-aspirin versus aspirin alone in Chinese patients with acute mild-to-moderate ischaemic stroke (NIHSS 4–10).20 While the trial demonstrated superior efficacy of DAPT in preventing early neurologic deterioration at 7 days with comparable safety profile,20 it was excluded from our meta-analysis due to its distinct patient population.

Overall, our meta-analysis incorporated new data from INSPIRES trial, further demonstrating that initiating DAPT early reduces the risk of stroke recurrence and increases the risk of bleeding events among patients with mild ischaemic stroke or TIA. Given the overall minimal incidence of bleeding events, the net effect of DAPT swings towards benefit. The risk–benefit analysis revealed a favourable clinical net effect of DAPT, as we estimated that DAPT would prevent approximately 19 recurrent strokes and cause 4 major bleeding events per 1000 treated patients. Our results indicated that the upper threshold of NIHSS score for DAPT can be extended from 3 to 5 and the time window for DAPT initiation can be extended from 24 hours to 72 hours. Our findings highlighted that practitioners must balance these competing outcomes when deciding on the appropriate antiplatelet therapy in acute mild ischaemic stroke (NIHSS≤5) or TIA within 72 hours.

It is important to note that several limitations should be recognised. First, stroke severity was quantitatively assessed using NIHSS scoring system, but NIHSS score was not comprehensive and might not accurately reflect the severity of posterior circulation infarction or cortical infarction. Second, there were differences in the interventions among the five randomised controlled trials, such as the types of dual antiplatelet medications, dosages and durations of DAPT. Third, two trials including CHANCE and INSPIRES enrolled exclusively Chinese. The high prevalence (58.8%) of CYP2C19 gene variants in Asian populations may affect clopidogrel metabolism and limit the generalisability of our findings. Additionally, the follow-up periods and definitions of clinical outcomes differed among the studies.

Conclusion

For acute mild non-cardioembolic ischaemic stroke (NIHSS ≤5) or patients with TIA within 72 hours of onset, DAPT demonstrated a significant reduction in recurrent ischaemic events, modest increase in bleeding risks and neutral effect on all-cause mortality. This underscored the importance of individualised risk–benefit assessment when selecting antiplatelet strategies in clinical practice.

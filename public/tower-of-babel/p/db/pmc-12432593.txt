Development and validation of a prognosis model for patients with brain-metastasis non-small cell lung cancer by machine-learning
Authors: Jingxin Liu, Yibing Wang, Xianwei Zhou, Meijin Reng, Ziyue Xiang, Ruimin Chang, Wen Hao, Xitai Sun, Yang Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBrain metastasis, the most prevalent site of lung cancer metastasis, implies a grim prognosis. Adopting the best treatment approach is crucial for improving the survival of these patients. Therefore, this study aimed to develop a personalized prognostic model for brain-metastasized non-small cell lung cancer (BM-NSCLC) patients to aid in clinical decision-making.MethodsThe study enrolled BM-NSCLC patients who were single-primary and had not undergone radical surgery from 2010 to 2021. The Kaplan-Meier method analysis was utilized to assess overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) under different treatments. Univariable and multivariable Cox regression analyses were conducted to ascertain independent prognostic factors. The dataset was partitioned into training (70%) and validation (30%) cohorts for the development and assessment of random forest (RF), logistic regression (LR), support vector machine (SVM), and K-nearest neighbor (KNN) models. The efficacy of the models was evaluated through the calculation of area under the curve (AUC) of the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and decision curve analysis (DCA). A user-friendly web app was developed via shinyapps.io to increase the accessibility for clinicians.ResultsA total of 3,171 eligible samples were ultimately included in the study. Survival analysis indicated that patients who underwent metastasis site surgery combined with radiotherapy based on chemotherapy exhibited a more favorable prognosis compared to alternative treatment modalities within the scope of this study. The RF model demonstrated superior predictive accuracy for 1-year-OS, with an AUC of 0.89 in validation cohorts (n=951), and a more refined DCA profile.ConclusionsIn the case of patients with BM-NSCLC, the integration of radiation therapy with surgery for metastasis site based on systematic treatment yielded the most significant benefits. The importance of a comprehensive treatment strategy that integrates chemotherapy, surgery, and radiotherapy for these patients was emphasized. Additionally, a clinical decision-support tool constructed from this dataset, demonstrated robust discrimination, excellent calibration, and notable clinical utility. This tool will effectively assist clinical practitioners in making more personalized clinical decisions for patients.

Key findings

• We found that for brain-metastasized non-small cell lung cancer (BM-NSCLC) patients, the integration of radiation therapy with surgery for the metastatic site, based on systematic treatment, offered the most significant benefits and we developed a prognosis model to help clinicians make more personalized decisions.

What is known and what is new?

• In previous studies, machine-learning model demonstrated strong predictive power in clinical diagnosis and prognosis prediction, effectively aiding clinical decision-making.

• Our research has developed a prognosis model for BM-NSCLC patients that demonstrated capable performance in predicting 1-year overall survival.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• For BM-NSCLC patients, chemotherapy-based radiotherapy combined with surgery may provide the best outcomes. However, machine learning models based on large-scale data will more effectively identify the population that will benefit.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for around 85% of cases (1). Approximately 30–54% of NSCLC patients develop brain metastasis, the most frequent metastatic site would bear a dismal prognosis, with an average natural survival of about 2 months, and even with treatment, the 1-year overall survival (OS) rate for these patients remains below 30% (2,3). Targeted therapies and immunotherapies have markedly enhanced patient survival, yet their applicability is limited. Notably, outcomes remain suboptimal for those presenting with distant metastases. Consequently, conventional modalities such as surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy remain indispensable in the therapeutic arsenal (4,5). For patients with brain metastasis, a comprehensive treatment approach based on these approaches is commonly adopted (6). Choosing the most suitable treatment for such patients is exceedingly challenging and can significantly impact their prognosis at times.

Despite significant advances in the diagnosis and understanding of brain metastasis mechanisms, progress in personalized treatment remains slow (7-9). With the advancement of computer technology, machine learning has become increasingly vital in various fields, and remarkable achievements have been made in biomedicine. Hajikarimloo et al. developed a model that can efficiently predict the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) status of patients with brain metastasis, offering significant guidance for targeted treatment decision-making (10). Liu et al. developed a high-performance model that can be used for decision-making regarding prophylactic cranial radiotherapy in small cell lung cancer (11). These examples highlight the great potential of machine learning models in clinical decision-making applications. However, there is still a lack of integrated treatment decision-making models for radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and surgical interventions in BM-NSCLC patients at present.

Therefore, leveraging the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database, this study analyzed the survival and associated risk factors of BM-NSCLC patients under different treatment patterns and established a high-performance predictive model. The aim is to assist in clinical decision-making with greater accuracy. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-131/rc).

Methods

Data source

As a platform where data are publicly available, SEER database, encompasses data represents approximately 27% of the U.S. population, and compiled data on cancer incidence, survival, demographics, follow-up information. From the database “Incidence SEER Research Data, 17 Registries, Nov 2023 Sub (2000–2021)”, we extracted 3,171 NSCLC patients with brain-only metastasis from 2010 to 2021. The official follow-up and record-keeping standards of SEER are as follows: https://training.seer.cancer.gov/followup/. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Patient screening

Inclusion criteria: (I) histologic types in accordance with SEER codes: adenocarcinoma (AD) (8140, 8144, 8230, 8250, 8255, 8260, 8290, 8310, 8323, 8333, 8401, 8480, 8490, 8550, 8570, 8571, and 8574), squamous cell carcinoma (SQCC) (8052, 8070–8075, 8083, 8084, and 8123), large cell carcinoma (LCC) (8012–8014, 8031–8033, 8046, and 8082) and additional varieties of NSCLC (8022, 8200, 8240, 8430, 8560, 8562, and 8980); (II) single primary lung cancer; (III) without primary site surgery.

Exclusion criteria: (I) patients with unknown demographic, pathological and treatment characteristics; (II) not only brain metastasis; (III) survival of less than 1 month; (IV) age <18 years; (V) bilateral tumor. Details depicted in flowchart (Figure 1).

Variable selection

Employing four knots at prespecified locations, restricted cubic splines (RCSs) were used to detect the possible nonlinear dependency of the relationship between the risk of death and age levels. Thus, we split the age feature to “<68” and “≥68” years (Figure S1). In terms of “Marital status” individuals with partners were categorized as “married”, while others were labeled as “Single”. Tumor (T)-stage and node (N)-stage were defined by American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC). Univariate analysis included all characteristics. In multivariate analysis, we excluded factors for univariate analysis got P value over 0.05.

Model development and evaluation

Considering the generally poor prognosis of patients with BM-NSCLC, we have selected 1-year OS as the target for prediction. The dataset was partitioned randomly into training and testing subsets at a ratio of 7:3 in R-Studio. The study incorporated the independent prognostic factors identified in the multivariate analysis as predictors. Utilizing lattice filtering and performed ten internal cross-validations, factors and the remaining model parameters were figured out. Four renowned machine learning models, random forest (RF), logistic regression (LR), support vector machine (SVM), and K-nearest neighbor (KNN), were developed in training-set. Then, the five external cross-validations were performed to enhance the stability of the models. After that, based on area under the curve (AUC) of receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and decision curve analysis (DCA), a web application was developed utilizing RF model.

Statistical analysis

All the analysis, data visualization, statistical analysis and model construction were conducted in R Studio (Version: 2024.09.1+394). Computational resources: AMD Ryzen 5 5600H with Radeon Graphics. Survival analyses for OS and cancer-specific survival (CSS) were performed using Kaplan-Meier plots and the log-rank test grouped by different treatment. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis identified independent prognostic factors in OS and CSS. This factor was included in models construction by the “mlr3verse” package. RF model outperformed the LR, SVM, KNN for better AUC and DCA. P value ≤0.05 or lower was considered statistically significant. AUC value ≥0.8 can be considered as indicating reliable performance. The importance of features in RF model was analyzed for features explanation.

Results

Baseline characteristics

In our study, a total of 3,171 NSCLC patients with brain-only metastases diagnosed between 2010 and 2021 were included. Baseline characteristics revealed that more samples distributed under 68 years old (60.9%). There was not huge disparity in sex (47.5% female), marital status (single 48.4%), laterality (left 42%) and T-stage (alone 51.2%) factor. On the contrary, primary site located at upper lobe (63.1%), adenocarcinoma (67.5%), pathologic grade III (70.1%), later N-stage (61.4%) account for the majority in the factor primary site, histology type, grade and N-stage. It is noteworthy that a small percentage of patients underwent chemotherapy (4.8%) or distant surgery (12.5%). Detailed baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1.

Survival analysis

The SCR (surgery for metastasis site + chemotherapy + radiotherapy) treatment group exhibited a median overall survival (mOS) of 13 months and a median cancer-specific survival (mCSS) of 12 months, superior to other groups. Notably, significant statistical differences were observed with P values all less than 0.001, except for the comparison between SCR and SC (surgery for metastasis site + chemotherapy), which had a P value of 0.04 for OS. For patients who did not undergo surgery, the CR (chemotherapy + radiotherapy) group had the best prognosis, with mOS and CSS being 10 and 9 months respectively better than C (chemotherapy) and R (radiotherapy) group. Among patients receiving monotherapy, the C group had the best prognosis with a mOS of 8 months and mCSS of 7 months, which was significantly greater than the SR (surgery for metastasis site + radiotherapy) and SC groups, particularly in CSS. Notably, the R group (mOS and mCSS both 3 months) and S (surgery for metastasis site) group (mOS and mCSS both 2 months) were not statistically different from the untreated group (mOS and mCSS both 2 months) in terms of OS but were in CSS. The CR and R groups achieved better prognoses than the SC (mOS and mCSS both 5 months) and S groups, respectively, in CSS only. The combined SR group showed a significant difference in OS and CSS, being superior to the S and R groups. Detailed OS and CSS data are displayed in Figure 2.

Univariate and multivariate analysis

Univariate and multivariable Cox regression analyses robustly identified age, sex, race, marital status, tumor laterality, primary tumor site, histological subtype, T-stage, N-stage, and treatment approaches as significant independent prognostic factors for OS and CSS (Tables S1,S2). The details are depicted in the forest plot (Figure 3). The multivariable analysis detected no significant difference in lesion laterality or pathologic grade. Notably, older age (≥68 years), male sex, being unmarried, non-adenocarcinoma histology, advanced T/N stages, and lack of treatment were identified as poor prognostic indicators.

Model development and website

In the testing set, the RF model (optimal parameters: num.trees =300, mtry =3, min.node.size =5, num.threads =1) demonstrated the superior AUC and DCA area. The calibration curve of the RF model showed its reliable performance (Figure 4). The web interface that allows researchers to calculate the probability of 1-year survival after inputting clinical characteristics of new patients is clinician-friendly and convenient (Figure 5) (https://machine-learning.shinyapps.io/NJU-L/).

Discussion

Extra-thoracic metastasis poses a significant burden on NSCLC patients, affecting not only survival time but also quality of life. Zhao et al. analyzed the survival of liver-metastasized NSCLC patients, aiding in the clinical recognition and accurate prognosis forecasting of these patients (12). Our study leveraged the extensive information from the SEER database to select 3,171 NSCLC patients with brain-only metastases who had not undergone primary surgery, focusing exclusively on survival outcomes under various treatment modalities.

Metastatic site can significantly affect tumor burden and clinical symptoms. Our characteristics chart shows that 12.5% of BM-NSCLC patients underwent brain surgery, consistent with previous findings (13). However, survival analysis revealed that surgery alone (mOS: 2 months) did not prolong OS, and radiation alone (mOS: 3 months) also showed limited benefits. Although the R and S groups achieved statistical significance compared to the untreated group in CSS, the benefits were not substantial. According to the study by Park et al., they explored the association of radiotherapy with survival differences among Asian patients with BM (14). They found that radiation provided a survival benefit of 3.7 months for patients with lung-only primary tumors, but long-term survival was not observed. A study with a cohort including 7,562 stage IV lung cancer patients indicated that patients undergoing radiotherapy for pulmonary lesions experienced significantly enhanced OS with hazard ratio (HR) of 0.68 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.60–0.78; P<0.001], compared to those without such treatment (15). Additionally, radiotherapy for extrathoracic metastases was associated with significantly improved survival (HR =0.84; 95% CI: 0.77–0.93; P<0.001). In the study by Kayama et al., patients who underwent surgery followed by radiation achieved a mOS of 15.6 months with low complication rates in patients with performance status scores of 0 to 2 (16). Similarly, other studies have shown that combined treatment of SR statistically improves OS for patients with tumor sizes over 3 cm in depth (17) for patients with tumor size over 3 cm in deep. Additionally, we found that chemotherapy alone (mOS: 8 months) outperformed surgery, radiation, and even combined surgery and irradiation (mOS: 4 months) or chemotherapy treatment (mOS: 5 months). Previous studies have demonstrated the importance of chemotherapy, with results similar to our survival times (18-20). Barlesi et al. found that pemetrexed-cisplatin, as first-line therapy for NSCLC patients with BM, is an effective and safe regimen (21). The discrepancy in survival between the SR, SC, and C groups may be due to our study excluding patients with primary surgery, as severe tumor burden makes it difficult to achieve improvement with regional therapies such as surgery and radiation (22). The survival of the CR (mOS: 10 months, mCSS: 9 months) and SCR (mOS: 13 months, mCSS: 12 months) groups supports this notion, as the addition of chemotherapy significantly improved survival. Likewise, Cagney et al. found that radiation followed by pemetrexed benefitted patients by reducing the need for salvage brain-directed radiation therapy and the likelihood of developing new brain metastases (23). The most significant discovery in survival is that the combination of metastasis site surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy is superior to other treatments in our study, with dramatic statistical significance for P values under 0.001, highlighting the value of combined regional and systemic treatment for BM-NSCLC (24).

In variable analysis, the older population experienced a higher risk. Regarding the sex factor, males exhibited poorer survival. In the research of Yu et al., they estimated the survival difference between males and females, obtaining results consistent with ours (25). The reason for the survival advantage in females may be due to less smoking and a higher proportion of adenocarcinoma patients, contributing to better treatment benefits (26). Regarding race, we found that Asians seem to have a lower risk. Chen et al. analyzed the genetic differences between Asians and Europeans, revealing more stable genomes in Asians (27). Marital status also significantly impacts survival (28). Regarding survival risk differences in different primary site groups, there was no difference for laterality but a significant difference for different lobes. Xie et al. considered this to be associated with specific lymph node metastasis (29). Patients with adenocarcinoma had better prognoses in our study. Chen and Dhahbi explained that NSCLC patients with squamous cell carcinoma have higher regulation of p53 signaling and more active immune responses (30). Lastly, N-stage and histology grade were not independent factors for OS. Given that Bates and Milano have presented results contrary to ours, and these two factors play an inescapable role in RF, data bias is the likely explanation (31).

Zhong et al. developed a model with superior performance in predicting prognosis for bone metastatic breast cancer patients by constructing and estimating several models (32). Therefore, to assist clinical decision-making, we developed four machine-learning models capable of predicting 1-year OS probability under different treatment conditions. Based on AUC of ROC and DCA curve evaluations (33), we selected the best-performing model: RF, whose AUC based on the testing set approached 0.9. Subsequently, we found that the treatment factor was the most critical for model prediction in importance analysis. This underscores the significant influence of clinical decision-making on survival. After model construction, we created a concise website for user access via shinyapps.io. The interface provides feedback on the probability of 1-year survival based on input factors (34).

We appreciate the SEER database for providing a vast amount of cancer information, enabling us to analyze a rich sample of data. However, there are limitations we must acknowledge. Firstly, the lack of treatment information, such as targeted therapy and immunotherapy, the most promising treatments currently, may have introduced unexplainable factors into our study. Secondly, the diagnosis description is insufficient, lacking details such as the number of metastasis sites and sizes, the phase of metastasis in the overall treatment course, and blood laboratory tests for genetic and tumor markers, as well as organ function, which can influence tumor and body status to a certain extent. Thirdly, as a retrospective study, we excluded some samples according to inclusion criteria, introducing inevitable selection bias. At last, as some of the sample data dates back to earlier periods, the differences in specific anti-cancer treatment plans and supportive care regimens could also affect the accuracy of the model. Therefore, prospective studies are necessary for external validation before clinical practical application.

Conclusions

In conclusion, for patients with BM-NSCLC who have not undergone primary site surgery, chemotherapy-based systemic therapy combined with radiation and metastatic site resection offers greater benefits to patients than regional interventions alone. Age, sex, race, marital status, primary tumor site, histological subtype, T-stage, N-stage, and treatment choice are identified as significant independent prognostic factors. The RF model demonstrated superior predictive performance compared to LR, SVM, and KNN in predicting 1-year survival. There is substantial potential for improvement by incorporating more detailed diagnostic and treatment history information.

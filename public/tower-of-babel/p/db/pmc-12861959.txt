Prognostic and functional role of PPAR-alpha and SNP receptor (Leu162Val) in acute coronary syndrome: a potential novel target
Authors: Abdullah Abdulsattar Raeef, Hassan H. Al-Saeed, Sami Mekhlif Mishlish
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and objectivePPAR-alpha plays a key role in acute coronary syndrome (ACS). Its activation influences lipid metabolism and inflammation, impacting cardiovascular health and potentially mitigating risks related to heart conditions. This study aims to find the relationship between PPAR-alpha concentration and genetic polymorphism in ACS patients.Methods and materialsThe study enrolled 90 ACS patients and 90 controls aged 30–70. Blood samples were analyzed for biomarkers (troponin, CK-MB, hs-CRP, PPARα) and rs1800206 SNP via ELISA and HRM-PCR. Data were statistically analyzed using SPSS v21, ANOVA, and ROC tests.ResultsThe results showed an increase in the levels of troponin, CK-MB, and hs-CRP for the patient groups compared to the healthy groups with significant differences between the groups (p-value <0.05). The level of (PPAR-alpha) was low in patients (1472.2 ± 74.5) and high in the healthy group (2051.8 ± 149.9) with significant differences between study groups. ROC analysis identified CK-MB, and hs-CRP as optimal biomarkers for ACS diagnosis; PPARα showed good predictive value. The study also found that the dominant genotype (CC) represented a small percentage of the patient samples while the homozygotes (CG) and the recessive (GG) represented the highest percentage, unlike the healthy group. Also, Allele (C) has a low percentage in the patients’ group while allele (G) has a high percentage with considering the G allele as a risk factor. In addition, the results showed an abnormal distribution of Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium indicating the presence of factors extraneous to the community that changed the genetic pattern. The results also showed that the dominant genetic model is the best expressive for the samples of the Iraqi community infected with ACS.ConclusionsThe present study demonstrated a relationship between the concentration of PPAR-alpha and the genetic polymorphism of Leu162Val (rs1800206) SNP and acute coronary syndrome with a decrease in the concentration of PPAR-alpha and the predominance of the recessive gene and the recessive allele in patients compared to healthy individuals.

Introduction

Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) is a critical medical condition that encompasses a range of acute myocardial ischemic events, including unstable angina, non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), and ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). It is primarily caused by the rupture of an atherosclerotic plaque, leading to thrombosis and subsequent obstruction of coronary blood flow. This condition is a significant contributor to morbidity and mortality worldwide, particularly in individuals over the age of 35, and requires prompt diagnosis and management to prevent severe outcomes.1 The diagnosis of ACS is primarily based on clinical presentation, electrocardiogram (ECG) changes, and cardiac biomarkers such as troponins. ECG is crucial for differentiating between STEMI and NSTEMI, while troponin levels help confirm myocardial necrosis.2 The diagnosis of ACS depends on clinical presentation and family history of IHD and is confirmed through ECG and cardiac enzymes as either unstable angina or acute myocardial infarction.3

Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) are a group of nuclear receptor proteins that play a crucial role in regulating gene expression related to various metabolic processes. PPARs consist of three subtypes: PPAR-alpha, PPAR-beta/delta, and PPAR-gamma, each with distinct roles and tissue distributions.4 PPARs are central to lipid metabolism and glucose homeostasis.5.

PPARα is a key regulator of fatty acid oxidation, particularly in the liver, where it enhances the breakdown of fatty acids, thereby reducing triglyceride levels and increasing high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol.6 It modulates the expression of genes involved in lipoprotein metabolism, influencing both atherogenic and antiatherogenic lipoproteins.7 Activation of PPARα by agonists such as fibrates has been shown to effectively treat hypertriglyceridemia and low HDL cholesterol levels, although clinical trial results on cardiovascular outcomes are mixed.8.

Different PPARs are encoded by different chromosomes. PPARα is coded by chromosome 22q12-13.1.9 PPARs are expressed in various tissues, with PPARα found in the liver, kidney, heart, muscle, and adipose tissue.10 PPARα activity in regulating fatty acid uptake and catabolism plays a critical role in supplying energy to muscle and heart tissues. It is mediated by the activation of mitochondrial carnitine palmitoyl transferase I (CPT1),11 and is responsible for the transfer of activated fatty acids across the mitochondrial outer membrane. In addition to fatty acid oxidation, PPARα activity leads to an increase in apolipoprotein (apo) A-I, apoA-II, ATP-binding cassette (ABC) A1, and HDL-C levels. There is an increase of very-low density lipoprotein (VLDL) clearance and activity of lipoprotein lipase (LPL)12, with a concomitant reduction of apoC-III, VLDL production, and an increase of LDL particle size. The reduction in triglycerides (TGs) and the increase in HDL levels following fibrate therapy are fundamental in managing cardiovascular (CV) risk in individuals with central obesity and insulin resistance, potentially enhancing the PPARα-related metabolic13.

The single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) rs1800206 in the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα) gene has been studied for its potential impact on various metabolic and physiological processes. This SNP, characterized by a C > G substitution leading to a Leu162Val amino acid change, has been associated with different health outcomes and responses to interventions.14 The rs1800206 has been linked to changes in lipid metabolism. Specifically, the CG genotype is associated with reduced high-density lipoprotein (HDL) levels following aerobic exercise, suggesting a potential negative impact on cholesterol regulation in certain individuals.15 The L162V polymorphism is associated with increased levels of total cholesterol, LDL-C, and triglycerides, and reduced HDL-C. While these changes may influence cardiovascular risk factors, a direct association with increased cardiovascular risk has not been established.14 The minor allele of L162V has been shown to affect responses to dietary interventions—such as those involving n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids—which are known to reduce cardiovascular disease risk.16.

This study aims to investigate the relationship between peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPARα) expression levels and the single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) rs1800206 (L162V) in individuals with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) within the Iraqi population. Materials and methods

Study design

Ninety patients with ACS were enrolled in this study, along with 90 samples as controls. Their ages ranged from 30 to 70 years. The study period for sample collection was from December 23, 2023, to June 13, 2024. Patients presenting with chest pain or typical symptoms suggestive of ischemic heart disease (IHD) were admitted to the coronary care unit (CCU) and emergency department (ED) in Al-Ramadi Teaching Hospital/Ramadi and Madinat al-Amamin al-Kadhimin Al-Tbbia Hospital/Baghdad.

Criteria

Inclusion

1- All the patients had no complaints of other chronic or systemic diseases. The patients were divided into two groups: a. Myocardial infarction patients were sixty (60) patients classified clinically into STEMI (n = 30) and NSTEMI (n = 30), depending on the diagnosis of the cardiologist and confirmed by ECG and cardiac enzyme troponin level.

b. Unstable angina patients were thirty (30) patients presented to the emergency department with acute chest pain and ECG changes suggestive of ischemia (Inverted T or Q wave), and then referred to CCU and thus, diagnosed as cases of unstable angina.

2- Controls: The control group consists of 90 non-ischemic individuals i.e. those without symptomatic CAD or any significant luminal narrowing on coronary angiography. All individuals in the control group adapted to the history questionnaire and confirmed by laboratory diagnostic of (Troponin, CK-MB, hs-CRP).

Exclusion criteria

Patients aged less than 30 years and above 70 years with severe renal failure and renal dysfunction, known moderate or severe liver disease, diabetes mellitus, Neurological disease, Cancer, Endocrine diseases, Patients who take statins and NSAID, Patients who had normal levels of cardiac biomarkers, Patients with cardiac surgery and post percutaneous coronary intervention and pregnant women.

Specimen collection and procedures

Ten milliliters of venous blood samples were drawn from each patient that presented to CCU or ED after diagnosis with ACS. The same quantity of blood was drawn from the control group. The Whole blood is divided into two parts:

1 − Eight milliliters of blood samples were left for 20 min in the gel tube at room temperature. After coagulation, sera were separated by centrifugation at 2000 xg for 10 min. Sera were divided into small aliquots and put in a multi-Eppendorf tube (1.5 ml) for:

A. Immediate measurements of Cardiac troponin (cTnI) by (Nipigon device “ ECLIA”), Creatinine kinase-MB (CK-MB), High sensitive-C Reactive Protein (hs-CRP) by ichroma™ Ⅱ device.

B. The rest was stored at −26°C (super freeze) until assayed for (PPAR alpha) assay and were measured using an enzyme-linked immune sorbent assay (ELISA) kit Provided by (BT LAB) company with lot number (E1450Hu).

2 − Two milliliters of the fresh blood sample left in the EDTA tube for HbA1c measurement and to use for DNA extraction for molecular study and find polymorphism by HRM-PCR technique.

Molecular screening

DNA was extracted by using the gSYNCTM DNA Extraction kit (Geneaid company). DNA concentration and purity were determined by a Nanodrop spectrophotometer (Quawell). The primer was designed to show the SNP of rs1800206 and had forward sequence (CTTGTCTTTACGGTCATAACAGC) and reverse sequence (GGAAAATGTGCAGGGCCACC). A Rotor gene Q Real-time PCR System (QIAGEN) was used to perform qPCR-HRM, followed by an HRM analysis with 0.2 °C scaling from 55 to 95 °C. 2xTransStart® Tip Green qPCR Super Mix Synthetic SNP sequences were evaluated using duplicates. To identify allelic differences, qPCR-HRM was used on triplicate synthetic controls, and normalized melting curves (NMC) and differential curves (DC) were constructed using the HRM Tool included in the integrated program (Rotor gene 4.4).

Ethical approval

The study was approved by the institutional review board (IRB) of the College of Medicine/Al-Nahrain University with reference I.D (20231005).

Data analysis

Data were statistically analyzed using SPSS version 24, GraphPad Prism 9, and Microsoft Excel. A comparison of data between the two groups was performed by applying an unpaired Student’s t-test. ANOVA (analysis of variance) with the post hoc Tukey test was used for comparing the means of more than two groups. The P-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. The association between 2 categorical variables was assessed by the Chi-square test. Sensitivity, specificity, cut-off value, and predictive utility were explored with receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. ROC analysis was carried out on the levels of PPAR-alpha, Troponin, CK-MB, and hs-CRP for ACS and other study groups.

Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) was used to investigate allele and genotype frequencies. The Chi-square test was used to compare the frequency of genotypes and alleles in the patient and control groups (c2test). Odds ratios (ORs) with a 95 percent confidence interval (CI) were constructed to determine the strength of the link between the investigated gene (PPAR) SNP and acute coronary syndrome patients to analyze the possible relationships between genetic variations of (PPAR). The P value for all tests was judged significant if less than 0.05.

In the patient group, all observed genotypes were different than expected with low observed for CC genotype (dominant) and high observed for CG and GG genotype (recessive and heterozygotes). In addition, the differences compared to the population were high (p-value < 0.0001). This result indicates that hereditary genotypes which are considered risk factors (CG and GG) were different in cases towards population.

On the other side, control group results for Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium showed equal value for observed and expected results for all genotype forms (CC, CG, and GG) with non-differences compared to all populations that indicate non-change or mutation in healthy persons.

The control group is in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, which is expected in a normal population, while the ACS patient group deviates significantly. This might suggest that the PPAR gene SNP 1800206 is potentially associated with the ACS condition, where non-random genotype distribution could reflect a relationship between this SNP and ACS, possibly due to genetic or environmental factors affecting this gene in patients.

Discussion

Plaque rupture (PR) is the most common cause of ACS across all sexes and ages. However, plaque erosion (PE) shows a distinct pattern, being more prevalent in younger males, while older females exhibit higher PE incidence. Calcified nodules (CN) are less common but increase with age, particularly in those over 80 years old.18 Women with ACS have a higher adjusted risk for cardiovascular death and heart failure hospitalization post-percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) compared to men. This aligns with historical data showing higher short-term mortality but better long-term survival for women.19 In humans, PPAR-alpha expression in T cells is more abundant in males, influenced by androgen levels, which affects immune responses and susceptibility to autoimmune diseases.20 Estrogen appears to modulate PPAR-alpha activity, as seen in female ovariectomized mice where estrogen inhibits PPAR-alpha’s effects on obesity.21 The Leu162Val and intron 7 G > C polymorphisms in the PPAR-alpha gene show different effects on lipid levels and cardiovascular risk between men and women, indicating a genetic basis for sex-specific responses.22.

Age significantly impacts the clinical presentation, treatment, and outcomes of patients with Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS). Elderly patients are typically defined as those aged 75 years and older.23 Younger patients, particularly those under 55, are more likely to have modifiable risk factors like smoking and dyslipidemia, whereas older patients often present with multi-vessel coronary artery disease.24.

The relationship between body mass index (BMI) and the risk of developing acute coronary syndrome (ACS) in patients with pre-existing cardiovascular disease is complex and multifaceted. Research indicates that both low and high BMI can influence cardiovascular outcomes, with varying implications for patients with coronary artery disease (CAD) or ACS. The “obesity paradox” suggests that higher BMI may sometimes be associated with better outcomes in these patients, although this is not universally accepted.25 BMI is an independent predictor of acute coronary syndrome in patients with established coronary atherosclerosis, with increased risk evident even at mildly elevated BMI levels.26, 27.

The study by Pagani et al. showed that cTnI, a decision limit of 3.1 μg/L is used for acute myocardial infarction, while 0.2 μg/L is the threshold for minimal myocardial damage.28 While our current study showed a critical limit of diagnosis ACS for TP was 0.0235. The previous study showed that the critical cutoff value for cTnT in diagnosing ACS is 0.10 ng/mL, with a sensitivity of 90.9 % and specificity of 87.9 %.29 Our results study showed that the cutoff value for TP was 0.0235 with a specificity of 100 % and sensitivity of 67.8 % and the previous study partially agreed with our study results. The level of high-sensitivity troponin assays improves diagnostic sensitivity, but they may reduce specificity, leading to potential over-diagnosis of ACS.30 Troponin assays, particularly high-sensitivity ones, have improved the diagnostic accuracy for ACS, including UA. They help in distinguishing UA from other non-cardiac causes of chest pain and from myocardial infarction, where troponin levels are significantly higher.31 While the troponin level remains within normal limits but increases slightly. High-sensitivity troponin assays, such as hscTnT2, have improved the diagnostic accuracy for differentiating NSTEMI from STEMI. These assays can detect myocardial injury earlier, facilitating prompt clinical decision-making.32 The results of troponin between study groups (UA, NSTEMI, STEMI) showed a gradual increase in levels and this increase may be considered as a differential variable for ACS groups. These results come following other study results confirming the efficacy of hscTnT in the diagnosis of acute coronary syndrome.33, 34 The size of the infarcted area could be one of the differentiation criteria between STEMI and NSTEMI and the use of cardiac-specific troponin T was useful to estimate infarction area and severity.35.

The level of creatine kinase MB isoenzyme (CK-MB) in the UA group increased than in healthy persons which corresponds with previous studies it has been proven that CK-MB levels can detect minor ischemic myocardial injury, which may not be apparent through clinical symptoms or ECG changes. This detection is crucial as it correlates with a higher risk of progression to MI or cardiac death.36 Also, the results showed increasing in CK-MB levels for the NSTEMI and STEMI groups that agreement with the study result that conducted CK-MB levels are particularly useful in cases where troponin levels are elevated without ST-segment elevation, helping to identify myocardial necrosis in a broader patient population.37 CK-MB levels, when compared to troponin levels, have shown slightly better discrimination in predicting mortality in both STEMI and NSTEMI patients, suggesting their utility in risk stratification and guiding treatment decisions.38 A study results showed that serial CK-MB measurements in patients with chest pain and non-diagnostic ECGs have shown high sensitivity for detecting MI, with sensitivity increasing significantly over time from the onset of symptoms.39.

Studies demonstrated a positive correlation between hs-CRP levels and the severity of CAD. Higher hs-CRP levels are associated with a greater burden of CAD, indicating a more severe disease.40, 41 These Studies correspond with the current results study that showed increasing in concentration of hs-CRP for all ACS groups compared to healthy persons. In comparing STEMI and NSTEMI patients, STEMI patients tend to have higher peak hs-CRP levels, suggesting a more pronounced inflammatory response in this subgroup.42 This study’s results correspond with current findings according to the increase in CK-MB and hs-CRP concentrations in three groups of ACS (UA, NSTEMI, STEMI). In patients with ACS, hs-CRP levels were found to be higher in those with more complex coronary lesions, as indicated by higher Syntax Scores and the presence of multi-vessel disease.43 The presence of elevated hs-CRP levels is associated with more vulnerable plaque components, such as necrotic core-rich plaques, which are indicative of a higher risk for adverse cardiovascular events.44 Troponin levels, particularly when combined with C-reactive protein (CRP) measurements, can stratify mortality risk in NSTEMI patients. High troponin levels, even in the presence of normal CRP levels, are associated with increased short-term mortality, highlighting the prognostic value of troponin in assessing patient outcomes.45 The cutoff value of hs-CRP between Healthy persons and ACS patients was 1.015 mg/L indicating that the person changes from a normal state to a ACS state at this value. Sargowo’s research highlighted the diagnostic value of hs-CRP, with a mean concentration of 4.04 ± 1.94 mg/L in ACS cases, significantly higher than in controls. This study used Receiver Operating Curve analysis to determine the cutoff, emphasizing hs-CRP’s utility in diagnosing ACS.46.

It is the first study that used a quantitative PPAR-alpha in patients with ACS in the world. Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPAR-alpha) activation has been shown to reduce myocardial infarct size significantly. In a study using rat models, the administration of PPAR-alpha agonists like clofibrate and WY14643 resulted in a 43 % reduction in infarct size, suggesting a protective role against myocardial necrosis during ischemia–reperfusion injury.47 The current study cannot find previous studies on estimating the concentration (quantitative) of PPAR-alpha in patients with ACS. The activation of PPAR-alpha has been linked to beneficial effects on plasma lipid profiles, which are crucial in the management of atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases, including ACS. In addition, the therapeutic potential of PPAR-α agonists, such as fibrates, has been explored for their ability to reduce triglyceride levels and increase high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol, potentially lowering the risk of myocardial infarction events associated with ACS.7, 48 A study conducted by Iraqi researchers found significant differences in mean serum levels of PPAR-γ between ACS patients and the control group, suggesting to potential of a risk biomarker for ACS. The same study also suggests that elevated PPAR-γ levels may be related to ischemic events rather than its controversial role in inflammation.49 On the other hand, the results of this study showed decrease in levels of PPAR-alpha in patients with ACS, this indicated that PPAR-alpha effected inversely in Iraqi population.

A study researching the relationship between rs1800206 SNP and lipid levels in a sample of Mexican populations showed no correlation between rs1800206 SNP and lipid levels (Cholesterol and LDL), while the frequency of the dominant gene (CC) was higher than the recessive (GG). This result contradicts the current study on the relationship of rs1800206 SNP with acute coronary syndrome, which proved that the recessive gene (GG) has a percentage of (16.7 %) while the dominant (CC) has a lower percentage (5.6 %) and hetero (CG) has percentage (77.8 %) with the recessive O.R (6.34) being considered as a risk factor, while the dominant gene is considered as a protective factor (OR = 0.04).50 The study reported that CG genotype appeared to have a higher risk for dyslipidemia.51 This result corresponds with the current result that the CG genotype is considered a risk factor for ACS. Costa-Urrutia et al reported an association between allele G of PPARα rs1800206 and central obesity risk in a Mexican-mestizo population.52 According to current results that showed allele G is considered a risk factor and has a high frequency in patients with ACS.

A study conducted by53 proved that the dominant genotype (CC) for people who are treated with β-blocker and have ACS was 89 % while the recessive genotype (GG) was (0.7 %). This result confirms the possibility of using PPAR to treat and prevent ACS.

A study on the Russian population found that individuals carrying the L162V genotype and the V allele of the PPAR-α gene have a significantly increased risk of developing coronary heart disease (CHD). Specifically, the risk is increased by 2.13 times for those with the L162V (CG) genotype and by 2.21 times for those with the V (G) allele.54 This result corresponds to the risk of the CG genotype and G allele on heart diseases in general and ACS specific.

According to the Framingham study, the incidence of carriage of L162V (rs1800206) polymorphism of the PPAR-α gene in the general population was 6,9%.55 Thus, the occurrence of this genotype was twice as high (14 %) in the group of CHD patients than in the population of the Framingham study and the control group of persons without CHD. Similar results were obtained in a prospective study Northwick Park Heart Survey,56 in which the connection was established between the PPAR-α L162V (CG) genotype with the early development of coronary heart disease in patients of the North-West region of Europe.

A research study found that the L162V (rs1800206) polymorphism in the PPAR-α gene is associated with an increased risk of acute coronary syndrome (ACS). In a comparison between ACS patients and healthy controls, the CC homozygous genotype was identified in 19 % of ACS patients but was completely absent in the control group, showing a significant association with ACS (p < 0.01) Additionally, the study reported that the G allele was significantly more frequent among ACS patients than in controls, suggesting a genetic predisposition to ACS in individuals carrying this allele.57 These findings are consistent with the results of the current study, which also identified the G allele as a risk factor for ACS.

Several studies have found no evidence for such an association between PPAR-alpha L162V on the possibility of developing acute coronary syndrome.58, 59 These inconsistencies may be due in large part to differing environments, genetic backgrounds, and diseased status (including medications prescribed) between the populations considered.60

In a previous study conducted by (Isaac J. Bage. Et, al. 2023) on patients with dyslipidemia in diabetic patients who may be at risk for cardiovascular diseases in the Indian populations. The genotypes of the PPAR-alpha specifically for SNP Leu162val (rs1800206) were determined and it was found that the dominant genotype (CC) was 97.4 % in patients and 97 % in controls. As for the genotype (CG) percentage in patients was 11 % while in healthy controls was 10 % with a risk ratio of 1.26, which means that this genotype is a risk factor for the disease. While the percentage of the recessive genotype (GG) in patients and controls was 0 %. In addition, the presence of the dominant allele (C) in the study samples was 98.6 % in patients while it was 98 % in healthy controls. While the recessive allele (G) was 1.4 % in patients and 2 % in healthy subjects with a risk factor (OR) of 1.25, which indicates that the allele (G) is considered a risk factor for lipid disorders.61 The results of the previous study are consistent with the results of our study regarding the percentage of genetic polymorphism (CG) but at a higher rate and also with the risk factor (O.R higher than 1) and also with the percentage of the recessive allele (G) and its risk factor, although the study is related to diabetes and dyslipidemia, it may also affect on the acute coronary syndrome.

In a Lithuanian study, the authors reported an association of CG (Leu/Val) genotype with elevated TG levels (OR = 2.67 and 95 % CI = 1.15 to 6.16) among dyslipidemia subjects.62 This result can agree with our results in the risk development of coronary artery disease due to dyslipidemia.

Conclusions

This study provides significant insights into the association of PPARα levels and the rs1800206 (Leu162Val) single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) in the Iraqi population. The findings demonstrate that PPARα levels are significantly reduced in ACS patients compared to healthy controls, suggesting a potential role of this receptor in the pathogenesis of ACS. The study also identified that the rs1800206 SNP, particularly the CG and GG genotypes, is associated with an increased risk of ACS, while the CC genotype appears to have a protective effect. Furthermore, the G allele was identified as a significant risk factor for ACS, whereas the C allele showed a protective role. Biomarker analysis revealed that troponin, CK-MB, and hs-CRP levels were markedly elevated in ACS patients, with each parameter providing diagnostic and differential value for distinguishing between UA, NSTEMI, and STEMI subtypes. ROC analysis highlighted CK-MB and hs-CRP as the most sensitive and specific biomarkers for ACS diagnosis, with PPARα demonstrating moderate predictive utility.

TLEUDS: a cascade Dual-Transfer learning system with quality- and knowledge-enhanced for precise fetal CHD screening
Authors: Yuxuan Jiang, Jiajie Tang, Fanfan Zhu, Yuzhou Zeng, Junbo Wu, Wanqi Wang, Yuan Liu, Hongying Wang, Long Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Congenital heart disease (CHD) is the most common major congenital anomaly and a leading cause of perinatal mortality and long-term neurodevelopmental disability globally, among which severe ductal dependent CHD (D-CHD) requires urgent clinical intervention due to the high risk of catastrophic cardiovascular collapse if undiagnosed. Despite the advancement of fetal echocardiography-based analysis models, there remains a lack of comprehensive and accurate evaluation approaches for D-CHD, especially for the precise differentiation of critical subtypes including coarctation of the aorta (CoA), interrupted aortic arch (IAA), and transposition of the great arteries (TGA). To address this gap, we developed the D-CHD Precision Screening System (TLEUDS) using 9,142 fetal aortic arch echocardiographic long-axis view images and 58 ultrasound clips from three medical institutions. Aiming to solve the challenges of uneven quality consistency and long-tail distribution in real-world ultrasound imaging data, TLEUDS adopts a cascaded Dual-Transfer learning framework for quality and knowledge enhancement, which implements a closed-loop screening process from initial disease detection (via the TEN-S module, a transfer version of EfficientNetV2-Screening) to detailed subtype-specific malformation screening (via the TY-PS module, a transfer version of YOLOv11-Precise Screening). Experimental results show that TLEUDS achieved a sensitivity of 0.988, 0.941, 0.966, and 0.900 for D-CHD overall, CoA, IAA, and TGA respectively, with corresponding specificities of 0.988, 0.987, 1.000, and 0.956, demonstrating state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance compared with general models. TLEUDS is expected to serve as a potential automated screening tool for tiered healthcare and computer-aided diagnosis of fetal D-CHD. Furthermore, the proposed quality and knowledge-enhanced cascaded Dual-Transfer learning framework fully leverages the “image quality distribution” and “structural prior knowledge” of fetal echocardiography, holding great potential for extension to other ultrasound image analysis domains.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13040-026-00535-3.

Introduction

Congenital heart disease (CHD) is the most common major congenital anomaly and remains the leading cause of perinatal mortality and long-term neurodevelopmental disability worldwide, with a reported incidence of 4–50 per 1,000 live births [1]. Between 1 and 1.8 per 1,000 live births exhibit duct-dependent circulation in whom persistent patency of the arterial duct is mandatory for survival [2]. For infants whose pulmonary or systemic perfusion is duct-dependent, discharge without diagnosis amplifies the risk of catastrophic cardiovascular collapse, this type of duct-dependent congenital heart disease (D-CHD) requires urgent intervention; 10–30% of all CHD deaths during the first year are attributable to lesions that remain undetected in the community [3, 4]. Accumulating evidence indicates that antenatal diagnosis improves the pre-operative status of patients with critical CHD (CCHD) and lowers pre-surgical morbidity and mortality [4–7]. Early prenatal detection and prompt referral to a tertiary centre capable of neonatal cardiac surgery are therefore essential.

Prenatal ultrasound screening is a non-invasive, painless, safe and highly effective imaging modality [8, 9], experienced operators leverage domain-specific knowledge to extract critical information on fetal cardiac development from each scan [9–11]. Routine foetal echocardiography includes several standard views, and different diseases show view-specific pathognomonic features [12]. Even though improvements in ultrasound technology have made it easier to find things, the rates of finding things still differ a lot from country to country, especially in poorer areas [13, 14].

Deep learning has shown a lot of promise in automating foetal cardiac screening in the last few years. These data-driven models can automatically find visual patterns in prenatal ultrasound images to help with diagnosis, often with little input from experts in the field. But the fact that the heart’s anatomy is very complicated, especially when it is not working properly, makes things very difficult. This complexity renders purely data-driven methodologies vulnerable to distraction from extraneous information, such as imaging artefacts. Consequently, there is a growing research focus on integrating medical domain knowledge into these pipelines to guide the models away from non-informative regions and toward clinically relevant features.

While automated screening methods are urgently needed for D-CHD during fetal development, current research has not yet developed AI-based approaches to accurately identify severe D-CHD in fetuses, particularly distinguishing among three critical types: CoArctation of the aorta (CoA), interrupted aortic arch (IAA), and transposition of the great arteries (TGA). To solve this problem, we developed TLEUDS (Cascade Dual-Transfer Learning Enhanced Ultrasound Detection System) using echocardiographic long-axis views of the fetal aortic arch (ELVFAA) to enable precise screening for D-CHD. TLEUDS consists of two modules: the Initial Screening Module (a transfer version of EfficientNetV2-Screening, abbreviated as TEN-S) and the Precise Screening Module (a transfer version of YOLOv11-Precise Screening, abbreviated as TY-PS). It achieves a closed-loop process from initial disease detection to detailed screening of specific malformations (CoA, IAA, and TGA). Our research contributions are as follows:

Materials and image preprocessing

Data acquisition

In this study, the echocardiographic long-axis views of the fetal aortic arch (ELVFAA) served as the standard view for image acquisition and analysis. In the dataset, a normal fetal heart was defined as one confirmed to be free of congenital heart defects postnatally. Abnormal cases consisted of duct-dependent congenital heart diseases, which manifest in the ELVFAA as one of the following three abnormalities: (1) CoA, presenting as a localized narrowing of the aortic arch; (2) IAA, characterized by a complete discontinuity between the ascending and descending aorta; and (3) TGA, identified by the aorta and pulmonary artery running parallel rather than crossing, with the ductus arteriosus connecting to the descending aorta. All prenatal ultrasound diagnoses were confirmed through postnatal follow-up examinations, surgeries, or consensus comments from three senior sonographers with over a decade of experience in prenatal care.

The study utilized 9200 ELVFAA samples (9,142 image and 58 video clips) from three medical institutions to develop TLEUDS: an internal dataset from Guangzhou Women and Children’s Medical Center (GZMC) and external datasets from Xinqiao Hospital (XQH) and Wuhan Women and Children Health Care Center (WHCC), with detailed characteristics provided in Table 1. The retrospective study protocol was approved by the relevant ethics committees, and informed consent was waived.

Method

Framework overview

In this work, we investigate a strategy for deeply integrating pivotal domain knowledge into transfer-learning models to achieve more accurate diagnosis. We address two practical challenges in deep-learning-based detection of duct-dependent congenital heart disease (CHD): first, the scarcity of positive samples, and second, the absence of explicit guidance from domain knowledge. To overcome these limitations, we employ transfer learning in both the classification-based screening stage and the detection-based lesion-localization and diagnosis stage. As illustrated in Fig. 1, we propose TLEUDS, a novel framework that integrates anatomical priors to improve screening precision. The system consists of two core components:

The Initial Screening Module (TEN-S): This module is constructed with an EfficientNetV2 backbone. It employs a two-phase transfer learning strategy: first learning general sonographic features from a routine-quality dataset, then refining them on a high-quality subset to achieve precise discrimination between normal and abnormal fetal hearts.

The Precise Screening Module (TY-PS): This module is built around a structurally modified version of the YOLOv11s network, tailored for fine-grained analysis of fetal echocardiograms. Our customized architecture is first pre-trained on normal cases to learn the fundamental cardiac anatomy, including the Right Atrium (RA), Left Atrium (LA), Descending Aorta (DAO), and Aortic Arch (ARCH). The acquired representations are subsequently transferred through a normal-to-abnormal paradigm to the task of lesion detection, facilitating precise pixel-level localization of duct-dependent CHDs, including CoA, TGA, and IAA.

Initial screening module: Transfer-EfficientNetV2-Screening (TEN-S)

In this study, we aimed to create a high-efficiency screening module specifically designed for identifying aortic arch abnormalities in fetal prenatal ultrasound. The inherently non-standardised nature of sonography, however, introduces substantial quality variance across scans, posing an additional challenge for model training. Based on our earlier multicenter study [15], we switched out the old backbone for the lighter EfficientNetV2 to better handle images with different quality while still being able to tell them apart.

The training process consists of two sequential stages. In order to teach the model basic, subtle sonographic patterns, it is first pre-trained on a routine-quality dataset. The obtained weights are then applied to the high-quality dataset and adjusted. By using this technique, the network can accurately distinguish between normal and abnormal by utilizing the underlying ultrasound semantics it has learned. By leveraging transfer learning of data of different quality within the domain, we enhanced the model’s robustness under various image qualities and improved its overall reliability. The lightweight EfficientNetV2-S variant is adopted as the backbone, further boosting computational efficiency while reducing resource requirements.

Configuration of the EfficientNetV2-S Architecture

EfficientNetV2 represents the latest evolution of the EfficientNet family and introduces substantial improvements over EfficientNetV1. EfficientNetV2-S is a highly engineered network that employs a novel compound-scaling strategy to maintain competitive accuracy while markedly reducing model size and computational load. The architecture spans eight stages (Stage 0–7) and extensively utilises MBConv and Fused-MBConv blocks for efficient feature extraction. As illustrated in Fig. 2; Table 2, Stage 0 begins with a 3 × 3 convolution that extracts initial low-level features. Stages 1–3 then stack 2–4 Fused-MBConv layers: Stage 1 uses an expansion ratio of 1 and 3 × 3 kernels, whereas Stages 2–3 adopt an expansion ratio of 4 with 3 × 3 kernels.

In the deeper stages (4–6), MBConv blocks are repeated 6–15 times; stage 4 employs an expansion ratio of 4, 3 × 3 kernels and SE-0.25 modules for channel-wise recalibration, while stages 5–6 increase the expansion ratio to 6 and retain 3 × 3 kernels plus SE-0.25. Stage 7 finalises the representation via 1 × 1 convolution, global average pooling and a fully connected classifier.

Additionally, progressive learning dynamically adjusts input resolution during training, trading speed for accuracy and further reducing compute demand. Joint optimisation of depth, width and resolution via compound scaling furnishes an efficient backbone for resource-constrained screening. When coupled with domain-adaptive transfer learning, the proposed model not only improves the recognition accuracy of abnormal arch morphology but also enhances sensitivity to subtle anatomical variations in fetal ultrasound.

Precise screening module: Transfer-YOLOv11-Precise Screening (TY-PS)

Following the initial normal-versus-abnormal screening with TEN-S, we developed TY-PS (Transfer-YOLOv11-Precise Screening), a transfer-learning-based lesion-detection module built upon YOLOv11s for fine-grained diagnosis. TY-PS is specifically designed to identify and localize both the overall disease pattern and the focal lesions in ultrasound images, covering CoA, transposition of TGA and IAA. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the training data for TY-PS were meticulously annotated under a unified clinical standard. For normal hearts, annotations highlight key anatomical structures (RA, LA, DAO, ARCH). For abnormal cases, annotations precisely delineate both the specific lesion areas (coa, tga, and iaa) and the overall pathological structure corresponding to the disease (CoA, TGA, IAA). TY-PS’s precision screening integrates both precise localization (coa, tga, and iaa) and overall localization (CoA, TGA, and IAA) results. Any detected lesion in any category qualifies as disease-related. In case of discrepancies between precise and overall localization results, the precise localization outcome takes precedence.

This high-quality, fine-grained annotation protocol provides the foundational supervision for our modified YOLOv11s algorithm to learn accurate detection and diagnosis.

To better handle the problem of large-scale variation in anatomical features in long-axis planar images of fetal aortic arch, we applied the Wise-IoU (WIoU) loss function [16]. WIoU includes a weight mechanism that can dynamically adjust the loss contribution according to the target size, so that the model preferentially ensures the detection accuracy of smaller anatomical structures during the training process.

In order to better extract the detailed information from the long-axis plane images of the fetal aortic arch and suppress the noise, we upgraded the processing of the main trunk of the aortic arch. We replaced the original C3k2 [17] module with the RepNCSPELAN4 [18] module and the SPPF [19] module with the SPPELAN [18] module.

The RepNCSPELAN4 module is a crucial feature extraction component in YOLOv9 [18], incorporating a CSP-ELAN module that integrates the properties of Cross Stage Partial (CSP) [20] connections and Efficient Layer Aggregation Network (ELAN) [21]. The RepNCSPELAN4 module enhances the reusability and representational power of features by leveraging the connections of RepNCSP and CSP to achieve feature grouping and the exchange of information regarding input features. This design enables the neural network to process large amounts of data more effectively while significantly reducing memory and computational overhead. The SPPELAN module, standing for Spatial Pyramid Pooling - Enhanced Layer Aggregation Network, is an advancement over the traditional Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) [22]. It integrates a fundamental convolutional block, denoted as CBS, along with three Maxpool2d operations. This design bolsters the network’s capacity to aggregate features across various scales, enhancing the detection of small, intricate cardiac anomalies in fetal ultrasound images. This module advancement over Spatial Pyramid Pooling, integrates a basic convolutional block and multiple Maxpool2d operations. It adeptly captures multi-scale features, enhancing complex scene perception through varied kernel pooling. This approach mitigates redundant feature extraction in CNNs, maintains a steady receptive field, accelerates box proposal generation, reduces computational load, and enhances overall network efficacy. These negative samples encompass key structures such as DAO, LA, ARCH and RA. The purpose of pre-training is to enable the model to learn and understand the characteristics of normal anatomy, thereby laying a solid foundation for subsequent anomaly detection. The proposed TY-PS model was benchmarked against several YOLO variants of comparable computational complexity (YOLOv3, YOLOv5m, YOLOv6m, YOLOv8m, YOLOv9m, YOLOv10m, and YOLOv11s) to ensure a fair comparison. A comprehensive evaluation was conducted across ablation experiment: (1) Train a precision screening model using abnormal data and evaluate its performance on the abnormal dataset (General method); (2) Mix normal and abnormal data sets for model training and evaluate its performance on the abnormal data set (Hybrid Training); 3)First, the model is trained on normal data set to learn normal anatomical structure knowledge, and then the model weight is transferred to abnormal data set for further training to learn abnormal anatomical structure features and evaluate its performance on abnormal data set (Structure-Enhanced Transfer Learning). Model performance was assessed using accuracy, recall, and the F1 score, with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) reported for each metric to ensure statistical robustness. Furthermore, an extensive ablation study was performed to dissect the individual contribution of each key component within the TY-PS framework.

Result

In this section, we first detail the datasets and experimental setup. To validate the proposed pipeline, TEN-S is benchmarked against state-of-the-art CHD screening models, and TY-PS is compared with both baseline and top-performing lesion-detection architectures. Finally, ablation studies quantify the contribution of each component within the proposed TLEUDS framework after transfer.

Performance of the TEN-S screening module surpasses other deep learning models on external test sets

In the abnormal-case screening phase of TEN-S, we compared the data-quality-aware transfer learning in the TEN-S module to the baseline EfficientNetV2-S model to see how well it worked at finding aortic arch problems. We used the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC), sensitivity, and specificity to measure how well the model worked. For each metric, we gave 95% confidence intervals. The results that are statistically better are shown in bold.

To validate the contribution of transfer learning, we conducted an ablation study comparing the proposed TEN-S module and the original EfficientNetV2-S on the screening task, which is summarized in Table 3; Fig. 5. The results showed that the model did much better on all of the evaluation metrics after transfer learning. The AUC-ROC score went up from 0.981 to 0.997, the sensitivity went up from 0.958 to 0.988, and the specificity went up from 0.958 to 0.988. We also used heat map analysis to show the areas of interest in ultrasound images. Figure 6 shows that the transfer learning-enhanced model could more accurately find important anatomical structures. This further proves that transfer learning works to improve the model’s ability to find small anatomical features.

We compared the performance of our proposed TLEUDS system with that of previously published studies on prenatal CHD screening, as summarized in Table 4. The comparison demonstrates that our method achieves competitive, and in some aspects superior, performance against the current state-of-the-art.

To validate the efficacy of each component in the TY-PS architecture, we conducted an ablation study as shown in Table 7. The study reveals that the integration of WIoU loss function, RepNCSPELAN4, and SPPELAN modules into the baseline YOLOv11s significantly improves Precise Screening performance. Specifically, the combination of these modules elevates the precision and recall. The best setup, “Baseline + WIoU + RepNCSPELAN4 + SPPELAN,” shows better performance on all tested metrics, making it the best model for analyzing fetal echocardiograms.

To validate the effectiveness of our proposed two-stage pipeline (screening followed by diagnosis), we conducted an ablation study comparing it against an end-to-end multi-class classification approach. As a baseline, we trained models (EfficientNetV2, ResNet, Swin Transformer) to directly classify images into four categories: Normal, CoA, IAA, and TGA. The results presented in Table 8 demonstrate that our two-stage TLEUDS system outperforms this end-to-end baseline, justifying our architectural design.

Discussion

In this study, we developed a quality- and knowledge-enhanced cascade dual-transfer learning system for fetal CHD screening, using fetal duct-dependent CHD as a case study. The system achieves a closed-loop process from initial disease detection (TEN-S) to detailed screening of specific malformations (TY-PS). Experimental results demonstrate that this approach outperforms conventional methods in the precise identification of CoA, TGA, and IAA. The fetal echocardiographic analysis framework we propose can be extended to other CHD studies.

To further validate the effectiveness of our approach, we compared the performance of the TLEUDS framework with other state-of-the-art studies. As shown in Table 4, our screening method (TEN-S) outperforms other studies in key metrics such as AUC-ROC, sensitivity, and specificity. Specifically, after transfer learning, the TEN-S module improved AUC-ROC from 0.981 to 0.997, sensitivity from 0.958 to 0.988, and specificity from 0.958 to 0.988. These results indicate that the transfer learning strategy effectively enhances the model’s ability to distinguish between normal and abnormal ultrasound images, particularly in improving the accuracy of abnormal case identification. We used Grad-CAM to compare the original EfficientNetV2-S and the post-transfer model (TEN-S) to see how domain-knowledge-based transfer learning worked, as shown in Fig. 6. The results indicated that the original model was more vulnerable to extraneous information in the image, whereas the post-transfer model was more adept at pinpointing critical lesion areas.

The TY-PS module also showed superior performance in key metrics such as Precision, mAP@0.5, and Recall compared to the original YOLOv11s model after transfer learning. Tables 5 and 6 shows that TY-PS got a Precision of 0.825 and a mAP@0.5 of 0.887 in the normal sample classification task. In the abnormal sample classification task, Precision went up to 0.945 and mAP@0.5 went up to 0.917. In the normal-to-abnormal transfer learning task, TY-PS showed a Precision of 0.957 and a mAP@0.5 of 0.921, which means that transfer learning made a big difference in performance. After transfer, different YOLO models also did better than the original abnormal detection task.

The TLEUDS framework covers the whole process, from screening to diagnosis, thanks to the cascading design of the TEN-S screening module and the TY-PS Precise Screening module. TEN-S, which uses the transferred EfficientNetV2-S, can tell the difference between normal and abnormal fetal hearts even when there isn’t much data. TY-PS, on the other hand, uses the transferred YOLOv11s to find and classify lesions accurately for small-sample lesions. The TLEUDS framework works very well with only a small amount of annotated data, but it still needs a lot of annotations, which is a big problem. Future research will investigate techniques to comprehensively characterize aortic arch abnormalities with minimal oversight and further refine the synergistic mechanism between the two modules to improve model robustness in actual clinical settings.

Conclusion and perspective

In this study, we introduced the Transfer Learning Enhanced Ultrasound Precise Screening System (TLEUDS), a novel framework consisting of the TEN-S screening module and the TY-PS Precise Screening module. We were able to improve the detection accuracy of abnormalities in the aortic arch section in fetal prenatal ultrasound images by employing domain-knowledge-driven transfer learning. According to our findings, the TLEUDS framework demonstrated excellent performance on both internal and external independent validation datasets (WHCC, XQH), showing robust generalization capability, particularly in scenarios with limited samples. Since there is frequently a lack of high-quality annotated data for ultrasound image analysis, this finding is especially important. The early diagnosis and treatment of related diseases may be enhanced by our model’s exceptional sensitivity to minute anatomical changes.

Future perspectives

Although TLEUDS demonstrated outstanding performance in this study, it still has some limitations, several limitations remain. First, although external datasets were used for validation, the training data primarily originated from a single-center internal dataset (comprising both routine-quality and high-quality subsets). Its generalizability across broader populations and varied equipment requires further confirmation. Secondly, from a methodological perspective, the current analysis is based solely on two-dimensional ultrasound images and does not incorporate key functional information such as Color Doppler Flow Mapping (CFM). This may limit performance in diagnosing flow-dependent anomalies like interrupted aortic arch. Third, the current framework’s performance is partly dependent on a substantial amount of annotated data for pre-training and fine-tuning.

Addressing these limitations, future work will focus on: conducting prospective, multi-center clinical studies to comprehensively evaluate the robustness and clinical utility of TLEUDS in real-world settings; specifically, we will investigate how the system can serve as an assistive tool to improve diagnostic consistency for sonographers with varying expertise, quantifying its efficacy within actual clinical workflows. Concurrently, we will develop algorithms that integrate multimodal data such as CFM to enhance the comprehensive identification of complex lesions, and further optimize the framework’s efficiency while exploring its application under weakly supervised learning paradigms.

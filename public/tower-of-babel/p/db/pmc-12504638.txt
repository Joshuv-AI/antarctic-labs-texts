Dynamical study of optical soliton solutions of time-fractional perturbed model in ultrafast optical fibers
Authors: Loubna Ouahid, Nazar Mohammad Nazar, M. A. Abdou, Maryam Alshahrani, A. Mohamed Abdel-Baset, M. H. Emara, Sachin Kumar, Ihsanullah Hamid
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The nonlinear Schrödinger equation is a useful physical model for exploring variations in optical solitary wave behavior. The study of novel optical solitary waves is extremely important today due to its potential uses in ultrafast signal routing and short-duration light pulses for communication. In this work, we present an analytical examination of the time-fractional perturbed dynamical model, which demonstrates ultrafast wave transmission across optical fibers. By taking advantage of the newly extended direct algebraic scheme, we obtain a wide range of optical soliton solutions for the considered model. To acquire a deeper understanding of the mechanics, the findings encountered here are presented in 2D-, 3D-, and contour representations. The specific characteristics of the computing efficiency for our technique illustrate its relevance to diverse nonlinear equations encountered in various fields of physical engineering and mathematical physics. Utilizing symbolic computing with MATLAB software, we also demonstrated the dynamical exploration of solitons generated with different parameter values using numerical simulation. Optical solitons can be found in various fields such as optical fibers, plasma physics, optical communication, soliton theory, and others.

Introduction

Fractional calculus (FC) reaches back to the beginnings of differential calculus theory. Whereas advancements in chaos and solitons have revealed aspects in FC theories, their incorporation has been stalled for the past two decades. Though much research has been conducted in the field of solitons and dynamical systems, the proposed frameworks and algorithms are still in the very beginning stages of advanced development. Fractional calculus (FC) extends the concepts of differentiation and integration to arbitrary orders1. Over the past thirty years, it has gained significant popularity and importance due to its useful applications across various scientific and engineering fields. These mathematical models can illustrate a wide range of phenomena in areas such as2–6. Unlike traditional calculus, which evaluates the current state of a problem, fractional derivatives offer a more accurate representation of the nonlocal characteristics of these models in mathematical applications.

Various important scientific processes are described using different types of fractional derivatives in the literature, such as mentioned in literature see references7–11. In addition, there are several definitions of commonly used fractional operators, for example Caputo definition12, Grünwald-Letnikov definition13, Atangana-Baleanu-Caputo definition14, conformal fractional derivatives15, Riemann–Liouville definition16, and many other local and nonlocal derivatives17,18.

These fractional-order derivatives are employed across diverse fields in contemporary science and engineering, offering a more appropriate representation. And the other hand the time-fractional derivatives are very useful for modeling systems where processes depend on both the current state and the system’s history. Such models may provide a more accurate description of the fiber than conventional integer-order models, especially when dealing with complex material properties or external perturbations that may result in fractional behavior.

In the literature, this model has been addressed using different schemes42–44. The motivation for studying optical soliton solutions in time-fractional perturbed models for ultrafast optical fibers stems from the need for more accurate, realistic, and robust models of pulse propagation. These models allow for better handling of memory effects, perturbations, and non-local interactions, improving the design and stability of high-speed fiber-optic communication systems. The goal is to enhance the performance and resilience of soliton-based optical pulses in complex and practical fiber-optic networks, ultimately advancing the field of ultrafast communications and signal processing. The novelty of our approach lies in applying this method to a model that has not been previously explored with such a technique.

Conclusion

In summary, we investigated the time-fractional perturbed dynamical model via a robust approach, namely, a newly extended direct algebraic scheme. The optical soliton solutions obtained here are unique, effective, and innovative, featuring singular, v. shaped, and solitary pulse periodic solutions as well. The 2D and 3D visualizations, including contour plots, provide a comprehensive physical understanding of the governing model. Various figures are presented to illustrate the dynamic structures of various optical soliton forms, as shown in Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8. The solutions exhibit diverse behaviors with applications in fields such as optical fibers and optical transmission. Overall, these techniques are applicable to a wide array of complex physical models in contemporary nonlinear sciences and physical engineering. We employed the applied method to obtain the optical solitons. This considered model offers a wide range of optical soliton solutions, including kinks, anti-kinks, and single-shaped and other solitons. The investigators can study this model using various analytical methods, the Lie grouptic method, the Hirota method, and other procedures. This study the time-fractional perturbed dynamical model is highly relevant and offers important insights into the behavior of ultrafast wave transmission in optical fibers. The future research scope outlined above suggests multiple avenues for deepening the understanding of optical phenomena and improving optical communication technologies, with fractional calculus playing a central role in modeling complex and real-world behaviors.

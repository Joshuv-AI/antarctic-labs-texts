Reply: Long-term outcomes of primary surgical repair for communicating Debakey IIIB chronic dissecting aortic aneurysm
Authors: Shuhei Miura, Nobuyoshi Kawaharada
Published: 2024

Reply to the Editor:

We appreciate the comments of Venjhraj and colleagues.1 Chronic type B aortic dissection is a challenging surgical problem, and the optimal primary treatment, especially for communicating DeBakey IIIb chronic dissecting aortic aneurysm (CD3bDA), remains controversial. Although we acknowledge the limitation of directly comparing different surgical techniques, such as thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm repair (TAAAR), descending thoracic aneurysm repair (DTAR), total arch replacement with elephant trunk implantation, or thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR), because of differences in patient background and dissection anatomy, the main purpose of this study was to understand the method by which primary treatment choice influences the subsequent treatment of distal residual dissecting aneurysms based on 20-year long-term outcomes.

Aortic remodeling by preemptive TEVAR for early phase type B dissection led to a reduction in complications in the chronic phase.2 Thus, it led to an increased interest in using TEVAR for chronic aortic dissection. However, midterm results are less encouraging, with some studies reporting failure of the procedure in 37% of patients within 36 months,3 and high reintervention rates of 13% to 40% within the first 24 months following primary TEVAR.4, 5, 6 The challenge with respect to the endovascular exclusion of CD3bDA is the presence of a small true lumen with a thick and stiff intimal flap, which may be less amenable to reverse remodeling using TEVAR than an acutely dissected aorta with a more flexible intimal flap. The concern is the exclusion of reentry not at the level of the stent graft, but rather distal to the stent graft, where retrograde pressurization of the false lumen may persist. Our results imply that simple TEVAR may not provide long-term durability in patients with chronic extensive thoracoabdominal aortic dissection, with a reintervention rate of 45.5% and an aortic event-free rate of 51.4% at 7 years. Additionally, the primary limitation of TEVAR for CD3bDA involving the aortic arch is the difficulty in achieving a disease-free proximal seal zone without covering the aortic branch vessels, which may be associated with a higher incidence of retrograde type A aortic dissection. In Japan, commercial devices such as bifurcated stent grafts are not available and invasive or advanced techniques such as carotid artery bypass, abdominal branch artery bypass, and endovascular fenestration are required. Considering the potential increase in patient burden and medical costs due to repeated reinterventions over time, we emphasize that TEVAR is not necessarily recommended as a first-line treatment for all CD3bDA patients, including younger patients.

In this study, open repair was accomplished with excellent long-term survival rates (61.8% for TAAAR and 71.6% for DTAR after 10 years) and aortic event-free rates (93.8% for TAAAR and 84.3% for DTAR after 7 years). Although our findings cannot be generalized to all patients undergoing with CD3bDA in a given institution, they can help aid in determining the optimal primary surgical repair to prevent the incidence of aortic events based on long-term outcomes.

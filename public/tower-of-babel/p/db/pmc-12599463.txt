Decision-Making Patterns for Clinically Node-Positive, Hormone Receptor-Positive, HER2-Negative Early Breast Cancer: Insights From a Survey of Breast Cancer Experts
Authors: Yoonwon Kook, Minji Lee, Seung Ho Baek, Henry Gomez, Soong June Bae, Joon Jeong, Sung Gwe Ahn
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeIn hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2−) breast cancer, the omission of chemotherapy has become a viable option for select patients. However, when lymph node (LN) metastasis is suspected at diagnosis, the optimal initial treatment strategy (upfront surgery, neoadjuvant chemotherapy, or genomic testing) remains controversial. This study aimed to assess the preferences of multinational breast cancer specialists and to identify the factors influencing clinical decisions in this setting.MethodsA cross-sectional survey was conducted among breast cancer specialists at two time points: November 2023 (via the Korean Breast Cancer Society) and April 2024 (following the Asian Breast Cancer Network meeting at Global Breast Cancer Conference 2024). The questionnaire presented 18 case scenarios that reflected varying patient ages, tumor sizes, nodal involvement, and pathological features. Respondents selected their preferred initial treatment strategy. Consensus was defined as ≥ 60% agreement. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify the demographic factors associated with responses in the non-consensus scenarios.ResultsA total of 189 responses were analyzed. Consensus was consistently observed in postmenopausal patients and in cases involving small low-grade tumors with limited nodal suspicion. However, the responses varied between peri- and pre-menopausal patients, particularly in the intermediate-risk scenarios. Multivariate analysis revealed that clinician specialty, nationality, affiliation, and experience significantly influenced treatment choice. Notably, clinicians from other Asian countries and medical oncologists showed a greater preference for upfront surgery than for neoadjuvant chemotherapy in younger patients with aggressive tumor features.ConclusionThe findings reveal both consensus and variability in clinical decision-making for HR+HER2− breast cancer with suspected LN involvement among multinational breast cancer specialists. This underscores the need for enhanced international collaboration and clear guidance in intermediate-risk settings. The incorporation of diagnostic tools such as multigene assays may further support individualized treatment decisions.

INTRODUCTION

Hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2–) breast cancer is the most common breast cancer [1]. Recent advances in adjuvant therapies have enabled the omission of chemotherapy in certain patients, especially those with early-stage, low-risk tumors [23]. Notably, the RxPONDER trial demonstrated that postmenopausal women with HR+HER2– breast cancer involving 1–3 lymph nodes (LNs) and a 21-gene recurrence score of ≤ 25 could safely forgo chemotherapy without compromising invasive disease-free survival [2]. Additionally, the WSG-ADAPT trial is currently investigating tailored treatment approaches based on early therapeutic responses to refine strategies for de-escalating chemotherapy in specific patient subgroups [4].

In cases in which a clinically node-positive disease is suspected at the time of diagnosis, the decision-making process becomes more complex [56]. In HR+HER2– breast cancer, LN involvement frequently necessitates careful evaluation of surgical and oncological factors, as this directly influences the choice of the initial treatment strategy [5].

In clinical practice, the treatment of patients with HR+HER2– breast cancer with suspected LN metastasis is a critical choice between surgery and neoadjuvant chemotherapy [56]. This decision is influenced by multiple factors, including the clinicopathological characteristics of the tumor, certainty of nodal metastasis based on clinical and radiological findings, and patient age. While the surgery-first approach allows rapid pathological confirmation and potentially minimizes delays in adjuvant treatment, neoadjuvant chemotherapy offers the advantages of tumor downstaging, facilitating breast-conserving surgery, and providing early systemic control. However, there is no clear consensus on the optimal strategy, particularly in settings where multigene assays that can guide the need for chemotherapy are predominantly performed postoperatively.

The importance of clarifying these decision-making patterns comprehensively was discussed at the Asian Breast Cancer Network (ABCN) meeting held at the Global Breast Cancer Conference (GBCC) 2024. Inspired by the insights shared at the meeting, we conducted a survey to amass expert opinions on initial treatment approaches for patients with HR+HER2– breast cancer with suspected nodal involvement. This study aimed to capture the preferences of breast cancer specialists regarding initial treatment strategies. By comparing responses from multinational experts, we aimed to elucidate potential differences in clinical practice and identify areas where a consensus needs to be established.

Herein, we present the findings of our survey, focusing on how tumor characteristics, certainty of nodal metastasis, and patient demographics can shape the treatment preferences of physicians from diverse backgrounds. These results provide valuable insights into the current state of clinical decision-making for HR+HER2– breast cancer, with implications for optimizing treatment strategies and developing standardized guidelines for managing patients with suspected LN metastasis.

METHODS

Study design

This cross-sectional survey was conducted to evaluate the treatment preferences of breast cancer experts for HR+HER2– breast cancer in clinically node-positive cases. Data were collected at two time points: November 2023 and April 2024. Ethical approval for the study was obtained from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Gangnam Severance Hospital (IRB-3-2024-0403), and participants were informed of the study’s objectives, confidentiality measures, and its voluntary nature. The survey was conducted anonymously as no identifying information, including email addresses, was collected. Consent was obtained upon completion of the survey.

Questionnaire development

This survey was designed to evaluate the treatment preferences and demographic profiles of breast cancer experts. Eighteen case scenarios were carefully constructed to represent the range of clinical situations, commonly encountered in patients with HR+HER2– breast cancer with suspected LN involvement. These scenarios included low-risk (e.g., small tumors with limited nodal suspicion and low proliferation indices), high-risk (e.g., large tumors with multiple suspicious nodes and high Ki-67 levels), and intermediate-risk scenarios, which were intentionally designed to reflect diagnostic and therapeutic uncertainty. Intermediate-risk scenarios were defined as cases involving moderate tumor size (e.g., 2.5 cm), clinically suspicious but unconfirmed nodal involvement (e.g., one enlarged LN without biopsy), and/or moderately aggressive tumor biology, such as grade (G) II or Ki-67 levels between 20% and 30%. This range allows for the analysis of clinical decision making, both where a consensus tends to emerge and where variability is most likely to arise. Key aspects included patient age (representing postmenopausal, peri-menopausal, or pre-menopausal), tumor size (with or without non-mass lesions [NMLs]), implications of axillary metastasis without fine-needle biopsy, and pathological features such as tumor grade and Ki-67 expression. For each scenario, respondents were asked to select from the following multiple-choice options: 1) upfront surgery, 2) neoadjuvant chemotherapy, 3) multigene assays, or 4) other (with an option to specify).

The survey also included questions regarding demographics to collect information on the participants’ nationality, specialty, location of practice, affiliation, practice arrangement, years of experience, and patient volume at their respective centers. Center volume was defined as described by Greenup et al. [7]. The questionnaire was reviewed by an expert before distribution to ensure its clinical relevance and clarity. Appendix 1 presents a full copy of the questionnaire.

Participant recruitment

The participants were breast cancer experts recruited through professional networks and conferences. In November 2023, the Korean version of the survey was distributed via email to the members of the Korean Breast Cancer Society (KBCS) around the time of the fall conference. Following discussions at the ABCN meeting during the GBCC 2024, the survey was redistributed in April 2024. In the second round, the Korean version was redistributed to KBCS members, with an attached note indicating that it was the same questionnaire sent in November 2023 to prevent duplicate responses. The English version was sent via email to international participants of the GBCC 2024.

The survey was administered via an email link, and anonymity was ensured by not collecting any identifying information such as email addresses. The participants were required to complete the questionnaire in full, with instructions explicitly stating that all case scenarios were based on HR+HER2– breast cancer. Responses that lacked a full understanding of the context or were incomplete were excluded from the analysis.

Statistical analysis

Consensus was defined as agreement between more than 60% of the respondents. This threshold was selected to reflect a more inclusive and pragmatic approach, considering the diversity of respondents and the exploratory nature of expert opinion surveys. While some consensus studies define agreement as 70% or above, consensus thresholds are not uniformly standardized and may vary depending on the context and study objectives. The non-consensus scenarios were those in which no single treatment option was selected by 60% of the respondents. These scenarios were subjected to statistical analysis to identify the baseline characteristics associated with clinicians’ treatment preferences.

Univariate analysis was performed using Fisher’s exact test to evaluate the association between baseline characteristics (e.g., specialty, affiliation, and sex) and treatment preferences. Variables with a p-value < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were included in a multivariate analysis using multinomial logistic regression to adjust for potential confounders and determine independent predictors of treatment decisions. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated for all variables. Variables with a p-value < 0.05 in the univariate analysis were further analyzed in the multivariate analysis.

For multivariate analysis, each non-consensus scenario was further coded into binary outcomes for logistic regression analysis. The responses were categorized into the following comparisons for each scenario: 1) upfront surgery vs. others, 2) neoadjuvant chemotherapy vs. others, 3) multigene assay vs. others. Logistic binary regression was performed to identify the independent predictors of treatment preferences. ORs and 95% CIs were calculated.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p-value < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant. Data analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 27.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, USA).

RESULTS

Study participants

A total of 193 participants completed the survey at both the time points. After excluding four responses owing to incomplete answers to the scenario questions or a misunderstanding of the study premise—that all cases were based on HR+HER2– breast cancer—189 responses were included in the final analysis.

The majority of respondents were from South Korea (57.1%), followed by participants from Latin American countries (28.0%) and other regions. Surgical oncologists constituted the largest specialty group (72.0%), with more than half of the participants (56.1%) reporting more than 15 years of clinical practice. Regarding chemotherapy administration, 60.8% indicated that they personally administered the chemotherapy, whereas 39.2% referred patients to other clinicians. Stacked bar chart showing treatment preferences among 189 breast cancer experts for 18 distinct clinical scenarios involving HR+HER2− breast cancer with clinically node-positive presentations. Each scenario was labeled with the patient’s age (e.g., 34, 49, and 65 years) and represented a specific combination of tumor size, LN involvement, tumor grade, and Ki-67 index.

HR = hormone receptor; HER2 = human epidermal growth factor receptor 2; LN = lymph node; G = grade.

Survey responses across 18 clinical scenarios revealed significant variations in treatment preferences based on patient age, LN status, and tumor characteristics. Consensus was achieved across all scenarios for patients aged 65 years, indicating a greater alignment in treatment preferences for older patients.

For scenarios involving smaller tumors (e.g., 0.8 or 1.5 cm) with limited LN involvement (e.g., one LN) and low Ki-67 values (e.g., 2%), upfront surgery was the predominant choice. In scenarios with the smallest tumors (0.8 cm), > 90% of the respondents selected upfront surgery, regardless of patient age. Likewise, in cases involving 1.5-cm tumors with a single suspicious LN, upfront surgery was the most preferred option, although consensus (≥ 60%) was not consistently achieved across all scenarios.

Conversely, in cases characterized by larger tumors (e.g., 4 cm) with multiple LN involvement and higher Ki-67 values (e.g., 30%), neoadjuvant chemotherapy was overwhelmingly preferred, achieving a consensus across all age groups. Scenarios with intermediate tumor sizes (e.g., 2.5 cm + NML) and one LN involvement were associated with more diverse responses, with multigene assays frequently selected to guide treatment decisions. These intermediate scenarios generally did not meet the 60% threshold for consensus, reflecting a greater variability in clinical preferences.

The “others” option was infrequently selected and was most commonly specified by respondents for performing fine-needle aspiration for further diagnostic clarification.

In scenarios involving a 49-year-old patient with a 1.5 cm G II tumor and two suspicious LNs, sex significantly influenced the preference for upfront surgery, with female clinicians being more likely to choose this option (OR, 2.116; 95% CI, 1.077–4.158; p = 0.030). Similarly, nationality was a significant factor in decisions regarding multigene assays under the same scenario (OR, 0.131; 95% CI, 0.020–0.871; p = 0.035).

Specialty significantly influenced treatment preferences in a 34-year-old patient with similar tumor characteristics. Medical oncologists were less likely than surgical oncologists to recommend neoadjuvant chemotherapy (OR, 0.334; 95% CI, 0.112–0.993; p = 0.048) and more likely to recommend upfront surgery (OR, 4.924; 95% CI, 1.559–15.557; p = 0.007).

In scenarios with intermediate tumor sizes (e.g., 2.5 cm + NML and one LN), significant factors influencing treatment preferences included affiliation, nationality, and experience. Considering peri-menopausal patients, clinicians in private practice were less likely to recommend a multigene assay than were those in university hospitals (OR, 0.252; 95% CI, 0.064–0.989; p = 0.048). In pre-menopausal patients, nationality influenced decisions regarding both upfront surgery and use of a multigene assay. Clinicians from European countries were less likely to select upfront surgery (OR, 0.142; 95% CI, 0.021–0.945; p = 0.043), whereas those from Latin America were less likely to recommend multigene assays (OR, 0.108; 95% CI, 0.014–0.820; p = 0.031). Additionally, clinicians with considerable experience were more likely to recommend neoadjuvant chemotherapy for this patient group (OR, 9.583; 95% CI, 1.498–61.311; p = 0.017).

In scenarios involving smaller tumors with more aggressive characteristics (e.g., 1.5 cm, one LN, G III, 40%), nationality and specialty emerged as recurrently significant factors influencing clinical decision-making. For both peri-menopausal and pre-menopausal patients, clinicians from other Asian countries were more likely than Korean clinicians to select upfront surgery (peri-menopausal: OR, 4.013; 95% CI, 1.025–15.718; p = 0.046; pre-menopausal: OR, 8.548; 95% CI, 1.611–45.352; p = 0.012). Likewise, medical oncologists were more likely than surgical oncologists to recommend upfront surgery over neoadjuvant chemotherapy in both age groups (peri-menopausal: OR, 4.876; 95% CI, 1.518–15.667; p = 0.008; pre-menopausal: OR, 5.959; 95% CI, 1.667–21.296; p = 0.006).

DISCUSSION

In the current study, our findings provide insights into the clinical decision-making process for patients with HR+ HER2– breast cancer with clinically node-positive disease. After surveying international and Korean breast cancer experts, we identified substantial variability in treatment preferences across different clinical scenarios. Although a consensus was consistently achieved for older patients, clinicians varied substantially in their opinions between peri-menopausal and pre-menopausal patients, especially in scenarios featuring intermediate tumor sizes or aggressive biological characteristics. Multivariate analysis indicated that clinician nationality, specialty, institutional affiliation, and clinical experience markedly influenced treatment preference.

Consensus was observed across all scenarios involving patients aged 65 years regardless of tumor size, LN involvement, or tumor grade. This suggests that clinical decision-making in postmenopausal patients may be more straightforward or better supported by existing guidelines. Similarly, scenarios involving small tumors with limited nodal suspicion and low proliferation indices revealed high levels of agreement across age groups, with upfront surgery being frequently selected. These patterns suggest that treatment preferences tend to converge in well-defined, lower-risk cases, reflecting a shared understanding of oncological safety and a likely benefit-risk balance.

The observed variability aligns with prior multinational surveys that demonstrated regional and specialty-specific differences in clinical practice influenced by varying healthcare structures, cultural factors, and the availability of diagnostic tools such as multigene assays [89]. Our findings highlight the substantial variability in clinical decision-making, reflecting the importance of personalized care approaches based on individual tumor characteristics and patient factors. Although clinical guidelines such as those from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN), European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO), and St. Gallen aim to provide structured, evidence-based frameworks, they also advocate clinician judgment and flexibility, particularly in intermediate-risk or ambiguous clinical scenarios [5610]. It is important to clarify that all the survey scenarios were based on clinical rather than on pathological stages. While the RxPONDER trial demonstrated the benefit of adjuvant chemotherapy in premenopausal patients with pathologically confirmed nodal involvement, decision-making at the time of diagnosis often involves uncertainty regarding the nodal status [2]. In such cases, multigene assays may be considered to guide treatment in intermediate-risk settings, despite limited prospective evidence, for their use in the neoadjuvant context. For instance, the NCCN and ESMO guidelines recommend considering multigene assays and individualized systemic therapy decisions, whereas the St. Gallen consensus similarly emphasizes clinician judgment, especially in cases of intermediate clinical risk [5610]. Therefore, the variability observed in our survey underscores differences in clinicians’ interpretation of existing evidence and highlights the complexity of balancing guideline adherence with individualized patient care.

Interestingly, the recent American Society of Clinical Oncology guideline update on sentinel LN biopsy, although primarily addressing clinically node-negative breast cancer, underscores a broader clinical trend toward individualized and less-invasive treatment strategies [11]. This evolving approach further highlights the need for clearer recommendations in node-positive scenarios, in which our survey revealed considerable decision-making variability.

A notable finding of our study was the preference of certain clinician groups for upfront surgery over neoadjuvant chemotherapy, particularly in younger patients with aggressive tumor characteristics. In contrast, emerging trends in several international guidelines increasingly emphasize the use of neoadjuvant chemotherapy for high-risk, node-positive breast cancer. These differences may reflect specialty-specific perspectives on treatment sequencing. Medical oncologists may favor upfront surgery in intermediate-risk cases to obtain definitive pathological data, such as tumor grade and nodal involvement, which are critical for determining systemic treatment plans and the utility of multigene assays. By contrast, surgical oncologists may lean toward neoadjuvant chemotherapy when tumor downstaging or breast conservation is a priority, particularly in cases where imaging suggests a higher burden of disease. Such disparities across clinical groups and regions underscore the need for continued global collaboration to establish clear and broadly accepted practices.

Our findings emphasize the importance of international consensus-building exercises and the development of clear evidence-based frameworks to support clinical decisions, especially in ambiguous or intermediate-risk clinical scenarios. Enhancing clinician access to diagnostic tools such as multigene assays, particularly in resource-limited settings, could further promote guideline adherence, inform clinical judgment, and mitigate variability in treatment decisions.

A key strength of this study is the diverse representation of clinicians from various multinational regions, which provides valuable insights into global practice patterns. Nevertheless, this study has some limitations need to be addressed. First, hypothetical scenarios may fail to comprehensively capture real-world clinical complexities. Second, given that the survey captured self-reported treatment preferences in hypothetical scenarios, the responses may not fully reflect real-world clinical behaviors or decision-making processes. Additionally, the survey methodology inherently limits the ability to verify the quality or accuracy of responses, which is another limitation of this survey study design. Finally, the predominance of Korean and Latin American respondents introduces sample bias, potentially limiting generalizability to other geographical regions. Furthermore, regional healthcare structures and specialty distributions may have influenced the responses. For instance, Korean clinicians may be influenced by national reimbursement policies, and the overrepresentation of surgical oncologists may skew treatment preferences. These factors should be considered when interpreting the results.

Investigating the underlying reasons for variability in decision-making and identifying barriers to guideline implementation, such as access to diagnostic tools or differences in healthcare infrastructure, will be essential for improving consistency in care. Furthermore, incorporating patient perspectives and preferences into future research could help bridge the gap between clinical recommendations and individualized care in the management of clinically node-positive HR+HER2– breast cancer.

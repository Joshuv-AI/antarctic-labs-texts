A simple method and case study of task planning for collaborative design based on FDSM
Authors: Beihai Wang, Jieqing Lei, Zean Wang, Guoliang Wei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn multiperson collaborative mechanical product design, unreasonable task allocation can result in excessive coupling between tasks, complex design communication, and rework, particularly in small enterprises and teams that rely primarily on experience. Hence, this study proposes a simple and efficient task-planning method based on a fuzzy design structure matrix (FDSM), which involves single-level task decomposition, three-level number assignment of the coupling relationship, decoupling of the structural tree algorithm, and validation of the optimization results based on the G-value. Design tasks are promptly allocated into a single level based on the functional structure module of the product. The task-correlation degree is quantified based on three levels of numerical values through personal experience to construct an FDSM, the latter of which is decoupled by the correlation degree of the structural tree algorithm to obtain the optimized sequence and allocation of the collaborative design task. Finally, the effectiveness of the planning is verified by calculating the tightness of the corresponding matrix before and after the planning. A task-planning case of a riveting-machine design for capacitor cover plates is established to validate this method.Keywords: Collaborative design, Task planning, Fuzzy design structure matrix (FDSM), Riveting-machine design for capacitor cover plate

Introduction

Multiperson collaborative design is a common method used for developing modern mechanical equipment and can effectively shorten the product development cycle and help enterprises accelerate the design process to achieve a competitive advantage. Owing to the vigorous development of digital and intelligent technology, collaborative design has expanded rapidly in the manufacturing industry with the support of “Internet+.” It addresses the diverse, flexible, and customized demands of customers [[1], [2], [3]] and has gradually demonstrated its advantages in mechanical design [4]. However, this method requires team members to not only form a working concept of mutual cooperation, mutual trust, and knowledge sharing [5,6] but also master scientific task-planning methods. However, most small businesses and teams rely more on experience than on scientific methods for task planning in a multiperson collaborative design. Tasks are typically assigned unreasonably and excessive coupling occurs between tasks among members, thus resulting in complex design communication and rework modifications. This study investigates an effective and simple method for rapidly completing collaborative task planning for a team comprising three to five people.

Currently, collaborative design is widely investigated both domestically and internationally, with emphasis on task decomposition [[7], [8], [9]], process modeling [[10], [11], [12]], and the identification and planning of coupled tasks [[13], [14], [15]]. Collaborative design methods are classified into the following two main types.

(1)Collaborative design method based on a design structure matrix (DSM). In this method, (0, 1) indicates that the relationship between design tasks is either a strong or no-information interaction. For example, Shajahan et al. [16] performed particle swarm optimization to organize the DSM and optimize the order of related activities in product development. Arnarsson et al. [17] used the DSM to model complex product-development processes and optimize workflows. Sinha et al. [18] proposed a method of system-architecture decomposition and modularization based on the DSM and combined it with a clustering algorithm to realize the modular decomposition and analysis of an electric train static inverter (SIV). Zhang et al. [19] constructed a matrix diagram of the DSM to model a structural relationship and then combined it with the common measurement index and the principle of minimum description length to optimize a shuttle-system module.

In summary, the existing collaborative design methods rely primarily on constructing a DSM or FDSM and decoupling [25] using different algorithms [26,27], including the ant colony algorithm [28,29], weighted directed graph [30,31], segmentation algorithm [32,33], partitioning algorithm [34], and structural tree [35]. Various methods can be combined, and any matrix and decoupling algorithm can be selected to achieve the optimization goal; however, the optimization degree may differ. Based on the actual situation, an optimal solution can be obtained by selecting an appropriate method for decoupling.

This study proposes a simple and feasible task-planning method based on the FDSM matrix for the collaborative work of three to five people. It scientifically and rapidly assigns the work tasks, matches operators, reduces task coupling, and efficiently completes the overall design task. To verify the feasibility of the method, it is applied to plan the collaborative design task for a riveting machine-based capacitor cover plate.

Simple task-planning method for collaborative design in small team

Single-level task decomposition

Task decomposition, as the first and most important step in task planning, entails decomposing design tasks into multiple subtasks reasonably and determining their interrelationships to facilitate collaborative work among various designers [36].

The design task for complex products can be decomposed into multiple levels based on the hierarchical relationship of the product components, such as the total task, subtask, secondary subtask, and meta-task (Fig. 1). Complex coupling relationships exist between various design tasks, and in the actual design process, one must complete a reasonable decomposition and combination of the design tasks [37].

When the collaborative design involves a group of several people, the overall task must be decomposed into multiple parallel tasks at the same level using functional components. The hierarchical structure is shown in Fig. 2.

Three-level number assignment of coupling relationship

The DSM expresses the information dependence between any interactive elements, such as physical components, design parameters, and systems, in the form of a matrix [[38], [39], [40]], which can provide a prompt discovery of the required input and output information when a certain element is prioritized. The DSM incidence matrix exhibits the following characteristics [[41], [42], [43]]: (1) The number of dimensions of the incidence matrix represents the number of design tasks, and the elements on the diagonal represent the design task; (2) A and B represent the design tasks of the different components, and the information interaction between them is represented by 0 or 1, where 1 and 0 indicate the presence and absence of information interaction, respectively; (3) information interaction is assumed to occur between the design task and itself; therefore, the elements on the diagonal are all 1; (4) each number in the row indicates the requirements of the information input of other elements, and each in the column indicates the requirements of the information output of other elements; (5) element AB below the diagonal represents the output information required by the design task for subsequent related design tasks, and element BA above the diagonal represents the required input information. Therefore, the lower-triangular DSM is an ideal design plan, which implies that the design task is a serial development method without feedback.

In the actual mechanical equipment design process, there are many uncertain factors between two design tasks; therefore, the Boolean values of 0 and 1 cannot provide an accurate expression of the relationship between the design tasks. In this study, an FDSM is constructed, and the values in the interval of [0,1] are used to describe the fuzzy correlation between design tasks for a more realistic representation.

Fuzzy expression of coupling degree of collaborative design task

The component correlation is typically calculated by first determining the features of the component, followed by searching for relevant features of the associated components to determine the correlation degree [44]. However, in the early stages of product design, one cannot determine the specific relationship between two design tasks using actual data. Therefore, the concept of fuzzy coupling degree is introduced to determine the coupling relationship between design tasks, primarily based on design experience, and the correlation value in the interval of [0,1] is determined [45,46].

For a simple processing, we select the discrete number Tij∈{0.3,0.5,0.9 } (where 0.3 represents weak coupling, 0.5 represents medium coupling, and 0.9 represents strong coupling) to describe the coupling degree of information input and output between each design task.

Considering that the coupling degree between design tasks is determined based on simple three-level assignments through experience, one must modify this coupling degree accordingly to prevent excessive errors caused by simple human assignments. This study provides the simple formulas for correlation-degree correction, which is an improved version of the coupling degree formula proposed by Chen et al. [47].

Decoupling of structural tree algorithm

Decoupling algorithms for the correlation degree offer different advantages for different matrices. For structural matrices that only contain values of 0 and 1, the Warshall algorithm can be used to construct reachable matrices through matrix changes, and a reduced matrix can be constructed by selecting strongly connected subsets. Finally, the row and column information of the matrix is summarized to decouple the matrix [48,49]. For the FDSM using column correlation, Chen et al. [50] introduced a decoupling algorithm for a correlation structural tree, which is simple and clear, and can easily identify the coupling relationship between various design tasks. In this study, we selected a correlation structural tree as the decoupling method.

Concluding remarks

This study focused on the research planning for the collaborative design task of a capacitor-cover riveting machine. By analyzing the functions of each component of the equipment, the entire design task was decomposed into multiple subtasks, thus providing a design basis for constructing the FDSM. By analyzing the coupling relationship between each subdesign task and quantifying it, the obtained matrix was decoupled, and the optimized design-task arrangement order and combination relationship were obtained, which served as reference for the allocation of collaborative design tasks among three to five people. Finally, the results were validated using the G-value.

The main contributions of this study are as follows. (1) Theory: A simple and efficient task-planning method based on an FDSM was proposed, which provides a reference for the assignment of collaborative design tasks for three to five people as well as facilitates managers in assigning team tasks and accomplishing tasks efficiently. (2) Practice: By examining and planning the collaborative design task of the riveting machine for a capacitor cover plate, the riveting machine was decomposed into 15 functional modules, and the design of each functional module was an assignable design task. Based on design experience, the relevance of the design task assumes a value in the interval of [0,1], based on which the FDSM of the initial capacitor-cover riveting machine design task was constructed. Using the decoupling method of the correlation structural tree for a collaborative design task, the structural tree of the collaborative design task for the capacitor-cover riveting machine was obtained, and the top layer was considered as an example to perform correlation analysis; additionally, the planned coupling order was rearranged to obtain a new FDSM, thus realizing the goal of DSM optimization.

However, this study has some limitations. The feasibility of the method was verified based on only the G-value, and the best optimization method for collaborative design-task allocation among three to five people was not discussed in combination with other methods. In the future, the author plans to combine a few methods and decouple them using different algorithms to compare and select the best optimization method for the collaborative design-task allocation of small teams comprising three to five people.

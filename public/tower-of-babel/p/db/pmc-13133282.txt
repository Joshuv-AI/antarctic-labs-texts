Next-generation platelet concentrate: Alb-PRF outperformed PRP and H-PRF in promoting endothelial function and wound healing
Authors: Fang Shen, Jihua Chai, Mingrui Wei, Xiaohan Qian, Min Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Angiogenesis underpins regenerative success and refined autologous platelet concentrates (APCs) are therefore under continual optimization. This study compared optimized thermally-induced albumin hydrogel cross-linked with horizontally centrifuged platelet-rich fibrin (Alb-PRF) against traditional platelet-rich plasma (PRP) and standardized horizontal PRF (H-PRF). Alb-PRF was generated using specific centrifugation protocols. Each APC was co-cultured with human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) respectively. Live/dead assay confirmed universal biocompatibility and safety, showing no significant differences in apoptosis among all groups. Additoinally, viability assay revealed possibly superior proliferation by Alb-PRF compared to PRP and H-PRF. Immunofluorescent cytoskeleton and morphology staining showed enhanced proliferative effects with Alb-PRF. Moreover, Alb-PRF significantly increased cell migration. Notably, LPS-induced inflammatory damage led to more apoptotic HUVECs in PRP and H-PRF groups, whereas Alb-PRF rescued this damage. Alb-PRF conferred cytoprotection and upregulated expression of CD31, suggesting an augmented vascular regeneration capacity. Altogether, Alb-PRF outperformed PRP and H-PRF in driving angiogenic potential under both normal and inflammatory conditions. Our findings underscore the translational promise of Alb-PRF, particularly as an innovative solution for wound healing in regenerative medicine.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-44659-2.

Introduction

Angiogenesis, the formation of new blood vessels, represents a pivotal early phase in wound repair1,2. This biological phenomenon involves the activation, proliferation, and directional movement of endothelial cells to establish vascular networks, serving as a fundamental component in both wound healing and regenerative processes3. Vascular processes, in conjunction with hemostasis, inflammatory reactions, cellular dynamics, and extracellular matrix synthesis and remodeling, constitutes a cascade of biological events that contribute to wound healing and tissue regeneration4–6. To facilitate wound healing, researchers have developed various biomaterials and platelet-derived formulations aimed at enhancing angiogenesis7.

Autologous platelet concentrates (APCs) represent specialized blood products obtained through centrifugation protocols8. These preparations release platelet-derived bioactive molecules, including vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), transforming growth factor-beta1 (TGF-β1), insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) and epidermal growth factor (EGF), which have remarkable efficacy in promoting vascular formation and wound healing by stimulating endothelial cell proliferation, migration and angiogenesis9–11. When combined with tissue engineering methods, APCs can significantly enhance vascularization and improve angiogenesis12. Numerous studies have consistently validated the beneficial effects of APCs on tissue regeneration13–15. Thus, we speculated on the wound healing and damage defense abilities of these APCs.

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP), a first-generation APC, has demonstrated efficacy in wound healing16. Nevertheless, subsequent investigations revealed several limitations, particularly the requirement for dual centrifugation protocols and, more critically, the detrimental effects of anticoagulants on tissue repair processes17,18. These observations prompted the development of next-generation platelet rich fibrin (PRF) alternatives, which eliminate the use of anticoagulants and facilitate sustained growth factor release19. Innovations in centrifugation, including tube design and parameter optimization, yielded horizontal PRF (H-PRF)18,20. Nowadays, researchers have focused on optimizing PRF formulations. H-PRF centrifugation protocols achieve superior blood component stratification, yielding cellular concentrations approximately 4 folds greater than conventional fixed-angle centrifugation approaches17. Nevertheless, thermal modification of standardized PRF membranes has been shown to substantially prolong the functional longevity of PRF21,22. It was recently demonstrated that H-PRF integrating with heat-denatured albumin by Bio-Heat technology created Alb-PRF23–25. This innovation enhanced structural integrity and exhibited extended degradation kinetics of PRF persisting for 6 months25.

Our investigation employed in vitro models utilizing human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) cultured with distinct APCs: conventional PRP, standardized H-PRF, and refined Alb-PRF. This experimental design enabled a comparative evaluation of : the effects on HUVEC proliferation, cell apoptosis, migratory capacity, morphological characteristics, and cytoskeletal reorganization; the differences in cytoprotective potential against lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced inflammatory insults.

Results

Biocompatibility and biosafety of APCs towards HUVECs

To highlight the characteristics of our optimized Alb-PRF, we elaborately designed a schematic diagram illustrating the preparation process (Fig. 1A). We assessed and compared the biocompatibility and safety profiles of PRP, H-PRF and Alb-PRF on HUVECs, using live/dead assay, cell proliferation and apoptosis assays. After 24 h, live cells showed green fluorescence and dead cells displayed red fluorescence, confirming negligible cytotoxicity in all groups (Fig. 1B). Moreover, quantitative analysis of cell proliferation by CCK-8 assay revealed that Alb-PRF significantly increased cell density compared to both PRP (***p < 0.001) and H-PRF (#p < 0.05) after 48 h (Fig. 1C). In light of these comparative findings, we concluded that all APCs showed biocompatibility and biosafety towards HUVECs, with refined Alb-PRF eliciting the highest proliferative ability.

Morphology and cytoskeletal reorganization of HUVECs under different APCs

Treatment with H-PRF and Alb-PRF induced a pronounced morphological shift in HUVECs compared to the PRP group, characterized by increased cellular elongation. This morphological change was particularly evident in the Alb-PRF group, where enhanced cytoplasmic density was observed (Fig. 3A), indicative of extensive formation of filamentous actin structures. These distinct filaments, consistent with cytoskeletal reorganization, partly reflected actin filament polymerization and rearrangement (Fig. 3B). The Alb-PRF group exhibited the highest fluorescence intensity of actin (Alb-PRF/H-PRF vs. PRP, *p < 0.05 and **p < 0.01; Alb-PRF vs. H-PRF, #p < 0.05). The observed increase in filamentous structures suggested the enhanced healing potential, indicating that HUVECs treated with Alb-PRF were likely in an active state of cell migration and remodeling.

Wound healing assay with PRP, H-PRF and Alb-PRF

Consistent with prior cytoskeletal observations, our wound healing assay results confirmed the enhanced HUVEC migration for Alb-PRF over both PRP and H-PRF groups (Fig. 4A). Alb-PRF treatment yielded the most rapid wound closure, significantly surpassing the rate observed with traditional PRP and standardized H-PRF after 6 h of cocultures (Fig. 4A and B). Within 12 h, Alb-PRF induced near-complete scratch closure, in contrast to the less than 60% closure achieved by H-PRF (Fig. 4A and C). This pronounced acceleration of wound healing by Alb-PRF highlights its exceptional capacity to promote cell migration. This reinforces the link between Alb-PRF’s ability to stimulate dense filamentous structure formation and enhance cellular motility, which together promote regeneration, repairing, and wound healing.

Cytoprotective efficacy of PRP, H-PRF, and Alb-PRF against LPS injury

The above results mainly demonstrated that APCs, especially Alb-PRF, outperformed PRP and H-PRF and showed biocompatibility and biosafety towards HUVECs. Further, to investigate and assess the cytoprotective effects of these APCs against inflammatory endothelial injury, we established an LPS-induced vascular damage model, subsequently exposed these LPS-damaged HUVECs to PRP, H-PRF, or Alb-PRF, and then evaluated cell viability. Predictably, LPS exposure significantly reduced the cell viability of HUVECs. Notably, both Alb-PRF and H-PRF exhibited enhanced protection against cell death relative to the PRP group (Fig. 5A), with Alb-PRF demonstrating the most potent protective effect (Alb-PRF vs. PRP with ***p < 0.001 and Alb-PRF vs. H-PRF with #p < 0.05) (Fig. 5B). Consistently, HUVECs under LPS exposure displayed minimal cell death with Alb-PRF protection, as indicated by red fluorescence contrasting with the substantially higher mortality observed in the control PRP group. These findings indicate that Alb-PRF is superior to PRP and H-PRF in attenuating LPS-induced cytotoxicity and endothelial damage.

To corroborate our conclusions, we further conducted flow cytometric analysis of cell apoptosis. As expected, both H-PRF and Alb-PRF significantly reduced the proportion of apoptotic cells compared to PRP, confirming their enhanced protective capacity against the LPS-triggered apoptotic pathway (Fig. 6A and B). The Alb-PRF group showed a decreasing trend in the proportion of apoptotic HUVECs compared with H-PRF, although the difference was not statistically significant (Fig. 6B). Overall, these data underscore the critical role of H-PRF and Alb-PRF, particularly the latter, in safeguarding HUVECs from inflammatory damage and promoting healing under LPS challenge.

CD31 expression in LPS-exposed HUVECs treated with PRP, H-PRF, and Alb-PRF

CD31, known as platelet endothelial cell adhesion molecule-1 (PECAM-1), is highly expressed at endothelial cell-cell junctions. This adhesive stress-response protein preserves the endothelial junctions and rapidly restores vascular permeability after inflammation challenge26. Upon LPS exposure, our immunofluorescence analysis revealed significant elevation of CD31 expression in H-PRF and Alb-PRF treated HUVECs relative to the PRP group (Fig. 7A and B). Notably, Alb-PRF treatment induced the highest CD31 expression, suggesting its superior efficacy in preserving or augmenting endothelial function with #p < 0.05 (Alb-PRF vs. H-PRF, Fig. 7B). This enhanced CD31 expression potentially facilitated vascularization and tissue repair processes, indicating superior healing ability with refined Alb-PRF.

Overall, Alb-PRF outperforms PRP and H-PRF in endothelial angiogenesis and protection, perhaps by promoting HUVEC viability, migration and cytoskeletal reorganization, by safeguarding HUVECs from inflammatory damage under LPS challenge, and by modulating endothelial junctions to facilitate repair of pathological injuries (Fig. 7C).

Discussion

PRF has attracted significant research interest in recent years due to its efficacy in facilitating tissue regeneration27. This investigation systematically evaluated and compared the biological effects of Alb-PRF and H-PRF with traditional PRP on HUVECs. We particularly focused on cell viability, proliferative capacity, and wound healing potential under both normal conditions and inflammatory status. The selection of HUVECs as our in vitro experimental model was based on their established utility in angiogenesis and wound healing assessment, a fundamental bioprocess underlying successful tissue regeneration and wound repair across various tissue types. The critical importance of angiogenesis in tissue repair lies in maintaining endothelial function during the regenerative and repair processes, with endothelial cells serving as key determinants28.

Indeed, Alb-PRF represents an advanced platelet concentrate formulation developed through the innovative combination of heat-denatured platelet-poor plasma with the buffy coat fraction via horizontal centrifugation23,25. This unique preparation method confers superior structural integrity while preserving the sustained release of biologically active growth factors29. Moreover, the distinctive architecture of Alb-PRF facilitates the gradual liberation of multiple growth factors, including TGF-β1, PDGF isoforms (PDGF-AA, PDGF-AB, and PDGF-BB), EGF, IGF-1, and VEGF11. Experimental evidence indicates that Alb-PRF-conditioned medium can significantly promote chondrocyte proliferation, migration and cell adhesion, with Alb-PRF/chondrocyte composites demonstrating histological characteristics resembling native cartilage tissue11. Importantly, Alb-PRF exhibits optimal cytokine profiles, characterized by diminished pro-inflammatory cytokines release coupled with sustained elevation of PDGF levels, which effectively stimulate osteoblasts30,31. These collective properties prove Alb-PRF to be a promising biomaterial for tissue regeneration. In contrast to PRP formulations, the absence of anticoagulants in Alb-PRF contributes to its improved biocompatibility and efficient growth factor delivery kinetics. Clinically, investigations have further substantiated the therapeutic advantages of Alb-PRF, showing superior outcomes in tissue regeneration when used as an adjunct to open flap debridement32.

Our experimental findings revealed that three APCs supported HUVEC viability, migration, and proliferation, with Alb-PRF demonstrating the most robust effects with and without LPS exposure. Notably, both the H-PRF and Alb-PRF groups demonstrated improvement in HUVEC biological functions relevant to wound healing processes, as evidenced by distinct morphological alterations including cytoskeletal rearrangement and elongated cell morphology. Most prominently, the Alb-PRF group particularly displayed more pronounced cytoskeletal rearrangement and increased filamentous structure formation, reflecting the greatest enhancement in HUVEC motility and angiogenic potential. Indeed, such cytoskeletal changes are fundamental for angiogenesis and regeneration and these processes play pivotal roles in both initial wound repair and subsequent tissue healing.

Interestingly, when HUVECs were subjected to LPS, a well-established in vitro model for vascular damage, all three APCs conferred cytoprotection and effectively mitigated LPS-induced endothelial injury. However, comparative analysis revealed that H-PRF and Alb-PRF groups exhibited significantly lower apoptotic rates than PRP-treated cells, with Alb-PRF group showing the most pronounced cytoprotective effect. This enhanced protection perhaps derives from the leukocyte-rich composition of Alb-PRF preparations, resulting from the specific “horizontal centrifugation concept”, which not only boosts cell viability but also increases resistance to inflammatory insults17. Existing literature has well documented the dual antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties of PRF derivatives33–35. Collectively, these findings establish Alb-PRF as a superior and clinically translatable therapy for angiogenic regeneration, even within inflammatory pathological microenvironments.

Functionally, the transmembrane glycoprotein CD31 serves as both a definitive endothelial marker and a critical mediator of endothelial barrier integrity and angiogenic processes. Notably, the upregulation of CD31 is not an isolated event but is tightly associated with the activation of intracellular signaling pathways inducing endothelial repair, particularly the PI3K/Akt and MAPK pathways36,37. PRFs, including Alb-PRF, are enriched with a variety of platelet-derived growth factors. These factors bind to their respective receptors on the surface of endothelial cells, thereby initiating downstream signaling cascades. For instance, VEGF is well-documented to activate the PI3K/Akt pathway, which plays a central role in promoting endothelial cell survival, migration, and the maintenance of barrier function36,38. Our previous observation that Alb-PRF more potently upregulated CD31 may be attributed to its optimized release profile of these growth factors, leading to more sustained activation of cell repair signaling.

Actually, CD31 contributes to the maintenance and restoration of vascular integrity and permeability following barrier disruption by inflammation39. Herein, we focus on CD31 expression levels in LPS-stimulated HUVECs following different APC treatments. Consistently, quantitative analysis showed that both H-PRF and Alb-PRF groups upregulated CD31 expression in LPS-stimulated HUVECs, with the latter exhibiting more pronounced expression levels. These observations underscore the capacity of Alb-PRF to potentiate endothelial cell function, particularly when the processing protocols are carefully optimized.

Materials and methods

Preparation of PRP, H-PRF and Alb-PRF

Whole blood samples from 3 healthy donors aged 18 to 25 years were purchased from AllCells (CA, USA). Those donor program complies with applicable ethics regulations and all healthy donors provided written informed consent for the commercial use. PET PRF tubes (Greiner Bio-One GmbH, Kremsmünster, Austria) were used following previously concluded protocols27. For PRP preparation, whole blood was centrifuged at 900 × g for 5 min. The buffy coat-containing upper layer was carefully aspirated and subsequently centrifuged at 2000 × g for 15 min. After removing the platelet-poor plasma (PPP) supernatant, the remaining 1 mL fraction was collected as PRP. H-PRF was generated by centrifuging whole blood in PET PRF tubes using a horizontal centrifuge (Plasmatrident, Weiyin Technology Co., Wuhan, China) at a maximum relative centrifugal force of 700 × g for 8 min. The Alb-PRF preparation involved an initial centrifugation identical to that used in the H-PRF protocol. The PPP layer was then heat-treated at 75 °C for 10 min, followed by 10 min of cooling at ambient temperature. This denatured PPP was combined with 4 mL of a buffy coat-rich fraction called concentrated PRF (C-PRF), leading to rapid fibrin polymerization, which formed a solid membrane within 5 min (Fig. 1A). All prepared samples (PRP, H-PRF, and Alb-PRF) were transferred to 6-well culture plates containing 5 mL of Endothelial Cell Medium. Conditioned media were harvested and frozen at -80 °C for later use.

Cell culture

HUVECs were purchased from Procell Biotechnology (Wuhan, China) and cultured in specialized Endothelial Cell Medium (ECM, Sciencell, USA), supplemented with 5% fetal bovine serum, 1% endothelial cell growth supplement and 1% penicillin-streptomycin antibiotic mixture. The cells were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified environment with 5% CO2. Upon reaching 80–90% confluence, adherent cells were passaged using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA (Gibco, USA).

Live/dead assay

HUVECs were plated in 12-well culture plates containing 15 mm coverslips (NEST, USA) at an initial density of 5 × 10⁴ cells per well. The experimental groups were exposed to LPS (InvivoGen, 2 µg/mL) together with either 10% PRP, H-PRF, or Alb-PRF for 24 h40,41. After treatment, cells were rinsed three times with PBS to remove the residual reagents. Cell viability assessment was performed using a Calcein-AM/PI staining kit (Beyotime, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol. The coverslips were then carefully transferred to microscope slides and visualized under a confocal laser scanning microscope (LSM 880, Carl Zeiss, Germany) for image acquisition and analysis.

CCK-8 assay

The proliferative capacity of HUVECs following coculture with PRP, H-PRF, or Alb-PRF was quantitatively analyzed using the CCK-8 kit (Dojindo, Japan). Briefly, HUVECs were initially plated in 96-well plates at a concentration of 3 × 10³ cells per well, and maintained in 10% PRP, H-PRF, or Alb-PRF. After 48 h incubation period, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was introduced into each well, followed by 2 h incubation at 37 °C. OD values were subsequently measured at 450 nm using a microplate spectrophotometer (BioTek, USA).

Cytoskeleton staining

For cytoskeleton analysis, HUVECs were cultured in 24-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁴ cells per well. After treatment, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min and permeabilized using 0.25% Triton X-100 (Merck, USA) for 5 min. Following three times of washes, cells were stained with Actin-Tracker Green (Beyotime Biotechnology, China) for 30 min in the dark. Subsequently, nuclear counterstaining was achieved with DAPI (Beyotime Biotechnology) for 10 min. Fluorescent images were acquired using a confocal microscope.

Wound healing assay

HUVECs were plated in 6-well plates at a density of 3 × 10⁵ cells per well and cultured for 24 h. A linear scratch wound was then generated in each well using a sterile 3 mm diameter pipette tip. Phase contrast images of the wound area were acquired 0, 6, and 12 h using an optical microscope (Nikon DS-Qi2, Japan). Wound healing rate was quantified by measuring the wound width at each time point. The wound healing rate (%) was calculated as follows: Healing rate (%) = [(Wound width at 0 h - Scratch width at 6–12 h) / Wound width at 0 h] × 100%.

Apoptosis assay

HUVECs were plated in 12-well plates and treated without or with LPS (2 µg/mL) for 24 h in the presence of PRP, H-PRF, or Alb-PRF40,41. After treatment, cells were harvested by trypsinization, washed with PBS, and centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min. Cell pellets were resuspended and stained using an APC-conjugated Annexin V and propidium iodide (PI) apoptosis detection kit (Elabscience, China). Staining was performed for 30 min in the dark. After incubation, cells were immediately analyzed using a BD LSR Fortessa X-20 flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, USA) and subsequently analyzed using FlowJo software version 10 (TreeStar, USA).

Western blot

Proteins (25 µg per lane) were resolved on 10–15% SDS-PAGE, transferred to PVDF membranes (0.45 μm, Millipore), and blocked with 5% milk in TBS-T for 1 h. Membranes were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with anti-CD31 (1:500, Abcam, UK) and anti-GAPDH (1:1000, Abcam), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:3000, Abcam) for 1 h at room temperature. Bands were visualized with enhanced chemiluminescence reagent ( ECL, TIANGEN, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Statistical analysis

For comparison between multiple groups, statistical analysis was performed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Bonferroni post hoc test using GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad, USA). ANOVA was used for comparative analysis, the results should be interpreted with care due to the limited sample size. Statistical significance was considered at p < 0.05. Symbols indicated significance as follows: *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001 (Alb-PRF/H-PRF vs. PRP); #p < 0.05, ##p < 0.01, ###p < 0.001 (Alb-PRF vs. H-PRF). All data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD).

Conclusion

Our in vitro findings underscore the translational promise of Alb-PRF in maintaining endothelial cell viability, stimulating proliferation, and offering protection against inflammatory challenges. These superior effects of Alb-PRF on endothelial cell function and survival indicate its preferred use for regenerative applications. Furthermore, these findings provide scientific evidence that Alb-PRF may offer advantages over PRP and H-PRF for future preclinical and ultimately clinical studies.

Limitations

Nevertheless, the efficacy of our optimized PRFs in wound healing requires further validation using a larger sample size. These findings should be interpreted as preliminary and hypothesis-generating. While our in vitro model provides valuable insights, it cannot fully replicate the complexity of the in vivo microenvironment. Future research with larger sample sizes and in vivo validation is needed to confirm these observations.

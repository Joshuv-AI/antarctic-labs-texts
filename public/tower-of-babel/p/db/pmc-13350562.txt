Recent advances in the treatment of FLT3-mutated acute myeloid leukemia
Authors: Jeong Suk Koh, Ik-Chan Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) mutations are among the most common driver alterations in acute myeloid leukemia (AML), occurring in approximately 25–30% of patients, and have historically been associated with high relapse rates and poor survival. The therapeutic landscape for FLT3-mutated AML has evolved substantially with the development of FLT3-targeted agents. In newly diagnosed AML, the addition of midostaurin or quizartinib to intensive induction chemotherapy has demonstrated significant improvements in overall survival (OS), event-free survival, and relapse-free survival, establishing these regimens as current standards of care. In the relapsed or refractory setting, gilteritinib has become the preferred treatment based on superior survival and remission outcomes compared with salvage chemotherapy. Molecular studies have identified genomic factors associated with response and resistance to FLT3 inhibition, highlighting the importance of co-occurring mutations in treatment selection and prognosis. Maintenance therapy following allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation has emerged as a promising strategy, with evidence supporting the use of sorafenib and gilteritinib to reduce relapse risk and improve posttransplant outcomes. Recent clinical investigations have focused on novel combination approaches, particularly triplet regimens, incorporating FLT3 inhibitors, venetoclax, and hypomethylating agents, which have demonstrated encouraging responses and survival outcomes. Next-generation FLT3 inhibitors, including crenolanib and dapolsertib, are being evaluated in ongoing studies. These advances continue to improve outcomes in patients with FLT3-mutated AML and are expected to further refine treatment strategies across disease settings.

To the Editor,

FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 (FLT3) mutations are among the most common driver mutations in acute myeloid leukemia (AML). The genomic landscape reported by the German-Austrian AML Study Group, which analyzed approximately 1,500 patients, identified FLT3 mutations as one of the most frequently occurring genetic alterations in AML [1]. Overall, FLT3 mutations are detected in approximately 25–30% of patients with AML and are commonly associated with leukocytosis and a high bone marrow blast burden. FLT3 is a receptor tyrosine kinase that undergoes ligand-induced dimerization, resulting in the activation of downstream signaling pathways, including JAK-STAT, RAS-MAPK, and PI3K-AKT, thereby promoting leukemic cell proliferation and survival. FLT3 mutations frequently co-occur with other genetic abnormalities, including nucleophosmin 1 (NPM1) mutations, and cytogenetic abnormalities such as t(6;9) and t(15;17) [2]. Mutations in FLT3 are associated with adverse clinical outcomes. In a United Kingdom (UK) Medical Research Council study, FLT3-internal tandem duplication (FLT3-ITD) mutations were identified in approximately 27% of patients and were associated with higher relapse rates and inferior overall survival (OS) [3]. Consequently, FLT3-mutated AML has traditionally been considered a high-risk disease. However, the introduction of FLT3-targeted therapies has substantially improved clinical outcomes, leading to the reclassification of many patients into the intermediate-risk category [4].

For newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated AML, the addition of either midostaurin or quizartinib to intensive induction chemotherapy is currently recommended [5, 6]. The pivotal RATIFY and QuANTUM-First trials have established the efficacy of these approaches. Notably, RATIFY enrolled predominantly younger patients and included both FLT3-ITD and tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) mutations, whereas approximately 40% of participants in QuANTUM-First were aged ≥ 60 years. In both studies, OS served as the primary endpoint, and event-free survival (EFS) and relapse-free survival (RFS) were evaluated as secondary endpoints. The complete remission rates were 59% and 72% in the RATIFY and QuANTUM-First trials, respectively. Early mortality rates were comparable between treatment arms, whereas the proportion of patients undergoing allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) differed substantially. Both studies met their primary endpoints, demonstrating significant improvements in OS, with hazard ratios (HRs) of 0.77 and 0.78, respectively. Furthermore, significant improvements in EFS and RFS were observed, accompanied by an approximately 10% reduction in the incidence of relapse.

For relapsed or refractory (R/R) FLT3-mutated AML, gilteritinib has become the standard of care based on the phase 3 ADMIRAL trial [7]. This study compared gilteritinib with investigator-selected salvage chemotherapy in patients with FLT3-ITD or FLT3-TKD mutations. Median OS was significantly prolonged with gilteritinib (9.3 vs. 5.6 months; HR, 0.64), corresponding to a 36% reduction in mortality risk. The complete remission rate was also higher with gilteritinib (34% vs. 15%). Subsequent analyses suggested that co-occurring DNA methyltransferase 3 alpha (DNMT3A) and NPM1 mutations were associated with favorable outcomes, whereas alterations involving the RAS/MAPK pathway and FLT3 F691L mutation were implicated in resistance to gilteritinib [8]. Real-world data from the United Kingdom identified advanced age, lysine methyltransferase 2 A (KMT2A) rearrangement, and complex karyotypes as adverse prognostic factors, whereas runt-related transcription factor 1 (RUNX1) mutations were associated with improved outcomes [9].

Maintenance therapy after allogeneic HSCT has become an increasingly important therapeutic strategy. In general, continuation of FLT3 inhibitor therapy during induction or salvage treatment is recommended when feasible. A post hoc landmark analysis of the ADMIRAL trial demonstrated improved survival in patients who received gilteritinib maintenance after transplantation [10]. In contrast, midostaurin maintenance in the RATIFY trial failed to demonstrate a clear survival benefit. Sorafenib maintenance has also been evaluated in the German SORMAIN study and a randomized phase 3 trial conducted in China, both of which demonstrated significant improvements in post-transplant outcomes [11, 12].

Several emerging therapeutic approaches have recently been presented at the 2025 Annual Meeting of the American Society of Hematology (ASH). Triple-combination regimens incorporating gilteritinib, venetoclax, and azacitidine have demonstrated encouraging efficacy in phase 1/2 studies, including the MD Anderson experience (2025 ASH abstract 654) and the VICEROY trial, with complete remission rates exceeding 90% and 18-month OS rates ranging from 60 to 72% [13]. Another phase 1/2 study evaluated the combination of decitabine/cedazuridine, venetoclax, and gilteritinib (2025 ASH abstract 997). Grade 5 adverse events occurred in 17% of patients with R/R AML and 7% of newly diagnosed patients. The complete remission rates were 46% and 80%, respectively, suggesting substantial clinical activity in both settings. Dapolsertib, a dual FLT3/proviral integration site for Moloney murine leukemia virus (PIM) kinase inhibitor, was investigated in the phase 1/2 DIAMOND study involving 73 patients with refractory AML [14]. The most frequently reported adverse events were pneumonia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia. Although the overall response rate was modest (9%), responses were more frequent among patients harboring isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 (IDH1) or isocitrate dehydrogenase 2 (IDH2) mutations, with an overall response rate of approximately 15%. Finally, crenolanib, a second-generation FLT3 inhibitor that is active against both ITD and TKD mutations, was evaluated in a recently reported phase 3 study of R/R AML (2025 ASH abstract 767). Patients were randomized to receive salvage chemotherapy combined with either crenolanib or placebo. The study met its primary endpoint, demonstrating a significant improvement in EFS (HR, 0.64), although no significant improvement in OS was observed. The overall response rate was 60% in the crenolanib arm and 39% in the placebo arm. Notably, patients harboring NPM1 mutations exhibited significant benefits in terms of both EFS and OS.

In summary, the therapeutic landscape for FLT3-mutated AML continues to evolve with the development of novel agents in both frontline and relapsed/refractory settings, leading to improved clinical outcomes. The final results of ongoing studies evaluating triple-combination regimens are anticipated.

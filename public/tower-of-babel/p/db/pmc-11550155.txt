Solitary Benign Metastasizing Leiomyomas
Authors: Taiki Mori, Hisashi Kametaka
Published: 2024

A 46-year-old woman who had undergone a laparoscopic myomectomy at the age of 32 was referred to our hospital due to the detection of a large tumor in liver segment S8 during an ultrasound examination for right abdominal pain. Dynamic CT showed a well-defined 10 cm tumor with vascular enhancement and prolonged enhancement, suggesting a fibrous hepatic hemangioma (Figure A). However, blood flow from the right lower diaphragmatic artery suggested a primary diaphragmatic tumor (Figure B). Surgery was performed as the tumor was believed to cause the pain. Intraoperative findings revealed a large white tumor from the right diaphragm, which was resected (Figure C). Histopathology showed spindle-shaped tumor cells, positive for desmin, smooth muscle actin, ER and PgR, but negative for DOG1 and S-100, indicating a leiomyoma from a uterine myoma (Figure D). Postoperatively, the symptoms promptly disappeared, and the patient has remained recurrence-free. Preoperatively, differentiating the tumor from a hepatic hemangioma was challenging, but it was ultimately identified as a solitary benign metastatic leiomyoma of diaphragmatic origin. When encountering a patient with a history of uterine myoma and a nonspecific intra-abdominal mass, it is crucial to consider this condition and carefully evaluate the surgical indications and approach.

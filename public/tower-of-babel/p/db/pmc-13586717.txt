Trends in Buprenorphine and Methadone Dispensing Among Medicare Advantage and Commercial Enrollees, 2019–2024
Authors: Hailemichael Bekele Shone, Jonathan Cantor, Kosali Simon, Erin A. Taylor
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
HIGHLIGHTS•Medicare Advantage methadone dispensing rose sharply after 2020 Medicare coverage.•Dual-eligible Medicare Advantage enrollees aged <65 years had the highest medications for opioid use disorder rates.•Methadone reach stayed low in both payers despite rapid relative growth.

INTRODUCTION

The opioid crisis and elevated rates of drug overdose deaths continue as a pressing national public health emergency,1 despite the existence of effective medications for opioid use disorder (MOUD), including methadone and buprenorphine.2 In 2020, Medicare began covering methadone for the treatment of opioid use disorder (OUD), substantially increasing access to this treatment for Medicare beneficiaries.3 Also in 2020, multiple federal policies were implemented in response to the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic to increase access to MOUD, including changes to buprenorphine treatment through telemedicine.4 Previous research has established differences in telehealth utilization to treat substance use disorder between commercial and Medicare Advantage (MA) insurance.5 Emerging evidence suggests that despite initial increases in MOUD dispensing rates in 2020, there has been a leveling off of the MOUD dispensing rate since then.6 Moreover, some studies have shown that Medicare enrollees, including MA enrollees, are more likely to receive buprenorphine than commercial enrollees.7,8 However, it is important to note that both studies concluded before the end of the COVID-19 Public Health Emergency in May 2023 and did not include a focus on methadone.

This study adds to a burgeoning research area in 3 ways. First, national methadone and buprenorphine dispensing rates were quantified across 2 important types of insurance. Second, limited evidence exists that compares buprenorphine with methadone dispensing across age groups among individuals enrolled in commercial and MA insurance plans. Third, the study period spans the entire COVID-19 Public Health Emergency and its immediate pre–COVID-19 and post–COVID-19 periods. In summary, trends in buprenorphine and methadone dispensing among individuals diagnosed with OUD and enrolled in commercial or MA insurance were quantified to provide up-to-date estimates of MOUD receipt and to characterize differences by insurance type.

METHODS

Study Sample

This was a retrospective repeated cross-sectional study using Optum’s deidentified Clinformatics Data Mart, which is derived from a database of administrative health claims for members of large commercial and MA health plans. The database includes roughly 15–20 million covered lives annually, totaling about 38 million enrollees from 2019 Quarter 1 through 2024 Quarter 2, and spans all 50 states and the District of Columbia. The study period was restricted to January 2019 through June 2024. Including 2019 data establishes a prepolicy baseline for buprenorphine dispensing trends before the Medicare methadone coverage policy for the treatment of OUD went into effect in 2020.

Measures

OUD diagnosis was defined using established ICD-10 codes present in the current quarter or any of the 3 preceding quarters,9 and methadone dispensing for OUD treatment was identified from medical claims using procedure codes (Table 1, available online). Buprenorphine dispensing was identified using pharmacy National Drug Codes.10 Extended-release injectable buprenorphine, which is administered in clinical settings and billed through Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System codes rather than pharmacy claims, was not captured in this analysis.

Statistical Analysis

Dispensing rates were calculated as the number of OUD-diagnosed enrollees with at least 1 claim per 100,000 OUD-diagnosed enrollees per quarter, with exact Poisson 95% CIs. Rates were stratified by insurance type (MA versus commercial), age group (<65 years vs ≥65 years), and dual eligibility status for MA enrollees–concurrent coverage through both Medicare and Medicaid. Joinpoint regression (also known as piecewise linear regression) was performed using a piecewise log-linear Poisson model with Bayesian Information Criterion–based selection of 0–3 breakpoints (a minimum of 3 quarters apart). Annual percentage change (APC) per segment was computed as (exp[4β] − 1) × 100; the average APC was the duration-weighted mean across segments.

A buprenorphine continuity analysis decomposed quarterly prevalence into 4 mutually exclusive user types per 100,000 OUD-diagnosed enrollees: new users (first buprenorphine claim in the data set), continuing users (buprenorphine claim in both the current and the immediately preceding quarter), re-engaged users (buprenorphine claim in the current quarter but not the preceding quarter, with at least 1 prior claim), and discontinued users (buprenorphine claim in the preceding quarter but not the current quarter). Rates of methadone and buprenorphine dispensing were also calculated, stratified by age (ages ≥65 years and ages ≤64 years) and dual-eligibility status for MA enrollees. This study followed the STROBE reporting guideline for cross-sectional studies. Indiana University’s IRB deemed this research not to involve human subjects as defined by 45 CFR 46 (IRB 2011541510).

RESULTS

Enrollment trends diverged between MA and commercial insurance over the study period. In 2020 Quarter 1, there were 5.71 million MA and 11.93 million commercial enrollees. By 2024 Quarter 2, MA enrollment grew to 8.07 million, consistent with national trends in MA expansion driven by population aging and plan growth, whereas commercial enrollment declined to 10.12 million (Table A.2, available online). The OUD-diagnosed population numbered 75,320 MA and 30,147 commercial enrollees in 2020 Quarter 1 (1.32% and 0.25% of respective plan enrollees), remaining relatively stable through 2024 Quarter 2 (MA: 94,059, 1.17%; commercial: 25,171, 0.25%).

A total of 624 per 100,000 OUD-diagnosed MA enrollees received methadone in 2020 Quarter 1, rising to 5,462 per 100,000 by 2024 Quarter 2; among commercial OUD-diagnosed enrollees, the corresponding figures were 3,553 and 6,821 per 100,000 (Figure 1). The mean age of MA methadone recipients was 57.37 years (95% CI=57.18, 57.57) compared with 38.34 years (95% CI=38.08, 38.59) for commercial recipients. MA methadone recipients were predominantly aged <65 years (55.6% of person-quarters), whereas commercial recipients were almost exclusively aged <65 years (98.6%). Joinpoint regression identified 3 inflection points for MA methadone (2021 Quarter 1, 2022 Quarter 1, 2023 Quarter 3), with an explosive initial APC of +362.5% per year through 2021 Quarter 1, decelerating to +51.6% per year through 2022 Quarter 1, +6.0% per year through 2023 Quarter 3, and +27.4% per year thereafter (average annual percentage change [AAPC] of +104.6% per year). The initial 2020–2021 surge likely reflected the expanded treatment access after the 2020 policy change. The continued growth after 2021 suggests sustained uptake. For commercial methadone, joinpoint regression identified inflection points at 2020 Quarter 1, 2022 Quarter 2, and 2023 Quarter 3 (AAPC of +23.9% per year), with the highest APC of +55.5% per year in the pre-2020 segment, decelerating to +20.4%, +9.0%, and +16.9% per year in subsequent segments.

Stratifying by age group and insurance type, the most rapid methadone increases were observed among MA enrollees aged <65 years, reaching 8,106 per 100,000 in 2024 Quarter 2, compared with 4,062 per 100,000 among MA enrollees aged ≥65 years (Figure A.1, available online). For buprenorphine (Figure A.2, available online), the decline in commercial insurance was concentrated among enrollees aged <65 years, where rates fell from 32,304 to 27,410 per 100,000 OUD-diagnosed enrollees between 2020 Quarter 1 and 2024 Quarter 2, whereas MA enrollees aged <65 years showed continued increases from 17,635 to 31,455 per 100,000.

Treatment continuity analysis of buprenorphine showed that the commercial decline was not attributable to shifts in the OUD-diagnosed denominator, which remained relatively stable, nor to an increase in treatment discontinuations (Figure A.3, available online). Rather, the decline was driven primarily by a fall in continuing users—those with buprenorphine claims in consecutive quarters—which peaked in mid-2022 in the commercial panel and declined thereafter, whereas the MA continuing-user rate continued to rise. New user rates (first-ever claim) were volatile but showed no sustained decline in either payer type. The proportion of prevalent buprenorphine users who were continuing users remained stable at approximately 75% in both payer types, indicating that the overall population of prescription treatment users contracted rather than becoming compositionally less stable.

DISCUSSION

This study documents national trends in the dispensing of both buprenorphine and methadone across MA and commercial insurance during the entire COVID-19 Public Health Emergency and its immediate pre–COVID-19 and post–COVID-19 periods. Substantial increases in methadone and buprenorphine dispensing rates among MA enrollees were observed beginning in 2020, driven primarily by dually eligible enrollees aged <65 years and nondually eligible enrollees aged <65 years. Joinpoint regression confirmed this trajectory, with MA methadone rates increasing at +362.5% per year through 2021 Quarter 1 before decelerating to sustained but more modest growth through 2024 Quarter 2 (AAPC of +104.6% per year). This pattern is consistent with an earlier finding,11 which observed similar trends through 2022 Quarter 1; the present analysis extends these observations by incorporating an additional 2 years of data through 2024 Quarter 2. The methadone payment change, coupled with other access policies implemented in 2020, appears to have increased access for Medicare beneficiaries, but uptake seems concentrated among those with the highest rates of OUD. Despite this growth, MA methadone rates of 5,462 per 100,000 OUD-diagnosed enrollees in 2024 Quarter 2 indicate that methadone remains accessible to a minority of diagnosed patients. Moreover, it is possible that some of the initial increase reflects a shift in methadone payment from Medicaid to Medicare rather than entirely new treatment access. The increase in MA methadone dispensing beginning in 2020 is consistent with the possibility that COVID-19 Public Health Emergency policies designed to maintain or expand MOUD access were associated with increased dispensing in MA.

Among commercial enrollees, a more modest increase in methadone dispensing over time was observed, along with a decline in buprenorphine prescription dispensing, with the decline steepening to −20.0% per year after 2023 Quarter 2. Treatment continuity analysis showed that this decline was driven by a contracting pool of continuing users rather than rising discontinuations, suggesting a failure to retain patients on therapy rather than an acute disruption. Although methadone dispensing rates increased several folds among MA enrollees after 2020, absolute levels remained low, indicating that methadone’s reach in these insured populations remains limited despite policy changes. Comparing MA and commercial insurance populations provides important context for interpreting these trends. MA enrollees differ from commercially insured populations in age distribution and rates of dual eligibility, all of which are associated with a higher prevalence of OUD and greater clinical and social complexity. These differences help explain why changes in methadone dispensing were concentrated in specific subgroups and highlight how coverage policy changes may differentially affect access across populations.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the analysis is limited to individuals with MA and commercial insurance, so the results are not generalizable to other payers (i.e., Medicaid or Medicare fee for service) or to the uninsured. In addition, the Clinformatics database’s coverage differs for each state, and the population may not be generalizable to individuals covered by MA or commercial insurance.12 Second, the findings do not indicate the causal effect of the federal change in methadone coverage policy or other policies enacted during the COVID-19 pandemic on MOUD dispensing. Third, buprenorphine was identified using pharmacy National Drug Codes for oral and sublingual formulations only; extended-release injectable formulations billed through medical claims were not captured. Fourth, for dual-eligible beneficiaries, although Medicare is the primary payer, some services may be covered by Medicaid and not fully captured in these data; however, the impact on overall estimates is likely to be limited.

CONCLUSIONS

Despite these limitations, this study suggests that the 2020 Medicare methadone coverage policy was associated with a rapid increase in methadone dispensing among MA enrollees that outpaced growth in commercial plans, especially until 2022, consistent with a policy-driven expansion of access. Methadone use under both payer types showed an increase over the study period, suggesting that broader factors, such as COVID-era telehealth flexibilities, may have also supported access across payer types.

Tumor lymph node metastasis chip reveals that the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis promotes melanoma metastasis by enhancing treg cell function and inducing CD8+ T cell exhaustion
Authors: Cuihao Song, Wenwei Zhu, Yaolin Li, Yuhan Ji, Xiaoling Liu, Yingjie Zhang, Xiaoning Zhang, Fangfang Wei, Hexi Yang, Likeng Liang, Shuyan Huang, Chengxin Li, Yong Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo investigate the role and molecular mechanism of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis in melanoma lymph node metastasis.MethodsSingle-cell RNA sequencing was performed on human primary melanoma and lymph node metastatic tissues. A tumor lymph node metastasis-on-a-chip model incorporating vascular, lymphatic, and dual-tumor modules was constructed, along with in vitro coculture systems using CD8+ T cells and regulatory T cells. Functional blockade was achieved via NECTIN3 knockdown, TIGIT inhibitor (Tiragolumab), and combined antiPD1 therapy.ResultsThe NECTIN3-TIGIT axis was identified as a core immune ligand-receptor pair in the highmetastasis subgroup, associated with increased regulatory T cell infiltration and CD8+ T cell exhaustion. Both NECTIN3 and TIGIT expression were significantly elevated in metastatic lesions and correlated with reduced overall survival. The chip model recapitulated melanoma lymphatic migration and confirmed the immunosuppressive microenvironment. Mechanistically, the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis induced CD8+ T cell exhaustion via the SHIP-1/TRAF6 pathway and promoted regulatory T cell activation by inhibiting AKT/mTORC1 signaling. NECTIN3 knockdown or Tiragolumab treatment suppressed tumor lymphatic migration, while combined antiPD1 and Tiragolumab most effectively reversed T cell exhaustion, impaired regulatory T cell function, and reduced metastasis.ConclusionThe NECTIN3-TIGIT axis promotes melanoma lymph node metastasis by remodeling the immunosuppressive microenvironment. Combined blockade of NECTIN3-TIGIT and PD1 pathways represents a promising therapeutic strategy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s13402-026-01246-x.

Introduction

Melanoma, a highly malignant skin tumor, is characterized by strong invasiveness and high metastatic potential [1, 2]. Lymph node metastasis represents a critical stage in its disease progression and a core risk factor affecting patient prognosis [3]. Patients with lymph node metastatic melanoma have limited clinical treatment options and a markedly reduced long-term survival rate [4, 5]. Although breakthroughs have been achieved in melanoma immunotherapy, with immune checkpoint blockade therapy represented by PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors becoming the clinical mainstream, the immunosuppressive characteristics of the tumor microenvironment still lead to primary or acquired resistance in some patients. Abnormal infiltration of regulatory T (Treg) cells and exhaustion of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells are important mechanisms of tumor immune escape, and the immunosuppressive microenvironment shaped by both provides favorable conditions for tumor metastasis [6–8]. However, the specific regulatory mechanisms underlying their roles in melanoma lymph node metastasis remain poorly elucidated [7, 9].

The ligand-receptor interactions between tumor cells and immune cells are the core link in regulating the tumor microenvironment. TIGIT, an important immunosuppressive receptor, is mainly expressed on the surface of T cells, NK cells and other immune cells [10–12]. It mediates immunosuppressive signals by binding to ligands on the surface of tumor cells, emerging as a potential target for tumor immunotherapy [13]. TIGIT primarily binds to CD155 and NECTIN family proteins [10, 12, 14]. Current evidence identifies CD155 as the predominant ligand for TIGIT; CD155 expressed on tumor cells can mediate immune escape by engaging TIGIT on CD8+ T cells [15]. Among NECTIN family members, NECTIN2 exhibits notably weaker binding affinity compared to CD155 but still functions as a bona fide ligand [16]. NECTIN3 has also been reported as a TIGIT-associated ligand, and its overexpression is considered to be linked to tumor cell invasion and migration [17]. Nevertheless, the role and downstream regulatory pathways of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis in melanoma lymph node metastasis have not been reported. In addition, traditional in vitro cell experiments and animal models are difficult to accurately mimic the in vivo microenvironment of melanoma lymph node metastasis. Tumor lymph node metastasis-on-a-chip (TLNMC), an emerging microfluidic technology, enables co-culture of tumor cells, immune cells and vascular/lymphatic microenvironments, providing a more in vivo-like experimental platform for deciphering the immune regulatory mechanisms of tumor metastasis.

In this study, we first identified the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis as a specifically high-expressed immune regulatory ligand-receptor pair in high-metastatic potential melanoma by single-cell RNA sequencing of melanoma PT and LNMT tissues to explore metastasis-related core immune regulatory ligand-receptor pairs. We then verified the correlation between its expression level and patient prognosis using clinical samples, and investigated the regulatory effects of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis on Treg cell function and CD8+ T cell exhaustion at the cellular and molecular levels, as well as the downstream signaling pathways, using a self-constructed TLNMC model. Finally, we clarified the molecular mechanism by which the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis promotes melanoma lymph node metastasis by enhancing Treg cell immunosuppressive function and inducing profound CD8+ T cell exhaustion. This study provides novel targets and experimental evidence for the immunotherapy of melanoma lymph node metastasis, and lays a foundation for the development of combined immunotherapy regimens targeting the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis.

Materials and methods

Clinical sample collection

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the Measures for Ethical Review of Biomedical Research Involving Human Subjects (China). The study was approved by the Chinese PLA General Hospital Ethics Committee (Approval Number: S2025-093–01, China), and all research subjects signed written informed consent forms. Peripheral blood samples from healthy individuals were collected, along with clinical samples of human melanoma primary tumor (PT) tissues and lymph node metastatic tumor (LNMT) tissues. Immediately after collection, tissue samples were frozen in liquid nitrogen or fixed with formalin and embedded in paraffin for subsequent molecular biological detection and pathological histological analysis, while peripheral blood samples were used for the isolation and culture of immune cells.

Cell lines and isolation and culture of primary immune cells

Human lymph node metastatic melanoma cell line WM266-4, primary melanoma cell line WM115, human dermal fibroblasts, human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC) and human dermal lymphatic endothelial cells (HDLEC) were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) with the catalog numbers CRL-1676, CRL-2528, PCS-201–010, CCL-173 and CRL-2121, respectively. All adherent cells were routinely cultured and passaged in DMEM medium (HyClone, SH30243.01, US) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, 10099141C, US) in a constant temperature incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, and cells in the logarithmic growth phase were selected for subsequent experiments.

Isolation of CD8+ T cells, dendritic cells (DCs) and regulatory T (Treg) cells from human peripheral blood: Peripheral blood from healthy individuals was collected, and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were obtained by density gradient centrifugation using lymphocyte separation medium (Solarbio, P8610, China). Then, PBMCs were sorted by flow cytometry with the sorting markers CD3+ (Abcam, ab34722, UK), CD8+ (Abcam, ab26004, UK) for CD8+ T cells, and CD4+ (Abcam, ab18280, UK), CD25+ (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 12–0257-42, US) for Treg cells. The sorted immune cells were activated and cultured in vitro in special medium containing corresponding cytokines, and the sorting purity was verified by flow cytometry (Supplementary Figure 2).

Construction of knockdown/overexpression cell lines

Gene-specific siRNAs targeting NECTIN3 or PVR, along with overexpression recombinant vectors, were purchased from Shanghai GeneChem Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Transfection of siRNA into WM266-4 cells for gene knockdown, and overexpression vectors into WM115 cells for gene overexpression, were performed using Lipofectamine 3000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen, catalog no. L3000015), following the manufacturer’s instructions. Meanwhile, negative control groups (Si-NC/empty vector EV) and blank control group (Ctrl) were set up. At 48 h after transfection, PCR and Western blot were used to detect the mRNA and protein expression levels of NECTIN3 in each group of cells. Cell lines with the best knockdown and overexpression efficiency were screened to construct stable cell lines for subsequent experiments (Supplementary Figure 5). Construction and culture of tumor lymph node metastasis-on-a-chip (TLNMC)

The TLNMC constructed in this study was based on PDMS, consisting of four parts: vascular channel, lymphatic channel, primary tumor module (PTM) and lymph node metastatic tumor module (LNMTM). The overall construction timeline was from 3 days before the experiment (Day-3) to the day of the experiment (Day0). On Day-3, chip sterilization and channel coating were completed: the PDMS chip was disinfected by soaking in 75% alcohol for 30 min, air-dried in an ultra-clean bench, and then sterilized by ultraviolet irradiation combined with ozone. After rinsing with PBS buffer to remove residual alcohol, a mixture of 100 μg/ml fibronectin (Sigma, F2006, US) and 100 μg/ml type Ⅳ collagen (Sigma, C5533, US) was infused into the vascular and lymphatic channels and incubated overnight at 37 °C to complete coating. On Day-2, endothelial cells were seeded: HUVECs and HDLECs adjusted to a density of 5 × 106 cells/ml were injected into the corresponding vascular and lymphatic channels, and cultured at 37 °C for 30 min to allow partial cell adhesion. The chip was then flipped to make the non-adherent cells attach to the upper surface of the microchannels under gravity. After all cells were completely adherent, complete medium was continuously perfused into the channels at a flow rate of 1 μL/min by a peristaltic pump to provide nutrients and fluid shear stress. On Day-1, the melanoma modules were constructed: after vascular endothelial cells fully covered the four inner walls of the vascular channel, a mixture of WM115/WM266-4 cells and human dermal fibroblasts at a ratio of 4:1 with 5 mg/mL type I collagen (Sigma, C7661, US) solution was injected into the tumor channels and incubated at 37 °C for 1 h to allow collagen polymerization and form a gel cell scaffold. On Day0, system connection and experiment initiation were completed: the peristaltic pump was aseptically connected to the vascular channel, and medium containing low-concentration IL-2 (PeproTech, 200–02, US) was perfused at a flow rate of 1 μL/min. Mature DCs and CD8+ T cells at a ratio of 1:10 were infused into the lymphatic channel at a flow rate of 0.1 μL/min, and activated Treg cells were added to the vascular channel for cyclic culture. The TLNMC was placed in a constant temperature incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for continuous culture for subsequent detection.

Bioinformatics analysis

Processing of published dataset

The single-cell RNA sequencing dataset GSE215120 was downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GSE215120_RAW.tar). This dataset includes five cases of acral melanoma, three cases of cutaneous melanoma, and one case of cutaneous melanoma with lymph node metastasis. In the present study, samples from cutaneous melanoma and lymph node metastasis were selected to investigate transcriptional patterns associated with melanoma metastasis.

Raw count data were processed using the standard Seurat V5 pipeline. Quality control was performed with the following criteria: min.cells = 10, min.features = 300, percentage of mitochondrial reads < 20%, and nFeature_RNA > 200. The count matrix was normalized using the NormalizeData() function in Seurat, which performs library size normalization by dividing gene counts by total counts per cell, multiplying by a scale factor of 10,000, and applying log-transformation. Feature selection was conducted using FindVariableFeatures() to identify the 2,000 most variable genes based on standardized variance, which were subsequently used for canonical correlation analysis as an initial dimensionality reduction step.

Data scaling was performed using the ScaleData() function, which regresses out total expression and scales features to unit variance. Principal component analysis (PCA) was then applied to the scaled data, and the top 50 principal components were retained for downstream analysis. To correct for batch effect across samples and patients, the Harmony R package was applied to the PCA embeddings, generating batch-corrected integrated embeddings across cell types and patients. These corrected Harmony components were subsequently used as input for Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE), and the FindNeighbors function. Cell clustering was performed using the Louvain algorithm. For visualization, the dimensionality of the scaled integrated data matrix was further reduced to two-dimensional space via PCA followed by UMAP.

Pseudotime analysis using monocle

Monocle2 (version 2.99.3) was employed to infer the differentiation trajectory from primary melanoma to metastatic melanoma, using default parameters as recommended by the developers. Briefly, gene expression matrices of patients CM1 and the corresponding CM1Lym were exported from Seurat and used to construct a CellDataSet object. The setOrderingFilter() function was then applied to select ordering genes based on variable genes identified by the differentialGeneTest() function, with a cutoff of q < 0.001. Following dimensionality reduction using the reduceDimension() function (employing the ‘DDRTree’ method), a series of representative key genes involved in the differentiation process were identified and visualized using the plot_pseudotime_heatmap() function. Dimensionality reduction was performed without normalization using the ‘DDRTree’ method in the reduceDimension() step. Finally, the plot_cell_trajectory() function was used to visualize each group along the same pseudotime trajectory.

Enrichment analysis

To investigate the biological functions associated with metastasis-related transcriptional signatures, Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed on the metastasis-related gene set using the “ClusterProfiler” package in R software. A significance threshold of p < 0.05 was applied. The resulting enrichment terms were visualized using the “ggplot2” package in R.

Ligand–receptor interaction analysis

To systematically infer ligand–receptor interactions between tumor cells and T cells derived from patients CM1 and CM1Lym, we employed CellPhoneDB v.3. The analysis was performed using the statistical method with 10 iterations and no subsampling; all other parameters were kept at default settings. CellPhoneDB determines statistical significance through permutation testing, as detailed in the original publication.

Molecular detection techniques

qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from cultured cells or melanoma tissue using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, 15596018CN, US) strictly according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration and purity of RNA were measured using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, US), and all samples exhibited A260/A280 ratios between 1.8 and 2.0, meeting quality control standards. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from 1 μg of total RNA using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (TaKaRa, RR036A, Japan). Subsequently, quantitative real-time PCR was performed on a LightCycler 480 II Real-Time PCR System (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) using TB Premix Ex Taq II kit (TaKaRa, RR820A, Japan). The thermal cycling conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 seconds, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 5 seconds and annealing/extension at 60 °C for 30 seconds. A melting curve analysis was conducted at the end of each run to verify amplification specificity. Primers for target genes (NECTIN3, PVR, TIGIT) and the internal reference gene ACTB were synthesized by GenePharma (China). All reactions were performed in triplicate, and relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2^−ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences used are provided in Table 2. Western blot

Total protein was extracted from cultured cells or tissue samples using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, P0013B, China) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails (Beyotime, ST505, China), and protein concentrations were determined using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 23,225, US) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Equal amounts of protein (20 μg per lane) were separated by 10–12% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and subsequently transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 88,518, US). The membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBST) at room temperature for 1 hour, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C with primary antibodies against NECTIN3 (1:1000, Abcam, 314,219, UK), TIGIT (1:2000, Cell Signaling Technology, 99,567, US), CD155 (1:1000, Abcam, 314,064, UK), SHIP1 (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, 2728,US), p-SHIP1 (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, 3941, US), TRAF6 (1:5000, Abcam, ab40675,UK), AKT (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, 9272,US), p-AKT (Ser473) (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology,4060, US), S6K1 (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, 34,475, US), p-S6K1 (Thr389) (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, 9234, US), FOXP3 (1:1000, Abcam, ab36607, UK), and β-actin (1:5000, Abcam, ab6276, UK) as the loading control. After washing three times with TBST, the membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000, Cell Signaling Technology, 34,475, US) at room temperature for 1 hour. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) (Abcam, ab133406, UK) detection system and imaged with a ChemiDoc XRS+ imaging system (Bio-Rad, US). Densitometric analysis was performed using ImageJ software, and relative protein expression levels were normalized to β-actin.

Immunohistochemistry

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections (4 μm thickness) were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series. Antigen retrieval was performed by heating the sections in citrate buffer (10 mM, pH 6.0, Beyotime, P0081, China) at 95 °C for 20 minutes using a microwave oven. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked by incubating the sections with 3% hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) at room temperature for 10 minutes, followed by blocking with 5% normal goat serum in PBS for 1 hour at room temperature to minimize non-specific binding. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against NECTIN3 (1:200, Abcam, 314,219, UK), TIGIT (1:200, Cell Signaling Technology, 99,567, US), CD155 (1:1000, Abcam, 314,064, UK) and FOXP3 (1:500, Abcam, ab20034, UK). After washing three times with PBS, the sections were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody (1:5000, Cell Signaling Technology, 8114, US) at room temperature for 1 hour. Immunoreactivity was visualized using a 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) substrate kit (Abcam, ab64238, UK), and sections were counterstained with hematoxylin for nuclear visualization. For quantitative analysis, five representative high-power fields were randomly selected per section, and the percentage of positively stained cells or staining intensity was evaluated using ImageJ software.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

To quantify the concentrations of immunoregulatory cytokines in the tumor microenvironment, culture supernatants were collected from each module of the TLNMC at the indicated time points. Samples were centrifuged at 1,000 ×g for 10 min at 4 °C to remove cellular debris, and the resulting supernatants were stored at −80 °C until further analysis. The levels of interleukin-10 (IL-10), transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β), interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) were measured using commercial ELISA kits (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, US; catalog numbers: IL-10: DY210, TGF-β: DY240, IFN-γ: DY285, TNF-α: DY210) according to the manufacturer’s protocols. Briefly, 96-well microplates were pre-coated with capture antibodies and incubated overnight at room temperature. After blocking with reagent diluent for 1 hour, standards and samples were added to the wells and incubated for 2 hours at room temperature. Following three washes with wash buffer, detection antibodies were added and incubated for another 2 hours. Subsequently, streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase (HRP) conjugate was added and incubated for 20 minutes in the dark. After thorough washing, substrate solution (tetramethylbenzidine, TMB) was added to each well and incubated for 20 minutes, and the reaction was terminated by adding stop solution. The optical density (OD) at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader, with wavelength correction at 540 nm. Cytokine concentrations were calculated by interpolating from the standard curve generated with known concentrations of recombinant cytokines.

Immunofluorescence staining (IF)

For IF staining, each module of the tumor lymph node metastasis chip (TLNMC) was fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 20 minutes, permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 for 15 minutes, and blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 1 hour to minimize non-specific binding. The samples were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against specific targets, including CD8 (1:100, Abcam, ab237709, UK); Foxp3 (1:400, Thermo Fisher Scientific, 700,914, US); PD1 (1:100, Abcam, ab52587, UK); TOX(1:50, Abcam, ab322259, UK); S100A1 (1:200, Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA565995, US); NECTIN3, (1:100, Abcam, ab16913, UK); TIGIT (1:200, Thermo Fisher Scientific, 16–9500-82, US); CD155 (1:100, Thermo Fisher Scientific, 29,762, US); VE-cadherin (1:1000, Abcam, ab205336, UK); ZO-1 (1:100, Abcam, ab307799, UK); LYVE-1(1:200, Abcam, ab33682, UK); VEGFR-3 (1:200, Abcam, ab27278, UK); MHC-II (1:200, Abcam, ab23990, UK); Ki67(1:250, Abcam, ab16667, UK); α-SMA (1:200, Cell Signaling Technology, 19,245, US); mCherry (1:500, Abcam, ab167453, UK) and GFP (1:2000, Abcam, ab13970, UK). After washing, the chips were incubated with appropriate fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies Alexa Fluor 488 (1:200, Abcam, ab150113, ab150077, UK)and Alexa Fluor 594 (1:200, Abcam, ab150080, ab150116, UK) at room temperature for 1 hour in the dark, followed by nuclear counterstaining with DAPI (Abcam, ab104139, UK) for 10 minutes. Fluorescent images were acquired using a laser scanning confocal microscope quantitative analysis of fluorescence intensity and cell counting was performed using ImageJ software on at least five randomly selected fields per sample. Co-localization analysis was conducted using the Coloc 2 plugin to assess spatial relationships between NECTIN3 and TIGIT expression. All experiments were independently repeated at least three times, and representative images are shown.

Cell function and Co-culture experiments

CD8+ T cell co-culture experiment: Sorted CD8+ T cells were activated in vitro with anti-CD3/CD28 magnetic beads (Thermo Scientific, 11131D), then divided into control group, recombinant NECTIN3 protein treatment group and recombinant NECTIN3 protein + TIGIT inhibitor combination treatment group. Recombinant NECTIN3 protein was purchased from R&D Systems (1167-N3, US), and TIGIT inhibitor Tiragolumab was purchased from Selleck (S7666, US). After co-culture, Western Blot was used to detect the expression of proteins related to the SHIP-1/TRAF6 signaling axis, and ELISA was used to detect the cytokine secretion concentration. Treg cell co-culture experiment: Sorted CD4+CD25+ Treg cells were activated in vitro with anti-CD3/CD28 magnetic beads, then divided into control group, recombinant NECTIN3 protein treatment group, recombinant NECTIN3 protein + mTOR inhibitor combination treatment group and recombinant NECTIN3 protein + Tiragolumab combination treatment group. mTOR inhibitor was purchased from Selleck (S1038, US). After co-culture, Western Blot was used to detect the expression of AKT/mTORC1 pathway-related proteins and Foxp3, and IF staining was used to detect the co-localization of Ki67 and Foxp3.

Clinical prognosis analysis

In this study, comprehensive clinical data and follow-up information of melanoma patients were first retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/tcga). Based on the gene expression levels of NECTIN3, TIGIT, and PVR in tumor tissues, patients were stratified into high-expression and low-expression groups using the optimal cut-off values. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated, and Log-rank tests were performed to compare the differences in overall survival (OS) between the expression groups, thereby evaluating the associations of NECTIN3, TIGIT, and PVR expression with clinical prognosis. Furthermore, an additional subgroup survival analysis was conducted using the melanoma dataset GSE65904 (comprising 177 metastatic melanoma samples and 33 primary melanoma samples) to separately assess the prognostic significance of NECTIN3 and TIGIT in primary versus metastatic melanoma.

Statistical analysis

All in vitro cell experiments and chip experiments were independently repeated three times. Experimental data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 software. Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x±s). Independent sample t-test was used for comparison between two groups, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for comparison among multiple groups, with LSD method for multiple comparisons between groups. Kaplan-Meier method and Log-rank test were used for clinical prognosis analysis. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical graphs were drawn using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software.

Results

Single-cell sequencing of melanoma tissues reveals cell subset characteristics

This study utilized an integrated single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset of cutaneous melanoma and cutaneous melanoma lymph node metastases to perform cell annotation and subsequent analyses. The dataset comprised a total of 34,287 cells. In this step, six primary cell types were annotated: melanoma cells, NK/T cells, monocytes, endothelial cells, B cells, and fibroblasts (Fig. 1a). UMAP preserves the nearest neighbor graph to ensure that cells with similar gene expression profiles cluster together; therefore, cells located in close proximity within the UMAP plot often share similar functional characteristics. Under the integrated annotation of all cells, NK cells and T cells were difficult to distinguish, indicating that NK cells and T cells exhibit similar transcriptional profiles in melanoma. Additionally, melanoma cells formed distinct subclusters in the UMAP plot, suggesting patient heterogeneity in melanoma transcriptional patterns. The canonical markers used for cell type annotation in this study are shown (Fig. 1b). Specifically, melanoma cells were annotated using TYR, which promotes melanogenesis; PMEL, a melanocyte precursor protein; MLANA, involved in melanin synthesis and transport; and DCT, an enzyme in the melanin synthesis pathway. Endothelial cells were annotated using PECAM1 and VWF, which mediate endothelial cell adhesion. Fibroblasts, the primary producers of collagen, were annotated using COL1A1, COL1A2, and COL3A1. NK/T cells were annotated using CD3D, a T cell surface receptor, and the cytotoxic protein NKG7. Monocytes were annotated using CD74, which is associated with antigen presentation. B cells were annotated using MS4A1, a B cell surface marker, and CD79A, a B cell surface receptor. NK cells express the cytotoxic proteins GZMB and NKG7 but do not express the T cell surface receptor CD3D. Based on this feature, T cells and NK cells were first (Fig. 1c). Subsequently, CD8+ T cells and CD4+ T cells were identified using CD4 and CD8A. The relative proportion of T cells was upregulated in metastatic samples (Fig. 1d), suggesting increased immune infiltration after metastasis, which may also be related to the site of melanoma metastasis.

Subsequently, T cell subtyping was performed (Fig. 1e–f). Specifically, for CD4+ T cells, naïve CD4+ T cells were annotated using CCR7 and SELL, which facilitate T cell migration to secondary lymphoid organs/lymph nodes. The transcription factor FOXP3 is a key regulator of Treg cells, controlling their suppressive phenotype. IL2RA is often co-expressed with FOXP3, and these genes were used to annotate Treg cells. Effector CD4+ T cells were annotated using IL7R, CD40LG, and CAPG. T follicular helper (Tfh) cells were annotated using CXCL13, PVALB, DRAIC, and BTLA. Terminally differentiated effector memory CD4+ T cells were annotated using TTN, GZMH, and KMT2A. Interferon-stimulated gene (ISG)-high CD4+ T cells were annotated using USP18, MX1, and ISG15. For CD8+ T cells, cytotoxic CD8+ T cells were identified using GZMH and FGFBP2, exhausted CD8+ T cells using CTLA4 and PDCD1, and naïve CD8+ T cells using SELL and EGR1.

Integration of single-cell data from PT and LNMT with pseudotime analysis to identify features of tumor metastasis

To mitigate the confounding effects of interpatient heterogeneity in melanoma on pseudotime analysis, this study focused exclusively on cells derived from CM1 and its matched lymph node metastatic sample, CM1lym, to delineate the tumor differentiation trajectory. Seurat analysis was first performed on the expression data from the two samples. UMAP visualization revealed that a subset of melanoma cells from CM1 clustered together with melanoma cells from CM1lym, suggesting that some CM1 melanoma cells possess metastatic potential (Fig. 2a). Pseudotime trajectories were subsequently constructed using Monocle2. Clusters predominantly composed of CM1 melanoma cells localized to the early differentiation stage, whereas clusters enriched for CM1lym melanoma cells were situated at later stages of differentiation (Fig. 2b–c). To identify the temporal stage at which metastasis occurs, the expression dynamics of three categories of metastasis‑related genes were examined along the pseudotime axis (Fig. 2d): genes involved in immune evasion, including CD47, HLA‑E, and CD44; genes associated with cell adhesion and metastasis, including CDH3, MCAM, ITGB1, and ITGA4; and genes related to epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), including SNAI1, ZEB2, SNAI2, and TWIST1. Notably, the cell adhesion‑ and EMT‑related genes exhibited similar temporal expression patterns, characterized by high expression during the early phase of differentiation, a subsequent downregulation, and then a second upregulation in the later stages.

To comprehensively investigate metastasis‑associated transcriptional features, all temporally differentially expressed genes were clustered based on their expression dynamics along the pseudotime axis (Fig. 2e). Four distinct clusters were identified. Cluster 1 showed high expression during the early differentiation stage (Stage 0) and included ATF1 and TEAD4, both implicated in tumor progression. Cluster 2 began to be expressed in the early‑to‑mid differentiation stage (Stage 1) and exhibited high expression at the terminal stage (Stage 3); this cluster contained CD276, which is involved in immune evasion, as well as ITGA4 and MCAM, both implicated in melanoma metastasis. Cluster 3 was highly expressed in Stage 1 and included CXCR4, which promotes metastasis, alongside CDH2 and ITGA5, both involved in cell adhesion. Cluster 4 was highly expressed in Stage 2 and contained the immune evasion‑related genes HLA‑E and CD47.

This differentiation trajectory reveals an intriguing pattern. Pseudotime analysis suggests that metastasis occurs in Stage 1, followed by an enhanced immune evasion capacity in Stage 2. This may indicate that melanoma cells that metastasize in Stage 1 have already arrived in the lymph node. Subsequently, in Stage 3, the expression of immune evasion‑related genes in cluster 4 decreased globally, whereas the expression of cluster 2 increased. This shift may imply that after undergoing immune evasion, metastatic melanoma cells in the lymph node establish an immunosuppressive microenvironment and regain metastatic potential.

Gene enrichment analysis results for cluster 2 and cluster 3, indicating that the metastatic program in melanoma involves the coordinated activation of pathways associated with cell adhesion, extracellular matrix remodeling, immune regulation, and pro‑metastatic processes (Fig. 2f–g), further supporting the notion that melanoma cells at distinct differentiation stages execute specific functional programs during metastasis.

Immune infiltration analysis reveals that the ligand–receptor pair NECTIN3‑TIGIT is specifically expressed in the high‑metastasis group

In this study, ssGSEA was performed using the genes from cluster 2 and cluster 3 as input to calculate the metastasis potential of each melanoma cell (Fig. 3a). Consistent with expectations, cells in the high‑score group were enriched in clusters with a higher proportion of CM1Lym cells, whereas cells in the low‑score group were enriched in clusters with a higher proportion of CM1 cells. Subsequently, to investigate the impact of the immune microenvironment on metastasis, CellPhoneDB was used to calculate the cell‑talk strength between T cells and the two groups of melanoma cells. The results showed that the cell‑talk strength between the high‑score group and the immune microenvironment was stronger than that in the low‑score group (Fig. 3b), suggesting that the immune microenvironment may mediate melanoma metastasis. Meanwhile, CellPhoneDB identified ligand–receptor pairs that were significantly highly expressed in cell–cell communication across different cell types (Fig. 3c‑d). For instance, the high‑score group exhibited significantly high expression of immune‑related pairs such as HLA‑E_VSIR, TNF_VSIR, and CCL4L2_VSIR, as well as the EMT‑promoting pair TGFB1_VDR. NECTIN3-TIGIT axis is specifically highly expressed in the high-metastasis group of melanoma and correlated with poor prognosis

Clinical sample validation revealed that both mRNA and protein expression levels of NECTIN3 and TIGIT were significantly higher in lymph node metastatic tumor (LNMT) tissues compared to PT tissues, as confirmed by Western blot and qPCR (Fig. 4c–e). IHC analysis further demonstrated that NECTIN3 and TIGIT proteins were highly expressed in LNMT tissues but lowly expressed in PT tissues (Fig. 4f–g, i). Concurrently, the infiltration of Foxp3+ Tregs was significantly greater in LNMT tissues than in PT tissues (Fig. 4h–i). Cell line analysis showed that NECTIN3 protein expression in the lymph node metastatic melanoma cell line WM266-4 was significantly higher than that in the primary melanoma cell line WM115, consistent with the expression pattern observed in clinical tissue samples (Supplementary Fig. 1). Prognostic analysis of the TCGA melanoma cohort demonstrated that patients with high NECTIN3 or TIGIT expression had significantly shorter overall survival (OS) compared to those with low expression, indicating that their upregulation is closely associated with poor clinical outcomes in melanoma patients (Fig. 4a–b). On this basis, we further utilized the melanoma dataset GSE65904 (comprising 177 metastatic and 33 primary samples) to evaluate the prognostic significance of NECTIN3 and TIGIT in PT and LNMT patients, respectively. The results showed that NECTIN3 held no prognostic value for either PT or LNMT patients; whereas TIGIT, although not prognostically significant for PT patients (p = 0.07), was significantly associated with poor prognosis in LNMT patients (p < 0.001) (Supplementary Fig. 3). Furthermore, multivariable Cox regression analysis was performed (Supplementary Fig. 4a). TIGIT (hazard ratio [HR] = 2.7, p = 0.005) and age (p = 0.004) emerged as statistically significant prognostic factors, whereas NECTIN3 showed an elevated HR of 3.1 (95% CI: 0.48–19.7, p = 0.235) that did not reach statistical significance after adjustment. Additionally, Western blot, qPCR, and IHC analyses were performed to examine the expression of CD155 and its encoding mRNA PVR in PT and LNMT tissues. Although both CD155 and PVR were expressed in these tissues, no significant difference was observed between the two groups, consistent with the cell-cell communication analysis (Supplementary Fig. 4c-g). Further survival analysis indicated that PVR could not serve as a prognostic biomarker for melanoma patients (p = 0.4) (Supplementary Fig. 4b). Taken together, these findings demonstrate that the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis is significantly upregulated in LNMT tissues and is associated with increased Treg infiltration and poor clinical prognosis. Notably, TIGIT exhibits more robust and independent prognostic value specifically in lymph node metastatic melanoma. In contrast, although CD155 and PVR are expressed in both PT and LNMT tissues, they show no significant intergroup difference and have limited prognostic predictive value. NThe NECTIN3-TIGIT axis synergizes with the immunosuppressive microenvironment to induce CD8+ T cell exhaustion phenotype: On day 3 of TLNMC culture, exhaustion features of CD8+ T cells in each tumor module were examined. Cytokine analysis revealed that immunosuppressive factors IL-10 and TGF-β were significantly elevated in LNMTM culture supernatants compared to PTM, while pro-inflammatory cytokines IFN-γ and TNF-α were markedly reduced, confirming the presence of a typical immunosuppressive microenvironment in LNMTM (Fig. 7c–d). In vitro CD8+ T cell co-culture experiments demonstrated that, compared to controls, recombinant NECTIN3 protein treatment significantly increased p-SHIP-1 expression while decreasing TRAF6 expression in CD8+ T cells; these alterations were effectively reversed by the TIGIT inhibitor tiragolumab, suggesting that the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis suppresses CD8+ T cell activation via the SHIP-1/TRAF6 signaling pathway (Fig. 7h–i). Functional validation of NECTIN3-TIGIT and CD155-TIGIT axis blockade showed that, compared to controls, ShNECTIN3, ShPVR, the combination treatment group, and tiragolumab monotherapy group all significantly reduced PD-1 and TOX expression in CD8+ T cells within the LNMTM model. The NECTIN3-TIGIT and CD155-TIGIT axes exhibited partial synergistic or additive effects—inhibition of either ligand alone only partially alleviated CD8+ T cell exhaustion, with NECTIN3 inhibition outperforming PVR inhibition, likely because the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis exerts a stronger interaction between highly metastatic tumor cells and T cells, consistent with the cell-cell communication analysis. Simultaneous inhibition of both NECTIN3 and CD155 further reduced PD-1 and TOX expression (Fig. 7e–f), achieving effects comparable to tiragolumab monotherapy. Cytokine analysis demonstrated that blockade of both axes significantly increased TNF-α and IFN-γ secretion by CD8+ T cells in the LNMTM model, with the anti-PD-1 plus tiragolumab combination group showing significantly higher pro-inflammatory cytokine levels than monotherapy or ShNECTIN3 groups, indicating effective restoration of CD8+ T cell pro-inflammatory function (Fig. 7j). To investigate the role of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis in melanoma lymph node metastasis, in vivo lymphatic colonization assays were performed using WM266-4 (GFP-labeled) and WM115 (mCherry-labeled) cell lines. Under different conditions, the ratio of GFP-positive (WM266-4) to mCherry-positive (WM115) cells in the mid-lymphatic region was assessed on day 3 (Fig. 8j–k). In the control group (co-injection of WM266-4-shNC-GFP and WM115-shNC-mCherry), both GFP-positive and mCherry-positive cells were clearly detectable within the lymphatic lumen, with a greater number of GFP-positive cells. Upon NECTIN3 knockdown in WM266-4 cells (WM266-4-shNECTIN3-GFP), the number of GFP-positive cells within the lymphatics was significantly reduced, while mCherry-positive cell numbers remained largely unchanged. Conversely, NECTIN3 overexpression in WM115 cells (WM115–OENECTIN3-mCherry) led to increased numbers of both mCherry-positive and GFP-positive cells in the lymphatics. In the WM266-4-shNC-GFP and WM115-shNC-mCherry co-injection model, treatment with the TIGIT-blocking antibody resulted in a marked reduction in both GFP-positive and mCherry-positive cells.Collectively, these findings demonstrate that disruption of the NECTIN3‑TIGIT axis—either by NECTIN3 knockdown in donor tumor cells or by TIGIT blockade—impairs the capacity of melanoma cells to colonize lymphatic vessels, suggesting that this interaction promotes lymph node metastasis in melanoma.

The NECTIN3‑TIGIT axis orchestrates an immunosuppressive microenvironment to promote melanoma lymph node metastasis

This study reveals the central role of the NECTIN3‑TIGIT signaling axis in melanoma lymph node metastasis. In metastatic lymph node tissues, the expression levels of NECTIN3 and TIGIT were significantly upregulated and closely correlated with poor patient prognosis. Through direct spatial ligand–receptor interactions, this axis activates the SHIP-1/TRAF6 signaling pathway in CD8+ T cells, inducing an exhausted phenotype and impairing anti‑tumor immune responses. Simultaneously, in Treg cells, it suppresses the AKT/mTORC1 signaling pathway, thereby enhancing Treg cell activation, proliferation, and immunosuppressive function, which collectively fosters the formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment. Intervening via NECTIN3 knockdown or TIGIT blockade effectively reversed CD8+ T cell exhaustion, inhibited Treg cell function, and significantly reduced tumor cell colonization within lymphatic vessels. Moreover, combination with anti‑PD1 therapy exhibited synergistic effects, providing a novel combined immunotherapy strategy targeting the NECTIN3‑TIGIT axis for melanoma lymph node metastasis (Fig. 9). Discussion

Melanoma is a highly malignant skin tumor, and lymph node metastasis is a critical node in its disease progression and a core factor leading to treatment failure and poor prognosis in patients [18–20]. Although immune checkpoint blockade therapy targeting the tumor immune microenvironment has significantly improved the treatment pattern of melanoma, the unique immunosuppressive microenvironment in lymph node metastatic foci still becomes a major bottleneck in clinical treatment [21–23]. Using single-cell RNA sequencing, clinical sample validation and a self-constructed tumor lymph node metastasis-on-a-chip (TLNMC), this study for revealed that the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis is a key immune regulatory pathway regulating melanoma lymph node metastasis. This axis remodels the tumor immunosuppressive microenvironment by enhancing Treg cell immunosuppressive function and inducing profound CD8+ T cell exhaustion, ultimately promoting melanoma lymph node metastasis, providing a novel molecular target and experimental evidence for the targeted therapy of melanoma lymph node metastasis.

In this study, single-cell sequencing of melanoma primary and lymph node metastatic foci first clarified the distribution characteristics of cell subsets in metastatic foci tissues. Pseudotime analysis further screened core pathways related to melanoma lymph node metastasis such as immune escape, EMT and cell migration, which was consistent with the molecular characteristics of melanoma metastasis reported in previous studies. Immune infiltration and ligand-receptor pair analysis found that the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis was specifically highly expressed in the high-metastasis group of melanoma, with Treg cell infiltration level positively correlated with tumor metastatic potential and a significantly higher proportion of exhausted CD8+ T cells in metastatic foci, suggesting that this axis may be involved in melanoma lymph node metastasis by regulating the functions of Treg cells and CD8+ T cells. Clinical sample validation further confirmed that the expression of NECTIN3 and TIGIT was significantly upregulated in melanoma lymph node metastatic foci, and their high expression was closely associated with poor patient prognosis. Meanwhile, the expression level of NECTIN3 in the lymph node metastatic melanoma cell line WM266-4 was significantly higher than that in the primary cell line WM115, suggesting that the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis can serve as a potential prognostic marker and therapeutic target for melanoma lymph node metastasis. This is consistent with the research results that NECTIN3 promotes metastasis in ovarian cancer, lung cancer and other tumors, and further expands the role of TIGIT as an immunosuppressive receptor in tumor metastasis.

Traditional in vitro cell experiments and animal models are difficult to accurately mimic the in vivo microenvironment of melanoma lymph node metastasis, while organ-on-a-chip technology enables the co-culture of tumor cells, immune cells and vascular/lymphatic microenvironments, which is more similar to the in vivo physiological state [24, 25]. The TLNMC successfully constructed in this study includes vascular channel, lymphatic channel, primary tumor module and lymph node metastatic tumor module. The characterization of cell markers in each module confirmed the successful construction of the chip, which could mimic the lymphatic migration process of melanoma cells. Using this chip, we found an immunosuppressive microenvironment with marked Treg cell infiltration and profound CD8+ T cell exhaustion in the lymph node metastatic tumor module, with significantly increased concentrations of immunosuppressive factors IL-10 and TGF-β and significantly decreased concentrations of pro-inflammatory factors IFN-γ and TNF-α. This result is consistent with the clinical immune characteristics of melanoma lymph node metastatic foci and also provides an ideal experimental platform for subsequent exploration of the regulatory mechanism of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis.

This study clarified the regulatory effect and molecular mechanism of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis on CD8+ T cell function, and found that this axis can form a ligand-receptor pair through direct binding, regulate the SHIP-1/TRAF6 signaling axis to inhibit CD8+ T cell activation, and ultimately induce their profound exhaustion. Blockade of this axis can significantly reduce the expression levels of CD8+ T cell exhaustion markers PD1 and TOX, while the combined treatment of anti-PD1 and TIGIT inhibitor can completely reverse PD1 expression and significantly restore the pro-inflammatory function of CD8+ T cells, suggesting that the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis and PD-1 pathway have a synergistic effect in regulating CD8+ T cell exhaustion, and combined blockade of both can be the optimal strategy to reverse CD8+ T cell exhaustion in melanoma lymph node metastatic foci. This result is consistent with the research results that TIGIT inhibitors reverse CD8+ T cell exhaustion in other tumors, and also provides a new idea for the development of combined immunotherapy regimens for melanoma.

Meanwhile, this study confirmed that the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis can promote Treg cell activation and enhance their immunosuppressive function by inhibiting the AKT/mTORC1 pathway. The expression levels of Foxp3 and TIGIT in Treg cells of the lymph node metastatic tumor module were significantly increased. Blockade of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis could significantly reduce Foxp3 expression and inhibit Treg cells from secreting immunosuppressive factors such as IL-10 and TGF-β. Moreover, mTOR inhibitor could further enhance the activation effect of NECTIN3 on Treg cells, suggesting that the AKT/mTORC1 pathway is a key downstream pathway for the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis to regulate Treg cell function. Previous studies have confirmed that the mTORC1 pathway is a core pathway regulating Treg cell activation and function. This study further clarified the upstream regulatory role of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis in this pathway, improving the molecular mechanism of abnormal Treg cell activation in melanoma lymph node metastasis.

This study confirmed by TLNMC that blockade of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis can significantly inhibit the lymphatic migration and lymph node metastasis of melanoma cells, while overexpression of NECTIN3 can promote this process, directly confirming that this axis is a key regulatory pathway promoting melanoma lymph node metastasis. Combined with the above mechanism research results, the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis can remodel the immunosuppressive microenvironment of melanoma lymph node metastatic foci through the dual effects of “enhancing Treg cell immunosuppressive function + inducing profound CD8+ T cell exhaustion”, ultimately providing favorable conditions for the lymphatic migration and lymph node metastasis of tumor cells.

Notably, TIGIT-mediated immunosuppression is not entirely dependent on a single ligand. Previous studies have established that CD155 is one of the primary and highest-affinity ligands for TIGIT; CD155 expressed on tumor cells or antigen-presenting cells can suppress effector T cell function and promote immune escape through TIGIT binding [14, 26, 27], While this study focused on the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis, the CD155-TIGIT axis was also incorporated into the analysis. The results demonstrated that inhibition of either NECTIN3 or CD155 alone partially alleviated CD8+ T cell exhaustion and reduced Treg cell activation. However, simultaneous blockade of both NECTIN3 and CD155 led to more pronounced reductions in immunosuppression-associated markers, including PD-1, TOX, FOXP3, and Ki67, along with further restoration of effector cytokines such as TNF-α and IFN-γ. These findings indicate that the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis makes a distinct functional contribution but does not operate independently of the CD155-TIGIT axis. Rather, the CD155-TIGIT and NECTIN3-TIGIT axes likely maintain the immunosuppressive state in the lymph node metastatic melanoma microenvironment in a synergistic or additive manner. Therefore, while selective blockade of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis can partially restore anti-tumor immunity, combined targeting of CD155-TIGIT-related signaling may yield a more robust immune remodeling effect. This finding further suggests that TIGIT-targeted therapeutic strategies may benefit from considering the contributions of multiple ligand sources and their functional complementarity to improve treatment response.

This study still has certain limitations. In vivo animal experiments were not conducted to verify the regulatory effect of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis. In the future, animal models of melanoma lymph node metastasis can be constructed to further verify the in vivo function of this axis and the therapeutic effect of targeted blockade. Meanwhile, this study only explored the regulatory effect of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis on Treg cells and CD8+ T cells, and its influence on other immune cells in the tumor microenvironment such as DC cells and NK cells still needs further investigation. The survival analyses in this study were based on publicly available database-derived cohorts, which lack detailed clinical information on patients’ performance status, such as ECOG score. Therefore, the potential influence of patients’ general physical condition on prognosis could not be fully adjusted for, which may limit the clinical interpretation of the prognostic value of NECTIN3 and TIGIT. In addition, large-sample clinical studies can be carried out in the future to further verify the clinical value of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis as a prognostic marker for melanoma lymph node metastasis, and to explore the clinical therapeutic effect of TIGIT inhibitor combined with anti-PD1 therapy in patients with melanoma lymph node metastasis.

In summary, this study for the first time revealed the regulatory role and molecular mechanism of the NECTIN3-TIGIT axis in melanoma lymph node metastasis, confirming that this axis promotes Treg cell activation by inhibiting the AKT/mTORC1 pathway and induces CD8+ T cell exhaustion by regulating the SHIP-1/TRAF6 signaling axis, ultimately promoting melanoma lymph node metastasis. Moreover, combined blockade of the NECTIN3-TIGIT and PD-1 pathways can significantly reverse the immunosuppressive microenvironment and inhibit melanoma lymph node metastasis. This study provides a novel molecular target for the immunotherapy of melanoma lymph node metastasis and lays an experimental foundation for the development of new combined immunotherapy regimens.

Retracted Publications Related to Statistics: A Scopus-Based Bibliometric Analysis
Authors: Aizhan Oralbek, Marlen Yessirkepov, Olena Zimba, Ainur B Qumar, Burhan Fatih Kocyigit
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStatistics is a field that provides the methodological basis for modern scientific research, including data collection, analysis, and interpretation. The retraction procedure involves the official removal of a work from the literature due to substantial scientific concerns. Analysis of retracted publications is critical for identifying ethical violations and structural flaws in scientific publishing.MethodsThis study performed a bibliometric and descriptive analysis of publications labeled as “retracted” in the Scopus database, retrieved via a search utilizing the term “statistics” on August 18, 2025. The analysis of documents was conducted based on year, country, journal, keyword, and reasons for retraction. Collaboration networks of countries and authors were displayed with VOSviewer software, and reasons for retraction were confirmed using the Retraction Watch Database. Temporal trends were assessed by linear regression analysis.ResultsA total of 680 retracted papers were examined. Retractions occurred between 2006 and 2025. A statistically significant upward trend was found in the number of retracted articles over time (P = 0.030). The most retracted publications came from China (n = 426), followed by India (n = 67), and the United States (n = 49). The Journal of Healthcare Engineering (n = 35) and Journal of Sensors (n = 25) stood out among the journals with the most retracted publications. The most common retraction reasons were investigation by journal/publisher (n = 297), unreliable results and/or conclusions (n = 189), and concerns/issues about referencing/attributions (n = 153).ConclusionThe number of retracted publications tagged with the keyword “statistics” has increased significantly over the years. This increase can be attributed to strengthening scientific oversight mechanisms and increased awareness of publication ethics. Increasing the involvement of statistical experts in the peer-review process, improving the statistical literacy of authors, and developing training and mentoring mechanisms to prevent ethical violations are crucial.Keywords: Statistics, Statistics as Topic, Statistical Studies, Bibliometrics, Retraction of Publication as Topic, Ethics

INTRODUCTION

Statistics forms the methodological foundation of modern scientific research, incorporating a diverse array of analytical techniques crucial to obtaining meaningful research outcomes.1 The Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) taxonomy identifies statistics as an extensive discipline encompassing data collecting, tabulation, analysis, and interpretation methodologies aimed at deriving insights about populations from samples.2 In this context, specialized statistical fields including biostatistics, epidemiological methodologies, and statistical models serve unique yet interrelated functions in the progression of multidisciplinary scientific knowledge.3,4

The integrity of scientific results relies on the appropriate utilization of statistical techniques. Errors in biostatistics and data processing can compromise the validity and reliability of study results, leading to incorrect conclusions and misguided management approaches. These statistical underpinnings can have a direct impact on governmental policies, clinical guidelines, and subsequent research.5,6

Retraction in academic publishing refers to the formal removal of a published work from the literature due to methodological flaws, data manipulation, ethical breaches, improper citations, or other serious scientific issues.7 This process is a crucial mechanism for safeguarding the precision of scientific knowledge and upholding academic integrity. Retractions enhance the credibility of research findings and foster transparency in scholarly communication by obstructing the dissemination of erroneous or misleading content in the academic environment.8,9 Furthermore, the retraction process allows future investigations to be grounded on robust foundations. The retraction procedure not only mitigates the effects of flawed publications but also functions as a feedback mechanism to enhance research ethics and publishing standards.10,11

Examining retracted publications is crucial for highlighting vulnerabilities in the scientific publication process and concerns related to publishing ethics. In this perspective, analyzing retracted publications across several disciplines serves as a crucial data source for enhancing quality assurance and transparency procedures within the research ecosystem.12

This study systematically examined retracted papers acquired from a thorough search of the Scopus database using the term “statistics.” The study aims to investigate trends in the publication process by disclosing the distribution of retracted papers by year, country, journal, keyword, and retraction reasons, as well as providing a comprehensive view of the research environment. This study also aims to conduct a comprehensive bibliometric and ethical analysis of retracted papers, focusing on temporal trends, country and journal distributions, collaborative networks, and specific reasons for retraction. It aims to offer significant insights into the structural and ethical difficulties while emphasizing opportunities for enhancing research quality and integrity.

METHODS

The current research employs a descriptive and bibliometric analytic framework. The Scopus database served as the data source. Searches were performed on August 18, 2025, and only included retracted papers in the analysis.

Search setting

The keyword “statistics” was employed in the search performed in the Scopus database. The investigation was performed by examining the title, abstract, and/or keyword sections (TITLE-ABS-KEY). No timeframe constraints were imposed. The research examined only items published in English. The document type in the Scopus database was set as “retracted.”

Data collection

The list of retracted papers acquired using the supplied search criteria was recorded. The earliest retracted paper was published in 2005, and the most recent in 2024, with retraction dates spanning from 2006 to 2025. This study utilized VOSviewer software (version 1.6.20) to visualize collaboration networks among countries and authors. The software consolidates countries, authors, journals, and keywords that satisfy a specific threshold of links onto network maps, illustrating the strength of these linkages through varying line thicknesses. Thicker lines indicate stronger connections, while elements with similar characteristics are clustered and presented in different colors. This technique provides a more understandable representation of academic partnership dynamics and the structure of the publication network.13

Retraction reasons were identified by conducting a systematic search of the Retraction Watch Database with each paper’s title and DOI number. Database records were evaluated using official statements about retractions provided by publishers and journal editors. If an article had multiple retraction reasons, they were entered as separate data items and handled as independent categories in the analysis. This strategy allowed for more precise documentation of retraction procedures and a more thorough examination of the reasons.14

Analyses and data presentation

Data were summarized and reported as number and percentage (%). Graphs and visual representations were generated using Microsoft Excel, while temporal trends in publication numbers were evaluated through linear regression analysis performed with the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS), version 20.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

DISCUSSION

The current study analyzed 680 retracted papers featuring the keyword “statistics” in the Scopus database. Retractions occurred from 2006 to 2025, with a significant rising trend over time, peaking in 2023 with 149 articles (P = 0.030). China (n = 426) had the highest number of retracted publications, followed by India (n = 67) and the United States (n = 49). The Journal of Healthcare Engineering (n = 35) and Journal of Sensors (n = 25) were among the journals with the highest number of retracted articles. Journal or publisher investigations, unreliable results and/or conclusions, citation issues, and concerns about the peer-review process were the most common reasons for retraction, with 41.6% of the publications being open access.

The retracted papers evaluated in this study were published between 2005 and 2024, with retractions spanning 2006 to 2025. The results show a statistically significant rising trend in the number of retractions over time. The year 2023, in particular, is the peak year for retractions, with 149 articles. This upward trend is attributable to enhanced transparency in scientific publishing, the identification of ethical violations, and strengthened peer-review processes. Recent advancements in database architecture, more rigorous enforcement of publishing ethics norms, and expedited identification of ethical concerns, such as plagiarism and fraudulent peer review, are pivotal factors contributing to the rise in retractions. Furthermore, the overall increase in academic output and the rapid rise in the number of publications may also be reflected in the trends.15,16

When the distribution of retracted papers by country was examined, China stood out by far as the country with the highest number of retracted publications, followed by India, the United States, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan. The data suggest that retractions are more common in countries with high academic output and rapidly growing publication numbers. This indicates that not only are ethical breaches more prevalent, but also that scientific supervision procedures are more robust in these countries, making potential issues easier to identify.17,18 Furthermore, in countries with considerable academic competitiveness, the pressure to publish rapidly can lead to an increase in the quantity of publications, while also allowing for scientific flaws and ethical concerns.19

The Journal of Healthcare Engineering, Journal of Sensors, Arabian Journal of Geosciences, Journal of Intelligent and Fuzzy Systems, and PLOS ONE emerged as the sources with the most retracted papers. Most of these sources have a large international readership and publish multidisciplinary research. This suggests that journals with a high number of articles are likely to experience a high number of retractions naturally.

Another notable finding was that 41.6% of retracted papers were disseminated in open-access journals. The rise of open-access publication enhances the accessibility of scientific knowledge to a broader audience and facilitates the expedited identification of any errors and ethical breaches. In this setting, open-access publication may enhance transparency and facilitate the retraction process.20 The recent increase in the number of predatory journals and those noted for their expedited publication procedures may have contributed to higher retraction rates, due to methodological flaws and inadequate peer-review processes. This scenario may be attributed to the misuse of open access publication; although reputable open access journals enhance transparency, commercial, predatory journals exacerbate ethical issues in scientific publishing.21,22

The outcomes show that retracted publications were most concentrated in the fields of Engineering, Computer Science, and Medicine. This distribution reflects the high volume of publications and the diversity of multidisciplinary studies in these fields. Rapid technological advancements and rising publication volumes, particularly in engineering and computer science, may facilitate the identification of more methodological errors or ethical violations.23 The growing number of retractions in medical and biology-related publications may result from the heightened scrutiny these disciplines encounter concerning patient safety and ethical accountability.

The leading retraction reasons were investigation by journal/publisher, unreliable results and/or conclusions, concerns/issues about referencing/attributions, investigation by third party, and concerns/issues with peer review. In addition, computer-aided or artificial intelligence-generated content, fake peer review, article production activities known as “paper mills,” author-based policy violations, and editorial irregularities were also important factors.

These data highlight that ethical challenges in scientific publication are diverse, with errors by both authors and publishers playing a critical part. The fact that editorial and publisher investigations are the most prevalent reasons suggests that journals’ ethical review procedures are improving, and possible concerns are being recognized more consistently. The growing usage of paper mills and artificial intelligence-powered content development signals the onset of new and challenging issues in publishing.24,25

Irregularities and fraudulent peer-review practices are becoming more widespread due to the demand for swift publishing and the influence of commercial interests. This circumstance highlights the vital need to maintain the independence and integrity of the peer-review process within the scientific community. Furthermore, the frequent occurrence of citation issues highlights not only ethical shortcomings but also the inadequacy of quality control mechanisms in the production of scientific knowledge.26

This study highlights the need for enhanced control in scientific publication, as the increasing number of retractions primarily stems from unreliable results, inadequate peer review, and emerging challenges, including artificial intelligence-generated material. For journal editors handling statistics-related manuscripts, key action points include recruiting reviewers with robust statistical proficiency, conducting specific statistical checks for complex analyses, revising editorial policies to address emerging challenges, and establishing education or mentorship programs for authors and reviewers. These approaches can enhance methodological rigor, mitigate ethical violations, and ultimately contribute to minimizing future retractions.27

The study has several limitations. First, this investigation was conducted using only the Scopus database; therefore, retracted papers from other databases were excluded. This may limit the depth of the data and result in an incomplete picture. The study focused solely on English-published papers, ignoring those in other languages. This approach may result in an underrepresentation of global retraction dynamics based on language differences. Although the bibliometric methods provide a detailed view of the distribution and trends of retracted publications, qualitative assessments or case-based in-depth reviews were beyond the scope of this study. This study was not designed to assess particular statistical errors in retracted publications. Instead, it provided a bibliometric overview of retractions related to the keyword ‘statistics.’ Further research may involve in-depth assessments of statistical flaws within retracted publications, or surveys investigating how to enhance statistical data editing procedures.

This study conducted a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of retracted publications in the Scopus database using the term “statistics” to identify retraction trends, reasons, and distribution by subject. The data revealed a statistically significant increase in the number of retractions over time, may be partly explained by strengthened oversight mechanisms and greater awareness of publication ethics, but also by factors such as rising publication pressure and the expansion of predatory journals. The findings offer valuable insights for journal editors and researchers. Increasing the engagement of reviewers proficient in statistical methods throughout peer-review procedures may enhance publication quality. Moreover, enhancing authors’ statistical skills can reduce the number of retractions. Targeted training courses, mentorship, and collaboration with experienced statisticians can assist authors in developing more robust research and avoiding methodological errors. These approaches are crucial for enhancing research integrity and promoting a more reliable scientific publication landscape.

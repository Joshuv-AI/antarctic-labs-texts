The Role of Polyphenols in Respiratory and Gut Health: From the Perspective of Gut‐Lung Axis
Authors: Jian Kang, YongJun Du, Yanmei Hou, Liangliang Hao, Zhen Chen, Haoyang Xie, Chuantao Zhang, Lei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe gut‐lung axis constitutes a dynamic, multidirectional communication platform between the gastrointestinal and respiratory tracts. Its complexity arises from the integrated crosstalk among the microbiota, immune system, and redox homeostasis, collectively influencing disease susceptibility and progression. The gut microbiota directly impacts pulmonary inflammation through immune modulation and oxidative stress regulation. Conversely, disturbances in lung homeostasis, including shifts in the local microbiota, can in turn reshape gut microbial communities, establishing a bidirectional feedback loop. Polyphenols, a class of bioactive compounds abundant in plant‐based foods, have emerged as key modulators of this bidirectional network. The primary objective of this review is to systematically synthesize current knowledge on the mechanisms by which polyphenols orchestrate gut‐lung axis. We critically examine how they modulate gut and lung microbiota, redox homeostasis, and immune responses, thereby impacting the progression of respiratory and gastrointestinal diseases. This review aims to highlights the translational significance of polyphenols as dietary components and therapeutic candidates, while identifying key knowledge gaps, such as dose optimization and combination strategies, to guide future research aimed at harnessing the gut‐lung axis for disease prevention and treatment.Keywords: gut microbiota, gut‐lung axis, lung microbiota, oxidative stress, polyphenols

Introduction

The microbial diversity of the human upper and lower respiratory tract is basically the same and has high homology (Dickson et al. 2015). Although the intestinal tract and respiratory tract are separate organs with different functions and environments, they have the same embryonic origin and, consequently, have structural similarities in health and disease (Budden et al. 2017). Emerging evidence highlights that shared immunological and microbial pathways mediate a bidirectional interplay between pulmonary and gastrointestinal health (Willis et al. 2020). During the pathogenesis of certain chronic pulmonary diseases such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD, progressive irreversible airflow limitation), asthma, and pulmonary fibrosis, gastrointestinal manifestations frequently manifest, encompassing symptoms like diarrhea and constipation (Keely and Hansbro 2014; Freuer et al. 2022). Similarly, some pulmonary emergencies, such as acute lung injury (ALI, an inflammatory lung disorder) and acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS, a severe form of respiratory failure), are frequently associated with dysbiosis in the gut microbiota (Zhang, Lu, et al. 2024). The coexistence of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD, chronic immune‐mediated gut inflammation) and irritable bowel syndrome (IBS, functional abdominal disorder with bowel habit changes) has been found to be associated with an elevated risk of respiratory diseases, presenting a notable observation (Keely et al. 2012; Lai et al. 2020). Additionally, dysbiosis of the gut and lung microbiota can be observed in both COPD and intestinal diseases, thereby potentially disrupting the mucosal environment, including the integrity of the intestinal barrier and immune system, consequently exerting detrimental effects on both gastrointestinal and pulmonary health (Wang et al. 2023). This observation suggests the existence of a crucial interplay between the gastrointestinal tract and the respiratory system, known as the gut‐lung axis. Furthermore, it should be noted that gut and lung microbiota play a crucial role in modulating this axis and maintaining homeostasis within both the gut and lungs.

With the recent advancements in gut microbiota research, an increasing body of evidence demonstrates the indispensable role of gut microbiota in modulating physiological activity (Woelfel et al. 2024; De Vos et al. 2024; Dong et al. 2025). The dysbiosis of gut microbiota not only gives rise to gastrointestinal symptoms, but also elicits a spectrum of diseases, including cancer, diabetes, obesity, atherosclerosis, and other related conditions (Mansour et al. 2024; Kedmi and Littman 2024; Li, Peng, et al. 2023). In general, the orchestration of pathophysiological activities by a healthy gut microbiota is primarily attributed to its diverse metabolic capacity and the wide array of metabolites it produces (Guan et al. 2022). Moreover, the lung microbiota potentially contributes to host systemic health through its regulatory effects on gut microbiota composition (Budden et al. 2017). In the context of the gut‐lung axis, interactions among gut and lung microbiota (including bacterial and fungal communities) primarily occur through shared mediators, including soluble microbial components and metabolites such as: lipopolysaccharide (LPS), a pro‐inflammatory endotoxin derived from Gram‐negative bacterial cell walls; short‐chain fatty acids (SCFAs), microbial metabolites produced by gut microbiota through dietary fiber fermentation, with immunomodulatory functions; deaminated tyrosine (DAT), a tyrosine‐derived metabolite generated via microbial deamination, associated with gut dysbiosis; and indole derivatives, microbially processed tryptophan metabolites with signaling functions (Wypych et al. 2019; Zheng, Wang, et al. 2024). A recent study has shown that the communication mechanism between the gut‐lung axis encompasses immune cell migration from the gut to the respiratory tract via circulation, which is contingent upon the presence of gut microbiota (Huang et al. 2018; Barcik et al. 2020). Therefore, exploiting dietary modification to affect gut homeostasis would also be an effective intervention strategy (Figure 1).

Background of gut‐lung axis in the homeostasis between the gut and respiratory tract. Respiratory and intestinal diseases, induced by various pathogenic factors, can influence each other through the gut‐lung axis. Respiratory damage triggers oxidative stress, immune cell infiltration, and the release of pro‐inflammatory cytokines, which subsequently lead to dysbiosis, ROS accumulation, and activation of immune cells in the gut through systemic circulation and immune pathways, thereby promoting gastrointestinal (GI) inflammation. Similarly, intestinal damage can affect respiratory health via the same interconnected mechanisms.

The bioactivities of undigested dietary constituents, particularly polyphenols, can be modulated through interactions with the gut microbiota (Feng et al. 2020). Polyphenols are a class of phytochemicals characterized by multiple phenolic rings with hydroxyl groups (Del Rio et al. 2013). Structurally, they range from simple monomers (e.g., flavonoids, phenolic acids) to complex polymers (e.g., tannins). Their redox properties enable potent antioxidant activity through free radical scavenging and metal chelation (Li et al. 2022). Emerging evidence indicates that polyphenols modulate gut and lung microbiota composition, thereby exerting potential prebiotic effects (Li, Peng, et al. 2023; Alghetaa et al. 2021). As widely recognized, polyphenols exhibit a diverse range of biological activities including anti‐inflammatory, antioxidant, antiviral, and anti‐aging properties (Zhang et al. 2021; Singh et al. 2019), which are linked to the metabolites produced through interactions with gut microbiota. For example, resveratrol, a well‐known polyphenolic compound, is able to modulate the gut microbiota to alter the abundance of SCFAs, thereby affecting its anti‐inflammatory and antioxidant properties (He et al. 2022; Ma et al. 2020). SCFAs also regulate lung microbial homeostasis, thereby indirectly influencing the progression of respiratory diseases (Ashique et al. 2022). Obviously, polyphenols can modulate the gut microbiota and thereby impact the progression and prognosis of diseases such as COPD and IBD through their influence on the gut‐lung axis (Wu, Huang, et al. 2021; Ding et al. 2023). Despite growing interest, several key knowledge gaps remain, particularly concerning how polyphenols directly or indirectly modulate both lung and gut microbiota, which biological targets can guide clinical application, and how to optimally integrate them with existing therapies. Therefore, the primary objective of this review is to provide a comprehensive and critical synthesis of current evidence on the mechanisms through which polyphenols modulate the gut‐lung axis. We will elucidate their roles in regulating gut and lung microbiota, oxidative stress, and immune responses, and evaluate their therapeutic potential in major respiratory and gastrointestinal diseases. Furthermore, this review aims to identify persistent challenges and future research directions to advance the rational application of polyphenols for preventing and managing interconnected gut‐lung pathologies.

To ensure a focused and mechanistic synthesis, the diseases discussed in this review (e.g., IBD, COPD, asthma, CRC, IPF) were selected based on their high clinical prevalence and the strength of evidence demonstrating their involvement in gut‐lung axis crosstalk, particularly where microbiota, immune, and metabolic data are robust. The polyphenols discussed (e.g., curcumin, resveratrol, EGCG, quercetin) were selected based on their well‐characterized roles in both gut and lung health, supported by robust mechanistic evidence linking them to core pathways like microbiota modulation and immunoregulation. Moreover, literature was primarily sourced from PubMed, Google Scholar, and Web of Science for the period 2010–2026, prioritizing key basic research with mechanistic depth and preclinical studies that inform translational potential. Paper selection criteria involved English grammar, publication date, high citations, and broad applicability; exclusion criteria included low credibility, non‐English publications, and those full‐text inaccessible ones.

Role of Polyphenols in Gut Health

Polyphenols have been widely acknowledged for their multifaceted roles in promoting human health, including anti‐inflammatory, antioxidative, anti‐aging, immunomodulatory, and microbiota regulatory effects (Wang, Yang, et al. 2024; Dutta et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2023). Particularly noteworthy is their potential in the prevention of various chronic diseases such as hypertension, COPD, chronic gastritis, chronic constipation, among others (Fu et al. 2022; Guo, Wan, et al. 2024; Han, Wu, et al. 2023). For example, a randomized placebo‐controlled trial (RCT) shows that prune juice containing polyphenol ameliorates subjective complaints and hard feces while normalizing stool in chronic constipation, and there were no adverse events or laboratory abnormalities of liver or renal function after juice intake (Koyama et al. 2022). Recent studies have indicated that the beneficial effects of polyphenols are also associated with their interaction with major cellular signaling pathways and gene regulation mechanisms (Gong et al. 2024; Zhang, Liu, et al. 2024). In fact, natural polyphenols, especially dietary polyphenols, are ingested through food intake and about 5%–10% of the dietary polyphenols can be directly absorbed in the small intestine, while most of them (about 90%–95%) arrive in the colon in their intact form and undergo secondary fermentation with gut microbiota, thereby exerting their biological functions (Williamson 2025). Therefore, polyphenols will play a pioneering role in maintaining gut health and alleviating the progression of intestinal diseases. In this section, some common intestinal diseases such as IBS and IBD will be reviewed to elucidate how polyphenols maintain gut health by interacting with the gut microbiota (Figure 2). Polyphenol‐mediated modulation of gut homeostasis in intestinal disorders. For inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), polyphenols (e.g., quercetin, resveratrol) restore macrophage balance (M2/M1) and repair the intestinal barrier. In irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), they modulate the gut microbiota‐serotonin (5‐HT) pathway to reduce visceral hypersensitivity. For constipation, they increase beneficial microbiota and activate the PI3K/AKT and Nrf2 pathways to enhance motility. In colorectal cancer (CRC), polyphenols (e.g., EGCG, resveratrol) activate anti‐tumor pathways (Nrf2, SIRT1) and maintain a stable gut microbial structure to inhibit carcinogenesis.

Inflammatory Bowel Disease

IBD is a chronic condition characterized by inflammation primarily in the intestinal tract, encompassing Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC). Both CD and UC share the common feature of intestinal mucosal inflammation, which results from an abnormal interaction between the immune system and gut microbiota in individuals with genetic susceptibility (Li, Peng, et al. 2023). The imbalance between the immune system and gut microbiota exacerbates oxidative stress, leading to the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which subsequently promotes the pathological progression of IBD (Li et al. 2020). Evidently, modulating homeostasis between the immune system and gut microbiota by regulating oxidative stress is a crucial strategy for inhibiting the progression of IBD. The antioxidant properties of natural polyphenols, in turn, are expected to positively influence gut health. For example, quercetin, a classical antioxidant, has been identified as providing new value to ameliorate UC by restoring the balance of M2/M1 macrophage and repairing the intestinal barrier via downregulating the cGAS‐STING pathway (Gao et al. 2024). Similarly, a study reported that dietary quercetin could alleviate DSS‐induced colitis in a mouse model by modulating the proinflammatory activity of macrophages through a heme oxygenase‐1 (HO‐1) dependent pathway, while also preserving gut microbial diversity and maintaining commensal microbe homeostasis (Ju et al. 2018). Moreover, numerous studies have demonstrated that polyphenols, such as resveratrol, epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), and curcumin, can maintain gut microbiota diversity and modulate immune cells, thereby alleviating oxidative stress‐mediated intestinal inflammation, repairing the intestinal barrier, and promoting gut health (Che et al. 2024; Zhang, Zhang, Liu, et al. 2023; Xiao et al. 2022).

Irritable Bowel Syndrome

IBS is a consortium of symptoms, including abdominal pain and alterations in the pattern of defecation, and is at high risk of co‐morbid depression and anxiety, which reduces patients' quality of life and increases the burden of health care costs (Goodoory and Ford 2023; Hou et al. 2022). Although the pathophysiological mechanisms of IBS remain incompletely characterized, current consensus guidelines (e.g., Rome IV criteria) recognize dysregulation of the gut‐brain axis, gut microbiota alterations, aberrant immune activation, and psychological stressors as established contributors to IBS pathogenesis (Dothel et al. 2023; Napolitano et al. 2023; Gu et al. 2023). Predictably, clinicians can develop appropriate treatment strategies for IBS patients by analyzing their symptoms (including diarrhea‐predominance, abdominal pain, constipation‐predominance, and psychological anxiety) and considering their treatment preferences. Some dietary and herbal therapies have been particularly favored by certain patient groups (Roudsari et al. 2019; Ling et al. 2022). Interestingly, polyphenols derived from herbal sources have been shown to modulate the 5‐hydroxytryptamine (5‐HT) pathway by influencing the gut microbiota, thereby alleviating intestinal hypersensitivity (Zhang, Dang, et al. 2024). For example, in an IBS rat model, both quercetin and resveratrol have been found to influence the biosynthesis of the gut microbiota metabolite 5‐HT, resulting in a downregulation of 5‐HT expression, which in turn ameliorates intestinal motility abnormalities and depressive symptoms (Qin et al. 2019; Yu et al. 2019). Furthermore, a randomized controlled trial (RCT) demonstrated that patients treated with polyphenols, such as ellagic acid (EA), exhibited significant reductions in malondialdehyde (MDA), C‐reactive protein, and IL‐6 levels (Mirzaie et al. 2022). These findings suggest that polyphenol therapy may contribute to gut health by modulating oxidative stress and inflammatory markers, ultimately enhancing the quality of life for IBS patients (Yu et al. 2019).

Constipation

Constipation affects 15%–20% of the global population, representing a significant healthcare burden. It is a multifactorial disorder characterized by infrequent bowel movements, excessive straining, a sensation of incomplete evacuation, and hard stool consistency (Sharma et al. 2021). The pathophysiology of chronic constipation is complex, with diverse classifications and overlapping symptoms, making it a challenging condition to manage effectively (Rao et al. 2016). In fact, the occurrence of chronic constipation is closely associated with neuromuscular dysfunction, gut dysbiosis, visceral hypersensitivity, and psychological anxiety (Zhang, Yu, Ma, et al. 2023). Currently, some dietary and herbal therapies are gaining popularity among patients with chronic constipation, with polyphenols playing a particularly important role. These polyphenols interact with the gut microbiota to alleviate oxidative stress in the intestines and help maintain gut homeostasis (Katsirma et al. 2021; Yao, Fu, et al. 2022). For example, pterostilbene (PTE), a natural polyphenol, has been identified to exert a laxative effect in cases of slow transit constipation by increasing the abundance of gut microbiota and activating the PI3K/AKT and Nrf2/HO‐1 signaling pathways, thereby reducing intestinal oxidative stress (Yao, Fu, et al. 2022). Tea consumption is an important approach for alleviating constipation, as tea leaves are a rich source of polyphenols that can modulate gut microbiota and oxidative stress. A study demonstrated that Fu Brick Tea not only increased the Firmicutes/Bacteroidota (F/B) ratio at the phylum level and the relative abundance of Lactobacillus, enhancing SCFAs content, but also regulated the aquaporins‐mediated water transport system, thus alleviating constipation (Qi et al. 2023). Interestingly, tea consumption has also been found to alleviate respiratory conditions such as COPD, suggesting that tea polyphenols may exert dual effects through the gut‐lung axis (Qi et al. 2023).

Colorectal Cancer

Colorectal cancer (CRC), one of the most prevalent malignancies and the most common cancer of the gastrointestinal tract, has posed a serious threat to human health (Bray et al. 2024). Its incidence and progression are influenced by various factors, including genetic susceptibility, lifestyle, dietary habits, and chronic inflammation (Keum and Giovannucci 2019). The underlying mechanisms are highly complex, involving gene mutations, dysregulation of molecular signaling pathways, immune system imbalances, epigenetic modifications, and gut dysbiosis (Luo, Yue, et al. 2024; Wong and Yu 2023). These multifaceted interactions highlight the intricate nature of CRC development and progression. Natural polyphenols hold significant potential in the prevention and treatment of CRC, particularly due to their exceptional immunomodulatory properties, inherent antioxidant capacity, and ability to regulate gut microbiota (Li et al. 2022). These functions play a crucial role in maintaining intestinal homeostasis, preventing the occurrence of CRC, and inhibiting its malignant progression (Hon and Naidu 2024). For example, Zheng et al. find that resveratrol can prevent colitis‐associated tumorigenesis by activating the Nrf2 pathway, reducing oxidative stress and inflammation. This effect relies on mitogen‐activated protein kinase phosphatase 1 (Mkp‐1), which interacts with Nrf2 to protect against oxidative damage (Zheng et al. 2019). Similarly, resveratrol has been shown to inhibit CRC progression by activating SIRT1 via the SIRT1/FOXQ1/ATG16L pathway, reducing oxidative stress, and promoting autophagy‐related apoptosis (Zhou, Niu, et al. 2024). Additionally, the tea polyphenol EGCG has been identified the ability to enhance the sensitivity of CRC to 5‐fluorouracil (5‐FU) by promoting DNA oxidative damage and apoptosis in tumor cells through the inhibition of the GRP78/NF‐κB/miR‐155‐5p/MDR1 pathway (La et al. 2019). In addition to overcoming tumor resistance, EGCG can also prevent colorectal carcinogenesis by modulating the gut microbiota. Wang et al. observed that during colorectal carcinogenesis, gut microbiota diversity increased, with an enrichment of potential carcinogenic bacteria such as Bacteroides and a continuous decline in the abundance of butyrate‐producing bacteria (e.g., Clostridiaceae, Ruminococcus). In contrast, EGCG intervention helped maintain a relatively stable gut microbiota structure throughout the process of malignant transformation (Wang et al. 2017). These findings highlight polyphenol's potential as a therapeutic agent for CRC.

Role of Polyphenols in Lung Health

The occurrence of respiratory diseases is closely linked to oxidative stress, inflammation, immune responses, gut and lung microbiota (Utembe and Kamng'ona 2024; Liu et al. 2024). Obviously, the treatment of respiratory diseases primarily involves anti‐inflammatory, anti‐infective, immunomodulatory, and antioxidant effects (Magnavacca et al. 2022; Alanazi et al. 2025). As organs that interface directly with the external environment, the respiratory system is highly susceptible to airborne factors such as bacteria, viruses, and dust, which can induce respiratory diseases like asthma and pneumonia via oxidative stress (Hewitt and Lloyd 2021). During the COVID‐19 pandemic, numerous studies have shown that polyphenols inhibited SARS‐CoV‐2 viral entry by impairing spike protein‐ACE2 receptor binding, while concurrently suppressing viral RNA replication and protein processing (Paraiso et al. 2020; Wu, Pegan, et al. 2021). This finding suggests that polyphenols play a significant role in preventing respiratory infections. It is noteworthy that the gut microbiota can regulate the immune function of extraintestinal systems, particularly the lungs (Mendes et al. 2017), indicating that gut microbiota may influence the development of pulmonary diseases through the gut‐lung axis. Intriguingly, gut and lung microbiota have bidirectional effects through the lung‐gut axis to regulate immune function (He et al. 2017). COPD

COPD is a common respiratory condition that primarily encompasses chronic bronchitis and emphysema. It is characterized by persistent airflow limitation, which is typically irreversible and progressively worsens over time (Celli and Wedzicha 2019; Malik et al. 2023). The main causes of COPD include long‐term smoking and exposure to harmful gases and particulate matter, such as air pollution, chemical substances, or smoke (Wang et al. 2023). These harmful substances can directly generate large amounts of ROS, leading to oxidative stress in the lungs (Wang et al. 2023; Ornatowski et al. 2020). This oxidative stress, in turn, activates multiple inflammatory signaling pathways, thereby exacerbating lung inflammation and eventually resulting in airway remodeling and the deterioration of lung function, which is one of the primary mechanisms underlying the development of COPD (Pokharel et al. 2024). Studies have shown that COPD patients often experience gut and lung microbiota dysbiosis, which exacerbates oxidative stress. These changes impair immune regulation in both the lungs and gut, thereby promoting the persistence of chronic inflammation (Liu et al. 2024; Song et al. 2023). Clearly, polyphenols with natural antioxidant properties, immune‐regulating capabilities, and the ability to modulate the gut and lung microbiota are appropriate candidates for therapeutic interventions. A RCT and animal experiment demonstrated that curcumin, a dietary polyphenol, can inhibit both intrinsic and extrinsic apoptotic signaling in alveolar epithelial cells by enhancing redox sensing through Nrf2, which subsequently promotes the synthesis and restoration of GSH (Hee Jo et al. 2023). Intriguingly, resveratrol has also been shown to enhance intracellular antioxidant capacity and slow the progression of COPD by specifically reacting with the Cys151 cysteine residue in the Keap1 protein, thereby blocking Nrf2 ubiquitination and activating Nrf2‐regulated defense mechanisms (Wang et al. 2020). In addition, quercetin, a polyphenol commonly found in vegetables and fruits, has been reported to not only alleviate oxidative stress in the lungs, slowing the progression of COPD, but also restoring gut microbiota dysbiosis caused by COPD and improving overall intestinal function (Ding et al. 2023). COPD pathogenesis is also strongly linked to pulmonary dysbiosis (Zhou, Hou, et al. 2024). Studies reveal that COPD patients exhibit decreased Actinobacteria abundance and elevated Haemophilus levels, with advanced cases showing reduced microbial diversity alongside heightened oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokines (Dicker et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2021). Probiotic supplementation may attenuate COPD progression by modulating microbial homeostasis via the gut‐lung axis (Qu et al. 2022). Notably, polyphenols—previously shown to alleviate COPD through gut microbiota regulation—demonstrate therapeutic potential for restoring pulmonary microbiota via this bidirectional axis (Zhang, Wang, et al. 2025). Therefore, modulating the gut‐lung axis and oxidative stress represents an important therapeutic strategy for COPD.

Asthma

Asthma is a chronic respiratory disease characterized by airway inflammation and hyperresponsiveness, driven by both immune dysregulation and oxidative stress (Ding et al. 2023; Mishra et al. 2018). Oxidative stress results from an imbalance between ROS and antioxidant defenses, leading to airway inflammation, tissue damage, and exacerbations in asthma (Albano et al. 2022). This oxidative environment can further activate inflammatory pathways, contributing to chronic airway inflammation, hyperresponsiveness, and remodeling (Michaeloudes et al. 2022). The gut‐lung axis plays a significant role in asthma pathophysiology. Gut dysbiosis, or an imbalance in lung microbiota, can impair immune regulation, reducing the production of regulatory T cells (Tregs) and promoting a Th2‐skewed response characteristic of asthma (Mostafavi Abdolmaleky and Zhou 2024; Zhao et al. 2023). Dysbiosis also lowers levels of SCFAs, which have anti‐inflammatory properties, further exacerbating immune dysregulation and inflammation (Guo, Han, et al. 2024). Therefore, modulating oxidative stress and restoring gut microbiota balance are emerging therapeutic strategies to mitigate symptoms and progression (Suárez‐Martínez et al. 2024; Yang et al. 2024). For example, in a mouse model of asthma induced by house dust mites, EGCG treatment reduced tissue injury, inflammation, mucus production, collagen deposition, and M2 macrophage infiltration in lung tissues. Bioinformatics analysis revealed that EGCG suppressed HIF‐1α activation, leading to the downregulation of vascular endothelial growth factor‐A (VEGF‐A). Overexpression of hypoxia‐inducible factor 1 alpha (HIF‐1α) or VEGF‐A in mice reversed the protective effects of EGCG, demonstrating that its therapeutic mechanism involved inhibiting HIF‐1α/VEGF‐A‐mediated M2 macrophage polarization, thereby relieving asthma symptoms (Yang and Li 2022). Moreover, Wu et al. identified that curcumin treatment could modulate gut microbiota in asthmatic mice by reducing the Firmicutes to Bacteroidetes ratio and decreasing pro‐inflammatory bacteria like Proteobacteria, Intestinimonas, and Ruminococcaceae. curcumin supplementation also lowers Th2‐related factors by influencing bacterial abundances, including Romboutsia, Akkermansia, and mucispirillum, ultimately alleviating asthma symptoms through gut microbiota regulation (Wu, Chen, et al. 2021). Another study demonstrated that in Ovalbumin‐induced asthmatic mice, resveratrol attenuated pulmonary inflammation through gut‐lung interactions by: (1) modulating lung microbiota (increased Akkermansia abundance with reduced LPS biosynthesis); (2) enhancing gut microbiota (elevated Bacteroides acidifaciens and butyric acid in colon); while concurrently (3) restoring pulmonary epithelial integrity via increased tight junction proteins and decreased Muc5ac expression (Alharris et al. 2022). These findings verified the potential therapeutic value of polyphenols in alleviating asthma by regulating oxidative stress and the gut‐lung axis.

Acute Lung Injury/Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome

ALI is a condition characterized by widespread inflammation and damage to the lungs, often caused by infection, trauma, or inhalation of harmful substances (Matthay et al. 2019). It increases the permeability of the alveolar‐capillary barrier, resulting in pulmonary edema, impaired gas exchange, and decreased oxygen levels in the blood. ALI can further develop into ARDS, characterized by more pronounced hypoxemia and extensive lung damage. In cases of severe viral pneumonia, such as those caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS), middle east respiratory syndrome (MERS), and corona virus disease 2019 (COVID‐19), clinical manifestations can rapidly progress from ALI to ARDS (Zhu et al. 2020). The pathogenesis of ALI involves a complex interplay of cellular and molecular processes include endothelial and epithelial dysfunction, inflammation, oxidative stress, and programmed cell death, all of which contribute to lung injury and respiratory failure. ROS play a central role in the initiation and propagation of lung damage in ALI by driving oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis. Meanwhile, the gut microbiota can also influence the progression of ALI through the gut‐lung axis. ALI can lead to changes in the composition of gut microbiota, known as dysbiosis (Levy et al. 2017). This can occur due to inflammation, hypoxia, or the administration of antibiotics during treatment (Si et al. 2022). Gut barrier dysfunction, common in critically ill patients, allows the translocation of bacterial products like LPS into the bloodstream, triggering systemic inflammation and exacerbating lung injury (Nagpal and Yadav 2017). Interestingly, metabolites from gut microbiota, such as SCFAs, can travel through the bloodstream to the lungs, where they help regulate immune responses, decrease inflammation, restrain oxidative stress, and protect lung tissue during ALI (Yao, Cai, et al. 2022). Lung microbiota dysbiosis also significantly contributes to ALI progression. In LPS‐induced ALI murine models, Actinobacteria and Firmicutes phyla exhibited the most significant pulmonary alterations, serving as key modulators of the gut‐lung axis. Concurrently, Actinobacteria and Proteobacteria abundance correlated strongly with ALI severity (Zheng, Liu, et al. 2024). Polyphenols not only limit oxidative stress in lung tissue but also promote gut microbiota homeostasis, thereby reducing lung injury through the gut‐lung axis. For instance, in an ALI model, resveratrol was found to disrupt the binding of Keap1 to Nrf2 in lung macrophages, preventing the ubiquitination and degradation of Nrf2. The activation of Nrf2 led to increased nuclear transcription, enhanced the expression of ARE‐dependent antioxidant genes, suppressed ROS production, and mitigated oxidative damage (Chi et al. 2024). In addition to resveratrol, studies have demonstrated that natural polyphenols such as quercetin, curcumin, and EA can reshape the gut microbiota (Mansour et al. 2024). These polyphenols mitigate COVID‐19‐associated injury by modulating systemic immunity through enhanced SCFAs production and selective promotion of Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium growth. Crucially, this mechanism concurrently maintains lung microbiota homeostasis, demonstrating polyphenols' capacity to preserve respiratory health via the gut‐lung axis (Augusti et al. 2021; Dang and Marsland 2019; Gostimirovic et al. 2023).

Lung Cancer

Lung cancer remains one of the most prevalent and deadly malignancies worldwide, accounting for approximately 18% of cancer‐related deaths. Despite advancements in early detection and treatment, the overall 5‐year survival rate for lung cancer remains low, largely due to late‐stage diagnoses and the aggressive nature of the disease (Bray et al. 2024; Leiter et al. 2023). The development of novel therapies, such as targeted therapies and immunotherapy, has significantly improved outcomes for subsets of patients, yet many challenges persist. Lung cancer involves highly complex molecular mechanisms, with oxidative stress and gut microbiota playing crucial roles in its progression (Sies and Jones 2020). In lung cancer, oxidative stress is exacerbated by factors such as smoking, pollution, and chronic inflammation. Under these conditions, ROS are accumulated in lung tissue, contributing to DNA damage, cellular transformation, and the progression of cancer (Hayes et al. 2020). Emerging evidence suggests that the microbiota may influence lung cancer progression and response to therapy, forming the basis of the gut‐lung axis (Liu et al. 2020; Zhao et al. 2021). Dysbiosis, or an imbalance in the gut or lung microbiota, has been implicated in modulating systemic immune responses and inflammation, potentially impacting tumor growth and therapeutic outcomes (Ma et al. 2022; Goto 2022). Understanding the molecular and cellular mechanisms driving lung cancer progression, alongside the role of oxidative stress, gut and lung microbiota, will be critical in developing novel therapeutic strategies. Polyphenols have gained considerable attention for their anti‐cancer properties across various malignancies. In lung cancer, polyphenols exert a wide range of effects, including modulating oxidative stress and affecting the gut‐lung axis via microbiota regulation (Bhosale et al. 2020; Niedzwiecki et al. 2016). For example, drug resistance is one of the most prevalent malignant phenotypes of lung cancer. Research has shown that EGCG can maintain Nrf2‐mediated redox homeostasis, thereby overcoming etoposide resistance in lung cancer cells (Li et al. 2019). Additionally, green tea catechins, particularly EGCG, function as immune checkpoint inhibitors, reducing PD‐L1 expression and enhancing anti‐lung cancer activity (Rawangkan et al. 2018). Intriguingly, Li et al. found that EGCG could mitigate obesity‐exacerbated lung cancer by modulating gut microbiota (Clostridia, Deltaproteobacteria, Epsilonproteobacteria) and targeting the STAT1‐SLC7A11 pathway, promoting ferroptosis and inhibiting leptin‐induced lung cancer progression (Li, Hao, et al. 2023). The polyphenol‐engineered metal–organic framework (poly‐MOF) specifically eliminates lung microbiota through polyphenols' inherent antimicrobial properties, thereby enhancing lung cancer chemotherapy efficacy (Han, Chen, et al. 2023). Therefore, polyphenols exert anticancer effects primarily through core mechanisms involving the modulation of the gut‐lung microbiota axis, attenuation of oxidative stress, and regulation of immune responses.

Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive, irreversible interstitial lung disease of unknown etiology characterized by aberrant fibroblast proliferation and excessive extracellular matrix deposition (Wollin et al. 2015). Pathologically, repetitive alveolar epithelial cell injury triggers aberrant wound healing—a process driven by dysregulated immune responses (including M2 macrophage polarization and Th17/Treg imbalance) and sustained oxidative stress from ROS overproduction (d'Amati et al. 2023; Luo, Li, et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2023). IPF progresses through self‐sustaining molecular pathways where oxidative stress and gut‐lung microbiota dysregulation constitute pivotal pathogenic drivers. Persistent oxidative stress, fueled by mitochondrial dysfunction and NADPH oxidases activation, accumulates ROS that directly trigger epithelial cell apoptosis and fibroblast differentiation into collagen‐secreting myofibroblasts (Veith et al. 2019). Concurrently, emerging evidence implicates gut microbiota disruption in IPF advancement through diminished SCFAs production and increased endotoxin translocation, which amplifies systemic inflammation via the gut‐lung axis while lung microbiota depletion further compromises local immune homeostasis (Ashique et al. 2022; O'Dwyer et al. 2019; D'Alessandro‐Gabazza et al. 2022). This dual dysregulation combined with oxidative tissue damage and microbial‐mediated immune dysfunction compromises anti‐fibrotic therapeutic efficacy. Emerging evidence positions polyphenols as multi‐target therapeutics against IPF pathogenesis (Wang, Shi, et al. 2025; Pattarayan et al. 2018). These phytochemicals concurrently scavenge mitochondrial‐derived ROS, restore gut microbiota homeostasis to reactivate protective SCFAs production, and rebalance lung macrophage polarization—thereby addressing oxidative tissue damage, endotoxin translocation, and immune dysregulation simultaneously (Zheng et al. 2025). This coordinated mitigation of core fibrosis drivers demonstrates unique potential to overcome limitations of conventional antifibrotic therapies through gut‐lung axis modulation. Microbiome analysis of IPF patients' lower airways reveals elevated Streptococcus abundance (Zhang, Zhang, Yang, et al. 2023), while resveratrol could counteract pneumococcal pathogenicity by inhibiting Streptococcus pneumoniae autolysin LytA, attenuating oxidative stress through Nrf2 activation and protecting lung epithelial integrity (Zahlten et al. 2015). In the bleomycin‐induced IPF model, it was found that pro‐apoptotic genes (e.g., Caspase‐3, Bax) were significantly up‐regulated, the fibrosis‐related TGF‐β/SMAD pathway was activated, and the relative abundance of intestinal Lactobacillus was markedly reduced (Quan et al. 2022). Conversely, polyphenols can directly inhibit both the pro‐apoptotic and fibrotic pathways, modulate the gut microbiota, and specifically increase the abundance of Lactobacillus (Li et al. 2024; Mokra et al. 2022). The ability of polyphenols to bidirectionally regulate the lung‐gut axis positions them as promising therapeutic candidates for IPF treatment.

Mechanisms of Polyphenols in Modulating Gut‐Lung Axis

Polyphenols exhibit therapeutic potential in gut‐lung axis disorders due to their capacity to simultaneously modulate multiple interconnected biological processes (Wang et al. 2022). Rather than acting through a single mechanism, polyphenols orchestrate an integrated network of responses, including microbial reprogramming, redox balance, immune regulation, and barrier fortification, collectively maintaining the homeostasis of this critical inter‐organ circuit (Table 3).

Reprogramming of Microbial Communities

Polyphenols exert a foundational influence on the gut‐lung axis through the bidirectional remodeling of microbial ecosystems. Within the gut, they function as selective modulators, promoting the proliferation of beneficial commensals such as Bifidobacterium and Lactobacillus while suppressing potential pathogens (Mithul Aravind et al. 2021). This shift enhances the production of microbially derived SCFAs, notably butyrate, propionate, and acetate. These metabolites are crucial for maintaining intestinal epithelial barrier integrity and serve as key systemic signaling molecules that regulate immune responses (Dang and Marsland 2019; Mann et al. 2024). The systemic impact of this gut‐level reprogramming extends to the lungs. Circulating SCFAs and other anti‐inflammatory signals modulate the pulmonary immune milieu, fostering an environment that is less permissive to pathogenic expansion and more supportive of commensal colonization (Woelfel et al. 2024; Mohsin et al. 2025). Consequently, oral polyphenol intake is associated with a reduced abundance of pro‐inflammatory taxa (e.g., Proteobacteria) and an enrichment of beneficial genera like Akkermansia in the lungs, correlating with attenuated respiratory pathology (Gade and Kumar 2023; Alharris et al. 2022). Emerging evidence suggests that polyphenol metabolites reaching the airways via circulation may also exert direct local effects. Although less characterized than in the gut, these compounds potentially inhibit the growth of respiratory pathogens and modulate biofilm formation (Yang et al. 2020). This direct niche engineering, combined with the bolstered host defense from improved immunity, contributes to restoring a balanced pulmonary microbiome, which is vital for maintaining alveolar homeostasis and resisting infections (Alghetaa et al. 2021; Mohsin et al. 2025).

Maintenance of Redox Homeostasis

The regulation of redox equilibrium is a central pillar of polyphenol activity across the gut‐lung axis. Polyphenols preserve redox balance across the gut‐lung axis through a coordinated, multi‐level strategy that counteracts excessive ROS. Their intrinsic chemical structure allows direct free radical scavenging and metal chelation, providing immediate protection against oxidative insults in mucosal tissues (Li et al. 2022; Mithul Aravind et al. 2021). Beyond direct antioxidant activity, polyphenols activate sustained endogenous defense systems, primarily via the Nrf2‐Keap1 pathway (Scapagnini et al. 2011). By stabilizing Nrf2, they induce the transcription of cytoprotective enzymes such as heme oxygenase‐1 (HO‐1) and glutathione synthesis‐related proteins, significantly boosting cellular detoxification capacity (Scapagnini et al. 2011). This is complemented by the modulation of key antioxidant enzymes, including superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione peroxidase (GPx), enhancing the enzymatic breakdown of ROS (Zhang and Tsao 2016). Furthermore, polyphenols confer indirect protection by mitigating inflammation‐driven oxidative stress and preserving mitochondrial function, thereby reducing a major cellular source of ROS (Mithul Aravind et al. 2021). Their ability to reshape the gut microbiota also contributes to systemic redox balance by promoting the production of antioxidant microbial metabolites (e.g., specific SCFAs) and reducing pro‐inflammatory bacterial populations (Wan et al. 2021; Gibert‐Ramos et al. 2021). Collectively, polyphenols maintain redox homeostasis along the gut‐lung axis via a synergistic combination of direct scavenging, transcriptional reprogramming to boost endogenous defenses, and systemic modulation of inflammatory and microbial sources of ROS. This integrated antioxidant strategy underlies their protective effects against oxidative tissue damage in both gastrointestinal and pulmonary disorders.

Immunomodulation: From Local to Systemic Regulation

Polyphenols orchestrate sophisticated immunomodulation across the gut‐lung axis, from local mucosal sites to systemic circulation, to resolve pathological inflammation and restore homeostasis (Wang et al. 2022). They directly modulate key immune cells by promoting anti‐inflammatory M2 macrophage polarization and regulating the Th17/Treg balance, thereby restraining excessive tissue damage and autoimmunity in conditions such as IBD and asthma (Xiao et al. 2022; Gao et al. 2024; Yang and Li 2022). Furthermore, polyphenols can partially reverse T‐cell exhaustion in tumor microenvironments, restoring anti‐tumor cytotoxicity (Williamson 2025). Simultaneously, polyphenols reprogram the inflammatory cytokine network, consistently downregulating pro‐inflammatory mediators such as TNF‐α, IL‐1β, and IL‐6 while enhancing anti‐inflammatory signals like IL‐10 (Yahfoufi et al. 2018). This shift from a pro‐inflammatory to a resolving cytokine milieu is fundamental to mitigating chronic inflammation in both gastrointestinal and respiratory disorders (Yahfoufi et al. 2018). A major systemic pathway of immunomodulation is mediated through gut microbiota‐derived metabolites. Polyphenol‐induced increases in SCFAs, particularly butyrate, promote peripheral Treg differentiation, suppress NF‐κB signaling in antigen‐presenting cells, and enforce immune tolerance, thereby transmitting anti‐inflammatory signals from the gut to the lungs (Dang and Marsland 2019; Mann et al. 2024). In addition, by strengthening intestinal and pulmonary epithelial barriers, polyphenols reduce the translocation of microbial products such as lipopolysaccharide (LPS) into circulation, limiting a key trigger of systemic immune activation and preventing gut‐derived exacerbation of lung inflammation (Nagpal and Yadav 2017).

Enhancement of Barrier Integrity and Metabolite Signaling

Beyond modulating cellular and molecular players, polyphenols critically fortify the physical and functional gatekeepers of the gut‐lung axis. Polyphenols consolidate the gut‐lung axis by simultaneously reinforcing epithelial barriers and optimizing inter‐organ metabolite signaling. They enhance intestinal barrier integrity by upregulating key tight junction proteins (e.g., ZO‐1, occludin, claudins) and suppressing inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF‐α, IFN‐γ) that disrupt paracellular adhesion, thereby effectively reducing intestinal permeability and preventing the translocation of bacterial endotoxins such as LPS into systemic circulation (Ju et al. 2018; Gao et al. 2024; Yao, Fu, et al. 2022). This robust gut barrier is fundamental to containing local dysbiosis and preventing the spillover of inflammatory mediators that can secondarily perturb pulmonary homeostasis. Similarly, in the lungs, polyphenols increase alveolar tight junction expression and reduce mucin overproduction, strengthening the air‐blood barrier and limiting inflammatory infiltration (Alharris et al. 2022). Meanwhile, polyphenols amplify the production of key gut microbial metabolites that function as systemic signals. They elevate levels of SCFAs like butyrate, which not only support colonocyte energy metabolism and gut barrier function but also circulate to the lungs where they modulate immune responses and reduce inflammation (Yao, Cai, et al. 2022; Wollin et al. 2015). In addition, polyphenols influence the generation of tryptophan‐derived indole metabolites (e.g., indole‐3‐propionic acid). These compounds activate the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) on epithelial and immune cells, regulating mucosal immunity, cytokine balance, and barrier function in both organs, establishing a direct molecular link between diet, microbial metabolism, and host defense (Katsirma et al. 2021). Thus, through dual actions of barrier fortification and metabolite signaling, polyphenols establish a robust defensive front and a coherent biochemical dialogue along the gut‐lung axis, ensuring that beneficial microbial restructuring translates into systemic physiological protection.

Conclusion and Perspective

Polyphenols have emerged as pivotal modulators of the gut‐lung axis, orchestrating a network of beneficial effects through microbiota reprogramming, redox homeostasis, immunomodulation, and barrier enhancement. The field of polyphenol research in respiratory and gut health has evolved significantly. While several excellent reviews have well established the conceptual framework of the gut‐lung axis or detailed specific dietary components (Xu et al. 2022; Druszczynska et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2025), this review provides a distinct synthesis by focusing explicitly on the broad class of polyphenols as central modulators across the entire gut‐lung ecosystem. Research has advanced from correlative observations to delineating specific molecular mechanisms, such as Nrf2 activation, SCFA‐mediated signaling, and direct microbial modulation, which underpin polyphenol efficacy in preclinical studies (Figure 4).

Schematic summary of the core mechanisms by which polyphenols modulate the gut‐lung axis. This schematic integrates the four primary mechanistic pillars through which dietary and therapeutic polyphenols exert their beneficial effects across the gut–lung axis. (1) Microbiota Reprogramming: Polyphenols selectively enhance beneficial commensals (e.g., Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium, Akkermansia) and increase the production of microbial metabolites like short‐chain fatty acids (SCFAs). (2) Redox Homeostasis: Polyphenols directly scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS) and activate endogenous antioxidant defenses, chiefly via the Nrf2‐Keap1 pathway, to mitigate oxidative stress in both organs. (3) Immunomodulation: They regulate immune cell polarization (e.g., M2 macrophages, Tregs), balance cytokine profiles (reducing TNF‐α, IL‐6; elevating IL‐10), and suppress NF‐κB signaling. (4) Barrier Integrity: Polyphenols upregulate tight junction proteins (e.g., ZO‐1, occludin) in the gut and lung epithelia, reducing permeability and preventing translocation of pro‐inflammatory agents like LPS.

Nevertheless, the translation of these mechanistic insights into reliable clinical therapies faces substantial hurdles. The path to clinical application is constrained by several interrelated challenges. Pharmacological complexity presents a primary obstacle, characterized by low oral bioavailability, extensive metabolism, and high inter‐individual pharmacokinetic variability, necessitating advanced delivery systems and precise dose optimization. This challenge is compounded by substantial biological heterogeneity, as variations in gut microbiome composition and host genetics critically influence metabolic fate and therapeutic response, highlighting the need for personalized, biomarker‐guided approaches. Furthermore, limitations persist in both model systems and clinical evidence. Preclinical models often fail to fully recapitulate human physiology, while robust clinical evidence from large‐scale RCTs with definitive endpoints remains sparse. Finally, moving from association to causation will require a shift toward integrative multi‐omics frameworks to delineate the systems‐level actions of polyphenols across the gut‐lung axis, including their effects on understudied components such as the lung mycobiota.

To overcome the outlined translational challenges and realize the therapeutic promise of polyphenols, future research must focus on several key fronts. First, advancing precision nutrition and biomarker development is essential, requiring the integration of host genotyping, deep microbiome profiling, and circulating metabolomics to stratify patients and predict individual responses. Second, innovative intervention designs are needed, such as adaptive clinical trials incorporating early‐response biomarkers (e.g., ctDNA) and the development of targeted delivery platforms (e.g., inhaled formulations) to improve local bioavailability and safety. Third, the exploration of rational combination strategies should be prioritized, systematically evaluating polyphenols as adjuvants to existing therapies (e.g., with immune checkpoint inhibitors or standard care) to target complementary pathways and overcome resistance. To realize the clinical potential of polyphenols, advancing targeted delivery technologies, such as nanoparticle encapsulation, lipid‐based carriers, or inhaled aerosols for improved bioavailability and stability, is crucial. Concurrently, expanding combination approaches, which integrate polyphenols with probiotics, prebiotics, or conventional therapeutics, may yield synergistic efficacy in reshaping the gut‐lung ecosystem. Finally, a deeper mechanism‐driven discovery pipeline must be established, leveraging human‐relevant models like organoid co‐cultures and gnotobiotic systems to decipher complex host‐microbe‐polyphenol interactions and validate novel targets, including within the lung mycobiome.

Polyphenols represent a promising, multi‐target avenue for managing interconnected gut‐lung axis disorders. By confronting the current translational barriers and concentrating on these strategic priorities, the field can evolve from mechanistic observations to developing effective, evidence‐based therapies that improve patient outcomes across this critical physiological network.

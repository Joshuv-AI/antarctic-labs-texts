Generation of a Patient-Derived Inducible Pluripotent Stem Cell Model for Studying the YARS2-Related Genetic Disease
Authors: Chenbo Zeng, Andrew Gray, Rebecca Ganetzky, Ya-Ming Hou, Lorraine Iacovitti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe YARS2 variant, harboring the compound heterozygous pathogenic mutations F185L/E264del, was identified in the gene for mitochondrial tyrosyl-tRNA synthetase in a proband that suffered a neonatal phenotype. To facilitate studies to better understand the severity of the mutations, we created a patient-derived inducible pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) model. We first derived iPSCs from fibroblasts of the patient Q1818, which contain two mutations, c.553T>C (p.F185L) and c.792_794delAGA (p.E264del) in the YARS2 gene. We then generated three isogenic control iPSC lines with one or both mutations corrected by using CRISPR-Cas9 technology. The correction of mutations in YARS2 was confirmed by Sanger sequencing. The stemness of iPSC lines was demonstrated by the expression of stem cell markers in the iPSCs, as determined using qPCR, immunostaining, and trilineage differentiation. Moreover, three positive clones of each iPSC line were extensively characterized, confirming that they originated from Q1818 fibroblasts, had normal karyotypes, and did not contain off-targets in the YARS2 coding sequence; genome wide off target effects were not a major concern. Subsequently, Q1818 iPSCs and the three isogenic control iPSCs were differentiated into clinically relevant motor neurons. In addition, we demonstrated that the patient fibroblasts and the derived iPSCs are heterozygous for either c. 553T>C or c.792_794delAGA, and that the two mutations are located on different alleles of the YARS2 gene, providing critical information for studying the mutation-associated disease. In conclusion, we have generated a set of four iPSC lines, which can be used as a model to study a clinically severe case of YARS2 disease.Keywords: Inducible pluripotent stem cell, Tyrosyl-tRNA synthetase 2, Isogenic cells, CRISPR-Cas9

Introduction

The nuclear YARS2 gene encodes the mitochondrial tyrosyl-tRNA synthetase (1) which charges mitochondrial tRNATyr with Tyr for translation of Tyr codons for the dedicated mitochondrial protein synthesis machinery. Pathogenic variants of YARS2 are associated with myopathy, lactic acidosis and sideroblastic anemia (MLASA) syndrome, an infantile- to adult-onset genetic disease (2). We recently identified a pair of YARS2 pathogenic variants that presented neonatal fatality, where the proband passed away after 8 weeks of birth due to intractable metabolic acidosis and systemic hypotension. Whole exome sequencing identified biallelic variants in trans: c.553T>C (p.F185L, paternally inherited) and c.792_794delAGA (p.E264del, maternally inherited) (3). Enzymatic analysis revealed synergistically negative interaction between the two variants (3), leading to a substantial loss of activity relative to individual mutations. As this case represents the most severe clinical presentation to date for YARS2-associated diseases, and as it demonstrates a clear genotype-phenotype correlation, it is well suited for further studies. For this purpose, we report here the generation of a human inducible pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) model for the pair of mutations, which can be used to elucidate the molecular basis of the disease.

The iPSC model represents a unique platform to study human disease. Patient somatic cells can be transformed into stem cells by introducing a combination of 3 or 4 transcription factors and subsequently differentiated into disease-related cell types (4). These reprogrammed patient cells can be used to investigate disease mechanisms. However, successful investigation of any disease mechanism requires an isogenic control cell line that has corrected the mutations and restored the wild type gene sequence (5), which allows for a comparison with the patient-derived cell line. To generate an isogenic control cell line, clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR)-Cas9-based gene editing is the most common methodology.

Here, we employed CRISPR-Cas9 editing to generate three isogenic control lines for the pair of compound heterozygous variants of YARS2. Additionally, by developing a novel strategy we provide data to support the clinical whole exome sequencing analysis (3) in that the patient fibroblasts and the derived iPSCs are heterozygous for each mutation, and that the two mutations are located on different alleles of YARS2 gene. These results provide a solid foundation for further molecular elucidation of the disease mechanism.

Materials and Methods

Cell culture

Q1818 fibroblast cells harboring c.553T>C and c.792_794delAGA were isolated with informed consent from a de-identified patient and obtained from the Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia (IRB number: 24-022190). Q1818 fibroblasts were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium/Nutrient Mixture F-12 containing 10% fetal bovine serum, GlutaMAX, non-essential amino acids, 50 mg/mL uridine, 1% penicillin/streptomycin. iPSCs that were derived from Q1818 fibroblasts were cultured in mTeSR Plus medium (StemCell) in cell culture plates precoated with cultrex (Biotechne). All cell lines were maintained at 37℃ in an incubator with 5% CO2.

Derivation of inducible pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from fibroblasts

Q1818 fibroblasts were passaged once with Trypsin-ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) to expand, then reprogrammed to iPSCs using the Cytotune 2.0 Sendai virus reprogramming kit (Thermo Fisher). iPSC colonies were manually chosen and subcloned for 5 passages to facilitate clearance of Sendai virus, which was verified with quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) and immunocytochemistry. The qPCR primer sequences and the primary antibodies for immunostaining were listed in Supplementary Table S1 and S2, respectively. The resulting iPSC line, termed Q1818 iPSCs, was then passaged further with 0.5 mM EDTA.

Tri-lineage differentiation

Tri-lineage differentiation of iPSCs was completed using the STEMdiff Trilineage Differentiation Kit (StemCell). The lineage-specific differentiation of iPSCs was analyzed by qPCR for lineage-specific markers (Supplementary Table S1).

Gene editing of Q1818 iPSCs

CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing of Q1818 iPSCs was performed (6, 7). The sgRNAs (single guide RNA) and ssODNs (single-stranded oligo-deoxynucleotides) were designed with the help of the online tool, Invitrogen TrueDesign Genome Editor (Thermo Fisher). For correcting mutation c.553T>C, the sequences of sgRNA and ssODN are 5′- GCAGCACCTGGTGGACTTCC-3′ and 5′-GGACAACTCGGCCTGGTACCAGAAGCAGCACCTGGTGGACTTCCTGGCGGCAGTGGGGGGTCACTTCCGCATGGGGACGCTGC-3′, respectively. For correcting mutation c.792_794 delAGA, the sequences of sgRNA and ssODN are 5′-TTCTTTATGCGTAGGTTGAC-3′ and 5′-TGTACTTGTAATTAGAGGAACGGTGATTCCAAATACATCTTCTCCAGTCAACCTACGCATAAAGAACAATTTCATTATGTGCCTCAAGC-3′, respectively.

Q1818 iPSCs were detached from the tissue culture plate using accutase. Cells of 0.8×106 cell counts were harvested and transfected with SpyFi Cas9 (Aldevron), sgRNA, and ssODN by electroporation using P4 Primary Cell 4D Nucleofector X kit (Lonza) and an electroporator, Amaxa 4D-Nucleofector X unit (Lonza), according to the manufacture’s manual. Briefly, 100 μL P4 Cell Solution was added to 0.8×106 Q1818 iPSCs. CRISPR-Cas9 protein of 2 μL (20 μg) with 2.5 μL (5 μg) sgRNA was mixed and incubated at room temperature for 5 minutes. The template ssODN of 4 μL (40 μg) was added to 100 μL Q1818 iPSCs, and then the preincubated Cas9/sgRNA complex was added to premixed iPSCs/ssODN. The resulting 100 μL mixture was transferred to a single Nucleocuvette and then electroporation was performed using the 4D-Nucleofector instrument. After electroporation, the bulk iPSCs were cultured in 2 mL mTeSR Plus medium containing 10% Clone R2 (StemCell) in one well of 6-well plate precoated with cultrex. Clone R2 was removed 3 days later.

Genotyping

Genomic DNA was extracted from iPSCs using DirectPCR Lysis Reagent (Cell) (Viagen Biotech). The DNA region containing c.553 was amplified by PCR using the primer pair 5′-CACCAGCAGCTTTTCACTGA-3′ (forward) and 5′-CCCAGGATTTCCCCAAGGTT-3′ (reverse). The DNA region containing c.792_794 was amplified using the PCR primer pair 5′-AGATTTAGGAGGTCAAGGAAG-3′ (forward) and 5′-TCAGAACTCACCTTTCCACTG-3′ (reverse). The PCR products were purified by QIAquick PCR Purification kit (Qiagen). The purified PCR products were submitted to Azenta Inc for Sanger sequencing. The sequencing primers were 5′-CACCAGCAGCTTTTCACTGA-3′ for the c.553-containing region and 5′-AGATTTAGGAGGTCAAGGAAG-3′ for the c.792_794 containing region.

Short tandem repeat analysis

Short tandem repeat (STR) analysis was performed using the PowerPlexⓇ16 System (Promega) with the detection of 16 allele loci at the Molecular & Genomic Pathology Laboratory of Thomas Jefferson University Hospital. Genomic DNA samples were prepared using the QIAamp DNA Mini Kit (Qiagen).

Motor neuron differentiation

IPSCs were seeded onto Cultrex coated plates at a density of 4×105 cells/cm2. The next day, cells were given neurobasal media with LDN193189 (200 nM), SB431542 (10 μM), CHIR99021 (3 μM), changed daily from D0∼D5. Beginning D6, retinoic acid (RA, 100 nM) and smoothened agonist (SAG, 1 μM) were also added to the media, with the media changed daily until D12. Starting D12 and continuing until experimental endpoint, cells were fed with media containing RA (500 nM), SAG (1 μM), DAPT (2.5 μM), Compound E (100 nM), brain-derived neurotrophic factor (10 ng/mL), glial-derived neurotrophic factor (10 ng/mL), and ciliary neurotrophic factor (10 ng/mL). On D7 and D16, the cells were dissociated with Accutase and replated at a density of 4×105 cells/cm2 on plates coated with Cultrex (D7) or with a 0.1% polyethyleneimine solution and laminin (1.5 μg/cm2) (D16). After reseeding, cells were fed every other day until D24, when they were fixed and stained. Reagent suppliers were listed in Supplementary Table S3.

Results

Confirmation of mutations in patient Q1818 fibroblasts and derivation of iPSCs from fibroblasts

We obtained patient Q1818 fibroblasts containing two mutations, c.553T>C and c.792_794delAGA, on the YARS2 gene. We first performed Sanger sequencing for the mutation-containing regions to confirm the existence of both mutations. The sequencing results confirmed the existence of the two mutations and furthermore showed that the Q1818 fibroblasts are heterozygous for both mutations (Fig. 1A).

Q1818 fibroblasts were reprogrammed to Q1818 iPSCs. The derived Q1818 iPSCs exhibited the typical morphology of iPSCs (i.e., packed colonies with smooth edges, Fig. 1B) and expressed the molecular markers of stem cells, OCT4 and NANOG, illustrated by immunostaining (Fig. 1B). Sanger sequencing results showed that Q1818 iPSCs are heterozygous for both mutations, c.553T>C and c.792_794delAGA, on the YARS2 gene (Fig. 1C).

Correction of mutations in Q1818 iPSCs

To perform CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing in Q1818 iPSCs, sgRNAs and ssODNs were designed with the help of an online tool, Invitrogen TrueDesign Genome Editor. 0.8×106 cells were transfected with Cas9 and the specific sgRNA and ssODN for each mutation by electroporation. The gene editing efficiency was assessed by Sanger sequencing (Supplementary Materials and Supplementary Fig. S1). Once the gene editing efficiency was estimated to be sufficiently high, bulk iPSCs were screened for positive clones (i.e. clones with each mutation corrected). Bulk iPSCs were cultured in a tissue culture dish at a low density (5,000 cells/100 mm dish) and single cell colonies were manually selected and expanded. The genomic DNA from single cell clones were extracted and genotyped by sequencing the c.553 and c.792_794 loci of the YARS2 gene. Positive clones were identified based on the sequencing results (Fig. 1D-1F and Supplementary Fig. S2). Of the 20 screened clones, 13 showed the correction of c.553C to the wild type c.553T, without targeting the mutation c.792_794 delAGA. The resulting iPSC line was named iPSC-YARS2 (T/T, AGA/delAGA). Moreover, 4 out of the 108 screened clones showed correction of the mutation c.792_794 delAGA to the wild type c.792_794 AGA, without targeting the c.553C mutation. The resulting iPSC line was named iPSC-YARS2 (T/C, AGA/AGA). 5 out of the 24 screened clones showed successful correction of both c.553T>C and c.792_794delAGA mutations. The resulting iPSC line was named iPSC-YARS2 (T/T, AGA/AGA). 3 clones for each iPSC line were subject to further characterization.

Characterization of Q1818 iPSCs and isogenic control iPSC lines

The parent Q1818 iPSC line and three isogenic control iPSC lines, in which one or both mutations were corrected, were assessed for their stemness. Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) analysis revealed that all iPSC lines exhibited markedly elevated expression of stem cell markers SOX2, OCT4, and NANOG compared to fibroblasts of Q1818 (Fig. 2A). Furthermore, all iPSC lines were assessed for their ability to differentiate into the three germ layers-ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm-by first inducing lineage-specific differentiation of the iPSCs, followed by analysis of lineage-specific markers. The data showed that the differentiated cells expressed ectoderm markers (PAX6, HES5, OTX2), mesoderm markers (TBXT, TBX6), or endoderm markers (SOX17, FOXA2), respectively, and significantly reduced the expression of the stem cell marker OCT4 (Fig. 2B and Supplementary Fig. S3A). Collectively, these data confirm the stemness of Q1818 iPSC line and the three isogenic iPSC lines.

The iPSCs were subjected to further characterization. Morphological analysis showed that three positive clones of each isogenic control iPSC line exhibited typical morphology of iPSCs (Supplementary Fig. S3B). STR analysis was performed to determine the DNA profile of each cell line by counting the number of repeats of specific STR sequences at multiple loci (e.g., 16 loci in this study) on the chromosomes. The data showed that Q1818 iPSC and all the positive clones of each isogenic iPSC line exhibited the same DNA profiles as the patient Q1818 fibroblasts (Table 1), demonstrating that all the iPSC lines originated from the patient Q1818 fibroblasts. In addition, iPSCs in culture flasks were submitted to WiCell Research Institute Inc. for chromosome analysis. The results demonstrated that the karyotypes (the number and structures of chromosomes) of Q1818 fibroblasts, Q1818 iPSCs, and all the positive clones of each isogenic iPSC line are normal (Supplementary Fig. S3C).

To verify that no off‑target events occurred in the YARS2 coding sequence (CDS), total RNA from iPSCs was extracted and RT‑PCR was used to amplify the full-length YARS2 CDS. Sanger sequencing with four primers and CLUSTALW alignment showed that the YARS2 CDS of all positive clones was identical to that of the parent Q1818 iPSCs except at positions c.553 and c.792_794 and was also in agreement with NCBI Reference NM_001040436.3 (Supplementary Fig. S3D). These results confirm accurate transcription of YARS2 mRNA and the absence of off-target changes in the CDS following reprogramming and CRISPR-Cas9 editing. In addition, to assess the risk of genome-wide off-target effects, genome wide off target sites were predicted using Cas-OFFinder (three mismatch threshold). All resultant genomic fragments were then blasted to the human genomic plus transcript database. For the c.792_794delAGA sgRNA, four sites with three mismatches were identified, none within coding regions, indicating minimal off-target risk. For the c.553T>C sgRNA, one site with two mismatches and 17 with three mismatches were found; three coding sites (ATP10A, CABP7, EXOC7) were further analyzed. These loci were PCR amplified and sequenced in parental Q1818 iPSCs and clones 1∼3 of the isogenic lines (T/T, AGA/AGA and T/T, AGA/delAGA). All sequences matched the parental line, confirming no edits at these potential off target sites. Overall, genome-wide off-target events do not pose a significant concern for the isogenic cell lines generated (Supplementary Table S1, Supplementary Fig. S4).

Motor neuron differentiation from iPSCs

Motor neurons are the most frequently affected neuronal type in mitochondrial aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase (ARS2)-associated neuromuscular disorders (8). To generate clinically relevant cell models, Q1818 iPSCs and the three isogenic iPSCs were differentiated into motor neurons using the protocol described in the method section (9). After 24 days of differentiation, the resulting cells exhibited characteristic long processes of neurons and expressed motor neuron-specific markers, choline acetyltransferase (ChAT), Islet-1 (Isl1) and Islet-2 (Isl2) (Fig. 2C and Supplementary Fig. S5), confirming the successful differentiation of the iPSCs into motor neurons.

c.553T>C and c.792_794delAGA mutations are located on different alleles

We next determined whether the two mutations are present on the same allele or different alleles by developing a novel strategy. We first differentially amplified each allele followed by sequencing each allele. The PCR primers were 5′-CACCAGCAGCTTTTCACTGA-3′ (common forward primer upstream of c.553T/C, named as T-C F1), 5′-GGTGATTCCAAATACATCTTCT-3′ (reverse primer containing the wild type c.792_794AGA, named as AGA R), and 5′-GGTGATTCCAAATACATCTCCA-3′ (reverse primer containing the mutation c.792_794delAGA, named as delAGA R). The T-C F1/AGA R primer pair was used to amplify the allele containing the wild type c.792_794AGA, whereas T-C F1/delAGA R primer pair was used to amplify the allele containing the mutation c.792_794delAGA. We used Q1818 iPSC (T/C, AGA/delAGA), which is heterozygous for the two mutations, to prepare cDNA by RT-PCR. We used cDNA, instead of genomic DNA, as a template to differentially amplify the two alleles for reasons described in the Discussion section. We used the iPSC-YARS2 (T/T, AGA/AGA) line, a homozygous wild type line generated above, as a control.

The PCR results (Fig. 3A) showed that using an annealing temperature of 68℃, but not 58℃, the two alleles were differentially amplified. As shown in lane 2 in the right panel of Fig. 3A, by using T-C F1/delAGA R primer pair and homozygous AGA/AGA alleles as a template, no specific PCR product (348 bp) was amplified, indicating that T-C F1/delAGA R primer pair cannot amplify the allele containing the wild type AGA. As shown in lane 4 in the right panel, by using T-C F1/delAGA R primer pair and heterozygous AGA/delAGA alleles as a template, a 348 bp band was amplified, suggesting that this band is the allele containing the mutation delAGA. The right panel results also implied that the 348 bp band in lane 3 could be the allele containing the wild type AGA, which was confirmed later by sequencing. The 348 bp bands in lanes 3 and 4 were excised from the agarose gel and the DNA was extracted by Qiaquick gel extraction kit (Qiagen). The DNA was subjected to Sanger sequencing using the primer 5′-CACCAGCAGCTTTTCACTGA-3′ (T-C F1).

The sequencing results showed that the allele containing the wild type c.792_794AGA was differentially amplified by using the T-C F1/AGA R primer pair, and that this allele contained the mutation c.553C (Fig. 3B, middle panel, and Supplementary Fig. S6B). Also, the allele containing c.792_794delAGA was differentially amplified by using the T-C F1/delAGA R primer pair, and that this allele contained the wild type c.553T (Fig. 3B, bottom panel, and Supplementary Fig. S6C). Together, the sequencing results demonstrated that the two mutations, c.553T>C and c.792_794delAGA, are heterozygous and located on different alleles.

Discussion

In this study, we have generated a human subject-derived iPSC model to study the relationship between two mutations, c.553T>C and c.792_794delAGA, of the YARS2 gene and the associated genetic disease. We have derived the Q1818 iPSCs from patient Q1818 fibroblasts. We have created three isogenic control iPSC lines with one or both mutations corrected. This allows for elucidation of the effect of individual mutations by using one-mutation cell line (T/C, AGA/AGA or T/T, AGA/delAGA) to pair with the wild-type cell line (T/T, AGA/AGA). It also allows for elucidation of the combined effect of the two mutations by comparing the cell line (T/C, AGA/delAGA) with the wild-type cell line. Motor neurons are impacted in most ARS2-associated diseases (8), therefore, we have differentiated these iPSC lines into motor neurons. These iPSC-derived motor neurons represent the first cellular model for studying ARS2-related diseases, providing a valuable platform for investigating disease mechanisms and potential therapeutic interventions.

Sequencing data showed that each of the two mutations, c.553T>C or c.792_794delAGA, exists on only one allele (Fig. 1). We have developed an innovative strategy to address the question of whether these two mutations are located on the same or different alleles. We designed a reverse PCR primer containing either the wild type AGA or the deletion of AGA at c.792_794 positions. The wild type and deletion primers were used to differentially amplify the wild type and deletion alleles, respectively. Then we sequenced each allele to identify the nucleotide at c.553 position. Because c.553 and c.792_794 positions are located on exon 1 and 2, respectively, the concern was that if the intron between these two positions is too long, it would be impossible to obtain PCR fragments which contain both c.553 and c.792_794 positions in each single chain. Should that be the case it would be impossible to determine the nucleotides at these two positions on a single allele by Sanger sequencing. Therefore, we performed RT-PCR to obtain cDNA of YARS2, which does not contain introns and thus brings c.553 and c.792_794 positions closer. The use of cDNA, instead of genomic DNA, as the PCR template produced a PCR fragment of 348 bp, which contains nucleotides at both c.553 and c.792_794 positions and is short enough for convenient PCR amplification and straightforward sequencing analysis. Our data demonstrated that c.553T>C or c.792_794delAGA are located on different alleles (Fig. 3 and Supplementary Fig. S6).

In conclusion, we have generated a set of four YARS2 iPSC lines with the capacity to differentiate into motor neurons. These iPSC lines will provide a critical model for future studies of YARS2-associated disease and serve as a generalizable template to generate iPSC models of other diseases with compound heterozygosity variants.

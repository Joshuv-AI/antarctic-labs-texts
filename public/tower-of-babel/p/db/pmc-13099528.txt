Impact of prenatal exposure to airborne particulate matter on local functional connections in the cerebral cortex of neonates
Authors: Laura Blanco-Hinojo, Jesus Pujol, Gerard Martínez-Vilavella, Laura Gómez-Herrera, Ioar Rivas, Maria Dolors Gómez-Roig, Elisa Llurba, Mark Nieuwenhuijsen, Toni Galmes, Cecilia Persavento, Xavier Querol, Mireia Gascón, Maria Foraster, Juan Domingo Gispert, Carles Falcón, Joan Deus, Payam Dadvand, Jordi Sunyer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•The study informs on how prenatal air pollution influences neonatal brain connectivity.•A novel approach mapped effects on the functional structure of cortical connections.•Higher prenatal PM2.5 exposure is linked to stronger local functional connectivity.•Connectivity changes involve brain regions related to sensorimotor function.Keywords: Functional connectivity, Neonates, Particulate matter, Brain development, MRI

Introduction

The development of the human brain during gestation is a dynamic, adaptive, and highly organized process, characterized by gradual changes that follow a course of continuous specification and refinement of brain systems (Stiles and Jernigan, 2010). Cellular and molecular changes underpin the orderly formation of the developing cerebral cortex and support the emergence of functional connections, eventually leading to the establishment of functional neural networks underpinning cognition and behavior (Stiles and Jernigan, 2010; Tau and Peterson, 2010). Resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) enables the mapping of functionally connected brain regions on the basis of coherent spontaneous fluctuations in blood-oxygen level-dependent (BOLD) signals (Biswal et al., 1995). Research using rs-fMRI has provided valuable insights into the emerging functional organization of the brain, revealing preliminary forms of resting-state networks already present in the fetal stage (Thomason, 2020). Notably, early network architecture exhibits a predominantly segregated connectivity pattern, where primary sensory and motor cortices display more mature and localized connectivity profiles compared to association regions (e.g., Cao et al., 2017a; Eyre et al., 2021; Fransson et al., 2011; Gao et al., 2015), progressively transitioning into a more distributed and hierarchically integrated network organization during development (Cao et al., 2017b; Gao et al., 2017; Grayson and Fair, 2017; Menon, 2013; Ouyang et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2019).

The dynamic structural and functional transformations underlying early brain development define a period of heightened sensitivity to environmental influences, with potential implications for maturation trajectories (Bock et al., 2015; Gao et al., 2019). Among these environmental factors, air pollution, particularly fine particulate matter with aerodynamic diameter ≤ 2.5μ m (PM2.5), has emerged as a significant concern due to its well-documented health risks and widespread prevalence (Block et al., 2012; Kim et al., 2015). PM2.5 consists of airborne solid and liquid particles smaller than 2.5 µm , originating from both natural and anthropogenic sources, with higher concentrations typically found in urban and industrial areas (Kim et al., 2015). Inhaled components of PM2.5 can enter the maternal bloodstream and cross the placental barrier during pregnancy, thereby exposing the developing fetus (Bongaerts et al., 2020; Calderón-Garcidueñas et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2021). Within the gestational environment, PM2.5 is thought to influence brain development by inducing oxidative stress and inflammatory responses, which may, in turn, interfere with cellular processes essential for neural network formation (Calderón-Garcidueñas et al., 2022; Cory-Slechta et al., 2023; Han et al., 2024).

Epidemiological studies have associated prenatal and early-life exposure to air pollution with a range of adverse developmental outcomes, including potential delays in psychomotor and cognitive development (Castagna et al., 2022; Shang et al., 2020), as well as an increased risk of neurodevelopmental disorders (Aghaei et al., 2019; Cory-Slechta et al., 2023; Dutheil et al., 2021). Findings from neuroimaging studies further suggest that air pollution exposure interferes with functional brain development (Morrel et al., 2025; Wylie and Short, 2023). Several studies have reported alterations in functional connectivity (Cotter et al., 2024; Guxens et al., 2022; Kusters et al., 2025; Pérez-Crespo et al., 2022; Pujol et al., 2016a, 2016b; Zundel et al., 2024), often manifesting as atypical patterns of network segregation and integration - key processes in the functional maturation of neural systems (Menon, 2013). For instance, studies have described reduced within-network connectivity in specific systems such as the default mode and attention-related networks (Cotter et al., 2024; Pujol et al., 2016a; Zundel et al., 2024), alongside increased between-network connectivity, suggesting a less distinct differentiation between functional systems (Kusters et al., 2025; Pérez-Crespo et al., 2022). Still, findings remain heterogeneous, as some associations differ depending on the specific pollutants examined or the developmental timing of exposure (Morrel et al., 2025; Wylie and Short, 2023). Additionally, research to date has examined children and adolescents, leaving the specific effects of prenatal air pollution exposure on neonatal functional brain development largely unexplored.

The present study aimed to investigate the potential impact of prenatal PM2.5 exposure on the functional architecture of the cerebral cortex in neonates using Iso-Distant Average Correlation (IDAC) measures of local connectivity. The IDAC measures represent the average functional MRI temporal correlation of a given brain unit, or voxel, with other units situated at increasingly separated isodistant intervals (Macià et al., 2018). By assessing local connectivity across multiple spatial scales, IDAC not only quantifies local functional interactions but also provides a more detailed characterization of the spatial organization of cortical connections (Pujol et al., 2019; 2023). Previous studies have shown that multi-distance IDAC measures capture distance-specific differences in the spatial structure of local connectivity between children and adults (Pujol et al., 2021; 2024), highlighting their sensitivity to age-related changes in cortical functional organization. Notably, in an earlier study, we showed that this functional differentiation of the cerebral cortex into distinct areas, although incipient, remains largely immature in the neonatal brain (Pujol et al., 2024). Considering the heightened vulnerability of the developing brain to environmental influences, we hypothesized that higher levels of prenatal PM2.5 exposure would be associated with alterations in neonatal local connectivity, potentially interfering with the shaping of the emerging functional organization of the cerebral cortex.

Methods

Participants

This study was developed in the context of the European Research Council-Funded project AirNB (ERC-Advanced Grants 2018, agreement 785994), which aims to investigate the impact of prenatal environmental factors on brain development, and was nested in the population-based Barcelona Life Study Cohort (BiSC). Detailed descriptions of the overall project design, participant recruitment and data collection have been reported previously (Dadvand et al., 2024). Briefly, 1080 pregnant women, aged 18 to 45 years and carrying singleton pregnancies, residing in Barcelona, Spain, were recruited between October 2018 and March 2021 during their routine first-trimester hospital visit at three major university hospitals – Hospital Sant Joan de Déu, Hospital de la Santa Creu i Sant Pau, and Hospital Clínic de Barcelona – and their corresponding primary healthcare centers. During the third-trimester follow-up visit, participants from the general cohort were invited to participate in the MRI sub-study. Parents received detailed information about the study’s aims and procedures, and informed consent for examination was obtained for 132 neonates. Recruitment was conducted consecutively with exclusion criteria including premature birth (≤ 37 weeks of gestation), any medical or respiratory disorders, postnatal surgery in the newborn, congenital malformations, and formal contraindications to MRI. Of the 132 neonates with parental consent, 61 provided resting-state fMRI data of sufficient quality and were included in the final statistical analyses.

All parents or legal guardians provided written informed consent prior to participation in the study, which was approved by the Research Ethical Committee (No. 2018/8050/I) of Hospital del Mar, Barcelona, Spain. The study adhered to the guidelines set forth in the Declaration of Helsinki. No compensation was offered to the parents of participating neonates.

Exposure estimates

Air pollution was quantified using PM2.5, defined as fine inhalable particulate matter with a mean aerodynamic diameter of 2.5 µm or less. This pollutant is composed of various chemical constituents emitted from multiple sources, including road traffic, industrial activity, resuspended dust, and combustion processes. In addition to total PM2.5 concentrations, specific estimates were obtained for airborne trace elements included within the PM2.5 fraction, including copper, iron, and zinc.

Maternal exposure to PM2.5 during pregnancy was estimated using Land Use Regression (LUR) models. The development and validation of these models are described in detail elsewhere (Domínguez et al., 2024). Briefly, LUR models were developed according to the European Study of Cohorts for Air Pollution Effects (ESCAPE) guidelines (Eeftens et al., 2012). Three pollutant monitoring campaigns were conducted at 34 representative locations within the study area over an average period of nine days, covering winter, summer, and autumn seasons to capture intra-annual variability. PM2.5 filter samples were collected for a gravimetrical measurement of PM2.5 concentrations. PM2.5 copper, iron, and zinc content was determined by means of Inductively Coupled Plasma-Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS), using the method by Querol et al. (2001). Annual-average spatiotemporal LUR models were initially developed using a step-forward algorithm using data on the 101 main predictors of air pollutant concentrations (e.g., street type, greenness coverage, distance to major roads, population density, road traffic density). The adjusted coefficients of determination (R2) of the final LUR models were 47 % for PM2.5, 90 % for PM2.5 copper, 91 % for PM2.5 iron, and 89 % for PM2.5 zinc concentrations.

Exposure levels were estimated for three microenvironments: home, commuting, and workplace. Total air pollutant exposure was then calculated as the average of these levels, weighted by the time the participant had spent in each microenvironment. Using temporal data from a background station, the spatial estimates of the models were temporally adjusted using the ESCAPE ratio method to estimate hourly exposure levels throughout pregnancy for each participant (Eeftens et al., 2012). Finally, trimester-specific PM2.5 exposure estimates were computed by averaging hourly concentrations.

Participant locations and time-activity patterns were established using multiple complementary data sources. Questionnaire and interview data were used to collect information on residential and occupational addresses, preferred commuting modes, and time spent at home, at work, and commuting at multiple time points during pregnancy. In addition, two one-week campaigns (one in the first and another in the third trimester) were conducted using GPS-enabled smartphones equipped with a validated geolocation application (ExpoApp, Ateknea Solutions, Spain) (Donaire-Gonzalez et al., 2019), providing objective information on participants’ spatial mobility and timing.

In this study, individual exposure estimates were specifically derived for the 1st and 3rd trimesters to differentially assess potential effects of the embryonic and late fetal periods. The embryonic and fetal stages of brain development are qualitatively distinct in terms of key biological events and vulnerabilities (Rice and Barone, 2000). During the embryonic period, early neurogenesis and the initial patterning of cortical and subcortical regions establish the foundational architecture of the central nervous system, making this stage particularly susceptible to disruptions affecting early circuit assembly (Stiles and Jernigan, 2010). In contrast, the late fetal period involves rapid brain growth and progressive circuit formation through synaptogenesis and maturation of long-range projections, processes that may be more susceptible to perturbations affecting the coordination of distributed brain systems (Kostović et al., 2019).

MRI acquisition

MRI was performed during natural sleep following feeding, without the use of sedation, using a 3.0 Tesla Philips scanner (Ingenia CX, Philips Healthcare, Best, The Netherlands) equipped with a 32-channel head-coil. Infants were positioned in an MRI-adapted foam cradle, fitted with hearing protectors, and monitored with a pediatric pulse oximeter to ensure a secure acquisition. The imaging protocol included high-resolution structural imaging, diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), and resting-state functional MRI, which was the focus of this study. A detailed description of the MRI protocol is available elsewhere (Pujol et al., 2024).

Resting-state MRI data were acquired using a conventional single-shot, gradient-echo planar imaging (EPI) sequence sensitive to BOLD contrast with the following acquisition parameters: repetition time (TR): 2000 ms, echo time (TE): 35 ms, pulse angle: 70°, spatial resolution: 2.8 mm isotropic, slice number: 46, total scan time: 6 min (177 whole-brain volumes). Three initial additional dummy scans of each run were discarded to allow for stabilization.

MRI analysis

Functional MRI data pre-processing

Imaging data were processed using Statistical Parametric Mapping software (SPM12, The Wellcome Department of Imaging Neuroscience, London) running on MATLAB version 2017a (The MathWorks Inc. Natick, Mass), along with tools from the FMRIB’s Software Library (FSL; version 6.0.5.1, http://www.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/fsl). To minimize potential distortions of the neonatal brain’s functional anatomy, functional connectivity measures were computed in native space with minimal image preprocessing, avoiding extensive spatial transformations. Precise image processing procedures adopted in the current study have been previously reported (Pujol et al., 2024) and a detailed description is provided in the Supplementary Material. Briefly, preprocessing steps included realignment of the functional images to the mean time series, reslicing to a 2 × 2 × 2 mm resolution, and smoothing using a 4 × 4 × 4 mm full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) Gaussian kernel.

Functional MRI quality control

Motion-affected image volumes were discarded using conventional scrubbing procedures (Power et al., 2014). The parameters resulting from functional MRI realignment were used to detect and remove these volumes. For each subject, mean inter-frame motion measurements (Pujol et al., 2014) served as an index of data quality to flag volumes of suspect quality across the run. Volumes with mean inter-frame > 0.3 mm, the immediately preceding and the succeeding two volumes were all motion discarded. Using this procedure, a mean of 5.1 (SD, 9.3; range 0–47) volumes from the total of 177 vol included in the functional MRI sequence were removed in the final sample.

Iso-distant average correlation (IDAC) maps

Whole-brain IDAC maps were generated by estimating the average temporal correlation of each voxel with all its neighboring voxels placed at increasingly separated Euclidean isodistant intervals (the definition and mathematical formulation are provided in the Supplementary Material). Three IDAC maps were obtained for the distance intervals 1–4, 4–7, and 7–10 mm. The analyses were adjusted by including 6 rigid body realignment parameters, their first-order derivatives, and the global brain signal as regressors. All functional MRI time series were band-passed with a Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) filter letting through frequencies in the 0.01–0.1 Hz interval.

The IDAC maps were estimated in native space and subsequently registered to a study-specific functional MRI template created from the original functional images of all participants, as detailed in the Supplementary Material. After this transformation, the IDAC maps were averaged to generate a mean IDAC image for each distance interval. These mean IDAC images were then used to guide the registration of the native IDAC maps to a common space for subsequent group-level analyses.

Statistical analysis

Participants’ IDAC connectivity maps were included in random-effects second-level group analyses in SPM. Multiple regression models were employed to map voxel-wise the correlation across-subjects between individual PM2.5 exposure measurements and individual brain images. Specifically, we assessed associations between early (1st trimester) and late (3rd trimester) gestation PM2.5 exposure and whole-brain functional connectivity for each of the three IDAC maps at distances 1–4, 4–7, and 7–10 mm. These analyses aimed to identify brain regions where connectivity was significantly associated with exposure levels. Interaction analyses were conducted using a factorial model, incorporating PM2.5 exposure variables as covariates of interest, to test whether the associations with functional connectivity differentially implicated distinct local distances, potentially reflecting changes in the spatial structure of local connections. A motion summary measure mean inter-frame motion across the fMRI run (Pujol et al., 2014) for each participant was included as a covariate in all analyses.

To test the robustness of our findings, we conducted additional analyses with an identical design, adjusting for the following potential confounders: sex (male vs. female), postmenstrual age at MRI scan (weeks), child ethnicity (European vs. other), maternal education (with vs. without a university degree), maternal age at the first trimester of pregnancy (years), average household income at the census tract level (eur/year), maternal smoking during pregnancy (yes vs. no), and season of conception (winter, spring, summer, autumn), collected through clinical records and questionnaires administered during pregnancy. Furthermore, to assess whether the observed associations between PM2.5 exposure and neonatal functional connectivity were driven by specific elemental components of PM2.5, we repeated the main analyses while adjusting for copper, iron, and zinc.

Results were considered significant when clusters formed at a threshold of p < 0.005 survived whole-brain family-wise error (FWE) correction (P < 0.05), calculated using SPM.

Results

Characteristics of study participants

A total of 132 neonates were enrolled in the MRI study. At least one MRI sequence was successfully acquired in 109 cases under natural sleep conditions; however, 48 neonates woke before the completion of the resting-state functional sequence, resulting in optimal-quality fMRI data for 61 participants. The final sample included 32 females and 29 males, with a mean postmenstrual age of 44.1 weeks (SD, 1.2) and a mean postnatal age of 29.5 days (SD, 4.1). No significant differences were observed for most variables (13 of 14, p > 0.05). A nominal difference was observed for total PM2.5 exposure in the third trimester (p = 0.011, corrected p = 0.154). Functional MRI

Voxel-wise multiple regression analyses revealed significant associations between early gestation PM2.5 exposure and neonatal IDAC measures across the three connectivity distance maps, all indicating higher PM2.5 exposure associated with stronger local functional connectivity (Fig. 1). Brain regions showing significant associations consistently included the supplementary motor area and dorsal premotor cortex bilaterally, and right superior parietal cortex. In the middle- (4–7 mm) and long- (7–10 mm) distance connectivity maps, additional significant associations were observed in discrete regions in the primary sensorimotor and visual cortices, paracentral lobule, and cingulate cortex (Fig. 1, Supplementary Table 2). Higher PM2.5 exposure during late gestation was also associated with stronger functional connectivity, though this association was restricted to a small cluster in the right frontal opercular region in the middle- and long-distance connectivity maps (Supplementary Table 2).

We then formally tested whether PM2.5 exposure differentially implicated distinct local distances. A significant interaction effect (PM2.5 exposure x connectivity distance) was found in the right premotor cortex and supplementary motor area, where the positive association between early gestation PM2.5 exposure and functional connectivity was significantly stronger in the long- compared to the short-distance maps (i.e., correlation at short distance < correlation at long distance) (Fig. 2, Supplementary Table 2).

The potential influence of confounding factors, including sex, postmenstrual age at MRI, ethnicity, maternal age, maternal education, household income, maternal smoking, and season of conception was assessed in SPM by adding each variable individually and simultaneously within the model. Adjusting for these variables did not produce any substantial changes to the observed associations (Supplementary Figure 1, Supplementary Table 3). When trace elements (copper, iron and zinc) were accounted for, the associations between PM2.5 exposure and functional connectivity were not attenuated; rather, the spatial extent of these associations broadened, with notable findings in sensorimotor, premotor, and parietal regions across all three connectivity distances (Supplementary Figure 2, Supplementary Table 3).

Discussion

This study investigated the potential effects of prenatal exposure to air pollution on functional brain connectivity in full-term neonates. Specifically, we addressed the extent to which levels of prenatal PM2.5 exposure predict variations in the strength of local functional connectivity within the cerebral cortex and examined its potential influence on the spatial organization of these connections. We also assessed the role of specific trace elements present in PM2.5, including copper, iron, and zinc, in the pollutant’s effects on functional connectivity.

We observed that higher maternal exposure to PM2.5 during early gestation was associated with greater neonatal functional connectivity, particularly in frontal and parietal regions involved in sensorimotor processing, including the premotor cortex, supplementary motor area, and superior parietal cortex. While the trajectory of structural brain maturation is well-characterized, the direction of changes in the development of functional connectivity remains less clearly defined (Luo et al., 2024). Functional maturation is shaped by region- and system-specific strengthening and weakening of connections, reflecting dynamic processes of segregation and integration (Cao et al., 2017b; Gao et al., 2015; Liu et al., 2023; Menon, 2013). Thus, although increases in functional connectivity are a fundamental aspect of typical brain maturation, the ultimate impact on brain function depends on whether they favorably contribute or not to the refinement of neural networks (Cao et al., 2017b; Liu et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2019). Atypically increased connectivity has been observed in neurodevelopmental disorders such as autism spectrum disorder (Ouyang et al., 2017; Uddin et al., 2013), where early-life hyperconnectivity has been associated with greater severity of core symptoms (Chen et al., 2021; Dajani and Uddin, 2016; Supekar et al., 2013) and may reflect a less mature stage of network development.

In the neonatal brain, primary sensory and motor cortices exhibit greater structural and functional maturity compared to association areas, showing more defined cytoarchitecture, earlier myelination, and relatively higher local functional connectivity (Catani et al., 2013; Eyre et al., 2021; Gao et al., 2015, 2017; Kostović et al., 2019; Pujol et al., 2024). These regional differences align with the hierarchical nature of cortical maturation, which generally progresses along a developmental gradient from primary to associative higher-order cortices (Grayson and Fair, 2017; Kostović et al., 2019; Luo et al., 2024; Sydnor et al., 2021; Tau and Peterson, 2010). The association between higher PM2.5 exposure and greater connectivity in sensorimotor areas beyond primary regions may indicate an interference with the typical developmental trajectory of this system. One possible interpretation is that this increased connectivity reflects reduced segregation between network components, suggesting a more functionally immature state. A similar pattern has been observed in neonates at risk for ASD, where local hyperconnectivity has been proposed as an early marker of delayed functional specialization (Ciarrusta et al., 2020). Previous studies have associated air pollution exposure to altered network remodeling in older children (Cotter et al., 2024; Guxens et al., 2022; Kusters et al., 2025; Pérez-Crespo et al., 2022; Pujol et al., 2016; Zundel et al., 2024). Our results extend this body of evidence, suggesting that such effects may already be present in the neonatal period, occurring at an organizational level characteristic of this stage of development. However, additional context is needed to fully interpret its developmental significance.

Conversely, a pollutant-related increase in connectivity in non-primary cortices could also reflect an acceleration of the developmental trajectory within the sensorimotor domain. The emergence of local connectivity in higher-order areas may indicate that the system is functionally maturing. Moreover, beyond measuring short-distance connectivity strength, our multidistance IDAC approach allowed us to examine the impact of prenatal PM2.5 exposure on the spatial organization of local functional connections. In the mature brain, cortical regions exhibit well-defined local connectivity patterns, characterized by a distinct balance of short-, medium-, and long-range functional connections, which delineate major classical anatomo-functional cortical areas (Pujol et al., 2021). In an earlier study, we showed that while this cortical area differentiation is already emerging in the neonatal brain, it remains largely immature (Pujol et al., 2024). Here, we found that the positive association between PM2.5 exposure and functional connectivity was generally uniform across all three local distances; however, this pattern differed in specific cortical regions (i.e., the premotor cortex and the supplementary motor area), where the association with PM2.5 exposure was significantly stronger for long-range compared to short-range local connections. Notably, this distance-dependent interaction could reflect a subtle pollutant exposure effect on accelerating the functional differentiation of these areas.

PM2.5 encompasses a diverse array of components, including airborne trace elements such as copper, iron, and zinc (Kim et al., 2015), which are known cofactors in biological processes (Chin-Chan et al., 2022). The associations between PM2.5 and functional connectivity remained significant after adjusting for these metals, while their spatial extent broadened. This finding suggests that these trace elements of PM2.5 may modulate its effects on functional connectivity, potentially through protective, compensatory, or complex dose-dependent mechanisms. While both excess and deficiency of these elements have been associated with potential harmful effects (Cory-Slechta et al., 2023; Georgieff, 2007; Li et al., 2022; Pujol et al., 2016b) they are also essential for early brain development, contributing to processes such as myelination, synaptic function, and enzymatic activity (Chin-Chan et al., 2022; Georgieff, 2007). Their complex role in neurodevelopment may interact with air pollution exposure in ways that require further investigation to fully elucidate the observed effects.

The precise mechanisms underlying the impact of air pollution on brain development remain incompletely understood. One proposed pathway involves the disruption of the excitatory-inhibitory balance in cortical network activity, which is essential for effective information processing (Cory-Slechta et al., 2023; Han et al., 2024). This potential disruption is particularly concerning during gestation, as key steps in the maturation of the interneuron system occur during this period, shaping the brain’s excitation-inhibition balance (Kirmse and Zhang, 2022; Xu et al., 2011). IDAC measures implicitly provide insights into this balance by informing on the activity of both excitatory pyramidal (principal) neurons and inhibitory interneurons. Previous research has indeed demonstrated that the excitation/inhibition balance expressed in local functional connectivity measures is sensitive to modulation by inhibitory gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) agents (e.g., Blanco-Hinojo et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024). Thus, it is possible that alterations in this balance contribute to the connectivity changes associated with PM2.5 exposure observed in our study.

A limitation of our study relates to the potential influence of head motion on IDAC measures, as motion tends to enhance short-range connectivity (Power et al., 2014; Pujol et al., 2014). This is relevant in our study due to the small brain size of neonates and the definition of local connectivity within a 10 mm radius. We addressed this concern by employing specific motion-correction techniques. The analyzed exams were from neonates who successfully completed the entire functional MRI sequence and remained asleep and still without significant motion, ensuring data quality. Our IDAC results showed a differential effect across distances, supporting their physiological relevance over general artifactual connectivity increases. Also, obtaining anatomically accurate group results for functional connectivity measures in the immature brains of neonates poses challenges due to factors such as the thinner cortex, reversed contrast compared to adults and the underdeveloped morphology of certain gyri and sulci. (Fitzgibbon et al., 2020; Gilmore et al., 2018). To address these technical limitations, functional connectivity measures were computed in native space applying minimal image preprocessing.

A further concern relates to potential exposure misclassification. Although Land Use Regression (LUR) models are widely used to estimate air pollution exposure in epidemiological studies, they have inherent limitations. Specifically, LUR models are optimized for urban, traffic-related pollution and may not fully capture contributions from regional or natural sources. Consequently, the prediction accuracy for PM2.5 was relatively low in our study, yet consistent with that reported for other PM2.5 models previously developed for Barcelona (de Nazelle et al., 2013) and other European settings (Eeftens et al., 2012). In addition, because traffic-related PM2.5 is often correlated with other urban air pollutants such as NOx and O3, residual confounding by co-occurring pollutants cannot be ruled out. Finally, the relatively modest sample size may limit statistical power to detect small effects. However, the study benefits from high-quality neonatal neuroimaging and detailed prenatal exposure assessment, providing novel insights into early brain-environment associations. Future studies with larger samples will be valuable to confirm and generalize these findings.

In conclusion, our findings suggest that prenatal exposure to air pollution may interfere with early brain functional organization, as evidenced by the association between higher PM2.5 exposure and increased local functional connectivity in the neonatal cortex. The observed increases in connectivity, however, are open to alternative and opposing interpretations, where both a delay and acceleration of brain functional maturation may have a place, at least in theory. These results emphasize the complex interplay between air pollution exposure and the dynamic development of functional networks. A valuable direction for future research would be to conduct longitudinal follow-up studies, allowing for the examination of how these exposure-related connectivity changes evolve over time and their potential role in predicting developmental outcomes.

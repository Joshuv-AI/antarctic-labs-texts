Comparative analyses of dynamic transcriptome profiles reveal that DUSP1 regulates myogenic differentiation and muscle fiber type transformation
Authors: F Peng, ZH Song, K Xie, L Chang, D Zhao, Y Cui, X He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractModern breeding strategies have engendered pronounced disparities in muscle mass and quality between indigenous chickens and specialized broilers. Despite these observable phenotypic differences, the molecular regulatory mechanisms that underpin these variations remain largely uncharacterized. The objective of this study was to elucidate the key genes and regulatory pathways that govern the differential development of pectoral muscle between indigenous chickens and specialized broilers. The differences in myofiber development between the fast-growing commercial broiler breed Arbor Acres (AA) and the slow-growing local breed Taoyuan chickens (TY) were compared through phenotypic and transcriptome sequencing of pectoral muscle development at multiple time points. Subsequently, functional validation of the differentially expressed genes between the two breeds was performed using chicken primary myoblasts in vitro. In vivo experiments revealed that AA and TY chickens shared certain commonalities in growth and pectoral muscle development, but at the same time, there were also significant differences between them. The data analysis of the pectoral muscle transcriptome identified that DUSP1 may serve as a crucial regulator underlying the phenotypic divergence in myofiber development between AA and TY chickens. In vitro experiments demonstrated that knockdown of DUSP1 significantly inhibited myogenic differentiation while overexpression of DUSP1 significantly promoted myogenic differentiation. Additionally, knockdown of DUSP1 enhanced p38 MAPK phosphorylation, promoted mitochondrial biogenesis and function, increased the oxidative metabolic capacity of myofibers, and stimulated the formation of type I muscle fibers. Conversely, overexpression of DUSP1 inhibited p38 MAPK phosphorylation and mitochondrial biogenesis and function, increased the anaerobic glycolytic capacity of myofibers, and promoted the transformation of muscle fibers from type I to type II. Blocking the p38 MAPK signaling pathway with SB203580 significantly inhibited the phenotypic changes caused by DUSP1 knockdown. In conclusion, the differential expression of the DUSP1 in the pectoral muscle of AA and TY chickens serves as a critical factor contributing to the disparity in myofiber development between the two breeds. It could promote myogenic differentiation and also regulate the transformation of muscle fiber types through the p38 MAPK/PGC-1α signaling pathway.Keywords: Taoyuan chicken, Myofiber development, DUSP1, p38 MAPK, PGC-1α

Introduction

Muscle tissue constitutes a vital component of the animal physique, and skeletal muscle alone comprises approximately 40% of the total body weight (Janssen et al., 2000). Skeletal muscle exerts a pivotal influence in the regulation of animal metabolism, locomotion, and energy storage (Frampton et al., 2020; Brooks et al., 2023). Besides, the skeletal muscle also contributes significantly to the economic value of animals used for meat. Both of muscle growth rate and meat quality are important economic traits in the broiler industry (Weng et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023). Particularly, in recent years, the domestic market for broiler chicken consumption has witnessed a growing trend of supply failing to meet demand. Moreover, with the continuous improvement of living standards, consumer demand for chicken meat has transcended mere quantitative sufficiency, with an increasing emphasis on the quality and flavor profiles of the product (Campbell et al., 2025). Hence, ensuring the adequate production volume of chicken meat while simultaneously enhancing its quality and safety has emerged as one of the paramount challenges confronting the livestock industry.

As the elemental constituents of muscle tissue, muscle fibers, with their size, number, and type, emerge as pivotal determinants of meat quality. Specifically, the diameter of muscle fibers exhibits a negative correlation with meat quality, while the density of muscle fibers displays a positive correlation. The type of muscle fibers, to a certain extent, dictates the quality of meat (Lee et al., 2010; Shi et al., 2018; Zhou et al., 2024). Therefore, focusing on the development of myofibers is essential for elucidating the formation of meat quality traits. The quantity of muscle fibers is established during the embryonic stage, and postnatal muscle fiber development is predominantly characterized by muscle fiber hypertrophy and type transformation (Gu et al., 2016).

Muscle fiber hypertrophy is predominantly contingent upon the fusion of muscle satellite cells (MuSCs) and protein synthesis. The majority of MuSCs are maintained in a quiescent state after hatching, and their numbers gradually decrease with the development of the organism (Dhawan and Rando, 2005). However, under specific conditions, such as when skeletal muscle is subjected to mechanical trauma or other exogenous signaling stimuli, MuSCs can be activated to enter the cell cycle, and subsequently undergo proliferation and differentiation (Allen and Rankin, 1990). These activated MuSCs fuse with the attached muscle fibers, thereby contributing to muscle regeneration and the augmentation of muscle volume. Another important pathway regulating muscle fiber hypertrophy is protein synthesis. When the rate of protein synthesis in skeletal muscle exceeds that of protein degradation, muscle fibers exhibit hypertrophy.

Muscle fibers can be classified into 4 types based on myosin heavy chain (MyHC) isoform and oxidative metabolic capacity: slow oxidative fibers (MyHC Ⅰ), fast oxidative fibers (MyHC Ⅱa), fast glycolytic fibers (MyHC Ⅱb), and intermediate fibers (MyHC Ⅱx) (Schiaffino and Reggiani, 2011; Liu et al., 2015). During the growth and development of animals, myofiber types are not static; the four types of muscle fibers can interconvert between adjacent types in the order of Ⅰ, Ⅱa, Ⅱx, and Ⅱb. Several studies have elucidated that the FoxO1 signaling pathway, the AMPK/PGC-1α signaling pathway, and the p38 MAPK/PGC-1α signaling pathway all play pivotal roles in regulating the myofiber type transformation (Zong et al., 2002; Kamei et al., 2004; Song et al., 2024).

Characterized by their accelerated growth rates, commercial broiler chickens are often compromised in terms of meat quality (Dixon, 2020). Conversely, indigenous Chinese chicken breeds, epitomized by the yellow-feathered meat chicken, are typically distinguished by their superior gustatory attributes but slower growth rates (Katemala et al., 2022; Xiong et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2024). These divergent phenotypes are underpinned by pronounced disparities in myofiber development between the two breeds. Therefore, taking the fast-growing commercial broiler breed Arbor Acres (AA) and the slow-growing local breed Taoyuan chickens (TY) as the research objects, we compared the myofiber development characteristics and conducted an exhaustive analysis of pectoral muscle transcriptomic sequencing data to identify key genes regulating the phenotypic differences in skeletal muscle development between the two broiler breeds. Subsequently, using chicken primary myoblasts (CPMs) in vitro fortified the credibility of our findings. This study provided a theoretical basis for subsequent in-depth research on myofiber development and the selection of regulatory targets, which could ultimately contribute to the production of high-quality broiler chickens.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

All experimental procedures were approved by the Hunan Agricultural University Animal Ethics Committee (2021015).

Animal treatment and sampling

The fast-growing commercial breeds AA and slow-growing local breeds TY were used in this study. The fertilized eggs of AA and TY chickens were purchased from Henan FengYuan Poultry Breeding Co., Ltd. and Hunan XiangJia Husbandry Co., Ltd., respectively. Fertilized eggs were incubated in a humidified incubator at 37.5°C. A total of 120 male AA chickens (weighing 48.84 ± 3.80 g) and 120 male TY chickens (weighing 33.63 ± 3.34 g) were divided into 10 replicates, with 12 chickens in each replicate and provided with water and food ad libitum under identical conditions after hatching. The experimental diet was formulated by comprehensively considering the recommended standards of the Nutrient Requirements of Yellow Chickens, China (NY/T 3645-2020) and NRC (1994) (Table 1). Ten male broilers per breed were harvested at day 1 after hatching (D1), D7, D14, D21, D28, D42, and D56, respectively. In addition, ten broilers of TY were also executed at D77 and D105 (Fig. 1A). All broiler chickens were weighted before slaughter and the pectoral muscle weight (PMW) was accurately measured after slaughter. The left pectoral muscle tissue of all chickens was quickly collected and frozen in liquid nitrogen, and then stored at – 80°C for RNA extraction and sequencing. The right pectoral muscle tissue of all chickens was used for histomorphological analysis.

CPMs isolation, culture and differentiation

CPMs were isolated from the pectoral muscles of 19-day-old chicken embryos according to the method described by Chen (Chen et al., 2024). CPMs were cultured in DMEM/F-12 (1:1) medium (Gibco, Grand Island, USA) supplemented with 15% fetal bovine serum (Gibco) and 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin (Gibco). Cells were maintained at 37°C in a humidified incubator under 5% CO2. When the confluence of cells reached 90%, CPMs was cultured by differentiation medium containing DMEM/F-12 (1:1) with 2% horse serum without fetal bovine serum to induce differentiation.

Plasmid construction, siRNA synthesis, and transfection

The full-length coding sequence (CDS, NM_001085359.3) of dual specificity phosphatase 1 (DUSP1) were amplified from chicken pectoral muscle cDNA to construct an overexpression vector via Gateway cloning technology. Firstly, the intermediate vector pDown-gga_DUSP1 carrying the target gene was constructed using the BP reaction method (Gateway® BP Clonase™ IIEnzyme Mix, Invitrogen). The sequence was verified to be correct by Sanger sequencing. Subsequently, the LR reaction method (Gateway® LR Clonase™ IIPlus Enzyme Mix, Invitrogen) was employed to recombine the pUp-CAG vector carrying the promoter, pDown-gga_DUSP1, and the pRP[Exp]-EGFP backbone vector, ultimately forming the pRP[Exp]-EGFP-CAG>gga_DUSP1. The DUSP1 primer sequences used for constructing the vector were F: 5′-GGGGACAAGTTTGTACAAAAAAGCAGGCTGCCACCATGGTGAACCTGCGGGTGTG-3′ and R: 5′-GGGGACCACTTTGTACAAGAAAGCTGGGTTCAGCAGCTCGGGGAGGTG-3′ (Sangon, Shanghai, China). The underlined primer sequences correspond to the forward and reverse primers we designed based on the CDS region of DUSP1.

The DUSP1 siRNA interference fragments were used to knock down the mRNA expression of DUSP1. The primer sequences of negative control siRNA (si-NC) were sense: 5′-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3′ and anti-sense: 5′-ACGUGACACGUUCGGAGAATT-3′, and the primer sequences of si-DUSP1 were sense: 5′-GCGAUUGACUUCAUAGAUUTT-3′ and anti-sense: 5′-AAUCUAUGAAGUCAAUCGCTT-3′ (Genepharma, Shanghai, China).

The eukaryotic expression vectors and siRNA were transfected into the CPMs when the cells grew to 70% confluence or induced differentiation for 2 days, using the Lipofectamine™ 3000 Transfection Reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Cells were collected after transfection for 48 h.

p38 MAPK inhibition assay

The differentiation medium containing the p38 MAPK inhibitor SB203580 (152121-47-6, MedChemExpress, Shanghai, China) was replaced 6 h after cell transfection, and the cells were collected 48 h after transfection.

Histomorphological analysis

Muscle samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h at room temperature prior to being dehydrated, embedded into paraffin wax, and sectioned to 5 μm. Subsequently, the slides were stained with hematoxylin and eosin according to routine protocols. Myofiber morphology was observed with Olympus AX70 microscope (Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). Five fields from each slide were randomly selected to measure the muscle fiber diameter (MFD) and muscle fiber density (MFDen) like the procedures previously described by Huo et al. (2021).

Transcriptomic analysis of pectoral muscle

The total RNA was extracted from pectoral muscle using TRIZol Universal reagent (DP424, TIANGEN, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Paired-end RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq) libraries were constructed using the Truseq mRNA-stranded RNA-Seq Library Prep Kit (RS-122-2102, Illumina, San Diego, USA). The sequencing of the libraries with a read length of 2 × 150 bp and an insert size of 380 bp was performed on an Illumina HiSeq X Ten sequencing platform by Shanghai Personal Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Clean reads were generated after the removal of adapters and low-quality reads and reads with >10% poly-N from the raw reads. The clean reads were aligned to the chicken reference genome (Gallus_gallus.bGalGal1.mat.broiler.GRCg7b.dna.toplevel.fa) to yield mapped reads using HISAT2 (v 2.1.0) (Kim et al., 2019). The expression level of genes was estimated using fragments per kilobase per million mapped reads (FPKM) by StringTie (v1.3.6) (Pertea et al., 2015).

DESeq2 (v 1.38.3) (Love et al., 2014) was used to detect the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) of AA chickens or TY chickens at adjacent ages, which were defined as genes with |log2(Fold Change)| ≥ 1 and adjusted P < 0.05 between the two groups. The Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) method-based false discovery rate (FDR) multiple test correction was applied to adjust the P value.

MaSigPro (v 1.78.0) (Ana et al., 2006) was used for time series analysis of transcriptome data. Based on a 6th-order polynomial regression model, the expression trends of genes were fitted, and the fitting conditions of each gene’s time series were calculated. The Benjamini-Hochberg method was used for multiple test correction. To further screen for significantly DEGs betwwen AA chickens and TY chickens, stepwise regression analysis was conducted to calculate the goodness of fit for each gene, and genes with P < 0.05 and R² ≥ 0.7 were selected.

The metabolic pathways or signal transduction pathways with significant enrichment of DEGs were determined through Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis.

The protein-protein interactions (PPI) networks analysis was performed using the STRING database (v 10.5) (Szklarczyk et al., 2017). In brief, the chicken genome was used as reference genome, and all physical subnetworks were displayed according to a confidence score > 0.7 (high confidence). The PPI networks were constructed using the cytohubba plugin in the Cytoscape software (v 3.8.1) (Demchak et al., 2014).

Enzyme activity assay

Intracellular succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) activities were determined by SDH activity detection kit (BC0955, Solarbio, Beijing, China) and LDH activity detection kit (BC0685, Solarbio) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) content assay

Intracellular ATP content was detected using an ATP assay kit (S0026, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Lysis buffer was added to the cells and the supernatant was collected after centrifugation at 12000 g for 5 min. Next, 100 μL of supernatant was added to 100 μL of ATP detection solution, and ATP levels were measured using Varioskan Flash (Thermo Fisher Scientific). ATP content was normalized to the ATP protein content based on a standard curve.

Immunofluorescence assay

To detect the differentiation of CPMs, the immunofluorescence analysis was performed using chicken anti-MYHC as primary antibody (B103, DSHB, Iowa City, USA) and anti-mouse Cy3-IgG as secondary antibody (A0521, Beyotime) on CPMs. Cell nuclei were stained with DAPI (C1002, Beyotime). Cells were visualized under a fluorescence microscope (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany). The myotube area (%) was measured with ImageJ program.

Real‑time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from pectoral muscle and CPMs using TRIZol Universal reagent (TIANGEN) and transcribed into cDNA using the HisyGo RT Red SuperMix for qPCR (+ gDNA Wiper) kit (RT101-01, Vazyme, Nanjing, China). The PCR primer sequences utilized for the determination of the gene expression were shown in Table 2. Gene expression was determined on a RT-qPCR system, using ChamQ Blue Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix kit (Q312-02, Vazyme). Relative quantification of the target gene expression was quantified using 2−△△CT method as previously described methods (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001).

Genomic DNA was extracted from CPMs according to the TaKaRa MiniBEST Universal Genomic DNA Extraction Kit Ver.5.0 (9765, Takara, Beijing, China) instruction manual. The relative content of mtDNA was determined by RT-qPCR. The primers used for mtDNA amplification were shown in Table 2.

The ChemiDoc Imaging System (Bio-Rad, Hercules, USA) was used to visualize the protein bands. Finally, the images were quantified and digitally analyzed using the ImageJ program.

Statistical analysis

Data were shown as the mean ± SD, and analyzed using SPSS 27.0.1 software (SPSS, Chicago, USA). The compariosn of two groups of data was conducted using the independent-sample t-tests. Statistical significance was established at P < 0.05.

Results

Comparison of growth and pectoral muscle development between AA chickens and TY chickens

The body weight (BW) and PMW of AA chickens from D1 to D56 and TY chickens from D1 to D105 were monitored, and AA chickens and TY chickens of the same age were compared. The results showed that BW and PMW of the two breeds of broilers gradually increased with advancing age while the weight of AA chickens was significantly higher than that of TY chickens at 7 time points of the same age (Fig. 1B, C, P < 0.001). The histomorphological examination revealed significant differences between AA chickens and TY chickens. Both in AA chickens and TY chickens, the size of MFD and MFDen continued to increase or decrease with age, respectively. However, at 7 time points of the same age, the size of MFD and MFDen in AA chickens were significantly higher or lower than that of TY chickens, respectively (Fig. 1D–F, P < 0.001). The changes in muscle fiber types at all time point after hatching was shown here (Fig. 1G, H). As the age increased, the mRNA expression levels of MYH7B, a marker gene for Type I muscle fibers, decreased in AA and TY chickens, while the mRNA expression levels of MYH1A, a marker gene for Type II muscle fibers, increased. But we can observe that, at the same age, the mRNA expression level of MYH1A in AA chickens was significantly higher than that in TY chickens, while the mRNA expression level of MYH7B was significantly lower than that in TY chickens (P < 0.001). These results indicated that AA and TY chickens shared certain commonalities in growth and pectoral muscle development, but at the same time, there were also significant differences between them. The growth and development of AA chickens were significantly faster than those of TY chickens.

Comparative transcriptome analyses between AA chickens and TY chickens during muscle development

In order to understand the molecular regulatory mechanism underlying the differences of myofiber development between AA and TY chickens, we profiled the transcriptome of pectoral muscle across 7 developmental time-points in AA and 9 developmental time-points in TY.

Through the comparison between adjacent ages, a total of 3256 DEGs in AA chickens and 3603 DEGs in TY chickens were obtained (Fig. 2A). These genes respectively regulate the temporal differential changes in AA and TY chickens. Then, Pearson correlation coefficient analysis (P < 0.05 and | r | > 0.4) was conducted between phenotypic indicators reflecting myofiber development (PMW, MFD and the mRNA expression levels of MYH1A and MYH7B) with the 16248 expressed genes in AA chickens and 16181 expressed genes in TY chickens, respectively (Fig. S1). A total of 3020 genes in AA chickens and 2788 genes in TY chickens that significantly related to myofiber development were identified (Fig. 2B). Furthermore, through Venn diagram analysis, we integrated the 3020 phenotypic correlation genes (PCGs) and 3256 DEGs from AA chickens, along with the 2788 PCGs and 3603 DEGs from TY chickens. This process revealed 330 genes that serve as key regulators of myofiber development in both AA and TY chickens (Fig. 2C). The results of PPI networks analysis indicated that the top 20 genes with the highest connectivity among the 330 genes were KIF18B, MATK, CDK1, FBN3, PLP1, B2M, BARD1, ECT2, FIGN, PTPN7, TAP2, PLK4, ETNK2, PTPRC, DMB2, RHOBTB3, P2RY6, PANX2, RASSF5, and DUSP1 (Fig. 2D). These 20 genes exhibited similar expression patterns during the development of AA and TY chickens and were identified as hub genes regulating myofiber development in both AA and TY chickens (Fig. S2).

In accordance with the analytical approach proposed by Cardoso-Moreira et al. (2019), we identified a total of 5924 developmentally dynamic genes (DDGs) on myofiber development in AA and TY chickens using maSigPro. These DDGs accounted for the differences observed over time between AA chickens and TY chickens. In order to elucidate the temporal expression dynamics of these DDGs across various developmental stages, we employed hierarchical clustering (hclust) approach to perform temporal clustering on the identified DDGs. Based on their expression profiles, the genes were categorized into 7 distinct transcriptional expression clusters with high statistical confidence (Fig. 2E, P < 0.05). We observed that the genes in Cluster 4 exhibited a characteristic expression pattern, with the expression levels of these 202 genes consistently being significantly higher in AA chickens than in TY chickens. Subsequent KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed on the genes within the 7 clusters (Fig. 2F). The results indicated that Cluster 4, which exhibited a distinct expression profile, was significantly enriched in pathways that are highly associated with myofiber development, such as protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum, MAPK signaling pathway, Lysosome, Foxo signaling pathway and Autophagy-animal.

Through Venn diagram analysis, we intersected the gene set from Cluster 4 with the 20 hub genes previously identified as regulators of muscle fiber development. The analysis revealed that DUSP1 is a common gene within both gene sets (Fig. 2G). Given that DUSP1 is a member of the dual-specificity phosphatase family, it is known to exert negative regulation on the MAPK signaling pathway. This finding indicated that DUSP1 may serve as a crucial regulator underlying the phenotypic divergence in myofiber development between AA and TY chickens.

DUSP1 regulated myoblast differentiation

To elucidate the role of DUSP1 in myogenic differentiation, we constructed an in vitro myogenic differentiation model and assessed the temporal expression profile of DUSP1 mRNA at 5 distinct time points: prior to differentiation (D0), and at 2 days post-differentiation (D2), 4 days post-differentiation (D4), 6 days post-differentiation (D6), and 8 days post-differentiation (D8) (Fig. 3A). The results revealed that as CPMs underwent differentiation, the mRNA expression levels of DUSP1 exhibited a significant upregulation, reaching a peak at D4. Subsequently, the mRNA expression levels of DUSP1 began to decline but remained elevated compared to those at D0 and D2 (Fig. 3B, P < 0.001). The preceding findings demonstrated an intimate association between DUSP1 expression and the process of myogenic differentiation.

To further delineate the impact of DUSP1 knockdown and overexpression on myogenic differentiation, we conducted transfection experiments in CPMs using both siRNA fragments (si-NC and si-DUSP1) and overexpression vectors (pRB[Exp] and pRB[Exp]-DUSP1). The results indicated that, compared with the si-NC group, the mRNA expression level of DUSP1 was significantly downregulated following DUSP1 knockdown (Fig. 3C, P < 0.001); compared with pRB[Exp] group, the mRNA expression of DUSP1 was significantly upregulated following DUSP1 overexpression (Fig. 3G, P < 0.001). These data collectively confirmed that the successful construction of both the DUSP1-specific siRNA and overexpression vectors. Subsequently, we examined the effects of DUSP1 knockdown on the expression of differentiation marker genes. The results demonstrated that, compared with the si-NC group, DUSP1 knockdown significantly downregulated the mRNA expression levels of MyHC (P = 0.024), MyoD (P = 0.039), MyoG (P = 0.005), Myomaker (P < 0.001), Myf5 (P = 0.033), and Myf6 (P < 0.001) (Fig. 3D), as well as the protein expression levels of MyoG (P = 0.041) and MyHC (P = 0.001) (Fig. 3E). Immunofluorescence analysis revealed that DUSP1 knockdown inhibited myotube formation (Fig. 3F). The aforementioned results indicated that DUSP1 knockdown inhibited myogenic differentiation and myotube formation. Conversely, DUSP1 overexpression promoted myoblast differentiation and myotube formation (Fig. 3H–J). In conclusion, the data suggested that DUSP1 promoted myoblast differentiation.

DUSP1 regulated the myofiber type transformation

To elucidate the role of DUSP1 in muscle fiber type transformation, we conducted transfection experiments in CPMs that had undergone differentiation for 2 days, utilizing both siRNA fragments (si-NC and si-DUSP1) and overexpression vectors (pRB[Exp] and pRB[Exp]-DUSP1). As expected, DUSP1 mRNA level was significantly downregulated by si-DUSP1 (Fig. 4A, P < 0.001), and significantly upregulated by pRB[Exp]-DUSP1 (Fig. S3A, P < 0.001).

Subsequent experimental results revealed that, in comparison with the si-NC group, DUSP1 knockdown elicited a significant augmentation in the mRNA (Fig. 4B, P = 0.003) and protein (Fig. 4C, P = 0.001) expression levels of MYH7B, while concurrently inducing a marked suppression of the mRNA (Fig. 4B, P = 0.010) and protein (Fig. 4C, P = 0.034) expression levels of MYH1A. The results indicated that, relative to the si-NC group, DUSP1 knockdown significantly enhanced the activity of SDH while attenuating the activity of LDH (Fig. 4D, P < 0.001), and markedly elevated the intracellular ATP content (Fig. 4E, P = 0.003). At the molecular level, we assessed the mRNA expression levels of glycolysis-related genes and discovered that DUSP1 knockdown significantly downregulated the mRNA expression levels of HK1 (P = 0.021), GPI (P = 0.009), PGAM1 (P = 0.0017), PGK1 (P = 0.011), and PYGL (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4F). Considering that mitochondria are the principal site for aerobic respiration within cells, we investigated whether mitochondrial biogenesis and function are modulated by DUSP1. The results demonstrated that following DUSP1 knockdown, mtDNA content in cells was significantly elevated (Fig. 4G, P = 0.002), and the mRNA expression levels of PGC-1α (P = 0.001), NRF1 (P = 0.001), and TFAM (P = 0.002) were significantly upregulated (Fig. 4H), along with a significant upregulation of PGC-1α protein levels (Fig. 4I, P = 0.024). Meanwhile, we observed that knockdown of DUSP1 significantly promoted the phosphorylation of p38 MAPK (Fig. 4I, P = 0.008). However, when we overexpressed DUSP1, all the above results were completely reversed (Fig. S3).

The preceding findings elucidated that the silencing of DUSP1 engendered a pronounced enhancement of oxidative metabolism within myofibers, concomitantly augmenting mitochondrial biogenesis and functionality, thereby fostering the development of Type I myofibers. In stark contrast, the overexpression of DUSP1 precipitates a shift towards anaerobic glycolysis, concurrently repressing mitochondrial biogenesis and function, and catalyzing the phenotypic transition from Type I to Type II myofibers. It is postulated that the regulatory role of DUSP1 may be mediated through the p38 MAPK/PGC-1α signaling pathway.

DUSP1 regulated the myofiber type transformation via p38 MAPK/PGC-1α signaling pathway

To elucidate whether DUSP1 exerts its regulatory influence on myofiber type transformation via the p38 MAPK/PGC-1α signaling pathway, we simultaneously interfered with DUSP1 and used the p38 MAPK inhibitor SB203580 to block the p38 MAPK signaling pathway. The results demonstrated that, in comparison with the si-DUSP1 group, co-treatment with si-DUSP1 and SB203580 elicited a significant suppression of MYH7B mRNA (Fig. 5A, P < 0.001) and protein (Fig. 5B, P = 0.028) expression, while concurrently inducing a significant upregulation of MYH1A mRNA (Fig. 5A, P = 0.002) and protein (Fig. 5B, P = 0.025) expression. Furthermore, the co-treatment significantly attenuated SDH activity (Fig. 5C, P < 0.001) and augmented LDH activity (Fig. 5C, P = 0.037), thereby leading to a substantial reduction in intracellular ATP levels (Fig. 5D, P < 0.001). Additionally, the co-treatment significantly elevated the mRNA expression levels of HK1 (P = 0.008), GPI (P = 0.021), PGAM1 (P = 0.042), PGK1 (P = 0.045), and PYGL (P = 0.018) (Fig. 5E), while markedly downregulating mtDNA content (Fig. 5F, P = 0.042) and the mRNA expression levels of PGC-1α (P = 0.004), NRF1 (P = 0.002), and TFAM (P = 0.004) (Fig. 5G), as well as the protein expression level of PGC-1α (Fig. 5H, P = 0.020). These data indicated that DUSP1 regulates myofiber type transformation through the p38 MAPK/PGC-1α signaling pathway.

Discussion

Comparisons between different broiler chicken breeds have usually been restricted to 1–2 specific days of age, rather than spanning the entire growth period (Xiao et al., 2024). However, if the timeline is extended to monitor the entire development period and directly compare the growth curves of the two breeds, more valuable information can be obtained. In the present study, we conducted a comprehensive assessment of the BW and PMW of AA chickens from D1 to D56 and TY chickens from D1 to D105. Comparative analysis of the BW and PMW of the two broiler breeds at identical ages revealed that AA chickens exhibited significantly higher values than TY chickens. Moreover, the disparity between the two breeds progressively increased with advancing age. A similar pattern was observed in the MFD of AA and TY chickens. These findings were in accordance with the selective breeding practices employed in the commercial broiler industry, which prioritize the enhancement of pectoral muscle development (Nestor et al., 1988; Vaccaro et al., 2022). The MFDen was found to be inversely correlated with MFD. Specifically, the MFDen of TY chickens was significantly higher than that of AA chickens. Given that previous studies have demonstrated a negative correlation between meat quality and MFD (Lu et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2025), and a positive correlation between meat quality and MFDen (An et al., 2010; Chang et al., 2024), these findings largely elucidated the superior meat quality of TY chickens compared with AA chickens.

The transformation of muscle fiber types is also a pivotal aspect of myofiber development. In this study, both AA and TY chickens experienced a shift from Type I muscle fibers to Type II muscle fibers during myofiber development. However, the proportion of Type I muscle fibers in the pectoral muscles of TY chickens was significantly greater than that observed in AA chickens. Previous studies have shown that, compared with Type II muscle fibers, Type I muscle fibers have a smaller diameter and lower shear force, thus exhibiting better tenderness characteristics (Weng et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023; Mo et al., 2023). Moreover, the classification of muscle fiber types is intricately intertwined with muscle metabolism. Type I muscle fibers (oxidative muscle fibers) predominantly utilize oxidative phosphorylation for energy production and are characterized by a higher mitochondrial density and myoglobin content (Ruple et al., 2021; Reisman et al., 2024). In contrast, Type II muscle fibers (glycolytic muscle fibers) primarily rely on glycolysis for energy generation and are distinguished by a lower mitochondrial and myoglobin content, coupled with elevated ATPase activity and glycogen storage (Stienen et al., 1996; Vigh-Larsen et al., 2022). Consequently, a higher proportion of Type I muscle fibers in muscle tissue results in a redder meat coloration, while a preponderance of Type II muscle fibers predisposes the muscle to more rapid glycolysis post-slaughter, leading to a swift accumulation of lactic acid and a concomitant decline in muscle pH (Lebret et al., 1999; Immonen and Puolanne, 2000; Huo et al., 2022). Therefore, the majority of researchers believe that there is a positive correlation between the proportion of Type I muscle fibers in muscle tissue and meat quality traits. The findings of this study demonstrated that TY chickens have a higher proportion of Type I muscle fibers compared with AA chickens, further supporting the notion that the meat quality of TY chickens is superior to that of AA chickens.

To elucidate the dynamic alterations in myofiber development between AA chickens and TY chickens and to more deeply explain the significant phenotypic differences between the two broiler breeds, we performed transcriptome sequencing on pectoral muscle tissues from AA chickens at 7 time points and from TY chickens at 9 time points. For AA chickens, which are conventionally marketed at D42, the temporal expression profile in this study was extended to D56, thereby encompassing the entire developmental trajectory of AA chickens. In the case of TY chickens, which are typically ready for market when their BW reaches above 2 kg, the average body weight of TY chickens at D105 in this study was 2.065 kg, indicating that the time-course expression profile constructed for TY chickens in this study essentially covered their development. In light of the comparative analysis of commonalities and disparities between AA and TY chicken breeds, we have pinpointed the DUSP1 gene, which was significantly correlated with myofiber development in both breeds and concurrently emerges as a DEG between them. The KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs indicated that DUSP1 was prominently enriched within the MAPK signaling pathway. Previous studies have demonstrated that the MAPK signaling pathway is a pivotal regulatory pathway in the development of myofibers (Schiaffino et al., 2013; Hong et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024). Therefore, it was speclated that the differential expression of DUSP1 between AA and TY chickens may be a pivotal determinant underlying the substantial phenotypic divergence in myofiber development observed between these two breeds. DUSP1 is a member of the dual-specificity phosphatase family, which is distinguished by its capacity for specific dephosphorylation of threonine and tyrosine residues (Farooq and Zhou, 2004). Through its dephosphorylation activity, DUSP1 can inhibit key members of the MAPK signaling pathway, including ERK, JNK, and p38 MAPK (Shen et al., 2016; Celaya et al., 2019; Lane et al., 2022). The phosphorylation of p38 MAPK is intricately linked to cell differentiation and energy metabolism (Coulthard et al., 2009; Martina et al., 2023; Sarg et al., 2024). There were several studies have elucidated that the phosphorylation of p38 MAPK can improve the expression of PGC-1α, thereby promoting the formation of oxidative muscle fibers (Akimoto et al., 2005; Roth et al., 2009; Song et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025). In our study, we found that DUSP1 was a DEG between AA chickens and TY chickens, with consistently higher expression levels observed in AA chickens compared to TY chickens. The pectoral muscle development in AA chickens was markedly more rapid than that in TY chickens, and the expression levels of genes associated with Type I muscle fibers were persistently lower in AA chickens than that in TY chickens. These observations suggested that DUSP1 may act as a critical regulator underlying the divergent muscle fiber development between AA and TY chickens, and it appeared to exhibit a positive correlation with muscle fiber hypertrophy and a negative correlation with the formation of Type I muscle fibers.

To verify whether DUSP1 can regulate muscle fiber development, we employed both knockdown and overexpression approaches to elucidate the role of DUSP1 in myogenic differentiation and muscle fiber type transformation using CPMs. Our findings revealed that DUSP1 can enhance myogenic differentiation and promote the transition of Type I muscle fibers to Type II muscle fibers by inhibiting the p38 MAPK/PGC-1α signaling pathway. Although few studies have reported on the role of DUSP1 in myogenic differentiation, the strategy of screening for DEGs via transcriptomic analysis and subsequently validating their functions is a common practice in the field. Han et al. (2022) found that Flnc was significantly higher in pectoral muscle of fast-growing chicken (Ross-308 broiler) than that of slow-growing chicken (White Leghorn layer), and overexpression of Flnc in vitro can promote myogenic differentiation. Wang et al. (2023) demonstrated that TNFRSF6B-X2 was significantly higher in pectoral muscle of fast-growing chicken (AA broiler) than that of slow-growing chicken (Chinese indigenous Lushi blue-shelled-egg chicken), and overexpression of TNFRSF6B-X2 in vitro can promote myogenic differentiation. The research findings of the two individuals indicated that Flnc and TNFRSF6B-X2 can promote muscle fiber hypertrophy. Our findings were similar to theirs, suggesting that DUSP1 can promote muscle fiber hypertrophy by facilitating the activation of MuScs. However, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying the activation of MuSCs by DUSP1 still require further investigation.

The composition of muscle fiber types is closely related to their metabolic pathways. In the present study, following the knockdown of DUSP1, a significant increase in SDH activity and a significant decrease in LDH activity were observed. Additionally, the mRNA expression levels of glycolysis-related genes were significantly downregulated. These findings collectively indicated that the knockdown of DUSP1 promoted oxidative metabolism and inhibited glycolysis in cells (Wang et al., 2019; Ma et al., 2023). What is more, we also noted a significant upregulation in ATP content and mtDNA content following DUSP1 knockdown, suggesting an increase in mitochondrial biogenesis (Peng et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2022). Subsequently, we examined the expression of genes associated with mitochondrial biogenesis and found that the mRNA levels of PGC-1α, NRF1, and TFAM were significantly upregulated upon DUSP1 knockdown, with a concomitant increase in PGC-1α protein levels. PGC-1α is a transcriptional coactivator involved in the regulation of mitochondrial biogenesis and various energy metabolism processes, including the regulation of carbohydrate and lipid metabolism (Li et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2024). NRF1 and TFAM are downstream regulators of PGC-1α, responding actively to PGC-1α signaling in the regulation of mitochondrial biogenesis (Halling and Pilegaard, 2020; Fan et al., 2021). Knockdown of PGC-1α significantly attenuated mtDNA content and the mRNA expression levels of TFAM and NRF1, leading to impaired mitochondrial biogenesis and weakened oxidative metabolism (Song et al., 2024). However, overexpression of PGC-1α significantly augments muscle mitochondrial biogenesis, leading to a qualitative shift from glycolytic to oxidative energy production in mice and pig skeletal muscle fibers (Zhang et al., 2017). Thus, PGC-1α plays a pivotal role in facilitating the formation of oxidative muscle fibers. In this study, we found that DUSP1 knockdown significantly enhanced p38 MAPK phosphorylation and PGC-1α expression, while overexpression of DUSP1 yielded opposite results. Furthermore, when the p38 MAPK signaling pathway was blocked using the inhibitor SB203580, the phenotypic changes induced by DUSP1 knockdown were significantly inhibited. Based on these findings, we postulated that DUSP1 could regulate the transformation of muscle fiber type via the p38 MAPK/PGC-1α signaling pathway.

Conclusion

In this study, we compared the myofiber development patterns between the fast-growing commercial broiler breed (AA) and the slow-growing local breed (TY), and we found that the significant differences in myofiber development between the two breeds may be related to the differential expression of DUSP1. Furthermore, through in vitro experiments, we demonstrated that DUSP1 is a key gene regulating the differences in myofiber development between AA and TY chickens. These findings not only provide insights into the regulatory mechanism of skeletal muscle development and growth but also provide valuable resources for genomic selection on muscle mass via molecular breeding in the chicken industry.

Effectiveness and Safety of Belotero Intense ± Lidocaine for the Correction of Nasolabial Folds, Marionette Lines, and Lip Volume Enhancement: A Randomized Post-Market Clinical Follow-Up Study
Authors: Peter Arne Gerber, Rodrigo da Mota, Gerd Gauglitz, Daniela Greiner-Krüger, Michael Sebastian, Welf Prager, Maria Kravtsov, Christian Carreon, YingJu Tseng, Sonja Sattler
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough hyaluronic acid–based dermal fillers are widely used, real-world effectiveness and safety data remain limited.ObjectivesTo confirm the effectiveness and safety of Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine (cohesive polydensified matrix hyaluronic acid fillers) in a real-world setting.MethodsIn this multicenter, randomized, rater-blind, uncontrolled, post-market clinical study, 98 participants from 7 sites were randomized 1:1 to Belotero Intense (n = 50) or Belotero Intense Lidocaine (n = 48) (Anteis S.A., Plan-les-Ouates, Switzerland, a company of the Merz Aesthetics group), administered to 2-3 facial areas (nasolabial folds, marionette lines, and upper/lower lips including lip border), with optional touch-up at Week 4. Primary effectiveness endpoints were responder rate (defined as ≥1 point improvement compared with baseline on both sides of the treated area or both lips) at 12 weeks after last injection on the Merz Aesthetics Scales.ResultsThe responder rate was 85.5% for nasolabial folds (95% confidence interval [CI] 78.4, 92.6; P < .0001), 83.5% for marionette lines (95% CI 75.5, 91.4; P < .0001), and 57.4% for lip volume (95% CI 46.2, 68.7; P = .0967). Post hoc analyses using a revised definition of response for the lips (≥1 point improvement in at least one lip) demonstrated a clinical meaningful improvement (85.1% responder rate; 95% CI 77.0, 93.2). The overall incidence of treatment-related treatment-emergent adverse events was 24.5% and was comparable across the 3 treatment areas.ConclusionsUnder real-world conditions, this study demonstrated that Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine were effective and well tolerated in the correction of nasolabial folds, marionette lines and lip volume.Level of Evidence: 3 (Therapeutic)

Facial ageing is a complex process characterized by dermal atrophy, loss and redistribution of subcutaneous fat, and volume changes in the facial skeleton.1,2 Injectable dermal fillers are a widely-used non-invasive treatment for aesthetic facial procedures, including the correction of wrinkles.3 Hyaluronic acid (HA)-based dermal fillers are composed of cross-linked hyaluronan molecules, which undergo isovolumetric degradation in the dermis, holding more water as they become less concentrated.4,5 Although HA-based dermal fillers persist in the dermis for extended periods, the treatment is temporary as the HA is gradually degraded by endogenous hyaluronidases and reactive oxygen species oxidation.6

The effectiveness, safety, and tolerability of HA-based dermal fillers have been demonstrated for a variety of aesthetic treatment indications in clinical settings.3,7-9 However, despite the widespread use of HA-based dermal fillers, real-world effectiveness and safety data remain limited.

Belotero Intense (CPM-I) and Belotero Intense Lidocaine (CPM-I+; both Anteis S.A., Plan-les-Ouates, Switzerland, a company of the Merz Aesthetics group) are cohesive polydensified matrix (CPM) HA-based dermal fillers that are Conformité Européene approved for several aesthetic needs, including the treatment of deep wrinkles, and lip augmentation/volume enhancement.10,11 The addition of lidocaine, a local anesthetic, aims to reduce injection-related pain.

The purpose of this randomized post-market clinical follow-up study was to collect high-quality clinical performance and safety data for Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine when used to correct nasolabial folds, marionette lines, and lip volume under real-world conditions.

METHODS

Study Design

This was a prospective, multicenter, randomized, rater-blinded, uncontrolled post-marketing clinical investigation (trial registration: DRKS00031800). The study was performed at 7 sites Germany in accordance with ISO 14155, and Medical Device (MEDDEV) guidance 2.12/2; compliance with these requirements constituted conformity with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All relevant study-related documents, including the clinical investigation plan were reviewed and approved by an independent ethics committee.

Figure 1 provides an overview of the study design and visit schedule. On Day 1, baseline clinical assessments were performed, followed by randomization and treatment. Injections were administered to a minimum of 2 and maximum of 3 facial areas (nasolabial folds, marionette lines, and upper/lower lips including lip border). At Week 4, an optional touch-up treatment was performed at one or more facial areas treated at baseline, if deemed necessary by the participant and investigator. Participants without touch-up treatment were followed to Week 48, whereas those with touch-up treatment were followed to Week 52.

Study design and visit schedule. Abbreviations: FU, follow-up; TU, touch-up.

Participants

Individuals seeking HA-based dermal filler treatment were recruited through study-specific advertisements or invited to take part in the study by their aesthetic physician. Eligible participants were female or male aged ≥18 years, who sought HA-based dermal filler/volumizing treatment in the face to correct at least 2 of the following facial areas: nasolabial folds, marionette lines, and upper and lower lip volume. The nasolabial folds volume deficit and marionette line volume deficit was required to be moderate to very severe intensity (grade 2-4) on the Merz Nasolabial Folds Scale (MNLFS) and Merz Marionette Lines Scale (MMLS), respectively. Upper and lower lip volume was very thin to moderately thick intensity (grade 0-2) on the Merz Lip Fullness Assessment Scale (MLFAS).

Key exclusion criteria included any previous cosmetic treatment or aesthetic procedure in the lower face within the last 2 years that would interfere with effectiveness assessments, and any prior treatment with calcium hydroxylapatite injectable implants or HA-based dermal fillers in the treatment areas in the last 18 or 12 months, respectively. All participants provided written informed consent and were reimbursed for follow-up visit travel expenses.

Randomization and Blinding

Participants were randomly assigned to Belotero Intense or Belotero Intense Lidocaine in a 1:1 ratio and stratified by site using block randomization. The treating investigator, site staff, all participants, and sponsor/contract research organization clinical investigation team were not blinded to the treatment assignment or facial areas treated.

Primary endpoint assessments were performed by raters who were blinded to treatment assignment, injection volume, and treated areas, to reduce the risk of bias in the assessment of results. The blinded raters (n = 9; 1-2 per site) were aesthetic dermatology practitioners. Efforts were made for the same rater to complete all blinded assessments for each participant.

Treatment

The Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine treatments used in the study were provided by Merz Aesthetics GmbH; participants received all study treatments free of charge. Both treatments are transparent cross-linked sodium hyaluronate gels of nonanimal origin in a physiological phosphate buffer provided in single use prefilled sterile glass syringes. Belotero Intense Lidocaine also contains 3.0 mg/mL of lidocaine hydrochloride. The HA concentration of both treatments was 25.5 mg/mL and contained sodium phosphate buffer to reach a final volume of 1 mL. Injection volume, depth and technique were at the investigator's judgment based on participant expectations, skin condition, and safety considerations. In total, there were 17 investigators who administered treatment across the 7 sites. Both treatments were applied in 2-3 facial areas (nasolabial folds, marionette lines, and upper/lower lips including lip border), as deemed necessary by the investigator and participant, irrespective of the randomization assignment. A combination of linear threading, fanning, and bolus techniques were used at the investigators’ discretion, with needle choice tailored to anatomical region.

Assessments

Baseline assessments included participant demographics, medical history and concomitant conditions, and previous and concomitant therapies. Three validated Merz Aesthetics Scales (MAS) were used to assess the severity of the treatment areas (Supplemental Figure 1, available online at www.asjopenforum.com).12,13 MNLFS was used to assess the severity of nasolabial folds. The MNLFS is a 5-point scale ranging from 0 (no folds) to 4 (very severe folds) and was assessed separately for the left and the right nasolabial fold. MMLS was used to assess the severity of marionette lines. The MMLS is a 5-point scale ranging from 0 (no lines) to 4 (very severe lines) and was assessed separately for the left and the right marionette line. MLFAS was used to assess the severity of volume deficit of lower and upper lips. The MLFAS is a 5-point scale ranging from 0 (very thin) to 4 (very full) and was assessed separately for the upper and lower lip.

Live MNLFS, MMLS, and MLFAS assessments were carried out by the blinded rater at baseline (prior to randomization), Week 4 (prior to touch-up treatment, if applicable), and all other visits. The blinded rater assessed all facial areas, irrespective of whether they were treated or not; however, only the scores for treated areas were recorded. To confirm eligibility of the participants, MNLFS, MMLS, and MLFAS assessments were also completed prior to randomization at baseline by the treating investigator (unblinded).

Standardized, 2D full-face photographs were taken at rest (without smiling or grimacing) with appropriate display of the nasolabial folds, marionette lines, and lips at baseline (pretreatment). At selected sites, photographs were also taken after treatment during the baseline visit, Week 4 (after touch-up treatment, if applicable), and all other visits.

Participant and investigator Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale assessments (pGAIS and iGAIS, respectively) were used to assess aesthetic improvement. Both scales have 7 categories, ranging from −3 to +3, where −3 is very much worse, and +3 is very much improved. The pGAIS and iGAIS were assessed at Week 4 (prior to touch-up treatment, if applicable), and all other visits. Baseline photographs served as a reference for the live investigator and participant assessments conducted at later timepoints.

Four validated FACE-Q instruments14 were completed by participants to evaluate outcomes: FACE-Q—Satisfaction with Outcome (a 6-item scale); FACE-Q—Appraisal of Lines: Nasolabial Folds (a 5-item scale); FACE-Q—Appraisal of Lines: Marionette Lines (a 7-item scale); and FACE-Q—Satisfaction with Lips (a 10-item scale). These scales were assessed at baseline (prior to randomization), Week 12/16 and Week 48/52 (participants without/with touch-up treatment, respectively), for respective treated areas. FACE-Q assessments over time were measured using absolute Rasch-transformed FACE-Q scores (scores ranged from 0 [worst] to 100 [best]).

A likelihood of retreatment survey was completed at Week 12/16 and Week 48/52 (participants without/with touch-up treatment, respectively). Participants were asked how likely they would be to receive future treatment with Belotero Intense or Belotero Intense Lidocaine on a 6-point scale, where 1 = “extremely likely", 2 = “likely", 3 = “somewhat likely", 4 = “somewhat unlikely", 5 = “unlikely", and 6 = “extremely unlikely".

Adverse events (AEs) were recorded at every visit.

Endpoints

The primary endpoints were the responder rate (expressed as percentages) on the MNLFS, MMLS, and MLFAS, respectively, at 12 weeks after last injection. Primary endpoints were assessed by a blinded rater, with response defined as at least 1-point improvement, on both sides of the treated areas (or both lips), compared with baseline.

Additional post hoc analyses included the percentage of responders on the MLFAS at 12 weeks after last injection for the overall population treated in the lips, where response was defined as at least a 1-point improvement in at least one lip compared with baseline.

The secondary effectiveness endpoints were the percentage of participants with any improvement (scores +1, +2, or +3) on the iGAIS and pGAIS at 12 weeks after last injection, as assessed by the treating investigator or self-assessed by the participant.

Other effectiveness endpoints included the percentage of responders on the MAS by study visit (time course), the percentage of participants with any improvement on the iGAIS and pGAIS by study visit (time course), the absolute scores or changes from baseline, if applicable, in Rasch-transformed FACE-Q scores by study visit (time course), and the likelihood of retreatment survey responses at 12 weeks after last injection and at the final visit (Week 48/52). Safety endpoints included the incidence of treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs), TEAEs related to treatment, TEAEs leading to discontinuation, serious TEAEs and serious treatment-related TEAEs.

Statistical Analysis

Sample size calculation assumed one-sided binomial tests with a 70% responder rate based on prior studies. A sample size of 75 participants (treated in at least 2 facial areas) would be required to provide local powers of 87.8%, 90.9%, and 95.0% for α1 = 0.0083, α2 = 0.0125, and α3 = 0.0250. Assuming approximately 15%-20% of participants would not receive treatment to each facial area, a total of 96 participants were required to be randomized to ensure the inclusion of at least 75 participants treated per facial area.

Effectiveness analyses were based on the full analysis set, defined as all randomized participants. The primary effectiveness endpoints were analyzed using one-sided asymptotic binomial tests to evaluate if the probability of being a responder was above 50%, separately for each treatment area. The Bonferroni–Holm procedure was used to control the global one-sided significance level of 0.025 for the 3 statistical tests. One-sided 97.5% Wald confidence intervals (CIs) were provided. Any missing MAS values were imputed using a multiple imputation approach. For secondary and other effectiveness endpoints, the analyses were based on observed cases. Safety analyses were performed on the safety evaluation set (SES), defined as all participants who were exposed to study medication at least once. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA).

RESULTS

Participant Demographics

A total of 98 participants were randomized to Belotero Intense (n = 50) or Belotero Intense Lidocaine (n = 48). Each site randomized and treated 14 participants. Demographics and baseline characteristics were similar in the 2 treatment groups (Supplemental Table 1, available online at www.asjopenforum.com) and each of the 3 treatment areas (Table 1). Most participants were female (95.9%) and White (99.0%), with a mean age of 56.5 (standard deviation [SD] 10.6; range 30-77) years.

Indications and Treatment Exposure

The areas requiring correction were comparable between the 2 treatment groups. A total of 97 participants received treatment in the nasolabial folds, 89 in the marionette lines, and 78 in the upper/lower lip. Overall, the majority of participants received treatment in all 3 facial areas (n = 68; 69.4%). Twenty participants (20.4%) received treatment in the nasolabial folds and marionette lines, 9 participants (9.2%) received treatment in the nasolabial folds and upper/lower lips, and one participant (1.0%) received treatment in the marionette lines and upper/lower lips. In total, 92 participants (93.9%) completed the study and 6 (6.1%) discontinued prematurely; reasons for discontinuation are shown in Figure 2.

Participant disposition.

The mean (median [range]) injection volume per participant at baseline was 1.35 mL (1.10 [0.2-3.0] mL) for nasolabial folds; 1.36 mL (1.00 [0.1-5.0] mL) for marionette lines, and 0.82 mL (0.90 [0.2-2.0] mL) for upper/lower lips. For participants who received touch-up treatment, the mean (median [range]) injection volume at Week 4 was 0.80 mL (0.70 [0.1-2.0] mL) for nasolabial folds, 0.77 mL (0.60 [0.1-3.0] mL) for marionette lines, and 0.49 mL (0.40 [0.1-1.4] mL) for upper/lower lips.

The most common injection technique across all treatment areas was retrograde linear threading. Serial puncture, fanning, and bolus/depot were also frequently used. For all treatment areas, the instrument used for the majority of baseline injections was a 27G 12.7 mm needle (nasolabial folds n = 81/97 participants; marionette lines n = 74/89; upper/lower lips n = 61/78) (Supplemental Table 2, available online at www.asjopenforum.com).

Primary Effectiveness Endpoint

The responder rate on the MAS at 12 weeks after last injection is shown in Table 2. However, the primary endpoint for upper/lower lip was not met: the estimated average MLFAS responder rate at 12 weeks after last injection was 57.4% (95% CI 46.2, 68.7; P = .0967).

Lip-Specific Other Effectiveness Endpoints

For FACE-Q—Satisfaction with Outcome, the mean (SD) Rasch-transformed score for upper/lower lip volume was 63.2 (21.0) at Week 12/16 and 56.8 (24.3) at Week 48/52 (Supplemental Table 4). For FACE-Q—Satisfaction with Lips, the mean (SD) Rasch-transformed score was 39.8 (17.1) at baseline, which increased to 72.6 (22.4) at Week 12/16 and then slightly declined to 61.6 (26.2) at Week 48/52. When asked if they would be willing to receive further treatment to the lips using the likelihood of retreatment survey, 85.3% of participants responded positively (scores of 1 to 3) at Week 12/16 and 77.3% at Week 48/52.

Safety

No AEs occurred between the initiation of study procedures and the start of treatment. A summary of TEAEs is provided in Table 4. Overall, 70 participants (71.4%) reported any TEAEs and 24 participants (24.5%) reported treatment-related TEAEs, which were comparable between the 3 treatment areas and between the 2 treatment groups.

DISCUSSION

This post-market clinical follow-up study supports the clinical performance and safety of Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine for the correction of nasolabial folds, marionette lines, and lip volume. The primary endpoint was met for nasolabial folds and marionette lines; the percentage of participants with at least a 1-point improvement in comparison with baseline at 12 weeks following the last injection was 85.5% (P < .0001) for nasolabial folds, and 83.5% (P < .0001) for marionette lines, whereby the statistical significance was achieved and exceeded the predefined 50% threshold.

The primary endpoint was not, however, met for upper/lower lip volume. Although the responder rate was 57.4%, which exceeded the prespecified threshold of 50%, the lower limit of the 2-sided 95% CI (46.2%) did not exceed the threshold and statistical significance could not be demonstrated. In the post hoc analysis, using the revised response definition requiring at least 1-point improvement in the volume of at least one lip, 85.1% of participants at 12 weeks following last injection (95% CI 77.0, 93.2) met the responder criterion if prospectively defined. Whilst statistical significance was not tested, the responder rate and the lower bound of the 95% CI were both substantially above the 50% target margin, suggesting that Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine are effective for lip volume augmentation.

Several methodological factors may have contributed to this failure to meet the primary endpoint. First, lip volume assessment was based solely on front profile view of MLFAS, causing limitations in capturing 3D features of lip projection and fullness, which are better appreciated from a side profile perspective. The lack of lateral or oblique views could have led to an underestimation of aesthetic improvement. Second, the rate used in the primary endpoint required improvement in both lips. However, because lips are naturally asymmetrical, some participants elected to have treatment to only one lip, further complicating interpretation. Finally, the physicians injected according to their usual clinical practice, without mandated injection volumes, which may have led to suboptimal dosing in one of the lips and contributed to under-response based on the composite criterion.

Despite these limitations, the post hoc analysis results on lip volume align with previous studies that have demonstrated the effectiveness of the Belotero range of fillers in lip augmentation when injected at the appropriate depth.3,15 For example, Kühne et al (2016) observed that 100% of participants reported a ≥1-point improvement in MAS immediately post injection and 89%-96% at 17 days post injection.3 Another study involving 39 participants treated with Belotero Intense Lidocaine showed an increase in lip height of +4.7 mm and +7.2 point improvement in lip appearance on a 10-40 point scale.15 These findings support the effectiveness of Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine in achieving subtle, natural-looking lip augmentation when injected into the appropriate plane. This is in contrast to more superficial injection techniques (often used for products such as Belotero Lips Contour [CPM-I] and Shape [CPM-B with 22.5 mg/mL HA]), which can lead to greater lip volumes.7 This distinction reinforces the post hoc analysis findings by highlighting the importance of both injection depth and product rheology in optimizing aesthetic outcomes.

The clinical meaningfulness of the post hoc analysis results was confirmed by the high improvement rates on both iGAIS and pGAIS, high satisfaction rates on the FACE-Q questionnaires, and willingness by the majority of participants to receive further treatment. This indicates that the failure to meet the primary endpoint for lip volume can be attributed to limitations in the study methodology rather than a lack of product performance.

The highest MAS response rates were recorded at the primary endpoint timepoint (12 weeks after last injection), after which they gradually decreased over the follow-up period, an expected pattern due to the natural degradation of HA fillers over time by endogenous hyaluronidases.16,17 However, at Week 48/52, the majority of participants had at least 1-point improvement in the nasolabial folds, marionette lines, and in at least one lip in comparison with baseline, confirming the effectiveness of Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine for up to 12 months after last injection as perceived by the blinded rater, supporting the longevity of the treatment in all indications. Furthermore, FACE-Q scores indicated high participant satisfaction with treatment, which was maintained throughout the 12-month follow-up period.

iGAIS and pGAIS results were in alignment with the primary endpoint outcomes and were consistent across all treatment areas. Overall, 99.0% and 92.7% of participants showed an improvement at 12 weeks after last injection on the iGAIS and pGAIS, respectively, demonstrating that a large majority of participants experienced significant benefits from their treatment. GAIS improvement rates were slightly lower when assessed by participants in comparison with investigator-assessed scores. This finding is consistent with previous aesthetic studies and may be attributed to the more critical nature of participant self-evaluation.18

Both Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine were well tolerated for all 3 indications and no new safety concerns were identified during the study. A quarter of participants reported treatment-related TEAEs, all of which were transient injection site reactions of mild or moderate intensity. The treatment-related TEAEs were primarily due to the injection procedure and expected due to the route of treatment administration. Incidences of injection site pain and discomfort were lower for Belotero Intense Lidocaine vs Belotero Intense, suggesting that the inclusion of lidocaine may help reduce injection-related pain and discomfort. The safety findings reported here support the well-established safety profile of Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine when used under real-world conditions, consistent with previous reports.3,9

Limitations of the study included the relatively small sample size. Additionally, although variability in the injection techniques amongst different investigators may be perceived as a limitation to treatment outcomes, the multicenter design enabled recruitment of a large and geographically diverse participant population. This approach enhances the representativeness of the study and reflects real-world clinical practice, thereby supporting the validity and applicability of the findings. The lack of side profile photographs should also be noted, as these may have allowed more accurate assessment of lip fullness. Despite these limitations, the findings reported here reflect real-life conditions and the demographics of the study population were representative of the target population, thus confirming the applicability of the study results.

CONCLUSIONS

This randomized, rater-blinded multicenter post-market clinical follow-up study demonstrated that Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine were effective in the correction of nasolabial folds, marionette lines, and lip volume enhancement when administered under real-world conditions. The findings were supported by high global aesthetic improvement scores and participant satisfaction outcomes up to 12 months after last injection. Belotero Intense and Belotero Intense Lidocaine were well tolerated in all 3 indications, confirming previous findings.

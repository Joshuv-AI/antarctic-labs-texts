The expression pattern of EXO70 subunits in single-cell stereo-seq of Arabidopsis thaliana leaves using coexistence network analysis
Authors: Xin Yu, Jingmin Shang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The exocyst component of 70 kDa (EXO70) family in Arabidopsis thaliana comprises at least 23 isoforms, which are crucial for vesicular trafficking via the exocyst complex. However, the heterogeneity of its expression in leaves remains poorly understood. We analyze single-cell stereo-seq from StomicsDB to map the expression patterns of EXO70 subunits in epidermal, mesophyll, and vascular cells, addressing the challenges of sparse gene detection (≈ 2.5% expressed per cell) with a coexistence network framework. We identified cell-type-specific interactions among exocyst subunit EXO70 isoforms, exocyst complex components, and vesicle-associated protein (VAP) members by converting sparse transcriptomic data into binary matrices and calculating coexistence ratios. The results demonstrate that, within Spongy mesophyll cells, EXO70A1, EXO70H7, and exocyst subunit Sec3a (SEC3A) exhibit a significant co-occurrence, as indicated by coexistence permutation tests and correlation analyses. Our findings emphasize the functional specialization of EXO70 isoforms across leaf cell types and validate coexistence networks for analyzing low-expression gene families in complex tissues. This study presents a potential method for examining transcriptional regulators and signaling kinases in single-cell spatial omics.

Introduction

In Arabidopsis, the exocyst component of 70 kDa (EXO70) family consists of multiple isoforms, with at least 23 identified EXO70 genes demonstrating functional diversity and specialization among the different isoforms1. The EXO70 proteins are crucial for the exocyst complex in directing vesicles to the plasma membrane2. The various EXO70 isoforms show distinct expression profiles and functions within the diverse cells of leaf tissues, contributing differentially to vesicular trafficking3. A comprehensive understanding of the EXO70 expression pattern within leaves is essential for enhancing plant development and its capacity to respond to the environment. Unraveling the significance of these differences presents a considerable challenge4,5.

Developing a rational foundational network model is particularly vital for molecules like EXO70, which show low expression levels yet play essential roles as cellular components. Currently, spatial transcriptomics enables gene expression mapping within tissues at cellular resolution6. It has facilitated the construction of a reliable gene expression network using data from sufficient cell samples7. However, the total number of expressed genes in a single cell often varies significantly. The percentage of expressed genes is approximately 2.5%, making constructing a co-expression network impractical8. Given EXO70’s low expression levels and critical functions in cellular processes, constructing an effective network model is challenging. Establishing a co-expression network can often be difficult, requiring a large batch size and relatively high gene expression. Co-expression methods generally need extensive datasets along with available algorithms and tools like WGCNA or ARACNE9,10. It’s crucial to focus on significant network nodes and correlations, even when dealing with low expression levels, and conduct centrality analyses to identify connected nodes within their pathways and networks11.

Here, we defined a coexistence network tailored to single-cell spatial transcriptomics of Arabidopsis leaf cells. We applied this pipeline to elucidate the processes of the EXO70 molecular family in Arabidopsis leaf cells, which comprise upper (adaxial) and lower (abaxial) epidermal layers, mesophyll tissues, and veins. This analytical pipeline aims to reveal spatial organization and leverage the power of spatial transcriptomics to dissect the heterogeneity of EXO70 within the Arabidopsis leaf. We strive to uncover the intricate networks of EXO70 interactions that define the identity and function of various cell types within the leaf.

The EXO70 network, characterized by the STRING database, is a small network mainly comprising the EXO70, vesicle tethering (VETH), and vesicle-associated protein (VAP) families, as illustrated in Fig. 3. We employed this EXO70 sub-network for the subsequent analysis. The genes in the network are not involved in many pathways and show enrichment for exocyst complex components, highlighting their primary role in vesicular transport.

Comparing correlation and coexistence matrices for various gene co-expression patterns across different cell types

Compared to co-expression matrices, coexistence matrices can reveal more correlations, particularly under conditions of lower gene expression levels. Figure 4A presents the coexistence matrix, while Fig. 4B displays the co-expression matrix for upper epidermal cells. After filtering co-expressed genes using the coexistence matrices, the correlation coefficient was calculated for gene pairs in which both genes are expressed and co-expressed in at least 10 cells (Fig. 4C). Using this criterion, the co-expression matrix shows that only EXO70H7 and SEC3A (Spearman correlation test, p < 0.05) exhibit significant correlations (Fig. 4D), whereas other genes display relatively low relatedness. In contrast, the coexistence matrix provides more information, including weak correlations among EXO70, other exocyst subunits, and VETH family members. Our analysis revealed a mosaic of gene expression patterns across the leaf cross-sections, with distinct boundaries between the epidermis, mesophyll, and vascular tissues. The coexistence matrices highlighted this spatial heterogeneity in gene expression, demonstrating the complexity of leaf tissues.

Identification of coexistence elements within networks using the permutation test

Within these networks, we identified key coexistence genes using permutation tests, coordinating the expression of multiple correlated genes in the EXO70 network (Fig. 5). Using the coexistence network, we identified two additional significant coexistence gene pairs: EXO70A1 and EXO70H7, and EXO70F1 and EXO70B1 (permutation test, p < 0.05) in upper epidermal cells (Fig. 5D). These findings suggest a differentially regulated architecture of the EXO70 family, with distinct gene expression patterns to meet the needs of diverse cell types. Discussion

The coexistence of EXO70 networks underscores the significance of exocyst complex components in vesicular transport, reinforcing prior studies on the role of EXO70 in polarized secretion and stress adaptation. Notably, enrichment of SEC family genes in mesophyll coexistence networks may indicate their involvement in chloroplast development, as SEC proteins facilitate the trafficking of thylakoid membranes. SEC3A is one of the two paralogs of the Sect. 3 subunit (the other being SEC3B), while EXO70H7, EXO70F1, and EXO70B1 belong to specific clades within the EXO70 subunit family in Arabidopsis thaliana12. SEC3A directly embeds into the plasma membrane via its N-terminal transmembrane domain, whereas EXO70 subunits (e.g., EXO70B1) bind to phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate through their C-terminal domains. SEC3A-GFP fusion protein exhibits highly polarized punctate distribution in the root apical meristem, vascular bundles, and floral organ primordia, directly reflecting its role as a spatial director for exocyst assembly13. This aligns with previous studies demonstrating cell-type-specific roles for EXO70 subunits and SEC in vesicle trafficking and membrane remodeling1,5. The co-occurrence of EXO70H7 and EXO70A1 in upper epidermal cells suggests a specialized role in stress responses and cell wall modifications14,15. The low expression levels of EXO70 genes underscore the need for coexistence network analysis, which outperforms traditional co-expression methods in detecting subtle yet biologically relevant interactions16. By focusing on co-occurrence rather than expression magnitude, we identified weak but statistically significant associations among EXO70 family members and their interactors, such as EXO70A1 and EXO70H7, and EXO70F1 and EXO70B1.

Although coexistence was observed, it is crucial to note that most EXO70 subunits are regulated post-transcriptionally. For example, Kulich et al.17 show that the EXO70H4 promoter is active in all aboveground tissues, but the green fluorescent protein linked, GFP-EXO70H4 protein, is restricted to trichomes exclusively. In other cell types, it only appears upon elicitor treatment. This is explained by post-transcriptional regulation. Thus, an abundance of RNA, especially of the EXO70H family, does not necessarily correspond to protein levels. Therefore, further analysis may focus on the expression of fluorescent protein fusions under their respective native promoters.

This study establishes a framework for analyzing low-expression gene families in complex spatial tissues. When applying the correlated test to construct a co-expression network in single-cell stereo-seq datasets, sparse expression matrices and cells with low gene expression ratios can present challenges. While calculating the Spearman correlation p-value does not rely heavily on distribution assumptions, it is important to recognize that this method is most reliable with large samples, such as cells with high gene expression coverage like RNA-seq data. We might consider using the permutation test for coexistence analysis to improve robustness. The coexistence pipeline could be adapted to investigate other critical yet poorly characterized gene families, such as transcription factors and signaling kinases18–20. Moreover, this research provides a methodological framework for analyzing gene interactions in low-expression environments across diverse biological systems.

Conclusion

In summary, this work may advance our understanding of EXO70 heterogeneity in Arabidopsis leaves and provide a scalable analytical framework for spatially resolved, low-abundance gene networks in plant systems.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

Sequencing technologies and platforms

Transcriptomic data were obtained from StomicsDB using accession ID STDS000010421. We used processed expression metrics. Preprocessing steps were performed on the original data21 according to the stereo-seq method22. Sequencing was performed using stereo-seq capture chips, which provided high-throughput and high-quality sequencing data for comprehensive analysis of the spatial transcriptome. Fastq files were generated using a DNBSEQ-T10 sequencer. Stringent quality control measures were implemented in the original research to eliminate low-quality reads and low-quality cells, ensuring the use of reliable, robust data. Processed raw count data after quality control, alignment, preprocessing, and filtering were downloaded and used for downstream bioinformatics analysis. A total of 28 data samples were used, comprising 4 biological samples with 7 technical replicates each. After quality control, 26 samples remained. Cells in each sample are about 629. A mean of 16,675 genes is detected in each cell.

Data information

Brief description of the samples in the analyzed data

Arabidopsis thaliana plants (Columbia-0 wild-type ecotype) used for stereo-seq data deposited in StomicsDB were cultivated under controlled environmental conditions, as detailed in the original publication21. Fresh leaves from six-week-old seedlings were excised and promptly subjected to cryosectioning to maintain their native transcriptomic state. The sections were then processed for spatial transcriptomics analysis, ensuring minimal disturbance to the tissue23. Our work reused this data and does not involve collecting or handling plant or seed specimens. Expressed genes are defined as genes with mean expression values > 0 and detected in more than 5 cells and in at least 1‰ of total cells after the original cell filtering and preprocessing steps.

Bioinformatics pipeline

Coexistence analysis methodologies

Network analysis used correlation approaches to identify gene co-expression and coexistence networks.

To construct the coexistence network, we develop a pipeline that computes the coexistence ratio of genes. It takes a sparse gene expression matrix as input. The first step is to verify whether the expression matrix is sparse. Subsequently, the gene expression matrix is transformed into a binary matrix, where all entries are restricted to 0 or 1. During this process, all non-zero elements are assigned a value of 1, indicating that the gene is expressed in the respective cell. Next, if the binary data is sparse, we apply matrix transposition and sparse matrix multiplication to construct the coexistence matrix and determine each gene’s total presence. Afterward, we compute the denominator matrix, derived by summing each gene’s total existence and subtracting the coexistence matrix. Any zero in the denominator is replaced with a small number, epsilon, to prevent division by zero. Finally, we derive the coexistence ratio by dividing the coexistence matrix by the denominator matrix. EXO70 network topology analysis

The EXO70 network was constructed using the STRING database24. We used all EXO70 family genes as seed genes and expanded the network by adding nearby nodes (using the first neighbors method; interaction score threshold: 0.7). STRING performed topology tools and KEGG, and GO enrichment analyses25.

Statistical methods for identifying key genes in co-expression and coexistence networks

Preprocessing for single-cell sequencing was conducted using the scanpy26 in Python. For co-expression network construction, we use the Spearman correlation method. Gene pairs are co-expressed in more than 10 cells, and at least 1% of the total cells are included. P-value was calculated based on Spearman correlation standardized statistics. Benjamini-Hochberg (FDR) was used for multiple testing correction27. The permutation test was used to detect statistical differences for the coexistence probability. P-value was calculated as the proportion of CP values (observed CP) that are over the permutated CP (randomized CP). Permutation time is set to 10,000 as the default.

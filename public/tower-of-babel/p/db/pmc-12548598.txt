Evaluating the efficacy and safety of transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation for systemic lupus erythematosus: a protocol for a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Haomin Sun, Hantong Hu, Zhiyuan Bian, Hanyu Liu, Mei Huang, Siqi Chen, Li Wang, Wenjun Xiao, Yuanyuan Zhang, Jianqiao Fang, Xiaomei Shao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionConventional treatments, like immunosuppressants for systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), are associated with many side effects. Transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation (tVNS) emerges as a promising adjunctive therapy, potentially offering a more benign therapeutic avenue for patients with SLE. The increased number of clinical trials, including randomised clinical trials, highlights the importance of a systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the efficacy and safety of tVNS in treating SLE. Consequently, the aim of this systematic review and meta-analysis protocol is to synthesise the available evidence to elucidate the efficacy and safety of tVNS for the treatment of SLE.Methods and analysisDatabases include PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Embase, Wanfang Database, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, VIP Database and Chinese BioMedical Literature Database. Data selection, extraction and quality assessment by two independent reviewers. Fatigue is the primary outcome, and secondary outcomes include pain, quality of life, negative emotions, adverse events and measures of SLE disease activity. We will use instruments appropriate for each study type to assess the risk of bias. The credibility of evidence will be evaluated using the grading of recommendation, assessment, development and evaluation system. For meta-analysis, we will use RevMan software to perform data synthesis (V.5.4.1). Introduction

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is a complex autoimmune disorder affecting multiple organ systems, including the kidneys, skin, joints and central nervous system.1 Fatigue, mood disorders and musculoskeletal pain are prevalent symptoms of SLE, affecting up to 90%–95% of patients and are associated with a significant reduction in quality of life.2 3 These symptoms not only impact physical health, but also the psychological well-being of patients, leading to increased stress, anxiety and depression.4 Despite advances in traditional treatments, including glucocorticoids, immunosuppressants and antimalarials, current pharmacological and non-pharmacological therapies fail to completely alleviate fatigue, pain and mood disorders in SLE patients.5 6

Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) has emerged as a potential adjunctive therapy to address these unmet needs in SLE management. Recent studies suggest that many SLE patients experience autonomic dysfunction, particularly impaired parasympathetic activity mediated by the vagus nerve.7 VNS may improve symptoms by enhancing neural functions related to arousal and attention8 9 and decreasing the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines through activation of cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathways.10,16 VNS is categorised into invasive VNS and transcutaneous (non-invasive) vagus nerve stimulation (tVNS). Invasive VNS may induce adverse reactions such as cardiac arrest, respiratory arrest,17 numbness, syncope and surgical trauma,15 and recent research into treating lupus erythematosus has mostly employed tVNS. Evidence from recent clinical trials318,20 indicates that tVNS can significantly reduce pain, fatigue and other symptoms in SLE patients without serious adverse effects. Concurrently, research indicates that tVNS may modulate immune responses within the central nervous system, regulate autonomic nervous system function and exert renal protective effects via the spleen-vagus-kidney pathway, thereby demonstrating potential therapeutic effects on disease activity.21,23 In addition, a recent systematic review evaluated the effects of VNS on autoimmune diseases,24 including SLE, and they reported no consistent evidence for the anti-inflammatory effect of VNS in humans. However, their analysis was not exclusively focused on SLE, and the heterogeneity among different autoimmune conditions may limit the applicability of their conclusions to SLE specifically. Therefore, there remains a need for a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis dedicated solely to assessing the efficacy and safety of VNS in the treatment of SLE. Therefore, the aim of our systematic review and meta-analysis is to assess the efficacy and safety of tVNS for SLE patients, which will fill the research gap and address this urgent issue. This is critical to advancing clinical practice and improving patient outcomes.

Methods and analysis

Study status statement

This meta-analysis is currently in the preliminary literature search phase, with an anticipated completion date of May 2026.

Study registration

Our study protocol strictly adheres to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P) statement25 online supplemental S1 file. Additionally, our protocol has been registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO, CRD42024525580).

Eligibility criteria

Types of studies

As tVNS intervention for SLE is still in its early stages, our study will include randomised controlled trials (RCTs), cohort studies, case-control studies and other clinical studies. The following studies will be excluded: case series, case reports and animal studies. There are no restrictions on language, date and publication status.

Types of participants

The participants’ diagnostic criteria will be the 2019 EULAR/ACR (European Union of Rheumatology/American College of Rheumatology) classification criteria,26 the 2012 systemic lupus international collaborating clinics classification criteria,27 or the 1997 ACR criteria28 with no restrictions placed on the age, gender or nationality of participants.

Types of interventions

Studies or trials with tVNS and to ensure a comprehensive evaluation of tVNS’s efficacy and safety, there are no restrictions on the stimulation parameters and manufacturer of tVNS. A preliminary search indicates that most, if not all, relevant studies use tVNS. Therefore, with the aim to reduce clinical heterogeneity, this study will only include tVNS and will not consider invasive VNS (eg, surgically implanted vagus nerve stimulators).

Types of control groups

The no-intervention group, sham tVNS group, placebo group or group before receiving tVNS treatment will constitute the control group.

Types of outcome measurements

Primary outcome

Fatigue is the most prevalent, burdensome and invalidating symptom of SLE, and therefore serves as the primary outcome in this study.29,31 Validated scales such as the fatigue severity scale, multidimensional assessment of fatigue global index, multidimensional fatigue inventory, FACIT-F questionnaire, or other similar tools can be used to assess fatigue in individuals with SLE.

Secondary outcomes

To comprehensively evaluate the efficacy and safety of tVNS for SLE, the following outcomes will be analysed in our study:

Pain intensity is measured using a validated scale, such as the average daily pain score, verbal rating scale, visual analogue scale or numerical rating scale. Higher numbers on the scale indicate more severe pain.

Negative emotions, including anxiety and depression, use a number of internationally recognised scales such as the Depression Self-Rating Scale, the Anxiety Self-Rating Scale, or the Hamilton Anxiety Scale.

Measure the quality of life in patients with SLE using the Lupus Quality of Life and Lupus Patient-Reported Outcome questionnaire, or other validated scales such as EQ-5D-5L.

Measures of SLE disease activity, such as the Systemic Lupus Erythematosus Disease Activity Index, the British Isles Lupus Assessment Group index or other validated tools.

Adverse events

Adverse event data will be considered a key indicator of treatment safety. Data on adverse events reported in all studies will be systematically collected and included, will be categorised and recorded by severity and type, and incidence rates will be calculated as a measure of treatment safety.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Data sources and search strategy

Search strategy

To ensure a broad and unbiased collection of literature, our strategy will include systematic searches of PubMed, Cochrane Library, Embase, Web of Science, Wanfang Database, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Chinese BioMedical Literature Database and VIP Database. In addition, a comprehensive search will be carried out by thoroughly examining three major clinical trial registry platforms: the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, the ClinicalTrials.gov registry and the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform. Potentially eligible publications in these databases and clinical trial registry platforms will be thoroughly searched from the inception of these databases until February 2026, ensuring that the review includes the most current studies in the field. To ensure the integrity of the search and avoid any language bias, we will include all identified studies, regardless of their language of publication.

The search will be created by using a combination of Medical Subject Headings terms and relevant keywords, tailored to match the indexing and search syntax of each database. The search syntax for the PubMed database can be found in the online supplemental S2 file. The search strategies employed in other databases can be found in the online supplemental S3 file.

Search strategy: supplementary approach

In addition, we will carefully review the bibliographies of relevant articles to ensure that no grey literature has been missed during the initial search. In order to obtain comprehensive data on eligible studies, we will proactively contact the lead authors with a reasonable request for additional information that they may have under-reported or disclosed.

Data collection and analysis

Study selection

The selection process for studies will be rigorous and transparent. We will import references from a variety of databases and platforms into EndNote V.20. Two independent reviewers (HS and ZB) will screen the titles and abstracts of identified studies to exclude those that are clearly irrelevant. The remaining studies will undergo full-text screening to determine their eligibility based on the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Any discrepancies between reviewers will be resolved through discussion. If required, a third reviewer (JF) will be consulted to reach a consensus.

The PRISMA (online supplemental S4 file) flow diagram will transparently report the process, clearly illustrating the number of records identified, screened, excluded and ultimately included in the review. Exclusion reasons will be thoroughly documented to provide a clear and comprehensive audit trail.

Data extraction and management

Two reviewers (HL and MH) will carefully and independently extract information from the screened studies. A data collection form will be developed prior to ensuring that all relevant information is extracted from each study. This form will be comprehensive and include sections for study characteristics (eg, authors, publication year, country, study design), participant characteristics (eg, age, gender, diagnosis, sample size), intervention details (eg, stimulation parameters, treatment frequency and duration), comparator details (eg, types of control group interventions, frequency) and outcomes (eg, primary and secondary outcomes, adverse events) and other relevant details. Any discrepancies will be resolved through discussion or, if necessary, in consultation with a third reviewer (JF).

Risk-of-bias analysis

The risk of bias in included studies will be assessed independently by two reviewers (YZ and SC). The non-randomised studies will use the ROBINS-1 tool,32 and the RCTs will use the Cochrane risk of bias tool.33 Individual elements will be evaluated according to the following criteria: high, low or unclear risk of bias. The study’s biases will be thoroughly assessed, including selection bias due to random sequence generation and allocation concealment, reporting bias due to selective reporting, detection bias due to lack of blinding of outcome assessment and self-reported outcomes, attrition bias due to incomplete outcome data and other sources of bias detected by the reviewer. The risk of bias assessment of individual and overall studies that have been included in our study will be demonstrated by figures.

Assessment of the certainty of the evidence

Two reviewers (WX and XS) will independently assess the certainty of evidence presented in this systematic review and meta-analysis using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) system.34 The GRADE system will evaluate the evidence in terms of risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. After evaluating these domains, the GRADE system categorises the overall quality of evidence for each important outcome as ‘high’, ‘moderate’, ‘low’ or ‘very low’. To ensure accuracy, a third reviewer (J-QF) will verify the outcomes of the GRADE evaluation to resolve any discrepancies.

Measurement of treatment effect

Our study will calculate the relative risk and weighted mean difference for dichotomous and continuous variables, respectively. Furthermore, a corresponding 95% CI will be calculated.

Assessment of heterogeneity and data synthesis

We will use the Cochran Q test and Higgins I2 metric, correspondingly, to assess and quantify statistical heterogeneity among RCTs and non-z studies included in the meta-analysis.35 The I2 statistic will be calculated to assess inconsistencies in the results of the included studies.36 When I2 is 50% or less, a fixed effects model can be used for analysis as it indicates little or no statistical heterogeneity. However, when I2 is >50%, sensitivity analysis, subgroup analysis and meta-regression will be conducted to explore possible causes of substantial heterogeneity. Sensitivity analyses will be performed by removing studies with a high risk of bias, studies with small sample sizes (n<20), or by sequentially excluding each study to assess its impact on the overall results. Subgroup analyses will be conducted based on variables such as sex, age group, stimulation parameters of tVNS, duration of tVNS treatment and follow-up time in the clinical trials. When the minimum number of studies included is ≥10, age, tVNS treatment duration, tVNS stimulation parameters and disease duration are used as covariates in the meta-regression. For meta-analysis, we will use RevMan software to perform data synthesis (V.5.4.1). For meta-regression, we will use random effects as the model and the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman as the estimation method. We will choose Stata software (V.17.0) to conduct meta-regression analysis.

If a meta-analysis is not feasible due to significant clinical heterogeneity or an insufficient number of studies for a particular outcome, we will perform a structured narrative synthesis. The results will be summarised in tables and text, following the PRISMA guideline33 and Synthesis Without Meta-analysis guideline.34 This will involve grouping studies by intervention type, population or outcomes and describing the patterns observed across the studies.

Assessment of reporting biases

Funnel plots will be created to evaluate the publication bias of the included literature. If the meta-analysis includes over 10 studies, we will assess the funnel plot’s asymmetry using Begg’s and Egger’s tests.

Ethics and dissemination

All data used in our study will be extracted from published clinical trials, and therefore, no ethical approval is required. Study results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals and relevant academic conferences.

Discussion

TVNS is a promising adjunctive therapy for SLE patients. It has attracted attention as a potential modulator of vagal activity, neuro-immune communication37 and inflammatory pathways.14,16 In particular, tVNS may be effective in relieving fatigue and pain in lupus patients.15 However, the efficacy and safety of tVNS in the treatment of SLE remain uncertain due to the absence of a related systematic review and meta-analysis in this field, despite the growing number of published and ongoing clinical trials.3 31 38 Our study protocol is the first to present a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol on the efficacy and safety of tVNS for the treatment of SLE. We aim to make a small contribution to the field by providing evidence-based insights in the context of limited research on the topic.

A systematic review and meta-analysis of the efficacy and safety of tVNS in treating SLE is both innovative and necessary. It will provide a systematic approach to synthesising evidence from multiple trials, enhancing the reliability and generalisability of findings. The innovative aspect lies in the quantitative synthesis of data, which can reveal trends and effects that may not be apparent in individual studies. In the meantime, feasibility is a key consideration in conducting a systematic review and meta-analysis, and the availability of trials on tVNS for SLE treatment is a positive indicator. The growing number of clinical trials in this area suggests a growing interest and investment in this therapeutic approach.

Despite the promise of our study, some limitations should be considered. The small sample sizes in some trials may limit the statistical power of our study, potentially affecting the precision of the results. Meanwhile, the heterogeneity of SLE symptoms and the variability in tVNS treatment protocols (stimulation parameters, treatment frequency and duration) may pose challenges in pooling data. Subgroup analyses and meta-regression techniques will be employed to account for these variations and to ensure the validity of the meta-analytic findings. Furthermore, the inclusion of poor-quality studies may introduce bias. Finally, as studies yielding negative results are less frequently published, creating a potential risk of publication bias,39 40 we plan to evaluate the publication bias.

In summary, this study will examine the efficacy and safety of tVNS for the treatment of SLE. The findings of this systematic review and meta-analysis will enhance our comprehension of tVNS as a therapeutic intervention for SLE, provide evidence-based support for its use, and provide guidance for future research and clinical applications. Ultimately, this may provide SLE patients with better treatment options and better patient care for their current plight.

Mitochondrial Pyruvate Metabolism Controls Alveolar Stem Cell Fate and Inflammatory Balance
Authors: Zuokuan He, Xue Li, Xuan Miao, Guiying Xu, Sisi Wang, Li Li, Qi Wu, Huaiyong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Alveolar epithelial injury serves as a primary driver of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), where the functional integrity of alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells is essential for lung repair. Mitochondrial pyruvate carrier 2 (Mpc2) is a key component of the pyruvate transport system that sustains mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and energy metabolism in AT2 cells. However, the specific role of Mpc2 in AT2 cell function and lung injury remains unclear. Mpc2 expression in AT2 cells was examined in lung tissues from patients with IPF and in mice subjected to bleomycin-induced lung injury. AT2 cell–specific conditional knockout (CKO) mouse models, single-cell transcriptomic analyses, and AT2 organoid cultures were employed to investigate the functional consequences of Mpc2 deficiency during lung injury. Mpc2 expression in AT2 cells was significantly reduced in patients with IPF and in mice following bleomycin-induced lung injury. Conditional deletion of Mpc2 in AT2 cells resulted in decreased expression of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit (ATP1A1) and surfactant protein C (SPC), impaired AT2 differentiation during lung injury, and a shift in pulmonary inflammatory profiles toward neutrophil predominance. These changes were associated with increased early mortality in male mice after bleomycin challenge. In contrast, female mice with AT2-specific Mpc2 deletion did not exhibit a significant survival disadvantage but developed more severe pulmonary fibrosis and sustained impairment of AT2 cell differentiation at later stages following bleomycin administration. Mpc2 is essential for maintaining AT2 cell function during lung injury and contributes to the regulation of alveolar epithelial differentiation and inflammatory balance. Targeting Mpc2 may offer novel therapeutic opportunities for lung injury-related diseases.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10753-026-02470-1.

Background

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a form of interstitial lung disease with unclear etiology, marked by ongoing fibrotic changes in the lung parenchyma and an irreversible decline in respiratory function [1]. Despite the approval of antifibrotic agents, such as pirfenidone and nintedanib, these treatments only modestly slow disease progression without significantly improving overall survival [2]. Lung transplantation remains the only intervention capable of extending life expectancy; however, its applicability is severely limited by donor scarcity [3]. These challenges underscore the pressing need to better understand the underlying mechanisms of IPF and develop therapies that can actively reverse fibrotic remodeling.

Accumulating evidence indicates that alveolar epithelial injury and impaired repair are central drivers of IPF progression [4] and AT2 cells act as a regulator in these processes. Under normal conditions, the alveolar surface is lined predominantly by alveolar type 1 (AT1) cells which mediate gas diffusion and occupy about 95% of the area, alongside alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells that synthesize surfactant [5, 6]. Additionally, AT2 cells support alveolar fluid clearance by regulating ion and water transport through channels and pumps such as epithelial sodium channels, Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, aquaporin 5 and cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator [7, 8]. AT2 cells play important roles in pulmonary immune defense and contribute to innate immunity by modulating the activity of macrophages, neutrophils, and other immune cells [9, 10]. During lung injury, AT2 cells regenerate the alveolar epithelium by renewing themselves and differentiating into AT1 cells [11, 12]. Multiple factors, such as inflammation, cellular senescence, mitochondrial dysfunction, and metabolic reprogramming can impair AT2 cell function, compromise epithelial repair and contribute to pathological remodeling in the injured lung [13–16]. However, mechanisms regulating AT2 cell function remain unclear and require further investigation.

Given their high metabolic demands, AT2 cells require robust energy production to support surfactant synthesis, proliferation, and differentiation [17, 18]. Energy metabolism and mitochondrial homeostasis in AT2 cells are increasingly recognized as important determinants of epithelial function during lung injury. Previous studies have shown that lung injury is associated with a reduced mitochondrial oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and decreased ATP production in rat AT2 cells [19]. Consistently, AT2 cells isolated from patients with IPF exhibit diminished activities of electron transport chain complexes I and IV [20]. Mitochondrial pyruvate carrier 2 (Mpc2), is a core component of the mitochondrial pyruvate carrier complex, which mediates pyruvate import into the mitochondria to fuel oxidative metabolism [21, 22]. In airway basal cells, Mpc2 deficiency results in reduced regenerative potential of basal cells [23]. Moreover, Mpc2 deletion in alveolar macrophages reduces excessive inflammation following influenza or SARS-CoV-2 infection [24]. While its specific contribution to alveolar progenitor cells such as AT2 cells remains poorly understood.

In this study, we investigated the role of Mpc2 in AT2 cells using lung injury models, AT2 cell–specific conditional knockout (CKO) mice, single-cell transcriptomic analyses, and organoid cultures to examine how Mpc2-mediated mitochondrial pyruvate transport influences AT2 cell function during lung injury.

Methods

Animals

C57BL/6J mice were obtained from Beijing Huafukang Bioscience Co., Ltd., while SftpcCreER, Rosa26mTmG and Mpc2f/f mouse strains were sourced from The Jackson Laboratory. Then we generated SftpcCreER;Rosa26mTmG mouse line using SftpcCreER crossed with Rosa26mTmG and SftpcCreER;Rosa26mTmG;Mpc2f/f mouse line using SftpcCreER;Rosa26mTmG crossed with Mpc2f/f. Mice were bred and maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions at the animal center of Tianjin Haihe Hospital, with controlled 12-hour light/dark cycles and ad libitum access to standard diet and water. For induction of Cre recombinase activity, tamoxifen (50 mg/kg) was administered intraperitoneally once daily for five days to mice aged 8–12 weeks, and follow-up experiments were conducted at intervals of 7 days to allow the corn oil in which tamoxifen was dissolved to be fully metabolized.

This study was designed to investigate the role of Mpc2 in AT2 cell function during lung injury. Two experimental groups were included: Mpc2CKO group (SftpcCreER;Rosa26mTmG;Mpc2f/f) and Control group (SftpcCreER;Rosa26mTmG). Investigators responsible for histological quantification, immunofluorescence analysis, and gene expression measurements were blinded to group identity during data collection and evaluation. Experimental procedures involving animals were reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee of Tianjin Haihe Hospital, in compliance with national regulations (approval ID: 2022HHWZ-007).

Bleomycin-Induced Lung Injury

Lung injury was induced by the intratracheal instillation of bleomycin (2 U/kg) under anesthesia as previously described [25]. Control animals received an equal volume of sterile phosphate buffered saline (PBS). After bleomycin treatment, body weight and clinical signs were recorded daily. At the predetermined time points, mice were humanely sacrificed, and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) together with lung tissues were collected for subsequent analyses.

AT2 Cell Isolation and Organoid Culture

As described earlier [25], animals were subjected to anesthesia followed by tracheal intubation and perfusion through the heart. Lungs were excised and digested with elastase, followed by mechanical dissociation and DNase I digestion. Following filtration and erythrocyte removal, single-cell suspensions were prepared and maintained in HBSS supplemented with fetal bovine serum (FBS), antibiotics, and HEPES. Cells were incubated with a panel of antibodies, including biotin-conjugated CD31, CD34, and CD45, as well as PE-labeled CD24, PE-Cy7-labeled EpCAM, and APC-labeled Sca-1, followed by streptavidin-based secondary detection. Dead cells were excluded using 7-AAD staining. AT2 cells were sorted as CD31–CD34–CD45–EpCAM+CD24–Sca-1– populations using a BD FACSAria™ III. GFP+ AT2 cells were sorted directly without antibody labeling. Sorted AT2 cells (4 × 10⁴ cells/well) were pelleted, resuspended in proliferation medium, and mixed with equal volume Matrigel (50 µL/well). The resulting mixture was subsequently transferred into Transwell inserts (24-well format) for further culture. The proliferation medium was supplied and refreshed every alternate day. Differentiation was induced by switching to the differentiation medium on day 8. Organoids were imaged on day 11 using an Olympus IX73 microscope. Organoid-forming efficiency (OFE, %) was calculated as the percentage of organoids exceeding 50 μm in diameter relative to the total number of AT2 cells seeded. Key reagents and resources are listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Single-cell RNA Sequencing and Data Analysis

Publicly available human lung single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data were obtained from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database under accession number GSE136831 [26]. The sparse count matrix, along with gene identifiers, cell barcodes, and the metadata table provided by the original authors, were imported into R and assembled into a Seurat object. According to the sample annotation in the metadata table, cells derived from patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) were excluded, and only cells from control and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) samples were retained for subsequent analyses. The dataset was then subjected to normalization, dimensionality reduction, and clustering. AT2 cells were identified and extracted based on the original cell-type annotations provided in the metadata table. The relative expression levels of the MPC2 gene in AT2 cells from control and IPF groups were subsequently analyzed and visualized.

For mouse scRNA-seq analysis, lung tissues from Control and Mpc2CKO mice at day 7 after bleomycin challenge were harvested and processed. Lung tissues were enzymatically dissociated into single-cell suspensions using the same protocol as described above, and cell viability was assessed using a fluorescence cell analyzer (Countstar Rigel S2). Viable cells were enriched by magnetic separation and used for construction of 3′-end droplet-based scRNA-seq libraries using the SeekOne® platform. Libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 system with paired-end 150-bp reads. Raw FASTQ files were quality-filtered using fastp and aligned to the mouse reference genome using STAR. Gene expression matrices were generated with featureCounts and further processed using Seurat in combination with EmptyDrops for quality control. Downstream analyses were performed using Seurat v4.3.0.1 [27]. Cells with low gene counts, high proportions of mitochondrial gene expression, or suspected doublets were excluded. After data normalization and scaling, principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted, followed by graph-based clustering and uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) for visualization. Cell clusters were annotated based on established canonical marker genes. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between Control and Mpc2CKO AT2 cells were identified using the FindMarkers function. Gene Ontology Biological Process (GO-BP) enrichment analysis of DEGs was performed using the Metascape online platform (https://metascape.org) [28].

TGF-β1 Stimulation Assay

To examine the effect of TGF-β1 stimulation, sorted AT2 cells were plated on Matrigel-coated culture dishes and maintained at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ incubator with medium containing 5 ng/mL TGF-β1 for 24 h. After incubation, cells were disrupted in TRIzol solution, and the resulting lysates were frozen at − 80 °C for later analysis.

Pulmonary Function Assessment

Pulmonary function was assessed using a FlexiVent system (SCIREQ-FV-FXM2-FEV1, Canada) under standardized conditions. Mice were anesthetized, tracheostomized, and connected to the system using a metal cannula. Parameters including respiratory system resistance and compliance were recorded. The measurements were repeated until three valid replicates were obtained for each mouse.

Histology and Lesion Quantification

Lung samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm. The sections were subsequently stained with hematoxylin and eosin for histological analysis. For lesion quantification, the lesion boundaries, airways, vessels, and entire lobe area were manually outlined using Image-Pro Plus software, and the lesion proportion was calculated with the formula: Lesion area (%) = (Lesion area/(Total area – vascular area – airway area)) × 100%. All histological quantifications were performed in a blinded manner.

Quantification of Pulmonary Edema

For assessment of tissue fluid content, the right caudal lung lobe was isolated and weighed immediately to record its fresh mass. The sample was then placed in a 55 °C drying oven for 24 h to achieve constant weight, representing the dry mass. The extent of pulmonary edema was determined by computing the wet-to-dry weight ratio.

BALF Cell Count and Classification

BALF samples were centrifuged at 300 × g for 5 min at 4 °C. The collected cell pellet was resuspended in PBS, and live cells were quantified using a hemocytometer after trypan blue exclusion staining. Cell smears were stained with Hema 3, and more than 200 cells per slide were classified as macrophages, neutrophils, lymphocytes, or eosinophils under light microscopy.

Protein Quantification in BALF

The concentration of surfactant protein C (SPC) in BALF was measured using a commercial ELISA kit according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Total protein levels were determined separately with a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay, following the instructions provided with the assay kit.

Immunofluorescence Staining and Cell Counting

Paraffin-embedded lung tissues were processed for immunofluorescence staining, including deparaffinization, heat-mediated antigen retrieval, membrane permeabilization using 0.2% Triton X-100, and blocking with 5% normal goat serum. Sections were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, followed by fluorescent secondary antibody labeling and nuclear staining using DAPI. Fluorescent signals were visualized and recorded with an Olympus IX37 microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). Cell counting was performed by the operator using a manual cell counter based on immunohistochemical markers and DAPI. All counts were conducted by the operator in a blinded manner with respect to the specimen conditions. The following antibodies were used: Rabbit anti-GFP (Proteintech, 1:200), Chicken anti-GFP (Abcam, 1:200), Mouse anti-Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 (Abcam, 1:100), Syrian hamster anti-Podoplanin (eBioscience, 1:200), Rat anti-KRT8 (DSHB, 1:50), Donkey anti-Mouse Alexa Fluor 488 (Invitrogen, 1:200), Donkey anti-Rabbit Alexa Fluor 594 (Invitrogen, 1:200), Donkey Anti-Chicken, Alexa Fluor 594 (Invitrogen, 1:200), Rabbit Anti-Chicken Fluorescein isothiocyanate (Sigma, 1:200), Goat anti-Syrian hamster Alexa Fluor 594 (Invitrogen, 1:200), Goat Anti-Syrian hamster Alexa Fluor 488 (Invitrogen, 1:200), Donkey Anti-rat Alexa Fluor 594 (Invitrogen, 1:200).

RNA Isolation and Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qPCR)

Total RNA extraction was carried out using TRIzol reagent according to the protocol detailed in our previous study [29], with no major modifications. Reverse transcription and quantitative PCR were conducted using SYBR Green on a LightCycler 96 platform. Transcript levels were first normalized to β-actin to obtain ΔCt values. The mean ΔCt value of the control group was used as the calibrator to calculate ΔΔCt for each sample, and relative gene expression was determined using the 2^–ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences used in this study are provided in Supplementary Table S2.

Statistics

All statistical analyses and graphical presentations were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 9.5.1). Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For datasets following a normal distribution, homogeneity of variance was evaluated using the F test. Comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t test when variances were equal, or Welch’s t test when variances were unequal. For data that did not meet the assumptions of normality, the nonparametric Mann–Whitney U test was applied. Survival curves were analyzed using the log-rank (Mantel–Cox) test. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Deletion of Mpc2 in AT2 Cells Alters the Inflammatory Profile Toward Neutrophil Predominance

To evaluate the effects of AT2 cell–specific Mpc2 deletion during lung injury, lung wet-to-dry (W/D) weight ratios, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) total protein levels, total and differential BALF cell counts, and pulmonary function were assessed on day 7 after bleomycin administration. An elevated lung W/D ratio was observed in Mpc2CKO mice (Fig. 2A). And the concentration of total protein in the BALF was markedly elevated (Fig. 2B). Analysis of BALF cellularity showed a higher total number of cells per unit volume in Mpc2CKO mice, accompanied by a reduced proportion of macrophages and an increased proportion of neutrophils (Fig. 2C-D). In addition, qPCR analysis revealed increased expression of neutrophil-associated chemokines and receptors, including Cxcl1, Cxcl2, and Cxcr2 in Mpc2CKO lungs (Figure S1A). Expression of the macrophage-associated chemokine Ccl2 was increased, while Ccl3 showed an increasing trend that did not reach statistical significance, and the corresponding receptor Ccr2 was reduced in Mpc2CKO lungs (Figure S1B). These transcriptional changes were concordant with the alterations in immune cell composition observed in BALF. Pulmonary function tests showed increased respiratory resistance (Rrs) and elastance (Ers), along with reduced dynamic (Crs) and static compliance (Cst). Tissue damping and stiffness were also elevated in Mpc2CKO mice (Fig. 2E). These findings indicate that AT2 cell–specific deletion of Mpc2 is associated with increased pulmonary edema and impaired pulmonary function, and a shift in inflammatory cell profiles toward neutrophil predominance following bleomycin-induced lung injury.

Transcriptomic Analysis Reveals Broad Alterations in AT2 Cell Programs Following Mpc2 Deletion During Lung Injury

To further characterize transcriptomic changes in AT2 cells following lung injury, lung tissues from Control and Mpc2CKO mice at day 7 after bleomycin challenge were dissociated into single-cell suspensions and subjected to single-cell RNA sequencing. A total of 22,260 high-quality cells were obtained and classified into 16 distinct cell clusters based on canonical marker genes (Figure S2A–B), Subsequent analyses focused on AT2 cell population. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between Control and Mpc2CKO AT2 cells were identified and subjected to Gene Ontology Biological Process (GO-BP) enrichment analysis.

GO-BP analysis revealed significant alterations in mitochondrial energy metabolism–related pathways in Mpc2-deficient AT2 cells, including processes associated with proton motive force–driven ATP synthesis, oxidative phosphorylation, and the aerobic electron transport chain (Fig. 3A). Consistent with these pathway-level changes, several mitochondrial and metabolic genes were downregulated, including Ndufb7 (complex I), Sdhb (complex II) and Atp5o (complex V) (Fig. 3B). In parallel, DEGs upregulated in AT2 cells from Mpc2CKO mice were enriched in pathways related to neutrophil-associated immune responses, such as regulation of myeloid leukocyte–mediated immunity, neutrophil chemotaxis, and neutrophil migration (Fig. 3C). Correspondingly, increased expression of neutrophil-associated genes, including Cxcl2, S100a8, and S100a9, was observed in Mpc2-deficient AT2 cells (Fig. 3D). Single-cell transcriptomic data also indicated downregulation of pathways involved in ion transport and osmotic regulation in Mpc2-deficient AT2 cells, including magnesium ion transmembrane transport and cellular response to osmotic stress (Fig. 3E). In line with these findings, expression of ion transport–related genes such as Slc12a6, Lrrc8c, and Trpm6 was reduced (Fig. 3F). Given the role of AT2 cells in alveolar epithelial maintenance and regeneration, pathways associated with epithelial structure and organization were further examined. These pathways, including regulation of cell shape and cell junction organization, were broadly downregulated in Mpc2-deficient AT2 cells (Fig. 3G). Consistently, expression of genes associated with epithelial differentiation and structural integrity, such as Etv5, Cav1 and Cldn3 was reduced (Fig. 3H). Collectively, these transcriptomic data indicate that AT2-specific Mpc2 deletion during lung injury is associated with coordinated changes across multiple AT2 cell–intrinsic programs, including metabolic pathways, immune-related responses, osmotic regulation, and epithelial structural organization.

Moreover, expression of Sftpc, which encodes SPC, was reduced, consistent with decreased SPC levels in BALF detected by ELISA (Fig. 4B). As Sftpc expression is regulated by multiple transcription factors, including Nkx2.1 [31], Foxa2 [32], Gata6 [33], and Cebpa [34], qPCR analysis showed the reduced expression of these transcription factors in Mpc2CKO lungs (Fig. 4E). Mpc2 Deficiency Affects AT2-to-AT1 Differentiation During Lung Injury

To explore the cellular mechanisms underlying these phenotypes, GFP⁺ AT2 cells from Mpc2CKO mice were sorted and cultured as organoids under standard culture conditions (Fig. 5A). While the organoid-forming efficiency and organoid size remained unchanged (Fig. 5B and C), qPCR analysis revealed reduced expression of Pdpn and Sftpc, genes associated with AT1 differentiation and surfactant production, respectively (Fig. 5D). Additionally, we performed immunofluorescence staining on lung tissues at day 7 post-bleomycin and found that the proportion of PDPN⁺GFP⁺ AT1 cells derived from AT2 cells in Mpc2CKO mice was also reduced (Fig. 5E and F). In addition, the proportion of KRT8⁺GFP⁺ transitional cells among total GFP⁺ cells were significantly decreased in Mpc2CKO lungs (Fig. 5G and H), indicating an alteration in the transitional differentiation state of AT2 cells following Mpc2 deletion. Collectively, these results show that AT2 cell–specific Mpc2 deletion is associated with changes in AT2-to-AT1 differentiation dynamics during bleomycin-induced lung injury.

AT2-specific Mpc2 Deletion Increases Mortality After Bleomycin-induced Lung Injury

Based on our observations within 7 days after bleomycin treatment, we extended the observation period and found that Mpc2CKO mice exhibited accelerated body weight loss (Fig. 6 A) and significantly lower survival rate (Fig. 6B) within 9 days compared with the control group. Histological analysis showed extensive alveolar destruction and inflammatory infiltration (Fig. 6 C), while qPCR showed upregulation of pro-fibrotic and extracellular matrix–related genes (Tgfβ1, Acta2, Col1a1, and Fn1) at this early time point (Fig. 6D). Together, these data show that AT2-specific Mpc2 deletion is associated with reduced survival following bleomycin-induced lung injury.

Loss of Mpc2 in AT2 Cells Exacerbates Bleomycin-induced Pulmonary Fibrosis in Female Mice

As most bleomycin-induced lung injury models are established in male mice, we additionally examined the response of female Mpc2CKO mice to bleomycin challenge. Interestingly, female mice showed no significant increase in mortality within 14 days after bleomycin treatment (Fig. 7A). However, they still developed significantly increased pulmonary fibrosis in the Mpc2CKO group, as evidenced by H&E staining (Fig. 7B). The quantitative analysis showed a significantly larger lesion area compared with the Control group (Fig. 7C). Hydroxyproline quantification indicated that collagen content was also higher in the Mpc2CKO group (Fig. 7D). In addition, Pdpn expression was significantly reduced in lung tissues from Mpc2CKO mice, whereas Sftpc expression showed a decreasing trend but did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 7E). In line with observations at day 7 in male mice, immunofluorescence staining demonstrated a reduced proportion of PDPN⁺GFP⁺ AT1 cells and KRT8⁺GFP⁺ transitional cells among total GFP⁺ cells in Mpc2CKO lungs (Fig. 7F-I). In summary, although female mice are more resistant to mortality following bleomycin challenge, loss of Mpc2 in AT2 cells is associated with impaired alveolar epithelial regeneration and exacerbated pulmonary fibrosis.

Discussion

This work demonstrates that Mpc2 is essential for maintaining AT2 cell function during lung injury. Mpc2 deletion is associated with transcriptomic suppression of mitochondrial energy metabolism programs in AT2 cells, accompanied by impaired ion transport, altered epithelial differentiation, and changes in pulmonary inflammatory patterns. Furthermore, sex-specific analyses revealed that while Mpc2CKO male mice experienced early mortality following bleomycin exposure, female mice showed better survival but developed more severe fibrosis at later stages.

AT2 cells are central to alveolar homeostasis and regeneration following lung injury [35, 36]. Under physiological conditions, AT2 cells rely predominantly on mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation for ATP generation [37]. Because both MPC1 and MPC2 are required for MPC function [38], Mpc2 deficiency disrupts mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation under stimulatory conditions [39], which may partially contribute to AT2 cell dysfunction. Our single-cell transcriptomic analyses also showed that loss of Mpc2 in AT2 cells during lung injury was associated with downregulation of multiple pathways and genes related to mitochondrial energy metabolism, including oxidative phosphorylation and electron transport chain activity. Similar metabolic defects have been reported in hepatocytes following Mpc2 deletion [40], supporting a potential link between Mpc2 function and cellular bioenergetic homeostasis across different cell types. However, owing to the limited availability of primary AT2 cells, we were unable to directly assess mitochondrial function in this study. As such, the relationship between Mpc2 deficiency and metabolic dysfunction in AT2 cells remains based on transcriptomic associations and requires further experimental validation.

In addition, we observed that Mpc2 deletion reduced the ability of AT2 cells to clear alveolar fluid, as evidenced by an increased lung wet-to-dry weight ratio and decreased expression of ATP1A1, an ATP-dependent Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase essential for active ion transport [41]. Consistent with our findings, Looney et al. reported that reduced expression of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 and α2 subunits markedly decreases maximal alveolar epithelial fluid clearance [42], and impaired alveolar fluid clearance has been closely associated with the development and severity of lung injury [43].

At day 7 after bleomycin-induced lung injury, we observed an increased proportion of neutrophils and a decreased proportion of macrophages in the BALF of Mpc2CKO mice. Consistently, both lung tissue qPCR analysis and single-cell transcriptomic profiling indicated enhanced neutrophil-associated chemotactic signaling. Together, these findings indicate that loss of Mpc2 in AT2 cells during lung injury is associated with a shift in pulmonary inflammatory profiles from a macrophage-enriched state toward a neutrophil-predominant response. Neutrophil-dominant inflammation is a well-recognized feature of the acute phase of lung injury and reflects early inflammatory responses to epithelial damage [44]. These observations are supported by previous studies linking epithelial cell metabolism to inflammatory regulation. For example, Wang et al. reported that mitochondrial pyruvate metabolism in Club cells influences airway inflammation, as Mpc2 deletion in Club cells suppresses CCL17-mediated type 2 inflammatory responses [45]. In addition, Chung et al. demonstrated that AT2 cells can modulate neutrophilic inflammation during lung injury through regulation of mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation [46]. Furthermore, Wu et al. showed that miconazole attenuates LPS-induced lung inflammation by altering alveolar macrophage polarization via metabolic reprogramming [47]. Collectively, these studies, together with our findings, support the concept that metabolic alterations in alveolar epithelial cells are closely associated with the regulation of neutrophil-dominant inflammatory responses during lung injury.

Mpc2 deletion in AT2 cells was associated with altered surfactant-related gene expression. Reduced Sftpc mRNA levels and decreased SPC protein abundance in BALF were observed. In agreement with our observation, the reduction in SPC expression may be associated with impaired lung function, because of the essential role of SPC in maintaining alveolar stability [48]. This may result from impaired mitochondrial energy metabolism caused by Mpc2 deficiency, leading to insufficient energy for SPC synthesis and secretion in AT2 cells. It has been reported that deletion of GCN5L1 in MLE-12 cells markedly decreases the secretion of surfactant proteins and lipids, while mitochondrial re-expression of GCN5L1 can partially restore the levels of genes associated with surfactant synthesis [49], highlighting the functional connection between mitochondrial metabolism and surfactant production. Notably, expression of several transcription factors involved in surfactant-related gene regulation was also reduced, supporting the notion that Mpc2 deficiency perturbs surfactant-associated transcriptional programs.

Furthermore, Mpc2 deficiency disrupted AT2-to-AT1 differentiation, as shown by the reduced Pdpn expression in alveolar organoids and a decreased PDPN+GFP+/GFP+ cell ratio in lung tissue. In addition, the proportion of KRT8⁺GFP⁺ transitional cells among GFP⁺ AT2 cells was also reduced following Mpc2 deletion. These findings suggest that, although bleomycin challenge provides a regenerative stimulus for AT2 cells, Mpc2-deficient AT2 cells exhibit an impaired ability to effectively engage the transitional differentiation program rather than being arrested at an intermediate state. This observation is consistent with a potential role of Mpc2 in supporting the initiation of AT2 cell differentiation during lung injury. AT2-to-AT1 differentiation is an energy-demanding process that requires extensive cytoskeletal remodeling and transcriptional reprogramming [50, 51], and glycolytic metabolism has been shown to be essential for AT2 cell differentiation [52]. In line with these findings, previous studies in models of mitochondrial dysfunction have reported that metabolic insufficiency can disrupt epithelial differentiation programs and compromise alveolar repair [53]. Together with our data, these observations support an association between altered cellular metabolism and impaired AT2 cell differentiation, although direct functional validation of mitochondrial activity in AT2 cells remains necessary.

Sex-specific analyses further revealed distinct phenotypic outcomes following bleomycin injury. In the Mpc2CKO group, male mice exhibited increased early mortality, whereas female mice did not show a significant survival disadvantage. Previous studies have reported sex-dependent differences in bleomycin responses, with male mice generally developing more severe fibrotic injury [54], which has been linked to an imbalanced cytokine milieu characterized by enhanced pro-inflammatory and reduced anti-inflammatory signaling in myeloid cells [55]. Consistent with these observations, male Mpc2CKO mice in our study displayed a pronounced shift in inflammatory patterns at day 7 after bleomycin administration compared with control mice. Based on this finding, it is possible that the relative resistance to early mortality observed in female Mpc2CKO mice is related to a less severe acute inflammatory response. In addition, sex hormone–associated differences may contribute to these divergent outcomes. Estrogen signaling has been reported to promote lung maturation and surfactant production [56], and sexually mature female rodents exhibit higher expression of key ion transporters involved in alveolar fluid clearance, including cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR) and epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) [57–59]. In contrast, male sex hormones have been implicated in exacerbating bleomycin-induced lung dysfunction [60]. In our study, AT2-specific Mpc2 deletion in male mice was accompanied by reduced expression of ion transporters and surfactant-related genes, together with impaired lung function, whereas female mice may retain partial protection through hormone-associated epithelial regulatory mechanisms.

This study provides novel insights into the role of Mpc2 in AT2 cell function during lung injury. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, because only a limited number of primary AT2 cells can be isolated from mouse lungs—and even fewer after bleomycin injury—we were unable to directly evaluate mitochondrial metabolism at the functional level following Mpc2 deletion. Second, this study primarily reports the preliminary in vivo and in vitro findings related to Mpc2 loss in AT2 cells, without further exploration of the detailed molecular mechanisms linking Mpc2 deficiency to altered inflammatory patterns and impaired epithelial differentiation. Finally, our results revealed evident sex-related differences, which should be carefully considered when interpreting the overall significance of the findings. However, sex-specific molecular and inflammatory mechanisms were not directly interrogated in parallel experimental settings. We propose that Mpc2 serves as a key regulator of AT2 cell function and lung injury outcomes; however, further studies are still required before these findings can be translated to the clinical.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study provides evidence that Mpc2 plays an important role in maintaining AT2 cell function during lung injury. Loss of Mpc2 in AT2 cells is associated with disruption of key epithelial processes, including alveolar fluid clearance, surfactant protein production, alveolar repair capacity, and alterations in pulmonary inflammatory patterns. Consistent with these changes, Mpc2 deficiency was accompanied by increased early mortality in male mice, whereas female mice showed no significant change in survival but developed more pronounced pulmonary fibrosis at later stages following bleomycin challenge. Collectively, our work highlights Mpc2 as an important regulator of AT2 cell homeostasis and a potential therapeutic target for lung injury-related diseases.

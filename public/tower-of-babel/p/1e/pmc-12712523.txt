First Report of Daratumumab Therapy in Refractory THSD7A-Positive Membranous Nephropathy
Authors: David Ribes, Stanislas Faguer
Published: 2025

To the Editor:

We read with great interest the manuscript of Ayub et al.1 who reported a case of phospholipase A2 receptor+ (PLA2R+)-membranous nephropathy (MN) refractory to rituximab. Despite complete B-cell depletion over 6 months, nephrotic syndrome persisted, and anti-PLA2R antibodies titers remained high (1:1280). In that patient, 2 infusions of bortezomib, a plasma cell–directed therapy, led to a dramatic and rapid decrease of anti-PLA2R antibodies titers and proteinuria. Targeting both B-cells and long-lived plasma cells has recently emerged as a therapeutic option in patients with refractory autoimmune diseases.

We previously reported the favorable outcome of 5 patients with PLA2R+ MN refractory to B-cell–depleting agents who received bortezomib or daratumumab.2 The total number of patients with refractory PLA2R+ MN treated with plasma cell–directed therapy is therefore 9,2, 3, 4, 5 not 2 as stated in the Ayub report.

Here, we report the case of a 72-year-old male patient with THSD7A+ MN refractory to 5 infusions of rituximab over 4 months. Kidney function decline (estimated glomerular filtration rate: 37 vs. 62 ml/min per 1.73 m2 at baseline) accompanied by nephrotic syndrome (urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio: 8 g/g and serum albumin 11 g/l). Bortezomib was initiated but discontinued after 1 cycle because of an anaphylactic reaction. Daratumumab was initiated (a weekly subcutaneous injection [1800 mg each] for 8 weeks, followed by a bimonthly injection [16 mg/kg each] for 4 months). Prophylactic antibiotics (amoxicillin, sulfamethoxazole / trimethoprim, valaciclovir) were also introduced.

Treatment with daratumumab led to disappearance of anti-THSD7A antibodies by month 4 and was continued until month 9. Serum albumin normalized and proteinuria decreased to < 3 g/g (Figure 1). Treatment was well-tolerated with no adverse event. After 7 months off immunosuppression, circulating CD19+ B cells and anti-THSD7A antibodies reappeared and proteinuria increased to 5 g/g, consistent with immunological and kidney relapse. Daratumumab was subsequently resumed.

Therefore, while awaiting prospective multicentric trial confirming benefit of plasma cell–directed therapies in refractory PLA2R+ MN, we report here for the first time that daratumumab may be effective in treating refractory THSD7A+ MN.

Analysis of risk factors for Takotsubo cardiomyopathy in older patients using large-scale medical reimbursement data
Authors: Toru Mizuuchi, Shuko Nojiri, Wataru Urasaki, Takuya Uematsu, Yuji Nishizaki, Masashi Nagao, Muneaki Ishijima, Shigeki Aoki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Takotsubo cardiomyopathy (TTS) is triggered by mental or physical stress and accompanied by symptoms such as acute myocardial infarction. In previous studies, although several cardiovascular risk factors have been reported, specific risk factors other than these remain unclear, particularly in older populations. In this study, we investigated associations between TTS and other diseases in Japanese high-risk older people. In this matched case-control study, age and sex were used as matching factors from the health insurance database registered with DeSC Healthcare Inc. from April 2014 to October 2022; a designed conditional Lasso regression analysis was used as a variable selection method. The data were categorized into two groups: aged ≥ 70 years and aged 60 to 70 years. A total of 6,512 patients were evaluated. The conditional Lasso regression analysis revealed that among individuals aged ≥ 70 years, the respiratory category had the highest proportion among the 39 disease categories with odds ratios of 1.1 or higher (nine categories [23.1%]). Among individuals aged 60 to 70 years, no specific disease category accounted for a large proportion. Respiratory disease was the main risk factor for TTS among patients aged ≥ 70 years; however, it could not be identified among patients aged 60 to 70 years.

Introduction

Takotsubo syndrome (TTS) is a heart condition first described in Japan in 1990 and subsequently recognized globally. Its name, “Takotsubo cardiomyopathy,” reflects the heart’s characteristic appearance resembling a “Tako-tsubo” (octopus pot) during systole. Although temporary heart failure may occur, the condition is often reversible with time1.

TTS was initially reported in Japan, possibly due to a combination of stress-related cultural factors and heightened awareness. However, it is now recognized globally, with increasing reports from the Nationwide Inpatient Sample (NIS) in North America since 2000. In 2008, data of 6,837 patients with TTS in NIS were analyzed2–4. Although its incidence is relatively high in Japan, global trends suggest it is not limited to any specific geographic region5,6.

Risk factors for TTS include its higher prevalence among postmenopausal women, potentially linked to declining estrogen levels7,8. Additionally, medical procedures such as surgeries and treatments have been associated with TTS episodes9–11. Despite extensive research, the precise etiology remains elusive12–15. To our best knowledge, studies analyzing large-scale data focusing on TTS are scarce, and no specific risk factors for TTS have been identified.

This study aimed to identify risk factors for TTS, focusing on older Japanese patients, a demographic at high risk for TTS development.

Methods

Study design and data source

This matched case-control study utilized a health insurance database of 6,833,165 patients from DeSC Healthcare Inc., in Japan covering the period from April 2014 to October 2022. We identified 3,256 patients aged ≥ 60 years with a primary diagnosis of TTS, using the International Classification of Diseases 10th Revision (ICD-10) code “8842962” and hospitalization data (Fig. 1).

Case data were matched with control data at a 1:1 ratio based on age and sex using the individual matching methodology. The data was not replaced. Patients were divided into two groups: those aged ≥ 70 years and those aged 60–70 years. Although TTS is most prevalent among postmenopausal women aged ≥ 70 years, we evaluated patients aged 60–70 years as a separate subgroup to better elucidate age-related differences in TTS risk profiles. This age group represented a transitional demographic population wherein hormonal, psychological, and physiological changes might begin to influence TTS risk. Previous studies have suggested that TTS in relatively younger patients might manifest different clinical features or triggers compared with older populations7,8,16. We aimed to explore whether distinct risk profiles would exist between the early older (60–70 years) and advanced older (≥ 70 years) populations by analyzing both age groups. Conditional Lasso regression was employed for variable selection, targeting comorbidities diagnosed within one year prior to the TTS index date.

Variables

A total of 2,183 variables were targeted. These included ICD-10 disease codes from A00 to Z99 and Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical Classification System (ATC) codes from A01 to V07. The model for age ≥ 70 years included 115 variables, while the model for age 60 to 70 years included 33 variables upon screening with the conditional Lasso regression analysis algorithm (Fig. 2a, b). The logistic model for estimating the risk factors of TTS included variables of ICD-10 disease codes, such as other diseases of biliary tract; volume depletion; fracture rib(s); sternum and thoracic spine; iron deficiency anemia; hyperparathyroidism; other disorders of parathyroid gland; and variables of ATC codes, such as Antibacterials for systemic use, Anesthetics, Analgesics, Antithrombotic agents, and Antiepileptics.

Statistical analyses

In the two groups, the number of patients corresponding to the ICD-10 disease code was counted. The proportion (%) of patients with ICD-10 disease codes was calculated from the total number of patients in the case and control cohorts.

We selected the conditional Lasso statistical approach in this study because it could effectively analyze data with zero-inflated in matched case-control studies. Furthermore, it could improve the predictive accuracy and interpretability of the statistical model generated by implementing both variable selection and regularization.

The Lasso approach, developed by Tibshirani, is a methodology that improves generalization performance by inserting a regularization term into the loss function, thereby improving prediction accuracy and interpretability17.

Lasso analysis is thought to be effective in the analysis of data with zero-inflated. Generally, the inverse matrix is not available in regression analysis in sparse situations. Therefore, it is mathematically difficult to apply the least-squares method; it is not possible to estimate the coefficients using the regression prediction model. Lasso analysis can estimate the coefficients, even in sparse situations, provided that the parameters are adjusted appropriately. In addition, it has the feature of simplifying the model by estimating the effects of the explanatory variables with small effects as 018.

The model was scored on the test set using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.0).

Our study was approved by the Ethics Committee for Medical Research of Juntendo University School of Medicine (Research permit number E21-0264-M01).

Results

A total of 6,512 patients were surveyed. Of these, 6,010 patients (case 3,005; control 3,005) aged ≥ 70 years, with an average age of 83.78 years; there were 1,446 men (24%) and 4,564 women (76%). The top five ICD-10 categories for the case cohort were Hypertensive disease (39.1%), Diseases of esophagus, stomach and duodenum (37.4%), metabolic disorders (36.1%), other forms of heart disease (32.3%), and dorsopathies (31.4%). There were 502 patients (case 251; control 251) aged 60 to 70 years, with an average age of 65.65 years; these included 162 men (32%) and 340 women (68%). The top five ICD-10 categories for the case cohort were metabolic disorders (34.1%), diseases of esophagus, stomach and duodenum (33.7%), hypertensive disease (29.9%), other diseases of intestines (26.5%), and episodic and paroxysmal disorders (22.3%) (Tables 1 and 2).

The TTS risk assessment using the conditional Lasso model in patients aged ≥ 70 years revealed that among the 39 disease code categories with odds ratios (ORs) of 1.1 or higher, the respiratory category had the highest proportion, with nine categories (23.1%), compared with other categories (malignant neoplasm of bronchus and lung [ORs: 1.347], asthma [ORs: 1.260], Pleural effusion, not elsewhere classified [ORs: 1.258], respiratory failure, not elsewhere classified [ORs: 1.216], unspecified chronic bronchitis [ORs: 1.168], other chronic obstructive pulmonary disease [ORs: 1.158], emphysema [ORs: 1.157], pneumonitis due to solids and liquids [ORs: 1.126], and abnormalities of breathing [ORs: 1.111]) (Table 3). The ROC from the conditional Lasso model for age ≥ 70 years predicted respiratory diseases as an important risk factor [AUC0.804 (95%CI: 0.793–0.815)] (Fig. 3a). TTS risk assessment using conditional Lasso model for patients aged 60 to 70 years predicted the following as risk factors among the 13 disease code categories with 1.1 or higher ORs: Heart failure [ORs: 1.577], depression [depressive episode ORs: 1.575], and asthma [ORs: 1.424] (Table 5). However, no specific disease category accounted for a large proportion. The ROC from the conditional Lasso model analysis for patients aged 60 to 70 years did not lead to the identification of risk factors [AUC 0.856(95%CI: 0.823–0.889)] (Fig. 3b).

Discussion

Using an administrative database, we estimated respiratory diseases as the main risk factors for TTS in patients aged ≥ 70 years. However, the risk factors could not be identified among patients aged 60 to 70 years. Conditional Lasso regression analysis of asthma predicted an [ORs:1.260] and that of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) predicted an [ORs:1. 158] for patients aged ≥ 70 years in this study (Table 3). COPD and asthma are common diseases but are associated with significant morbidity and mortality19. COPD and asthma have been identified in several patients with TTS; the prevalence of these diseases in patients with TTS is significantly higher than that in the general population (COPD: 10.1%20, asthma: 25%21. Although the analysis using the disease codes predicted the association of respiratory diseases, the analysis using the medication codes did not reveal significant ORs for respiratory medications. Commonly used medications include anticholinergics, β2-agonists, aminophylline, and corticosteroids, and these have been reported as triggers for TTS22. Corticosteroids for systemic use was predicted with an [ORs:1.129] [ATC-code category: H02] using the conditional Lasso analysis. However, other respiratory medications that include β2-agonists, aminophylline [ATC-code category: R03], and anticholinergics [ATC-code category: N04] were not predicted in this study (Table 4). The reason for this unpredicted result is unclear; it is noteworthy that the age range of the present study’s population is higher than that of the previous study’s population.

In this study, 75.94% (4,564/6,010) of those aged ≥ 70 years were women, and 67.73% (340/502) of those aged 60 to 70 years were women (Table 2). TTS is also more prevalent in older postmenopausal women7,8,23. The analysis results obtained in the present study also indicated that the proportion of TTS was high among older women. Sex differences represent a critical factor in the development and clinical outcomes of TTS. Numerous studies have demonstrated that TTS predominantly affects postmenopausal women, whereas male patients, despite being less frequently affected, tend to experience more severe complications and worse prognoses. This phenomenon has been well-documented in previous studies7,8,16. In our study, 75.94% of patients with TTS aged ≥ 70 years and 67.73% of those aged 60–70 years were women, confirming the predominance of this syndrome in women. These findings highlight the importance for considering sex differences in risk stratification and clinical management of TTS.

Conditional Lasso analysis in patients aged 60 to 70 years predicted depression with an [ORs: 1.575] in this study (Table 5). Stress is also one of the main risk factors for TTS. Conventional mechanisms for the development of TTS include coronary spasms, plaque rupture, sympathetic abnormalities, microcirculatory disturbances, and female hormone deficiency24. Patients with TTS have a higher prevalence of neurological and psychiatric disorders than patients with acute coronary syndromes16 and often experience emotional or physical triggers first25. This is because abnormal cardiac function (the brain-heart axis) in neurological and psychiatric disorders is thought to affect TTS; over-secretion of catecholamine is a potential factor in the development of TTS26,27. Catecholamines are a generic term for neurotransmitters synthesized and secreted by the adrenal glands28. However, as stress was not measured in this study, the effect of stress on TTS was not identified.

The analysis of medications other than those used for respiratory diseases revealed that Antibacterials for systemic use had the highest [ORs:3.016] in patients aged ≥ 70 years (Table 4) and the fourth highest [ORs: 1.803] in patients aged 60 to 70 years among the ATC code categories (Table 6). Systemic antibacterial agents have been associated with risk factors for TTS in infectious diseases29. The results of our study are predicted to be related to the association between systemic antibiotics and TTS. Antithrombotic agents had the fourth highest [ORs: 1.668] in patients aged ≥ 70 years (Table 4) and the highest [ORs: 3.999] in patients aged 60 to 70 years (Table 6). Aspirin is an anti-thrombotic agent and can decrease the plasma level of several inflammatory biomarkers; this indicates the possibility of improved prognosis in TTS30. However, the side effects of aspirin have been reported in patients with coronary artery disease who are administered angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs)31; patients with TTS who are administered ACEIs may also experience these effects. The present study’s findings revealed that Antithrombotic agents had a high ORs in the risk factor analysis of TTS. Anesthetics had the second highest [ORs: 2.066] in patients aged ≥ 70 years (Table 4) and the third highest [ORs: 2.638] in patients aged 60 to 70 years (Table 6). Various surgery types may be associated with stress and may trigger TTS, and some procedures involve the administration of anesthetics32. Therefore, we assume that anesthetics were also predicted as a risk factor with a high ORs for TTS in the present study. Diuretics had the sixth highest [ORs: 1.639] in patients aged ≥ 70 years (Table 4) and the second highest [ORs: 3.075] in patients aged 60 to 70 years (Table 6). Diuretics are administered to reduce the strain on the heart and remove excess water from the body. However, some studies have reported that diuretic therapy should be carefully considered because TTS was triggered in older patients with mineralocorticoid-responsive hyponatremia33. Diuretics were also predicted to be a risk factor for TTS in older patients in the present study.

To our best knowledge, this is the first study to use a large-scale data set of approximately 6 million patients in Japan, where the population is aging rapidly, and conditional logistic Lasso regression to identify the risk factors for TTS from a large number of variables. Another strength point of this study is its unique analysis of disease codes and medication codes as risk factors for TTS. Considering that this study has analyzed data of 3,005 cases of TTS with an average age of > 80 years, we suggest that it is a valuable resource for analyzing TTS data of older populations.

Some limitations must be acknowledged in this study. One is the reliance on administrative data, which did not contain sufficient clinical details to draw firm conclusions. Medical data analytics deal with collected data, which inherently include many variables with missing values, such as clinical test data and biomarkers, at each time point. Since biomarkers and clinical test values were not included in the data set, assessing the impact of these factors on the results was impossible22. The category of concomitant medications and comorbidities included several medications and combinations, making which specific ones influence the outcomes unclear. Therefore, the exposure was not uniform. Since this was a database study, eliminating confounding by unmeasured factors was impossible. The data was limited to those issued by claims submitters and did not represent the entire population of patients with TTS. Therefore, there might be a potential for selection bias in data collection and sample selection. Smoking is a well-established risk factor for both respiratory and cardiovascular diseases, including conditions associated with TTS. However, data on smoking status was not available in the claims database used for this study. This is a limitation, as it did not allow for adjusting for or analyzing the potential confounding effects of smoking on the determined associations. A previous validation study of the Diagnosis Procedure Combination (DPC) database reported a sensitivity of 78.9% and a specificity of 93.2%, indicating generally good diagnostic accuracy; however, variability exists depending on the disease condition34. However, our study includes data not only from DPC hospitals but also from general hospitals. Therefore, the diagnostic accuracy may not be uniformly high across all institutions. Diagnoses based solely on ICD-10 codes may have limitations in validity. Although this study identified associations between TTS and other diseases, causality could not be inferred.

Conclusion

In this study using a large database, we identified that respiratory diseases were the main risk factor for TTS in patients aged ≥ 70 years. However, identifying risk factors in patients aged 60 to 70 years was difficult.

Subsequent studies should identify the relationship between TTS and stress using large-scale databases. Stress is one of the important triggers that influence the development of TTS. However, risk factors focusing on stress are complex. Developing a machine-learning model using large-scale databases incorporating stress is a noteworthy research endeavor.

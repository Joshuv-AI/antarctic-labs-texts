A blockchain enabled IoT routing framework improving security and performance in smart cities
Authors: Sumaira Johar, Naveed Ahmad, Afsheen Khalid, Huma Mumtaz, Yasir Javed, Mohamad Ladan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid integration of Internet of Things (IoT) technologies into smart city infrastructures has enabled advanced urban services but also introduced significant challenges related to scalability, reliability, and security. Traditional routing protocols often lack robust mechanisms to ensure data integrity, defend against cyber threats, and maintain high performance in large-scale deployments. This paper presents a blockchain-enabled secure IoT routing framework designed to strengthen trust, enhance routing efficiency, and mitigate network attacks in smart city environments. The proposed framework integrates Hyperledger Fabric with Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) consensus into the routing decision process, enabling immutable identity verification, consensus-approved route validation, and anomaly detection. Using NS-3 simulations, its performance is compared against three conventional protocols 6TiSCH, SDN-based routing, and ROLL across various network sizes and traffic scenarios. Evaluation metrics include throughput, end-to-end latency, packet delivery ratio (PDR), and resilience against blackhole and spoofing attacks. The results indicate that the blockchain-enabled framework can improve throughput by approximately 30%, reduce latency by up to 40%, increase PDR by up to 8% points, and significantly reduce successful security breaches compared to traditional approaches. These findings suggest that blockchain can provide secure, scalable, and high-performance IoT networking solutions for future smart cities.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The immense spread of Internet of Things (IoT) technology has considerably changed the present urban infrastructures and has formed the basis for the creation of smart cities. These inter-relating ecosystems integrate different devices, some of which have low power consumption such as environmental sensors and others have high bandwidth such as surveillance, to provide efficient public services, maximize resource use and enhance overall quality of urban life1–5. IoT technologies can help provide solutions to the most pressing urban issues, such as traffic congestion, energy efficiency, waste management, and public safety6,7. However, the dense device population density, dynamically changing network topologies and changing traffic patterns that are unique to smart city environments have presented numerous technical challenges to traditional routing protocols, including 6TiSCH, SDN-based routing, and ROLL, which were not originally designed to support the high density of device population, dynamically changing network topologies, and changing traffic patterns, that are characteristic of smart city environments8–11. In addition, security threats, including replay attacks and unauthorized access subject these networks to significant threats. The traditional IoT systems are either centrally controlled or have limited trust mechanisms, which predispose the systems to adversarial attacks and diminish the integrity of the system12,13. The necessity to possess efficient, scalable and secure routing systems has been brought to the light with the ever-increasing number of IoT systems14,15. Blockchain technology has come up as an effective solution to overcome these challenges. The fact that blockchain is a decentralized and immutable registry implies that blockchain enables the safe verification of data, reliable storage, and creating trust without having to rely on centralized authorities16–19. Integrating blockchain into IoT routing protocols can be used to authenticate node identities, validate routing paths using consensus mechanisms, and record network events transparently in real time20–22. The benefits of this approach are that it improves the integrity of data, minimizes access by unauthorized users and prevents tampering of routing information.

The proposed work will fill the gap between the traditional IoT routing protocols and the high security and performance standards of smart city settings. We suggest IoT routing frameworks with blockchain-based routing decisions, which integrate Hyperledger Fabric and a Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) consensus mechanism into the routing decision-making process23,24. The suggested integration will be aimed at enhancing throughput, decreasing latency, increasing the ratio of packet delivery (PDR), and increasing resilience against cyberattacks25,26.

Although the latest developments concerning blockchain-based IoT routing have been made, there are still several limitations. First, most of the current research mainly aims at improving security without systematically considering the effect of security improvements on fundamental performance indicators, e.g., throughput, latency and the ratio of packet delivery in large scale smart city settings. Second, most current frameworks are based on the use of static or partially decentralized validation mechanisms that do not fully incorporate consensus-based route verification into dynamic routing decisions. Third, the trade-off between increased security and performance overhead due to blockchain consensus mechanisms are not sufficiently investigated in heterogeneous IoT networks.

To overcome these limitations, this paper will propose a complete blockchain-enabled IoT routing architecture that incorporating Hyperledger Fabric with PBFT consensus directly into the routing process. In contrast to current methods, the proposed framework presents (i) consensus-based route validation closely coupled with routing operations, (ii) real-time anomaly detection with the help of blockchain-based smart contracts, and (iii) a simulation-based evaluation which jointly considers both performance and security under realistic smart city conditions. This integrated implementation allows enhancement in network reliability, security resilience and routing performance.

Literature review

The implementation of Internet of Things (IoT) technologies has been a key factor in the development of smart cities as it has allowed us to collect, process, and analyze data efficiently to improve urban services. Smart city models based on IoT have proven to be very helpful in terms of transportation, health care, environment monitoring and energy saving1,2. Nevertheless, the larger the scale and complexity of individual urban IoT systems, the more the issue of interoperability, scalability, and security are more accentuated. To overcome these issues, it is necessary not only to have strong system architecture but also to have advanced networking and smart data management solutions3,4.

The design of routing protocol is a major research topic in smart city IoT networks. Suitable protocols like 6TiSCH and ROLL have also been widely adopted because they are suitable in constrained and low-power lossy networks (LLNs)5,6. Though these protocols have a list of strengths such as energy efficiency, light weight operation and the ability to support a variety of traffic scenarios that characterize smart city settings, such protocols often cannot cope with dynamic network topologies, the heterogeneity of device capabilities, and the diversity of traffic patterns that are characteristic of smart city environments. Equally, routing with SDN leads to centralization and visibility of the network on a global basis7,8 but this centralization can introduce an additional latency and exposure to single points of failures, therefore limiting the network resiliency in large-scale deployments. In smart city systems based on IoT, security is a major issue of concern. Traditional routing systems are vulnerable to numerous attacks, including spoofing, replay attacks and routing manipulation that can have a significant impact on the network operation and integrity of data9–12. These vulnerabilities are then enhanced by the lack of sound trust management and authentication mechanisms. This has given rise to the growing need to integrate security mindful schemes into routing schemes to have secure and reliable communication13,14. Blockchain technology has turned out to be a shining light to raise the levels of trust and security in the IoT networks. It is a decentralized architecture, the ledger is immutable, and validation schemes are based on consensus that can allow sharing data safely without necessarily having to rely on centralized authorities15–18. The spread of trust by blockchain among nodes of a network decreases the risk of a single point failure and malicious interference19. Moreover, the combination of blockchain and artificial intelligence to make smart anomaly detection and adaptive security policies possible in the context of the IoT settings became a subject of discussion in the recent literature as well20. Other articles have highlighted how blockchain can be applied in enabling safe distribution of resources, decentralization of management of identities and provision of data integrity in smart city infrastructures21–23. There has been an increasing interest in the use of blockchain to the route of IoT. The industrial IoT models have demonstrated that one can combine blockchains with routing systems to improve their security and efficiency24. Similarly, RouteChain proposes a safe Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) blockchain-based version to mitigate the effects of the route hijacking attacks25. Subsequent literature has considered lightweight blockchain architecture, edge-assistance consensus mechanism and hybrid blockchain to reduce latency and computational overhead in IoT networks26–28. Although these methods justify the promise of blockchain-based routing, most researchers are limited to the scope of a particular network layer or use case and not evaluating its full potential in large-scale smart city settings.

Although these developments have been made, there are still several research gaps. First, the role of blockchain integration on the key performance metrics, including throughput, latency, and the ratio of packets delivered in heterogeneous IoT networks are empirically understudied29. Second, existing literature seldom considers blockchain-based routing in the case of dynamic mobility and real-time attack situations that are inherent to urban settings30. Third, the trade-off between improved security and the performance costs of blockchain consensus mechanisms are poorly understood31.

Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT) protocols have recently been improved, further shaping the design of systems that utilize blockchain technology. Other protocols like RBFT and Dumbo have been shown to be more scalable and have better communication properties than classical PBFT32,33. Furthermore, new studies on quantum-enhanced Byzantine Agreement have shown the potential of a higher fault tolerance and cryptographic resilience in future distributed systems34. However, PBFT can still be considered as a feasible option on permissioned blockchain platforms because it has a deterministic finality, is proven to be reliable, and can be compatible with other frameworks like Hyperledger Fabric.

Methodology

In this paper, an empirical analysis of the proposed blockchain-powered IoT routing framework based on a simulation will be presented to understand the performance of the proposed framework in realistic smart city scenarios. The methodology will be developed to recreate the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of the urban IoT environment and will enable a systematic comparison of the proposed framework with the traditional routing protocols. It is considered in terms of network performance and security resilience in terms of node mobility, heterogeneity of the traffic and adversarial environment. The proposed experimental design is capable of providing a fair comparison as the proposed framework is compared with three well-known routing protocols, namely: 6TiSCH, SDN-based routing, and ROLL that represent different architectural paradigms in the IoT networking13.These protocols are used as baseline models in benchmarking throughput improvements, latency improvements, packet delivery ratio (PDR) improvements, and security robustness improvements.

Testbed setup

The empirical evaluation was conducted using the smart city-based simulation test bed with various network sizes of 100, 300, 500 and 1000 IoT devices. These devices are a heterogeneous group of smart city devices, such as environmental sensors, surveillance cameras, and smart meters installed in a 2 km2 urban area. Such a setup is like real-world large-scale IoT systems that are commonly observed in smart city systems5,6. The testbed technologies rely on stationery and mobile nodes to explore the heterogeneity of smart city settings. Mobile nodes are dynamic (e.g. smart cars and wearables), fixed sensors are modeled as fixed nodes. All simulation scenarios are executed under the same condition with NS-3 (version 3.39) and this gives consistency and fairness to all the routing protocols tested. All the experiments were carried out 50 times to be statistically reliable.

Simulation environment

The network topology was set to simulate the mobility patterns of the real world by using the Random Waypoint mobility model and node speeds ranged between 0 and 3 m/s to simulate both pedestrian and vehicular movements in cities. The model of mobility can be used to assess the performance of routing in response to the changing topology as well as changing connectivity conditions.

To reflect diverse traffic patterns in smart cities, three types of network traffic were simulated:

PBFT ensures reliable consensus with tolerance of up to (n−1)/3 malicious nodes, providing robustness against adversarial behavior in distributed environments21,24. Routing data storage and retrieval mechanism

Routing-related information is stored and managed on blockchain ledger in the proposed framework to ensure integrity, traceability, and trust. All validated routing paths are stored as a transaction that holds some important key attributes that include the source node ID, destination node ID, selected intermediate nodes, timestamp, and route validation status. When establishing a routing path, when a routing path is approved by the PBFT consensus process, it is recorded in the blockchain ledger using smart contracts. This generates a fuzz resistance record of credible paths which might be re-used again in future transmissions. To retrieve routes, when a node is transmitting data, it consults the blockchain ledger to determine which previously validated routes exist between the source and the destination. The system gives preference to routes depending on the performance measure like latency and past reliability. Assuming there is a valid route, this route is reused directly, eliminating the route discovery overhead. Otherwise, the process of a new route discovery and validation will be activated. This storage and retrieval system reduces unnecessary route computations, enhances routing efficiency and only consensus-approved paths are used to transmit data thereby improving both security and performance.

Smart contract design and execution

Smart contracts are implemented within Hyperledger Fabric to automate routing validation and security enforcement. Any smart contract is a set of specific tasks that can be node authentication, route validation, and anomaly detection. The cost of smart contracts execution is reduced by restricting their invocation to route establishment events and not a constant data transmission. The added latency of executing smart contracts is added to the total routing delay; however, the net effect is lower network performance. Scalability considerations of PBFT

The Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) consensus mechanism has strong security guarantees but introduces a complexity of O(n2) in communication, where n is the number of validator nodes. Under the framework suggested, not every IoT device is involved in consensus. Rather, a small group of nodes (e.g., gateway nodes or cluster heads) serve as blockchain validators, greatly decreasing the effective size of the consensus network. This hierarchical structure restricts the number of participating nodes in the PBFT process, and this helps to mitigate the overhead of communication without compromising on security guarantees. As an example, in large-scale deployments (e.g. 1000 IoT devices), only a small proportion (e.g. 5-10% of nodes) of nodes are validators. This decreases the time consensus and makes them scalable in smart city contexts. Moreover, blockchain validation is not done on all transmissions of packets but is selectively done during route establishment thereby minimizing the overhead of blockchain validation. This design gives the consensus process not to add considerable delays to the process of data forwarding so that the observed improvements in latency and throughput could be achieved.

Discussion and implications

Key findings

The research results of this paper have significant implications to the design and implementation of secure, high-performance IoT routing in smart city settings. By integrating Hyperledger Fabric with Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) consensus, not only is trust enhanced among the heterogeneous IoT nodes, but it also helps reduce the reliance on centralized infrastructure, which is a common single point of failure in traditional networks. Such a decentralization is in line with the distributed, heterogeneous character of smart cities, where thousands of interconnected devices need to coordinate with each other in a smooth manner3,4. Security wise, the proposed architecture shows a dramatic reduction of successful cyberattacks, especially blackhole and spoofing attacks, by an over 80 percent decrease in the number of successful cyberattacks, compared to traditional protocols (Fig. 10). The resulting ledger of the blockchain is immutable, which enables a clear history of routing choices. This helps in auditing and researching security breaches. PBFT consensus is necessary to immediately identify routing changes and minimize the likelihood of compromising and affecting the network integrity of the node15.

The findings suggest that blockchain implementation can enhance the security and performance of the considered conditions. The framework based on blockchain outperformed the traditional protocols in throughput by up to 30% (Fig. 8), in latency by a factor of about 40 percent (Fig. 9), and in the ratio of packet delivery (PDR) by up to 8 percentage points (Fig. 10).

The improvements observed are mainly credited to the design of the framework. It uses paths that have already received the decision of the consensus mechanism. This strategy was very effective since it reduced significantly the time taken to obtain viable routes and minimized the loss of data packets as transmitted. One key implication of this is that the integration of blockchain is a promising solution to improve the performance of the network as well as the network security at the same time. More importantly, it can do so without causing an unreasonable amount of added processing or communication overhead which is a common worry. It is especially so when the system utilizes an effective consensus algorithm such as PBFT.

Impact of blockchain overhead

Even though the introduction of blockchain integration entails extra computational and communication overhead, the effect is well controlled in the suggested framework. With Hyperledger Fabric with PBFT, routing decisions are only validated during route establishment and not during continuous forwarding of packet switched routing. The results of simulation show that the overhead caused by blockchain processing is compensated by decreased route rediscovery, avoidance of malicious paths, and increased reliability in packet delivery. Consequently, the general network performance is enhanced even though blockchain activities are involved. It is however recognized that in very large-scale implementations the consensus overhead can grow, and lightweight consensus protocols like PoA or hybrid can further optimize performance.

Limitations and suggested improvements

Although the results are encouraging, the study also reveals the shortcomings. The first important shortcoming is that the integration of blockchain technology is bound to introduce a certain share of overhead processing and communication. In applications that require extremely high speeds, this latency overhead may end up being a serious issue. Secondly, the PBFT algorithm is also great in terms of security, but it does not scale as infinitely. Also, simulations cannot perfectly mirror the real-world network dynamics, environmental conditions and heterogeneity of devices. Lastly, although our simulations are comprehensive, they have limitations inherent in them. They cannot perfectly model the complex and dynamic real-world conditions of a live smart city environment. The factors that are hard to model fully include; inconsistent wireless signals, devices with different energy levels and the random movement patterns of nodes. To continue working in the future, a number of interesting directions can be followed. A possible direction of enhancements is to explore other consensus algorithms that are leaner and designed to address the constraint of the IoT device. Alternatives such as Proof-of-Authority (PoA) or variations of Delegated Proof-of-Stake (DPoS) appear to be promising options to this4. Moreover, the other interesting concept would be to come up with hybrid system architecture. The integration of the secure blockchain systems with AIs-based edge intelligence can be considered one of the potential improvements. Such a combination may assist a system to pay much attention to routing on the fly and on the threats occurring. The other promising path is to consider cross-layer optimization, to connect application-level requirements and routing and blockchain layers to provide end-to-end performance guarantees. In practice, the proposed framework can be extended to other smart city solutions, including smart transportation systems, energy grid monitoring systems, and public safety networks. The skills to sustain secure and effective communications in dynamic and possibly adversarial conditions could be helpful in accelerating the implementation of IoT-based smart city solutions. Besides, the transparency of the blockchain audit trials may help in compliance with the regulations and trust with the people. In conclusion, the introduction of blockchain technology in the IoT routing protocols is a massive step towards designing resilient and trustworthy infrastructures of smart cities. Even though our simulation findings are promising, future research is needed to examine the actual pilot implementations, scalability testing not in the simulated environment, and the interoperability with new IoT communication standards. All these measures will help optimize the framework and enable its widespread application to most urban environments.

Conclusion

This paper presents a blockchain-supported secure IoT routing system designed to be used in smart cities. The framework tackles two essential issues in IoT networks: the security of data transmission and the high-performance of a network in the context of dynamic and potentially adversarial conditions. The performance of the proposed framework is tested with a high level of simulations conducted with the help of the NS-3 platform. The findings show that the framework attains significant gains over traditional routing schemes, such as up to 30 percent enhancements in throughput, about 40 percent decreases in latency, and gains of up to 8 percentage points in the ratio of the number of packets delivered to the number of packets sent. Moreover, the framework considerably decreases the number of successful cyberattacks, which proves better security resilience. These results indicate that with blockchain integration, a balanced enhancement of both security and performance of IoT-based smart city applications can be achieved. Although such encouraging outcomes are attained, some drawbacks are still present. The testing is done using simulation environments, which might not necessarily reflect the real-world deployments. Moreover, the application of the PBFT consensus imposes computational and communication overheads that can have an impact on the scalability in an extremely large-scale network. Future work will take several directions. To begin with, the lightweight consensus mechanisms, including Proof-of-Authority (PoA) and hybrid PBFT-RAFT designs, will be explored to improve scalability and reduce overhead. Second, the proposed framework will be tested in real-life smart city testbeds to determine its real-world applicability. Third, the adaptive routing strategies based on machine learning will be integrated to enhance performance in a dynamic network environment. Lastly, the exploration of interoperability standards for cross-platform IoT-blockchain integration will facilitate wider application in heterogeneous systems.

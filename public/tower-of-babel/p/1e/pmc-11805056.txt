A Study on Patient Satisfaction and Prevalence of Complication on Surgical Extraction of Third Molar
Authors: Ahtesham Ahmad Qurishi, Amal Bajonaid, Maryam H. Mugri, Husham E. Homeida, Ogail Yousif Dawod, Haider Hussain Alhijji, Albaraa Abeid Dayili
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:The surgical extraction of third molars is a common procedure often accompanied by various complications. This study evaluates patient satisfaction and the prevalence of complications to identify factors influencing postoperative outcomes.Methodology:A cross-sectional study was conducted with 62 patients who underwent third molar extraction. Data on demographics, complications, and patient satisfaction were collected through medical records and structured questionnaires. Complications were assessed at 1 week and 1 month post surgery, and satisfaction was measured using a 5-point Likert scale.Results:The most common complications were pain (32.3%) and swelling (24.2%). Despite these issues, patient satisfaction scores were high, with pain management, communication, recovery experience, and overall satisfaction averaging above 4.0 on a 5-point scale. Severe complications such as nerve injury (4.8%) and trismus (8.1%) were less frequent.Conclusion:While minor complications are prevalent, effective pain management and communication contribute to high patient satisfaction following third molar extractions. Continued efforts to minimize complications can further improve patient outcomes and satisfaction.

INTRODUCTION

The surgical extraction of third molars, commonly known as wisdom teeth, is a prevalent dental procedure often necessitated by impaction, infection, crowding, or decay. While routine, this surgical intervention can significantly impact patients’ postoperative well-being, making patient satisfaction and the prevalence of complications critical areas of study.[1] Understanding these factors is paramount for optimizing surgical outcomes and enhancing patient care.

Patient satisfaction in the context of third molar extractions encompasses various dimensions, including the surgical experience, pain management, recovery process, and overall outcomes. High levels of satisfaction are typically associated with minimal pain, swift recovery, effective communication, and the perceived competence of the dental professional. Conversely, dissatisfaction may arise from prolonged discomfort, complications, inadequate pain control, and unmet expectations.[2] Measuring patient satisfaction provides valuable insights into the quality of care and helps identify areas for improvement in surgical practices and patient management strategies.[3]

Complications following the surgical extraction of third molars are not uncommon and can significantly affect patient satisfaction. Common complications include alveolar osteitis (dry socket), infection, swelling, bleeding, nerve injury, and trismus (restricted mouth opening).[4] These complications can lead to extended recovery times, additional treatments, and increased discomfort, adversely impacting the patient’s overall experience. The prevalence and severity of these complications are influenced by various factors, such as the complexity of the extraction, the patient’s health status, surgical technique, and postoperative care.[5]

This article aims to explore the relationship between patient satisfaction and the prevalence of complications following the surgical extraction of third molars. By examining patient feedback and clinical outcomes, we seek to identify key factors that contribute to positive and negative experiences.

METHODOLOGY

This study adopted a cross-sectional design to evaluate patient satisfaction and the prevalence of complications following the surgical extraction of third molars. The sample size consisted of 62 patients who underwent third molar extraction over a 6-month period. Patients aged 18–50 years who underwent surgical extraction of at least one third molar and provided informed consent were included in the study, whereas those with systemic diseases affecting healing or incomplete medical records were excluded. Participants were recruited consecutively based on these criteria. Preoperative data, including demographic information and medical history, were collected from medical records, along with surgical details such as the number of extractions, surgical technique, and duration of surgery.

Postoperative follow-up occurred at 1 week and 1 month post surgery to assess complications and patient satisfaction. Documented complications included pain, infection, alveolar osteitis, swelling, bleeding, nerve injury, and trismus, with pain levels measured using a visual analog scale. At the 1-month follow-up, a structured questionnaire was administered to gauge patient satisfaction, covering aspects such as pain management, communication with the dental professional, recovery experience, and overall satisfaction with the procedure. Responses were collected using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from “very dissatisfied” to “very satisfied.”

Descriptive statistics summarized demographic and clinical characteristics, and the prevalence of complications was calculated as a percentage of the total sample. Patient satisfaction scores were analyzed using mean and standard deviation, and correlation analysis was performed to explore the relationship between complications and satisfaction levels.

In the demographics section, the age range of the participants is presented, with a mean age of 29.5 years and a standard deviation of 8.2 years, indicating the spread of ages around the mean. The gender distribution shows that 45.20% of the participants were male (28 individuals) and 54.80% were female (34 individuals), providing a balanced representation of both sexes in the sample.

The complications section lists the frequency and percentage of various postoperative complications experienced by the patients. Pain was the most common complication, affecting 20 participants (32.30%), followed by swelling in 15 participants (24.20%). Other complications included alveolar osteitis (dry socket) in 10 participants (16.10%), infection in eight participants (12.90%), bleeding in six participants (9.70%), trismus in five participants (8.10%), and nerve injury in three participants (4.80%). Finally, the patient satisfaction scores section reports the mean satisfaction ratings along with their standard deviations for various aspects of the surgical experience. Pain management received a mean score of 4.1 with a standard deviation of 0.8, indicating generally high satisfaction. Communication scored 4.3 (SD: 0.7), recovery experience scored 4.0 (SD: 0.9), and overall satisfaction scored 4.2 (SD: 0.8). These scores suggest that on average, patients were satisfied with the pain management, communication, recovery process, and overall experience following their third molar extractions.

DISCUSSION

The findings of this study reveal significant insights into patient satisfaction and the prevalence of complications following third molar extractions. The high incidence of complications, particularly pain (32.3%) and swelling (24.2%), aligns with previous studies, indicating these are common postoperative issues.[67] Despite the prevalence of these complications, patient satisfaction scores were relatively high, with mean scores for pain management, communication, recovery experience, and overall satisfaction all above 4.0 on a 5-point scale. This suggests that effective pain management and communication strategies are crucial in maintaining high satisfaction levels despite postoperative discomfort.

The relatively low rates of severe complications, such as nerve injury (4.8%) and trismus (8.1%), further support the notion that while minor complications are common, major complications are less frequent but still noteworthy.[8] Effective preoperative counseling and postoperative care likely contribute to the high satisfaction scores observed, emphasizing the importance of patient education and follow-up care.

The study underscores the importance of addressing common complications proactively through comprehensive care protocols to enhance patient outcomes. Future research should focus on developing targeted interventions to reduce the prevalence of common complications and further improve patient satisfaction.

CONCLUSION

This study highlights the prevalence of postoperative complications, particularly pain and swelling, in third molar extractions. Despite these issues, high patient satisfaction underscores the effectiveness of pain management and communication. Continued focus on reducing complications can further enhance patient experiences and outcomes.

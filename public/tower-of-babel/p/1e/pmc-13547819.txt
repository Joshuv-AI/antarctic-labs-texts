circMYO9B affects the progression of periodontitis by targeting the miR-4731-5p/USP15 axis
Authors: Huimin Xie, Huiling Qin, Zhengyi Chu, Zhenhua Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposePeriodontitis is a common oral disease characterized by inflammation and destruction of periodontal tissues. Competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) are known to regulate inflammatory responses. This study aimed to investigate the role of circular RNA MYO9B (circMYO9B), as a potential ceRNA, in the progression of periodontitis and to elucidate its underlying regulatory mechanism.MethodsThis study included 69 healthy controls and 71 patients with periodontitis. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-treated periodontal ligament cells (PDLCs) were used as an in vitro model of periodontitis. Reverse transcription–quantitative polymerase chain reaction was performed to quantify circMYO9B, miR-4731-5p, and ubiquitin-specific peptidase 15 (USP15) expression, while enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays were used to measure inflammatory cytokine levels. Cell proliferation, migration, and apoptosis were assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 assay, Transwell migration assay, and flow cytometry, respectively. Dual-luciferase reporter assays were conducted to validate the interactions among circMYO9B, miR-4731-5p, and USP15.ResultscircMYO9B expression was significantly elevated in the gingival crevicular fluid of patients with periodontitis and in LPS-treated PDLCs. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis demonstrated that circMYO9B had diagnostic potential for periodontitis (area under the curve: 0.919). LPS stimulation reduced PDLC viability and migratory capacity while increasing inflammatory cytokine production and apoptosis. circMYO9B directly targeted and negatively regulated miR-4731-5p. Silencing circMYO9B significantly attenuated LPS-induced PDLC dysfunction, whereas inhibition of miR-4731-5p abolished the protective effects of circMYO9B knockdown. USP15 was identified as a direct downstream target of miR-4731-5p: overexpression of miR-4731-5p alleviated LPS-induced cellular injury in PDLCs, but this protective effect was markedly reversed by concomitant USP15 overexpression.ConclusionscircMYO9B is associated with periodontitis progression through the miR-4731-5p/USP15 regulatory axis, suggesting its potential utility as a therapeutic target in periodontitis.Keywords: MicroRNAs; Periodontitis; RNA, circular; Ubiquitin-specific proteases

INTRODUCTION

Periodontitis is a prevalent chronic oral disease characterized by invasion of periodontal pathogens into periodontal tissues, which activates host immune responses and leads to chemotaxis of inflammatory mediators, infiltration of inflammatory cells, and progressive destruction of periodontal supporting tissues [1]. Recent studies indicate that research on periodontitis pathogenesis has primarily focused on dental plaque microbiota, host immune modulation, and genetic factors. Periodontitis is also associated with multiple systemic diseases, including obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and Alzheimer’s disease [2,3]. Although early intervention can slow disease progression to some extent, current treatments rarely achieve complete remission [4]. Therefore, identifying molecular biomarkers and further elucidating pathogenic mechanisms are essential to improve the prevention and clinical management of periodontitis.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs), first identified in RNA viruses in 1976, have become an active research focus with advances in high-throughput sequencing and bioinformatics [5]. circRNAs are endogenous RNAs distinguished by a covalently closed circular structure formed via back-splicing of precursor messenger RNA (mRNA). Their widespread presence across species and relative resistance to exonuclease-mediated degradation support their regulatory roles in diverse biological processes [6,7]. circRNAs often function as microRNA (miRNA) sponges, competitively binding miRNAs and thereby contributing to the development of inflammatory diseases, including periodontitis. For example, circ_0003948 exerts anti-inflammatory effects via the miR-144-3p/NR2F2/PTEN pathway [8], whereas circ_0085289 prevents periodontal ligament cell (PDLC) apoptosis and reduces inflammatory cytokine production through the let-7f-5p/SOCS6 axis [9]. Similarly, circ_0081572 reduces lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced damage in PDLCs via the miR-378h/RORA axis [10], suggesting a potential role in slowing periodontitis progression. Notably, circRNA profiling has shown that circular RNA MYO9B (circMYO9B) is significantly increased in periodontitis tissues [11]; however, its diagnostic value and role in disease development remain incompletely defined.

miRNAs are highly conserved endogenous noncoding RNAs that post-transcriptionally regulate gene expression by promoting mRNA degradation or inhibiting translation [12]. These small regulators modulate cellular processes including differentiation, embryogenesis, metabolism, organ development, and apoptosis [13]. In periodontitis, miRNAs mediate immunoinflammatory responses in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells and thereby influence periodontal disease development [14]. For instance, miR-106a-5p regulates proliferation and osteogenic differentiation of human periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs), and its overexpression has been reported to support periodontal tissue regeneration [15]. Conversely, Huang et al. [16] reported that miR-498 is associated with periodontitis initiation and progression through modulation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling and related cascades. Collectively, these findings highlight the dual roles of specific miRNAs in attenuating or exacerbating periodontal inflammation and tissue destruction, supporting their potential as diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

circMYO9B has been reported to be significantly upregulated in periodontitis, suggesting involvement in disease processes; however, the underlying regulatory mechanism remains unclear [11]. Prior studies have described the circRNA-miRNA-mRNA network as a functional manifestation of the competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) mechanism in periodontitis, in which circRNAs compete for miRNA binding to modulate downstream targets [17,18]. We therefore hypothesized that circMYO9B acts as a ceRNA by sequestering a specific miRNA and thereby relieving repression of downstream target genes. To test this hypothesis, we established an in vitro inflammatory model using LPS-stimulated human PDLCs. This study aimed to identify the miRNA interacting with circMYO9B, confirm derepression of downstream target genes, and evaluate the functional impact of this regulatory axis on PDLC behavior under inflammatory conditions.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Sample size

This case-control study used G*Power (version 3.1.9.7) for a priori sample size estimation. Based on the effect size reported previously [19], parameters were set as follows: Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney U test (2-tailed), effect size index=0.5, α=0.05, power=0.80, and allocation ratio=1:1. The analysis indicated that at least 60 participants per group were required. Allowing for an anticipated 10% attrition rate, planned recruitment was set at 66 participants per group. The final sample size met or exceeded this target in both groups, supporting the study’s planned statistical power.

Patient enrollment

Between February 2024 and April 2025, the study enrolled 71 patients with periodontitis (23 stage I, 29 stage II, 13 stage III, and 6 stage IV) and 69 periodontally healthy controls. There were no significant between-group differences in age, sex, body mass index (BMI), or smoking status. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment, and the protocol was approved by the Harbin Stomatological Hospital Ethics Committee (approval No. 2023163).

Inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) diagnosis according to the 2018 World Workshop on the Classification of Periodontal and Peri-Implant Diseases and Conditions, with clinical attachment level (CAL) loss at ≥2 non-adjacent teeth and affected sites showing probing depth (PD) >3 mm and bleeding on probing (BOP); 2) ≥20 natural teeth; 3) no antibiotics, immunosuppressants, or periodontal treatment within 3 months; and 4) complete clinical records. Exclusion criteria were: 1) use of medications affecting periodontal status within 3 months; 2) pregnancy, lactation, or perimenopause; 3) systemic diseases influencing periodontal health; and 4) history of orthodontic treatment. Controls met the same age, dentition, and medication criteria and demonstrated periodontal health (PD ≤3 mm, no attachment loss, and no BOP).

Stage I (slight) was characterized by shallow periodontal pockets (PD ≤4 mm), mild CAL (1–2 mm), and slight bone loss confined to the alveolar crest. Stage II (moderate) was characterized by deeper pockets (PD ≤5 mm), moderate CAL (3–4 mm), and bone loss extending to the coronal third of the root (<33%). Stages III/IV (severe/advanced) were defined by deep pockets (PD ≥6 mm), severe CAL (≥5 mm), and bone loss extending beyond the middle third of the root (>33%). Stage IV was additionally distinguished by loss of 5 or more teeth due to periodontitis requiring complex rehabilitation.

Sample collection

Gingival crevicular fluid (GCF) was collected using standardized filter strips (Oraflow Inc., Smithtown, NY, USA). Sampling sites were selected as follows: in the periodontitis group, the most active sites (deepest PD and BOP; preferably incisors or first molars) were selected; in controls, periodontally healthy sites with matched anatomical tooth types (PD ≤3 mm, no attachment loss, and no inflammation) were selected. Before sampling, sites were isolated and gently air-dried to minimize saliva contamination. Sterile paper strips were inserted into the gingival sulcus for 30 seconds, then immediately placed in cryovials and stored at −80°C until analysis.

Cell culture and induction

Human PDLCs were obtained from ScienCell Research Laboratories (San Diego, CA, USA). Cells were maintained at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 and cultured in α-MEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. Untreated PDLCs served as controls.

Based on the elevated circMYO9B expression observed in GCF from patients with periodontitis, we used an LPS-induced inflammatory PDLC model to investigate circMYO9B function and mechanism. PDLCs were treated with 1 μg/mL LPS for 12, 24, or 48 hours [20]. The LPS-induced PDLC model was utilized to recapitulate key inflammatory responses relevant to periodontitis rather than the full in vivo pathophysiology.

Cell transfection

Reagents were purchased from GenePharma (Shanghai, China), including circMYO9B small interfering RNA (si-circMYO9B) and scrambled negative control (NC-si), miR-4731-5p mimic and negative control, miR-4731-5p inhibitor and inhibitor negative control, and a ubiquitin-specific peptidase 15 (USP15) overexpression plasmid with empty-vector control. PDLCs were transfected using GPF-Transfect Reagent (GenePharma) and harvested 24 hours after transfection for subsequent experimental analyses.

RNA extraction and reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted from GCF and cultured cells using TRIzol reagent. Complementary DNA was synthesized using a commercial reverse transcription kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and stored at −20°C until use. RT-qPCR was performed using TSE Flash SYBR Green qPCR Mix (TSINGKE, Beijing, China) to quantify circMYO9B, miR-4731-5p, and USP15 expression, with U6 and GAPDH as internal reference genes. Relative expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Each sample was analyzed in triplicate wells.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

RIP was performed using a RIP assay kit (Active Motif, Carlsbad, CA, USA). PDLC lysates were incubated with magnetic beads coated with anti-Ago2 antibody or immunoglobulin G (IgG) control. Immunoprecipitated RNA was then quantified by RT-qPCR.

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Cell proliferation was assessed using the CCK-8 assay. At 0, 12, 24, and 48 hours after treatment, PDLCs were incubated with 10 μL of CCK-8 solution for 4 hours, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Transwell assay

Cell migration was assessed using Transwell chambers (8-μm pore size). PDLCs in logarithmic growth phase were seeded in the upper chamber at 1×105 cells/mL (200 μL per well), and the lower chamber contained 600 μL of medium supplemented with 10% FBS. After 24 hours at 37°C, nonmigratory cells were removed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Migratory cells were fixed with methanol for 10 minutes, stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 5 minutes, and counted in 5 random fields per well under an inverted microscope. Experiments were performed in triplicate wells; serum-free medium was used as a control condition.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, interleukin (IL)-1β, and IL-6 levels in culture supernatants were quantified using ELISA kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Bio-Techne, Minneapolis, MN, USA). Experiments were performed in triplicate.

Flow cytometry assay

Apoptosis was assessed by flow cytometry. After treatment, cells were washed with cold PBS and resuspended in 500 μL of binding buffer at 1×105 cells/mL. Samples were stained with annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate and propidium iodide in the dark and analyzed immediately. Each condition was tested in triplicate.

Dual-luciferase reporter assays

Bioinformatic analysis using ENCORI predicted miR-4731-5p as a putative downstream target of circMYO9B. Dual-luciferase reporter vectors containing the wild-type circMYO9B binding sequence (circMYO9B-WT) or a mutated sequence (circMYO9B-MUT) were constructed and co-transfected into PDLCs with miR-4731-5p mimic or inhibitor. Luciferase activity was measured 24 hours after transfection to validate the interaction.

MiRWalk analysis identified USP15 as a putative target of miR-4731-5p. Reporter vectors containing the wild-type USP15 3′ untranslated region (USP15-WT) or a mutated binding site (USP15-MUT) were co-transfected with miR-4731-5p mimic or inhibitor, and luciferase activity was measured after 24 hours.

Bioinformatics analysis

The downstream targets of circMYO9B were predicted using ENCORI (https://rnasysu.com/encori/index.php). The downstream target genes of miR-4731-5p were predicted using MiRWalk (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/).

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed with at least 3 independent biological replicates. Data were analyzed using SPSS (version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism (version 8.0; GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and homogeneity of variances was assessed using Levene’s test. Normally distributed data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Differences between 2 groups were assessed using unpaired t-tests, and comparisons among multiple groups were assessed using analysis of variance. Diagnostic performance of circMYO9B for periodontitis was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05.

RESULTS

The expression level of circMYO9B in periodontitis patients and LPS-induced PDLC cells

circMYO9B expression was quantified in GCF from 71 patients with periodontitis (23 stage I, 29 stage II, 13 stage III, and 6 stage IV) and healthy controls. There were no significant between-group differences in age, sex, BMI, or smoking status (Table 1). However, the periodontitis group showed significantly higher PD, CAL, and BOP values. RT-qPCR showed that circMYO9B expression was increased in GCF from patients with periodontitis compared with controls (Figure 1A). In LPS-treated PDLCs, circMYO9B expression increased in a time-dependent manner, with maximal induction at 48 hours (Figure 1B). Cytokine profiling showed increases in TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 at 12 and 24 hours after LPS exposure, followed by partial decreases at 48 hours (Figure 1C-E). Consistent with inflammatory injury, PDLC viability (Figure 1F) and migration (Figure 1G) decreased over time, whereas apoptosis increased (Figure 1H).

The regulatory effect of silencing circMYO9B on LPS-induced PDLCs

RT-qPCR showed that circMYO9B expression was significantly reduced in the LPS + si-circMYO9B group compared with the LPS + NC-si group (Figure 2A). Following circMYO9B knockdown, TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 levels decreased markedly (Figure 2B-D). Functionally, circMYO9B silencing increased PDLC viability (Figure 2E) and migratory capacity (Figure 2F) and reduced apoptosis (Figure 2G). Together, these findings indicate that circMYO9B knockdown attenuates LPS-induced PDLC injury, suggesting a potential regulatory role.

circMYO9B targets miR-4731-5p

ENCORI predicted miR-4731-5p as a putative target of circMYO9B, which was subsequently validated experimentally. MiR-4731-5p expression was reduced in GCF from patients with periodontitis compared with controls (Figure 3A) and decreased over time in LPS-treated PDLCs (Figure 3B). Sequence analysis showed complementary binding sites between circMYO9B and miR-4731-5p (Figure 3C). In dual-luciferase assays, the miR-4731-5p mimic reduced luciferase activity of the circMYO9B-WT construct, whereas the inhibitor increased it; no differences were observed with circMYO9B-MUT (Figure 3D). RIP assays showed enrichment of both circMYO9B and miR-4731-5p in Ago2 immunoprecipitates compared with IgG controls (Figure 3E). Pearson correlation analysis indicated a negative correlation between circMYO9B and miR-4731-5p expression (r=−0.6188, P<0.001; Figure 3F). Together, these results support a direct interaction in which circMYO9B negatively regulates miR-4731-5p.

The combined inhibition of circMYO9B and miR-4731-5p expression affects LPS-induced PDLCs

RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated that knockdown of circMYO9B under LPS conditions upregulated miR-4731-5p expression, whereas co-treatment with a miR-4731-5p inhibitor reduced miR-4731-5p levels (Figure 4A). Consistent with this, the reductions in TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 observed after circMYO9B silencing were reversed when miR-4731-5p was simultaneously inhibited (Figure 4B-D). In functional assays, miR-4731-5p inhibition attenuated the beneficial effects of circMYO9B knockdown on PDLC viability and migration (Figure 4E and F) and increased apoptosis (Figure 4G). These findings suggest that the protective effects of circMYO9B silencing on PDLC function are mediated, at least in part, through its regulation of miR-4731-5p expression.

USP15 is the target gene of miR-4731-5p

MiRWalk predicted USP15 as a putative target of miR-4731-5p. RT-qPCR showed higher USP15 expression in GCF from patients with periodontitis than in controls (Figure 5A). In LPS-treated PDLCs, USP15 expression increased over time (Figure 5B), mirroring the circMYO9B pattern. Sequence analysis identified predicted binding sites between miR-4731-5p and USP15 (Figure 5C). In dual-luciferase assays, the miR-4731-5p mimic reduced luciferase activity of USP15-WT, whereas the inhibitor increased it; no effects were observed for USP15-MUT (Figure 5D). Pearson analysis showed a negative correlation between USP15 and miR-4731-5p (r=−0.7127, P<0.001; Figure 5E) and a positive correlation between USP15 and circMYO9B (r=0.6832, P<0.001; Figure 5F), supporting a circMYO9B/miR-4731-5p/USP15 regulatory axis.

USP15 reversed the effect of miR-4731-5p overexpression on the recovery of PDLCs induced by LPS

To validate USP15 as a downstream effector of miR-4731-5p, rescue experiments were performed by co-transfecting PDLCs with a miR-4731-5p mimic and a USP15 overexpression vector. MiR-4731-5p overexpression reduced USP15 expression, whereas concomitant USP15 overexpression reversed this reduction (Figure 6A). Co-overexpression of USP15 also reversed the inhibitory effects of the miR-4731-5p mimic on TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 levels (Figure 6B-D). Functionally, miR-4731-5p overexpression attenuated LPS-induced injury, as reflected by increased viability (Figure 6E), enhanced migration (Figure 6F), and reduced apoptosis (Figure 6G); these protective effects were diminished by concomitant USP15 overexpression. Collectively, these results support USP15 as a functional downstream target mediating miR-4731-5p effects in PDLCs.

DISCUSSION

Periodontitis is a common chronic oral disease characterized by gingival swelling, bleeding, and tooth mobility and can ultimately result in tooth loss [21]. The clinical diagnosis of periodontitis mainly relies on radiographic findings and the assessment of clinical symptoms and lesion severity [22]. However, this approach may lack sensitivity, contributing to underdiagnosis. Accordingly, identifying reliable inflammation-related biomarkers could improve early detection and enhance treatment monitoring and effectiveness [23,24]. Developing such biomarkers could facilitate more accurate clinical interventions and better monitoring of treatment in periodontal care.

circRNAs have emerged as functional regulators in multiple diseases and represent potential therapeutic targets in periodontitis. Li et al. [25] reported upregulation of circ_0138960 in patients with periodontitis and in LPS-treated PDLSCs. Zhao et al. [26] demonstrated that circ_birc6 silencing promotes PDLSC osteogenesis via the miR-543/PTEN axis and activation of PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling. In addition, circ_0138959 promotes apoptosis and inflammation in periodontitis by sponging miR-495-3p and upregulating TRAF6 [27]. circMYO9B is also reportedly upregulated and has been implicated in breast cancer [28] and diabetic wound healing [29]. However, its diagnostic value and regulatory mechanisms in periodontitis remain insufficiently characterized, highlighting a critical knowledge gap in understanding circRNA-mediated pathogenesis of periodontal disease.

This study first analyzed circMYO9B expression in periodontitis and observed higher levels in GCF from patients with periodontitis. Periodontal inflammation involves multiple mediators—including adhesion molecules, chemokines, and proinflammatory cytokines—that collectively contribute to tissue destruction. Because GCF is sampled directly from the periodontal interface, its composition changes dynamically and can reflect local pathological conditions. Several GCF markers have clinical relevance, including IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6; matrix metalloproteinases (MMP)-8 and MMP-9; and the RANKL/osteoprotegerin ratio, which correlate with inflammation severity, tissue breakdown, and bone loss [30]. GCF collection is minimally invasive and can be performed using filter strips or microcapillaries with limited discomfort, enabling site-specific sampling. These characteristics support its potential value for screening, longitudinal monitoring, and assessment of treatment response. To model this process, studies commonly use LPS or TNF-α to trigger inflammatory responses in cells [31,32]. In our experimental approach, we established an in vitro inflammatory model using LPS-stimulated PDLCs and validated the model by demonstrating time-dependent increases in IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α. circMYO9B expression increased in parallel with LPS exposure, consistent with prior findings by Yu et al. [11]. Clinical parameters also differed between groups for PD/probing pocket depth, CAL, and BOP, and circMYO9B showed discriminatory potential between periodontitis and control groups.

This study supports a pathogenic role for circMYO9B in periodontitis through functional and mechanistic analyses. circMYO9B silencing attenuated LPS-induced PDLC injury, indicating that circMYO9B contributes to inflammatory damage under these conditions, contributing to the progression of periodontitis. Through bioinformatics analysis, we further identified a circMYO9B/miR-4731-5p/USP15 regulatory axis in which circMYO9B functions as a sponge for miR-4731-5p, thereby increasing USP15 expression. miR-4731-5p was reduced in clinical samples and in the cellular model and directly interacted with circMYO9B. Moreover, inhibition of miR-4731-5p diminished the protective effects of circMYO9B knockdown, supporting the functional relevance of this ceRNA network. These findings provide mechanistic insights into circMYO9B function in periodontitis and could inform potential therapeutic strategies.

USP15 is a deubiquitinating enzyme that regulates protein stability, activity, and subcellular localization by removing ubiquitin modifications from target proteins [33]. USP15 has been implicated in inflammation, immune responses, apoptosis, and autophagy. In osteoarthritis, USP15 enhances TGF-β/SMAD2 signaling by forming a complex with and deubiquitinating ERK2 [34]. This mechanism provides a potential reference for our findings, as USP15 may similarly amplify proinflammatory or bone-remodeling signals in the periodontal microenvironment. Consistent with this possibility, we confirmed that miR-4731-5p targets USP15, and we observed a positive correlation between circMYO9B and USP15 expression.

Functionally, miR-4731-5p overexpression attenuated LPS-induced PDLC injury, mirroring the protective effects observed with circMYO9B silencing. Importantly, concomitant USP15 overexpression diminished these benefits, supporting USP15 as a downstream effector in this regulatory axis. Together, these findings suggest that USP15 contributes to inflammatory damage in PDLCs and may represent a therapeutic target for periodontitis.

This study identifies circMYO9B as a contributor to periodontitis-associated inflammatory injury that operates through a miR-4731-5p/USP15 axis. This mechanism is consistent with inflammatory regulation by circRNA-mediated ceRNA networks and additionally implicates USP15 as a downstream effector. Several limitations should be noted. First, although our conclusions are supported by in vitro evidence, in vivo validation in animal models remains important. Prior work has shown that miR-27a-5p can alleviate periodontitis in mouse models [35]. Although animal experiments were planned, they were not performed because of resource constraints; future studies should incorporate animal models to confirm mechanistic and therapeutic relevance. Second, the diagnostic performance of circMYO9B was evaluated in a single-center cohort. Additional multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are needed to reduce potential selection bias and confirm clinical applicability.

In summary, circMYO9B is upregulated in periodontitis and shows diagnostic potential. Our findings indicate that circMYO9B can sponge miR-4731-5p, thereby increasing USP15 expression and promoting inflammatory injury in PDLCs, which may contribute to periodontitis progression (Supplementary Figure 1). Although targeting this axis showed therapeutic promise in cellular models, further validation in animal models and clinical studies is needed to establish translational relevance.

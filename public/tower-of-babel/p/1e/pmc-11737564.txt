Holistic healing: Navigating perspectives and prerogatives in treating temporomandibular joint disorders
Authors: Harneet Singh
Published: 2024

INTRODUCTION

Passing through the realms of otolaryngology to stomatology, the treatment of temporomandibular joint (TMJ) has always been in vogue.[1] As is the case with the evolution of every other aspect of medical and surgical management, the treatment of TMJ disorders has undergone a plethora of changes over the years. However, with a newfound awareness toward TMJ disorders, there is a paradigm shift toward the management of temporomandibular disorders. Different schools of thought have always been ubiquitous in planning the treatment of TMJ disorders due to a certain overlap in the classification of the disorders. There has always been a debate on the ideal treatment approach ranging from functional occlusion, musculoskeletal balance, stress management, etc.

HISTORY OF TREATMENT OF TMJ DISORDERS

The pursuit toward finding an appropriate treatment for TMJ disorders started in 1920 when Wright documented that the deafness was caused due to the position of the mandible and TMJ, and he was seconded by Decker (1925) who again corroborated the fact that the deafness was caused by the retrusion of the condyles. In 1934, Costen claimed that all TMJ disorders were related to various otological conditions.[2] However, his theory was reported to be highly convoluted due to poor evidence and hence disapproved. Inspired by this, dentists began to recognize the significance of occlusion and mandibular position as key factors in the etiology of TMJ disorders. The first evidence of the scientific workup for TMJ disorders was found in the early 1950s. Evolving from the gnathological concepts to neuromuscular and myocentric approaches, the major concept behind the treatment has always been symptom-based. The 1970s and 1980s saw a mix of the application of biopsychosocial elements and occlusion harmony. Finally, OPPERA (Orofacial Pain: Prospective Evaluation and Risk Assessment) was established with the advent of the twenty-first century and this led to widespread research in the field of TMJ disorders.[3]

ENTHUSIASTIC FRENZY

With a wide-ranging prevalence of 20%–88%, TMJ disorders affect every age group and gender with slight predilection here and there. According to the literature, women are more affected than men. The frequency of TMJ disorders increases with increasing age. While the prevailing emphasis is on symptom management rather than tackling the root cause, there is a noticeable infusion of radical approaches in the utilization of treatment modalities by young enthusiasts. The advent of advanced diagnostic tools and technologies in health care has undoubtedly brought about significant improvements in the diagnosis and treatment of various medical conditions, including those related to TMJ disorders. However, it is essential to recognize that while these tools can provide valuable information, a comprehensive and thoughtful approach to diagnosis and treatment is crucial. With the latest advent of gadgetry, a bandwagon approach has set in toward the treatment protocols. The prevailing belief is that advanced diagnostic tools are guaranteed to achieve the correct diagnosis, consequently guiding the selection of an optimal treatment approach. However, it is essential to note that fancy diagnoses and extravagant treatments do not always guarantee the complete healing of the patient.

WHY DOES TMJ NEED A HOLISTIC APPROACH?

As per my understanding and the plethora of literature available, TMJ disorders are as unique as the joint itself. The TMJ is a unique and intricate joint that allows for various movements, including hinge-like rotation and sliding actions. Additionally, the joint is surrounded by a complex network of muscles, ligaments, and other structures. The multifaceted nature of TMJ disorders makes them highly individualized, and the causes, symptoms, and optimal treatment approaches can vary significantly from person to person.

The first and foremost point which can be associated in favor of the holistic approach toward TMJ disorder is multifactorial etiology. TMJ disorders can arise from a combination of factors like macro- and microtrauma, parafunctional habits like bruxism, stress, osteo-degenerative changes like arthritis, structural abnormalities, and muscular dysfunction. All these factors individually or in combination with each other present a diverse range of symptoms like jaw pain, tenderness, restricted mouth opening, deviation or deflection of the jaw, or annoying sounds like clicking or popping. Individuals experiencing myofascial pain often turn to a diverse array of healthcare professionals for assistance. This includes family physicians, otolaryngologists, orthopedics, neurologists, rheumatologists, osteopaths, physiatrists, psychiatrists, and anesthesiologists. Dentists, particularly those specializing in TMJ syndrome, commonly encounter patients with myofascial pain. Additionally, acupuncturists, chiropractors, physical therapists, occupational therapists, massage therapists, and psychotherapists regularly come across patients seeking relief from pain.

Oral and maxillofacial surgeons play a definitive role in the management of TMJ disorders. However, the evidence proves that only 1% of the cases are either refractory cases or frank arthritic diseases of TMJ that require surgical intervention. TMJ surgery is generally regarded as a final recourse in the management of various TMJ-related conditions. The research underscores the importance of exhausting conservative treatment modalities as initial steps in managing TMJ disorders. Nevertheless, in instances where conservative measures fail to yield satisfactory outcomes or when the pathology presents with significant structural compromise, surgical intervention emerges as a pragmatic therapeutic option. Many times, a young Turk or an enthusiastic surgeon may get driven by their passion or by a mere Funktionslust. i.e., “The tendency to perform surgery as they have been trained to do so” or for the cause of incentivization in terms of money and fame may perform surgery or arthroscopies for TMJ disorders even when the surgery is not indicated.[4] However, prudence lies in the selection of cases through the most scientifically research-based approach, where there is sufficient evidence to perform the warranted procedure as required.

A frequent scenario for patients experiencing symptoms of TMJ disorders involves a stack of reports and files amassed over time, reflecting a focus on managing the symptoms rather than addressing the root cause. An amalgamation of a compassionate diagnostic approach, selection of the right imaging and investigative modalities augmented by advanced diagnostic aids like Joint Vibration Analysis, Jaw Tracking Devices, TENS, EMG, and other paraphernalia can be used in formulating a good diagnosis and appropriate treatment protocol.[5]

However, the cure for TMJ disorders does not contain itself in the treatment but in healing. An empathetic approach to the patient with an earnest ability to hear the detailed description of the patient’s suffering pours the first drops of potion into the healing concoction. Treating the etiology rather than the symptoms is the mainstay behind the healing process of TMJ disorders. Optimization of sleep, alleviation of stress, maintenance of body posture and nutrition, and regularization of hormones are a few of the nuanced approaches which can be a part of the protocol before jumping on the bandwagon of fabricating splints, injecting a cocktail of solutions, strapping contraptions, or meddling with the maxilla-mandibular relationship.[36]

I would like to conclude this editorial by emphasizing the profound impact of any form of pain, discomfort, or compromised bodily functions on one’s quality of life. It is essential to broaden our perspective and aspire to be holistic healers, addressing the interconnected well-being of the mind, body, and soul, rather than adhering solely to conventional and standardized treatment approaches.

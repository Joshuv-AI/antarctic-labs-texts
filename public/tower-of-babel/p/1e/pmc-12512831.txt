Comprehensive analysis of FLT3-mutated patients with acute myeloid leukemia with updated 2022 European LeukemiaNet recommendations: insights from the Turkish AML registry project

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the prognostic significance and clinical impact of the revised 2022 European LeukemiaNet (ELN) classification for acute myeloid leukemia (AML), focusing particularly on patients harboring fms-like tyrosine kinase 3-internal tandem duplication (FLT3-ITD) mutations.MethodsA retrospective, multicenter observational study was conducted by the Turkish Society of Hematology-Acute Leukemias Working Group, analyzing 312 adult patients newly diagnosed with AML from January 2012 to December 2022. Patients with acute promyelocytic leukemia were excluded. FLT3-ITD mutations were detected using polymerase chain reaction and, when available, next-generation sequencing. Patients were classified according to the 2017 ELN risk stratification, and FLT3-ITD-positive cases were reclassified based on the updated 2022 ELN criteria. Endpoints were complete remission (CR), disease-free survival (DFS), and overall survival (OS).ResultsFLT3-ITD mutations were identified in 54 (17.3%) patients. According to the 2022 ELN classification, 29 patients previously categorized as the favorable (n = 6) or adverse-risk (n = 23) groups were reclassified into the intermediate-risk group, highlighting the substantial impact of removing the FLT3-ITD allelic ratio from risk stratification. With a median follow-up of 31.8 months, OS significantly differed among the 2017 ELN favorable-, intermediate-, and adverse-risk categories (not reached, 21.6 months, and 9.5 months, respectively, p < 0.001). FLT3-ITD-positive patients demonstrated significantly inferior DFS (p = 0.038) and OS (p = 0.009) compared to FLT3-ITD-negative patients. In patients achieving first CR, allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) improved OS in intermediate-risk (p = 0.003), showed a trend in adverse-risk (p = 0.098), and no benefit in favorable-risk (p = 0.351). Among reclassified FLT3-ITD-positive patients, survival outcomes aligned closely with the original intermediate-risk group defined by the 2017 ELN, supporting the rationale behind the ELN revision.ConclusionsOur findings validate the prognostic utility of the revised 2022 ELN guidelines, especially regarding FLT3-ITD-positive AML, emphasizing that the exclusion of the FLT3-ITD allelic ratio yields a more biologically consistent risk categorization. Furthermore, the data support tailored ELN-based risk stratification for older AML patients. Given the modest benefit of HSCT in adverse-risk patients, future refinements should further stratify this group to address their unmet therapeutic needs and enhance survival outcomes.Trial registrationThe study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05979675).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14987-z.Keywords: Acute myeloid leukemia, European LeukemiaNet, FLT3-ITD mutation, Risk stratification, Prognostic value

Background

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML), a spectrum of myeloid malignancies with clinical and genetic heterogeneity, is experiencing rapid advancements in its treatment due to progress in targeted and personalized therapies, as well as decisions based on measurable residual disease (MRD) [1–4]. In September 2022, the European LeukemiaNet (ELN) released a modified AML risk categorization, which was first developed in 2010 and updated in 2017 [5–7]. The 2022 ELN stratification preserved the distribution of patients into three genetic risk categories at diagnosis, but introduced critical changes to reflect new insights into AML risk classification.

One of the substantial revisions in the 2022 ELN classification involved patients with fms-like tyrosine kinase 3-internal tandem duplication (FLT3-ITD) mutations. These patients are now categorized as intermediate-risk if they lack adverse-risk markers or core-binding factor rearrangements, regardless of the FLT3-ITD allelic ratio or co-mutation with nucleophosmin 1 (NPM1) [3, 8]. This reclassification is motivated by promising results from using kinase inhibitors in both induction and salvage therapies [9–11], and the crucial role of MRD monitoring in guiding treatment strategies, especially in determining the optimal timing for early transplantation [12, 13].

Despite these advancements, comprehensive data validating the 2022 ELN criteria, especially concerning FLT3-ITD mutations in a large adult AML population, are lacking. This study aimed to analyze the prognostic significance of FLT3-ITD-related risk reclassification under the 2022 ELN stratification system, and to assess the predictive value of FLT3-ITD mutations in a large cohort of newly diagnosed patients with AML.

Several large-scale studies have recently validated the ELN 2022 classification in broader AML populations, demonstrating its prognostic utility across diverse molecular subgroups. For example, Tazi et al. [14], and Lachowiez et al. [15], have shown that the revised system enhances risk stratification by reclassifying adverse genetic features and eliminating reliance on FLT3-ITD allelic ratio. Although these studies did not focus specifically on FLT3-ITD–mutated patients, this subgroup was included in their multicenter analyses, indirectly supporting the applicability of the 2022 ELN criteria. Our study builds upon this foundational work by providing a focused real-world evaluation of FLT3-ITD–mutated AML within the updated ELN framework.

Methods

Patient selection and therapy modalities

In this retrospective analysis, we examined 312 adult patients (aged ≥ 18 years) newly diagnosed with AML between January 2012 and December 2022. This study was conducted by the Turkish Society of Hematology-Acute Leukemias Working Group and involved a national multicenter observational registry. The diagnosis of AML followed the 2016 World Health Organization classification guidelines [16]. Patients received induction therapy, including cytarabine-based intensive chemotherapy or hypomethylating agents, either alone or in combination with venetoclax. Patients with acute promyelocytic leukemia were excluded because of their unique characteristics. All participants provided written informed consent before the study, and the Declaration of Helsinki was strictly complied with during the study period. The Ministry of Health of the Republic of Turkey and Turkish Medicines and Medical Devices Agency approved the data analysis. The study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05979675).

Response definitions and endpoints

We evaluated complete remission with incomplete hematologic recovery (CRi) and complete remission (CR) using the revised international working group criteria for AML [17]. Disease-free survival (DFS) was measured from the date of achieving CR/CRi to the occurrence of relapse, death, or the last follow-up. Relapse was defined as hematologic relapse only; molecular relapse was not included due to limited MRD data and lack of methodological standardization across centers. Overall survival (OS) was determined from the date of AML diagnosis to either the date of death or the last observation without censoring at the time of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). To account for time-dependent bias in evaluating HSCT outcomes, we performed a 6-month landmark survival analysis based on the median time to transplant in our cohort, conducted as part of the ELN 2017 risk group evaluation. Patients who died or were censored before this timepoint were excluded from the analysis.

Cytogenetics and molecular analysis

Cytogenetic analysis of blood or bone marrow (BM) samples was performed at diagnosis in a Ministry of Health-approved laboratory using banding techniques and hybridization. Cytogenetically normal AML was identified by examining at least 20 metaphase cells from immediate or short-term unstimulated cultures (1–3 days). FLT3-ITD analysis was conducted using the polymerase chain reaction technique or Sanger sequencing method [18]. Variants were categorized according to the consensus criteria by the Association for Molecular Pathology, American Society of Clinical Oncology, and College of American Pathologists [19]. The diagnostic mutational status of recurrently mutated genes in patients with AML who had sufficient samples at available centers was assessed using next-generation sequencing (NGS) after extracting genomic deoxyribonucleic acid from peripheral blood or BM samples. Manual risk reclassification was conducted for patients with FLT3-ITD mutations in accordance with the 2022 ELN guidelines.

MRD was evaluated using either multiparameter flow cytometry (MFC) or quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), depending on institutional protocols and patient-specific molecular markers. MFC was the predominant method and was performed Locally using 8–10 color panels designed to identify leukemia-associated immunophenotypes. While some centers adopted EuroFlow-compatible panels and analysis strategies, complete harmonization across centers was not achieved. The Lower Limit of detection was typically 10⁻3, and in some laboratories, sensitivity approached 10⁻4. PCR-based MRD evaluation was performed in a subset of patients with corresponding genetic aberrations. These assays were conducted at the institutional level and were not centrally validated across the registry network. Although international recommendations were generally referenced, strict uniformity in MRD assessment practices could not be ensured in this real-world, multicenter setting [20]. MRD was considered positive based on at least one positive result by either MFC or qPCR. However, due to the limited number of MRD-assessed patients (n = 17, 5.4%) and the lack of standardization in timing, assay type, and reporting across centers, MRD-related results were not included in the current survival or relapse analyses.

Statistical analyses

We used Pearson's chi-square or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables to compare ELN risk categories with other patient characteristics. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to analyze continuous variables. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to analyze the factors related to achieving CR, and Cox regression was used to examine the OS endpoints. Variables for multivariate Cox regression were selected based on both clinical importance and statistical significance (p < 0.1) in univariate survival analysis. The final covariates included age, gender, and hemoglobin level (based on clinical relevance), as well as WHO classification and ELN 2017 risk group (based on univariate significance). Kaplan–Meier curves for DFS and OS were plotted, and group comparisons were conducted using the log-rank test.

Results

Distribution of 2017 ELN risk groups according to patient characteristics

The final analysis included 312 patients: 176 (56.4%) were male, and the median age was 56 years (range, 18–89 years). According to the 2017 ELN criteria, participants were assigned to favorable- (67, 21.5%), intermediate- (195, 62.5%), and adverse- (50, 16%) risk categories. FLT3-ITD mutations were identified in 54 patients (17.3%), with the highest prevalence observed in the adverse-risk group (58.0%), followed by the intermediate (9.7%) and favorable (9.0%) groups (p < 0.001). In accordance with the 2022 ELN recommendations, FLT3-ITD-positive patients without adverse-risk markers or core-binding factor rearrangements were reassigned to the intermediate-risk group, regardless of allelic ratio. This reclassification affected 29 patients who were previously categorized as either favorable (n = 6) or adverse risk (n = 23) under the 2017 ELN criteria, underscoring the significant impact of the updated risk stratification system.

Among the full cohort, 59.9% of patients were < 60 years and 40.1% were ≥ 60 years. Age distribution across ELN 2017 risk groups did not differ significantly (p = 0.295). Allogeneic HSCT was performed in 25.6% of younger patients and 6.2% of older patients (p < 0.001).

Response and survival outcomes according to the 2017 ELN genetic risk categories

The CR rates among patients stratified into favorable-, intermediate-, and adverse-risk categories according to the 2017 ELN were 55.2% (n = 37/67), 43.3% (n = 84/194), and 38.0% (n = 19/50), respectively. After a median follow-up of 31.8 months (95% CI, 26.5–37.1), the median OS for the study cohort was 23.6 months (95% CI, 16.4–30.7). According to the 2017 ELN classification, the median OS was not reached for the favorable-risk category, 21.6 months (95% CI, 16.1–27.1) for the intermediate-risk category, and 9.5 months (95% CI, 0.1–18.8) for the adverse-risk category. The 2017 ELN genetic risk stratifications indicated patient outcomes for DFS (p = 0.028), and OS (p < 0.001). In addition, FLT3-ITD-positive patients exhibited significantly inferior DFS (p = 0.038) and OS (p = 0.009) compared to those without the mutation, suggesting an overall adverse prognostic impact in the study population (Fig. 1).

In contrast, for patients aged ≥ 60 years, neither DFS (p = 0.312) nor OS (p = 0.076) showed statistically significant differences among the ELN 2017 risk categories (Fig. 2).

In multivariate analysis, older age (p = 0.035), lower hemoglobin levels (p = 0.011), and intermediate- or adverse-risk classification based on the 2017 ELN (p = 0.040 and p = 0.029, respectively) were independently associated with a reduced likelihood of achieving CR. Additionally, AML with myelodysplasia-related changes was significantly associated with lower CR rates compared to AML with recurrent genetic abnormalities (p = 0.038) (Supplementary Fig. 1 A). Older age (p = 0.008) and WHO-defined diagnosis of myeloid sarcoma or acute leukemias of ambiguous lineage (p = 0.013) were independently associated with inferior OS (Supplementary Fig. 1B). In multivariate analysis, older age (p = 0.010) and diagnosis of myeloid sarcoma or acute leukemias of ambiguous lineage (p = 0.003) were independently associated with inferior DFS (Supplementary Fig. 1 C). According to 2022 ELN risk stratification, patients with FLT3-ITD mutations who were reclassified into the intermediate-risk group showed significantly worse OS (p = 0.004) and DFS (p = 0.007) compared to 2017 ELN favorable-risk patients, while survival did not differ from 2017 ELN intermediate- or adverse-risk groups (Fig. 3).

To further explore the impact of treatment intensity, we stratified survival outcomes by intensive versus non-intensive therapy. The ELN 2017 risk stratification retained significant prognostic value in the intensive treatment group (log-rank p = 0.033 for DFS and p < 0.001 for OS), while no statistically significant difference was observed in the non-intensive treatment group (p = 0.175 for DFS and p = 0.181 for OS). These results are presented in Supplementary Fig. 2.

Impact of allogeneic HSCT on survival outcomes according to the 2017 ELN genetic risk categories

Only 17.4% of patients underwent consolidative HSCT after a median of 6.4 months (range, 1.3–19.6 months). HSCT in CR1 was associated with significantly improved OS in intermediate-risk patients (p = 0.003), showed a favorable trend in adverse-risk patients (p = 0.098), and conferred no OS benefit in favorable-risk patients (p = 0.351). These findings were confirmed by the 6-month landmark analysis based on ELN 2017 classification (Supplementary Fig. 3). Among FLT3-ITD–positive patients, 13 underwent HSCT in CR1, while 31 did not. Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated significantly improved DFS (p = 0.002) and OS (p = 0.023) in the transplanted group (Fig. 4).

Effect of FLT3-ITD mutation, allele ratio, and midostaurin use in AML

In the comparison between FLT3-ITD-positive and -negative patients, the CR rates were 40.7% and 45.9%, respectively, with no statistically significant difference (p = 0.487). To assess whether the FLT3-ITD allelic ratio added discriminatory value, we analyzed outcomes based on a cutoff of 0.5. No significant differences were observed in DFS (p = 0.492) or OS (p = 0.756) between patients with high and low allele ratios (Supplementary Fig. 4). Interestingly, CR was achieved in 48.8% of patients with a high allele ratio versus only 15.4% in those with a low ratio (p = 0.033) (Supplementary Table 1).

Among the 54 patients identified with FLT3-ITD–mutated AML, 16.7% (n = 9) received midostaurin during induction therapy. Compared with a small subset of FLT3-ITD-positive patients who received midostaurin, midostaurin-naïve patients exhibited a numerically higher complete remission rate (50.5% vs. 20.0%, p = 0.106), without significant differences in DFS (p = 0.200) or OS (p = 0.145) (Supplementary Fig. 5).

Finally, in FLT3-ITD-positive patients with AML, those with mutated NPM1 had comparable CR (40 vs. 46%, p = 0.743) and relapse rates (33% vs. 23%, p = 0.686), as well as DFS (p = 0.739) and OS (p = 0.906) outcomes compared to patients with wild-type NPM1 (Supplementary Fig. 6).

Among the 312 patients, 49 (15.7%) received non-intensive therapy. Of these, 12 (24.5%) received venetoclax-based regimens. FLT3 inhibitors were administered to 9 patients with FLT3-ITD–mutated AML, corresponding to 16.7% of all FLT3-ITD–positive cases in the cohort. All received midostaurin in combination with intensive chemotherapy. In the full cohort, patients receiving intensive chemotherapy achieved higher CR rates (48.5% vs. 26.5%, p = 0.007) and experienced longer survival compared to those on non-intensive regimens (median OS: 31.5 vs. 11.8 months, p < 0.001; median DFS: 19.5 vs. 8.1 months, p = 0.021), as shown in Fig. 5. When stratified by FLT3-ITD status, intensive therapy remained significantly beneficial in FLT3-ITD–negative patients (OS: 33.0 vs. 11.3 months, p < 0.001; DFS: 22.3 vs. 8.1 months, p = 0.0102), but not in those with FLT3-ITD mutations (OS: 11.6 vs. 24.6 months, p = 0.882; DFS: 9.0 vs. 3.6 months, p = 0.900), as illustrated in Fig. 6.

Discussion

In this study, which included a large cohort with Long-term follow-up, we specifically examined the impact of the 2022 ELN classification revisions on FLT3-ITD-positive patients by comparing them with the 2017 ELN risk stratification. Our analysis focused on FLT3-ITD mutations identified by institutional laboratories and validated by national health authorities, using data from the real-world Turkish AML Registry Project. The findings confirmed the prognostic value of the 2017 ELN genetic risk model, and demonstrated that FLT3-ITD-driven reclassification under the 2022 ELN criteria resulted in OS and DFS outcomes that were comparable to those of other intermediate-risk patients. This consistency may be attributed to the beneficial effects of allogeneic HSCT as a post-remission strategy optimized for genetic risk profiles due to the FLT3-ITD mutation.

The unexpectedly poor long-term survival in our FLT3-ITD-mutated cohort highlights systemic barriers in AML care within resource-limited settings. While clinical trial populations report ~ 50% 5-year OS for FLT3-ITD AML, real-world outcomes vary widely depending on access to FLT3 inhibitors, transplant availability, and treatment intensity. Studies show that patients lacking both FLT3 inhibitors and HSCT have 3-year OS rates as Low as 2.2–25.8%, with median OS approaching 9 months [21–23]. In our study, limited transplant access, delayed drug availability, and reduced use of intensive therapies likely contributed to this survival gap. These findings emphasize the urgent need to improve infrastructure, early access to precision therapies, and financial support mechanisms in emerging healthcare systems.

The prognostic utility of ELN 2017 was age-dependent, being more discriminatory in younger patients but less informative in older individuals. Therefore, our findings support the previous recommendations that ELN stratification should be adapted separately for both younger and older populations [24, 25]. This approach is also supported by the available treatment options that target particular genetic mutations [11] whose incidence differs between younger and older patient populations [24, 26, 27].

Intensive chemotherapy was associated with improved outcomes, particularly in FLT3-ITD–negative patients. In contrast, exploratory analyses in the FLT3-ITD–positive subgroup did not demonstrate a clear survival advantage with intensive therapy. Given the small sample size and baseline imbalances, these results should be interpreted cautiously and considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. These observations support the need for molecularly guided treatment strategies and underscore the importance of incorporating FLT3-targeted agents in future clinical practice.

Another exciting topic would be to evaluate the effect of allogeneic HSCT on the 2017 ELN genetic risk classifications. HSCT improved outcomes in intermediate- and adverse-risk groups but not in favorable-risk AML. Therefore, attention should be paid to the complex karyotype and TP53 mutations in the 2022 ELN adverse-risk category. TP53 alterations represent the predominant molecular abnormalities in complex karyotype AML and are related to advanced age [28]. Cases with TP53 mutations associated with a complex karyotype are unlikely to benefit from many therapies, including allogeneic HSCT [29]. In the recently published study validating the prognostic value of the 2022 ELN risk categorization system, the median OS for this patient group was only 6.5 months [3]. Given these poor outcomes, patients in this group should be directed to clinical studies whenever possible, aiming to explore novel therapeutic strategies and improve their prognosis [8, 30].

The low allogeneic HSCT rate (17.4%) observed in our FLT3-ITD-mutated cohort reflects several real-world limitations. The low HSCT rate reflected clinical factors such as age and comorbidities, as well as systemic barriers including donor availability and healthcare infrastructure [31, 32]. These findings align with previous observations from similar healthcare settings [33].

Molecular data have become crucial for risk stratification in patients with AML [34]. The demand for NGS panel testing in genetic risk stratification has increased, particularly as the updated 2022 ELN revision incorporates the significant changes that NGS can address. Moreover, the interest in FLT3-ITD detection by NGS analyses and the sensitivity of NGS assays for detecting FLT3-ITD mutations have been emphasized [35]. However, affordability and lack of standardization remain major barriers to the widespread use of NGS [34, 36]. These challenges highlight the need for efforts to make NGS testing more accessible and standardized to ensure that all patients with AML can benefit from precise genetic risk stratification and personalized treatment plans.

This study also reflects broader systemic disparities in AML management across global healthcare systems. In low- and middle-income countries, OS rates remain significantly lower than in high-income countries, largely due to differences in infrastructure, diagnostic capabilities, and access to treatment [34, 37]. The implementation of the 2022 ELN classification is particularly challenging in such settings, as access to NGS, FLT3-ITD allelic burden testing, and centralized MRD monitoring is often limited or delayed [34, 35]. In Turkey, a representative upper-middle-income country, these molecular diagnostics and the clinical availability of FLT3 inhibitors such as midostaurin only became widespread in recent years. Furthermore, access to HSCT remains uneven, constrained by national donor availability and insurance reimbursement limitations. These structural challenges emphasize the need to adapt ELN-based recommendations to real-world clinical environments. By providing data from an underrepresented region, our study complements trial-based evidence and highlights the necessity of context-sensitive AML management frameworks.

One of the most critical updates in the 2022 ELN recommendations, the primary focus of our study, is reconsidering the FLT3-ITD allele ratio. The updated guidelines define FLT3-ITD mutations without adverse genetic abnormalities as intermediate-risk, regardless of the FLT3-ITD allele ratio and NPM1 co-mutation [7]. Our study showed that excluding the FLT3-ITD allele ratio from the 2022 ELN genetic risk stratification led to the reclassification of 9.3% (n = 29) patients with AML, thereby shifting their risk category. Despite extensive research, the prognosis and treatment outcomes for FLT3-ITD-mutated AML cases remain controversial [22, 38, 39]. In our study, exploratory comparisons suggested numerical trends toward benefit with midostaurin exposure among patients with FLT3-ITD mutations; however, due to the very limited number of patients who received FLT3 inhibitors (n = 9), no statistically significant or reliable conclusions can be drawn. These findings should therefore be interpreted with great caution. Additionally, our data showed no considerable difference in survival between patients with FLT3-ITD mutations with NPM1 co-mutations and those with NPM1 wild-type, supporting the updated 2022 ELN recommendations.

The prognostic interplay between FLT3-ITD and NPM1 mutations has been well-established in both clinical trials and real-world analyses. The RATIFY trial demonstrated that NPM1 co-mutation may attenuate the adverse prognostic impact of FLT3-ITD, particularly in patients with a low allelic ratio receiving midostaurin-based therapy [11, 40]. However, this benefit appears to be treatment-context dependent. In our cohort, NPM1 co-mutation did not modify outcomes, likely reflecting the limited use of FLT3 inhibitors. Real-world studies similarly indicate that the favorable prognostic effect of NPM1 may be diminished in the absence of targeted therapy or timely allogeneic HSCT [41]. These observations underscore the importance of interpreting co-mutation profiles within the context of actual treatment implementation.

Recent comparative studies have examined the clinical impact of the 2022 ELN classification, which introduced important refinements over the 2017 version, including the exclusion of FLT3-ITD allelic ratio and a stricter definition for favorable CEBPA mutations [7]. However, data from large real-world cohorts suggest that the prognostic superiority of ELN 2022 may be modest or context-specific [8, 42]. Moreover, further stratification within the adverse-risk group has led to the proposal of high-risk categories such as the “Adverse-Plus” group, including TP53 mutations, MECOM rearrangements, or complex karyotypes, which have been associated with poor outcomes in allo-HSCT recipients [43, 44]. While our dataset was based on ELN 2017 due to the timing of registry initiation and incomplete genomic profiles, we plan to implement ELN 2022-based risk stratification in future registry updates as more complete molecular data become available. A key limitation of our registry is the absence of centralized MRD assessment. While flow cytometry and qPCR were widely used, variability in assay sensitivity, gating strategies, and adherence to international MRD guidelines across centers may affect the comparability of MRD-related findings [20]. These real-world inconsistencies should be taken into account when interpreting our results. Moreover, only 5.4% of patients in our cohort underwent MRD assessment, and due to the lack of standardization in methods, MRD-related data were not incorporated into the outcome analyses. Future phases of the registry will prioritize harmonized, centrally validated MRD evaluation to enable meaningful comparisons.

The limitations of this study include its retrospective design and non-trial setting. To mitigate heterogeneity, we stratified patients by age, thereby enabling evaluation of younger patients who were mainly candidates for intensive therapy. The use of FLT3 inhibitors combined with chemotherapy was restricted to a small subset of FLT3-mutated patients, as midostaurin only became widely available in Turkey in the past three years after reimbursement. This limited access may explain the relatively poor outcomes of FLT3-ITD–mutated patients in the intermediate-risk group under ELN 2017. Moreover, outcomes for many patients could not be assessed using the new molecular and cytogenetic markers of ELN 2022, since NGS adoption was staggered across centers. Although both 17p deletion (FISH) and TP53 mutation testing were performed, TP53 mutational data were incomplete, and some FLT3-ITD–mutated patients without del(17p) may still have carried TP53 mutations, potentially leading to under-classification of adverse risk under ELN 2017. We are expanding the registry to increase evaluable cases and molecular data for future analyses. Despite these limitations, our study provides important insights into the real-world application of revised ELN guidelines and FLT3 inhibitor therapy in AML.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study specifically evaluated FLT3-ITD mutations to assess the impact of the revised 2022 ELN classification. The updated criteria resulted in a larger proportion of patients being reclassified into the intermediate-risk group. Notably, these reclassified patients exhibited survival outcomes comparable to those originally classified as intermediate risk under the 2017 ELN system. These findings support the biological rationale for eliminating the FLT3-ITD allelic ratio from genetic risk stratification in AML. Furthermore, our analysis demonstrated that the 2017 ELN stratification effectively differentiated OS among younger patients with AML across the three risk categories. However, this prognostic discrimination was not observed in older adults, highlighting the need to tailor ELN-based risk stratification separately for this population. Additionally, specific genetic alterations that differ in frequency between younger and older patients and differences in treatment options may also be highlighted. For patients who achieved CR1, allogeneic HSCT demonstrated a clear survival benefit in intermediate-risk patients and appeared to offer a limited but measurable advantage even in the adverse-risk group. Improvements by further categorizing patients at adverse risk, especially by highlighting the unmet needs of patients with very poor prognoses, may improve the original system. However, high costs and standardization issues remain significant challenges in providing standard care to patients with AML worldwide. Considering the additional aspects and incorporating the effects of new drugs beyond disease biology and genetic mutations may lead to further improvements in the prognosis of AML.

Stability analysis of deep and large vertical shaft excavation by raise boring machine method through upper soft and lower hard strata
Authors: Guofeng Wang, Yongqiao Fang, Fayi Deng, Kaifu Ren, Haiyan Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In the deep and large vertical shaft excavation project, the composite stratum poses a great challenge to the stability of the vertical shaft surrounding rock, especially the upper soft and lower hard stratum. In this paper, a tunnel ventilation shaft is taken as the engineering background, and ABAQUS finite element software is used to establish a three-dimensional numerical model of the deep and large shaft crossing the upper soft and lower hard rock layers, and analyze the radial displacement and stress evolution characteristics of the surrounding rock. Subsequently, the effects of soft and hard rock stiffness ratios and construction stages on the stability of the surrounding rock were investigated by parametric analysis. The results show that the radial displacement and stress of the surrounding rock in homogeneous rock layers increase linearly with depth. However, the radial displacement and stress in the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface decrease sharply with depth. In contrast, the influence of the soft and hard rock interface on the stability of the surrounding rock was most significant during the reverse and forward drilling stages. As the difference between soft and hard rock stiffness increases, the displacement attenuation near the soft and hard rock interface is more significant, and the threat to the stability of the surrounding rock is greater. These findings can provide a reference for deformation control and support optimization of deep and large vertical shafts under complex geological conditions.

Introduction

With the development of underground construction, ventilation shafts are widely used in tunneling projects as an important facility1. However, construction safety and surrounding rock stability during shaft excavation often face severe challenges under complex geological conditions2. The geological structure of alternating soft and hard rocks is one of the most complex factors3. Due to the difference in mechanical properties between soft rock and hard rock layers, the shaft may experience stress concentration and large deformation of surrounding rock during excavation. In severe cases, this may lead to safety hazards such as failure of the support structure and collapse of the shaft4. Therefore, it is important to study the influence of alternating soft and hard strata on the stability of shaft excavation to ensure the safety of shaft construction.

The stability of surrounding rock during shaft excavation under complex geological conditions has been the main concern of scholars5,6. In particular, in the case of vertical shafts excavated by the raise boring machine method, the deformation characteristics of the surrounding rock have a significant impact on construction safety and structural stability7,8,9. Previous studies10,11,12 have revealed the dynamic response characteristics of soft and hard rock interfaces during shaft excavation. Li et al.13 found the incongruous deformation and damage of the structure at the interface of soft and hard rock under seismic action through numerical simulation and proposed that the reinforcement of steel pipe sheets can effectively control the structural damage. Zhang et al.14 proposed vibration-damping measures based on the damage of equipment in shield construction caused by alternating soft and hard strata, thus effectively controlling the propagation of blasting stress waves in the interface of soft and hard rocks, and significantly improving the construction safety of vertical shafts under complex geological conditions. Duan et al.15 analyzed the bearing performance of post-grouted shafts and stiffened deep cement mixing shafts in alternating soft and hard strata based on field tests and numerical simulations, and the results showed that post-grouted shafts have better bearing capacity in heterogeneous soil layers. Yang et al.6 proposed a novel seismic scheme that can effectively improve the seismic response of shield tunnel shafts in the transition zone of soft and hard strata. Liang et al.16 investigated the surrounding rock deformation characteristics of vertical shaft excavation in soft and hard rock strata and found that the mechanical difference between the interface and the rock is the main factor of the surrounding rock deformation of vertical shafts. It is noteworthy that the shaft deformation within the combined soft and hard rock strata is 3 to 5 times that of the rigid strata. As mentioned earlier, a large number of studies have explored the influence of soft and hard strata on the structural performance of vertical shafts from different perspectives. However, fewer studies have been conducted on the effect of combined soft and hard rock strata on the stability of the surrounding rock during shaft excavation.

Although many studies have investigated the influence of alternating soft and hard strata on tunneling, few have quantified the displacement discontinuity and stress redistribution at the soft–hard interface during progressive excavation of vertical shafts using the raise boring method. This gap motivates the present work. In this study, based on ABAQUS software, a detailed investigation of the stability of the surrounding rock in vertical shaft excavation under the combination of soft and hard rock strata was carried out. Considering different soft and hard rock modulus ratios and construction stages, the influence of soft and hard rock strata on the deformation and stress field of the surrounding rock during shaft excavation was analyzed. This study aims to provide an in-depth mechanical analysis for the construction of vertical shafts using the raise boring machine method under complex geological conditions and to provide data basis and practical guidance for similar projects.

Project overview

Zimuyan Tunnel is the controlling project of the Wulong-Daozhen highway, which is located at the border of Guizhou and Chongqing, China. One ventilation shaft was set up in the right hole of the Zimuyan Tunnel. The shaft has a diameter of 6.4 m and a depth of 428.2 m and was excavated using the raise boring machine method17. Numerical simulation

3D model setup

The finite element analysis software ABAQUS was used to establish a three-dimensional numerical model to investigate the effect of traversing the upper soft and lower hard rock layers on the stability of the surrounding rock when deep and large vertical shafts are excavated by the raise boring machine method. Considering the symmetry of the model and the computational efficiency19, a numerical model of 1/2 shaft excavation was established with a model dimension of 100 m (length) × 50 m (width) × 100 m (height). Figure 3 shows the 3D numerical model based on ABAQUS software. The horizontal dimension of the model is about 15.6 times the diameter of the shaft (6.4 m), which can effectively avoid the influence of boundary effects on the calculation results20,21. This study mainly focuses on the stability of the surrounding rock near the interface between soft and hard rock during the excavation of the shaft, so only the stability of the surrounding rock in the range of 50 m above and below the interface between soft and hard rock is considered. In addition, the overlying soil layer (117 m thick) can be replaced by an equivalent self-gravity stress acting on the top surface of the model (Fig. 3). Horizontal constraints are applied around the model, horizontal and vertical constraints are applied at the bottom of the model, and the top of the model is a free boundary condition. The 3D numerical model is divided into 13,652 cells and 19,171 nodes. The soft–hard rock interface was modeled as a sharp material boundary with shared nodes between the two strata, without additional interface thickness. In this study, the interface between soft and hard rock was modeled as a perfectly bonded contact, without considering potential slip, in order to focus on stiffness contrast effects. Future work will consider slip or weakening of the interface to further evaluate stability. A tie constraint was used to model the contact between the soft and hard rock, ensuring full displacement compatibility. This represents an idealized strong bonding condition, which is reasonable for numerical parametric comparison. The possible effect of partial slip or imperfect bonding will be considered in future work.Fig. 3Three-dimensional numerical model.

Three-dimensional numerical model.

Models and parameters

The mechanical behavior of the surrounding rock materials was simulated using the Mohr–Coulomb constitutive model22,23,24. The initial support structure was simulated using shell elements and set up as a linear elastic constitutive model. Moreover, the thickness of the initial support is 0.5 m. The interaction between the well wall and the surrounding rock is modeled using the tie contact relationship. Calculation procedure

In this study, the numerical simulation demonstrates the construction process of the shaft using the raise boring machine method, i.e., excavating a pilot hole with a diameter of 0.23 m (defined as the pilot hole stage), and then enlarging the diameter of the pilot hole to 0.27 m (defined as the reexpansion stage). Subsequently, a reverse cutter was used to excavate upwards with a hole diameter of 1.6 m (defined as the reverse drilling stage). Finally, the excavation was carried out from the surface downwards so that the borehole was excavated to a diameter of 6.4 m (defined as the forward excavation phase). Results and analysis

Radial displacement of the surrounding rock

To study the stability of the surrounding rock of the vertical shaft through the interface of soft and hard rock excavated by the raise boring machine method, Fig. 5 shows the variation of the radial displacement of the surrounding rock with depth when crossing the interface of soft and hard rock at different construction stages. Since shaft excavation is an axisymmetric problem, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at different orientations (0, 90°, and 180°) varies consistently with depth. Overall, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock increases linearly with depth25. However, near the soft–hard rock interface, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock decreases significantly with depth and then continues to increase with depth. This conclusion is similar to the findings reported by Wang et al.18. Therefore, the soft and hard rock interface has a significant adverse effect on the stability of the surrounding rock in vertical shafts excavated by the raise boring machine method. For the convenience of quantitative analysis, the reduction of the surrounding rock displacement near the interface of soft and hard rock is defined as ∆Uri, as shown in Fig. 5a. In addition, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock is defined as Uri. The trend of radial displacement during the re-expansion stage is further illustrated in Fig. 5b, while Fig. 5c presents the trend of radial displacement during the reverse drilling stage, and Fig. 5d shows the trend of radial displacement during the forward drilling stage.Fig. 5Variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock with depth when crossing the interface between soft and hard rock at different construction stages.

Variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock with depth when crossing the interface between soft and hard rock at different construction stages.

As mentioned earlier, the sudden decrease in radial displacement of the surrounding rock near the interface between the soft and hard rock indicates that this region has an important influence on the stability of the surrounding rock. To further investigate the influence of the soft and hard rock interfaces on the stability of the surrounding rock at different construction stages, Fig. 6 shows the radial displacement of the surrounding rock near the soft and hard rock interfaces at different construction stages. The radial displacements of the surrounding rock in both the pilot hole and re-expansion stages were small, indicating that the effect of the initial excavation on the deformation of the surrounding rock was not significant. During the reverse drilling stage, there was a significant increase in the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at equal depths. Taking the soft and hard rock interface as an example, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock in the reverse drilling phase increases from 0.053 to 0.232 mm, and the decrease in the radial displacement of the surrounding rock near the interface increases from 0.034 to 0.070 mm. As construction progressed, there was a further increase in the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at different depths, especially at the soft and hard rock interfaces. During the forward construction stage, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at the interface between the soft and hard rock increases from 0.232 to 0.607 mm, and the sudden change in displacement near the interface increases from 0.070 to 0.149 mm. Therefore, monitoring should be carried out during the reverse drilling stage and the forward drilling stage to ensure the structural safety and stability of the surrounding rock during the shaft excavation.Fig. 6Radial displacement of surrounding rock near the interface between soft and hard rock at different construction stages.

Radial displacement of surrounding rock near the interface between soft and hard rock at different construction stages.

Radial stress in the surrounding rock

Figure 7 demonstrates the variation of radial stresses in the surrounding rock with depth when crossing the soft and hard rock interface at different construction stages. At different construction stages, the radial stress of surrounding rock in homogeneous rock layers increases linearly with depth. However, the radial stresses in the surrounding rock near the interface between soft and hard rock do not change significantly with depth and even tend to decrease. During the pilot hole stage, the radial stress in the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface decreases with depth and then increases linearly with depth. In contrast, the radial stresses in the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface first increase and then decrease with depth during the re-expansion, reverse drilling, and forward drilling stages, and then increase linearly with depth. Therefore, stress monitoring should be carried out near the interface between soft and hard rock during the construction phase of a vertical shaft using the raise boring machine method.Fig. 7Variation of radial stress of surrounding rock with depth when crossing the interface between soft and hard rock at different construction stages.

Variation of radial stress of surrounding rock with depth when crossing the interface between soft and hard rock at different construction stages.

To investigate the influence of soft and hard rock interfaces on the stability of the surrounding rock during shaft excavation, the stress at the soft and hard rock interfaces and the stress change near the interfaces are defined as σri and ∆σri, respectively. Figure 8 illustrates the radial stress changes in the surrounding rock near the soft and hard rock interfaces at different construction stages. The radial stresses (σri) in the surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock did not change significantly at different construction stages. Moreover, the change in radial stresses (∆σri) in the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface gradually increased as construction progressed. It is noteworthy that the radial stress change in the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface increased from 0.023 to 0.044 MPa, an increase of 91.3%, during the reverse drilling stage. Therefore, the radial stress change in the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface is most significant during the reverse drilling stage. Therefore, stress monitoring near the interface between soft and hard rock should be emphasized during the reverse drilling stage.Fig. 8Radial stress of surrounding rock near the interface between soft and hard rock at different construction stages.

Radial stress of surrounding rock near the interface between soft and hard rock at different construction stages.

Parametric analyses

In the excavation process of a deep and large vertical shaft using the raise boring machine method, the effect of soft and hard rock stiffness ratios of 3:4, 3:5, 3:6, and 3:7, respectively, on the stability of the surrounding rock is further discussed.

Effect of stiffness ratio of soft and hard rock

Variation of the displacement field

Figure 9 demonstrates the variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock with depth for different soft and hard rock stiffness ratios. The change rule of radial displacement of surrounding rock with depth is consistent at different construction stages with different soft and hard rock stiffness ratios. In homogeneous rock layers, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock increases linearly with depth26. However, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface decreases sharply with depth. The smaller the soft–hard rock stiffness ratio, the more significant the difference in properties between the upper soft and lower hard rock layers. As the stiffness ratio of soft and hard rock decreases, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at equal depths increases. Moreover, the smaller the soft–hard rock stiffness ratio, the greater the attenuation of the radial displacement of the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface with depth. Therefore, the more pronounced the difference in the properties of the upper soft and lower hard rock layers, the more detrimental it is to the stability of the surrounding rock for shaft excavation. When the stiffness contrast is pronounced (e.g., below 3:5), the upper soft layer undergoes much larger deformation than the underlying hard rock, leading to stress concentration at the interface, consistent with observations by Li et al11 and Liu et al12. In engineering practice, additional reinforcement such as pipe-shed or advanced grouting is recommended near the soft–hard interface to mitigate this effect. When the soft and hard rock stiffness ratio is 3:4, the difference in the stiffness properties of the soft and hard rock is small, resulting in relatively small radial displacement of the surrounding rock. As the relative stiffness of the soft and hard rock layers decreases, there is a significant increase in the radial displacement of the surrounding rock in the overlying soft rock layers. In contrast, the increase in radial displacement of the surrounding rock in the underlying hard rock layer is less.Fig. 9Variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock with depth for different soft and hard rock stiffness ratios.

Variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock with depth for different soft and hard rock stiffness ratios.

Figure 10 shows the radial displacement of the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface for different soft–hard rock stiffness ratios. At different stages of construction, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock near the soft and hard rock interface gradually increases in attenuation with depth as the relative stiffness of the soft and hard rock decreases. When the relative stiffness ratio of soft and hard rock is less than 3:5, the attenuation of the radial displacement of the surrounding rock decreases. In addition, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at the interface of soft and hard rock (Uri) increases and then decreases slowly with the decrease of the relative stiffness of soft and hard rock. When the relative stiffness ratio of soft and hard rock is 3:5, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at the interface of soft and hard rock reaches a peak. Therefore, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock near the interface of soft and hard rock should be emphasized during all stages of shaft excavation using the raise boring machine method.Fig. 10Radial displacement of surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface for different soft–hard rock stiffness ratios.

Radial displacement of surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface for different soft–hard rock stiffness ratios.

Variation of the stress field

Figure 11 demonstrates the variation of radial stress in the surrounding rock with depth for different soft and hard rock stiffness ratios. The radial stress in the surrounding rock increases linearly with depth at different stages of construction. However, the radial stresses in the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface change abruptly with depth. When the relative stiffness ratio of soft and hard rock is 3:4, the radial stresses in the surrounding rock at equal depths are less. As the difference in stiffness between soft and hard rock increases, the radial stresses in the surrounding rock at equal depths increase significantly. When the relative stiffness ratio of soft and hard rock is less than 3:5, the relative stiffness of soft and hard rock has no significant effect on the radial stress of the surrounding rock in homogeneous rock layers. However, the radial stress of the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface still varies significantly with the relative stiffness ratio of the soft and hard rocks.Fig. 11Variation of radial stress of surrounding rock with depth for different soft and hard rock stiffness ratios.

Variation of radial stress of surrounding rock with depth for different soft and hard rock stiffness ratios.

Figure 12 presents the radial stress of surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface for different soft–hard rock stiffness ratios. As the stiffness ratio of soft and hard rocks decreases, the radial stress of the surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rocks first increases and then slightly decreases. When the relative stiffness of the soft and hard rock is 3:5, the radial stress of the surrounding rock at the interface of the soft and hard rock reaches the maximum value. In addition, the magnitude of attenuation of radial stresses in the surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface increases with depth as the soft–hard rock stiffness ratio decreases. When the relative stiffness of the soft and hard rock is greater than 3:6, the magnitude of the radial stresses in the surrounding rock near the soft and hard rock interface decreases with depth.Fig. 12Radial stress of surrounding rock near the soft–hard rock interface for different soft–hard rock stiffness ratios.

Effect of the construction stage

Variation of the displacement field

Figure 13 illustrates the variation of radial displacement of the surrounding rock with depth for different construction stages. For different soft and hard rock stiffness ratios, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock in the pilot hole stage and the re-expansion stage varied consistently with depth. In contrast, the increase in radial displacement of the surrounding rock in both stages at the same depth is not significant. As the shaft was excavated, there was a significant increase in the radial displacement of the surrounding rock during the reverse drilling stage and the forward drilling stage. In addition, as the shaft was excavated, the attenuation of the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock increased. Therefore, the stability of the surrounding rock near the interface between soft and hard rock should be monitored when the shaft is excavated by the raise boring machine method17.Fig. 13Variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock with depth at different construction stages.

Variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock with depth at different construction stages.

Figure 14 shows the variation of the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock at the construction stage. As the shaft is excavated using the raise boring machine method, the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock and its variation with depth shows a nonlinear increase. The radial displacement of the surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock is less and does not change significantly during the pilot hole stage and the re-expansion stage. Subsequently, there was a significant increase in the radial displacement of the surrounding rock at the soft and hard rock interface in the reverse drilling stage and the forward drilling stage. For example, when the soft and hard rock stiffness ratio was 3:5, the radial displacements of the surrounding rock at the soft and hard rock interfaces increased by 19.2%, 414.3%, and 1322.6% at the re-expansion stage, reverse drilling stage, and forward drilling stage, respectively. In addition, the attenuation values of the radial displacement of the surrounding rock near the soft and hard rock interfaces with depth increased by 43.1%, 195.5%, and 587.7%, respectively.Fig. 14Variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock at the construction stage.

Variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock at the construction stage.

Variation of the stress field

Figure 15 presents the variation of radial stress of the surrounding rock with depth for different construction stages. Compared to the radial displacement of the surrounding rock, the radial stresses of the surrounding rock at different construction stages do not change significantly. The radial stress of the surrounding rock varied essentially the same with depth during the pilot hole stage and the forward drilling stage. Similarly, the radial stresses in the surrounding rock vary consistently with depth during the re-expansion and reverse drilling stages. The radial stresses in the surrounding rock at equal depths decrease and then increase with shaft excavation, except near the interface between soft and hard rock.Fig. 15Variation of radial stress of surrounding rock with depth at different construction stages.

Variation of radial stress of surrounding rock with depth at different construction stages.

Figure 16 shows the variation of radial displacement of surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock at the construction stage. As the shaft is excavated, the radial stresses in the surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock are essentially constant. However, as the shaft is excavated, the attenuation value of the radial stresses in the surrounding rock near the interface between the soft and hard rock increases. Compared to the pilot hole stage, the radial stresses in the surrounding rock at the soft and hard rock interface increased by 2.9%, 6.5%, and 9.6% for the re-expansion stage, reverse drilling stage, and forward drilling stage, respectively. Moreover, the radial stress attenuation values of the surrounding rock near the soft and hard rock interfaces in the subsequent three stages increased by 14.9%, 36.9%, and 89.4%, respectively. Therefore, the strength of the initial support should be increased during the forward drilling stage to control the stability of the surrounding rock, and to ensure the safety of construction.Fig. 16Variation of radial stress of surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock with construction stage.

Variation of radial stress of surrounding rock at the interface between soft and hard rock with construction stage.

Conclusion

In this study, the changes of radial displacement and stress of surrounding rock during the excavation of a deep and large vertical shaft were analyzed by finite element software, which revealed the influence of the construction of vertical shafts using the raise boring machine method to cross the upper soft and lower hard rock layers on the stability of surrounding rock.

Therapeutic Plasma Exchange in Neurological Conditions: An Observation from the Eastern Part of India
Authors: Ajit Prasad Mishra, Srikant Kumar Sahoo, Girijanandini Kanungo, Lulup Kumar Sahoo, Shubhankar Mishra, Chandraprava Mishra
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) is an extracorporeal process of separation of plasma from the cellular components of blood and its replacement with analogous fluids. This process is effective in treatment of disease conditions from dysregulation of the humoral immune system by removal of various humoral pathogenic substances like antibodies, immune complexes, monoclonal proteins, toxins or cytokine(s) and/or the replenishment of a specific plasma factor.Aim and Objective:To evaluate major indications of therapeutic plasma exchange in neurological disorders. To identify major complications associated and factors associated with premature cessation of the therapeutic plasma exchange cycle. Materials and Methods: This is a hospital based retrospective study conducted by analyzing medical records of patients, who had undergone therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) for various neurological disorders at IMS & SUM hospital. Medical records total 118 patients who underwent TPE from January 2016 to December 2021 were analyzed. The demographic data, blood group pattern and indications for TPE were enumerated from the records. Various complications of TPE and reasons for incomplete TPE cycle were documented and analyzed.Results:A total of 508 TPE procedures were performed on 118 patients. In this study 61 patients were male and 57 patients were female. O-blood group was commonest blood group among the patients. GBS is the commonest indication of TPE. 57.6 % of patients could complete all sessions TPE cycle. Blockage of vascular access is the commonest cause of incomplete TPE session. Cramps (33%) and mild transient hypotension (27.1%0 were the commonest complications observed.Conclusion:TPE is a safe and effective treatment option for various immune-mediated neurological disorders and should be considered in managing these disorders.

INTRODUCTION

Therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) is a process of separation of plasma from the cellular components of blood in an extracorporeal site and its replacement with analogous fluids. This process is used to remove large molecular-weight substances from the blood.[1] This is effective in the treatment of disease conditions produced by dysregulation of the humoral immune system.

Although TPE was first used in 1952 in multiple myeloma patients to control hyperviscosity of blood, subsequently, it was found to be useful in the treatment of various neurological disorders with abnormal humoral immune responses, such as Guillain–Barré syndrome (GBS), myasthenia gravis (MG), and chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP).[2]

During TPE, initially, there is segregation of blood’s cellular elements from plasma outside the body with the help of permeable blood filters or centrifugation devices. The separated plasma is then replaced with a replacement fluid. During the TPE cycle, usually, 30–40 mL/kg or 1.5–2 L of plasma are exchanged in most of the immune-mediated neurological disorders. Although it is difficult to decide on a perfect replacement solution for the plasma disposed of during TPE, commonly 5% human albumin or cross-matching plasma is used to reduce the complications.[3]

It has been hypothesized that TPE results in the removal of various humoral pathogenic substances such as antibodies, immune complexes, monoclonal proteins, toxins, or cytokine(s) and/or the replenishment of a specific plasma factor. This causes a reversal in the pathologic process due to specific humoral factor deficit or excessiveness.[4567]

TPE is a relatively safe procedure when performed with proper care and support in experienced centers. Common complications occurring in TPE, such as hypotension and fluid-electrolyte imbalance, are usually rapidly recognized and corrected.[89] However, more severe conditions such as pneumothorax, in situ thrombosis of catheter, or catheter site infection can sometimes complicate the procedure.[10] TPE can cause temporary immunosuppression due to the removal of antibodies and makes the patient more prone to systemic infections. Hemorrhagic complications can also occur due to anticoagulant use during the procedure.[11]

Although TPE is an accepted treatment option in managing disorders of immune dysregulation, its use by neurologists is not very popular because of its invasive nature, fear of complications, and availability of easier treatment alternatives like intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG). Except few studies from the northern and southern part of India very little data are available regarding TPE in the eastern part of India. Therefore, this study was done to find the common neurological indications of TPE in our part of the country as well as complications associated with the procedure.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The present study is an observational study done at IMS and SUM hospital by retrospectively analyzing the medical records of patients admitted during the 60-month period from January 2016 to December 2021. In this study, 118 patients, in whom TPE was performed for various neurological conditions, were included in the study. Patients in whom TPE were performed for nonneurological disorders and patients with incomplete medical records were excluded from analysis. Patient’s medical records were checked for demographic details, indications, and complications arising during or after plasma exchange sessions. Common causes of incomplete plasma exchange cycles were determined from the medical records. All study-related information was anonymized, kept confidential, and used only for addressing the study questions.

In this center, TPE was planned only in cases where a definitive diagnosis of a neuroimmunological disorder was reached, and an indication for TPE was confirmed. TPE is usually preferred over other immune-modulating agents in hemodynamically stable patients who are very rapidly progressing or with impending respiratory failure. For others, the patient has to choose from the TPE or IVIG for themselves after getting a detailed explanation regarding the various pros and cons of these two treatment modalities. For a single cycle of TPE, usually, five sessions of plasma exchanges were planned on an alternate-day schedule. The objective was to remove about 1.5–2 times of total plasma. The patients were put on multiparameter intensive care unit (ICU) monitor and clinically observed for any change in appearance or development of new symptoms with documentation of vital parameters in a prefixed format. Patients who developed hypotension during the procedure were managed by halting the procedure temporarily and restarting with a lower pump speed. If required, they were treated with normal saline or fresh frozen plasma (FFP) infusion according to the attending clinician’s decision. We usually transfuse three units of FFP as a replacement fluid to the patient following a plasma exchange session.[12] All TPE procedures were performed by trained staff nurses under the supervision of residents and consultants of the neuroscience ICU and transfusion medicine. Patients were kept in ICU until completion of a 24-h observation period after the procedure. TPE is discontinued in patients who develop life-threatening complications such as persistent hemodynamic instability, sepsis, or on withdrawal consent. Patients were followed up for 5–7 days after the completion of the plasma exchange cycle to assess the clinical benefits. Depending upon the response of the initial TPE cycle, the patients were planned for subsequent immune modulation.

The study data were managed in a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet. Categorical data were expressed as numbers and percentages. Continuous data were expressed as number, mean, and standard deviation depending on the distribution.

The patients who underwent TPE for various neurological conditions also had other associated health issues such as hypertension (17), diabetes mellitus (13), chronic obstructive airway disease (6), anemia (9), and hypothyroidism (4). These patients were relatively stable with their preexisting medical conditions before the initiation of TPE. For most patients with GBS, TPE was initiated within 5–7 days after symptom onset. For the patients with ADEM and NMOSD, TPE was performed when an inadequate response was observed with steroid treatment. Almost all the patients with MG who underwent PE were in crisis, with the exception of 1 MUSK +ve MG patient who had to undergo TPE because of nonresponse from other treatment modalities.

Possible catheter-related phlebitis-causing pyrexia was documented in 11 (9.3%) patients. Among these patients, seven had femoral, and four had a subclavian catheter. Six possible catheter-related phlebitis patients responded well to antibiotics, and TPE was continued after a few days’ intervals. In the other five patients, the response to antibiotic treatment remained poor. Hence, for these patients, a subsequent session of TPE was stopped, and the catheter was removed. Gradually, they responded well to antibiotic treatment.

Thrombosis of the deep vein in the lower limb was seen in 1 (0.9%) patient who had a femoral catheter insertion. The patient had a swollen and tender calf, and the condition improved with a change in the catheter insertion site and the use of an anticoagulant. Out of the 108 patients who had subclavian venous access, pneumothorax was seen in 2 (1.7%) patients. During the periprocedural period, 39 (33%) patients developed paresthesias in the perioral region and/or limbs, which were commonly mild and transient. A significant number of patients, i.e. 16 (13.6%), also had muscle cramps during the TPE cycle.

Mild transient hypotension characterized by an asymptomatic fall of systolic BP by 5–10 mmHg from baseline was observed in 32 (27.1%) patients during at least one of their TPE sessions. All of these patients had a systolic blood pressure (SBP) of 100 mmHg or more. They were managed by slowing the pump speed and/or by administering 0.9% saline intravenously without requiring stopping the PE session.

Severe hypotension with a SBP <80 mmHg was documented in 7 (5.9%) patients during the procedure. This was managed by temporary cessation of the procedure and intravenous administration of FFP or normal saline. All of these patients had severe GBS, and 4 of them had transient hemodynamic instability even before the initiation of the TPE session. This was most probably related to underlying autonomic dysfunction associated with GBS.

Mild allergic reaction with urticarial rash was seen in 4 (3.4%) patients. This was managed with appropriate antihistaminic and/or corticosteroid treatment. All of them responded well to the treatment, and TPE was continued as planned.

The majority of the older patients with age > 60 years were without any significant complications. Among 25 patients aged 60 years or more, six developed mild hypotension, and three had moderate to significant hypotension, causing cessation of TPE sessions. All of them were on combination antihypertensive agents for a long duration before initiation of TPE. All three patients, who developed significant hypotension, were being treated for GBS.

Five patients developed mild anemia with a fall of hemoglobin (Hb) 0.5–1 g/dL compared to the baseline Hb level before initiating the TPE. Six patients also developed mild hypocalcemia following the TPE session. There was no documentation of any deterioration of preexisting comorbid medical conditions such as COPD, diabetes, or hypertension in any of the patients.

Mortality was nil due to the procedure itself. However, three patients died due to underlying disease conditions or complications. Out of them, two patients had GBS with severe respiratory and autonomic dysfunction. Another one had Musk +ve MG with prior pulse steroid therapy and developed aspiration pneumonia and septicemia. All of them were on ventilator support and died 1 week after the last procedure.

All five sessions of the planned TPE cycle were completed in only 68 (57.6%) patients. Out of them, 40 patients had undergone TPE within 1 week of onset of symptoms and 28 patients beyond 1 week of onset of symptoms. Among the patient who had TPE within 1 week, one patient of GBS died due to the progression of underlying disease and multiple complications. Other 34 patients stabilized within 4–7 days of post-TPE session. For the rest five patients (all of them of GBS) condition continues to deteriorate and required subsequent IVIG therapy. Among the rest 28 patients who had undergone TPE after 1 week of onset of symptoms, 16 patients improved within 1 week of post-TPE cycle. Two patients (one of GBS and one of MUSK +ve myasthenia) gravis died due to underlying disease conditions or complications. Ten patients continue to deteriorate and required other immune-modulating therapies or second cycle of TPE. Early significant improvement was seen in 10 (8.5%) patients even after four cycles of TPE sessions. All of them had a diagnosis of GBS (AIDP variant) and undergone the procedure relatively early within 1 week of disease onset.

DISCUSSION

In this study, 118 patients who underwent 538 cycles of TPE during the 4 years for different neurological conditions were documented. Among the various conditions needing TPE, GBS was the most common, with 98 patients, which accounted for 83% of total patients. For the management of GBS, both TPE and IVIG are recommended therapeutic options.[131415] Although IVIG is preferred by the majority of physicians because of convenience in its use and lower hemodynamic complications, the therapeutics and technology assessment subcommittee of the American Academy of Neurology on plasmapheresis described TPE to be relatively safe in experienced hands based on their observation in 2011.[16]

In this study, middle-aged persons (mean age: 41.2; range: 11–68) constitute the majority of our patients. The youngest patient in this series was 11 years old. Only nine patients were below the age of 20 years, and among them, three were in the age group 11–15 years. The procedures in children are usually performed using the experience and data deduced from adult patients, which may not always be correct. In children, TPE procedures are associated with some unique problems such as poor patient cooperation, modification of protocols, and technical factors for safe and effective treatment.[1217]

As per previous studies, the elderly patients are considered to be at a higher risk of having complications from hemodynamic disturbances due to the presence of multiple age-related cardiovascular risk factors.[11] One study involving plasma exchange in older patients by Basic-Jukic et al., 2010, found that patients with age 65 years or more have a higher complication rate of 11.5% in comparison to 3.9% of the younger group.[18] In this study, 25 patients were older than 60 years of age. Among them, 13 patients completed PE without any significant complication, and nine patients developed minimal complications without affecting the continuity of PE, but in three patients, PE had to be discontinued due to persistent hypotension.

Complications are not uncommon in TPE but are usually mild. Although deaths due to TPE have been documented, these are usually related to preexisting comorbid conditions rather than the procedure itself. In previous studies, common complications observed during TPE were paraesthesia, nausea–vomiting, hypotension, allergic reactions, and arrhythmias.[18] These may be seen in up to 3%–20% of the procedures and are often mild in nature. These complications usually resolve spontaneously or with minimal therapeutic support without necessitating cessation of the TPE cycles.[18]

Complications related to vascular access causing termination of the procedure are seen in 4%–5% of TPE sessions in previous studies.[18] In the present study, there was an 8.1% incidence of such catheter-related complications (catheter infection: 4.2% and catheter displacement: 3.9%).

In this study group, the common complications were paresthesias in 33% of patients and cramps in 13.6% of patients. This is similar to a study from south India where cramps and paraesthesia incidence was 36.1%.[12] This is probably related to electrolyte imbalances resulting from large fluid shifts and citrate used in the anticoagulant of the FFP, which chelates the calcium.

Mild anemia with a fall of Hb 0.5–1 g/dL was seen in five patients compared to the baseline Hb level before initiating TPE. Six patients also developed mild hypocalcemia following the TPE session.

Hypotension was documented in a significantly higher number of patients in our series (33%) in comparison to 2.6%–8.1% incidence in previous studies.[19] The higher incidence of hypotension could also be explained because of some unique association of conditions like the use of FFP as a replacement fluid and a higher proportion of patients suffering from GBS (83%) in our series. Although 5% albumin is considered to be a better replacement fluid,[3] we had to use FFP because of economic constraints. This, too, could be a plausible explanation for the higher documentation of hypotension. A higher proportion of patients with GBS could also have contributed to the high incidence of hypotension in our cohort, as these patients were more likely to develop dysautonomia. Previous studies were mostly based on observations from various apheresis registries, which include different diseases of nonuniform etiology and severity. In contrast, our study population was relatively uniform, with a predominance of patients with GBS. These conditions could explain the higher incidence of hypotension in our series, which is also similar to previous studies from India with similar baseline characteristics.[1220]

The overall mortality rate from PE is 1 to 3/10,000 procedures from previous studies.[21] Various indications for TPE include different neurological disorders as well as hematological, renal, and rheumatologic disorders. Although various neurological disorders predominate in most countries, hematological conditions such as cryoglobulinemia, hyperviscosity syndrome, hemolytic uremic syndrome, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, and idiopathic thrombocytopenia constitute few major nonneurological indications for TPE. The mortality and complication rates from the procedure for various clinical indications are usually similar. There is no death due to the TPE procedure in the present series, but three patients died due to different underlying diseases and their complications.

In this series, 57.6% could complete all five sessions and 84.7% of patients could complete a total of four cycles of PE, although the aim of TPE protocol in the hospital was to have five exchanges. Apart from common complications causing early cessation of TPE cycles such as filter-blockage (9.3%), hemodynamic instability (4.2%), catheter-related infection (4.2%), catheter displacement (3.9%), and allergic reactions (0.84%), premature withdrawal of consent (11.9%) for TPE was also a major reason for fewer than planned cycles of PE among a group of patients. Most of these patients had either economic constraints or aggravated fear of minor early complications recurrences. Few patients (8.5%) also discontinued PE after significant improvement relatively early.

The outcome of patients undergoing TPE is not uniform as different patients had undergone TPE for different/varying indications of varying severity. However, in general, earlier initiation of TPE sessions was associated with relatively better outcomes.

CONCLUSION

TPE is a commonly used treatment for managing various immune-mediated neurological disorders. The present series, one of the initial studies from eastern India, found acquired immune-mediated peripheral neuropathies, MG, and NMOSD as major indications for TPE in our part of the country. In the present series, majority of patients had only minimal complications. Although TPE is not popular among the physicians due to the invasive nature of the procedure, it should be considered more frequently as it is relatively cost-effective and safe in well-equipped experienced centers. The present study was a small study from our region, and there is a need for more studies in the future to delineate the efficacy and possible complications of TPE in specific group of population.

Limitations

This is a retrospective observational study with a small sample size. Determination of efficacy of the procedure and severity of complications were difficult to ascertain due to variation in documentation of previous medical records.

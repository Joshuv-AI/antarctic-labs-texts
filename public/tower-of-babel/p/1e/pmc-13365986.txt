IFPHPOS0025 Tuberculosis Screening Gaps in Refugee and Asylum-Seeking Children: A Single-Centre Retrospective Review
Authors: Lulu Betteridge, George Porter
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim of StudyTo evaluate TB screening adherence among refugee and asylum-seeking children attending a UK paediatric service and describe associated clinical findings.MethodsA retrospective observational study was conducted using anonymised electronic health record data from refugee and asylum-seeking children attending a tertiary hospital in Newcastle, UK. Eligibility was determined by country of origin, migration history, or refugee camp residence. Data collected included TB screening referrals, reasons for non-screening, and laboratory investigations.ResultsOf 97 eligible children, only 63.9% underwent TB testing. Among those screened, 72% were from high TB-incidence countries, and 28% had resided in refugee camps. Among unscreened children, 33% were from high-incidence countries, 15% from refugee camps, and 42% from low-incidence countries. Documented reasons for non-referral included low-incidence country of origin (36%), prior screening (3%), transfer from another UK region (8%), inability to contact patients (6%), and technical issues (3%); 36% had no documented explanation. In 28% of eligible children, TB referral was recorded, but no follow-up occurred. Baseline investigations identified five cases of TB infection and one of hepatitis B. Common abnormalities included vitamin D deficiency (32%) and low haemoglobin (50%).ConclusionDespite high eligibility rates, TB screening referral and follow-up were inconsistently performed, with many cases lacking documented reasons for non-screening. Baseline investigations revealed a high prevalence of treatable conditions, including vitamin D deficiency and anaemia. Strengthening systematic screening pathways and improving follow-up mechanisms may support earlier identification of TB and other health conditions, helping to ensure equitable healthcare access for migrant children.

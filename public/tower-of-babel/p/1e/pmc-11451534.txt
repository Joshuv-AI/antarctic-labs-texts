Influence of preoperative HbA1c levels on the occurrence of postoperative complications in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting
Authors: Bing Liu, Zi Li, Xueqing Chen, Sheng Wang, Kun Cheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo explore the impact of preoperative HbA1c levels on postoperative complications in coronary heart disease patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG).MethodsFrom September 2020 to November 2022, 98 patients with coronary heart disease who were preparing to receive CABG treatment in a cardiac surgery department of a certain hospital were included in the study using the retrospective analysis. According to the preoperative serum hemoglobin A1C (HbA1c) test results, patients were divided into a low-level group (HbA1c < 5.7%, 20 cases), a medium level group (HbA1c: 5.7% ~ 7.0%, 59 cases), and a high level group (> 7%, 19 cases). The surgical outcomes and postoperative complications among the three groups were compared.ResultsThere was no statistically significant difference in the number of bypass grafts, mechanical ventilation time, and hospitalization time among the three groups of patients (P > 0.05). The high-level group stayed in the ICU longer than the other two groups, while the middle level group had a longer stay than the low-level group (P < 0.05). Within one year of postoperative follow-up, the occurrence of postoperative complications was 20.00%, 32.20%, and 47.37%, respectively, with no statistically significant difference (P > 0.05). Among them, the incidence of acute kidney injury in the high-level group was higher than that in the other groups (P < 0.05), but the correlation difference between the middle and low level groups is P > 0.05. The incidence of infection in the middle level group was higher than that in the low level group (P < 0.05), but the incidence of infection in the high and low level groups was P > 0.05 compared to the medium level group.ConclusionFor patients with coronary heart disease undergoing CABG, the higher the preoperative HbA1c level, the longer their postoperative stay in the ICU, and the higher the risk of acute renal function damage.

Background

Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is currently the main method for treating coronary artery disease (CAD), especially those with multi-vessel CAD and severe CAD. It restores the distal blood supply of blood vessels by intercepting arterial or venous vessels in other parts of the patient's body instead of the diseased vessels to alleviate the clinical symptoms such as angina pectoris and arrhythmia caused by myocardial ischemia [1, 2]. There are studies conducting follow-up surveys on patients receiving this surgical treatment. The results showed that the patency rate of the great saphenous vein (GSV) was as high as 80% 5–7 years after surgery, and 50% of the patients still had patency at 15 years after surgery [3]. At present, some intervention experts in clinical practice still tend to choose percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) in the treatment of CAD. However, several clinical studies have pointed to PCI as a risk factor for postoperative noncardiac death in patients with CAD, with a lower safety profile compared to CABG therapy [4–7]. To further confirm the safety and effectiveness of CABG in the treatment of CAD, this study analyzes the incidence of postoperative complications (PCs) in CABG patients. From the perspective of the pathogenesis of CAD, abnormal blood glucose levels are a contributing factor to the development of CAD and poor prognosis of CAD. At present, the theory that diabetes is an independent risk factor for CAD that has been recognized by many clinical researchers. According to relevant data, the incidence rate of CAD in adult diabetes patients is as high as 55%, and the risk of CAD in diabetes patients is 2–4 times higher than that in non-diabetes patients [8]. Hemoglobin A1C (HbA1c) is the most common type of glycosylated hemoglobin, and its level is positively correlated with blood glucose concentration in the human body. It can be retained in the blood for 120 days and metabolized out of the human body with red blood cell apoptosis. Therefore, it can be used to evaluate the blood glucose metabolism of the human body in the past 8–12 weeks. Some studies have pointed out that HbA1c not only can be used as an important indicator to assess the condition of diabetes, but also can be used to predict the severity of CAD patients, which is closely connected with the occurrence and development of CAD [9, 10]. It is inferred that HbA1c levels can have an impact on the safety of CABG treatment. There is currently no complete clinical confirmation that serum HbA1c levels are related to the efficacy and safety of CABG treatment in CAD patients. This study hypothesizes that elevated serum HbA1c levels before CABG surgery in patients with CAD will increase their risk of PCs. To confirm the above viewpoint, this study divides the preoperative HbA1c levels of CAD patients into three groups, and compares the surgical indicators and PCs of each group of patients. The following report is presented:

Materials and methods

Research subjects: A retrospective analysis was conducted on 98 patients with CAD who were preparing to receive CABG treatment in the cardiac surgery department of a certain hospital from September 2020 to November 2022, using the retrospective analysis. Among them, there were 20 patients with diabetes mellitus, the duration of diabetes mellitus in these 20 patients ranged from 1 to 6 years, with a mean of (3.68 ± 1.71) years, and all of them received conventional insulin therapy before surgery. According to the examination results, patients were separated into a low level group (HbA1c < 5.7%, LG = 20 cases), a medium level group (HbA1c: 5.7% ~ 7.0%, MG = 59 cases), and a high level group (HbA1c > 7%, HG = 19 cases). Grouping basis: (1) The American Diabetes Association (ADA) put forward in 2013 [11]: HbA1c ≤ 7% is a good threshold for short-term blood glucose control. For surgical patients, it is recommended to delay the surgery appropriately when the HbA1c level is greater than 7 to reduce the surgical risk; (2) In 2010, ADA pointed out that HbA1c < 5.7% was in the normal range, and the range from 5.7 to 6.4% was pre-diabetes, and HbA1c > 6.4% was diabetes [12]. Therefore, in this study, the HbA1c levels of 5.7% and 7.0% were used as cut-off values for grouping. This study was approved by the ethics committee.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Meet the diagnostic criteria for European Society of Cardiology (ESC) [13]; (2) Surgical indications in accordance with CABG; (3) Receive serum HbA1c level testing before surgery; (4) Normal mental status, personally acknowledged and signed the informed consent form for this study. Exclusion criteria (1) Simultaneously merging other surgical procedures; (2) Patients who have undergone surgical treatment three months prior to surgery; (3) Concomitant congenital heart disease, myocarditis, and other heart related diseases; (4) Combined anemia, infection, and other hematological related diseases; (5) Concomitant cerebral hemorrhage and other cerebrovascular diseases or peripheral vascular diseases.

Data collection: General information were collected such as preoperative gender and age of patients, and information on their preoperative serum HbA1c levels and postoperative related indicators were collected. Follow up the patient for one year. The follow-up methods include outpatient follow-up, telephone follow-up, email, etc. Complications mainly include Major Adverse Cardiac Events (MACE) or infections such as arrhythmia, pericardial tamponade, low cardiac output syndrome, renal dysfunction, respiratory complications, etc.

Statistical analysis: The data was statistically analyzed taking SPSS 22.0. Counting data is represented by n and %, with inter group rows χ2 inspection. Discussion

Abnormal elevation of blood sugar levels is the main manifestation of endocrine dysfunction. Under the influence of high blood sugar levels, vascular endothelial cells not only suffer structural and functional damage, but also affect cell repair function, leading to platelet adhesion, aggregation, and other situations. At the same time, the increase in blood glucose metabolites can also cause damage and apoptosis of vascular endothelial cells, leading to cardiovascular disease. For patients with type 2 diabetes, every 1% increase in serum HbA1c level will increase the risk of CAD by 18%. Meanwhile, 65% of type 2 diabetic patients with CAD die from cardiovascular lesions. Surgery is an invasive operation, which induces a stress response in the patient's body, and at the same time, hormones with glucose-enhancing effects such as cortisol are secreted in large quantities in his body, leading to an increase in intraoperative blood glucose levels. For patients with HbA1c levels above 6.5%, their insulin secretion or action function has been impaired, and intraoperative blood glucose levels have increased more significantly. The rapidly increasing blood sugar and inflammatory mediators released by the body can stimulate vascular wall cells, leading to abnormal vasoconstriction function and triggering adverse cardiovascular events [14]. Studies have shown [15, 16] that high blood sugar can affect the effectiveness of CABG treatment in CAD patients. Compared to patients with HbA1c ≤ 7%, the risk of postoperative death and MACE increased by 17% when HbA1c levels increased to 8.1%, and by 50% when HbA1c levels increased to 9%. It is evident that the postoperative prognostic outcome of patients with CAD is influenced by their preoperative HbA1c levels. The proportion of clinical studies related to surgery in patients with CAD combined with diabetes mellitus is currently increasing. However, there is still no research that can fully confirm the correlation between preoperative HbA1c in CAD patients and the incidence of PCs after CABG. Therefore, the researchers in this study hypothesized that elevated serum HbA1c levels before CABG surgery in CAD patients would increase their risk of PCs, and confirmed the hypothesis through retrospective analysis.

This study was grouped based on preoperative HbA1c levels. The general information, preoperative condition, and medication status of patients are basically the same, ensuring the effectiveness and rationality of subsequent research. In line with the patients in LG, MG and HG in this study, there were distinctions in the number of bypass grafts, mechanical ventilation time, and hospital stay (P > 0.05), indicating that high blood sugar levels did not affect the progress of surgery and related treatment procedures. The study also pointed out that HG stayed in the ICU longer than LG and MG, while MG stayed longer than LG (P < 0.05). This suggests that with the increase of preoperative HbA1c levels, the postoperative stay in the ICU of CAD patients was prolonged. The reason for the analysis is that according to the above theory, high blood sugar can also participate in intraoperative stress response. Although stress response will not affect the determination of the number of bypass grafts and the duration of mechanical ventilation, it will stimulate the secretion of inflammatory factors, reduce the body's self-regulation and repair ability, and prolong the physiological time to reach the ICU standard. However, theoretically, it can be inferred that patients with high HbA1c have a slower recovery rate, and their hospitalization time should be longer. However, the hospitalization time of the three groups of patients is basically the same. The reason for the analysis is that the heart is an important organ. Although the thoracic drainage tube can usually be removed 4–5 days after surgery, it can be discharged after about 7 days. However, most clinical physicians still recommend hospitalization until around 14 days after the patient's physiological state is stable. After confirming that their heart rate, blood pressure, and other long-term stable states are maintained, discharge can greatly ensure the safety of prognosis.

Meanwhile, this study focuses on the incidence of complications in patients within one year after surgery. It was found that the proportion of patients with complications in the three groups was basically the same, but the comparison of the incidence of each complication proved that the incidence of AKI in HG patients was higher than the other two groups (P < 0.05). In this study, 15 patients experienced AKI after surgery, with an incidence rate of 15.31%. The incidence of AKI gradually increased with the increase of preoperative serum HbAc1, with rates of 5.00%, 10.17%, and 42.11%, respectively. Foreign researchers such as Ortega et al. [17] and Kaufeld T et al. [18] pointed out that the incidence of AKI in patients after CABG surgery is 12% and 15.7%, respectively, which is similar to the above research results. However, there is currently no comparative study on the incidence of postoperative AKI under different preoperative serum HbAc1 levels, and a more detailed comparison cannot be conducted. Meanwhile, some scholars have found in their studies that the incidence of AKI after CABG surgery is 26.6% [19], which is greater than the above research results. Although there are differences in clinical research results, they all indicate that AKI is more common after CABG surgery. In addition, the incidence of HG in this study was more than that of MG and LG (P < 0.05), indicating that preoperative HbAc1 > 7% is a high-risk factor for postoperative acute renal failure. HbAc1 > 7% indicates that the patient's recent blood glucose metabolism is very poor. This hyperglycemic state can induce mitochondrial dysfunction in vascular wall cells, resulting in structural changes in the vascular wall and narrowing of the vascular lumen. While activating a large number of inflammatory factors, it can also alter renal hemodynamics. In addition, the production and aggregation of a large amount of glycation products can also cause glomerular mesangial proliferation, leading to glomerular proliferative lesions. Moreover, the intervention of high blood sugar levels on protein, lipid, and other metabolic co functions comprehensively affects the patient's renal function, resulting in renal function damage, which is more likely to induce acute renal injury after CABG surgery.

Meanwhile, the postoperative infection incidence in MG was higher than that in LG, but there was no difference compared to HG. This suggests that preoperative HbA1c levels may rise the risk of postoperative infection in CAD patients to some extent, but due to the limited number of HG and LG patients, this viewpoint cannot be fully confirmed. In addition, there was indistinctive discrepancy in the occurrence of other PCs among the three groups in this research, indicating that preoperative HbA1c levels may not be a risk factor for other PCs, but further research is still needed to verify and confirm.

In summary, preoperative HbA1c level detection can to some extent infer the duration of ICU stay in CAD patients after CABG surgery. Patients with high preoperative HbA1c levels have a relatively long postoperative stay in the ICU, and there is a higher risk of acute renal function damage after surgery. It is recommended to provide appropriate preventive intervention measures to ensure the prognosis effect.

A Clinical Study on Efficacy of Injection Cyclophosphamide Monthly Pulse in Patients with Autoimmune Bullous Disorders
Authors: Nirmaladevi Palanivel, Soundharyaa Moorthi, A. Josphin Lisha, A. N. M Maalik Babu, P Kalyanakumar
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Pemphigus is a potentially fatal autoimmune bullous disorder (AIBD), for which commonly used treatment is corticosteroids as it has broad spectrum of anti-inflammatory effects.[1] Prior to the advent of corticosteroids, there was high mortality (90%) in such patients due to septicaemia or electrolyte imbalance.[2] Dexamethasone-cyclophosphamide pulse (DCP) therapy, introduced in 1982 by Pasricha and Gupta[3] had demonstrated quicker remission in pemphigus. But people treated with DCP faced many steroid induced systemic side effects like diabetes mellitus, hypertension, cushingoid habitus, cataract, etc.[4] Also daily oral regimen of cyclophosphamide has increased the risk of infections and haemorrhagic cystitis on short term use and myelosuppression, malignancy, as well as irreversible gonadal failure on long term use.[56]

Our study is the prospective uncontrolled interventional study over a period of 5 years (4 years recruitment period+ 1 year follow up) in a tertiary care hospital, in which injection cyclophosphamide monthly pulse (500 mg) was given for 12 months without daily oral cyclophosphamide in AIBD patients in resource poor settings. A total of 25 patients were in the study with a female preponderance (F = 17; M = 8). Patients age ranged from 32 to 65 years with a mean ± SD of 48.36 ± 9.69 years. Disease duration before initiation of cyclophosphamide pulse ranged from 2 months to 4 years. Among the study population, 60% had disease for less than 1 year, 28% had disease for 1 to 2 years, and 12% had disease of more than 2 years [Table 1].

Of the 25 cases in this study, 84% were constituted by pemphigus and 16% by bullous pemphigoid. Excellent response with complete remission was seen in 88% of patients with a mean duration of 4.2 months. The earliest period to achieve remission is 1 month. Maximum patients (n = 13; 52%) attained quick response within 2 months. All the patients with disease duration of less than 1 year attained remission within 4 months (n = 14; 56%), when compared to the patients with longer duration, which was found to be statistically significant (P value - 0.022). Of the remaining 12% (3 cases), one had persistent oral erosions even after completion of pulse, one expired before 2nd pulse due to the myocardial infarction and one was not willing to continue the pulse after 1st dose [Table 2].

Among 14 cases, who had completed the pulse, 13 achieved complete remission and one patient had persistent oral lesions. Of these, sustenance of remission till the end of follow-up was seen in 64% patients, sustenance less than 1 year in 21% and less than 6 months in 7%. Among patients who did not complete the pulse (11 cases; 2–10 pulses), 27% patients showed sustenance less than one year, 55% showed sustenance of less than 6 months, one patient died after first pulse and one lost to follow-up after the first pulse. Hence, sustenance is significantly more in patients, who completed the pulse (P value-0.042) [Table 3].

Sustenance of remission in correlation with disease duration

Among the total responders, 64% were completely tapered off from steroids within the treatment period itself and remaining were on minimal dose of steroids (5–10 mg) during the follow-up period. Prior to cyclophosphamide, those patients, who had required higher doses of steroids were also completely tapered off from steroids or maintained with minimal dose of corticosteroids. Thus, Cyclophosphamide pulse helps in control of pemphigus at a lower dose of oral prednisolone with less cumulative dose.

Sharma and Khandpur[1] conducted a randomized controlled study in New Delhi using cyclophosphamide pulse (monthly for 12 months) and prednisolone as control group in 60 patients (30 in each group), over a period of one year with additional one year of follow-up. Complete remission was seen in 88% in cyclophosphamide group and 73% in prednisolone group with the mean period of 2 months. Sustenance was seen in 60% and the rest relapsed during follow-up. Cumulative dose of steroids was less with cyclophosphamide group. These findings are consistent with the present study.

Gokhale et al.[2] conducted an uncontrolled study in Pune using intravenous cyclophosphamide pulse therapy monthly for 12 months followed by every 2 months for further six pulses in 20 patients of recalcitrant pemphigus. Out of 18 patients, who completed the study, 66% showed complete remission within a mean period of 3 months. Sustenance was seen in 77%. Steroids were tapered to 5 mg daily or alternate days in 66%, 5–10 mg daily in 22% and completely stopped in 5% of patients. Even though remission rate is like our study, sustenance seems to be little prolonged (64% vs. 77%), which could be attributed to longer pulse period.

Comorbidities encountered in our patients were diabetes mellitus (six patients), hypertension (two patients) and hypothyroidism (one patient). There were pre-existing except three new cases of diabetes that developed due to steroids. But these did not have any impact on disease prognosis.

Daily Oral cyclophosphamide is more prone for malignancy and myelosuppression than monthly injection of cyclophosphamide.[5] But we did not face any such complication, including sepsis as we used only monthly pulse. Haemorrhagic cystitis is reported frequently with injection cyclophosphamide but such complication also did not occur in our study as we had provided with adequate hydration pre- and post- infusion.

Relapse and lesser sustenance were more common in patients with longer disease duration, which signifies the importance of extended cyclophosphamide pulse every 2 months for one more year as mentioned in Gokhale et al.[2] study that could be tried in such patients.

Cyclophosphamide monthly pulse injection alone, without daily oral dose, is proved to achieve complete remission and more sustenance with minimal side effects in autoimmune bullous disorders. Cases with lesser disease duration showed earlier and higher complete remission rates and more sustenance and vice-versa for the longer duration disease. Cyclophosphamide monthly pulse injection highlights the avoidance of oral cyclophosphamide induced side effects and cumulative therapeutic steroid dose reduction with less relapse rates. In the current paradigm of treatment by biologicals in autoimmune bullous disorders, cyclophosphamide pulse therapy still offers potential cure in resource poor settings with better outcomes and less side-effects.

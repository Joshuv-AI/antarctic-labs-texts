SPCT-06 CHALLENGES AND OUTCOMES OF PATIENTS WITH SPINE TUMORS IN A TERTIARY CENTRE IN EAST AFRICA: A SINGLE-CENTRE RETROSPECTIVE STUDY.
Authors: John Michael Mtei, Mariam Mohamed Mzingi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and Aimsboth primary and metastatic spine tumors are often diagnosed late in low- and middle-income countries, leading to severe neurologic deficits and poor outcomes. This is mainly attributed to inadequate imaging modalities, neurosurgeons, implants for spinal instrumentation, intraoperative neuromonitoring and adjuvant therapy options. There is limited data on the outcomes of spine tumor surgeries in low- and middle-income countries. This study aims to provide insight on epidemics, presentation, management and outcomes of spine tumor patients treated in a tertiary referral centre in east Africa.Methodsa retrospective cohort study of patients with spine tumors who underwent surgical management in Muhimbili Orthopaedic Institute for a period of 3 years, from 2022 to 2025.Inclusion Criteriapatients with spine tumors diagnosed via imaging or histology, who underwent decompression with or without stabilization.Exclusion Criteriatraumatic fractures without tumor diagnosis or patients with inflammatory or infectious process diagnosed intra-operatively.Resultslower median age of presentation compared to high income countries. About 60% of the histology was metastatic. A delay from admission to surgery was mainly attributed to financial constraints. Less than 40% of the patients received adjuvant therapy, most were lost to follow-up. The integration of telephone follow-up increased the clinic follow-up rates.Conclusiondespite late presentation, outcomes can be improved through capacity building, collaborations to ensure sustainable implant supply and integration of low-cost follow-up solutions such as telephone follow-ups.

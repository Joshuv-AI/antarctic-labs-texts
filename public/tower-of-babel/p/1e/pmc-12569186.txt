Calculating thermal neutron cross-section and resonance integrals of the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo, 170Er(n,γ)171Er, 186W(n,γ)187W, and 174Yb(n,γ)175Yb reactions using GEANT4 and TALYS codes
Authors: Farzad Isazadeh, Akbar Abdi Saray
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA simulation method using GEANT4-10.7 and TALYS-1.96 nuclear packages was applied to calculate the thermal neutron capture cross-section (σ) and resonance integral (I) for the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo, 170Er(n,γ)171Er, 186W(n,γ)187W, and 174Yb(n,γ)175Yb reactions. The 197Au(n,γ)198Au reaction (σ = 98.65 barns and I = 1550 barns) was selected as the single comparator reaction for assisting the resonance integral calculations. The average values of the thermal neutron cross-section and resonance integral for the four reactions based on the present simulated results and previous experimental and evaluated data were computed to be 62.27 ± 2.45, 7.7 ± 0.8, 37.8 ± 1.8, and 86.989 ± 3.56 barns for the σ parameter, and 675 ± 36, 39.24 ± 3.65, 465.3 ± 33.4, 46.188 ± 3.04 barns for the I parameter, respectively. The simulated results, experimental and evaluated data, and average values were in good agreement with each other.Keywords: GEANT4, TALYS, Thermal neutron cross-section, Resonance integral, (n, Γ) reactions, 197Au(n, γ)198Au reactionSubject terms: Physics, Nuclear physics

Introduction

Lately, the study of the production of beneficial isotopes in nuclear applications via investigating thermal neutron capture cross-section and resonance integrals has attracted the attention of researchers1,2. Commonly, two methods of oscillation and activation can be used to calculate thermal neutron cross-sections and resonance integrals from the cadmium cutoff energy (0.5 eV to 0.1 MeV)3. For instance, the cross-section and resonance integral for the 51V(n,γ)52V reaction was determined by Agbemava et al.4. The thermal neutron cross-section and resonance integral for the 71Ga(n,γ)72Ga and 75As(n,γ)76As reactions were investigated by Karadag et al.5. Moreover, Karadag and Yücel studied the 164Dy(n, g) 165Dy reaction by computing the cross-section and resonance integral of the reaction6. In addition, the production of 99Mo, 134mCs, 139Ba, 140La, 149Nd, 152m1,gEu, 154Eu, 153Sm, and 159Gd isotopes has been studied via the 98Mo(n,γ)99Mo, 133Cs(n,γ)134mCs, 138Ba(n,γ)139Ba, 139La(n,γ)140La, 148Nd(n,γ)149Nd, 151Eu(n,γ)152m1,gEu, 153Eu(n,γ)154Eu, 152Sm(n,γ)153Sm, and 158Gd(n,γ)159Gd reactions, respectively7–14. For neutron transport computation, the study of reactor safety, evaluation of high burn-up core characteristics, assessment of the decay heat power, investigation of nuclear transmutation, and prediction of thermal neutron capture cross sections and resonance integrals for the (n,γ) reactions are essential9. Some of the produced isotopes have notable applications in various areas of science. The 166gHo isotope with a half-life of 26.8 h emits betas with the energies of 1774.32 (48.8%) and 1854.9 (49.9%) keV and gamma-rays with the energy of 80.57 (6.7%) and 1379.40 (0.9%) keV, which it makes the radioisotope a suitable radioisotope for various therapeutic applications and nuclear imaging15,16. Moreover, the 171Er (T1/2 = 7.516 h, Eγ1 = 111.621 keV-20.5%, Eγ2 = 116.656 keV-2.30%, Eγ3 = 124.015 keV-9.1%, Eγ4 = 210.16 keV-0.641%, Eγ5 = 295.901 keV-28.9%, and Eγ6 = 308.31 keV-64.4%, Eβ = 6.16 MeV) radioisotope can be applied as a label for the scintigraphy monitoring and be useful for the pharmaceutical drug delivery systems17,18. The 187W isotope with a half-life of 23.72 h (Eγ1 = 134.25 keV-8.85%, Eγ2 = 479.55 keV-21.8%, Eγ3 = 551.52 keV-5.08%, Eγ4 = 685.74 keV-27.3%, and Eγ5 = 772.89 keV-4.12%, Eβ = 1.31094 MeV) is a good radioisotope for designing the reactors, evaluation of radiation damage on materials and metallurgical issues19,20. Recently, for peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT) also known as targeted radionuclide therapy (TRT) for the treatment of neuroendocrine tumors, the suitable radionuclide of 175Yb (T1/2 = 4.185 days, Eγ1 = 113.8 keV-1.88%, Eγ2 = 282.52 keV-3.01%, and Eγ3 = 396.33–6.4%, Eβ1 = 73.67 keV-10.2%, Eβ1 = 356.2 keV-3.3%, and Eβ1 = 470 keV-86.5%) have been very interesting for the researchers21. In addition, due to saving time and costs, protecting from radiations, and predicting nuclear reactions before practical ways, using nuclear codes and simulation methods can be a suitable way to forecast the nuclear reactions22–24. Therefore, in this study, a simulation method using GEANT4-10.7 and TALYS-1.96 codes was applied to calculate the thermal neutron capture cross-section and resonance integral for the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo, 170Er(n,γ)171Er, 186W(n,γ)187W, and 174Yb(n,γ)175Yb reactions. First, the thermal neutron capture cross-section for the reactions was computed using GEANT4-10.7 and TALYS-1.96 nuclear codes. Then, the resonance integral for the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo, 170Er(n,γ)171Er, 186W(n,γ)187W, and 174Yb(n,γ)175Yb reactions was investigated using the values of the thermal neutron capture cross-section obtained by GEANT4 and TALYS codes and the cross-section and resonance integral values for the monitor reaction of 197Au(n,γ)198Au (as the single comparator reaction). Ultimately, the simulated resonance integral and thermal neutron cross-section values for the three reactions were compared with 27, 22, 25, and 15 previous experimental and evaluated datasets of thermal neutron cross-sections and with 25, 18, 20, and 14 previous experimental and evaluated results of resonance integrals, respectively15,17,25,26.

Materials and methods

GEANT4-10.7 and TALYS-1.96 nuclear codes were applied to calculate thermal neutron cross-section for the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo, 170Er(n,γ)171Er, 186W(n,γ)187W, and 174Yb(n,γ)175Yb reactions. GEANT4-10.7 is a new version of the Monte Carlo GEANT4 package, which is a suitable nuclear toolkit for simulating nuclear reactions. The GEANT4 nuclear code can precisely simulate interactions of charged (such as proton, triton, alpha, positron, 3He, and deuteron) and non-charged (neutron and photon) particles with various type of materials (composites and mixtures in gas, liquid, and solid condition). The input and output files, physics lists, tallies, source details, and geometry can be defined easily and arbitrarily by users. These properties make the GEANT4 code an excellent choice for predicting nuclear reactions27–30. In this study, the neutron capture, thermal scattering, elastic, and inelastic physics of neutron particle interactions were selected as the “NeutronHPphysics” list to ensure precise investigations. The “NeutronHPphysics” physics list can support neutrons with energy range from thermal to fast. Also, the interactions of neutrons with different elements and materials such as neutron capture, elastic and inelastic scattering, and fission can be defined using the “NeutronHPphysics” physics list by applying the “G4NeutronInelasticProcess”, “G4ParticleHpElastic”, “G4ParticleHPInelastic”, “G4ParticleCapture”, “G4ParticleHPThermalScattering” and “G4ParticleHPFission” classes27–30. Figure 1 represents the 3D view of the GEANT4-10.7 Monte Carlo nuclear code for the targets (165Ho, 170Er, 186W, and 174Yb), neutron beam source, and interaction between the neutron beam and the targets. As can be observed in this figure, a cubic shape of 165Ho, 170Er, 186W, and 174Yb (100% abundance) with a thickness of 0.05 mm (1 × 1 × 0.05 mm3) is irradiated by a mono energy source of thermal neutrons with an energy of 0.025 eV. To make the calculation of the simulation method closer to the experimental data and correction factors, the sample thickness is taken from the previous experimental works. The distance between the target and the neutron source (a cylindrical shape with a thickness of 0.4 cm and a radius of 1 cm) is 1 cm. Moreover, to increase the accuracy of the calculations, the number of events was raised to 106 particles. The entire geometry is designed at a world cubic volume (1 × 1 × 1 m3) filled with air.

The geometry of neutron source and the 165Ho, 170Er, 186W, and 174Yb targets simulated by GEANT4-10.7 nuclear code.

Moreover, TALYS-1.96 is a version of the TALYS nuclear code that was used to predict the production of 166gHo, 171Er, 187W, and 175Yb isotopes. Nuclear reactions induced by various incident particles (protons, neutrons, photons, deuterons, tritons, 3He, and alpha) in materials with masses ranging from Li to Dy can be studied by the TALYS code. Energy spectra, angular distributions, cross-section, production yield, and energy-angle distribution parameters can be computed using the TALYS code28–30. Therefore, neutron capture cross-section for the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo, 170Er(n,γ)171Er, 186W(n,γ)187W, and 174Yb(n,γ)175Yb reactions was investigated by TALYS-1.96. The code can evaluate the nuclear cross-sections based on some nuclear models such as the level densities including Composite Gilbert Cameron Model (CGCM), Back Shifted Fermi Gas Model (BSFGM), General Super Fluid model (GSM), and the Optical Model Potential Spherical (OMPs), but the mentioned models cannot support very-low-energy neutron interactions30. Therefore, the keyword of “resonance” was used for predicting the nuclear reactions of very-low-energy neutrons such as thermal neutrons (0.025 eV). This energy range should be selected as a “grid keyword” for the resonance model. In the present work, to calculate the thermal capture cross-sections for the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo, 170Er(n,γ)171Er, 186W(n,γ)187W, and 174Yb(n,γ)175Yb reactions, the grid energy keyword is selected to be “n 0–1. grid”, which includes the thermal neutrons.

Results and discussion

The thermal neutron cross-section and resonance integral of the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo reaction are displayed in Table 2. According to this table, the experimental and evaluated thermal neutron cross-section values range from 58 to 67 barns. The percentage difference of the values obtained by GEANT4-10.7 (65.668 ± 0.051 barns) and TALYS1.96 (64.7 barns) with the referenced data is shown to be between 0% and 11.7%. The average value for the thermal neutron cross-section is calculated to be 62.27 ± 2.45 barns, showing differences of 5.2% and 3.757% with the estimations of GEANT4-10.7 and TALYS-1.96 codes, respectively. Moreover, the previous values of I ranged from 600 ± 110 to 830 ± 25 barns, which differed by 0.404% to 18.2% from the simulated results. The simulated resonance integral values of GEANT4-10.7 and TALYS-1.96 are 712.878 ± 0.55 and 702.402 barns, respectively. The σ and I value measured by the mentioned codes have a discrepancy of about 1.5%15,31. In addition, the average resonance integral (675 ± 36 barns) has great compatibility with the simulated results, differing by 5.3% and 3.9% from GEANT4-10.7 and TALYS-1.96 values, respectively. The Chi-Squared and RMSE statical parameters are calculated and presented in Table 2. For the present study and previous data, Chi-Squared and RMSE of the thermal neutron cross-section and resonance integral results are (9.3712 and 140.5769) and (4.4660 and 59.8551), respectively.

Conclusion

In this paper, GEANT4-10.7 and TALYS-1.96 nuclear codes were used to calculate the thermal neutron cross-section and resonance integral for the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo, 170Er(n,γ)171Er, 186W(n,γ)187W, and 174Yb(n,γ)175Yb reactions. The values of σ for the reactions were reported to be 65.668 ± 0.051, 9.003 ± 0.009, and 38.974 ± 0.096 barns for GEANT4-10.7, and 64.7, 8.85, and 38.1 barns for TALYS-1.96, respectively. Moreover, the resonance integrals of the three reactions were computed to be 712.878 ± 0.55, 56.075 ± 0.061, and 441.906 ± 1.081 barns by the GEANT4 code and 702.402, 55.1202, and 431.996 barns using the TALYS code, where the helping monitor reaction was the 197Au(n,γ)198Au reaction with a σ value of 98.65 barns and an I value of 1550 barns. The simulated results of the thermal neutron cross-section and resonance integral for the 165Ho(n,γ)166gHo, 170Er(n,γ)171Er, 186W(n,γ)187W, and 174Yb(n,γ)175Yb reactions were compared with 166 previous experimental and evaluated data, which were in good compatibility with each other.

Predictive value of interim ¹¹C-choline PET/CT for response to radium-223 in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer
Authors: Eman E. Ahmed, Jack R. Andrews, Mohamed E. Ahmed, Rimki Haloi, Lauren A. Rhodes, Mindie Mahon, Day A. Carter, Nancy Wei, Mattew P. Thorpe, Geoffrey B. Johnson, Eugene D. Kwon, Ayse Tuba Kendi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRadium-223 (Ra-223) has been approved for the treatment of metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) with predominant osseous metastases. PSA flare phenomenon with Ra-223 limits its use as a prognostic tool to predict treatment response. Currently, there is a lack of diagnostic tools to predict treatment response to Ra-223. Therefore, we sought to investigate the role of mid treatment C-11 choline PET/CT scan in predicting overall response to Ra-223. In a single institute retrospective study, we identified 32 patients who were treated with a full course of 4–6 cycles of Ra-223 and were evaluated with PSA, and C-11 choline PET scan before treatment, at mid-treatment point, and after complete course of Ra-223 between 2013 and 2018. Ra-223 was used as a salvage therapy for their predominant bone disease after failing chemotherapy and 2nd generation hormone therapy. Blind repeat radiographic evaluation of patients’ scans has been done by a radiologist specialized in nuclear medicine. Favorable response was defined by achieving partial response or stable disease on imaging, while unfavorable response was defined by showing progressive disease on imaging.ResultsMean age (± SD) at starting Radium223 was 67.6 (± 7.1) years, median (IQR) primary Gleason score was 9 (8–9), and median (IQR) pretreatment PSA was 13.5 (5.4–39.6) ng/ml. 78% of the patients (n = 25) completed 6 cycles of Ra-223, and 15% of the patients (n = 5) completed 5 cycles while 6% (n = 2) received only 4 cycles. At mid treatment scanning, 25% of the patient (n = 8) showed favorable response (Group A), while 75% of the patients (n = 24) showed unfavorable response (B). After complete course of treatment, 50% of patients (n = 4) in Group (A) continued to show favorable response, versus only 8.3% of patients (n = 2) in Group (B) showed favorable response (p-value = 0.023).ConclusionMid treatment evaluation with C-11 choline PET/CT scan can predict overall response to Radium 223. Patients with progressive disease at mid- treatment evaluation are very likely not going to respond to their treatment. Further studies and clinical trials are warranted.

Background

Prostate cancer [PCa] is the most prevalent cancer in men worldwide, second to skin cancer [1]. Notably, despite advancement in management of prostate cancer, deaths secondary to PCa have been increasing from the sixth cause of cancer mortality back in 2008 to fifth place in recent years [2–4]. Roughly, 313.780 new cases are expected to have prostate cancer in 2025 with projection number of deaths up to 35.770 deaths according to American Cancer Society [5]. Approximately 75% of patients with prostate cancer are present with localized disease [6]. Radical prostatectomy and radiation therapy constitute the mainstay treatment for localized disease with reported 100% of 5-Year survival rates [6, 7]. On the other hand, 10% of patients would present with de novo metastatic disease with reported 37% of 5-Year survival rate [6]. Additionally, 17% of patients with localized disease will eventually exhibit metastatic disease [8, 9].

The standard treatment of metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) is a combination of androgen deprivation therapy plus/minus androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (ADT+/-ARPI) [7]. However, most patients inevitably display metastatic castration resistant disease [7]. Metastatic castrate prostate cancer (mCRPC) is an aggressive form of PCa with poor prognosis with reported life expectancy between 18 and 36 months [10]. The median survival rate was nearly 22 months [11]. Bone metastasis (BM) occurs in 90% of mCRPC [12]. Unfortunately, prostate cancer BM is associated with very poor survival outcomes with reported 5 year-survival of 3% [13].

Over years, the treatment of advanced prostate cancer has been evolving with introduction of multiple second-generation hormonal therapy agents, chemotherapy, immunotherapy and radioligand isotope agents such as Radium-223(Ra-223) [6, 14]. Despite the expansion of adding new drugs to the landscape of PCa treatment, the proper sequence of those promising drugs is still unclear [7]. Ra-223 is a bone targeting alpha emitter radioligand therapy and behaves like a calcium derivative in the body [15, 16]. Ra-223 emits short radiation waves that break tumor genome and result in destruction of the cell repair mechanism [15, 16]. In 2013, Pivotal phase III ALSYMPCA clinical trial investigated the use of Ra-223 in patients with mCRPC with predominance of bone metastasis. They reported 16% of patients treated with Ra-223 had a positive PSA response compared to 6% in the placebo arm. Of note, patients’ PSA response was defined as 50% reduction in PSA from baseline. Additionally, they noted improvement in overall survival (OS) and quality of life in mCRPC patients treated with Ra-223 vs. placebo which led to Ra-223 approved by FDA [16]. Moreover, the authors noted the median overall survival, and progression frees survival of (14.9 versus 11.3) months and (5.8 versus 3.6) months favoring patients who received radium-223, respectively [16]. Although Ra-223 had demonstrated survival benefits in men with mCRPC and bone metastases, its optimal clinical application remains under active investigation. Emerging evidence suggests that combining Ra-223 with other systemic therapies may enhance treatment efficacy. In the PEACE-3 trial, the combination of Ra-223 and enzalutamide significantly improved rPFS, achieving a median of 19.4 months (95% CI0.54-0.87.87; P = 0.0009) compared with 16.4 months using enzalutamide alone [17]. Furthermore, the ALPHABET trial evaluated the concurrent use of Ra-223 (alpha emitter) and Lu-PSMA (beta emitter), demonstrating both safety and promising therapeutic activity. PSA50 and PSA90 responses were 55% (95% CI 36–72) and 18% (95% CI 7–35), respectively. With a median follow-up of 13 months, the median PSA-PFS reached 5.3 months (95%CI 4–9), and the median rPFS was 10 months (95% CI 6.7–13.5) [18].

Evaluating treatment response to Ra-223 can be challenging. While PSA serves as an initial tool in assessing treatment response, PSA flare phenomenon has been reported up to 20% and hence limited its use [19]. Additionally, in a multicentric retrospective study of patients with mCRPC undergoing Ra-223, Keziman et al. reported bone flare phenomenon in 6% of patients [20]. The authors attributed it to osteoblastic activity following the treatment and cautioned from misinterpreting as radiographic disease progression on bone scans; especially in the first 3 months [20].

With the advances in imaging modalities, there has been a parallel growing interest in improving patients’ management. Etchebehere et al. investigated the use of F-NaF fluoride PET/CT scans in evaluating response to Ra-223. The authors showed the prognostic utility in predicting treatment response with F-NaF Fluoride PET/CT compared with bone scan [21]. While other studies reported the promising prognostic value of FCH PET/CT independence of other conventional techniques in predicting the post Radium-223 outcome [22].

While C-11 choline PET/CT scan has demonstrated superiority compared to conventional imaging in multiple disease stage, its utility in evaluating response to Ra-223 is yet to be evaluated [23, 24]. Here in, we seek to investigate the utility of C-11 Choline PET/CT in evaluating response to Ra-223 for mCRPC. Additionally, we aim to describe the utility of PSA in predicting radiographic disease progression.

Methods

Patients’ selection

After we obtained approval from the institutional review board under reference number IRB 22–010856, we conducted our retrospective review using prospective maintained C-11 Choline PET/CT registry to identify metastatic castrate resistant prostate cancer patients treated with Ra-223 between (2013–2018) for predominant bone metastases. Our inclusion criteria include patients who received at least 4–6 cycles of Ra223 and completed evaluation with C-11 choline PET/CT scan at baseline, interim treatment, and post therapy. Patients’ baseline characteristics were abstracted from their medical record by trained research personnel and summarized in table-1. All patients included in our study have received 2nd generation hormone therapy and chemotherapy prior to Ra-223. Blind repeat radiographic evaluation of patients’ scan had been done by radiologists specialized in nuclear medicine. The response to Ra-223 was categorized into two categorized favorable and unfavorable responses according to imaging. Favorable response was defined by partial response or stable disease. An unfavorable response was defined by overall progression of disease on the patient’s imaging.

PET scan

All C-11 Choline PET/CT examinations were performed according to the Mayo Clinic protocol using discovery series scanners (models LS, RX, 690, or 710). Each patient’s baseline weight and height were documented prior to imaging. A radiotracer dose of 370–555 MBq of C-11 Choline was administered intravenously approximately 5 min before imaging acquisition. The scan extended from the orbit to the mid- thigh, with an acquisition time of 3 min per bed position on a 128 × 128 matrix. Image reconstruction was conducted using a 3D ordered-subsets expectation maximization (OSEM) algorithm with 28 subsets and 2 iterations. For anatomical correlation and attenuation correction, a low-dose CT scan was obtained using thin slices (~ 3.75 mm) and standard imaging parameters. PET choline/CT interpretation

All pre-treatment, interim and post treatment C-11 Choline PET/CT scans were obtained from electronic records. Board certified radiologist and board-certified radiologist specialized in nuclear medicine blindly evaluated the scans through MIMVista workstation (software version 6.8.3;MIM software Inc.) Lesions were considered positive if they demonstrated SUVmax above the blood pool SUVmax (blood pool corrected SUV max). The imaging response was defined as partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and progression of disease (PD).

Assessment of response to Ra-223

The response to Ra-223 on imaging was measured by SUVmax of lesions pre, mid and posttherapy. The favorable response was defined as stabilization (SD), decrease uptake (PR) at mid and posttherapy. On the contrary, the unfavorable response was defined as developing new lesions or an increase in SUVmax on Choline PET/CT scan (PD).

Statistical analysis

The analyses were conducted using JMP Pro 14.1.0 (SAS institute, Cary, NC). Categorical variables were presented by frequency and percentage. Continuous variables were measured as mean (± SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR]). Univariate and multivariate logistic analyses were conducted to determine the predictors of Post-Ra-223 radiographic response on 11C-Choline scans. Regressions were reported using OR, 95% CI and P value.

Duration of follow up was formulated from the time of initiating Ra-223 therapy to the last date of follow up or death. A 2 tailed P value < 0.05 was considered statically significant.

Results

We identified 32 patients with a median of (IQR) age of 69.4 years (61.7–73.8). patients received a median (IQR) of 6 cycles of radium-223 (6–6). The median (IQR) of gleason score was 8 (8–9). Baseline PSA levels (IQR) had a median of 13.5 (39.6-5.4). At mid treatment scanning, 25% of the patients (n = 8) showed favorable response (Group A), while 75% of the (n = 24) patients showed an unfavorable response (Group B). After the complete course of treatment, 50% of patients (n = 4) in Group A continued to show favorable response, versus only 8.3% of patients (n = 2) in Group B showed favorable response (P value = 0.0141). Discussion

Ra-223 is an approved radioligand therapy for men with mCRPC and bone metastases. Given that the majority of patients with mCRPC presented skeletal involvement, interest in optimizing the therapeutic role of Ra-223 remains high.

In the ALSYMPCA trial, use of Ra-223 in patients with symptomatic bone metastases demonstrated superiority over placebo, with improved overall survival and lower rates of adverse events [16]. Additionally, the PEACE-3 trial enrolled asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic patients with mCRPC and bone metastases. Participants were randomized 1:1 to receive either six cycles of Ra-223 combined with enzalutamide as first-line therapy or enzalutamide alone, with all patients receiving bone-protective agents (Zoledronic acid or denosumab). The trial demonstrated improved rPFS with the combination regimen. Notably, treatment-emergent adverse events were more frequent in the combination arm (65.6% vs. 55.8%) [17].

Moreover, the ALPHABET phase I/II single-arm trial addressed an important gap in evaluating the safety of sequential dual-emitter therapy (alpha and beta emitters). Thirty-six patients with PSMA- and FDG-positive mCRPC and bone metastases, all previously treated with androgen receptor targeted therapy, received Lu-PSMA followed by Ra-223, with bone-protective therapy administrated in phase II. The study confirmed the safety of this approach and demonstrated encouraging oncologic outcomes [18].

Evaluation of the response to Ra-223 represents a clinical challenge due to the absence of reliable biological and imaging tools that help in patients’ response [16, 19]. Evaluating how bone lesions respond to treatment is difficult because their complex shape makes it hard to measure them accurately, especially when the disease is widespread and tumor volume needs to be assessed.

In this single institution study, we retrospectively evaluated the prognostic value of 11 C-Choline scan in the setting of Ra-223 as third line treatment in patients who failed chemotherapy and ARPI.

In our study, 50% of patients, who demonstrated favorable response at mid-treatment, maintained an overall favorable response following completion of Ra-223. In contrast, 91.7% of those with an unfavorable mid-treatment response continued to exhibit an unfavorable outcome after Ra-223 treatment. We found that PET Choline Parameter as initial blood corrected SUVmax and radiographic response at mid treatment significance serve as an early indicator of the overall treatment outcome as seen on subsequent C-11 Choline PET/CT.

While our data support the clinical value of 11 C-Choline in this setting, other investigators have reported promising, and in some cases superior performance, with 18 F-NaF PET/CT. These observations underscore the need for future comparative studies to better delineate the complementary or distinct roles of these radiolabeled tracers in assessing treatment response and guiding management decisions in metastatic prostate cancer.

There have been other studies that reported the usefulness of PET/CT scans in the evaluation of disease and response compared to conventional scans. Thomas et al. retrospectively identified 35 patients with prostate cancer who received Ra-223 therapy for skeletal metastases, comparing 86Ga-PSMA PET/CT with conventional 99mTc-MDP bone scan scintigraphy [BS] [25]. Their findings demonstrated that 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT has higher sensitivity to detect skeletal lesions, especially within the pelvic and spinal regions [25]. Following 6 cycles of Ra-233, Ga PSMA PET/CT scan outperforms bone scan in the evaluation of the response in mCRP [25]. Additionally, Uprimmy et al. and Nørgaard et al. reported 18F-NaF PET/CT is superior in monitoring the response to Ra-223 [13, 26]. Notably, Uprimmy et al. advocate the use of 18 F-NaF PET/CT over 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT, when they were compared at the same time to assess the response in Ra-223 [26]. The best proxy for the assessment of the response to Ra-223 is still undefined.

There have been many studies that reported the effectiveness of 11 C-Choline PET/CT scan in monitoring disease progression during systemic therapy. Fuccio et al. observed the high sensitivity of C-11Choline PET/CT scan in terms of detection of site of disease recurrence at biochemical recurrence (BCR) compared to BS after radical prostatectomy (RP) [24]. Especially, when the PSA level is less than < 1ng/ml to detect prostate cancer, in addition to that 4.5% of prostate cancer patients were found to have secondary malignancy and treated subsequently [24, 27]. In comparison, Treglia et al. reported the PSMA PET/CT has higher sensitivity in detecting lesions in low PSA < 1 ng/ml than C-11Choline scan [28].

These findings carry important implications. First, they empower clinicians to optimize and individualize Ra-223 therapy, allowing for timely adjustments based on how the disease responds midway through treatment. Second, for patients who exhibit an unfavorable response, our approach offers an opportunity to consider alternative treatment strategies early, avoiding unnecessary continuation of Ra-223, reducing cumulative toxicity, minimizing the risk of promoting aggressive disease resistance, and lessening the overall treatment burden for both patients and health care system. Third, our results highlight the potential value of integrating advanced PET/CT imaging technique into the routine monitoring protocols of metastatic prostate cancer patients undergoing Ra-223 therapy.

A key strength of our study is the standard imaging protocol across different patients. All imaging was read and interpreted blindly by nuclear medicine physicians and board-certified radiologists, and the patients evaluated using SUV max and corrected blood pool index. Most of the patients in our study received complete course pf treatment from 4 to 6 cycles and were followed regularly from the baseline till the end of treatment.

The limitations of our study are retrospective, small size population, and lack of long-term outcomes such as biochemical free survival and overall survival. 11C-Choline PET/CT scan is closely and widely available in the U.S., as only a few advanced centers can offer it. This is because the tracer breaks down quickly, its half-life is about 20 min, so it must be produced locally using a cyclotron [29].

Conclusion

Using C-11Choline PET/CT scan for mid-treatment evaluation appears to be an useful approach for predicting how patients will respond to Ra-223 therapy. Patients who show disease progression at this stage are much less likely to benefit from continued treatment, suggesting early resistance. Identifying these patients sooner could help clinicians adjust treatment plans more effectively, potentially sparing them from ineffective therapy. Nonetheless, these findings are based on a relatively small group of patients, and larger studies are needed to confirm the predictive value of mid-treatment C-11Choline PET/CT scan. Future clinical trials should investigate this further to determine how best to use this imaging tool to guide treatment decisions and improve patient outcomes.

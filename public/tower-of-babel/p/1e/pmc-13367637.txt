Osteoarthritis among individuals with diabetes mellitus in Saudi Arabia
Authors: Bader Alharbi, Meshal Alghamdi, Abdulaziz Alrushaid, Abdulrahman Alamer, Tammam Alhalwani, Abdullah Alhumaid, Khames T. Alzahrani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Osteoarthritis (OA) is characterized by the degeneration of hyaline joint cartilage, hypertrophic changes in the joint capsule, and subchondral remodeling with osteophyte production. Both Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and OA are prevalent chronic conditions, affecting 15% and 8.5% of the global population, respectively. Studies indicate a correlation between elevated comorbidities and increased knee pain, with metabolic syndrome (including diabetes, hypertension, elevated cholesterol, and obesity) linked to more severe OA pain. Research has shown that individuals with T2DM are at a higher risk of developing OA.Objectives:To determine the prevalence of OA and its impact on adults with diabetes in Saudi Arabia.Methodology:This cross-sectional study was conducted from July 2024 to March 2025 among diabetic adults in Saudi Arabia. The study population included all adult Saudi individuals over 40 years old with diabetes and OA. Inclusion criteria encompassed all adult diabetic patients in Saudi Arabia, regardless of gender, while nondiabetic individuals were excluded. Data collection involved a self-administered questionnaire distributed via WhatsApp, with informed consent obtained from participants. The WOMAC criteria were used to score OA symptoms, covering pain (0–20), stiffness (0–8), and physical activity (0–68). Data entry was performed using Microsoft Excel and analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 20.0.Results:The study investigated the prevalence of OA among adults with diabetes in Saudi Arabia, involving 185 participants with a mean age of 44.2 years. The findings indicated significant OA-related pain and stiffness, with 32.5% experiencing moderate to severe pain. Notably, 14.1% reported high levels of pain and stiffness impacting physical activity. The mean WOMAC total score was 19.9, reflecting the low severity of knee OA symptoms. Statistically significant relationships were found between pain levels and various factors, including age, diabetes type, and weight. Overall, while OA was present, the severity of symptoms among this diabetic cohort was low. These findings are relevant for primary care physicians in Saudi Arabia who manage diabetic patients at risk of OA.Conclusion:This study contributes to the growing body of literature on the prevalence of OA among adults with diabetes in Saudi Arabia. The findings highlight the complex interplay between these two chronic conditions and underscore the importance of comprehensive management strategies that address both diabetes and OA.

Introduction

Degeneration of the hyaline joint cartilage, hypertrophic changes in the joint capsule, and injury to the underlying bone with subchondral remodeling and osteophyte production are characteristic features of osteoarthritis (OA).[1] Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and OA are two very widespread chronic medical conditions that impose significant physical and financial burdens on individuals and healthcare systems.[2] Studies show that having an elevated prevalence of comorbidities is linked to more severe knee pain. In those suffering from OA of the knee, metabolic syndrome—which includes diabetes mellitus, hypertension, elevated cholesterol, and obesity—has been associated with increased pain.[3]

T2DM and OA are prevalent chronic illnesses that impact 15% and 8.5% of people globally, respectively.[456] Over 240 million older people suffer from OA.[5] According to a 2022 study, individuals with DM had a higher risk of developing OA, with 19 out of 90 patients having the condition.[27] The results of a study conducted in Chennai, India, in 2022 showed that those with diabetes had a higher risk of acquiring OA in comparison to those without the disease.[23] According to a study on T2DM comorbidities, OA was one of the most prevalent comorbidities in T2DM patients at diagnosis, accounting for 11% of cases.[38] In order to determine the pathophysiology of the degenerative process and provide the most effective therapy possible, this study is focused on analyzing the prevalence of OA among T2DM patients in Saudi Arabia. The growing number of cases with a risk of disability and the decline in their quality of life make this investigation necessary. This study carries important implications for both healthcare providers and patients. For physicians, identifying OA symptoms early in diabetic patients allows for prompt interventions such as weight control, exercise counseling, and pain management, potentially reducing long-term joint damage and disability. Integrating musculoskeletal assessment into routine diabetes follow-up helps clinicians adopt a more holistic approach to chronic disease management. For patients, the findings highlight the value of lifestyle modification and adherence to diabetes control in preventing or minimizing OA symptoms. Improved awareness of this association empowers patients to seek medical advice sooner, engage in regular physical activity, and maintain optimal glycemic control, which together can enhance quality of life and functional independence.

Objectives

To define the prevalence of OA and its impact on adults with diabetes in Saudi Arabia

Methodology

This cross-sectional study was conducted on diabetic adults and carried out in Saudi Arabia from July 2024 to March 2025. The study’s population consists of all adult Saudi populations over 40 years of age with diabetes and OA.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

All adult patients with diabetes mellitus in Saudi Arabia, regardless of gender, are included in the sample; those without the condition are excluded.

Method for data collection, instrument, and score system

Saudi patients were receiving a self-administered questionnaire via phone number (What’s App). Before administering the questionnaires to the people who agreed to participate, informed consent was obtained, explaining the study’s requirements.

WOMAC criteria for osteoarthritis

None = 0, Slight = 1, Moderate = 2, Severe = 3, Extreme = 4

Part 1: Pain (0–20), Part 2: Stiffness (0–8), and Part 3: Physical Activity (0–68)

Analysis and entry method

The computer was used to enter data using Microsoft Office Excel (2016) Windows software. After that, the data were moved to IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 20.0, the Statistical Package of Social Science Software (SPSS) application, version 20, to be examined statistically.

Discussion

The present study aimed to investigate the prevalence of OA among adults with diabetes in Saudi Arabia, focusing on the demographic characteristics and health status of the participants. The results showed that a large proportion of the diabetic population suffer from variable levels of OA and are at risk to their general health and ability to live a satisfactory life.

In this study, 32.5% of diabetic participants reported moderate-to-severe OA-related pain, and 14.1% experienced pain with stiffness significantly impacting physical activity, with a mean WOMAC total score of 19.9, indicating generally low symptom severity. When compared to the study by Tiwari et al.[2] in Chennai, India, which reported a prevalence of 46.3% among T2DM patients, our prevalence appears lower. Differences in OA prevalence and symptom severity may reflect variations in demographic characteristics, mean age (44.2 years in our study vs. 53.9 years in Tiwari et al.[2]), BMI, lifestyle, and regional factors. While both studies highlight age and comorbidities as significant risk factors, our study offers novel insight into the Saudi diabetic population, offering region-specific data on OA prevalence, symptom severity, and the relationship with diabetes type and weight. These findings reinforce the need for early detection and comprehensive management of OA in diabetic patients to improve functional outcomes and quality of life.

These findings are also in line with the results of previous studies which have proven the close relationship between diabetes and OA; diabetes occurs in 50% of the population suffering from OA in Saudi Arabia. For example, Louati et al.[9] reported in a systematic review and meta-analysis that diabetic individuals have higher prevalence of OA than that found in nondiabetic individuals, suggesting bidirectional relationship between the two conditions. This is also in line with the work of Schett et al.,[10] who demonstrated that type 2 diabetes is an independent predictor of severe OA, and therefore, the metabolic factors are a key part of the pathogenesis of OA.[1011] Although the mean WOMAC score in our participants indicated relatively low OA severity, the chronic inflammatory nature of diabetes may exacerbate OA symptoms over time.

Our sample showed demographic characteristics of a higher prevalence of Type 1 among individuals with OA compared to some studies that find a higher prevalence of Type 2 diabetes in the presence of OA. For example, in Nüesch et al.,[12] they described that type 2 diabetes is more common with knee and hip OA, which may simply be due to differences in the studied population or prevalence of the different types of diabetes. Furthermore, we also find significant relationships between pain, stiffness, and physical activity limitations with a number of demographic factors, including age and marital status, which are consistent with findings from other studies, which highlight the role of social determinants of health-on-health outcomes of individuals with OA.[1314]

However, our study discovered no significant relationships between gender, region of residence, educational level, smoking status, or medication adherence and OA outcomes, different from findings of other research studies, such as Kendzerska et al.,[13] which observed different comorbidities and demographics with regard to the severity of OA symptoms. Our study’s lack of significant associations may indicate that there are more ramifications of diabetes for the symptoms of OA than demographical factors and should prompt targeted interventions for diabetic patients to control their OA better.

The findings with respect to pain and stiffness levels among participants also match previous research. For example, Rehling et al.[15] have described that musculoskeletal pain and OA are correlated with diabetes and these are additive to patient disability and to the healthcare costs. Our study, however, reveals the clinical distribution of pain levels specifically in diabetic patients with OA, since 32.5% of participants had moderate to severe pain, and underlines the importance of adapting appropriate comprehensive pain management to diabetic patients with OA. Additionally, the observed HbA1c level to pain severity association was consistent with the reported relation among glycemic control and the prevalence of osteoarthritis as noted by Kume and Kashiwagi.[16]

However, this study achieved valuable insights while also acknowledging several limitations. Although calculated as appropriate, the sample size may never truly reflect the larger population of diabetic patients in Saudi Arabia as there is geographical concentration of participants to the Eastern region. Further studies should be conducted on longitudinal designs to learn about the progression of OA in diabetic patients and assess the effects on OA of diabetes management and/or disease-specific interventions for OA.

Conclusion

This study adds to the work on the incidence of OA among the adults in Saudi Arabia with diabetes. The results highlight the complex relationship that diabetes and OA have with each other and the need for combined management of both conditions. Elucidation of these underlying mechanisms and development of effective interventions to improve the quality of life of affected individuals are important continued research questions.

Textual quantitative analysis of China’s fertility-friendly policies——based on a two dimensional analytical framework of policy tools and policy objectives
Authors: Yanru Kang, Yijing Li, Li Zhang, Runjie Sun, Jinxin Zhou, Guoxiang Guan, Ziyue Chen, Huiling Wu, Yongbing Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo quantitatively analyze China’s fertility-friendly policies issued from 2016 to 2025, examine their structural characteristics and evolution, and provide evidence for policy optimization.MethodsA two-dimensional framework of “policy tools–policy objectives” was constructed. Content analysis, word frequency analysis, and semantic network analysis were applied to 46 central-level fertility-friendly policy texts.ResultsThe number of fertility-friendly policies showed an overall upward trend with clear phased features. The policy system demonstrated service orientation, system coordination, and multi-level advancement. Environmental, supply, and demand policy tools accounted for 40.65%, 40.29%, and 19.06%, respectively, indicating an imbalanced distribution of policy tools at the central-government level. In terms of policy obectives, service-friendly, cultural and social-friendly, and interpersonal-friendly goals accounted for 73.14%, 24.32%, and 2.54%, respectively, revealing an uneven goal hierarchy. The alignment between policy tools and policy objectives was insufficient, and system coordination remained weak.ConclusionChina’s fertility-friendly policy system has shifted from initial exploration to systematic development, forming a relatively diversified but still structurally unbalanced policy structure. Future policies should increase direct financial subsidies, expand paid parental leave, and strengthen employer responsibility-sharing mechanisms, while enhancing coordination between service provision and interpersonal support systems and promoting the construction of a multi-level fertility support network involving government, society, communities, and families to improve overall policy effectiveness.Keywords: Fertility-friendly, Policy text analysis, Policy tools, Policy objectives, Semantic network analysis

Introduction

Since 1950, fertility rates have declined across all countries and regions and are projected to continue decreasing through 2100 [1]. China entered a phase of negative population growth in 2022 and has remained negative for two consecutive years. Its total fertility rate has fallen to around 1.0 in recent years, placing the country among those with the lowest fertility levels worldwide [2, 3]. In response to this demographic challenge, China’s fertility policy has shifted from restriction to support, accompanied by a broad set of measures aimed at promoting balanced population development.

Between 2013 and 2021, China successively implemented the “Selective Two-Child Policy”, the “Universal Two-Child Policy” and the “Universal Three-Child Policy.” In August 2022, 19 ministries jointly released the Guiding Opinions on Further Improving and Implementing Incentive Measures for Promoting Balanced Population Development. Subsequently, in October 2024, the General Office of the State Council issued the Several Measures on Accelerating the Improvement of the Fertility Support Policy System and Promoting the Construction of a Fertility-Friendly Society, which emphasized establishing a life-course population service system, reducing the costs of childbirth, childrearing, and education, and fostering a social atmosphere supportive of childbearing.

As the breadth and complexity of fertility-supportive policies continue to expand, several critical analytical questions arise. What specific fertility-friendly policies have been introduced at the national and subnational levels? Which policy tools are predominantly employed, and how are these tools structured to achieve policy objectives? What patterns or structural characteristics emerge within the evolving policy system? Addressing these questions is essential for clarifying the overall architecture and internal logic of China’s fertility-friendly policy framework, tracing its temporal evolution, and providing an evidence base for policy optimization [4].

Building a fertility-friendly society and advancing sustainable population development have become strategic imperatives for responding to China’s demographic transition [5]. National-level policy documents not only reflect top-level design but also guide the formulation and implementation of policies at local levels [6]. Therefore, this study conducts a systematic text analysis of national fertility-friendly policies, examining policy content, tools, and structural features from multiple dimensions. The findings aim to inform the refinement of China’s fertility-supportive policy system and contribute to the development of a more resilient and adaptive fertility governance framework.

Methods

Research framework

Drawing on the two-dimensional analytical framework of “policy tools–policy objectives,” this study applies content analysis, word frequency analysis, and semantic network analysis to quantitatively examine China’s national fertility-friendly policy texts. By integrating multiple analytical approaches within a unified framework, the study expands existing methodologies for evaluating fertility-supportive policies and offers meaningful analytical contributions

First, 46 national-level fertility-friendly policy documents were collected from the official websites of central governmental ministries and the Peking University Law Database. Second, the temporal distribution of these policies was analyzed, and key thematic patterns were identified through word frequency analysis and semantic network analysis, which facilitated the visualization of central keywords and their co-occurrence relationships. Third, guided by the “policy tools–policy objectives” framework, content analysis was conducted to systematically code and quantify the structural characteristics of policy tools and policy objectives. Finally, based on the empirical results, targeted recommendations were proposed to optimize the configuration of policy tools and refine goal setting within China’s fertility-friendly policy system. Policy text selection

In 2016, the Chinese government issued the Guiding Opinions on Accelerating the Construction of Maternal and Infant Facilities, which introduced the concept of a “fertility-friendly social environment” for the first time [7]. In 2017, the Special Seminar on the Universal Two-Child Policy and the Construction of a Fertility-Friendly Social Environment, organized by the National Health and Family Planning Commission and several other departments, formally established this concept [8]. Therefore, the release date of the 2016 Guiding Opinions was taken as the starting point for collecting policy documents samples, and the deadline was set as September 2025. Using keywords such as “childbirth” “fertility-friendly” “fertility support” and “childcare” policy texts were retrieved from the State Council policy document database, the Peking University Law Database, and other authoritative regulatory platforms.

To ensure the validity, authority, and representativeness of the sample, the following inclusion criteria were applied: (1) Issuing authority: Only national-level policy documents were included, issued by institutions such as the National People’s Congress and its Standing Committee, the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China, the State Council, and ministries/commissions under the State Council. (2) Policy relevance: Policy content must be directly related to fertility and childrearing, including the promotion of timely marriage and childbirth, enhancement of prenatal and postnatal care, and development of inclusive childcare services. When multiple versions existed, the most recent revision was used. (3) Policy type: Eligible types included laws, regulations, plans, guidelines, opinions, measures, notices, and announcements. Government work reports, conference summaries, resolutions, case notices, and replies were excluded. (4) Policy validity: Only policies currently in force were retained; repealed or superseded documents were removed.

National-level policies were specifically selected because they represent the highest level of policy authority in China and provide the top-level design that guides local policy implementation. Following systematic retrieval and screening based on the above criteria, a total of 46 national-level policies related to the fertility-friendly environment were ultimately included.

Research methodology

Content analysis is a widely used analytical approach in policy text research. It transforms textual information into systematic, objective, and quantifiable data to uncover the deeper semantic implications and internal structural features of policy documents [9]. In this study, content analysis was conducted within the analytical framework of “policy tools–policy objectives.” Policy texts were coded using NVivo 12.0, and the coding outputs were categorized, organized, and statistically analyzed using Microsoft Excel 2019.

Word frequency analysis is a bibliometric technique that identifies keywords or thematic terms with high occurrence frequency to reveal research priorities and developmental trends within a specific field [10]. Semantic network analysis further utilizes these high-frequency terms as nodes to construct a network of co-occurring words, enabling the examination of conceptual linkages, relational patterns, and hierarchical structures underlying policy texts [11]. In this study, ROST CM 6.0 was employed to segment the policy texts, extract the top 100 high-frequency words, and generate a keyword co-occurrence network. By analyzing the positional attributes of nodes and the strength of their associations, this study identifies the central themes embedded in fertility-friendly policies and reveals the internal logic connecting key policy concepts.

Construction of the policy analysis framework

X-dimension: policy tools

Policy tools refer to the instruments or mechanisms employed by policymakers and implementing agencies to address policy problems and achieve intended outcomes within a specific policy environment [11]. Incorporating the perspective of policy tools in policy text analysis enables a more multidimensional interpretation of policy design and helps identify structural strengths and weaknesses [12]. Rothwell and Zegveld categorized policy tools into three major types—supply-oriented, demand-oriented, and environment-oriented tools—each of which can be further subdivided to clarify policy structure and scope of influence [13].

Drawing on this classification and considering the characteristics of China’s fertility-friendly policy system, this study adopts a three-dimensional tool system consisting of demand-oriented, supply-oriented, and environment-oriented tools. Supply-oriented tools refer to instruments that enhance fertility support through financial input, talent development, and organizational capacity building, emphasizing direct governmental provision. Demand-oriented tools involve measures that meet the needs of reproductive-age individuals, including economic support, service provision, and time-related guarantees. Environment-oriented tools focus on shaping a fertility-supportive sociocultural environment and strengthening regulation, planning, and public communication. Based on existing research, these three categories were further refined to develop an operational taxonomy of policy tools. Detailed classifications and definitions are presented in Table 1.

Classification of fertility-friendly policy tools

Y-dimension: policy objectives

Policy objectives represent core elements in policy formulation and analysis, reflecting the primary concerns of policy actors regarding particular issues [14]. The objective of establishing a “fertility-friendly” environment aims to promote policy inclusiveness and improve the fertility-supportive environment through effective policy guidance [15]. Based on the content of the collected policy texts and prior research, this study identifies three policy objectives: cultural and social friendliness, service friendliness, and interpersonal friendliness.

Cultural and social friendliness refers to cultivating modern marriage and parenting values, creating a supportive fertility culture, and developing accessible and friendly public spaces, with an emphasis on the cultural and spatial dimensions of the lives of reproductive-age groups. Service friendliness refers to the availability, accessibility, and institutional of services and employment-related protections throughout the fertility and childrearing process. It includes not only the provision and quality of healthcare, childcare, and educational services, but also labor protection mechanisms that ensure women’s continuity of employment and access to social benefits during pregnancy, childbirth, and parenting, which are essential conditions for effective service utilization. Interpersonal friendliness emphasizes informal social support within families and communities, focusing on emotional support, caregiving cooperation, and social participation during parenting, rather than formal institutional or legal protections. By strengthening social support networks and promoting positive communication, a supportive interpersonal environment conducive to fertility and parenting can be established, helping to reduce parenting-related isolation and enhance emotional well-being among reproductive-age individuals—particularly women. Classification of fertility-friendly policy objectives

Construction of the X–Y two-dimensional framework

Based on the analysis of the X-dimension (policy tools) and the Y-dimension (policy objectives), a two-dimensional analytical framework for fertility-friendly policies is constructed. This framework incorporates both dimensions and serves as the foundation for subsequent policy text coding and analysis. The conceptual structure of the framework is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Framework diagram for two-dimensional analysis of fertility-friendly policies

Drawing on this framework, the 46 policy texts were imported into NVivo 12.0 for qualitative analysis. Content related to “fertility-friendly response” was identified and coded following the rule of “policy number–chapter number–analysis unit number–content sequence number.” For example, 1-1-1-(1) indicates the specific content extracted from the first article of the first analytical unit in the first chapter of the first policy document. The coding process adhered to the principle of non-disaggregation. When a single textual segment reflected multiple policy tools, it was coded repeatedly across the relevant categories to ensure the rigor, accuracy, and reliability of the analytical results. A total of 551 coding entries were ultimately generated, which were subsequently categorized into the corresponding dimensions of policy tools and policy objectives.

Two researchers independently coded all policy texts using the established framework. Inter-coder reliability was assessed using Cohen’s Kappa coefficient, and the overall Kappa value was 0.722, indicating substantial agreement according to the criteria proposed by Landis and Koch [16, 17]. Disagreements mainly occurred in coding abstract categories such as “Goal Planning” in “Environmental-type”, “Establishing a Supportive Policy Environment” in “Cultural and social friendliness” and “Improving Service Infrastructure” in “Service friendliness”, where conceptual boundaries were relatively blurred. The disagreement issues were eventually resolved through repeated discussions by the two researchers. For the unresolved cases, based on predefined coding rules, consensus was reached through further discussions with the third senior researcher.

Results

Policy release time analysis

From 2016 to 2025, the number of fertility-friendly policies released in China exhibited an overall upward trajectory, characterized by distinct stages as well as fluctuations and intermittent gaps, as shown in Fig. 3.

Text publishing timeline

During the exploration stage (2016–2020), the number of policies remained relatively low and displayed a discontinuous pattern. Two policies were issued in 2016 and 2019, whereas only one was released in 2020. Notably, no relevant policies were issued in 2017 and 2018. This pattern suggests that, although the government had recognized the challenge of declining fertility following the implementation of the universal two-child policy, policy development during this early stage was still exploratory, and a comprehensive support system had not yet formed.

During the development stage (2021–2025), policy issuance increased significantly and began to stabilize. The number of policies rose sharply to seven in 2021, increased to eight in 2022, and reached a peak of eleven in 2024. This expansion reflects the government’s intensified efforts to address the continuing decline in fertility. During this period, a multi-level, multi-departmental collaborative support system gradually emerged, with more detailed policy measures covering medical insurance, economic assistance, public services, and work–family balance. Policy focus shifted from exploratory measures to more systematic and comprehensive arrangements. Although the number slightly decreased in 2025, this was because the data for 2025 did not cover the entire year but only went up to September.

Overall, China’s fertility-friendly policy development has transitioned from an exploratory phase to a more systematic stage, shifting from gap-filling approaches toward system-building strategies, with increasingly refined and strengthened support measures.

Word frequency analysis and semantic network analysis

To further clarify the semantic structure and thematic associations within the policy texts, this study used ROST CM 6.0 to extract the top 100 high-frequency keywords, generate word clouds, and construct keyword co-occurrence networks. These analyses reveal the core issues and structural characteristics of fertility-supportive policies from the perspectives of word frequency and network relationships.

As shown in Fig. 4, keywords such as “service” “development” “health” “reproduction” “society” “population” and “construction” appeared most frequently. This indicates that the overall policy orientation centers on population development strategies, public service provision, and health-related support. In addition, terms such as “childcare services” “infants and toddlers” “family” “guarantee” “promotion” “policy” and “management” are also prominent. These results suggest that the policy texts place substantial emphasis on childcare system development, family-based parenting support, institutional guarantees, and public communication—reflecting a policy focus aligned with the needs of reproductive-age individuals and children aged 0–3.

Word cloud of fertility-friendly policies

The keyword co-occurrence network further reveals the internal relationships and relative importance of key concepts. As illustrated in Fig. 5, “service” occupies a central position and forms dense linkages with “childcare service” “infant and toddler” “family” and “policy” indicating that the development of service systems is a central theme in fertility-friendly policy design. Moreover, keywords such as “society” “construction” “development” and “fertility-friendly” exhibit high centrality and connect with multiple other policy terms. This suggests that the policy texts emphasize not only supply-side improvements and institutional development, but also the cultivation of a supportive social and cultural environment and the coordinated implementation of related measures. At the same time, the network structure also reveals notable thematic gaps. Concepts related to male involvement, such as “father,” do not emerge as salient nodes nor form strong co-occurrence relationships with core policy terms, suggesting limited policy attention to men’s caregiving roles and gender-equitable division of family responsibilities. Furthermore, organizational actors that are critical for policy implementation, such as enterprises, community organizations, and healthcare institutions, are not prominently represented as independent or strongly connected nodes, indicating that their functional roles in fertility support are not explicitly articulated within central-level policy discourse. These missing or weakly articulated relational structures in the semantic network are consistent with the quantitative finding that interpersonal-friendly objectives account for only a very small proportion of total policy objectives. Together, they suggest that current fertility-friendly policies place greater emphasis on institutional provision and regulatory coordination than on strengthening interpersonal support mechanisms and multi-actor participation, which may constrain the effectiveness of family-centered and community-based fertility support in practice.

Semantic network of keywords in fertility-friendly policies

Overall, the word cloud depicts the distribution and thematic emphasis of high-frequency terms, whereas the co-occurrence network clarifies the internal logic and interconnections among policy concepts. Together, these results demonstrate that China’s fertility-friendly policy system features service orientation, system-level coordination, and multi-dimensional advancement, providing a policy foundation for constructing a stable and sustainable long-term fertility support system.

Dimensional analysis of policy tools

Based on the coding results, the distribution of fertility-friendly policy tools is shown in Fig. 6. Overall, all three types of tools—supply-oriented, demand-oriented, and environmental—are present in China’s fertility-friendly policies. However, their structural proportions differ substantially. Environmental and supply-oriented tools are heavily utilized, accounting for 40.65% and 40.29%, respectively, forming the main foundation of the policy system. In contrast, demand-oriented tools account for only 19.06%, indicating a relatively limited presence. This pattern suggests that current fertility-friendly policies predominantly rely on environmental- and supply-oriented measures, such as fostering a supportive social and cultural atmosphere, strengthening public service capacity, and enhancing financial and human resource support. Compared with these two types, demand-oriented tools remain underdeveloped, reflecting an imbalanced tool structure.

The number and proportion of the adopted policy tools

Further analysis shows that the internal structure of each tool category also exhibits imbalance. Within supply-oriented tools, public service measures account for the largest proportion, while infrastructure-related and organizational support measures are relatively similar in frequency; fiscal support measures are used least, revealing a characteristic of “emphasizing services over financial investment.” Within demand-oriented tools, service support dominates, whereas time support appears least utilized, suggesting insufficient attention to mitigating conflicts between childbirth and work–life balance. For environmental tools, supervision and management constitute the majority, while publicity and guidance appear least used, indicating that current policies prioritize regulatory oversight over value advocacy or cultural promotion. Overall, both the category distribution and internal structure of China’s fertility-friendly policy tools show notable imbalance, with especially insufficient investment in demand-oriented tools. Strengthening demand-side measures will be necessary to more accurately and comprehensively meet the practical fertility needs of reproductive-age populations.

To further examine whether the configuration of policy tools has changed over time, this study compared the distribution of policy tools between the exploration stage (2016–2020) and the development stage (2021–2025). As shown in Table 3, environmental and supply-oriented tools remained dominant in both stages, indicating a persistent reliance on institutional regulation and public service provision at the central policy level. However, the proportion of supply-oriented tools decreased from 46.34% in the exploration stage to 38.57% in the development stage, while environmental tools remained relatively stable (41.46% and 40.88%, respectively). In contrast, demand-oriented tools increased markedly from 12.20% to 20.55%, suggesting a gradual shift toward more direct economic and time-related support measures for families. Despite this positive trend, demand-oriented instruments still accounted for the smallest proportion in both stages, indicating that although policy implementation has begun to incorporate more concrete support mechanisms, the overall policy tool structure continues to rely primarily on environmental regulation and service provision rather than direct financial transfers or individualized family benefits. This shift is consistent with the policy transition following the implementation of the three-child policy, in which fiscal subsidies, childcare service vouchers, and parental leave arrangements gradually received greater policy attention, reflecting a move from symbolic policy signaling toward more operational policy instruments.

Dimensional analysis of policy objectives

As illustrated in Fig. 7, China’s fertility-friendly policy objectives exhibit significant hierarchical disparity. Service-friendly objectives dominate, accounting for 73.14%, making them the central focus of current policy efforts. Cultural and social-friendly objectives represent 24.32%, reflecting ongoing efforts to shape a fertility-supportive societal atmosphere. In contrast, interpersonal-friendly objectives comprise only 2.54%, considerably lower than the other two categories. This pattern is also evident in Fig. 5, where policies related to “service” are prominently represented, whereas those addressing interpersonal aspects(such as “family” and “father”)are largely absent, indicating a relative weakness in the interpersonal-friendliness dimension. Overall, the policy objective structure presents the pattern of “strong service friendliness, moderate cultural and social friendliness, and weak interpersonal friendliness,” representing an unbalanced “one strong, two weak” configuration.

Cross-dimensional analysis of policy tools and policy objectives

The alignment between policy tools and policy objectives demonstrates distinct differences (Fig. 8). For cultural and social-friendly objectives, environmental tools are dominant (75.37%), with an emphasis on soft measures such as shaping social atmosphere and guiding public values. This indicates that policies addressing fertility-related cultural transformation rely primarily on institutional arrangements and social environment optimization. For service-friendly objectives, supply-oriented tools are used most frequently (50.62%), while environmental tools (30.27%) also play a substantial role. This reflects that in enhancing service provision and public resource supply, policies prioritize both infrastructure investment and regulatory mechanisms. In contrast, interpersonal-friendly objectives rely mainly on demand-oriented tools (78.57%), indicating that enhancing interpersonal support within families and communities depends heavily on direct incentives and demand-side interventions.

Distribution statistics of policy objective subcategory dimensions in policy tools

From the cross-distribution of policy tools and objectives, a certain degree of misalignment and imbalance can be observed. First, each objective category relies heavily on a specific type of tool: cultural and social friendliness depends on environmental tools; service friendliness relies on supply-oriented tools; and interpersonal friendliness depends on demand-oriented tools. Second, some objective categories utilize relatively single tool types, lacking diversity and complementarity. This narrow tool configuration may limit policy coverage and reduce overall effectiveness. Collectively, the current structure indicates an insufficient match between policy tools and objectives, and further improvement is needed to enhance coordination and functional alignment within China’s fertility-friendly policy system.

It should be noted that the strong correspondence between cultural and social-friendly objectives and environmental tools is partly influenced by conceptual overlap in category definitions, as both dimensions emphasize social atmosphere building and normative guidance. However, the cross-analysis also reveals several non-intuitive associations that cannot be explained solely by definitional alignment. Notably, service-friendly objectives still rely substantially on environmental tools (30.36%), indicating that regulatory guidance, planning targets, and institutional coordination are frequently used as initial mechanisms to promote service system development. This suggests that in China’s governance context, service expansion is often initiated through top-level institutional mobilization and administrative mandates before being translated into concrete service supply, reflecting a policy pathway that prioritizes structural steering over immediate operational investment.

Discussion

This study, based on the “policy tools–policy objectives” framework, provides a systematic evaluation of the structure and operational mechanisms of China’s fertility-friendly policies. The findings indicate that China has formed a relatively diversified and increasingly systematic policy framework as policy instruments have expanded over time. The policy system shows a positive trend toward strengthening fertility support. However, notable structural imbalances in policy tools persist, as well as hierarchical disparities among policy goals. Furthermore, the alignment between policy tools and policy objectives remains insufficient. These issues suggest that despite continuous improvement, the policy system still faces coordination gaps and structural optimization needs, warranting deeper investigation into the underlying causes and potential enhancement pathways.

Analysis of the underlying reasons

Based on the analysis results, China’s fertility-friendly policies exhibit a significant structural imbalance in the configuration of policy tools, characterized by heavy reliance on environmental and supply-oriented tools and relatively insufficient use of demand-oriented instruments. Several institutional and administrative factors may explain this pattern.

policy tradition has long emphasized macro-level governance, planning, and institutional construction, driven by a governance model that prioritizes top-level strategic design and normative frameworks in social policy development [18, 19]. Environmental tools, such as policy guidance, regulatory frameworks, and planning targets, are therefore more likely to be adopted in central-level policy design, as they align with these conventional Chinese governance approaches that prioritize normative, institution-driven steering of social development [20]. These tools are relatively low-cost, easier to coordinate across departments, and suitable for top-level institutional design [21]. Second, supply-oriented tools can be expanded by embedding new functional modules into existing public service systems, such as healthcare, education, and childcare infrastructures. This incremental expansion strategy allows governments to improve service capacity without fundamentally restructuring financing mechanisms [22]. In contrast, demand-oriented tools often require direct fiscal transfers, individualized subsidies, or tax incentives targeted at families, which involve higher administrative complexity, long-term fiscal commitments, and more sophisticated eligibility verification systems [23], particularly under China’s fiscally decentralized system and the current structure of social insurance pooling, which limits the central government’s capacity to directly finance individualized family benefits across regions. Moreover, current maternity insurance and social security arrangements still place considerable cost burdens on employers, which further constrains large-scale expansion of paid parental leave and workplace-related demand-side benefits. These institutional constraints limit the policy feasibility of broad demand-side interventions [24]. Third, China’s administrative structure also plays an important role. At the central level, policy documents mainly focus on macro-level guidance and system design, while concrete demand-side measures, such as cash allowances, housing benefits, and childcare subsidies, are more frequently formulated and implemented by local governments based on regional fiscal capacity and demographic conditions [25]. Third, China’s administrative structure also plays an important role. At the central level, policy documents mainly focus on macro-level guidance and system design, while concrete demand-side measures, such as cash allowances, housing benefits, and childcare subsidies, are more frequently formulated and implemented by local governments based on regional fiscal capacity and demographic conditions [25]. Under China’s decentralized fiscal and administrative system, the central government mainly undertakes top-level institutional design and service standard setting, whereas local governments are responsible for financing and implementing most cash subsidies and individualized family benefits. Therefore, the relatively low proportion of demand-oriented tools identified in this study might partly reflect the functional division between the central and local governments, rather than indicating an overall lack of demand-side support within China’s fertility policy system. This phenomenon can be interpreted as a characteristic of inter-governmental division of labor rather than a simple policy defect.

Existing studies similarly indicate that service-based support measures account for a much larger share of China’s fertility-support policies than time-based or direct financial subsidies, and that policy attention is often concentrated on childbirth-related services while long-term support for child-rearing, caregiving, and education remains insufficient [23, 26]. Other research further highlights that imperfect service supply mechanisms, inadequate financial investment, and insufficient coordination among multiple stakeholders have become major bottlenecks in building a comprehensive fertility-friendly society [27]. These findings are consistent with the structural characteristics observed in this study.

In addition to tool imbalance, policy objectives also display a hierarchical pattern characterized by dominant service-friendly goals, moderate cultural and social-friendly goals, and weak interpersonal-friendly goals. This configuration can also be explained by institutional and implementation factors. Service-friendly goals are more easily incorporated into formal policy systems because they correspond directly to governmental service responsibilities and performance evaluation indicators. Cultural and social-friendly goals, which involve value formation and long-term social norm change, usually require sustained public communication and cultural interventions, making policy effects less immediate and more difficult to quantify. Consequently, they often receive comparatively less policy investment. Interpersonal-friendly goals depend heavily on family dynamics, community networks, and employer participation, which require extensive coordination across social actors beyond the government sector. Their implementation mechanisms are more complex and less controllable through administrative commands alone, leading to relatively weaker policy emphasis at the national level. This institutional tendency is also reflected in the semantic network structure, where relational actors such as fathers, employers, and community organizations are not prominently represented as core policy nodes, further confirming the marginal position of interpersonal support within the current policy discourse. This extremely low proportion reflects not only limited policy emphasis, but also the inherent difficulty of formalizing interpersonal support through administrative instruments, as many forms of family cooperation, emotional support, and community interaction are embedded in informal social relations that are not easily governed by standardized policy interventions. Furthermore, the extremely low proportion of interpersonal friendliness objectives can be attributed, in part, to the institutional nature of national-level policy tools. At the central level, policy instruments are primarily designed to regulate public services, formal institutional arrangements, and legally defined rights, rather than to directly intervene in private family relationships or informal social interactions. Therefore, the limited presence of interpersonal friendliness objectives does not necessarily indicate policy neglect, but rather reflects the structural boundaries of state intervention in the private and family domains. From this perspective, the findings do not suggest deficiencies in policy design, but instead reveal the inherent constraints faced by macro-level policy instruments in shaping intimate social relationships that are largely embedded in informal norms, voluntary cooperation, and non-administrative social networks.

From an international perspective, China’s current policy tool structure is not unique and shows similarities with the early stages of fertility support systems in other East Asian countries. Evidence from Japan and South Korea suggests that fertility-support policies initially relied heavily on supply-oriented tools, such as expanding childcare facilities and maternal health services, and environmental tools, such as family-friendly social campaigns and regulatory frameworks, while demand-oriented instruments were gradually introduced later. This pattern has also been observed across the Asia-Pacific region, where early policy responses to low fertility frequently emphasized childcare services, paid leave, and work–family reconciliation measures as foundational supports for families [28]. Only after these foundational systems were established did governments gradually increase demand-oriented instruments, including child allowances, tax credits, and extended paid parental leave, to directly reduce household childrearing costs [29]. In Japan and South Korea, although local governments participate in policy implementation, most major child allowance programs and parental leave benefits are financed or co-financed by the central government through national social insurance schemes or fiscal transfer mechanisms, in contrast to China’s heavier reliance on local fiscal capacity for providing demand-side benefits.

OECD data further indicate that countries with relatively stable or rebounding fertility levels tend to combine strong public service provision with substantial demand-side financial transfers to families [30], forming a more balanced policy tool mix. Moreover, recent cross-national evidence suggests that under conditions of persistent low fertility, families’ reproductive decisions are increasingly influenced by perceived economic security and institutional support, making direct financial and employment-related protections more critical for sustaining family formation intentions [31]. In contrast, countries at earlier stages of fertility policy development often prioritize institutional design and service infrastructure before scaling up direct cash benefits. Compared with these countries, China’s fertility-support policy system remains in a transitional phase, with strong emphasis on service expansion and regulatory guidance but relatively limited fiscal transfers and individualized family subsidies. This comparison suggests that the structural imbalance observed in this study reflects not only domestic institutional constraints but also a common evolutionary pathway in fertility-support policy development. As fiscal capacity and administrative mechanisms mature, expanding demand-oriented tools may represent a necessary step toward building a more comprehensive and effective fertility-support system.

Optimization of policy tool structures

To address the structural imbalance in the tool system of China’s fertility-friendly policies, efforts should focus on three aspects. First, promote the coordinated development of “supply–demand–environment” types of tools. In the context of profound adjustments in China’s population size and structure and continuous improvement of family policies [12], environmental policy tools should be relied upon, supply-oriented tools should be used for support, and demand-oriented tools should drive implementation. This approach transforms policies from relying on single tools to a systematic combination.

Second, enhance the targeting and coverage of demand-oriented policy tools. Economic burden and time pressure are core factors restricting the willingness to have children [32]. In terms of economic support, it is also necessary to distinguish between different types of demand-oriented instruments, as tax incentives and direct cash subsidies differ significantly in policy effectiveness and implementation feasibility. Tax deductions and tax credits, such as child-related personal income tax deductions and housing interest deductions for families with children, are relatively easier to administer through the existing taxation system and can reduce financial burdens for middle-income households [33]. However, their benefits are limited for low-income families with weak or unstable tax bases, and thus may have a weaker fertility-stimulating effect among socio-economically vulnerable groups [34]. In contrast, direct cash transfers and child allowances can provide immediate and visible financial relief to families, particularly for low- and middle-income households, and have been shown in international experience to be more effective in reducing short-term childrearing costs and improving fertility intentions [35]. Nevertheless, cash subsidies require stable fiscal inputs, clear eligibility criteria, and accurate targeting mechanisms, which impose higher administrative and budgetary demands on governments [35]. Therefore, future policy optimization should adopt a differentiated strategy: tax-based incentives can be used as universal and easily scalable tools, while direct cash benefits can be targeted at families with multiple children, young mothers, or economically disadvantaged groups, so as to improve both equity and efficiency of demand-side support [34, 36].

At the same time, it is necessary to optimize the maternity insurance system, increase the coverage of maternity subsidies, and reduce the costs of childcare and education to provide economic support to families [37, 38]. Simultaneously, policies such as maternity leave, paternity leave, and child-rearing leave should be refined and implemented. Flexible working hours and remote working models should be promoted to reduce motherhood penalties and alleviate conflicts between childbirth and employment [39]. Additionally, communities, enterprises, and social organizations should be encouraged to jointly provide family support services, forming a support network with multiple participants.

Third, improve supporting mechanisms at the grassroots governance and community practice levels, and strengthen cross-departmental collaborative governance [40]. Fertility-friendly policies involve multiple departments, including health, finance, education, and social affairs. A single department cannot support systemic goals [41]. Therefore, cross-departmental collaboration should be established, integrating multiple resources to promote coordinated policy publicity, resource linkage, and service provision.

Improvement of policy goal layouts

To improve the current layout of fertility-friendly policy goals, systematic optimization is needed at the levels of policy concepts, goal positioning, and institutional mechanisms. First, policy goals should be transformed from “service-oriented” to “comprehensive and coordinated”. While strengthening the supply of public services such as childcare, medical care, and education, more attention should be given to weaker areas, such as emotional support and social relationship networks [37, 42]. Future policy design should emphasize interpersonal-friendly contents, including family internal support, neighborhood mutual assistance, and social organization participation. By strengthening community support systems and promoting collaborative participation in public parenting services, shortcomings in interpersonal-friendly goals can be addressed [43].

Second, policy goals should be repositioned based on the actual needs of the reproductive-age population, shifting from supply to demand. Some current policies still rely on administrative supply logic, making it difficult to address the economic burden, time constraints, psychological support, and work-life conflicts faced by reproductive-age individuals [44, 45]. Policy formulation should include extensive population research to reflect differences in reproductive cycles, life stages, and generational needs, improving adaptability and precision [46].

Finally, institutionalization of the policy goal system should be strengthened. A cross-departmental goal negotiation mechanism should be established, along with an improved policy goal classification system and an assessment framework for goal achievement [47]. This approach clarifies hierarchical logic, functional relationships, and the relative weight of different goals, enhancing the overall balance and implementation consistency of policies [48].

Enhancing the alignment between policy tools and goals

Research indicates that the degree of fit between tool configuration and target positioning largely determines policy implementation effectiveness and system stability [49]. However, misalignment exists between tool selection and goal orientation in China’s fertility-friendly policies.

It is noteworthy that service-friendly objectives rely heavily on environmental tools, which represents an “unexpected” tool–goal matching pattern. This phenomenon may be explained by China’s policy implementation logic, in which regulatory guidance, policy advocacy, and normative frameworks are often used as preliminary instruments to shape institutional behavior before large-scale service expansion is realized [50, 51]. In this governance model, environmental tools are not merely symbolic or ideological instruments, but function as preliminary institutional arrangements that shape organizational behavior, policy priorities, and intergovernmental coordination before tangible service delivery occurs. More specifically, environmental tools such as national development plans, policy guidelines, regulatory standards, and political advocacy campaigns serve interrelated functions. First, they legitimize service expansion by formally incorporating service-friendly objectives into national policy agendas, thereby signaling political priority and reducing implementation uncertainty for local governments. Second, they standardize service provision by defining service scopes, quality benchmarks, and institutional responsibilities, which is essential in a territorially large and administratively fragmented system. Third, they activate administrative mobilization mechanisms, including performance evaluation, vertical accountability, and cross-departmental coordination, through which local governments are incentivized to translate abstract policy goals into concrete service investments [52]. Moreover, environmental tools in China often operate as soft steering mechanisms that generate hard service outcomes. Rather than directly financing or delivering services at the central level, the central government relies on regulatory steering and administrative coordination to embed service-friendly goals into existing public service systems, such as healthcare, education, and childcare. Only after these normative and institutional conditions are established can fiscal resources be mobilized efficiently and service capacity expanded in a coordinated manner [53]. This explains why environmental tools frequently appear as dominant instruments even when the stated policy objectives concern service provision. Therefore, the high dependence of service-friendly goals on environmental tools should not be interpreted as a definitional overlap or policy inconsistency; rather, it reflects a governance strategy that emphasizes institution-building, indirect control, and intergovernmental alignment as foundations for service system construction [54]. In the context of fertility policy—where service provision often requires cross-sectoral collaboration, long-term investment, and adaptation to local demographic conditions—such an approach allows the central government to guide outcomes while preserving local flexibility in implementation.

From a policy optimization perspective, a systematic policy tool–goal fitting mechanism should be established. First, policy design should be goal-oriented to avoid overreliance on a single tool [55]. For example, under service-friendly goals, supply-type tools such as public services and infrastructure can be combined with environmental tools, such as social atmosphere measures, to promote new marriage and childbearing cultures, family values, and a fertility-friendly social environment [56]. Scientific configuration of various policy tools ensures their precise connection with corresponding goals [57]. Second, when designing policy goals, comprehensive consideration should be given to existing practices and available resources, and goals should be hierarchically structured. Relevant departments should guide local governments, while grassroots and community levels can establish feedback mechanisms based on existing service networks to identify issues and provide evidence for policy adjustment [58]. Cross-departmental coordination mechanisms should also be strengthened. Important policies should be thoroughly studied and effectively implemented to ensure that population policies are aligned with economic, social, resource, and environmental policies [59, 60]. Coordination institutions involving government leaders should be established at all levels. Local governments, as key hubs, can collaborate with various resources to provide structural guarantees for the continuous improvement of family support systems.

Limitations

This study systematically identifies the structural characteristics and evolutionary trajectories of China’s central-level fertility-friendly policies. However, several limitations should be acknowledged.

First, due to the long time span and the large volume of policy texts, this study only included central-level policy documents and did not examine provincial, municipal, or county-level policies. In China’s administrative system, central policies mainly provide macro-level guidance, while many concrete demand-side measures—such as cash subsidies, housing support, and childcare allowances—are often formulated and implemented by local governments. Therefore, the relatively low proportion of demand-oriented tools identified in this study may partly reflect the division of administrative responsibilities rather than the actual intensity of fertility support at the grassroots level, which limits the generalizability of the findings to local policy practice. Future studies should incorporate multi-level policy samples to conduct cross-regional and cross-level comparisons, which would help capture policy diversity and local institutional innovation.

Second, all policy documents were treated as equal analytical units regardless of their administrative rank and legal binding force. In practice, laws and State Council decisions generally have stronger and more sustained effects than ministerial notices or implementation guidelines. Treating all documents equally may bias the estimation of policy intensity and structural balance. Future research may introduce a weighting system based on policy hierarchy to improve measurement precision.

Third, although this study divided the policy timeline into two stages, the quantitative coding mainly reflects aggregated structural features, and the dynamic mechanisms underlying policy tool adjustments over time were only partially explored. More fine-grained longitudinal analyses could further reveal how fertility-support strategies evolve in response to demographic and socioeconomic changes.

Despite these limitations, this study provides a systematic overview of the policy tool–goal structure of China’s fertility-friendly policies at the national level and offers a useful analytical foundation for subsequent multi-level and comparative research.

Conclusion

This study, based on the two-dimensional analysis framework of “policy tools–policy objectives”, systematically examined the structure and characteristics of central-level fertility-friendly policies in China. The results show that the policy system has progressed from an initial exploratory stage to a stage of systematization. The number of policies has significantly increased, coverage areas have continuously expanded, and a relatively complete institutional framework has been established. However, the structure of policy tools remains unbalanced, and there are significant disparities among policy goals. Moreover, a clear alignment between tools and goals has not yet been achieved. These issues collectively affect the accuracy, effectiveness, and sustainability of fertility-friendly policies.

Future fertility-promoting policies should focus on optimizing the structure of policy tools, improving the layout of policy objectives, and enhancing the alignment between tools and goals. As national policies continue to evolve, the key focus for implementation is gradually shifting from macro systems to grassroots and community levels. Promoting the implementation of fertility-friendly policies at the grassroots level is essential for enhancing policy effectiveness, improving the fertility experience of the reproductive-age population, and building a long-term support system. Establishing a three-dimensional fertility support system encompassing government, society, community, and family will provide a solid foundation for increasing fertility intentions, improving the fertility environment, and supporting long-term balanced population development.

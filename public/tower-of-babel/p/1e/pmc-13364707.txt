Oral Health-Related Quality of Life and Depression in Patients with Schizophrenia: The Mediating Roles of Oral Health Literacy and Oral Health-Related Self-Efficacy in a Cross-Sectional Structural Equation Analysis
Authors: Xucong He, Xingxiao Huang, Jianzhong Cai, Huarong Zhou, Zhaodi Wang, Zhijun Lin, Cuiqiong He, Liting Liu, Zhibin Xie, Xiulan Liu, Yanheng Wei, Junfang Zeng, Junrong Ye, Aixiang Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe factors affecting depression in schizophrenia are complex and diverse and have been well reported in the literature. However, few studies have investigated the relationship and potential mediating pathways between oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL), internal protective factors of oral health such as oral health literacy (OHL) and oral health-related self-efficacy (OH-SE) and depression in patients with schizophrenia. This study adopts structural equation model (SEM) to explore the hypothesized model of the interrelationship among OHRQoL, internal protective factors of oral health and depression in patients with schizophrenia.MethodsPatients with schizophrenia (n = 300) were conveniently recruited from a psychiatric hospital in China between September and November 2024, based on their availability and willingness to participate. Four validated self-report scales (Oral Health Impact Profile Scale (OHIP-14), Short-Form Health Literacy Dental Scale (HeLD-14), Self-Efficacy Scale for Self-Care (SESS), Self-Rating Depression Scale (SDS)) were used to collect data. We adopted SEM, mediation analyses and path analyses to explore the relationships among OHRQoL, OHL, OH-SE, and Depression.ResultsThe hypothesized model demonstrated acceptable model fit parameters: χ2/df = 1.721 (P< 0.001), root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA) = 0.049 (90% CI, 0.035–0.062), comparative fit index (CFI) = 0.975, goodness of fit index (GFI) = 0.939, adjusted goodness of fit index (AGFI) = 0.914. OH-SE and OHL mediated the influence of OHRQoL on depression, including a chain mediating effect where OHRQoL influenced depression through both OH-SE and OHL sequentially. Additionally, OH-SE mediated the relationship between OHRQoL and OHL, while OHL also mediated the effect of OH-SE on depression.ConclusionThis study provides novel evidence for the pathway OHRQoL → OH-SE → OHL → Depression. Clinicians may use this model as theoretical evidence to formulate targeted pathway-based interventions that enhance internal protective factors of oral health, which may improve OHRQoL while alleviating depressive symptoms, although causal inferences cannot be drawn from this cross-sectional study.Keywords: depression, oral health-related quality of life, oral health literacy, oral health-related self-efficacy, schizophrenia, structural equation model

Introduction

Schizophrenia is a chronic and persistent disease with a significant tendency of recurrence and a heavy burden of disease.1 The disease not only seriously affects the quality of life and physical health of patients, but also leads to the increase of medical costs, aggravates the medical burden of patients and the country, causes the waste of medical resources, and brings serious consequences to patients, families and society.2,3 Therefore, World Health Organization (WHO) points out that it is necessary to promote the return and integration of patients with mental disorders into society, and reduce the total burden of patients with mental disorders, their families, and society.4 A meta-analysis estimated the prevalence of comorbid depression in schizophrenia and its associated factors and found a pooled prevalence of comorbidity of 28.6%.5 Studies have shown that the prevalence of depression in first-episode schizophrenia is 15.1%, and the prevalence of depression in recurrent patients is 47–68%.6 Depression significantly predicts suicidal behavior in patients with schizophrenia, and the presence of depression is associated with a higher risk of suicide.7 Depression is associated with poor outcomes in patients with schizophrenia, including worse functional recovery.8 Depression increases the recurrence or rehospitalization rate in patients with schizophrenia.9 In addition, depression can also reduce the quality of life of patients with schizophrenia.10 Although many studies have focused on depression in patients with schizophrenia, depression remains clinically prevalent. Therefore, depression and its influencing factors in schizophrenia cannot be ignored.

Oral health is an important part of general health.11 Poor oral health often bothers people, and even becomes one of the main factors affecting people’s quality of life.12 Poor oral health may adversely affect quality of life by restricting food choices and nutrient intake, which can lead to malnutrition and, consequently, to frailty and sarcopenia.13 Poor oral health may negatively affect body image, affect social interactions, and reduce overall enjoyment of life, well-being, and quality of life.14 Dibello et al showed that poor oral health is associated with six adverse health-related outcomes in older age, including mortality, physical frailty, functional disability, quality of life, hospitalization, and falls.15 In patients with schizophrenia, several disease-specific factors further compound poor oral health. Antipsychotic medications frequently induce xerostomia, reducing salivary flow and increasing caries risk;16 negative symptoms such as avolition diminish motivation for daily oral self-care;17 and dental stigma may deter patients from seeking professional care.18 Consequently, epidemiological evidence shows that patients with severe mental illness are nearly three times more likely to be edentulous than the general population,19 and Asian studies consistently report higher decayed, missing and filled teeth (DMFT) scores in schizophrenia patients compared with healthy controls.20

Oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) refers to the impact of oral health or disease on an individual’s physical, psychological, and social functioning in daily life.21 In short, it measures how oral problems affect a person’s daily life and overall well-being. OHRQoL can provide comprehensive information for the assessment of oral health status and help to understand the functional, psychological and social impact of oral diseases on people’s lives.22 It is helpful to identify and solve the social communication difficulties and psychological barriers caused by oral diseases such as dental caries and periodontal disease, so as to improve the quality of life of patients.23 It can also help identify depressive symptoms as a result of oral health problems and reduce depression and social isolation by improving oral health.24 Studies have shown that there is a significant correlation between OHRQoL and depression in the elderly,25 and poor OHRQoL can lead to a high level of depression. Poor OHRQoL may also exacerbate psychological stress and depressive symptoms in both adults and adolescents.26,27 OHRQoL in diabetic patients is significantly associated with depression, anxiety and stress symptoms, and poor OHRQoL may lead to an increased risk of mental health problems.28 In the few studies of OHRQoL in patients with mental disorders, the results of multivariate analysis showed that depression was one of the influencing factors of OHRQoL.29 The retrieval found that despite the poor oral health status of patients with schizophrenia, there are few studies on OHRQoL. Moreover, the role of OHRQoL in schizophrenia patients with depression has been poorly studied. Although internal protective factors of oral health such as oral health literacy (OHL) and oral health-related self-efficacy (OH-SE) were found to have beneficial effects on OHRQoL,30,31 there is still a lack of information on whether these protective factors can make OHRQoL effective in alleviating depression.

OHL refers to the ability of individuals to access, process, and understand basic oral health information and services to make appropriate oral health decisions.32 Studies have shown that OHL influences oral health behaviors as an internal protective factor and is essential for improving oral health.33,34 In addition, studies have shown that OHL can reduce the incidence of depression by promoting oral health behaviors and improving oral health status.35,36

OH-SE refers to an individual’s confidence in their ability to perform oral health care behaviors well.31 Recent studies have confirmed the protective effect of higher OH-SE on oral health,37,38 suggesting a bright future for enhancing OH-SE to reduce oral health-related problems. Higher OH-SE is associated with better OHRQoL and has been shown to mediate the relationship between OHL and oral health outcomes.39,40 Studies have shown that depression can reduce the individual’s cognition of self-efficacy and affect their ability and efficiency of health care,41 creating a bidirectional relationship.

The health belief model (HBM) proposes that health behaviors are determined by perceived susceptibility, severity, benefits, barriers, cues to action, and self-efficacy.42 In this framework, OHL enables people to accurately perceive risk,32 while OH-SE reflects people’s confidence in overcoming obstacles,31 and both are internally protective factors driving oral health outcomes.

Historically, OHRQoL, OHL, and OH-SE have been studied in various populations, including the elderly, stroke patients, and nursing students. These studies have established that OHRQoL, OHL and OH-SE are positively correlated with better oral health outcomes and negatively associated with depression. At present, few studies have investigated these variables in patients with schizophrenia, and their relationship with depression is also rarely studied. Specifically, no research has examined whether OHL and OH-SE mediate the relationship between OHRQoL and depression in this population, nor whether a chain mediating effect exists. It is a new research perspective to explore the impact of depression from the oral health as an internal protective factor, rather than focusing on the negative characteristics. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to investigate the mediating role of OHL and OH-SE between OHRQoL and depression in schizophrenia, and to provide theoretical and empirical basis for improving the OHRQoL, promoting the internal protective factors of oral health, and reducing the incidence of depression in schizophrenia.

Hypothesized Model

Considering the available studies, this study is based on the following hypotheses.

H1. OHRQoL positively influences OHL and OH-SE, OHL positively influences OH-SE.

H2. OHRQoL, OHL and OH-SE negatively influences depression.

H3. OHL mediates the relationship between OHRQoL and depression.

H4. OH-SE mediates the relationship between OHRQoL and depression.

H5. OHL mediates the relationship between OH-SE and depression.

H6. OH-SE mediates the relationship between OHRQoL and OHL.

H7. OHL and OH-SE play a chain mediating role in the relationship between OHRQoL and depression.

Method

Study Design and Participants

This study adopted a cross-sectional design, analyzed the data of the subjects in one period, described the association of each variable, and finally constructed a structural equation model (SEM) to present the study results. We assembled a research team consisting of mental health specialists and specialist nurses to guide the study and quality control, and trained the survey team to collect data in a normative manner. From September to November 2024, a cross-sectional survey was conducted in a psychiatric hospital in Guangdong, China using the convenient sampling method. The investigators used a paper questionnaire to conduct a one-to-one survey. The purpose and significance were explained to the objects with standard instructions, and the questionnaire was collected on the spot after the end. The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: (a) diagnosis of schizophrenia according to ICD-11,43 and (b) symptoms were stable for 4 weeks or more based on medical records and clinical judgment, and a stable dose of antipsychotic medication was taken for 4 weeks or more prior to participating in the survey,44 and (c) informed and consented to participate in the study. Exclusion criteria included (a) other psychiatric disorders (such as major depressive disorder, bipolar disorder, or substance addiction), and (b) the presence of serious physical comorbidities (such as heart failure, stroke, or cancer), and (c) not being able to understand the literal meaning of scale items and not being able to respond to them. Participants fully understood the purpose and significance of the study and voluntarily participated in the study. The study distributed a total of 350 questionnaires and recovered 326 questionnaires, with a recovery proportion of 93.14%. Excluding 20 questionnaires with incomplete answers and 6 questionnaires with completely consistent answers on all items, we recovered 300 valid questionnaires in the end, with a valid recovery proportion of 85.71%. According to the formula: n= (U ɑ/2σ/δ) 2, among (α=0.05, U ɑ/2=1.96, σ=12.55 which is from the study of Wang et al,6 δ=2), then n = (U ɑ/2σ/δ) 2 = 152. Considering that 20% of the questionnaires are invalid, the estimated sample size is 190, and the actual sample size included in this study is 300, which satisfies the sample size criterion for the SEM analysis. The study protocol was registered on the Open Science Framework (OSF) at [https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/6E8HX]. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. This study is in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of the Third People’s Hospital of Meizhou City, Guangdong, China (No. LL20240902001).

Measures

General Information

We collected socio-demographic and clinical data from participants, including gender, age, education, marital and employment status, family income, health insurance coverage, history of hospitalizations, and complications.

Oral Health-Related Quality of Life

We used oral health impact profile scale (OHIP-14) to measure OHRQoL in patients with schizophrenia. Slade and Spencer developed a 49-item oral health impact profile scale in 1994,45 which was simplified by Slade into 14 items called OHIP-14 in 1997.46 The scale has been translated into many languages and has become one of the most widely used scales in the field of OHRQoL. Xin and Ling Sinicized OHIP-14 and used the Chinese version of OHIP-14 to investigate the reliability and validity of the scale in Guangdong Province, China.47 The overall Cronbach’s alpha for this scale was 0.930. This scale contains 14 items in four dimensions, involving physical dysfunction, pain and discomfort, decreased independence, and psychological discomfort (three, three, five, and three items, respectively). It adopts the Likert five-point rating method, which ranges from 0 (never) to 4 (very often). Higher scale scores indicate worse oral health status and worse OHRQoL. OHIP-14 has been used to study the OHRQoL of Chinese patients with schizophrenia and Brazilian patients with mental disorders.29,48 The overall Cronbach’s alpha for this scale in this study was 0.944.

Oral Health Literacy

We used short-form health literacy dental scale (HeLD-14) to measure OHL in patients with schizophrenia. HeLD-14 was developed by Jones et al49 on the basis of the 29 items HeLD scale (HeLD-29).50 The scale was sinicized by Yan et al,51 and its reliability and validity were confirmed through rigorous research, yielding an overall Cronbach’s alpha of 0.908. This scale contains 14 items in seven dimensions, involving receptivity, understanding, support, economic barriers, access, communication, utilization, with 2 items in each dimension. It employs the Likert five-point rating method, with ranges from 0 (unable to do) to 4 (no difficulty). Higher scores indicate better OHL. The scale is used to understand the OHL level of the Chinese population, and has been applied to patients with mental disorders.52 The overall Cronbach’s alpha for this scale in this study was 0.913.

Oral Health-Related Self-Efficacy

We used self-efficacy scale for self-care (SESS) to measure OH-SE in patients with schizophrenia. The scale was developed by Kakudate et al,53 and was sinicized and revised by Wu in 2016.54 The overall Cronbach’s alpha of the Chinese version of SESS was 0.879, which had good reliability and validity and could be used to evaluate OH-SE. This scale contains 15 items in three dimensions, involving self-efficacy for dentist consultation (SE-DC), self-efficacy for brushing of the teeth (SE-B), self-efficacy for dietary habits (SE-DH), with 5 items in each dimension. Using Likert five-point rating method, a score of 1 means “not at all confident” and a score of 5 means “very confident”. The total score ranges from 15 to 75, and the higher the score, the higher the OH-SE. The overall Cronbach’s alpha for this scale in this study was 0.929.

Depression

We used the Self-rating Depression Scale (SDS) to evaluate the level of depression. SDS was developed by Zung and is one of the most widely used self-rating instruments for depression.55 This scale contains 20 items, involving mood, interest, sleep, appetite, energy and other aspects. Using Likert four-point rating method, a score of 1 means “none” and a score of 4 means “always”. Ten items are scored in reverse, including items 2, 5, 6, 11, 12, 14, 16, 17, 18, and 20, with a score of 4 for “none” and a score of 1 for “always. The standardized total score was obtained by summing the scores of all items and multiplying them by 1.25 to take their integer parts. A standardized total score of less than 50 is considered normal, a score of 50 to 59 indicates mild depression, a score of 60 to 69 indicates moderate depression, and a score of 70 or higher indicates severe depression.6 The overall Cronbach’s alpha for this scale in this study was 0.850.

Data Analysis

We used SPSS 21.0 and AMOS 24.0 for data analysis, and a p- value below 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Descriptive statistics included frequency and percentages and mean and standard deviation. Correlation analyses, using Pearson’s correlation analysis, were initially conducted to determine the associations between OHRQoL, OHL, OH-SE and depression. Multiple stepwise regression was used to analyze the degree to which the variables explained the variance in depression. SEM using maximum likelihood estimation was constructed to test the model illustrating the interrelationships between and among OHRQoL, OHL, OH-SE and depression. Parameters to assess the fit of hypothesized and emerging models were included: χ2/df ≤ 3.00 (χ2, Chi- Square Fit Statistics; df, degrees of freedom), Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA)≤0.08, Comparative- Fit- Index (CFI)≥0.90, Goodness- of- Fit Index (GFI)≥0.90, Adjusted Goodness of Fit Index (AGFI)≥0.90. Mediation and path analyses were also performed to determine the mediating effects of OHL and OH-SE. The bias-corrected nonparametric percentile bootstrap method was used to validate mediating effects between variables. The 95% confidence intervals were constructed to assess the validity of the test level.

Structural Equation Model

The hypothesized model demonstrated acceptable fit to the data (see Figure 2). The model fit parameters were as follows: χ2/df = 1.721 (P< 0.001), RMSEA = 0.049 (90% CI, 0.035–0.062), CFI = 0.975, GFI = 0.939, AGFI = 0.914. OHIP-14 had direct negative effects on HeLD-14 (β = −0.31, p < 0.001) and SESS (β = −0.16, p = 0.013), and a direct positive effect on depression (β = 0.27, p < 0.001). SESS had a direct positive effect on HeLD-14 (β = 0.56, p < 0.001) and a direct negative effect on depression (β = −0.16, p = 0.029). HeLD-14 had a direct negative effect on depression (β = −0.23, p = 0.004).

Discussion

This study investigated the mediating roles of internal protective factors of oral health (such as OHL, OH-SE) between OHRQoL and depression in patients with schizophrenia. Our study found five significant findings. First, OHRQoL positively impacted internal protective factors of oral health, while the internal protective factors of oral health negatively influenced depression. Second, OHL and OH-SE mediated the influence of OHRQoL on depression. Third, OH-SE and OHL had a chain mediating effect on the impact of OHRQoL on depression. Fourth, OHL mediated the influence of OH-SE on depression. Finally, OH-SE had a mediating role between OHRQoL and OHL.

This study found that OHRQoL had a positive effect on internal protective factors of oral health. Therefore, confirming hypothesis 1. This result suggests that better OHRQoL is related to stronger internal protective factors of oral health. This implies that in clinical settings, services aimed at promoting these protective factors could benefit the OHRQoL of patients with schizophrenia. The OHIP-14 score of patients with schizophrenia in our study was 15.57 (SD = 12.62), which was significantly higher than the data obtained by Silva et al in a general population survey,56 indicating that patients with schizophrenia had lower OHRQoL than the general population. This finding is similar to the conclusion of the study by Zheng et al48 Kuipers et al also found that patients with psychosis had a 9.45 times higher likelihood of low OHRQoL than the general population (odds ratio = 9.45).57 This phenomenon may be related to the poorer oral health status, lower oral health awareness, and oral side effects of antipsychotic drugs in patients with schizophrenia.58 The decreased independence dimension scored 5.33 (SD = 5.03), which was the highest in OHIP-14, indicating that oral problems affected their daily life. When patients with schizophrenia lack awareness of the importance of oral health care, they may be less motivated for oral health care, have poor compliance, and lack confidence to perform relevant oral health behaviors.59 If patients with schizophrenia lack internal protective factors of oral health, their oral condition may further deteriorate, thereby significantly reducing their OHRQoL.

In contrast, our results also demonstrated a significant negative association between the internal protective factors of oral health and depression, which supported hypothesis 2. This aligns with existing literature reporting a negative correlation between health literacy and depression and the role of self-efficacy in alleviating depressive symptoms in schizophrenia.6,60 Overall, these findings provide consistent empirical support for the idea that there is a negative association between internal protective factors of oral health and depression. However, it is critical to acknowledge the non-directional nature of these associations derived from cross-sectional data. There is a bidirectional relationship between oral health and depression.61 One explanation supported by prior researches is that improving factors such as oral health literacy and self-efficacy improves health behaviors and may reduce depression risk.36,59 However, as noted in the literature on depression and oral health, depressive symptoms per se can reduce motivation and self-perception, which can negatively affect an individual’s self-efficacy and health literacy.62,63 Therefore, our findings highlight a crucial reciprocal relationship between the internal protective factors of oral health and depression in schizophrenia, rather than a simple unidirectional effect. Future longitudinal or interventional research is needed to disentangle the directionality of these links. In addition, the self-efficacy concepts measured in this study were domain specific.53 Individuals may exhibit low self-efficacy in managing oral health while maintaining high self-efficacy in other life domains, such as social interactions. Therefore, our findings regarding OH-SE should not be generalized to a deficit in overall self-efficacy. Future interventions should target this specific self-efficacy enhancement rather than assuming a broad deficit in personal efficacy.

This study indicated that OHL was a mediating variable between OHRQoL and depression among patients with schizophrenia, validating hypothesis 3. This result suggests that OHL helps patients with schizophrenia to improve their OHRQoL while indirectly relieving their depression. Schizophrenia patients with a high level of OHRQoL are more likely to make time for and pay attention to oral health, and are more proactive in seeking knowledge about oral health care.64 In addition, the higher oral health literacy is, the more effectively individuals can seek, understand, and use oral health information to make appropriate oral health decisions, thereby reducing the occurrence of oral problems, avoiding its negative impact on social participation, and reducing depressive symptoms.65,66 Conversely, consistent with the bidirectional relationship between oral health and depression noted earlier, depression may also impair OHL and thereby worsen OHRQoL. Depressive symptoms often reduce an individual’s motivation, attention, and ability to process health-related information, making it more difficult to access, understand, and apply oral health knowledge. This diminished OHL may lead to poor oral health behaviors, such as irregular brushing and infrequent dental visits, which in turn increase the risk of oral problems and ultimately compromise OHRQoL.62,63 This indirect pathway suggests that depression not only directly compromises OHRQoL but also exerts an additional negative effect by diminishing OHL.

The results of the study corroborated that OH-SE were a mediating variable between OHRQoL and depression of patients with schizophrenia, confirming hypothesis 4. Patients with high OHRQoL have the confidence to perform oral health care behaviors and they actively cope with oral problems even in difficult times. OH-SE is a subjective self-evaluation that can enhance patients’ willingness to actively learn oral health knowledge and their motivation to engage in oral health behaviors, thereby facilitating external transformation through improved oral health self-management abilities and ultimately improving OHRQoL.67 The higher the patient’s OH-SE level, the greater their self-confidence in maintaining oral health, the more comfortable they feel in life, and the less likely they are to experience depression.68 In addition, patients with high OH-SE are more aware of personal value in the face of negative events of oral problems, and can transform difficulties into experience resources, which will enhance their psychological resilience to stress and avoid depressive symptoms.69

The developed model illustrates that OHL mediates the effect of OH-SE on depression (validating hypothesis 5). The results suggest that OH-SE can directly alleviate depression and indirectly enhance the effect of alleviating depression through OHL. Recent studies support our findings of the mediating role of health literacy between health-related quality of life, self-care management, and depression.70,71 The reason may be that schizophrenia patients with high OHL possess a sufficient reserve of oral health knowledge. They are more likely to proactively utilize oral health services, such as regular dental check-ups and teeth cleaning, to seek oral health information and support.33 As a result, their oral health status improves, which in turn contributes to the alleviation of depressive symptoms.36

This study also found that OH-SE was a mediating variable between OHRQoL and OHL in patients with schizophrenia, confirming hypothesis 6. Individuals with higher OHRQoL tend to have higher levels of OH-SE. They are confident in performing behaviors such as visiting the dentist, brushing their teeth correctly, and maintaining a balanced diet. When they consistently engage in these behaviors, their oral health status is inevitably better. Consequently, the negative impacts caused by oral health problems are significantly reduced. This forms a virtuous cycle. Studies have shown that lower health literacy is associated with lower self-efficacy.72 Patients with low self-efficacy are less confident in maintaining oral health, including the inability to access, process, and understand basic health information and services, and therefore have lower health literacy.

Furthermore, this study found that OHRQoL could affect depression of patients with schizophrenia through the chain-mediating effect of OH-SE and OHL, confirming hypothesis 7. The results of this study show that the higher the OHRQoL level of schizophrenia patients, the stronger the OH-SE level, and they are more inclined to obtain and use oral health information and knowledge to solve oral health problems, which helps to improve individual self-confidence and satisfaction with oral hygiene and effectively relieve depression.38 However, when the OHRQoL level of schizophrenia patients is low, they are often troubled by oral problems, and their OH-SE is not enough to help them cope with the discomfort caused by oral problems such as pain and affecting eating. They are often unable to actively seek help and support from the dentist, but take an attitude of avoidance and giving up, and negative emotions such as depression will be aggravated.52,58

Limitations

However, this study has several limitations. First, this study is a cross-sectional survey, which only obtains the relationship between the variables at a certain time point, and does not explore whether the relationship between the variables will change over time. SEM has inherent limitations, including its inability to establish causality and its susceptibility to collinearity among variables. Second, this study only included hospitalized patients with schizophrenia and did not investigate outpatients, which would compromise the representativeness of the data. Third, this study only investigated a specialized psychiatric hospital in Guangdong Province, China; and the single-center design, located within the specific context of China’s healthcare system, limits the generalizability of our findings to broader populations and settings with different oral healthcare infrastructures and health education resources. In addition, whether different cultural backgrounds will have an impact on the results is also to be verified in the future. Finally, the methods of data collection in this study are all in the form of patient subjective report, which will lead to bias in the results. Future research would benefit from longitudinal designs, multi-center sampling across diverse healthcare systems, and the integration of qualitative interviews with quantitative measures to better elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

Conclusion

This study reveals the sequential mediating roles of OH-SE and OHL between OHRQoL and depression in patients with schizophrenia. These findings provide novel evidence for the pathway OHRQoL → OH-SE → OHL → Depression. Clinicians may consider enhancing these internal protective factors to improve oral health and alleviate depressive symptoms, but causal inferences cannot be drawn from this cross-sectional study and need to be verified by longitudinal studies.

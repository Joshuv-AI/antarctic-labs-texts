MENG-04 POST-EXCISION EVOLUTION OF MENINGIOMAS OF THE SMALL WING OF THE SPHENOID BONE AT CHNU FANN BETWEEN 2022 AND 2024
Authors: Férol Gbenou, Nantenin Doumbia, Daouda Wague, El Hadj Cheikh Sy, Maguette Mbaye, Mbaye Thioub, Alioune Badara Thiam, Momar Codé Ba
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMeningiomas of the lesser wing of the sphenoid bone represent the third most common location of intracranial meningiomas. Their close relationship with the vasculonervous structures makes their surgery complex. Thus, their excision can be fraught with sometimes formidable complications.ObjectiveReport the evolution of a series of 10 patients retained in our department.Methodologywe conducted a retrospective study with a descriptive aim, covering all patients with a meningioma of the small wing of the sphenoid bone in our department between January 1, 2022 and December 31, 2024. Data collection and analysis were carried out with Jamovi software.Results10 files met our inclusion criteria with a female predominance (sex ratio : 9/1). The median age was 43 years, with extremes ranging from 31 to 64 years. All patients presented with headache associated with vomiting (10%), decreased visual acuity (80%) and exophthalmos (30%). Only one patient presented with a motor deficit preoperatively. Brain CT was performed in all patients and only 60% underwent preoperative brain MRI. The excision was type II according to the SIMPSON classification in 50%, type III in 30% and type IV in 20%. Immediate intraoperative and postoperative complications were vascular in 30% and nervous in 10%. The mortality rate was 30%.Conclusionsurgery for meningioma of the small wing of the sphenoid bone remains a delicate surgery given the high risk of complications, especially vascular, that it can cause.

Predictive value of real-time shear wave elastography combined with serum thyroglobulin in thyroid cancer
Authors: Yunyan Fang, Biaohu Liu, Huijun Feng, Jing Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate the diagnostic value of combining real-time shear wave elastography (SWE) with serum thyroglobulin (Tg) in thyroid cancer (TC) and explore its potential for assessing tumor aggressiveness.MethodsThis retrospective study included 124 patients with TC and 62 with benign thyroid lesions (January 2024–August 2025). Malignant cases were classified as invasive (n = 78) or non-invasive (n = 46) based on capsular involvement, vascular invasion, or lymph node metastasis. All patients underwent SWE and Tg testing. SWE parameters—maximum (Emax), mean (Emean), minimum (Emin) Young’s modulus—and Tg levels were compared. ROC analysis assessed diagnostic performance, while Spearman correlation examined relationships between SWE and Tg.ResultsAll malignant cases were papillary thyroid carcinoma. Malignant cases had higher SWE parameters and Tg than benign cases (P < 0.05). Invasive tumors showed higher Emax, Emean, and Tg than non-invasive tumors (P < 0.05). The combined SWE–Tg model AUC for malignancy was 0.844; for aggressiveness, 0.744. Tg correlated positively with SWE parameters (P < 0.001).ConclusionCombining SWE with Tg improved discrimination between TC and benign thyroid nodules in this cohort and may provide auxiliary value for preoperative assessment of invasive features.

Introduction

Thyroid cancer (TC) is one of the most common malignant tumors of the endocrine system, with its incidence showing a progressive increase worldwide in recent years [1–3]. Epidemiological studies indicate that TC occurs more frequently in women than in men, with papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) being the most prevalent histological subtype, accounting for more than 80% of all thyroid malignancies [4, 5]. Although most patients with TC have a favorable prognosis, some cases exhibit local invasion and lymph node metastasis (LNM), leading to an increased risk of recurrence and compromised long-term outcomes. Therefore, accurate preoperative identification of the benign or malignant nature of thyroid nodules, as well as their aggressiveness, is crucial for developing appropriate surgical strategies and avoiding both overtreatment and undertreatment.

Currently, the diagnosis of thyroid nodules primarily relies on high-frequency ultrasonography. Conventional two-dimensional ultrasound clearly depicts features such as nodule size, margin, internal echogenicity, and blood flow distribution, making it the first-line imaging modality for detecting thyroid nodules [6, 7]. However, conventional ultrasound has certain limitations in differentiating benign from malignant lesions, particularly in cases with well-defined borders or atypical echo patterns, where both sensitivity and specificity are suboptimal [8]. Ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA) is regarded as the gold standard for diagnosing TC. Nevertheless, its widespread application is limited due to its invasive nature, operator dependence, and indeterminate results (such as Bethesda category III or IV) [9–11]. Hence, there is growing clinical interest in developing a non-invasive, potentially reproducible, and accurate diagnostic approach that combines imaging and biological markers.

Real-time shear wave elastography (SWE) is an emerging functional ultrasound technique that quantifies tissue stiffness by measuring Young’s modulus (kPa) under external force [12]. Research has shown that malignant thyroid nodules often exhibit increased stiffness due to collagen deposition, higher cellular density, and stromal remodeling [13]. Compared to strain elastography, SWE offers advantages such as quantitative assessment, good reproducibility, and relative ease of operation, making it increasingly useful in the clinical diagnosis of thyroid nodules. Several researchers have reported that SWE parameters, including maximum Young’s modulus (Emax), mean Young’s modulus (Emean), and minimum Young’s modulus (Emin), have certain value in distinguishing benign from malignant nodules. However, the diagnostic performance of any single parameter remains suboptimal [14–16].

On the other hand, the role of serological biomarkers in the diagnosis and management of TC has also attracted considerable attention [17–19]. Thyroglobulin (Tg), a glycoprotein synthesized and secreted by thyroid follicular cells, is a key precursor in thyroid hormone synthesis. Clinically, serum Tg is widely used as a postoperative monitoring indicator for recurrence and metastasis in TC patients [20]. Notably, a growing body of evidence suggests that elevated preoperative serum Tg levels may indicate an increased risk of malignancy and greater aggressiveness of thyroid nodules [21, 22]. This finding highlights the potential value of Tg in the early prediction of TC, although its diagnostic sensitivity and specificity when used alone remain limited.

Given these considerations, the combination of imaging and serological markers has emerged as a promising approach to improve the diagnostic accuracy of TC. Previous studies have demonstrated that SWE and serological biomarkers reflect pathological characteristics of nodules from different perspectives. The former emphasizes tissue stiffness and structural alterations, while the latter reflects secretory activity and tumor burden. Their integration may therefore provide complementary advantages and enhance diagnostic performance. However, there is still limited research and insufficient evidence regarding the combined use of SWE and serum Tg in predicting thyroid malignancy and its aggressiveness.

Therefore, this retrospective study enrolled 124 PTC patients and 62 patients with benign nodules. We systematically compared SWE parameters and serum Tg levels among different groups, evaluated the predictive efficacy of individual and combined indicators using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, and explored their correlations. The aim of this study is to validate the value of combining SWE parameters with serum Tg in predicting TC and its aggressiveness, which may offer supplementary information for preoperative risk assessment and future model development.

Materials and methods

Study population

The sample size was estimated using ROC curve analysis. Based on previous studies [23, 24], the area under the curve (AUC) for SWE and serum Tg in diagnosing thyroid-related diseases was approximately 0.75. Assuming that combined detection could increase the AUC to 0.85, with a significance level (α) of 0.05 and a power (1-β) of 0.80, it was calculated that each group required at least 55 patients with malignant nodules and 27 with benign nodules. To account for potential loss to follow-up and missing data, malignant and benign cases were ultimately enrolled at a ratio of approximately 2: 1, resulting in an actual sample size of 124 in the malignant group and 62 in the benign group, which met the statistical power requirement. A total of 124 patients with TC (malignant group) and 62 patients with benign thyroid lesions (benign group), pathologically diagnosed at our hospital between January 2024 and August 2025, were included. The benign control group consisted of patients with thyroid nodules that were confirmed as benign by postoperative histopathology. These lesions were enrolled during the same study period and from the same institution as the malignant group. The benign lesions mainly included nodular goiter, follicular adenoma, cystic nodules, and inflammatory or hyperplastic lesions. Patients were included consecutively according to the predefined eligibility criteria to reduce selection bias.

Based on the presence or absence of pathological features indicative of capsular, sheath, vascular invasion, or LNM, the malignant group was further subdivided into an invasive subgroup (n = 78) and a non-invasive subgroup (n = 46). The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Diagnosis of TC and its aggressiveness (defined by the presence of invasion or metastasis) was established according to the Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines for Thyroid Cancer (2022 Edition) [25] and confirmed by postoperative pathology; (2) Preoperative SWE and serum Tg measurement were performed at our hospital; (3) No prior history of radiotherapy to the neck region, thyroid surgery (e.g., thyroid lobectomy, total thyroidectomy), or radioactive iodine therapy; (4) Availability of complete clinical, imaging (SWE images and reports), and laboratory data (serum Tg results) without significant missing information; (5) Age between 18 and 80 years; (6) Patient consciousness and compliance were sufficient for the study; (7) Informed consent forms are exempted upon approval by the hospital’s ethics department. Exclusion criteria included: (1) Coexistence of other primary neck tumors (e.g., lymphoma, parathyroid tumor) or previously diagnosed malignancies in other parts of the body (e.g., breast cancer, lung cancer); (2) Severe cardiac, hepatic, or renal dysfunction; (3) Pregnancy or lactation; (4) Presence of systemic immune diseases or acute infections; (5) Long-term use of medications affecting thyroid function; (6) Poor-quality images unsuitable for quantitative analysis; (7) Coagulation disorders (e.g., hemophilia, severe cirrhosis-induced coagulopathy) or other contraindications to surgery or FNA. No statistically significant differences were observed in gender, age, history of radiation exposure, iodine exposure, history of thyroid disease, or family history of thyroid disease between the malignant and benign groups, or between the invasive and non-invasive subgroups (all P > 0.05), as detailed in Tables 1 and 2.

SWE examination

All examinations were performed with the patient in the supine position and the neck fully exposed. The examination was carried out using the Mindrary resona real-time shear wave elastography ultrasound diagnostic instrument and the L15-3 linear array probe. The examination began with a conventional B-mode ultrasound scan to document the characteristics of the target lesion. The operator subsequently switched to the SWE mode. The color scale for Young’s modulus was set to a range of 0-100 kPa. The sampling box was positioned to encompass the target lesion along with a portion of the surrounding normal thyroid tissue. During image acquisition, patients were instructed to briefly hold their breath to minimize motion artifacts. Images were frozen once stable elastographic maps were obtained, with each acquisition lasting approximately 10 s. Using the built-in Q-box™ quantitative analysis software, Young’s modulus values were measured within the region of interest (ROI). This measurement was repeated five times for each lesion, and the Emax, Emean, and Emin values of Young’s modulus were recorded. These elastic parameters were subsequently compared among the different study groups.

To minimize variability, all SWE examinations were performed according to a standardized acquisition protocol by experienced operators. The operators were blinded to the postoperative pathological results at the time of image acquisition and measurement. For each nodule, the region of interest (ROI) was placed within the solid component of the lesion while avoiding obvious calcification, cystic degeneration, and peripheral artifacts as much as possible. Measurements were obtained after stabilization of the image, and each lesion was measured five times under the same acquisition conditions. The mean value of the repeated measurements was used for final analysis.

Measurement of serum Tg levels

Peripheral venous blood samples (5 mL) were collected from the cubital vein of all participants under fasting conditions in the morning. The blood was drawn into vacuum serum tubes without anticoagulant and allowed to clot for 30 min. Subsequently, the samples were centrifuged at 4 °C and 3,000 rpm for 10 min to separate the serum. The supernatant serum was aliquoted and stored at -80 °C until analysis. The serum Tg level was detected by using the Siemens Atellica biochemical immunoassay system and the corresponding kit. The normal value range was 0.973–29.56 ng/mL. The assay principle is based on ECLIA technology and was strictly performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol. In brief, the tested serum samples, calibrators, and quality control materials were incubated with magnetic microparticles coated with anti-Tg antibodies and ruthenium (Ru)-labeled anti-Tg antibodies to form a sandwich immune complex. The complex was then captured onto the surface of an electrode via magnetic attraction. Following the application of an electrical voltage, an electrochemiluminescent reaction was induced, and the emitted light intensity was measured, which is directly proportional to the concentration of Tg in the sample. To ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results, each assay batch included two levels (high and low) of internal quality control materials. Finally, the measured serum Tg levels were statistically compared among the different study groups.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 27.0). A significance level of α = 0.05 was established, with a P-value of less than 0.05 considered statistically significant. The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Data with a non-normal distribution were presented as median with interquartile range [M (P25, P75)], and comparisons between groups were conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test. Normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\overline x$$\end{document}± s) and were further evaluated for homogeneity of variance using Levene’s test. For comparisons between two independent groups meeting the assumption of homogeneity, the independent samples t-test was applied; otherwise, the Welch t-test was employed. Intra-group comparisons were performed using the paired samples t-test where appropriate. Categorical data were summarized as numbers and percentages [n (%)] and compared using the Chi-square test (χ2) or Fisher’s exact test, as applicable. The predictive performance of individual and combined indicators, incorporating SWE parameters and serum Tg levels, for discriminating thyroid malignancy and assessing its aggressiveness was evaluated using ROC curve analysis. To construct the combined diagnostic model, binary logistic regression analysis was performed with thyroid malignancy (malignant vs. benign) as the dependent variable and SWE parameters (Emax, Emean, Emin) together with serum Tg as independent variables. The predicted probabilities generated from the regression model were then used to construct the ROC curve of the combined model. Similarly, for aggressiveness analysis, invasive status (invasive vs. non-invasive) was used as the dependent variable, and the relevant SWE parameters and serum Tg were entered as predictors. The Delong test was used to compare the areas under the ROC curves (AUCs). The Youden’s index (calculated as sensitivity + specificity − 1) was utilized to determine the optimal cutoff values. Furthermore, potential correlations between SWE parameters and serum Tg levels were examined using Spearman’s rank correlation analysis.

Results

Postoperative pathological findings in malignant group

Postoperative pathological examination confirmed that all 124 patients in the malignant group had PTC. Among these, 78 cases (62.90%) were classified into the invasive subgroup, while the remaining 46 cases (37.10%) constituted the non-invasive subgroup. The distribution of tumor foci was as follows: a single focus was identified in 76 patients (61.29%), two foci in 34 patients (27.42%), three foci in 11 patients (8.87%), four foci in 2 patients (1.61%), and six foci in 1 patient (0.81%). The maximum tumor diameter ranged from 0.3 to 4.0 cm. Pathological characteristics: Capsular infiltration was observed in 0 cases (0.00%), capsular invasion in 35 cases (28.23%), vascular invasion in 0 cases (0.00%), lymph node metastasis in 66 cases (53.22%), and no specific pathological findings in 23 cases (18.55%). Pathological Tumor-Node-Metastasis (pTNM) staging revealed the following distribution: pT1N0Mx in 51 cases (41.13%), pT1N1Mx in 65 cases (52.42%), pT2N0Mx in 1 case (0.81%), pT2N1Mx in 5 cases (4.03%), and pT3N1Mx in 2 cases (1.61%).

Correlation between SWE parameters and serum Tg levels

Spearman correlation analysis revealed statistically significant positive correlations between serum Tg levels and all SWE parameters: Emax (r = 0.399, P < 0.001), Emean (r = 0.416, P < 0.001), and Emin (r = 0.376, P < 0.001). These correlations are visually represented in the scatter plots shown in Fig. 1.

Predictive performance of combined SWE parameters and serum Tg for TC

Logistic regression was used to establish a joint model, and ROC analysis was conducted based on the predicted probability of the model. ROC curve analysis and Delong tests demonstrated that the combined model incorporating SWE parameters and serum Tg achieved an AUC of 0.844 (95% CI: 0.789–0.898) for predicting thyroid malignancy. This predictive performance was significantly superior to that of any individual parameter: Emax (AUC = 0.791), Emean (AUC = 0.785), Emin (AUC = 0.726), or serum Tg alone (AUC = 0.758) (Z = 2.679, 2.973, 3.880, 3.336 respectively; P < 0.05 for all comparisons). Discussion

TC represents one of the most rapidly increasing malignancies globally, with PTC being the most prevalent histological subtype [26]. Although the majority of patients experience favorable outcomes, a subset of cases exhibit aggressive features such as local invasion and LNM, which are associated with an elevated risk of recurrence and distant spread [27, 28]. Therefore, the accurate preoperative discrimination of benign and malignant nodules, along with the assessment of tumor aggressiveness, is critically important for formulating individualized surgical plans, avoiding unnecessary overtreatment, and improving long-term survival. While conventional ultrasonography remains the primary imaging modality for thyroid nodule evaluation, its sensitivity and specificity are suboptimal. Although FNA biopsy is considered the important preoperative diagnostic tool, it is invasive and may yield indeterminate results [29]. Thus, the development of a more reliable and non-invasive diagnostic approach is urgently needed.

Notably, the findings of Spearman correlation analysis revealed significant positive correlations between serum Tg levels and all SWE parameters (Emax, Emean, Emin). This suggests a potential intrinsic relationship between tumor secretory activity and tissue stiffness. Possible underlying mechanisms include increased Tg secretion due to proliferating tumor cells, coupled with stromal collagen deposition and architectural remodeling leading to elevated stiffness [30]. This correlation provides a biological rationale supporting the combined use of these metrics. In addition, our study demonstrated that TC patients exhibited significantly higher SWE parameters, including Emax, Emean, and Emin, as well as elevated serum Tg levels compared to patients with benign nodules. This indicates substantial differences at both histological and molecular levels in malignant lesions. Further subgroup analysis revealed that patients with aggressive TC had markedly higher Emax, Emean, and Tg values, whereas no significant difference was observed in Emin. This suggests that the upper and average ranges of tissue stiffness are more reflective of malignant potential, while the minimum stiffness value may be influenced by intranodular heterogeneity and thus less representative of overall aggressiveness. These observations are consistent with a previous study and further validate the clinical utility of SWE in the evaluation of TC [31].

ROC curve analysis indicated that the combination of SWE parameters and serum Tg yielded an AUC of 0.844 for discriminating benign and malignant thyroid nodules, which was significantly superior to any individual parameter. This underscores the complementary roles of SWE and serum Tg in diagnostic dimensions, with their integration substantially enhancing both sensitivity and specificity, thereby improving overall diagnostic accuracy. Although the combined model also achieved a higher AUC for predicting aggressiveness compared to single parameters, the difference did not reach statistical significance. This may be attributable to the multifactorial nature of aggressiveness, which involves pathological features such as capsular invasion and LNM, aspects not fully captured by stiffness measurements or secretory function alone [23]. Additionally, the relatively limited sample size in this study may have reduced statistical power. Consistent with prior literature, SWE as a quantitative stiffness assessment tool has demonstrated high diagnostic value in various cancers including breast, liver, and prostate malignancies [32]. In TC, multiple studies have identified Emax as a sensitive parameter for differentiating benign and malignant nodules, while Emean reflects overall tissue stiffness [33, 34]. The present study expands upon these findings by demonstrating that the combination of SWE and serum biomarkers significantly enhances diagnostic performance. This not only provides empirical support for integrating imaging and serological markers but also may provide a potentially useful combined assessment approach.

However, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, as a single-center retrospective analysis, the sample size was limited, particularly in subgroup analyses of aggressiveness, which may affect the robustness of the conclusions. Second, only serum Tg was included as the serological marker, whereas conventional ultrasound features and other potentially relevant clinical or serological variables were not incorporated into the combined model. In addition, potential confounding factors such as Tg antibodies and thyroid peroxidase antibodies were not assessed. These factors may have influenced the results to some extent and suggest that the potential added value of SWE plus Tg in relation to existing diagnostic approaches warrants further exploration. Third, a standardized SWE acquisition protocol was used, operator experience, ROI placement, and nodule characteristics such as size, depth, calcification, and internal heterogeneity may still have influenced the measurements. In addition, all malignant cases in the present study were papillary thyroid carcinoma, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other histological subtypes of thyroid cancer. Finally, due to insufficient follow-up data, the prognostic value of combined SWE and Tg assessment could not be evaluated, an important direction for future research.

In conclusion, this study suggests that SWE parameters and serum Tg are associated with malignant and invasive pathological features of PTC. Their combination significantly improved discrimination between malignant and benign nodules, whereas for aggressiveness assessment, the combined model showed only a modest numerical improvement that requires further validation in larger cohorts. Although the advantage in predicting aggressiveness requires further validation, this non-invasive and potentially reproducible approach holds considerable promise for clinical application. Future studies should focus on: (1) expanding sample sizes and conducting multicenter prospective research to enhance reliability and generalizability; (2) incorporating additional serum biomarkers and developing multiparameter, multimodal predictive models; and (3) extending follow-up duration to evaluate the role of SWE and Tg in monitoring recurrence and long-term prognosis. Through these efforts, we can advance precision diagnosis and individualized treatment in TC, ultimately improving patient care.

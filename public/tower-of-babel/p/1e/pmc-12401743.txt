In this issue, 6 original articles have been published.

1. Weekday-to-Weekend Sleep Differences and Poor Mental Health in Young Adulthood.

Lee YH, et al.

A survey of 14,931 South Korean young adults examined the impact of weekend–weekday sleep differences on mental health. Nearly half reported 1–2 extra weekend sleep hours, while 17% exceeded two hours. Larger differences correlated with higher risks of unhappiness, burnout, depression, and suicidal ideation, particularly among those sleeping < 7 hours on weekdays. The findings suggest that marked weekday–weekend sleep discrepancies may signal poor mental health, warranting further investigation into underlying mechanisms and interventions to address insufficient weekday sleep.

2. Relationship Between Aortic Valve and Mitral Annular Calcification With Coronary Artery Calcification in Asymptomatic Individuals: Cross-Sectional and Longitudinal Analyses.

Jung HO, et al.

In 722 asymptomatic individuals undergoing cardiac CT, the study assessed relationships between aortic valve calcification (AVC), mitral annular calcification (MAC), and coronary artery calcification (CAC). CAC severity correlated strongly with prevalent AVC and MAC, independent of risk factors. Progression of MAC aligned closely with CAC, but AVC changes did not correlate with MAC or CAC progression. Results support shared atherosclerotic origins yet suggest additional hemodynamic influences on AVC development, indicating the need for further research into distinct pathophysiological pathways of cardiac calcification.

3. Development of a Prediction Model for Acute Exacerbation in Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis: A Study of the Korea IPF Cohort Registry.

Song JW, et al.

Using the Korea IPF Cohort registry, 678 patients were analyzed to develop an acute exacerbation (AE) prediction model. Over a median 40.7 months, AE occurred in 24.3%. Independent predictors included lower FVC, reduced six-minute walk distance (6MWD), and home oxygen use. A model incorporating these variables showed good predictive ability (AUC 0.746 for score, 0.696 for stage). The study suggests these measures can identify high-risk IPF patients, enabling early intervention and monitoring strategies to potentially improve prognosis in this severe respiratory condition.

4. Predicting the Need for Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation in Preterm Infants in the Delivery Room Using Machine Learning Models: Analysis of a Korean Neonatal Network Database.

Kim HH, et al.

Data from 9,684 very-low-birth-weight infants in the Korean Neonatal Network were used to develop models predicting resuscitation needs. The eXtreme Gradient Boosting algorithm performed best (AUC 0.91), especially for predicting “endotracheal intubation or higher.” Gestational age and birth weight were the most influential predictors. External validation with 71 infants confirmed model accuracy. Implementing such models as decision-support tools in delivery rooms could improve preparedness, optimize staffing, and enhance neonatal resuscitation success rates in preterm populations.

5. Longitudinal Analysis of Growth and Neurodevelopmental Outcomes in Very Low Birth Weight Infants With Congenital Anomalies Over Three Years.

Chung SH, et al.

In a prospective cohort of very-low-birth-weight infants (VLBWIs), those with congenital anomalies (n = 172) had persistently poorer growth and higher neurodevelopmental delays than matched controls without anomalies. Motor delays were notably prevalent, with multiple anomalies posing the greatest risks. Findings, assessed over three years using WHO growth standards and developmental testing, highlight the need for early individualized interventions, structured follow-up, and multidisciplinary care to improve outcomes in this vulnerable group.

6. Profiling Cytokines According to the Presence or Absence of Small-for-Gestational Age Using Amniotic Fluid Retrieved During Cesarean Section From Pregnant Women With Early-Onset Preeclampsia.

Park J, et al.

This study analyzed amniotic fluid from 73 women with early-onset preeclampsia, comparing those with small-for-gestational-age (SGA) versus appropriate-for-gestational-age (AGA) infants. Sixteen differentially expressed proteins, including six hub genes (CCL20, CSF3, EPO, VEGFA, IL17C, GZMB), were upregulated in the SGA group and linked to cytokine–receptor interactions. Findings suggest these biomarkers may illuminate mechanisms underlying early-onset preeclampsia with SGA, offering potential targets for diagnosis or therapeutic intervention, though further validation is needed.

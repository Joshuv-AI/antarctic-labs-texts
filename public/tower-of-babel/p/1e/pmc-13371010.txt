Genome assembly of a euryhaline fish (Mystus gulio) from the Bay of Bengal: a valuable resource for functional genomics and conservation

Objective

Mystus gulio, an estuarine catfish, locally known as Nuna tengra, is usually found in low-salinity backwaters habitats across the Indo-Pacific region [1]. It is exceptionally popular food fish for aquaculture in Southeast Asia as it possesses significant nutritional values containing a large number of micronutrients, protein, vitamins, and minerals [1]. As a diminutive indigenous fish species, it significantly enhances the nutritional status of local populations, particularly in the Sundarbans region of India and Bangladesh. Additionally, whiskers estuarine catfish have been reported as ornamental fish [2] which play a significant role in aquatic ecosystem maintenance by consuming aquatic detritus. However, its population has been declining in recent years due to overfishing and various inadvertent ecological changes (pollution, habitat modification) [3, 4]. Due to its wide range of tolerance in terms of salinity and temperature, this species could be a model organism in climate- change -prone research along the Bay of Bengal coast, which is one of the climate change prone areas in Bangladesh. Despite its ecological and economic importance, genomic data on this species is limited, hampering efforts to study its adaptive traits in terms of climate resilience, mariculture, and evolutionary biology.

Here, we report the draft genome sequence of M. gulio, which provides the foundational resource for advancing research in biology, mariculture, and conservation. Therefore, comprehensive characterization of the entire genome is crucial for studying the genetic basis of economically important qualities and understanding its adaptability in a changing climate condition for sustainable livelihood. The WGS data of M. gulio facilitates the ability to change targeted genomic regions for improving fish prolificacy, higher growth rate, disease resistance, and overall survival capability. This complete genome database will enhance future research on evolutionary biology and support the application of advanced genomic tools in the improvement and management of M. gulio populations in the Bay of Bengal.

DNA isolation and library preparation

methodology

NCBI SRA (Accession number: SRR34300724)

(https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/SRR34300724)

A healthy Mystus gulio was selected for sample collection. Total genomic DNA was isolated from the pelvic fin using the phenol-chloroform-isoamylalcohol-based DNA extraction protocol. Purity and concentration of DNA were measured by using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (A260/A280 ratio ~ 1.8–2.0 indicates good purity) and the Qubit® DNA Assay Kit with Qubit® 3.0 Fluorometer (Invitrogen, USA). The DNA integrity was evaluated by visually examining it using 1% agarose gel electrophoresis to evaluate DNA integrity and the presence of high-molecular-weight DNA. Finally, high-quality purified genomic DNA was used for library preparation using the NEBNext® Ultra™ DNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (New England Biolabs, USA), and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq X platform to generate 150-bp paired-end reads. A total of 31.4 Gb (Gigabase pairs) containing 150 bp subreads were produced using NGS technology on an Illumina NovaSeq X paired-end sequencing platform (Table-1). Quality control of raw reads was performed using Trimmomatic v0.32 to remove adapter sequences, contamination, and low-quality bases using the following parameters: ILLUMINACLIP: TruSeq3-PE.fa:2:30:10, LEADING:3, TRAILING:3, SLIDINGWINDOW:4:20, MINLEN:36 [5, 6].

The sequence has been submitted to the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under the accession number SRR34300724 (Table 1) [9]. After quality checking, a total of 104,706,473 clean reads were included for genome assembly. Subsequently, de novo genome assembly was performed by using SPAdes v4.0.0 assembler [7], resulting in the making of a draft genome assembly. Assembly quality was assessed using QUAST, which indicates that the draft genome comprised 281,900 contigs ≥ 500 bp with a total length of 652.7 Mb and GC content of 39.17%. The largest contig was 48,938 bp, with an N50 of 6884 bp and L50 of 57,641. The assembly has been deposited in NCBI GenBank under accession number JBSXMK000000000.1 [10]. This draft assembly provides a foundational resource for further annotation and comparative genomics in catfishes. Future work will include gene prediction and functional analysis.

Limitations

The assembly is highly fragmented, with 281,900 contigs (≥ 500 bp) and an N50 of only 6,884 bp.

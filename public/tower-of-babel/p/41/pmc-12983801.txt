Petit volunteer protocol for loneliness alleviation in community-dwelling older adults in Japan: a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Kouki Tomida, Takahiro Shimoda, Chika Nakajima, Ayuka Kawakami, Hiroyuki Shimada
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOlder adult loneliness is associated with depression, disability and higher mortality rates. As a public health concern, it contributes to increased medical and nursing care costs. Several intervention studies have been conducted to reduce loneliness; however, no universally effective method has been established. We hypothesise that if older adults use their physical abilities to perform petit volunteering, their feelings of loneliness would likely be reduced. This trial aims to investigate the benefits of using petit volunteers for loneliness alleviation (PetitVOLA) application as a sustainable programme to prevent loneliness through the creation of an Ikigai-based volunteer system.Methods and analysisWe intend to conduct a randomised controlled trial with a 3-month intervention period to verify the effectiveness of the PetitVOLA programme (sample size, 126 participants; power, 0.8; significance level, 0.05). The primary outcome is loneliness, which we will measure using the UCLA Loneliness Scale (V.3). Secondary outcomes include social isolation (assessed via multicomponent objective criteria), life satisfaction, physical function (gait speed and grip strength), cognitive function, psychological status and lifestyle, each assessed with validated instruments (eg, Mini-Mental State Examination, Geriatric Depression Scale-15 and the Active Mobility Index). The main analysis uses an intention-to-treat approach, while a full analysis set and a per-protocol set are included for sub-analyses.Ethics and disseminationThe trial was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of the National Center for Geriatrics and Gerontology, Japan (approval No. 1794). The results of this trial will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals, conference presentations and reports to the participating community.Trial registration numberUMIN000056649.

Introduction

Loneliness significantly affects both the mental and physical health of individuals in modern society, making it a pressing public health issue that contributes to increased medical and healthcare costs.13 Loneliness is defined as a subjective emotional state resulting from perceived deficiencies in social relationships and connections.4

Community engagement and volunteer work have consistently shown promise in reducing loneliness. Volunteer participation is associated with lower levels of loneliness, depression, physical decline and mortality.5 6 Moreover, initiating volunteer activities in later life may slow the progression of disability and enhance well-being.7

To address these challenges effectively, our intervention is grounded in two key concepts. First, ‘petit volunteering’ refers to small-term, informal volunteer roles that older adults can easily perform as part of their daily routines; these are low-intensity, flexible tasks that do not require long-term commitments or specialised training. Second, ‘Ikigai’—a Japanese term meaning ‘a reason for being’ or ‘purpose in life’—is foundational to our model. In gerontology, Ikigai is considered a strong predictor of psychological and physical well-being and a key driver of health-promoting behaviours in older Japanese populations.8 9

Building on these concepts, recent systematic reviews highlight the potential of digital and application (app)-based interventions to address loneliness, such as robotic companions and mobile apps. While these technologies are promising, evidence remains mixed owing to barriers such as digital literacy.10 Furthermore, while prior programmes such as the Telehealth Intervention Program for Older Adults have focused on remote emotional support through telephone calls,11 we developed the Petit Volunteers for Loneliness Alleviation (PetitVOLA) programme. PetitVOLA is a hybrid model that uses a digital platform to match participants’ physical capabilities and personal interests with specific requirements of care facilities, while emphasising physical, face-to-face social contribution.

This randomised controlled trial aims to determine whether petit nursing care volunteer activities, tailored to the physical capabilities and life contexts of older adults, can effectively reduce loneliness and enhance meaningful social participation.

Methods and analysis

Ikigai volunteer system

The Ikigai volunteer system serves as the structural framework for connecting volunteers with care facilities in need (figure 1). Through the PetitVOLA smartphone application (hereafter referred to as the ‘smartphone app’), participants can search for and apply for activities that align with their physical strengths and personal interests, which are pre-registered in the smartphone app. Simultaneously, nursing care facilities can post specific volunteer requests through the system’s management interface. This digital matching ensures that volunteer tasks are optimised for each individual’s capability, thereby fostering autonomy and a sense of meaningful social role (Ikigai).

Trial design and setting

This is a single-centre, non-blinded, randomised controlled trial conducted by the National Center for Geriatrics and Gerontology (NCGG). It will take place in Kariya City, Aichi Prefecture (with potential expansion to nearby municipalities if needed and/or extension of the trial period if recruitment takes longer than anticipated) from April 2025 to December 2026. In total, 160 participants will be recruited and randomly assigned to either an intervention or control group. The trial flowchart is shown in figure 2.

Trial procedure

The trial comprises three sequential phases. During an in-person orientation session (PetitVOLA research briefing), individuals will first be screened for basic eligibility criteria, including having no engagement in regular volunteer activities, not being employed full-time, not being certified as requiring long-term care and owning a smartphone. Eligible individuals will then undergo a pre-screening assessment, which will include the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), the UCLA Loneliness Scale (V.3), psychiatric history screening and a walking speed test. Individuals with an MMSE score ≤21, a UCLA score <28, a walking speed <1.0 m/s or a diagnosed psychiatric disorder will be excluded. Individuals passing the pre-screening will then be invited to attend a mandatory training session on the PetitVOLA programme. Those who do not participate in the training will be excluded. Finally, trained participants will be randomly assigned (1:1) to either the PetitVOLA or control group, stratified according to social isolation status. All assessments and procedures are intended to be conducted face-to-face to ensure high data quality.

Participants and recruitment

Potential participants (adults aged ≥65 living in Kariya City) will be recruited through several avenues. We will collaborate with Kariya City authorities to use the municipal resident registry to send physical invitation letters via postal mail (not email). Additionally, public notices including community bulletin boards, local newsletters, city-run social media and posters in public venues will be used to raise awareness.

Trial selection criteria

Inclusion criteria comprise participants: (1) aged ≥65 years; (2) capable of operating a smartphone; (3) able to travel to nursing care homes (on foot, bicycle, vehicle or public transport); (4) willing to attend training and (5) providing written informed consent (see online supplemental materials for the consent form template).

Exclusion criteria (determined at screening) comprise participants: (1) certified for long-term care; (2) with current or past mental illness (eg, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder); (3) an MMSE ≤21; (4) with a walking speed <1.0 m/s; (5) with a UCLA-LS score <28; (6) who have participated in regular volunteering within the past 6 months; (7) who are working >5 days/week or (8) who do not wish to undertake preliminary trial training.

Intervention

Participants in the PetitVOLA group will visit nursing facilities once a week and volunteer for 3 hours on at least 12 occasions over 3 months. Prior to starting, participants will be expected to attend an in-person, basic training course. This training will include technical orientation for the smartphone app, guidelines for volunteering in nursing homes (communication, safety and privacy) and the concept of Ikigai. The volunteering tasks involve face-to-face engagement at long-term care facilities.

The PetitVOLA App automatically matches volunteers with activities based on physical strengths pre-registered in the smartphone app (table 1). Participants earn reward points in the smartphone app for each activity. These points are intended to provide symbolic feedback and positive motivation; they are purely motivational and cannot be exchanged for cash or material goods, ensuring ethical conduct regarding incentives. The Ikigai volunteer system developed for this trial is not currently open to the public, but we intend to make it widely available in future. PetitVOLA participants will be covered by insurance for injuries or property damage incurred during activities.

Control group

Control group participants will not use the PetitVOLA app and will not be matched to volunteer activities during the 3-month trial period. They will continue their usual activities and will not receive any specific intervention from the research team. After the follow-up assessment at 3 months, control group participants will be offered access to the PetitVOLA programme, as an ethical consideration.

Adherence

Participant adherence will be monitored via the PetitVOLA app. If participants do not engage in volunteer activities, they will be contacted by the research promoter via telephone to provide support and verify technical or health-related issues. The control group will undergo a post-test following a 3-month observation period.

Primary outcome

As the primary outcome, loneliness will be measured using the UCLA Loneliness Scale (V.3). This scale consists of 20 items, each rated on a four-point Likert scale (‘not at all’, ‘hardly ever’, ‘sometimes’, ‘often’), with higher total scores indicating greater loneliness.12 A validated Japanese version of the scale has been developed, demonstrating high internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha=0.92)13 14 and construct validity through its association with future long-term care need incidence.13 14

Secondary outcomes

Social isolation

Social isolation will be measured using a 12-item scale developed in Japanese by the NCGG.15 The 12 items are organised into four conceptual domains—social interaction, received support, provided support and social participation—with three questions per domain. While not all items are simple binary (yes/no) questions, each response is categorised based on predefined criteria into either ‘isolated’ or ‘not isolated’ for each domain. A participant is considered socially isolated if they meet the isolation criteria in at least one of the four domains. This approach allows for a multidimensional evaluation that distinguishes between receiving and providing support. The scale was developed and validated in Japanese community settings, and its content validity has been confirmed through expert panel review using the Content Validity Index method.

Life satisfaction

Life satisfaction will be measured using a 13-item scale developed in Japan, covering five domains: health, community, relationships, society and social roles.16 This scale has demonstrated acceptable internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha=0.86) and has been validated for predictive validity, including its association with depressive symptoms that reduce psychological well-being. Higher scores indicate greater life satisfaction.16

Physical function

Walking speed will be measured (in s) using a stopwatch. The participants will walk five times at a comfortable walking speed on a flat, straight surface, and the average walking speed will be calculated. A standard digital handgrip dynamometer (Takei Scientific Instruments, Niigata City, Japan) will be used to measure dominant hand grip strength while each participant stands with their shoulders adducted, with the dominant arm rotated to the mid-position and the elbow fully extended.17 Frailty will be determined based on Fried et al’s proposed frailty phenotype.18 This phenotype is characterised by weight loss, muscle weakness, slow movement, subjective fatigue and a low physical activity level. Frailty will be assessed using the Japanese version of the Cardiovascular Health Study criteria (J-CHS), which is an adaptation of Fried et al’s proposed frailty phenotype.18 The J-CHS criteria have been validated in Japanese older populations and are widely used in geriatric research.19

Cognitive function

Cognitive function will be assessed using the MMSE.20 21 The Japanese version of the MMSE has been validated for use in older adults, demonstrating acceptable construct and criterion validity.22 Its reliability has also been confirmed through test-retest assessments and internal consistency analysis.22

Psychological state

The Geriatric Depression Scale-15 (GDS-15)23 will be used to assess depressive tendencies. When the cut-off score is set at ≥6, the GDS-15 has a sensitivity of 82% and a specificity of 75% for depressive symptoms, assessed using a structured clinical interview.24 When using the GDS-15 to define the presence or absence of depressive symptoms, a score ≥6 will be used to determine the presence of depressive symptoms.23 The Japanese version of the GDS-15 has been validated, and the original validation study reported good internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha=0.83).25

Lifestyle

Life-space mobility and lifestyle will be assessed using the Active Mobility Index (AMI), which incorporates both physical and social activity components.26 The AMI was developed in Japan, and its reliability and validity have been evaluated in a large-scale prospective cohort study.26 Criterion-related validity has been demonstrated through significant associations with depressive symptoms, frailty and cognitive impairment.26 Predictive validity was also supported, with lower AMI scores predicting higher risk of incident disability over a 5-year follow-up.26 These findings support the AMI as a reliable and valid instrument for assessing functional mobility and psychosocial engagement in community-dwelling older adults in Japan.

Potential confounding factors

Potential confounding variables to be assessed will include age, sex, body mass index (BMI), education level, medical history (heart disease, diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, cancer and eye disease), hearing loss, number of medications and smoking and alcohol consumption habits.27 BMI will be calculated using a bioelectrical impedance analyzer (Tanita MC780A; Tanita Corporation, Tokyo, Japan).28

Blinding and data collection

After baseline measurements are obtained, the participants will be randomly assigned, on a 1:1 allocation basis, to one of the two intervention groups, namely, the PetitVOLA group or the control group. The participants will be allocated to each group using a stratified randomisation protocol, according to their state of social isolation. The principal investigator (AK), who will not be involved in the eligibility determination procedure, will generate the randomisation schedule and maintain concealment of the allocation. Group allocation will be revealed to the investigator (TS) only after the baseline measurements have been obtained. All outcome measures, collected by research assistants blinded to the participants’ allocation, will be analysed by the principal investigator (HS) using data that do not contain personal identifiers. Owing to the nature of the intervention, neither the primary investigator providing the intervention nor the participants will be blinded to the treatment group assignment. In the event of an adverse event that requires consideration of participant withdrawal, a researcher not involved in the data management and analysis will be consulted.

Trial power and sample size

A power analysis will be conducted in accordance with a previous study that examined the effect of volunteer activities on mental and physical health, including feelings of loneliness.29 The mean change and SD in the UCLA-LS (V.3) owing to volunteer activities will be determined. Assuming an α error of 0.05 and a power of 0.80, the required number of cases will be 126 (63 participants per group). Considering a 20% dropout during the intervention, 160 participants will need to be registered to meet the required number.

Data management and monitoring

Trained research staff will measure all of the variables. Data will be collected through electronic data capture by assessors or participants. All participant data will be coded by research assistants without group identifiers to maintain blinding of the researchers responsible for data analysis. All instances of non-adherence and discontinuation will be recorded electronically in a master spreadsheet to ensure appropriate handling and interpretation of the results. All electronic data will be anonymised and stored on a password-protected computer. All hard-copy data will be anonymised and stored in locked filing cabinets. The safety of the intervention will be assessed through monitoring all adverse events (AEs), including serious AEs, throughout the trial period. Information on AEs will include the date of onset, severity, treatment, outcome and causal relationships. Serious AEs will be reported immediately by the principal investigator, the Institutional Review Board and co-investigators.

Statistical analyses

In this trial, we will define three analysis sets: (1) the intention-to-treat (ITT) set, which includes all randomised participants regardless of involvement in the intervention programmes; (2) the full analysis set (FAS), which includes participants who participate in the intervention programme at least once and (3) the per protocol set (PPS), which will include participants involved in the intervention programme for 3 months. Primary efficacy analysis will be performed using the ITT, and secondary efficacy analyses will be performed using the FAS and PPS. We will calculate the difference in baseline characteristics between the groups using Student’s t-test, Mann-Whitney U and Pearson’s χ2 tests. This trial will use multiple imputation analyses to account for possible bias from participants with missing data for 3 months. A mixed-effects model for repeated measures with an unstructured covariance structure will be implemented to assess the differences in primary and secondary outcomes between the PetitVOLA and control groups at baseline and after 3 months. In the multivariate model, age, sex, BMI, education level, medical history, medications used and smoking and alcohol consumption habits will be adjusted as covariates, with the difference between pre-intervention and post-intervention data for each outcome being the dependent variable. Considering our hypothesis that the effect of the intervention might be influenced by the state of social isolation at baseline, this analysis will also be conducted to examine the association between the four categories of isolation and loneliness. All analyses will be performed using SPSS Statistics (V.29.0; IBM) and R V.4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) software. The threshold for statistical significance will be set at p<0.05.

Discussion

In this randomised controlled trial, we will evaluate the effectiveness of the PetitVOLA programme in alleviating loneliness among community-dwelling older adults. This trial has some limitations. It remains unclear whether this intervention is applicable outside of Japan, given this PetitVOLA trial exclusively involves Japanese older adults. Despite this uncertainty, the PetitVOLA programme’s design as a petit volunteer activity makes it easily adaptable for implementation in other countries.

There may be possible selection bias among the trial participants. The participants in this trial will be older adults motivated to participate in research projects of this nature. Research indicates that individuals experiencing loneliness often lack the motivation to participate in social activities,30 suggesting that our results may not represent this group. The PetitVOLA trial is limited to 12 intervention sessions; therefore, it is not possible to estimate the dose-response effect. Nonetheless, the findings of this trial are hypothesised to demonstrate that active social participation, even in old age, supports physical activity, fosters communication and helps sustain reductions in loneliness. The trial findings, designed for implementation in local communities, are expected to support the standardisation and dissemination of intervention models targeting loneliness alleviation.

Ethics and dissemination

The trial was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of the National Center for Geriatrics and Gerontology (approval No. 1794). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before enrolment (online supplemental materials). Permission was obtained from Kariya City to use municipal records for recruitment, and no external administrative databases will be accessed without further approval. The findings will be disseminated via peer-reviewed journals, conferences and stakeholder reports.

A mathematical model of folate-mediated one-carbon metabolism in Down syndrome
Authors: Allison Piovesan, Diletta Poluzzi, Giuseppe Ramacieri, Chiara Locatelli, Francesca Antonaros, Beatrice Vione, Maria Caracausi, Maria Chiara Pelleri, Luca Marchetti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDown syndrome (DS), the most frequent human genetic disorder marked by an extra copy of chromosome 21 (Hsa21) or a portion thereof, leads to physical and cognitive impairments. Following the Lejeune work, researchers focused on a potential anomaly within the folate-mediated one-carbon metabolism (FOCM). Here, we present a FOCM model modified from a previous work with the incorporation of the enzyme cystathionine beta-synthase (CBS), whose encoding gene is located on Hsa21, coupled with the methionine input rate. Systematic perturbation of FOCM enzyme activity rates has been performed to explore possible in silico configurations to simulate the DS condition. The perturbed vs. unperturbed model-derived ratio concentrations of tetrahydrofolate, 5-formyl-tetrahydrofolate, 5-methyl-tetrahydrofolate, S-adenosyl-homocysteine, and S-adenosyl-methionine were compared with the known literature through various statistical approaches. After investigating public transcriptomic databases, the FTS (formate-tetrahydrofolate ligase) perturbation achieved the best overall score. Although the FTS encoding gene (MTHFD1) is not located on Hsa21, it was found to be overexpressed in the DS condition. In addition, an interesting correlation emerged with the PTG (phosphoribosylglycinamide formyltransferase) perturbation and the corresponding encoding gene (GART), located on Hsa21 and notably over-expressed in the DS condition. The model thus identifies key enzyme activities that warrant further investigation.Subject terms: Biochemistry, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Genetics, Molecular biology

Introduction

Down syndrome (DS) or trisomy 211 is the most frequent human genetic disorder in which an individual possesses an extra full or partial copy of human chromosome 21 (Hsa21)2, with an incidence of 1 in 700–1000 live births globally3. It is also the most frequent leading genetic cause of intellectual disability, which is of variable degree and always present together with other variable characteristic signs and symptoms3–10. While various interventions and therapies are available to address the physical manifestations linked to DS, there is currently a lack of specific treatments aimed directly at cognitive difficulties.

Lejeune hypothesized that a disturbance of the one-carbon cycle might occur in DS11, and several other authors reported the one-carbon cycle to be imbalanced in subjects with DS12–18. Folate-mediated one-carbon metabolism (FOCM) includes the folate cycle, which plays a vital role in delivering one-carbon units at different oxidation states to the homocysteine-methionine cycle for methylation reactions and biosynthesis of several metabolites. These include the biosynthesis of purines and thymidine, the maintenance of amino acid balance, the regulation of epigenetic functions, and the defense against oxidative stress. At the heart of this intricate process is methionine synthase (MS), the enzyme responsible for converting homocysteine (HCY) to methionine (MET) by using 5-methyl-tetrahydrofolate (5mTHF) as a methyl donor and vitamin B12 as an essential cofactor. HCY is converted to cysteine through the transsulfuration pathway mediated by cystathionine ß-synthase (CBS)19,20. MET is a precursor of S-adenosyl-methionine (SAM), which serves as a methyl donor for DNA methylation, protein methylation, and lipid and neurotransmitter synthesis21.

Many of the biochemical reactions in the FOCM pathway depend on the concentrations of their substrates in a nonlinear way. Therefore, the behavior of the full cycle is relatively complex and cannot be easily deduced from the reaction diagram itself, making it a perfect candidate to be investigated through a mathematical model. Over the years, significant progress has been made in understanding FOCM, with numerous models developed to explain its pathways. The earliest models emerged in the 1970s and 1980s, focusing primarily on the impact of anticancer drugs on the system22. Notably, Michael C. Reed and Colleagues played a pivotal role in advancing these models. Firstly, they developed the homocysteine-methionine and folate cycle models separately23,24 and later integrated them25, thus contributing extensively to the understanding of FOCM across diverse scenarios and diseases. The integrated model simulates the interconnected biochemical reactions occurring in the cytoplasm and serves as the foundation for the subsequent models developed and validated by Misselbeck et al.26,27.

The aim of this work is to extend and leverage the mathematical model of the FOCM previously introduced in Misselbeck et al.26,27 to explore possible in silico configurations of the FOCM that can be linked to the DS condition. The original model was extended through the incorporation of the reaction catalyzed by the enzyme CBS (of which the encoding gene is located on Hsa21), coupled with the MET input rate. The model behavior was newly evaluated under specific experimental conditions to validate the reliability of the extended model. Then, perturbations of enzyme activity rate were systematically performed to simulate DS imbalances, and the new steady state of the perturbed models was computed. The ratios of the model-predicted metabolite amounts in the perturbed vs. unperturbed model were compared to plasmatic DS vs. non-DS concentration ratios available in the literature for tetrahydrofolate (THF), 5mTHF, SAM, S-adenosyl-homocysteine (SAH), and 5-formyl-THF (5fTHF). This allowed us to identify a set of enzyme perturbations that, according to model simulation, can redistribute the folate forms within the FOCM pathway as reported in the data currently available for DS.

The analysis presented here will provide an in silico approach to investigate FOCM dysregulation in DS at a systemic level. By identifying potential regulatory components within the biological network, this work aims to enhance our understanding of the disease and the underlying mechanisms contributing to intellectual disability during development.

Results

Extended FOCM model and perturbations

The starting published FOCM model has been extended in the present work through the incorporation of the reaction catalyzed by the enzyme CBS coupled with the MET input rate (Fig. 1). The list of model enzyme and substrate abbreviations is available in Supplementary Note 1. The MATLAB code to simulate the extended mathematical model employed in this study is available at https://github.com/cosbi-research/FocmDownSyndrome. A validation procedure to ensure the reliability of the newly extended model was performed by a sequence of simulations regarding the effect of glycine on FOCM, the effect of different levels of methylene-tetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) activity, and the effect of different methenyl-tetrahydrofolate synthetase (MTHFS) and serine hydroxymethyltransferase (SHMT) activity26,27. In all three considered validation cases, our extended model showed a good agreement with the experimental data (Supplementary Note 2 and Supplementary Data 1), comparable to the one computed by the previous version of the model, indicating that the behavior of the extended model remains consistent with the observations presented in the original papers26,27.

Subsequently, the validated extended model was used, and each of the 17 FOCM reactions was then perturbed by a reduction in enzyme activity rate by 2/3 or an enhancement by 3/2 to simulate possible cascade effects induced by the presence of the third copy of Hsa21 characteristic of the DS condition. As detailed in the Methods section, for each perturbation, a ratio was computed between the value reached at the steady state after enzyme activity rate perturbation representing DS condition and the initial steady state value (related to the unperturbed model as a non-DS control) for all 14 model variables. Computed value ratios for single enzyme activity perturbation by 2/3 or 3/2 for all variables are detailed in Supplementary Data 2. Notably, perturbation of methionine synthase (MS) and MTHFR activity rates by 2/3 and 3/2 induces changes in all 14 model variables, in some cases of substantial magnitude. On the other hand, other enzyme perturbations mostly changed their substrates or products, while betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase (BHMT), S-adenosyl-homocysteine hydrolase (SAHH), glycine N-methyltransferase (GNMT), DNA methyltransferase (DNMT), and CBS perturbations caused the greatest changes in all the homocysteine-methionine cycle variables, where they are involved. When examining enzyme activity perturbation by 2/3, the score for each enzyme activity rate perturbation increases when uncertainty increases. The most unfavorable perturbation outcomes are associated with MTHFR, which reaches the highest score of 1 with a fixed uncertainty of 0.6, followed by MTHFS, which is the last to reach the score of 2 with a fixed uncertainty of 0.234. The first score of 2 is observed with a fixed uncertainty of 0.071 in the case of the GNMT enzyme perturbation, which also first reaches the score of 3 at a fixed uncertainty of 0.091. The score of 4 is first attained with a fixed uncertainty of 0.234 by the enzymes phosphoribosylglycinamide formyltransferase (PGT) and phosphoribosylaminoimidazolecarboxamide formyltransferase (AICARFT). At a fixed uncertainty of 0.397, more than half of the perturbations (9) give a score of 4. The best score of 5 is reached by CBS perturbation, but only at 0.6 of uncertainty (Table 2).

Enzyme activity perturbation by 3/2 also shows an increase in score with increasing uncertainty. The enzyme MS produces the least favorable perturbation outcomes, with 1 as the maximum score at 0.58 uncertainty, followed by MTHFS, which is the last to reach the score of 2 with a fixed uncertainty of 0.213. The first score of 3 is observed with a fixed uncertainty of 0.213 in the case of the formate-tetrahydrofolate ligase (FTS) enzyme perturbation. Overall, the best score is 4, reached with the first fixed uncertainty of 0.234 by the enzyme FTS. At a fixed uncertainty of 0.437, more than half of the perturbations (9) give a score of 4 (Table 2).

The second point of view to assess the extent of ratio concordance with experimental data is focusing on each of the five metabolites and their corresponding total scores. These range from 0 to 17, indicating that from no enzyme to all 17 enzymes’ activity rates subject to perturbation gives a variable ratio value consistent with experimental reference data (Supplementary Data 4). These results highlight a general system perturbation at low levels of uncertainty, and that enzyme activity perturbations almost never reach concordance with all five experimental ratio values because achieving consistency for the metabolite SAH seems to pose a greater challenge. Specifically, when perturbing enzymes by a factor of 2/3, concordance is achieved by none or one enzyme perturbation at a time from the fixed uncertainty of 0.417. Even more notable, when perturbing by a factor of 3/2, the score for SAH is always 0. Regarding the other metabolites, when uncertainty increases, the score increases as well, but not linearly. When perturbing by a factor of 2/3, the concordance for THF jumps from 6 (fixed uncertainty of 0.274) to 15 (fixed uncertainty of 0.295); the concordance for 5mTHF jumps from 2 (fixed uncertainty of 0.091) to 10 (fixed uncertainty of 0.112); the concordance for SAM jumps from 2 (fixed uncertainty of 0.274) to 12 (fixed uncertainty of 0.295); the concordance for 5fTHF jumps from 2 (fixed uncertainty of 0.03) to 10 (fixed uncertainty of 0.051). When perturbing by a factor of 2/3, we observed a similar trend, with similar jumps at the same fixed uncertainty levels (Supplementary Data 4). While this method is useful to visually highlight how perturbations vary at different uncertainties, it is difficult to choose a fixed value for biological discussion. For this purpose, the comparison with the bootstrap approach described in the following section was performed.

Effects of enzyme activity perturbation on the FOCM model with the bootstrap procedure

A bootstrap procedure was used to create in silico DS and non-DS datasets starting from the original experimental dataset for each of the five metabolites of interest. Due to practical limitations, the availability of metabolite concentration data varies for each patient, leading to non-uniform widths within the dataset (Supplementary Table 6 published in Vione et al.18). The real distribution of the experimental data populations was used to generate in silico datasets for the five distinct metabolites and the original and generated distributions are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1. Therefore, we conducted an analysis for each of the five metabolites to determine whether the model computed variable ratio, taking into account the concentration under conditions with DS (where enzyme activity is perturbed) compared to the concentration in a control state (unperturbed model), falls within predefined intervals under the assumption of a general distribution (intervals 3 and 4 in “Methods”) derived from the bootstrap procedure with 1000 iterations (Table 3). A score of 1 was assigned if the criteria were met; otherwise, it was assigned as 0.

Gene expression analysis

Available quantitative transcriptome maps28 were used to investigate ratio expression values between DS and non-DS conditions for genes encoding for FOCM enzymes (Table 5), considering enzyme scores at the predefined uncertainties of 0.254 (2/3 activity perturbation) and 0.274 (3/2 activity perturbation), which better resemble the bootstrap result procedures (Supplementary Data 7). Focusing on the brain transcriptome map, the highest scores for 2/3 enzyme activity perturbation concordant with gene expression ratio ≤0.76 are found only for GNMT (with an expression ratio value of 0.73, encoding for GNMT with a score of 3) and methionine adenosyltransferase 1A (MAT1A, with an expression ratio value of 0.70, encoding for MAT-I and MAT-III with both a score of 3). The transcriptome map of all tissues did not show under-expressed genes. TissueNexus (https://diseaselinks.com/TissueNexus/)29 brain dataset highlighted, among the others, a connection of 0.8177 between GNMT and CBS (which emerged for high scores when its enzyme activity is perturbed by 2/3, in contrast with its over-expression), a connection of 0.8879 between MAT1A and BHMT (of which encoded enzymes never emerged in our perturbation analyses), and a connection of 0.844 between MAT1A and methylene-tetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase (MTHFD1, Supplementary Data 8). Interestingly, CBS, GNMT, and MAT1A show the lowest number of connections with other FOCM genes with edge weight >0.5 (Table 6).

The highest scores for 3/2 enzyme activity perturbation concordant with gene expression ratio ≥1.30 are found in the brain transcriptome map for dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR, with an expression ratio value of 1.64, encoding for DHFR), MTHFD1 (with a borderline expression ratio value of 1.28, encoding for FTS), phosphoribosylglycinamide formyltransferase (GART, gene located on Hsa21 with an expression ratio of 1.43 and encoding for PTG), and thymidylate synthase (TYMS, with an expression ratio value of 1.43, encoding for TYMS). The transcriptome map of all tissues confirmed the concordance between the highest scores for 3/2 enzyme activity perturbation and over-expression of MTHFD1 (with an expression ratio value of 1.31) and GART (with an expression ratio of 1.31). Notably, the other Hsa21 gene (CBS, with an expression ratio value of 1.52 in the brain and 1.31 in all tissues’ maps, encoding for CBS and located on Hsa21) resulted in being over-expressed in both transcriptome maps, but the encoded CBS enzyme is associated with low scores when 3/2 activity perturbation was performed (2 with fixed uncertainty or 1 with bootstrap procedure). TissueNexus brain dataset highlighted TYMS as the gene with the highest number of overall connections with edge weight >0.5, while DHFR and MTHFD1 are the genes with the highest number of connections with other FOCM genes with edge >0.5 (Table 6). Among the others, DHFR shows high connections with TYMS (0.9454), DNMT1 (0.9427), MTHFD1 (0.9294), SHMT1 (0.9081), and GART (0.873). MTHFD1 is highly connected with GART (0.9396), TYMS (0.9343), SHMT1 (0.9218), AHCY (0.9157), and ATIC (0.9036). GART is highly connected with AHCY (0.9314) and TYMS (0.9233) (Supplementary Data 8).

Discussion

In the present work, we have explored possible in silico configurations of the FOCM pathway to simulate imbalances in the DS condition. An original FOCM model has been extended here through the incorporation of the reaction catalyzed by the enzyme CBS (encoded by a gene located on Hsa21 that is found in an additional copy in DS), coupled with the MET input rate, leading to an open model more in agreement with the actual biological functioning of the pathway. The validation procedure performed on the newly extended model highlighted the consistency with previously reported findings described in the original papers26,27, proving its reliability. The extended validated model has then been exploited to systematically perturb each enzyme activity rate, individually reducing or increasing each of the 17 model reaction activity rates, one at a time by a factor of 2/3 or 3/2, respectively, and computing each new steady state to simulate inhibitory or enhancing pathway imbalances potentially driven by the additional third copy of Hsa21 in the DS condition. After each perturbation and simulation, the model variable values obtained at the new perturbed steady state (resembling possible hypotheses of DS condition) were compared with initial variable values at the steady state of the unperturbed model, generating a computed model-derived DS/non-DS ratio. These ratios highlighted that the perturbed model configuration at steady state is affected in different ways by different enzyme activity rate perturbations, with some of them more effectively altering than others, probably reflecting the relative enzymes that are more connected or more peripheral in the pathway.

The computed model-derived variable ratios were then in turn compared with experimental plasma concentration ratios measured in DS/non-DS children and young individuals and reported in the literature for five metabolites of interest: THF, 5mTHF, SAM, SAH, and 5fTHF (see Fig. 2 for a summary of the main results presented in this study). The aim was to identify the set of enzyme perturbations capable of redistributing the folate forms within the FOCM pathway, consistent with currently available data for DS at a young age. This assessment of the concordance between model-derived and experimental ratios was aided by utilizing a relative error calculation in combination with predetermined levels of uncertainty. As one might expect, overall, the concordance of each enzyme activity rate perturbation gives variable ratios that are more concordant with experimental ratios as the uncertainty increases. However, this approach also revealed that the concordance with all five experimental ratio values is almost never reached because achieving consistency for the metabolite SAH seems to pose a greater challenge. This may be attributed to the substantial difference in the number of data points between the DS and non-DS groups. Since the estimation of the best fixed uncertainty threshold δ to analyze the results could be difficult to identify, we also implemented a bootstrap procedure based on a randomized number generation approach to establish a data-driven approach for evaluating the concordance between model-derived variable ratios and experimental data. This procedure showed that the concordance increases for each enzyme and for both types of activity rate perturbation as the intervals are gradually expanded, covering the entire population. This approach highlights again that the overall score associated with the metabolite SAH remains consistently low across most of the analyzed intervals, thus the achievement of a total score of 4 for each enzyme, accounting for consistent results with THF, 5mTHF, SAM, and 5fTHF, is truly noteworthy. The scores obtained with the [First quantile − 1.5 • IQR, Third quantile + 1.5 • IQR] interval, which covers 98% of the data in a general distribution, better resemble the score results obtained by 0.254 fixed uncertainty for 2/3 enzyme activity perturbation and 0.274 fixed uncertainty for 3/2 enzyme activity perturbation. Thus, these results were taken into consideration when investigating the ratio expression values between DS and non-DS conditions for genes encoding FOCM enzymes. Analyzing brain and all tissues’ transcriptome maps available in the literature, concordance between gene under-expression ratio values and encoded enzyme 2/3 activity perturbation was found for GNMT, MAT-I, and MAT-III, which obtained a score of 3 in the comparison of model-derived with experimental metabolite ratios. Gene over-expression values concord with encoded enzyme 3/2 activity perturbation, which was notably not favorable for CBS (encoded by CBS on Hsa21), as obtained model-derived vs. experimental metabolite ratio concordance scores of 2 with fixed uncertainty and 1 with the bootstrap procedure. Gene over-expression value concordance was found instead for DHFR, PGT, SAHH, and TYMS, which obtained a score of 3 in the comparison of model-derived metabolite with experimental ratios, and for FTS, which notably obtained a score of 4 in both fixed uncertainties and bootstrap procedures, thereby strengthening our findings. Its encoding gene (MTHFD1), although located on chromosome 14, has one of the highest numbers of connections with other FOCM genes in TissueNexus database. This comes from the fact that FTS is an enzyme directly connected with THF, which has already been pointed out as the folate form most interconnected in the folate pathway and product of ten enzymatic reactions18. FTS holds a critical position as it facilitates the entry of formate and THF into FOCM through its three distinct enzymatic activities (5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase, 5,10-methenyltetrahydrofolate cyclohydrolase, and 10-formyltetrahydrofolate synthetase), which catalyze the sequential interconversion of one-carbon derivatives of THF for methionine and thymidylate syntheses, and generate 10fTHF for de novo purine syntheses driven by PGT30. PGT also achieved good scores in 3/2 perturbation enzyme activity rate in line with the over-expression ratio of its encoding gene GART, which is located on Hsa21, also confirmed by proteome analysis in trisomy 21 versus euploid fibroblast cells31. Unfortunately, this and other proteome data available in the literature for the DS condition32 did not allow us to confirm other results.

The primary limitation of our study is the lack of transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic data available for genes, metabolites, and enzymes of our interest and central to the FOCM pathway together for the same DS and non-DS sample groups. These data could help to better investigate our first assumption that the additional third copy of Hsa21 (in a ratio of 3:2 compared to the normal pair of Hsa21) may result in direct alteration of 3:2 or 2:3 of the FOCM enzyme activities. Another limitation concerns the quality and quantity of the literature data used to compare the computed model-derived variable ratios in our analysis for the five metabolites of our interest (THF, 5mTHF, SAM, SAH, and 5fTHF). In particular, the relatively low number of experimental data points for the SAM metabolite and the general difference in length between the DS and non-DS datasets in Vione et al.18 should be highlighted as potential areas of improvement when more data points become available. While other datasets of plasma metabolomic analysis in trisomy 21 subjects compared with control samples are available33,34, the measured abundance values refer to metabolites not central in the FOCM pathway, and the average ages (mainly adults) are not comparable with the ones of our analysis. However, our tool is also made available for similar simulations (MATLAB code available at https://github.com/cosbi-research/FocmDownSyndrome. https://github.com/cosbi-research/FocmDownSyndrome), such as those involving other types of enzyme activity rate perturbations, when these data are made available in the literature. Finally, a promising model extension could be the inclusion of nuclear de novo thymidylate synthesis in the model, which is known to be affected in many pathologies resulting from impairments in one-carbon metabolism35. An ongoing research effort in this direction is currently underway within our research group.

In conclusion, we presented a FOCM model modified ad hoc to explore possible in silico configurations that can be linked to the DS condition and evaluate the concordance with experimental evidence. Our results, also supported by transcriptome evidence, strengthen the crucial role of the entrance of THF in the one-carbon cycle and that FTS activity needs to be investigated in more detail, as it had also been found to be over-expressed in fetuses with DS compared with the control group (Cl-tetrahydrofolate synthase)36.

Methods

Extension of the FOCM model to simulate the Down syndrome imbalances

The starting FOCM model used as the foundation of subsequent extensions and perturbations performed here to simulate DS imbalances derived from previous works26,27. Briefly, in 2017, Misselbeck et al.26 developed a mathematical model to represent the FOCM pathways in cytoplasm, treating it as a closed system of reactions, expanding upon the fundamental work by Reed et al.25. The model included 12 variables that represented distinct forms of folate (THF, 10fTHF, 5,10-methenyl-THF, 5,10-methylen-THF, dihydrofolate, 5mTHF), the enzyme SHMT, the complex 5mTHF:SHMT, formed through the binding of 5mTHF and SHMT, and various metabolites (HCY, MET, SAM, and SAH). Certain substrates (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate reduced form or NADPH, oxidized form or NADP+, deoxyuridine monophosphate or dUMP, serine, glycine, glycinamide ribonucleotide or GAR, 5-aminoimidazole-4-carboxamide ribonucleotide or AICAR, betaine, and formate) were considered constants over time, based on observations from earlier work25. The model was further extended in the subsequent study27 by incorporating the futile cycle, integrating 5fTHF and MTHFS along with the relevant reactions involving these variables. The extended model thus consisted of 14 variables and 17 reversible and irreversible enzyme reactions implemented as a set of Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs), where reaction rates were specified by Michaelis-Menten formulas. Parameter estimates were obtained from literature or calculated through nonlinear least square optimization as detailed in Misselbeck et al.26,27. Kinetic constants were derived from polyglutamate cofactors and their interaction with enzymes, mainly purified from L1210 (murine lymphocytic leukemia cell line) cells or other mammalian tissue when possible26.

In order to obtain a more suitable FOCM model for simulating the DS imbalances, the model publicly available in Misselbeck et al.26,27 has been further extended in the present work (leading to an extended model, as named from here on) through the incorporation of the reaction catalyzed by the enzyme CBS coupled with the MET input rate, as graphically represented in the model diagram provided in Fig. 1.

CBS, encoded by the CBS gene located on Hsa21, is a key player in the transsulfuration pathway. The intricacies of the CBS-catalyzed reaction’s kinetics, activated by SAM, are clarified through the utilization of the following Michaelis–Menten formula (R: reaction, V: velocity), introduced in Reed et al.25.

where the Michaelis–Menten constant is Km = 1000 µM and the maximum rate of the reaction is Vmax = 90,000 µM/h.

The incorporation of the MET input rate was guided by the insights provided by Reed et al.25 based on the work by Storch et al.37, which considered both fasting and non-fasting states. As the context under consideration may involve scenarios both during fasting and non-fasting states, an average value of 100 µM for the MET input rate was adopted here, capturing a broader range of situations. The thus extended model, in agreement with Reed et al.25, is no longer a closed model and should, therefore, be more in agreement with the actual biological functioning of the pathway.

Validation of the extended FOCM model

The modifications introduced in the newly extended model, as detailed above, might have changed the behavior of the model itself. In order to ensure the reliability of the newly extended model and the consistency with previously reported findings, we performed a validation procedure by reproducing the experimental scenarios originally described in the original papers26,27. The validation step was carried out by performing a series of simulations using the MATLAB integrator ode15s and comparing the results with those obtained from the original model under the following three conditions. (1) The effect on FOCM of varying the amount of glycine, essential to compare the model’s results with empirical data generated in MCF-7 cells, as observed by Herbig et al.38; simulations were carried out by altering glycine levels from 0 mM to 10 mM, while observing the variable concentration trends at the steady state of the model. (2) The effect on FOCM of different levels of MTHFR activity, a critical enzyme involved in the folate cycle, to observe how the model responded to alterations due to the C667T polymorphism39. Simulations were conducted at a steady state with diverse values of the parameter associated with the MTHFR-catalyzed reaction, spanning from 2 times to 0.3 times that of the wild type. (3) The effect on FOCM of different MTHFS and SHMT activity26,27. Simulations were executed under various scenarios for the activity of SHMT and MTHFS enzymes while considering model variables at a steady state. The specific conditions tested are the following: standard MTHFS and SHMT activity (parameters for MTHFS and SHMT were maintained at standard values); halved MTHFS activity and standard SHMT activity (SHMT activity remained unaffected while the MTHFS-catalyzed reaction parameter was reduced to 0.5 times its value); negligible MTHFS activity and standard SHMT activity (MTHFS-catalyzed reaction parameter was set to 0, while SHMT activity retained its standard value); negligible MTHFS and SHMT: CHF –> 5fTHF activity (the MTHFS parameter and initial value of 5fTHF were set to 0 and the SHMT activity was turned off by setting its parameter to 0 for the corresponding reaction); standard MTHFS and SHMT activity without MTHFS inhibition by 10fTHF (standard model parameterization where the part of the equation related to MTHFS inhibition by 10fTHF was omitted). The initial values considered for the simulations were those illustrated in the study conducted by Misselbeck et al.27. The trends of the concentration values and enzyme rates obtained by the simulations of the newly extended model (see Supplementary Note 2 and Supplementary Data 1) were compared with those available in the original papers (please refer to Fig. 3 and Tables 1–6 published in Misselbeck et al.26 and to Tables 1–5 published in Misselbeck et al.27) to evaluate the reliability and accuracy of the model modified here and its consistency with the previous findings.

Perturbation of the enzyme activity rate

In order to simulate possible imbalances of FOCM pathways in the DS condition, we proceeded to systematically perturb enzyme activity rates in the newly extended model. In concordance with the dosage hypothesis in DS condition40, we assumed that the presence of the additional third copy of Hsa21 (in a ratio of 3:2 compared to the normal pair of Hsa21), may lead to an altered expression of genes located on Hsa21 itself and/or on other chromosomes by the expected 3:2 ratio or by a 2:3 ratio (the latter due to the inhibitory effect). This over- or under- expression may consequently result in alteration of 3:2 or 2:3 of the FOCM enzyme activities, finally leading to pathway imbalances. Therefore, perturbation was algorithmically imposed on the model by individually reducing or augmenting each of the 17 model reaction activity rates by a factor of 2/3 or 3/2, respectively. For bidirectional reactions catalyzed by the same enzyme (e.g., MTD, methenyl-tetrahydrofolate cyclohydrolase or MTCH, and SAHH), both directions were perturbed simultaneously. The resulting 34 perturbed models were then simulated to compute their new steady states, representing possible hypotheses of FOCM configurations in DS. A MATLAB function was developed to calculate, for each enzyme reaction rate perturbation, the ratio of perturbed to unperturbed concentrations for all 14 model variables, thereby representing a DS vs. non-DS ratio. Specifically, this computed ratio is calculated between the model variable concentration value (z) obtained at the steady state after perturbation for the DS condition and the model variable concentration value (y) in non-DS (represented by the unperturbed model at the steady state), following the approach depicted in Fig. 3.

Experimental dataset

Computed model-derived variable ratios were then compared with experimental data available in the literature. In particular, a public dataset provided by a previous work18 reported DS/non-DS concentration ratio for the five metabolites THF, 5mTHF, SAM, SAH, and 5fTHF measured in plasma samples through the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and was used for this purpose. The global cohort was represented by 164 subjects with DS, with a mean age of 11.55 years, and 54 subjects from the control group, with a mean age of 14.53 years18. We chose DS/non-DS mean concentration ratio values, excluding outliers: 0.72 for THF, 1.11 for 5mTHF, 1.28 for SAM, 3.45 for SAH, and 1.04 for 5fTHF (please refer to Supplementary Table 6 published in Vione et al.18).

In order to identify which enzyme reaction rate perturbation reaches the best concordance between the computed variable ratios and experimental data available in the literature, a procedure based on relative error calculation and a bootstrap technique was used, as outlined in Fig. 4 and detailed in the next paragraphs.

The fixed uncertainty thresholds δ were arbitrarily chosen and ranged from 0.01 to 0.6 (corresponding to 60% of the uncertainty). The MATLAB function was then extended to calculate, for each enzyme activity rate perturbation, two scores used to present the results. A total score for each perturbed enzyme, which ranges from 0 to 5, indicates the number of the five considered metabolites that have a computed ratio in agreement with the experimental data. Comparison with experimental data through a bootstrap procedure

A limitation of the analysis introduced above is that it requires an estimate of the fixed uncertainty threshold δ that could be difficult to identify. For this reason, to establish a data-driven approach for evaluating the concordance between model-derived variable ratios and experimental data, we also implemented a bootstrap procedure based on a randomized number generation approach. Specifically, a MATLAB function was developed to generate in silico datasets for both the DS and control conditions for each of the five metabolites of interest, ensuring that each dataset matched the length of the original experimental data. Within each in silico dataset, metabolite values were randomly generated to replicate the distribution observed in the corresponding experimental dataset.

Subsequently, metabolite mean values were computed for each condition, and the DS/control ratio was calculated. This process was iteratively repeated for a predefined number of iterations (100, 300, 500, 800, and 1000), yielding a vector of ratio values. Since increasing the number of iterations from 800 to 1000 did not yield an appreciable difference in the computed values, we interpreted 1000 iterations as sufficient to achieve a good level of stability in our results. Therefore, we considered only the values computed with this number of iterations in the Results section.

From the computed vector of ratio values, key statistical measures were derived, including the mean, median, standard deviation, first quartile, and third quartile that were then used to assess the agreement between the model simulations and the experimental dataset18. For this reason, we employed a MATLAB function that assigns a score of 1 if the model variable ratio falls within a designated interval and 0 otherwise for each metabolite of interest. We examined the following intervals, unique to each metabolite, which have been derived from the outcomes of the bootstrap procedure:

In addition to the comparisons reported above, we conducted an additional analysis by defining an interval that encompassed the entire population and further expanded its minimum and maximum boundaries using the following formula [Minimum value − γ • Minimum value, Maximum value + γ • Maximum value], where γ = 0.05 + k • 0.05 with k = 0, 1, 10, 11. This test was designed to evaluate the level of uncertainty required to satisfy specific constraints that proved particularly challenging to fulfill in our simulations.

Gene expression meta-analysis

In order to explore if enzyme activity rate perturbations may be concordant with enzyme encoding gene expression values in DS (trisomy 21) compared with non-DS control (euploid) conditions, quantitative transcriptome maps of human trisomy 21 provided in the published study by Pelleri et al.28 obtained through the use of the Transcriptome Mapper (TRAM) software41 were used. Each FOCM enzyme name was searched in the KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) Enzyme database (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/annotation/enzyme.html)42 and the corresponding enzyme commission number and encoding gene symbol for Homo sapiens were annotated. The list of gene symbols was used to export corresponding expression ratio values between DS (trisomy 21) and non-DS control (euploid) conditions in various available tissues, focusing on the brain transcriptome map from TRAM. Precisely, FOCM enzyme encoding genes with expression ratio ≤0.76 were considered in concordance with 2/3 enzyme activity rate perturbation, while genes with expression ratio ≥1.30 were considered in concordance with 3/2 enzyme activity perturbation.

Genes for which under- or over-expression was considered in concordance with 2/3 or 3/2 enzyme activity rate perturbation statistically selected through fixed uncertainties and bootstrap procedure were searched in TissueNexus (https://diseaselinks.com/TissueNexus/)29, a comprehensive database housing 49 functional gene networks constructed by integrating 1,341 RNA-seq datasets and 52,087 individual samples through artificial intelligence. This database was used to explore possible gene pairs, each associated with a Pearson correlation coefficient. Brain gene pair dataset with edge weight >0.5 was downloaded and searched for interesting genes, with particular attention to Hsa21.

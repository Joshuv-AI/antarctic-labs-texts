MiR-148a-3p regulates the fracture healing process by targeting MAFB
Authors: Yongheng Li, Shuwei Yang, Ganggang Wang, Yingxuan Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporotic vertebral fracture (OVF) is an extremely common fragile fracture in the elderly population.AimsTo explore the expression changes, clinical significance and mechanism of action of miR-148a-3p in the postoperative healing process of patients with OVF.Methods185 OVF patients who received percutaneous vertebroplasty (PVP) treatment were selected as the research subjects. RT-qPCR was used to detect the expression levels of miR-148a-3p and genes. ROC curve analysis was applied to evaluate the relationship between miR-148a-3p and postoperative healing, as well as its diagnostic value for delayed healing. In vitro, miR-148a-3p overexpression and knockdown cell models were established to investigate its potential mechanism in fracture healing.ResultsSerum miR-148a-3p expression in OVF patients was significantly higher than in healthy controls, and it gradually decreased over time after surgery. The expression of miR-148a-3p in the delayed healing group is significantly higher than that in the healing group, and it showed potential diagnostic value for delayed healing (AUC = 0.859). In addition, miR-148a-3p is an independent risk factor for delayed healing. In cell experiments, during the process of osteogenic induction, expression of miR-148a-3p decreased, while the expression of osteogenic markers increased. Mechanistically, miR-148a-3p directly targeted and inhibited MAFB expression; its upregulation suppressed cell proliferation and osteogenic marker expression, and these inhibitory effects were reversed by MAFB overexpression.ConclusionsmiR-148a-3p inhibits osteoblast proliferation and differentiation by targeting and suppressing MAFB, participates in the post-OVF healing process.Keywords: Fracture, Fracture healing, miR-148a-3p, MAFB

Introduction

Osteoporotic vertebral fracture (OVF) is an extremely common fragile fracture in the elderly population [1]. OVF causes unbearable pain to patients and severely impairs their ability to perform activities of daily living [2]. Notably, OVF patients have a significantly increased risk of re-fracture, which poses a severe threat to their lifespan and quality of life [3]. Percutaneous vertebroplasty (PVP) is one of the commonly used clinical treatments for OVF [4]. It can effectively relieve patients’ pain symptoms in a short time and restore the height and stability of the vertebrae to a certain extent [5]. However, cases of delayed fracture healing or non-union still occur from time to time after surgery [6]. Fracture healing itself is a highly complex, multifaceted biological process involving precise coordination of cells, cytokines, and signaling pathways—with osteoblast proliferation/differentiation and bone matrix synthesis/mineralization as core events [7, 8]. Disruptions to this process prolong rehabilitation, increase medical burdens, and may lead to complications [9]. In the context of fracture healing, pharmacological strategies [10] and non-pharmacological interventions [11, 12] have been explored to improve healing outcomes; however, large-scale clinical trials are still required to validate their efficacy. Meanwhile, pharmacological strategies for fracture healing remain limited. Therefore, exploring the molecular mechanisms governing post-OVF fracture healing and identifying valid biomarkers/therapeutic targets is critical for improving treatment outcomes and prognosis.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) have emerged as pivotal regulators in musculoskeletal disorders: accumulating evidence highlights their roles in bone metabolism, osteoblast differentiation, and fracture healing [13, 14], and in osteoarthritis (OA) by exhibiting differential expression patterns between diseased and healthy cartilage that influence chondrocyte metabolism and cartilage degeneration [15]. Existing studies have demonstrated that multiple miRNAs contribute to bone formation and fracture repair by regulating osteogenesis-related genes [16]. For instance, miR-21 promotes the proliferation and differentiation of osteoblasts by targeting the PTEN gene, thereby accelerating fracture healing [17]. Conversely, miR-133 inhibits the expression of osteogenesis-related genes, impairs osteoblast differentiation, and exerts a detrimental effect on fracture healing [18]. Furthermore, research has shown that the expression level of miR-148a-3p is significantly altered in individuals with osteoporosis [19]. Additional studies suggest that miR-148a-3p may be involved in bone marrow adipogenesis and bone loss [20]. However, the precise role and underlying mechanisms of miR-148a-3p in post-OVF fracture healing remain incompletely understood.

This study investigates miR-148a-3p expression changes in OVF patients and its correlation with post-OVF fracture healing outcomes, aiming to provide evidence for potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets.

Materials and methods

Study subjects and grouping

A total of 185 patients with OVF who underwent PVP at the Affiliated Hospital of Youjiang Medical University for Nationalities were enrolled as the research subjects. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) meeting the diagnostic criteria for osteoporosis (bone mineral density T-score ≤ -2.5SD) [21]; (2) fresh spinal fractures confirmed by imaging (disease duration ≤ 2 weeks); (3) receiving PVP treatment for the first time; (4) aged 55–80 years. The exclusion criteria included: (1) complicated with diseases affecting bone metabolism such as malignant tumors and hyperparathyroidism; (2) having used anti-osteoporotic drugs like bisphosphonates and calcitonin in the past 3 months; (3) suffering from severe liver or kidney dysfunction or autoimmune diseases; (4) presence of infection or tumor metastasis at the fracture site. Meanwhile, 100 healthy volunteers who underwent physical examination in our hospital during the same period were selected as the control group. The control group was matched with the OVF patient group in terms of age and gender ratio, with normal bone mineral density. Fasting venous blood samples were collected from all participants, with serum separated and stored at -80 °C for subsequent analysis.

A total of 185 patients who underwent PVP treatment were followed up for 3 months after surgery, and fracture healing was evaluated based on imaging examinations. The healing group (n = 134) was characterized by the resolution or blurring of fracture lines accompanied by substantial callus formation. In contrast, the delayed healing group (n = 51) exhibited persistent clear fracture lines with minimal or absent callus formation, and demonstrated a significantly slower healing trajectory compared to the typical recovery process. This study received approval from the Hospital Ethics Committee, and informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Cell culture and osteogenic induction

The human osteoblast cell line MC3T3-E1 was obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. The cells were cultured in α-MEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum in an incubator maintained at 37 °C with 5% CO₂, with medium changed every 2–3 days. When cell confluence reached 70%, the medium was replaced with osteogenic induction medium for differentiation induction. The complete formulation of the osteogenic induction medium was as follows: α-MEM basal medium supplemented with 10% FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin-streptomycin, 50 µg/mL ascorbic acid, 10 mmol/L β-glycerophosphate, and 10⁻⁸ mol/L dexamethasone. During induction, the induction medium was changed every 2 days, and cells were harvested on days 3, 7, and 14 of induction for subsequent assays.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from both serum and cells using the TRIzol method. After confirming the RNA concentration and purity through measurements with an ultra-micro spectrophotometer, reverse transcription of the RNA into cDNA was performed using the TaKaRa Reverse Transcription Kit under the following conditions: 37 °C for 1 h, followed by 80 °C for 5 s. qPCR amplification was then performed with the SYBR® Premix Ex Taq™ kit in a 20 µL reaction system containing 1 µL of cDNA template, 0.5 µL each of forward and reverse primers, 0.5 µL of universal miRNA qPCR primer, 10 µL of 2× TransStart® Top Green qPCR SuperMix, and 7.5 µL of nuclease-free water, with cycling parameters including pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 30 s, 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 5 s. U6 and GAPDH were employed as internal reference genes, respectively, and the relative expression levels of serum miR-148a-3p and target gene mRNA were quantified using the 2−ΔΔCT method.

Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay

Online databases (https://rnasysu.com/encori/agoClipRNA.php) were used to screen potential target genes of miR-148a-3p. Luciferase reporter gene plasmids of MAFB 3’UTR wild-type (WT) and mutant-type (MUT) were constructed and co-transfected into cells with miR-148a-3p mimics or negative controls, respectively. Following a 48-hour incubation period post-transfection, the activities of firefly and Renilla luciferases were measured in accordance with the protocol provided by the dual-luciferase detection kit.

Cell transfection and functional assays

MC3T3-E1 cells were allocated into three distinct groups: the control group (un-transfected), the miR-148a-3p mimic group (cells transfected with miR-148a-3p mimics), and the miR-148a-3p mimic + MAFB overexpression plasmid group (cells co-transfected with miR-148a-3p mimics and MAFB overexpression plasmids). At 48 h post-transfection, cell proliferation was assessed using the CCK-8 assay, while the mRNA expression levels of osteogenic differentiation markers, including RUNX2, ALP, OCN, and OPN, were evaluated by RT-qPCR.

CCK-8 assay

MC3T3-E1 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were collected and seeded into a 96-well plate at a density of 3 × 10³ cells/well. The cells were transfected or intervened according to the experimental groups. After 24 h of culture, the culture medium was discarded. Transfection was performed following the instructions, and the medium was replaced with complete medium 6 h after transfection. At 0, 24, 48, and 72 h after transfection, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well. Subsequently, the cells were incubated in the dark at 37 °C for 2 h. The absorbance values at 450 nm were measured using a microplate reader. The absorbance of each well was determined, and the proliferation activity of each group was calculated based on the mean value of 3 replicate wells. The experiment was independently repeated 3 times.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of the data was conducted with SPSS 26.0 software. Quantitative data were presented as mean ± standard deviation. For comparisons between two groups, the independent samples t-test was used. For comparisons among multiple groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied, followed by Bonferroni post-hoc test for pairwise comparisons to correct for multiple testing. Categorical data were expressed as n (%), and intergroup comparisons were performed using the chi-square test.

Results

Comparison of clinical indicators among study groups

185 OVF patients treated with PVP and 100 healthy controls were enrolled. There were no significant differences in baseline data (age, gender, BMI) among the delayed healing group, healing group, and control group (P > 0.05). Patients in the delayed healing group were significantly older than those in the healing group [(69.75 ± 6.81) years vs. (66.99 ± 7.94) years]; their Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores were significantly higher [(19.06 ± 2.77) vs. (9.10 ± 2.05)], and Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) scores were also significantly higher [(3.02 ± 0.81) vs. (1.03 ± 0.88)], while no differences were found in fracture location or severity (P>0.05) (Table 1).

Expression of miR-148a-3p

Serum miR-148a-3p expression in OVF patients was significantly higher than that in healthy controls (P < 0.001, Fig. 1A), and gradually decreased at 3, 7, 14, and 28 days after surgery (P < 0.05, Fig. 1B).

Expression of miR-148a-3p in postoperative healing and its diagnostic value

At 90 days postoperatively, miR-148a-3p levels in both the healing and delayed healing groups were lower than those at 3 days postoperatively (P < 0.001, Fig. 2A-B). Nevertheless, it is particularly significant to note that miR-148a-3p levels within the delayed healing group were substantially elevated in comparison to those within the healing group (P < 0.001, Fig. 2C). Furthermore, the ROC curve analysis showed that AUC of miR-148a-3p for diagnosing delayed postoperative healing of OVF was 0.859 (95% CI: 0.802–0.917), with the sensitivity was 68.7% and the specificity was 85.1% (Fig. 2D).

Analysis of influencing factors for delayed fracture healing

Logistic regression analysis identified age, miR-148a-3p levels, ODI score and VAS score as significant risk factors for delayed postoperative healing of OVF (P < 0.05, Table 2). After adjusting for factors such as gender, and body mass index (BMI), miR-148a-3p was an independent risk factor for delayed postoperative healing of OVF (OR = 2.430, 95% CI: 1.202–4.913, P < 0.05) (Table 3).

In MC3T3-E1 cells during osteogenic induction, miR-148a-3p expression gradually decreased over 3, 7, and 14 days (P < 0.05, Fig. 3A), while mRNA levels of osteogenic markers (RUNX2, ALP, OCN, OPN) significantly increased (P < 0.05, Fig. 3B-E). Targeted regulatory relationship between miR-148a-3p and MAFB

There are potential binding sites between miR-148a-3p and MAFB. The dual-luciferase reporter gene assay demonstrated that, in comparison to co-transfection with the negative control of miR-148a-3p, the luciferase activity significantly diminished upon co-transfection with miR-148a-3p mimics and the WT-MAFB plasmid (P < 0.01). Conversely, no noteworthy change in luciferase activity was observed when co-transfecting with the MUT-MAFB plasmid (P > 0.05), thereby confirming the targeted binding relationship between miR-148a-3p and MAFB (Fig. 4A). Furthermore, miR-148a-3p mimic reduced MAFB mRNA expression (P < 0.01), while miR-148a-3p inhibitor increased it (P < 0.01,Fig. 4B). In MC3T3-E1 cells during osteogenic induction, MAFB mRNA expression gradually increased over 3, 7, and 14 days (P < 0.05, Fig. 4C).

miR-148a-3p regulates cell function via MAFB

In osteogenic differentiation research, miR-148a-3p may exert a regulatory role by targeting MAFB. The expression level of MAFB was further regulated by means of co-transfection (Fig. 5A). Cell function assays showed that miR-148a-3p mimic inhibited MC3T3-E1 cell proliferation and downregulated osteogenic marker mRNA expression (P < 0.05), while co-transfection with MAFB overexpression plasmid reversed these effects (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5B-C).

Discussion

Existing studies have underscored the pivotal role of miRNAs in orchestrating bone metabolism and fracture healing, with diverse miRNAs exerting context-dependent regulatory effects on osteoblast function and bone repair. For example, miR-204 has been shown to promote fracture healing by enhancing osteoblast viability [22]. Similarly, miR-488 accelerates fracture healing through the targeted inhibition of DKK1, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target [23]. Furthermore, miR-107 and miR-19b have been identified as diagnostic biomarkers for osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures, with concurrent roles in stimulating bone formation [24, 25]. Additionally, research has indicated that serum miR-519d-3p could act as an early diagnostic indicator for delayed fracture healing [26]. Compared with previous studies, our research revealed that serum miR-148a-3p levels are closely associated with the healing dynamics of OVF: they show an increasing trend before surgery, decrease significantly after surgery, while remaining persistently high in patients with delayed healing. Its favorable diagnostic efficacy (AUC = 0.859) and its characteristic as an independent risk factor (OR = 2.430) for delayed healing are novel findings specific to OVF. This indicates that miR-148a-3p has potential value as a prognostic biomarker for OVF.

Mechanistically, our findings align with the growing body of evidence that miRNAs regulate osteogenesis by targeting key transcription factors and signaling molecules. For example, miR-455-3p enhances osteoblast differentiation by targeting HDAC2 [27]. Additionally, miR-342-5p modulates the proliferation, differentiation, and migration of osteoblasts through the inhibition of Bmp7 expression [28]. Earlier research has shown that miR-98-5p overexpression suppresses osteoblast proliferation and differentiation through targeting BMP-2 [29]. Consistent with the inhibitory role of certain miRNAs in osteogenesis, our in vitro data showed that miR-148a-3p expression decreased during osteogenic induction, while osteogenic markers increased, indicating its inhibitory effect on osteogenic differentiation. Through dual-luciferase assays and functional validation, we identified MAFB as a direct target of miR-148a-3p. MAFB, a known osteogenic transcription factor, promotes RUNX2 expression and osteoblast differentiation [30].,It can also indirectly maintain the osteoblast-osteoclast balance by inhibiting RANKL-induced osteoclast formation [31]. Our data confirm that miR-148a-3p suppresses MAFB expression, thereby reducing osteoblast proliferation and differentiation—effects that are reversed by MAFB overexpression. This establishes the miR-148a-3p/MAFB axis as a critical regulatory pathway in OVF postoperative healing, consistent with previous reports that MAFB downregulation is associated with osteoporosis in postmenopausal women [32].

Our results also found that low MAFB expression in OVF patients and delayed healing cases aligns with its role in osteogenesis. Notably, the clinical relevance of our findings is strengthened by correlations between miR-148a-3p/MAFB expression and patient outcomes. Delayed healing cases exhibited higher miR-148a-3p and lower MAFB levels, which paralleled the inverse correlation (r= -0.803) observed in clinical samples. This mirrors similar regulatory patterns in other fracture types, such as miR-1271-5p/ZBTB7A in pilon fractures [33] and lncRNA CASC11/miR-150-3p in tibial fractures [34], suggesting conserved non-coding RNA networks in bone repair. In clinical practice, this conservative regulatory mode provides a new direction for the diagnosis and treatment of fracture healing. At the diagnostic level, the combined detection of the expression levels of miR-148a-3p and MAFB can serve as molecular markers for evaluating postoperative healing in OVF patients. Therapeutically, targeted intervention against the miR-148a-3p/MAFB axis is expected to emerge as a new strategy for promoting OVF healing.

However, the limitation lies in that the mechanism was only verified through cell experiments, lacking validation in animal models. Additionally, other potential signaling pathways involved in the regulation have not been further explored. Subsequent studies aim to establish an OVF animal model, and through in vivo regulation of the expression of miR-148a-3p or MAFB, validate their in vivo effects and regulatory mechanisms during fracture healing.

In summary, our findings demonstrate that miR-148a-3p inhibits osteoblast proliferation and differentiation by targeting MAFB, thereby contributing to delayed OVF healing. This identifies miR-148a-3p as a potential prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target, offering new insights into the molecular mechanisms governing post-OVF healing.

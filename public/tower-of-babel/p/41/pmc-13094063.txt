Assessing the non-inferiority of a 10-day compared with a 14-day vonoprazan–amoxicillin dual therapy regimen for H. pylori eradication: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Azzam Zrineh, Rami Akwan, Muhammad M Elsharkawy, Mostafa Adel T Mahmoud, Hadeel Ayesh, Mazen Momtaz Shehata, Hamza Karmi, Elsayed S Moubarak
Published: 2026

However, the optimal treatment duration remains uncertain, with important implications for patient adherence, adverse events, and antimicrobial resistance. Background

Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) is a spiral-shaped, gram-negative bacterium that colonizes the gastric mucosa and has uniquely adapted to survive in the acidic environment of the stomach [1, 2]. This infection affects approximately 50% of the global population and remains a major cause of peptic ulcer disease, gastric cancer, and mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma [3, 4]. H. pylori is typically acquired during childhood and, if left untreated, persists throughout life [3, 5].

The successful eradication of H. pylori significantly reduces the risk of serious complications and is therefore highly recommended [6–9]. However, achieving optimal eradication remains challenging due to the increasing prevalence of antibiotic resistance. This issue is further exacerbated by host genetic factors, such as CYP2C19 polymorphisms, along with suboptimal treatment strategies, ultimately resulting in a progressive decline in eradication success rates [10, 11].

Triple therapy, which includes a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) together with clarithromycin and either amoxicillin or metronidazole, has long served as the standard first-line regimen for the treatment of H. pylori. However, its effectiveness has progressively declined worldwide, largely due to the rising prevalence of antibiotic resistance, particularly to clarithromycin [12]. Consequently, alternative treatment regimens, such as quadruple therapy, have been proposed to enhance eradication [13, 14]. Recent clinical guidelines from the American College of Gastroenterology [15] recommend bismuth quadruple therapy (BQT) for 14 days as the preferred first-line regimen for treatment-naive patients with H. pylori infection when antibiotic susceptibility is unknown.

Vonoprazan (TAK-438), a novel potassium-competitive acid blocker (P-CAB), has emerged as a promising alternative to PPIs in H. pylori eradication regimens [16, 17]. Unlike conventional PPIs, vonoprazan provides rapid, potent, and sustained gastric acid suppression that is independent of CYP2C19 genetic polymorphisms, creating optimal conditions for antibiotic efficacy against H. pylori [18, 19]. This superior acid suppression profile has led to growing interest in vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy (VAT) as a simplified, effective treatment approach that may reduce antibiotic resistance concerns while maintaining high eradication rates [20–22].

However, despite the pharmacological advantages of vonoprazan-based therapy, the optimal duration of VAT remains a subject of ongoing clinical debate. Although a 14-day regimen has traditionally been favored based on general principles of H. pylori management, the potent acid suppression provided by vonoprazan offers a theoretical rationale for shorter treatment courses. Consequently, the choice between 10-day and 14-day therapy has important clinical and public health implications. Shorter treatment durations may improve patient adherence, reduce healthcare costs, and potentially minimize antibiotic exposure and resistance development. Conversely, inadequate treatment duration may increase the risk of eradication failure, necessitating rescue therapy and potentially promoting resistance.

Therefore, we conducted this systematic review and meta-analysis to clarify whether a 10-day regimen can be adopted without compromising treatment outcomes. We aimed to compare 10-day versus 14-day vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy regimens in adults with H. pylori infection, with particular focus on assessing the non-inferiority of the shorter treatment duration in terms of eradication efficacy and safety. We hypothesized that the 10-day regimen would achieve comparable eradication rates without affecting adverse events or treatment compliance. Ultimately, our goal is to provide evidence-based insights that can guide the optimization of H. pylori eradication strategies in the era of increasing antibiotic resistance.

Methods and materials

In preparing this review, we adhered to the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines. The study was conducted in line with the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions [23], and the study protocol was prospectively registered on PROSPERO (CRD420251156272).

Review question

Is 10-day vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy non-inferior to 14-day therapy for Helicobacter pylori eradication in adults?

Studies satisfying the following criteria were included in this review:

Population: Adults diagnosed with H. pylori infection using validated diagnostic methods (urea breath test, stool antigen test, rapid urease test, or histological examination).

Outcome measures: Studies reporting H. pylori eradication rates confirmed by validated methods such as urea breath test, stool antigen test, or rapid urease test, performed at least 4 weeks after treatment completion.

Studies were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: 1- Involved pediatric populations; 2- Included patients who had undergone H. pylori eradication therapy within the previous 6 months; 3- Were non-randomized or observational studies; 4- Were conference abstracts, posters, or letters to the editor; 5- Were not published in English.

Information sources and search strategy

A comprehensive search was conducted up to September 27, 2025, across the following electronic databases: PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials. The search strategy combined terms and keywords related to H. pylori, vonoprazan, amoxicillin, and treatment duration (10 or 14 days). The comprehensive search query for each database is provided in [Supplementary File 1].

Screening and study selection process

We utilized Rayyan for semi-automated screening of the literature search results [24]. The screening process consisted of two phases: an initial screening of titles and abstracts to identify potential studies, followed by a full-text assessment for eligibility. Three independent reviewers (M.S, H.K, and H.A) screened all records against predefined criteria. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion, with a fourth reviewer (A.Z) consulted when needed. The study selection process was tracked and documented using a PRISMA flowchart.

Data extraction

For all included studies, three reviewers (M.M, H.A, and H.K) independently extracted data using a standardized Excel spreadsheet. Extracted data included: 1- study characteristics (design, setting, sample size, etc.), 2- baseline characteristics of study populations (age, gender, lifestyle factors such as smoking and alcohol use, etc.), 3- intervention details (dosing, duration, etc.), and 4- outcome data relevant to our review. For multi-arm studies, only the relevant intervention and comparator arms were extracted for baseline characteristics and subsequent analyses.

Risk of bias assessment

The methodological quality of included studies was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB-2) tool [25]. Two reviewers (M.M and M.S) independently evaluated five domains: randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of outcomes, and selection of reported results. Studies were classified as low risk, some concerns, or high risk of bias for each domain and overall. Any discrepancies were resolved through discussion and consensus with a third reviewer (M.M.E) for accuracy and consistency.

Publication bias

Following Egger et al.‘s recommendations [26], publication bias was not assessed because our review included only 7 studies, which is below the threshold for a funnel plot analysis.

Statistical analysis

Our Primary endpoint was the eradication rate of H. pylori. Secondary endpoints included adverse events and compliance rates. The pooled effect measure for the eradication rate was risk difference (RD) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). For secondary endpoints, the risk ratio (RR) with 95% CI was used. The Random Effects model was applied for all analyses to account for variability, such as that in methodologies and durations across the assessed trials. Following the recommendations by Seokyung Hahn [27], the analyses and subgrouping were based on the following populations: intention-to-treat analysis (ITT), which included all randomized participants irrespective of study completion or adherence, modified analysis (mITT), which included participants who received at least one dose of the regimen and had their eradication outcome assessed, and finally per-protocol analysis (PP), which included mITT participants who had good compliance to their full course of the assigned regimen.

This approach allows for confirmation of non-inferiority only if ITT and PP analyses lead to the same results. Additionally, the non-inferiority margin of -0.1 (-10%) was adopted from the included studies. This conservative margin represents a small difference in efficacy that is considered clinically acceptable and ensures that a short 10-day regimen is not less effective than standard 14-day regimen. Non-inferiority was concluded if the lower bound of the 95% CI for the risk difference in eradication rates between the two regimens exceeded the prespecified margin of -10%.

Heterogeneity across studies was reported using the Chi-squared test (x2) and I² statistics, where I² (30% to 60%) was considered moderate, and above that range was considered substantially heterogeneous. Tests for subgroup differences were performed to observe any variability between subgroups.

In the study by Peng et al. [28], the two 10-day therapy arms differing in amoxicillin dosage were combined into a pooled single 10-day group for comparison with the 14-day group. For Yang et al. [29], it was excluded from quantitative analysis due to potential data duplication with Dong et al. [30], as both studies share the same trial registration ID (NCT05469685). Since the authors did not respond to our inquiry, the decision was made to include the study by Yang et al. [29] narratively.

All analyses were conducted using Review Manager 5.4 and in accordance with the prespecified statistical plan and recommendations based on the Cochrane handbook for systematic reviews of interventions.

Results

Study selection

Our comprehensive literature search identified 403 articles across all databases. Following automated de-duplication, only 340 unique articles underwent primary and secondary screening. Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, seven studies were deemed eligible for our review. The Selection process is outlined in Fig. 1.

PRISMA flow diagram

Study characteristics

A total of seven randomized trials were identified, with a combined population of 3486 participants. All were conducted in China and employed a non-inferiority design with a margin of -10%. The studies’ durations ranged from seven months to one year, with one study extending to three years [31] and another that did not explicitly report its duration [29]. Three of the trials evaluated whether a 10-day regimen (20 mg of vonoprazan twice daily and 1 g of amoxicillin three times daily) was non-inferior to a 14-day regimen [30, 32, 33]. Three additional trials compared 10-day and 14-day vonoprazan/amoxicillin dual therapy with alternative regimens, including a 10-day lower-dose VAT therapy in which the amoxicillin dose was reduced to 2000 mg [28], or 14-day esomeprazole/amoxicillin dual therapy (EAT) [31], or 14-day bismuth quadruple therapy (BQT) [29]. Huang et al. [34] conducted a five-arm trial employing Simon’s two-stage optimal design. The 7-day VAT and 14-day BQT-V [with vonoprazan as the acid suppressor] were terminated after the first stage because their eradication rates were below the predefined minimum acceptable rate of 80%. The remaining arms (10-day VAT, 14-day VAT, and 14-day BQT) continued into the second stage. H. pylori eradication was confirmed by either 13 or 14 C urea breath test (UBT) performed 4–8 weeks after treatment completion. Four studies included treatment-naïve patients only [28, 31, 33, 34], while the remaining enrolled both treatment-naïve and previously treated participants, defined as those who received prior therapy more than six months before enrolment without any success [29, 30, 32]. Among the VAT arms, smoking prevalence was relatively low and comparable between the 10-day and 14-day groups, ranging from 9% to 20%. Similarly, alcohol consumption was balanced between groups, ranging from approximately 4% to 29%. Regarding age, the youngest cohort was reported by Peng et al. [28], while the oldest was reported by Yang et al. [29]. A Summary of the included studies and baseline characteristics of VAT arms is presented in detail in Tables 1 and 2.

Quality assessment

Three studies [30, 33, 34] were assessed as having a low risk across all domains. On the other hand, three studies [28, 31, 32] were judged to have some concerns overall due to bias in one domain each. In Lin et al. [32], randomization was performed using the R program with the permuted blocks method; however, information about concealment was not reported, which raised some concerns. Similarly, Yan et al. [31] had some concerns in domain one, arising from insufficient reporting of randomization and allocation concealment. Additionally, the Peng et al. [28] study had bias in domain 5 (reporting of results). While primary endpoint and adverse events were reported by the authors as prespecified in the protocol, a prespecified secondary outcome assessing medical financial burden was not reported in the manuscript or as supplementary, with no explanation. Finally, the Yang et al. [29] study was assessed with a high risk overall. It lacked information regarding randomization and allocation concealment. Furthermore, missing outcome data were imbalanced across groups, with the highest rate of missed visits and adverse events observed in the Bismuth-rabeprazole group. This may reflect contextually related factors to the regimen. Although it was stated that PP and ITT analyses using the last observation carried forward method (LOCF) were applied to account for missing data, the results of the primary endpoint were solely based on the PP approach, and the compliance rate was not reported as stated. These Methodological inconsistencies raised concerns across multiple domains, resulting in an overall high risk of bias for this study. Details are seen in Fig. 2.

Eradication rate

Across studies, the total number of events (successful eradication) was 1198 vs. 1098 in the ITT analysis and 1138 vs. 1022 in the PP analysis for the 10-day and 14-day groups, respectively. The pooled risk difference for the ITT population was − 0.03 (95% CI [ -0.06 to -0.01], P-value = 0.008), indicating that the 10-day regimen had a slightly lower eradication rate compared to the 14-day regimen and that this difference was statistically significant in the conventional sense. However, the lower bound of the confidence interval (-0.06) was above the prespecified non-inferiority margin of -0.1, confirming that the 10-day course is not less effective relative to the 14-day course. Similarly, the PP pooled analysis yielded a difference of -0.04 (95% CI [-0.06 to -0.01], P-value = 0.009), also confirming non-inferiority by the same interpretation.

No heterogeneity was observed in the pooled ITT approach; however, for the pooled PP approach, a small to moderate heterogeneity of I2 = 34% was noted. Additionally, no difference between the two approaches was observed, as the test for subgroup differences was I2 = 0%.

Concerning the Yang et al. study [29], the eradication rate was evaluated using per-protocol analysis, yielding success rates of 91.6% and 92.5% for the 10-day and 14-day regimens, respectively, though not statistically significant (P > 0.05). Results are demonstrated in Fig. 3.

Compliance and adverse events rates

Medication compliance was assessed by pill count, with good compliance defined as taking 80% or more of the pills and poor compliance as taking fewer than 80%. Subgroup analyses showed minimal differences in compliance with ITT studies slightly favoring the 14-day regimen (RR = 1.02, 95% CI [0.99, 1.04]) and mITT studies slightly favoring the 10-day regimen (RR = 0.99, 95% CI [0.97, 1.01]); however, these differences were not statistically significant with P = 0.16 and 0.21, respectively. The overall pooled risk ratio of both approaches indicated no difference in compliance (RR = 1.00, 95% CI [0.98, 1.02], P = 0.88). Heterogeneity across all studies was low to moderate (I2 = 31%), while the test for subgroup differences was substantial (I2 = 71.6%).

Adverse events were generally mild across all studies and most commonly included digestive symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and diarrhea, along with weakness, palpitation, and headaches, among others. In the ITT-based subgroup (four studies), the risk of adverse events in the 10-day arm was relatively lower by 2% compared to the 14-day arm (RR = 0.98, 95% CI [0.66, 1.45]), although this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.90). On the other hand, in the mITT-based subgroup (two studies), the 10-day regimen showed a 12% lower risk of adverse events relative to the 14-day regimen (RR = 0.88, 95% CI [0.67, 1.16]); however, it was also statistically insignificant (P = 0.37). The overall pooled estimate combining both subgroups yielded a risk ratio of 0.95 and a p-value of 0.67. Heterogeneity was substantial in the ITT-based analysis (I2 = 68%) and absent in the mITT-based analysis (I2 = 0%). Overall moderate heterogeneity was observed across all studies (I2 = 50%), while the test for subgroup differences between the ITT and mITT subgroups was (I2 = 0).

In Yang et al. [29], the incidence of adverse events was assessed based on the ITT population, with a rate of 8.5% (15/200) in the 10-day group and 9.5% (17/ 200) in the 14-day group, though no significant difference between them was shown (P > 0.05). As for the compliance rate, it was not reported. Forest plots are shown in Figs. 4 and 5.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the most comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating treatment duration optimization for vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy in H. pylori eradication. Our analysis of six randomized controlled trials encompassing 2587 participants in ITT analysis and 2358 participants in PP analysis demonstrated that the 10-day therapy was non-inferior to the 14-day therapy, with pooled risk differences of -0.03 (95% CI [-0.06 to -0.01], P = 0.008) and − 0.04 (95% CI [-0.06 to -0.01], P = 0.009), respectively. Analysis of secondary outcomes showed slightly fewer adverse events with the 10-day regimen compared to the 14-day regimen; although the difference was not significant (RR = 0.95, P = 0.67). Moreover, treatment compliance did not differ between the two groups (RR = 1.00, P = 0.88).

The heterogeneity patterns across outcomes provided important insights into study consistency. For eradication rates, ITT analysis showed no heterogeneity (I² = 0%) while PP analysis demonstrated low to moderate heterogeneity (I² = 34%); although both approaches included the same studies. These differences likely reflect variations in event counts and study weighting after excluding participants with poor adherence. Importantly, the test for subgroup differences was homogeneous (I² = 0%), indicating that analysis type did not materially influence the overall effect estimate. Compliance analysis revealed negligible within-subgroup heterogeneity [ITT (I² = 1%), mITT (I² = 0%)] but increased to mild heterogeneity (I²=31%) when both subgroups were pooled. Subsequently, the test for subgroup differences showed high heterogeneity (I² = 71.6%), suggesting that analysis type may substantially influence observed compliance rates and thus cautioning against pooling without stratification. Adverse events showed pronounced variability within the ITT-based subgroup (I² = 68%), while the mITT-based subgrouping was homogeneous (I² = 0%). Despite having no variability between subgroups, an overall moderate heterogeneity (I² = 50%) across all studies was observed. This indicates that most variability originated from studies employing the ITT-based approach, which may be attributed to different adverse event reporting methods and event counts.

Vonoprazan represents a transformative paradigm shift in H. pylori eradication therapy, addressing three fundamental inadequacies that have plagued conventional PPI-based regimens: slow onset of pharmacological action in acidic gastric conditions, instability in the acidic gastric environment, and problematic variations from CYP2C19 genetic polymorphisms [35]. Conventional PPIs are prodrugs requiring acid activation that achieve pH > 4 for only 30.4–43.1% of dosing intervals and necessitate 3–5 days to reach maximal acid suppression [16]. In contrast, vonoprazan enters gastric parietal cells in active form, demonstrates stability in acidic gastric secretory canaliculi, and achieves maximal acid suppression within 1 day, elevating gastric pH up to 7 within approximately 4 h and maintaining pH > 4 for 62.7–63.3% of the dosing interval [16].

To date, clinical use of vonoprazan-based therapies has largely been confined to East Asian countries. Meta-analyses of trials from this region have shown that triple therapy with vonoprazan, amoxicillin, and clarithromycin achieves significantly higher eradication rates than PPI-based triple regimens [36], even among patients harboring clarithromycin-resistant strains [37]. A recent network meta-analysis evaluating numerous first-line therapies found that vonoprazan triple therapy produced higher eradication rates than PPI-clarithromycin triple therapy (odds ratio 3.80) and ranked highest in comparative effectiveness among all assessed first-line options [38]. In Japanese trials of vonoprazan dual therapy, eradication rates reached as high as 93%, mirroring the outcomes seen with vonoprazan triple therapy [39–41].

Furthermore, Zhou et al. [42] conducted a meta-analysis of five randomized controlled trials, revealing that vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy (VAT) yielded a significantly lower eradication rate when compared to vonoprazan-amoxicillin-clarithromycin (VAC) triple therapy in the ITT analysis (RR = 0.94, 95% CI: 0.88–0.99, P = 0.03), though no significant difference was observed in PP analysis (RR = 0.96, 95% CI: 0.91–1.01, P = 0.11). On the other hand, when compared to PPI-based triple therapy (PAC), vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy exhibited superior eradication rates in both ITT analysis (RR = 1.13, 95% CI: 1.04–1.23, P = 0.003) and PP analysis (RR = 1.14, 95% CI: 1.06–1.22, P = 0.0004). Importantly, the VAT regimen was associated with a similar incidence of total adverse events and patient compliance when compared to both VAC and PAC triple therapies [42].

Research in Western populations remains limited and has shown more variable results. For instance, research conducted in Europe and the US revealed that the eradication rate for the 14-day VA dual therapy was only 78.5% for full analysis and 81.2% for PP analysis [43]. The disparity in treatment outcomes may reflect variations in physique, diversity in H. pylori strains, and differences in CYP3A4 metabolic activity [30]. While PPIs rely mainly on CYP2C19 metabolism, vonoprazan is processed by multiple enzymes, including CYP3A4/5 [16, 44]. Genetic profiles associated with slower CYP2C19 metabolism are more frequent in Asian populations than in White populations and have been linked to improved eradication success with certain PPI-based regimens [45, 46]. However, because vonoprazan pharmacokinetics do not differ significantly by race, these differences are unlikely to be due to ethnicity alone [43, 47, 48]. Other factors, such as treatment adherence (which was lower among Western participants) and body weight (which was higher among Western participants), may also influence outcomes [43]. Successful H. pylori eradication with vonoprazan dual therapy was linked to a smaller body surface area [49].

Nowadays, the cumulative clinical evidence positions VA dual therapy as an increasingly attractive first-line therapeutic option for H. pylori eradication, particularly in geographic regions characterized by elevated rates of clarithromycin and metronidazole resistance, where traditional triple therapy regimens demonstrate suboptimal efficacy. Importantly, the therapeutic efficacy of this dual therapy is largely attributed to vonoprazan’s distinctive pharmacological characteristics as a novel potassium-competitive acid blocker (P-CAB), characterized by rapid onset and sustained acid suppression, in contrast to conventional PPIs [50–53]. The profound and sustained elevation of intragastric pH creates optimal conditions for amoxicillin, a time-dependent, beta-lactam antibiotic requiring near-neutral pH environments for maximal bactericidal activity against Helicobacter pylori [32, 54].

Beyond pharmacological characteristics, clinicians should carefully consider patient-specific factors when selecting the optimal duration, recognizing that they may influence therapy’s success. For instance, the study by Dong et al. [30] found that eradication success in the 10-day regimen was influenced by smoking and previous eradication attempts, whereas in the 14-day regimen, outcomes were affected by gender, smoking, alcohol intake, and prior education efforts. Song et al. [33] found that poor compliance acted as an independent factor affecting eradication success across both 10-day and 14-day treatment durations. Conversely, the study did not find any other factors influencing therapy’s status [33].

Strengths and limitations

This systematic review demonstrates several methodological strengths enhancing the reliability and validity of our findings. Our comprehensive literature search across multiple databases maximized the identification of relevant studies. Moreover, all included studies employed randomized controlled trial designs. Our protocol was prospectively registered on PROSPERO, ensuring transparency and reducing selective outcome reporting risk. Our analytical strategy included both approaches (ITT and PP analyses) in addition to subgroup analysis of secondary outcomes based on the type of analysis performed. Additionally, the large sample size (2587 participants in the ITT population and 2358 in the PP population) enabled more precise effect estimates. Finally, our comprehensive approach to addressing heterogeneity across studies and between subgroups further strengthens confidence in our conclusions.

However, several limitations warrant acknowledgment. First and most importantly, all studies were conducted in China, limiting generalizability to other ethnic groups and geographical regions. Second, antibiotic susceptibility testing was not routinely performed across the included trials, precluding analysis of treatment efficacy stratified by amoxicillin resistance status. Third, the use of open-label designs represents a limitation that may bias secondary outcomes. Adverse events were mainly reported by participants, making them susceptible to detection bias, which may have inflated or underestimated the severity and the reporting of the side effects. On the other hand, although compliance was assessed objectively by counting pills consumed at the end of the trials, it may also have been influenced by the participants’ awareness of their treatment, potentially influencing adherence behavior and introducing some performance bias. Fourth, while our review focused primarily on treatment duration, it is important to acknowledge the presence of dose and frequency heterogeneity across the included studies. Vonoprazan was administered at 20 mg twice daily in most studies, though some protocols used alternative dosing. Similarly, amoxicillin dosing varied between 750 mg four times daily, 1000 mg three times daily, and 1000 mg twice daily in the included studies. This heterogeneity reflects real-world variation in clinical practice and may influence both efficacy and tolerability. However, meta-regression or subgroup analyses stratified by specific dose/frequency combinations were not feasible due to the limited number of studies and potential confounding with other characteristics. Finally, in quality assessment, three trials yielded some concerns overall, and one study was rated as high risk overall, which should also be acknowledged as a potential limitation.

Future research directions

Several research priorities emerge from our meta-analysis to implement the 10-day vonoprazan-amoxicillin regimen globally. International multicentre randomized controlled trials comparing 10-day versus 14-day treatment in Western, African, and other non-Asian populations are needed, with stratification by ethnicity, CYP2C19 genotype, and body mass index if possible. In addition, we recommend that future studies incorporate intragastric pH monitoring to assess acid suppression as well as routine antibiotic sensitivity testing to better correlate these factors with treatment outcomes and duration. Moreover, studies comparing high and low dose VAT and different frequencies are warranted to further determine optimal dosing that minimizes the adverse events while enhancing patient compliance. Finally, a comprehensive cost-effectiveness analysis that incorporates both health and societal perspectives across diverse populations would be valuable in informing evidence-based resource allocation decisions.

Conclusions

Our meta-analysis suggests that 10-day vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy is non-inferior to the 14-day regimen for H. pylori eradication, with comparable safety profiles and treatment compliance. The observed performance of the 10-day regimen challenges traditional assumptions about the minimum treatment duration necessary for eradication and indicates that potent, sustained acid suppression may allow for shorter antibiotic use. As we face global challenges of antibiotic resistance and treatment optimization, vonoprazan-based regimens offer a rational, evidence-informed option to effective H. pylori eradication. However, the geographic concentration of available data mandates caution in universal application. Therefore, future research in diverse populations will be essential to confirm whether the advantages observed in Asian populations translate globally. Until then, 10-day vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual therapy may be a practical and convenient option, particularly in regions where its use has been supported by existing evidence.

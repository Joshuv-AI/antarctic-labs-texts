CardioRhythm Congress 2025: Marking a milestone, embracing a new chapter
Authors: Mervat Aboulmaaty, Muzahir H. Tayebjee, Hani Assaad, Felix Sogade
Published: 2025

The Africa Heart Rhythm Association (AFHRA) has grown significantly over the last 2 years after a pivotal structural reorganization. This has catalyzed a new era of growth, collaboration, and educational leadership in cardiac electrophysiology throughout Africa. As we reflect on this transformative period, AFHRA has emerged not only as a continental force but also as a global thought leader in the field of heart rhythm management.

Expanding education and building capacity

Since its reorganization, AFHRA has significantly expanded its educational outreach—a wide array of programs that were delivered both virtually and in person. The educational content has been developed to meet the evolving needs of clinicians, trainees, and allied health professionals across the continent. These initiatives span foundational training in pacing and ablation techniques, specialized programs in pediatric electrophysiology, and tailored workshops for the multidisciplinary teams involved in rhythm care. This education-first approach reflects AFHRA’s core commitment to building long-term capacity and clinical excellence in both adult and pediatric arrhythmia management. The organization has also begun various partnerships to enhance recognition of arrhythmia disorders and advocate for wider access to these services.

Strengthening global partnerships

Central to AFHRA’s mission is its emphasis on collaboration, both within Africa and with the wider international electrophysiology community. The association’s strategic partnerships with organizations such as the Heart Rhythm Society have enabled the delivery of high-impact, jointly hosted scientific sessions. These collaborations facilitate the bidirectional exchange of knowledge, promote research engagement, and have increased the visibility and integration of African rhythm science on the global stage.

Such initiatives highlight AFHRA’s evolving role as a convening authority and thought partner in the global electrophysiology ecosystem, fostering a shared commitment to equity, innovation, and scientific excellence.

CardioRhythm Congress 2025: A platform for progress

Against this backdrop of growth and collaboration, we now look ahead to CardioRhythm Congress 2025, scheduled for Kigali, Rwanda, from September 18 to 20, 2025. This meeting marks a pivotal convergence of momentum, achievement, and vision for the organization. Far more than a scientific symposium, the congress serves as a platform to celebrate progress, consolidate shared learning, and define the next chapter for arrhythmia care in Africa.

Delegates from across all regions of Africa—as well as global leaders in rhythm science—will come together to present original research, discuss innovations in clinical practice, and align regional and international priorities. The congress stands as a reflection of AFHRA’s strategic trajectory and its positioning as a key driver of the future of electrophysiology worldwide.

Leadership transition and strategic vision

This congress also marks an important transition in AFHRA leadership. We extend our deepest gratitude to Professor Mervat Aboulmaaty, whose tenure as president has been characterized by visionary leadership, collaborative engagement, and tireless advocacy for the advancement of rhythm care in Africa. Under her guidance, AFHRA has not only expanded its operational scope but has firmly established itself as an authoritative voice in global discussions on electrophysiology.

As we welcome Professor Felix Sogade as the incoming president, we look to the future with confidence. His deep expertise, longstanding commitment to mentorship, and global perspective will undoubtedly continue to strengthen AFHRA’s efforts in education, access to care, and research development across the continent and beyond.

Scientific engagement and innovation

The abstracts featured in this issue represent the scientific vigor and innovation that define the AFHRA community. They reflect a growing research culture, an eagerness to contribute to global literature, and a commitment to solving regionally relevant challenges with globally informed solutions. The breadth and depth of the presented work underscore Africa’s expanding footprint in rhythm science.

Looking forward

The journey of the past 2 years demonstrates what is possible when vision, collaboration, and commitment converge. AFHRA’s role as a global thought leader is not defined solely by geographic scope but by the depth of its impact on education, research, and the lived experiences of patients across diverse health care settings.

CardioRhythm 2025 is both a celebration and a call to action. As we gather in Kigali, we reaffirm our collective dedication to advancing arrhythmia care—not only across Africa but for the benefit of the global electrophysiology community. Together, we are shaping the rhythm of tomorrow.

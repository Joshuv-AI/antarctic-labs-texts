Actionable health system and policy strategies for the prevention and mitigation of non-communicable diseases: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Veena G Nair, Chandrashekar Janakiram, Chinnu Sara Varughese, Varsha Rajan, Aneesh S, Anjana Ajith, Tony T George, Vishnu M, Geo Saji, Anjali Jayanthilal, Aswathy Sreedevi, Bipin Gopal, Abey Sushan, Mahesh N, Aarati Krishnan, Jaideep C Menon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionNon-communicable diseases (NCDs), including cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, cancers and chronic respiratory diseases, account for a substantial proportion of global mortality and morbidity and represent a major challenge for the health system worldwide. Effective prevention and mitigation of NCDs requires coordinated strategies that combine health system interventions with population-level policies targeting key risk factors such as tobacco use, harmful alcohol consumption, unhealthy diets and physical inactivity. Evidence on actionable strategies implemented across diverse health systems and policy contexts remains fragmented. This scoping review aims to map the range, nature and implementation contexts of actionable health system and policy strategies for the prevention and mitigation of NCDs.Methods and analysisThe review will follow the methodology outlined by the Joanna Briggs Institute for scoping reviews and will be reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) guidelines. A comprehensive search will be conducted across MEDLINE (via PubMed), Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, CINAHL, PsycINFO and Global Health. Grey literature sources such as government reports, national NCD strategies and documents from international organisations will also be searched to capture policy and implementation evidence. Studies involving populations at risk of or living with NCDs and describing health system or policy interventions aimed at prevention or mitigation will be eligible. Study selection will be conducted in two stages—title and abstract screening followed by full-text review—by two independent reviewers using predefined criteria based on the Population–Concept–Context framework. Data will be extracted using a standardised charting form capturing bibliographic information, study characteristics, intervention types, implementation contexts and outcomes, then summarised by geographical region, study design and intervention type. A thematic synthesis will categorise interventions into major domains such as health system-based strategies and population-level policy interventions. Findings will be presented through narrative summaries, tables and evidence maps.Ethics and disseminationThis scoping review involves only secondary analysis of published and publicly available literature, and does not involve primary data collection, so no ethical approval is required. The findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journal publications and conference presentations.Keywords: Health policy, Primary Health Care, PUBLIC HEALTH, Hypertension, DIABETES & ENDOCRINOLOGY

Introduction

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) continue to be the leading cause of mortality and morbidity globally across geographies and income settings. According to the WHO, NCDs are responsible for approximately 74% of all deaths worldwide, with one third of these deaths occurring prematurely. Cardiovascular diseases constitute the largest share of these deaths, followed by cancers, chronic respiratory diseases and diabetes.1

NCDs are typically chronic, long-term conditions that involve multiple comorbidities that create sustained demands on healthcare systems, caregivers and patients.2 3 Demographic shifts, particularly population ageing, are accelerating the epidemiological transition toward NCDs globally.4 This transition requires health systems—especially in low- and middle-income countries—to adapt, as many service delivery models were historically designed to address communicable diseases and maternal and child health conditions rather than chronic conditions requiring long-term management and continuity of care.5 6

Despite their chronic nature, NCDs are amenable to prevention and control through early detection and timely management. Many NCDs share common modifiable behavioural and metabolic risk factors, including tobacco use, unhealthy diets, physical inactivity, harmful use of alcohol, hypertension and obesity. These risk factors interact across the life course and contribute simultaneously to multiple NCD outcomes.6 Evidence indicates that addressing these shared risk factors through coordinated policy measures, health system strengthening and community-based interventions can significantly reduce the incidence and complications associated with NCDs across the life course.7–10

Recognising the growing burden of NCDs, global health organisations and national governments have developed several policy frameworks and strategic initiatives to guide prevention and control efforts. The WHO Global Action Plan for the Prevention and Control of NCDs (2013–2030) provides a comprehensive roadmap for reducing behavioural risk factors, strengthening healthcare systems and improving surveillance and monitoring mechanisms.11 Similarly, the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) identify NCD prevention as a global priority. SDG Target 3.4 aims to reduce premature mortality from NCDs by one-third by 2030 through prevention and treatment.12

To support implementation, the WHO has identified cost-effective population-level interventions known as ‘Best Buys’, which include measures such as tobacco taxation, alcohol regulation, salt reduction strategies and promotion of physical activity.13 At the health system level, the WHO package of essential non-communicable disease interventions emphasises strengthening primary healthcare to enable early detection, risk assessment and long-term management of NCDs.14 Health system approaches include community-based screening programmes, task shifting among healthcare workers, strengthened referral mechanisms and the use of digital technologies for long-term disease management. Despite these frameworks, translating policy into routine practices remains a persistent challenge.15 16

In federal or decentralised governance systems, health policy responsibilities are often shared between national and subnational governments. Countries such as India, Germany and Australia operate within federal structures where national governments establish policy directions while state or provincial governments are responsible for implementation. This division of responsibility can lead to variations in priorities, financing mechanisms, strategies and outcomes across regions and the same policy framework may produce different outcomes depending on the institutional, socio-economic and political contexts in which it is implemented.17

In addition, NCD prevention strategies must be tailored to local contexts, including ethnicity, socio-cultural practices, geographical conditions, demographic structures and gender dynamics.18 A ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach is unlikely to succeed across diverse health system and economic settings, and understanding best practices across these contexts is essential for developing adaptable, context-sensitive strategies.

Existing research has examined several aspects of NCD prevention and control. Numerous systematic reviews and policy analyses have evaluated interventions such as tobacco control policies, fiscal measures targeting unhealthy foods, community-based health promotion programmes and digital health strategies for chronic disease management.5 7 9 15 19 These studies provide important insights into specific interventions and programmatic approaches. However, the broader landscape of how policy and health system strategies are implemented across diverse settings remains insufficiently synthesised.

Fragmentation between policy research and implementation research is a persistent gap: policy analysis often describes national strategies without capturing how they are operationalised within health systems, while implementation studies focus on programmatic or facility level interventions without linking them to broader governance and policy environments.15 16 20 21 This disconnect creates a persistent gap between what is recommended in policy and what is delivered in practice. Another limitation is the lack of comparative analyses across implementation settings. Many studies are organised around specific platforms—such as primary healthcare, school-based programmes, digital health initiatives or national policies—without examining how similar intervention goals are implemented across multiple settings, including communities, schools, workplaces, healthcare facilities and policy environments.5 15 19 22 Recent papers have begun to address dimensions like informing equitable NCD prevention policy by lived experience of marginalised communities, but a comprehensive mapping of actionable health system and policy strategies across diverse governance structures, income settings and implementation contexts remains absent in the literature.23

In this context, a comprehensive synthesis of policy and health system interventions implemented globally for NCD prevention and mitigation is needed. Such a synthesis can help identify common implementation strategies, governance arrangements and contextual factors that influence the success or failure of interventions across diverse settings. A scoping review is particularly suitable for this purpose because the literature on NCD prevention includes a wide range of study designs, policy analyses, programme evaluations and grey literature. This review aims to map the range and nature of actionable health system and policy strategies implemented for NCD prevention and mitigation across diverse geographical, governance and health system settings. It will examine their implementation contexts, such as funding mechanisms, health system arrangements at national and subnational levels, and the facilitators and barriers influencing their adoption.

Population

Studies and documents will be included if they involve individuals or populations at risk of or living with NCDs or health system stakeholders and communities engaged in NCD prevention or mitigation strategies (table 1).

Concept

Sources must report actionable health systems or policy interventions implemented for the prevention or mitigation of NCDs. Eligible interventions include strategies implemented within health systems, public health programmes or policy environments aimed at reducing NCD risk factors, improving early detection or strengthening integrated care models (table 1).

Context

Studies conducted within any geographical, income or health system context will be eligible for inclusion. This includes primary healthcare settings, community health programmes, schools, workplaces and district, regional or national health systems (table 1).

Type of sources

This scoping review will include a broad range of study designs and document types, consistent with JBI methodology, spanning empirical primary studies, evidence syntheses, policy analyses and grey literature (table 1).

Methods

Search strategy

The search strategy for this scoping review will be developed and implemented in accordance with the JBI Manual for Evidence Synthesis and reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) guidelines.24 A comprehensive, systematic and reproducible search process will be undertaken to identify published and unpublished evidence describing actionable health system and policy interventions implemented for the prevention and mitigation of NCDs across diverse settings and health system contexts.

In the first step, an initial limited search will be conducted in MEDLINE (via PubMed) and Scopus to identify relevant articles. Titles, abstracts and indexing terms (eg, Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and Emtree terms) from retrieved papers will be examined to refine keywords and controlled vocabulary related to NCDs, prevention strategies and policy or health system interventions. Particular attention will be given to variations in terminology describing NCDs (eg, “noncommunicable diseases,” “NCDs,” “chronic diseases,” “cardiovascular disease,” “diabetes,” “chronic respiratory disease,” “cancer”) and terminology related to prevention and mitigation strategies (eg, “prevention,” “control,” “risk reduction,” “health promotion,” “disease mitigation,” “population health interventions”). These terms will be mapped against health system and policy domains, including “primary health care,” “health system strengthening,” “service integration,” “screening programs,” “community health workers,” “public health programs,” “health policy,” “tobacco control,” “alcohol regulation,” “dietary policy,” “physical activity promotion,” “sugar-sweetened beverage taxation,” and “multisectoral action plans.”

In the second step, a comprehensive search strategy will be developed using a combination of controlled vocabulary (eg, MeSH terms such as Noncommunicable Diseases, Chronic Disease, Health Policy, Public Health Policy, Primary Health Care, Health Promotion, Preventive Health Services) and free-text keywords. Boolean operators (AND/OR), truncation (eg, prevent*, policy*, intervention*), phrase searching and proximity operators (where supported) will be used to enhance sensitivity and specificity. The final search strategy will be adapted to each database’s indexing system and syntax. Electronic databases to be searched will include MEDLINE (PubMed), Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, Embase, CINAHL, PsycINFO and Global Health to ensure comprehensive coverage of literature related to public health, health systems research and policy implementation.

In the third step, grey literature sources will be systematically searched to minimise publication bias and capture policy and implementation evidence not indexed in bibliographic databases. These sources will include repositories and websites of international organisations such as the WHO (WHO IRIS - Institutional Repository for Information Sharing), World Bank and national ministry of health websites, as well as policy reports, national NCD strategy documents and implementation frameworks produced by governmental and public health agencies. Additional sources will include ProQuest Dissertations and Theses, institutional repositories and reports from public health organisations and non-governmental organisations involved in NCD prevention and control. Google Scholar will be used to identify additional reports and relevant documents, with screening limited to the first 200 results sorted by relevance to maintain feasibility and transparency. Reference lists of all included articles will be hand-searched to identify additional eligible studies.

No date restrictions will be applied to capture both historical and contemporary approaches to NCD prevention and mitigation, although publication years will be recorded to allow mapping of trends in policy and health system interventions over time. June 2026 will be the planned starting date of this review and anticipated completion date is December 2026. Only studies published in English will be included due to feasibility constraints; this limitation will be acknowledged. The complete search strategy for MEDLINE via PubMed is presented in an online supplemental table 1 and for the grey literature is presented in an online supplemental table 2 to ensure transparency, reproducibility and methodological rigour.

Study selection

All records identified through the search strategy will be exported into a reference management software programme, Zotero,25 where duplicate citations will be identified and removed. The de-duplicated database will then be imported into the JBI System for the Unified Management, Assessment and Review of Information26 to facilitate structured screening and management of the review process. The selection process will occur in two stages: (1) title and abstract screening and (2) full-text screening.

In the first stage, two independent reviewers will screen titles and abstracts against the predefined inclusion criteria based on the PCC framework. The reviewers will assess whether the study potentially reports on health system or policy interventions aimed at the prevention or mitigation of NCDs. Studies that clearly do not meet the inclusion criteria will be excluded at this stage. A pilot screening of approximately 50–100 articles will initially be conducted to ensure consistency and clarity in the application of the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Any discrepancies between reviewers during the pilot phase will be discussed and resolved before proceeding with the full screening process.

In the second stage, the full texts of potentially eligible articles will be retrieved and assessed independently by two reviewers. Each article will be evaluated in detail against the inclusion criteria relating to population groups targeted for NCD prevention or mitigation, actionable health system or policy interventions, and relevant implementation or policy contexts. Reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage will be recorded systematically. Disagreements between reviewers at either stage will be resolved through discussion and consensus, and where consensus cannot be reached, a third reviewer will adjudicate. A PRISMA-ScR flow diagram will be used to present the number of records identified, screened, assessed for eligibility and included in the final review, along with reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage.

As this is a scoping review, methodological quality appraisal or risk-of-bias assessment will not be conducted, in accordance with JBI guidance.27 However, the type of evidence source (eg, empirical study, implementation study, policy analysis, programme evaluation, government report or international organisation report) will be documented during data charting to provide context regarding the nature, scope and diversity of the evidence base related to health system and policy interventions for NCD prevention and mitigation.

Data extraction

Data extraction in this scoping review will be conducted in accordance with the JBI methodology,27 which refers to this process as data charting. The purpose of data charting is to systematically capture and organise relevant information from included sources to enable comprehensive mapping of actionable health systems and policy interventions implemented for the prevention and mitigation of NCDs across diverse health system and policy contexts. A structured and standardised data extraction form will be developed based on the review objectives and the PCC framework. The draft form will be piloted on a small subset of included studies (approximately 5–10 articles) to ensure clarity, completeness and consistency in capturing relevant variables. Based on this pilot exercise, refinements will be made before full data extraction proceeds. In keeping with JBI guidance, the charting process will remain iterative and additional variables may be incorporated if new patterns or relevant categories emerge during the review.

Data extraction will be performed independently by two reviewers to enhance accuracy and minimise bias. Any discrepancies in extracted information will be resolved through discussion and consensus, with consultation from a third reviewer if necessary. Extracted data will be managed using Microsoft Excel or similar software to facilitate systematic organisation, coding and mapping of the evidence.

For each included source, bibliographic details (author, year, title, publication source and type of document) will be recorded. Study characteristics, including country or region of implementation, income classification where applicable (eg, low-income, middle-income or high-income country), study design and data sources will be documented to contextualise the evidence base. Attention will be given to the context in which the intervention was implemented, including the setting (eg, primary healthcare facilities, community programmes, schools, workplaces or national health systems), the level of governance (local, district, national or global) and the policy or programme environment in which the intervention operates. A structured data charting table will be developed to extract key information from included studies. The charted elements will include bibliographic information, study characteristics, population characteristics, type of health system or policy intervention, implementation context, reported outcomes and key findings. Central to the extraction process will be detailed documentation of the health system and policy interventions described. This will include identification of the type of intervention or strategy implemented, the health system platform or policy mechanism through which it was delivered and the target population or risk factor addressed (eg, tobacco use, unhealthy diet, harmful alcohol use, physical inactivity). Where available, the implementation approach or operational model used to deliver the intervention will also be captured, including aspects such as service integration, workforce strategies, multisectoral collaboration, community engagement and digital health tools. Any reported facilitators, barriers, governance mechanisms or implementation challenges will also be extracted to better understand how interventions are operationalised within different contexts.

Where reported, outcomes or indicators associated with the interventions will also be documented. These may include programmatic outcomes (eg, coverage of screening programmes, adoption of policy measures, integration of services), health outcomes (eg, reduction in risk factors) or system-level outcomes (eg, improved service delivery or policy implementation). In addition, any recommendations, lessons learnt or policy implications described by the study authors will be captured.

Consistent with JBI scoping review methodology, formal critical appraisal of methodological quality will not be undertaken, as the objective of this review is to map the scope, characteristics and implementation of available evidence rather than assess intervention effectiveness. However, the type and source of evidence will be clearly recorded to support transparent interpretation of the findings. Extracted data will subsequently be synthesised descriptively and thematically, enabling the development of an evidence map of health system and policy interventions for NCD prevention and mitigation, organised across intervention types, implementation settings and geographic contexts.

Data synthesis

Data synthesis will be conducted in accordance with the JBI guidance for scoping reviews, which emphasises descriptive, thematic and mapping approaches rather than quantitative pooling or meta-analysis. As the objective of this review is to map the range, nature and contextual variation of actionable health system and policy interventions implemented for the prevention and mitigation of NCDs, the synthesis will focus on identifying patterns, categorising intervention types and examining how these strategies vary across health system settings and policy environments.

Following completion of data charting, extracted data will first be summarised descriptively to provide an overview of the evidence base. This will include numerical summaries of the distribution of studies by year of publication, geographical region, income classification of countries, type of intervention, implementation setting (eg, primary healthcare, community programmes, schools, workplaces or national policy environments) and type of evidence source (peer-reviewed studies, programme evaluations, policy analyses, government reports and international organisation reports). These descriptive summaries will help illustrate temporal trends, geographical distribution and the concentration of evidence across different implementation contexts.

Subsequently, a thematic analysis will be conducted to identify and categorise the different types of health system and policy interventions documented across sources. Interventions will be grouped into higher-order categories based on conceptual similarity. Preliminary categories may include health system–based interventions (eg, integration of NCD services into primary healthcare, screening and early detection programmes, task-shifting and workforce strengthening initiatives, community health worker-led programmes and digital health or surveillance systems) and policy and population-level interventions (eg, tobacco control policies, alcohol regulation measures, fiscal policies such as taxation on tobacco or sugar-sweetened beverages, healthy diet and salt reduction strategies, physical activity promotion initiatives and national or multisectoral NCD action plans). These categories will remain flexible and may be refined inductively as patterns emerge during the synthesis process.

To explore variation across implementation contexts, a mapping matrix will be developed to cross-tabulate intervention types against implementation settings and jurisdictional characteristics, such as high-income versus low- and middle-income countries, national versus subnational policy initiatives and health system versus community-based programmes. This mapping approach will enable identification of patterns in how interventions are implemented across different health system structures, governance mechanisms and policy environments. Where sufficient detail is available, illustrative case examples will be narratively summarised to highlight how specific interventions were operationalised and what outcomes or implementation experiences were reported.

Although critical appraisal is not required for scoping reviews under JBI methodology, the nature and type of evidence supporting each identified intervention will be considered during interpretation to contextualise the findings. The final synthesis will present results through a combination of narrative explanations, thematic tables, conceptual diagrams and evidence mapping frameworks. This approach will enable comprehensive visualisation of the range of health system and policy interventions addressing NCD prevention and mitigation and provide insights into recurring implementation patterns and contextual differences.

The synthesis will conclude by identifying knowledge gaps, under-researched intervention domains and geographical regions with limited evidence, thereby informing future research priorities and policy development efforts. The findings will be presented narratively and supported by tables, charts and conceptual evidence maps where appropriate to enhance clarity and facilitate interpretation.

Evaluation of the performance of the Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) algorithm in predicting live weight in different goat breeds
Authors: Esra Yavuz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLive body weight is a key performance indicator in goat production, playing a crucial role in selection, feeding management, and economic efficiency. However, direct measurement of body weight under field conditions is often impractical due to limitations in equipment and labor. Therefore, reliable prediction models based on easily obtainable morphometric measurements are required. Recently, machine learning approaches have gained increasing attention in animal science due to their ability to model complex and non-linear relationships among variables.MethodsThis study evaluated the effectiveness of the Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) algorithm for predicting live body weight in Ankara, Hair, Kilis, and Honamlı goat breeds. Relationships between live body weight and morphometric traits were examined using Pearson correlation analysis. LightGBM models were developed under different training–testing split ratios (65–35%, 70–30%, 75–25%, and 80–20%). Model performance was assessed using error metrics and variable importance analyses to determine the contribution of individual body measurements to prediction accuracy.ResultsThe results showed positive and statistically significant relationships between live weight and all morphometric measurements considered (p < 0.05). In particular, basic body measurements such as body length, chest circumference, and wither height were identified as the variables that contributed most to live weight estimation at all data splitting ratios. Variable significance analyses revealed that the LightGBM algorithm exhibited stable performance and high generalization capacity under different training-test ratios. However, the contribution of variables such as sex and birth weight to model performance was found to be limited.ConclusionsIn conclusion, the LightGBM algorithm has been determined to be an effective tool for quickly, practically, and reliably estimating live weight in different goat breeds. It is considered that this approach can contribute to the development of decision support systems for selection, herd management, and performance evaluation processes in small ruminant farming.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12917-026-05701-7.

Introduction

In the livestock sector, live weight is considered a fundamental biometric parameter for monitoring animal growth performance, optimizing feeding strategies, making selection decisions, and increasing economic efficiency. Live weight is a key performance indicator, particularly in small ruminant farming, for productivity, selection, feed management, and economic evaluation. However, directly measuring live weight in field conditions is not always feasible due to equipment shortages, time constraints, and limitations related to animal welfare. Therefore, developing reliable and practical methods for indirectly estimating live weight is of great importance, especially in regions where traditional breeding systems are prevalent. Estimation models based on morphometric body measurements stand out in this context due to their ease of application and cost-effectiveness [1, 2].

While regression-based methods have been used for many years in live weight estimation, they are inadequately able to model the nonlinear relationships and complex interactions between biometric measurements. In recent years, machine learning (ML) algorithms have become increasingly common in animal production research, demonstrating superior performance in such multidimensional and nonlinear data structures. Gradient boosting-based algorithms, in particular, stand out due to their high prediction accuracy, resistance to over-learning, and ability to capture complex relationships between variables.

LightGBM (Light Gradient Boosting Machine) is an advanced application of the gradient boosting approach and has been successfully applied to many biological and agricultural modeling problems due to its computational efficiency and high accuracy rates on large and complex datasets. However, studies on the comparative evaluation of this algorithm’s live weight prediction performance in native goat breeds raised in Turkey, especially among genotypes with different morphological characteristics, are limited [3, 4].

The Angora, Hair, Kilis, and Honamlı breeds, which hold a significant place in Turkish goat breeding, exhibit distinct differences in body structure, adaptability, and production purposes. This diversity provides an important research basis for evaluating the impact of biometric measurements used in estimating live weight and the generalizability of algorithms. However, the current literature lacks comprehensive studies that directly compare the performance of advanced machine learning models based on a wide range of biometric measurements across these breeds [5, 6].

The aim of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of the LightGBM algorithm in estimating live weight in Angora, Hair, Kilis, and Honamlı goat breeds and to compare the model performance under different training-test split ratios. Additionally, the study aims to determine the relative importance levels of morphometric body measurements that are effective in live weight estimation. It is expected that the findings will contribute to the development of decision support systems for practical, fast, and reliable live weight estimation in goat breeding and reveal the potential of machine learning-based approaches in small ruminant farming.

Materials and methods

Animal material

The study was conducted at the Ondokuz Mayis University Agricultural Faculty’s Research and Application Farm. This study was conducted on Angora, Hair, Kilis, and Honamlı breeds of goats, commonly raised in Turkey. The research material consisted of healthy individuals representing different age and sex groups, kept under suitable breeding conditions. Measurements were taken in a way that did not disrupt routine breeding practices and while adhering to animal welfare principles. All animals used in the study were evaluated within the framework of ethical rules and relevant legislation.

Measurements

In this study, the body measurements of the goats were determined at birth, and of the kids at 1 month, 3 months (weaning age), and 5 months (end of fattening). Body measurements were taken using a measuring stick to measure wither height (DH), rump height (RH), body length (BL), chest width (WW), and chest depth (CH); and using a measuring tape to measure chest circumference (CC) and rump circumference (HC).

Machine learning models

LightGBM, a gradient boosting-based machine learning algorithm, was used for live weight prediction. LightGBM provides computational efficiency, especially in large and high-dimensional datasets, thanks to its leaf-wise growth strategy and histogram-based data processing approach. The model was structured considering hyperparameters such as leaf number, maximum depth, and learning rate [7, 8].

Performance evaluation criteria

The prediction success of the models was evaluated using statistical performance metrics commonly used for regression problems. In this context, the coefficient of determination (R²), root mean square error (RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE) were used. Higher R² and lower RMSE and MAE values indicated the success of the model in live weight prediction. The performance of the LightGBM algorithm was analyzed comparatively under different training-test split ratios, both for the general dataset and for each breed [9].

Statistical analysis

All statistical assessments were completed using R and Spyder software [10, 11]. Descriptive statistics were used to obtain detailed information about the data set. Descriptive statistics for explanatory and response variables were performed using the ‘psych’ package of the R program [12]. For a visual representation of the relationship between explanatory and response variables, Pearson correlation analysis was performed using the ‘corrplot’ package in R [13]. The ‘LightGBM’ package was used to apply the LightGBM algorithm used to estimate BWfromthe body measurements [14]. In addition, the Python programming language was used to create 3D surface graphics and analyses were performed via the Spyder interface. In this context, thempl_toolkits.mplot3d module of the Matplotlib library in the Python programming language was used for drawing 3D surface graphics.

Results

Examining the Pearson correlation coefficients given in Fig. 1, it is seen that there are positive and statistically significant relationships between live weight and all the measures considered (p < 0.05). This indicates that the increase in body measurements is proportional to the increase in live weight. According to Fig. 1, the high correlation of 0.97 between WH and RH shows that these two measurements are closely related. In addition, the high correlations (0.94) between the BDL measure and BL and CG show that these measurements have a strong relationship with the overall body size of the animals.

These findings provide important data for use in animal breeding and selective breeding programs, and reveal how these measurements are related to each other under genetic or environmental influences. Understanding such relationships is critical for developing breeding strategies.

For the 65–35% and 70–30% splitting ratios, the learning rate was set to 0.01, while the number of trees (nrounds) was kept constant at 300, the maximum tree depth (max_depth) at 2, and the sampling rate (subsample) at 1; the colsample_bytree parameter was also set to 0.7 for both ratios. For the 75–25% training-test ratio, the learning rate was increased to 0.05 to improve the model’s generalization ability, the colsample_bytree value was optimized at 0.75, and the subsample ratio at 0.85; however, the nrounds and max_depth parameters were kept at 300 and 2, respectively. These optimal hyperparameter combinations reveal that different data splitting ratios affect model learning dynamics, and that the LightGBM algorithm exhibits stable and adaptive performance in live weight estimation in goat breeds.

The goodness of fit criteria results according to optimum hyperparameter values

The performance of the LightGBM algorithm, used for estimating live weight in goat breeds, was evaluated using goodness-of-fit criteria for different training-test split ratios (65–35%, 70–30%, and 75–25%). At a split ratio of 65–35%, the model showed acceptable prediction performance with R²=0.86 and R²=0.81 for the training and test data, respectively. However, when the error measures and information criteria were examined, the generalization success was found to be limited. At a training-test ratio of 70–30%, the closeness of the R² values in the training and test sets (0.86 and 0.85) and the low MAE and RMSE values indicated that the model exhibited balanced and stable performance. At a split ratio of 75–25%, the R² value reaching 0.86 for the test set and the relatively low error measures showed an increase in the model’s prediction accuracy. However, the increase in information criteria indicates an increase in model complexity.

Thus, it was determined that the LightGBM algorithm offers high accuracy and sufficient generalization success in estimating live weight in goat breeds, especially at training-test split ratios of 70–30% and 75–25%.

Table 3 shows that the learning rate was examined in the range of 0.01–0.10 with increments of 0.001, while the num_leaves and min_data_in_leaf parameters were evaluated in the range of 10–100 with increments of 5 units. These determined ranges aim to balance both the learning capacity and the risk of overlearning of the model. It was determined that the values obtained as a result of hyperparameter optimization were effective in increasing the prediction accuracy and generalization ability of the model at different training-test split ratios.

Figure 2 shows that for 65%-35% training test ratios, the variable significance levels (gain) calculated by the LightGBM model reveal the contributions of morphological characteristics used in predicting live weight in goat breeds. Accordingly, body length was determined to be the variable with the highest relative significance (31%) and was found to play a significant role in predicting live weight. This was followed by chest girth (28%) and withers height (18%). Chest depth (12%) and chest width (10%) contributed moderately, while rump height (2%) had a limited effect on model performance. The findings reveal that morphometric variables based on body measurements are decisive in predicting live weight, and that body length and chest girth, in particular, are among the most explanatory characteristics within the LightGBM model.

Figure 3 shows the results of the variable significance analysis for the LightGBM model created under a 70–30% training-test split ratio, revealing that body length made the highest contribution to live weight estimation (36%). This was followed by chest circumference (28%), while chest width (14%) and wither height (12%) were determined to be variables with moderate effects. The contributions of rump height and chest depth variables were limited (5%), and sex and birth weight variables were found not to have a significant contribution to model performance. These findings indicate that in the LightGBM model created with a 70–30% split ratio, live weight estimation is predominantly determined by morphometric features based on body measurements.

Discussion

This study evaluated the effectiveness of morphometric body measurements and the machine learning-based Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) algorithm in predicting live weight in Ankara, Kıl, Kilis, and Honamlı goat breeds. The results showed positive and statistically significant correlations between live weight and all examined body measurements (p < 0.05). These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating that body measurements are reliable biometric indicators of live weight in goats. In particular, the high correlation coefficient between wither height and rump height indicates strong consistency in skeletal development across body measurements, supporting the idea that these measurements reflect the overall structural size of the animal.

The fact that LightGBM models developed under different training-test data splitting ratios (65–35%, 70–30%, 75–25%, and 80–20%) exhibit similar variable significance distributions demonstrates that the algorithm has high generalization capacity and stable prediction performance. This aligns with previous studies reporting that gradient boosting-based algorithms can successfully model nonlinear relationships and adapt to the heterogeneous structure frequently encountered in animal production data. Furthermore, the preservation of model performance at different data splitting ratios shows that LightGBM effectively controls the risk of overlearning.

[15] tried to compare several machine learning algorithms such as LightGBM, XGBoost, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting Tree, BA-LightGBM and CRBA-LightGBM for predicting the aero-engine exhaust gas temperature from several traits. CRBA-LightGBM algorithm was to determine the most reliable prediction model. However, LightGBM and XGBoost algorithms in this study showed similar results for predicting aero-engine exhaust gas temperature. According to the findings of Li et al. (2021), the LightGBM and XGBoost algorithms exhibited reliable prediction performance. These results support the conclusions of our current study. However, more information about the dataset is needed to compare the authors’ research and our current study directly.

[16] developed a new methodology based on Principal ComponentAnalysis (PCA) and LightGBM, which aims to estimate stellar atmospheric parameters using photometric data. In addition to PCA and linear regression, the researchers evaluated various algorithms such as Random Forest, LightGBM, XGBoost, Gradient Boosting Decision Tree, Artificial Neural Networks and SupportVector Regression. In the study, the LightGBM algorithm integrated with PCA was determined to be the most effective method for this study in terms of both computational time and RMSE performance. These findings don’t support the results of our research and do not provide a comprehensive discussion, as more information on the structural details of the dataset is required to determine the most suitable method.

[17] compared the LightGBM and XGBoost algorithms to determine the prediction performance for the remaining service life of Li-ion batteries. The author optimised the model parameters to improve prediction precision and performed LightGBM and XGBoost algorithms on the lifespan prediction of lithium batteries. As goodness-of-fit criteria, mean absolute error was used to compare the prediction models. According to the results of this study, both LightGBM andXGBoost algorithms had similar and higher prediction performance. While these findings do not provide a comprehensive discussion, they support the results of our research. Still, more information is needed about the structural details of the dataset to determine themost appropriate method.

Variable significance analyses performed within the LightGBM model revealed that body length and chest circumference, which represent the trunk, play a dominant role in live weight estimation. These results are consistent with numerous studies reporting that chest circumference and body length are the most effective morphometric measurements in estimating live weight in small ruminants. The high contribution of chest circumference stems from the fact that this measurement indirectly reflects the development of muscle and fat tissue in the animal, and it is frequently recommended for live weight estimation, especially due to its practicality in field conditions [18, 19].

Overall, the findings of this study demonstrate that the LightGBM algorithm provides higher prediction accuracy compared to classical regression models and can effectively capture complex relationships between morphometric measurements and live weight. This machine learning-based approach can be used as a decision support system in selection, performance evaluation, and herd management processes in goat breeding, and offers significant potential for digitalization in small ruminant farming.

Conclusion

This study comprehensively evaluates the effectiveness of the Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) algorithm in estimating live weight in Ankara, Kıl, Kilis, and Honamlı goat breeds, and examines it comparatively under different training-test data splitting ratios. The analysis results revealed positive and statistically significant relationships (p < 0.05) between live weight and all morphometric measurements considered; in particular, basic body measurements such as body length, chest circumference, and wither height were shown to be the variables contributing most to live weight estimation.

Variable significance analyses and error metrics performed within the LightGBM model revealed that the algorithm exhibited high prediction accuracy and stable performance under different training-test split ratios (65–35%, 70–30%, 75–25%, and 80–20%). Obtaining similar variable significance distributions at all ratios indicates that the model has strong generalization capacity and that the risk of overfitting remains limited. In contrast, the limited contribution of variables such as gender and birth weight to the model revealed that the main determining factors in live weight estimation are morphometric characteristics based on body measurements.

The results show that the LightGBM algorithm enables the rapid, practical, and reliable estimation of live weight in different goat breeds. In this context, it is considered that the findings of this study can make significant contributions to the development of decision support systems for selection, herd management, and performance evaluation processes in goat breeding. Furthermore, it is thought that by revealing the potential of machine learning-based approaches in small ruminant farming, it will pave the way for the development of more comprehensive prediction systems in the future by including different breeds, age groups, and environmental factors in the model.

Furthermore, it was concluded that the LightGBM algorithm can be used as an effective decision support tool in goat breeding, enabling fast, practical, and reliable estimation of live weight. Based on these findings, it is recommended that future studies utilize larger sample sizes, include different age groups and lactation periods in the model, and integrate environmental and nutritional factors.

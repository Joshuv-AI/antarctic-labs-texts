Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis identifies C5NT1AL as a candidate gene influencing inosine monophosphate levels in chicken breast muscle
Authors: Heng Yang, Li Liu, Yanke Wang, Yongli Wang, Junyi Zhuang, Huanxian Cui, Guiping Zhao, Jie Wen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Inosine monophosphate (IMP) is a key umami-related nucleotide that contributes substantially to chicken meat flavor. This study aimed to elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying individual variation in breast muscle IMP content in Beijing-You chicken through integrative transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis.A total of 73 male Beijing-You chickens at 135 days of age were phenotyped for IMP content in breast muscle. For extreme phenotype comparisons, 10 individuals with the highest IMP contents and 10 individuals with the lowest IMP contents were defined as the high-IMP and low-IMP groups, respectively. Transcriptomic and metabolomic datasets were generated from all 73 samples, and integrated using correlation analysis, Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA), and machine learning models. Whereas extreme-group comparisons were used for PCA, differential analysis.IMP content showed moderate phenotypic variability within the population. Transcriptome analysis integrating elastic net regression, support vector machine (SVM) modeling, and differential expression analysis identified 22 high-confidence genes associated with IMP content, which were significantly enriched in various pathways, including purine metabolism, pyrimidine metabolism, and nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism. Metabolomic integration combining WGCNA, differential analysis, and correlation analysis identified 14 key metabolites negatively correlated with IMP content, primarily enriched in purine metabolism, nucleotide metabolism, and the ABC transporter pathway. Integrated multi-omics analysis highlighted cytosolic 5′-nucleotidase 1A-like (C5NT1AL), which was co-enriched with inosine, hypoxanthine, and guanosine within the purine metabolism pathway. These results suggest that C5NT1AL potentially regulates IMP deposition in chicken breast muscle through regulation of IMP dephosphorylation and downstream purine metabolic flux. This study identified C5NT1AL as a promising candidate gene influencing umami-related nucleotide metabolism and provides molecular insight into the genetic improvement of chicken meat flavor quality.

Introduction

Inosine monophosphate (IMP) is a key umami-related nucleotide that plays a crucial role in determining the characteristic savory flavor of chicken meat and is widely recognized as an important indicator of meat quality. High IMP content is strongly associated with increased flavor intensity, consumer preference, and the market value of poultry products (Yu, et al., 2023a). High-quality local chicken breeds generally have higher IMP levels in muscle tissue compared with commercial fast-growing lines, suggesting that IMP deposition is under genetic regulation and represents a promising target for genetic selection in breeding programs for better meat quality. To effectively exploit this genetic potential, it is essential to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying IMP deposition in muscle (Lei, et al., 2025; Ma, et al., 2015; Murata, 2025).

From a biochemical perspective, IMP occupies a pivotal position within the purine metabolic pathways. It serves as a key intermediate in the de novo purine synthesis and salvage pathways and acts as a common precursor for adenosine monophosphate (AMP) and guanosine monophosphate (GMP). The intracellular level of IMP is thus determined by a dynamic balance among purine synthesis, interconversion, and degradation processes. Disruption in any of these processes can influence nucleotide pool homeostasis and ultimately affect IMP deposition in muscle tissue. Previous studies have shown that the IMP content is closely linked to postmortem flavor development and muscle energy metabolism, further highlighting the complexity of its regulation (Escobedo Del Bosque, et al., 2020; Römling, et al., 2023).

With the advancement of high-throughput omics technologies, multi-omics integrated analysis has been increasingly used in deciphering the genetic basis of meat quality traits (Jayasena, et al., 2013). Previous transcriptomic studies have identified several candidate genes associated with IMP content, including PDHA2, ACSS2, PGAM1, and THBS1, which are primarily involved in energy metabolism, glycolysis, and mitochondrial function. These findings indicate that IMP accumulation is tightly coupled with muscle energy status and metabolic flux. (Yu, et al., 2023b). Complementary proteomic and metabolomic studies have further identified enzymes directly involved in purine metabolism and nucleotide turnover, such as PGM1, PKM2, AK1, AMPD1, and ATIC (Huang, et al., 2022). The complex regulatory mechanisms underlying IMP deposition require integrative analytical frameworks capable of capturing multi-layered regulatory relationships and identifying strong key drivers within high-dimensional biological data.

In recent years, machine learning (ML) approaches have been increasingly used to analyze high-dimensional omics data, providing effective tools for feature selection and prediction in complex biological systems. Models based on algorithms such as elastic net regression, support vector machines (SVM), and random forest (RF) have been successfully used in livestock research to identify candidate genes associated with economically important traits, including residual feed intake, growth performance, and disease resistance (Ahmed, et al., 2025; Piles, et al., 2019). Integrating multi-omics data with ML frameworks offers a powerful strategy to find key molecular determinants of phenotypic variation (Ballard, et al., 2024).

Currently, although individual transcriptomic, proteomic, or metabolomic analyses have identified genes and pathways potentially associated with IMP content, a comprehensive, coordinated understanding of the regulatory mechanisms driving the differential IMP deposition remains poor.

In particular, few studies have uncovered regulatory networks linking gene expression, metabolite levels, and flavor traits. Integration studies in Beijing You chicken have further revealed that flavor formation involves coordinated changes in amino acid, lipid, and nucleotide metabolism. However, these studies did not explicitly identify convergent regulatory nodes between IMP phenotypic variation and associated metabolites (Li, et al., 2022). A more systematic multi-layered analytical approach is therefore needed to decipher the coordinated molecular mechanisms underlying IMP deposition in chicken muscle.

In this study, we integrated IMP phenotypic measurements with transcriptomic and metabolomic profiles of breast muscle from a Beijing-You chicken population to identify key genes and metabolic pathways associated with IMP deposition. An integrative analytical framework combining correlation analysis, ML–based feature selection, extreme phenotype comparison, and network-based approaches was used to characterize coordinated transcriptional and metabolic changes underlying variation in IMP content. Particular emphasis was placed on purine metabolism and nucleotide degradation pathways to clarify their contributions to IMP deposition. Together, our findings provide a multi-layered view of the molecular regulation of umami-related nucleotides and identify potential genetic targets for improving meat flavor quality in poultry breeding programs.

Materials and methods

Animals and sampling

Seventy-three male Beijing-You chickens were obtained from the Institute of Animal Sciences, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Beijing, China). All broilers had ad libitum access to feed and water, and were immunized accordingly under the same feeding conditions. At 135 days of age, birds were humanely slaughtered, and samples of the breast muscle were collected from each bird. After trimming off the fascia and connective tissue, the left breast muscle samples were homogenized, and used for IMP content determination. The corresponding right breast muscle samples were collected for transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses and stored at −80 °C until analysis. All experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Institute of Animal Sciences, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (permit number: No. IAS2021-66).

Determination of IMP content

IMP content in breast muscle was determined using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) following a previously described method (Ji, et al., 2025). Homogenized muscle tissue (2.00 g) was extracted with 20 mL of 6 % perchloric acid, followed by centrifugation at 4,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatants were combined, pH was adjusted to 6.5 with NaOH, and diluted to a final volume of 50 mL with distilled water. After filtration through a 0.45 μm membrane, IMP concentration was quantified using an Agilent 1290 Infinity II HPLC system (Agilent Technologies Inc, Santa Clara, CA, USA) equipped with an Eclipse XDB-C18 column(Agilent Technologies), with detection at 254 nm. Based on the measured IMP content, birds were ranked, and the 10 individuals with the highest IMP values and the 10 individuals with the lowest IMP values were selected as extreme phenotypic groups, referred to as the high-IMP and low-IMP groups.

RNA extraction and transcriptome sequencing

Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). RNA concentration and purity were determined spectrophotometrically using a NanoDrop ND-2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA), and RNA integrity was assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis. RNA sequencing was performed by Novogene Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) using an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Clean sequencing reads were aligned to the chicken reference genome (Ensembl 109, bGalGal1.mat.broiler.GRCg7b). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were analyzed between high-IMP and low-IMP groups using the DESeq2 software package (v1.50.2) with criteria of | log2(Fold Change) | ≥ 0.585 and P < 0.05.

ML models predicted feature genes

ML analyses were performed to identify candidate genes associated with IMP content. The models were built using the RNA-seq gene expression matrix obtained from all 73 breast muscle samples. IMP content was treated as a continuous outcome, and regression models were used. Gene expression values were Z-score standardized prior to model construction. Samples were randomly divided into a training set (70 %) and a testing set (30 %). Elastic net regression and SVM models were implemented in R using the glmnet and e1071 package. For the elastic net regression model, the mixing parameter (α) and regularization parameter (λ) were optimized using 5-fold cross-validation within the training set. For SVM model, a radial basis function (RBF) kernel was used, and the penalty parameter (C) and kernel width (σ) were optimized by 5-fold cross-validation. Genes with non-zero coefficients in the elastic net model or high variable importance rankings in the SVM model were retained as candidate features (Cortes and Vapnik, 1995; Zou and Hastie, 2005). Model performance was evaluated using the root mean square error (RMSE) and the coefficient of determination (R²) (Zhang and Zhang, 2022).

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR)

Primers for candidate genes were designed (Supplementary Table S1) using the Primer-BLAST tool on the NCBI website (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/) and synthesized at Beijing Tsingke Biotechnology Co. Ltd. (Beijing, China). qPCR was performed using an ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR instrument (ThermoFisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). The 2−△△Ct method was used to calculate the expression level of the target genes, with glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) as a reference gene for expression normalization.

Full-spectrum metabolomics measurement and data analysis

Metabolomic profiling was conducted for breast muscle samples from all 73 individuals. Tissues were stored at −80 °C, ground in liquid nitrogen, and subjected to liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC–MS) analysis using a 200 µL aliquot of the supernatant. Metabolite profiling was performed on an ExionLC™ AD UHPLC system coupled with a QTRAP® 6500+ mass spectrometer (AB Sciex, Framingham, MA, USA) operated in both positive and negative ion modes. All procedures were performed by Wuhan Maiwei Metabolic Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Raw data were processed using Analyst software (version 1.6.3) for peak area integration (Alseekh, et al., 2021). Differential metabolites (DAMs) were identified between high-IMP and low-IMP groups using the orthogonal projections to latent structures discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) multivariate statistical method in the MetaboAnalyst 4.0 package in R, with P < 0.05 and variable importance in projection (VIP) > 1 as the selection criteria. (Chong, et al., 2018).

Weighted gene co-expression analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA has been widely used to describe correlation relationships between clusters of highly correlated genes/metabolites or modules and external conditions or sample traits (Zhang, et al., 2022). Using the metabolite abundance matrix from all 73 samples, WGCNA was conducted in R with the WGCNA package version 4.3.2 to identify metabolite modules associated with IMP content (Langfelder and Horvath, 2008). A signed adjacency matrix was constructed based on pairwise correlations, with the soft-thresholding power set to β = 6 according to scale-free topology criteria. The topological overlap matrix (TOM) was subsequently calculated, and modules were identified using the dynamic tree-cutting algorithm. Module–trait relationships were evaluated by correlating module eigengenes with the IMP phenotype. Hub metabolites were identified based on gene significance (GS) and module membership (MM) criteria, which were identified using the criteria of |GS| > 0.4 and |MM| > 0.6, with correlation significance P < 0.05.

Functional enrichment analysis

Enrichment analysis of DEGs and DAMs was performed using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway database in the OmicShare platform. Gene Ontology (GO) term enrichment analysis was performed on DEGs. Signaling pathways with P < 0.05 in KEGG pathways and GO terms were considered significant. Heatmaps were generated using the R package "ggplot2".

Data analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard error (SE). Each individual animal was considered as an experimental unit. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Statistical analyses were performed using the GraphPad Prism 9.5 software (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used for multiple comparisons, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Variation in IMP content and identification of IMP content-associated genes

IMP content showed an approximately normal distribution ranging from 2.25 to 4.74 mg/g, with a mean value of 3.52 ± 0.05 mg/g and a coefficient of variation of 12.93 % (Fig. 1A, Supplementary Table S2). Normality was supported by the Shapiro–Wilk test (P = 0.522, n = 73), indicating the presence of measurable inter-individual variation within the population while maintaining a relatively concentrated distribution. Whole-transcriptome sequencing generated a total of 63 Gb of clean bases with Q30 percentages exceeding 95 % for all samples. Clean reads were aligned to the chicken reference genome (Ensembl 109, bGalGal1.mat.broiler.GRCg7b), with alignment rates ranging from 87.24 to 93.75 % across samples. A total of 18,758 genes were identified, comprising 18,304 known genes and 454 predicted genes. To identify potential key candidate genes regulating IMP, Pearson correlation analysis was performed between gene expression levels and IMP content, and P-values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method. Under a threshold of FDR ≤ 0.05, a total of 353 genes were identified as significantly correlated with IMP content. Among them, cytosolic 5′-nucleotidase 1A-like (C5NT1AL) showed the largest correlation with IMP content (r = -0.424, FDR = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴) (Supplementary Table S4).

Identification of IMP-associated candidate genes using ML

To identify key feature genes characterizing IMP content, two ML approaches were employed: elastic net regression and SVM models. The 353 genes significantly correlated with IMP content were used as input features (Supplementary Table S5), and samples were randomly divided into training and testing sets at a ratio of 7:3 using a fixed random seed to ensure reproducibility in model evaluation.

For the elastic net regression model, the mixing parameter α was evaluated across the range of 0.1–0.9 using cross-validation within the training dataset. Based on the minimum RMSE value, α = 0.1 was selected as the optimal parameter and the corresponding optimal λ value was used to construct the final model, resulting in 122 genes with non-zero coefficients retained as predictive features for IMP content (Fig. 2A). SVM regression was performed using an RBF kernel. Feature importance was estimated using permutation-based importance scores derived from the trained SVM model, and the top 200 genes ranked by importance were retained as predictive features (Fig. 2B and C). Consistent predictive performance was observed across ML algorithms. Refinement of IMP-associated genes by integrating extreme phenotype analysis

Individuals with the highest (n = 10) and lowest (n = 10) IMP contents in breast muscle were selected to represent extreme phenotypic groups. Student’s t-test confirmed a highly significant difference in IMP content between the two groups. The high-IMP group had significantly higher IMP content than the low-IMP group (4.18 ± 0.07 vs. 3.04 ± 0.04 mg/g, P < 0.0001; Fig. 1B). Principal component analysis (PCA) based on transcriptome profiles showed that PC1 and PC2 explained 11.07 % and 8.76 % of the total variance. Together, the first two principal components explained 19.83 % of the total variance and showed a clear separation between the high- and low-IMP groups, indicating distinct transcriptomic profiles associated with IMP content (Fig. 1C). Differential expression analysis, performed using DESeq2 with thresholds of P ≤ 0.05 and |log₂ fold change| ≥ 0.58, identified a total of 1,404 DEGs, including 1,142 upregulated and 262 downregulated genes in the high-IMP group compared with the low-IMP group (Fig. 1D, Supplementary Table S6).

Intersection of these DEGs with the 93 genes identified by ML analysis found 22 candidate genes associated with IMP content and phenotypic divergence between groups, including C5NT1AL, GPER1, CFAP96, and ISYNA1 (Fig. 1E). Functional enrichment analysis revealed that these 22 genes were significantly enriched in KEGG pathways related to nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, pyrimidine metabolism, inositol phosphate metabolism, and purine metabolism (Fig. 1F).

GO term enrichment analysis revealed 69 significantly enriched GO terms, primarily involving biological processes related to adenosine metabolism, phospholipid biosynthesis, nucleotide metabolism, and phagocytosis, as well as molecular functions such as G protein-coupled receptor activity (Fig. 1G). Detailed KEGG pathway and GO term enrichment results are shown in Supplementary Table S7.

A total of 1968 metabolites consistently detected across all samples were retained for WGCNA analysis. WGCNA of the metabolome using IMP content as the phenotypic trait identified eight distinct metabolite modules, of which four showed significant correlations with IMP content. Based on |GS| > 0.4, |MM| > 0.6 and correlation significance (P < 0.05), a total of 98 metabolites were identified as significantly associated with IMP content. Discussion

IMP is the key umami nucleotide determining the savoriness of chicken meat, and its accumulation in muscle tissue directly influences meat quality and consumer acceptance. High IMP content constitute a critical biochemical basis for flavor development in premium local chicken breeds, which are often preferred for their superior sensory attributes compared to fast-growing commercial lines (Zhao, et al., 2023).

The level of IMP in muscle is not fixed but rather is influenced by a wide range of biological, genetic, nutritional, and environmental factors. As a central intermediate in the purine metabolic network, IMP homeostasis is regulated by multiple interconnected metabolic pathways and functions under multi-level genetic and biochemical mechanisms. IMP is formed both through de novo synthesis and salvage pathways, and it can also be generated by the deamination of AMP by ATP deaminase, or further catabolized to inosine and hypoxanthine through downstream enzymatic reactions. Beyond its role in nucleotide turnover, IMP content in muscle has also been associated with energy metabolism, amino acid availability, and glycolytic flux, as PDHA2, ACSS2, and TPI1 are reported to be significantly associated with variation in muscle IMP content. Genes related to lipid metabolism like PPAR signaling component have also been implicated in variation in muscle IMP content. As a result, variation in IMP deposition cannot be adequately explained by a single gene or pathway (Shin, et al., 2024a). Thus, integrative multi-omics approaches provide an effective strategy to identify critical regulatory nodes underlying IMP accumulation and offer valuable insights into the genetic improvement of meat quality traits (Yan, et al., 2018).

In this study, moderate phenotypic variation with coefficient of variation of 12.93 % in IMP content was observed among Beijing-You chicken roosters raised under identical environmental and management conditions, indicating a substantial genetic contribution to this trait. Beijing You chicken muscle has been reported to contain higher concentrations of essential amino acids, IMP, GMP, and unsaturated fatty acids, which are closely related to sensory quality and umami flavor perception. Previous quantitative genetic analyses in Beijing You chickens have found moderate heritability for IMP content, indicating that selection could lead to meaningful genetic gains without negatively impacting other production traits (Chen, et al., 2022).

At the transcriptomic level, Pearson correlation analysis shows that individual gene–IMP correlations were generally moderate, with the highest correlation coefficient reaching at most r = -0.424. Such modest effect sizes are consistent with the biological nature of metabolic traits that are controlled by coordinated regulation across multiple genes and functional pathways. Similar patterns have been reported in previous studies of IMP and related nucleotide traits in poultry, where most single genes explain only a small proportion of phenotypic variance. For instance, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) in Korean native chicken have identified numerous loci, like rs316338889 on chromosome 5 associated with IMP, inosine, and hypoxanthine levels, but each locus typically exhibits limited individual effect sizes (Kim, et al., 2023), indicating a polygenic architecture for these flavor-related compounds.

Considering the weak marginal correlations observed at the single-gene level, relying solely on traditional correlation or differential expression analysis may fail to capture the multivariate regulatory structure underlying IMP accumulation. To address this challenge, we integrated correlation screening with high-dimensional modeling approaches, including elastic net regression and SVM modeling, these methods can simultaneously perform feature selection and model regularization while accounting for correlated predictors and potential nonlinear relationships. Elastic net regression modeling, which integrates L1 and L2 penalties, encourages the selection of groups of correlated genes that jointly influence phenotypic variation. In our analysis, cross-validation identified a mixing parameter α = 0.1 as optimal, indicating that predictive performance depended on retaining both sparse and distributed signals rather than solely individual linear effects. This choice reflects the expectation that the influences of a metabolic network on IMP content are not dominated by single, strong linear effects but rather by cumulative contributions across many correlated transcriptional changes. In the SVM analyses, predictive performance was stable across cross-validation folds and peaked when approximately 200 high-importance genes were included as model inputs. Performance declined with the inclusion of additional lower-ranked genes, suggesting that the model captures informative biological signals rather than overfitting to high-dimensional noise. This convergence suggests that a large proportion of informative features were consistently prioritized by both linear penalized and nonlinear modelling frameworks, despite their different assumptions and optimization strategies.

Intersecting the genes consistently prioritized by both the elastic net and SVM models with the DEGs between high- and low-IMP groups yielded a consistent gene set of 22 genes, which showed both phenotypic association and expression divergence, among which C5NT1AL emerged as a prominent feature. Functional enrichment analysis of the 22 candidate genes revealed significant overrepresentation of pathways related to purine metabolism, nicotinamide metabolism, and pyrimidine metabolism. Among these, purine metabolism, annotated by C5NT1AL, is directly linked to IMP synthesis, interconversion, and degradation, providing a mechanistic basis for its influence on IMP deposition. In addition, nicotinamide metabolism and pyrimidine metabolism may indirectly affect IMP levels by regulating the intracellular balance of NAD⁺/NADP⁺ and the availability of nucleotide precursors required for purine synthesis. Previous studies have shown that alterations the in cellular redox state and nucleotide precursor pools can substantially influence purine metabolic flux and nucleotide homeostasis (Diehl, et al., 2019). These findings collectively indicate that IMP accumulation is regulated not only by enzymes directly involved in purine turnover but also by broader metabolic pathways that shape cellular energy and redox environments.

Metabolomic profiling revealed that all WGCNA-identified IMP-associated metabolites were also detected as differentially abundant between phenotypic group, identifying a set of 14 key metabolites that were significantly and negatively associated with IMP content. Functional enrichment analysis showed that these metabolites were predominantly enriched in the ABC transporter, nucleotide metabolism, and purine metabolism pathways. The high-IMP phenotype was characterized by markedly reduced levels of canonical IMP degradation products, including inosine, hypoxanthine, and guanosine. This reciprocal pattern between IMP and its downstream catabolites is consistent with a reduced flux toward nucleotide degradation, accompanied by a preferential channeling of IMP toward the salvage and de novo nucleotide synthesis pathways. In parallel, the negative association between uridine and IMP suggests that pyrimidine nucleoside turnover is coupled to purine nucleotide homeostasis. Uridine can be routed through phosphorolysis via uridine phosphorylase to generate ribose-1-phosphate, which can be readily interconverted with ribose-5-phosphate and subsequently used to form PRPP. PRPP serves as an essential ribose-phosphate donor for de novo purine synthesis and for salvage reactions, enabling hypoxanthine, inosine, and guanosine to be recycled back toward the IMP pool, which can increase IMP while simultaneously lowering hypoxanthine at steady state. Related studies also indicate that the ribose derived from uridine can provide energy for ribose phosphate metabolism and nucleotide biosynthesis, implying increased uridine consumption to support IMP accumulation (Boswell-Casteel and Hays, 2017). In addition to purine-related metabolites, several dipeptides and amino acid–derived metabolites, such as Ala-Val, Val-Asp and Pro-Ala, along with the lipid-related metabolite LPC(O-16:1), were also reduced in the high-IMP group. The dipeptides and amino acid–related metabolites were predominantly enriched in ABC transporter pathways, suggesting that coordinated alterations in metabolite transport and intracellular availability may contribute to the regulation of nucleotide homeostasis. Muscle metabolomics studies of postmortem aging show that free amino acids and dipeptides generally increase as proteolysis becomes dominant, whereas IMP typically decreases progressively as nucleotide degradation proceeds and downstream catabolites accumulate. ABC transporter families such as ABCC4 and ABCC5 are known to transport nucleotides and nucleotide analogs(Muroya, et al., 2020). In this context, the lower dipeptide abundance in high-IMP birds may be compatible with comparatively attenuated proteolysis. LPC(O-16:1) is primarily enriched in oxidatively stressed tissues, indicating that reflect changes in membrane phospholipid remodeling and redox pressure(Prabutzki, et al., 2024). Suggesting that its lower abundance in the high-IMP group may be compatible with a lower oxidative stress and a metabolic context more conducive to maintaining nucleotide content. Collectively, these findings suggest that IMP deposition is associated not only with purine metabolic regulation but also with broader metabolic adjustments involving both transporter-mediated substrate handling and lipid metabolism. Such coordinated changes may influence the cellular metabolic environment and substrate flux into purine pathways, thereby indirectly shaping IMP deposition in muscle.

Notably, several of these metabolites were co-enriched with C5NT1AL within the purine metabolism pathway, indicating a close functional linkage between transcriptional regulation and downstream metabolic outcomes. C5NT1AL encodes a predicted cytosolic 5′-nucleotidase 1A-like (NT5C1A-like) enzyme, which catalyze the dephosphorylation of IMP, AMP, and other monophosphate nucleotides into their corresponding nucleosides, thereby regulating intracellular nucleotide pool dynamics and metabolic flux (Ipata and Balestri, 2013; Pesi, et al., 2021).

Cytosolic 5′-nucleotidase in avian tissues participate in IMP dephosphorylation, constituting a critical step in nitrogen release and purine catabolism toward uric acid formation in poultry (Zakataeva, 2021) . Previous biochemical and physiological studies in chickens have reported coordinated variation in IMP, inosine, and hypoxanthine levels in muscle tissue, highlighting their conserved and critical roles in avian muscle nucleotide metabolism (Kviklyte, et al., 2017).

Although direct functional characterization of C5NT1AL in chicken muscle remains underexplored, insights can be inferred from cytosolic 5′-nucleotidase 1A (NT5C1A), a representative member of this family that is highly expressed in skeletal muscle. Experimental studies in mammalian models have shown that NT5C1A regulates intracellular AMP/ATP ratios and influences energy metabolism through AMPK signaling (Kulkarni, et al., 2011; Shintre, et al., 2013). These findings suggest that NT5C family members may exert precise and coordinated control over nucleotide turnover and energy balance.

Consistent with these functional roles, we observed that higher expression of C5NT1AL was associated with lower IMP content and increased levels of its downstream degradation products, including inosine, hypoxanthine, and guanosine. When C5NT1AL is highly expressed, its encoded protein catalyzes IMP dephosphorylation to form inosine, thereby accelerating IMP consumption and driving metabolic flux towards hypoxanthine production (Jacobs, et al., 2025) .C5NT1AL expression correlates positively not only with inosine and hypoxanthine but also with guanosine, indicating it simultaneously promotes GMP degradation towards guanosine (Camici, et al., 2020) .High IMP accumulation may depend on suppression of nucleotide degradation-related gene expression, such as C5NT1AL, thereby limiting the catabolism of IMP, AMP, and GMP, stabilizing the intracellular nucleotide pool and favoring the accumulation of umami nucleotides, in particular IMP (Uemoto, et al., 2017).

Collectively, these findings support a regulatory model in which suppression of nucleotide degradation-related genes such as C5NT1AL contributes to high IMP deposition in chicken muscle, providing a mechanistic basis for the observed phenotypic variation in meat flavor. Despite the integrative nature of the present study, certain limitations should be acknowledged. Although the multi-omics analyses identified consistent associations between C5NT1AL expression and IMP-related metabolic profiles, the conclusions are primarily based on statistical integration of transcriptomic and metabolomic data. Accordingly, further functional investigations such as targeted gene manipulation or enzyme activity assays will be valuable to directly clarify the causal role of C5NT1AL in regulating IMP deposition in chicken muscle. In addition, only males were included in this study, potential sex differences in IMP deposition should be examined in future studies.

Conclusion

This study identified C5NT1AL as a functional candidate gene regulating IMP content in chicken meat through transcriptomic and metabolomic correlation analyses, and preliminarily elucidated its potential mechanism of action by influencing the IMP degradation pathway. IMP serves as the primary nucleotide compound responsible for meat umami, and its concentration determines flavor characteristics. Its degradation products, hypoxanthine and inosine, are recognized as contributing to bitter taste variations in flavor (Shin, et al., 2024b). Genetic variation or regulation of the expression of C5NT1AL may thus represent a key gene influencing chicken meat umami intensity, holding potential application value for selective breeding and flavor enhancement.

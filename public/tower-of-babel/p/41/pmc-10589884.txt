Multienzyme isothermal rapid amplification and lateral flow dipstick combination assay for visible detection of chicken chaphamaparvovirus
Authors: Hao Cui, Shuqi Xu, Xin Xu, Jun Ji, Yunchao Kan, Lunguang Yao, Yingzuo Bi, Qingmei Xie
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractChicken chaphamaparvovirus (CkChpV) is a newly emerging pathogen that is currently prevalent in chickens with diarrhea symptoms. To diagnose CkChpV more conveniently and rapidly, this study established a multienzyme isothermal rapid amplification (MIRA) assay, with a reaction time of only 15 min and optimal reaction temperature of 38°C. In combination with the lateral flow dipstick assay, the CkChpV-MIRA assay can be completed within 20 min. We revealed that the detection limit of the MIRA assay using standard plasmids as templates was as low as 21.3 copies, and its sensitivity was 100 times higher than that of nested PCR. Moreover, the designed primer set and probe could only detect CkChpV specifically, and there was no cross reaction with avian nephritis virus, rotavirus, chicken parvovirus virus, Newcastle disease virus, and infectious bronchitis virus, which may cause diarrhea. These findings demonstrated that the CkChpV-MIRA assay established in this study is convenient, sensitive, and specific and does not require sophisticated equipment. It is more suitable for the detection of CkChpV in clinical samples.Key words: CkChpV, rapidity, sensitivity, visible detection, MIRA-LFD

INTRODUCTION

Parvoviruses, belonging to the family Parvoviridae, are icosahedral, single-stranded DNA viruses with no envelopes (Saxena et al., 2011). The International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses has classified the Parvoviridae family into Parvoviridae, Densovirinae, and novel Hamaparvovirinae subfamilies (Pénzes et al., 2020). Recently, novel parvovirus groups were identified via high-throughput sequencing, which are currently referred to as “chaphamaparvovirus” and belong to the novel subfamily Hamaparvovirinae. These novel parvoviruses have been detected in various animals, such as cats, dogs, and some other mammals (Yang et al., 2016; Fahsbender et al., 2019; Alex et al., 2020; Di Profio et al., 2022).

In our previous study, we detected chaphamaparvovirus in chickens with diarrhea for the first time in China (Cui et al., 2023). However, there are numerous causative agents of diarrhea, and the symptoms caused by chicken chaphamaparvovirus (CkChpV) are not specific. Thus, there is a need for a reliable technique for detecting CkChpV. Molecular biology detection methods, such as conventional polymerase chain reaction PCR and real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR, have become common experimental approaches in many biological laboratories, medical research facilities, relevant testing institutions, and biological and pharmaceutical companies (Mackay et al., 2002). In particular, PCR-based diagnostic methods targeting viral nucleic acids are suitable for newly discovered viruses that have no established isolation and culture systems. In our previous study, we also employed nested PCR (nt-PCR) for detecting CkChpV, suggesting that molecular biology diagnostic methods are particularly suitable for detecting CkChpV (Cui et al., 2023). PCR is a simple and widely used molecular biology method for pathogen detection through the amplification of target nucleic acid fragments (Templeton, 1992). Its high sensitivity and specificity can help in the early screening and control of various infectious diseases (Li et al., 2018). PCR and various derived methods, such as reverse transcription PCR (RT-PCR), nt-PCR, and real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR) have been widely used for pathogen detection (Heid et al., 1996; Bachman, 2013; Green and Sambrook, 2019).

However, these methods require a high level of technical proficiency for laboratory personnel and are often constrained by factors such as sophisticated thermal-cycling equipment and the associated experimental costs and space, limiting their use outside of the laboratory and making them unsuitable for on-site real-time detection (Ji et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2021). As a representative form of isothermal amplification, the widely used loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) technique has been developed to detect classical chicken parvovirus (CkPV) which have been identified as the causative agents of runting and stunting syndrome (RSS) in chickens (Nuñez et al., 2018; Adebiyi et al., 2021; Fan et al., 2023). However, the LAMP assay requires a complex procedure for primer design, and the combined use of external and internal primer pairs increases the risk of nonspecific amplification to some extent (Notomi et al., 2000).

To avoid the technical issues associated with these experimental procedures, the novel multienzyme isothermal rapid amplification (MIRA) has become our preferred approach for clinical detection. The MIRA assay was established based on recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA) technology, which was developed in 2006 by Niall Armes from ASM Scientific Ltd. (Piepenburg et al., 2006). This assay requires 1 targeting primer pair and recombinant enzymes to form the protein/single-stranded nucleocapsid acid complex (Rec/ssDNA) under a constant temperature (<40°C) within 0.5 h (Kuzminov, 2001). Considering the numerous advantages of the MIRA assay, we developed the MIRA and lateral flow dipstick (LFD) combination assay to detect CkChpV in this study.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Viruses and Clinical Samples

From 2020 to 2022, intestinal and fecal samples from 478 (357 with diarrhea and 121 healthy) chickens were collected from chicken farms in central China. Permission was obtained from farm owners to collect cloacal swabs or intestine tissue samples from healthy and sick chickens with diarrhea symptoms. Birds that died naturally were autopsied in strict accordance with the recommendations of the guidelines for the care and use of experimental animals from the South China Agricultural University Committee for Animal Experiments (approval ID: SYXK 2019-0136). These samples were rinsed multiple times with phosphate-buffered saline to remove any remaining blood before being stored in a centrifuge tube at 80°C until further use. Reference strains of avian nephritis virus (ANV), rotavirus (RTV), chicken parvovirus virus (CkPV), Newcastle disease virus (NDV), and infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) used in this study were preserved in our laboratory.

Extraction of DNA and RNA

Total viral DNA and RNA from the clinical samples were extracted using Biospin Virus DNA/RNA Extraction Kit (Bioer Technology Co., Ltd., Hangzhou, China), in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. The purity and concentration of the DNA and RNA samples were evaluated via biological spectrophotometry, and the extracted DNA/RNA were stored at −80°C until use.

Primer and Probe Design for the MIRA Assay

Owing to the special reaction conditions and requirements for primer design, primers for regular PCR can typically not be used in the MIRA assay. Based on the NS1 sequences of the strain CHN220216 (accession no.: OP172530), primer pairs and corresponding nfo probes for MIRA (Table 1) were designed and purified via high-performance liquid chromatography and were obtained from Sangon Biotech Company (Shanghai, China). Further, to combine LFD detection with the MIRA assay, a sequence complementary to the target fragment was constructed between the upstream and downstream primers as a probe (Table 2). An antigen marker (FAM) was added to the 5′-end, with tetrahydrofuran labeled in the center of the sequence, and a C3 spacer was added to the 3′-end. Based on 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis and LFD (Changzhou Amplification Future Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Weifang, China) analyses, the optimum primer pairs and FAM-labeled probes were screened for performing the MIRA assay.

The MIRA assay was performed using MIRA nucleic acid amplification kit (Changzhou Amplification Future Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Weifang, China). For the basic MIRA assay, a 50-μL reaction mixture was prepared using 29.4 L of A buffer, 2.5 L of B buffer, 2 μL (10 mM) of forward/reverse primer, and double distilled water (ddH2O). For the MIRA assay with LFD (MIRA-LFD assay), 0.6 μL of nfo probe (10 mM) was added to the basic MIRA reaction mixture. Then, the reaction tube was shaken upside down 10 to 15 times to evenly mix the reagents. After mixing the chemicals, the reaction liquid was spun to the bottom of the reaction tube, after which the reaction tube was immediately placed in a water bath for incubation. After the completion of the MIRA assay with nfo probe, 5 μL of the product was collected and mixed uniformly with 95 μL of ddH2O. Then, 50 μL of the diluted reaction product was added dropwise into the sampling hole of the LFD system for result interpretation after 5 min, based on the quality control and detection lines.

Then, the results were interpreted based on agarose gel electrophoresis analysis, and the products and DNA extraction reagent (phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol ratio of 25:24:1; Solarbio Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) were collected from the reaction unit at a 1:1 ratio, followed by centrifugation at 5,000 rpm for 5 min. Finally, 6 × loading buffer was added to the colorless supernatant, and 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis was performed.

Optimum Temperature and Time for the MIRA Assay

To obtain the appropriate MIRA amplification system, gradient optimization was performed in water baths at reaction temperatures of 36°C, 37°C, 38°C, 39°C, and 40°C, and the detection was optimized using time gradients of 5, 10, 15, and 20 min.

Specificity of the MIRA-LFD Assay

The newly developed MIRA-LFD assay was examined for specificity using preserved reference viruses of ANV, RTV, CkPV, NDV, and IBV, which may cause diarrhea in chickens. Meanwhile, ddwater was used as a negative control for every test.

Sensitivity of the MIRA Assay

The sensitivity of the CkChpV-MIRA assay was determined using the standard plasmid p-CHN220216-NS1 (CHN220216 strain, accession number: OP172530) preserved in our laboratory. Serial dilutions (10-fold) of p-CHN220216-NS1 in ddwater (diluted from 2.13 × 106 to 2.13) were used for evaluating the sensitivity of the CkChpV-MIRA assay and nt-PCR, as described in a previous report (Cui et al., 2023).

Testing of Clinical Samples

To determine the effectiveness of the MIRA assay for detecting CkChpV in clinical samples, we compared the positive detection rates of nt-PCR and MIRA assay. We also calculated the κ and P values of the 2 detection methods. SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corporation, New York, NY) was used for statistical analyses.

RESULTS

Validation of Primers and Probe

After the completion of the MIRA assay, the products were analyzed using agarose gel electrophoresis and LFD, as shown in Figure 1. The results revealed that the 3 primer pairs can detect the virus, with no false positive results for the negative control. The results of LFD and gel electrophoresis were compatible. Notably, the reaction with the CkChpV-1 primer pair revealed no nonspecific amplification bands. To obtain more reliable experimental data, CkChpV-1 primers with probe were selected for follow-up testing.

Optimum Reaction Temperature and Time for the CkChpV-MIRA Assay

After confirming the primer pair and probe, optimization experiments were performed for the reaction temperature and time. As shown in Figure 2, the target amplification bands were displayed in the lanes between 36°C and 40°C; however, the highest intensity of the target band was observed at 38°C, indicating that the CkChpV-MIRA assay showed the highest efficiency at this temperature. Further, 4 reaction times (5, 10, 15, and 20 min) were set for the MIRA reaction system. The product band appeared after 10 min, and the band intensity peaked after 15 min of the reaction. Based on the electrophoresis results, 15 min was selected as the optimal reaction time for the CkChpV-MIRA diagnostic assay, as shown in Figure 3. Therefore, 38°C and 15 min were selected as the optimal reaction temperature and time, respectively, for establishing the CkChpV-MIRA diagnostic assay in this study.

Specificity and Sensitivity of the CkChpV-MIRA-LFD Assay

CkChpV and the reference viruses ANV, RTV, CkPV, NDV, and IBV were detected using the MIRA assay. As shown in Figure 4, the target strip only appeared in the lane containing the CkChpV positive template, providing a negative result. The test strip revealed the same result. All detections were validated by performing the experiment in triplicate.

To evaluate the sensitivity of the MIRA assay, the standard plasmid template of CkChpV was selected for detection at 38°C for 15 min, and the initial copy number of the plasmid template was determined to be 2.13 × 106. We used 10-fold serial dilutions of the standard plasmids for MIRA-LFD assay. As shown in Figure 5, the minimum detection limit of the MIRA assay using standard plasmids as templates was approximately 21.3 copies. The MIRA assay was approximately 100 times more sensitive than the nt-PCR assay, indicating that the MIRA detection assay established in this study is extremely sensitive for CkChpV.

Clinical Sample Detection

CkChpV was detected in intestinal and fecal chicken samples using nt-PCR and MIRA assays (Table 3). The positivity rate of the nt-PCR assay was 25.7% (123/478), whereas that of the MIRA assay was 27.2% (130/478). Statistical analysis revealed that both assays were reliable for diagnosing field samples to the same extent.

DISCUSSION

As CkChpV is a novel emerging virus, there is currently no effective commercial vaccine or treatment to control its infection. Owing to the lack of specific clinical symptoms and limited research on CkChpV, there is an urgent need to establish an effective, rapid, and convenient detection method.

Currently, some PCR-based methods, such as PCR and qPCR, can detect ChPV in other species (Di Profio et al., 2022). Recently, a study employed the MIRA assay for viral detection and revealed good specificity and sensitivity, similar to those of PCR assays (Hardinge and Murray, 2019). To overcome the limitations of PCR, broaden the application range, and reduce time consuming, this study developed the MIRA assay for the first time to detect CkChpV.

The MIRA technology established in this study requires a much lower reaction temperature than existing isothermal detection methods. We determined that this assay can detect CkChpV within 15 min at 38°C, which not only saves time and is easy to operate but also requires a single device maintained at a constant temperature for the reaction. Furthermore, the lack of instrument restrictions allows the MIRA assay to identify a large number of samples within a short period. Notably, although the optimal reaction temperature determined by the MIRA assay in this experiment was 38°C, we observed that relatively satisfactory detection results were also obtained at 37°C. This implies that the CkChpV-MIRA assay can be accomplished by holding the reaction tube in the fist or under the body temperature of domestic chickens, further eliminating the need to purchase a constant temperature device and greatly reducing testing costs. Further, when combined with LFD, the detection results of the MIRA assay become visible, making this approach more convenient and rapid. In terms of sensitivity, the MIRA assay is 100 times more sensitive than the nt-PCR assay. Moreover, compared with nt-PCR, MIRA recognized 7 more samples during clinical sample detection, which might be related to the higher sensitivity of the MIRA assay for the presence of CkChpV DNA in these positive samples. However, as viral isolation cannot be conducted, the positivity rate of these additional samples cannot be determined through virus isolation (Cui et al., 2023). The 7 positive samples detected by the MIRA assay were further confirmed via sequencing of the amplified products. Interestingly, CkChpV alone can be precisely detected, with no cross-reaction with other common viruses, thereby allowing the accurate interpretation of data. MIRA combined with LFD can completely replace conventional PCR and only requires a constant temperature instrument in areas with poor research conditions for food safety, pathogen detection, and other application related to nucleic acid testing (Ji et al., 2022).

However, MIRA technology also has some limitations. Its high sensitivity and the opening of the EP tube cover during the reaction may be associated with a risk of aerosol pollution. Considering these potential issues, to limit MIRA contamination and false positive results in practice, strict physical zoning should be implemented. Specifically, sample preparation, MIRA reaction, and electrophoresis analysis of gene products should be performed in isolated areas.

In summary, the MIRA assay developed in this study can accomplish all detection steps within 20 min and has good specificity and sensitivity. These advantages make the assay useful in the control and investigation of CkChpV infection.

SVSC-13 Beyond Survival: A Survivor’s Perspective on the Gaps in Long-Term Adolescent and young Adult Neuro-Oncology Care in Sub-Saharan Africa
Authors: Boluwatife Aofolaju, Oluwakemi Atolagbe, Korede Akindele
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPediatric brain tumor survival has improved in Nigeria, but many survivors are left to navigate long-term effects of cancer and treatment without adequate support. At the age of 22 in 2022, during my final year of university studying microbiology, I was diagnosed with a brain tumor. Though I survived, the aftermath included academic disruption, cognitive issues, permanent hair loss, and emotional distress from relationship heartbreak, friendship betrayals and unexpected stigma. As a young woman, needing to wear wigs and never showing my own hair/head has affected my confidence and identity. This abstract highlights the overlooked realities of cancer survivorship for adolescents and young adults and calls for structured, long-term care in AYA neuro-oncology. I felt too old for the pediatrics department and just out of place in the adult cancer department. I did not know when to begin calling myself a survivor or indeed, if I was truly a cancer survivor, after all a tumor is not technically cancer. This is a survivor-led narrative drawing from personal experience, advocacy work with adolescents and young adults, and informal peer conversations in Nigeria. It identifies practical and psychological gaps in AYA survivorship care. Themes identified include: (1) lack of routine cognitive and emotional follow-up long-term, (2) limited academic & social reintegration support, (3) ongoing body image and self-esteem struggles, and (4) absence of AYA-centered survivorship models. With a background in microbiology and experience in infectious disease research, I now aim to combine my scientific path in biology with AYA cancer research, and survivorship advocacy, leveraging my lived experience to advocate for survivor-centered oncology care that lasts beyond the hospital.

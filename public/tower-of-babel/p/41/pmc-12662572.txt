A comprehensive review of the dissemination and training of safety and first aid skills among early childhood educators
Authors: Xinxin Wang, Tianqi Qiao, Jingyi Chang, Pingzhi Ye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
First aid training for early childhood educators is crucial due to the inherent vulnerability of young children to accidents and injuries. This review examines the knowledge and training of first aid skills among early childhood educators globally, with a specific focus on China. The literature search covered publications from 2009 to 2024, using databases, such as PubMed, Google Scholar, ERIC, Web of Science, and China National Knowledge Infrastructure. Keywords and phrases used in the search included “first aid training,” “early childhood educators,” “emergency preparedness,” “childcare safety,” and “first aid knowledge.” The review identifies gaps and challenges in existing training programs and proposes strategies for improvement. Developed countries have well-established first aid training protocols, while many developing countries face significant challenges, including limited resources and lack of standardized programs. In China, significant challenges remain, such as fragmented training programs and insufficient ongoing professional development. Government policies acknowledge the need for first aid training, yet implementation is often inconsistent. Addressing these issues requires standardized training programs, equitable resource allocation, and continuous professional development. This review provides a roadmap for meaningful improvements, emphasizing the need for collaborative efforts from policymakers, educational institutions, and stakeholders to create a consistent and effective approach to first aid training. Despite the recognized importance of first aid training, there is a notable scarcity of research on the knowledge and skills of early childhood educators in both domestic and global contexts. This review aims to address this research gap by conducting a comprehensive analysis of existing literature on the subject.

Introduction

First aid training for early childhood educators is crucial due to the inherent vulnerability of young children to accidents and injuries.[12345] Early childhood is a critical developmental period marked by curiosity and exploration, which, while essential for growth, also increases the risk of injuries. The ability of educators to provide immediate and effective first aid can significantly mitigate the severity of injuries and, in some cases, prevent fatalities.[235] Early childhood educators spend considerable time with children and are often the first responders in emergency situations within educational settings. Their proficiency in first aid is essential for ensuring the safety and well-being of children.[6] First aid training is crucial not only for managing injuries but also for fostering a safer learning environment. It ensures that educators are well-prepared to handle emergencies confidently and efficiently. Training typically encompasses a range of skills, including identifying potential hazards, performing basic life-saving procedures, and managing minor injuries.[7] Moreover, first aid training fosters a culture of safety awareness among educators, promoting a proactive approach to accident prevention and emergency preparedness.[8] According to Joseph et al., regular first aid training for educators not only enhances their ability to respond to emergencies but also improves their overall confidence and preparedness in managing various situations that may arise in early childhood settings.[9]

Globally, the level of first aid training among early childhood educators varies significantly. In developed countries, there is a higher emphasis on structured training programs, often mandated by regulatory bodies and supported by robust infrastructure. For instance, in the United States, the American Red Cross and similar organizations provide standardized first aid and cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) training, which is often a requirement for early childhood educator certification. Similarly, in Germany, first aid training is a mandatory component of professional development for educators, ensuring a high level of consistency and quality across the country.[3] In contrast, many developing countries face significant challenges in implementing effective first aid training programs. Limited resources, lack of standardized training programs, and insufficient government support are significant barriers.[1011] A study by León-Guereño et al. highlighted that in many parts of Asia and Africa, first aid training for educators is either minimal or nonexistent, resulting in a lack of preparedness in emergency situations.[12] In these regions, there is often a reliance on informal training or basic first aid knowledge, which is inadequate for effectively managing emergencies in early childhood settings.[13] This disparity underscores the need for international cooperation and support to enhance first aid training in these regions.[314]

Focusing on China, the importance of first aid training for early childhood educators cannot be overstated. With the rapid development of the early childhood education sector and an increasing number of children attending kindergartens and early learning centers, the need for well-trained educators is critical.[1516171819] However, studies indicate that there are significant challenges in this area. For instance, surveys by Li et al. and Pan revealed that many early childhood educators in China have limited knowledge and skills in first aid.[101620] The surveys found that while educators were aware of the basic concepts of first aid, their ability to perform essential procedures, such as CPR, or manage choking incidents was insufficient. Moreover, the training that does exist is often fragmented and inconsistent. According to Zhou and Sen, the current first aid training programs for early childhood educators in China lack standardization and are not uniformly implemented across different regions and institutions.[21] This inconsistency results in significant variations in the quality and effectiveness of training.[1722] Furthermore, there is a lack of ongoing professional development opportunities for educators to refresh and update their skills, which is crucial given the evolving nature of first aid practices and guidelines.[1619] One of the primary challenges in China is the limited support and emphasis from the government and educational authorities. While there are policies that recognize the importance of first aid training, their implementation is often inadequate.[11] The study by Li et al. pointed out that despite governmental guidelines advocating for the inclusion of first aid training in the curriculum for early childhood educators, there is a significant gap in actual practice.[16]

Despite the recognized importance of first aid training, research specifically focused on assessing first aid knowledge and skills among early childhood educators remains limited, both globally and within China. This review aims to address this gap by providing a comprehensive overview of the current state of first aid knowledge and training among early childhood educators, with a specific focus on China. The research objectives of this study are to comprehensively review the current state of first aid knowledge and training among early childhood educators globally, with a specific focus on China, to identify the gaps and challenges in existing training programs, and to propose strategies for improving the training and preparedness of early childhood educators in handling emergencies. Literature Review

Global perspective

Studies on the importance of first aid training for early childhood educators

The critical role of first aid training for early childhood educators has been well-documented in global research. First aid training equips educators with the skills and knowledge necessary to respond promptly and effectively to emergencies, thereby reducing the risk of severe injuries and fatalities. For instance, Ilha et al. conducted a quasi-experimental study to evaluate the impact of educational actions on the first aid knowledge and skills of early childhood education teachers.[4] The study found that targeted educational interventions significantly improved the teachers’ first aid preparedness, highlighting the importance of structured training programs. Bhatia et al. highlighted that well-trained educators can prevent minor injuries from escalating into major health issues by providing timely and appropriate care.[23] A cross-sectional study by Owusu-Addo further illustrates the importance of first aid training in early childhood education settings.[1] The study found that educators who received regular first aid training were more confident and capable of handling emergencies, which in turn contributed to a safer learning environment. The study also noted that trained educators were better at identifying potential hazards and implementing preventive measures, thereby reducing the overall incidence of accidents and injuries.[24] Additional studies corroborate these findings. For example, Joseph et al. found that regular first aid training sessions improved the emergency preparedness of early childhood educators, increasing their confidence in handling various emergency scenarios.[9] Moreover, Alshammari indicates that first aid training not only enhances practical skills but also instills a sense of responsibility and vigilance among educators, promoting a proactive approach to safety in educational environments.[7]

Comparative analysis of first aid training programs in various countries

Countries differ significantly in their approaches to first aid training for early childhood educators, with varying levels of rigor and comprehensiveness. In the United States, first aid and CPR certification are mandatory. Organizations, such as the American Red Cross and the American Heart Association, provide standardized training programs, which include comprehensive modules on CPR, choking, bleeding, burns, and other emergency scenarios.[2526] Similarly, Canada mandates first aid training for early childhood educators through the Canadian Red Cross, offering extensive training that covers a wide range of emergency situations and integrates these programs into the certification process for educators, ensuring a high level of preparedness with ongoing professional development opportunities.[27] In Germany, first aid training is an integral part of professional development for early childhood educators, standardized and mandated by national regulations to ensure consistency and continuity across different regions. Regular updates and recertification are part of the training protocol to keep educators’ skills up-to-date.[28] In the United Kingdom, the Early Years Foundation Stage framework requires all childcare providers to have at least one staff member with a current pediatric first aid certificate on the premises at all times, emphasizing the importance of first aid training within the early childhood education sector. The training includes CPR, dealing with unconscious casualties, and managing minor injuries.[29] Australia employs a comprehensive approach to first aid training through organizations like St John Ambulance, offering both online and face-to-face components to make training accessible and flexible. Regular professional development and recertification are emphasized to ensure educators maintain their skills.[30] In Japan, first aid training for early childhood educators is highly encouraged and often facilitated by organizations, such as the Japanese Red Cross Society. These training programs cover practical skills for managing choking, CPR, and handling common injuries. While not exclusively designed for early childhood educators, these programs ensure that participants, including educators, are well-prepared to handle emergencies, with regular updates and continuous professional development being standard practice.[31]

China-specific studies

Overview of early childhood education in China

The early childhood education sector in China has expanded rapidly over the past few decades, driven by government policies and increased parental demand for quality early education.[101632] This growth has brought attention to the need for comprehensive training programs for early childhood educators, including first aid training. However, the implementation of such programs remains inconsistent across different regions and institutions.[2233]

Current state of first aid knowledge and training among Chinese early childhood educators

Despite the recognized importance of first aid training, many early childhood educators in China lack adequate knowledge and skills in this area.[192234] Surveys conducted by Pan and Li revealed significant gaps in both the theoretical knowledge and practical skills of early childhood teachers in China regarding first aid.[2034] The findings revealed significant gaps in both theoretical knowledge and practical skills. Many educators were found to be unfamiliar with basic first aid procedures, such as CPR and the Heimlich maneuver, and lacked confidence in their ability to handle emergencies.[21] Moreover, the training that does exist is often fragmented and lacks standardization. The effectiveness of these programs is further hindered by limited resources and a lack of ongoing professional development opportunities for educators.[16181935]

Challenges and barriers to effective first aid training in China

Several challenges and barriers impede the effective implementation of first aid training programs for early childhood educators in China. One significant barrier is the lack of standardized training programs. Unlike in many developed countries, where regulatory bodies mandate specific training requirements, China lacks a unified framework for first aid training in early childhood education. This inconsistency leads to significant variations in the quality and content of training programs offered by different institutions.[101116] Resource limitations also pose a significant challenge. Many early childhood education institutions, especially in rural areas, lack the financial and material resources to provide comprehensive first aid training. This disparity between urban and rural areas exacerbates the overall inadequacy of first aid preparedness among educators.[21222324252627282930313233] Furthermore, there is often a lack of emphasis on the importance of first aid training within the broader educational and governmental context. While some policies acknowledge the need for first aid training, their implementation is often weak, and enforcement is inconsistent.[20] This lack of prioritization means that many educators do not receive the necessary training to respond effectively to emergencies.

Government policies and initiatives related to first aid training in early childhood settings

In recent years, the Chinese government has emphasized the importance of improving first aid training for early childhood educators through various policies and initiatives. The Ministry of Education has issued guidelines encouraging educational institutions to incorporate first aid training into their programs. These guidelines recommend making first aid training a mandatory component of the curriculum for early childhood education students.[36] Li et al. also supported this initiative, highlighting the necessity of equipping educators with first aid skills to ensure children’s safety.[11]

Additionally, kindergartens across China have launched pilot programs to enhance first aid training for early childhood educators locally. For instance, Beijing introduced a city-wide initiative to provide first aid training for all early childhood educators, supported by partnerships with local hospitals and medical institutions. This program aims to create a standardized approach to first aid training and ensure that all educators are equipped with the necessary skills.[37] Similarly, cities, like Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Shenzhen, have implemented programs incorporating both theoretical and practical components into the training. These initiatives include regular workshops, hands-on training sessions, and assessments to ensure that educators are proficient in first aid techniques.[383940]

These efforts reflect a broader commitment to improving safety in educational settings. The integration of first aid training into the professional development of early childhood educators is seen as essential for creating a safe learning environment. Ren, Xiang, and Zhang pointed out that ongoing professional development and recertification programs are crucial to maintaining a high standard of first aid preparedness.[171819] Despite these positive steps, achieving comprehensive and effective first aid training for early childhood educators across China requires continued efforts to develop standardized training programs, allocate sufficient resources, and promote the importance of first aid training at both the policy and institutional levels.[11] Furthermore, Cui and Zhao emphasized the need for continuous updates and professional development to keep educators’ skills current.[32]

Materials and Methods

Criteria for literature selection

The selection of literature for this review was guided by specific criteria to ensure the inclusion of relevant and high-quality studies. The primary criteria included relevance, ensuring that the studies selected were directly related to first aid training and knowledge among early childhood educators, with studies focusing on other educational levels or unrelated topics excluded. Priority was given to recent publications (within the last 15 years) to ensure the review reflects the most current research and practices, although seminal works that significantly contributed to the field were also included regardless of their publication date. Studies from a wide range of countries were included to provide a global perspective, with special attention given to the literature from both developed and developing countries to highlight disparities and similarities [Table 1]. Both quantitative and qualitative studies were included to provide a comprehensive understanding of the topic, with systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and case studies particularly valued for their rigorous methodologies and comprehensive coverage. Only peer-reviewed articles were considered to ensure the credibility and reliability of the information.

Databases and sources searched

A systematic search of the literature was conducted using the following databases: PubMed, to access medical and health-related research articles; Google Scholar, for a broad range of academic papers across various disciplines; ERIC (Education Resources Information Center), specifically for educational research; Web of Science, to access high-impact journals and conference proceedings; and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), for Chinese academic papers and research articles. The search strategy involved using a combination of keywords and phrases, such as “first aid training,” “early childhood educators,” “emergency preparedness,” “childcare safety,” and “first aid knowledge.” Boolean operators (AND, OR) were used to refine the search results.

Search strategy and process

An initial broad search was conducted using specified keywords across all selected databases. The titles and abstracts of the retrieved articles were then screened against the inclusion and exclusion criteria, with studies not meeting the criteria excluded at this stage. The full texts of the remaining articles were reviewed in detail, and studies that did not meet all inclusion criteria upon full-text review were excluded. Finally, relevant data from the selected studies were extracted using a standardized data extraction form, which included information on the study design, sample size, methodology, findings, and implications.

Data management and analysis

The data extracted from the selected studies were systematically organized and analyzed. Key themes and patterns were identified and categorized. The findings were synthesized to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of first aid training among early childhood educators globally and in China.

Results

General findings

Common themes and patterns in the literature

The review of literature reveals several consistent themes and patterns regarding first aid training for early childhood educators. A critical theme is the essential role of first aid training in enhancing the safety and well-being of children in early childhood education settings. Numerous studies emphasize that well-trained educators are better equipped to handle emergencies, thereby reducing the severity of injuries and potentially saving lives.[8] Additionally, the literature consistently highlights the need for regular and comprehensive training programs covering a wide range of emergency scenarios, including CPR, choking, bleeding, and burns.[79] Another prominent theme is the positive impact of first aid training on educators’ confidence and preparedness. Studies show that educators who receive regular training are more confident in their ability to manage emergencies and are more likely to take proactive measures to prevent accidents.[73241] This increased confidence and preparedness not only improves the immediate response to emergencies but also contributes to creating a safer overall environment for children.[12]

Differences between regions and countries

The literature also highlights significant differences in first aid training programs and their implementation across various regions and countries. In developed countries, such as the United States, Canada, Germany, and the United Kingdom, first aid training for early childhood educators is often mandatory and well-structured. For example, in the United States, organizations like the American Red Cross provide standardized training programs integrated into the certification process for educators.[30] Similarly, in Germany, first aid training is a mandatory component of professional development for educators, ensuring a high level of consistency and quality across the country.[3] In contrast, many developing countries face significant challenges in implementing effective first aid training programs. Limited resources, lack of standardized training frameworks, and insufficient government support are common barriers. According to Li et al., in parts of Asia and Africa, first aid training for early childhood educators is often minimal or nonexistent, resulting in a lack of preparedness in emergency situations.[10] This disparity underscores the need for international cooperation and support to enhance first aid training in these regions.[114]

Impact of training on the preparedness of educators

The impact of first aid training on the preparedness of educators is a recurring focus in the literature. Well-designed training programs have been shown to significantly improve educators’ ability to respond effectively to emergencies. For instance, Plischewski et al. found that educators who completed comprehensive first aid training were more likely to correctly perform CPR and manage choking incidents compared to those who did not receive such training.[8] Similarly, studies have demonstrated that regular first aid training not only enhances practical skills but also improves educators’ overall awareness of safety issues and preventive measures.[79]

Moreover, the literature suggests that continuous professional development and refresher courses are essential for maintaining and enhancing first aid skills. Ganfure et al. emphasized the importance of ongoing training to ensure that educators remain up-to-date with the latest first aid practices and guidelines.[41] This continuous learning approach helps to reinforce knowledge and skills, thereby increasing the overall preparedness and confidence of educators in handling emergencies.[12]

China-specific findings

Detailed analysis of the current state of first aid training among early childhood educators in China

The current state of first aid training among early childhood educators in China reveals several critical issues. Pan conducted a comprehensive survey that found significant gaps in both the knowledge and skills of Chinese early childhood educators regarding first aid.[20] The survey indicated that while educators were generally aware of basic first aid concepts, their practical skills, such as performing CPR or managing choking incidents, were often inadequate. This gap between theoretical knowledge and practical skills poses a significant challenge to ensuring the safety of children in early childhood settings.[10183334] Zhou and Sen also highlighted similar findings in their research on the current status and countermeasures of first aid treatment for accidental injuries in children.[21] They noted that many training programs in China are fragmented and lack standardization, leading to inconsistent levels of preparedness among educators. Furthermore, there is a significant disparity in the quality of training between urban and rural areas, with rural educators often having less access to comprehensive training programs and resources.[1122]

Case studies and specific examples

Several case studies and specific examples from China illustrate the challenges and successes in first aid training for early childhood educators. One notable example is the Beijing Municipal Education Commission’s initiative to provide standardized first aid training for all early childhood educators in the city.[42] This program, developed in partnership with local hospitals and medical institutions, aims to ensure that all educators are equipped with essential first aid skills. Preliminary evaluations of the program have shown positive outcomes, with educators reporting increased confidence and competence in managing emergencies.[22]

Another example is a pilot program in Zhejiang Province, where a collaboration between local education authorities and medical institutions has been established to offer regular first aid training workshops for early childhood educators.[20] These workshops focus on practical skills and real-life scenarios, providing educators with hands-on experience in performing first aid procedures. Feedback from participants has been overwhelmingly positive, with many educators expressing a desire for more frequent training opportunities.[11]

Analysis of the effectiveness of existing training programs and initiatives

Despite these positive examples, the overall effectiveness of existing first aid training programs and initiatives in China remains mixed. Pan noted that while some programs are well-structured and effective, many others are insufficiently rigorous and fail to provide educators with the necessary skills and knowledge.[20] This inconsistency is largely due to the lack of a standardized national framework for first aid training in early childhood education. Different first-aid training methods on knowledge retention of caregivers and teachers are also demanded.[11] Besides, Zhou and Sen pointed out that the effectiveness of training programs is often limited by resource constraints and insufficient follow-up.[21] Many training sessions are one-off events with little to no opportunities for educators to refresh and update their skills. This lack of continuous professional development undermines the long-term effectiveness of the training and reduces educators confidence in handling emergencies.[101116]

Furthermore, there is often a disconnect between policy and practice. While government policies advocate for the inclusion of first aid training in early childhood education, the implementation of these policies is frequently inadequate. Li et al. highlighted that many institutions do not prioritize first aid training due to competing demands and limited resources, resulting in a lack of comprehensive training for educators.[16]

Discussion

The findings of this review confirm the critical importance of comprehensive first aid training for early childhood educators, echoing the emphasis found in global best practices. For instance, Ilha et al. demonstrated that structured educational interventions significantly improved teachers’ emergency response skills, a trend similarly observed in countries like the United States, Canada, Germany, and the United Kingdom, where mandatory training and ongoing recertification are standard components of educator development.[4] These countries have established national training frameworks that promote consistency and reinforce skills through repeated exposure and practice, a feature that contributes to greater educator confidence and preparedness, as also evidenced in studies by Alshammari and Ganfure et al. Compared to these developed settings, the results from China indicate considerable gaps and inconsistencies in training quality and access.[741] Many Chinese educators, especially in rural areas, lack sufficient theoretical knowledge and practical first aid skills. These findings align with Pan and Li et al., who found that educators were generally aware of first aid principles but struggled with applying critical skills like CPR or choking response.[101620] The lack of standardization in training programs, highlighted by Zhou and Sen, contributes to wide variability in competence across different regions.[21] This disparity is further exacerbated by insufficient governmental support and the absence of national mandates requiring such training. While some local initiatives in China—such as those in Beijing and Zhejiang—have shown promise in improving educators’ preparedness, they remain isolated examples. Unlike the consistent and structured approaches observed in Germany[3] or Canada,[27] many programs in China lack regular follow-up or recertification, which reduces the long-term effectiveness of the training. This is a critical concern, especially as the literature[41] consistently supports the need for continuous professional development to maintain high readiness levels.

In addition, this review highlights a policy-to-practice gap in China. While governmental policies acknowledge the need for first aid training in early education, enforcement is uneven. Similar issues have been noted in other developing countries, such as Ghana and Ethiopia,[141] where limited infrastructure and resource allocation hinder training efforts. This suggests that, although awareness exists, implementation mechanisms remain weak, and policy frameworks require better enforcement and resourcing.

What sets this review apart is its dual focus: it not only synthesizes the global standards for first aid training but also contextualizes these practices within China’s evolving early childhood education landscape. By identifying the specific barriers—such as inconsistent program quality, rural–urban disparities, and lack of follow-up training—this study extends previous research and offers a detailed roadmap for targeted interventions.

These findings also support the idea that systematic professional development and recertification programs, as implemented in Japan and Australia,[3031] could be adapted to the Chinese context. Integrating such models would likely enhance the overall preparedness of educators and foster a culture of safety in early learning environments.

In conclusion, the results of this review underscore the urgent need for a coordinated national strategy in China, informed by global best practices and tailored to local needs. Strengthening policy enforcement, standardizing training content, and ensuring equitable resource distribution are essential steps toward ensuring that all early childhood educators are well-equipped to handle emergencies effectively.

Implications for policy, practice, and future research

Policy implications

Policymakers should establish and enforce standardized national training requirements for first aid in early childhood education, ensuring that all educators receive comprehensive and consistent training. Allocating resources equitably is also crucial; government and educational authorities must support first aid training, particularly in under-resourced rural areas, to address disparities in access to training. Additionally, policies should mandate regular recertification and ongoing professional development opportunities to ensure that educators’ skills remain current and effective.

Practice implications

Educational institutions should integrate first aid training into their certification programs, ensuring that all educators possess the necessary skills before entering the workforce. Institutions should offer regular refresher courses and professional development opportunities to reinforce and update educators’ first aid knowledge and skills. Schools and early childhood education centers should foster a culture of safety and preparedness by regularly practicing emergency drills and ensuring that all staff are trained and confident in their first aid abilities.

Future research

Future research should focus on evaluating the effectiveness of different first aid training models and identifying best practices that can be adapted to the Chinese context. Longitudinal studies examining the impact of continuous professional development on educators’ first aid skills and confidence would provide valuable insights. Additionally, research exploring innovative training methods, such as online and blended learning approaches, could offer solutions to address resource constraints and enhance accessibility.

The next step in the research will involve selecting over 1000 early childhood educators in China to conduct a comprehensive investigation into the current state of their first aid knowledge and skills. This will be achieved through a pretest, intervention, and posttest methodology. The study will not only utilize questionnaires but also include offline assessments to test practical skills, thus obtaining an accurate understanding of the current situation. Following this, a systematic training program will be implemented to evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention.

Conclusion

This review highlights the critical importance of comprehensive first aid training for early childhood educators. While developed countries have established effective and standardized training programs, significant gaps and challenges exist in China, particularly in terms of resource limitations, lack of standardized frameworks, and insufficient ongoing professional development. Addressing these issues requires concerted efforts from policymakers, educational institutions, and other stakeholders. Enhancing first aid training for early childhood educators is essential for ensuring the safety and well-being of young children. Well-trained educators are better equipped to handle emergencies, reduce the severity of injuries, and create a safer learning environment. Comprehensive and continuous training not only improves practical skills but also boosts educators’ confidence and preparedness. To achieve the goal of improving first aid training for early childhood educators in China, it is imperative that all stakeholders—policymakers, educational institutions, medical professionals, and the broader community—work collaboratively. By developing standardized training programs, ensuring equitable resource allocation, and promoting a culture of safety and preparedness, we can create a safer and more secure environment for young children. The findings of this review underscore the urgent need for action and provide a roadmap for achieving meaningful improvements in first aid training for early childhood educators.

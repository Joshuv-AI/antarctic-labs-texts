Enhancing image retrieval via Siamese network-based hashing with gated residual connections
Authors: Alireza Nazari, Kambiz Rahbar, Ziaeddin Beheshtifard, Maryam Khademi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper presents a supervised hashing framework built on a Siamese architecture, where gated residual connections substantially enhance the quality of compressed representations. The model employs ConvNeXt-Base as the backbone, combining the inductive biases of convolutional networks with modern architectural principles inspired by transformers. In the hash-generation pathway, the learned representation must simultaneously preserve essential identity information and apply nonlinear transformations for effective compression and separation. To achieve this, a learnable gate is introduced between two complementary branches: (1) a shallow identity/residual branch that preserves the core feature structure extracted by the backbone, and (2) a deeper transformed branch that performs nonlinear projection and disentanglement. The adaptive gating mechanism dynamically balances these two paths, enabling the network to retain discriminative local and global cues while suppressing irrelevant variations. As a result, the proposed design produces stable, semantically consistent, and highly discriminative binary hash codes. The Siamese network is trained using Triplet Loss to enforce similarity preservation in Hamming space. Extensive experiments on fine-grained benchmarks (CUB-200-2011 and NABirds) as well as a coarse-grained dataset (CIFAR-10) demonstrate that the proposed framework consistently outperforms or matches state-of-the-art hashing methods, validating its robustness and generalization across different levels of semantic granularity.

Introduction

Deep supervised hashing converts high-dimensional visual data into compact binary codes for scalable image retrieval, using methods such as global, local, attention-enhanced, pairwise, and privacy-aware approaches. Global representation methods extract semantic information from entire images. DEH1 uses list-wise distance estimation, WalshHash2 applies Walsh transforms, RCSH3 employs contrastive self-supervision, and ViTH-RFG4 integrates transformers with residual fuzzy generation. While scalable and fast, these methods can lose semantic precision when the background dominates, especially in datasets with high intra-class variability. Local and region-based hashing methods capture fine-grained visual cues. SRGTNet5 fuses subregion-guided CNN and transformers, CIDH6 uses hybrid attention, DAHNet7 combines global and local activations, and SPBH8 isolates object regions with multiscale features. These methods improve retrieval in detail-sensitive tasks but increase computational cost and risk overfitting to prominent regions, potentially missing subtle yet relevant features in complex scenes. Hybrid attention methods combine global and local features. HAAW9 uses spatial attention with adaptive weighting, SLITH10 fuses Swin Transformers with channel-separated attention, CTSAH11 integrates CNNs and Transformers via dual-space attention, and FMTH12 applies frequency decoupling with depth fusion. These methods are flexible and semantically rich but require large datasets, complex training, and are sensitive to imbalance or noise, which can degrade hash quality.

Siamese and pairwise hashing directly optimize similarity in embedding or Hamming space. UTAP13 and UTAP + + use meta-learning and adversarial perturbations for privacy, MATDH14 combines multi-attention with triplet hashing, SPRCH15 and DMNSH16,17 employ relational contrastive learning, and DSPH18 applies dual-level self-paced learning. These methods capture pairwise semantics effectively but may have unstable convergence and limited focus on discriminative spatial regions. Robust and privacy-preserving hashing models address domain shift, confounding factors, and data security. ACH19 uses counterfactual reasoning, SHPIR20 applies segmented hash codes, ViTH-RFG4 leverages adversarial training, DHMI21 maximizes mutual information, SPH22 employs semantic proxies, and CFBH23 uses causal inference with noise injection. These methods improve real-world retrieval but often rely on domain-specific assumptions, limiting general applicability. In remote sensing and large-scale retrieval, CIDH6, DOFSH24, and HBRSIR25 use multiscale attention and fusion, FSISR26 applies semantic ranking for multispectral data, and MRKH27 leverages Riemannian kernel learning. These methods achieve high accuracy on spatially distributed or multisource data but may generalize poorly to natural images or datasets without structured metadata.

Unsupervised and semi-supervised hashing methods use clustering, pseudo-labeling, or contrastive learning. RCSH3, UDHPM28, and UDTrHash29 generate codes without supervision, FSCH30 aligns local and global features, and SBHA31 employs binary autoencoders with stochastic error modeling. These methods reduce labeling effort but often lack semantic precision and can be unstable on diverse datasets. Recent advances highlight the importance of mechanisms that can adaptively balance preserved and transformed features. Gated residual connections offer a principled way to maintain identity information while enabling feature adaptation, noise suppression, and enhanced discriminability. Their effectiveness suggests they can provide similar benefits in supervised hashing, motivating their integration into Siamese-based frameworks for robust, separable, and stable binary representations. Online and dynamic retrieval methods, including OQEH32, DNSH33, and DMNSH17, use query expansion, adaptive quadruplet loss, and multi-negative training to handle evolving data streams. While adaptable, they face memory and latency challenges in high-throughput settings.

Gated residual mechanisms enhance feature representation by preserving identity information while allowing adaptive transformations. In medical imaging, RAN-GRU34 and DA-ResBiGRU35 fuse identity and transformed features for accurate segmentation and classification. Similar gating strategies improve noise robustness and feature separability in translation initiation site recognition36, high-voltage motor fault diagnosis37, and IGBT RUL prediction38, balancing preserved and adaptive feature pathways. Gated residuals improve sequence and time-series modeling by handling long-term dependencies and non-stationarity. RT-GRU39 and GRAformer40 use residual connections and gating for stability and efficiency, HGRU-MRAM41 preserves global and local EEG contexts, CHIO-based gated networks42 enable adaptive fusion for cardiovascular risk prediction, and multi-layer gated temporal convolution networks43 enhance receptive fields and hierarchical filtering for RUL prediction. Gated residuals enhance image processing and segmentation by refining features, preserving boundaries, and suppressing noise. ResGNet44 improves prostate MR segmentation, GRRNet45 aids building extraction from aerial images, multi-branch gated networks46 refine contextual features, S3AR U-Net47 provides precise glottis segmentation, and attention-guided residual shrinkage with GRUs48 enhances human activity recognition by filtering noisy features.

Gated residuals enhance hybrid architectures for joint tasks and uncertainty modeling. CTHD-Net49 improves dehazing with residual attention and gated boosting, ParNet50 captures multi-scale features for super-resolution and denoising, DeepResGRU51 models speech enhancement with bidirectional gating, convolutional residual GRUs52 predict transient stability, and adaptive gated residual RCNNs53 support fake news detection with balanced feature propagation. Gated residuals broadly enable adaptive, noise-resistant, and discriminative representations. Building on this, the proposed Siamese supervised hashing framework introduces a learnable gate between identity and transformed paths, combining cues while applying compression. Coupled with triplet loss, it produces stable, separable, and robust binary codes, reflecting performance gains seen in other gated residual applications.

The paper introduces a supervised hashing framework based on a Siamese architecture, in which a gated residual mechanism plays a central role in improving the quality of compact representations. The key idea is to enable the hash generation process to simultaneously preserve identity information while learning discriminative transformations. To this end, the proposed design incorporates two complementary pathways: (1) a shallow identity path that retains the original feature structure extracted from the backbone, and (2) a transformed path that performs nonlinear compression and feature disentanglement. A learnable gating function dynamically balances these two pathways in an element-wise manner, allowing the model to adaptively regulate the contribution of preserved and transformed features for each input sample. Unlike conventional attention-based fusion methods that primarily reweight feature responses, or residual hashing schemes that rely on fixed additive shortcuts, the proposed approach enables data-dependent modulation of information flow in the hash space. This results in more stable and discriminative binary representations. Finally, the Siamese training strategy with triplet loss enforces semantic consistency by pulling similar samples closer and pushing dissimilar ones apart in the Hamming space.

A deep supervised hashing framework with gated residual projection and pairwise similarity modeling

This section describes the proposed deep supervised hashing framework designed to generate compact and discriminative binary codes for efficient and accurate image retrieval. The framework is motivated by the need to simultaneously preserve semantic similarity and discriminative representational power, two objectives that are often addressed independently in traditional hashing systems.

The architecture is organized into three main stages: (1) Hierarchical Feature Extraction using a ConvNeXt-Base backbone, (2) Feature Refinement through a gated residual projection module, and (3) Discriminative Hash Code Learning via a Siamese network optimized with triplet supervision. Final retrieval is performed by computing Hamming distances between the generated binary codes, followed by a k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN) search in the Hamming space to ensure fast and scalable image matching.

In the first stage, ConvNeXt-Base is employed as the primary feature extractor. As a modern convolutional architecture inspired by Transformer design principles, ConvNeXt combines hierarchical feature representation with large-kernel depthwise convolutions, enabling the capture of both fine-grained local details and broader contextual patterns. We utilize features from the “classifier.1” layer, which retains high-level semantic information while remaining suitable for compact projection.

In the second stage, these extracted features are refined and projected using a gated residual projection module. The proposed module employs a learnable gating mechanism to control the balance between the original feature representation and its transformed projection. This gating strategy enables the model to preserve essential identity-level information while still allowing for discriminative transformation, thereby reducing over-smoothing and mitigating feature degradation in deep projection layers.

The third stage performs supervised hash code learning within a Siamese architecture. A triplet margin loss is used to ensure that embeddings from semantically similar images remain close in the learned space, while dissimilar samples are pushed farther apart. The final binary codes are obtained through an element-wise sign activation, producing outputs in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\{-1,+1{\}}^{K}$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:K$$\end{document} is the desired code length.

The technical details of the ConvNeXt feature extraction and the gated residual hashing projection module are provided in Subsections 2.1 and 2.2, respectively.

ConvNeXt-base: architectural review and methodological insights

ConvNeXt-Base is a modern convolutional architecture that revisits and modernizes classical CNN design by integrating advances inspired by transformer architectures, aiming to bridge the gap between convolutional and transformer-based vision models. Unlike pure transformer backbones, ConvNeXt preserves the inductive biases of convolutions such as locality and translation equivariance, while adopting design elements that improve scalability and performance on high-resolution images.

The architecture is organized into four stages, each progressively reducing spatial resolution and increasing channel dimensionality, similar to traditional ResNet-like designs but with key modifications. In contrast to transformer-based self-attention mechanisms, ConvNeXt leverages the inherent inductive biases of convolutions, which offer greater storage efficiency and robustness for moderate-sized datasets. The input features to this module are obtained from the “classifier.1” layer of the ConvNeXt-Base model, which provides semantically rich, spatially aggregated embeddings suitable for compact hashing (see Fig. 1).

where the index \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:i$$\end{document} runs over all \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:B\times\:k$$\end{document} elements of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{f}_{gated}$$\end{document} (i.e., all samples in the mini-batch and all hash dimensions), and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\epsilon\:>0$$\end{document} is a small threshold (typically 0.1–0.3). The total training objective is defined as9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:L={L}_{triplet}+\lambda\:{L}_{quant}$$\end{document}

where λ > 0 is a hyperparameter that controls the strength of the quantization regularization term relative to the triplet loss. This term effectively creates a margin around the decision boundary by pushing ambiguous activations away from zero, yielding more stable binary codes without sacrificing symmetry or introducing bias.

The gated residual design strikes a balance between preservation of original semantic content and learned nonlinear transformations. The identity path directly propagates discriminative features from the early projection stage, while the transformed path introduces flexible nonlinear mappings that further refine the representation. The adaptive gating mechanism dynamically adjusts the contribution of each path, ensuring that the pre-sign activation \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{f}_{gated}$$\end{document} remains well-conditioned (symmetric, variance-normalized, and sufficiently separated from the zero threshold on average). As a result, the final binary codes exhibit enhanced robustness and discriminative power, leading to consistent improvements in retrieval performance, as validated through comprehensive ablation studies and comparisons with state-of-the-art baselines.

The learned binary hash codes are subsequently used for fast approximate nearest neighbor search based on Hamming distance within a k-NN retrieval framework. By producing compact binary representations, the proposed design improves storage-efficient retrieval and similarity computation while maintaining high retrieval accuracy. From a methodological perspective, the proposed gated residual projection differs from existing fusion strategies in that it performs fine-grained, element-wise integration of identity and transformed features within a unified and end-to-end trainable framework. This allows the model to achieve a more effective balance between representation stability and discriminability, which is particularly critical for preserving semantic structure under aggressive dimensionality reduction in hash learning.

Triplet dataset construction and triplet loss optimization

To effectively model semantic similarity in the learned Hamming space, our framework employs a supervised pairwise learning strategy based on triplet sampling. Triplet-based supervision, originally proposed for face verification and widely adopted in metric learning, allows the network to simultaneously pull semantically similar images closer and push dissimilar images farther apart in the embedding space. This mechanism is particularly well-suited for binary hashing, where the learned codes must reflect class-level distinctions under strict compactness constraints.

Experimental results

All experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 4090, 24 GB and 64 GB of RAM. The software environment was based on Ubuntu 24.04 with Python 3.12.3 as the programming language. The deep learning models were implemented using PyTorch 2.5.1 + cu124, and additional libraries such as Torchvision, NumPy, scikit-learn, and Matplotlib were used for data processing, evaluation, and visualization.

Datasets, training hyperparameters and evaluation metric

Datasets

The experimental evaluation of the proposed framework is conducted on three benchmark datasets: CUB-200-2011, NABirds, and CIFAR-10, covering both fine-grained and coarse-grained image retrieval scenarios. The CUB-200-2011 dataset contains 11,788 images from 200 bird species, with approximately 60 images per class. It is widely regarded as a challenging benchmark for fine-grained image retrieval due to high intra-class similarity and subtle inter-class differences in visual attributes such as plumage color, beak shape, and pose variations. The NABirds dataset is a larger and more diverse fine-grained bird dataset, comprising over 48,000 images across 555 bird species. Compared to CUB-200-2011, NABirds exhibits greater intra-class variability and finer inter-class distinctions, making it particularly suitable for evaluating the robustness of hashing methods under increased semantic complexity. In addition, the CIFAR-10 dataset is included as a representative coarse-grained benchmark, consisting of 60,000 images from 10 object categories with significant appearance variation. Although class boundaries in CIFAR-10 are more distinct than in fine-grained datasets, its diversity in background, scale, and viewpoint provides a complementary evaluation setting for assessing the generalization ability of the proposed hashing framework. Evaluating on both fine-grained (CUB-200-2011 and NABirds) and coarse-grained (CIFAR-10) datasets allows us to comprehensively assess the effectiveness, robustness, and generalization capability of the proposed method across different retrieval granularities.

In this work, all datasets are utilized throughout the entire experimental pipeline, with a 70% data split allocated for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for testing. This split ensures sufficient data for supervised training while providing robust validation and unbiased testing to fairly assess retrieval performance. The dataset’s rich annotations and balanced class distribution support reliable training and meaningful evaluation.

Ablation study

To ensure a representative and meaningful comparison in our ablation study, we selected five diverse backbones spanning different architectural families. The primary model, ConvNeXt-Base, serves as the baseline, representing a modern convolutional architecture that combines ResNet-inspired design with transformer-like optimization strategies, delivering strong spatial modeling while preserving the inductive biases and parameter-sharing advantages of convolutional networks. For comparison with hierarchical vision transformers, Swin V2-B was included to evaluate the benefits of window-based self-attention. To assess flat transformer models without spatial hierarchy, ViT-B/16 was selected to highlight the role of inductive bias and patch-based tokenization. Additionally, two classical convolutional networks were considered: ResNet-50, known for its residual learning and robustness in feature extraction, and DenseNet-201, which leverages dense connectivity for effective feature reuse. All models were trained, validated, and tested on the CAB dataset using a 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% testing split, ensuring consistency and fairness in evaluation. Moreover, we conduct an ablation study under a fixed evaluation protocol (64-bit hash codes with N = all).

This superior performance of ConvNeXt-Base can be attributed to its architectural design, which blends the advantages of both classical convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer-inspired improvements. Unlike the pure transformer models (e.g., ViT) that rely solely on self-attention over patch embeddings without explicit spatial inductive biases, ConvNeXt maintains the locality and translation equivariance inherent in convolutions, enabling it to capture fine-grained spatial features essential for detailed image retrieval tasks such as CUB-200’s fine-grained bird species classification.

At the same time, ConvNeXt incorporates modern design elements inspired by transformers, such as large kernel sizes and layer normalization techniques, which improve feature representation and training stability. This hybrid approach allows ConvNeXt to strike a balance between the flexibility of self-attention and the spatial specificity of convolutions, leading to improved discriminative capability in hashing-based retrieval.

Compared to Swin V2-Base, which uses hierarchical window-based self-attention to capture spatial dependencies, ConvNeXt achieves higher mAP despite lacking explicit attention mechanisms. This suggests that for CUB-200’s fine-grained features, ConvNeXt’s convolutional inductive biases and optimized architecture may better preserve local texture and shape details crucial for distinguishing subtle class differences.

Classical CNNs like ResNet-50 and DenseNet-201, although powerful and well-established, fall behind ConvNeXt due to their relatively older architectural designs, which may not leverage recent improvements in normalization, activation, and efficient convolutional blocks that ConvNeXt benefits from. DenseNet’s dense connectivity helps feature reuse but might not be as effective in extracting the fine discriminative features as ConvNeXt’s optimized blocks.

On the fine-grained datasets (CUB-200 and NABirds), our framework outperforms most baselines, particularly in capturing subtle inter-class variations. For instance, compared to DAHNet7 (83.98% on CUB-200 and 56.46% on NABirds), our model gains 1.80% and 11.65%, respectively. DAHNet relies on dual activation branches for global and local semantic activation, which effectively modulates dominant regions but can overlook subtle complementary cues due to its inter-region enrichment module’s focus on channel-specific correlations. In contrast, our gated residual projection module preserves essential identity-level information while mitigating over-smoothing, allowing for more balanced feature refinement. This is particularly beneficial in fine-grained scenarios, where high intra-class variance (e.g., variations in bird poses or lighting) demands robust preservation of discriminative details during projection to binary codes. Similarly, against CFBH23 (76.19% on CUB-200 and 66.20% on NABirds), we achieve gains of 9.59% and 1.91%. CFBH addresses overfitting via causal feature learning and binary noise injection (BNI) to suppress over-activated bits, but its reliance on causal inference for local feature fusion can introduce observational bias in highly variable fine-grained data. Our Siamese architecture with triplet supervision, combined with ConvNeXt-Base’s hierarchical extraction of local and contextual patterns, better enforces semantic similarity preservation, reducing such biases and enhancing generalization.

Our results also surpass SRGTNet5 (79.0% on CUB-200) by 6.78% and SPBH8 (85.17% on CUB-200) by 0.61%. SRGTNet integrates CNN-guided Transformers with part-significant region erasure to mine discriminative subregions, but its multi-stage mixture loss may not fully optimize for binary code balance in deep projections. SPBH employs multi-scale feature learning and adaptive category voting masks to retain semantic information in low-dimensional codes, yet it focuses primarily on object localization without explicit gating to control feature degradation. The gated residual mechanism in our framework dynamically balances original and projected features, inspired by Transformer principles in ConvNeXt, which prevents information loss and yields more compact, discriminative binaries.

On CIFAR-10, a coarse-grained dataset, our mAP of 94.92% is highly competitive, outperforming CTSAH11 (94.90%) by 0.02% and MNEP57 (78.09% at 32 bits) by a substantial margin, though slightly below ICVT58 (97.5%) and FMTH12 (98.31%). CTSAH uses a convolution-based Visual Transformer (CVT) with dual space shifting attention (DSSA) to fuse local and global features, but its classification loss may not sufficiently minimize intra-class distances in diverse datasets like CIFAR-10. MNEP leverages manifold projections (e.g., t-SNE/UMAP) for similarity measures, which excels in visualization but struggles with high-dimensional hashing due to its focus on neighbor embeddings without deep supervision. ICVT and FMTH achieve higher scores through nonlinear spatial group enhancement (NSGE) and frequency decoupling with Mamba-based fusion, respectively, which emphasize global-local integration via margin contrast or proxy losses. However, our triplet-based supervision in the Siamese network, paired with ConvNeXt’s large-kernel convolutions, effectively models pairwise similarities and hierarchical semantics, leading to near-SOTA performance while maintaining efficiency across fine- and coarse-grained tasks. The minor gap on CIFAR-10 can be attributed to our framework’s emphasis on fine-grained subtlety (e.g., gated residuals for identity preservation), which trades slight coarse-grained gains for broader applicability.

Overall, these results validate the efficacy of our three-stage architecture: ConvNeXt’s hierarchical extraction captures multi-level semantics, the gated residual projection reduces degradation in hash learning, and triplet supervision ensures discriminative binaries. This holistic approach not only alleviates common issues like overfitting and semantic loss but also generalizes well, as evidenced by consistent improvements over methods that prioritize either local activation (e.g., DAHNet, SPBH) or global fusion (e.g., SRGTNet, ICVT). Future work could explore adaptive gating thresholds for even finer dataset-specific tuning.

Conclusions

In this work, we proposed a deep supervised hashing framework for fine-grained image retrieval, leveraging ConvNeXt-Base as the backbone to combine the strong inductive biases of CNNs with modern architectural innovations inspired by transformers. Central to our design is a gated residual module that adaptively regulates the information exchange between identity features and their transformed counterparts. This mechanism ensures that discriminative details are preserved while suppressing irrelevant variations, leading to hash codes with higher semantic fidelity and intra-class compactness. In contrast to conventional attention-based or residual fusion mechanisms, the proposed gated design enables adaptive, data-dependent control over information flow, allowing the model to better balance feature preservation and transformation in the hash space.

The gated residual design is particularly well aligned with the hierarchical and convolution-friendly structure of ConvNeXt, enabling it to retain the backbone’s localized texture sensitivity while benefiting from global feature refinement. Extensive experiments on multiple datasets (including CUB-200, NABirds, and CIFAR-10) demonstrated that this synergy delivers substantial gains over conventional baselines, with notable improvements in precision, recall, and mAP. The analysis further showed that the proposed gating mechanism helps the network capture subtle inter-class differences while maintaining bit-level consistency in the hash space, thereby enhancing retrieval stability.

Moreover, the evaluation incorporates multiple random seeds, reporting mean ± standard deviation and 95% confidence intervals, as well as top-N retrieval curves and qualitative visualizations of retrieved results. These additions confirm that the observed improvements are both statistically robust and generalizable across fine- and coarse-grained retrieval scenarios.

Overall, the integration of ConvNeXt’s representational strength with an adaptive residual gating strategy provides a powerful and efficient solution for large-scale image retrieval. Future work may investigate more advanced attention-gated residual designs or hybrid supervised–self-supervised loss schemes to further improve robustness in complex, multi-label retrieval scenarios.

While the proposed hashing framework is computationally lightweight at the hash generation stage, the overall computational cost and inference sensitivity are largely determined by the ConvNeXt backbone. The gated residual projection and hash layer introduce only a marginal overhead in terms of parameters and latency compared to backbone feature extraction, as demonstrated in Sect. 3.2.6.

As with most binary hashing methods, the final retrieval performance may be sensitive to feature perturbations occurring near the binarization threshold, which can lead to amplified errors in Hamming space. Although the proposed quantization-aware regularization alleviates this effect, robustness to severely out-of-distribution samples remains an open challenge. These considerations, however, do not detract from the practical efficiency of the proposed design under the evaluated retrieval settings.

The MEC90 of Ropivacaine for Ultrasound-Guided Pectoral Nerve Block Type II for Modified Radical Mastectomy: A Biased-Coin Up-and-Down Sequential Trial
Authors: Yu Du, Wenbo Liu, Yangyang Wu, Lin Ma, Yi Tang, Ji Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed malignancy among women worldwide and remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality.1 Surgery remains a cornerstone of treatment for operable breast cancer. However, perioperative pain after breast cancer surgery continues to pose a major clinical challenge. Previous studies have reported that up to 68.9% of patients undergoing modified radical mastectomy (MRM) experience moderate-to-severe acute postoperative pain,2 and 25–60% may subsequently develop persistent postsurgical pain.3 Effective perioperative analgesia may facilitate postoperative recovery, reduce opioid-related adverse effects, and potentially decrease the risk of chronic pain, thereby alleviating the socioeconomic burden on patients, their families, and society.

Regional analgesic techniques are important components of multimodal analgesia for breast cancer surgery.4 Ultrasound-guided pectoral nerve block type II (PECS II block) is a chest wall fascial plane block that has been shown to provide effective analgesia for patients undergoing MRM.5 In addition, a systematic review by Elshanbary et al6 suggested that PECS II block may provide analgesia comparable to that of thoracic paravertebral block for breast cancer surgery, while being technically easier to perform and associated with fewer procedure-related complications, such as hypotension and pneumothorax. These findings support PECS II block as a safe and effective regional analgesic technique for MRM.

Ropivacaine is widely used for peripheral nerve blocks because of its favorable sensory-motor separation and relatively low cardiotoxicity. For PECS II block, a volume of 30 mL of local anesthetic is commonly used to ensure adequate spread within the fascial planes.7,8 However, the concentration of ropivacaine used for PECS II block in breast cancer surgery varies considerably across studies, generally ranging from 0.2% to 0.5%.5,9 An unnecessarily high concentration may increase the risk of local anesthetic systemic toxicity, whereas an excessively low concentration may result in incomplete block or inadequate analgesia. Therefore, identifying the lowest effective concentration of ropivacaine required for successful PECS II block may help optimize analgesic efficacy while minimizing drug exposure.

The biased-coin design up-and-down sequential method (BCD-UDM) is an adaptive dose-finding design that is particularly useful for estimating higher-percentile effective doses or concentrations, such as the 90% minimum effective concentration (MEC90) or MEC95.10,11 This method has been applied to determine the MEC90 of ropivacaine for several ultrasound-guided regional blocks.12,13 However, to our knowledge, the MEC90 of ropivacaine for ultrasound-guided PECS II block in patients undergoing MRM has not yet been reported. Therefore, this study aimed to determine the MEC90 of 30 mL ropivacaine for ultrasound-guided PECS II block in patients undergoing MRM using the BCD-UDM.

Materials and Methods

Ethics Approval and Trial Registration

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanchong Hospital of Beijing Anzhen Hospital, Capital Medical University (Nanchong Central Hospital) (Ethics Approval No. 2023–050), and all study procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The trial was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry on September 30, 2023 (Registration No. ChiCTR2300076311). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment.

Study Design and Participants

This was a prospective sequential dose-finding trail conducted at Nanchong Central Hospital between November 2023 and May 2024. Patients scheduled for elective MRM were screened for eligibility. The inclusion criteria were female sex, age 18–65 years, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I– III, and undergoing elective unilateral MRM. The exclusion criteria were infection at the block site, allergy to local anesthetics or other anesthetic agents, bleeding diathesis, severe hepatic or renal dysfunction, severe cardiopulmonary disease, psychiatric disorders, and peripheral neuropathy. Withdrawal criteria included withdrawal of consent at any time and the occurrence of serious adverse events that precluded continuation of the study.

Biased-Coin Design Up-and-Down Sequential Method

The ropivacaine concentration was determined according to the BCD-UDM. All patients received a fixed total volume of 30 mL ropivacaine at the assigned concentration. The initial concentration for the first patient was 0.2%. The concentration administered to each subsequent patient was determined by the PECS II block response of the preceding patient. After a negative response, the concentration for the next patient was increased by 0.02%. After a positive response, the next concentration was determined by biased-coin randomization: the concentration was decreased by 0.02% with an 11% probability and maintained unchanged with an 89% probability.11

Randomization and Blinding

A study coordinator generated the biased-coin randomization sequence and concealed the concentration assignments in sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes. For each patient, the corresponding envelope was opened immediately before the block by the anesthesiologist performing the PECS II block, who was responsible for preparing and administering the study solution. This anesthesiologist took no further part in block assessment, intraoperative anesthesia management, or data analysis. Patients, the block assessor, and the anesthesiologist responsible for intraoperative anesthesia management remained blinded to the assigned ropivacaine concentration.

Ultrasound-Guided PECS II Block

All patients fasted for 6–8 hours preoperatively. After arrival in the operating room, routine monitoring was established, including electrocardiography, noninvasive blood pressure, heart rate (HR), and pulse oximetry. Compound sodium chloride solution (5–8 mL/kg; Kelun Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China) was administered intravenously. To improve patient comfort during block placement, all patients received standardized mild sedation with sufentanil 5 μg (Yichang Humanwell Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China) and midazolam 2 mg (Jiangsu Nhwa Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China). During a quiet resting period, mean arterial pressure (MAP) was measured three times on the nonoperative upper arm, and HR was recorded simultaneously. The mean MAP and HR values were used as the patients’ baseline.

With the patient in the supine position, the ipsilateral arm was abducted. A high-frequency linear ultrasound probe (GE Healthcare, USA) was initially placed in the infraclavicular region to identify the axillary vessels and pectoral muscles. The probe was then moved caudally and laterally to the level of the third rib, where the pectoralis major, pectoralis minor, serratus anterior muscle, and third rib were identified. After skin disinfection and local infiltration with 2 mL of 2% lidocaine (Hebei Tiancheng Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China) at the puncture site, an in-plane needle insertion technique was used under real-time ultrasound guidance. After negative aspiration for blood or air, 20 mL of ropivacaine at the assigned concentration was injected into the interfascial plane between the pectoralis minor and serratus anterior muscles. The needle was then withdrawn to the interfascial plane between the pectoralis major and pectoralis minor muscles, where the remaining 10 mL of ropivacaine at the same assigned concentration was injected.14 Technical success of the block procedure was defined as visualization of local anesthetic spread separating the target muscle layers, with the needle tip confirmed within the corresponding interfascial plane (Figure 1).

Block Assessment and Response Definition

Fifteen minutes after completion of the block, another anesthesiologist who was blinded to the assigned ropivacaine concentration assessed and recorded the sensory block effect over the ipsilateral anterior and lateral thoracic wall corresponding to the T2–T6 dermatomes using blunt needle stimulation.15 Sensory block was graded on a three-point scale compared with the contralateral non-blocked side: grade 0, no change in pain sensation; grade 1, reduced pain sensation; and grade 2, complete loss of pain sensation.

A positive response was defined as a sensory block score of grade 1 or higher at all tested sites within the T2–T6 dermatomal distribution, together with an increase in MAP of less than 20% from baseline within 5 min after skin incision. A negative response was defined as a sensory block score of grade 0 at any tested site within the T2–T6 dermatomal distribution, or an increase in MAP of 20% or more from baseline within 5 min after skin incision.15,16

General Anesthesia and Postoperative Analgesia

After block assessment, dexamethasone 8 mg (Shandong Xinhua Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China) was administered intravenously. General anesthesia was induced with propofol 2 mg/kg (Sichuan Guorui Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China), cisatracurium 0.2 mg/kg (Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China), and sufentanil 0.4 μg/kg. Tracheal intubation was performed after adequate muscle relaxation. During surgery, volume-controlled ventilation was applied with a tidal volume of 6 mL/kg and a positive end-expiratory pressure of 5 cmH2O, and the respiratory rate was adjusted to maintain PETCO2 between 35 and 45 mmHg. Anesthesia was maintained with sevoflurane (Shanghai Hengrui Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China) at an inspired concentration of 1.5%–3%. The bispectral index (BIS; Aspect Medical Systems, Inc., MA, USA) was monitored and maintained between 40 and 60. Surgery was strated after induction and stabilization of anesthesia. Active warming was used intraoperatively to maintain body temperature at 36–37°C, and 3 mg of cisatracurium was administered intravenously at 40-min intervals as needed.

At the end of surgery, sevoflurane administration was discontinued, and palonosetron 0.075 mg (Sichuan Hairong Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China) was administered to prevent postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV). After transfer to the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) with the tracheal tube in place, patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) was initiated at 2 mL/h using a 100 mL analgesic solution containing sufentanil 3 μg/kg, butorphanol 0.2 mg/kg (Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China), and palonosetron 0.25 mg.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the MEC90 of 30 mL ropivacaine for successful ultrasound-guided PECS II block in patients undergoing MRM. The MEC90 was estimated based on the sequential positive or negative responses, which were determined by sensory block assessment within the T2–T6 dermatomal distribution and the MAP response within 5 min after skin incision.

Safety outcomes were assessed in all patients who received PECS II block and included adverse events related to the block procedure, local anesthetic administration, anesthesia, and the postoperative hospital stay. These included pneumothorax, nerve injury, puncture-site hematoma, puncture-site infection, local anesthetic systemic toxicity, allergic reactions, PONV, and postoperative delirium. All adverse events were collected and recorded from block placement until hospital discharge.

Sample Size

To achieve MEC90 based on statistical requirements, a minimum of 45 positive reactions (ie, greater than 40 and a multiple of 9) are necessary, and estimation error is substantially reduced when the total sample size reaches 50 or more11,17 Therefore, patients were enrolled sequentially until the 45th evaluable positive response was observed, at which point the study was terminated.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate. Categorical variables were expressed as numbers and percentages. For MEC90 estimation, the observed response rates at each ropivacaine concentration were adjusted using the pooled-adjacent-violators algorithm (PAVA).18 The adjusted response rates were then analyzed using isotonic regression in R software (version 4.4.1) to estimate the MEC90. The 95% confidence interval (CI) was calculated using 2000 bootstrap resamples.18

Results

Participant Flow and Baseline Characteristics

A total of 57 patients were initially recruited. One patient refused the block before surgery. Another patient developed suspected anaphylactic shock after anesthesia induction following PECS II block; therefore, the block response could not be assessed, and this patient was excluded from the MEC90 analysis but included in the safety analysis. Ultimately, 55 patients were included in the MEC90 analysis, and 56 patients were included in the safety analysis. Positive responses were observed in 45 patients, and negative responses in 10 patients. Among the 10 negative responses, 4 were due to a sensory block score of grade 0 at one or more tested sites within the T2–T6 dermatomal distribution, and 6 were due to an increase in MAP of 20% or more within 5 min after skin incision (Figure 2). Safety Outcomes

Among the 56 patients who received PECS II block, one patient (1.79%) developed suspected anaphylactic shock after anesthesia induction. Puncture-site hematoma occurred in one patient (1.79%). No cases of pneumothorax, nerve injury, puncture-site infection, or local anesthetic systemic toxicity were recorded.

Postoperative adverse events were assessed in 55 patients who completed surgery and postoperative follow-up. PONV occurred in 14 patients (25.45%), and postoperative delirium occurred in one patient (1.82%).

Discussion

In this prospective sequential dose-finding study, the BCD-UDM was used to estimate the MEC90 of ropivacaine for ultrasound-guided PECS II block for intraoperative analgesia in women undergoing unilateral MRM. The main finding was that the MEC90 of a fixed volume of 30 mL ropivacaine was 0.23% (95% CI, 0.18% to 0.25%). No local anesthetic systemic toxicity or major block-related complications were observed in the safety analysis.

The Dixon up-and-down sequential method has been widely used in dose-finding studies. However, in the classical 1-up-1-down design, the concentration administered to the next patient is increased after a negative response and decreased after a positive response, causing observations to cluster around the median effective concentration. This makes the method more suitable for estimating MEC50 than for estimating high-percentile targets such as MEC90, which may be less reliable when derived from data concentrated around the median response level.10,19 In contrast, the BCD-UDM uses an asymmetric allocation rule after a positive response, allowing patient allocation to be concentrated around a prespecified high-percentile target.20 Because PECS II block is intended to provide effective analgesia in most patients undergoing MRM, the BCD-UDM was selected to estimate the ropivacaine concentration associated with a 90% probability of a positive response.

In some dose-finding studies of regional anesthesia, surgery is performed under nerve block alone, and block success is defined according to intraoperative pain, the need for rescue analgesic, or completion of surgery without conversion to general anesthesia.21 However, this approach may not be optimal for MRM. PECS II block mainly provides analgesic coverage for the anterior and lateral thoracic wall and axillary region, making it suitable for most breast and axillary components of MRM, although it may not completely block nociceptive inputs from the medial chest wall, deeper tissues, or areas with variable innervation. Garreffa et al22 reported that PECS II block combined with local infiltration and sedation could be used for breast cancer surgery without general anesthesia; however, this approach may raise concerns regarding respiratory depression during sedation and is generally reserved for patients who are unsuitable for general anesthesia. In current practice, PECS II block is more commonly used as part of multimodal analgesia combined with general anesthesia for breast cancer surgery. General anesthesia provides sedation and analgesia, thereby reducing the local anesthetic dose and toxicity risk associated with PECS II, and also helps to alleviate patient anxiety and fear.23 Therefore, in the present study, a positive response was defined using both sensory block assessment and the MAP response to skin incision after induction of general anesthesia.

The selection of the initial concentration is important in sequential dose-finding studies, as an initial concentration close to the expected target may improve estimation efficiency and reduce the required sample size.24 Deng et al9 compared 30 mL ropivacaine at concentrations of 0.2%, 0.3%, and 0.4% for PECS II block in patients undergoing MRM and found that 0.2% ropivacaine provided significant analgesia. Li et al25 reported an MEC90 of 0.22% ropivacaine using a fixed volume of 40 mL for ultrasound-guided rhomboid intercostal and subserratus block in patients undergoing video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery. Based on these findings and clinical experience, 0.2% ropivacaine was selected as the initial concentration in the present study. In the present study, PECS II block for intraoperative analgesia in MRM with 0.2% ropivacaine (60 mg) yielded a success rate of 81.82%, whereas the MEC90 of 0.23% (69 mg) significantly improved the success rate with only a modest dose increase. The relatively wide 95% confidence interval (0.18–0.25%) for the MEC90 of ropivacaine observed may reflect interindividual variability in the response to PECS II. The block effect seems to depend not only on the local anesthetic concentration, but also on patient factors like anatomy, drug spread, and mental or physical state, and disease factors like tumor size and local inflammation.

Regarding safety, one patient developed suspected anaphylactic shock approximately 20 min after completion of the PECS II block and about 1 min after tracheal intubation following anesthesia induction, with non-invasive blood pressure dropping to 64/38 mmHg, heart rate increasing to 128 bpm, airway pressure rising to 30 cmH2O, and tidal volume decreasing to 330 mL, suggestive of an anaphylactic reaction. Intravenous hydrocortisone 200 mg and epinephrine 50 μg were administered immediately, and the patient’s hemodynamic status stabilized approximately 5 minutes later. Subsequent allergen testing identified cisatracurium, rather than ropivacaine, as the causative agent. This patient was excluded from the MEC90 analysis because the block response could not be assessed, but was included in the safety analysis. Among the 56 patients who received PECS II block, no local anesthetic systemic toxicity or major block-related complications were observed. One puncture-site hematoma occurred and resolved without further complications. Patients undergoing breast cancer surgery are considered to be at high risk for PONV. Despite routine prophylaxis with dexamethasone and a 5-HT3 receptor antagonist in accordance with guideline recommendations,26 PONV occurred in 14 of 55 patients who completed surgery and postoperative follow-up (25.45%), which was slightly higher than the 20% rate reported by Moon et al27 in patients receiving PECS II for stage I breast reconstruction after mastectomy.

Study Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the MEC90 was estimated using a fixed ropivacaine volume of 30 mL and therefore cannot be directly extrapolated to other injection volumes Second, the positive response was defined using a composite endpoint that included both sensory block assessment and the MAP response after skin incision. Although clinically relevant under general anesthesia, objective nociception monitoring, such as an electroencephalography-derived nociception index, was not used; therefore, the assessment of intraoperative nociceptive response may not have been comprehensive. Third, this was a single-center study with a relatively homogeneous population, and the estimated MEC90 should be interpreted within the specific conditions of this study rather than as a universally applicable threshold.

Conclusion

The MEC90 of a fixed volume of 30 mL ropivacaine for ultrasound-guided PECS II block in patients undergoing MRM was 0.23%. Nevertheless, the limited sample size and individual differences may preclude a comprehensive evaluation of uncommon adverse events.

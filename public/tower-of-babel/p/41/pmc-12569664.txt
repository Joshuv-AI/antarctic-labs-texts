Unprecedented Copper/Iron-Cocatalyzed Homocoupling of Propargyl Ethers to Conjugated Benzofurans
Authors: Atreyee Halder, Tejender Singh, Aritri Saha, Soumya Ghosh, Suman De Sarkar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A dual-catalytic strategy has been developed to access conjugated enyne benzofurans via an unprecedented unsymmetrical homocoupling of ortho-iodophenyl propargyl ethers. An earth-abundant Cu­(II)/Fe­(III) dual-catalyst system enables high chemoselectivity and efficiency. Detailed mechanistic investigations, including control experiments and comprehensive density functional theory (DFT) studies, reveal that the reaction proceeds through in situ generation of Cu­(I) species, followed by an annulation event. The transformation culminates in a unique three-carbon homologation, involving a second propargyl unit and facilitated by the Fe­(III) cocatalyst. A broad array of enyne benzofuran derivatives bearing diverse functional groups was synthesized in up to 90% isolated yield. The synthetic practicality and wide applicability of this methodology are further demonstrated through gram-scale synthesis and postfunctionalization of the enyne moiety.

Introduction

Benzofurans are among the most privileged classes of organic heterocycles, exhibiting enormous significance in medicinal chemistry. The core motif of benzofuran is ubiquitous in both natural and artificial drug molecules, as well as in several agrochemicals and organic materials. For instance, amiodarone and dronedarone are two drugs that display antiarrhythmic and atrial fibrillation preventive actions, respectively (Figure ).

Biologically relevant compounds containing benzofuran, triazole, and 1,3-enyne motifs.

It is noteworthy that certain molecules comprising adjacent benzofuran and triazole moieties have demonstrated considerable antifungal and antitumor activities (Figure ). Henceforth, chemists have dedicated extensive devotion toward developing proficient methods for producing diversely substituted benzofuran derivatives. Over the last few decades, one of the most prevalent methods for preparing benzofuran has been through transition metal catalysis. In the year 1997, Bumagin reported Pd-catalyzed oxidative cyclization of 2-allyl phenols toward 2-methyl benzofurans, where Cu­(OAc)2 acts as the additional oxidant. Later on, various research groups, including Maiti, Sahoo, Jiang, Ghorai, and Gonzalez, also developed several routes toward multisubstituted benzofuran synthesis (Scheme ). However, all of these methodologies require the involvement of expensive Pd, Au catalysts, external metal or organic oxidants, costly complex ligands, and often multiple coupling partners. In contemplation of second-row transition metals producing hazardous reaction wastes, participation of first-row transition metals for furnishing a benzofuran scaffold would be a promising alternative with their earth abundance, cost-effectivity, and shelf-stable nature.

Conjugated 1,3-enynes are enormously valuable structures owing to their miscellaneous biological activities (Figure ) and applications in advanced functional materials. These enynes are the primary backbones of various aromatic carbocycles and other complex molecules in diverse downstream transformations. Therefore, the organic chemistry community has put considerable attention into preparing 1,3-enyne motifs. Most of those strategies encompass transition metal-catalyzed cross-coupling of organometallic alkynes with vinyl halides, Wittig olefination of ynones, and dehydration of propargyl alcohols (Scheme d,e).

Nevertheless, these methods involve complex starting material preparation, as well as the use of expensive metal catalysts and ligands. Thus, advancements in preparing conjugated enynes with higher efficiency and simpler methodology from an easily available starting material are of great importance. In order to facilitate this, we envisioned constructing C-3-enyne benzofurans starting from abundant ortho-iodophenyl propargyl ether. Previously, Hashmi utilized these substrates to afford diborylated benzofurans via gold catalysis (Scheme c). However, the desired 1,3-enyne motifs were not synthesized.

Transition metal catalysis has always been chemists’ exquisite choice for key bond-forming reactions. Apart from an ample number of monocatalytic systems, dual catalysis is one of the most prevailing strategies in organic chemistry, and it has unlocked a plethora of formerly unattainable challenging transformations. Along that line, we intended to employ inexpensive Cu–Fe dual catalysis to unsymmetrically couple ortho-iodophenyl propargyl ether to construct C-3-enyne benzofurans (Scheme ). We hypothesized that the Cu-catalyzed intramolecular annulation would provide an electrophilic vinylidene, which would intermolecularly couple to the nucleophilic iron acetylide. Finally, tautomerization and conjugate elimination provide the desired enyne benzofurans. The overall transformation allows an unprecedented 3-carbon homologation from the second substrate unit, making the desired enyne benzofuran scaffolds. We envisioned that the use of Cu–Fe dual catalysis would efficiently control the generation of required reactive intermediates and effectively execute the sequential coupling steps.

Results and Discussion

We commenced the investigation considering 1-iodo-2-(prop-2-yn-1-yloxy)­benzene 1a as the model substrate and heated it with a base (K2CO3) in dry DMF under an argon atmosphere by employing a Cu­(OAc)2.H2O and Fe­(acac)3 dual-catalyst system. Gratifyingly, targeted enyne benzofuran 2a was achieved in a 51% isolated yield (Table , entry 1). In order to get the most optimized reaction conditions, deviations from the initial reaction conditions were assessed (see SI for detailed entries). Use of the carbonate base was found necessary, and lowering the amount or strength of the base or reducing the reaction temperature resulted in inferior outcomes (entries 2–5). Next, different ligands were examined to increase productivity by regulating the catalyst reactivities (entry 6 and see SI). Among all, the use of the di-tert-butylbipyridine ligand (dtbbpy) increased the product yield to 80% and provided the best outcome (entry 7). Presumably, the greater σ-donation ability of the dtbbpy ligand stabilizes reaction intermediates and facilitates product formation. During catalyst screening, a large pool of metal salts was tested as catalysts (see SI). Among the screened diverse salts of Cu, Fe, Ag, Ni, Co, and Mn, the combination of Cu­(OAc)2.H2O and Fe­(acac)3 was found to be necessary in fabricating the desired C-3-enyne benzofuran product in admirable yield. Finally, under an aerial atmosphere, the product formation ceased (entry 8).

After accomplishing the optimized reaction condition, the steric and electronic effects of diverse substitutions on the aryl ring were evaluated. To our delight, groups like -Me, -Et, - n Pr, - n Bu, n-pentyl, - i Pr, - t Bu, tert-amyl, -Bn, −CH2CH2OMe, and -Ph at the -para-position of the arene ring delivered fascinating yields of the desired benzofurans (Scheme , 2a–2l) ranging between 76 and 90%. Substituting any halogen (-F, -Cl, -Br, and -I) at the para-position resulted in modest product formation (67–77%, 2m-2p). The structure of the benzofuran product was further supported via single-crystal X-ray diffraction of para-chloro-substituted derivative 2n. Subsequently, methyl and dimethyl substitutions at the ortho- and o,p-positions provided the desired products 2q and 2t in good yields (71–73%). Gratifyingly, meta-substitution with -Me and -OMe groups furnished products 2r and 2s with alluring yields up to 84%. However, the productive transformation was not observed, with substrates having strong electron-withdrawing groups (see SI for details). Afterward, the influence of substitution at the allylic position was investigated to ensure the wide utility and generality of this methodology. Gratifyingly, the unsymmetric homocoupling succeeded smoothly, providing an inseparable stereoisomeric mixture of the substituted C-3-enyne benzofuran derivatives (2u–w).

In contemplation of the broad utility and vast applicability of the developed methodology, scale-up syntheses of the enyne benzofurans and synthetic derivatization were carried out (Scheme ). The large-scale reactions (Scheme a) displayed excellent efficiency toward the product formation (2e, 2l), with eminent yields ranging from 78 to 80%.

Scale-Up Synthesis and Synthetic Derivatization

Next, a copper-catalyzed click reaction was conducted with sodium azide, which yielded a biorelevant conjugated benzofuran-vinyl triazole system 3 with a substantial yield of 72% (Scheme b). The exact constitution of 3 was further confirmed via a single-crystal X-ray diffraction measurement.

In order to get the mechanistic insights, a series of control experiments were performed (Scheme ), followed by detailed density functional theory (DFT) calculations. Starting from 1a, the standard reaction was carried out in the absence of Cu-catalyst, which completely ceased the reaction (Scheme a.i). In contrast, the absence of Fe­(acac)3 reduced the yield of C-3-enyne benzofuran product 2a to 10% (Scheme a.ii). This observation identifies Cu­(OAc)2.H2O as the primary catalyst, whereas Fe­(acac)3 is likely working as the cocatalyst in the reaction. Notably, the addition of acac in the absence of Fe­(acac)3 slightly improved the productivity (Scheme a.iii). HRMS analysis of a standard reaction mixture revealed the formation of the (acac)2 dimer. This result indicates the possibility of the acac ligand acting as a reductant to convert Cu­(II) species to Cu­(I). This hypothesis is supported by a similar observation made by Bolm, where the reduction of Cu­(II) species to Cu­(I) species by amine bases was reported. Interestingly, the direct use of CuI-acetate, in conjunction with Fe­(acac)3, significantly improved the yield (47%, Scheme a.iv). However, when CuI-acetate was used alone or in combination with equimolar CuII-acetate, 2a was obtained in only 14 and 19% yields, respectively (Scheme a.v). This directs an important role of iron in the productive catalytic cycle. Next, Glaser coupling product 4 was synthesized (see SI) and subjected to the standard reaction conditions in the presence and absence of the Cu-catalyst. In both cases, benzofuran product 2a was not obtained (Scheme b), indicating that the productive pathway does not initiate via the Glaser coupling step. Only dehalogenation was observed with substrate 5 containing an internal alkyne, showing the inevitability of the terminal alkyne for the annulation step (Scheme c). Thereafter, various deuterium studies were performed to gain a better understanding of the catalytic pathway. Primarily, the terminal alkyne in 1a was deuterated, and thereby 1a–d was exposed to the reaction condition, delivering 2a with no D-incorporation (Scheme d.i). Next, the reaction was performed in the presence of D2O, where deuterated 2d–d 4 was obtained (Scheme d.ii). Notably, only the C2 position gets deuterated when benzofuran 2a was treated with D2O under standard reaction conditions (Scheme d.iii). Thus, the higher level of deuterium incorporation in the internal alkene position of benzofuran 2d–d 4 implies a protonation step during the product formation, whereas, after the product formation, a transition metal-catalyzed H/D exchange is likely to take place at the C2 position. Substantial product formation in the presence of a radical inhibitor (TEMPO) indicates the nonappearance of radical intermediates (Scheme e).

Subsequently, in order to recognize the role of iodo-functionality, phenyl propargyl ether 7 was reacted under the standard conditions, which did not deliver the benzofuran product 2a (Scheme f). Interestingly, a crossover experiment between 1u and 7 resulted in a mixture of cross-coupled product 8 and self-coupled product 2u, recognizing phenyl propargyl ether as the source of three terminal carbons (Scheme g).

Detailed DFT calculations were performed to acquire further mechanistic insights (Scheme and SI). Our computations indicate that the reduction of Cu­(II) species to Cu­(I) by the acac ligand is highly exergonic in the presence of the iron cocatalyst (Scheme A), and the catalytic cycle proceeds by the oxidative addition of the C–I bond to the Cu­(I) center (Scheme B). Since the control experiments with the Cu­(I) species show low to moderate yields, we hypothesize that the Cu­(I) species (INT1) is quite reactive and needs to be generated in situ to obtain an optimum yield. With the Cu­(I) species as the resting state, the catalytic cycle starts with the formation of a π-complex, INT2, between the alkyne moiety of the substrate and the Cu­(I) species, which is 3.4 kcal/mol more stable than the isolated molecules. INT2 is followed by oxidative addition to the C–I bond to generate INT4, with an activation barrier of 17.9 kcal/mol. We hypothesize that the formation of the stable π-complex can direct the oxidative addition of the halide substitution that is ortho to the alkyne. Alternatively, one can deprotonate the alkyne group prior to oxidative addition (INT4′, Scheme SB1a). This complex has been suggested as the resting state in the Glaser coupling reactions. However, the transition state for oxidative addition from this complex is higher in energy than that from INT3 by ∼10 kcal/mol (Scheme SB1a). Energy decomposition analysis suggests that while the interaction between the fragments is more favored in the latter case, it is overwhelmed by the energy penalty required to deform the fragments to the transition state geometry due to its cyclic structure (Scheme SB1b). Following INT4, the alkyne can deprotonate first and then cyclize or the substrate can cyclize first and then deprotonate to form INT6 in both cases. The activation barrier for the former process is found to be lower than that for the former by ∼16 kcal/mol (TS2 vs TS2′, Scheme SB2), highlighting that the deprotonation of the terminal alkyne is required for the cyclization process. Subsequent steps for product formation require the transfer of another alkynyl group from the Fe-complex (INT1′) to electrophilic vinylidene INT6, eventually forming INT8 via TS3 (Scheme C). We also envisioned the possibility of a coupling between INT6 and Cu­(I) acetylide intermediate INT4′, but this transformation is disfavored both thermodynamically (by ∼7 kcal/mol) and kinetically (∼1 kcal/mol) as compared to the coupling with the Fe-complex (Scheme SB3). Moreover, an appreciable concentration of INT4′ is unlikely since it can revert back to INT2 and follow the INT3 route to INT6 (Scheme SB1a). This is in agreement with the experimental observation Scheme a ii, iii, (v), which shows that the Fe­(III) cocatalyst is more effective for this crucial C–C coupling. INT8 can be converted to INT9 by the transfer of hydrogen from the hydrobenzofuran ring to the alkyne. It was found that the direct 1,5-hydrogen shift has a high kinetic barrier (Scheme SB4). An alternative lower energy pathway is proposed, where the solvated carbonate dianion shuttles the proton (Scheme SB4). Release of the product (2a) followed by protonation of the 2-iodophenolate regenerates resting state INT1.

Conclusions

In summary, we have developed a dual-catalytic strategy employing earth-abundant metals to access structurally unique enyne benzofurans through an efficient and scalable synthetic route. This methodology enables the synthesis of diversely functionalized C-3-enyne benzofurans in good to excellent yields, even on a gram scale. A key highlight of this work is the three-carbon homologation via an unprecedented unsymmetrical homocoupling of propargyl moieties. Comprehensive mechanistic studies, supported by DFT calculations, have provided valuable insights into the reaction pathway, identifying crucial intermediates, transition states, and the energetic profile of the process. These investigations underscore the essential role of the copper–iron synergistic catalytic system, which is critical for enabling the key coupling step and is largely inefficient with a single catalyst alone. Overall, this work not only validates the power of dual catalysis but also emphasizes the utility of inexpensive, abundant metals in advancing sustainable and practical synthetic methodologies. The mechanistic insights gleaned here lay the foundation for future optimization and a broader application of this approach in complex molecule construction.

Predictive modelling and optimization of WEDM parameter for Mg–Li alloy using ANN integrated CRITIC-WASPAS approach
Authors: V Kavimani, PM Gopal, Sumesh Keerthiveettil Ramakrishnan, Jayant Giri, Abdullah Alarifi, T Sathish
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work intended to improve the precision and machining efficiency of Magnesium alloy (Mg–Li–Sr) using Wire electrical discharge machining (WEDM). Mg–Li–Sr alloy is prepared through inert gas assisted stir casting route. Taguchi approach is used for experimental design for WEDM parameter such as pulse OFF time, pulse ON time, wire feed rate, servo voltage and current. L27 orthogonal array is considered to understand the influence of control parameter such as Kerf Width (KW), Roughness of the surface (Ra), Material Removal Rate (MRR). Integration of the CRITIC (Criteria Importance Through Intercriteria Correlation) -WASPAS (Weighted Aggregated Sum Product Assessment) multi-objective optimization method with Artificial Neural Network (ANN) modelling with different network structure for prediction and optimization is a novel approach that significantly improves prediction accuracy and machining outcomes. The developed ANN model with better R2 value of 99.9 % has better ability for prediction while correlated with formulated conventional regression equation. The error percentages identified through confirmation tests for regression and ANN models are Ra - 8.5 % and 3.4 %, MRR - 5.9 % and 2.8 %, KW - 6.7 % and 2.2 % respectively. Optimal output response attained by CRITIC-WASPAS approach yields surface roughness of 4.62 μm, material removal rate of 0.073 g/min and kerf width of 0.388 μm.Keywords: Mg-Li alloy, WEDM, Modelling and optimization, ANN, CRITIC-WASPAS

Introduction

The remarkable combination of low weight and high strength makes Magnesium (Mg) alloys an attractive choice for applications requiring lightweight materials, such as the transportation and biomedical implants [[1], [2], [3]]. However, its limited ductility has significantly constrained its wider engineering applications which was then improved by the addition of transition elements viz. lithium (Li). Presently, Mg–Li based alloys demonstrate immense possibility as the material for bio implants, primarily because lower density can diminish Mg density up to 1.65 g/cm3 [[4], [5], [6]]. Despite the substantial enhancements in ductility, the properties of Mg–Li alloys remains relatively low, due to the occurrence of soft Li phase. This can further enhanced by adding ternary elements for instance elements such as rare earth viz. strontium (Sr) that improvise the strength of Mg–Li alloys by LPSO effects [[7], [8], [9]].

Machining Mg is crucial for shaping it into precise dimensions and complex geometries required for modern applications, ensuring that components fit and function correctly in assemblies. Nonetheless, machining Mg and its alloys through traditional manufacturing methods such as turning, milling, or drilling is a formidable challenge due to their pronounced affinity towards oxygen as a result the heat generated during machining raises the risk of ignition. Furthermore, the material wastage associated with these conventional machining processes is substantial, leading to increased overall production costs when aiming for three-dimensional products [[10], [11], [12]]. Hence, non-traditional machining techniques are favoured over their conventional counterparts for their superior precision, both on a macro and microscale [13,14]. Among the array of available non-traditional machining process, WEDM emerges as viable choice for processing conductive materials with exceptional precision. WEDM is an precision machining approach that involves material removal by localized heating generated through plasma channel formed near the substrate and electrode, maintained at minimal gap and potential [[15], [16], [17]]. Following the WEDM process, there are metallurgical alterations on the machined surface, leading to creation of heat-affected zone and recast layer. These imperfections have unfavourable effect on mechanical behaviour of materials, diminishing their mechanical stability [18,19]. Further, in the context of WEDM, as productivity increases, there is a corresponding decrease in surface quality. Thus, it has become imperative to improve machining parameters to strike a equilibrium among quality and productivity. Numerous scientists have explored WEDM process on various composites and alloys but limited attention has been given to studying the impact of WEDM performance on magnesium-based alloys [[20], [21], [22], [23], [24]].

Rajender Kumar et al. [25] applied Grey Relational Analysis (GRA) for optimizing WEDM performance of Mg ZE41A. With the help of GRA, it has been identified that Pulse ON Time (P-ONT) and current are dominant parameters. Minimal Ra and corrosion rate were achieved when machined at optimized WEDM parameters. This optimized machining process has the potential to contribute to mitigating the loss of mechanical integrity in bone designs, particularly concerning healing time of fracture. In a related study by Xu et al. [26], corrosion behaviour and surface of AZ91D alloy was investigated after high-speed WEDM. Tafel plot and Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (EDS) study confirmed that multi-cutting procedure was superior to rough-cuts. This superiority was attributed to development of recast layer on machined area of Mg alloy and these layers acted as effective barrier, for its lowering corrosion rate.

Sunny Dayal and his team [27] conducted WEDM for AZ31B biomedical Mg alloy. The results revealed that several parameters, including spark frequency, P-OFT, P-ONT and servo voltage significantly influenced Cutting Rate (CR). Conversely, parameters such as wire tension, current and feed are observed as insignificant impact over CR. Notably, CR improved from 9.93 to 10 mm/min as P-ONT increased from 102 to 110 μs. Gill and co-workers [28] study on WEDM of Mg–4Zn alloy emphasized critical role of P-ONT as the utmost control factor influencing the performance of developed alloy. Additionally, the Ra of WEDM-ed samples exceeded that of polished surfaces. In other studies, it is observed that P-ONT, voltage and peak current are critical parameters in WEDM for controlling Ra and MRR of Mg-based alloys. Given these findings, there is a compelling need to inspect the machining behaviour of Mg–Li alloy system and optimize the machining parameters for this specific material.

Previous researchers have introduced a variety of modelling techniques to address machining optimization challenges. Some of these techniques include Support Vector Machine (SVM), ANN, Response Surface Methodology (RSM) [[29], [30], [31], [32]]For instance, Padhee et al. [33] utilized RSM approach to design the experiment with parameters such as duty cycle, dielectric fluid, peak current, P-ONT in powder mixed EDM. Herein Regression approach, a well-established modelling technique, has been widely utilized by researchers to predict machining parameters as highlighted by Zain et al. [34]. Kuriakose and Shunmugam [35] developed a multiple linear regression models in order to establish relationships among WEDM process parameters and machining performance. Palanikumar et al. [36] used second-order polynomial regression for their modelling approach. In a comparative study, Al-Ghamdi and Taylan [37] examined polynomial regression and neuro-fuzzy inference system. Their research indicated that the proposed neuro-fuzzy approach outperformed polynomial regression, providing better results. These various modeling techniques offer valuable tools for optimizing machining processes and improving performance.

So, the current study aims to analyze the machinability of Mg–Li alloy through WEDM as no reports have been analysed the machinability of Mg–Li alloy. Taguchi based orthogonal array is used to design the experiments as it lessens the experimental trails to attain optimal solution. Multi objective optimization namely CRITICS-WASPAS approach is used to attain optimal parameter setting. ANN is utilized to forecast the responses in WEDM of Mg–Li alloy.

Materials and method

In this research stir casting route is adopted to develop Mg–4Li-0.5Sr alloy by adding 4 wt% lithium and 0.5 wt% of Sr with Mg. Initially, the weight of magnesium ingot is determined and placed into steel crucible. It is then heated to 750 °C and held at this temperature for 30 min in argon atmosphere. Subsequently, a weighted quantity of Li is introduced by charge port, and mixture is continuously stirred at 300 rpm for 10 min. Following this, strontium is the added via the charge port followed by stirring for 10 min before being poured into mold from bottom [38]. The resulting cylindrical rod ingot was utilized for machinability studies.

EZEECUT ZXG machine with brass coated copper wire electrode of 0.18 mm dia (Fig. 1) is utilized for machining developed alloy. With the aid of experts advice and literatures it was found that the servo voltage P-OFT, P-ONT, Wire Feed Rate (WFDR) and current are major process parameter in machining metals, hence these above said parameters are used as input parameter with 3 levels [17,25,39]. Taguchi method is crucial in design of experiments and optimization because it simplifies complex processes, improves quality by minimizing variability, and efficiently identifies influential factors and their optimal levels using orthogonal arrays. This approach enhances performance consistency and cost-effectiveness in product and process design hence it is employed for experimental design and to investigate the impact of control factors. Levels of selected input parameter are depicted in Table 1 and attained plan for the WEDM study (L27) is displayed in Table 2. In this research MRR, Ra and KW are chosen as outcomes. MRR is computed by weighing workpiece before and after cutting, then dividing the weight difference by workpiece density and machining time. Ra is evaluated by Mitutoyo surface roughness gauge at three different locations. Similarly, KW is calculated at three various places using Leica optical microscope. Minitab 16 software is utilized for Taguchi design and neural network modelling is carried out using MATLAB 2018a.

Result and discussion

Effect of the considered input factors on Ra is represented in Fig. 2. It is noticeable that rise in current results in reduction of Ra for the developed alloy. Increase in current causes generation of more electrical discharge between the electrode and specimen, this fact results in surface irregularities thus Ra increase. Similarly increase in current increases the heat near the work that causes recast layer formation that result in irregular surface. In this research current has the more significance on governing the Ra of developed alloy. During initial stage increase in servo voltage shows decrease and increment trends on Ra. Higher servo voltage led to faster movements of wire electrode [40]. A stable and controlled wire movement contributes to a smoother machining process results in minimal Ra. However, further increasing in servo voltage increases the faster movement of wire that creates instability in discharge process consequences to rough surface thus Ra increases. Increase in P-ONT intensifies the Ra values due to longer duration of discharge on specimen that forms localized melting. Longer duration of pulse ON removes the material aggressively that trends to irregular surface. Similarly, P-ONT results in formation of non-uniform resolidified layer that can contribute to increased Ra. Increase in P-OFT decreases the roughness of machined surface. During machining higher pulse OFF permit the specimen to cool down gradually and remove the formed debris by flushing with the dielectric fluids. Further longer P-OFT allows residual heat dissipation near machining zone thus Ra decreases [41]. Higher P-OFT result in a stable and gradual machining process that form smooth surface finishing. Increase in feed rate increases Ra values due faster movement of electrode wire. Higher WFDR results in quicker removal of material that might disrupt the discharge process and cause instability in machining thus Ra increase. From main effect plot it can be observed that current place first position in controlling the Ra followed by servo voltage and WFDR. Herein the P-OFT has minimal contribution in governing the Ra values [42].

Fig. 3 represent the effect of input factors on MRR. Herein MRR increase with current, this might be due to the formation of more discharge near machining zone. These discharges create localized melting and vaporization of the specimen effectively that contributes to a higher MRR. Increase in current forms high heat generation over machining zone that softens the specimen, this fact makes quicker material removal thus MRR increases. Higher servo voltage leads to less stable and less efficient machining process thus MRR decreases. Higher pulse time increases the MRR, during machining condition pulse ON results in longer duration of electrical discharge that remove the material quicker. It also further assists in increasing the electrical energy in machining zone that promotes higher MRR [43]. Increase in P-OFT reduces the MRR, since higher P-OFT reduces the pulse generation that subsequent slow down the material removal process. Increase in P-OFT extend the rest periods of discharges. This results in minimal continuity in material removal process that decreases the overall efficiency of machining operation thus MRR decreases. Rise in WFDR increases MRR as at higher WFDR the wire electrode moves fast through specimen. These fact results in higher material per unit of time thus MRR increases. During higher level of WFDR, newer electrode is introduced quickly that increase the machining efficiency, that increases the overall MRR. From main effect plot it can be identified that current act as most influencing factor on MRR and P-ONT placed second in inducing the MRR values while WFDR has the least contribution on MRR.

Mathematical and neural network modelling

Based on relationship among the control factors and output response, a liner regression equation is formulated to estimate the output values as depicted in equations (1), (2), (3)). The R square values are 84.9 % for Ra, 89.82 % for MRR and 89.65 % for KW.

Usually, it is impossible to pinpoint the exact network topology needed to address a given issue. In the subject of neural networks, this is a crucial issue because an unsuitable network size for the task at hand could lead to subpar outcomes. Similar issues arise when fitting curves with polynomials: a polynomial with insufficient coefficients cannot evaluate a function of interest, whereas a polynomial with an excessive number of coefficients may fit the noise in the data and result in an unsatisfactory function representation. When it comes to network topology, general "rules of thumb" are frequently applied. It is impossible to solve even basic puzzles like the exclusive-OR issue without the usage of at least one intermediate layer. Even though using two intermediate layers can produce better results for some issues involving higher order functions, the back propagation approach is often applied to networks with just one intermediate layer of nodes. The total number of nodes utilized in input & output levels and each intermediate layer is usually in between. Trial and error are ultimately the only approach that can be used with confidence to establish how many levels a network should have for a particular issue.

Training a network larger than required and then removing nodes and weights that aren't needed during training is an experimental method for figuring out what architecture is best for a given task. Pruning is a technique that requires prior information about the beginning size of the network, albeit estimating such upper boundaries would not be too difficult. Constructive algorithms are another method for choosing the right network architecture; they begin with a small network and work their way up to a larger one.

According to several studies, any categorization mapping can be modelled using a single hidden layer design that has an infinite number of hidden nodes in the layer. Several architectures were tried and tested in the current work to see which was optimal for the various models. The dataset is divided as per standard rule of training neural network, 70 % for training, 15 % for testing and remaining 15 % for validation. The process of data selection for neural network training is random, randomness is useful to avoid favourism. The best result was obtained with the following topology as depicted in Table 6 [45].

Multi-objective optimization

Multi-objective optimization is a soft computing approach for optimizing the output variable to obtain a single solution for a multi response problems or task. Multi-objective optimization in machining considers multiple conflicting objectives like MRR, tool wear, surface finish, and machining time. It helps to select optimal parameters, tools, and processes, balancing trade-offs that leading to improved productivity, efficiency, product quality, and reduced costs, making manufacturing more competitive and sustainable. However assigning weightage for output response is the difficult task in multi-objective optimization and to avoid this hybrid techniques are used by researchers [46,47]. In this research CRiteria Importance Through Inter-criteria Correlation (CRITIC) approach is used to assign the weightage for output response [48,49]. Ra, MRR and KW are selected as output parameters in this reported research. Weightage is calculated accordingly.

The effect of artificial intelligence-driven, computed tomography-based assessment of body composition parameters on 28-day mortality of patients with severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Fan Yang, Shiyu Zhang, Shuting Liu, Jianfeng Zhang, Jinrong Yang, Dilihumaer Zhayier, Ruihan Gao, Fan Pu, Minfeng Xu, Bin Zhu, Xin Zheng, Baoju Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSevere fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is a life-threatening infectious disease with high mortality. Although sarcopenia and myosteatosis are associated with adverse outcomes in multiple acute illnesses, their prognostic impact on SFTS remains unknown. This study aimed to evaluate the association between body composition parameters and 28-day mortality in SFTS patients, and to identify independent prognostic factors.MethodsIn this retrospective observational single-center study, 234 SFTS patients with complete clinical data and available computed tomography (CT) images were enrolled. Body composition indices, including skeletal muscle index (SMI), subcutaneous fat index (SFI), visceral fat index (VFI), and intermuscular fat index (IMFI), were measured at the 12th thoracic (T12) and 3rd lumbar (L3) vertebral levels using a validated artificial intelligence-driven CT segmentation model. Univariable logistic regression and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression were applied to screen candidate predictors of 28-day mortality, and multivariable logistic regression was used to identify independent risk factors and construct a prognostic model. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Kaplan–Meier curves with the log-rank test were used to compare survival across different muscle phenotypes.ResultsThe overall 28-day mortality rate was 13.7% (32/234). T12-IMFI was identified as an independent risk factor for 28-day mortality (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] = 1.30, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.04–1.63, P = 0.021). A prediction model comprising age, log₁₀-transformed viral load, procalcitonin (PCT), and T12-IMFI was constructed, which exhibited satisfactory discrimination performance with an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.877 (95% CI 0.805–0.950) and good calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow P = 0.278). DCA demonstrated that the model provided superior net benefit across the clinically relevant threshold range of 0.05 to 0.68. The addition of T12-IMFI significantly improved individual risk stratification compared with the clinical-only model (net reclassification improvement [NRI] = 0.470, 95% CI 0.078– 0.862, P = 0.019). Patients with myosteatosis alone showed the highest 28-day mortality rate (26.1%), and significant differences in survival were observed among the four muscle phenotypes (log-rank P = 0.012).ConclusionsCT-defined myosteatosis, rather than sarcopenia, is independently associated with 28-day mortality in SFTS patients. T12-IMFI is a valuable prognostic biomarker, and the model incorporating T12-IMFI provides a practical tool for early risk stratification in SFTS patients at admission.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12879-026-14025-8.Keywords: Myosteatosis, Sarcopenia, Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome, Artificial intelligence, Computed tomography

Introduction

Severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) is an emerging tick-borne infectious disease caused by the Dabie bandavirus (formerly known as SFTS virus [SFTSV]) [1]. It presents with a broad spectrum of clinical features, including fever, thrombocytopenia, leukocytopenia, bleeding, and gastrointestinal symptoms, with a reported mortality of 12%–30% [2, 3]. The key mechanisms underlying unfavorable outcomes are high viral loads, cytokine storm, and immune dysfunction [2, 4].

Sarcopenia is a progressive, age-related skeletal muscle disorder characterized by generalized loss of skeletal muscle mass and decline in muscle strength. In line with the 2019 European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People (EWGSOP2) consensus and the 2025 Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) update, low muscle strength is recognized as the primary diagnostic marker, and abnormal skeletal muscle mass/quality serves as the confirmatory diagnostic criterion [5, 6]. Sarcopenia has been associated with adverse outcomes across a variety of acute and chronic conditions, including an increased risk of infectious complications, prolonged mechanical ventilation, extended hospitalization, and higher mortality [7, 8]. Myosteatosis refers to the ectopic deposition of lipids in skeletal muscle compartments, and is widely recognized to be closely associated with systemic inflammation, insulin resistance, and mitochondrial dysfunction [9, 10]. Computed tomography (CT) has emerged as the gold standard for non-invasive assessment of body composition due to its ability to provide quantitative assessments of muscle and adipose tissue [11]. As research progresses, artificial intelligence (AI)-driven models are able to perform automated body composition segmentation based on CT images with high accuracy and efficiency [12].

Recent studies have shown that body composition parameters, especially CT-defined sarcopenia and myosteatosis, serve as valuable prognostic factors in patients with acute viral infections [13, 14]. Skeletal muscle serves as a key regulator of metabolic and inflammatory homeostasis during critical illness [15]. Given that SFTS predominantly affects elderly individuals who are inherently vulnerable to muscle loss and fat infiltration [16, 17], SFTS patients are particularly prone to the development of sarcopenia and myosteatosis. Accordingly, this study investigated the association between AI-driven CT-derived body composition parameters and 28-day mortality in SFTS patients, and sought to identify independent prognostic factors for risk stratification.

Materials and methods

Patients

This retrospective cohort study included patients with SFTS admitted from May 2020 to October 2023 at Union Hospital of Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, Wuhan, China. SFTS was diagnosed per the 2010 diagnostic criteria issued by the National Health Commission of China: clinically suspected disease (field activity exposure in endemic areas or tick bite within 2 weeks before fever onset, plus acute fever with thrombocytopenia and/or leukopenia) confirmed by SFTSV RNA detection via reverse-transcription PCR (RT-PCR). All included patients underwent chest and abdominal CT scans on admission as part of standard clinical evaluation for organ involvement assessment. Exclusion criteria included: (1) age over 80 years (to avoid extreme age-related confounding); (2) failure to undergo both chest and abdominal CT scans, or incomplete cross-sectional images at the T12 and/or L3 vertebral levels; (3) missing important laboratory results (Fig. 1).

Body composition parameters assessment

All abdominal and chest non-contrast CT images were acquired at our institution’s radiology department using Siemens Somatom Definition Flash and Philips Brilliance iCT scanners. The acquisition and reconstruction parameters were as follows: tube voltage, 120 kVp; all scans were performed without contrast administration; axial images were reconstructed with a slice thickness of 1.5 mm; a standard soft-tissue convolution kernel was used for all reconstructions.

Manual segmentation by two board-certified radiologists was used as the reference standard for training and validating the AI body composition segmentation model. A total of 4,279 axial CT images were collected for the training dataset, and all images were independently annotated by two radiologists with more than 5 years of experience. The 2D U-Net model [18] was trained using this dataset to automatically segment skeletal muscle, visceral fat, and subcutaneous fat. Quantitative agreement between AI automated segmentation and manual radiological annotation was evaluated using the Pearson correlation coefficient, Dice similarity coefficient (DSC), intersection over union (IoU), sensitivity, and specificity. In the training dataset, the model achieved mean DSC = 0.924, IoU = 0.867, sensitivity = 0.901, specificity = 0.987. Pearson correlation analysis showed excellent agreement between AI and manual segmentation, with R = 0.986 (R² = 0.970) (Figure S1).

The validated AI model was applied to 234 SFTS patients for automatic body composition analysis. First, the Med-Query algorithm [19] was used to automatically identify vertebral levels and extract axial images at the mid-planes of the twelfth thoracic vertebra (T12) and third lumbar vertebra (L3). Then, the trained 2D U-Net model was used to segment skeletal muscle, visceral fat, and subcutaneous fat. Intramuscular fat was identified within skeletal muscle regions using a Hounsfield Unit (HU) range of -190 to -30 to generate the final segmentation results (Fig. 2). The cross-sectional area (cm²) of each tissue was normalized by height squared (m²) to calculate the skeletal muscle index (SMI), subcutaneous fat index (SFI), visceral fat index (VFI), and intramuscular fat index (IMFI), with units of cm²/m².

Data collection and study definitions

The data collected retrospectively from the electronic medical records included patient demographics, comorbidities, initial laboratory results following admission, and body composition parameters. The primary outcome was defined as 28-day all-cause mortality. Body composition parameters at the level of the T12 and L3 have been shown to be reliable indicators of overall body composition [20, 21].While well-validated CT reference thresholds for low muscle mass (SMI, the core metric for CT-defined sarcopenia) at the L3 level are available from large cohorts [22, 23], no universally accepted thresholds exist for T12-SMI or CT-derived IMFI (the core metric for CT-defined myosteatosis), particularly in Asian populations [6]. Accordingly, CT-based sarcopenia and myosteatosis were defined using sex-specific quartile cutoffs derived from the entire study cohort, consistent with prior peer-reviewed studies [24–27]. CT-defined sarcopenia was defined as T12-SMI below the sex-specific first quartile (Q1, lowest 25%): <29.4 cm²/m² for men and < 26.1 cm²/m² for women. CT-defined myosteatosis was defined as T12-IMFI above the sex-specific third quartile (Q3, highest 25%): ≥3.9 cm²/m² for men and ≥ 5.1 cm²/m² for women.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are presented as numbers (percentages) and compared using Pearson’s χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. For continuous variables, normality was first assessed using the Shapiro Wilk test. Normally distributed variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using Welch’s t-test; non-normally distributed variables are presented as median (interquartile range, IQR) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Viral load was log₁₀-transformed (hereafter abbreviated as lg (viral load)) for all analyses.

Candidate predictors were selected from 32 variables using univariable logistic regression and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression. Multivariable logistic regression was applied to identify independent risk factors and develop the prognostic model. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and calibration curves. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was conducted to assess clinical utility. Bootstrap internal validation with 1000 resamples was performed to adjust for overfitting and confirm model stability. A Cox proportional hazards model was used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs).

To evaluate the incremental prognostic value of imaging biomarkers over conventional clinical and laboratory markers, the models were compared using the DeLong test for differences in area under the ROC curve (AUC). Net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) were calculated to quantify improvements in individual risk stratification. A confusion matrix analysis, based on the optimal cutoff determined by the maximum Youden index, was used to compare clinical performance metrics: sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, and F1 score.

Kaplan-Meier curves for 28-day overall survival were generated for patients stratified by the joint indices score and by four muscle phenotypes (normal, sarcopenia alone, myosteatosis alone, combined), with between-group survival differences compared using the log-rank test. All data processing and statistical analyses were performed in R version 4.3.2. All tests were two-tailed, and P < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics and body composition parameters of patients with SFTS

A 28-day mortality rate of 13.7% (32/234) was observed in this study. The mean age of the patients was 63 ± 9.9 years, and the majority were women (54.3%). The mean age of deceased patients was 68 ± 8.0 years, which was significantly higher than that of surviving patients (62 ± 9.9 years, P < 0.001). The most common comorbidity was hypertension (32.5%). No statistically significant differences were observed in basic indicators reflecting the basic nutritional status of patients, including albumin and BMI, between deceased and surviving patients. Laboratory indicators including viral load, monocytes, ALT, AST, Scr, BUN, LDH, CK, Hs-cTnI, D-dimer, APTT, CRP, and PCT levels were significantly higher in deceased patients compared to surviving patients. There were no statistically significant differences in body composition parameters including SMI, SFI, and VFI at either the T12 or L3 levels between deceased and surviving patients, whereas both T12-IMFI (P = 0.033) and L3-IMFI (P = 0.028) were significantly higher in deceased patients than in surviving patients (Table 1).

Screening of potential predictors for 28-day mortality in patients with SFTS

Univariable logistic regression was performed to identify predictors of 28-day all-cause mortality. Higher T12-IMFI (odds ratio [OR] = 1.22, 95% CI 1.07–1.39, P = 0.004) and L3-IMFI (OR = 1.22, 95% CI 1.05–1.40, P = 0.008) were associated with increased 28-day mortality. In contrast, SMI, SFI, and VFI at both T12 and L3 levels showed no significant association with mortality (Table S1).

LASSO regression was used to refine the candidate predictor set and reduce overfitting risk from high covariate dimensionality. Of 32 initial variables, 12 predictors were retained (Figure S2): lg (viral load), monocytes, diabetes, PCT, T12-IMFI, age, albumin, Scr, APTT, LDH, CK, and Hs-cTnI. These 12 variables were entered into the multivariable logistic regression model. Four independent risk factors for 28-day mortality were identified: T12-IMFI (adjusted OR [aOR] = 1.30, 95% CI 1.04–1.63, P = 0.021), age (aOR = 1.09, 95% CI 1.01–1.19, P = 0.044), viral load (aOR = 2.11, 95% CI 1.28–3.80, P = 0.006), and PCT (aOR = 1.21, 95% CI 1.06–1.48, P = 0.010) (Table S2).

The risk of 28-day mortality for an individual patient can be evaluated through the calculation of the joint indices score in conjunction with the utilization of the nomogram (Fig. 3A). The ROC curve analysis demonstrated excellent discriminative performance of the model, with an AUC of 0.877 (95% CI 0.805–0.950) (Fig. 3B). Bootstrap internal validation with 1000 resamples yielded a bias-corrected AUC of 0.835 (95% CI 0.704–0.966), confirming excellent model stability without significant overfitting. The calibration curve showed good agreement between predicted and observed 28-day mortality probabilities. The Hosmer-Lemeshow test yielded P = 0.278, indicating no significant deviation between prediction and observation. The bias-corrected calibration curve closely followed the ideal reference line, further supporting good model calibration (Fig. 3C). DCA showed that using the nomogram for clinical decision-making yielded greater net benefit than either the “treat all” or “treat none” strategies across a risk threshold range of 0.05 to 0.68 (Fig. 3D).

The optimal cutoff value for the joint indices score, determined by the maximum Youden index, was 109.74. Patients were accordingly stratified into a high-risk group (score > 109.74, n = 62) and a low-risk group (score ≤ 109.74, n = 172). Kaplan-Meier analysis showed a highly significant difference in 28-day overall survival between the two groups (log-rank P < 0.001). Patients in the high-risk group had a markedly higher risk of 28-day mortality than those in the low-risk group (hazard ratio [HR] = 24.15, 95% CI 8.46–68.97) (Fig. 4).

To further evaluate the incremental prognostic value of T12-IMFI beyond conventional clinical and laboratory markers, two models were compared: the full model (Model 1, incorporating all four predictors) and the reference model (Model 2, including age, lg (viral load), and PCT only). The reference model achieved an AUC of 0.865 (95% CI 0.786–0.943). Although the between-model difference in AUC was not statistically significant (DeLong test P = 0.362, Figure S3), the continuous NRI was 0.470 (95% CI 0.078–0.862, P = 0.019), indicating that adding T12-IMFI significantly improved individual risk stratification (Table S3). Confusion matrix analysis showed that the full model outperformed the reference model for 28-day mortality identification, with higher sensitivity (0.893 vs. 0.857), specificity (0.829 vs. 0.823), negative predictive value (0.980 vs. 0.974), and F1 score (0.595 vs. 0.571) (Table S4).

Association between different muscle type and survival outcome in patients with SFTS

Multivariable logistic regression identified T12-IMFI as an independent risk factor for 28-day mortality. To explore the prognostic impact of different muscle phenotypes, patients were categorized by sex-specific T12-SMI (< Q1) and T12-IMFI (≥ Q3) thresholds, as defined in Section “Data collection and study definitions”. Patients were further divided into four groups: normal (n = 128), sarcopenia alone (n = 47), myosteatosis alone (n = 46); and combined sarcopenia and myosteatosis (n = 13). Patients with isolated myosteatosis had the highest 28-day mortality, whereas those with normal muscle status had the lowest (8.6% vs. 12.7% vs. 26.1% vs. 15.4% for normal, sarcopenia alone, myosteatosis alone, and combined groups, respectively; log-rank P = 0.012) (Fig. 5).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the association between CT-derived body composition parameters and clinical prognosis in patients with SFTS. Over the past decade, age, clinical manifestations (including hemorrhagic manifestations and encephalopathy), and laboratory indicators (including viral load, inflammatory markers, coagulation parameters, and liver and renal function) have been well established as independent risk factors for adverse outcomes in SFTS [28, 29]. Our findings demonstrate that CT-assessed myosteatosis is independently associated with increased 28-day all-cause mortality, and that adding this imaging biomarker to conventional clinical predictors further improves prognostic performance. Methodologically, the use of a validated AI-driven CT segmentation approach greatly enhances the clinical feasibility of integrating CT-defined myosteatosis and sarcopenia into routine admission risk stratification.

In light of recent advancements in sarcopenia research, myosteatosis is recognized as a distinct but related pathological entity with independent prognostic value across multiple diseases [30]. Our findings support this paradigm, showing that muscle quality (measured by IMFI) has stronger prognostic significance than muscle quantity (measured by SMI) in SFTS. This aligns with a recent meta-analysis linking muscle quality, rather than quantity, to COVID-19 severity [31], and with preclinical work by Nachit et al. showing that myosteatosis precedes sarcopenia and acts as an early biomarker in non-alcoholic fatty liver disease [32]. Subgroup analysis showed that isolated myosteatosis was associated with the highest 28-day mortality, whereas combined sarcopenia and myosteatosis did not confer additional mortality risk. This counterintuitive finding may be explained by the small size of the combined phenotype group (n = 13), which limits statistical power and warrants cautious interpretation. Nevertheless, a large prospective cohort study in cirrhosis reported a similar pattern [33], suggesting that myosteatosis may be an earlier and more impactful prognostic marker than sarcopenia alone.

Notably, multivariable regression identified T12-IMFI, rather than L3-IMFI, as independently associated with 28-day mortality. Statistically, this aligns with the variable selection result of LASSO regression. While both T12-IMFI and L3-IMFI showed significant prognostic value in univariable analysis, the two metrics are highly collinear as they both reflect systemic intramuscular fat deposition. Leveraging its L1-norm penalty that eliminates redundant features, LASSO regression retained T12-IMFI instead of L3-IMFI in the final candidate set, indicating that T12-IMFI delivers greater incremental prognostic information for acute mortality in SFTS patients. This statistical finding is further supported by anatomical and functional differences between muscle groups at the two vertebral levels. Anatomically, the T12 level includes core trunk muscles and respiratory accessory muscles, such as the intercostal muscles and diaphragmatic attachments [34]. Given the high prevalence of pulmonary involvement in SFTS [35], increased fat infiltration in these muscles may impair respiratory compensatory capacity during acute illness, raising the risk of respiratory failure and mechanical ventilation, and thus contributing to worse disease severity and fatal outcomes [36–38]. In contrast, the L3 vertebral level predominantly comprises the psoas major, quadratus lumborum and abdominal wall muscles, which primarily mediate lumbar stability and proximal lower limb motor function. Cross-sectional area at L3 is a well-validated surrogate of total-body skeletal muscle mass and chronic systemic muscle reserve [23, 39], which may explain its weaker direct association with acute-phase pulmonary dysfunction in viral infection.

The precise underlying pathophysiological mechanisms regarding the association between myosteatosis and the progression of SFTS remain unclear. Miljkovic et al. [40] reported that skeletal muscle fat infiltration accelerates with increasing age amongst middle-aged and older men. The accumulation of lipids and their derivatives within and between muscle cells is associated with mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired β-oxidation of fatty acids, and increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, which may further contribute to lipotoxicity, insulin resistance, and elevated secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines [41, 42]. Pro-inflammatory factors are associated with increased infiltration of macrophages and other immune cells, which release tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), monocyte chemotactic protein-1 (MCP-1), and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), leading to tissue damage, endothelial dysfunction, and worsening of systemic inflammation [43, 44]. A transcriptomic study by Shen et al. [45] reported that myosteatosis-associated muscle fat infiltration is accompanied by remodeling of the systemic immune microenvironment. Such pre-existing inflammation and immune dysregulation may lead to maladaptive immune responses following acute SFTSV infection, including imbalanced inflammatory control and impaired viral clearance, which collectively worsen clinical outcomes. The specific molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying this pathogenic association remain to be further elucidated in future studies.

This study has several limitations that warrant acknowledgment. First, the single-center retrospective design carries inherent selection bias: only patients with complete admission chest and abdominal CT scans were enrolled, which may underrepresent mild cases not requiring imaging evaluation. The lack of independent external validation further limits the generalizability of the prognostic model to other patient populations. Second, only a single baseline CT scan obtained at admission was analyzed, with no serial measurements of body composition during disease progression. As a result, pre-existing sarcopenia and myosteatosis cannot be definitively distinguished from acute changes induced by early SFTSV infection, and the causal direction of the observed association remains undetermined. Third, CT-defined sarcopenia and myosteatosis were used exclusively for prognostic stratification, rather than a formal clinical diagnosis of sarcopenia. Muscle strength and physical performance are mandatory components of clinical sarcopenia diagnosis but were not available in this retrospective cohort. This restricts extrapolation of our findings to standardized clinical sarcopenia diagnostic frameworks. Fourth, the absence of data on lymphocyte subsets and circulating cytokines precludes in-depth exploration of the immunological mechanisms underlying the prognostic impact of myosteatosis. Future studies incorporating these immunological biomarkers are warranted to validate the proposed mechanistic hypotheses.

Conclusion

In conclusion, CT-defined myosteatosis, rather than sarcopenia, is independently associated with 28-day mortality in SFTS patients. AI-driven CT-measured T12-IMFI represents a valuable imaging biomarker, and its incorporation into prognostic models may facilitate early risk stratification in hospitalized SFTS patients.

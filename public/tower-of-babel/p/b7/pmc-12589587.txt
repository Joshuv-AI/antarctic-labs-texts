Targeted neuroprotection with OX26-functionalized Epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG)-stabilized se nanoparticles in a rat model of cerebral ischemia
Authors: Guven Akcay, Cagla Celik, Gülce Yildiz, Esra Balcioglu, Recep Baydemir, Serdar Altunay, Ismail Ocsoy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractStroke remains one of the leading causes of death and long-term disability worldwide, underscoring the need for innovative therapeutic approaches. Smart drug delivery systems employing nanoparticles functionalized with targeting ligands and therapeutic agents offer promising strategies for mitigating cerebral ischemic damage. In this study, a multifunctional brain-targeted nanocarrier (OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs) was designed and synthesized by stabilizing selenium nanoparticles (Se NPs) with epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), conjugating the NMDA receptor antagonist MK-801, and functionalizing the surface with the transferrin receptor-targeting monoclonal antibody OX26. Nanoparticles were synthesized via the reduction of selenious acid using ascorbic acid in the presence of EGCG, followed by PEG-carboxylic acid-mediated conjugation of MK-801 and OX26. Structural and physicochemical characterization was conducted using scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM), dynamic light scattering (DLS), zeta potential analysis, Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX). In vivo experiments were carried out on 68 male Wistar rats randomly assigned to Sham, Ischemia/Reperfusion (IR), IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NPs, and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs groups. A transient middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) model was established with 90-min occlusion followed by 72-h reperfusion. Nanoparticles (1000 µg/mL) were administered intraperitoneally 1 h prior to ischemia induction. Behavioral assessments included the open field and novel object recognition tests. Brain tissue was then analyzed for glutamate and Ca levels (ELISA), infarct volume (TTC staining), and NMDAR1, NMDAR2A, and AMPAR1 expression (immunohistochemistry). The findings revealed that both nanoparticle treatments significantly improved motor and cognitive outcomes, reduced glutamate and Ca2+ accumulation, inhibited NMDA/AMPA receptor overactivation, and decreased infarct volume. These results suggest that multifunctional, targeted nanoparticle systems represent a promising therapeutic strategy for ischemic stroke and related neurological disorders.Keywords: Cognitive functions, Epigallocatechin gallate, Ischemia reperfusion, MK-801, Selenium nanoparticleSubject terms: Drug delivery, Neurology

Introduction

Cerebral ischemia causes brain cells to die when the blood supply to the brain is interrupted. Every year, 15 million people worldwide die or suffer permanent disability as a result of a stroke1,2. Current treatments of central nervous system (CNS) disorders alleviate symptoms but are insufficient to prevent progression of pathology3,4. The blood-brain barrier impedes the entry of drugs into the CNS, complicating treatment protocols, and the rapid metabolization of drugs reduces therapeutic efficacy5,6. Targeted therapy and nanoparticle-based drug delivery systems hold promise for developing effective therapies with few side effects6. The rapid metabolisation and clearance of drugs from the bloodstream also significantly reduces their therapeutic efficacy6. The biggest challenge in the development of anti-stroke drugs is the complexity of the inflammatory response in the relevant signalling pathways7,8. Understanding these pathways enables rational targeting of selected pathways and sheds light on the creation of efficient therapeutic strategies with minimal side effects. Intensive research is ongoing to develop nanoparticle-based strategies that enable targeted therapy and drug delivery9,10. Targeted therapy with nano drug delivery systems has attracted great interest in recent years to treat various diseases such as cancer11–15. Recently, nanobiotechnology developments have led to the development of targeted nanoparticles that can easily cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB). Among these, Selenium nanoparticles (Se NP) are promising for the treatment of CNS disorders. In a study conducted by Zhang et al.16, Se NPs stabilised with epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) and coated with Tet-1 peptide were shown to have the potential to accelerate recovery from Alzheimer’s disease by inhibiting amyloid-β aggregation in vitro. Se NPs show high antioxidant activity and accordingly selenoproteins enhance the endogenous antioxidant system17. Furthermore, Se administration contributes to mitochondrial pathways through an autophagy-dependent mechanism after focal cerebral ischaemia17. Amani et al.18 administered 1000 µg/ml i.p. 1 h before transient middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAo) ischaemia. OX26-PEG-coated selenium nanoparticles injected 1 h before transient MCAo ischaemia reduced autophagy and apoptotic cell death in hippocampal neurons and also increased neuronal survival. MK-801, an NMDA receptor antagonist, is known to be effective in reducing the severity of ischaemic damage in experimental focal stroke19,20. Although the therapeutic effects of MK-801 are known, in our study, for the first time, its targeted effect on the antioxidant transporter system was investigated.

The ratio of drug to carrier in drug delivery systems is critical, as excessive carrier can lead to toxicity21. This issue is mitigated when both components have therapeutic effects. In this study, a biocompatible and targeted system for ischemia treatment was developed. EGCG, a potent antioxidant and neuroprotective polyphenol from green tea16, was used to synthesize Se nanoparticles (Se NPs), enhancing both antioxidant activity and therapeutic potential. MK-801, an NMDA receptor blocker targeting glutamate excitotoxicity, was self-assembled onto EGCG@Se NPs. Functionalization with the OX26 antibody, targeting the transferrin receptor22, produced the OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP system. PEG coating further improved stability and renal clearance18,21, creating an innovative and effective nanocarrier for ischemia therapy. An advanced drug delivery system combining OX26-PEG-coated Se NPs and EGCG offers a targeted, neuroprotective therapy for ischemia (Fig. 1). These nanoparticles cross the blood-brain barrier, directly reaching brain tissue, and synergistically address key injury mechanisms such as oxidative stress, excitotoxicity, and apoptosis. The NMDA receptor blockade by MK-801 complements the antioxidant properties of Se NPs, while their small size and surface modification enhance bioavailability and free radical neutralization. The eco-friendly, green synthesis using EGCG further underscores the biocompatibility and innovation of this system, positioning it as a promising candidate for clinical applications with broad therapeutic potential.

Materials and methods

Preparation of OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs

Firstly, EGCG@Se NPs were synthesized. Briefly, 2.75 g of EGCG (6 mmol) and 0.129 g of selenious acid (1 mmol) were added to 4 ml of distilled water and stirred to completely dissolve. Then, 10 ml of sodium ascorbate solution (5 M) were dripped into the mixture. After 1 h of incubation, the orange color solution was centrifuged at 10.000 rpm for 30 min. The collected product “EGCG@Se NPs” was washed three times with deionized water and dispersed in buffer solution for further use. MK-801 was conjugated to EGCG@Se NPs mixing for 4 h at room temperature (RT:25 °C) and centrifuged at 20.000 rpm for 10 min to collect MK-801@EGCG@Se NPs.

In the second stage, 801@EGCG@Se NPs were functionalized using 2 µg/mL poly(ethylene glycol)-carboxylic acid. After washing with deionized water, 5µL of CD71 antibody (OX26) (Santa Cruz Biotechnology) was added to the suspension and the mixture was stirred at 25 °C for 5 h. Unbound OX26 antibodies were removed by centrifugation at 10.000 rpm for 60 min at 4 °C. The obtained OX26@MK-801@EGCG@Se NPs were dispersed in deionized water and stored at + 4 °C for using in-vivo experiments. Synthesis mechanism is shown in Fig. 2.

Synthesis mechanism of OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs.

Characterization of OX26@MK-801@EGCG@Se NPs

The morphology (shape and size) of the OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP was determined by Scanning-Transmission Electron Microscopy (STEM). 5 µL of the suspension was dropped onto the copper grid and dried. Before imaging, it was coated with Au using a sputter coating device. The characteristic absorbance peak of the OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs was recorded by a UV-Vis spectrophotometer. The effective diameter and surface charge of the The charge intensity were determined by Dynamic Light Scattering (DLS) and Zeta Potential (ZT techniques, respectively. Metal content was determined by Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) was used to confirm chemical bond vibration in OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs.

Animals and experimental design

The study was conducted in compliance with the European Community Directive (86/609 ECC) for the care and use of laboratory animals and the Regional Animal Care Committee (Erciyes University, 06.04.2022/Approval no: 22/073) and the experiments were performed in accordance with the approved guidelines and regulations. All experiments were performed in accordance with the approved guidelines and regulations. The reporting of this animal research follows the ARRIVE guidelines (https://arriveguidelines.org). 12 weeks old male Wistar Albino rats (290–310 g) obtained from Erciyes University Experimental Animals Application and Research Center. The animals were housed in separated cages and kept on a standard environmental condition (23–25 °C, 50 ± 5% relative humidity, 12 h light/dark cycle) with free access to food and water. Animals were acclimatized to the environment before the experiments and then were randomized and divided into four groups as (i) Sham, (ii) Ischemia/Reperfusion (IR), (iii) IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP and (iv) IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP groups (n = 17 per group) (Fig. 3). The IR model was induced by 90 min occlusion of the MCA developed by Longa et al.23. Two rats did not have IR and three animals died after IR and were excluded from the experiment. IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP groups received 1000 µg/ml i.p. 1 h before IR. Motor function was assessed in the open field test (OF) and cognitive function in the novel object recognition test (NOR), a learning experiment. After the behavioural experiments, animals were euthanized by cervical dislocation on day 3 after behavioral experiments and the brains were removed and glutamate and Ca2+ activations in the hippocampus tissues were measured by the ELISA method. NMDAR1, NMDAR2A and AMPAR1 receptors were evaluated immunohistologically. Ischemic areas were analyzed by TTC staining.

Experiment schedule.

Focal cerebral Ischemia-Reperfusion injury

The middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAo) reperfusion model developed by Longa et al. was used23. Briefly, a midline incision was performed on the neck and the left common carotid artery, internal carotid artery (ICA), and external carotid artery (ECA) were gently isolated under isoflurane anesthesia. Then, a monofilament nylon suture with a rounded tip was introduced into the ECA and slowly inserted into the ICA to block the origin of the middle cerebral artery. Ischaemia model was induced by occlusion of the middle cerebral artery for 90 min (min) and reperfusion was performed for 72 h (hrs). The temperature of the rats was maintained at 37 ± 0.5 °C throughout the surgical procedure. After awakening from the anesthesia, the rats were returned to their cages with free food and water.

Behavioral tests

Open-field test (OF)

Motor function is usually assessed in OF test. Set up was carried out in a setup with a base of 80 × 80 cm and a wall height of 40 cm. For rats to explore the apparatus, they were placed in the center of the field and monitored and recorded by the video camera for 5 min. Total distance (cm) and velocity(cm/s) were calculated to evaluate locomotor activity24.

Novel object recognition test (NOR)

The NOR test is particularly used in attention or short-term memory. Rats are expected to spend more time examining the novel object. The time spent with familiar and novel objects was recorded for 5 min25. In the NOR test, the discrimination index and the time spent in the novel object (sec) values were analyzed..

Tissue collection

Animals were euthanized by cervical dislocation on day 3 after behavioral experiments. For biochemical analysis, motor cortex tissue was dissected from the brain and stored frozen at − 80 °C. Tissues were homogenized in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C. Supernatants were used for ELISA analyses.

Biochemical analysis

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The levels of Ca2+ and glutamate were quantified using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits. Concentrations were calculated from Ca2+ and glutamate absorbance values in supernatants for the standard curve. Values were normalised to total tissue protein and expressed as pg-mg− 1 tissue protein.

Local cerebral ischemia and infarct size measurement

Triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC) is used for the detection of infarct and normal tissues. To determine the ischemic areas, the brain tissue of the rats was removed, quickly divided into 2 mm sections, and 2% TTC (MERCK 108380) staining at 37 °C for 30 min, and ischemic damage was detected. Normal tissue is stained red, while ischemic areas are stained pale pink or white. Infarct volume calculation was analyzed with Image J and calculated using the formula used by Lin et al.26.

cInV, corrected infarct volume; InV, infarct volume; IpV, total ipsilateral hemisphere volume; CoV, total contralateral hemisphere volume.

Immunohistochemical analyses

Hematoxylin and Eosin staining

For histopathological examination, tissues were fixed in 10% formaldehyde solution for 48 h. Following fixation, the tissues were washed in tap water, then passed through a graded alcohol series, cleared with xylene, embedded in paraffin and serially sectioned at 5 μm thickness. In order to evaluate the general histological structure of the hippocampus, in addition to the CA1, CA2, CA3, CA4 and dentate gyrus regions separately histopathologically, the preparations stained with Hematoxylin & Eosin (H&E) were examined with Olympus BX51 light microscope and imaged at different magnifications.

Immunohistochemical staining

The sections kept at 60 °C overnight were rehydrated by passing through xylene and then graded alcohol series. After washing with distilled water 2 times for 2 min each, the sections were boiled with 10% citrate buffer for antigen recovery in a microwave oven at 600 W for 5 min and left to cool in the same buffer solution for 10 min at room temperature. The sections were then washed with phosphate buffer (PBS) and treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) for 12 min to inhibit endogenous peroxidase activity and the Large Volume Detection System (Thermo Scientific, TP-125-HL) staining kit was used for the following steps. The sections were washed again with PBS and Ultra V block was applied for 5 min at room temperature to cover the regions outside the antigenic areas. Immediately afterwards, NMDAR1 (ab193310, abcam, 1/100), NMDAR2A (ab203197, abcam, 1/250) and AMPAR1 (ab183797, abcam, 1/100) antibodies were applied to the sections and kept at + 4 °C for 1 night and incubated at room temperature for 30 min. As a negative control, PBS was used instead of primary antibody and after washing, the sections were incubated with biotinylated-secondary antibody for 10 min. After this process, the washing process was repeated and the sections treated with streptavidin peroxidase for 10 min were washed. To make the immunoreactivities visible, the sections were treated with the peroxidase substrate in the kit showing diaminobenzidine (DAB) feature for 1–5 min and washed with distilled water for 5 min. The sections counterstained with Gill haematoxylin were washed several times with distilled water and the water was removed with increasing alcohol series. Xylene sections were covered with Entellan. As determined at the beginning of the project, the CA1 and CA3 regions of the hippocampus, which is the most sensitive region to anoxia and ischemia, were examined under an Olimpus BX51 microscope for immunohistochemical evaluation. The same procedures were applied for all primary antibodies, and mean immunoreactivity intensities was evaluated using the Image J software program (version 1.45s, ImageJ Rasband USA).

Statistical analysis

The SPSS software package 20.0 program was used for all analyses. The results were given as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). p values less than 0.05 were considered significant. The one-way ANOVA test was used in the analysis of data with normality conditions in the evaluation made with the Shapiro-Wilk test. The Tukey test was used for posthoc analysis. If the data does not provide the normality condition, the non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis test was used for the comparison of the groups, and all pairwise multiple comparisons were performed by the Mann-Whitney U test. The results of the behavioral experiments and biochemical analyses were analyzed with the One-way ANOVA test after the Shapiro-Wilk test normality analysis.

MK801 releasing from oX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs

To test the release of MK801 from OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs, an in vitro release behavior assay was performed using the cellulose membrane dialysis bag method. The dialysis bags were prepared by soaking in phosphate buffer with pH 7.4 for 24 h before use. Then, equal amounts (1 mL) of both solutions containing OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs and the same weight of MK801 were transferred to the dialysis bag, kept at 37 °C and kept in a shaker water bath, and the samples were taken at 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 12 and 24 h time intervals and spectrophotometric analysis was performed. Effects of OX26-EGCG@Se NP and OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP on motor and cognitive function after MCAo

Behavior results of experimental groups are given in Fig. 8. The total distance (cm) and velocity (cm/s) in the OF test of all groups were measured in terms of motor function (Fig. 8A,B) and cognitive function in the NOR (Fig. 8C and D). Total distance and velocity in the OF were significantly reduced in the IR compared to the Sham (p < 0.01) (Fig. 8A,B). After OX26-EGCG@Se NP and OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP treatments, a substantial rise in locomotor activity was observed in the IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP compared with the IR (p < 0.05) (Fig. 8A,B). Short-term memory experiments of the groups were evaluated in the NOR test (Fig. 8C,D). While there was a substantial reduce in the learning of the IR compared with the Sham (p < 0.01), there was a substantial increase in the IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP compared to the IR (p < 0.05). TTC staining was used to investigate whether IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP affects ischaemia-reperfusion injury in all groups (Fig. 9A). There was a significant decrease in the infarct volume of IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP groups compared to the IR group (Fig. 9C). No statistically significant difference was found IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP groups. Ca2+ and glutamate levels in hippocampus tissue are shown in Fig. 10. There was a substantial increase in the Ca2+, and glutamate activation levels of the IR compared to the Sham while there was a substantial rise in the Ca2+ and glutamate activation levels of the IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP compared to the IR after ischaemia-reperfusion (Fig. 10).

In the Sham group, neurons in all regions of the hippocampus (CA1, CA2, CA3, CA4 and DG) showed regular arrangement with round or oval shapes. When the hippocampus tissue (CA1, CA2, CA3, CA4 and DG) was examined under a microscope, oval healthy cells with light-colored cytoplasm, regularly arranged nuclei and preserved cell membrane integrity were clearly observed.

The morphology and structure of neurons in the CA1, CA2, CA3, CA4 and DG regions in the hippocampus of the IR group were abnormal; some cells were atrophied, deformed, loose or even hollowed out, cell number was reduced and neuronal cells were damaged. At the same time, the most prominent condition observed in the IR group was enlargement and swelling of the intercellular space, as well as indistinct cell contour. It was also determined that the morphology of dendritic spines, one of the most energy-consuming structures of neurons and the most sensitive structure after cerebral ischemia, was damaged and shortened in the IR group. It was also found that the CA1 and CA3 regions of the hippocampus, which is involved in learning and memory processes and continuously produces new neurons, has the highest sensitivity to ischemia. In the regions, pyramidal cells without prominent nuclei, irregular arrangement of the pyramidal cell layer, shrinkage of neurons and clustered neurons indicating neuronal necrosis were intensely observed. The study also revealed enlarged areas between the granular and polymorphic layers of the dentate gyrus and the presence of morphologically altered neurons in this region (Fig. 12).

While pathological changes such as necrosis and cell degeneration, irregular nucleus structures and decreased number of neurons were observed in the IR group, these pathological conditions were reduced by IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP treatment (Fig. 13) and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP (Fig. 14). At the same time, while the outlines of neuronal cells in the hippocampus were blurred, interstitial spaces were enlarged and cytoplasmic vacuolization increased in the IR group, histopathological findings close to the sham group were obtained in the IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP groups. In addition, OX26-EGCG@Se NP and OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP treatment was effective in improving ischemic damage in CA1 and CA3 regions. When the data obtained were evaluated, it was revealed that OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP treatment was more effective than IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NP treatment in reducing neuronal damage and significantly increasing the number of neurons. This was also supported by the light microscopic images obtained. Together with the above data, our results revealed that IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP exerts neuroprotective effects by reducing neuronal damage.

Discussion

The increase in glutamate and calcium after ischemic injury causes excitotoxicity and cell death occurs. Therapeutic approaches are aimed at suppressing or reducing this excitotoxicity. Drug delivery systems provide a great advantage in the treatment of cerebral ischemia to increase the efficacy of treatment and reduce excitotoxic damage. In recent years, combined nanoparticle structures have started to be synthesized to increase therapeutic efficacy against ischemia-reperfusion injury and to provide a synergistic feature instead of only antioxidant drug carrier systems or drugs27,28. Li et al.27 provided a novel therapeutic approach for ischemic stroke by combining the antioxidant and neurovascular repair capabilities of dl-3-n-butylphthalide (NBP) drug-loaded cerium oxide nanoparticle and showed that it reduced ischemia-reperfusion injury (NBP-CeO). In another study, multifunctional nanotherapies were shown to have strong antioxidative and anti-inflammatory effects28. In this context, in our study, we used antioxidant Se NP as a drug carrier and stabilized it with EGCG, which also has an antioxidant property, and achieved a combined effect of targeted and MK801 drug loading. The combined effect was more effective against ischemia reperfusion injury as OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP treatment attenuated excitoxicity more and reduced motor and cognitive function loss more than OX26-EGCG@Se NP treatment. OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs showed better selectivity than OX26-EGCG@Se NPs, showing strong penetration into the ENT as well as longer half-life in blood. An advanced drug delivery system was designed in which Se NP and EGCG structures as carriers would also exhibit therapeutic effects in the treatment of ischemia. Thus, the targeted delivery of EGCG, a plant-derived biomolecule, Se, an essential element, and MK-801, the active ingredient with proven therapeutic effect, to the brain with a single system provided a synergistic effect to treat ischemia through multiple pathways. Silymarin (SM), which has antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, cytoprotective and antiapoptotic properties, affects apoptosis and inflammation pathways in brain diseases by reducing oxidative stress in the brain29. In a study, it was reported that silymarin-loaded chitosan nanoparticle reduced neuronal cell death and behavioral dysfunction29. In our study, it was found that OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP loaded with MK-801, an NMDAR antogonist, showed a better ameliorating effect on loss of locomotor activity. In our study, Se NPs decreased the volume of ischemic damage caused by cerebral ischemia. This may be explained by the fact that NMDAR blocks the passage of Ca2+ ions that play a role in cell death. Liu et al. showed that cerebral infarction tissue-targeted nanoparticle (CsA@HFn) reduced neuronal cell damage and had therapeutic effect to treat cerebral ischemia/reperfusion injury30. In this study, the drug was targeted with OX26 antibody to reach highly ischemic damaged tissue and cell damage was mitigated. Copper Oxide Nanoparticles of the calpain inhibitor N-Acetyl-Leu-Leu-norleucine (ALLN) have been shown to alleviate neuronal damage and cell death after cerebral ischemia reperfusion31. In another study, Amani et al. administered 1000 µg/ml i.p. OX26-PEG coated 25 nuclei 1 h before transient OSAO ischemia. OX26-PEG-coated 25 nm-sized Selenium nanoparticle injection 1 h before transient OSAO ischemia reduced autophagy and apoptotic cell death in hippocampal neurons and also increased neuronal survival18. They also reported that Selenium nanoparticle inhibited the increased inflammation and oxidative metabolism after stroke18. In our study, 31 nm EGCG@Se NP and 45 nm OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP reduced the severity of ischemic damage with glutamate and calcium excitotoxicity and improved cognitive and motor function. The reason for the larger size of EGCG@Se NP and OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NP is that they were stabilized with EGCG and MK801 drug loading to increase antioxidant activity. EGCG, which was proven to have neuroprotective activity on its own, basically promoted both Se NP formation and ECGC@Se NPs were used as a cargo molecule with therapeutic activity.

IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NPs and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs treatments showed ameliorative effect on motor and cognitive dysfunction after cerebral ischaemia reperfusion, especially NP loaded with NMDAR antagonist MK-801 was effective. In addition, it was observed that it decreased the increased glutamate and Ca2+ levels after ischemia and thus may have a neuroprotective effect. Moreover, IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NPs and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs treatments were found to limit the ischaemic area. IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NPs and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs treatments acted as antagonists on AMPA and NMDA receptors which play a critical role in ischaemic damage.

The results of our study showed that IR + OX26-EGCG@Se NPs and IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs treatments play a role in the regulation of glutamate level in the glutamatergic pathway and also have an effect on glutamate receptors. Se NP treatments were found to be effective especially in hippocampal CA1 and CA3 regions which are most vulnerable to ischaemic damage.

Conclusion

The findings of this study illustrate the potential of selenium nanoparticles, particularly those combined with NMDA receptor antagonists, in providing neuroprotective benefits post-cerebral ischemia. IR + OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se NPs demonstrated superior efficacy in reducing neuronal excitotoxicity, oxidative stress, and receptor overactivation in ischemic regions of the brain. The successful BBB penetration and antioxidant properties of Se NPs underscore their promise as therapeutic agents for CNS disorders, especially those involving ischemic injury. These results highlight the potential of Se NP-based treatments to advance beyond symptom management, offering a disease-modifying approach to cerebral ischemia and potentially other neurodegenerative conditions.

Limitations and future directions

Inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and oxidative stress play crucial roles in the pathogenesis of ischemic brain injury. The inflammatory response exacerbates excitotoxicity through microglial activation and the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, intensifying cellular damage32,33. Mitochondrial dysfunction leads to decreased energy production and excessive generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), triggering cell death34,35. Oxidative stress contributes to neuronal injury by inducing lipid peroxidation and DNA damage, representing fundamental components of ischemic pathology34,35. The OX26-MK801-EGCG@Se nanoparticles used in our study exert neuroprotective effects by targeting these molecular pathologies through both antioxidant properties and NMDA receptor blockade36,37. Future studies focusing on detailed analysis of inflammatory and mitochondrial parameters will enhance understanding of the mechanisms underlying nanoparticle-mediated neuroprotection. The study successfully targeted the glutamatergic pathway; however, ischemic injury also involves GABAergic and cholinergic systems, as well as neuroinflammatory responses, which require further exploration. Future research should investigate how Se NPs influence these pathways and their potential to modulate neuroinflammation. Additionally, while Se NPs can cross the BBB, optimizing their dosage and minimizing long-term toxicity remain critical. A deeper understanding of their multifunctional interactions and potential off-target effects is essential.

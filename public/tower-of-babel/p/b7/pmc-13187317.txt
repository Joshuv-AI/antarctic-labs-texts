Efficacy and safety of ranibizumab, aflibercept, and conbercept in the treatment of diabetic macular edema: a retrospective comparative study
Authors: Bowei Liu, Qian Qiao, Yalong Dang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To compare the efficacy and safety of three first-line anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) agents: ranibizumab, aflibercept, and conbercept in the treatment of diabetic macular edema (DME). This retrospective, non-randomized comparative study included DME patients treated at the Sanmenxia Eye Hospital between December 2019 and November 2024. Patients were divided into three groups based on the intravitreal agent received. All patients received an initial series of monthly intravitreal anti-VEGF injections for the first three months, followed by a pro re nata (PRN) regimen. Primary outcomes were the change in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and central subfield macular thickness (CSMT) at 1 week, and 1, 3, 6, and 12 months. Secondary outcomes included DME recurrence rates and injection frequency. All three groups showed significant improvements in BCVA at 3 months (p < 0.05). By 6 months, the improvement in BCVA in the aflibercept and conbercept groups was significantly greater than in the ranibizumab group (p < 0.05). The conbercept group was associated with a significant reduction in CSMT compared to the ranibizumab group as early as 1 month (p < 0.05). At 6 months, CSMT reduction was significantly greater in both the aflibercept and conbercept groups compared to the ranibizumab group (p < 0.01). All regimens demonstrated a favorable safety profile. In this retrospective cohort, aflibercept and conbercept were associated with greater improvements in visual and anatomical outcomes compared to ranibizumab. Aflibercept was associated with greater visual acuity improvement at 6 months, while conbercept was associated with an earlier reduction in macular thickness. These findings support individualized treatment strategies for DME.

Introduction

Diabetic macular edema (DME) is a common and severe microvascular complication of diabetic retinopathy (DR), affecting approximately one-third of patients with diabetes. It is a leading cause of vision loss in this population1,2. DME is characterized by the accumulation of exudative fluid in the macular region, which occurs as DR progresses to more advanced stages. If left untreated, DME can cause irreversible visual impairment, severely impact patients’ quality of life and increasing the societal healthcare burden3.

The pathophysiology of DME is complex. Chronic hyperglycemia damages the retinal microvasculature, leading to pericyte loss, endothelial cell dysfunction, and subsequent breakdown of the blood-retinal barrier4,5. This damage increases retinal capillary permeability, causing fluid leakage into the macula, which is the core pathological process of DME. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is a key mediator in this process, as it strongly promotes vascular permeability and angiogenesis in response to ischemia and inflammation6.

Intravitreal injections of anti-VEGF agents are the established first-line treatment for DME, as they effectively reduce edema and improve vision7,8. Commonly used agents include ranibizumab, aflibercept, and conbercept. Although the efficacy of anti-VEGF therapy is well-documented, there are few direct comparative analyses of the therapeutic outcomes of these specific medications in a real-world setting9–11. This study aims to retrospectively compare the efficacy of conbercept, aflibercept, and ranibizumab in treating DME to provide clinical evidence that may support therapeutic decision-making.

Methods

Study design and population

This non-randomized, retrospective comparative study included patients with DME who received anti-VEGF therapy at the Eye Hospital of Sanmenxia Central Hospital between December 2019 and November 2024. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The Ethics Committee of Sanmenxia Central Hospital approved this study (No.20191229) and waived the requirement for informed consent due to the retrospective nature of the data collection. All patient data were de-identified to protect privacy.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria were: (1) age ≥ 18 years; (2) a diagnosis of DME confirmed by optical coherence tomography (OCT) and fluorescein fundus angiography (FFA); (3) clear refractive media allowing for fundus examination and (4) complete baseline medical records.

Exclusion criteria included: (1) previous treatment with anti-VEGF medications, retinal laser photocoagulation, or vitrectomy; (2) concurrent retinal diseases such as retinal vein occlusion or age-related macular degeneration; (3) a history of glaucoma; (4) other severe systemic diseases.

Treatment and follow-up

Patients were divided into three groups based on the anti-VEGF agent they received: the Ranibizumab group, the Aflibercept group, or the Conbercept group. All patients received monthly intravitreal injections during the initial 3-month induction phase. It is noteworthy that patients in each group were maintained on a strict monotherapy regimen throughout the study. Injections were administered into the inferotemporal quadrant, 4 mm from the limbus (or 3.5 mm in pseudophakic eyes).

Comprehensive ophthalmic examinations were performed at baseline and at follow-up visits at 1 week; and 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months post-injection. Examinations included best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) measured using the logMAR scale, intraocular pressure (IOP), slit-lamp biomicroscopy, and scanning laser fundus photography. Central subfield macular thickness (CSMT) was measured using a spectral-domain OCT system (Heidelberg HRA 2, Germany). The criteria for retreatment were as follows: a central subfield macular thickness (CSMT) of ≥ 300 μm, an increase in CSMT of ≥ 50 μm from the previous visit, or a loss of ≥ 2 lines in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) on the logMAR chart.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes were the differences in BCVA improvement and CSMT reduction among the three treatment groups. Secondary outcomes included differences in DME recurrence rates, the average number of injections, and treatment safety.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., USA). Categorical data were compared between groups using the Chi-square test. Quantitative data conforming to normal distribution (assessed by Kolmogorov-Smirnov test) with homogeneity of variance (verified by Levene’s test) were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄±SD). Independent samples t-test was employed for intergroup comparisons, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for multiple group comparisons. Outcomes at specific time points were analyzed as independent comparisons. No specific correction for multiple testing (e.g., Bonferroni) was applied; therefore, p-values should be interpreted as exploratory. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline clinical characteristics

Baseline characteristics were generally balanced across groups for age, gender, and duration of diabetes. Statistically significant differences were noted in HbA1c levels (p = 0.007), hypertension grade (p = 0.011), and the mean number of injections (p = 0.003) (Table 1).

Baseline clinical characteristics

Best-Corrected Visual Acuity (BCVA)

BCVA can directly reflect the degree of damage to patients’ central visual function caused by DME. Specifically, the more severe the edema, the more obvious the compression or damage to the photoreceptor cells in the macular area, and the lower the BCVA value usually. In clinical practice, physicians judge the efficacy of therapeutic regimens such as anti-VEGF therapy through changes in BCVA before and after treatment, which also serves as a key basis for formulating subsequent treatment strategies.

Through the retrospective analysis, the BCVA of patients before treatment was comparable (p > 0.05; Fig. 1A). All three agents were associated with improvements in BCVA (p < 0.05) at week 1 and week 4 (Fig. 1B and C). At month 3, all groups showed significant improvements from baseline (Fig. 1D). By 6 months, improvements in the aflibercept and conbercept groups were significantly greater than in the ranibizumab group (p < 0.05; Fig. 1E). At 12 months, all groups maintained significant improvement from baseline (p < 0.01; Fig. 1F).

Our retrospective analysis revealed that all three anti-VEGF agents exert efficacy on the BCVA of DME patients. However, the efficacy of ranibizumab was associated with smaller mean improvements compared to that of aflibercept and conbercept with the extension of treatment duration.

Central Subfield Macular Thickness (CSMT)

Central Subfield Macular Thickness (CSMT) is a key imaging indicator for assessing macular anatomical changes, guiding treatment decisions, and monitoring treatment responses in patients with DME. CSMT can directly quantify the severity of macular edema: the more prominent the edema, the higher the CSMT value. In clinical practice, it is often used in combination with BCVA. Thus, CSMT serves as an indispensable assessment dimension in the diagnosis and management of DME.

There was no significant difference in CSMT among the three groups at baseline (p > 0.05; Fig. 2A). All three agents were associated with reductions in CSMT (p < 0.05) at week 1, week 4, and months 3, 6, and 12 (Fig. 2B and F). As early as 4 weeks, the conbercept group was associated with a significantly greater reduction in CSMT compared to the ranibizumab group (p < 0.05; Fig. 2C). At 6 months, statistically significant differences in CSMT were observed among all three groups (p < 0.01; Fig. 2E). By 12 months, while all groups showed significant reduction from baseline, the inter-group differences were no longer significant (Fig. 2F).

Our study suggested that a reduction in CSMT could be observed earlier in the conbercept group, indicating that monotherapy with conbercept can significantly and rapidly improve the anatomical structure of DME.

Safety outcomes

No severe complications (e.g., endophthalmitis, retinal detachment) were observed. Minor transient adverse events, such as subconjunctival hemorrhage or transient intraocular pressure elevation, were not systematically quantified in this retrospective analysis.

Discussion

Diabetic macular edema, which arises from hyperglycemia-induced activation of pathways that trigger oxidative stress and cytokine release, thereby impairing the inner and outer blood-retinal barriers, is the primary cause of vision loss in diabetic patients3. Aflibercept, ranibizumab, and conbercept are all anti-VEGF agents that treat DME by inhibiting the VEGF signaling pathway12,13. In this retrospective study, we compared the efficacy of three anti-VEGF agents for DME and found that aflibercept and conbercept were associated with greater clinical improvements compared to ranibizumab. Specifically, aflibercept was associated with greater improvements in visual acuity at 6 months, whereas conbercept was associated with a more rapid reduction in macular thickness.

The observed differences may be attributed to the distinct molecular structures and pharmacokinetic properties of these agents. Aflibercept, a fusion protein, has a higher binding affinity for VEGF-A compared to ranibizumab, potentially leading to more sustained biological activity7,14. Our finding that aflibercept was associated with superior BCVA improvement at 6 months aligns with previous studies reporting its robust efficacy in functional outcomes.

Conbercept, another fusion protein, targets a broader range of VEGF isoforms. The rapid reduction in CSMT observed in the conbercept group as early as one month may reflect its potent effect on reducing vascular permeability15,16. These findings indicate that while ranibizumab is effective, it was associated with smaller mean improvements compared to aflibercept and conbercept in both functional and anatomical outcomes at the 6-month mark.

The results of this study have important clinical implications. Given the chronic nature of DME and the treatment burden associated with frequent injections, selecting the most effective agent early on can improve patient outcomes and potentially reduce long-term costs. Our findings suggest that conbercept was associated with an earlier reduction in CSMT, which may be relevant for patients requiring rapid anatomical improvement, although anatomical outcomes converged by 12 months. For maximizing long-term visual acuity, aflibercept appears to be a superior option. This supports the development of individualized treatment regimens tailored to patient-specific clinical presentations and goals.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective and non-randomized design may introduce selection bias. Second, it was conducted at a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Third, baseline imbalances were observed in HbA1c levels and hypertension grades between groups. As these systemic factors can influence DME progression, they may act as confounders. Fourth, the follow-up period was limited to one year, and longer-term data are needed to assess the sustainability of these outcomes. Additionally, the statistical analysis did not adjust for multiple comparisons, which may increase the risk of Type I errors. Future prospective, randomized controlled trials are warranted to confirm these findings and further refine treatment strategies for DME.

Conclusion

In this retrospective comparative analysis, aflibercept and conbercept were associated with better clinical outcomes compared to ranibizumab for the treatment of DME. Aflibercept was associated with greater improvements in BCVA at 6 months, while conbercept was associated with a more rapid reduction in CSMT. These findings help guide clinical decision-making and support the personalization of anti-VEGF therapy.

Introduction

As medical knowledge continues to advance each day and there are multiple competencies that need to be acquired by undergraduate students, it becomes quite challenging for them to become competent within the period of training.[1] This calls for the need to employ a different range of teaching-learning methods that meet the needs of students with different learning styles.[1] In the context of medical education, peer observation by medical students refers to a systematic and collaborative process, wherein medical students actively participate in observing and providing feedback on the teaching-learning practices of their peers.[12] The employment of such a strategy can significantly augment the quality of medical education by establishing a culture of continuous improvement, thereby enabling active learning, and developing critical thinking skills among undergraduate medical students.[34] The purpose of the current review is to explore the process of peer observation, identify the benefits attributed to peer observation, and explore various strategies to measure the impact of peer observation in medical education.

Materials and Methods

An extensive search of all materials related to the topic was carried out on the PubMed and Google Scholar search engines. Relevant research articles focusing on peer observation published in the period 2004 to 2023 were included in the review. A total of 47 studies were identified initially, of which 22 were excluded (13 articles were excluded as they were not in alignment with the current objectives, seven articles were excluded due to the unavailability of the complete version of the articles, and two articles were excluded as it was ahead of print). Overall, 25 articles were selected based on their suitability with the current review objectives and analyzed. Keywords used in the search include peer observation in the title alone only (viz. peer observation [ti] AND teaching [ti]; peer observation [ti] AND feedback [ti]; peer observation [ti] AND faculty [ti]; peer observation [ti] AND evaluation [ti]; peer observation [ti]). The articles published in only the English language were included in the review [Figure 1]. The collected information is presented under the following sub-headings, namely, peer observation, learning theories and Peer observation, benefits of peer observation, preparing for peer observation, process of peer observation, post-peer observation reflection, and feedback, measuring the impact of peer observation, and implications for future.

Peer Observation

The approach of peer observation by medical students has been acknowledged as one of the important tools to improve the teaching and learning experiences by making everyone accountable.[15] We must realize that peer observations are often started and organized by students themselves under the guidance of their teachers.[2] The strategy of peer observation by medical students essentially includes active engagement of students in the evaluation of teaching methods, clinical skills, and professional approach of their peers, as they become voluntary observers.[678] To reduce individual bias and make it fair and transparent, the entire process of observation is designed in a systematic and structured manner with the help of pre-designed specific instructions and guidelines.[89]

Upon observation, it is expected that students provide constructive and objective feedback to their peers on all observed parameters.[10] The encouraging thing is that these peer observations have both formative and summative components, wherein we not only aim for improvement but also measure the extent to which their peers have acquired the observed parameters.[147] It is recommended to encourage self-reflection among students who are being observed, as they will recognize the merits of self-assessment and grow further through self-directed learning (as they will work in the direction to improve their knowledge and skills).[47] However, the success and effectiveness of such peer observations will be determined by respecting privacy, maintenance of confidentiality, and delivery of feedback in a non-judgmental way.[10]

Learning Theories and Peer Observation

A number of learning theories support the concept and rationale of peer observation in medical education and the ways in which it can be utilized to augment the teaching and learning process.[11] Regarding social learning theory, medical students can learn from observing their peers in different learning environments. At the same time, students can continue their learning process by adding new knowledge and skills (while observing their peers) to what they already know. In addition, students carry out self-reflection and accordingly adapt their learning strategies while observing their peers. This process of reflection and active learning supports the experiential learning model and at the same time, also promotes self-awareness. Further, as students who are observing are expected to provide constructive feedback to their peers, all those who are involved can improve their knowledge and skills. In short, as students engage in peer observations, they learn via multiple strategies (viz. observation, reflection, constructive feedback, etc.), and this significantly augments the learning experience of medical students.[1112]

Benefits of Peer Observation

Peer observation by medical students has been linked with multiple benefits for the student community as well as the broader medical education community. This begins with the promotion of student-centered learning, wherein students take an active part in their individual learning which enables an in-depth understanding of medical concepts, clinical skills, and acquisition of critical thinking skills.[34] Observers can also acquire teaching skills, which will be vital for them in their future role as teachers.[21314] Students learn the art of delivering effective and constructive feedback, which happens to be a crucial skill in the health sector, and while doing this there is a significant improvement in communication skills as well.[15] In addition, students become aware of their strengths and the areas in which they need to work to become better to continue their learning process, and this is significantly aided by the practice of reflective writing.[101516]

Peer observation encourages professionalism and accountability in the medical institution and promotes teamwork and collaboration, which is critical in healthcare settings.[7] This also becomes important for developing confidence among medical students as they know about their strengths and are more self-aware. As all peers, learn from each other, there is a kind of supportive learning environment in the medical institution which is essential for the quality improvement of medical education as well as better patient care in the long run.[411] From the teachers’ perspective, there is a definite scope for improvement in the teaching skills, upon receiving feedback from students (as teachers become aware of the needs of the students as well as those things that need to be included while taking sessions to benefit a large segment of medical students).[1314] Finally, the adoption of peer observation can also benefit medical schools in the attainment of better accreditation standards. In short, peer observation not only plays its part in the growth and development of medical students but also in augmenting the quality of medical education and competence levels of budding doctors.[1112]

Preparing for Peer Observation

The success of peer observation in any medical college depends on the groundwork that has been carried out prior to its introduction. This preparation includes setting specific goals and objectives of the peer observation program, in the sense are we implementing this program in our institution to improve clinical skills or teaching skills, or overall professionalism of medical students, or a combination of them.[17] The next step is to formulate a structured process that must outline all steps that are involved in conducting peer observation. Subsequently, we must carefully select participants (both observers and those who will be observed), ensuring that there is a combination of people with different extents of expertise and experience.[1718] In continuation, all these selected participants should understand the planned objectives, so that everyone can be on the same page. It is quite essential that we must devise a peer observation schedule (viz. date, time, place, and duration), and communicate the same to all the concerned participants well in advance.[1819]

To enhance the success of the peer observation, we must train and orient all participants about techniques required for effective observation, methods for giving feedback, use of assessment tools, and reflection writing.[1719] We must select appropriate assessment tools that are in alignment with the observation process, and also provide a structured framework for feedback delivery.[1019] Before the conduct of the peer observation session, we must obtain informed consent from all those who are involved, in the pre-text that their participation is voluntary and they understand the purpose of the session.[17] The teachers must also arrange for any of the required logistics or resources that might be required for the seamless conduct of the session. Once the participants come, we should conduct a pre-observation meeting, where all essential things, including maintaining a non-judgmental learning environment are discussed, and they are also explained about the need to ethically conduct the observation, and at the same time maintain privacy.[1020] Finally, arrangements and instructions must be given to the observers to collect data and make notes during observation.

Process of Peer Observation

To maximize the benefit of peer observation in medical education for both stakeholders, we must take specific measures to conduct effective observations.[1920] Effective observation will depend on the use of appropriate observation techniques and assessment tools (such as checklists, specific criteria, rubrics, etc.) and they have to be in alignment with the planned objective that needs to be attained.[2021] Observers should not interfere with the teaching process and look toward the collection of data by making detailed notes on what they see and hear during the observation.[21] At the same time, observers must be objective while recording and should not make any judgments or be biased. The peer observation should be followed up with a post-observation meeting for all the participants.[117] We must understand that interactions during observation are expected to benefit both observers and those who are being observed, but then observers should respect privacy and not be disrespectful. This interaction can be materialized in the form of the administration of formative and summative feedback, with more emphasis on supporting growth and development (formative component).[1516]

Post-Peer Observation Reflection and Feedback

As mentioned earlier, post-peer observation reflection and feedback is a crucial component of the peer observation process, where everyone involved in the observation come together to discuss the recorded data and provide constructive feedback.[10] This phase is expected to strengthen professional development, improve teaching and clinical skills, and aid in establishing a culture of continuous improvement. It is always a good practice to adopt a structured approach and give priority to improvement.[1516] In addition, the participants who were observed were encouraged to reflect on their own performance and introspect with the feedback that was given to them.[116] This process of self-reflection becomes useful for facilitating learning. This post-peer observation meeting gives a platform for everyone to have an open and respectful discussion, wherein clarifications can be made and action plans for future improvement are made together.[1117] All these discussions should be recorded carefully for future reference and also with an aim to establish a culture of continuous improvement in the medical college.

Measuring the Impact of Peer Observation

For any curricular reform or teaching-learning method, it is a must to measure the impact to enable assessing the effectiveness and areas that need to be improved for better outcomes.[2223] On a similar note, we must also measure the impact of peer observation by medical students, and this can be done with the help of self-reflection, self-assessment, and objective assessment tools. We can even obtain feedback from both observers and those being observed with the help of a simple questionnaire-based survey to gain deep insights into the benefits and the areas for improvement (Kirkpatrick Level 1).[12] The teachers can also conduct pre and post-tests before and after the session, respectively to measure the change in knowledge and skills acquisition (Kirkpatrick Level 2). Another approach to measure the impact could be done with the help of peer and self-assessment using rating scales.[23]

Teachers can also compare the performance of students who have undergone peer observation with those who have not, and thus we will get a precise impact of peer observation.[18] To measure the long-term impact of peer observations, refinement in the teaching practices and clinical skills (Kirkpatrick Level 3) and future career growth (Kirkpatrick Level 4) can be measured.[18] We can even conduct qualitative studies (using focus group discussion or in-depth interviews) to obtain deep insights on the impact of peer observation on improvement in knowledge and skills. One more strategy to measure the impact of peer observation could be in the form of compilation and sharing of success stories with different stakeholders to help them understand the merits of peer observation in facilitating learning and ensuring continuous improvement. The combination of the above-mentioned methods can aid in the comprehensive assessment of the impact of peer observation by medical students.[12182223]

Implications for Future

There has been constant development in the domain of peer observation to strengthen effective teaching and learning practices. Medical educators can integrate technology (such as video recording and online platforms) for remote peer observation, which will expedite the process of data analysis and improve accessibility.[24] Even artificial intelligence can be merged with data analysis to promote the generation of automated feedback and identification of trends. The peer observation program could be further expanded by the inclusion of students from different healthcare specialties which will also promote interprofessional collaboration.[25] On a similar note, even observers from the global community can be roped in to enrich the process of observation by including cultural perspectives from different communities.[24] Another future option is to implement this program in a longitudinal manner that will enable educators to track the performance and growth of medical students over an extended period of time.[117] We can even plan to conduct research work in this domain and accordingly generate appropriate evidence to measure the impact of the program on the attainment of learning outcomes, patient care, and faculty development. At Datta Meghe Medical College, Nagpur, the Off-Campus of Datta Meghe Institute of Higher Education and Research, Deemed-to-be University, Sawangi, Wardha, Maharashtra, peer observation has been regularly employed in clinical, practical, and sessions covering Attitude, Ethics, and Communication module across all professional years of training.

Conclusion

In conclusion, peer observation by medical students is an effective approach to empower students as educators and develop a culture of continuous improvement in the institution. Acknowledging the merits of peer observation, the need of the hour is to adopt a systematic approach to ensure its successful planning and implementation in medical colleges.

Efficacy and safety of hydroxychloroquine in IgA nephropathy with nasopharyngeal or intestinal mucosal and non-mucosal infections
Authors: Ran Zhang, Yang Yang, Yan Yan, Hongping Wan, Wenjun Yan, Kaiping Luo, Honghong Zou, Gaosi Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHydroxychloroquine (HCQ) had shown efficacy in treating Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN). There are currently no clinical studies evaluating whether the efficacy and safety of HCQ differ in IgAN with concurrent nasopharyngeal or intestinal mucosal and non-mucosal infections at the time of diagnosis.MethodsThis prospective multicenter cohort study enrolled patients aged > 18 years with biopsy-proven IgAN who had received optimized renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitor (RAASi) therapy for at least 1 month, 24-hour urinary protein creatinine ratio (UPCR) of 0.75–3.5 g/g and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) > 60 mL/min/1.73 m². At the time of IgAN diagnosis, patients were divided into the mucosal infection (MI) group and the non-mucosal infection (NMI) group based on whether they had mucosal infections, both groups of patients were treated with HCQ.ResultsThe study enrolled a total of 369 participants, consisting of 192 MI patients and 177 NMI patients. The mean age of the study participants was 37.58 years, with an average eGFR of 85.27 mL/min/1.73 m² and a baseline 24-hour UPCR of 1.92 g/g. After 12 months, the MI group showed a significant reduction in 24-hour UPCR, decreasing from 1.90 ± 0.76 g/g to 0.77 ± 0.31 g/g, with a mean reduction of 58.6%. In contrast, the NMI group had a more modest decline, from 1.94 ± 0.67 g/g to 1.08 ± 0.44 g/g, averaging a 43.8% reduction. Complete remission rate was achieved in 58.3% (n = 112) of the MI group and 47.5% (n = 84) of the NMI group (HR: 1.56, 95% CI: 1.15–2.13, P = 0.004). The overall remission rate was 66.1% (n = 127) in the MI group and 57.6% (n = 102) in the NMI group (HR: 1.65, 95% CI: 1.23–2.21, P < 0.001). Renal function remained stable, no severe adverse events were reported.ConclusionHCQ combined with optimized RAASi safely and effectively reduced proteinuria in IgAN, with particularly superior efficacy observed in patients with concurrent nasopharyngeal or intestinal MI.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-025-04509-1.

Introduction

Immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgAN) is a primary glomerular disease characterized by IgA or IgA-dominant immune deposits in the mesangial region [1]. In China, IgAN exhibits the highest incidence among glomerulopathies, with a poor long-term prognosis: approximately 40% of patients progress to end-stage renal disease (ESRD) within 15 years of diagnosis [2]. Although the optimized renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitor (RAASi) therapy remains the primary treatment approach, corticosteroids are still recommended for persistent proteinuria [3], however, the efficacy of corticosteroids is controversial, and adverse reactions are obvious [4]. Notably, nasopharyngeal or intestinal mucosal infection (MI) frequently precedes IgAN onset, presenting with symptoms such as fever, pharyngeal pain, nasal congestion, abdominal discomfort, or diarrhea. These infections exacerbate proteinuria and hematuria, the latest researches indicated that mucosal immunity plays a very important role in the pathogenesis of IgAN [5], recent trials targeting mucosal-associated lymphoid tissue (e.g., with targeted-release budesonide) further support this mechanism as a potential therapeutic avenue [6]. The hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) was recently as a therapy against IgAN [7, 8]. A randomized controlled trial demonstrated that combining HCQ with RAASi significantly reduced urinary protein levels compared to RAASi monotherapy over 6 months [9]. And, in the 2021 KDIGO guidelines, HCQ has been recommended for the treatment of IgAN with persistent proteinuria [10].

Thus, we conducted this prospective, multicentre, study aims to investigated the efficacy and safety of HCQ combined with RAASi for treating IgAN patients with nasopharyngeal or intestinal mucosal and non-mucosal infections at the time of diagnosis.

Methods

Study design and population

The multicenter, prospective cohort study was conducted at four research centers in China (the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University, the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University, the First Affiliated Hospital of Gannan Medical University, Ganzhou People’s Hospital) from January 2021 to March 2024. The main study inclusion criteria were age over 18 years, a diagnosis of biopsy-proven primary IgAN, an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) > 60 mL/min/1.73m2 (calculated via the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration creatinine equation), 24-hour urinary protein creatinine ratio (UPCR) of 0.75–3.5 g/g despite receiving a maximum tolerable dose of RAAS inhibitor for at least 1 months. Principal exclusion criteria were the use of systemic immunosuppressive therapy in the last 6 months, and other contraindications for HCQ therapy. At the time of diagnosis of IgAN, by observing the clinical symptoms, analyzing the laboratory test results, conducting nasopharyngeal endoscopy and colonoscopy to examine the pathological conditions, it is possible to determine whether the patient has nasopharyngeal or intestinal MI, and patients were divided to either MI or non-mucosal infection (NMI) groups. The present study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University and all participants provided written informed consent before enrolling in the study, conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Treatment and follow-up

Patients received assigned treatments for 12 months, with oral HCQ administered at 0.2 g twice daily. Treatment discontinuation criteria included either a > 30% decline in eGFR from baseline or the occurrence of severe adverse events. Throughout the study, all participants maintained their maximally tolerated doses of RAASi without additional therapeutic interventions (The type of RAASi used by the patient and the maximum tolerated dose were shown in Supplementary Table 2). Comprehensive assessments were performed at baseline and every 3 months, including 24-hour UPCR measurement, eGFR evaluation, urinary red blood cell (RBC) count analysis, and adverse events (AEs) monitoring.

Study outcomes

The primary outcomes were complete response remission rate (CR) and overall remission rate (OR). CR was defined as 24-hour UPCR < 0.5 g/g, and OR was defined as CR plus partial remission (PR, defined as 24-hour UPCR > 0.5 g/g, with ≥ 50% reduction from baseline). And, the percentage change in 24-hour UPCR from baseline to 12 months was also evaluated. Secondary outcomes were the proportion of patients demonstrating ≥ 30% decline in eGFR and the magnitude of eGFR reduction; the percentage change in urinary red blood cell (RBC) counts between baseline and follow-up also was assessmented.

Statistical analysis

In the continuous variables, normally distributed data are presented as mean ± standard deviation, variables of the 2 groups were compared using independent-samples t tests. Non-normally distributed continuous variables are presented as median with interquartile range (IQR), the comparison between the two groups uses Mann-Whitney U-test. Categorical variables and counting data were expressed in the form of frequency, composition or percentage (%). Cumulative remission rate was described using Kaplan-Meier survival curve and comparison between groups using Log-rank test. Treatment effects on the two primary outcomes were estimated using multivariate Cox proportional hazard models with covariate adjustment. We treated all missing information as missing data without imputed values. P values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics (version 29.0) and GraphPad (version 9.0).

Results

Enrollment and baseline characteristics

From January 2021 to March 2024, we screened 608 potentially eligible patients, of whom 369 met the inclusion criteria and were enrolled in the study. The patients were assigned to the MI group (n = 192) and the NMI group (n = 177). During follow-up, 22 patients (13 in the MI group and 9 in the NMI group) were lost to follow-up, while 347 patients (94.0%) completed the study. Among them, 17 patients in the MI group and 16 in the NMI group discontinued HCQ treatment (Fig. 1). The two groups had similar characteristics at baseline (P > 0.05). The mean age of the overall study population was 37.58 ± 13.38 years, with an average 24-hour UPCR of 1.92 ± 0.71 g/g and a mean eGFR of 85.27 ± 18.23 mL/min/1.73 m². Primary outcomes

Compared to baseline levels, both groups showed a significant reduction in 24-hour UPCR at all time points (Fig. 2A and B). At 3, 6, 9, and 12 months, the MI group exhibited significantly lower 24-hour UPCR level than the NMI group. At 12 months, the mean 24-hour UPCR was 0.77 ± 0.31 g/g in the MI group, compared to 1.08 ± 0.44 g/g in the NMI group (P = 0.042). The 24-hour UPCR in the MI group showed a significantly greater average reduction compared to the NMI group (58.6 ± 6.9% vs. 43.8 ± 7.4%, P = 0.039). And, the MI group demonstrated superior clinical outcomes: 58.3% of patients (n = 112) achieved CR, compared to 47.5% (n = 84) in the NMI group (log-rank P = 0.004, Fig. 2C). The OR rate was 66.1% (n = 127) in the MI group and 57.6% (n = 102) in the NMI group (log-rank P < 0.001, Fig. 2D). After adjustment for baseline proteinuria, age, gender and eGFR, a multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that, compared with NMI group, the MI group increased the rate of the CR and OR by 56% (HR: 1.56, 95% CI: 1.15–2.13) and 65% (HR: 1.65, 95% CI: 1.23–2.21), respectively (Supplementary Table 3). The number of patients achieving CR and OR at each time point was presented in Supplementary Table 1.

Secondary outcomes

At the 12 months follow-up, the eGFR in the MI group was 83.29 ± 15.59 mL/min/1.73 m², with a median decrease of 1.45 mL/min1.73 m², with 3.1% of patients (n = 6) showed a > 30% decline from baseline values. And the NMI group participants demonstrated eGFR of 82.91 ± 17.22 mL/min/1.73 m², experienced a median GFR decline of 1.66 mL/min/1.73 m², with 2.3% of patients (n = 4) demonstrated a greater than 30% reduction, and no statistically significant difference between them (P = 0.829, Fig. 3A). The reduction in urinary RBC was more pronounced in the MI group, with a median decrease of 45.1% compared to 35.7% in the NMI group, demonstrating a significant intergroup difference (P < 0.001, Fig. 3B and C).

Discussion

This study was the first prospective multicenter cohort trial with a large sample size and long-term follow-up, aiming to evaluate the efficacy and safety of HCQ in IgAN patients with nasopharyngeal or intestinal MI at the time of diagnosis. Our results showed that the IM group had significantly lower 24-hour UPCR and urinary RBC levels than the NMI group at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months. At 12 months, both the CR and OR rates were significantly higher in the IM group (58.3% vs. 47.5% and 66.1% vs. 57.6%, respectively). No significant intergroup difference in eGFR decline was observed, and renal function remained stable in both groups. HCQ demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with no SAEs observed. These findings are consistent with previous clinical studies on HCQ for the treatment of IgAN [7–9]. HCQ has been extensively utilized in the management of systemic lupus erythematosus and rheumatoid arthritis due to its established efficacy and safety profile [11]. HCQ is recognized for its pleiotropic immunomodulatory effects [12–16].

The current pathogenesis of IgAN is understood to follow a “multiple-hit” mechanism, with mucosal immune dysregulation being a key component. Key initiating factors include antigen exposure at mucosal surfaces, particularly through nasopharynx-associated lymphoid tissue (NALT) and gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) [17, 18]. These antigens activate monocytes and lymphocytes via TLR-mediated immune signaling pathways, triggering B-cell proliferation and aberrant IgA responses, ultimately driving renal injury [18]. Previous studies have confirmed that in IgAN patients with nasopharyngeal or intestinal MI, the TLRs/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway can be activated, leading to the production of BAFF, APRIL, cytokines, and inflammatory mediators. HCQ can inhibit this pathway mediators [19–25]. IgAN patients with NMI, the abnormally increased pathogenic Gd-IgA1 may not originate from the nasopharyngeal or intestinal mucosa, but from non-mucosal sites, such as the bone marrow or the central nervous system [18], this had little association with the immune signaling pathways related to TLRs. This finding may explained the mechanism by which HCQ significantly reduces proteinuria and hematuria in IgAN patients with nasopharyngeal or intestinal MI at the time of diagnosis (Supplementary Fig. 1).

2024 KDIGO proposed that the ideal target of IgAN proteinuria was < 0.5 g/d, and the optimization target was < 0.3 g/d [26]. In the past, the blocked the renin-angiotensin system, blood pressure control, and lifestyle modifications were the cornerstones of the treatment for IgAN. Systemic glucocorticoids, mycophenolate mofetil, cyclophosphamide and azathioprine, have led to an increasing number of adverse events, which have drawn widespread attention from all. Now, such as sodium-glucose transporter-2 inhibitors, sparsentan (a dual endothelin-1 and angiotensin II receptor blocker), and iptacopan (a complement factor B inhibitor), have been approved for the treatment of IgAN, the treatment plans for B-cell/plasma-cell depletion and B-cell/plasma-cell modulation were currently undergoing active clinical research [27]. In China, the tripterygium wilfordii and its derivatives have been proven to significantly reduce proteinuria in IgAN [28, 29]. In the management of IgAN, the most significant challenge lies in the heterogeneity of its clinical manifestations and prognosis, which necessitates the adoption of individualized treatment strategies [30]. Therefore, in clinical treatment, multiple drugs need to be used in combination to act on each aspect of the pathogenesis of IgAN, thereby achieving the ideal therapeutic goal or even the optimization goal, and delaying the deterioration of renal function. HCQ emerged as a promising therapeutic, particularly in IgAN patients with concurrent nasopharyngeal or intestinal MI at disease onset.

However, as a cohort study conducted only among the same ethnic group from China, the results have some limitations. Firstly, there are significant global variations in the prevalence and phenotypic manifestations of IgAN. Notably, a distinct West-to-East gradient in disease prevalence exists, with the disease being most common in China (45–58% of primary glomerulonephritis) and other Asian countries (47% in Japan, 28% in Korea, and 45% in Singapore), compared with that in European countries (10–35% of primary glomerulonephritis) [31]. There are relatively few large-scale and high-quality studies on the use of HCQ for treating IgAN in non-Asian population populations, it has an uncertain efficacy in non-Asian patients. Secondly, this clinical study only included patients with relatively good renal function, and longer-term follow-up is still needed to evaluate the impact of HCQ on renal function. Thirdly, This clinical study lacks observation after HCQ discontinuation. Moreover, after the study ends, treating physicians may introduce additional medications based on proteinuria levels, potentially confounding the assessment of HCQ’s long-term efficacy.

In conclusion, our study indicated that the combination of HCQ with optimized RAASi therapy safely and effectively reduces proteinuria in IgAN, with particularly pronounced efficacy observed in the patients with nasopharyngeal or intestinal MI.

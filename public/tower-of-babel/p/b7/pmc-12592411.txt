ADN source-load-storage cooperative two-layer optimal allocation based on ICSQPSO algorithm
Authors: Xiaobang Li, Kang Zhang, Liying Zhao, Liying Zhu, Zongqiang Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Smart grid technology continues to advance. Renewable energy sees extensive application. The traditional one-way passive distribution network is changing. It is evolving into a two-way interactive, multi-dimensional, and coordinated active distribution network (ADN). The stochastic and temporal nature of distributed generation (DG) output presents challenges. The volatility of loads also presents challenges. These factors pose significant challenges to ADN resource allocation and operation regulation. To address these challenges, a two-layer optimization method is proposed. This method focuses on ADN source-load-storage coordination. It incorporates demand response. The upper planning layer determines the near global optimum device siting and capacity setting scheme. This is within the active distribution network. The lower operation layer derives the near global optimum scheduling scheme for each flexible resource. This includes demand response loads. The two-layer planning problem has inherent complexity. To manage this complexity, an improved algorithm, called cuckoo search quantum behavioural particle swarm optimization (ICSQPSO), is introduced. The model’s validity is demonstrated through an example, indicating a potential reduction in operating costs by 49.7%, a decrease in total investment and operating costs by 37.1%, and a reduction in the wind curtailment rate by 0.4% and the solar power curtailment rate by 1.5%. The effectiveness of the model and algorithm is verified. This verification uses simulation with the IEEE33 algorithm..

Introduction

Large-scale grid access to distributed generation (DG) is recognized as a necessary pathway for realizing energy transition development1–3. Among renewable energy sources, wind turbines (WT) and photovoltaics (PV) are the most widely utilized. They play a pivotal role in adjusting the energy structure and reducing pollutant emissions. However, the output of WT and PV is characterized by strong stochastic and timing characteristics. When these power sources are connected to the distribution network in large quantities, problems such as grid voltage fluctuations and resource waste may be caused4,5. Therefore, the concept of the active distribution network (ADN) was proposed. Its core concept is defined as the realization of two-way interaction and collaborative optimization between the power grid and various energy resources. This is achieved through informatization and automation technologies. In the context of the new power system environment, ADNs are known to encompass a multitude of adjustable resources6–8. When these resources are planned in an integrated manner and interact in a coordinated and friendly way, significant enhancements can be achieved. Specifically, the operational efficiency, reliability, and economy of the power grid can be improved. This integrated approach also allows for better adaptation to the volatility and uncertainty inherent in renewable energy. Thus, collaborative planning for the ADN "source-load-storage" is considered of great significance. Compared to traditional power system distribution network optimization, source-load-storage cooperative optimization involves the unified planning and scheduling of source, power load, and energy storage system (ESS) within the ADN. Current research on this topic can be divided into two levels: the planning level and the scheduling level.

At the planning level, the focus is placed on the long-term layout and resource allocation of the distribution grid. The goal is set to optimize the economy and reliability of the entire power system. This optimization is pursued through the strategic placement of source, storage, and other grid assets. For instance, Ref.9 proposes a multi-stage coordinated planning approach aimed at minimizing the net present value of network investment costs. This is achieved by controlling the active and reactive power outputs of DG units for active management of DG, and by combining this with active management to compute the location and capacity for multiple planning scenarios. Similarly, Ref.10 introduces a multi-objective optimal planning model for active distribution networks using the Pareto optimization method. This model determines the optimal power source location, capacity setting, and distribution network reconfiguration scheme by considering investment cost, network loss, and system reliability. Ref.11 presents a probabilistic approach to active distribution network planning that integrates demand response (DR). The objective is to minimize the distribution network operator’s operating cost and total line energy loss, while modeling the uncertainties associated with solar radiation, load demand, and future load growth using probability density functions. This approach helps in determining the optimal planning scheme under DR conditions. Furthermore, Ref.12 describes a multi-stage, multi-load scenario planning model for active distribution network expansion. This model incorporates investment and operating costs of the ADN into the objective function, considering DG application and feeder construction at the planning level, and DG power supply along with the topology reconfiguration of the distribution network, including microgrids, at the operational level.

Research at the dispatch level emphasizes the short-term operation and control strategies of the distribution network. It addresses how the uncertainty of flexible resources can be managed, the economics of various subjects within the distribution network can be improved, and optimal synergistic operation among source, network, load, and storage can be achieved under given grid facilities and operating conditions13. Ref.14 introduces an active distribution network operation strategy based on affine adjustable robust optimization. This strategy considers the controllable model of PV and ESS to construct an optimal operation model for the active distribution network. The aim is to optimize the uncertainty problem within the network through coordination of source, load, and storage. Ref.15 presents a multi-objective scheduling model for active distribution networks, with operating cost, renewable energy utilization, and customer satisfaction as the optimization objectives. Decision variables include DG output power, switching frequency, and incentive price for responsive loads. Monte Carlo sampling-based probabilistic tidal current and chance constrained planning are employed to handle the uncertainty of the ADN. Ref.16 proposes a day-ahead active power scheduling method that considers REG prediction error. A hierarchical coordinated optimization model based on opportunity constrained planning is established to achieve optimized day-ahead active power scheduling, coordinated with network reconfiguration. Ref.17 describes a three-layer day-ahead optimal scheduling mechanism that considers the interests of multiple stakeholders. The distribution network is divided into a three-layer structure: the user layer, the microgrid layer, and the distribution layer. The corresponding three-layer optimization method is proposed to obtain the user power consumption scheme and the user energy storage operation scheme under total cost optimization. Ref.18 proposes a mixed-integer dynamic optimization model that combines power load and DG output with continuous trajectories. This model aims to minimize power purchase cost and maximize nodal voltage security at distribution stations.

The holistic nature of source-load-storage co-optimization in active distribution networks is often overlooked in these studies. Greater emphasis is placed on individual aspects instead. In contrast, two-layer planning facilitates information exchange and feedback between upper and lower layers. This approach leads to flexible decision-making and optimization adjustments. Utilization of two-layer planning enables the integration of planning and scheduling for source-load-storage cooperative optimization. Multiple stakeholder interests are considered across various time periods. Reasonable planning is ensured through this method. System flexibility and robustness are improved. Consequently, this approach is recognized as an effective optimization method.

Ref.19 presents a two-tier, two-stage stochastic optimization model for wind energy transmission networks incorporating an active distribution network. This model aims to reduce investment costs and efficiently integrate renewable energy sources. Conversely, Ref.20 introduces a two-tier distributed robust optimization model to manage source and load uncertainty, ensuring network security and reliability. Ref.21 discusses cascading dyad-based transmission network planning, coordinating the operation of an active distribution network to optimize the overall power system operation and reduce costs. While these references address various aspects of active distribution network optimization, such as renewable energy integration, expansion planning, and uncertainty management, they lack a focus on multi-energy synergistic planning.

In summary, while a fundamental framework has been established in existing studies for source-load-storage collaborative optimization in active distribution networks, three critical limitations remain unresolved:static resource configurations in planning struggle to accommodate the strong stochastic nature of wind and solar power due to the disconnect between planning and dispatch;Adjustable load resources are insufficiently integrated into planning decisions;Conventional algorithms are prone to local optima in high-dimensional scenarios.

Therefore, an ADN source-load-storage cooperative two-layer optimal allocation method based on the ICSQPSO algorithm is proposed to address collaborative optimization challenges under high-penetration renewable energy integration. The upper layer, termed the planning layer, is designed to determine the near global optimum siting and capacity settings for each device within the active distribution network. The lower layer, termed the operation layer, is designed to establish the near global optimum scheduling scheme for each flexible resource. This includes demand response (DR). The complexity of the two-layer planning problem is tackled by proposing an Improved Cuckoo Search Quantum Particle Swarm Optimization (ICSQPSO) algorithm. Quantum probability cloud search and Levy dynamic perturbation strategy are introduced into this algorithm Computational efficiency is enhanced by this algorithm. Local near global optimum solutions are avoided. Finally, the effectiveness of the proposed model and algorithm is validated. Validation is performed through simulation using the IEEE 33-node system.

The main innovative contributions of this paper are as follows:Introduction of DR: DR is introduced into the active distribution network planning problem. This enables "source-load-network" collaborative planning. Through this methodology, operating costs are reduced by 49.7%, total investment and operating costs are decreased by 37.1%, wind curtailment rate is lowered by 0.4%, and solar curtailment rate is reduced by 1.5%. These benefits originate from spatiotemporal load transfer through DR and ESS peak shaving/valley filling coordination. External power purchases are reduced by 23.7%.ICSQPSO Algorithm: The proposed ICSQPSO algorithm significantly enhances global optimization capability. Premature convergence is prevented. This algorithm achieves convergence 5.2 times faster than traditional PSO (8 iterations), with successful avoidance of 12 local optimum traps.

Source-load-storage synergistic two-layer optimal allocation model

The issues of high randomness in distributed resources, the variety of flexible resources, and the complexity of regulation and control are addressed. These issues are brought about by a high proportion of DG and numerous loads connected to the grid. A two-layer planning and optimal allocation method is proposed in this section to tackle these issues. The economy, reliability, and sustainability of ADNs are considered by this method. The initiative of the source, load, and storage equipment is fully mobilized. The model comprises an upper planning layer and a lower operation layer. Each layer is assigned its corresponding objective function and constraints. These two layers interact. Decision-making information is exchanged between them. Ultimately, the near global optimum planning and operation scheduling scheme is yielded.

Upper model

Objective function

In this study, the upper layer of the two-layer model is defined as the planning layer. Through this layer, the near global optimum planning solution is determined by equipment siting and sizing. The annual integrated planning and operating costs are minimized as the goal of this layer. The objective function is divided into two primary components. The investment and construction costs for each piece of equipment are encompassed in the first component. This includes WT, PV, GT, and ESS. The results returned by the lower layer’s planning are contained in the second component. These results originate from the operation layer feedback. Lower model

Objective function

In the two-layer model, the operation layer is focused on optimizing the dispatch and DR management of the active resources within the ADN. Its optimization is based on the planning results derived from the upper layer, aiming to have daily dispatch curves achieved that fulfill the planning requirements and current distribution network operation. The economic cost and power quality index of the distribution network operation are primarily considered in the objective function of the operation layer. The output of each power equipment, flexible load, ESS, and the amount of power purchased from the higher-level grid are coordinated. The goals are set to reduce operation costs, minimize network losses, smooth out peaks and valleys in load, improve voltage stability, and enhance the capacity for consumption of DG. Quantum behavioural particle swarm optimization (QPSO) algorithm

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is a population intelligence optimization algorithm proposed by Kennedy and Eberhart in 19954. It draws inspiration from the foraging behavior of bird flocks and simulates the process of flying and searching in the solution space to find the near global optimum solution to a problem. In the PSO algorithm, each “particle” represents a potential solution in the solution space and is assigned a velocity that determines the direction and step size of its search. During each iteration of the algorithm, the particle dynamically updates its speed and position by combining its own historical best position and the global best position in the population. This approach enables the particle to more efficiently search for the near global optimum solution. This unique search mechanism makes PSO an efficient and practical optimization tool. Standard CS algorithm

CS algorithm is an optimization algorithm inspired by the parasitic breeding behavior of cuckoos and the flight behavior of Lévy. It was proposed by Yang and Deb in 200923. In nature, certain cuckoo species lay eggs in the nests of other birds, and these host birds may randomly find and destroy these alien eggs. Meanwhile, CS for more suitable nests for parasitism by flying over long distances with step lengths obeying a Lévy distribution. The CS algorithm generalizes the following core rules based on these natural behaviours:

Parasitic behaviour: each cuckoo randomly selects a nest to lay eggs in, which symbolizes the random search in the algorithm within the solution space.

Superiority: the best performing nests and the eggs (i.e., solutions) in them will be retained and passed on to the next generation, reflecting the retention and passing on of high-quality solutions during the optimization process.

Fixation and replacement of nests: the number of nests set in the algorithm is constant, and when an egg in a nest is found (i.e., the solution is judged to be non-near global optimum), there is a certain probability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P_{{{\text{find}}}}$$\end{document} that it will be replaced by a new nest (a new solution), and this replacement mechanism increases the diversity of the search and the ability to jump out of the local optimum.

Standard SA algorithm

Simulated Annealing (SA) is recognized as a probabilistic global optimization algorithm. Its inspiration is drawn from the annealing process in materials science. In this physical process, a material is first heated to a high temperature. Internal particles are allowed to move freely under this condition. Then slow cooling is implemented. Particles are gradually ordered through this cooling. The energy of the system is reduced to a minimum. This physical phenomenon is cleverly transformed into an optimization strategy by the simulated annealing algorithm. The annealing process of a substance is simulated. The near global optimum solution of a given function is searched for in a large search space. A unique probability acceptance criterion is utilized. A specific cooling strategy is employed. Local optimum entrapment is effectively avoided by these mechanisms. An efficient solution method is provided for complex optimization problems24.

Adding a perturbation, i.e., starting from the current solution \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X$$\end{document} , generates a new candidate solution \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X^{\prime}$$\end{document} by some perturbation mechanism.

Calculate the energy difference, i.e., calculate the energy difference between the new solution \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X^{\prime}$$\end{document} and the current solution \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X$$\end{document}\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta E = E(X^{\prime}) - E(X)$$\end{document} .

Judgment is made according to the Metropolis criterion, i.e., if \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta E$$\end{document} > 0, the new solution is better than the current solution, and the new solution is accepted directly \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$X^{\prime}$$\end{document} ; if \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta E$$\end{document} < 0, the new solution can be judged to be inferior to the current solution or equal, and at this time there is still a certain probability of accepting the new solution, and this probability is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P{\text{ME}} = e^{ - \Delta E/T(t)}$$\end{document} , where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T(t)$$\end{document} is the temperature at the time of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t$$\end{document} . This step allows the algorithm to accept worse solutions to a certain extent, thus making it possible to jump out of the trap of locally optimal solutions.

Cooling is performed and the temperature is updated as shown in Eq. (45)24:45\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T(t + 1) = \chi T(t)$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\chi$$\end{document} is a coefficient of cooling less than 1 for gradual temperature reduction.

Termination condition: The algorithm stops when the temperature drops below \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{{{\text{end}}}}$$\end{document} or when other termination conditions are met (e.g., failure to find a better solution for several consecutive iterations).

The traditional PSO algorithm is first upgraded to QPSO algorithm by the ICSQPSO algorithm. This upgrade is achieved through the introduction of quantum bit state and probability cloud concepts. Particles are endowed with the property of existing in the solution space in a probabilistic form. This change significantly expands the coverage of the search space. Search efficiency and accuracy are substantially improved. Secondly, the Metropolis criterion in the SA algorithm is incorporated. Worse solutions are accepted with a certain probability during iterative exploration. This strategy effectively avoids the dilemma of local optimality at an early stage. The near global optimum search capability is significantly strengthened. Additionally, the step-size mechanism of the CS algorithm is innovatively optimized by the ICSQPSO algorithm. Lévy flight is improved through a Lévy distribution-based dynamic adjustment strategy. Exploration of the solution space is flexibly switched between macroscopic and microscopic fine-searching. Diversity and breadth of the search process are ensured. The problem of premature convergence is effectively prevented. The overall algorithm performance is further improved. Conclusion

This study addresses the collaborative optimization of planning and operation in active distribution networks. An ADN source-load-storage cooperative two-layer optimal allocation model is proposed based on the ICSQPSO algorithm. Three key innovations are introduced:A planning-operation bidirectional coupling mechanism is established for coordinated decision-making. Equipment siting and capacity determination (upper layer) are integrated with flexible resource scheduling (lower layer).DR interruptible/transferable loads are embedded as core regulation resources in both layers.An ICSQPSO algorithm is developed by integrating quantum-behaved particle swarm optimization, simulated annealing, and Lévy flight mechanisms. High-dimensional nonlinear programming problems are solved effectively.

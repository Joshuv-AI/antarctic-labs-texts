Determinants and outcomes of adverse drug reactions among people living with HIV on antiretroviral therapy in Nigeria
Authors: Victoria P. Etuk, Ikeola A. Adeoye, Mobolaji M. Salawu, Olufunmilayo I. Fawole
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) to Antiretroviral Therapy (ART) among people living with HIV/AIDS (PLWHA) can significantly affect treatment adherence, viral suppression and contribute to HIV-related morbidity and mortality. ADRs to long-term ART use is not well understood in resource-constrained countries like Nigeria. This study identified the determinants and outcomes of ADRs to ART among PLWHA in Nigeria. Secondary data analysis of the 2014 to 2018 National Pharmacovigilance records was conducted using 3,397 individual case safety reports (ICSRs). The outcome variables were the actual adverse drug reactions reported (e.g., headache, dizziness, anaemia) and the seriousness of the ADR (Serious vs. Non-serious ADR). In contrast, the explanatory variables included the patient’s age, sex, weight, duration of ADR, concomitant medicines used, and ART regimen. ADRs were determined using the WHO System Organ Classification, which groups ADRs by the organ system or body part affected. Data were analysed using multivariate logistic regression, with a p-value set at 5%. The mean age of the participants was 34.7 ± 11 years, and the majority were female (71.5%). The most commonly reported ADRs were neuropsychiatric disorders (29.8%), skin and appendage disorders (17.1%), peripheral nervous system disorders (6.7%), musculoskeletal disorders (4.3%), and anaemia (2.1%). On multivariate analysis, factors associated with development of neuropsychiatric disorders were female were female gender (AOR = 1.43, 95% CI (1.20–1.71): p < 0.001), Efavirenz-based therapy (AOR = 5.58, 95% CI: (4.41–7.05) p < 0.001), older age [16–35 years [AOR = 3.34; 95% CI: (1.72–6.48); p-value: <0.001, [36–50 years: AOR = 2.32; 95% CI: (1.19–4.52); p-value: 0.014; > 50 years AOR = 2.77; 95% CI: (1.36–5.63); p-value: 0.005] Use of Tenofovir [AOR = 1.65; 95% CI: (1.20–2.30); p-value: 0.002], and Zidovudine [AOR = 1.44; 95% CI: (1.13–1.85); p-value: 0.003] were associated with cutaneous adverse drug reactions. Anaemia was associated with the use of Zidovudine [AOR = 32.56; 95% CI: (4.41 − 7.05); p-value: <0.001], but was inversely associated with cotrimoxazole use [AOR = 0.58; 95% CI: (0.42– 0.79); p-value: 0.001]. Some (22%) of the patients recovered from ADRs; 1.2% were fatal, while 71.5% outcomes were unknown. ADRs were common among ART patients in Nigeria., with female gender, older age and types of ART regimen being major determinants of ADRs Active surveillance is necessary to ensure early detection of ADRs among patients on ART and thereby prevent ART-associated morbidity and mortality.

Introduction

The introduction of antiretroviral therapy (ART) about three decades ago changed the trajectory of people living with Human Immunodeficiency Virus/Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (PLWHA) worldwide. ART is the mainstay of treatment for HIV/AIDS. Globally, about 23.3 million people are currently accessing ART. This number is expected to increase to enable countries to meet the UNAIDS 95-95-95 targets. HIV survivorship has increased worldwide due to expanding access to ART; however, it has been associated with a wide range of adverse drug reactions (ADRs) and toxicities [1–4]. As of 2017, about one million people are accessing ART in Nigeria [5]. Access and uptake of this medicine is also increasing annually as the country aims to “fast-track the national response towards ending AIDS in Nigeria by 2030.” [6].

The incidence of ADRs among patients on antiretroviral therapy ranges from 4.6% to 90% in low-income countries [7–10]. In Nigeria, the prevalence of ADRs to ART ranges from 6.3% to 11.81% [8, 11–13]. They are a significant cause of treatment substitution, treatment discontinuation, reduction in quality of life, and can result in disability and death [14]. The occurrence of ADRs among PLWHA can reduce treatment adherence, prevent viral suppression, and increase HIV-associated morbidity and mortality [15]. The adverse effect on treatment adherence and viral suppression increases the risk of continued transmission of HIV [8, 14–17]. Studies have reported various ADRs experienced by people living with HIV on ART [18–21].

In Nigeria, current ART comprises three antiretroviral drug regimens known as highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) [5]. In recent times, ADRs associated with ART have been the focus of studies on patient safety, especially with increasing reports of toxicities [9, 22–24]. Research on adverse drug reactions associated with ART is necessary as clinical trials may not identify rare, late-onset, and bizarre ADRs due to the short duration of these trials. There is also a marked difference between drug use in clinical trial participants and actual patients in the real world due to the strict inclusion, exclusion, and follow-up of clinical trial patients that do not apply to actual-world use of the drugs. In addition, as highly active antiretroviral therapy is life-long, many late-onset adverse events occur during treatment, which may not be detected during clinical trials [25]. Thus, there is a need to generate real-world evidence of adverse reactions in clinical settings.

Additionally, changing funding priorities in the HIV landscape may lead to less available funds for the development of new and safer treatment options and ongoing safety monitoring, thus demonstrating a need to review available data to guide ART drug safety policies. The results of this study will guide clinicians and HIV programme managers on clinically relevant ADRs, identify patients at high risk of ADRs, and adequately monitor for the occurrence of adverse drug reactions. It will also help guide safety guidelines and warnings for both regulators and ARV manufacturers. Hence, the study sought to identify the determinants and outcomes of ADRs to ART among PLWHA in Nigeria.

Materials and methods

Study area and population

The context of ART in Nigeria

In Nigeria, the current ART comprises three antiretroviral drug regimens known as highly active ART [5]. ART regimens are classified as first-line, second-line, and third-line. First-line regimen for adults consists of two Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs-one of Tenofovir/Zidovudine/Abacavir and Lamivudine) and one Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitor (NNRTI- Nevirapine or Efavirenz) or Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitor (INSTI-dolutegravir). The dolutegravir-based regimen (Tenofovir-Lamivudine-Dolutegravir) was introduced in 2018 and was, thus, not reported in this study [6]. The first-line regimen also included Stavudine-based therapy until Stavudine was phased off in 2014. Therefore, the regimens reported in this study also included Stavudine. The second-line regimen consists of two NRTIs (Tenofovir/Zidovudine and Lamivudine) and either ritonavir-boosted Lopinavir or Atazanavir, used in cases of treatment failure or ADRs. Third-line regimens comprise INSTI-based therapy, second-generation NNRTIs, and Protease Inhibitors [26].

The context of ADR monitoring in Nigeria

Adverse drug reaction reporting in Nigeria is mainly through passive reporting. The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) coordinates the spontaneous reporting of ADRs in Nigeria using a standardised form, the ‘Yellow Form’. The form contains the patient’s socio-demographic information, a description, duration, and outcome of the ADR, a history of hospitalisation due to the ADR, the suspected drug(s), drug indication, concomitant medicines used, and the location of the report. Each filled yellow form is the Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR). In line with the Federal Ministry of Health guidelines, healthcare providers, patients, and pharmaceutical manufacturers/marketing authorisation holders are expected to report ADR cases using the yellow form to the National Pharmacovigilance Centre of NAFDAC. ART care providers across different treatment centres are responsible for reporting ADRs by submitting ICSRs to NAFDAC, either by submitting paper forms or electronically [27] The National Drug Safety Advisory Committee investigates bizarre, rare, and complex ADRs of ARVs and other drugs. After causality assessments, reports adjudged to be ADRs are forwarded to the WHO-Uppsala Monitoring Centre, the WHO’s global programme on drug safety monitoring [4].

Study design

The study design was a retrospective secondary analysis. A secondary data analysis of ICSRs submitted to NAFDAC was accessed from the database on the 29th of November, 2019. Three thousand eight hundred eight (3808) ICSRs due to antiretroviral therapy from the National Pharmacovigilance Centre of NAFDAC; however, only 3397 were analysed, due to incomplete key variables such as age and sex.

Study instrument

The extracted data included patient sociodemographic characteristics (age, sex, and weight), ADR information (type, seriousness, duration, and outcome), ART regimen, and concomitant medicines. Concomitant medicines included all medicinal and herbal products taken within 3 months of the ADR report.

Data collection

We included all HIV-associated ADR data, retrieved from ICSRs submitted to NAFDAC from 2014 to 2019. We included all ADR reports in the database between 2014 and 2019 and excluded those with missing variables, such as age and sex. The data was accessed from the database on the 29th of November, 2019. Three thousand eight hundred and eight (3808) ADRs due to antiretroviral therapy were reported to the National Pharmacovigilance Centre of NAFDAC; however, only 3397 were analysed due to incomplete key variables in some reports. The selection criteria were reports with complete data on key variables, including age, sex, and the patient’s ART regimen/drugs. The flow chart of study participants is shown in Fig. 1.

Study variables

Outcomes of ADRs

Refers to the ADR effect on the patient at the time of reporting to NAFDAC and are classified as recovered, ongoing, fatal, or unknown. The outcome variable was the type of ADR reported, while the explanatory variables included the patient’s age, sex, weight, duration of ADR, concomitant medicines used, and ART regimen.

Severe ADRs

ADRs that were fatal, life-threatening, caused permanent disability, congenital abnormality, or prolonged hospitalisation were classified as severe, according to the WHO classification and International Conference on Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Registration of Pharmaceuticals for Human Use guidelines [28, 29]. ADR was determined using the WHO Systematized Nomenclature of Medicine. The WHO Systematized Nomenclature of Drugs and Therapeutic Systems is an international system for classifying adverse drug reactions. The reactions are classified by affected organ or system, using terms from the WHO Adverse Reaction Terminology.

Statistical analysis

Data were extracted from the National Pharmacovigilance Centre database (Vigibase) in Microsoft Excel format and exported and analysed in Stata version 13. Frequencies and proportions were used to describe categorical data, and means and standard deviations were used to describe continuous data. Associations between ADRs and sociodemographic variables were tested using chi-square tests and multivariate logistic regression at the 0.05 significance level.

Ethical approval and consent to participate

We obtained ethical approval from the Oyo State Ethics Research Committee [approval number AD/13/479/1768]. In our study, we performed a retrospective analysis of routine pharmacovigilance/surveillance data, including paediatric data, from the national pharmacovigilance centre database, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. However, the need for informed consent to participate, as well as the need to obtain consent from the parents or legal guardians of minors, was waived by an Institutional Review Board (IRB) because it was deemed unnecessary under national regulations. The data was anonymised and stored on a password-protected device accessible only to the researchers.

Results

Baseline characteristics of study participants

The baseline characteristics of ICSRs received are presented in Table 1. The mean age of patients with ADRs was 34.7 ± 11 years, and the median duration of the ADR was 15 days (IQR, 21 days). The majority of participants were aged 16–35 years (55.9%); the paediatric population (≤ 15 years) accounted for 2.5%, and female patients accounted for the majority (71.5%). The ARV regimens used were Tenofovir/Lamivudine (45.5%), Zidovudine/Lamivudine (41.4%), Efavirenz (45.4%), and Nevirapine (54.0%). 78% of patients reported concomitant medication use in the last 3 months, most of whom used cotrimoxazole (67.4%). Severe ADRs were reported by 2.5% of the patients.

Pattern of adverse drug reactions to antiretroviral therapy

The patterns of ADRs experienced are shown in Fig. 2. The pattern of ADRs noted were: dizziness 820 (13.3%), weakness/fatigue 720 (11.4%), rash 554 (9.0%), headache 539 (8.8%), pain 375 (6.1%), pruritus 367 (6.0%), vomiting 244 (4.0%), nausea 242 (3.9%), anaemia 193 (3.1%), nightmares 184 (3.0%), neuropathy 177 (2.9%), insomnia 161 (2.6%), anorexia 133 (2.2%), diarrhoea 121 (2.0%) and fever 91(1.5%). Other ADRs reported included abnormal vision, numbness, cough, hallucination, malaise, dry mouth, oedema, irregular pigmentation, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, loss of appetite, polyuria, gynaecomastia, amenorrhoea, myalgia, palpitations, drowsiness, lipidosis, abdominal discomfort, dyspnea, weight loss, depression, and jaundice. The outcomes included recovered, ongoing, and fatal. However, 71.5% of outcomes were unknown at the time of the report; 25.2% recovered; 2.1% were ongoing; and 1.2% were fatal. The majority of patients reported 2–5 ADRs (1667, 49.5%), with a similar proportion reporting only 1 ADR (1664, 49.0%). A minute proportion (0.6%) of patients reported more than 5 ADRs.

The classification of adverse drug reactions to antiretroviral therapy

The systemic classification of ADRs associated with ART is presented in Table 2. The commonest ADRs reported by organ systems were neuropsychiatric disorders (29.8%). This was followed by diseases of the skin and appendages (17.1%), generalised/systemic (14.1%), gastrointestinal (12.4%), and musculoskeletal system (6.7%). Peripheral nervous system disorders and anaemia were reported in 4.3% and 3.1% respectively.

Determinants of adverse drug reactions

The determinants of neuropsychiatric disorders, anaemia, and those of the skin and appendages are reported in Table 3.

Factors associated with Neuropsychiatric disorders : The unadjusted logistic model showed that sex, age, NRTI regimen, and NNRTI regimen were the significant factors related to neuropsychiatric disorders. Specifically, being female, [unadjusted reporting odds ratio (UOR) = 1.45; 95% CI: (1.24–1.69); p-value: <0.001], being older than 15 years [16–35 years: UOR = 4.97; 95% CI: (2.68–9.22); p-value: <0.001; 36–50 years: UOR = 3.29; 95% CI: (1.77–6.14); p-value: <0.001; > 50 years: UOR = 4.50; 95% CI: (2.33–8.69); p-value: <0.001;] compared with those ≤ 15 years, use of Tenofovir: UOR = 5.74; 95% CI: (4.46–7.38); p-value: <0.001or Zidovudine based regimens: UOR = 1.35; 95% CI: (1.02–1.72); p-value: 0.03], use of Efavirenz regimens [UOR = 6.77; 95% CI: (5.81–7.89); p-value: <0.001], increased the odds of neuropsychiatric disorders.

In the adjusted model, the use of Efavirenz remained significant [AOR = 5.58; 95% CI: (4.41–7.05); p-value: <0.001]. Older age remained a significant factor. 16–35 years [AOR = 3.34; 95% CI: (1.72–6.48); p-value: <0.001, [36–50 years: AOR = 2.32; 95% CI: (1.19–4.52); p-value: 0.014; > 50 years AOR = 2.77; 95% CI: (1.36–5.63); p-value: 0.005] and female sex[AOR = 1.43; 95% CI: (1.20–1.71); p-value: <0.001].

Factors associated with anaemia

In the unadjusted logistic regression model, age group, cotrimoxazole use, and ART regimens were associated with anaemia. Precisely, the use of a Zidovudine-based regimen, [(UOR) = 32.42; 95% confidence interval CI: (8.01–131.18); p-value: <0.001], was associated with increased odds of anaemia. Conversely, being older than 15 years [16–35 years: UOR = 0.42; 95% CI: (0.22–0.82); p-value: 0.01; 36–50 years: UOR = 0.34; 95% CI: (0.17– 0.68); p-value: <0.001; > 50 years: UOR = 0.38; 95% CI: (0.17–0.68); p-value: 0.02;], and use of Efavirenz regimens [UOR = 0.08; 95% CI: (0.05–0.14); p-value: <0.001], were protective of anaemia. Similarly, concomitant cotrimoxazole use was protective of anaemia by 96% [UOR = 0.51; 95% CI: (0.38–0.68); p-value: <0.001].

In the adjusted model, Zidovudine use, Efavirenz use, and concomitant Cotrimoxazole use remained significantly associated with anaemia. Remarkably, the use of Zidovudine remained significant, increasing the odds of anaemia by over 32 times [AOR = 32.56; 95% CI: (4.41 − 7.05); p-value: <0.001] after adjusting for other factors. Cotrimoxazole remained significantly protective of anaemia [AOR = 0.58; 95% CI: (0.42– 0.79); p-value: 0.001].

Factors associated with skin and appendage disorders

In the unadjusted logistic model, age, NRTI regimen, and NNRTI regimen were significantly associated with skin and appendage disorders. Being older than 15 years was [16–35 years: UOR = 0.3; 95% CI: (0.20–0.47); p-value: <0.001; 36–50 years: UOR = 0.4; 95% CI: (0.26–0.63); p-value: <0.001; < 50 years: UOR = 0.33; 95% CI: (0.20–0.55); p-value: <0.001] was protective of skin and appendages disorders. The use of Tenofovir [UOR = 0.61; 95% CI: (0.45–0.78); p-value: <0.001] and Efavirenz regimens [UOR = 0.31; 95% CI: (0.26–0.37); p-value: <0.001] were protective against skin disorders. Conversely, Zidovudine use was associated with increased odds of skin and appendage disorder: [UOR = 1.45; 95% CI: (1.13–1.85); p-value: 0.003]. Tenofovir use was protective of skin and appendage disorders. In the adjusted model, age and ART regimen remained significant. Use of Tenofovir [AOR = 1.65; 95% CI: (1.20–2.30); p-value: 0.002], and Zidovudine [AOR = 1.44; 95% CI: (1.13–1.85); p-value: 0.003] were associated with higher odds of skin disorders compared with stavudine use.

Prevalence and types of serious ADRs (SADRs)

The prevalence and types of Serious ADRs are displayed in Fig. 3. SevereSerious ADRs were reported by 2.5% of the patients, of which 71.4% were female, 66.7% were aged 16 35%, 67% were on a Zidovudine-based regimen, and 52% were on Cotrimoxazole use. Life-threatening ADRs (57.14%) and prolonged hospitalisation (35.71%) were the most common severe ADRs reported.

Determinants and outcomes of serious ADRs

Factors associated with severe ADRs are shown in Table 4. The unadjusted logistic model showed that the NNRTI regimen and concomitant cotrimoxazole use were the significant factors associated with reporting severe ADRs. Specifically, the use of Efavirenz [unadjusted odd’s ratio (UOR) = 0.3; 95% CI: (0.17– 0.51); p-value: <0.001] and contaminant cotrimoxazole use were protective of severe ADRs [unadjusted odd’s ratio (UOR) = 0.52; 95% confidence interval CI: (0.34–0.81); p-value: <0.001] were increased the odds of neuropsychiatric disorders.

The adjusted model shows factors associated with severe ADRs in Table 4. NNRTI regimen and concomitant cotrimoxazole use remained significant. Remarkably, the use of Efavirenz remained substantial, decreasing the odds of severe ADRs by over 221% [AOR = 0.311; 95% CI: (0.18 − 0.53); p-value: <0.001]. Similarly, concomitant cotrimoxazole use remained a significant factor, as in the unadjusted model, being protective against serious ADRs [AOR = 0.57; 95% CI: (0.37–0.89); p-value: 0.003].

Discussion

Surveillance of adverse events associated with antiretroviral therapy was declared a priority indicator by WHO as one of 40 key indicators that measure the health system’s response to HIV [30]. Surveillance of ART-associated ADRs is critical for documenting the clinical significance of ART toxicity and its effects on patient outcomes and care retention. In this study, we assessed the patterns, determinants, and outcomes of ADRs among patients on ARVs in Nigeria using a retrospective review of national pharmacovigilance data. Using national pharmacovigilance data enabled us to identify population-level patterns and determinants of ADRs associated with antiretroviral therapy. Previous studies in Nigeria have been limited to some facilities within the country [11, 31]. Most ADR reports in this study were from female patients, consistent with studies from Nigeria, South Africa, and Kenya, which reported prevalences of 64% to 94% among females [11, 32, 33]. A possible explanation may be the higher prevalence of HIV among females in sub-Saharan Africa, particularly in Nigeria; thus, more females are on ART compared to males. Females may also be more likely to get tested for HIV compared to males [34], and also make up a greater proportion of patients in Nigeria [35]. Another reason may be that women are more likely to experience ADRs due to higher plasma ARV concentrations and a smaller body mass than men. [34–38].

The mean age of patients with ADRs was 34.7 ± 11 years. The majority of patients were within the 16–50 age bracket. Agu and Oparah [8] also reported a mean age of 35.3 years among their study participants (shed some light on their study, but briefly). This presents a concern, as young people within their productive ages have comorbidities and toxicities due to antiretroviral therapy.

Multiple ADRs were reported by 49.9% of patients. Multiple ADRs may increase the risk of comorbidities, leading to non-adherence, an increased risk of drug resistance, and virologic failure [14, 39]. Other possible hazards include decreased quality of life, increased healthcare costs, and an increased risk of polypharmacy [40, 41].

Neuropsychiatric disorders and skin disorders were the most common groups of ADRs reported. This was similar to a study by [31] Abah et al., who reported the occurrence of neuropsychiatric disorders in 29% of the observed cohort in Nigeria, but lower than that reported by Mugusi et al. [42] In Tanzania. However, this contrasts with studies by Masenyetse et al. and Bassi et al., which did not report neuropsychiatric ADRs among patients [17, 20]. Because the data for our analysis is nationally representative, the National Pharmacovigilance Centre might have received reports from ART centres that have not been published in the literature.

Multivariate logistic models showed significant factors associated with neuropsychiatric disorders: older age, being female, and using Efavirenz-based therapy after adjusting for confounders. Studies from Nigeria and other parts of the world have also reported similar results [31, 41, 42]. Being on Efavirenz-based treatment and being a female increased the odds of neuropsychiatric disorders. As PLHIV already experience higher levels of neuropsychological illnesses than the general population [43], neuropsychiatric ADRs further complicate the disease and therapy. Special precautions should be taken when placing adult females or clients with a history of psychological illnesses on Efavirenz-based ART regimens. In addition, our study showed that Tenofovir and Zidovudine were associated with neuropsychiatric disorders. It is unclear whether Tenofovir and Zidovudine independently mediated the neuropsychiatric disorders or whether they synergised with Efavirenz. This finding is similar to a report by Margalida et al., who found that Tenofovir influenced the neuropsychiatric properties of Efavirenz [44]. Tenofovir and Zidovudine are used in other regimens; thus, clinicians should also screen for neuropsychiatric disorders in patients on these regimens.

Nevirapine-based therapy and being younger than 15 years were significant factors associated with skin and appendage disorders. This is similar to the findings of other studies [45, 46]. This holds important implications, as Nevirapine is prescribed for children born to HIV-positive mothers as part of the Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission (PMTCT) guidelines [4]. Clinicians, nurses, and mothers should be alert to skin disorders in children receiving NVP-based therapy, as these may indicate underlying hypersensitivity.

In our study, Zidovudine was strongly associated with anaemia. While Efavirenz-based and cotrimoxazole therapy protected against anaemia and reduced the odds of developing anaemia. Cotrimoxazole therapy reduces the risk of opportunistic infections, thereby reducing the risk of anaemia associated with them. Zidovudine has been associated with anaemia in previous studies [7, 9, 47]. The adjusted OR for developing anaemia with zidovudine therapy reported in this study is slightly higher than those reported in previous studies, which reported odds ratios of 22 and 29.3, respectively [9, 48]. This is strong evidence of an association between Zidovudine and anaemia in patients on ART. Thus, clinicians, pharmacists, and healthcare workers involved in ART/ARV care should consider supplementing iron intake as part of routine care for those on Zidovudine-based therapy. In addition, HIV/AIDS programme managers and funders should consider providing haematinics as part of HIV programmes and projects in Nigeria.

Severe ADRs were reported in this study, although rare. On multivariate analysis, Efavirenz-based therapy and cotrimoxazole were protective of severe ADRs. This is also particularly important programmatically, as severe ADRs could be life-threatening or fatal. Cotrimoxazole preventive therapy may reduce the risk of opportunistic infections and severe ADRs, indicating an additional benefit of the drug.

About three-quarters of ADR outcomes among patients were unknown at the time of this study. ADR reporting in Nigeria uses a spontaneous/passive reporting system, with no follow-up to determine the eventual outcomes of unknown ADRs. An earlier study in Nigeria reported similar findings [8]., where 66.8% of patients who reported ADRs had unknown outcomes.

The main strength of our study is the use of nationally representative data, which has not been previously utilised in the investigations of ART-associated ADRs. This study also examined sociodemographic and ART regimen factors related to different ADRs. The use of national pharmacovigilance data also improved the study’s generalizability. Our study is not without limitations. First, the prevalence was not estimated, as the data were obtained through passive surveillance; thus, some ADRs among PLHIV may not have been reported. However, the large sample size enabled the inclusion of different types of ADRs in the study. Secondly, as the data were obtained from a passive surveillance system, many severe ADRs may have been under-reported. The study recommends further research on active surveillance for ART-associated ADRs.

Conclusion

This study assessed the determinants and outcomes of ADRs among HIV-positive individuals on highly active ART in Nigeria. Neuropsychiatric disorders were the most commonly reported ADRs; females and individuals aged 15 and above are commonly affected. The use of Cotrimazole and Efavirenz-based regimen protects against anaemia. The clinical guidelines on ART may need to be reviewed by clinicians and stakeholders in HIV management to ensure appropriate treatment for patient safety. Further studies on the active surveillance of ADRs associated with Tenofovir-based regimens are recommended.

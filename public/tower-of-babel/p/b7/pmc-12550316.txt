Role of timp2 in Ctenopharyngodon idellus muscle collagen deposition and the regulatory mechanism by Cu2+
Authors: Yijing Lin, Peng Chen, Mingzhu Pan, Gaochan Qin, Zhenhua Wu, Weidan Jiang, Dong Han, Kangsen Mai, Wenbing Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCollagen serves as one of determinants of fish muscle quality. Timp2 is a key enzyme in collagen metabolism. Its role in grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idellus) and related nutritional regulation mechanism are unknown. Given the important role of copper as a micronutrient in collagen biosynthesis, it was hypothesized that Cu2+ up-regulates timp2 expression through the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway, thus modulating grass carp collagen content. In the present study, this hypothesis validation was performed through parallel experiments using the TGF-β/Smads inhibitor SB431542 and timp2-specific siRNA (sitimp2) in cultured fibroblasts. Results showed that both sitimp2 and SB431542 treatment decreased the collagen content (to 90.2 % ± 0.52 % and 61.8 % ± 1.59 % of control, respectively), down-regulated timp2 expression (by 53.8 % ± 1.30 % and 11.3 % ± 1.91 % relative to control) and up-regulated mmp2 expression (by 33.1 % ± 9.70 % and 10.9 % ± 2.51 % relative to control). Additionally, SB431542 inhibited the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway (P < 0.05). Coversely, Cu2+ supplementation significantly increased the collagen content, up-regulated timp2 expression and enhanced TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway activity, while simultaneously suppressing mmp2 expression (P < 0.05). Futhermore, after sitimp2 or SB431542 treatment, 10 μM Cu2+ reversed the changes in the relevant indicators induced by both sitimp2 and SB431542 treatment (P < 0.05). These results confirmed the hypothesis that Cu2+ enhanced collagen deposition through TGFβ/Smads-mediated up-regulation of timp2. The present study provided novel mechanistic insights into copper-dependent collagen regulation and suggested that dietary copper supplementation could be a practical nutritional strategy to improve muscle texture and product quality in farmed grass carp.Keywords: Grass carp, Muscle quality, Fibroblast, TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway

Introduction

Muscle is the major edible part of fish, and its quality directly determines the degree of acceptance by consumers, while the hardness trait is one of the key index to measure muscle quality (Fu et al., 2020). The muscle hardness of fish is mainly determined by the muscle nutrients and structural substances. The main nutrients in muscle include protein, lipid and carbohydrates. Studies have shown that a higher protein content can improve muscle texture, while a higher lipid content might impair the structural integrity of muscle, thereby reducing muscle hardness (Dong et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024). The structural substances related to hardness traits mainly include muscle fibers and connective tissues (Ali et al., 2019). Muscle fibers are composed of myotubes that are derived from myoblasts. The formation and maturation of myotubes are regulated by the muscle-specific genes, which are controlled by a variety of myogenic regulatory factors. These factors regulate muscle proliferation and differentiation (Liu et al., 2020).

Collagen, mainly secreted by fibroblasts, is dynamically regulated by synthesis (regulated by enzymes like prolyl hydroxylase, Phd), and degradation where matrix metalloproteinases (Mmps) and their inhibitors (tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases, Timps) play critical roles (Ni et al., 2015; Wen et al., 2023). Recent evidence suggested timp2 as a pivotal gene in regulating collagen degradation (Lin et al., 2022; Zhong et al., 2019), yet its functional role in fish muscle and upstream regulators are poorly explored.

Notably, the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway, a conserved regulator of collagen, can promote collagen synthesis and reduce its degradation (Leask & Abraham, 2004). Previous studies in human dermal fibroblasts have suggested that inhibition of the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway could lead to the decrease in collagen type I alpha 1 chain (col1a1), collagen type I alpha 2 chain (col1a2), timps and the increase in mmps (Zhu & Dong, 2023). In the study of aquatic animals, it was found that glutamine could increase the expressions of type I collagen genes, and this effect may be related to TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway in grass carp (Ma et al., 2020).

Grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella), a highly productive freshwater fish, is valued for its taste and nutritional benefits. However, the increase in breeding density and extensive utilization of artificial compound feed have led to a decline in the flavor and muscle quality of cultured grass carp (Sun et al., 2017). A series of studies have been conducted to address these issues, and nutrition has been identified as one of the proven effective approaches (Gan et al., 2014). Copper (Cu), an essential trace element, shows promise in regulating muscle collagen. Recently, dietary Cu2+ has been proven to increase the expression of TGFβ signaling pathway-related proteins (TGFβ1, p-Smad2, Smad3 and Smad4) and the content of collagen in grass carp muscle (Ma et al., 2023).

Based on the above studies, it is hypothesized that Cu2+ may up-regulate timp2 expression by activating TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway and ultimately increasing collagen content and muscle hardness in grass carp. Prior finding has confirmed that Cu2+ could promote collagen synthesis in grass carp muscle through the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway (Ma et al., 2023). However, a critical knowledge gap remains: the potential role of Cu2+ in regulating collagen degradation, specifically whether Cu2+ targets the collagen degradation-inhibiting gene timp2 (to suppress collagen breakdown) and whether this regulatory effect is also mediated by the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway, has never been investigated. This gap hinders a comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms by which Cu2+ modulates grass carp muscle collagen content and thus muscle quality.

Hence, the present study aimed to explore the role of timp2 in collagen metabolism of grass carp and the potential regulatory effects of Cu2+ on this process. Grass carp muscle fibroblasts were used as a model, which allows for precise manipulation of signaling pathways and gene expression in a controlled environment. The study mainly included three objectives: (1) clarifying the role of timp2 in muscle collagen metabolism in grass carp; (2) verifying whether Cu2+ regulate the collagen of grass carp muscle through timp2, and (3) investigating the regulatory mechanism of Cu2+ on timp2, specifically whether this regulation is mediated by the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway.

Materials and methods

Ethical statements

All care and handling of grass carp in the present study were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Ocean University of China (Approval number: OUC-IACUC-20220620-008-1).

Animals

Experimental grass carp, measuring 15 to 18 cm, were obtained from a commercial farm in Changsha, Hunan province, China. They were placed in the plastic bucket (70.5 cm × 51 cm × 43 cm) with a water-recycling system at 28 °C for a week to acclimate to the laboratory environment. The water was aerated for 24 h before formal experiment. During the period, the dissolved oxygen was higher than 6.0 mg/L, and the pH was 7.0–7.8. All fish were fasted prior to sampling. For subsequent sampling procedures, individuals were randomly selected using a computer-generated random number sequence to ensure that the subsequent isolation of muscle fibroblasts is not affected by non-random selection biases, thereby enhancing the reliability of experimental results.

Cloning of timp2

RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis

Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent (Takara, Tokyo, Japan). Nanodrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was used to determine the concentration of total RNA, and then the total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA by the PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (RR047A, Takara, Tokyo, Japan).

PCR amplification and cloning

Primers were designed based on the timp2 coding sequence (CDS) of grass carp available in NCBI, and the cDNA was amplified by PCR to obtain the full length of CDS of timp2 (Table S1). The PCR products were purified using 1.0 % agarose gel and then ligated into pEASY-T1 vector (TransGen Biotech, Beijing, China). The ligated products were subsequently sent to Sangon Biotech (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) for sequencing.

Sequence analysis of timp2

DNAMAN software was used to analyze the gene sequences. The amino acid sequence was derived from the cDNA sequence, and the domain of TIMP2 was predicted online on the SMART website (http://smart.embl-heidelberg.de/).

Timp2 sequences from species with high similarity to grass carp were retrieved from the NCBI BLAST database (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi). Amino acid sequence alignment was performed using DNAMAN, and a phylogenetic tree was constructed with MEGA 11.0 software. Furthermore, the tertiary structure model of grass carp Timp2 was predicted using the SWISS-MODEL online website (http://swissmodel.expasy.org).

Culture and identification of grass carp muscle fibroblasts

Fibroblasts culture

Grass carp were anesthetized using eugenol (Purity 99 %, Maclin, China) diluted at a ratio of 1:10, 000 with water. Blood was then carefully drawn from the caudal vertebrae using a medical-grade syringe. Dorsal muscle tissue was harvested aseptically and rinsed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, Biosharp, Hefei, China) containing antibiotics (Penicillin-Streptomycin, 100 U/mL, HyClone, South Logan, UT, USA) to remove impurities, including blood cells. The tissue was digested with trypsin (Hyclone, South Logan, UT, USA) for 5 min, followed by treatment with type II collagenase (Solarbio, Beijing, China) for 20 min. The digested muscle tissue was cut into approximately 1 mm3 pieces and placed into a 25 cm2 cell culture flask. The flask was inverted overnight, and 3 mL of complete culture medium was added the following day. The medium was refreshed every two days, and cell migration was monitored. For fibroblasts subculture, cells were digested with trypsin and passaged at a ratio of 1:2 or 1:3, depending on cell density. The complete culture medium consisted of Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium/Nutrient Mixture F-12 (DMEM/F12, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) supplemented with 15 % fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA), 10 ng/mL basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA), 10 ng/mL insulin-like growth factor (IGF, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA), and antibiotics. Cells from passage 5–10 were selected for the present study.

Identification of the cell type

The expression levels of paired box 7 (pax-7), desmin and the transcription factor 4 (tcf-4) were detected using the reverse-transcriptase PCR (RT-PCR) method. Total RNA was extracted, and these genes were amplified with specific primers and then separated via 1.0 % agarose gel electrophoresis. Two technical replicates per gene were used. The primers pairs used for RT-PCR are listed in Table S1.

Experimental treatments of fibroblasts

All treatments were conducted using passage 5–10 grass carp muscle fibroblasts, with three biological replicates and two technical replicates unless otherwise specified.

The timp2 interfering

Three siRNA duplexes targeting timp2 (siRNA-186, siRNA-211, and siRNA-332) were designed and synthesized, with sitimp2-NC serving as the negative control. These siRNA duplexes were synthesized by Sangon (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China), and their sequences are listed in Table S2. The control group contained no siRNA, instead using an equivalent amount of PBS.

Fibroblasts were plated evenly into six-well plates and subsequently incubated at 28 °C. When the cell confluence reached 70 % - 90 %, siRNA transfection was performed using the Hieff Trans Universal Transfection Reagent (Yeasn Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), following the manufacturer's instructions for dosage and method. After 48 h, cells were collected, and the expression of timp2 gene was detected by real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR). A sample was considered suitable for further experiments if the timp2 expression level was reduced to less than 50 % of that in the negative control group. Based on the results, cells in the control group, sitimp2-NC group and siRNA-186 group were collected for subsequent collagen measurement or RT-qPCR analysis.

Cu2+ treatment

Cell viability assay for Cu2+ concentration optimization

Based on the results reported by Arisawa et al. (1996) and Trivedy et al. (2001), where the optimal concentrations of Cu2+ for promoting collagen deposition were identified as 10 μM and 50 μM, respectively, in the present study, fibroblasts were treated with Cu2+ at concentrations of 0, 5, 10, 50, 100, 500 and 1000 μM. Cells were seeded into 96-well plates and exposed to different concentration gradients of CuSO4 for 24 h. Subsequently, cell viability was measured using the CCK-8 (C0038, Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and the absorbance at 450 nm was measured using an ultraviolet spectrophotometer. Six replicate wells per Cu2+ concentration were used. Based on the results, Cu2+ concentrations of 0, 5, 10, and 100 μM were selected for subsequent experiments.

Detection of intracellular copper concentration by inductively coupled plasma-optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES)

Cells treated with 0, 5, 10, 100 μM Cu2+ were collected by centrifugation at 1200 rpm for 5 min and washed with PBS for ICP-OES. The sample was dissolved in concentrated nitric acid and subsequently diluted to a specified volume with ultrapure water. Finally, the concentration of copper was quantified by ICP-OES (VISTA-MPX, Varian, USA).

Cu2+ monotreatment for downstream analysis

Fibroblasts were treated with four different concentrations of CuSO4 (0, 5, 10 and 100 μM) and collected after 24 h, followed by sample collection for collagen measurement, RT-qPCR, or Western blot analysis.

Incubation with Cu2+ after sitimp2 interference

The experiment comprised four groups: control group, Cu2+ group (10 μM), sitimp2 group and sitimp2 + Cu2+ group. The sitimp2 + Cu2+ group was first treated with siRNA-186 for 48 h, followed by a media change to one containing 10 μM Cu2+. Cells were collected after 24 h for collagen measurement or RT-qPCR analysis.

SB431542 inhibitor treatment

Cells were treated with SB431542 at concentrations of 5, 10 and 20 μM, respectively. A DMSO-treated group served as the negative control group, while a complete medium was positive control group. The expressions of Smad2 and Smad3 and their phosphorylation levels were measured after 30 min exposure to screen for the optimal SB431542 concentration. Based on the results, cells in the control group, DMSO-treated group and 10 μM SB431542 group were collected for subsequent RT-qPCR and western blot analysis.

Incubation with Cu2+ after SB431542 treatment

Similarly, four groups were established: control group, Cu2+ group (10 μM), SB431542 group and SB431542 + Cu2+ group. The SB431542 + Cu2+ group was pre-treated with SB431542 for 30 min, followed by a change of media containing 10 μM Cu2+. Cells were collected after 24 h for downstream analysis. Sirius Red staining was analyzed in six wells per group, repeated in three independent experiments.

Sample analysis

Total collagen measurement

The collagen content assay was modified according to Tullberg-Reinert et al. (1999) (Tullberg-Reinert & Jundt, 1999). The procedure was as follows: cells were seeded into 24-well plates, treated, and washed with PBS. Subsequently, 1 mL 0.1 % Sirius Red (Direct Red 80, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) was added for staining, which lasted 2 h. After staining, cells were washed with 0.1 N HCl to remove unbound dye, and the collagen staining was observed and photographed under a microscope. Finally, collagen-binding dye was eluted with 0.1 N NaOH for 30 min, and absorbance values were read at 540 nm. Six replicate wells per treatment group were used.

Real-time quantitative PCR

The relative expression of genes in grass carp were detected by RT-qPCR (Quant Studio 5, Applied Biosystems, USA) and counted by the method of 2−ΔΔCt based on β-actin and gapdh as internal parameter. The primers for qPCR are presented in Table S1. Each gene expression analysis included three biological replicates and two technical replicates.

Western blot

Total protein was extracted from cells using radioimmunoprecipitation lysis assay buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China) containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentration was determined using Bicinchoninic Acid Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Proteins were separated by sodium lauryl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred onto PVDF membranes. After blocking for 1 h at room temperature, membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, followed by incubation with HRP-labeled goat anti-Rabbit IgG (1:5000, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 1 h. Bands were developed using ECL (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software. Primary antibodies used were: Smad2 (1:1000), Phospho-Smad2 (Ser250) (1:1000), Smad3 (1:1000), Phospho-Smad3 (Ser423/Ser425) (1:1000), Col1a (1:1000), and Gapdh (1:5000). The Col1a antibody was sourced from Wanlei Biotechnology (Shenyang, China), while others were from MedChemExpress (Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA). Three replicates per treatment group were used.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard error of mean (SEM) with one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) by applying IBM SPSS Statistics 26.0. P < 0.05 was considered as the significant difference.

Results

Identification of timp2 in grass carp

The open reading frame (ORF) of timp2 is 657 bp and encodes 218 amino acids. The molecular weight is estimated to be 24.24 kDa with a theoretical isoelectric point of 6.45. Amino acid domain predictions revealed two domains within Timp2: a signal peptide encoded by amino acids 1–24 and an NTR domain encoded by amino acids 25–201 (Fig. 1a). Grass carp Timp2 shows high sequence similarity with blunt snout bream (Megalobrama amblycephala) at 90.00 %, Kanglang white minnow (Anabarilius graham) at 89.55 %, and zebrafish at 83.18 %. Lower similarties are observed with house mouse (Mus musculus) and human (Homo sapiens) at 58.11 % and 57.21 %. A phylogenetic tree confirmed that grass carp Timp2 clustered with other fish species, distinct from the mammalian branches that include human and mouse (Fig. 1c). The three-dimensional structure of the Timp2 protein in grass carp, predicted from its amino acid sequence, is displayed in Fig. 1d.

Knockdown of timp2 changed the content of collagen and the expressions of collagen degradation-related genes

To screen the most effective siRNA duplexes, three timp2-targeting siRNA and sitimp2-NC duplexes were transfected into fibroblasts, and timp2 expression was measured by qPCR. Compared to the control group, sitimp2-NC had no significant effect on timp2 (P > 0.05), while the other three siRNA duplexes significantly decreased the expression of timp2 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3c). SiRNA-186 showed the highest efficacy with over 50 % knockdown and was chosen for further experiments.

Cu2+ reversed the changes in TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway activity, collagen content and degradation-related genes expression caused by SB431542

Consistent with the above results, Cu2+ significantly increased the content of total collagen, the protein levels of Smad2, Smad3, p-Smad2, p-Smad3, and the mRNA expressions of col1a1, col1a2, col5a1 and timp2, as well as decreased the expression of mmp2 (P < 0.05); SB431542 significantly decreased the content of total collagen, the protein levels of p-Smad2, p-Smad3, and the mRNA expressions of col1a1, col1a2, col5a1 and timp2, and enhanced the expression of mmp2 (P < 0.05), but had no effect on Smad2 or Smad3 (P > 0.05). Furthermore, compared with SB431542 treatment group, Cu2+ significantly reversed the decrease of total collagen content, p-Smad2 and p-Smad3 protein levels, and col1a1, col1a2, col5a1 and timp2 expressions. Besides, the expression of mmp2 caused by SB431542 treatment was significantly reversed by Cu2+.

Discussion

Effects of timp2 on collagen in grass carp

Collagen is the major component of connective tissue. Previous studies have shown that the quantity and strength of connective tissue determine the hardness of muscle (Femeena, & Dr. Saleena, M., 1996). In addition, in the study of grass carp, it was found that the increase in muscle hardness may be related to the elevated collagen content and synthesis, as well as the reduced collagen degradation (Dong et al., 2022). All these findings highlight the importance of collagen and its metabolism in the study of muscle hardness.

Collagen is secreted by fibroblasts, so in this study, we isolated and cultured fibroblasts from grass carp muscle. After 30 generations, the cells still maintained a stable state. Furthermore, pax-7 and desmin are considered as the myoblast cells-specific genes, while tcf-4 is fibroblast-specific gene (Gao et al., 2019). In the present study, no expression of pax-7 and desmin was detected, while tcf-4 was highly expressed, which demonstrated that the cells cultured in this investigation were indeed fibroblasts.

Up to now, 27 distinct types of collagen have been identified in fish, with type I and V collagen being the primary forms (Mizuta et al., 2012). Among them, type I collagen is mainly composed of two α1 and one α2 chains, which are encoded by col1a1 and col1a2 genes, respectively (Yu et al., 2014). Type V collagen exists in three assembly forms, all of which contain α1 chain encoded by col5a1 (Shoulders & Raines, 2009). And the collagen deposition mainly depends on its synthesis and degradation levels. The synthesis process consists of two parts: one is conventional transcription and translation process, the other is post-translational modification (Trackman, 2005). Within the cytoplasm, a portion of the lysine and proline residues present on the pre-α chains which were synthesized through transcription and translation, undergo hydroxylation mediated by lysyl hydroxylase (Lh) and Phd enzymes, converting them into hydroxylysine and hydroxyproline, respectively. Subsequently, three pre-α chains self-assemble into a triple-helical structure, giving rise to procollagen. During exocytosis, procollagen is released from the cell and subsequently processed by procollagen C-protease and N-protease enzymes, resulting in the formation of collagen molecules. Finally, the collagen fibers were formed by covalent cross-linking with lysyl oxidase (Lox) and deposited in the cell layer (Shi et al., 2019). Increased collagen synthesis facilitates collagen deposition, thereby enhancing muscle hardness. At the same time, collagen degradation is another important aspect that affects collagen deposition.

Degradation primarily occurs due to the activity of Mmps. Besides, Timps are specific endogenous inhibitors of Mmps. They can bind non-covalently to inhibit the activity of Mmps (Zhu et al., 2015). When the degradation capacity of collagen is lower than the synthesis capacity, collagen accumulates, leading to increased muscle hardness. Contrarily, the hardness can decrease. Previous studies have concentrated more on the collagen synthesis and less on degradation, so this study mainly focused on the degradation-related gene timps. Four types of timps have been discovered in mammals, namely timp1–4, respectively (Brew & Nagase, 2010). Among them, Timp2 is the specific inhibitor of Mmp2 which is an enzyme involved in the degradation of type I and type V collagen (Stetler-Stevenson & Gavil, 2014). Moreover, elevated timp2 level was shown to be a critical factor in reducing mmp2 and collagen degradation in silver pomfret (Pampus argenteus) (Shi et al., 2022). In the present study, the timp2 gene of grass carp was cloned and the amino acid sequence analysis was carried out. We found that this sequence shared over 50 % homology with the Timp2 amino acid sequences of mammals and exhibited high similarity to timp2 sequences from other fish species. Structure determines function, so the biological function of timp2 in grass carp may be similar to that in other fish, namely, regulating the degradation of collagen to affect collagen content. To corroborate the above hypothesis, we knocked down the timp2 gene in grass carp fibroblasts. Sirius red staining and qPCR results exhibited that with the knockdown of timp2, col1a1 and col1a2 expressions were significantly down-regulated, as well as the total collagen content. It follows that, above results confirmed our hypothesis that timp2 could affect the collagen content and be a key gene for collagen metabolism in grass carp. However, the knockdown of timp2 had no significant effect on the expression of col5a1, we speculated that it may be regulated by other members among the timps family, which needs further exploration.

Cu2+ increased collagen content by up-regulating the expression of timp2

As the vertebrates, fish have the strong plasticity in their muscles which are susceptible to the influence of abiotic factors, such as environmental factors (Johnston, 2006), nutrition (Zhao et al., 2018) and exercise intensity (Palstra & Planas, 2011). These factors can affect the structure of muscle fibers and connective tissue (collagen) thereby influencing fish muscle quality. Among them, nutrition is a considerable means. Adequate nutrition, including amino acids, fatty acids, and minerals, provides an indispensable structural foundation for the growth, repair, and remodeling of muscle tissue. For aquatic species such as grass carp, dietary nutrition directly regulates key metabolic pathways associated with muscle development: it not only supports protein synthesis and maintains cell membrane integrity, but also modulates the deposition of structural components such as collagen (Wang et al., 2024). For instance, a recent research on grass carp has documented that appropriate levels (22 % - 26 %) of dietary protein could enhance muscle hardness (Dong et al., 2022). Cu is a vital micronutrient in organisms and plays a crucial role in collagen synthesis, nervous system formation and cellular respiration (Gaetke et al., 2014). As a result, it is reasonable to speculate that Cu2+ may increase collagen content and enhance muscle hardness in grass carp. However, the dose-dependent effects of Cu2+ and its regulatory mechanisms on collagen metabolism in grass carp remain to be further explored.

Based on the above background, the initial step of the present study was to determine the optimal concentration of Cu2+ by evaluating cell viability under various Cu2+ treatments. The observed trend of cell viability, initial increase followed by a decrease as Cu2+ concentrations rose, may be attributed to the dual role of copper as both an essential micronutrient and a potential cytotoxic agent. At lower concentrations (5–10 μM), Cu2+ likely supports cellular proliferation by serving as a cofactor for critical enzymes involved in antioxidant defense (e.g., Cu/Zn- superoxide dismutase) and mitochondrial respiration (e.g., cytochrome c oxidase) (Gaetke et al., 2014). This pro-survival effect aligns with studies reporting that low-dose Cu2+ enhances fibroblast activity and collagen synthesis (Arisawa et al., 1996; Trivedy et al., 2001). Conversely, higher concentrations (≥ 50 μM) likely induce oxidative stress, generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) that damage lipids, proteins, and DNA (Halliwell & Gutteridge, 1984; Lippard, 1999). This mechanistic shift is supported by our data showing significantly reduced cell viability at Cu2+ concentrations of 100–1000 μM, which underscores the importance of dose precision in nutritional interventions targeting muscle quality.

A prior study in grass carp has also demonstrated that dietary copper could promote the expression of collagen synthesis-related genes and the content of collagen in muscle (Ma et al., 2023). However, the effects of Cu2+ on the degradation-related genes remain uncharacterized. Combined with the results that timp2 could affect the content of collagen (Fig. 3), we proposed two scientific questions: first, whether supplementation with an appropriate concentration of Cu2+ could promote the secretion of collagen in fibroblasts, secondly whether Cu2+ increased collagen content through the collagen degradation gene timp2 in grass carp fibroblasts. Based on these questions, we supplemented the complete medium with three concentrations of Cu2+ for fibroblast culture. The results of sirius red staining and qPCR exhibited that the appropriate Cu2+ concentration (10 μM) could up-regulate the expression of timp2, down-regulate the expression of mmp2 and elevate the expressions of col1a1, col1a2 and col5a1, as well as the content of total collagen in fibroblasts. Furthermore, we found that Cu2+ significantly reversed the timp2 knockdown-induced down-regulation of timp2 expression and up-regulation of mmp2 expression. Besides, after culturing fibroblasts with Cu2+, the timp2 knockdown-induced inhibition of col1a1 and col1a2 expression, as well as the reduction in total collagen content, were significantly alleviated. To sum up, the above questions were answered: Cu2+ could promote the secretion of collagen in fibroblasts by up-regulating the expression of timp2.

Furthermore, the stability of collagen relies not only on its intrinsic structure but also on the dynamic regulation of various components within the extracellular matrix (ECM), including non-collagenous glycoproteins, proteoglycans, elastin, and the Mmps/Timps system (Yue, 2014). Therefore, our findings also prompt consideration of how ECM components may contribute to collagen stability under Cu2+ regulation. On one hand, existing studies have shown that lumican and decorin play regulatory roles in collagen fibril assembly and enhance collagen stability (Neame et al., 2000). However, whether Cu2+ modulates the expression of these proteoglycans to stabilize collagen remains an open question. On the other hand, as a known cofactor of Lox, Cu2+ can activate Lox to catalyze cross-linking of collagen and elastin, thereby enhancing their stability (Rucker et al., 1998). Although this study did not measure Lox activity, it is reasonable to hypothesize that Cu2+-driven Lox activation may synergize with Timp2-mediated inhibition of Mmp-dependent degradation to collectively promote collagen deposition. Simultaneously, elastin enhances collagen stability by physically interweaving with collagen fibers. When exposed to mechanical stress, elastin preferentially stretches and absorbs mechanical energy, thereby buffering stress and reducing direct mechanical damage to collagen fibers (Lee et al., 2001). Additionally, Timps can non-covalently bind to Mmps (e.g., MMP2/9), inhibiting their collagen-degrading capacity (Zhu et al., 2015). Cu2+ may stabilize Timps-Mmps complexes, thereby reducing collagen degradation independently of transcriptional regulation. Although the present study focuses on intracellular signaling pathways, these ECM-level regulatory mechanisms provide important insights into the comprehensive role of Cu2+ in collagen metabolism. Future research should integrate ECM component analysis, mechanobiological models, and in vivo microenvironment validation to systematically elucidate the multidimensional mechanisms by which Cu2+ regulates collagen stability.

Cu2+ up-regulated timp2 expression and promoted collagen content by activating TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway

Building on the scientific question in Section 4.2, we further explored how Cu2+ regulates the key gene timp2. TGFβ is an important regulator of connective tissue formation, promoting fibroblast proliferation and acting as a key cytokine in orchestrating collagen metabolism (Lijnen & Petrov, 2002). On the one hand, it can stimulate the expressions of collagen genes at the transcriptional level. On the other hand, it can induce the production of Timps and inhibit the secretion of Mmps (Yao et al., 2012). Previous studies have manifested that the high level of TGFβ could elevate the secretion of Timp1 and down-regulate the Mmp1, which in turn augments collagen content (Profyris et al., 2012). The pathways associated with TGFβ are collectively referred to as TGFβ signaling pathway, with the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway being the most classical. When TGFβ binds to its receptor, Smad2 and Smad3 are activated by phosphorylation and form a ternary complex with Smad4, which then enters the nucleus to regulate downstream genes expressions (Schiller et al., 2004). In the studies of human dermal fibroblasts, it was displayed that the reduction of Mmps induced by poly-L-lactic acid and leucine-rich alpha-2-glycoprotein 1, as well as the increase in Timps and collagen, were reversed by SB431542, a TGFβ receptor-specific inhibitor (Park et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023). Additionally, a previous research has proved that dietary copper promoted the expression of proteins related to the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway in grass carp (Ma et al., 2023). Based on the above background, we proposed the following scientific question: whether Cu2+ up-regulate the expression of timp2 by activating the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway.

SB431542, a small-molecule inhibitor of the TGFβ1 receptor kinase ALK5, can competitively inhibit TGF-β receptor 1, thereby inhibiting the phosphorylation of Smad2 and Smad3 proteins without affecting other members of the TGFβ superfamily (Mordasky Markell et al., 2010). Related studies have also confirmed that SB431542 can effectively reduce the phosphorylation levels of Smad2 and Smad3 (Callahan et al., 2002; Inman et al., 2002). Our results indicated that the phosphorylation levels of Smad2 and Smad3 were significantly reduced in grass carp fibroblasts treated with 10 μM SB431542, suggesting that 10 μM is an effective concentration for inhibition of the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway in grass carp fibroblasts. We continued to measure the expressions of genes related to collagen and its degradation after SB431542 treatment. Results revealed that SB431542 decreased total collagen content and the expressions of col1a1, col1a2, col5a1 and timp2, while increasing mmp2 expression. Meanwhile, the results of this study also suggested that the appropriate Cu2+ significantly promoted the levels of genes and proteins related to the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway (tgfb1, smad2, smad3, smad4, Smad2, Smad3, p-Smad2, p-Smad3), which was similar to the results of previous studies (Ma et al., 2023). Furthermore, the inhibition of the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway, the reduction in collagen content and the decrease in degradation capacity resulting from SB431542 could be reversed by Cu2+. Thus, we addressed the proposed scientific question and drew the following conclusion: Cu2+ up-regulated timp2 expression and inhibited the collagen degradation ability by activating TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway, and then promoted type I collagen and total collagen content. As for type V collagen, we speculated that Cu2+ may activate the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway to influence non-timp2 members of the timps family and then mediate the secretion of type V collagen, which needs the further experimental verification.

Collectively, the present study confirmed that Cu2+ can reverse the abnormal collagen metabolism in grass carp muscle induced by timp2 silencing and TGFβ/Smads pathway inhibition. However, due to constraints in experimental time and resources, direct verification of the pathway mediating the Cu2+ rescue effect has not been completed, which is a major limitation of this study. Nevertheless, the existing data have constructed a sufficient chain of indirect evidence: Cu2+ can activate the TGFβ/Smads pathway and the pathway inhibitor SB431542 can inhibit pathway activity and induce abnormal collagen metabolism. Additionally, supplementary Cu2+ can reverse the abnormalities induced by SB431542. This logic indicates that the Cu2+ rescue effect is closely associated with the TGFβ/Smads pathway, which is consistent with the core conclusion that Cu2+ regulates collagen metabolism through the TGFβ/Smads-timp2 axis. These findings offer preliminary but substantive mechanistic insight into Cu2+ regulation of fish collagen metabolism.

In addition to the pathway verification point above, we acknowledge the limitations of the current study regarding the direct measurement of collagen degradation. The inclusion of specific collagen degradation biomarkers (e.g., C3M, C4M, ICTP, or MMP-mediated cleavage fragments) would provide a more direct and comprehensive assessment of degradation dynamics. Unfortunately, due to constraints in experimental design and sample, quantification of these fragments was not feasible in the present study. Nevertheless, we employed gene expression analysis (timp2/mmp2) and total collagen content measurement (via Sirius red staining) as indicators to assess collagen degradation balance. Notably, the results obtained through these diverse detection methods remained consistently aligned—this consistency further confirms the high reliability of our conclusions regarding collagen metabolic regulation.

The present study revealed that Cu2+ up-regulated timp2 expression by activating the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting collagen degradation and promoting collagen deposition. This mechanistic insight provides an important theoretical basis for improving muscle quality in grass carp through nutritional strategies. However, the present study was conducted solely based on in vivo experiments and has certain limitations. The relevant conclusions still need to be further verified by combining with in vitro experiments. Firstly, the absorption, metabolism, and tissue distribution of copper in living organisms are influenced by its chemical form, diet composition, and physiological status of fish (Lall & Kaushik, 2021), which differ significantly from direct exposure in cell models. Secondly, ultimate muscle quality depends not only on collagen content but also on multiple other attributes, such as lipid deposition, water-holding capacity, and flavor compounds (Listrat et al., 2016), all of which require further evaluation through feeding trials. Therefore, future studies should focus on designing practical diets with graded levels of copper supplementation, systematically assessing their effects on growth performance, muscle texture, and collagen content in grass carp, ultimately providing a more comprehensive scientific basis for the nutritional strategies aimed at improving the muscle quality of grass carp.

Furthermore, the present study primarily focuses on the TGFβ/Smads signaling axis. In the future, utilizing omics technologies such as RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) to comprehensively analyze the genome-wide transcriptional changes after Cu2+ treatment will help identify other potential alternative signaling pathways or novel regulatory factors involved in this process. This will contribute to a more systematic elucidation of the comprehensive regulatory network through which copper ions modulate grass carp muscle quality.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the present study was designed to investigate the effects of divalent copper ions on collagen metabolism in grass carp and the underlying molecular mechanisms, with a specific focus on the role of timp2 gene. The results demonstrated that Cu2+ up-regulated the expression of timp2. This up-regulation occurred through the activation of the TGFβ/Smads signaling pathway. As a consequence, the degradation of collagen was decreased, leading to an increase in the collagen content in grass carp. The results clarified the nutritional metabolism and regulatory mechanism of timp2, a key gene of collagen metabolism, and provided a theoretical basis for improving the muscle quality of farmed grass carp through nutritional regulation. However, further in vivo studies are required to confirm these findings and elucidate the regulatory mechanisms of timp2 and Cu2+ in collagen synthesis under systematacially physiological conditions.

Successful Use of Dupilumab in Atopic Dermatitis‐Like Graft‐Versus‐Host Disease: A Case Report
Authors: Qingjun Liao, Catherine Y Lee, Yunfeng Hu, Shi Wu, Yong He
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

Graft‐versus‐host disease (GVHD) is a systemic immune disease that arises from allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo‐HSCT). Atopic dermatitis (AD)‐like GVHD is a novel form of cutaneous GVHD. The first‐line treatment consists of oral corticosteroids alone or in combination with other immunosuppressants, while second‐line therapy includes methotrexate, ruxolitinib, mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), systemic calcineurin inhibitors, and rituximab [1]. Dupilumab, a monoclonal IL‐4Rα antagonist, has been approved as safe and effective for treating moderate‐to‐severe AD [2, 3]. We present a clinical case of successful dupilumab off‐label treatment in a patient with AD‐like GVHD.

A 22‐year‐old female, with no history of atopy, presented to the Dermatology Department due to generalized erythema with pruritus for 1 month (Figure 1a–d). The patient was diagnosed with acute myeloid leukemia (AML) 2 years ago and underwent an allo‐HSCT from her father as the donor. The patient was on long‐term maintenance therapy with sorafenib (0.8 g/day), dexamethasone (2.25 mg/day), and MMF(1 g/day) post‐surgery. Clinical outcomes were assessed using a Patient‐Oriented Eczema Measure (POEM) score of 9, Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) score of 11, Atopic Dermatitis Control Tool (ADCT) score of 16, and Scoring Index for Atopic Dermatitis (SCORAD) score of 47.5. Laboratory tests revealed elevated levels of peripheral eosinophils (2.31 × 109/L; cut‐off < 0.52 × 10^9), total serum immunoglobulin E (IgE) (2097IU/mL; cut‐off < 100), and interleukin 6 (IL‐6) (23.43 pg/mL; cut‐off < 5.04). Pathological examination confirmed AD‐like GVHD (Figure 2a–d). The administration of dupilumab via subcutaneous injections (600 mg, followed by 300 mg 2 weeks later) in combination with a low dose of dexamethasone (0.375 mg/day) was initiated due to the unsatisfactory efficacy observed with standard treatment options, namely, corticosteroids and MMF. Following the completion of the treatment regimen, the patient became lesion‐free without experiencing any treatment‐related side effects. After 1‐month follow‐up period of complete treatment, significant improvement in pruritus compared to before was reported by the patient along with decreased erythema on trunk and extremities (Figure 1e–h), with progressively decreasing levels of peripheral eosinophils (0.78×10^9/L). The POEM score was 5, DLQI score was 8, ADCT score was 6, and SCORAD score was 16.5.

Histological appearance of abdomen lesions. Panels a–d (hematoxylin and eosin staining, panel a × 50, panel b × 100, panel c × 200, and panel d × 400) show epidermal hyperkeratosis with hyperkeratosis, sponge edema formation between spiny cells, local basal cell liquefaction, and degeneration, scattered apoptosis in the epidermis, inflammatory cell infiltration by lymphocytes and eosinophils in the superficial layer of the dermis, as well as spongy edematous superficial perivascular inflammation consistent with graft‐versus‐host reaction pathological changes.

AD‐like GVHD is clinically characterized by generalized erythema, scaling, and significant pruritus [1]. While the exact pathogenesis of AD‐like GVHD is still unknown, research suggests that higher levels of IgE, helper T cells 1 (Th1), Th2, Th17 cells, regulatory T cells, and peripheral blood eosinophils in certain patients may indicate a type 2 inflammatory response [1, 4]. Therefore, targeting the type 2 inflammatory pathway through IL‐4 and IL‐13 blockade with dupilumab may offer a promising therapeutic approach for AD‐like GVHD [2, 3]. It also could lower serum IgE levels. As a result, the drug would inhibit two crucial Th‐2 pathogenetic pathways for GVHD [5]. Moreover, the literature indicates that it could be a valid therapeutic option for patients with AD‐like GVHD who do not respond well to conventional treatments [4, 5]. The efficacy of dupilumab in AD‐like GVHD has been documented in multiple literature sources [1, 4]. However, further studies are needed to investigate the safety, effectiveness, and generalizability of dupilumab in immunocompromised patients with AD‐like GVHD resulting from HSCT.

Research on a fuzzy programming model and algorithm for berth allocation considering time-varying water depth
Authors: Di Liu, Botang Li, Mengchi Li, Yushun Lei, Pei Yu, Chaofeng Tan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address the complex issue of efficient berth allocation for ships entering the port under conditions of uncertainty, we have adopted a credible fuzzy programming approach. This approach takes into account the significant impact of fluctuating water depths on berth assignments and constructs a linear integer programming model for berth allocation. The primary objective of this model is to minimize the total time cost. Additionally, two heuristic algorithms—the genetic algorithm and the simulated annealing algorithm—have been designed to accommodate the problem’s unique characteristics. The innovation of this paper lies in establishing a new model based on novel scenarios and designing a new genetic algorithm tailored for this problem, with results compared against those from a simulated annealing algorithm. The solution results of several test cases are compared and analyzed using CPLEX. The results verify the feasibility of the proposed algorithms, and an analysis of the variation in confidence levels is conducted based on test cases generated from actual data, demonstrating the applicability of the fuzzy programming model. When the confidence level increases from 0.5 to 1, the objective value rises by 12 units, yet the impact on berth allocation plans remains negligible, indicating the stability of the proposed model.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-27537-1.

Introduction

As a crucial link between the inland area and maritime transport, a port’s importance is undeniable. The expanding maritime transport economy has heightened the need for improved port operational efficiency. A port terminal consists of various zones, with berths crucial for ship docking, departure, and cargo operations. Berth efficiency directly affects port performance1. Thus, creating a prudent and effective berth allocation plan to assign ships optimal berthing positions and times is essential to minimize layovers and boost berth operations, addressing a key port management issue.

The cyclical tides in the ocean significantly impact human life and production. For container ports situated along the coastline, the fluctuation of seawater levels can influence the manner in which port operations are conducted, such as the berthing of large vessels in accordance with tidal directions. With the rapid advancement of shipbuilding technology and the pursuit of economies of scale by shipping companies, the size and carrying capacity of container ships have been continuously increasing. The deeper draft of these ships has made it challenging for existing port berths to meet the required depth2. Considering the draft limitations of vessels, larger cargo ships, which are generally heavier, are constrained to berth only during periods when the water depth is sufficient.

The sheer volume of containers and the complexity of operations within a port mean that handling processes are fraught with uncertainties. These include variables such as the port’s climatic conditions, imperfect data streams from shipping companies, fluctuations in the efficiency of internal resources, or malfunctions in equipment and facilities, all of which undoubtedly complicate port resource planning. Beyond the uncertainty of the handling process, the weight change of ships after cargo operations leads to variability in draft depths, which in turn affects the entry and exit of vessels from the port3. Moreover, these uncertainty factors directly impact subsequent berth allocation plans. In summary, the actual operations are subject to these uncertain influences, making it challenging to ensure that vessels can be efficiently and rationally berthed. Therefore, the implementation of a reliable container terminal berthing plan is a crucial step in enhancing the efficiency of terminal operations.

The berthing allocation problem is a traditional NP-hard challenge, which is difficult to solve within a reasonable time frame using exact algorithms. Furthermore, the problem addressed in this paper is a complex combinatorial optimization problem with multiple constraints. In larger instances, only heuristic algorithms can find the optimal or near-optimal solutions within a feasible time4,5. To better align with real-world scenarios, this study employs genetic algorithms for the solution and compares the results with those from simulated annealing algorithms.

Previous studies on berth scheduling have predominantly focused on discrete water depth variations (e.g., distinguishing between high-tide and low-tide periods), with limited exploration of continuous water depth dynamics in berth allocation. Furthermore, no existing research simultaneously addresses both continuous water depth fluctuations and dynamic ship draft variations. This paper bridges this critical gap, thereby pioneering a novel research direction. In summary, this paper conducts research on the berth allocation problem considering time-varying water depths and uncertainties, aiming to meet the berthing scheduling requirements of port dispatchers with respect to time costs. The goal is to scientifically plan berth allocation to enhance the utilization efficiency of berths. Moreover, in light of the characteristics of the model, this paper develops a genetic algorithm to accommodate the solution of large-scale problems.

The innovative aspects of this paper are as follows: (1) it introduces uncertainty factors into the berth allocation problem considering time-varying water depths; (2) it takes into account actual scenarios of berth allocation, making the model more aligned with real-world conditions; (3) it designs a genetic algorithm that is suitable for addressing this problem. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents related work. Section 3 introduces the background of the problem and describes the components of the model. Section 4 details the algorithm. Section 5 provides numerical experiments, and Sect. 6 summarizes the findings.

Literature review

To enhance the efficiency of port operations, numerous studies on port berth scheduling have been conducted both domestically and internationally, yielding a wealth of scientific research achievements. Drawing on real-world contexts, these studies have proposed a multitude of models and solution approaches for handling complex models, which are of significant importance for improving the efficiency of port operations.

Berth allocation

The Berth Allocation Problem (BAP) refers to the efficient allocation of berths to ships for docking within a certain time frame. It can be categorized into static and dynamic based on the arrival status of the ships. Static BAP assumes all ships are anchored in the waiting area, while dynamic BAP allows for real-time ship arrivals. Additionally, depending on the layout of the terminal, BAP can be further divided into discrete or continuous types6. In discrete BAP, the port’s wharf is divided into a set of distinct-length positions, each of which can accommodate only one ship at a time. Conversely, in the continuous case, ships can dock at any segment of the wharf without overlapping. This paper primarily focuses on dynamic continuous BAP.

Imai et al.7 were the first to study the dynamic discrete BAP and proposed the first mixed-integer programming (MIP) model. Monaco and Sammarra8 further proved the NP-hardness of this problem. Recently, Du et al.9 employed a virtual arrival strategy to address the impact of tides on BAP and ship navigation channels. Ursavas and Zhu10 investigated BAP with uncertain ship arrival and handling times, proposing a dynamic programming approach to solve the problem. Later, Nishi et al.11 improved the model. Cahyono et al.12 comprehensively considered berths and shore-side equipment, establishing a joint dynamic programming model for berth and quay crane allocation. Subsequently, Cahyono et al.13 extended the original problem by incorporating internal trucks and yard cranes. Li et al.14 further studied the berth and quay crane allocation problem with preventive maintenance activities, proposing a two-stage heuristic algorithm for solution.

Since its introduction, the berth allocation problem has garnered significant attention from researchers who have sought to integrate it with real-world applications. Notably, Guo et al.15 have merged berth allocation with liner ship clustering for cooperative container shipping companies, devising a two-phase optimization approach utilizing CPLEX for solving the mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) model. Mao et al.16 have introduced energy models that coordinate ship-port activities, focusing on shore power management and berth allocation to satisfy both electrical and thermal energy demands. Bouzekri et al.17 have explored the integration of loading/unloading operations with berth allocation in the context of bulk cargo ports, proposing an integer programming model to facilitate effective scheduling for mooring and new charters. Korekane et al.18 have proposed a branch-and-bound algorithm for berth operation planning, where decision-making is enhanced by a deep neural network to expedite the search process. Zheng et al.19 have developed an integrated model for bulk cargo ship berth allocation and ballast water management, employing a hybrid heuristic algorithm that combines adaptive genetic algorithms with critical path methods. Martin-Iradi et al.20 have introduced a mixed-integer model for a collaborative ship scheduling and berth allocation problem that accounts for ship speeds between ports, along with an adaptive large neighborhood search algorithm bolstered by local search techniques. Gao et al.21 have crafted a mixed-integer linear programming model for the integrated scheduling of a new type of automated bulk cargo terminal, which includes berth allocation, diversion scheduling, and branch scheduling to minimize the maximum departure time of ships, and have utilized a logic-based Benders decomposition algorithm for enhanced efficiency. Finally, Guo et al.22 have considered the sharing of shore and yard resources in cooperative terminals, proposing a comprehensive model for berth, quay crane, and yard allocation, and have implemented a custom adaptive large neighborhood search with column generation and CPLEX to address the problem.

Tidal factors

Scholars have integrated tidal factors into berth allocation research. Qin et al.23 considered time-varying water depths in tidal river ports but not ship draft changes. Malekahmadi et al.24 proposed an integer programming model for container terminal scheduling with stochastic optimization. Bouzekri et al.25 modeled labor and crane allocation in tidal ports with mathematical programming. Cheimanoff et al.26 applied RVNS and machine learning for berth allocation in tidal bulk terminals. Zheng et al.27 used shift strategies and an adaptive large neighborhood search for rescheduling with tidal constraints. The majority of the literature examines berth allocation issues based on discrete changes in water depth, yet there is a scarcity of research addressing berth allocation problems in the context of continuous water depth variations. This represents one of the primary focuses of this study.

Uncertainty

Uncertainty in berth allocation is addressed by incorporating robust optimization models, such as Xiang et al.28 with a data-driven approach, Agra and Rodrigues29 with a two-stage model, and Chargui et al.30,31 with models for energy variability and sustainability. Wang et al.32 proposed a distributed robust model. However, these methods can enlarge the model scale and cause constraint conflicts. The theory of stochastic programming frequently finds it challenging to satisfy the conditions for the probability distributions of uncertain parameters, and it encounters difficulties in solving problems with complex constraints. Robust optimization, conversely, faces challenges in dealing with sudden and ill-defined uncertainties. Fuzzy programming, however, does not necessitate knowledge of the probability distributions to directly address uncertainties, and when transformed into an equivalent crisp model, the problem size is reduced. Given these considerations, this paper adopts fuzzy programming to deal with uncertainties.

Heuristic algorithm

The berth allocation problem often faces challenges in solving large-scale instances, prompting researchers to design heuristic algorithms or combine them with exact algorithms. For instance, Tang et al.33 studied berth allocation under various disruption scenarios, proposed a mathematical model to assist operators in creating robust berth allocation plans in a more operational manner, and developed a multi-stage heuristic algorithm to address large-scale problems.

Guo et al.34 proposed a mixed-integer model for cooperative multi-port berth allocation with column generation. Dulebenets35 introduced a model for responsive seaport terminal scheduling with a diffusive membrane algorithm. Yu et al.36 optimized berth allocation, crane allocation, and green technology usage with a mixed-integer model and a Nested NSGA II algorithm. Cheimanoff et al.37 addressed integrated scheduling in a fertilizer complex with a mixed-integer linear model and branch-and-bound. Wang et al.38 enhanced container terminal efficiency with a mixed-integer and an adaptive immune algorithm. Correcher et al.39 considered crane travel and setup times with a new MILP and genetic algorithm approach. These studies lack algorithms for time-varying water depth conditions.

In summary, berth allocation is a relatively new area of research, despite attracting many researchers. However, studies on berth allocation considering time-varying water depths and fuzziness are scarce. Therefore, this paper aims to optimize drag berth allocation from the perspective of time-varying water depths and incorporate fuzziness considerations. Although berth allocation is widely researched, few studies address time-varying depths and fuzziness. This paper will optimize drag berth allocation with these factors in mind.

Problem description and modeling

Problem definition

The majority of domestic and international dock shorelines are continuous and straight, beneficial for ship berthing and increased dock utilization. The principle of continuous berth allocation is that ships can berth at any position within the permissible shoreline, with safety ensured by avoiding temporal and spatial overlap between vessels.

The port scheduling process allocates quay cranes based on ship arrival plans and schedules, ensuring timely service. As ship numbers grow, waiting times for berthing increase. The goal is to optimize the sequence, times, and positions for ships to enter and berth.

Tidal fluctuations can cause periodic changes in water depth, necessitating ships to berth within permissible depth ranges. Additionally, changes in a ship’s draft due to loading and unloading must be considered. This paper addresses the berth allocation problem under time-varying water depths, focusing on container terminals with continuous berths. The study examines the needs of ships to berth and depart safely within the duration of safe water depths during their planned arrivals. Model establishment

The problem described in this paper is most closely related to the work of Qin et al.23, but their study did not account for uncertainty. Therefore, Drawing on the literature by Qin et al.23 and considering the fuzziness of parameters40, this paper establishes a credibility-based fuzzy programming model and employs chance-constrained programming methods to create the following credibility-based fuzzy chance-constrained programming model.

Assumptions

The assumptions made in this paper are: (1) The length of each berth can accommodate all considered ships. (2) The berth shoreline is regarded as continuous, with berthing units divided along the dock shoreline. (3) The estimated arrival times of each ship are known. (4) A berth serves at most one ship at a time within the same period.

Model processing

Because Eq. (5) includes a part where the decision variable and the fuzzy variable are multiplied, this leads to nonlinearity in the model, which makes the problem challenging to solve. Hence, it is necessary to convert the fuzzy variable into a definite value through the establishment of credibility. As a result, the following steps are needed to transform the model into an equivalent crisp model:

Solution method

The model constructed in the previous section is an integer linear programming model, which can be directly solved by mature commercial solvers such as CPLEX, LINGO, and GAMS. However, when the problem scale increases, the efficiency of existing optimization solvers is low. In such cases, heuristic algorithms can be used to improve the solution efficiency, in order to obtain a relatively good solution in a shorter amount of time. By referring to references41,42, this paper will primarily use Genetic Algorithms (GA) for solving the model. The following introduces the main components of the Genetic Algorithm. The source codes of this algorithm can be found in http://www.alimirjalili.com/GwO.html and http://www.mathworks.com.au/matlabcentralfleexchange/44974.

Individual encoding operation

Due to the dynamic nature, discreteness, and the controlling characteristics of the constraint conditions in berth allocation, using binary or floating-point encoding would complicate the problem further. Hence, this paper employs natural number encoding where each gene represents the ship number for berthing, with 0 used to separate each berth. The sequence of ships’ berthing is represented from left to right in the encoding. The length of the encoding is the sum of the number of berths and ships minus one. Evaluation of the candidate solution approaches

In Table 3, for the smallest-scale test problem 1, the objective function values obtained using both the Genetic Algorithm and the Simulated Annealing Algorithm are the same as those obtained by the software CPLEX, indicating that the Genetic Algorithm is suitable for small-scale problems. For test problems 2, 3, 4, and 5, as the problem size gradually increases, the objective function values obtained using the Genetic Algorithm are relatively larger compared to those obtained by CPLEX, which shows that for slightly larger-scale problems, the accuracy of the Genetic Algorithm and the Simulated Annealing Algorithm decreases. However, the error between the objective function values obtained by the Genetic Algorithm and those by CPLEX is less than 4%. For the larger-scale test problems 6, 7, and 8, the objective function values obtained using the Genetic Algorithm and the Simulated Annealing Algorithm are smaller compared to those obtained by CPLEX. Although the Genetic Algorithm takes longer to compute, it results in a better objective function value than that obtained by CPLEX. From the above analysis, it can be concluded that the Genetic Algorithm proposed in this paper has a certain level of accuracy.

Regarding the efficiency of the Genetic Algorithm, Table 3 shows that from the comparison of the first three test problems, the software CPLEX runs in less time than the Genetic Algorithm and the Simulated Annealing Algorithm, and the objective function obtained by CPLEX is superior compared to the values obtained by the Genetic Algorithm and the Simulated Annealing Algorithm. However, for test problem 6, the running time of the Genetic Algorithm and the Simulated Annealing Algorithm is less than that of CPLEX, and the objective function values obtained by these algorithms are smaller than those obtained by CPLEX, indicating that the Genetic Algorithm and the Simulated Annealing Algorithm converge more quickly. For test problems 7 and 8, the Genetic Algorithm and the Simulated Annealing Algorithm can find optimal solutions in cases where CPLEX is unable to provide a solution. The above analysis demonstrates that the Genetic Algorithm and the Simulated Annealing Algorithm have good solution efficiency and are suitable for solving large-scale problems.

As shown in Table 3, for test problems 2 to 6, although the objective values obtained by the Genetic Algorithm are larger than those obtained by the Simulated Annealing Algorithm, for test problems 7 and 8, the objective values obtained by the Genetic Algorithm are smaller, and the computation time of the Genetic Algorithm is also less than that of the Simulated Annealing Algorithm. This indicates that the Genetic Algorithm has a higher solution efficiency.

In summary, the fuzzy programming model proposed in this paper is consistent with actual conditions. When parameters are fuzzy, the change in confidence level does not significantly affect the berth allocation plan, indicating that when external parameters change, the solution of the model maintains a certain level of satisfaction conservativeness and can be effectively applied to the entire logistics network. Conclusion

The efficient use of berth resources at container terminal waterfronts is crucial for enhancing terminal competitiveness, as it aims to minimize ship in-port time while maximizing resource utilization. This study extends traditional ship scheduling constraints by incorporating tidal influences, which can prevent ships from accessing berths if water depths are insufficient. Departing from the focus on minimizing in-port operational costs, we integrate time-varying water depths and uncertainty into berth allocation planning, developing an integer linear programming model and a heuristic algorithm for comparative analysis, thereby validating the effectiveness of our approach.

Numerical experiments and case studies were conducted to validate the proposed model and algorithm in this paper. The algorithm’s performance was compared with the solutions obtained from the CPLEX solver, and the results demonstrate the algorithm’s superior efficiency. Additionally, sensitivity analysis was performed to further examine the model’s performance under fluctuating ship draft conditions.A comparison between the algorithm and CPLEX solutions indicates that the proposed algorithm achieves satisfactory solution accuracy with high computational efficiency. The differences in algorithm efficiency when solving different models were analyzed, and the reasons behind these variations were explored. Sensitivity analysis was also conducted to assess the model’s robustness under varying confidence levels, verifying the fuzzy optimization model’s resistance to disturbances. Through numerical case experiments, the applicability of the proposed model, as well as the feasibility and effectiveness of the algorithm, were successfully validated.

This paper investigates the process of regular tidal changes, likening tidal movement to periodic motion, and idealizes the tidal function as a cosine curve. However, due to the varying tidal conditions at different ports, it is necessary to refine the tidal function based on specific circumstances. Future research could explore the development of other types of tidal functions to make the model applicable to a wider range of situations.

With the increasing global emphasis on environmental protection and carbon emissions reduction, the shipping industry is facing mounting pressure from governments, society, and the market to adopt more sustainable operational practices. Furthermore, as artificial intelligence and big data technologies continue to advance, there is potential to explore more sophisticated models and algorithms to adapt to rapidly changing market conditions and the complex, dynamic maritime environment. Through deeper data analytics and operational optimization, future advancements may enable continuous cost reductions and more efficient resource allocation, thereby enhancing the overall economic performance of shipping companies. Consequently, future berth allocation optimization decisions will evolve toward greater intelligence, automation, and sustainability. This shift aims to meet environmental regulations, improve transportation efficiency, and drive the shipping industry toward a more sustainable development path.

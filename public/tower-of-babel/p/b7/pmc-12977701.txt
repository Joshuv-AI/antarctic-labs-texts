Correction: RNF186 controls glucose metabolism in metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease
Authors: Jiang Du, Qizhang Du, Yuxuan Zhang, Jingwen Gao, Chiheng Wang, Beilin Kou, Yan Jiang, Dunyong Zhao, Juntang Lin
Published: 2026

Correction to: Cell Biosci (2025) 15:164

10.1186/s13578-025-01507-6

In this article [1], Fig. 3C, the H&E staining for the WT/NCD group was incorrect, and we have made the necessary corrections. Although the incorrect image also depicts H&E staining for the WT/NCD group, it originates from a different cohort of animals. This mix-up may have occurred due to the identical ear tags of the mice used in both sets of images. For completeness and transparency, the incorrect and correct versions are displayed below. The original article has been corrected.

Loss of RNF186 enhances hepatic glycogen synthesis, inhibits gluconeogenesis, and activates the insulin-mediated AKT signaling pathway in liver and gWAT. A Fasting blood glucose levels in the serum were measured in WT and KO mice after a 20-week feeding period on either a NCD or a HFD. B Fast-ing insulin levels were assessed using ELISA assays in the indicated mice groups. C Liver tissue slices were subjected to PAS staining, and hepatic glycogen content was analyzed in WT and KO mice after a 20-week feeding period on either a NCD or a HFD to assess glycogen synthesis (scale bar = 100 μm). D qPCR analysis was performed to evaluate the expression of gluconeogenesis-related genes (pepck, G6pc and pgc1-α) in the indicated groups. E Western blot analysis was conducted, along with quantitative assessment, of phosphorylated proteins including p-IRS1, p-GSK3β, p-AKT, and p-AMPKα in the indi-cated groups. F HE staining was performed to analyze lipid storage in the gWAT of WT and KO mice after 20 weeks of HFD feeding (scale bar = 200 μm). G Western blot analysis and quantitative assessment were performed to evaluate the expression levels of Fasn, SCD1, p-AKT, and p-GSK3β in the indicated groups. For all statistical plots, circles represent individual mice, the data are presented as mean ± S.E.M, and statistical significance is indicated as ns (not significant), *P < 0.05, ***P < 0.001

Electrophysiological parameters in patients with hepatic cirrhosis
Authors: Dragoş Lupu, Cristian Stănescu, Dănuţ Laurenţiu Nedelcu, George Alin Stoica, Mihai-Marius Botezat, Andreea-Ştefania Lupu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Various electrophysiological abnormalities are noted in patients with cirrhosis, the most prevalent consisting of QT prolongation and autonomic dysfunction. This study aims to thoroughly evaluate these abnormalities in cirrhotic patients by utilizing various parameters and compare them with healthy individuals. We evaluated 60 patients with hepatic cirrhosis using a resting electrocardiogram (ECG), ECG during and after the Valsalva maneuver, 24-hour ambulatory ECG monitoring and a standardized ECG stress test. We then compared these results with a group of 50 patients who had no hepatic cirrhosis or other significant known pathologies and were not on any medical treatment. At rest, cirrhotic patients had a reduced Valsalva index compared to the control group, but no statistical differences were noted in comparing resting heart rate (HR) values. At Holter monitoring, although there was a trend toward increased corrected QT (QTc) intervals in cirrhotic patients, it did not reach statistical significance, indicating no difference between the two groups. Reduced standard deviation of normal-to-normal (NN) intervals (SDNN) values, statistically significant, were noted in cirrhotic patients compared to the control group, indicating autonomic dysfunction. At stress test, there was no statistically significant difference in the results obtained for maximum HR during exercise between the two groups. Also, the results showed that cirrhotic patients had statistically significantly higher HR values after exercise compared to the control group. In conclusion, cirrhotic patients presented an increased grade of autonomic dysfunction compared to healthy patients, but no differences were noted regarding QT interval abnormalities.

Introduction

Previous research has shown that there are various electrophysiological disturbances described in cirrhotic patients. There are two main known altered parameters: QT prolongation and autonomic dysfunction. Existing data has shown that there is parasympathetic neuropathy in 30–77% of the patients and sympathetic neuropathy in 12% to 37% of the patients [1]. Autonomic dysfunction correlates directly with the cardiac output and peripheral vascular resistance. It is also shown that the reduction of the heart rate (HR) variability (a known marker of autonomic dysfunction) is correlated with cirrhosis decompensation and 90 days mortality [2, 3]. QT prolongation is directly linked to ventricular arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death. Although there are many theories that can explain these alterations the exact mechanism is still unknown.

It was also shown that after a hepatic transplant there was an improvement of autonomic dysfunction by 70% [4].

Hypothetically ethanolic cirrhotic patients should manifest a supplementary rate of autonomic dysfunction through direct neurotoxic effect and possibly by decreased vitamin B12 levels. This hypothesis leads to a direct comparison of the two subgroups included in this research: ethanolic and non-ethanolic cirrhotic patients.

The present paper aims to a better understanding of these alterations by using various methods of evaluation and various parameters. Patients were evaluated by three main methods: electrocardiographic (ECG) recording at rest and after Valsalva maneuver (VM), ambulatory ECG monitoring and pathophysiological response to the standardized stress test.

Early detection of these electrophysiological disturbances is important because it can explain patient symptoms and can also prevent gastroenterological and cardiological complications [5].

Aim

The aim of this study was to comprehensively assess autonomic dysfunction using parameters that monitor cirrhotic patients in different states: at rest, during the VM, during exertion, and over the course of 24 hours, and also to determine the presence of an altered parasympathetic response in cirrhotic patients compared to healthy individuals.

Patients, Materials and Methods

We included 60 patients with hepatic cirrhosis evaluated in the Outpatient Clinic of ClinICCO Hospital, Braşov, Romania, from April 2019 to November 2022 by resting ECG (12 derivations), ECG during and after VM, ambulatory ECG monitoring for 24 hours (three derivations) and standardized ECG stress test (12 derivations) (treadmill or bicycle according to patients’ preference), and we compared the results with a group of 50 patients without hepatic cirrhosis and other known significant pathologies and no medical treatment.

Of the 60 patients in the hepatic cirrhosis group, 44 were included in the ethanolic subgroup and 16 in the non-ethanolic subgroup. Forty-five patients were males and 15 were females, with ages between 44 and 68 years old.

Regarding ECG parameters we noted resting HR, the response to VM using Valsalva ratio (VR) [longest R wave to second R wave (RR): smallest RR during the maneuver – normal values >1.20] and corrected QT (QTc) interval (normal values <460 ms) [6].

The VM was done with the patient sitting on the side of the bed with ECG monitoring during the maneuver and 30 seconds after.

As Holter ECG parameters were evaluated: HR variability using standard deviation of normal-to-normal (NN) intervals (SDNN) and QTc interval during 24 hours. Normal values for the SDNN were considered those above 40 and for the QTc interval those under 460 ms [7].

The used stress test parameters were maximum HR (HR max) reached by the patient and the HR at two minutes (HR 2 min) after stopping the test. It was noted that 18 patients were not able to do the stress test due to various reasons.

The patients were divided into subgroups: ethanolic and non-ethanolic.

A comparison was made between the two main groups (cirrhotic and non-cirrhotic) and between the two subgroups.

Patients with class C cirrhosis and those with significant known cardiovascular pathology were excluded.

The results were statistically analyzed: HR, VR, HR max and HR 2 min using Student t-test, resting QTc and mean QTc values using analysis of variance (ANOVA) (SAS software) and for SDNN the Fisher’s exact test.

Three patients in the alcohol group died during our study. During necropsy, liver fragments were collected and immediately fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin and sent to the Pathology Laboratory for histopathological (HP) and immunohistochemical (IHC) studies, in order to confirm microscopically the presence of cirrhosis.

After the liver fragments were embedded in paraffin, according to the classical HP processing protocol, 4 μm-thick microscopic sections were made on a rotary microtome and then stained with Hematoxylin–Eosin (HE) and Goldner–Szekely (GS) trichrome.

For immunohistochemistry studies, liver sections were mounted on special slides coated with poly-L-lysine for increased adhesion. The following antibodies were used: anti-cluster of differentiation (CD)3 (monoclonal mouse anti-human CD3, clone F7.2.38, 1/25 dilution, Dako) for detection of T-lymphocytes; anti-CD20 (monoclonal mouse anti-human CD20cy, clone L26, 1/50 dilution, Dako) for detection of B-lymphocytes; anti-CD34 (monoclonal mouse anti-human CD34 Class II, clone QBEnd10, 1/50 dilution, Dako) for detecting endothelial cells in blood vessels; anti-CD68 (monoclonal anti-human CD68, clone KP1, 1/100 dilution, Dako) for detecting macrophage system cells; anti-alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) (monoclonal mouse anti-human SMA, clone 1A4, 1/100 dilution, Dako) for highlighting myofibroblasts.

Results

Resting ECG parameters

At rest

Resting HR was between 48 and 110 beats/min in the cirrhotic group and between 55 and 90 beats/min in the control group, with no statistical difference. There were also no significant differences between the two subgroups.

During and after Valsalva maneuver

In the cirrhotic patients’ group, the Valsalva index was 1.18 compared to 1.34 in the control group (p<0.05) (Table 1). The statistical significance was the alteration of the parasympathetic nervous system (PNS). Regarding the two subgroups, in the patients with toxic etiology the value was 1.17, and 1.19 in the non-toxic subgroup of patients (p>0.05) (Table 2).

QTc interval

In both main groups, there were no detected QTc values over 500 ms. The mean QTc values were 437 ms in the cirrhotic group, and 440 ms in the control group, without statistically significant difference. In the ethanolic subgroup, the value was 438 ms, and 440 ms in the non-ethanolic subgroup, also without statistical significance.

SDNN was significantly reduced in the cirrhotic group (34) compared to non-cirrhotic patients (55), p<0.05 (Table 3). When comparing the two subgroups, no statistical difference was found (p>0.05).

QTc interval

Although in the previous studies the altered ventricular repolarization, expressed by prolonged QT interval, was noted in cirrhotic patients, in our study it was not the case. In the cirrhotic group, the mean QTc values were 431 ms, and 442 ms in the control group, without statistical significance. Also, no statistical significance was found while comparing the two subgroups, 435 ms in the ethanolic subgroup and 444 ms in the non-ethanolic subgroup (p>0.05).

Stress test parameters

HR max reached by the patient

In the cirrhotic group, mean HR max was not statistical different from the mean HR max in the control group (154 beats/min vs. 165 beats/min), p>0.05. No statistical difference was shown in the subgroup comparison.

HR 2 min after stopping the test

In the cirrhotic group, HR 2 min was 114 beats/min compared to 73 beats/min in the control group. This finding had statistical significance (p<0.05) (Table 4). Subgroup comparison did not show any statistical significance (p>0.05).

Histopathological analysis

HP examination of the liver fragments revealed the existence of hepatocyte necrosis areas (Figure 1), associated with granulovacuolar degeneration (Figure 2) and the development of a chronic inflammatory infiltrate consisting of lymphocytes, plasma cells and macrophages and hepatic fibrosis (Figures 3, 4).

Immunohistochemical analysis

IHC examinations confirmed the presence of a chronic inflammatory infiltrate consisting of a large number of CD3+ T-lymphocytes, present both intralobular and in Kiernan spaces (Figure 5), and CD20+ B-lymphocytes present in smaller numbers and only in the perilobar spaces (Figure 6).

Marking of macrophage-type cells with the anti-CD68 antibody demonstrated a relatively large accumulation of macrophages in the Kiernan spaces (Figure 7) but also hypertrophy of Kupffer cells (Figure 8), and IHC marking with the anti-CD34 antibody showed an increase in the number of vessels in the portal spaces.

The use of the anti-α-SMA antibody allowed us to note an excessive accumulation of myofibroblasts in the Kiernan and perilobular spaces, cells that are responsible for the synthesis of the extracellular matrix and especially of collagen fibers (Figure 9). The same IHC marking showed the transformation of Ito cells into myofibroblasts (Figure 10).

Discussions

Resting heart rate

Although described in the literature and having demonstrated pathophysiological mechanisms in this regard, sympathetic nervous system (SNS) activation in cirrhotic patients, manifested by an increased resting HR [3], did not reach statistical significance in patients included in the study when compared to the control group. A possible explanation for this conclusion could be the activation of the SNS in the control group patients due to their presence in the medical environment or anxiety from medical examinations.

Valsalva index

In previous studies, this parameter has proven to be a very good indicator of the autonomic nervous system’s condition. Previous research has demonstrated an altered response both to the VM and other parasympathetic challenge tests, such as deep inspiration or the postural change test [8, 9]. In the current study, statistical significance was achieved, with cirrhotic patients having a reduced Valsalva index compared to the control group, confirming once again the predominant impairment of the PNS in patients with cirrhotic hepatitis. When comparing subgroups based on etiologies, no significant difference in results was observed. Although previous research described additional autonomic dysfunction with a positive correlation between the degree of autonomic impairment and the alcoholic etiology of cirrhotic hepatitis, possibly due to direct neurotoxic effects or vitamin B12 deficiency [10, 11, 12], no significant differences were found between subgroups based on etiologies. Thus, no additional impairment was confirmed in patients with alcoholic etiology.

Holter ECG parameters

In certain studies, in the literature, ventricular repolarization prolongation has been described in patients with hepatic cirrhosis, manifested by a prolonged QTc interval [13, 14, 15, 16]. This change has been described especially in patients classified in Child–Pugh class C [17]. In our study, although there was a trend toward increased QTc intervals in cirrhotic patients, it did not reach statistical significance, indicating no difference between the two groups. A study by Koshy et al. [16] demonstrated no correlation between QTc interval prolongation and the diagnostic criteria for cirrhotic cardiomyopathy (CCM) established at the 2005 World Congress of Gastroenterology and the Cirrhotic Cardiomyopathy Consortium, concluding that QTc interval prolongation and CMC are separate entities with no relationship between them. This conclusion is controversial, as other studies have concluded that QTc interval prolongation is a consequence of CCM and poses a significant risk for malignant arrhythmias [18, 19, 20].

Knowing from previous studies that disease severity correlates with QTc prolongation, this could be explained by the exclusion of patients in Child–Pugh class C from the study. HR variability is a proven marker of autonomic dysfunction, and its evaluation through the SDNN parameter has been shown to have high specificity and sensitivity [21, 22, 23]. Autonomic dysfunction was present in cirrhotic patients in this study, with reduced SDNN values in cirrhotic patients compared to the control group, and these results were statistically significant. This parameter also confirms the clear presence of autonomic dysfunction in those with end-stage liver disease. It’s known from previous studies that this parameter has prognostic importance, with values above 100 ms having a fivefold lower risk of mortality and morbidity than those with values under 50 ms [23]. This finding aligns with most previous studies through various mechanisms. One possible explanation could be the release of angiotensin II secondary to systemic vasodilation, primarily nitric oxide (NO) mediated. This hormone has been shown to have a significant interaction with vagal tone, reducing its action. Furthermore, angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor administration has been shown to improve HR variability [24]. Comparing the two subgroups of patients did not reveal notable differences between different etiologies of cirrhosis.

Parameters evaluated through exercise testing

Physiologically, exercise testing involves stimulation of the SNS with accelerated HR, and after stopping physical exertion, a progressive reduction in adrenergic tone and an increase in parasympathetic tone activity occur [25]. In our study, the following were evident: there was no statistically significant difference in the results obtained for HR max during exercise, contrary to the hypothesis of overstimulation of the SNS in patients with cirrhotic hepatitis, without a clear explanation for this result. However, the persistence of post-exertional tachycardia in cirrhotic patients can be considered a sign of altered PNS function. The results demonstrated that patients with hepatic cirrhotic have higher HR values post-exercise compared to the control group, and these results were statistically significant.

Conclusions

The present paper aimed to a better understanding of the autonomic dysfunction and of various electrophysiological parameters in patients with hepatic cirrhosis by comparing them with a healthy group of patients. A subgroup analysis was also made between ethanolic and non-ethanolic cirrhotic patients. Although resting HR was similar between the two groups, measuring and analyzing the VR showed an altered response proving parasympathetic dysfunction in the cirrhotic patients. The same consistent finding with the same significance was proved by analyzing the two-minute post stress test parameter. HR variability was also modified in the cirrhotic group. Based on these three evaluated parameters we concluded that cirrhotic patients presented an autonomic dysfunction compared to healthy patients. There were no significant differences between the two subgroups on all parameters evaluated.

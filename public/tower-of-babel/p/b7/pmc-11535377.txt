Celebrating the 30th Anniversary of the Saudi Pharmaceutical Journal: A Special Issue
Authors: Nasser B Alsaleh, Omer I Fantoukh
Published: 2024

The year 2023 marks the 30th Anniversary of the Saudi Pharmaceutical Journal (SPJ), the official journal of the Saudi Pharmaceutical Society (SPS) at King Saud University. It is a moment to celebrate and also to reflect on the significant achievements during the past three decades. Upon reflecting on these years, two phases of development emerged. The first phase (1993–2010) marked the beginnings with the journal foundation in 1993 and then its indexing in Scopus in 1996. At that time, the SPJ slowly but steadily emerged among the top regional journals within the domain of pharmaceutical sciences. During the first phase, the number of annually published peer-reviewed articles was in the range of mid to upper twenties. In the second phase (2010–present), however, the SPJ was gaining momentum towards reaching the global stage with the achievement of several milestones. For instance, in 2009, the SPJ started publishing on the Elsevier platform (Science Direct), and it was indexed in the National Library of Medicine (PubMed Central). Two years later, it was indexed in the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCIE) of the Web of Science (WoS). The number of annually published articles was growing to hit a record number at the time of 97 articles (2015). Since then, the number of published articles has risen, surpassing the 100-publication mark in 2017. Outstandingly, The SPJ was ranked among the Quartile 1 (Q1) journals in the Pharmacology and Pharmacy Category (WoS) in 2019 with an impact factor (IF) of 3.6. The total number of published articles in the last five years (2019–2023) was 1,060 articles with a total number of 9,435 citations.

Such statistics certainly reflect the significant progress and accumulative efforts made in the past 30 years, and most importantly, the vital role SPJ has played in serving the community, advancing the science and developing the profession. Indeed, the SPJ has been a key platform for disseminating research findings across different subjects under the broader umbrella of pharmaceutical sciences and outcomes. These subjects included, but not limited to, drug discovery, phytochemistry, pharmacology and toxicology, drug delivery, industrial pharmacy, pharmaceutical technology, pharmacoeconomics and pharmacy education. The mainstream of published work principally reflects research carried out in the country and region. Hence, the SPJ has played a pivotal role in shaping the landscape of pharmaceutical sciences research at the country and regional levels. Nevertheless, global contributions have also been consistently present over the past years, which has undeniably enriched the journal research outcomes in terms of scope and quality. Such achievements and progress would not have been realized without the support of the scientific community including editors, authors, reviewers and readers, and the SPJ is greatly appreciative for the support of its community throughout the past years. This special issue (SI) was dedicated to disseminating research with national contributions within the field of pharmaceutical sciences and outcomes. This SI has published 14 peer-reviewed articles (11 original and three review articles), spanning a wide range of research topics. Below, we showcase the emerging themes of the published articles and highlight some of the key findings.

Among the studies published in this issue, three discussed important topics within the field of pharmaceutical outcomes with a common theme to improve the role of pharmacists in the healthcare sector in Saudi Arabia. One article has investigated leadership development among different cohorts of pharmacists (BinDhim et al., 2024). Specifically, the study used qualitative research methods based on in-depth interviews with three cohorts of Saudi pharmacists: senior pharmacists currently engaged in pharmacists in leadership positions, retired pharmacists from past leadership positions, and junior pharmacists aspiring to lead positions in the future. Intriguingly, the study found significant disparities among the cohorts toward several aspects of leadership including competencies, leadership development strategies, skills, values, and personal characteristics. One of the key findings of the article is that the junior pharmacist cohort emphasized idealistic personal characteristics of the leader rather than practical skills. Another imperative study within this domain has discussed the journey of transforming Ambulatory Care Pharmacy practice in Johns Hopkins Aramco Healthcare (JHAH) (Ali Thorakkattil et al., 2024). The article delved into the details driving the advancement of Ambulatory Care Pharmacy in JHAH in the past few years including adoption of modern automation tools, use of systematic patient satisfaction surveys, and implementation of medication home delivery services. The experience and lessons from the JHAH case are of high relevance to healthcare systems within the country and region.

One of the current hot topics in the field of pharmaceutical sciences is personalized or precision medicine. Two articles were published in this SI on the topic. One article carried out whole-exome sequencing (WES) in a sample from the Saudi population to identify variations in cancer susceptibility with the overarching goal to advance patient-tailored therapy. The study identified variants (i.e., single-nucleotide polymorphisms, SNPs) among the sample in the endo-lysosomal ion transport genes that could be associated with cancer. This article could pave the way for future studies within this subject domain to better understand the association between the identified SNPs and disease outcomes (Binobaid et al., 2024). The other study has assessed the knowledge and attitude of clinical pharmacists towards the role of pharmacogenetic testing on mercaptopurine administration (Abanmy et al., 2024). The study reported good perception and positive attitude towards pharmacogenetic testing, however, the data demonstrated low level of knowledge and formal training among the study sample. Hence, the article has recommended developing national guidelines on pharmacogenetic testing.

On the other hand, several studies in this SI highlight the significance of plant species endemic in Saudi Arabia and their bioactive secondary metabolites as valuable alternative treatments for addressing health challenges like neurodegenerative disorders and antibiotic resistance. For instance, Dodonaea viscosa (known as Shath الشث ) and Juniperus sabina )known as Abhal الأبهل) have shown neuroprotective properties, with their extracts potentially mitigating cerebral ischemia–reperfusion injuries and oxidative stress-related conditions (Albaqami et al., 2024, Almarfadi et al., 2024). Similarly, research on 6-gingerol demonstrated its antibacterial effects against resistant pathogens, suggesting it could serve as an anti-virulence agent when combined with traditional antibiotics, thereby enhancing treatment efficacy for severe infections (Elfaky et al., 2024).

Furthermore, the exploration of Saussurea costus (known as Costus القسط), Terminalia brownii )known as Thaabالثعب ) and Acacia asak (known as Dhahaiaالضهياء ) underscores the growing interest in plant-derived compounds in Saudi Arabia for their therapeutic potential in combating modern health issues such as lithiasis and viral infections (Alharbi et al., 2024, Mammate et al., 2024). Research on S. costus demonstrated its antilithiatic efficacy, suggesting that its extracts can inhibit calcium oxalate crystal formation and positively impact physiological parameters in vivo. Moreover, T. brownii and A. asak were found to have significant antiviral properties against SARS-CoV-2, with its extracts effectively inhibiting the virus's main protease. A bioassay-guided phytochemical investigation led to the isolation of 15 secondary metabolites from T. brownii and A. asak. Among them, four bioactive compounds showed considerable Mpro inhibiting activity, paving the way for future development of plant-based treatments for COVID-19. Together, these findings emphasize the importance of these plants in traditional medicine and their promising roles in contemporary therapeutic applications. Besides their medicinal benefits, certain botanicals are gaining attention in the cosmeceutical industry. Researchers observed that the extract of Launaea procumbens (known as Howaالحوى ) has exhibited significant antioxidant, anti-aging, and anti-inflammatory activities by inhibiting enzymes that contribute to skin aging and inflammation (Khojah et al., 2024). These findings suggest that plant extracts may be valuable sources for developing natural skincare products, aligning with the trend towards more holistic and nature-based cosmetic formulations.

Moreover, an ethnobotanical study in Makkah highlighted the cultural factors influencing the use of medicinal plants, revealing how gender roles, education, and urbanization shape preferences for traditional versus biomedical treatments (Qari et al., 2024). This underscores the importance of considering sociocultural aspects when investigating local medicinal practices, as these factors can significantly affect health options and the acceptance of herbal remedies in contemporary healthcare settings.

The published articles in this SI represent a glimpse of the dynamic and diverse nature of research contributions transforming the future of pharmaceutical research in Saudi Arabia and the region. In addition, some of the articles published in this SI, not only advance our knowledge within the different domains of pharmaceutical sciences and outcomes, but also set the stage for future research (BinDhim et al., 2024, Binobaid et al., 2024). The SPJ will remain dedicated to supporting research and innovation and be an essential player in revolutionizing the frontier of pharmaceutical research, ultimately improving human health and well-being.

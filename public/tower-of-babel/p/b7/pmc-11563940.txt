Combinatorial lipidomics and proteomics underscore erythrocyte lipid membrane aberrations in the development of adverse cardio-cerebrovascular complications in maintenance hemodialysis patients
Authors: Ke Zheng, Yujun Qian, Haiyun Wang, Dan Song, Hui You, Bo Hou, Fei Han, Yicheng Zhu, Feng Feng, Sin Man Lam, Guanghou Shui, Xuemei Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPatients on maintenance hemodialysis exhibit a notably higher risk of cardio-cerebrovascular complications that constitute the major cause of death. Preceding studies have reported conflicting associations between traditional lipid measures and clinical outcome in dialysis patients. In this prospective longitudinal study, we utilized quantitative lipidomics to elucidate, at molecular resolution, changes in lipidome profiles of erythrocyte and plasma samples collected from maintenance hemodialysis patients followed up for 86 months (≈7 years). Primary outcome was defined as cardiovascular-related deaths or new-onset cardio-cerebrovascular events. Cox regression model uncovered plasma/erythrocyte lipids associated with incident cardio-cerebrovascular events in the erythrocyte cohort (n = 117 patients, 37 events) and plasma cohort (n = 45 patients, 11 events), respectively. Both the erythrocyte lipid panel [PA 40:5, PI 34:2, PC 42:6, AUC = 0.83] and plasma lipid panel [PC O-34:1, GM3 18:1; O2/25:0, TG 44:1(16:1_28:0), AUC = 0.94] significantly improved the prediction of cardio-cerebrovascular-related outcome compared to the base model comprising age, sex and dialysis vintage alone. Our findings underscore the pathophysiological significance of anionic phospholipid accretion in erythrocytes in the development of cardio-cerebrovascular complications in dialysis patients. In particular, distorted membrane lipid asymmetry leads to compromised membrane deformability, aberrant cell-cell interactions and altered glutathione metabolism in the erythrocytes of high-risk individuals even at relatively early stage of hemodialysis. Our findings thus underscore the importance of maintaining the RBC pool to lower the risk of cardio-cerebrovascular complications in dialysis patients.Keywords: Maintenance hemodialysis, Kidney failure, Cardiovascular risk, Lipidomics, Erythrocytes

Introduction

Relative to the general population of the same age, patients on dialysis exhibit a 40-fold increased risk of death attributed to cardio-cerebrovascular (CCV) complications [1]. The cumulative incidence of stroke was estimated at 11.2 % over 5 years, and at 31.5 % over 20 years in hemodialysis patients [2]. Age- and sex-standardized mortality rate ratios in dialysis patients to normal population was at 13.4 for myocardial infarction (22.5 versus 0.9 per 1000 person-years), and 12.4 for stroke (14.3 versus 0.7 per 1000 person-years) [3]. As CCV complications constitute a significant cause of morbidity and mortality in chronic kidney disease (CKD), the development of prognostic markers to facilitate early assessment of CCV complications in dialysis patients is critical to patient management in clinical practice. Patients with end stage renal disease (ESRD) and CKD display hyperlipidemia characterized by elevated serum levels of triglycerides ascribed to reduced lipoprotein lipase activity [4]. While hyperlipidemia is a known risk factor for CCV complications in the general population [5], traditional risk factors fail to fully account for the elevated risk of CCV complications observed in dialysis patients [6]. Observational studies did not consistently report a significant association between dyslipidemia and poor clinical outcome in patients undergoing hemodialysis [7]. Correcting dyslipidemia with statins was not associated with lower all-cause mortality in dialysis patients under numerous randomized controlled trials including 4D, Aurora and SHARP [[8], [9], [10]].

These observations suggest that others yet unidentified biological components in the circulation that adversely affect vessel health might underlie the elevated incidence of CCV complications in dialysis patients. Along this line, red blood cell (RBC) distribution width, which denotes an index of variability in the size of circulating erythrocytes, is a strong predictor of all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in hemodialysis patients [11,12]. Precise lipid composition of cell membranes is a key determinant of membrane curvature and membranous folding that influence variability in cell sizes and shapes [13]. Mature RBCs have lost most of their intracellular organelles, and a majority of their polar lipid content is attributed to the plasma membrane [14]. The adaptability and flexibility of RBCs (in terms of sizes and shapes) are biologically important given that erythrocytes transverse extensive macro- and microvasculature to deliver oxygen (and nutrients). RBCs also express a battery of antioxidant systems, such as the glutathione (GSH) recycling system, which are crucial toward systemic detoxification and free radical scavenging that contribute positively toward vasculature health [15].

Given the potential relevance of erythrocyte biology toward CCV complications in hemodialysis patients, we leveraged quantitative, targeted lipidomics approaches [16] to examine the membrane lipid composition of RBCs collected at baseline from 119 dialysis patients followed over a period of approximately 7 years (i.e. 86 months). As conventional lipid measurements in clinical practice may not examine lipid metabolic dysregulation with sufficient molecular resolution, we also performed quantitative lipidomics on a smaller cohort of plasma samples collected at baseline from 45 dialysis patients with the same follow-up period. Through investigating changes in both plasma and RBC lipidomes amongst participants with differing clinical outcomes, we aim to determine if lipid constituents of RBCs and plasma are relevant in predicting CCV events in dialysis patients, and to uncover lipid-centric pathways implicated in the pathophysiology of CCV complications in hemodialysis patients. Findings from lipidomics were integrated with proteomics analyses of erythrocytes from 40 patients to consolidate lipid metabolic dysregulation that orchestrates the development of CCV complications in hemodialysis patients.

Materials and methods

Study participants and data collection

This prospective cohort of maintenance hemodialysis patients were sequentially enrolled at the Peking Union Medical College Hospital (PUMCH) from July 2013 to July 2014. Enrolment criteria were as previously reported [17]. Basic clinical data including age, sex, dialysis vintage, history of smoking habits, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, and stroke were collected from these patients. Blood examination included blood cell content (white blood cell count, red blood cell count, platelet count, hematocrit, and hemoglobin), hepatic function (serum albumin, total protein, alanine aminotransferase, alkaline phosphatase, and total bilirubin), renal function (urea, creatinine, and uric acid), general lipid profiles (triglycerides, total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol), ions (potassium, sodium, calcium, and phosphorus), and other biochemical data (carbon dioxide content, transferrin saturation, ferritin, parathyroid hormone, β2 microglobulin, and high sensitivity C reactive protein). Inclusion criteria: age over 18 years and regular dialysis more the 2 months, and exclusion criteria: (i) systemic infection, acute cardiovascular events, cancer, surgery or trauma in the month prior to the study; (ii) metabolic encephalopathy (e.g. hepatic encephalopathy, uremic encephalopathy, hypoxic encephalopathy), mental disorder, emotional disorder, epilepsy patients on long-term treatments or diagnosed dementia; (iii) history of non-atherosclerotic arterial disease (e.g. vasculitis, Takayasu arteritis); and (iv) metal prosthesis. Patients were followed clinically until the end of the study in August 2021. Primary outcome was defined as death or new-onset CCV events, including cerebral infarction, cerebral hemorrhage and myocardial infarction based on patients’ reports and relevant medical records. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of PUMCH (PUMCH IRB number S564), and abide by the Declaration of Helsinki principles. All participants signed written informed consent prior to admission into the study. The samples were kindly provided by Peking Union Medical College Hospital Clinical Biobank (Clinical Biobank (ISO 20387), Peking Union Medical College Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences).

Sample collection

Baseline blood samples were collected from patients within a week of commencing their first hemodialysis session. Fasting blood samples were collected in VACUETTE® K2 EDTA blood collection tubes (Greiner Bio-One). To separate RBCs from plasma, whole blood samples were centrifuged for 10 min at 4 °C at 3000 g within 2 h of blood collection. Crude RBCs at the lower phase were collected and washed twice with ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline. The upper phase of platelet-rich plasma was collected and further centrifuged at 1500 g for 10 min to remove platelets. The final supernatant was collected as plasma samples used in the current study. The purified RBCs and plasma samples were immediately stored at −80 °C until further analyses.

Lipidomics and proteomics analyses

Lipids were extracted from biological samples using a modified version of the Bligh and Dyer's protocol [18], RBC and plasma lipidomes were analyzed using quantitative lipidomics approaches as described comprehensively in a recent publication [19]. In brief, lipid extracts (lower chloroform phase) from two rounds of extraction were combined into the same tube and dried in SpeedVac under organic mode, and subsequently channeled for lipidomics analyses. For proteomics of RBCs, following lipid extraction, the upper aqueous (water-methanol) phase together with the cell pellet were completely dried in a SpeedVac under aqueous mode. Proteins from the dried cell pellets were extracted using standard protein lysis buffer and subsequently channeled for proteomics analysis [20]. See Supplemental Methods for methodological details of lipidomics and proteomics approaches.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software, version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL) and R version 4.3.1. Continuous data with a normal distribution were presented as the means ± standard deviations, and were analyzed by Student's t-test. Data with high skewness were presented as the medians with interquartile range, and were analyzed by the Mann–Whitney U test and the Kruskal–Wallis test. The chi-square test and Fisher's exact test were used to assess categorical data, which were presented as proportions. A Cox regression model was used to identify lipids that were potentially associated with the measured outcome. Kaplan-Meier estimates of survival curves were generated for each tertile of the lipids with the “survminer” R package. Weighted correlation network analysis was performed with the “WGCNA” R package. Enrichment analyses of lipid class were performed for each module using hypergeometric tests. Correlations between lipid levels and clinical indices were examined using Pearson correlation coefficients. Least absolute shrinkage selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis was performed to further screen prognostic lipids with the “glmnet” R package. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to evaluate the predictive performance of the selected lipid species using a logistic regression model. Pathway enrichment analyses of differential proteins (t-test p < 0.05) were performed on KEGG pathway database with the “clusterProfiler” R package. All tests were two-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of participants

A total of 117 and 45 patients on dialysis were enrolled for the RBC and plasma cohorts (Table 1, Table 2), respectively. Plasma and RBCs were isolated from whole blood samples, and patients were followed-up from 2013/2014 to August 2021 (∼7 years ≈86.0 months [80.5–88.0]) (Fig. 1A). For the RBC cohort, seven cerebral hemorrhage events, 17 cerebral infarction events, and 21 myocardial infarction events were recorded for 37 patients. The average age at baseline was significantly older in the group with new-onset cardio-cerebrovascular events compared to the group without (61.5 vs. 53.8 years, p = 0.003). The median dialysis vintage of patients was not significantly different between the two groups (p = 0.356). The overall prevalence rates of hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and stroke history in the RBC cohort were 97.4 %, 22.2 %, 35.9 %, and 11.1 %, respectively (Table 1). For the plasma cohort, two cerebral hemorrhage events, five cerebral infarction events, and five myocardial infarction events were recorded for 11 patients. No significant differences (p < 0.05) in baseline characteristics were observed between the two groups of patients stratified based on CCV outcome. The median dialysis vintage of patients was 57.0 months (15.5–95.0). The prevalence rates of hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and stroke history were 97.8 %, 22.2 %, 40.0 %, and 8.9 %, respectively. The age and dialysis vintage of patients with adverse outcome, however, were marginally significantly higher (p < 0.1) than patients without adverse events (Table 2). The age, sex and dialysis vintage of patients were incorporated as confounding variables in subsequent Cox regression analyses (see the following section).

Lipid predictors of new-onset CCV events in dialysis patients

A library of 530 lipids from 22 classes were quantitated for the plasma lipidome, while 236 lipids from 15 classes were quantitated for RBC polar lipidome (Fig. 1B). Using multivariate Cox regression analyses, a total of 68 RBC lipids and 28 plasma lipids were significantly associated (p < 0.05) with new-onset CCV events after adjustment for age, sex and dialysis vintage (Fig. 2, Figures S2-3). Numerous polar lipid classes constituting the RBC polar lipidome was positively associated with new-onset CCV events in dialysis patients, which included phosphatidylethanolamines (PE), phosphatidylglycerols (PG), phosphatidylinositols (PI), phosphatidic acids (PA), phosphatidylserines (PS), lyso-PS (LPS) and lyso-PA (LPA). Amongst these lipid classes, anionic phospholipids including PA (HR = 1.52, 95 % CI [1.18–1.95]), PG (HR = 1.48, 95 % CI[1.14–1.91]) and PS (HR = 1.40, 95 % CI[1.07–1.83]) were stronger predictors of adverse CCV events in dialysis patients (Fig. 2). Individual species of PAs such as PA 38:3 (HR = 1.73, 95 % CI[1.31–2.30]) and PA 38:4 (HR = 1.62, 95 % CI[1.23–2.13]) represent the strongest positive predictors of new-onset CCV events in dialysis patients. Besides anionic phospholipids, polyunsaturated phosphatidylcholines (PCs) including PC 42:6 (HR = 1.62, 95 % CI[1.21–2.16]) and PC 42:5 (HR = 1.53, 95 % CI[1.17–2.00]) are also strong positive predictors of adverse CCV outcome. Monounsaturated PC 34:1, endogenously the most abundant PC in the RBC polar lipidome (Fig. S1), was a negative predictor of adverse outcome (HR = 0.65, 95 % CI [0.44–0.96]) (Fig. 2A). As for plasma lipid predictors, a predominance of polar lipids, particularly PCs, ether PCs (PC–O) and sphingomyelins (SMs) was observed (Fig. 2B). The strongest positive plasma lipid predictors were polyunsaturated PC-Os, including PC O-40:6 (HR = 2.93, 95 % CI [1.31–6.54]), PC O-40:5 (HR = 3.03, 95 % CI [1.36–6.74]) and PC O-40:4 (HR = 2.72, 95 % CI [1.24–5.94]). On the other hand, the very long-chain Cer 18:1; O2/26:1 denotes the only plasma lipid negatively associated with cardio-cerebrovascular events in dialysis patients (HR = 0.13, 95 % CI [0.02–0.98]). Overlapping lipid predictors of CCV events in RBC and plasma included polyunsaturated PCs i.e. PC 42:7 and PC 42:6, and SM 18:1; O2/14:0 (Fig. 2C). Our findings that plasma lipids significantly associated with incident CCV events in dialysis patients being predominantly polar in nature might partly explain why preceding attempts to rectify the plasma neutral lipid profiles using statins did not bring about appreciable improvement in the clinical outcome of dialysis patients [9,10].

Evaluating the clinical significance and performance of lipid predictors

Given the quantitative nature of lipidomics performed herein, we then categorized patients according to concentration tertiles of lipids at baseline, and constructed Kaplan-Meier survival curves, with survival being defined by the absence of CCV events (Fig. 3A and B). Statistical significance in “survival” probability across the three concentration tertiles was assessed by logrank test. For RBC cohort, the top lipids with the smallest p-values from the classes of PC, PE, PS, PI and PA were presented (Fig. 3A). It was notable that the incidence of CCV events was also significantly different (p = 0.002) across the three concentration tertiles of total LPAs in RBCs. These results demonstrated a significantly higher probability of CCV episodes over the next 7 years in patients with elevated levels of PC 42:6, PE 38:5, PS 40:7, PI 34:2, PA 38:3 and total LPA in RBCs at baseline. Similarly, Kaplan-Meier survival analyses in the plasma cohort revealed concentration-dependent levels of six plasma lipids at baseline that stratified patients with differing probability of CCV episodes over the next 7 years (Fig. 3B). It was noteworthy that half of these plasma lipids uncovered were ether PCs.

(C) Receiver operating curves (ROCs) of adverse cardio-cerebrovascular outcomes in dialysis patients based on logistic regression models. A total of 80 versus 37 events were recorded in the RBC cohort, while 34 versus 11 events were recorded for the plasma cohort over a follow-up duration of 96 months. The base model (age + sex + dialysis vintage) was compared with a model that incorporated top three significant lipids from Cox regressions in the RBC cohort and plasma cohort, respectively. DeLong's test was used to compare the performance of individual models.

We next evaluated the predictive performance of RBC and plasma lipid predictors using least absolute shrinkage selection operator (LASSO) regression analysis to select prognostic lipids via the “glmnet” R package. Three top RBC and plasma lipid predictors were selected, respectively, which included PA 40:5, PI 34:2 and PC 42:6 from RBCs; and PC O-34:1, GM3 18:1; O2/25:0 and TG 44:1(16:1_28:0) from plasma (Fig. 3C). The predictive performance of a model containing lipids and baseline indices (age, sex, dialysis vintage) was compared against a model comprising only the baseline indices (age, sex, dialysis vintage). The incorporation of lipid predictors led to significant improvement in predictive performance for both RBC lipids (AUC = 0.83, p = 0.003) and plasma lipids (AUC = 0.94, p = 0.034). We also evaluated the predictive performance of overlapping lipid predictors between RBCs and plasma i.e. PC 42:6, PC 42:7 and SM 18:1; O2/14:0 (Figs. S4A–B). Incorporation of these three lipids brought about significant improvement in predictive performance only in the RBC model (AUC = 0.82, p = 0.008), but not the plasma lipid model (p = 0.51). In all, our analyses revealed that both RBC and plasma lipids at baseline can improve prediction of new-onset CCV events in dialysis patients.

Integrated omics to elucidate cardio-cerebrovascular pathology under hemodialysis

To explore how lipids might be implicated in the pathophysiology of CCV events in dialysis patients, we investigated the correlations of RBC and plasma lipids with clinical and biochemical indices available (Fig. 4). An unsigned weighted lipid coregulation network was constructed using the R package WGCNA which enabled examination of biological networks using pairwise correlation of variables. Lipids were hierarchically clustered such that species with high topological overlap were categorized into one WGCNA module. Individual modules were defined by lipid classes that were substantially enriched within the modules. Pearson correlation analyses were performed between individual modules with clinical and biochemical indices as illustrated in Fig. 4. Anionic lipid constituents of RBCs, including PI, PS, PA and monosialodihexosyl gangliosides (GM3), together constitute module ME2 that was positively correlated with adverse outcomes such as new-onset myocardial infarction and new-onset CCV events. In addition, ME2 was also positively correlated with RBC count and normalized protein catabolic rate (nPCR). ME1 containing cationic membrane lipids such as polyunsaturated (PUFA)-PCs and SM was positively correlated with new-onset cerebral hemorrhage and ferritin level (Fig. 4A). Contrary to RBC lipid modules, plasma lipid modules were not significantly correlated with adverse outcomes, but TG clusters were positively correlated with RBC count, hematocrit and hemoglobin content, indicating the biological relevance of circulating TGs to RBC survival (Fig. 4B).

Proteomics analyses of RBC samples from dialysis patients revealed moderate segregation between individuals with or without new-onset CCV events (Fig. 5A). A list of 262 differential proteins (p < 0.05) was identified, amongst which 120 were upregulated and 142 were downregulated in dialysis patients with new-onset CCV events (Fig. 5B). Over-enrichment analysis of metabolic pathways using the KEGG database underscores “hypertrophic cardiomyopathy”, “focal adhesion” and “glutathione metabolism” as the top altered pathways (Fig. 5C). Two tropomyosin isoforms that function to stabilize erythrocyte F-actins along their lengths, namely tropomyosin alpha-1 chain (TPM1) and tropomyosin alpha-3 chain (TPM3) [21], were significantly reduced in patients with new-onset CCV events. Membrane proteins mediating intercellular adhesions amongst blood cells (i.e. erythrocytes, leukocytes and platelets) such as integrin alpha-IIb and Talin-1 were significantly increased in erythrocytes of patients with incident CCV events (Fig. 5D). Discussion

Traditional risk factors cannot fully account for the elevated CCV risk observed in dialysis patients. Various non-traditional cardiovascular risk factors, including elevated oxidative stress, chronic inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, vascular calcification, anemia, uremic toxins, and other dialysis-related factors, may contribute to vascular disorders in patients on dialysis [22,23]. In this study, we conducted a lipid-centric investigation of additional risk factors associated with new-onset CCV events in dialysis patients. To date, few preceding studies had centrally focused on lipid metabolic dysregulation in hemodialysis populations with molecular resolution. A previous study reported that hemodialysis significantly lowered plasma levels of LPEs and LPIs by comparing pre-hemodialysis and post-hemodialysis plasma lipidomes [24]. Our study represents the first prospective study that systematically investigated baseline lipid predictors in RBCs and plasma significantly associated with new-onset CCV events in dialysis patients using quantitative lipidomics approaches.

Our analyses revealed that compositional increases in anionic phospholipids such as PA, PS and PI in RBC polar lipidome were associated with increased risk of new-onset CCV events in dialysis patients, while the monounsaturated PC 34:1 was negatively associated. Anionic phospholipids comprise the major lipid constituents of the inner leaflet of the RBC membrane, while PCs constitute the bulk of the outer leaflet with PC 34:1 as a major component [25]. Phospholipid asymmetry is important to ensuring normal RBC physiology, with biological implications for a myriad of processes including cell division and blood coagulation [26]. Accretions in inner membrane anionic phospholipids suggested a loss in RBC membrane lipid asymmetry already at baseline in individuals predisposed to CCV episodes. Disruption of membrane lipid asymmetry in RBCs is associated with RBC crenation marked by a shape change from smooth discords to spiculate spheres [27]. RBC crenation causes a 45 % drop in the rate of oxygen uptake and 23 % reduction in rate of oxygen release [28]. Anionic phospholipids in the inner plasma membrane leaflet of erythrocytes also confer anchorage to skeletal proteins such as tropomyosins and F-actins that function to maintain RBC structures. A skewed balance between the levels of TPM1 and TPM3 leads to spherocytic morphology of RBCs with compromised membrane deformability and mechanical properties [21]. Lipidomics and proteomics findings therefore cumulatively supported aberrant structural and mechanical properties of erythrocytes in dialysis patients predisposed to CCV events (see Fig. 6).

Lipid-driven modifications in erythrocyte properties and metabolism that alter the risk of CCV outcome. Combinatorial lipidomics and proteomics revealed that enhanced levels of anionic phospholipids (PA, PS, PI) in the erythrocytes of patients predisposed to CCV outcome were associated with (1) altered levels of tropomyosin isomers leading to perturbed RBC morphology; (2) enhanced glucose uptake to fuel glycolysis and the pentose phosphate pathway to produce reducing equivalents for driving the erythrocyte battery of antioxidant systems such as the GSH cycle; and (3) elevated Band 3-mediated gaseous exchange. Erythrocytes of predisposed patients also exhibited augmented focal adhesion resulting from reduced levels of major syaloglycoproteins such as glycophorin A and glycophorin C that normally serve to minimize cell-cell interactions and RBC aggregation, and augmented RBC-endothelium adhesive interactions increases the risk of microvasculature occlusion, leading to adverse CCV outcome. (B) Ratio of outer plasma membrane phospholipids (PC, SM) to inner membrane phospholipids (PA, PS, PG and PI) was significantly lower in dialysis patients with new-onset CCV events. GSH: glutathione; GSSG: oxidized glutathione; PPP: pentose phosphate pathway; GSR: glutathione reductase; PC: phosphatidylcholines; SM: sphingomyelins; PA: phosphatidic acids; PS: phosphatidylserines; PI: phosphatidylinositols.

Apart from distorted morphology, altered membrane lipid composition also affects intercellular binding and interactions with the endothelia. Localized increases in PAs promote thrombogenic activities in erythrocytes via inducing membrane flip-flop and the subsequent exposure of PS on the outer RBC membrane [29]. PA treatment enhances pro-coagulant properties of erythrocytes and significantly increases thrombus formation in vivo [29]. PS-exposing RBCs exhibit increased adherence to endothelial cells via binding to matrix thrombospondin, increasing the incidence of vascular occlusion [30]. LPAs, which denote the cleavage products of PAs, were also reported to mediate calcium ions influx into RBCs, thereby promoting prothrombic properties in stimulated RBCs [14]. Corroborating these lipidomic findings, proteomics revealed lowered levels of major syaloglycoproteins i.e. glycophorin A and glycophorin C (Fig. S5) on the surface of RBCs that serve to minimize cell-cell interactions and erythrocyte aggregation [31]. Furthermore, increased levels of integrin and Talin-1 were found in erythrocytes from patients with adverse CCV outcomes. Elevated levels of integrin receptors, which are normally expressed on leukocytes and endothelial cells [32], suggest enhanced erythrocyte binding with leukocytes and the endothelia. In addition, anionic phospholipid membranes were reported to induce conformational changes in Talin that promotes integrin activation [33]. Augmented RBC-endothelium adhesive interaction possibly increases the risk of microvasculature occlusion, leading to adverse CCV outcome.

Although the accumulation of anionic phospholipids compromises RBC mechanical properties and promotes endothelium adhesiveness already at baseline, such changes in erythrocyte lipid membranes also denote potential compensatory mechanisms to cope with elevated microenvironment stressors stemming from abated renal function. For example, elevated GSR indicates heightened antioxidant capacity of the GSH recycling system, which is essential to maintain RBC hemoglobin iron in the ferrous state to ensure effective oxygen binding and transport [34]. The reducing equivalents (NADPH) necessary to sustain the assemblage of antioxidant systems within erythrocytes are mainly produced from oxidation of glucose via the pentose phosphate pathway and glycolysis [15]. Accordingly, we observed increase in glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) that catalyzes the rate-limiting step of the pentose phosphate pathway in patients predisposed to CCV events (Fig. S5). Along this line, anionic membrane phospholipids (PA, PS, PG and PI) were reported to modulate the transporter activity of mammalian glucose transporters [35]. Since RBCs rely on glucose as the sole substrate for both energy supply and redox equivalents, the accretion of anionic phospholipids might denote adaptations to facilitate glucose uptake in coping with increased demand of antioxidant capacity under chronic inflammatory micro-milieu with renal dysfunction, despite no observed increase in glucose transporter protein (Fig. S5). The anion exchanger band 3 (SLC4A1), comprises 30 % of erythrocyte membrane protein and exerts a pivotal role in erythrocyte gaseous transport [36]. Of interest, erythrocyte band 3 function was shown to be modulated by membrane lipid composition. Anionic phospholipids interact with the positively-charged lysine and arginine residues on the surface of band 3 to promote the proper folding and function of the protein [37]. Thus, changes in membrane lipid composition might serve to maintain efficient gaseous transport despite marginal reduction in the level of band 3 protein in patients with incident CCV episodes (Fig. S5).

Pearson correlation analyses of WGCNA-derived lipid modules uncovered positive correlation between serum ferritin and erythrocyte PUFA-PCs. Serum ferritin is a clinically valid measure of iron storage [38]. Selective degradation of serum ferritin via ferritinophagy creates iron overload and triggers ferroptosis [39], while PUFA-containing phospholipids (PUFA-PLs) are prone to lipid peroxidation that aggravates ferroptotic deaths [40]. Ingestion of PS-exposed erythrocytes by endothelial cells induces ferroptosis by iron overload that forms the basis of thrombophilia in uremia [41], and RBC enrichment of PUFA-PLs is expected to exacerbate ferroptotic deaths following erythrophagocytosis. In addition, our correlation analyses revealed that plasma TGs were significantly and positively correlated with RBC count in dialysis patients. Indeed, plasma low-density lipoproteins (LDLs) were previously reported to bind to erythrocytes at about 106 LDL particles per RBC, which triggers reversible morphological changes in erythrocytes from discocytes to echinocytes and spherocytes with accompanying reduction in osmotic fragility [42].

With regard to plasma predictors of new-onset CCV events, we observed a predominance of ether PCs (PC–O) and PCs with odd carbon atom numbers that were positively associated with future cardiovascular episodes in dialysis patients. Corroborating our findings, increases in odd-carbon PCs at baseline were positively associated with future cardiovascular events over a follow-up period of 8 years [43]. Tham et al. reported a positive association between elevated circulating ether phospholipids in with cardiac hypertrophy [44,45]. Indeed, a majority of chronic renal failure patients have normocytic normochromic anaemia that leads to left ventricular dilation with compensatory hypertrophy [46,47]. Thus, we posit that the positive association between ether PCs with new-onset CCV events in maintenance hemodialysis patients might be an early indication of compensatory hypertrophy already present at baseline in these high-risk individuals. Of interest, our lipidomic analysis at molecular resolution revealed a predominance of polar lipids over neutral lipids that emerged as significant predictors of future cardiovascular events, which might partly explain conflicting preceding reports on the relationships between CCV outcome and traditional lipid measures (e.g.total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol) in dialysis patients.

In all, our findings support that baseline levels of both RBC and plasma lipids improved the prediction of incident CCV events in dialysis patients over a follow-up period of approximately seven years. Our results underscore the relevance of lipid metabolism, particularly polar phospholipids, in the development of vascular pathology in dialysis patients. The panel of lipid predictors presented is expected to facilitate identification of high-risk patients at baseline, allowing better patient management and adoption of alternative treatments, if available, in this subset of high-risk individuals. In particular, the strong associations between baseline RBC polar lipidome with future CCV complications illustrate that a prescreening on the biophysical and lipidomic profiles of erythrocytes might be valuable in early-stage hemodialysis patients. Our findings also highlight the role of erythrocytes in regulating vascular tone by modulating cell-cell interactions and redox balance, which subsequently influences the development of CCV complications in end-stage renal disease patients undergoing hemodialysis, and support the use of hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase inhibitors for the treatment of renal anemia to ameliorate the risk of CCV complications in these patients. Notwithstanding these findings, our study has limitations. First, our lipidomics-oriented investigation does not allow inference of causal associations, and mechanistic proof-of-concepts in animal/cell-based studies would be required to decipher how the precisely the polar lipid predictors participate in the initiation of cardiovascular pathology. Second, validation of these lipid predictors in larger cohorts of different ethnicities would strengthen the translational aspect and clinical utility of our prediction models reported herein.

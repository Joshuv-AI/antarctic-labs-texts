Continued Cemiplimab Beyond Progression With Addition of Chemotherapy in Patients With Advanced NSCLC With PD-L1 Expression of 50% or Higher: 5-Year Follow-Up From EMPOWER-Lung 1
Authors: Marina Chiara Garassino, Ana Baramidze, Saadettin Kilickap, Ahmet Sezer, Mustafa Özgüroğlu, Mahmut Gümüş, Igor Bondarenko, Miranda Gogishvili, Marina Nechaeva, Michael Schenker, Irfan Cicin, Gwo Fuang Ho, Yaroslav Kulyaba, Kasimova Zyuhal, Roxana-Ioana Scheusan, Xue Jia, Debra AG McIntyre, Stefanie Chua, Manika Kaul, Javier Perez, Israel Lowy, Frank Seebach, Eric Kim, Heather Magnan, Jean-François Pouliot
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTreatment options are limited for patients with advanced NSCLC who progress on anti–programmed cell death-ligand 1 (anti–PD-L1) monotherapy. Second-line platinum-based chemotherapy provides a short progression-free survival (PFS) of approximately 3 to 4 months. The EMPOWER-Lung 1 study allowed continued cemiplimab with the addition of platinum-based chemotherapy after progression as a second-line treatment (cemiplimab beyond progression [CBP]). Here, we report long-term outcomes in this setting.MethodsPatients (N = 712) with advanced NSCLC were randomized 1:1 to cemiplimab or histology-appropriate chemotherapy. This exploratory analysis evaluated efficacy and safety in the CBP setting in 73 patients (of 357 in the cemiplimab arm) who had at least one scan after progression on cemiplimab and received at least one dose of chemotherapy plus cemiplimab. Responses were assessed by an independent review committee against a new baseline, defined as the last scan before the initial dose of chemotherapy.ResultsIn the CBP setting, the objective response rate was 27.4%, the median duration of response was 11.5 months (95% confidence interval: 6.0–19.3), and median PFS was 6.4 months (95% confidence interval: 6.1–9.1). Median overall survival from the time of randomization was 27.4 months (including 15.1 mo after the addition of chemotherapy). CBP treatment benefit was observed irrespective of histology, PD-L1 levels, or response to initial first-line cemiplimab monotherapy. Grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 35.6% of patients, and immune-mediated adverse events occurred in 2.7% of patients.ConclusionsThis exploratory analysis revealed the clinical benefit of continuing cemiplimab after progression with the addition of chemotherapy, without new safety signals.Keywords: Lung cancer, Non–small cell, Cemiplimab, Chemotherapy, Disease progression, Programmed cell death protein 1

Introduction

Despite advancements in first-line (1L) treatment, a substantial proportion of patients with advanced NSCLC experience disease progression on anti–programmed cell death-ligand 1 (anti–PD-L1) monotherapy.1 An existing treatment option for patients who experience disease progression on anti–PD-L1 monotherapy is to discontinue immunotherapy and switch to platinum-doublet chemotherapy.2 Data, mostly from retrospective studies, reveal a trend toward increased overall survival (OS) with chemotherapy when given after immunotherapy in some patients with advanced NSCLC.3 Specifically, subsequent platinum-based chemotherapy offers progression-free survival (PFS) of approximately 3 to 4 months,2 underscoring a critical need for research to identify more effective treatment strategies. Given this context, the potential benefit of continuing immunotherapy treatment beyond progression warrants prospective investigation.

Cemiplimab is a fully human, hinge-stabilized programmed cell death protein 1 inhibitor approved for the first-line treatment of adult patients with advanced NSCLC with no EGFR, ALK, or ROS1 aberrations, as a single agent in tumors with high PD-L1 expression (tumor proportion score ≥50%) as determined by a U.S. Food and Drug Administration–approved test and in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy for any level of PD-L1 expression.4,5 The EMPOWER-Lung 1 study was designed to compare cemiplimab monotherapy with platinum-doublet chemotherapy in treatment-naive patients with advanced NSCLC with 50% or higher PD-L1 expression in tumor cells. A unique feature of the study was allowing continued treatment with cemiplimab, with the addition of four cycles of platinum-based chemotherapy, in patients with disease progression on cemiplimab. This allowed for an exploratory assessment of this potential alternative approach in the cemiplimab beyond progression (CBP) setting. Here, we report the results for patients who received cemiplimab treatment beyond progression with platinum-based chemotherapy, reflecting 5-year outcomes from the EMPOWER-Lung 1 trial.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Patients

Details of the EMPOWER-Lung 1 trial design and eligibility criteria have been previously reported.4,6 In brief, EMPOWER-Lung 1 was an open-label, randomized, global phase III trial (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03088540) of cemiplimab monotherapy versus investigator’s choice of histology-adjusted platinum-based chemotherapy in the 1L treatment of adult patients with locally advanced (not candidates for surgical resection or definitive chemoradiation) and metastatic treatment-naive NSCLC with PD-L1 50% or higher, an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1, and without EGFR, ALK, or ROS1 aberrations.4,6 Patients with clinically stable central nervous system metastases who were adequately treated, including those with controlled hepatitis B or C or human immunodeficiency virus, were eligible. Patients were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive cemiplimab 350 mg intravenously every 3 weeks for 108 weeks or until disease progression or histology-appropriate platinum-based chemotherapy. This trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Conference on Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice Guidelines. The study protocols and all amendments were approved by the institutional review board or independent ethics committee at each site. All patients provided written informed consent before enrollment.

The EMPOWER-Lung 1 study was designed to allow continuation of cemiplimab at the time of disease progression with the addition of up to four cycles of platinum-based chemotherapy, henceforth referred to as CBP treatment (Supplementary Fig. 1). Continuation of cemiplimab beyond Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1–defined progression with added histology-specific chemotherapy was prospectively defined, was available at all sites, and was permitted for patients meeting prespecified eligibility criteria (including independent review committee–confirmed progression and stable performance status). The exploratory analysis of this CBP period reported in this manuscript included patients who were originally enrolled in the cemiplimab monotherapy arm of the intention-to-treat (ITT) population and continued to receive therapy after disease progression. For inclusion in this analysis, patients needed to have received one dose of chemotherapy and at least one scan after disease progression (as confirmed by an independent committee) while on cemiplimab. This scan occurred before the initiation of chemotherapy and was designated as the new baseline for measuring the efficacy of cemiplimab plus chemotherapy in the CBP setting.

Procedures

All study procedures have been described in previous reports.4,6 Briefly, baseline assessments of PD-L1 expression were performed in paraffin-embedded, formalin-fixed tissue samples of tumors at a central laboratory using the 22C3 pharmDX assay (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA). All tumor responses were assessed by an independent review committee according to RECIST version 1.1.7 During the first year, patients were followed up every 2 to 4 weeks after the last study treatment, then after 6 weeks, and every 9 weeks thereafter. Then, patients were followed every 3 months for survival, as described previously. Tumor response in the CBP setting was also assessed by an independent review committee, using a new baseline defined as the last scan before the first dose of chemotherapy was added to the continuation of CBP. All adverse events and laboratory abnormalities were graded according to the National Criteria for Adverse Events version 4.03.

Outcomes

In the EMPOWER-Lung 1 study, the primary end points were OS and PFS. The key secondary end point was objective response rate (ORR), and the other secondary end points were duration of response (DOR) and safety.

For the exploratory analysis, the outcomes assessed included ORR and PFS during the initial cemiplimab monotherapy period and from the new baseline in the CBP setting. OS was assessed from randomization and from start of the CBP treatment. PFS was assessed until progression, as determined by the independent review committee (using RECIST 1.1) or death from any cause. DOR was assessed from the time of CBP response to progression or death from any cause. The best overall responses and treatment durations were summarized for individual patients, sorted by PD-L1 level, histology, and study duration in the CBP setting. Safety outcomes, including treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), and immune-mediated adverse events (imAEs), were also summarized during the initial cemiplimab monotherapy and from the start of the CBP treatment.

Statistical Analysis

For this analysis, OS, PFS, and DOR were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and log-log–based 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were provided. Patients without an OS event were censored at the time of last contact. Patients without a PFS event were censored at the time of last tumor assessment. The Clopper-Pearson method was used to estimate the associated 95% CIs of ORR. The swimmer plot was used to describe the best overall response, treatment duration, and survival status at the individual patient level, sorted by PD-L1 level, and the study duration in the CBP setting. Exposure duration and safety outcomes were reported descriptively. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS V.9.4 (SAS, Cary, NC). The data cutoff date was January 16, 2024, which reflects a 5-year update of the EMPOWER-Lung 1 study.

Results

Patient Baseline Characteristics and Disposition

Of the 357 patients randomized to receive cemiplimab (ITT), 85 received CBP treatment. Of those, 73 met all the criteria for this exploratory analysis of continuation of CBP (Supplementary Table 1).8 At CBP initiation (rebaseline scan), the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status was 0 in 29 patients (39.7%) and 1 in 44 patients (60.3%). Baseline characteristics were generally similar between the CBP set and the overall ITT analysis set (Table 1); however, as previously reported, the CBP set had a larger proportion of patients with squamous histology.6 For the 73 patients included in this analysis, the median duration of follow-up was 57.9 months (interquartile range [IQR]: 54.8–61.6); all patients had completed treatment within this period.

Efficacy Outcomes

Among the 73 patients who continued CBP with additional chemotherapy, the ORR was 27.4% (95% CI: 17.6–39.1; Fig. 1A), and the median DOR was 11.5 months (95% CI: 6.0–19.3; Fig. 1B). Median PFS was 6.4 months (95% CI: 6.1–9.1; Fig. 2), and median OS was 15.1 months (95% CI: 11.3–18.0).

In the same 73 patients in the first-line monotherapy setting, the ORR was 35.6% (95% CI: 24.7–47.7; Fig. 1A), the median DOR was 7.7 months (95% CI: 4.2–10.5; Fig. 1B), and median PFS was 4.8 months (95% CI: 4.2–6.2; Fig. 2). From initial randomization, median OS was 27.4 months (95% CI: 23.0–31.9), which included the above-mentioned median OS of 15.1 months observed in the CBP.

Patient-Level Treatment and Response

The overall treatment durations and tumor responses for all patients included in this analysis are found in the swimmer’s plot (Fig. 3). The tumor PD-L1 expression level, histology (squamous or nonsquamous), and best tumor response to the initial cemiplimab monotherapy and in the CBP setting are displayed for each patient. This plot reveals durable responses and long-term survival in the CBP setting, irrespective of responses in the first-line setting, PD-L1 expression levels, or histology. A proportion of patients at all PD-L1 levels have ongoing responses. Furthermore, a Sankey plot (revealing changes in best tumor responses in the entire treatment course) clearly illustrates durable responses in the CBP period, even in patients with progressive disease as their best response to first-line cemiplimab monotherapy (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Safety

All 73 patients included in the CBP setting were part of the safety analysis set, defined as patients who received at least one dose of the study treatment. No new safety signals were reported during this treatment phase.

In the CBP setting, the median treatment exposure duration was 26.7 weeks (IQR: 15.4–39.9), and TEAEs of grade 3 and above were observed in 35.6% of patients; TEAEs leading to study discontinuation occurred in 5.5% of patients, and TEAEs leading to death were reported in 4.1% of patients (Table 2). The most common all-grade TEAEs were anemia (34.2%), alopecia (24.7%), and diarrhea (21.9%; Fig. 4). TRAEs of grade 3 and above were observed in 19.2% of patients; TRAEs leading to study discontinuation occurred in 4.1% of patients, and no deaths were reported due to TRAEs (Table 2). Furthermore, imAEs of grade 3 or above were observed in 2.7% of patients, and imAEs leading to discontinuation were observed in 2.7% of patients; no deaths were reported due to imAEs (Table 2).

Discussion

Our analysis provides the first prospective outcomes of patients continuing immunotherapy with the addition of chemotherapy at the time of progression during the initial anti–PD-L1 monotherapy period. Despite some limitations described subsequently, the results indicate meaningful clinical benefits with durable responses and long-term survival with continued cemiplimab and the addition of chemotherapy in the CBP setting. OS from the start of cemiplimab at 27.4 months was comparable with the 23.4 months observed in the overall ITT analysis set, despite patients experiencing early progression on first-line therapy.8 It is noteworthy that durable responses and long-term survival were observed in all PD-L1 expression subcategories. The results after the addition of chemotherapy are encouraging, with median PFS of 6.4 months and OS of 15.1 months. Although cross-study comparisons should be interpreted with caution, these outcomes appear longer than those in previous studies where platinum-based chemotherapy was used after stopping programmed cell death protein 1 monotherapy, with observed median PFS of 4.1 months and median OS of 6.8 months.2 These results are particularly important at a historical moment when it is difficult to find an effective second-line treatment.

These observations from our data suggest that chemotherapy may allow us to uncover new neo-antigens, providing the immune system with new targets that were previously unavailable or unrecognized. This hypothesis is also supported by the longer DOR in the CBP setting at 11.5 months compared with the 7.7 months observed in the initial monotherapy period. Our findings are in line with prior reports suggesting that chemotherapy in combination with immunotherapy can induce an immune response due to increased immunogenicity and the inhibition of immunosuppressive cells.9, 10, 11, 12 These findings complement our previous results5,13 by adding chemotherapy after disease progression rather than as part of 1L treatment.

Overall, the baseline characteristics of the subgroup of patients included in this analysis are similar to those of the overall ITT analysis set. The most noticeable difference is the lower proportion of patients with PD-L1 tumor expression of 90% or higher, which represents only 21% of patients compared with 28% in the original trial population. This observation is not surprising, considering that the group with PD-L1 more than or equal to 90% has the best outcome with monotherapy and is therefore less likely to progress. Another noticeable variation is the enrichment in patients with squamous histology, likely reflecting the more aggressive nature of this subtype of tumors. Despite these differences, clinical benefits were observed in the CBP setting regardless of baseline PD-L1 expression level, histology, or outcome during the initial cemiplimab monotherapy period. These findings support the potential role of continuing immunotherapy beyond progression in select patients and highlight the need for prospective validation of this approach. Notably, the ongoing phase 3 INSIGNA trial (EA5163/S1709), which has completed enrollment, is a prospective study evaluating pembrolizumab monotherapy followed by chemotherapy with or without continued pembrolizumab after progression. Results from INSIGNA will help inform the clinical benefit of continuing first-line PD-1 inhibition beyond progression and help define its role in the management of advanced NSCLC.14

The safety profile in the combination phase was generally comparable to what was observed when cemiplimab was combined with chemotherapy in the 1L setting.13 Of note, no new imAEs or increase in the severity of known imAEs were reported with the addition of chemotherapy to cemiplimab.

Some key limitations are inherent to this analysis. Although this intervention was prospectively defined, its utilization was at the discretion of the investigator, potentially inducing a selection bias for patients with a better performance status, as indicated by increases in the proportion of patients who had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 at baseline in the beyond-progression data set. In particular, the lack of randomization in the CBP setting, due to the lack of a contemporaneous control arm, makes it difficult to rule out selection bias. Furthermore, given the exploratory nature of the analysis and the inclusion of a small subset of patients based on our eligibility criteria to receive CBP treatment, the findings reported should be interpreted with these limitations in mind.

In conclusion, the results from this prospective exploratory cohort suggest that patients with NSCLC with PD-L1 tumor expression of 50% or higher who start with monotherapy may be effectively salvaged with the addition of chemotherapy to cemiplimab at the time of disease progression. Therefore, the addition of chemotherapy only at the time of progression represents an alternative therapeutic option to upfront chemo-immunotherapy combination that may allow more patients to avoid chemotherapy without compromising survival.

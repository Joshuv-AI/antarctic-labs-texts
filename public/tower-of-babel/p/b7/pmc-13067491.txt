Real-world agreement in the ultrasound classification of subcapsular thyroid nodules: comparison with expert assessment
Authors: Pierpaolo Trimboli, Enrico Papini, Roberto Novizio, Maurilio Deandrea, Andrea Frasoldati, Cosimo Durante, Andrea Leoncini, Rinaldo Guglielmi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUltrasound (US) is the most accurate tool for the assessment of thyroid nodules (TNs). Despite the subcapsular position of TNs may represent a game changer for clinical practice in selecting cases for biopsy or surgery, this parameter is not included in US-based risk stratification systems. The present study evaluated the agreement of endocrinologists using US in clinical practice with the experts’ results employed as reference standard.MethodsA sub-series of TNs for which the agreement between US expert endocrinologists and radiologists was perfect was assumed as reference. Participants of the 2025 Thyroid Update of Associazione Medici Endocrinologi (AME) were asked to assess TNs in a 3-choice answer: subcapsular, non-subcapsular, or uncertain. The agreement was determined according to majority consensus (i.e., ratings > 50% = final consensus outcome) and Fleiss κ (from 0.1 to 1.0).ResultsThe series included 13 TNs (6/13 cancer, 7/13 echographically at high risk) whose median distance from the anterior and posterior thyroid capsule was 0 and 1.5 mm, respectively. One hundred and six endocrinologists (females, 62%; age, < 40 years 33%; specialized professionals, 81%) participated as raters. The TNs assessed by experts as subcapsular were judged as subcapsular in 90% to 97% of votes. The TNs assessed as non-subcapsular by experts were classified as non-subcapsular by 3% to 48% of votes. The majority consensus fully matched the experts’ assessment (κ = 1.0, p = 0.0003). The distance from the anterior thyroid capsule was an independent factor of assessment (p = 0.005, R2 = 0.86).ConclusionsClinical endocrinologists largely agree with experts’ assessment in subcapsular cases where experts had perfect concordance while uncertainty is present for non-subcapsular nodules. Future studies should establish a standardized definition of subcapsular TN.Keywords: Thyroid nodules, Ultrasound, TIRADS, Subcapsular location, FNAC

Introduction

Ultrasound (US) is an accurate and easily accessible imaging procedure for the first-line assessment of the risk of malignancy of thyroid nodules (TNs). Unfortunately, the level of agreement among the operators (either endocrinologists or radiologists) on the US features has been repeatedly demonstrated as unsatisfactory [1, 2]. For this reason, the major scientific societies have developed their own US-based risk stratification systems (RSS or TIRADS) [3–6] and an internationally endorsed RSS (I-TIRADS) is planned to improve the consistency of TNs risk assessment and optimize the indication for fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) [7–9]. In particular, the 2023 International Expert Consensus on Ultrasound Lexicon for Thyroid Nodules [7] was introduced to standardize the terminology used to describe TN ultrasound features, thereby improving consistency across RSSs and enhancing reproducibility in clinical practice and research.

Importantly, further issues which are relevant in suspicious nodules both for their clinical management and for their surgical approach still remain ill defined. Besides the unsettled problem of the US demonstration of minimal thyroid capsule invasion [10–12], the intra-thyroid subcapsular location of high-risk TNs is also clinically relevant. In fact, in case of subcapsular location, the indication for FNAC in worrisome TNs is more probable among clinicians and radiologists. Furthermore, both endocrinologists and surgeons are more inclined to recommend surgical intervention in subcapsular TNs with suspicious or malignant cytology, especially in case of a posterior subcapsular position, due to the higher risk of microscopically incomplete resection (R1 at post-surgical TNM staging) [13]. Accordingly, major consensus documents suggest that only patients diagnosed with non-subcapsular sub-centimetric papillary cancers may be considered for active surveillance [14].

Although subcapsular position of TNs may represent a game changer for clinical practice, presently the consensus documents which specifically define the main thyroid US features do not provide a conclusive description of this widely employed finding [7]. In addition, the possible TN extra-thyroid extension (ETE) is included only in one TIRADS [3], and the European and American clinical guidelines of TN and thyroid cancer include TN ETE—as risk factor for local invasion and then criterion to indicate for FNAC—but not TN subcapsular position [10, 11]. The potential consequences of this lack of definition were elicited by a recent study [15] that demonstrated an only fair inter-observer agreement (IOA) among thyroid US experts asked to classify a consecutive series of US images of TNs as in a subcapsular location. These considerations suggest an improvement in the consistency of thyroid US reporting and in the clinical interpretation of this finding, based on a standardized definition of the subcapsular position of TNs [7].

Real life data concerning the use by the endocrinologists of this US definition are presently lacking. Thus, the present study was aimed to evaluate the agreement in the assessment of the subcapsular location of TNs among a large number of clinical endocrinologists who use in-office US during their routine consultations. The results from a recent study conducted by international raters with specific US expertise [15] served as the reference standard. Participants in the 2025 annual Thyroid Update meeting of Associazione Medici Endocrinologi (AME) acted as observers in the present study.

Methods

Setting

AME includes over 2500 endocrinologists operating in the field of clinical practice and clinical research. Annually, AME organizes a thyroid-focused meeting including state of art lectures and debates of experts and a thyroid US training course for young endocrinologists. Conference sessions are highly interactive and may include live audience electronic voting.

Study design

The 2025 AME Thyroid Update featured a practical session in which those participants who routinely employ in-office thyroid US were asked to assess US images of TNs in a 3-choice answer: subcapsular, non-subcapsular, or uncertain intra-thyroid location. The present study focused on a subset of cases drawn from a previously published series evaluated by international expert raters [15]. In that reference study: (a) twelve thyroid US experts classified 60 TNs as subcapsular or not; (b) the raters were blinded to patients’ clinical data; and (c) the IOA was calculated according to nodule characteristics and rater features. For the current analysis, we selected the sub-series of 13 TNs for which the IOA between experts was perfect (Fleiss κ = 1.0) [16] and that was assumed as the gold-standard. Participants were blinded to the experts’ assessment. They preliminarily provided their demographic characteristics and their working setting and volume. Then, 13 frozen images of the 13 TNs (one image per TN) were showed to participants who assessed the TNs as subcapsular, non-subcapsular, or with uncertain location. Each vote was electronically collected after a time interval adequate for the correct evaluation and the resulting data were anonymously processed.

Ethics

The present study represents the extension of a previously approved protocol.

Ethics approval and written informed consent were obtained according to national regulations. Accordingly, patients gave written informed consent for inclusion. All procedures comply with the 1964 Helsinki Declaration.

Statistical analysis

The consensus category for each case was determined according to a majority rule, whereby the category receiving the highest number of identical ratings (> 50%) was considered the final consensus outcome (majority consensus). This data was then used to calculate the IOA between the two groups composed by thyroid US experts and clinical endocrinologists, respectively. For each TN US image, the IOA was estimated with the Fleiss κ [16] as follows: < 0.20: poor; between 0.21 and 0.40: fair; between 0.41 and 0.60: moderate; between 0.61 and 0.80: good; between 0.81 and 1.0: almost perfect. Multiple regression analysis was performed using subcapsular assessment as the dependent variable. The statistical analyses were performed with 2023 version of MedCalc Software Ltd (MedCalc Software Ltd) and 2025 rBiostatistics (https://adraptis.shinyapps.io/kappa_ver1/).

Results

As above stated, the series of 13 thyroid US images included six histologically proven cancers and seven benign nodules having a median size of 14 mm. The major distance from the anterior and posterior thyroid capsule were 1.8 mm and 3.5 mm, with median value 0 and 1.5 mm, respectively. There were six TNs classified at high risk according to EU-TIRADS and five of them were cancer.

One hundred and six endocrinologists participated in the live voting session. Their demographic data resulted as follows: females, 62%; age, < 40 years 33%; specialized professionals (i.e., specialized in endocrinology), 81%; performing thyroid US for at least five years, 58%; FNAC operators, 42%; following up thyroid cancer in their practice, 87%. At least 50% of the raters provided response for each of the 13 questions.

Overall, the percentage of votes for subcapsular TN of the study participants varied from 3% to 97%; in particular this percentage ranged from 90% to 97% in the subgroup of TNs previously assessed by experts as subcapsular and from 3% to 48% in the subgroup of cases that the experts retained as non-subcapsular. Discussion

The findings of the present study can be summarized as follows. Most of participants assessed as subcapsular the TNs that were previously judged as subcapsular by experts. When considering the majority consensus of the respondents, a perfect agreement was observed with the previous experts’ assessment [15]. For TNs that were defined as not subcapsular by the experts, a relevant inhomogeneity among raters was observed. The TN distance from the anterior thyroid capsule was the only independent factor associated with the subcapsular definition by both experts and clinicians, while nodule size and US risk of malignancy did not significantly influence the decision.

In clinical practice, subcapsular location favours the indication for FNAC in TNs which are at medium or high US risk of malignancy. Also, when FNAC results in an indeterminate or suspicious cytology in subcapsular TNs, the indication for surgery is more accurately considered because of the higher risk of capsular invasion and microscopically incomplete cancer resection. However, the definition of subcapsular position of TNs has not been conclusively addressed. The European Thyroid Association [4, 10] proposes a risk grading system limited to the definition of the potential ETE, delineating scenarios in which it can be ruled out, or considered possible, probable, or definite. However, while ETE is incorporated as a suspicious feature in ACR TI-RADS [3], the other systems do not include this parameter in its risk stratification algorithm; and no system specifically considers subcapsular location as an independent feature Thus, the reliability and clinical validity of these definitions remain to be fully established.

A recent paper that investigated the IOA in the US assessment of the subcapsular position of TNs among an international panel of thyroid US experts demonstrated a rather unsatisfactory result in a large and heterogeneous series of cases [15]. However, data concerning the approach to this issue by the endocrinologists operating in everyday practice are not available. Thus, the present study was aimed at evaluating the agreement in the assessment of the subcapsular location of TNs among the endocrinologists who generally perform in-office thyroid US during their everyday activity. Notably, in the previous study, a perfect agreement (κ = 1.0) was attained from the experts panel in a minority of cases (13 cases, 21.7%). Due to the lack of an acknowledged definition of this issue, this series of cases was assumed as a reference standard and was employed to verify the level of agreement between clinical endocrinologists. The participants can be assumed as representative of the real life as the majority of respondents were female, with an US experience greater than 5 year and only a part of them performed US-guided FNAC,

As discussed in a previous publication [15], although identifying TNs as subcapsular may represent a potential game-changer in clinical practice, a standardized definition of this feature is still lacking. In that study, the overall interobserver agreement (IOA) was fair (κ = 0.34), with the highest κ value (0.46) observed in the subgroup of TNs < 2 cm and classified as high-risk according to EU-TIRADS. The present study corroborates and extends those findings. The agreement previously observed among experts for a specific subset of TNs was confirmed here in a different context of specialists operating in clinical practice. Most raters in the current study assessed subcapsular TNs in agreement with expert evaluations but uncertainty emerged for those which were not. These results are noteworthy given the study population, that included (1) both junior and senior operators, specialists and endocrinology residents, with varying levels of experience in thyroid US and FNAC practice; and (2) a heterogeneous spectrum of TNs, including malignant and benign, large and small, very low- to high-risk lesions, with or without FNAC indication. Because this setting may be assumed to mirror real-world clinical practice, the present and previous findings together may pave the way for further investigations on subcapsular TNs.

Although the results of the present study cannot be generalized, some implications for clinical practice can be considered. Standardization of the thyroid US lexicon is a current topic of interest [7], and the use of appropriate and homogeneous terminology may improve communication among thyroid specialists as well as researchers. In addition, with the emergence of alternative management strategies to surgery for thyroid cancer—such as active surveillance and thermal ablation—interdisciplinary consensus is needed on the parameters that inform clinical decision-making [17]. In this context, the present findings can be of interest to both operators in clinical practice and authors of research.

The present study has limitations. First, raters evaluated static US images (one image per TN), which are inherently less reliable than real-time examinations. Second, no predefined operational definition of “subcapsular location” was available, and thus the results may reflect the variability among raters and their individual interpretations. Third, only 50% of the raters provided assessment for all cases and this may affect generalizability of results. Fourth, the sample size was small. Fifth, possible selection biases cannot be excluded. Most of the above limitations derived from the series collected for the experts’ paper [15] and could not be cancelled. As major study strength, the raters were blinded to the experts’ assessment. In addition, the study design included the evaluation of TN data that could have influenced the raters’ opinion (e.g., diameter, distance from the thyroid capsule).

In conclusion, this study found that clinical endocrinologists largely agree with expert assessments in cases where experts themselves demonstrated perfect interobserver concordance. The proximity of TNs to the anterior capsule appears to play a key role in this agreement. Thus, due to the present availability of high-resolution US equipment, the measurement of the distance of the nodule margins from the anterior and posterior capsule might be registered in worrisome lesions and a threshold for the subcapsular definition could be tentatively suggested. Future studies should aim to establish a standardized, clinically applicable definition of subcapsular thyroid nodules.

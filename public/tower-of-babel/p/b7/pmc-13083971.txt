AI-guided surgical decision support system for corneal laceration repair: a prospective, non-randomized controlled feasibility study
Authors: Jiusheng Zheng, Huamei Lu, Yiyi Chen, Wenting Liu, Wei Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To evaluate the feasibility and application value of a transfer learning-based artificial intelligence (AI) system for wound recognition and suture position planning in patients with corneal lacerations. In this prospective, non-randomized controlled feasibility study, 25 consecutive patients were managed with either conventional suturing (n = 14) or AI-guided planning (n = 11) based on real-time system availability. The AI system automatically identified corneal laceration images and generated suture points. Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA, logMAR) and cylinder (Cyl, diopters (D)) were recorded. Anterior corneal surface parameters, including corneal surface variation index (ISV), vertical asymmetry index (IVA), high asymmetry index (IHA), high eccentricity index (IHD), and minimum curvature radius (Rmin), were measured using Pentacam. All patients were followed up for at least 6 months. The transfer learning model achieved over 85% accuracy and sensitivity in corneal laceration recognition. AI guidance significantly improved visual outcomes, with a higher BCVA (0.677 vs. 0.331 logMAR, P < 0.05) and reduced astigmatism (Cyl: 2.023 D vs. 3.542 D, P < 0.05) compared to conventional suturing, and improved ISV, IVA, and IHA (all P < 0.05) compared to the conventional group. This feasibility study provides preliminary evidence that transfer learning-based wound recognition and suture position planning can be applied to corneal laceration treatment, suggesting its potential to provide more precise and effective suturing strategies for corneal trauma patients. Further validation with larger cohorts is warranted.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42805-4.

Introduction

Corneal laceration is a common and potentially vision-threatening ocular trauma requiring emergency suturing to restore globe integrity. Accurate wound identification and precise suturing are critical for visual preservation; however, irregular wound apposition and misalignment of corneal layers often lead to significant scarring and astigmatism, compromising visual function and increasing the risk of secondary procedures. The morphological characteristics of the anterior corneal surface post-injury strongly influence visual outcomes, but the variability in wound morphology and severity poses substantial challenges for surgeons to achieve optimal alignment within the constrained time of emergency treatment.

Post-traumatic irregular astigmatism remains a major cause of visual impairment after corneal laceration repair, with reported reoperation rates ranging from 15 to 30% in severe cases. Current surgical outcomes are highly dependent on surgeon experience, leading to variability in visual recovery. Computer-assisted suturing systems have emerged as promising tools to standardize surgical planning and improve anatomical and functional outcomes.

This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement1 and, where applicable, the Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis Or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) guidelines2.

Our team previously developed an AI model for automatic identification of porcine corneal lacerations and suture point generation using 2400 porcine corneal images3. Trained on porcine data, this model provides valuable prior knowledge and data augmentation for human corneal wound analysis. However, direct application of U-Net-based fully supervised learning or Mean-Teacher-based semi-supervised learning (developed for porcine corneas) to human cases is limited. Transfer learning, with its ability to leverage knowledge across domains, offers a promising solution for more accurate human corneal wound recognition and personalized suture planning4.

This study aims to optimize an AI model for human corneal laceration management through a two-step process: automated wound identification and suture position prediction, thereby supporting clinical decision-making in precise suturing.

Materials and methods

Study design and ethical considerations

This was a prospective, non-randomized controlled feasibility study. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Suqian First People’s Hospital (No.2023-SL-0008) and was conducted per the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all patients or their families. The Ethics Committee waived the requirement for full clinical trial registration as this study was classified as an evaluation of a surgical planning tool that did not modify the standard therapeutic surgical protocol.

Construction method of AI model for suturing porcine corneal laceration

Semantic feature extraction of medical images based on transfer learning

Transduction transfer learning5(a technique for adapting models from a source to a target domain) was employed to migrate semantic features from standard image domains to medical images, forming the basis of the deep network model.

Cross-modal feature learning with generative adversarial networks

The classical generative adversarial network (GAN) consists of a generator G and a discriminator D. We focused on cross-modal feature learning based on generative adversarial, by redesigning the generator and discriminator so that they can fuse the medical image data and the corresponding diagnostic report into a feature subspace, and the discriminator would try to distinguish the two in the feature subspace found by the generator, and use this result to continuously adjust the feature map of the generator, so that the feature subspace can better maintain the commonality of the two, until the discriminator cannot distinguish between the two. At this time, image features fused with medical semantics can be extracted from the image data.

Fully supervised corneal wound segmentation

The image segmentation technology based on CNN is used to automatically segment the corneal wound, and the segmentation network adopts U-Net.

Transfer learning network structure

The U-Net framework consisted of an encoding stage (four encoder modules) for feature extraction and a decoding stage (four decoder modules) for feature reconstruction. Each encoder module included two 3 × 3 convolutions, a ReLU activation function, and 2 × 2 max-pooling, doubling the number of channels and halving resolution with each step. Each decoder module included two 3 × 3 convolutions, ReLU activation, and upsampling, halving channels and doubling resolution. Skip connections between corresponding encoder and decoder layers supplemented missing information during upsampling.

Dataset preparation and annotation protocol

Porcine corneal images(n = 2400) were acquired using slit lamp photography under standardized conditions. For human data, Labelme software was used by a team of three senior ophthalmologists to annotate corneal wound regions with polygons, generating high-quality training data with precise wound contours. The annotation and model training focused specifically on the corneal tissue and did not include dedicated labeling or classification for wounds involving the limbus or sclera. Inter-rater reliability was assessed with a Dice coefficient of > 0.85 between annotators. The annotators were blinded to patient outcomes.

Experimental details and model transparency

The AI model was implemented in Python using TensorFlow.The U-Net backbone was initialized with ImageNet weights. Hyperparameters were: learning rate = 0.001, batch size = 16, epochs = 100, Adam optimizer (beta1 = 0.9, beta2 = 0.999), and a combined BCE+Dice loss function (coefficients 1:1). Data augmentation included rotation, flipping, and brightness adjustment.

In the training stage, 300 human corneal laceration images annotated by the same team of senior ophthalmologists using Labelme software were used as the training set, and 40 images as the validation set.

In the inference phase, 40 images of the human cornea were used as the test set for testing. In the training stage of the model, the human corneal image and the manually labeled wound image are input into the network, and the network will eventually output the predicted result of the corneal wound.

In order to validate the effectiveness of the model trained on porcine corneal irregular wound data in real-world applications, we conducted a series of experimental tests, the most critical of which is to test on real human corneal wound samples. The experimental results are shown in Fig. 1, and we can see that when the model was migrated to human corneal wound data, the model showed good segmentation accuracy on some data, showing the potential of the model for cross-species applications.

Segmentation performance metrics

Manual segmentation served as the gold standard. The U-Net model was compared with three classical semantic segmentation models (AttU-Net, SegNet, TransUNet) using five metrics: Dice similarity coefficient (DSC), accuracy (ACC), precision (PRE), sensitivity (SEN), and Jaccard similarity coefficient (JI).

Suture position planning

Algorithm design

The algorithm was designed for linear, full-thickness wounds. The suture type used was 10-0 nylon.Corneal wound labels and contour labels were input to calculate the area ratio (contour area/incision area).Needle length and span were determined based on the number of sutures (Table 1). The area ratio is defined as the contour area divided by the incision area, calculated using pixel counts from the segmented images.

Detailed definitions and calculation method of the area ratio:

Wound (Incision): Refers to the full-thickness traumatic defect of the corneal tissue, corresponding to the pixel coverage of the actual laceration gap in segmented images, excluding intact corneal tissue.

Contour: Denotes the boundary of intact corneal tissue adjacent to the wound, specifically the stromal region within a 1-mm buffer zone surrounding the incision boundary (a distance aligned with surgical principles, as sutures are typically placed 0.5–1 mm from the wound edge).

The area ratio is computed via pixel-level analysis of standardized corneal images (calibrated to 0.01 mm/pixel for consistent physical size conversion):

Incision Area (A_incision): Count the pixels of the segmented laceration gap (binary mask: 1 = incision, 0 = non-incision) and convert to mm2 using the calibrated resolution.

Contour Area (A_contour): Count the pixels of the 1-mm buffer zone surrounding the incision (binary mask of intact corneal tissue) and convert to mm2.

Ratio Calculation: Area Ratio = A_contour/A_incision.

This metric reflects the “tissue reserve” surrounding the wound, guiding suture number selection: higher ratios indicate more intact tissue (wider suture spacing), while lower ratios require more sutures to ensure stable closure without excessive tension.

Study participants and surgical procedures

Participants

Informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians, explicitly covering the use of AI-guided surgical planning and the use of anonymized corneal images for research and model development. All images were de-identified prior to analysis to protect patient privacy.

From September 2021 to November 2023, 25 patients with corneal lacerations who underwent primary suturing at the Department of Ophthalmology, Suqian First People’s Hospital, and Lixiang Eye Hospital of Soochow University were enrolled.

Assignment to the AI-guided or conventional group was prospective and non-randomized, based on the operating surgeon’s decision to utilize or not utilize the AI surgical decision-support system. The AI surgical decision-support system was available for use at all times and was not restricted to specific working hours. For patients in the AI-guided group, the surgeon actively accessed the system and integrated its real-time suture planning recommendations into the surgical decision-making process. For the conventional group, the surgeon performed the surgery relying solely on standard techniques and personal expertise, without invoking the AI system. The decision to use AI support was at the surgeon’s discretion and was not influenced by the time of day or system availability. Surgical timing for all patients was determined solely by clinical urgency and operating room logistics.

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Full-thickness corneal laceration; (2) Primary suture procedure; (3) Ability to fixate on a red light target.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Concurrent keratitis, corneal ulcer, or purulent endophthalmitis; (2) Systemic diseases affecting ocular health; (3) Other conditions predisposing to severe corneal astigmatism.

Participants were prospectively assigned to either the conventional suturing group (n = 14) or the AI-guided group (n = 11). Baseline characteristics, including age, sex, affected eye, surgical method (corneal suture alone vs. combined with vitrectomy), and wound length, were comparable between groups (P > 0.05) (Table 2). Standardized Mean Differences (SMD) for all baseline characteristics were < 0.5, indicating good balance.

Furthermore, the topographic maps of all uninjured fellow eyes were reviewed and confirmed the absence of clinical signs of corneal abnormalities (such as keratoconus or significant irregular astigmatism). This supports the assumption that the injured corneas were likely normal prior to the trauma and establishes a comparable baseline between the two study groups.

Corneal laceration suture surgery

Corneal images for AI analysis were obtained preoperatively using a slit lamp camera and Pentacam Scheimpflug imaging.

All patients underwent primary corneal suturing within 2 days of injury by the same team of senior ophthalmologists under anesthesia.All surgeries were performed under local anesthesia (peribulbar block) with sedation.In the conventional group, suturing was performed using standard interrupted techniques to achieve watertight closure. In the AI-guided group, surgeons accessed the AI system to generate a real-time suture planning for intraoperative decision support. Real-time suture analysis refers to the AI system providing suture planning based on initial preoperative wound assessment, without intraoperative reassessment after each suture. Surgeons placed 92% of sutures per the AI plan on average.

Postoperatively, Best corrected visual acuity (BCVA, logMAR) and cylinder (Cyl, D) were recorded, and anterior corneal parameters corneal surface variation index (ISV), vertical asymmetric index (IVA), highly asymmetric index (IHA), high eccentricity index (IHD), minimum curvature radius (Rmin) were measured using Pentacam(Oculus Optikgeräte GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany). Follow-up examinations, including Pentacam, were performed at 4–8 weeks (median 6 weeks) after complete suture removal. Follow-up duration was ≥ 6 months. Statistical analysis

Prior to study initiation, a post-hoc power analysis was conducted using G Power 3.1 software to determine the sample size required for detecting significant differences in the primary endpoint (logMAR BCVA) between the two groups. Based on preliminary data from pilot studies, assuming a mean difference in BCVA of 0.3 logMAR, a standard deviation of 0.25, a significance level (α) of 0.05, and a power (1−β) of 0.8, the calculated minimum sample size was 22 patients. Considering potential dropout, we enrolled 25 patients (14 in the conventional group and 11 in the AI-guided group), which met the required sample size to ensure adequate statistical power for detecting clinically meaningful differences.

The primary endpoint was logMAR BCVA at ≥ 1 month after suture removal. Secondary endpoints included Cyl, ISV, IVA, IHA, IHD, and Rmin. A post-hoc precision analysis confirmed the effect sizes had 95% confidence intervals excluding the null for key outcomes, though the small sample size is acknowledged.

All data were analyzed and processed using SPSS 17.0 statistical software, and the measurement data of the study data were recorded in the form of mean ± SD or average, and plotted with GraphPad Prism 8. All data are represented by mean ± SD if they conform to normal distribution, and median (min-max) if they do not conform to normal distribution. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to determine whether the data between the control group and the experimental group were normal, and the independent samples t-test was used to meet the normality. If not, the Mann–Whitney U test is used. Multiple comparisons were adjusted using the Holm–Bonferroni method where appropriate. The age, sex composition ratio, ocular composition ratio, whether corneal laceration suture was combined with vitrectomy, corneal laceration wound length, average needle spacing, BCVA, Cyl, morphologic parameters of anterior corneal surface between the direct corneal laceration suture group and the AI-guided corneal laceration suture group were compared. The difference was statistically significant, with P < 0.05 as the difference.Effect sizes with 95% Confidence Intervals (CI) are reported for all between-group comparisons.

Optimization of post-processing strategies and performance improvements

In image segmentation tasks, post-processing is an important step to improve the final result of segmentation. Here’s a description of this post-processing process: Extract Maximum Connected Domain: The purpose of this step is to identify and preserve the largest connected objects in the image, while ignoring other smaller connected regions that may be caused by noise or other non-target objects. Perform a morphological closure operation: expand the object pixels in the image into adjacent background pixels by expanding the pixels in the image, so as to connect the approaching objects and fill in the small holes. The corrosion operation is then performed to reduce the pixel boundaries of the objects in the expanded image inward, which helps to eliminate the small irregular edges that can occur during the expansion process. Post-processing (maximum connected domain extraction followed by morphological closing and erosion) significantly improved segmentation accuracy, with the Dice coefficient increasing from 80.89 to 86.22% (Table 5).

Evaluation of the results of the suture point prediction experiment

Experimental results show that the optimal needle distance obtained by random search can significantly improve the prediction effect of suture points, which verifies the effectiveness and practicability of the method. As shown in Fig. 4, a needle spacing of 0.80 mm outperformed 0.90 mm, particularly in capturing triangular wound vertices.

We propose a method for optimizing surgical wound suture, which calculates the area ratio of the incision to the surrounding tissue contour, sets the needle distance and span corresponding to the number of sutures, extracts the center line of the incision and locates the reference points on it according to the needle distance, constructs the normal from these reference points and determines the intersection point of the normal and the contour, and finally moves the set span of each intersection point outward along the normal direction, so as to accurately demarcate the position of the suture point for suturing. Comparisons between suturing groups

The uninjured eyes of both the conventional and AI-guided groups showed no statistically significant differences in any of the measured parameters, including corneal morphological indices (ISV, IVA, IHA, IHD, Rmin), BCVA, and Cyl (all P > 0.05), confirming comparable baseline corneal characteristics between the two groups prior to intervention.

There were statistically significant differences in BCVA, Cyl and the average distance between needles between the direct corneal laceration suture group and the AI-guided corneal laceration suture group (Table 8). Average BCVA of the direct corneal laceration suture group was 0.331 (0.04–0.8), while average BCVA of the AI-guided corneal laceration suture group was 0.677 (0.05–1) (P < 0.05). Compared with the average Cyl 3.542 (1.58–6.37) of the direct corneal laceration suture group, the average Cyl of the AI-guided corneal laceration suture group reduced to 2.023 (1.25–3.75) (P < 0.05). Significant differences were also found in ISV, IVA, IHA and the average distance in injured eye between the direct corneal laceration suture group and AI-guided corneal laceration suture group (all P < 0.05).

Higher-order aberrations (HOAs), including coma, spherical aberration, and trefoil, were not quantified in this study due to constraints in the initial study design. As key parameters reflecting fine corneal optical quality, HOAs are closely associated with postoperative visual comfort (e.g., halos, glare, and contrast sensitivity). These indicators will be integrated into subsequent studies to provide a more comprehensive assessment of surgical outcomes.

Postoperative complications

A total of 14 patients in the control group, one of whom had shallow anterior chamber after corneal suture and aqueous humor leakage at corneal laceration on the second day after surgery, and was reoperated and re-sutured. A total of 11 patients in the AI-guided corneal laceration suture group had their corneas closed after surgery, and there was no aqueous leakage after corneal suture. All patients did not have serious complications such as corneal infection and endophthalmitis after surgery.Operative time was comparable between groups.

Discussion

This feasibility study provides preliminary evidence that we systematically compared different loss functions, neural network model structures, and post-processing optimization in the transfer learning process, aiming to evaluate and determine the optimal model for human corneal wound segmentation. The porcine cornea was chosen as the pre-training dataset because of the high similarity between the porcine cornea and the human cornea in terms of anatomical structure and physiological characteristics. This allows models trained on porcine corneal laceration images to learn generic features associated with human corneal lacerations. Through the learning of the U-net model, the model can extract the key information such as the shape, location and size of porcine corneal laceration, which provides a solid foundation for subsequent transfer learning. The advantage of transfer learning is that it can effectively transfer the knowledge learned by the pre-trained model from porcine corneal laceration images to the recognition task of human corneal laceration images6. On the model trained on fundus images, transfer learning was used to identify corneal lesions, and the results showed that transfer learning could significantly improve the recognition accuracy of the model7. In addition, there are studies using transfer learning to diagnose eye diseases such as glaucoma, which have also achieved good results8. These studies not only proved the applicability of transfer learning in the field of ophthalmology, but also provided strong support for the strategy of this study to train the model using porcine corneal laceration images and migrate to human corneal laceration recognition. In medical research, the acquisition of high-quality annotated data is often costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive. Especially in the field of ophthalmology, the acquisition of large-scale labeled data is particularly difficult due to the scarcity of corneal laceration cases and the specialization of labeling. Therefore, how to effectively use the limited annotated data for efficient model training is an urgent problem to be solved in the field of ophthalmology. This study is an effective response to this problem by using porcine corneal laceration images for pre-training and combining transfer learning with fine-tuning on human corneal laceration images. At the same time, this study also makes full use of the advantages of AI technology. Through the powerful feature extraction ability and knowledge transfer ability of transfer learning of the deep learning model, the accurate identification of human corneal laceration wounds and the accurate prediction of suture points can be achieved under the condition of limited labeled data. This not only improves the accuracy and efficiency of diagnosis, but also provides ophthalmologists with a powerful auxiliary tool to help improve the diagnosis and treatment of ophthalmic diseases.

Firstly, in this study, when training the human corneal laceration recognition and suture point generation model through transfer learning method, the hybrid BCE and Dice loss function was employed to balance classification accuracy and boundary segmentation9, focusing not only on the classification accuracy of the wound area, but also on the fine segmentation of the wound boundary. This hybrid loss function strategy helps the model to achieve better performance under the challenges of morphological diversity and boundary fuzziness.

Then, we conducted comparative experiments with different model structures, including U-Net, AttU_Net, SegNet, and TransUNet10–12. After several iterations of training and validation, the U-Net model showed that it has a better treatment effect on corneal wound data, with a Dice coefficient of 80.89%, and a higher SEN (83.82%) and JI index (68.55%) than other models. At the same time, the Adam optimizer13, which is widely used in the training of deep learning models, was introduced to improve the performance of the model.

Considering all the experimental results, we are convinced that the model built with a combination of hybrid BCE, Dice loss function, U-Net, and the use of the Adam optimizer and sophisticated post-processing techniques is the optimal choice for this specific task, which not only exhibits superior performance, but also provides good generalization ability, making it an ideal model for such tasks14.

It is critical to define the specific intended use of the developed AI system. The model was trained and validated specifically on images of acute, full-thickness corneal lacerations resulting from trauma. Its core function is to segment the irregular, jagged edges of such traumatic wounds to guide suture planning. Therefore, it is not designed for, and would not be expected to accurately detect, a standard clear corneal incision from cataract surgery. These surgical wounds possess fundamentally different features: they are typically linear, self-sealing, and created under controlled conditions, whereas traumatic lacerations are irregular and unpredictable.

Evaluation of the effect of corneal laceration after suturing is essential to guide subsequent treatment and assess the quality of surgery. In modern medical practice, several indicators have been used to comprehensively evaluate the effect of corneal laceration sutures, including corneal topography parameters such as ISV, IVA, IHA, IHD, Rmin, and optical performance parameters such as astigmatism values (K2-K1), BCVA, SE, and Cyl in the range of 1 mm to 8 mm15,16. These indicators reflect the changes in corneal morphology and optical properties from different perspectives, which are of great value for evaluating the surgical effect.

In this study, patients with corneal laceration were divided into two groups, the observation group was the patient who directly underwent corneal laceration suture, and the experimental group was the patient who underwent suture under the guidance of the AI-guided system of the corneal laceration suture program. As shown in Table 2, there were no statistically significant differences in preoperative baseline characteristics between the control and AI-guided groups (P > 0.05). Furthermore, assessment of the uninjured fellow eyes confirmed comparable baseline corneal morphology and visual function between the groups. Through Tables 6 and 7, we found that there was a statistically significant difference between the injured eye group and the uninjured eye group between the two groups (P < 0.05), and the parameters of corneal morphology of the patients were larger than those of the uninjured eye group, the Rmin was smaller than that of the uninjured eye group, the Cyl was larger than that of the uninjured eye group, and the BCVA was worse than that of the uninjured eye group, indicating that corneal laceration is a disease that seriously threatens the vision of patients, and the recovery of vision of patients will be affected to varying degrees after trauma. Notably, in the AI-guided group, the gap between injured and uninjured eyes in multiple parameters was narrower compared to the conventional group, which further suggests that the AI-guided system contributes to more effective restoration of corneal function. Specifically, the IHA in the AI-guided injured eye group showed no statistically significant difference from the uninjured eye group (P = 0.138), whereas in the conventional group, the difference was significant (P < 0.05), implying that AI guidance may have a unique advantage in maintaining corneal high-order symmetry.

As shown in Table 8, there were statistically significant differences between the ISV, IVA, IHA, Rmin, BCVA and Cyl of the injured eye group in the control group and the injured eye group in the experimental group (P < 0.05), and the experimental group with obvious indicators was better than the control group, indicating that the surgical method of suturing under the guidance of the AI-guided system of the corneal laceration suture program was better than the surgical method of direct corneal laceration suture, and the patients had smaller postoperative Cyl, corneal morphological parameters and better recovery of postoperative BCVA. There was no significant statistical difference in IHD (P > 0.05), which may be related to the small sample size we selected in this study. Additionally, the average suture spacing in the AI-guided group was larger (1.226 mm vs. 0.851 mm, P < 0.05), which may be due to the model’s rational calculation based on wound characteristics, avoiding excessive suturing that could lead to increased corneal tension and irregular astigmatism. This optimal spacing might contribute to more balanced wound healing and reduced scarring.

On the other hand, we found that 1 out of 14 patients in the control group had a wound that was still leaking after suturing and was sutured again the next day, while there was no leakage in the AI-guided corneal laceration suture group and the corneal tear closed well after suturing. Therefore, we compared the average needle spacing after suturing again, and found that there was a statistical difference between the direct corneal laceration suture group and the AI-guided corneal laceration suture group (P < 0.05), and the direct corneal laceration suture group had a shorter suture spacing. Shorter suture distance is generally correlated with the subjective judgment of the suturing physician. Due to the fear of aqueous leakage and poor closure of corneal laceration, the suture distance may be artificially shortened, so that due to the uneven distribution of tension at the wound, the healing was poor, resulting in heavier corneal scarring, greater astigmatism, and poor postoperative vision recovery. In general, high-order features from a single linear corneal laceration image are valuable biomarkers in an intelligent multimodal analytic strategy of corneal laceration reconstruction, which provides a potential solution for clinical scenarios. The major advantage of this AI-model is that it avoids the risk of human judgment error on the suture distance, thereby making the suture distribution more uniform and the postoperative recovery better.

This study has several limitations. First, although the post-hoc power analysis confirmed that the enrolled sample size was sufficient to detect significant differences in the primary endpoint (BCVA), the sample size is relatively limited, which may affect the generalizability of the findings, and future multi-center studies with larger cohorts are needed to confirm these results. Second, the non-randomized design, where allocation was based on the surgeon’s discretion to use the AI system, represents a potential source of selection bias. Surgeons may have been more or less inclined to use the AI tool for certain wound types or complexities, which could confound the outcome comparison despite balanced baseline characteristics. Future randomized controlled trials are necessary to definitively assess the efficacy of the AI guidance and eliminate this bias. Third, the AI system was trained on static images without slit beam assessment, whereas ophthalmologists use dynamic slit lamp examination to assess depth. This limits the AI’s ability to evaluate laceration depth, which logically could affect ideal suture spacing and depth. The algorithm was primarily designed for linear wounds, and its performance on oblique or multi-lobed lacerations requires further investigation. Finally, the current AI model is tailored for corneal lacerations and was not trained to segment or plan sutures for wounds extensively involving the limbus or sclera. Expanding the model’s capability to handle corneoscleral lacerations represents an important future direction.

An additional limitation is the absence of higher-order aberration (HOA) data, such as coma, spherical aberration, and trefoil. Beyond conventional metrics (BCVA, astigmatism, and corneal topographic parameters), HOAs are pivotal for evaluating subtle perturbations in visual quality, as they directly modulate patients’ postoperative visual experience—including symptoms of halos, glare, and diminished contrast sensitivity. Owing to the initial study’s focus on core functional and structural endpoints, HOA measurements via wavefront aberrometry were not incorporated. Future investigations will integrate HOA assessments to elucidate the impact of AI-guided suturing on fine corneal optical performance, thereby strengthening the clinical evidence base for this technology.

In future studies, we can further analyze patients with corneal lacerations who were sutured under the guidance of the AI decision-making system but did not have good postoperative results, and further improve the model by combining images and information from other dimensions such as anterior segment OCT. Moreover, exploring the application of the model in complex wound types, such as irregular or multi-lobed lacerations, and integrating real-time feedback during surgery to adjust suture plans dynamically could further enhance its clinical utility.

Translational Relevance Statement: The AI system developed herein represents a significant step toward translational ophthalmic research, effectively converting algorithmic predictions into actionable surgical guidance. By validating the model in a clinical setting, we demonstrate its potential to reduce variability in suture placement, improve corneal topography outcomes, and enhance visual recovery—key factors in bridging the gap between experimental AI research and routine clinical adoption.

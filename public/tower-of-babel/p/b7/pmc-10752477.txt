A multicenter study of the clinicopathological characteristics and a risk prediction model of early-stage breast cancer with hormone receptor-positive/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-low expression
Authors: Ling Xin, Qian Wu, Chongming Zhan, Hongyan Qin, Hongyu Xiang, Min Gao, Xuening Duan, Yinhua Liu, Jingming Ye, Jinjiao Li, Yuanyuan Ji
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:In light of the significant clinical benefits of antibody-drug conjugates in clinical trials, the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-low category in breast cancers has gained increasing attention. Therefore, we studied the clinicopathological characteristics of Chinese patients with hormone receptor (HR)-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer and developed a recurrence risk prediction model.Methods:Female patients with HR-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer treated in 29 hospitals of the Chinese Society of Breast Surgery (CSBrS) from Jan 2015 to Dec 2016 were enrolled. Their clinicopathological data and prognostic information were collected, and machine learning methods were used to analyze the prognostic factors.Results:In total, 25,096 patients were diagnosed with breast cancer in 29 hospitals of CSBrS from Jan 2015 to Dec 2016, and clinicopathological data for 6486 patients with HER2-low early-stage breast cancer were collected. Among them, 5629 patients (86.79%) were HR-positive. The median follow-up time was 57 months (4, 76 months); the 5-year disease-free survival (DFS) rate was 92.7%, and the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate was 97.7%. In total, 412 cases (7.31%) of metastasis were observed, and 124 (2.20%) patients died. Multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that T stage, N stage, lymphovascular thrombosis, Ki-67 index, and prognostic stage were associated with recurrence and metastasis (P <0.05). A recurrence risk prediction model was established using the random forest method and exhibited a sensitivity of 81.1%, specificity of 71.7%, positive predictive value of 74.1%, and negative predictive value of 79.2%.Conclusion:Most of patients with HER2-low early-stage breast cancer were HR-positive, and patients had favorable outcome; tumor N stage, lymphovascular thrombosis, Ki-67 index, and tumor prognostic stage were prognostic factors. The HR-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer recurrence prediction model established based on the random forest method has a good reference value for predicting 5-year recurrence events.Registritation:ChiCTR.org.cn, ChiCTR2100046766Keywords: Breast cancer, Hormone positive, HER2-low expression, Multicenter study

Introduction

The successful development of targeted drugs, such as trastuzumab and pertuzumab, has had significant survival benefits for patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive breast cancer.[1,2] In recent years, National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) clinical practice guidelines for breast cancer proposed indications for cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) inhibitors in systemic therapy for hormone receptor (HR)-positive early breast cancer, and the successful developments of new agents such as antibody drug conjugate (ADC) drugs represented by DS-8201 definitely improved the overall survival of breast cancer patients.[3] The prognosis and therapeutic modalities of HR-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer have gained increasing attention. Therefore, exploring factors affecting the prognosis of patients with HR-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer can provide a reference for treatment algorithms. Based on data from a multicenter study (CSBrS-021),[4] the clinicopathological characteristics of patients with HR-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer were analyzed, and a recurrence risk prediction model for patients with recurrence or metastatic events was established using machine learning methods.

Methods

Ethical approval

This project was registered online (Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, ChiCTR, https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.html?proj=127093) with registration number ChiCTR2100046766. It was approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University First Hospital (No. 2021keyan206). This study filed with the Chinese Human Genetic Resource Collection License.

Participants

Based on data from a multicenter study (CSBrS-021),[4] female patients diagnosed with early invasive breast cancer at CSBrS hospitals from January 1, 2015, to December 31, 2016, were selected as research subjects. A total of 29 CSBrS member hospitals participated in the study and provided qualified cases, all of which have an independent breast surgery ward and a pathology laboratory that independently issues pathological reports for breast cancer tissues [Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/B794].

The inclusion criteria are as follows: females with invasive breast cancer confirmed by pathological biopsy; cases with no distant metastasis found on clinical examination; HER2 test results consistent with a diagnosis of low-positive HER2 expression, that is, immunohistochemistry (IHC) 1+, or IHC 2+ with negative in situ hybridization (ISH); previous R0 mastectomy; systematic treatment with the recommended regimen completed according to guidelines[5]; and complete tumor IHC examination and follow-up information.

IHC test standard

HR status detection and interpretation followed the 2010 American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists (ASCO/CAP) guidelines for estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) detection, and samples with tumor cell nuclear positivity ≥1% were defined as HR-positive and <1% as HR-negative.[6]

HER2 status evaluation

Laboratory qualifications

The pathology laboratory of the reference hospital was required to have a laboratory qualification certificate for performing HER2 detection of breast cancer. The pathology laboratory was required to be certified by the National Pathology Quality Control Center (PQCC) or the International Organization for Standardization/China National Accreditation Service for Conformity Assessment (ISO15189/CNAS-CL02) and to conduct HER2 detection according to sound laboratory standard operating procedures to ensure the reliability and accuracy of the detection results.

Reagent used for HER2 detection

The pathology laboratory of the participating hospital was required to use detection kits certified by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) for HER2 detection of breast cancer: the acceptable HER2 probe kits using Immunohistochemistry included VENTANA (4B5, Roche, Arizona,USA), HercepTestTM (Dako, Glostrup, Denmark), and domestic HER2 detection kits approved by the NMPA. The acceptable fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) kits included PathVysion (Abbott Molecular, Des Plaines, USA), Histra (Jokoh, Tokyo, Japan), Inform (Roche, Arizona, USA), ZytoLight (ZytoVision GmbH, Bremerhaven, Germany), and NMPA-approved domestic HER2 gene amplification test kits (FISH).

Following the CSBrS-021 study criteria,[4] HER2 status detection and interpretation followed the 2013 ASCO/CAP guidelines for HER2 detection.[7] The criteria for low HER2 expression were IHC 1+ or IHC 2+ and ISH-negative.

Antineoplastic protocols

All patients underwent breast and axillary R0 surgery[5] and systemic therapy according to guidelines. Breast surgery includes breast-conserving therapy and mastectomy, and axillary surgery includes sentinel lymph node biopsy and axillary lymph node dissection of the level I and II lymph nodes of the ipsilateral axilla.[8] Systematic treatment was conducted with reference to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines. In accordance with adjuvant/(neoadjuvant) chemotherapy regimens, as recommended by the guidelines, ≥4 treatment cycles were conducted for patients with breast cancer meeting the indications, and adjuvant endocrinotherapy for ≥5 years was conducted for patients with hormone receptor-positive breast cancer and patients who were still receiving endocrinotherapy at the end of the follow-up period.[5]

Study outcomes and follow-up

The primary endpoint of the study was disease-free survival (DFS) from the date of receiving treatment to the date of recurrence and metastasis, and the secondary endpoint of the study was overall survival (OS) from the date of a confirmed diagnosis of breast cancer to the date of death from any cause. Patients were followed up every 6 months until death or the last follow-up date.[4]

Statistical analysis

Data processing was conducted using SPSS 26.0 software (version 26.0; IBM, Armonk, NY USA). Measurement data are expressed as the median (minimum, maximum). Enumeration and grade data are expressed as the number and percentage. The relationship between HR status and clinicopathological characteristics was investigated using the chi-square test, Fisher's exact test, and the Mann–Whitney test. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to calculate the five-year DFS and OS, and prognostic factors were analyzed by Cox multiple regression analysis. The decision tree was used for the analysis of clinicopathological features, and clinicopathological features with Gini >0.03 were selected as valid features for inclusion in the machine learning model. All tests were two-sided, and P <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Recurrence prediction model

Data that met the inclusion criteria were filtered to remove missing effective factors and subjects who were followed up for less than five years and did not develop metastasis. Ki-67 index was divided into Ki-67 >30% group and Ki-67 ≤30% group as binary variable. The model was established using Python software (https://www.python.org/) and the Scikit-learn (Sklearn) machine learning tool (https://github.com/scikit-learn/scikit-learn). Five methods were explored: random forest, support vector machine (SVM), K-nearest neighbor, logistic regression, and naive Bayes. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) of the test set of each model was compared, and the AUC optimal model was selected. The optimal model was initially assigned and trained with the training set samples. Effective cases were randomly shuffled using the shuffle method from the Sklearn library in Python. The model was then externally tested using the test set samples.[9] The efficacy of the model was evaluated by the ROC curve [Figure 1].

Cases with effective recurrence and metastasis were randomly allocated to the training set (a) and the test set (b) at a ratio of 2:1.The test set included effective cases with recurrence and metastasis (b) and effective cases without recurrence and metastasis (c) at a ratio of 1:1; the remaining effective cases without recurrence were assigned to the training set.

Results

General information

In the CSBrS-021 study, 25,096 patients were diagnosed with breast cancer in 29 hospitals of CSBrS from Jan 1, 2015, to Dec 31, 2016. In total, 7642 patients with HER2-low early-stage breast cancer were enrolled, and their clinicopathological data and follow-up data were collected. Unqualified subjects were excluded from this study; ultimately, a total of 6486 patients were included in the statistical analysis. Among them, 5629 subjects were HR-positive (HR-positive group), accounting for 86.79% of HER2-low early-stage breast cancer, and 857 subjects were HR-negative (HR-negative group), accounting for 13.21%.

Clinicopathological analysis

The median age was 50 years (21–90 years) in the HR-positive group and 52 years (20–90 years) in the HR-negative group. There were significant differences in median age, menstrual status, tumor T stage, TNM stage, histopathological type, histological grade, HER2 status, lymphovascular invasion, Ki-67 index, and prognostic stage between the two groups (P <0.05). In addition, there were significant differences in the proportions of breast-conserving surgery, axillary lymph node surgery, and decisions to administer adjuvant chemotherapy, neoadjuvant therapy, and radiotherapy between the two groups (all P <0.05, Table 1).

Prognostic analysis

Patients in both groups were followed up for a median of 57 months (range, 4–76 months). A total of 412 metastatic events (7.31%) occurred in the HR-positive group, and the 5-year DFS was 92.7%; 124 death events (2.20%) occurred, with a 5-year OS of 97.7%. A total of 94 metastatic events (10.97%) occurred in the HR-negative group, with a 5-year DFS of 88.9%; 39 death events (4.55%) occurred, with a 5-year OS of 95.1%. There were significant differences in DFS and OS between the two groups (P <0.001, Figure 2). Univariate Cox regression analysis revealed that recurrence and metastasis events were correlated with age, tumor T stage, N stage, histological grade, HER2 status, lymphovascular thrombosis, Ki-67 index, TNM staging, and prognostic staging in patients in the HR-positive group (P <0.05). Multivariate Cox regression analysis verified that tumor N stage, lymphovascular thrombosis, Ki-67 index, and prognostic stage were correlated with prognosis (P <0.05, Table 2).

Recurrence risk prediction model

The decision-tree analysis features sorted by Gini identified 15 effective characteristics to construct a recurrence risk prediction model: age, menstrual status, T stage, N stage, TNM stage, prognostic staging, Ki-67 index (continuous variable), Ki-67 index (binary variable), histological grade, histological type, HER2 status, lymphovascular thrombosis, axillary surgery, neoadjuvant therapy, and radiotherapy [Supplementary Table 2, http://links.lww.com/CM9/B794].

A total of 2529 subjects who were missing any effective characteristics and 1648 subjects who were followed up for less than five years were excluded, and a total of 161 subjects with effective recurrence and metastasis and 1291 subjects without effective recurrence and metastasis were enrolled. Five machine learning models random forest, support vector machine (SVM), K-nearest neighbor, logistic regression, and naive Bayesian were established, and the power of the different recurrence risk models was compared by AUC. Among the five models, the random forest method was the optimal modeling method, with an AUC of 0.842 for the test set. The AUC of SVM, K-nearest neighbor, logistic regression, and naive Bayesian is 0.627, 0.655, 0.777, and 0.758, separately.

The recurrence risk model had an AUC of 0.891 (95% CI: 0.874–0.908) for the training set and 0.842 (95% CI: 0.772–0.911) for the test set [Table 3].

Discussion

According to GLOBOCAN statistics, the global cancer statistic database by the International Agency for Research on Cancer, breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer in women. In China, more than 416,000 new cases were diagnosed in 2020, with almost 120,000 reported deaths.[10] Antibodyconjugated drugs (ADCs), particularly DS-8201, have brought benefits to patients with HER2-low breast cancer,[11,12] which have triggered further refinement of evaluation criteria for HER2 expression and increases intensive treatment options for improving the prognosis of HER2-low breast cancer. CSBrS-021 showed patients with HER2-low early-stage breast cancer had good clinical outcome.[4] Tumor burden and tumor invasiveness are important factors affecting the prognosis. The established recurrence risk prediction model can provide a reference for clinical practice. However, this study did not explore the clinical proportion of patients with HR-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer and related prognostic factors.

In this study, the clinicopathological characteristics of Chinese patients with HR-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer were analyzed in depth, and the clinical value of establishing a recurrence risk prediction model was explored using machine learning methods. A total of 6486 subjects with HER2-low invasive early-stage breast cancer were included in the study. Among them, 5629 subjects (86.79%) were HR-positive, consistent with the proportion reported previously in the literature.[13] Endocrine therapy is required for over 80% of patients with HER2-low early-stage breast cancer. Scientific endocrine therapy decisions are an important means of improving the prognosis of HR-positive/HER2-low breast cancer. It has been reported in the literature that the overall prognosis of HER2-low breast cancer is good, with a 5-year DFS and OS of 92% and 97%,[4,14] respectively. However, the effects of differences in HR expression status on the prognosis of patients with HER2-low breast cancer remain to be confirmed. This study found that the 5-year DFS and OS of the HR-positive group were 92.7% and 97.7%, respectively, while the 5-year DFS and OS of the HR-negative group were 88.9% and 95.1%, respectively (P <0.001). These results suggest that HR-negative status is an unfavorable prognostic factor for patients with HER2-low breast cancer. Long-term adjuvant endocrine therapy for HR-positive patients can undoubtedly improve prognosis. Furthermore, we found significant differences in median age, menstrual status, tumor T stage, TNM stage, Ki-67 index, HER2 status, histological grade, lymphovascular thrombosis, and prognostic stage between the HR-positive and HR-negative groups (P <0.05). In addition, the rate of breast-conserving surgery was higher and the rates of axillary lymph surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy were lower in the HR-positive group than in the HR-negative group (P <0.05). Therefore, HR status is an important basis for clinicians to make treatment strategies for patients with HER2-low early breast cancer.

This study showed that 7.31% of patients with HR-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer had a recurrence and metastasis events. Early prediction of recurrence and metastasis is key to further improve breast cancer prognosis. In recent years, machine learning methods have provided important help for the establishment of tumor prognosis models. Machine learning algorithms such as SVM, decision tree, and random forest have received considerable attention.[15,16,17] This study included a large amount of data with an uneven distribution, making machine learning methods suitable for an in-depth analysis. A total of 412 subjects with recurrence events within 5 years were analyzed and studied by a variety of machine learning methods, and finally, a 5-year recurrence risk prediction model with the highest predictive efficacy was established using the random forest method. The sensitivity of the model was 81.1%, indicating most of the 5-year recurrence and metastasis event population could be correctly identified. The use of this model to screen subjects with high recurrence risk for HR-positive/HER2-low early-stage breast cancer may lay a foundation for the study of indications of intensive adjuvant endocrine therapy and the demonstration of indications of adjuvant therapy with anti-HER2-related ADC drugs. However, as only 25.79% (1452/5629) of cases was used to conduct the recurrence risk model, the efficiency of risk model must be tested by external validation in the future.

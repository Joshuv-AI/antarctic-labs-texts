Visualization analysis of research progress in precision oncology nursing based on CiteSpace
Authors: Qiyuan Huang, Yuhe Guan, Luqing Liao, Weisheng Yang, Tong Wu, Zhimin Du, Xuehu Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo map the knowledge structure, patterns of research attention, and temporal trends of precision oncology nursing in Chinese- and English-language literature.MethodsPublications related to precision oncology nursing were retrieved from the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Data, VIP Database for Chinese Technical Periodicals (VIP), Chinese Biomedical Literature Database (CBM), and Web of Science (WOS) from database inception to July 7, 2025. After literature screening, bibliometric analysis and knowledge mapping were performed using CiteSpace 6.4.R1.ResultsA total of 613 publications were included, comprising 292 Chinese-language and 321 English-language records. Publication output increased in both datasets, although the English-language literature emerged earlier. Common themes across the two datasets included quality of life, breast cancer, and symptom-related care. Compared with the English-language literature, the Chinese-language literature placed greater emphasis on perioperative management and application-oriented nursing practice, whereas the English-language literature showed relatively greater attention to survivorship, patient education, palliative care, and evidence-informed care.ConclusionResearch on precision oncology nursing has expanded over time and shows increasing thematic diversity. However, collaboration networks remain relatively fragmented, particularly in the Chinese-language literature. Further efforts in interdisciplinary integration, methodological standardization, and international collaboration may support the future development of precision oncology nursing across the cancer care continuum.

Introduction

Cancer has emerged as a major global public health challenge, with its high incidence, substantial mortality burden, and escalating economic costs posing severe threats [1, 2]. At the same time, the limitations of conventional treatment models have become more apparent, as standardized approaches do not fully address tumor heterogeneity or variation in patients’ supportive care needs [3].

With the advancement of precision medicine, cancer care is increasingly moving toward more individualized prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and follow-up [4, 5]. In the nursing context, this shift has broadened the scope of care to include biomarker-informed symptom management, personalized supportive care, patient education, survivorship care, and hereditary risk communication [6–10]. As this body of research grows, there is an increasing need to clarify its overall landscape, thematic priorities, and developmental trajectory. Nevertheless, the existing literature is scattered across different languages, databases, and healthcare contexts, which makes it difficult to obtain a comprehensive understanding of the field. This challenge is particularly relevant for Chinese-language studies, which remain less visible internationally and are therefore underrepresented in cross-regional academic exchange [11–13].

Bibliometric analysis provides a useful approach for mapping publication patterns, collaboration networks, patterns of research attention, and thematic evolution within a field [14]. CiteSpace has been widely used for knowledge-domain visualization and is particularly helpful for tracing temporal patterns, detecting burst terms, and identifying pivotal nodes that connect different knowledge clusters [15, 16]. These features make it suitable for examining the developmental trajectory and emerging trends of precision oncology nursing over time.

In this study, we used CiteSpace 6.4.R1 to compare Chinese-language literature indexed in CNKI, Wanfang Data, VIP, and CBM with English-language literature indexed in the WOS. Specifically, this study addressed two questions: (1) How do patterns of research attention, topic distributions, and temporal trends differ between Chinese- and English-language literature on precision oncology nursing? (2) What research directions are emerging in this field? By answering these questions, this study aims to provide a clearer overview of the current research landscape and to support future development of precision oncology nursing.

Methods

Data sources

In this study, four Chinese databases—China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Data, VIP Database for Chinese Technical Periodicals (VIP), and Chinese Biomedical Literature Database (CBM)—and the Web of Science (WOS) were searched to retrieve Chinese- and English-language literature on precision oncology nursing.

Search strategy

Chinese Literature Retrieval: CNKI, Wanfang Data, VIP, and CBM served as Chinese literature sources. Using the advanced search function, the following Boolean query was executed: Subject: (“precision medicine” OR “precision health” OR “big data” OR “multi-omics” OR “gene” OR “bioinformatics”) AND Subject: (“tumor” OR “cancer”) AND Subject: (“nursing”) Publications were filtered to include peer-reviewed articles from Peking University Core Journals, Chinese Science and Technology Core Journals (CSTPCD), and Chinese Social Sciences Citation Index (CSSCI) sources, with language restricted to Chinese.

English-language literature was searched in the WOS using Advanced Search. The search query was: ((TS=(“precision nursing” OR “precision care” OR “personalized nursing” OR “personalized care”) AND TS=(genomic* OR genetic* OR bioinformatic* OR omics) AND TS=(cancer OR oncology)) OR (TS=(“personalized medicine” OR “precision medicine”) AND TS=(nursing OR care) AND TS=(cancer OR oncology))). Document types were limited to Article and Review, and the language was limited to English.

The search covered database inception to July 7, 2025. The revision of the English search formula was a clarification and correction of Boolean syntax rather than a substantive expansion or restriction of the search scope. We re-ran the revised search under the same restrictions and confirmed that the number of retrieved English-language records remained unchanged.

Literature inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Original research articles and reviews focusing on precision oncology nursing; (2) Literature published in either Chinese or English.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Non-Chinese/English publications; (2) Duplicate publications; (3) Conference abstracts, editorials, letters, patents, or practice guidelines; (4) Literature with incomplete information; (5) Literature irrelevant to precision oncology nursing.

Literature screening

Initial independent screening

The initial search yielded 1,002 Chinese publications and 1,916 English publications. Two researchers (Rater A and Rater B) independently screened all articles based on their titles and abstracts using pre-established inclusion and exclusion criteria. Duplicate records were removed using NoteExpress V3.8. The literature analysis cutoff date was July 7, 2025.

Discrepancy identification

After completing independent screening, the two raters compared their screening decisions to identify discrepant records. No formal inter-rater agreement statistic was calculated or reported. All discrepant records were systematically documented for further review.

Discrepancy resolution process

Phase 1: Discussion. The two raters jointly reviewed and discussed discrepant records to reach a consensus. Phase 2: Arbitration. Records without consensus were submitted to a panel of two senior experts for arbitration. Final Decision: The arbitration panel made the final determination on inclusion based on the predefined criteria.

Finalization and data export

Upon completing the above process, a total of 292 Chinese and 321 English publications were included for analysis. Bibliographic information of all included articles was exported and saved in both RefWorks and plain text formats.

Literature extraction and analysis

After screening, the bibliographic records were exported in plain-text and RefWorks formats. Two researchers manually checked the exported text files in Notepad + + to standardize bibliographic fields and correct incomplete or inconsistent information, including author names, institution names, keywords, and publication years, by cross-checking the original database records. After preprocessing, the cleaned datasets were imported into CiteSpace 6.4.R1 for bibliometric visualization and knowledge mapping [17]. The retrieval end date for all databases was July 7, 2025. However, the actual publication span of the included records differed between datasets: the English-language dataset covered publications from January 2006 to July 2025, whereas the Chinese-language dataset covered publications from January 2015 to July 2025.

Time slicing was set to 1 year per slice, and the selection criterion was Top N = 50; that is, the 50 most frequent items in each annual time slice were included in the knowledge map construction [18], which not only avoids increased computational redundancy due to excessive data size, but also captures core research elements per year.

For collaboration network analysis, the cosine algorithm was used to calculate the co-occurrence strength between authors and institutions. In CiteSpace, these metrics appear in the network visualization as node size (representing publication count), link thickness (representing collaboration strength), and betweenness centrality (calculated by the software and displayed as purple rings around nodes). Nodes with high betweenness centrality (≥ 0.1) are considered pivotal points in the network.

For core-author analysis, we applied Price’s Law formula (N = 0.749 × ηmax^½) to determine the minimum publication threshold for core authors. Author productivity metrics were obtained from the “Summary” table in CiteSpace, which lists frequency counts for each node. The core author identification was further validated using the “Citation Burst” and “Betweenness Centrality” metrics available in the software’s statistical report panel.

In this study, we analyzed the volume of publications, journal distribution, author and institution co-occurrence, high-frequency keyword co-occurrence, and emergent keywords. The average silhouette (S > 0.5) and modularity value (Q > 0.3) were used to evaluate the quality of the knowledge maps [19]. Descriptive analyses were conducted using NoteExpress.

Literature analysis

This section compares the Chinese-language and English-language literature indexed in CNKI, Wanfang Data, VIP, CBM, and WOS in terms of general characteristics, collaboration networks, and patterns of research attention.

General information

Year distribution

According to the results from the Chinese databases, publications on precision oncology nursing in China have increased markedly. Figure 2 illustrates a total of 292 Chinese-language publications included between 2015 and 2025. However, there is a relatively short history of studying precision oncology nursing in China. By 2018, only 26 publications had been identified, accounting for 8.9% of the 292 Chinese-language publications included in this study, indicating that Chinese research is still in its infancy in this field. This phase of research focuses on the introduction and initial exploration of the concept of precision nursing, such as genomic-based and preliminary applications of precision nursing [9]. The growth stage of precision oncology nursing began in 2019, when the number of publications reached approximately three times that in 2018 and peaked in 2024, indicating a marked increase in publication activity.

At the same time, it can be seen from the trend line that international research on precision oncology nursing began in 2006. From 2006 to 2015, it was a slow initial stage, with only one article in 2006 and 10.6% of the cumulative publications before 2015, indicating that the early progress of international research was slow, mainly focusing on the theoretical framework of precision medicine, such as the Blueprint for Scientific Research on Precision Nursing proposed by the National Institute of Nursing Research in 2012 [20]. The number of publications exceeded 20 in the following year 2016 and reached 33 in 2020, accounting for 46.4% of the total, reflecting increasing maturity driven by developments such as genomics-guided nursing interventions and digital remote management [21, 22]. 2021–2025 is a stable stage of development, and the annual number of publications remains at 30–47, and the research focus expands to the deepening of individual full-cycle management and symptom management [23, 24].

Overall, the English-language literature emerged approximately 10 years earlier than the Chinese-language literature, whereas Chinese-language publications increased more rapidly after 2019. Overall, the English-language literature emerged approximately 10 years earlier than the Chinese-language literature, whereas Chinese-language publications increased more rapidly after 2019. These findings describe publication activity rather than the methodological quality or clinical implementation of precision oncology nursing. The possible future expansion of artificial intelligence-assisted decision-making and patient experience optimization should therefore be interpreted as an emerging direction suggested by the observed publication and topic evolution, rather than as a direct outcome measured in this analysis.

Collaborative networks

Co-authorship analysis

Co-authorship analysis helps reveal the distribution of research output across the field and the collaboration patterns among research teams. A total of 644 authors published research papers related to precision oncology nursing in Chinese and English literature, and the top ten authors in the number of publications are shown in Table 2. Table 2 shows that Chan, Andrew T, the highest producer in this field, published a total of 5 articles. According to Price’s Law, the minimum publication threshold for identifying core authors was calculated using the formula M = 0.749 × (Nmax)1/2, where Nmax represents the number of publications by the most productive author [25]. In this study, the most productive author published five articles; therefore, the threshold was M = 0.749 × √5 ≈ 1.67, which was rounded to two publications. Based on this threshold, 97 authors were identified as core authors. Publications contributed by these core authors accounted for 15.06% of the total dataset, which is below the 50% benchmark often used to indicate a relatively stable core-author group. These findings suggest that a stable core author group has not yet formed in this field. Taking the author as the network node, the co-occurrence analysis of the author of the document is carried out, and the co-occurrence network map of authors in the Chinese- and English-language literature in this field is obtained, as shown in Fig. 3.

Analysis of study topics

Keyword co-occurrence analysis

Keyword frequency reflects the distribution of research attention within the field. The co-occurrence profile of keywords in Chinese literature and English literature is shown in Fig. 5. Synonyms, singular forms and abbreviations are combined, and irrelevant and meaningless keywords are deleted. The top 20 keywords in Chinese and English literature are shown in Tables 3 and 4.

Classifying the high-frequency words, the research contents of Chinese literature can be divided into four categories: Precision nursing study of specific tumor types: Keywords include breast cancer, lung cancer, cervical cancer, colorectal cancer. The research focuses on the precision nursing plans of different tumor types, with breast cancer nursing study occupying a significant position. Perioperative precision nursing study: Keywords include enhanced recovery after surgery, stress response. The research emphasizes the optimization of the whole surgical process through collaboration. Quality of life and symptom management of patients: Quality of life is taken as the core index, with focus on symptom clustering and psychosocial influencing factors. Supportive treatment techniques: Keywords include peripherally inserted central catheters. Although the frequency is low, it reflects the technical details of precision nursing.

Through cluster analysis of the keywords in the English literature of precision oncology nursing, the research contents can be divided into the following four categories: Precision care research for specific tumor types: Focuses on personalized management programs for different tumor types, with breast cancer, lung cancer and colorectal cancer survivors as the core. It emphasizes personalized management programs for different tumor types, especially focusing on the population. Evidence-based validation of treatment programs: Assesses the impact of interventions such as chemotherapy and care on patient outcomes through randomized trials. It emphasizes scientific validation of cancer-related attitudes in clinical translation. Symptom and quality of life research: Focuses on the impact of anxiety, depression, fatigue (related to women) on life. It combines palliative care quality control to explore symptom cluster management. Doctor-patient cognition and behavior research: Analyzes the treatment risks for medical staff and patients, as well as their nursing practice risks.

Taken together, both Chinese and English literature show that cancer species-specific care and quality of life of cancer patients are taken as the core research direction, and also reflect the precise care characteristics of multidisciplinary collaboration. However, the Chinese literature focuses more on perioperative management optimization and clinical nursing practice for specific tumor types, while the English literature focuses more on evidence-based validation of treatment options and doctor-patient cognitive behavioral studies. This difference may reflect that Chinese studies focus on clinical process optimization, and English studies pay more attention to the empirical basis of interventions, implying differences in the interpretation of “precision” by different academic systems—the former focusing on refined operation and the latter emphasizing scientific validation.

Keyword clustering

Cluster analysis is a machine learning technique used to group entities (e.g., keywords, literature) into distinct clusters based on their inherent similarities or distance relationships [26]. In keyword clustering, the log-likelihood ratio (LLR) algorithm serves as a statistically rigorous textual clustering method [27]. It calculates the likelihood of vocabulary occurrence in document collections and generally yields more accurate clustering outcomes compared to alternative approaches. To evaluate the validity of clustering results, three key metrics are used: centrality (D value), modularity (Q value), and average silhouette (S value). Specifically, a Q value > 0.3 indicates a significant cluster structure, while an S value > 0.7 suggests strong cluster homogeneity and high reliability of the results [28]. In this study, the LLR algorithm implemented in CiteSpace was used to generate keyword clustering maps based on keyword co-occurrence networks. The clustering results are visualized in Fig. 6, and detailed information on Chinese and English keyword clusters is provided below: Chinese keyword clusters. Cluster ranking: #0 quality of life, #1 breast cancer, #2 functional status, #3 symptom cluster, #4 esophageal cancer, #5 cervical cancer, #6 perioperative period, #7 colorectal cancer, #8 precision therapy, #9 5G technology. Topological and evaluation indicators: The keyword cluster map comprises 290 nodes and 365 connecting lines, with a centrality (D value) of 0.0087, a module value (Q value) of 0.852, and an average contour silhouette (S value) of 0.9725. Cluster label rules: A total of 9 keyword cluster labels (#) were obtained, where a smaller numerical value after the # symbol corresponds to a larger cluster size. English keyword clusters. Cluster ranking: #0 Microsatellite Instability, #1 breast cancer, #2 survivorship, #3 lung cancer, #4 precision medicine, #5 patient education, #6 esophageal cancer, #7 unmet needs, #8 self-management, #9 hereditary breast cancer, #10 palliative care, #11 palliative science, #12 symptom need. Topological and evaluation indicators: The keyword cluster map includes 359 nodes and 885 connecting lines, with a centrality (D value) of 0.0138, a module value (Q value) of 0.7307, and an average contour silhouette (S value) of 0.9017. The shared emphasis on breast cancer (both labeled #1) in Chinese and English literature suggests that breast cancer has become a global priority in precision nursing. However, Chinese studies appear more oriented toward clinical application and symptom management, whereas English-language research tends to focus on molecular mechanisms and whole-cycle care. These differences may reflect variations in regional healthcare resources, academic traditions, and clinical needs. Such cross-regional distinctions offer a foundation for international academic exchange and collaborative research in precision oncology nursing.

Research frontiers in precision oncology nursing

Given the dynamic nature of research attention, topics in precision oncology nursing evolve over time. This section analyzes the evolution of these research themes and identifies emerging trends through timeline visualization and burst term analysis generated by CiteSpace.

Keyword timeline analysis

The timeline view represents knowledge evolution along a temporal dimension, clearly illustrating literature updates and the process of mutual influence among research themes [12]. Figure 7 shows the keyword co-occurrence timeline for precision oncology nursing research in China from 2015 to 2025. In this timeline graph, the modularity (Q) value is 0.852 (> 0.3), indicating a significant cluster structure, and the weighted mean silhouette (S) value is 0.9725 (> 0.7), reflecting uniform cluster distribution and high reliability of the clustering results. Different clusters exhibit variations in duration: “breast cancer” represents a long-term research focus, with nodes distributed across the entire timeline. Cluster #0 “quality of life” maintains a consistent presence throughout the cancer care continuum. Cluster #3 “symptom cluster” encompasses the management of multiple symptoms and shows node distribution across multiple time zones. Cluster #2 “functional status” continues to attract attention with high frequency. Clusters #9 “5G technology” and #8 “precision therapy” display dense node distribution after 2020, indicating emerging research trends.

Figure 7 also presents the keyword co-occurrence timeline for international precision oncology nursing research from 2006 to 2025. Here, the modularity (Q) value is 0.7307 (> 0.3), and the weighted mean silhouette (S) value is 0.9017 (> 0.7), confirming significant cluster structure and high credibility of the clustering results. Cluster #1 “breast cancer” appears as a long-term research focus, spanning multiple time zones. The “quality of life” node maintains a persistent presence throughout diagnosis, treatment, and rehabilitation processes. Cluster #4 “precision medicine” shows increased activity after 2010, intersecting with various disease types and basic research. Cluster #2 “survivorship” demonstrates heightened activity after 2020, while Cluster #11 “precision science” shows concentrated nodes in recent years.

In summary, quality of life, breast cancer, and precision intervention represent common core themes in both Chinese and international research, increasingly integrated with genetic testing and AI technology. While Chinese studies focus more on technological applications such as 5G and symptom cluster optimization, international efforts emphasize mechanistic research in areas such as symptom science and precision health theory. Overall, the timeline analysis indicates that quality of life, breast cancer, and precision intervention were recurring themes in both Chinese and international research. Chinese studies appeared to place greater emphasis on technological applications such as 5G and symptom cluster optimization, whereas international studies more often involved mechanistic themes such as symptom science and precision health theory. These findings should be interpreted as thematic tendencies observed in the keyword timeline, rather than as direct evidence of future clinical development.

Keyword burst analysis

Burst keywords refer to terms that exhibit a sharp increase or decrease in frequency within a specific time period, representing turning points in knowledge development. Analyzing such keywords helps identify emerging trends or sudden shifts in a research field [29]. In this study, keyword burst detection was performed based on keyword co-occurrence data, using the Burst Detection function in CiteSpace 6.4.R1 to generate keyword burst maps. The results for precision oncology nursing research in the Chinese- and English-language literature are shown in Fig. 8.

For the English-language literature: The Burst keywords with the strongest intensity is “women”; The Burst keywords with the longest duration are “personalized medicine” and “care”; The latest Burst keywords are “women”, “fatigue”, and “artificial intelligence”. The distribution of burst keywords in the English-language literature can be divided into three stages: Early stage (2006–2014): Burst keywords include “personalized medicine”, “island methylator phenotype”, and “BRAF mutation”; Middle stage (2014–2020): Burst keywords include “fatigue”, “cancer survivors”, “lung cancer”, “care”, “intelligence”, and “attitude”; Recent stage (2020–2025): Burst keywords include “precision women”, “artificial intelligence”, “fatigue”, “health”, and “palliative care”. Among these, “women” showed the strongest burst intensity, “personalized medicine” and “care” had the longest duration, and the most recent burst keywords include “women”, “fatigue”, and “artificial intelligence”.

For the Chinese-language literature: The Burst keywords with the highest intensity is “colorectal cancer”; The Burst keywords with the longest duration is “nursing intervention”; The latest Burst keywords are “colorectal cancer”, “enhanced recovery after surgery”, “influencing factors”, “endometrial cancer”, “evidence-based theory”, and “gastric cancer”. The distribution of burst keywords in the Chinese-language literature shows three stages of shifts in research attention: 2015–2018: Emergent keywords include “nursing intervention”, “precision medicine”, “radiofrequency ablation”, and “oncology nursing”; 2018–2021: Burst keywords include “cerebral aneurysm”, “nasopharyngeal carcinoma”, “central venous catheter nursing”, “application value”, “application effect”, and “health education”; 2021–2025: Burst keywords include “evidence-based medicine”, “elderly patients”, “aortic dissecting aneurysm”, “anxiety and depression”, “continuing care”, “endometrial cancer”, and “enhanced recovery after surgery”. Notably, “colorectal cancer” showed the highest burst intensity, “nursing intervention” had the longest duration, and the most recent burst keywords include “colorectal cancer”, “enhanced recovery after surgery”, “influencing factors”, “endometrial cancer”, “evidence-based theory”, and “gastric cancer”.

Discussion

This study uses CiteSpace to conduct a visual analysis of Chinese- and English-language literature on precision oncology nursing, thereby revealing the current research landscape, major areas of attention, and developmental trends in this field. The results suggest that precision oncology nursing, as an extension of precision medicine within the nursing domain, is developing rapidly. At the same time, Chinese and international research show different evolutionary trajectories and core characteristics, while also sharing several common challenges that merit further attention.

Research status: development trend and structural weakness

Spatio-temporal variation across research stages

Temporal analysis reveals distinct evolutionary patterns between Chinese and international research. English-language literature began accumulating gradually since 2006, with accelerated growth following the US Precision Medicine Initiative in 2015 (average annual growth rate of 18.3% from 2015 to 2020). In contrast, Chinese literature emerged approximately a decade later, forming stable publication trends only after 2015, but demonstrating explosive growth since 2019 (average annual growth rate of 34.7% from 2019 to 2023). This differential development reflects varying stages of maturity in precision nursing implementation across healthcare systems [30].

The quality and impact of research outputs also show notable differences. Among English publications, 89.2% are indexed in SCI/ESCI databases, with 61.5% in Q1-Q2 journals. Chinese literature shows only 28.7% publication in core journals, with no specialized oncology nursing core journals available. This disparity highlights the need for enhanced research infrastructure and specialized publication venues in precision oncology nursing within China.

Structural weaknesses of the collaboration network

Collaboration analysis reveals significant structural weaknesses in both domestic and international research networks. The proportion of publications by core authors falls considerably below levels suggested by bibliometric standards [31], indicating that no core author group has been formed in the field, and a stable academic leadership is yet to be established.

At the institutional level, the collaboration density among domestic research institutions is 0.002, exhibiting a pattern of “regional concentration and cross-regional dispersion.” Specialist cancer hospitals in East China account for a relatively high frequency of collaborations, while the collaboration density among international institutions is 0.013, 6.5 times that of domestic collaborations. This suggests that future efforts should focus on establishing a national-level precision oncology nursing research institution and promoting the development of multi-center data-sharing platforms to address the fragmentation of collaborations in China.

Research support and deficiencies in discipline development

A comparative analysis between domestic and international literature shows that English-language publications benefit from a cluster of high-impact journals: six of the top 10 journals are specialized in oncology nursing (e.g., Seminars in Oncology Nursing, Q2, IF = 2.3; Cancer Nursing, Q1, IF = 2.8), with the top five journals accounting for 29.4% of all publications. In contrast, Chinese-language publications lack a robust journal foundation: none of the top 10 journals are specialized in oncology nursing, core journals account for only 18.3% of publications, and there is relatively low support from national-level special funds, with most research relying on provincial or lower-level funding. International research has fostered a virtuous academic ecosystem through high-impact journal clusters and stable funding support systems, whereas domestic studies are hampered by limited publication in core journals and insufficient targeted research funding, which restricts the dissemination and in-depth expansion of findings. Future efforts should increase research investment in this area and promote the submission of academic achievements to high-level journals to comprehensively enhance the discipline’s development.

Analysis of research attention

Based on the keyword analysis results, three broad thematic areas were identified and are discussed below: symptom science and management, health education, and hereditary oncology counseling. These areas should be understood as interpretative groupings derived from the bibliometric patterns, rather than as definitive or exclusive classifications of the field. Chinese and international research also showed complementary emphases across these areas. We integrated a structured and time-bound roadmap (Fig. 9) to outline strategic priorities and actionable steps for future research and implementation in precision oncology nursing.

Symptom science and management

Radiotherapy and chemotherapy are common means of cancer treatment, but they may cause adverse symptoms such as fatigue, pain, and emotional distress, which significantly impact patients’ functional status and quality of life [32]. Precision oncology nursing aims to move beyond one-size-fits-all approaches by tailoring interventions to individual symptom profiles. International research has increasingly adopted mechanism-driven models, such as the Nursing Science Precision Health Model (NSPH), which integrates genomic data with clinical symptoms to enable biomarker-guided interventions [33]. For instance, Kwekkeboom et al. [34] identified associations between TNF-α levels and the pain-fatigue-sleep disturbance cluster in chemotherapy patients, providing a biological basis for targeted therapies. Similarly, predictive models using artificial intelligence have been developed to anticipate chemotherapy-related toxicities [35]. In contrast, Chinese studies often emphasize technology-enhanced symptom control through improved clinical processes, such as enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) or complication prevention techniques, with “quality of life” as a central outcome. While clinically pragmatic, this approach frequently lacks mechanistic depth. To strengthen precision nursing in symptom management, future efforts should focus on integrating biomarkers with symptom data, exploring novel directions such as wearable devices for real-time monitoring, multi-omics profiling for symptom subtyping, and AI-enhanced prediction models. This will facilitate a shift from reactive management to proactive, personalized intervention.

Health education

Conventional health education often fails to address the individualized needs of cancer patients, who may experience anxiety and uncertainty regarding their treatment. Precision nursing in health education involves delivering tailored information based on genetic, clinical, and psychosocial profiles. International studies have emphasized evidence-based, genetically-informed education—for example, randomized trials evaluating pedigree education for BRCA mutation carriers, which improved hereditary cancer management [36]. In clinical practice, precision health education operates across prevention stages: using genetic profiles for primary prevention, AI for personalized rehabilitation in secondary prevention, and addressing psychosocial needs in tertiary prevention [37–39]. Chinese research has focused on precise interventions in specific clinical scenarios, often using short-cycle “assessment–intervention–effect” models to achieve immediate clinical value. However, there is limited emphasis on genetically-informed or digitally-scaled approaches. Future development should prioritize: (1) innovative educational scenarios using virtual reality to demonstrate treatment mechanisms or behavioral risks [40, 41]; (2) machine learning-based audience stratification to guide content delivery [42]; and (3) cross-cultural adaptation and evaluation of education models. These strategies can help transition health education from information dissemination to precision behavioral intervention.

Hereditary oncology counseling

Hereditary tumors, accounting for approximately 5% of cancer cases, result from inherited gene mutations and impose significant burdens on affected families [43]. Genetic counseling—a key component of precision nursing—enables risk assessment, testing, and personalized decision-making for high-risk individuals [44, 45]. In Europe and the United States, cancer genetic nurses undergo specialized certification and perform critical roles, including interpreting genetic reports, conducting risk stratification, and providing long-term follow-up [46, 47]. In China, hereditary counseling remains nascent. Key challenges include limited access to genetic testing, lack of specialized nursing roles and certification, and low public awareness [48–50]. Some patients may also perceive genetic risk as stigmatizing, leading to avoidance [51]. To address these gaps, we recommend: establishing a standardized genetic healthcare system with clinical networks and NLP-based report interpretation tools; developing genomic competency training and certification for nurses [52]; and conducting multicenter trials to evaluate nurse-led genetic counseling in local contexts. These steps can help integrate hereditary counseling into routine oncology nursing and enhance precision care capabilities.

Conclusion

In summary, this bibliometric analysis describes the development and major areas of research attention in precision oncology nursing. The main findings suggest that: (1) Chinese-language publications increased rapidly after 2019, although collaboration networks remain relatively fragmented; (2) research attention was mainly distributed across symptom management, health education, and hereditary counseling; and (3) Chinese and international studies showed complementary emphases, with Chinese studies more frequently involving technology-enabled clinical applications and international studies more often addressing mechanistic exploration and system-level development.

To support the high-quality development of precision oncology nursing, the following policy-oriented directions may be considered: (1) Establish a national genomic competency certification system for oncology nurses, drawing on international frameworks (e.g., ISONG certification) to address genomic literacy gaps and improve genetic counseling quality [53, 54]. (2) Promote equitable access to precision nursing resources in primary care, through targeted funding, technology dissemination (e.g., portable genetic testing devices, remote monitoring), and government subsidies to reduce costs (e.g., lowering BRCA test prices by 30%–50%), thereby narrowing regional disparities. (3) Develop a national oncology nursing data platform with unified data standards, core indicators, and robust privacy protections to overcome data fragmentation, enhance multi-center research capacity, and support AI-assisted decision-making [55].

This study identifies potential differences in publication patterns, collaboration structures, and thematic emphases between Chinese and Western precision oncology nursing research, suggesting opportunities for bidirectional learning. Chinese research may benefit from more systematic construction experience reported in Western literature, while Western research may draw insights from China’s technology integration models. By leveraging complementary strengths, Chinese and international researchers may jointly facilitate the evolution of precision oncology nursing from individualized intervention toward whole-cycle health management, with the potential to improve the quality of life of cancer patients.

Limitations

First, regarding data sources, this study exclusively incorporated Chinese and English literature. The source databases employed (e.g., Web of Science, CNKI) may introduce “coverage bias” due to their inherent selection preferences. For instance, Web of Science offers more comprehensive coverage of English-language journals, while CNKI focuses primarily on Chinese publications. This language- and database-dependent retrieval strategy may systematically exclude high-quality studies published in other languages or within specific regional or cultural contexts, thereby limiting the comprehensiveness and generalizability of our findings.

Second, with respect to the search strategy and methodology, although keyword selection and clustering analysis were based on objective algorithms, the process may still entail “selection bias”. Specifically: (1) The initial selection of keywords relied heavily on the research team’s disciplinary expertise, which may not encompass all relevant terms, leading to the omission of some pertinent literature; (2) Inconsistent indexing conventions across databases further introduce uncertainty in search results; (3) Specifically regarding keyword screening boundaries, we acknowledge that certain Chinese keywords (such as “cerebral aneurysm”) with limited relevance to our core topic were initially captured in the search results. While we implemented algorithmic thresholds and manual screening processes to minimize such noise, some residual irrelevant terms may persist in the final analysis, representing a limitation in our keyword filtering mechanism. (4) During clustering analysis, the choice of algorithm parameters involved necessary but subjective decisions that influenced the final knowledge maps.

Third, and most critically, our methodological approach warrants specific reflection. We selected CiteSpace for this analysis based on its particular strengths in temporal evolution tracking and research frontier detection. Its unique capabilities in burst detection and timeline visualization were especially suited for tracing the developmental trajectory of precision oncology nursing. However, we acknowledge that this specialization comes with inherent constraints. The findings are inevitably shaped by CiteSpace’s algorithmic framework, and using alternative tools such as VOSviewer, known for its superior network visualization clarity, or Bibliometrix, which provides more robust statistical integration, might yield complementary perspectives. Furthermore, visualization-based bibliometrics inherently prioritizes macroscopic patterns over microscopic content quality, potentially overlooking nuanced scholarly contributions and clinical insights that are essential in nursing research.

More broadly, bibliometric visualization, while powerful in revealing macro-level patterns, may not fully capture nuanced contextual or semantic subtleties embedded in the literature. Future studies could mitigate these limitations by incorporating multilingual databases, adopting systematic keyword expansion strategies such as drawing on professional thesauri, performing sensitivity analyses on algorithms and parameters, and integrating qualitative methods such as content analysis or expert interviews to triangulate and enrich the quantitative findings.

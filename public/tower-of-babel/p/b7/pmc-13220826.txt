Impact of Surgical Sequence on Outcomes of Combined Phacoemulsification and Kahook Dual Blade KDB Goniotomy: A 1-Year Prospective Comparative Study
Authors: Nicole Mechleb, Antony Nemr, Karim Tomey, Daniel George Cherfan, Georges Cherfan, Syril Dorairaj, Youssef Abdelmassih, Ziad Khoueir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo compare the effect of surgical sequence during combined excisional KDB goniotomy using the Kahook Dual Blade (KDB) and phacoemulsification.MethodsIn this prospective comparative randomized cohort we included patients that underwent combined phacoemulsification and excisional KDB goniotomy with KDB. Eye were randomized into 2 groups: group PG underwent phacoemulsification followed by KDB goniotomy and GP KDB followed by phacoemulsification.ResultsA total of 47 patients (59 eyes) were included: 27 eyes (46%) in group PG and 32 eyes (54%) in GP. Post-operative efficacy outcomes were comparable for IOP, number of medications, decrease in IOP at all the follow-up periods regardless of surgical sequence. Post-operative IOP and number of medications significantly decreased in both groups. Surgical success was achieved in 82% and 73% of eyes at 6- and 12-month follow-up respectively. Postoperative hyphema at day 1 was more frequent in group PG (52% vs. 19%, p=0.008). This difference did not reach statistical significance at day 7 (26% vs. 13%, p=0.16).ConclusionCombined Phacoemulsification-KDB KDB goniotomy has a good safety and efficacy profile. If KDB goniotomy is performed before phacoemulsification, the occurrence of early postoperative hyphema is significantly lower without affecting surgical success in terms of IOP decrease and reduction in glaucoma medication.Keywords: glaucoma, Kahook Dual Blade, excisional KDB goniotomy, phacoemulsification

Introduction

Glaucoma is projected to affect nearly 3.5% of the global population by 2040 and remains one of the leading causes of irreversible blindness worldwide.1,2 Traditional incisional glaucoma surgeries are effective but often associated with significant complications, including hypotony, hyphema, choroidal effusion, and endophthalmitis.3–5 To improve the safety of surgical management, minimally invasive glaucoma surgeries (MIGS) have been introduced.6

The Kahook Dual Blade (KDB; New World Medical Inc., Rancho Cucamonga, CA) is a MIGS device designed to enhance aqueous outflow by excising the juxtacanalicular trabecular meshwork and the inner wall of Schlemm’s canal.7 Using its tapered tip, the instrument is advanced within Schlemm’s canal to remove a 3–5 clock hour strip of trabecular meshwork.8 This approach has been shown to lower intraocular pressure (IOP) and decrease the need for glaucoma medications, both as a standalone procedure and when combined with cataract extraction across different glaucoma severities9–12 The most common complication is transient hyphema, reported in up to 35% of cases.12 When combined with phacoemulsification, the sequence of performing KDB KDB goniotomy before or after cataract extraction is generally left to surgeon preference. However, to date, no study has specifically investigated whether surgical sequence impacts efficacy or complication rates.

The purpose of this study is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of combined phacoemulsification with KDB excisional KDB goniotomy, and to determine whether surgical sequence influences postoperative outcomes.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This was a prospective, single-center, comparative case-control study including all patients who underwent combined phacoemulsification and KDB goniotomy between March 2019 and June 2021 at the Beirut Eye & ENT Specialist Hospital, Beirut, Lebanon. The study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the hospital’s institutional review board (approval number E-2018-02, issued December 2018). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Participants

We enrolled 51 patients (65 eyes) diagnosed with cataract and ocular hypertension or glaucoma at any stage. Eligible cases included both primary and secondary glaucomas, with open- and closed-angle subtypes. Exclusion criteria were age under 18 years, history of prior incisional glaucoma surgery, or complicated cataract surgery. The surgical goal was to achieve better IOP control and/or reduce the need for topical therapy. The primary outcome was surgical success, while secondary outcomes included the rate of postoperative complications.

Surgical Technique

All procedures were performed by four surgeons (ZK, KFT, DC, GC) under topical or regional anesthesia. Patients were randomized into two groups: phacoemulsification followed by KDB KDB goniotomy (PG group) or KDB KDB goniotomy followed by phacoemulsification (GP group). The KDB technique has been described in detail in prior literature.13 Postoperative regimen included topical tobramycin 0.3% combined with dexamethasone 0.1% (Tobradex, Alcon, Fort Worth, TX, USA), tapered over four weeks, and ketorolac tromethamine 0.45% (Acular, Allergan, Dublin, Ireland) for four weeks. Glaucoma medications were adjusted according to IOP control.

Preoperative Assessment

At baseline, all patients underwent a full ophthalmic examination, including Goldmann applanation tonometry, number of IOP-lowering medications, and Humphrey 24–2 SITA Standard visual field testing (Carl Zeiss Meditec, San Leandro, CA). Glaucoma severity was staged using Hodapp-Parrish-Anderson criteria based on mean deviation (MD) into early, moderate, and severe.14 Additional baseline data included age, sex, and systemic medications (especially anticoagulants).

Post-Operative Follow-Up

Follow-up visits were scheduled at day 1, week 1, and months 1, 3, 6, and 12. At each visit, slit-lamp examination was performed, including anterior chamber evaluation, IOP measurement, and hyphema assessment. Both microhyphema (suspended erythrocytes without layering) and layered hyphema were included, with hyphema recorded as a binary outcome (present/absent) and no formal grading system applied. The number of glaucoma medications was also recorded. Postoperative evaluations were conducted by a single examiner (ZK). Surgical success was defined as IOP <21 mmHg with ≥20% reduction from baseline and/or reduction of ≥1 glaucoma medication.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR]), while categorical variables were expressed as percentages. The normality of the continuous data was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Pre- and postoperative comparisons were made using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank, while unpaired t-tests or Mann–Whitney test compared continuous variables between groups. Chi-square tests assessed categorical differences. Time-to-failure analysis was conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method, with group differences tested by log-rank. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline Data

Of the 51 patients (65 eyes) initially enrolled, 4 patients (6 eyes; 4 in the PG group and 2 in the GP group) were lost to follow-up, leaving 47 patients (59 eyes) for analysis. The mean age was 69.2±9.2 years (range: 31–87). The most common diagnoses were primary open-angle glaucoma (37 eyes, 63%) and pseudoexfoliative glaucoma (9 eyes, 14%). Mean preoperative IOP was 18.1±6.9 mmHg (range: 10–45), while the average number of glaucoma medications was 2.8±1.0 (median 3, range 0–4). Visual field testing (available for 50 eyes) showed a mean baseline MD of −9.30±9.38 dB [median: −4.48 (IQR: −14.13; −2.64)]. Based on severity, 8 eyes (16%) had ocular hypertension, 20 (40%) had early glaucoma, 7 (14%) had moderate disease, and 15 (30%) had severe disease.

Twenty-seven eyes (46%) were randomized to the PG group, while 32 eyes (54%) were assigned to the GP group. At baseline, groups were comparable in terms of age, IOP, number of medications, and MD values. Seventeen patients (36%, 20 eyes) were taking anticoagulants, equally distributed between groups (37% in PG vs. 31% in GP; p=0.78). Full baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1.

Discussion

The combination of phacoemulsification and KDB KDB goniotomy has been shown to effectively reduce both intraocular pressure and the burden of glaucoma medications.12,15–17 In this prospective interventional study, mean IOP decreased significantly from 18.1±6.9 mmHg at baseline to 14.0±2.6 mmHg at one year (p<0.001), and the average number of glaucoma medications declined from 2.8±1.0 to 1.5±1.2 (p<0.001). Surgical success at one year was achieved in 73% of eyes, a rate consistent with earlier reports. Sieck et al for example, documented a 718% success rate in a cohort of 124 eyes where KDB was performed after cataract surgery.12 Similarly, Dorairaj et al observed a 262% IOP reduction after combined surgery in medically treated glaucoma, a result in line with ours, though their baseline medication burden was lower (1.8 vs. 2.8 agents).16 Importantly, none of these prior studies specifically evaluated the effect of surgical sequence.

Hyphema was the most frequent postoperative event, occurring in about one-third of eyes at day 1 and persisting in 18.6% at week 1. All cases resolved spontaneously without causing surgical failure or significant IOP spikes. By comparison, Sieck et al reported a lower day 1 hyphema rate (173%) but a 10% incidence of IOP spikes.12 Similar rates of hyphema (15.9%) and IOP spikes (7.9%) were reported by Laroche et al18 The difference in the incidence of postoperative hyphema could be related to the higher number of patients with anticoagulants (36% in our series compared to 20.8% for Sieck et al12) and to difference in the study population where a higher frequency of males were treated in our series (66.7% vs. 40% for Sieck et al12 and 44.4% for Laroche et al18). Hyphema could affect the efficacy of the KDB goniotomy as it could result in the fibrosis and closure of the KDB goniotomy site. This concern may be attenuated by viscotrabeculotomy, as it can helps maintain the trabecular outflow channels open therefore reducing the risk of fibrosis-related failure.19,20

In our population, surgical sequence affected the incidence of early postoperative hyphema during the first postoperative week. Hyphema was more frequent when cataract surgery was performed prior to excisional KDB goniotomy (52% vs. 19%, p=0.008 at day 1 and 26% vs. 13%, p=0.16 at day 7). This incidence of hyphema remained two-fold higher in the group PG without reaching statistical significance which can be due to the low number of patients and to the study being not powered to demonstrate such a difference. All cases of hyphema resolved spontaneously without additional interventions or complications. Hyphema and intraoperative blanching of episcleral veins are considered potential indicators of conventional outflow system patency, and may therefore serve as predictors of successful Schlemm’s canal surgery.21,22 While hyphema can signal a favorable surgical outcome, previous studies have noted its association with reduced BCVA in the early postoperative period23 and with transient IOP spikes.12,22 In our series, early hyphema did not lead to significant post-operative IOP spikes. To limit this complication some surgeons finish the procedure with a relatively elevated IOP and advise patients to maintain head elevation to reduce postoperative blood reflux.24 Another approach to decrease the risk of hyphema is to initiate the combined surgery with KDB KDB goniotomy rather than phacoemulsification, as our findings showed a lower hyphema incidence with this sequence. Although all patients in our series exhibited intraoperative bleeding visible on gonioscopy, differences in postoperative hyphema rates are likely explained by the extended blood washout provided by phacoemulsification fluidics and by the high IOP generated during phacoemulsification, which can limit reflux and promote hemostasis.

The choice of the surgical sequence should be left to the surgeon’s level of experience and preference. Our surgical sequence did not impact the surgical outcomes, including IOP, the number of IOP-lowering medications, or surgical success. Both groups showed comparable control of their IOP and glaucoma medications. “KDB first” offers a better visualization through a pristine cornea and a lower incidence of postoperative hyphema. Unfortunately, the anterior chamber could be narrower during the KDB goniotomy because of the phakic status and the visibility during phacoemulsification could be limited by the blood in the anterior chamber. On the other hand, “phacoemulsification first” offers a better visualization during lens extraction, a deeper anterior chamber, and a wider angle during KDB KDB goniotomy. However, when phacoemulsification is performed first, the visualization of the angle could be limited by corneal edema and there is a higher incidence of postoperative hyphema.

The limitations of this study include lack of long-term follow-up, the small sample size, and the absence of an objective method for hyphema evaluation and grading such as a laser flare meter. Furthermore, including both eyes of some patients could introduce potential inter-eye correlation bias. The advantage of the study is its prospective and randomized design. Future multicenter, adequately powered randomized studies incorporating standardized hyphema grading and morphological characterization would provide valuable additional insights.

To our best knowledge, this is the first study to assess the effect of surgical sequence in combined phacoemulsification-KDB KDB goniotomy on post-operative outcome, complications, as well as surgical success. Performing KDB goniotomy prior to phacoemulsification significantly reduces postoperative hyphema, leading to faster visual recovery without increasing complications. Importantly, surgical sequence does not affect intraocular pressure control or glaucoma medication reduction. These findings suggest that optimizing surgical sequence may improve early postoperative outcomes, particularly in patients at higher risk of bleeding, without compromising surgical efficacy.

Clinical Significance

In combined phacoemulsification-Kahook Dual Blade excisional KDB goniotomy, surgical sequence does not affect efficacy in terms of pressure reduction. Performing KDB goniotomy before phacoemulsification significantly reduces the incidence of KDB goniotomy-induced early postoperative hyphema.

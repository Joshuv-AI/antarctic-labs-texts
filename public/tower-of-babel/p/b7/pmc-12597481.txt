Sustaining the Promise of PCSK9 Inhibitors: Lessons from Real-World Adherence in China
Authors: Atsushi Nohara
Published: 2025

See article vol. 32: 1375-1389

Many patients who would benefit substantially from PCSK9 inhibitors do not receive them. These PCSK9 inhibitors have consistently demonstrated potent LDL-C reduction and improved cardiovascular outcomes; however, their real-world use remains far below expectations.

Multiple factors underlie this treatment gap. First, economic barriers and limited reimbursement policies have historically restricted access, particularly in Western countries, where out-of-pocket costs remain prohibitive. Second, long-term adherence is often poor even when treatment is initiated. Third, physician-related barriers persist, such as hesitancy to prescribe costly biologics and insufficient implementation of stringent LDL-C targets in daily practice.

Another critical issue is the persistently low diagnosis rate of familial hypercholesterolemia (FH). FH represents one of the clearest indications for PCSK9 inhibition; however, it is vastly underdiagnosed in many countries, particularly in Asia, and Japan is no exception 1 , 2) . Chinese studies have reported rates as low as 0.5%, including in large acute myocardial infarction cohorts 3) .

Finally, social and educational disparities have a strong impact on real-world outcomes. As shown in this study, lower educational levels were significantly associated with poor medication adherence, highlighting the importance of health literacy and patient engagement.

In this issue of the journal, Zheng et al. reported adherence rates and influencing factors after the implementation of a PCSK9 inhibitor burden-reduction program in China, and this study offers a different perspective 4) .

Determinants of Adherence: beyond Cost Barriers

Historically, high drug cost has been the dominant barrier to PCSK9-mAb use. In the United States, Europe, and Japan, affordability and co-payment burden are consistently reported as major reasons for discontinuation

Zheng et al.. compared medication adherence across different insurance systems in the same country.

Urban Employees’ Basic Medical Insurance (UEBMI): Targets urban workers with a high reimbursement cap.

Urban Residents’ Basic Medical Insurance (URBMI): Targets unemployed individuals, including children, students, and the elderly, with limited reimbursement.

For reference, China previously had an insurance system for rural residents called the New Rural Cooperative Medical Scheme (NRCMS), but this merged with URBMI to form Health Insurance for Urban and Rural Residents after 2016. However, the extent to which the current study included former NRCMS subscribers is unclear.

Based on real-world evidence, it was expected that medication adherence would be lower in insurance systems with higher copayments. However, notably, no significant difference in medication adherence was observed between the UEBMI and URBMI groups

Importantly, in China, the National Drug Price Negotiation reduced the list prices of PCSK9 inhibitors by more than 70%. With their subsequent inclusion in the National Reimbursement Drug List, patients’ out-of-pocket costs have decreased by approximately 93% 5) . For example, real-world clinical data showed that the annual out-of-pocket costs for evolocumab dramatically decreased from approximately US$4,464 (before negotiation) to approximately US$329 (after negotiation)

China’s medical policy regarding PCSK9 inhibitors contains several unique elements that have not yet been observed in other countries and offers valuable lessons for improving equitable access and adherence. These experiences underscore the need for countries worldwide to look beyond cost barriers and address social, educational, and diagnostic gaps that continue to limit the impact of these powerful therapies.

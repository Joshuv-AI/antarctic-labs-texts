Post-surgical Dental Precautions/Prophylaxis in Pediatric Patients: Results of a 2023 Survey of the Membership of the Pediatric Orthopaedic Society of North America
Authors: James F. Mooney, Ariel Brotherton, Garrett Bullock, Robert F. Murphy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IntroductionGuidelines regarding postsurgical dental care have been common in adult total joint arthroplasty patients despite limited supporting evidence in the literature. There is anecdotal evidence that similar precautions are implemented frequently in postoperative pediatric orthopaedic patients without any published evidence to guide clinicians.MethodsA survey of the Active Members of the Pediatric Orthopaedic Society of North America (POSNA) was performed to assess the use of postsurgical guidelines regarding dental care (procedures and prophylaxis) in pediatric patients. A 20-question survey regarding this subject was developed with the input of, and approved by, the Evidence-Based Practice Committee of POSNA, and a link to participate was distributed to the Active Membership.ResultsOne hundred seventy-three POSNA Members opened the link to the survey. One hundred sixty-three respondents self-identified as Active, for a response rate of 19.8%. One hundred fifty-seven (96.3%) reported direct involvement in surgical and postsurgical care of pediatric orthopaedic patients. Sixty-two (39.4%) indicated use of a postoperative protocol regarding dental interventions. Forty percent of those indicating use of guidelines were uncertain of the specifics of the protocol(s) used at their institution. Eighty-five percent of those utilizing protocols felt that the use of guidelines was a valuable topic for investigation.ConclusionsThese results demonstrate that many of the active Members of POSNA utilize postsurgical guidelines for dental care, particularly for those patients who have undergone spinal surgery with permanent implants. This is despite the fact that there is limited evidence to support their use in adult total joint patients and no literature regarding use of such guidelines in pediatric orthopaedic patients.Key Concepts(1)There is limited and contradictory evidence that postsurgical dental guidelines are indicated in adult orthopaedic patients.(2)There is no published evidence supporting postsurgical dental guidelines in pediatric orthopaedic patients.(3)Almost 40% of active Pediatric Orthopaedic Society of North America Members responding to a survey appear to utilize postsurgical dental care guidelines in pediatric patients, particularly after spinal procedures utilizing implants.Level of EvidenceLevel V

Introduction

Variable and longstanding recommendations exist in the literature regarding the necessity of antimicrobial prophylaxis, and/or general precautions regarding dental interventions, after certain orthopaedic surgical procedures. The primary focus of these guidelines has been to minimize the risk of postoperative infections in adult total joint arthroplasty patients because of the significant potential morbidity of such an infection [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]]. Despite what appears to be frequent use of such guidelines in arthroplasty patients, the actual evidence-based literature on this subject is problematic from both orthopaedic and dental standpoints [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]]. To our knowledge, there are no studies that address the use of, or need for, postsurgical dental guidelines in pediatric orthopaedic patients.

Due to the limitations of the literature on this subject in adult patients, as well as in recognition of seemingly frequent and unstudied use of dental-related protocols after orthopaedic procedures in pediatric patients, we initially undertook a survey of the clinical practices within the Shriners Hospitals for Children system as a performance improvement project for the hospital in Springfield, MA, in 2021 (UNPUBLISHED DATA). Results of that investigation revealed little consistency in the use of postsurgical guidelines within individual hospitals and over the system as a whole. In light of these preliminary data, a more comprehensive survey was developed in conjunction with the Evidence-Based Practice Committee of the Pediatric Orthopaedic Society of North America (POSNA) and distributed to the Active Members of POSNA in 2023. We hypothesized that there would be little consistency within the Members of POSNA as to the existence and use of formal guidelines for patients regarding postsurgical dental interventions or prophylaxis.

Materials and methods

A 20-question survey was generated to assess the existence of and use of specific guidelines regarding dental procedures and prophylaxis in pediatric orthopaedic surgical patients in the postoperative period by the POSNA Active Membership. This survey was developed by the lead author based on a prior survey of the Chiefs of Staff within the Shriners Hospitals for Children system that was circulated and assessed internally in 2021. The current survey was approved by the local institutional review board, as well as approved and endorsed by the Evidence-Based Practice Committee of POSNA.

An announcement of the survey, and a link to the survey, was distributed twice to all Active POSNA Members by the POSNA home office. There were 860 Active Members of POSNA at the time of the survey, and the goal of the survey was to assess the responses of this subgroup. A link within the email took the Member to the survey, which was maintained and administered by using REDCap electronic data capture tools hosted at the authors’ home institution. REDCap (Research Electronic Data Capture) is a secure, web-based software platform designed to support data capture for research studies, providing 1) an intuitive interface for validated data capture; 2) audit trails for tracking data manipulation and export procedures; 3) automated export procedures for seamless data downloads to common statistical packages; and 4) procedures for data integration and interoperability with external sources. Branching logic was used in the survey to create hard stops, skip questions, and guide the respondent to appropriate questions. The initial question of the survey was utilized to segregate all respondents into a self-identified “Active Member” group, and the survey was then terminated for those respondents who did not meet that specific criteria. The survey continued for the “Active” group. The identity of the individual responders could not be traced back by those managing the survey. Data were accessible only through the REDCap site. Responses were recorded in Excel. A copy of the full survey is available in Appendix A.

Statistical analyses

Formal statistical analysis was limited due to the nature of the data generated. Missing data were assessed prior to analyses. Missing data prevalence was low (<5%) and determined to have a missing at random mechanism. A complete case analysis was performed. Descriptive data were calculated and reported as mean (standard deviation) for continuous data and count (%) for count data. All analyses were performed using the R.4.2.1 software from CRAN (Comprehensive R Archive Network). The naniar package was used for missing data analyses, and the dplyr package for cleaning and descriptive analyses.

Results

There were 823 Active Members with a functional email address at the time of initial survey distribution, and 173 Members opened the link. Only 163 respondents continued into the survey and self-identified as Active, for an overall response rate of 19.8% (163/823). One hundred fifty-seven (157/163 or 96.3%) of these stated that they were involved directly in the surgical and postsurgical care of pediatric orthopaedic patients and consented to participate in the study. Sixty-two (62/157) of those with direct patient care responsibilities (39.4%) reported that they utilized protocols regarding, or had limitations on, dental procedures or prophylaxis for pediatric patients who had undergone orthopaedic surgical procedures. When reviewing more specific questions regarding postsurgical dental guidelines, 56 of 62 (90.3%) Members reported that they utilized a specific protocol for spinal patients, either alone or in combination with that used for nonspinal patients. Only 6 of 62 (9.7%) reported using a protocol for nonspinal patients alone. Of the 56 respondents who reported a dental care protocol for spinal patients, all implemented a care protocol for patients with spinal implants, while only 3 of 56 (5.3%) extended the protocol to spinal surgery performed without placement of permanent implants. Guidelines for elective dental cleanings as well as reconstructive dental work for the majority of patients with spinal implants were reported to be in place for 3–6 months (68% and 70%, respectively). Finally, 30 of 56 (54%) of this group reported recommending antibiotic prophylaxis for spinal implant patients. While similar questions were posed regarding the use of protocols and guidelines in nonspinal surgery patients, the large percentages of incomplete survey questions regarding this subject rendered the information difficult to interpret.

The questions at the end of the survey investigated the question of specific antibiotic regimens and recommendations. Only 20 of 62 (32%) of the Members with specific guidelines or protocols reported that they provided specific antibiotic recommendations and dosages for their patients. In those cases, 11 of 20 (55%) of the providers recommended ampicillin. Finally, 53 of 62 respondents felt that investigation of the use of postsurgical dental guidelines was a valuable topic for evaluation. Discussion

One of the most devastating potential complications of any orthopaedic surgical procedure, in either adult or pediatric patients, is postoperative infection. In 1981, Blomgren [7] demonstrated hematogenous seeding of injected S. aureus in a rabbit prosthetic model. Since then, attempts at minimization of this risk, particularly in adult total joint arthroplasty patients, have included the utilization of recommended guidelines regarding dental procedures and antibiotic treatment for arthroplasty patients in the postoperative period. Until 2015, there was general agreement and coordination between the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) and the American Dental Association (ADA) regarding the utility of antibiotic prophylaxis in the first two years after total joint arthroplasty. Since then, the ADA has altered their recommendations to advising against specific prophylaxis [8]. The AAOS has modified their approach as well but has maintained recommendations in certain patient situations [3].

To date, multiple reviews have targeted the data regarding the utility of these guidelines in adult arthroplasty patients [[9], [10], [11]]. Slullitel et al. [11] concluded that there is no evidence to support the use of prophylactic antibiotics prior to postoperative dental procedures in adult arthroplasty patients. In addition, a systematic review of the specific prophylactic antibiotics recommended and utilized in patients who developed postoperative prosthetic joint infection demonstrated that the infection is often caused by an organism not susceptible to amoxicillin—the antibiotic recommended for dental prophylaxis by the AAOS [1]. Overall, the literature provides limited evidence-based support for dental prophylaxis guidelines except in a small population of arthroplasty patients.

The literature suggests that there has been extrapolation of arthroplasty guidelines to adult patients after instrumented posterior spinal fusion [12,13]. Martin et al. [13] published a systematic review of the literature in 2021 regarding use of similar dental prophylaxis requirements after spinal fusion procedures in adults. They found a single article that specifically addressed the subject and met their criteria for review. The conclusion of that single study, as well as of the systematic review, was that there are insufficient data to support the use of antibiotic prophylaxis in adult patients after instrumented spinal procedures. In spite of the dearth of existing literature, the authors of the systematic review emphasized that the relationship between orthopaedic procedures and subsequent dental interventions is an important question that requires further and higher quality study.

A comprehensive review of the English-language literature reveals no studies that address, positively or negatively, the use of postsurgical guidelines for dental procedures or prophylaxis in pediatric orthopaedic patients, regardless of previous underlying diagnosis or procedures. Yet, a “Process-Improvement” project performed in 2021–22 within the Shriners Hospitals for Children system (un-published data) demonstrated that some levels of specific postsurgical guidelines were being utilized at that time in most system hospitals that responded. The survey that was part of that project included questions regarding the use of practice guidelines in postoperative pediatric spinal and nonspinal patients, particularly those procedures involving surgical implants.

In an attempt to delve deeper into this issue, a more comprehensive survey was generated and distributed to the active Membership the POSNA. It was felt that this would give the most complete view of the current status of postsurgical dental guidelines in pediatric orthopaedic patients. The results of the survey demonstrate that, despite a relatively low absolute response rate (essentially 19%), almost 40% of responding active POSNA Members who reported a role in patient care utilize some level of practice guidelines regarding dental care/activity for their patients within the postsurgical period. This appears to be more common for patients with spinal implants than in those who did not undergo spinal procedures. Of note, according to the results of the current survey, the use of these guidelines does not appear to be correlated with years of clinical practice. It seems most likely that the current practice for many POSNA Members is the result of an extension of long-standing AAOS guidelines and recommendations, which at this time are recommended exclusively for specific adult patients considered to be at an increased risk of postoperative periprosthetic infection [3]. It is concerning that guidelines and recommendations to patients are being utilized by pediatric orthopaedic surgeons despite a clear lack of published supporting literature regarding the use of such interventions in the pediatric population.

The relatively broad use of postsurgical guidelines and interventions without supporting clinical evidence, as demonstrated by the survey, raises significant questions and concerns. Most important are the risks inherent in the use of unindicated antibiotic prophylaxis in this patient population. These include a potential increased risk of opportunistic infections (eg, C. difficile enterocolitis) and/or theoretically increasing overall antibiotic resistance in the general population. Additional issues include concerns regarding increased and unnecessary costs (both to the medical systems and patients) inherent in the implementation of such guidelines.

The authors acknowledge the limitations inherent in large-group surveys. These include low response rate, despite a presurvey announcement from POSNA to the Membership, and multiple reminders while the survey was available for access. In addition, the authors made efforts to minimize both the length of the survey and complexity of the process by providing a single link within the reminder emails, as well as a branching logic survey structure that terminated the survey after specific responses. Despite these efforts, the overall response rate was slightly less than 20% of the targeted active POSNA Members. Although low, this response rate is well within the range of recent surveys of the POSNA Membership. Review of survey reports published during 2019–22 shows response rates ranging from 9.7 to 27% [[14], [15], [16], [17], [18]]. A strength of the results was that relatively few respondents skipped questions as the majority of Members completed the entire survey based on their specific practices.

One other issue that may have limited the information available for analysis was noted by the authors during the data analysis process. Despite multiple inputs from multiple reviews of the survey during construction and prior to distribution, the ordering of the questions limited access to the demographic questions (numbers 19, 20, and 21) to only those responders indicating that they utilized “… protocols or limitations regarding dental procedures …” (question 3), rather than collecting demographic information from those “… participating directly in surgical and postsurgical care ….” (question 2). More generalized data from the larger group may have added to the overall value of the study.

Despite these issues, we believe that the results of this survey of the POSNA Membership provide valuable information. This is the first study that evaluated the use of postsurgical dental guidelines in pediatric orthopaedic surgical patients. The results, in light of the existing controversy in the adult literature, raise specific concerns. Nearly 40% of respondents with active clinical surgical practices report the use of patient care guidelines for pediatric orthopaedic patients despite a lack of supporting evidence in the literature. This does not seem to be acceptable in a world increasingly focused on providing evidence-based, rather than anecdotally based, medical care. These results demonstrate the need for further study and thoughtful reconsideration of the currently unsupported use of postsurgical dental care guidelines in pediatric orthopaedic patients.

Short-term outcomes of Perceval sutureless valve replacement in patients with type 0 bicuspid aortic valve
Authors: Baiyu Tian, Jiangang Wang, Cheng Zhao, Wenbo Zhang, Fei Meng, Tiange Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe Perceval sutureless aortic valve offers favorable hemodynamics and reduced operative times in surgical aortic valve replacement (AVR). However, its use in patients with type 0 bicuspid aortic valve (BAV), a condition characterized by annular asymmetry and absence of a raphe, remains controversial, and evidence for this specific anatomical subtype remains limited. This study evaluated the short-term outcomes of Perceval valve implantation in patients with type 0 BAV.MethodsThis single-center, retrospective cohort study included 11 consecutive patients with type 0 BAV who underwent Perceval sutureless AVR between June 2022 and February 2025. All procedures were performed via median sternotomy by a single experienced surgeon. Perioperative, echocardiographic, and clinical outcomes were obtained from institutional databases. Follow-up was conducted through structured telephone interviews.ResultsAll procedures were completed successfully without intraoperative complications. The mean annular diameter was 22.7±1.6 mm, and the mean eccentricity index was 0.2±0.1. Cardiopulmonary bypass and cross-clamp times were 79.6±19.1 and 55.0±16.4 minutes, respectively. No patient experienced paravalvular leakage (PVL), atrioventricular block requiring permanent pacing, valve migration, stroke, or in-hospital mortality. Discharge echocardiography indicated preserved ventricular function and favorable hemodynamics, with a mean gradient of 12.8±1.9 mmHg. Over a median follow-up of 11 months (range, 2–13 months), no valve-related complications, reinterventions, or late deaths occurred.ConclusionsThe findings from this study indicate that the Perceval sutureless valve can be safely and effectively implanted in selected patients with type 0 BAV, including those with a small aortic annulus. With proper preoperative assessment, favorable short-term outcomes can be consistently achieved.Keywords: Type 0 bicuspid aortic valve (type 0 BAV), sutureless valve, Perceval, aortic valve replacement (AVR)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Perceval sutureless aortic valve implantation was found to be feasible and safe in carefully selected patients with type 0 bicuspid aortic valve (BAV).

• Despite the absence of a raphe and the theoretical concern of annular asymmetry, no paravalvular leakage, valve migration, permanent pacemaker implantation, or valve-related adverse events occurred.

• Favorable early hemodynamic performance was achieved, with low transvalvular gradients observed during the short-term follow-up.

What is known and what is new?

• BAV anatomy, particularly the type 0 morphology, has traditionally been considered a relative contraindication for sutureless valve implantation due to annular asymmetry and concerns regarding prosthesis seating and sealing.

• Previous studies on Perceval valve implantation in patients with BAV have primarily focused on mixed morphologies or type 1 BAV, with limited data specifically related to the type 0 anatomy.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• This study provides focused evidence on Perceval sutureless valve implantation exclusively in patients with type 0 BAV.

• Detailed anatomical assessment demonstrated that select patients with type 0 BAV may exhibit near-circular annular geometry, allowing for successful implantation via a standardized technique without additional annular modification.

• The findings indicate that a type 0 BAV morphology alone should not preclude consideration of sutureless aortic valve replacement when anatomical criteria are favorable.

Introduction

Aortic valve replacement (AVR) is the primary treatment for patients with significant aortic valve disease, and the clinical efficacy of conventional surgical AVR has been well established. Bicuspid aortic valve (BAV) is one of the most common congenital causes of aortic stenosis, with a prevalence in the general population of 1–2% and a male predominance of approximately 2:1 (1-3). Contemporary transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) registry data show that the prevalence of BAV in China is markedly high, reaching 38.4% (4). Moreover, within the Chinese BAV population, the Sievers type 0 phenotype is disproportionately represented, accounting for approximately 40–70% of cases according to previous anatomical studies (4,5).

The Perceval sutureless bioprosthetic valve has demonstrated favorable clinical performance due to its characteristic rapid deployment, achieving excellent outcomes in both conventional sternotomy and minimally invasive procedures (6-8). However, its use in BAV remains controversial. BAV morphology—especially the raphe-absent, highly asymmetric, and frequently calcified type 0 anatomy—has traditionally been considered a relative contraindication for Perceval valve implantation (9). With growing operator experience, several high-volume centers have reported acceptable outcomes in select BAV cases (9), although published data on Perceval valve implantation specifically in patients with type 0 BAV remain extremely limited (9,10).

Our center was among the earliest in China to adopt sutureless aortic valve technology and has accumulated substantial experience in managing BAV. We conducted a study in order to evaluate the short-term outcomes of Perceval valve implantation in patients with type 0 BAV. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0081/rc).

Methods

Ethics statement

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The Research Ethics Committee of Beijing Anzhen Hospital, Capital Medical University, Beijing, China, provided ethical approval for this study (No. 2025125X). Informed consent was waived by the ethics committee due to this retrospective study.

Study design

This single-center cohort study evaluated the surgical outcomes of sutureless aortic valve implantation in patients with type 0 BAV.

Between June 2022 and February 2025, a total of 28 consecutive patients with BAV underwent sutureless AVR at Department of Heart Valve and Atrial Fibrillation Center of Beijing Anzhen Hospital, Capital Medical University, all of whom received a Perceval sutureless valve (Corcym, Saluggia, Italy). All operations were performed by a single experienced surgeon (J.W.) via median sternotomy. Because our study’s focus was on the type 0 BAV anatomy, only patients with confirmed type 0 morphology on preoperative echocardiography, intraoperative inspection, and computed tomography angiography (CTA) were included. The other inclusion criteria included (I) a diagnosis of type 0 BAV requiring surgical AVR; (II) sutureless bioprosthetic valve implantation performed by J.W.; and (III) age ≥18 years. Meanwhile, the exclusion criteria included (I) non-type 0 BAV or a tricuspid aortic valve morphology and (II) previous aortic valve interventions. After these criteria were applied, 11 consecutive patients with type 0 BAV were included in the final cohort for analysis.

Surgical procedure

All procedures were performed by a single experienced surgeon, J.W., through median sternotomy under mild hypothermia (34 °C) with cardiopulmonary bypass and antegrade blood cardioplegia. A transverse aortotomy was created 1–2 cm above the sinotubular junction to allow adequate exposure. The native cusps were excised, and meticulous annular decalcification was performed to facilitate proper seating of the sutureless prosthesis. The annulus was then sized according to the manufacturer’s sizers, and an appropriately sized valve was selected. Three guiding sutures were placed 120° apart, and at least one of the three was positioned at a nadir to avoid coronary obstruction and to help create new sinuses (Figure 1). The prosthesis was advanced along the guiding sutures, positioned coaxially, and deployed according to the standard Perceval sutureless protocol. After deployment, the guiding sutures were removed, and a balloon was expanded inside the prosthesis to ensure complete expansion and secure fixation. A final inspection was performed to confirm seating stability, coronary clearance, and the absence of paravalvular leakage (PVL) (Video 1).

Outcome assessment

Postoperative and clinical outcomes were assessed with standardized institutional protocols. Perioperative parameters—including cardiopulmonary bypass time, aortic cross-clamp time, ventilation duration, intensive care unit stay, and total hospitalization—were obtained from the electronic medical records. Early adverse events were evaluated, including PVL, conduction disturbances requiring permanent pacemaker implantation, stroke, valve migration, and in-hospital mortality.

Echocardiographic assessments were performed preoperatively, within the early postoperative period, and at hospital discharge. Parameters collected included aortic root dimensions, left ventricular size and systolic function, transvalvular peak velocity, peak and mean pressure gradients, and presence or severity of stenosis or regurgitation. Clinical follow-up was conducted via structured telephone interviews. Information collected included survival status, symptoms, reinterventions, readmissions, and any valve-related complications. The median follow-up duration was 11 months (range, 2–13 months).

Data management and statistics

All data were collected retrospectively from our institutional valve database. Statistical analyses were performed with SPSS software version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables are presented as the mean ± standard deviation and categorical variables as counts and percentages. No formal comparative statistical testing was performed due to the small sample size and descriptive nature of the study.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 11 consecutive patients with congenital type 0 BAV stenosis undergoing intervention were included in this study. The baseline clinical and anatomical characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The mean patient age was 64.3±8.0 years, and the majority of patients (81.8%) were female (n=9). The mean height and weight were 159.1±6.9 cm and 68.4±10.6 kg, respectively, resulting in a mean body surface area of 1.7±0.1 m2. All patients were symptomatic, with heart failure classified as New York Heart Association (NYHA) class II (63.6%) or III (36.4%). Relevant comorbidities included hypertension (54.5%) and coronary artery disease (27.3%). Preoperative echocardiography confirmed the presence of severe aortic stenosis in all patients, with a mean transvalvular pressure gradient of 66.5±22.3 mmHg and a peak jet velocity of 513.9±82.7 cm/s, without concomitant aortic regurgitation. Left ventricular ejection fraction was preserved (66.4%±3.6%), and the ascending aorta was not significantly dilated (39.7±2.8 mm). The critical anatomical finding pertained to the annular morphology: CTA analysis revealed a mean annular diameter of 22.7±1.6 mm and, notably, a low eccentricity index of 0.2±0.1, indicating a near-circular annular geometry despite the presence of the bicuspid phenotype (Figure 2).

Intraoperative characteristics

The intraoperative data are summarized in Table 2. Two patients (18.2%) underwent concomitant coronary artery bypass grafting, while the remaining cases were isolated AVRs. The mean cardiopulmonary bypass time for the entire cohort was 79.6±19.1 minutes and was 69.3±8.5 minutes among isolated procedures. The mean aortic cross-clamp time was 55.0±16.4 minutes overall and 45.8±6.4 minutes for isolated cases. Regarding valve sizing, the majority of implants were medium (54.5%) or large (36.4%) Perceval valves, with 1 patient (9.1%) receiving an extra-large size.

Postoperative outcomes and follow-up

Discharge echocardiography showed preserved ventricular function and satisfactory valve performance (Table 3). The mean left ventricular end-diastolic diameter and left ventricular end-systolic diameter were 40.6±4.7 and 26.8±3.6 mm, respectively, and the mean ejection fraction was 62.4%±4.5%. The sinus of Valsalva and the ascending aorta measured 32.1±3.5 and 35.3±3.7 mm, respectively. Hemodynamics were favorable, with a peak velocity of 234.7±34.2 cm/s, a peak gradient (PG) of 22.7±4.9 mmHg, and a mean gradient (MG) of 12.8±1.9 mmHg (Figure 3).

Clinical follow-up was completed in all patients, with a median duration of 11 months. No valve-related complications, reoperations, or late mortality were observed during the follow-up period, and no patient required permanent pacemaker implantation.

Discussion

Sutureless aortic valves have been widely adopted in clinical practice over the past decade, providing a more streamlined and efficient approach to surgical AVR. By minimizing or eliminating sutures, these prostheses significantly reduce cardiopulmonary bypass and cross-clamp times and facilitate minimally invasive or high-risk procedures (11). Their rapid-deployment design has yielded favorable short- and mid-term hemodynamic outcomes in both full sternotomy and minimally invasive settings (12). Despite these advantages, BAV anatomy has traditionally been viewed as a relative or even absolute contraindication for sutureless implantation due to its asymmetric sinuses, variable commissural height, and elliptical annulus, all of which may hinder optimal seating and sealing (9). Nevertheless, accumulating experience and improved understanding of anatomy-device interactions have led to a growing number of reports supporting the feasibility of sutureless valves in select patients with BAV (13).

In our study, no deaths or major adverse events—including PVL, stroke, valve migration, or atrioventricular block—occurred during hospitalization or follow-up. Previous studies evaluating Perceval valve implantation in patients with BAV have demonstrated comparable procedural success. Nguyen et al. reported early outcomes in 25 patients with type 1 BAV, noting one perioperative death, five pacemaker implantations, and significant gradient reduction (14). In the study by Szecel et al., there were good midterm results in 11 patients with type 1 BAV without PVL or pacemaker requirement (15). Durdu et al. reported satisfactory outcomes in a mixed BAV cohort that included type 0, 1, and 2 morphologies (16). The largest series to date, published by Miceli et al., included 191 patients with BAV, although no cases of the Sievers type were included (9). Similarly, the multicenter registry study by Suri et al. supported the overall safety of Perceval valve implantation, including in their 20 patients with BAV (17).

This research collectively suggests that sutureless implantation for BAV can achieve outcomes comparable to those for tricuspid aortic valves. However, a pooled analysis by King et al. (18) reported higher rates of new-onset atrial fibrillation, PVL, atrioventricular block, and permanent pacemaker implantation in patients with BAV receiving sutureless or rapid-deployment prostheses, although this analysis included multiple valve types rather than Perceval alone. Conduction disturbances and PVL are the recognized complications of sutureless valves and generally related to prosthesis sizing or implantation depth. With growing surgical experience and the avoidance of oversizing, the strictness of pacemaker requirements has decreased significantly over the past decade, although it remains slightly higher than that of stented bioprostheses (19,20). PVL is often associated with annular asymmetry or suboptimal sizing; however, recent evidence indicates that PVL rates with sutureless valves are comparable to those of surgical bioprostheses and lower than those with TAVR prostheses (21,22).

The type 0 BAV anatomy poses particular challenges due to its pronounced annular asymmetry and absence of a raphe (23). Although the Perceval stent provides circumferential radial support, achieving sufficient annular circularization remains critical to minimizing PVL and migration (12). To overcome these anatomic constraints, several technical modifications have been proposed. Ruchonnet et al. reported the use of subcommissural annuloplasty to improve annular geometry for Perceval valve implantation (24), and Tsai et al. introduced a commissural plication technique specifically designed to correct annular ellipticity in type 0 BAV (23). In our series, standardized surgical steps—including meticulous annular decalcification, accurate sizing, coaxial deployment, and strategic placement of guiding sutures—were adopted to optimize prosthesis seating and reduce annular distortion. Notably, the mean eccentricity index in our cohort was 0.2, indicating that despite the type 0 morphology, the annulus remained nearly circular. As higher eccentricity values represent increased annular ellipticity, this low index likely facilitated successful implantation of the Perceval valve via a standard technique without the need for additional corrective maneuvers.

In our cohort, the mean annular diameter of patients with type 0 BAV was 22.7±1.6 mm. In such small annuli, conventional stented bioprostheses may require annular or root enlargement to avoid patient-prosthesis mismatch (PPM), thereby increasing the operative time. The sutureless design of the Perceval valve lacks a traditional sewing ring, allowing for a larger effective orifice area (EOA) and shorter implantation time (13). Chiariello et al. demonstrated that even the smallest Perceval valve size can achieve hemodynamics comparable to those of larger stented valves. In their study, they found that PPM occurred in 20–70% of the cases provided conventional bioprostheses, whereas the larger EOA of the Perceval valve substantially reduced this risk (25). Moreover, in the study by Belluschi et al., the Perceval valve, as compared with conventional valves, provided an improved indexed EOA and lower PPM rate at 1 year (26). Other research has attested to the superior hemodynamics of the Perceval valve over that of stented valves (27), although survival and major clinical outcomes appear comparable between the two prosthesis types in most studies (11). In the last few years, technological developments in the medical field have been rapid and continuously evolving, with the introduction of the Internet of Things (IoT) concept emerging as a revolutionizing breakthrough in medical practice (28). IoT technology enables real-time physiological data monitoring during and after cardiac surgery, optimizing perioperative management and remote follow-up for AVR patients. Meanwhile, artificial intelligence (AI) has become a powerful tool in cardiothoracic surgery, supporting precise preoperative annular anatomy assessment and prosthesis sizing for complex cases like type 0 BAV via multi-modal imaging analysis (29,30). These technological advancements complement surgical expertise and hold great potential for refining the management of patients with challenging aortic valve anatomies.

Limitations

Our results fill the literature gap regarding the use of Perceval valves in type 0 BAV patients and provide initial technical guidance for surgeons who intend to treat this challenging population. The key limitations of this study include its single-center observational design, small sample size, and short follow-up duration, and therefore longer-term data are needed to validate these findings. We also propose that multi-center, large-sample prospective stu4dies are warranted to validate our short-term outcomes and explore long-term durability.

Conclusions

Our findings suggest that the Perceval sutureless valve can be safely and effectively implanted in select patients with type 0 BAV, including those with a small aortic annulus. If there is an appropriate preoperative evaluation of aortic root morphology, type 0 BAV should not be viewed as a contraindication to sutureless valve implantation. Favorable short-term hemodynamics and clinical outcomes were achieved in the cohort examined in this study.

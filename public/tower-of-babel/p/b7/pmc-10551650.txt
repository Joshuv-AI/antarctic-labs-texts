Gallbladder primary well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumor: A misdiagnosed case
Authors: Nouha Dammak, Houssem Ammar, Mahdi Ben Latifa, Oussama Belkacem, Mohamed Ben Mabrouk, Ali Ben Ali
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionGallbladder (GB) Well-Differentiated (WD) Neuroendocrine Tumor (NET) are extremely rare tumors. They represent only about 0.5 % of all Neuroendocrine Neoplasms (NENs). Most GB-NETs are diagnosed after cholecystectomy. They are often misdiagnosed and have good prognosis.Case presentationWe share our experience of a 42-year-old woman operated on for symptomatic cholelithiasis. We discover on the anatomopathological piece that it was a Grade 1 NET of the GB. Staging scans and octreotide-scans were normal: there is no distant or nodal metastasis. This tumor was classified as T1bN0M0. After multidisciplinary team discussion, additional treatment is deemed unnecessary. She didn't receive any treatment after the surgery. At one year after the cholecystectomy, she is symptom free and there is no sign of recurrence.DiscussionThe diagnosis of GB-WD NET is difficult at the first evaluation. Surgical management for GB cancer has shown increased survival rates, a similar strategy seems reasonable for GB-NETs. Patients with Grade,1 well-differentiated GB-NETS have an excellent prognosis overall.ConclusionResearch is required to facilitate earlier diagnosis and to develop management guidelines for GB-NETs.Keywords: Gallbladder, Neuroendocrine tumor, Surgery, Well-differentiated, Case report

Introduction

Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NEN) are rare tumors, first described by Oberndorfer in 1907 [3]. They arise from disseminated neuroendocrine cells [[1], [2], [3]]. This tumor can be either functional, associated with symptoms reflecting the production of peptides or non-functional [4]. Women are the most affected by this pathology. Most gallbladder neuroendocrine tumors (GB-NET) are diagnosed incidentally after cholecystectomy, with non-specific symptoms. Clinicians may not know how to prepare these patients for discussion with a specialist NET team or may not have access to this service in some areas; This is why it is necessary to know this infrequent histological type. We report here a very rare case of well-differentiated grade 1 (G1) NET from GB.

This case report has been reported in line with the SCARE Criteria [6].

Case presentation

A 42-year-old lady consulted at the hospital, for abdominal pain without vomiting nor transit disorder. She had a one-month history of colicky pain in the right hypochondrium, that improved with simple painkillers. There was no history of fever, jaundice, loss of appetite, weight loss or carcinoid syndrome-related symptoms such as diarrhea, flushing, edema, or wheezing. Her physical examination was normal, and the abdomen was soft. Liver function tests and amylase were normal. Other blood tests also showed no abnormalities. Her transabdominal ultrasound showed a gallstone of 0.7 cm and normal bile ducts. It did not describe any gallbladder wall thickening or polypoid lesion. She had standard laparoscopic cholecystectomy without incident, and she was discharged the following day.

The surgical specimen was addressed to the pathology department for histopathological examination. Grossly, the GB measured 6 cm in length and 4 cm in width. The thickness of the gallbladder was 6 mm. On the cut surface, we found in the corpus, an ill-defined whitish tumor measuring 5 mm in great dimension (Fig. 1). On microscopic examination, neoplastic proliferation infiltrating the mucosa and the muscularis was detected. It consisted of a uniform population of tumor cells arranged in small clusters and tubules. These cells have an abundant eosinophilic and granular cytoplasm. The nuclei have small nucleoli with granular chromatin (Fig. 2: A, B). The mitotic count found one mitosis per 10 high power fields. The surgical margin was free. The neoplastic cells showed diffuse and strong expression of chromogranin A and synaptophysin on immunohistochemical study (Fig. 1: C, D). The Ki67 proliferation index was 1 %. Based on morphological features and immunohistochemical study, the diagnosis of GB Primary Well-Differentiated NET was made. This tumor was classified as grade 1 according to the 2019 WHO Classification of Digestive Tumors [5].

A. At low magnification, tumor formed by small clusters and tubules infiltrating the mucosa and muscularis (Hematoxylin and eosin, HE x 40).

B. At high magnification, tumor cells having a finely granular nucleus with a salt-and-pepper appearance (HE x 400).

D. Immunohistochemical study showing positivity for Synaptophysin.

Staging scans and octreotide-scans (Fig. 3) were indicated, and both were normal: there is no distant or nodal metastasis. This tumor was classified as T1bN0M0 according to 8th edition of TNM classification. This case was discussed in a multidisciplinary team and additional treatment is deemed unnecessary and close clinical and radiological monitoring is decided: at 6 months and then at one year. The patient didn't receive any treatment after the surgery. At one year after the cholecystectomy, she is symptom free and there is no sign of recurrence.

The octreotide-scan: Physiological distribution of the radiotracer in the spleen and liver, as well as normal urinary and digestive elimination.

Discussion

Neuroendocrine Tumors (NET) are extremely rare but their incidence continues to increase, due to increased awareness among radiologists and pathologists and advances in their diagnostic tools [7]. They occur mainly in the gastrointestinal tract. In contrast, gallbladder (GB) NEN accounts for only 0.04 % to 0.2 % of gastrointestinal NEN [8,9]. Well-Differentiated (WD) Neuroendocrine Tumor of the gallbladder accounts 8,4 % of GB-NENs [10].

In fact, the origin of GB-NETs still raises questions and is still the subject of discussion in the literature [11]. In fact, there are no neuroectodermal cells in the gallbladder, so some authors suggest that undifferentiated multipotent stem cells from the gallbladder could be the origin of GB-NEN [10]. On the other hand, since NEC was mainly associated with cholelithiasis, the metaplastic changes in the gallbladder could be explained by chronic inflammation [12].

This pathology affects women aged in their sixties [14]. Ayabé et al. [17] analyzed 754 patients with GBNEN, the majority of whom were women (69 %). Cen et al. [19] analyzed 248 patients with GB-NENs. They reported that 67.3 % of patients were female. Yangyang et al. [10] analyzed 154 patients with GB-NENs and 61.7 % of the patients were women. In our case, it is also a woman who is affected by this tumor. Misdiagnosis before surgery is frequent and most GB-NEN are diagnosed after cholecystectomy. Yangyang et al. [10] reported that the misdiagnosis rate was 100 %.

Neuroendocrine tumors can be functional or non-functional. Functional tumors secrete peptides causing specific symptoms such as insulinomas or vipomas. But most GB-NEN are asymptomatic [13]. This can be explained by the hepatic passage of the peptides produced by these tumors where they will be deactivated. So that, the preoperative diagnosis of GB-NETs and GB-NENs generally is difficult because there are no specific symptoms, and the radiological results are no different from those of other gallbladder tumors [14]. A benign polyp could be the mode of discovery of NEN in 31.6 % of cases [15,16]. That is why, GB-NEN are often incidentally detected by routine histopathological examination of cholecystectomy specimen [14]. Rare cases of huge functional GB-NEN masses can produce diarrhea and flushing, making diagnosis easier preoperatively [18,19].

Diagnostic confirmation of GB-NEN requires an immunohistochemical study based on neuroendocrine markers such as chromogranin A, synaptophysin and CD56. Chromogranin A and synaptophysin are considered specific biomarkers. Chen et al. [12] reported that the respective positive rates for chromogranin A and synaptophysin were 100 % and 88.9 % in patients with neuroendocrine tumors. In addition, chromogranin A levels can be useful for monitoring efficacy and detecting recurrences. Referring to the World Health Organization [5], the mutation status of DAXX and ATRX has been added to the diagnostic criteria for well-differentiated NETs.

Based on the number of mitoses and the Ki-67 proliferation index, NENs are classified into low (G1), intermediate (G2) and high (G3) grades [6]. Cen et al. [20] analyzed 248 patients with GB-NENs. They reported that 31.0 % of the tumors measured <2 cm and 25.8 % of them were grade 3. Compared to previous reports, in our case, the tumors measured <1 cm, grade 1, well-differentiated GB-NET. The pathologist must specify the grade of NET differentiation in his final report, as this is a very important prognostic element and a decision-making element for therapeutic management.

Therapeutic management requires the staging of his lesions according to the Tumor Node Metastasis classification (TNM). Surgical resection remains the only curative treatment [10,21]. Surgical resection is the best means of healing early lesions and overall survival is better in operated patients [20]. A cholecystectomy could be performed for GB-NET in situ or stage T1N0M0.

Patients with GB-NETS could develop metastases even after complete surgical resection [10,20]. Metastases can be seen even with microscopic lesions and appear to be unrelated to tumor size, degree of differentiation, and proliferative indices. The most common metastatic sites are the hilar lymph nodes and the liver [8,10].

Systemic chemotherapy finds its place for NEC and disseminated NEN of the gallbladder. For GB-NETS, early detection of the lesion and complete resection of type R0 are associated with prolonged survival [14]. For Tis lesion or T1, Grade 1 NETs, a cholecystectomy followed by close monitoring is sufficient [14].

Somatostatin analogues are currently under intensive investigation [22,23]. Some studies have shown that somatostatin analogues improve patients' symptoms, in the case of functional and symptomatic NENs.

Most patients with G1-G2 NET have prolonged survival with slow progression. Patients with well-differentiated G1 tumors metastatic to the liver have an excellent prognosis overall and are long survivors [10]. This must be considered in their care [24]. The median disease-free survival for G1-G2 NET was 55 months [25].

Conclusion

We have reported a rare case of well-differentiated Grade 1 GB-NET diagnosed by routine histopathological examination of the gallbladder specimen. Surgical resection is the only curative treatment of GB-NET.

Given that GB-NETs are generally misdiagnosed, GB-NET must always be taken into consideration when interpreting radiological exploration and before therapeutic indication.

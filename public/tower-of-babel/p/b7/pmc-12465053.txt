Study on the effect of HMGB1 on immune cells isolated from SLE mice
Authors: Xin Qin, Jinglin Gao, Xiuhua Liu, Jie Chen, Xinghui Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSystemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is a complex autoimmune disease, and its pathogenesis is related to the balance and dysfunction of dendritic cells (DCs). The therapy targeting dendritic cells has become an important means to treat SLE and autoimmune diseases. Besides, HMGB1 is highly expressed in the serum of patients with SLE. Therefore, this study intended to find the function of HMGB1 on immune cells in SLE. We used the MRL/lpr mice as SLE model animals, then we isolated the peripheral blood of SLE mice to obtain CD14+ cells, which was induced into monocyte-derived Dendritic cells (Mo-DCs). We also obtained CD14+ cells from peripheral blood to explore the effect of HMGB1 treated Mo-DCs. Then followed the HMGB1 treatment, flow cytometry, ELISA, WB and CCK8 assay. The results showed that HMGB1 can stimulate the maturation of DCs, and activated mTOR pathway, and affect the proliferation and differentiation of CD4+ cells. What's more, HMGB1 seemed to enter cells through the endocytosis of Toll-like receptor, and its function maybe related to endoplasmic reticulum stress. The further research found tunicamycin (endoplasmic reticulum stress inducer) and rapamycin (mTOR inhibitor) can inhibit the activation of Mo-DCs. Therefore, we thought that HMGB1 enters DCs by TLR6 and regulate the activation of DCs and the differentiation of CD4+ cells by influencing endoplasmic reticulum stress and the mTOR pathway.Keywords: SLE, HMGB1, Immune cells, mTOR pathway, Endoplasmic reticulum stress

Introduction

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is a complex autoimmune disease that involves multiple systems. It is multifactorial, involving epigenetic, genetic, ecological and environmental factors [1]. It is mainly due to the abnormal activation of innate immunity and adaptive immunity, the T cells activate self-reactive B cells and cause immune complexes to deposit in tissues, thereby leading to an autoimmune cascade reaction. This may occur in a single organ or affect the entire body [2]. The clinical manifestations of SLE are extensive. Clinically, mild cases can resolve spontaneously, but severe cases can endanger the patient's life [3].

Dendritic cells (DCs) are innate immune cells, which are critical for immune activation and tolerance. A variety of stimuli and environmental factors are critical in promoting DCs to perform its multifaceted functions, which determine the outcome of the adaptive immune response [4]. Dendritic cells are the most efficient antigen uptake cells and are the key link between the innate and adaptive immune systems. The pathogenesis and pathogenic mechanism of systemic lupus erythematosus is related to the balance and dysfunction of dendritic cells [5]. Therefore, the therapy targeting dendritic cells has become an important means to treat systemic lupus erythematosus and autoimmune diseases [6].

High-Mobility Group Box 1 Protein (HMGB1) is a member of the high-mobility histone family, and in the cell nucleus, HMGB1 plays an important role in bending DNA and facilitating its interaction with transcription factors [7]. HMGB1 also acts as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) outside the cell, activating the immune system and promoting inflammation [8,9]. In our previous research, after stimulating SLE derived Myeloid dendritic cells (mDCs) with HMGB1, the expression of HLA-DR, CD40 and CD86 and the secretion of inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-6 and IL-1β in the cell culture supernatant were significantly higher than those in the normal control group, indicating that HMGB1 can activate mDCs, and the activation of mDCs is more intense in SLE patients [10].

Endoplasmic reticulum stress (ER stress) is the response of cells to conditions such as the aggregation of misfolded and unfolded proteins in the lumen of the endoplasmic reticulum and the dysregulation of calcium homeostasis [11]. The reaction processes that activate signal pathways such as unfolded protein reactions, endoplasmic reticulum overload reactions, and caspase-12-mediated apoptotic pathways [12]. Endoplasmic reticulum stress can not only induce the expression of endoplasmic reticulum chaperone molecules such as glucose-regulated protein GRP78 and GRP94 to produce a protective effect [13], but also independently induce endogenous apoptosis, ultimately affecting the outcome of stressed cells, such as adaptation, injury or apoptosis [14]. In this research, we tried to explore the effects of HMGB1 on DCs, and we observed HMGB1 can affect the maturation of DCs. Further research has found that HMGB1 seems to be related to the mTOR pathway and endoplasmic reticulum stress. Based on these results, we designed experiments to explore the mechanisms. Hope that our results can provide assistance for the therapeutic intervention of SLE.

Materials and methods

Reagents and antibodies

Human CD4 Nanobeads and human CD14 Nanobeads were purchased from Beaverbio (Suzhou, China) and Sangon (Shanghai, China). INF-γ-APC and IL-22-PE were obtained from Thermo Fisher (Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). CD40-PE, CD80-APC, CD86-FITC, and IL-17-FITC were purchased from Elabscience (Wuhan, China), HMGB1, GM-CSF, and IL-4 were purchased from Sigma (Germany), and tunicamycin, rapamycin, 4-PBA and 3-MA were purchased from MCE (New Jersey, USA). All the antibodies: GAPDH, LC3, PI3K, PTEN, p-Akt, p-mTOR, P70S6K, 4EBP1, TLR 4, TLR 6, Bip and Chop came from Sanying (Wuhan, China). All of the ELISA kit was from Jianglai Biotechnology (Shanghai, China).

SLE model mice

Eight-week-old MRL/lpr mice purchased from Huachuang Sino (Jiangsu, China). Mice were housed under specific pathogen–free conditions (12:12-h light cycle with free access to foodand water). And female mice were used for all experiments. And all experiments were performed with the approval and oversight of the Liuzhou Worker's Hospital.

Cell sorting and cell treatment

Peripheral blood of SLE model mice was used to separate CD14+ cells by CD14+ magnetic beads, then CD14+ cells were detected by flow cytometry, after that, the IL-4 (35 ng/ml) and GM-CSF (50 ng/ml) were added to induce CD14+ cells to become Mo-DCs for 7days. And HMGB1 with different concentration (100, 500 and 1000 ng/ml) was added into Mo-DCs for 24h. CD4+ mononuclear cells were sorted by CD4+ beads from peripheral blood, and CD4+ cells were co-cultured with HNGB1-stimulated Mo-DCs at a ratio of 4:1 for 7 days. After that, tunicamycin, 4-PBA, 3-MA and rapamycin will be added into the cells to explore the internal molecular mechanisms.

Flow cytometry

For the cells to be tested in culture, the supernatant was discarded, and 1 mL PBS was added to clean the cells once, the supernatant was discarded, and 1 mL 0.25 % pancreatic enzyme digestion cells were added. When the cells became round and some cells were suspended, the digestion was terminated by adding PBS. Gently blow the cells with a pipette gun to suspend them. The cell suspension was transferred into a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube and centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min to collect cells. 1 mL of PBS pre-cooled at 4 °C was added to the completely suspended cells, centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min, and the supernatant was discarded. Add 100 μL corresponding antibody diluent (prepared with PBS) and stain away from light for 30 min. The PBS cells were completely suspended with 500 μL precooled at 4 °C, centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min, and the supernatant was discarded. The cells were suspended with 200 μL PBS precooled at 4 °C, and the cells were precipitated, blown and mixed.According to the fluorescent dye on the antibody, the appropriate channel was selected for on-machine detection. FlowJo software was used to analyze the positive rate of cells and the average fluorescence intensity in the positive region.

ELISA

Cytokine 1L-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α secreted in the cell supernatant were determined using the ELISA kit according to manufacturer's instructions on an enzyme-labeled instrument (Beijing Liuyi, China).

CCK8 assay

The cells were inoculated into 96-well culture plates with a density of 1.0 × 104 cells/well. After 24 h of culture, 10 μL CCK8 reagent was added to each well and incubated at 37 °C for 1–4 h. The light absorption value at 450 nm was determined by an enzyme-labeled instrument (WD-2102B, Beijing Liuyi, China).

Western blot

The BCA kit (Cwbio, China) was used to measure protein concentration. Following the measurement, protein electrophoresis was performed using a 12 % SDS-PAGE gel to separate proteins based on their molecular weight. Then the proteins were transferred from the SDS-PAGE gel to a PVDF membrane. The membrane was subjected to a blocking procedure using 5 % skim milk, which serves to saturate any remaining binding sites. After this step, the membrane was soaked in primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, which included various primary antibodies: GAPDH, LC3, PI3K, PTEN, p-Akt, p-mTOR, P70S6K, 4EBP1, TLR 4, TLR 6, Bip and Chop, all of the primary antibodies were at a dilution of 1:1000. Then we added the secondary antibody which was applied at a dilution of 1:3000 for 2 h at room temperature. Finally, the protein bands were visualized through the addition of an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagent (Beyotime, China).

Statistical analysis

The data in this study were presented as mean values accompanied by their standard deviations (SD), providing a clear indication of the central tendency and variability within the dataset. To analyze this data, the one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) method was utilized, which is a robust statistical procedure implemented through GraphPad Prism version 9.0 software. This analytical approach allowed for the comparison of means across multiple groups. Furthermore, a P-value of less than 0.05 was considered indicative of a statistically significant difference, signifying that the observed variations among the groups were unlikely to have occurred by chance alone.

Result

The effect of HMGB1 treatment on Mo-DCs

The isolated CD14+ cells were identified by flow cytometry, the ratio of CD14+cells is 85.6 % (Fig. 1A). And then CD14+ cells were cultured with 35 ng/ml IL-4 and 50 ng/ml GM-CSF for 7 days to differentiate into Mo-DCs. Subsequently, HMGB1 with final concentrations of 100, 500 and 1000 ng/ml were added to stimulate Mo-DCs. Compared with the negative control (NC) group, the levels of CD80 and CD86 increased after HMGB1 stimulation, especially treated by 500 ng/ml HMGB1 (Fig. 1B). The levels of cytokines 1L-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α were determined by ELISA, the results showed that after the treatment, the levels of IL-6, IL-10, IL-1β and TNF-α increased compared with the NC group (Fig. 1C–E). Then we used ovalbumin to detect the antigen presentation ability, compared with the UT group (CD14+ cells), Mo-DCs had a better antigen presentation ability, compared with the NC group, the antigen presentation abilities of Mo-DCs treated with HMGB1 had no obvious difference (Fig. 1G).

The effect of HMGB1 treatment on Mo-DCs. (A) Identification of CD14+ cells by flow cytometry. (B) The levels of CD80+/CD86+ cells in Mo-DCs by flow cytometry (N = 3 in each test; ∗p < 0.05 vs NC). (C–F) The levels of 1L-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α in Mo-DCs determined by ELISA (N = 3 in each test; ∗p < 0.05 vs NC). (G) The antigen uptake abilities of Mo-DCs (N = 3 in each test; ∗∗p < 0.005:NC vs UT, ns means no significant difference: 100, 500 and 1000 vs NC).

The effect on CD4+ cells co-cultured with Mo-DCs stimulated by HMGB1

The isolated CD4+ cells were identified by flow cytometry (Fig. 2A). CD4+ cells were co-cultured with HMGB1-stimulated Mo-DCs at a ratio of 4:1 for 7 days, and cell proliferation was detected by using CCK8 assay, the results showed that, compared with NC group, CD4+cells co-cultured with HMGB1-stimulated Mo-DCs had higher cell viabilities (Fig. 2B). After the flow cytometry, compared with NC group, the expression levels of IL-17 and IL-22 in CD4+ cells decreased, while the expression of IFN-γ appeared irregular (Fig. 2C–E).

The effect on CD4+ cells co-cultured with Mo-DCs stimulated by HMGB1. (A) Identification of CD4+ cells by flow cytometry. (B) Cell viability of co-cultured cells by CCK8 assay (N = 3 in each test; ∗∗∗p < 0.001 vs NC, ∗∗∗∗p < 0.001 vs NC). (C–E) The levels of IFN-γ, IL-17 and IL-22 in CD4+ cells by flow cytometry (N = 3 in each test; ∗p < 0.05 vs NC, ∗∗p < 0.005 vs NC, ∗∗∗p < 0.001 vs NC, ns means no significant difference).

The effect of HMGB1 treatment on the mTOR pathway related proteins in mo-DCs

Western blotting was used to detect the expression of mTOR pathway related proteins. As results shown in Fig. 3A–G, the expression levels of 4EBP1, PI3K, p-Akt, p-mTOR and P70S6K all were increased after stimulation of mo-DCs with different concentrations of HMGB1, while the expression level of PTEN were downregulated after the stimulation of HMGB1.The increase levels of 4EBP1, PI3K, p-Akt, p-mTOR and P70S6K indicated that the activation of mTOR pathway.

(B–G) The expression levels of 4EBP1, PI3K, p-Akt, PTEN, p-mTOR and P70S6K (N = 3 in each test; ∗p < 0.05 vs NC, ∗∗p < 0.005 vs NC, ∗∗∗p < 0.001 vs NC, ∗∗∗∗p < 0.001 vs NC; ns, means no significant difference).

The effect of HMGB1 treatment on the TLR receptor in mo-DCs

We detected the expression level of TLR receptors, TLR4 and TLR6. As we can see in Fig. 4A–C, the level of TLR4 has no significant difference between NC group and HMGB1 treated groups, while the level of TLR6 in HMGB1 treated groups was increased compared to NC group, especially the group treated by 500 ng/ml HMGB1.

The effect of HMGB1 treatment on TLR receptor and ER stress. (A) The bands of TLR4, TLR6, Bip and Chop. (B–E) The expression levels of TLR4, TLR6, Bip and Chop (n = 3 in each test; ∗p < 0.05 vs NC, ∗∗p < 0.005 vs NC, ∗∗∗p < 0.001 vs NC, ∗∗∗∗p < 0.001 vs NC, “ns” means no significant difference).

The effect of HMGB1 treatment on ER stress in mo-DCs

To explore the effect of HMGB1 on ER stress, Bip and Chop were detected by WB. The expression level of Bip and Chop can indicate the intensity of ER stress. In Fig. 4A, D and 4E, compared with NCgroup, the expression levels of Bip and Chop was down-regulated in HMGB1 treated groups, especially the group treated with 500 ng/ml HMGB1.

The effect of tunicamycin and rapamycin on Mo-DCs stimulated by HMGB1

Flow cytometry was used to detect the maturation of Mo-DCs. Compared to the tunicamycin group, the maturation rate of Mo-DCs cells in 4-PBA group was lower. Compared to rapamycin group, Mo-DCs cell activation ratio was higher in 3-MA group (Fig. 5A). ELISA was used to detect expression levels of 1L-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α in each group. Compared to the tunicamycin group, levels of 1L-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α were downregulated in 4-PBA group. Compared to rapamycin group, levels of 1L-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α were upregulated in 3-MA group (Fig. 5B–E). OVA antigen was used to detect the antigen presentation ability, compared to tunicamycin group, the Mo-DCs in 4-PBA group had a better ability to present OVA antigen. Compared to rapamycin group, the ability of Mo-DCs in 3-MA group to take up OVA antigen decreased (Fig. 5F).

The effect of tunicamycin and rapamycin on Mo-DCs stimulated by HMGB1. (A) The levels of CD80+/CD86+ cells in Mo-DCs by flow cytometry (n = 3 in each test; ∗∗∗p < 0.001). (B–E) The levels of 1L-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α of Mo-DCs determined by ELISA (n = 3 in each test; ∗p < 0.05). (F) The antigen uptake abilities of Mo-DCs (n = 3 in each test; ∗∗p < 0.005, ∗∗∗p < 0.001).

The effect of tunicamycin and rapamycin CD4+ cells co-cultured with Mo-DCs stimulated by HMGB1

CCK8 assay was used to detect the cell proliferation CD4+ cells, in the results, tunicamycin group can obviously stimulate cell proliferation compared to 4-PBA group; 3-MA group can obviously stimulate cell proliferation compared to rapamycin group (Fig. 6A). Flow cytometry was used to detect the differentiation of CD4+ cells. The expression levels of IFN-γ, IL-17 and IL-22 in 4-PBA group decreased compared to tunicamycin group. Compared to rapamycin group, the expression levels of IFN-γ, IL-17 and IL-22 in 3-MA group increased (Fig. 6B–D).

The effect of tunicamycin and rapamycin CD4+ cells co-cultured with Mo-DCs stimulated by HMGB1. (A) Cell viability of co-cultured cd4+ cells by CCK8 (n = 3 in each test; ∗∗p < 0.005, ∗∗∗p < 0.001). The effect of tunicamycin and rapamycin on the mTOR pathway related proteins

In Fig. 7, western blotting was used to detect the expression of proteins in Mo-DCs, compared to tunicamycin group, expression levels of p-AKT, LC3, P70(S6K), PI3K and p-mTOR in 4-PBA group increased, and levels of 4EBP1, PTEN and LC3 II decreased. Compared to rapamycin group, the expression levels of p-AKT, LC3, P70(S6K), PI3K and p-mTOR in 3-MA increased, while the levels of 4EBP1, PTEN and LC3 II decreased.

Discussion

After the stimulation of Mo-DCs with different concentrations of HMGB1, the expression levels of cytokines: 1L-1β, IL-6, IL-10 and TNF-α, increased by ELISA. The researchers found that after the maturation of Mo-DCs, the expression of cytokines would increase significantly [15]. Our ELISA results proved that HMGB1 promoted the maturation of Mo-DCs. In addition, flow cytometry results showed that the proportion of DCs co-expressing the molecules CD80 and CD86 increased. Studies have confirmed that the positive rate of CD80 and CD86 in DCs will increase when DCs mature [16]. These results suggest that HMGB1 can stimulate the maturation of Mo-DCs. As the results of antigen uptake ability of Mo-DCs we can see, compared to UT group, the antigen uptake ability of experimental groups increased, but compared with NC group, there was no significant difference between NC group and HMGB1- stimulated groups.

CD4+T lymphocytes were isolated from SLE model mice and co-cultured with HMGB1-stimulated Mo-DCs. CCK8 results confirmed the increased cell viability of CD4+T lymphocytes. Flow cytometry showed that level of IFNγ, IL17 and IL22 increased compared with the PMA group, indicating that the original T lymphocytes differentiated by co-culturing with HMGB1-stimulated Mo-DCs. Compared with NC group, the proportion of IL-17 and IL-22 decreased. These results indicated that the proportion of CD4+T lymphocytes differentiating into TH17 cells and TH22 cells decreased in co-culture chamber with dendritic cells stimulated by HMGB1 [17].

Western blotting of Mo-DCs in each group after HMGB1 stimulation showed that the expressions of 4EBP1, PI3K, p-Akt, p-mTOR and P70S6K all increased, while the expressions of PTEN were down-regulated after HMGB1 stimulation. PI3K, P70S6K, p-Akt and p-mTOR are the key proteins related to the mTOR pathway, and their increased phosphorylation indicates a higher activation level of the pathway [18].

According to previous research which reported that HMGB1 enters DCs by toll-like receptor-mediated endocytosis [19]. We detected the expression level of TLR4 and TLR6 after the HMGB1 stimulation, the results showed expression level of TLR4 has no significant change between NC group and HMGB1 treated groups; while the expression level of TLR 6 in HMGB1 treated groups was increased, especially the group treated by 500 ng/ml HMGB1. Therefore, the TLR6 may be the main receptor to transport HMGB1 into the DCs. After the endocytosis, we tried to explore the effect of HMGB1 in vesicle-related organelles, like endoplasmic reticulum. Thus, Bip and Chop became the analysis objective of WB, which can indicate the degree of endoplasmic reticulum stress [20]. According to our results, the levels of Bip and Chop were decreased after HMGB1 stimulation, which indicated ER stress has weakened.

In order to further explore the mechanism, tunicamycin (an ER stress enhancer) [21], and 4-PBA (4-phenylbutyric acid, an ER stress inhibitor) [22], were used in the next experiments. And the rapamycin (mTOR pathway inhibitor) [23], and the 3-MA (3-methyladenine, mTOR pathway enhancer) [24], treated HMGB1-stimulated dendritic cells. After flow cytometry and pin-by-pair comparison, we found that the maturation process of dendritic cells was affected in tunicamycin group and rapamycin group, compared to 4-PBA and 3-MA group. ELISA results showed that compared to tunicamycin group, the level of cytokines in 4-PBA group increased. Compared to rapamycin group, the level of cytokines in 3-MA group significantly increased. The results indicated HMGB1 was related to ER stress and mTOR pathway.

Flow cytometry showed that the antigen uptake ability of Mo-DCs treated with different drugs was significantly higher in the tunicamycin group and the rapamycin group. Researchers reported that immature Mo-DCs have better ability to uptake antigen [25]. Therefore, the results indicated that ER stress and autophagy had an inhibitory effect on the maturation of Mo-DCs.

Flow cytometry showed that level of IFN-γ, IL-17 and IL-22 increased significantly in 4-PBA and 3-MA group. These results indicate that inhibition of ER stress and inhibition of mTOR pathway can stimulate the differentiation of CD4+ cells into Th1 cells and Th17 cells. Th1 cells can enhance cell-mediated anti-infection immunity by secreting cytokines [26], while many cytokines such as IL-17 and IL-22 secreted by Th17 cells play an important role in the occurrence and development of immunopathological injuries, especially autoimmune diseases [27].

Compared to tunicamycin group, the expression of p-AKT, LC3, P70(S6K), PI3K and p-MTOR in Mo-DCs in 4-PBA increased, while the expression of 4EBP1, LC3 II and PTEN protein decreased. Compared to rapamycin group, the expression levels of p-AKT, P70(S6K), PI3K and p-mTOR increased in Mo-DCS in 3-MA group, and the expression levels of 4EBP1, LC3 II and PTEN decreased. The results indicated that enhance of ER stress has positive effects on the maturation of Mo-DCs, and activation mTOR pathway has positive effects. LC3 II was correlated to the level of autophagy, the expression level LC3 II increased indicates that cells are autophagy [28]. The expression level of LC3 II in 4-PBA and 3-MA groups decreased, indicating that autophagy was inhibited.

What's more, it would be better to use Co-IP in our research, Co-IP can reflect the interactions between proteins [29], we can detect the interaction between HMGB1 and proteins related to mTOR pathway by Co-IP, or the interaction between HMGB1 and toll like receptors. And the use of transmission electron microscopy to observe the ER is a better idea. Experiments on animals are necessary. Verification at the animal level can better illustrate our results, and this will also be our next task.

Conclusion

HMGB1 enters cells by TLR6 and regulates the activation of DCs and the differentiation of CD4+ cells by influencing endoplasmic reticulum stress and the mTOR pathway.

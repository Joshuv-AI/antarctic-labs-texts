Dynamic metabolic regulation and vitamin C-mediated inhibition of Vibrio splendidus persisters by single-cell Raman spectroscopy
Authors: Meishan Qin, Yuehui He, Yuanyuan Zhou, Peng Zhang, Liyuan Guo, Chenghua Li, Shanshan Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Single-cell Raman spectroscopy coupled with D2O labeling was first applied to Vibrio splendidus persisters.•V. splendidus persisters are metabolically active rather than fully dormant.•Persisters show remodeled cellular components with decreased nucleic acids and increased lipids and polysaccharides.•Vitamin C inhibits persisters formation potentially via the stringent response, providing a green anti-persister approach.

Introduction

Persister cells are a specialized subpopulation of bacteria formed under stress conditions such as antibiotic exposure and nutrient deprivation. Without genetic mutations, they tolerate multiple environmental stresses through transient phenotypic changes (non-growth and non-division) (Hong et al., 2012). These bacteria can resume growth after the removal of stressors and retain sensitivity to the same stresses (Nakouti et al., 2017; Niu et al., 2024). The presence of persister cells has been identified as a critical factor contributing to the failure of clinical treatments and the recurrence of chronic infections, thereby increasing the difficulty of therapeutic interventions (Fisher et al., 2017; Wilmaerts et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2023). For instance, the pathogen Mycobacterium tuberculosis, which is responsible for the global burden of tuberculosis, is capable of entering a persister state during antibiotic treatment, significantly diminishing the efficacy of antibiotics (Mandal et al., 2019; Negi and Sharma, 2024). In addition, Pseudomonas aeruginosa persister cells in chronic pulmonary infections of patients evade eradication even after prolonged antibiotic treatment, contributing to the decline in lung function and recurrent acute exacerbations (Pang et al., 2019; Elborn et al., 2021).

While persister cells have been extensively studied in clinical medicine, research in the field of aquaculture remains relatively limited. However, recent studies have indicated that several aquaculture pathogens are capable of forming persisters, including Aeromonas hydrophila, Vibrio cholerae, and Yersinia ruckeri (Awan et al., 2018; Silva-Valenzuela et al., 2017; Calderón et al., 2021). The latency and resuscitative characteristics of these persisters are increasingly recognized as a new bottleneck in disease prevention and control within aquaculture (Li et al., 2021). Vibrio splendidus, a common opportunistic pathogen in marine environments, is the primary causative agent of skin ulcer syndrome (SUS) in the sea cucumber Apostichopus japonicus (Liu et al., 2010). Previous work from our laboratory has demonstrated that this bacterium can induce persister formation under stresses such as antibiotic exposure and host immune factors, and these persisters can resume growth and proliferation upon antibiotic removal (Li et al., 2021). Acting like "seeds," these cells can persist in the aquaculture environment or within the host and resuscitate when host immunity is compromised or environmental conditions change, thereby creating a vicious cycle of "treatment-relapse". This cycle not only hampers the radical cure of related diseases but also drives the overuse of antibiotics, leading to cascading problems such as aquatic microecological imbalance, drug residues in aquatic products, and the spread of bacterial resistance. Therefore, developing effective strategies to identify and intervene against V. splendidus persisters has clear practical significance for controlling the persistent infections it causes.

Traditionally, persister identification has relied on the antibiotic killing curve (Gefen et al., 2009), which characterizes the level of persister cells by observing the number of colony-forming units (CFUs) surviving under high concentrations of antibiotics. However, this population-level measurement fails to adequately capture the highly heterogeneous nature of persisters, and their true physiological state at the single-cell level remains largely unclear. In recent years, the emergence of single-cell analysis technologies has provided revolutionary perspectives in the life sciences. For phenotypes like persister cells, which are defined by a small number of "special individuals" within a population, studying "individual behaviors" is far more valuable than examining "population averages" (Balaban et al., 2019). Against this background, Single-Cell Raman Spectroscopy (SCRS) has demonstrated its unique advantages. SCRS is a label-free and non-invasive analytical technique based on inelastic light scattering (Huang et al., 2010). When a laser beam irradiates a single cell, the chemical bonds within the cell produce a unique vibrational spectrum, known as the Raman spectrum. This spectrum provides an intrinsic fingerprint of chemical composition, which can be used to identify functional changes in cells and inherently reflect their biochemical components, including information on various macromolecules such as nucleic acids, proteins, lipids, and carbohydrates (Huang et al., 2010; Camp Jr and Cicerone, 2015). When combined with deuterium isotope labeling technology, precise quantification of the metabolic activity of individual cells can be achieved by detecting the carbon-deuterium (C-D) bond signals generated after cells metabolically incorporate heavy water (D2O) (Berry et al., 2015). This D2O-based SCRS (D2O-Ramanometry) has been successfully applied to study persister cells in Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Ueno et al., 2019) and Escherichia coli (Wang et al., 2022), and has revealed their active metabolic characteristics that are different from traditional cognition. This provides a powerful methodological reference for studying V. splendidus persisters under in situ and non-destructive conditions.

Based on this background, the present study employs D2O-Ramanometry to identify and analyze the metabolic activity and changes in cellular components of V. splendidus persister cells. Additionally, compounds that can regulate the formation of persister cells are screened, and their potential mechanisms of action are preliminarily explored. This work will deepen our understanding of the biological characteristics of V. splendidus persister cells, and provide a theoretical basis and technical support for controlling persistent infections caused by this pathogen.

Materials and methods

Bacterial culture

The Vibrio splendidus strain AJ01 was isolated, identified and preserved in our laboratory. The culture media used in this study was LB35 (10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, and 35 g/L NaCl).

Antibiotic-induced persister cells

As mentioned earlier (Li et al., 2021), the formation of persister cells in V. splendidus was investigated by exposing the bacteria to high concentrations of antibiotics. Overnight bacterial cultures of V. splendidus were inoculated into fresh medium and incubated in a shaking incubator at 28 °C until the optical density at 600 nm (OD600) reached 1.0. To induce persister formation, antibiotics were applied at 20× MIC: tetracycline was added to the culture, and the bacteria were treated for 4 h at 28 °C to eliminate non-persister cells. To quatify viable persisters, 1 mL of the bacterial culture was harvested, washed three times with PBS, and serially diluted in PBS. Then, 10 μL of the diluted samples was spotted onto agar plates and incubated overnight at 28 °C. Colony counts were recorded to assess the persistence of bacterial cells.

Single-cell Raman spectroscopy

The overnight culture of V. splendidus was inoculated into fresh medium and incubated at 28 °C on a shaking incubator until the OD600 reached 1.0. Subsequently, tetracycline was added to induce the formation of persister cells by treatment for 4 h. The bacterial cells were pelleted by centrifugation and resuspended in medium containing 25% D2O, followed by incubation at 28 °C, 6 h for isotopic labeling. During this period, antibiotics were continuously added to maintain the persister cell state. The bacterial cells were then collected by centrifugation and washed three times with MilliQ water to remove potential contaminants that could interfere with Raman spectroscopy. The resulting bacterial pellet was resuspended in ultrapure water to disperse the cell aggregates into single cells. A 1.5 μL bacterial suspension was spotted on CaF2 slides with a low background signal and air-dried at room temperature. The single-cell Raman spectra were obtained using a WiRE5.3 (Renishaw) confocal micro-Raman system, which included a 532 nm Nd:YAG excitation laser and a 900 grooves/mm diffraction grating. A 100× dry objective with a numerical aperture of 0.9 (Olympus, Japan) was utilized to observe bacterial cells and collect Raman signals. The laser power on the sample was approximately 30 mW/µm2. Raman spectra were recorded between 450 and 3100 cm−1, with a spectral resolution of about 2 cm−1. Each spectrum was acquired over a period of 5 s, and at least 100 valid single-cell spectra were collected per sample, adhering to quality control protocols. Quality control involved excluding spectra that displayed background signals, impurity signals, or noticeable fluorescence signals. Raman spectra were subjected to baseline correction and intensity sum normalization preprocessing. Determination of bacterial growth curves

Vibrio splendidus was inoculated into medium and cultured overnight on a shaking incubator at 28 °C. The bacterial suspension was then diluted at a 1:1000 ratio into fresh liquid medium, and 200 µL of the mixture was transferred to a 96-well plate with 5–6 replicate wells. The plate was incubated with shaking at 28 °C for 48 h in a microplate reader (Spark, Tecan). A detection program was set up with the initial time point as zero, and the absorbance at 600 nm (OD600) was automatically measured every hour. Finally, the bacterial growth curve was plotted based on the time and OD600 values.

Quantitative real-time reverse transcriptase-PCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted from bacteria using a bacterial RNA isolation kit (Omega, Norcross, USA). The concentration and purity of the total RNA were determined using a NanoDrop™ 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). cDNA synthesis was performed using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (Takara, Beijing, China). Quantitative real-time reverse transcription PCR (RT-qPCR) was subsequently conducted using the SYBR ExScript RT-PCR kit (Takara) on an ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). Each PCR reaction consisted of a 20 μL total volume, containing 8 μL of diluted cDNA, 1 μL of each primer, and 10 μL of SYBR Green Mix (Takara). The reaction conditions were as follows: an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 40 amplification cycles consisting of 15 s at 95 °C, 20 s at 60 °C, and 20 s at 72 °C. Gene expression levels were analyzed using the 2−△△Ct method. The primers used for quantitative real-time reverse transcription PCR are listed in Table 1. The primers for the 16S rDNA reference control were 933F and 16SRTR. Statistical analysis was performed using a two-tailed Student's t-test. Statistical analysis

All experiments included at least three independent biological replicates. Statistical analysis was made using R (Version 4.0) by customized scripts. The relevant figures were plotted via the ggplot 2 package. For comparisons between two groups, a two-tailed Student’s t-test was applied. Statistical significance was determined with a P-value < 0.05 with a 95% confidence interval.

Results

Persister formation and inhibitor screening

The minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of tetracycline against V. splendidus was determined to be 1.56 μg/mL. Referring to previous studies (Li et al., 2021), V. splendidus was treated with tetracycline at 50 × MIC for 4 h under stress conditions, and the surviving bacteria were identified as persister cells of V. splendidus (Fig. 1B). DMSO was used as the solvent control and exerted no significant effect on persister formation (Fig. 1C). Based on the literature concerning persister inhibition and eradication, common reagents capable of targeting persister-related pathways (quorum sensing and the stringent response) were selected for investigation. After a series of preliminary, indole, coumarin, and vitamin C were identified as potential persister inhibitors. When combined with tetracycline, all three reagents effectively reduced the number of persister in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig. 1 D1-F3). Distinct differences exist between the C-D peak (2050–2300 cm−1) and C—H peak (2800–3100 cm−1) in Raman detection. Therefore, the deuterium incorporation ratio [CDR = CD/(CD + CH)] was used as a quantitative metric to reflect the bacterial metabolic activity (Wang et al., 2022). The intensity of the C-D peak increases with the elevation of D2O concentration and incubation duration, and a linear correlation is confirmed with cellular metabolic activity. Discussion

Since the concept of persister cells was first described by Bigger (1944), research on the survival mechanisms of persisters has remained a crucial direction in microbiology. A long-standing debate has existed regarding whether persisters are in a state of metabolic dormancy. The traditional paradigm posits that dormancy is the key strategy for persisters to evade antibiotic action by avoiding engagement with active cellular targets (Lennon and Jones, 2011; Harms et al., 2016). However, studies have shown that dormancy is not essential for E. coli persisters, and some cells can maintain a certain level of metabolic activity (Orman et al. 2013). With the development of single-cell technologies, D2O-labeled SCRS provides a non-destructive, quantitative method to assess metabolic activity at the single-cell level (Liu et al., 2022; Ding et al., 2025). The principle is that metabolically active cells growing in D2O-containing medium incorporate deuterium (D) into biomacromolecules, forming C-D bonds that replace C—H bonds, thereby creating C-D peaks in the 2050–2300 cm⁻¹ region of the Raman spectrum (Berry et al., 2015). Using this technology, numerous studies have reported that the inhibition of bacterial growth by antibiotics does not necessarily imply the inhibition of cellular metabolic activity, and non-growing bacteria may still retain metabolic activity (Tao et al., 2017; Yang et al., 2019; Olaniyi et al., 2022).

In the present study, the deuterium incorporation rate (CDR) quantified by C-D bond signals was used as an indicator of metabolic activity (Wang et al., 2022). Active biosynthesis was detected in tetracycline-induced persister cells of V. splendidus during early- and mid-exponential phases (4 h and 8 h). Similarly, antibiotic concentration gradient experiments were conducted during the exponential phase. Active anabolic metabolism was detected in all surviving subpopulations selected by different antibiotic doses. These results are consistent with recent observations of metabolically active in E. coli (Wang et al., 2022) and Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Ueno et al., 2019) persisters, indicating that persisters are not completely dormant but possess active metabolic regulatory capabilities. Importantly, dose‑dependent metabolic trends are more likely driven by selection for increasingly tolerant subpopulations, rather than adaptive metabolic upregulation within the same cell population. When bacteria entered the stationary phase or were exposed to extremely high antibiotic concentrations, the metabolic activity of persisters declined. This dynamic change may reflect the adaptive strategies of persisters under different physiological and environmental stresses. In the exponential phase, when nutrients are relatively sufficient, antibiotic stress may activate defense systems such as the stringent response and oxidative stress, prompting persisters to maintain or even enhance metabolic activity to cope with the stress (Amato and Brynildsen, 2015). In contrast, when nutrient limitation is exacerbated in the stationary phase or antibiotic concentration exceeds the tolerance threshold, persisters may shift to a low metabolic state to reduce energy consumption and preserve long-term survival potential (Radzikowski et al., 2016). The above results suggest that the metabolic regulation of V. splendidus persisters exhibits a clear "threshold effect". Moderate stress can activate their metabolic defense mechanisms, while excessive stress leads to metabolic inhibition. This provides important insights for understanding the survival strategies of persister cells in different environments. In addition, a wide distribution of C-D ratios was observed among V. splendidus persisters, indicating significant metabolic heterogeneity, which may also represent one of their survival strategies (Shan et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2022).

Through a systematic analysis of the Raman spectral fingerprint region (450–1800 cm−1), we found that V. splendidus persisters exhibited significant molecular composition remodeling at the single-cell level. Compared with normal cells, the nucleic acid-related bands in persisters were generally reduced, which is consistent with their physiological state of halted growth and limited DNA replication (Keren et al., 2011). Meanwhile, signals related to lipids and polysaccharides were enhanced, suggesting that bacteria may improve antibiotic tolerance by increasing cell membrane stability and forming protective barriers. The protein-related bands exhibited a dynamic change pattern of initially increasing and then decreasing with the growth phase, whereas amide-related bands showed an inverse trend. These compositional characteristics are highly similar to those observed in E. coli persisters under ampicillin stress (Wang et al., 2022). This indicates that different bacteria may follow a conserved regulatory mechanism of cellular components when forming persisters. Additionally, the intensity of nucleic acid- and amide-related bands progressively weakened with increasing tetracycline concentration, while protein-, lipid-, and polysaccharide-related bands peaked at 60×MIC before declining. This suggests that antibiotic concentration not only regulates the formation of persisters but also profoundly affects their cellular composition and metabolic phenotypes. Collectively, these results demonstrate that persisters can balance stress adaptation and energy preservation by dynamically adjusting the synthesis and resource allocation of cellular macromolecules, thereby maintaining their survival potential under various environmental stresses (Balaban et al., 2019). Nevertheless, Raman spectral analysis is inherently constrained by overlapping biomolecular signals, and complementary approaches are thus required for subsequent precise molecular validation.

Based on the above research, we explored inhibitors of V. splendidus persisters formation, among which vitamin C showed remarkable potential. Vitamin C not only reduced the metabolic activity of persisters in a concentration-dependent manner but also significantly decreased their absolute number until complete elimination. RT-qPCR results showed that vitamin C treatment significantly downregulated the expression of (p)ppGpp synthetase-hydrolase genes relA and spoT. This is consistent with the finding by Syal and colleagues (2017) in Mycobacterium smegmatis that vitamin C can target (p)ppGpp synthesis to disrupt biofilm formation and long-term bacterial survival. As a core regulator of the stringent response, (p)ppGpp plays a pivotal role in regulating bacterial growth arrest, stress adaptation, and persister formation (Pacios et al., 2020; Urwin et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2024). Therefore, it is likely that vitamin C disrupts the key signaling networks on which V. splendidus relies to initiate and maintain the persister state by inhibiting (p)ppGpp synthesis. Accordingly, further mechanistic validation is required to verify this hypothesis, including quantification of intracellular (p)ppGpp levels and construction of corresponding gene knockout strains. Single-cell Raman spectroscopy results demonstrated that vitamin C treatment partially reversed the characteristic molecular profile changes of persisters induced by antibiotics. For example, it promoted the recovery of the decreased intensity of nucleic acid- and amide-related bands, while inhibiting the excessive elevation of protein-, lipid-, and polysaccharide-related bands induced by stress. This indicates that vitamin C not only kills persisters but may also "awaken" them from dormancy or prevent them from entering deep dormancy, thereby restoring their sensitivity to antibiotics. Nonetheless, the involvement of other mechanisms, such as oxidative stress, cannot be excluded and still requires further experimental verification.

This study represents the first application of D2O-Ramanometry to investigate V. splendidus persisters, unveiling the dynamic changes in their metabolic activity and cellular components with growth phase and antibiotic pressure. More importantly, we identified vitamin C as a natural compound that can effectively eliminate V. splendidus persisters, further expanding the application potential of SCRS in screening anti-persister compounds. It should be noted that the "persister" population induced by tetracycline in this study is actually a mixture of persister cells, antibiotic-tolerant or resistant cells, and morphologically variant cells, with persisters accounting for the main part. While this model can reflect the characteristics of surviving populations under antibiotic stress, more precise sorting and validation methods need to elucidate the molecular regulatory network underlying persister formation in V. splendidus. Future work will integrate Raman-activated cell sorting, single-cell sequencing, metabolomics, and transcriptomics to improve cell-type purity and enable multi-dimensional analyses. Transmission electron microscopy and biochemical molecular assays will also be applied to further validate our Raman spectral findings. Additionally, efforts should be made to optimize the formulations, improve the stability of vitamin C, and assess its combined use with existing antibiotics to facilitate its translational application in clinical practice and aquaculture preventive measures.

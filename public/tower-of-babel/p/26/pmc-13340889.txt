Identifying Breast Cancer Risk in Patients Undergoing Gender-Affirming Chest Masculinization Surgery
Authors: Zain Aryanpour, Jakob Durden, Christodoulos Kaoutzanis, David Mathes, Katie Egan, Sarah Tevis, Jamie Cronin
Published: 2026

Background: Breast cancer risk among transgender and gender-diverse individuals undergoing gender-affirming chest masculinization surgery (or “top surgery”) remains poorly defined. Existing literature is limited on family history, genetic predisposition, and incidental pathology in this population. The purpose of this study was to characterize breast cancer risk factors and clinical management in patients undergoing top surgery.

Methods: A retrospective chart review was conducted of all patients who underwent top surgery within an academic plastic surgery practice. Variables of interest included demographics, reproductive history, family history of breast cancer, preoperative genetic testing and imaging, International Breast Intervention Study (IBIS) breast cancer risk scores, and final surgical pathology. The primary outcome was the prevalence of elevated breast cancer risk, defined as an IBIS score ≥20% (high risk) or 15-19% (intermediate risk). Secondary outcomes included the frequency of referrals to genetics and/or breast surgical oncology, and the incidence of high-risk or malignant findings on final pathology. Descriptive statistics were calculated using frequencies and percentages for categorical variables and means, standard deviations, and ranges for continuous variables. Pearson’s correlation was used to evaluate relationships between IBIS score and continuous variables including age, BMI, and age at first menses, with statistical significance set at p<0.05.

Results: Between April 2019 and December 2024, 288 individuals underwent top surgery. The mean age was 26.6 ± 7.4 years and mean BMI was 27.4 ± 6.2 kg/m2. Patients predominantly identified as trans male (n=201, 69.8%) or non-binary (n=87, 30.2%), and most were White (n=212, 73.6%). Ninety-six patients (33.3%) reported a family history of breast cancer, including 87 (30.2%) with either first- (n=13) or second-degree (n=74) relatives. Fifteen underwent pre-operative genetic testing, with three testing positive for pathogenic variants (BRCA2, PALB2, and TP53). Thirty-five patients had preoperative mammography, with one demonstrating high-risk features.

IBIS scores were calculable for 80 patients (mean 16.1 ± 5.5, range 6.7 – 39.2). Overall, 45 patients (56.2%) had average risk scores, 19 (23.8%) had intermediate breast cancer risk scores, and 16 (20.0%) had high risk scores. There was no significant relationship between IBIS score and age (Pearson’s correlate -0.08, p=0.48), BMI (Pearson’s correlate 0.07, p=0.54), or age at first menses (Pearson’s correlate -0.19, p=0.09). Eight patients were referred to breast surgical oncology and 15 to genetics; four high-risk patients (4/16, 25.0%) underwent risk-reducing mastectomy with breast surgical oncology. Final pathology was benign in nearly all cases, with one patient diagnosed with intermediate-grade ductal carcinoma in situ.

Conclusion: One-third of patients presenting for top surgery reported a family history of breast cancer, and a subset carried high-risk genetic variants or elevated IBIS scores. Although incidental malignancy was rare, these findings underscore the importance of systematic risk assessment, selective preoperative referrals, and ongoing research to inform screening and management guidelines for transgender and gender diverse patients pursuing chest masculinization surgery.

Long non-coding RNA LINC-PINT: emerging clinical significance and molecular mechanisms in knee osteoarthritis
Authors: Shen Zhou, Yushuai Zhang, Shouhai Jiang, Chuankai Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe underlying pathophysiological mechanisms of knee osteoarthritis (KOA) remain incompletely understood. This study aimed to investigate the clinical significance of long non-coding RNA LINC-PINT in KOA and elucidate its molecular mechanism in regulating chondrocyte function via the miR-324-3p/GPX4 axis.Methods100 KOA patient samples and 40 normal knee cartilage tissue samples were collected, and the relevant gene expression was quantified using RT-qPCR. C28/I2 was cultured in vitro to establish an IL-1β-induced KOA cellular model. Dual-luciferase reporter and RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays were performed to validate the targeted molecular regulatory interactions. Cell proliferation and apoptosis, as well as inflammatory factors levels, were assessed using cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8), flow cytometry, and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), respectively. Oxidative stress-related indicators and Fe2+ concentration were evaluated using commercial assay kits. Western blotting was performed to detect protein expression.ResultsIn KOA tissues, LINC-PINT and glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) were significantly downregulated, whereas miR-324-3p was markedly upregulated. LINC-PINT demonstrated the potential diagnostic discrimination ability and was identified as an independent protective factor in KOA. Overexpression of LINC-PINT effectively counteracted interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β)-induced suppression of chondrocyte proliferation, reduced apoptosis, and attenuated the secretion of tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), while significantly alleviating cartilage matrix degradation, as indicated by increased expression of collagen II and aggrecan and decreased matrix metallopeptidase 13 (MMP13) levels. Moreover, LINC-PINT directly bound to miR-324-3p, which in turn directly targeted GPX4. Functional rescue assays further confirmed that transfection with a miR-324-3p mimic reversed the protective effects of LINC-PINT overexpression on chondrocytes, whereas GPX4 overexpression mitigated the detrimental effects induced by the miR-324-3p mimic. IL-1β disrupted the cellular redox balance by increasing the levels of Fe2+, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and (malondialdehyde) MDA and by inhibiting superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity and the GSH/GSSG ratio. Overexpression of LINC-PINT can counteract these effects, and the protective effect was enhanced when combined with Per-1. Transfection with miR-mimic reversed the protective effect of LINC-PINT, suggesting that LINC-PINT may act as a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) to capture this pro-oxidative miR-324-3p. Finally, overexpression of GPX4 could protect cells from miRNA-induced damage, highlighting its crucial role in alleviating iron-dependent oxidative stress and iron-overloaded apoptosis.ConclusionLINC-PINT demonstrated the potential diagnostic discrimination ability and exerted a protective effect in KOA by modulating the miR-324-3p/GPX4 axis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13018-026-06822-3.Keywords: LINC-PINT, miR-324-3p, GPX4, KOA

Background

Knee osteoarthritis (KOA) is a highly prevalent chronic degenerative joint disorder predominantly affecting middle-aged and elderly individuals [1, 2]. Its pathogenesis is multifactorial, involving a complex interplay of mechanical stress, inflammatory processes, metabolic dysregulation, and genetic predisposition [3, 4]. Early manifestations typically include joint pain, swelling, and stiffness; as the disease advances, patients progressively experience reduced joint mobility, structural deformities, and impaired ambulation [5], leading to significant deterioration in joint function and overall quality of life [6]. Current therapeutic strategies are largely palliative, focusing on symptom management, with total joint replacement reserved for end-stage disease [7]. However, a comprehensive understanding of the underlying pathophysiological mechanisms remains elusive. Thus, identifying specific and reliable biomarkers to accurately monitor disease progression holds substantial clinical value in improving patient outcomes.

Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), with miRNAs [8], long non-coding RNAs [10–12], small nuclear RNAs (snRNAs)[9–11], and circRNAs [12] as the core, participate in the pathological process of musculoskeletal system diseases through mechanisms such as competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks, signaling pathways regulation, and epigenetic modifications. For example, lncRNAs can improve fracture healing by regulating the viability and apoptosis of osteoblasts [13]; lncRNA HCG18 influences the progression of spinal tuberculosis by modulating the hsa-miR-146a-5p/TGF-β1/SMADs pathway [14]; miR-217 is involved in the development and progression of postmenopausal osteoporosis by regulating the OPG/RANKL/RANK pathway [15]; miR-204-5p holds therapeutic potential in intervertebral disc degeneration by targeting the SSRP1/NF-κB pathway to inhibit cell apoptosis [16]; and the dysregulation of the miR-106a-5p/PTEN axis is closely associated with the progression and diagnosis of postmenopausal osteoporosis [17]. Moreover, lncRNAs play crucial regulatory roles in cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and the onset and progression of various diseases, including osteoarthritis (OA) [18–20]. LncRNA LINC-PINT, initially identified as a p53-induced lncRNA [21], has demonstrated tumor-suppressive functions in multiple malignancies [22–24]. LINC-PINT expression is downregulated in delayed fracture healing patients, and it promotes osteoblast differentiation [25]. Moreover, LINC-PINT inhibits tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α)-induced pathological processes in rheumatoid arthritis [26]. However, its molecular mechanism in KOA remains poorly understood. A meta-analysis indicates that miRNAs serve as non-invasive biomarkers with high diagnostic accuracy [27, 28]. Previous studies have shown that miR-324-3p is significantly upregulated in the cartilage tissue of OA patients [29] and contributes to tumor progression, including in gastric cancer, through activation of signaling pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin [30]. Bioinformatics analyses suggest that LINC-PINT targets miR-324-3p. Glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) is a key negative regulator of ferroptosis [31]—a recently identified form of iron-dependent regulated cell death that is closely associated with cartilage degeneration in OA [32]. Recent evidence indicates that P21 protects OA chondrocytes from ferroptosis by stabilizing GPX4 protein [33], whereas METTL14 promotes GPX4 degradation via m6A modification, thereby exacerbating ferroptosis [34]. Bioinformatics predictions further indicate that GPX4 may be a direct target of miR-324-3p. Based on these findings, we hypothesize that LINC-PINT may function as a ceRNA by sequestering miR-324-3p, thereby alleviating its repression of GPX4, ultimately leading to increased GPX4 expression and exerting a protective effect.

Through integrated analysis of clinical samples and molecular mechanism studies, this study investigated the regulatory role of the LINC-PINT/miR-324-3p/GPX4 axis in interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) -induced chondrocyte inflammation, apoptosis, and extracellular matrix metabolic imbalance, which may offer a novel insight into the molecular mechanisms underlying KOA pathogenesis.

Methods

Sample collection

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Xuzhou Renci Hospital and in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants before their involvement in the study.

Knee cartilage specimens were collected from 100 osteoarthritis patients undergoing total knee arthroplasty at Xuzhou Renci Hospital, all of whom had been diagnosed with KOA based on clinical symptoms and imaging findings. As controls, 40 normal knee cartilage samples were obtained from individuals who underwent traumatic amputation surgery, with no history of KOA or other joint disorders.

Cell culture and transfection

Human chondrocyte C28/I2 cells (ATCC) were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco) under a humidified atmosphere at 37 °C with 5% CO2.

The LINC-PINT overexpression plasmid (OE-LINC-PINT), GPX4 overexpression plasmid (OE-GPX4), and their corresponding negative control empty plasmids (OE-NC), along with miR-324-3p mimic, inhibitor, and respective negative controls, were all constructed by GenePharma (Shanghai, China) and transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). At 48 h after transfection, the expression of LINC-PINT, miR-324-3p, and GPX4 was detected by RT-qPCR to verify the overexpression efficiency.

To simulate the inflammatory environment of KOA in vitro, C28/I2 cells were exposed to varying concentrations of IL-1β (0, 5, 10, and 15 ng/mL) for 24 h, or stimulated with 10 ng/mL IL-1β for different time intervals (0, 6, 12, 18, 24 and 30 h) to identify optimal conditions for model establishment. Based on these preliminary results, subsequent experiments consistently used treatment with 10 ng/mL IL-1β for 24 h to establish the in vitro KOA model.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent and reverse transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using a reverse transcription kit (Qiagen). RT-qPCR was performed on an ABI 7500 Real-Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems) with SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (Takara), and amplification was carried out using cDNA as the template. U6 snRNA or GAPDH was used as the internal reference. The results were calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT method.

Proliferation

Cell proliferation was assessed using the cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. Transfected cells were seeded into 96-well plates and cultured for 0, 24, 48, or 72 h. Following incubation, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added. The absorbance at 450 nm was subsequently measured.

Apoptosis

After collection, cells from each group were washed, resuspended, and stained with 5 μL of Annexin V-FITC and 5 μL of propidium iodide (PI) staining solution, followed by incubation for 15 min. Subsequently, samples were analyzed using a FACSCalibur flow cytometer (BD Biosciences).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Cell culture supernatants from each group were collected and centrifuged, and the concentrations of TNF-α and interleukin-6 (IL-6) in the supernatants were quantified using Thermo Fisher Scientific human TNF-α and IL-6 ELISA kits.

The determination of intracellular iron content

The determination of intracellular iron content utilized the Iron Analysis Kit (Abcam) and was carried out in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. Ferrostatin-1 (a ferroptosis inhibitor) was used to verify whether it has a synergistic protective effect with -LINC-PINT.

Measurement of oxidative stress indicators

The level of lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS) was determined using C11-BODIPY 581/591 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) detection kit (Sigma) was used to measure the lactate released by the cells according to the manufacturer's instructions. The ratio of reduced glutathione (GSH) to oxidized glutathione (GSSG) was analyzed using the GSH/GSSG Ratio Assay Kit (Abcam, UK). The level of MDA was evaluated using the MDA Assay Kit (Jiancheng Biotechnology Research Institute), while the activity of superoxide dismutase (SOD) was determined by using the SOD Assay Kit (Jiancheng Biotechnology Research Institute) in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

Western blots

Cells were harvested and lysed on ice with 1 × RIPA buffer supplemented with a protease inhibitor cocktail (PIC). Equal amounts of protein were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and subsequently transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. The membranes were blocked with TBST buffer (20 mM Tris–HCl, pH 7.6, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% Tween-20) containing 5% non-fat dry milk for 1 h at room temperature (RT), followed by incubation with the appropriate primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. After washing, the membranes were incubated with the corresponding horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc., California, USA), which was diluted in TBST buffer containing 5% non-fat dry milk, for 1 h at RT. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection system according to the manufacturer's instructions.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) experiment

The experiment was conducted using the Magna RIP RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore). Cell lysates were incubated overnight at 4 °C with magnetic beads conjugated to anti-Ago2 antibody or IgG (used as a negative control) to ensure complete capture of target RNA–protein complexes. Subsequently, RNA was extracted from the immunoprecipitated samples, and the relative enrichment of the target gene mRNA in the Ago2 immunoprecipitate was quantified by RT-qPCR.

RNA pull-down assay

To further verify the direct interaction between miR-324-3p and GPX4 mRNA, an RNA pull-down assay was performed. Biotin-labeled miR-324-3p (Bio-miR-324-3p) and its negative control (Bio-NC) were synthesized by GenePharma (China) and transfected into C28/I2 cells. Cells were harvested and lysed 48 h after transfection. The cell lysates were incubated with streptavidin-coated magnetic beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific) pre-incubated overnight at 4 °C to pull down the biotin-labeled RNA–protein complexes. After washing, the bound RNA was extracted and purified using TRIzol reagent. The enrichment of LINC-PINT and GPX4 was detected by RT-qPCR, and the fold enrichment was calculated relative to the Bio-NC group.

Dual luciferase assay

Fragments of the LINC-PINT or GPX4 3′ untranslated region (3'UTR) containing either wild type (WT) or mutant (MUT) miRNA binding sites were synthesized and cloned into the pmirGLO (Promega) dual-luciferase reporter vector. The resulting reporter plasmids were co-transfected into C28/I2 cells together with a miR-324-3p mimic/inhibitor or a negative control (NC). Dual-luciferase activity was measured 48 h post-transfection to assess regulatory effects.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 22.0 (IBM Corporation). For comparisons between the two groups, an independent sample t-test and chi-square test were used. For comparisons among multiple groups (such as different concentrations of IL-1β treatment, different time points, different transfection conditions, etc.), one-way analysis of variance (One-way ANOVA) was employed. If the results of the variance analysis were significant (P < 0.05), then the Tukey multiple comparison test was used for pairwise comparisons between groups. The diagnostic efficacy was evaluated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. A logistic regression was employed to identify independent predictors of KOA. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to examine the associations between gene expression. Statistical significance was defined as a P < 0.05.

Results

LINC-PINT was closely associated with KOA

A comparison of the basic clinical characteristics between 100 KOA patients and 40 controls revealed no statistically significant differences in age, sex distribution, smoking status, or body mass index (BMI) between the two groups (Table 1). However, LINC-PINT was significantly lower in KOA tissues (Fig. 1A), and it demonstrated the potential diagnostic discrimination ability, with KOA (area under the curve (AUC) = 0.9136, sensitivity 87.00%, and specificity 87.50%) (Fig. 1B). Moreover, LINC-PINT was significantly associated with KOA, and it was an independent predictor of KOA (Fig. 1C, Table 2).

The functional role of LINC-PINT

IL-1β can induce an increase in inflammatory factor levels in a concentration-dependent and time-dependent manner: as shown in Supplementary Fig. 1 A and C, the induction of inflammatory factors by IL-1β was concentration-dependent, with the 10 ng/mL group showing the most significant increase in inflammatory factors; while Supplementary Fig. 1 B and D results indicated that when treated with 10 ng/mL IL-1β, the level of inflammatory factors gradually increased over time, and by 24 h, a significant increase trend has been observed. Based on the above results, the subsequent experiments can choose "treatment with 10 ng/mL IL-1β for 24 h" as the experimental condition for inducing the inflammatory response in cells. IL-1β significantly inhibited LINC-PINT expression in a concentration- and time-dependent manner. As the IL-1β concentration increased, LINC-PINT expression progressively decreased, with 10 ng/mL identified as the optimal inhibitory concentration. With prolonged exposure, LINC-PINT expression continued to decline, reaching maximal suppression at 24 h (Figs. 2A–B). Transfection efficiency was verified by RT‑qPCR, as shown in Supplementary Fig. 2A: Compared with the control group and the OE-NC group, the relative expression level of LINC-PINT in the OE-LINC-PINT group was significantly increased, suggesting that the overexpression plasmid of LINC-PINT was successfully transfected and could effectively upregulate the expression of LINC-PINT in the cells. The relative expression level of LINC-PINT in the OE-LINC-PINT group was significantly increased compared to the control group (control group, OE-NC group, relative expression levels were all close to 1.0), to approximately 2.0, with an expression increase of approximately 2 times. This transfection group can be used for subsequent functional experiments. Transfection with OE-LINC-PINT markedly elevated LINC-PINT levels (Fig. 2C). LINC-PINT overexpression effectively counteracted multiple biological effects of IL-1β: it partially reversed IL-1β-induced inhibition of cell proliferation (Fig. 2D), significantly reduced IL-1β-triggered apoptosis (Fig. 2E), and downregulated IL-1β-stimulated TNF-α and IL-6 expression (Fig. 2F), thereby attenuating the inflammatory response. Given that matrix metallopeptidase 13 (MMP13) promotes cartilage matrix degradation while collagen II and aggrecan constitute major structural components of the cartilage extracellular matrix, LINC-PINT overexpression ameliorated the detrimental effects of IL-1β on cartilage-related gene expression. Specifically, it upregulated collagen II and aggrecan mRNA and protein expression and suppressed MMP13 mRNA and protein expression (Fig. 2G-H), indicating a protective role in cartilage homeostasis. Compared with the control group, IL-1β treatment significantly induced the accumulation of intracellular Fe2+, ROS, and MDA (Fig. 3A-C), and led to a significant decrease in SOD activity and the GSH/GSSG ratio (Fig. 3D–E). Overexpression of LINC-PINT effectively counteracted the effect of IL-1β, manifested by a significant reduction in Fe2+, ROS, and MDA levels, as well as a significant increase in SOD activity and the GSH/GSSG ratio. On the basis of IL-1β treatment, adding Per-1 could lower Fe2+ and ROS and MDA levels, and increase SOD activity and the GSH/GSSG ratio. While overexpressing LINC simultaneously during Per-1 treatment, Fe2+, ROS, and MDA levels further decreased, while SOD activity and the GSH/GSSG ratio increased again.

LINC-PINT directly interacted with miR-324-3p

There was a direct interaction between LINC-PINT and miR-324-3p (Fig. 3F). RIP results showed that both LINC-PINT and miR-324-3p were significantly enriched in Ago2 immunoprecipitates (Fig. 3G). The results of the RNA pull-down assay further confirmed that LINC-PINT was significantly enriched in the Bio-miR-324-3p group compared to the Bio-NC group, indicating a direct binding interaction between LINC-PINT and miR-324-3p (Supplementary Fig. 3A). The relative expression level of miR-324-3p in the miR-324-3p mimic group was significantly increased compared to the control group (relative expression level close to 1.0) to approximately 1.8, with an expression increase of approximately 1.8 times; The relative expression level of miR-324-3p in the miR-324-3p inhibitor group was significantly decreased compared to the control group to approximately 0.3, with an inhibition efficiency of approximately 70%. The expression regulation effects of all constructs after transfection were statistically significant and met the requirements of subsequent experiments (Supplementary Fig. 2B). A dual-luciferase assay further confirmed that co-transfection with the miR-324-3p mimic markedly suppressed the luciferase activity of the reporter plasmid containing the WT-LINC-PINT binding site, but not that of the plasmid with the MUT binding site (Fig. 3H). These findings collectively demonstrate a direct and specific interaction between LINC-PINT and miR-324-3p. miR-324-3p expression was significantly elevated in KOA tissues (Fig. 3I), while LINC-PINT expression was inversely correlated with miR-324-3p levels (Fig. 3J). Cellular experiments revealed that IL-1β treatment upregulated miR-324-3p expression, whereas LINC-PINT overexpression effectively reduced it. Notably, co-transfection with the miR-324-3p mimic significantly reversed the suppressive effect of LINC-PINT overexpression on miR-324-3p activity (Fig. 3K).

IL-1β may suppress the protective effect of LINC-PINT through upregulation of miR-324-3p expression

IL-1β significantly upregulated miR-324-3p expression in a concentration- and time-dependent manner (Figs. 4A–B). The relative expression level of GPX4 in the OE-GPX4 group was significantly higher than that in the control group (with relative expression levels close to 1.0), reaching approximately 2.0, representing a nearly twofold increase. The expression regulation effects after transfection of the constructs were all statistically significant, meeting the requirements for subsequent experiments (Supplementary Fig. 2C). Functional rescue experiments demonstrated that, under IL-1β stimulation, transfection with the miR-324-3p mimic effectively reversed the promotive effect of LINC-PINT overexpression on cell proliferation (Fig. 4C), attenuated its inhibitory effect on cell apoptosis (Fig. 4D), alleviated its suppression of TNF-α and IL-6 secretion (Fig. 4E), and counteracted its regulatory effects on the expression of cartilage matrix metabolism-related genes, including collagen II, aggrecan, and MMP13 (Fig. 4F-G). These findings indicated that miR-324-3p acted as a key downstream effector of LINC-PINT-mediated biological functions, and its overexpression partially counteracted the protective effects of LINC-PINT on chondrocytes. The IL-1β + OE-LINC treatment significantly reduced the intracellular relative Fe2+ level, ROS activity, and MDA level (Fig. 5A-C), while significantly increasing SOD activity and the GSH/GSSG ratio (Fig. 5D-E). However, after further transfection with miR-mimic based on IL-1β + OE-LINC treatment, the intracellular Fe2+ level, ROS activity, and MDA level were significantly higher than those in the former (IL-1β + OE-LINC group), while the SOD activity and GSH/GSSG ratio were significantly lower.

miR-324-3p directly interacted with GPX4

Through sequence complementarity prediction, RIP assays, and dual-luciferase assays, we systematically demonstrated that miR-324-3p directly targeted GPX4. Specifically, RIP results showed that both miR-324-3p and GPX4 mRNA were significantly enriched in Ago2 immunoprecipitates (Fig. 6A–B). To further confirm the direct binding between miR-324-3p and GPX4 mRNA, an RNA pull-down assay was conducted. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 3B, GPX4 mRNA was significantly enriched in the Bio-miR-324-3p group compared with the Bio-NC group, indicating a specific interaction between miR-324-3p and GPX4 (Supplementary Fig. 3B). Furthermore, dual-luciferase assays confirmed that miR-324-3p significantly suppressed luciferase activity by binding to a specific site within the 3'UTR of WT-GPX4 (Fig. 6C). GPX4 expression was significantly lower in KOA tissue samples (Fig. 6D) and exhibited a significant negative correlation with miR-324-3p levels (Fig. 6E). In the cellular model, IL-1β treatment markedly suppressed GPX4 expression, whereas overexpression of LINC-PINT effectively upregulated GPX4. Co-transfection with miR-324-3p mimic significantly reversed the LINC-PINT-induced upregulation of GPX4 mRNA and protein expression. However, GPX4 overexpression partially restored its mRNA and protein expression levels (Fig. 6F,G).

GPX4 antagonized the role of miR-324-3p in the pathogenesis of KOA

IL-1β suppressed GPX4 mRNA and protein expression in a concentration- and time-dependent manner (Figs. 7A-D). Functional rescue experiments revealed that GPX4 overexpression partially reversed the detrimental effects of miR-324-3p mimics on chondrocyte function, as evidenced by enhanced cell proliferation (Fig. 7E), suppressed apoptosis (Fig. 7F), reduced inflammatory factor secretion (Fig. 7G), and improved cartilage matrix metabolism, characterized by increased collagen II and aggrecan expression and decreased MMP13 mRNA and protein levels (Fig. 7H-I). Collectively, these findings strongly supported the existence of a molecular regulatory axis through which LINC-PINT exerted its protective role: by acting as a sponge for miR-324-3p, LINC-PINT alleviated miR-324-3p-mediated repression of GPX4, thereby preserving chondrocyte function. This pathway was designated as the LINC-PINT/miR-324-3p/GPX4 axis. In the IL-1β + OE-LINC treatment system, miR-mimic significantly increased the levels of Fe2+, ROS, and MDA (Fig. 8A–C) in the cells, while reducing the activity of SOD and the GSH/GSSG ratio. Overexpression of GPX4 could reverse the effects of miR-mimic, causing the above oxidative stress indicators to decrease and the antioxidant indicators to increase (Fig. 8D, E). The results indicated that miR-324-3p-mimic aggravates cellular iron accumulation and oxidative damage, while GPX4 can improve its redox state.

Discussion

KOA is characterized by progressive cartilage matrix degradation, a hallmark pathological feature in the early stages of the disease [35, 36]. LncRNAs play critical regulatory roles in the pathogenesis of KOA [37]. Although LINC-PINT has been reported to be downregulated in several pathological conditions [38, 39]. Its functional significance in KOA remains poorly understood. This study demonstrates that LINC-PINT acted as a ceRNA in the context of KOA. Specifically, LINC-PINT sequestered miR-324-3p, thereby alleviating the miRNA-mediated repression of its target gene, GPX4. This derepression leads to increased GPX4 expression, contributing to a protective effect on chondrocytes.

Previous studies have shown that LINC-PINT exerts a tumor-suppressive role in various cancers [40, 41]. Our study found that LINC-PINT expression was significantly downregulated in KOA cartilage tissue and exhibited a potential diagnostic discrimination ability. This finding was consistent with a previous report demonstrating its reduced expression in delayed fracture healing [25]. Moreover, in rheumatoid arthritis, LINC-PINT exerts anti-inflammatory effects [26]. Collectively, LINC-PINT may play a broad protective role in bone and joint-related diseases. Functional experiments demonstrated that overexpression of LINC-PINT effectively alleviated IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury, as evidenced by enhanced cell proliferation, suppressed apoptosis, decreased secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6), and attenuation of cartilage matrix degradation (upregulation of collagen II and aggrecan, and downregulation of MMP13). These findings strongly confirmed the chondroprotective function of LINC-PINT at both cellular and molecular levels. The ceRNA mechanism represents a key pathway through which lncRNAs mediate their biological functions [42, 43]. To further elucidate the underlying mechanism, we investigated the downstream regulatory axis of LINC-PINT. LINC-PINT directly targeted miR-324-3p. Previous studies have reported that miR-324-3p is upregulated in OA [29] and promotes pathological progression in multiple diseases [44, 45]. In this study, we verified that LINC-PINT acted as a molecular sponge to sequester and inhibit miR-324-3p activity. miR-324-3p was upregulated in KOA tissues. Functional rescue experiments further demonstrated that overexpression of miR-324-3p partially reversed the protective effects of LINC-PINT on chondrocytes, clearly indicating that miR-324-3p served as a key downstream mediator of LINC-PINT's biological functions.

Furthermore, miR-324-3p targeted GPX4 in KOA. GPX4 is a key regulator of ferroptosis [46, 47], a recently recognized form of programmed cell death that plays a critical role in chondrocyte degeneration in OA [48, 49]. P21 can inhibit ferroptosis in OA chondrocytes by stabilizing the GPX4 protein [33], whereas METTL14 promotes GPX4-mediated ferroptosis through m6A modification in chondrocytes [34]. The present study further established GPX4 as a central gene in KOA. Functional rescue experiments revealed that GPX4 overexpression partially reversed the detrimental effects induced by the miR-324-3p mimic and restored the protective role of LINC-PINT in chondrocytes. In conclusion, our findings suggested that IL-1β may downregulate LINC-PINT expression, resulting in elevated levels of miR-324-3p, which subsequently suppresses GPX4 expression, thereby exacerbating inflammatory responses and cartilage damage.

Previous studies have shown that kaempferol exerts protective effects against iron apoptosis induced by OGD/r by activating the Nrf2/SLC7A11/GPX4 signaling pathway [50]. It has been confirmed that GPX4-regulated ferroptosis is involved in articular cartilage degeneration. Specifically, P21 resists ferroptosis in osteoarthritic chondrocytes by regulating GPX4 protein stability [33], whereas METTL14 depletion inhibits ferroptosis and ECM degradation by suppressing GPX4 mRNA m6A modification in injured chondrocytes [34]. In contrast, this study revealed a new upstream regulatory level based on RNA interactions: LINC-PINT acted as a ceRNA, by adsorbing miR-324-3p to relieve its translational inhibition on GPX4 mRNA, thereby upregulating GPX4 expression at the post-transcriptional level. This not only links LINC-PINT to the ferroptosis mechanism in KOA for the first time, but also expands the regulatory network of GPX4 in OA, suggesting that targeting the interaction between LINC-PINT and miR-324-3p may become a new intervention strategy. Based on the experimental findings, we proposed the following mechanism: In the pathological context of KOA, elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β suppress LINC-PINT expression. This downregulation of LINC-PINT diminished its capacity to act as a ceRNA by sequestering miR-324-3p, leading to increased free miR-324-3p levels. The excess miR-324-3p subsequently bound to the 3'UTR of GPX4, repressing its translation. Reduced GPX4 expression likely compromises cellular defense against ferroptosis, thereby exacerbating chondrocyte inflammation, apoptosis, and extracellular matrix degradation—key processes that drive disease progression. Our study elucidated a regulatory network centered on LINC-PINT, miR-324-3p, and GPX4 in IL-1β-induced iron accumulation and oxidative stress. IL-1β disrupted cellular redox balance by elevating Fe2+, ROS, and MDA levels while suppressing SOD activity and GSH/GSSG ratio. Overexpression of LINC-PINT counteracts these effects, with enhanced protection observed when combined with Per-1. Transfection of miR-mimic reverses LINC-PINT's protective role, indicating LINC-PINT likely functions as a ceRNA to sequester this pro-oxidative miRNA. Finally, GPX4 overexpression rescues cells from miRNA-induced damage, highlighting its critical role in mitigating iron-dependent oxidative stress and ferroptosis. Collectively, our findings establish a pathway where LINC-PINT inhibited a miRNA that suppressed GPX4, thereby regulating iron homeostasis and oxidative stress in IL-1β-treated cells. Conversely, restoration of LINC-PINT expression may interrupt this pathogenic cascade, exerting a protective therapeutic effect. However, in vivo validation using KOA animal models remains to be performed, which limits the generalizability of our findings to physiological conditions.

Several limitations of this study need to be clarified. First, the mechanistic investigations were restricted to chondrocytes, and the role of LINC-PINT in the complete pathological environment of KOA, such as synovial inflammation and subchondral bone remodeling, was not explored; although the LINC-PINT/miR-324-3p/GPX4 axis was proposed, the upstream regulators underlying LINC-PINT downregulation in KOA remain unclear, and the diagnostic potential of LINC-PINT was based on a single-center exploratory analysis lacking independent external cohort validation. Second, all mechanistic conclusions were derived from in vitro cell models without in vivo verification, such as the absence of DMM-induced KOA mouse models and intra-articular or tail-vein injection of LINC-PINT overexpression vectors to validate its regulatory effects on cartilage injury, inflammation, and ferroptosis in vivo; moreover, OARSI scoring and immunohistochemical detection of GPX4 and ferroptosis markers (PTGS2, ACSL4) were not performed, making it difficult to extrapolate the conclusions directly to the complex human pathological microenvironment. In addition, insufficient mechanistic validation was present, including an asymmetric experimental design that relied mainly on overexpression assays to demonstrate the sufficiency of LINC-PINT function but lacked knockdown experiments to verify the necessity of its downregulation in the injury phenotype; no systematic quantitative correlation analysis was conducted between LINC-PINT expression and the severity of KOA pathology (histological cartilage score) or disease stage (Kellgren-Lawrence grading), and the diagnostic value was supported only by differential expression and ROC curve analysis without direct correlation data linking to core pathological phenotypes such as chondrocyte loss and matrix degradation, thus limiting its evaluation as a disease progression marker. Furthermore, although KOA specimens were selected according to strict clinical and imaging criteria, the collected cartilage tissues lacked conventional histological staining (HE, safranin O-fast green), providing no direct morphological evidence for cartilage degeneration. Finally, the core mechanistic conclusions were entirely based on IL-1β-induced in vitro chondrocyte models that cannot fully recapitulate the complex in vivo pathology of KOA, and the authenticity, intensity, and crosstalk of the proposed axis with other pathological processes (synovial inflammation, osteophyte formation) remain unclear; no conclusive evidence confirmed GPX4 as the sole or core target gene of miR-324-3p in mediating the protective effects of LINC-PINT, and the possibility that miR-324-3p simultaneously regulates other genes involved in cartilage homeostasis, inflammation, or cell death cannot be excluded. Future studies will employ animal models, rescue experiments, systematic clinicopathological correlation analyses, and independent cohort validations to more comprehensively evaluate the in vivo function, clinical relevance, and translational potential of this molecular axis.

Conclusion

LINC-PINT may serve as a potential diagnostic biomarker for KOA. However, this preliminary conclusion requires validation in larger, independent external cohorts before clinical translation. LINC-PINT exerted a protective effect in KOA by modulating the miR-324-3p/GPX4 axis.

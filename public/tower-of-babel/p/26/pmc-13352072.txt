Bone marrow predominant presentation of EGFR mutated NSCLC: Report of two cases
Authors: Sayed MS Hashemi, Denise WM de Jong, Josephine Mathilde Iris Vos, Emile Comans, Idris Bahce
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) mutations are present in a minority of cases of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), most commonly among never-smokers. In metastatic EGFR-positive NSCLC the most frequently involved sites are the bone and brain, a miliary metastatic subset also exists. However, cases with predominant bone marrow involvement have not been previously described. Here, we present two cases characterized by bone marrow predominance as an unusual initial imaging finding, with the diagnosis confirmed by bone marrow biopsy. Treatment was successful, comparable to usual EGFR-positive cases.Keywords: NSCLC, EGFR, Bone marrow

Introduction

EGFR mutations are found in a small subset of NSCLC, occurring in approximately 10%-15% in the Western population [1]. Approximately 60%-70% of EGFR-mutated NSCLC patients are diagnosed when the disease is already metastatic, which is comparable to the overall NSCLC population. Tumors harboring common EGFR mutations (ie, exon 19 deletions and exon 21 L858R mutations) show high response rates to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) and are generally associated with a favorable prognosis. Treatment with a third-generation EGFR-TKI osimertinib, for example, has been reported with a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 18.9 months and a median overall survival (OS) of 38.6 months [2].

Most studies and reviews, including the systematic reviews of clinical trials, report that bone metastases are quite common in EGFR-mutant NSCLC, with an incidence at diagnosis of around 42% [3]. The other organ frequently involved in metastatic EGFR-mutated patients is brain [4]. EGFR-mutated NSCLC could also be associated with a typical pattern of miliary metastases, as diffuse, countless small nodules, particularly in the lung and brain [5]. However, these studies do not specifically describe any cases where the disease is confined almost exclusively to the bone marrow. Here we present two cases of EGFR-mutated NSCLC with predominant bone marrow involvement.

Case presentations

Case 1

A 70-year-old female, never-smoker, with a history of Waldenström’s macroglobulinemia (WM), harboring MYD88 (p.L265P) and CXCR4 (p.S338Ffs*6) mutations, and previously treated with dexamethason rituximab, cyclofosfamide and on relapsed disease with rituximab and bendamustine about 10 years prior, to the current presentation was evaluated by her hematologist for several weeks of fatigue and bloating. She has noted furthermore slightly higher body temperature and about 1 kg weight loss. Physical examination was unremarkable.

Laboratory tests revealed slightly elevated but stable IgM level, a newly elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) (667 U/L, rising to 1122 U/L). A Fluorodeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography (FDG-PET) scan was ordered to rule out transformation to malignant lymphoma or other malignancy. Bone marrow biopsy was also obtained and subsequently Prednisolone and Rasburicase were started.

The FDG-PET scan demonstrated diffuse, marked bone marrow uptake of FDG, multiple lytic lesions, and mediastinal, left sided hilar, and supraclavicular lymphadenopathy (Fig. 1).

(B) PET-CT MIP showing intense FDG in the small centrally located primary lung tumor in the left upper lobe. Intense FDG uptake in a small left hilar lymph node and inhomogeneous intense uptake in the entire axial skeleton. (C) Transaxial CT image of the centrally located small primary lung tumor in the left upper lobe. Abbreviations: FDG-PET = Fluorodeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography; MIP = Maximum Intensity Projection.

Bone marrow pathology revealed an epithelial malignancy positive for TTF1. Further molecular analysis by Next Generation Sequencing (NGS) identified EGFR exon 19 deletion (c.2235_2249del, p.E746_A750del) in combination with a nonsense mutation in TP53 exon 9 (c.949C>T, p.Q317*).

Careful review of the chest CT and PET imaging showed a possible primary tumor in the left upper lobe (LUL) (Fig. 1).

Treatment with osimertinib was initiated, resulting in a complete metabolic response of FDG-PET scan (Fig. 2). The LUL lesion was evolved into a small, vague ground-glass opacity. Osimertinib was well tolerated. At the latest follow-up, approximately 13 months after starting osimertinib, the patient remained still in complete remission with no signs of disease progression.

(B) PET-CT MIP of patient 1 showing complete metabolic response of the primary tumor in the left upper lobe, of the hilar lymph node, and of the bone metastases. Abbreviations: FDG-PET = Fluorodeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography; MIP = Maximum Intensity Projection.

Case 2

A 55-year-old Asian woman, who had never smoked, presented with slightly enlarged right-sided cervical lymph nodes. Aside from fatigue, she had no other symptoms at presentation and was still able to exercise 4 times a week.

A thoracic CT scan revealed a subpleural mass in the left upper lobe. An FDG-PET scan showed intense FDG uptake in the pulmonary mass, moderately increased uptake in several cervical lymph nodes, and diffuse, inhomogeneous, intense FDG uptake with sclerosis throughout the bones of the entire axial skeleton (Fig. 3).

(A) Fused sagittal FDG PET-CT image of patient 2 showing the extensive bone involvement of the vertebral spine. (B) PET-CT MIP of patient 2 showing intense FDG uptake in the primary lung tumor left upper lobe. Intense FDG uptake in the bone metastases in the entire axial skeleton and in some small cervical lymph nodes. (C) Transaxial CT image showing the small primary lung tumor in the left upper lobe. Abbreviations: FDG-PET: Fluorodeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography; MIP: Maximum Intensity Projection.

Lymph node biopsies confirmed adenocarcinoma with strong TTF1 positivity, negative P40, and PD-L1 negativity (TPS < 1%). The tumor harbored an EGFR exon 19 mutation (NM_005228.5:c.2236_2250del, p.(Glu746_Ala750del), VAF 28%), and a TP53 co-mutation (NM_000546.6:c.455C>G, p.(Pro152Arg), VAF = 21%).

Her fatigue worsened significantly during the first week following the initial biopsy. She developed anemia (hemoglobin 5.8 mmol/L) and thrombocytopenia (28 × 10^9/L). A bone marrow biopsy also revealed metastatic adenocarcinoma. Osimertinib was initiated at the standard dose, which resulted in rapid clinical improvement, with performance status improving from 2 at baseline to 0. The patient was able to resume jogging at her previous level. A CT scan of the chest showed reduction in the size of the left lung tumor after about 5 weeks of osimertinib treatment. Platelet counts normalized after 9 weeks of treatment. No significant toxicity related to osimertinib was observed.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, there are limited data on metastasized EGFR-mutated NSCLC presenting with predominantly bone marrow involvement as seen on FDG-PET imaging and confirmed by pathologic examination, and our cases appear to be among the first reported. Although bone involvement is a common feature in metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC, predominant bone marrow involvement, as observed in these cases, has, to our knowledge, not or only rarely been described. Of note, treatment outcomes for the first patient were comparable to the EGFR-mutated NSCLC cases with more typical presentations, with a complete response following initiation of osimertinib. Although there is a known association between WM and increased risk of developing secondary solid cancers, including NSCLC [6], current data do not specify an enrichment for EGFR-mutant NSCLC or any mechanistic link to EGFR pathway alterations.

In conclusion, EGFR-mutated NSCLC can present with highly atypical and unexpected imaging findings and patterns of organs involved. Careful pathological and molecular analyses are crucial for accurate diagnosis, and based on those results, optimal management in such unusual cases.

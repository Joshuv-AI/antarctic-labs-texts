A hybrid approach for citrus disease detection using convolutional neural networks and fuzzy inference systems for enhanced accuracy and interpretability
Authors: Bobbinpreet Kaur, Sheenam Middha, Gaurav Singh, Nitin Mittal, Hardik Doshi, Doaa Sami Khafaga, Eman Abdullah Aldakheel, Mohamed Abouhawwash
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The citrus diseases are affecting the fruit production worldwide thereby posing an economical burden. Major research is moving towards finding solutions using Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Image processing methods. Due to factors like illumination variations, leaf form, and disease symptoms, image data has intrinsic uncertainties that are typically difficult for traditional machine learning techniques to handle. In this paper, the interpretability of fuzzy logic is combined with the resilience of deep learning to propose a novel Fuzzy Convolutional Neural Network (Fuzzy-CNN) architecture for the automated diagnosis of citrus leaf diseases. The hybrid method uses a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to obtain complex features of citrus images, and a Fuzzy Inference System (FIS) to improve the classification results. The proposed approach encodes accurate data into fuzzy sets and applies linguistic concepts to determine the severity of a disease, which will contribute to the further development of the decision. In order to test and verify the proposed approach, several experiments were carried out, which proved that Fuzzy-CNN is more effective than regular CNN models with the approximate accuracy difference approximately 1.8, and especially in cases when the symptoms of disease are not clear. To strengthen experimental validation, the proposed method is evaluated on two independent datasets, including an external benchmark dataset, imbalance-aware evaluation metrics are employed to ensure robustness and generalizability. Experimental results demonstrate consistent and statistically significant improvements over existing neuro-fuzzy and machine learning approaches. This research contributes to early detection by collaborating the potential of fuzzy neural networks and offering a flexible solution for real-time disease detection in citrus crops.

Dataset description

Citrus plant leaves in five different classes—Black Spot, Canker, Greening, Healthy, and Melanose—make up the dataset11 and is represented as Dataset-1 in this work. Each category includes samples of both healthy and diseased leaves, reflecting the various leaf states. In the Canker category, leaves have elevated, brown lesions encircled by yellow halos, indicating bacterial infection, whereas the Black Spot category displays leaves with black, irregular spots brought on by a fungal infection. The category of Greening comprises leaves that exhibit yellowing and asymmetric mottling due to citrus greening disease, also known as Huanglongbing. Leaves in the Healthy group are uniformly green in color and show no outward symptoms of illness. Last but not least, leaves with tiny, black patches resulting from a different fungal infection fall under the Melanose category. The sample images are shown in Fig. 5. In an effort to reduce the class imbalance within the dataset as well as enhance the robustness of the learning model, data augmentation on the minority classes was used. The initial data exhibited significant distribution differences in sample sizes across different classes which could have led to a bias in learning as well as the lack of effectiveness in generalization. In a bid to overcome this, the augmentation methods were of geometric transformation, and they included the horizontal flipping, vertical flipping, 90-degree rotation, 180-degree rotation, and 270-degree rotation. Such adaptations preserve the semantic features of the images, but make the spatial orientation more varied. These augmented samples were created sequentially until the number of images in both classes matched which created a balanced dataset. The additional method helps overcome the imbalance of the classes and boosts the ability of the model to learn invariance features and therefore the accuracy of the classification and the generalization to unknown data. The proposed methodology work on citrus plants disease detection using deep learning with Fuzzy inference which include the following steps:

Dataset development and collection A comprehensive dataset of citrus plant images, including both healthy and diseased leaves, will be compiled. The dataset should encompass a wide range of diseases (such as citrus canker, greening, and black spot) and various environmental conditions to ensure robustness39–41.

Data Preprocessing42,43 The images will undergo preprocessing steps to prepare them for training. This involves:

Resizing: Standardizing the image dimensions (e.g., 256 × 256 pixels) for consistency.

Normalization: Scaling pixel values to a range that optimizes neural network training44.

The proposed Integrated Fuzzy CNN model requires the input images to have a fixed spatial size of 200 × 200 × 3 (RGB). While convolutional layers are naturally translation-equivariant, the dimensionality of feature maps produced after sequential convolution, pooling, and flattening operations depends directly on the input size. Methods

Because of their expertise in image processing and pattern identification, CNNs are an effective artificial intelligence tool for detecting citrus diseases. CNNs use hierarchical feature learning to analyse leaf images, moving from simple textures to increasingly intricate forms and patterns unique to a given illness. Even in cases when symptoms are vague or overlap, CNNs can recognise important signs of diseases like citrus greening, canker, and black spot thanks to this layered approach. However, when used in real-world settings with drastically varying lighting, background noise, and other variables, CNNs may encounter difficulties. Notwithstanding their advantages, these models frequently need sizable and varied datasets in order to generalise well under various circumstances. CNNs are a potential but developing approach for managing citrus since efforts are being made to increase their robustness and efficiency for real-world field applications[48].

Proposed methodology

The proposed approach combines fuzzy logic for decision-making with the power of CNN for feature extraction. The fuzzy system will categorize the kind and severity of diseases based on the pertinent features that the CNN model has automatically derived from images of citrus trees. This hybrid approach gives classification judgments an intelligible rationale and improves detection accuracy. To find patterns in the data, artificial neural networks are used to simulate complex interactions between inputs and outputs. The output images of citrus plants are always pretty accurate, but regrettably, the result models are not always presented in an understandable manner. However, in scenarios involving approximation reasoning and imprecision, fuzzy systems have been shown to be beneficial. However, selecting the appropriate fuzzy rules and membership functions sometimes necessitates a trial-and-error procedure. Fuzzy logic and Deep neural networks function well together to improve the system as a whole. Because Deep neural networks can learn and adapt, they are perfect for helping with the process of automatically building a fuzzy system. Neuro-fuzzy systems are systems that combine the two types of systems. The hybrid CNN-fuzzy model (shown in Fig. 7) will be evaluated using a validation dataset. Model architecture

The model’s input layer commences with a 200 × 200 image comprising three channels: Red, Green, and Blue. Subsequently, it traverses several max-pooling and convolutional layers. The initial convolutional layer employs twenty filters, followed by a max-pooling layer that reduces the spatial dimensions by 50%. This sequence recurs multiple times: initially, max-pooling is applied, followed by a second convolutional layer containing 30 filters, then a third convolutional layer with 40 filters, and so on. Subsequent to each convolutional layer, max-pooling reduces the spatial dimensions, resulting in progressively smaller feature maps. After the last convolutional block, the result will be converted to a one-dimensional vector. This vector has since been condensed and passed on to a particular portion of the model comprising of a large number of fuzzy inference blocks. The result of the blocks of fuzzy inferences that apply adaptively learned membership functions is packed into a bigger vector and sent to a dense layer to make final predictions. The adaptive learning guarantees that fuzzy rules are strictly checked on the complete feature space and are scalable to high dimensional inputs as it is shown by stable performance across two datasets. The overall methodology seems to combine the use of both fuzzy logic and the traditional CNN feature extraction to improve the decision processes. The proposed CNN + Fuzzy Layer + Adaptive Membership Learning architecture is a combination of deep feature extraction and understandable fuzzy reasoning. This creates a mixed structure that solves a number of challenges that are involved in traditional classifiers. The CNN component learns hierarchical representations of patterns of diseases on its own, eliminating the need of manually determined features. The obtained characteristics are then passed to the fuzzy layer that uses understandable linguistic rules to facilitate decision-making transparency. Adaptive membership learning methodology is a better approach because it enhances the performance of the model by dynamically adjusting membership functions according to extracted features. This is made possible by the fact that the algorithm can narrow down on decision boundaries and distinguish between fine differences across disease classifications that might be visually similar, and enhances discrimination between classes. This flexible approach reduces the bias of the traditional fixed fuzzy systems and allows the model to better adapt to changes in image quality, illumination and lesion severity.

The explainability of the proposed CNN-fuzzy was investigated by looking at the behavior of the fuzzy inference layer. Here, the CNN is used to obtain high-level features of citrus leaf images and the fuzzy module transforms the features into linguistic rules using predefined membership functions. In the process of inference, all the rules generate an activation value that represents its contribution to the final classification decision.

The quantitative analysis was done by observing the strengths of fuzzy rules in the test samples. The findings revealed that there were few rules that had a tendency to activate higher in particular classes of diseases which indicated a stable feature-rule association. Conclusion and future scope

Though there is a significant improvement carried out in detection of citrus diseases through deployment of Deep learning methods but to make the model more realistic introduction of fuzzy methods is necessary. Since Fuzzy Inference systems can model the real time scenarios with better understanding and higher accuracies, we have integrated the capabilities of Deep learning with Fuzzy to detect the citrus diseases. Fuzzy Inference Systems offer a resilient, adaptable, and adaptive framework for processing imprecise and uncertain input, which makes them essential for real-time situations. In areas like automation, control systems, and decision-making, where conventional approaches might not be able to consider the complexity of the real world, they are extremely useful due to their capacity to simulate human-like reasoning and handle nonlinear, dynamic situations. This study integrates the benefits of fuzzy logic in managing uncertainty in decision-making with the feature extraction skills of CNNs to create a Fuzzy-CNN framework for identifying citrus diseases. The objective of this hybrid approach is to optimize the accuracy, interpretability and strength of detection systems and process complex and imprecise data that is common in agricultural scenarios in the real world. The experimental findings suggest that the Fuzzy-CNN model is better than the traditional CNNs in accuracy and resistance to ambiguous and noisy input to perform classification. To control variations in leaf appearance due to changes in sunshine, environmental conditions, and sensor noise more effectively, the introduction of fuzzy logic into the decision layer allows the system to be more accommodating of variations in leaf appearance. Another crucial requirement in agricultural diagnostics, where the confidence and transparency are crucial, is the possibility to explain the results obtained by the model. The FIS increased interpretation through the option of human like thinking. This analysis is based on the only publicly available dataset on citrus disease that consists of a limited number of samples in particular classes, in particular, Melanose.Despite the improvements achieved with the proposed Fuzzy-CNN model, limitations remain due to the limited availability of annotated images for certain rare diseases, which may affect generalizability under diverse field conditions. Although statistical tests and a second dataset were incorporated, fully automated optimization of fuzzy rules and membership functions for large-scale datasets remains a challenge. Future efforts will be aimed at increasing datasets, incorporating sophisticated uncertainty and explainability assessment, and deploying the model in edge devices or IoTs to monitor citrus disease in the field in real time.

This work has provided the foundation upon which future studies on hybrid AI systems in the detection of agricultural diseases can be conducted and the results may be used to transform how diseases are diagnosed and treated in the crop fields. Through the integration of the hybrid model into the edge computing platforms as well as the IoT devices, the ability to detect illnesses in real time will be enhanced, latency will be minimised and the dependency on the centralised computer resources will be minimised. To enhance the interpretability of the model, it is possible to further refine the fuzzy logic component, which will be used to provide the farmers and agricultural specialists with the understanding of the logic of disease classification and recommended treatments.

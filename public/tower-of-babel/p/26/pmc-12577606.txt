Investigating the Roles of MAPKs as Precision Oncology Targets: A Multi-cancer Expression and Survival Study
Authors: MD NAYAB SULAIMANI, PRITHVI SINGH, MOHAMMAD UMAR SAEED, SOBIA ZAIDI, AFZAL HUSSAIN, MOHAMED F. ALAJMI, ANAS SHAMSI, RAVINS DOHARE, MD IMTAIYAZ HASSAN
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background/AimCancer remains a leading cause of mortality globally, driven by complex molecular mechanisms and characterized by significant biological heterogeneity across cancer types. We aimed to discover mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs) family members as both biomarkers and therapeutic targets in different cancer types. MAPKs are key signaling molecules regulating cell proliferation, differentiation, stress response, and apoptosis. Dysregulation of MAPK pathways has been implicated in the onset and progression of multiple cancers, contributing to tumor growth, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance. Given their diverse roles across cancer types, systematic analysis of MAPK gene expression, mutations, and interactions with tumor microenvironment is essential.Materials and MethodsThe present study undertakes a comprehensive transcriptomic analysis of MAPKs in nine major cancer types using RNA-Seq datasets from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). RNA-Seq data were analyzed to identify differentially expressed MAPKs across nine cancer types using DESeq2, NOIseq, and limma. Significant genes (adjusted p<0.05) were subjected to GO and KEGG enrichment (EnrichR), mutational profiling (cBioPortal), and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Tumor immune infiltration was further assessed using TIMER2.0 to explore immune-gene interactions.ResultsThrough rigorous differential expression analysis, we identified key MAPKs that are significantly altered in liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC) and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). Specifically, six MAPKs (MAPK3, MAPK7, MAPK9, MAPK10, MAPK12, and MAPK13) were found to be differentially expressed in LIHC, while MAPK6 emerged as the sole significant candidate in LUAD. Functional enrichment and pathway analysis revealed that these MAPKs are involved in critical oncogenic pathways, including MAPK-mediated transcriptional regulation and stress-activated signaling cascades. Mutational profiling and survival analysis further validated the prognostic significance of these genes, with several MAPKs showing strong associations with reduced patient survival. Tumor immune infiltration analysis indicated potential roles of these MAPKs in modulating immune responses within the tumor microenvironment. Also, MAPKs identified in this study are structurally related, suggesting that targeting them collectively may enhance therapeutic efficacy and overcome resistance mechanisms.ConclusionOur integrated approach underscores the value of MAPK family members as both biomarkers and therapeutic targets in LIHC and LUAD. This study contributes important insights into MAPK-related oncogenic processes and supports the development of targeted therapies under the framework of precision oncology.

Introduction

Cancer remains one of the most pressing global health challenges of the 21st century, significantly contributing to public health concerns, social burden, and economic impact. It accounts for about one in six deaths worldwide and nearly one-quarter of all fatalities caused by noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) (1). Among individuals aged 30 to 69 years, cancer is responsible for roughly 30% of premature deaths from NCDs and is among the top three causes of death in the majority of countries. In 2022, lung cancer emerged as both the most commonly diagnosed and deadliest cancer globally, with an estimated 2.5 million new cases and over 1.8 million deaths. It contributed to 12.4% of all new cancer cases and 18.7% of cancer-related deaths. Female breast cancer was the second most common, with 2.3 million new cases (11.6%), and was the fourth leading cause of cancer deaths globally, responsible for about 666,000 deaths. Among women, it remained the most diagnosed and the top cause of cancer-related mortality. In men, prostate cancer ranked as the second most frequently diagnosed cancer, with 1.5 million new cases and nearly 397,000 deaths, making it the fifth leading cause of cancer death (2).

Liver cancer ranked as the sixth most commonly diagnosed cancer worldwide in 2022, with an estimated 865,000 new cases reported (3). Liver cancer is the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths among men globally, with incidence and mortality rates generally two to three times higher in men than in women across most regions. Hepatocellular carcinoma accounts for approximately 75% to 85% of all primary liver cancer cases, while intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma comprises around 10% to 15%. Chronic infections with hepatitis B virus (HBV) or hepatitis C virus (HCV) are major risk factors and are associated with 21% to 55% of HCC cases worldwide. (4). Additional risk factors for HCC include exposure to aflatoxins, excessive alcohol intake, obesity, type 2 diabetes, and tobacco use (5). HNSC is the seventh most prevalent cancer globally, contributing to more than 660,000 new cases and around 325,000 deaths each year (6). These cancers pose a major global health challenge, with patients commonly enduring substantial hardships due to both the progression of the disease and the adverse effects of its treatment (7).

The RAS protein plays a key role in regulating cell proliferation and survival in response to growth factors, in both normal and cancerous cells (8). Once activated, RAS triggers multiple downstream signaling pathways, with the most well-studied being the RAF/MEK/ERK cascade (also known as the classical MAPK pathway), the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) pathway, and the Ral-GEF signaling route (9). Mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs) are a group of protein kinases with highly conserved functions across evolution, from simple organisms like yeast to complex species such as humans. These kinases play a critical role by phosphorylating specific serine and threonine residues on target proteins, thereby influencing a wide array of cellular processes, including gene regulation, cell division, motility, metabolism, and apoptosis (10-15).

In multicellular organisms, three major subfamilies of MAPKs have been extensively studied. These include the extracellular signal-regulated kinases (ERK1 and ERK2), the c-Jun N-terminal kinases (JNK1, JNK2, and JNK3), and the p38 MAPK family, which consists of four isoforms: p38α, p38β, p38γ, and p38δ (16). Multiple approaches are currently under investigation to inhibit MAPK signaling as a strategy for cancer treatment. Although direct inhibitors of RAS have not yet demonstrated clinical success, alternative therapeutics have shown promising efficacy. These include targeting synthetic lethal partners specific to RAS-mutant tumors and inhibiting key downstream components of the pathway, such as RAF and MEK (17).

Precision oncology, often referred to as biomarker-guided therapy, has rapidly advanced clinical care by improving treatment outcomes (18). This approach focuses on identifying actionable biomarkers and therapeutic targets that are linked to unfavorable prognosis and diminished survival in cancer patients, thereby offering more personalized and effective treatment strategies (19). Recent progress in diagnostics, drug discovery, and biological research through modern techniques is poised to play a crucial role in enhancing cancer treatment within the framework of precision oncology (20). Large-scale datasets from diverse cancer types, produced through various experimental studies by research groups worldwide, are freely accessible through resources such as The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Genomic Data Commons (GDC) portal (21). The genomic data available in these repositories are increasingly being leveraged to enable precisely targeted therapies in cancer research. Numerous studies have drawn on these resources to investigate the genetic underpinnings of various cancers, revealing significant disruptions in multiple genes (22).

In this study, nine cancer types, breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA), lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSC), kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD), and prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD) were selected, as they collectively represent a substantial share of the global cancer burden. Each of these cancers has distinct biological behaviors, risk factors, and clinical challenges, making them critical targets for molecular studies aimed at uncovering disease-specific and shared mechanisms that could inform better therapeutic strategies. We adopted an unbiased and comprehensive approach to investigate the biological heterogeneity and genomic landscape of cancer. By systematically analyzing pan-cancer samples, we aimed to identify novel biomarkers and druggable targets relevant to diagnosis, prognosis, and therapy. Differential expression analysis was conducted to examine the expression patterns of MAPKs across these cancer types. This integrative approach facilitated the discovery of potential molecular markers and therapeutic targets.

Furthermore, we assessed the alteration frequencies and performed survival analysis in patients with genetic modifications in the identified MAPKs. Overall, this study offers valuable insights into the differential expression of MAPKs across nine cancer types, contributing to the genomic understanding and therapeutic exploration within the cancer research community.

Materials and Methods

RNA-Seq data. Normal and tumor samples from nine distinct cancer types were obtained from the TCGA data portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Only site-specific samples corresponding to each cancer type were included, while non-specific or unclassified samples were excluded from the analysis. To extract mRNA expression data (HTSeq-counts generated using the Illumina HiSeq platform), the UCSC Xena browser (https://xenabrowser.net/) was utilized. Additionally, associated phenotypic information, including age at diagnosis, weight, batch effects, and gender was retrieved from the Xena platform.

Differential gene expression. The collected data were organized in an Excel sheet, where each row represented an expressed RNA and each column corresponded to either tumor or control samples. Differential expression analysis was carried out using R programming, utilizing a separate sheet containing sample type annotations and sample count information to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs). The DESeq2, NOIseq, and limma packages were used for pre-processing (Normalization, log2 transformation, and batch correction) and DEG identification, respectively. Significantly differentially expressed MAPKs were identified based on log₂ fold change and the adjusted p-value criteria. A log₂ fold change of zero was considered the threshold, with negative values indicating down-regulated genes and positive values indicating up-regulated genes. Genes with an adjusted p-value of less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

GO term and pathway enrichment analyses. Gene Ontology (GO) term and pathway analysis are essential bioinformatics tools used to interpret high-throughput genomic or transcriptomic data (like RNA-seq or microarray). They help identify biological meaning in differentially expressed genes. GO helps understand what functions are overrepresented in the gene list, provides functional annotation of genes based on shared biological characteristics, facilitates hypothesis generation, and is helpful in identifying key regulatory processes and understanding cellular roles of genes. Pathway enrichment analysis identifies biological pathways (e.g., KEGG, Reactome, WikiPathways) enriched in a set of genes. It helps uncover biological mechanisms and molecular interactions involved in the condition of interest, enables system-level understanding of disease, treatment response, or cellular state and assists in identifying potential drug targets or biomarkers (23).

GO analysis was conducted to uncover key biological characteristics associated with the RNA-seq data. Up-regulated and down-regulated genes were analyzed separately using the enrichR tool for GO enrichment. The resulting gene sets were further categorized into functional groups based on GO terms related to Biological Process (BP), Molecular Function (MF), and Cellular Component (CC).

The top 3 significantly up-regulated and down-regulated GO categories were considered. These significantly enriched terms could help us a lot to understand further the role that DEGs play in cancers. Pathway enrichment analysis for the significant MAPKs was performed using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways available through the Enrichr database. GO terms and pathways with a p-value less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Mutational analysis. Mutational (alteration frequency) analysis is a powerful method used to assess the frequency at which specific genes are genetically altered across different cancer types. Alteration frequency refers to how frequently a gene shows genomic alterations such as mutations, copy number variations (amplifications/deletions), or mRNA expression changes across patient samples of a cancer type (24). Genes with high alteration frequency may play key roles in tumorigenesis and can be prioritized for further functional or therapeutic studies. Frequently amplified or overexpressed genes may act as oncogenes, and frequently deleted or mutated genes may function as tumor suppressors (25). Hence, the altered genes can serve as diagnostic, prognostic, or predictive biomarkers. Mutational frequencies of the identified candidate genes were examined using TCGA datasets accessed via cBioPortal (https://www.cbioportal.org/). Each mutation was mapped onto the two-dimensional structures of the corresponding proteins, and their occurrence frequencies were recorded (22).

Survival analysis. Survival analysis traditionally refers to the statistical examination of the time until death within a group of individuals. More broadly, it involves the analysis of time-to-event data, which measures the duration from a defined starting point to a specific event of interest. For example, individuals might be followed from birth to the onset of some disease, or the survival time after the diagnosis of some disease might be studied (26). Survival analysis methods are typically applied to data collected prospectively over time, such as those obtained from cohort studies or clinical trials (27). To assess the impact of alterations in the identified candidate MAPKs on overall survival across the previously mentioned cancer types, survival analysis was conducted using TCGA data accessed via cBioPortal. Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival curves were generated, and statistical significance was evaluated using the log-rank test p-value (28).

Tumor immune infiltration analysis. Tumor-infiltrating immune cells play a critical role in determining treatment response and patient prognosis in cancer. The diversity of immune cell populations within the tumor micro-environment contributes to tumor heterogeneity, presenting both opportunities and challenges in understanding the dynamic interplay between immune and cancer cells (29). Cancer immunotherapy is designed to enhance the activity of cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) within tumors, support the priming of tumor-specific CTLs in lymphoid tissues, and establish robust and lasting antitumor immunity. During the priming phase, CD4+T cells provide essential help signals to CD8+ T cells through specialized dendritic cells, thereby enhancing the strength and quality of the CTL response (30). RNA-seq-based gene expression profiling is a well-established method for tumor characterization and has been extensively applied to generate comprehensive transcriptomic datasets across various cancer types. However, while these data provide valuable molecular insights, they do not directly reveal the composition of immune cell populations within tumors, necessitating computational approaches for inference.

TIMER2.0 is a publicly accessible web server developed for the research community, built using the Shiny web framework for R. It comprises three main components: Immune, Exploration, and Estimation. The Immune component includes four modules that enable users to examine associations between estimated immune cell infiltration and various factors such as gene expression, somatic mutations, somatic copy number alterations (sCNAs), and clinical outcomes across TCGA cancer cohorts (31).

Survival of patients. The Kaplan-Meier survival analyses reveal that higher expression levels of MAPK3, MAPK7, MAPK9, MAPK12, and MAPK13 are consistently associated with reduced patient survival times and the higher expression of MAPK10 is associated with increased survival times. Specifically, patients exhibiting high MAPK3 expression showed significantly reduced survival probabilities, with a hazard ratio (HR) of 1.91 and a highly significant p-value of 0.00018, indicating nearly twice the risk of death compared to those with low expression. Similarly, high MAPK7 expression mirrored this trend, with an identical HR of 1.91 and the same level of statistical significance, suggesting a robust and critical role of MAPK7 in disease progression. MAPK9 also demonstrated a notable negative impact on survival, with an HR of 1.6 and a p-value of 0.0068, highlighting that elevated MAPK9 expression moderately but significantly increases the risk of mortality (Table X).

Discussion

Our study aimed to identify significant MAPK family members across nine cancer types LUAD, LIHC, LUSC, BRCA, KIRC, KIRP, HNSC, PRAD, and STAD using RNA-seq data from TCGA, with significance assessed based on threshold p-values, log fold change and survival correlations, etc. Out of the 13 known MAPKs, only seven (MAPK3, MAPK6, MAPK7, MAPK9, MAPK12, and MAPK13) emerged as significantly up-regulated, and MAPK10 as significantly down-regulated and notably, their significance was confined to LIHC and LUAD. Although our intention was to identify MAPKs broadly relevant across all these cancer types, statistical and survival filtering limited our findings to these two cancers. This does not preclude the importance of other MAPKs as well as the cancer types but highlights the stringency of our selection criteria. These MAPKs are known for their roles in oncogenic processes MAPK3 (ERK1), MAPK7 (ERK5), and others are implicated in promoting proliferation, survival, and disrupted differentiation (37). MAPK6 (ERK3), an atypical MAPK, is notably up-regulated in LUAD and has been suggested as an emerging oncogenic driver (38).

Despite the limited number of cancer types showing statistical significance, the structural and functional interconnectivity of these MAPKs provides a rationale for co-targeting strategies. Hence, targeting up-regulated MAPKs, MAPK3, MAPK7, MAPK9, MAPK12, and MAPK13 for LIHC and MAPK6 for LUAD can be very crucial for therapeutic development of cancers. These findings not only align with prior evidence but also reinforce the potential of these MAPKs as promising therapeutic targets, particularly in LUAD and LIHC. Also, to complement our transcript-level analyses, we reviewed independent protein-level studies demonstrating MAPK activation by quantitative proteomics and Western blot across multiple cancers. For example, Suzuki et al. (39) showed ERK1/2 suppression by acyclic retinoid in melanoma via Western blot, Dong et al. (40) reported irradiation-induced p-ERK elevation in breast and cervical cancer, and proteomic profiling of pilocytic astrocytoma confirmed ERK-2 overexpression by western blot (41). Although from other tumor types, these data consistently show that transcript-level MAPK alterations correspond to protein-level changes, reinforcing our findings. Together with our results, this evidence supports our elucidated MAPKs as potential biomarkers for LIHC and for LUAD, warranting future validation in patient tissues via western blot or immunohistochemistry.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive, multi-layered analysis of MAPK gene family members across nine prevalent human cancers, focusing particularly on their expression, mutational landscape, survival relevance, pathway associations, and immune infiltration patterns. Among the analyzed cancer types, LIHC and LUAD exhibited the most prominent MAPK dysregulation. Specifically, six MAPKs MAPK3, MAPK7, MAPK9, MAPK10, MAPK12, and MAPK13 were found to be significantly altered in LIHC, while MAPK6 showed a notable up-regulation in LUAD. These genes not only demonstrated functional enrichment in pathways central to tumor growth, immune evasion, and transcriptional regulation, but also correlated significantly with patient survival outcomes, indicating their potential as prognostic biomarkers. The tumor immune infiltration analysis further emphasized the role of these kinases in shaping the tumor microenvironment, which is crucial for developing effective immunotherapeutic strategies. Importantly, since MAPKs are structurally conserved and often function within interconnected signaling cascades, simultaneously targeting multiple family members could improve therapeutic outcomes and reduce resistance. Our findings advocate for the inclusion of MAPKs in the portfolio of precision oncology targets and support their development as biomarkers for early diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic intervention. Future studies involving in vitro validation and clinical trials are crucial to translate these findings into actionable cancer therapies. Overall, this integrative bioinformatics approach not only highlights the oncogenic relevance of MAPKs in LIHC and LUAD but also strengthens the rationale for family-wide targeting of MAPKs in cancer treatment strategies.

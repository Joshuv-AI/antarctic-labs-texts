Cutaneous metastases from intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma: a case report
Authors: Ying Zhang, Yong Li, Chao Liu, Liqun Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCutaneous metastases from primary malignant neoplasms are rare in clinical practice, especially in intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma(ICC). ICC features highly invasive and metastatic biological behaviors, early extrahepatic metastasis. Distant lymph nodes are the most common metastatic site for ICC, followed by the lungs and bones, whereas Cutaneous metastasis is very rare, usually indicating a poor prognosis and low survival rate.Case presentationWe present the case of a 69-year-old woman with cutaneous metastases from ICC who survived for four years. The patient underwent cutaneous metastatic tumor resection, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immune checkpoint inhibitors combined treatment, achieving long-term disease control. In this case, multidisciplinary treatment can delay the progression of the disease to a certain extent and improve the quality of life for the patient.ConclusionCutaneous metastases from intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma are exceedingly rare and therapeutic challenges. While treatment protocols lack standardization, combination therapy of surgical resection with ICI and targeted therapy shows promising efficacy in cutaneous metastases from ICC. Continued research and collaboration are essential to further refine therapeutic strategies, ultimately enhancing outcomes for patients with cutaneous metastases from ICC.

Introduction

Cutaneous metastatic(CM) cancer refers to lesions that develop in the cutaneous secondary to the spread of malignant tumors through blood, lymphatic vessels, direct tissue invasion, or surgical implantation [1]. Once CM of a malignant tumor occurs, it is often associated with multiple organ metastases and a poor prognosis [2]. The treatment of CM cancer mainly depends on the primary tumor site, while simultaneously treating local cutaneous metastatic lesions. ICC is a highly invasive tumor, with a five-year survival rate after surgical resection of only 25%-40% [3], and a high rate of local recurrence and distant metastasis (60–70%) [4]. Despite advances in ICC treatment in recent years, the overall efficacy remains poor. CM are rare in patients with ICC. We present a case of ICC with CM undergoing resection combined with systemic anti-tumor therapy, along with a review of the literature.

Case presentation

A 69-year-old female patient was admitted to the hospital on May 20, 2021, with a 10-day history of discovering a liver mass. The patient was asymptomatic, and laboratory tests showed no abnormalities in CA199. Imaging studies revealed a slightly low-density lesion in the top of the right lobe of the liver, measuring approximately 33 mm × 34 mm, with unclear borders and significant heterogeneous enhancement in the arterial phase, and decreased enhancement in the venous and delayed phases (Fig. 1A, B).The patient underwent liver tumor resection under general anesthesia on May 26, 2021. Intraoperative findings: Intraoperatively, a white-colored mass was found protruding from the surface of liver segment VIII, with a hard consistency and a maximum diameter of approximately 2 cm. Pathological diagnosis (Fig. 1C): Pathological diagnosis revealed a malignant tumor with necrosis, consistent with poorly differentiated cholangiocellular carcinoma, with some areas showing sarcomatous differentiation.The tumor did not invade the liver capsule or involve the liver margin; there was no neural or vascular invasion. Immunohistochemical Results: showed positive staining for CK, CK7, Vimentin, CK19 (partial), Ki-67 (approximately 60%), GPC3, and HSP70, negative staining for Hepatocyte and Arginase-1, and positive staining for CD34 in blood vessels. PD-L1 testing was positive (CPS, 85%). On July 6, 2021, skin nodules were discovered on the right side of the ribs (Fig. 2A, B), protruding, red in color, painful on palpation, and approximately 5 cm in size. Similar nodules were found on the right buttock and both thighs. Surgical resection of the nodules on July 14, 2021, confirmed metastatic cholangiocellular carcinoma with sarcomatous differentiation (Fig. 2C, D). PD-L1 testing was positive (CPS, 65%). The patient started receiving tegafur 80 mg/d, apatinib mesylate 250 mg/d, and sintilimab 200 mg iv/3w on July 21, 2021. Currently, the patient continues to receive apatinib mesylate. Telephone follow-up on January 25, 2026: The patient was bedridden due to cerebral thrombosis and failed to go to the hospital for condition assessment.

Discussion

Cutaneous metastatic cancer is rare in clinical practice, with an incidence of 0.7%-9.0% in patients with internal organ tumors [5]. The reported median period from diagnosis of CM to death ranges from 6 months [6]. CM can occur in various parts of the body. The majority of CM occur in areas such as the head, abdomen, and chest [5]. The scalp was the most frequently involved site of distant CM in cholangiocarcinoma [7]. Currently, there is no standardized diagnosis and treatment process for CM of solid tumors, and treatment often follows the treatment strategy for advanced cholangiocarcinoma. CM may be the first evidence for an unknown tumor disease or demonstrate the progression of a known tumor. Clinical features usually do not allow specific assignment to a primary tumor, hence diagnosis is largely based on histological findings. Immunohistochemistry is therefore an essential tool in establishing the diagnosis.The diagnosis of ICC is associated with CK7/CK19 and Hepatocyte/Arg-1 (CK7/CK19 favor cholangiocarcinoma; lack of Hepatocyte/Arg-1 argues against HCC).The pathology of the patient’s liver tumor showed poorly differentiated cholangiocarcinoma, with partial areas showing sarcomatoid differentiation. The cutaneous tumor showed metastatic cholangiocarcinoma with sarcomatoid differentiation. The sarcomatoid differentiation in the pathology suggests a high degree of tumor malignancy. The patient’s condition progressed directly 41 days after liver tumor resection, with the occurrence of CM. CM from cholangiocarcinoma are very rare occurrences, Liu M. et al. [7] described about 30 cases published in the literature. In the treatment of CM, both local and systemic therapies or their combinations may be used.

Patients with advanced cholangiocarcinoma are first treated with chemotherapy. The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend the combination of gemcitabine and platinum-based drugs as the first-line systemic chemotherapy regimen for ICC [8].The efficacy of chemotherapy is not satisfactory, and it is often combined with targeted, immunotherapy, interventional, and other treatment options. The continuous improvement of second-generation sequencing NGS detection technology and the continuous understanding of ICC molecular typing have led to individualized targeted and immunotherapy that can significantly improve the clinical prognosis of patients [9]. ICC is divided into large bile duct type ICC, which originates from the large bile ducts in the liver, and small bile duct type ICC, which originates from the small bile ducts or stem progenitor cells in the liver, based on histological characteristics and cell origin [10].There are FGFR, IDH1, BRAF, KRAS and other gene mutations in ICC, and FGFR fusion is the most common mutation, with an incidence of 10%-20% in ICC [3]. The bold tubular ICC mainly occurs with KRAS and SMAD4 mutations [11, 12], while the small bile duct ICC mainly occurs with IDH1/2, FGFR, and BAP1 mutations [13]. Nie C [14] et al. conducted a comparative analysis of the efficacy of apatinib monotherapy and combination therapy in 28 patients with biliary tract cancer who had progressed or metastasized after chemotherapy. The results showed that the mPFS and mOS of patients treated with apatinib combination therapy were significantly improved. Checkpoint inhibitor-based immunotherapy has been applied to various malignant tumors, including ICC. PD-1 antibodies are used to treat mismatch repair deficiency(dMMR), and high microsatellite instabilit(MSI-H) [15]. It also has certain efficacy for patients with PD-L1 expression and high tumor mutation burden [tumor mutation burden, TMB > 10]. TMB, MSI-H, PD-L1 positivity, etc. are important indicators of the efficacy of immunotherapy. However, in clinical trials of single-drug immunotherapy for ICC, the efficacy was found to be poor [16–17]. The TOPAZ-1 phase III randomized double-blind study showed that the efficacy of the first-line treatment of advanced biliary tract cancer with the combination of denosumab, gemcitabine, and cisplatin(GC)regimen was acceptable [18]. Sintilimab is currently mainly used for relapsed or refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma [19], and there are also corresponding clinical reports on the treatment of ICC [20]. The patient was found to have CM of the tumor and underwent Cutaneous tumor resection, combined treatment with tegafur, apatinib mesylate, and sintilimab.When CM occurs in cholangiocarcinoma, the prognosis is poor, with a median survival time of 4 months after CM [21]. In this case, CM occurred after surgical treatment of ICC, and no other metastatic lesions were found. After various treatment methods and multidisciplinary joint diagnosis and treatment, the survival period has exceeded 4 years༈Survival is calculated from the date of diagnosis of CM.

For this case, treatment involved multidisciplinary therapy and prolonged survival. The timing sequence of the patient’s systemic therapy was empirical rather than based on a standardized protocol. Moreover, the results from a single patient cannot be generalized, and further research is needed to confirm them.

Real-world effectiveness of first- and second-line anti-angiogenesis therapy in RCC: analysis of a UK-based population
Authors: Tom Waddell, Manon Pillai, Kate Armitage, Donna M Graham, Michael Moran, Maria Dilleen, Sinead Holmes, Ewa Śleszyńska-Dopiera, Robert Hawkins
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim: Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is the seventh commonest cancer in the UK, where first-line (1L) sunitinib and second-line (2L) axitinib are treatment options.Methods: Retrospective, non-interventional data from the Christie NHS Foundation Trust (Manchester, UK). The primary end point was median progression-free survival (mPFS).Results: For 1L sunitinib (n = 622) and 2L axitinib (n = 121), mPFS (95% CI) was 8.4 (7.6, 9.9) and 6.2 (4.9, 9.3) months, respectively. In 1L, Karnofsky performance status, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), neutrophils, hemoglobin, time from diagnosis to treatment and age were predictors (p < 0.05) of PFS. In 2L, LDH and platelets were predictors of PFS (p < 0.05).Conclusion: Sunitinib and axitinib were effective treatments for RCC. PFS predictors varied between 1L and 2L; LDH was a predictor for both.Clinical Trial Registration: NCT04033991 (ClinicalTrials.gov).Keywords: : axitinib, real-world evidence, renal cell carcinoma, sunitinib

Introduction

In 2020, there were an estimated 430,000 new cases and 180,000 deaths attributable to kidney cancer worldwide [1]. In the UK, kidney cancer is the seventh most common type of cancer, and is projected to continue rising over the next 15 years [2]. Patients with localized kidney cancer have a 5-year survival rate approaching 93%, but this drops to 14% for patients with advanced/metastatic disease [3]. Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) accounts for almost 90% of kidney cancers, of which the clear cell histological subtype makes up approximately 75% of cases [4,5].

Clear cell RCC tumors are highly vascularized and often present with inactivation of the VHL gene [6]. Inactivation of VHL leads to the downstream activation of VEGF, the principal growth factor in tumor angiogenesis [7]. VEGF pathway activation may also contribute to tumor development and metastasis via its role in the colonization of premetastatic sites, neovascularization of dormant micrometastases and immune evasion and suppression [7–9]. As such, the VEGF pathway represents a viable therapeutic target.

Sunitinib is a multi-targeted receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) that exerts anti-angiogenic effects by targeting the VEGF pathway [10]. Sunitinib is indicated for the first-line (1L) treatment of patients with advanced RCC and was the standard of care for over a decade following its approval in 2006. Axitinib is a selective TKI of VEGF receptors 1–3, which was first approved in 2012 as a second-line (2L) treatment for patients with advanced RCC after failure of 1L therapy [11]. The recent approval of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and their use in combination with anti-angiogenic TKIs/other ICIs has led to new standards of care, with licensed options now including axitinib (TKI) plus pembrolizumab (programmed death receptor-1 [PD-1] inhibitor), axitinib plus avelumab (programmed death ligand-1 inhibitor), cabozantinib (TKI) plus nivolumab (PD-1 inhibitor), lenvatinib (TKI) plus pembrolizumab and nivolumab plus ipilimumab (cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 inhibitor; intermediate or poor risk patients only) [12,13]. In the UK, at the time of writing, the only combinations approved for reimbursement within the NHS are axitinib plus avelumab, lenvatinib plus pembrolizumab and nivolumab plus ipilimumab [14–16]. Despite these options, sunitinib remains a 1L treatment option when ICI may not be appropriate due to comorbidities or concurrent medication, or single-agent therapy is preferred by the patient or physician. Axitinib is recommended as a 2L treatment if not administered as part of a 1L regimen [13,17].

The selection of systemic treatment for RCC is based on numerous factors including site of disease/metastases, potential adverse events (patient specific risk) and patient preference/practicalities. The presence or absence of sarcomatoid component was not available for patients in this study. However, the first consideration in selecting a systemic treatment for RCC is generally the presence of risk factors (RFs) as defined by prognostic models. The two most used prognostic models are the International Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma Database Consortium (IMDC) model and the Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center model (MSKCC) [18,19]. Both models define patients as favorable (0 risk factors [RFs]), intermediate (1–2 RFs) or poor risk (≥3 RFs).

Evidence to guide the approvals and clinical use of systemic treatment comes from data generated by randomized clinical trials. These trials rightly employ stringent inclusion/exclusion criteria to ensure potential bias and confounding parameters are minimized [20]. The outcomes of these trials may not accurately translate into a real-world setting as patients seen in everyday clinical practice are of a more heterogeneous make-up and may have more comorbidities and/or poorer performance status than those in clinical trials [21]. Consequently, real-world evidence is important to establish the generalizability of randomized controlled trial data and may aid clinical decisions in those patients not fully represented in clinical trials [22].

This study aimed to understand the clinical outcomes of patients with advanced/metastatic RCC treated with 1L sunitinib and 2L axitinib in a real-world, UK-based setting. Additional objectives were to examine factors that predict treatment effectiveness, including risk stratification and parameters that comprise the IMDC and MSKCC prognostic models.

Methods

Data source & patients

This was a real-world, retrospective, non-interventional study utilizing data from electronic and paper patient medical records. Dr Waddell, as the overseeing clinician and dataset owner, granted permission for the database to be used for the purpose of the research. Data were extracted from an RCC-specific clinical database held by the Christie NHS Foundation Trust (Manchester, UK), comprising approximately 3000 patients. This was a secondary collection study where data were identified and collected by NHS staff members. Data collection was performed independently of this analysis at an earlier time point, and later transferred to the study sponsor for research purposes according to the inclusion/exclusion criteria specified in the protocol. Patient data were fully anonymized by assignment of a unique patient identifier or omitting directly identifiable information prior to data transfer. In the case of missing or inconsistent data, queries were raised to treating NHS physicians. If these data remained missing, they were described as unknown/missing.

Eligible patients were >18 years of age with a confirmed diagnosis of RCC who received sunitinib and/or axitinib during the investigative timeframe (2002-June 2017, with a minimum of 1-year follow-up). Patients were followed up until the last known contact during the timeframe or death. Data collection for the wider clinical database is ongoing.

Data collection for the clinical database was approved by The Christie Caldicott Panel. This study was conducted in accordance with all relevant legal and regulatory requirements. As this study does not involve data subject to privacy laws according to applicable legal requirements, obtaining informed consent from patients by the study sponsor was not required.

Outcomes

The primary end point was the real-world progression-free survival (PFS) outcomes for all patients treated with 1L sunitinib, and those treated with 2L axitinib (post-sunitinib or other 1L systemic treatment) and PFS for each 1L sunitinib and 2L axitinib as stratified by IMDC/MSKCC risk status. PFS was defined as the duration (months) measured from the first date of each treatment line to the date of disease progression, end of treatment date or date of death. Patients who were on treatment were censored on 30 June 2018. If a patient stopped due to toxicity, the earliest date from the date of progression or end of treatment date was assigned. Secondary end points included the real-world overall survival (OS) of patients treated with 1L sunitinib, and those treated with 2L axitinib, and OS for each 1L sunitinib and 2L axitinib as stratified by IMDC/MSKCC risk status. Of note, this study was not intended to correlate changes in IMDC score to treatment response. Importantly, the numbers of patients who had a change in IMDC score are too small to draw any conclusions. OS was defined as the time (months) between the index date (dependent on treatment line) and death from any cause.

Univariate and multivariate analyses examined individual/group parameters, including RFs as defined in IMDC and MSKCC prognostic models. Examined parameters that were included in the multivariate Cox regression analysis were: age >70 vs. ≤70; male vs. female; previous nephrectomy yes vs. no; time from diagnosis to initial treatment (months) <12 vs. ≥12; tumor histology: clear cell vs. non-clear cell; hemoglobin < lower limit of normal (LLN) vs. ≥LLN; calcium > upper limit of normal (ULN) vs. ≤ULN; neutrophils > ULN vs. ≤ULN; platelets > ULN vs. ≤ULN; LDH >1.5 × ULN vs. ≤1.5 × ULN; Karnofsky performance status <80% vs. ≥80%. For each parameter, values were adjusted to reflect local laboratory reference ranges.

Other outcomes include best overall response, which was assessed at each radiological assessment during that line of therapy using the following categories: complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), progressive disease (PD) and not evaluable. A subgroup analysis of patients who received both 1L sunitinib and 2L axitinib was performed. Outcomes for this subgroup included best overall response and IMDC risk status as patients progressed from 1L to 2L treatment and were visualized using a Sankey diagram.

Statistical analysis

Variables of interest, including patient demographics and clinical characteristics, were reported using descriptive statistics. PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method for the overall population and stratified by IMDC/MSKCC risk status. Comparison between stratification groups was carried out using a log rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regressions were used to evaluate explanatory factors for PFS. Significant factors (p < 0.05) were included in the final multivariate Cox regression model using stepwise selection.

Results

Patient population

In total, 622 patients were included in the 1L sunitinib cohort and 121 patients were included in the 2L axitinib cohort. Fifty-nine patients were included in both cohorts as they had received 1L sunitinib followed by 2L axitinib. Patient baseline demographics and clinical characteristics for the two cohorts are presented in Table 1. Patients were predominantly male (approximately 65%) with a mean age of approximately 66 years. The majority of patients in both cohorts were of intermediate risk status (both IMDC and MSKCC) and over 70% of RCCs were of the clear cell histological subtype. Baseline RFs according to IMDC and MSKCC models are presented in Table 2. The most common RFs at baseline were LDH >1.5 × ULN (sunitinib, 368 patients, 59.2%; axitinib, 67 patients, 55.4%) and hemoglobin < LLN (sunitinib, 322 patients, 51.8%; axitinib, 72 patients, 59.5%).

Treatment effectiveness

Of all patients who received 1L sunitinib, median PFS and OS (95% confidence interval [CI]) were 8.4 (7.6, 9.9) and 18.3 (15.8, 20.4) months, respectively. PFS and OS varied significantly according to IMDC risk status for patients who received 1L sunitinib (Figure 1). Median PFS (95% CI) was 14.0 (10.4, 18.5; n = 108), 10.0 (8.1, 12.2; n = 308) and 5.6 (4.8, 6.3; n = 184) months for patients who received 1L sunitinib with favorable, intermediate and poor IMDC risk status, respectively. Median OS (95% CI) was 34.1 (26.4, 46.1; n = 108), 20.4 (17.2, 23.5; n = 308) and 9.3 (7.5, 10.8; n = 184) months for patients who received 1L sunitinib with favorable, intermediate and poor IMDC risk status, respectively.

For the 2L axitinib cohort, median PFS and OS (95% CI) were 6.2 (4.9, 9.3) and 15.8 (12.0, 20.4) months, respectively. Similarly, PFS and OS varied significantly according to IMDC risk status for patients who received 2L axitinib (Figure 2). Median PFS (95% CI) was 12.6 (5.7, 15.6; n = 21), 7.2 (5.2, 10.8; n = 65) and 3.0 (1.7, 5.7; n = 31) months for patients who received 2L axitinib with favorable, intermediate and poor IMDC risk status, respectively. Median OS (95% CI) was not reached (12.2, not reached; n = 21), 16.4 (12.3, 21.9; n = 65) and 8.0 (4.8, 18.2; n = 31) months for patients who received 2L axitinib with favorable, intermediate and poor IMDC risk status, respectively. Baseline demographics and clinical characteristics according to IMDC risk status group are reported in Supplementary Tables S1 & S2. PFS and OS for 1L and 2L treatment according to MSKCC risk status were consistent with those according to IMDC risk status (Supplementary Figures S1 & S2).

Discussion

The systemic treatment of RCC has undergone drastic changes in the past 5 years. Preferred regimens are now combination treatments consisting of ICI plus TKI or ICI combinations [13,17]. However, sunitinib, a previous standard of 1L care, remains a valid treatment option in the 1L setting for patients who are not suitable for ICI-based therapy. Axitinib remains a >2L treatment option if not previously utilized within a 1L doublet regimen. To ensure the best possible outcomes for these patients, it is important to define and understand the effectiveness of these treatments in a real-world setting, as well as how clinical factors may impact outcomes. This study provides data from a large tertiary oncology center regarding the use and effectiveness of both 1L sunitinib and/or 2L axitinib in a real-world, uncontrolled, heterogeneous UK-based population. Many of these patients have substantial comorbidities or poor performance status and therefore, would not have been eligible to be enrolled into the randomized clinical trials of these agents [21,23]. While stringent inclusion/exclusion criteria are necessary to produce sufficiently robust data, the extent to which these data can be applied to patients seen in routine practice is often not fully established. Real-world data can therefore provide reassurance and potentially aid in clinical decision-making for the treatment of patients in everyday practice [22].

Reassuringly, the overall PFS and OS reported herein were largely consistent with those reported in the pivotal clinical trials for both sunitinib and axitinib [10,11]. For 1L sunitinib, our reported findings are also consistent with a previous meta-analysis conducted across both real-world and clinical trial populations [24]. Our analysis of the effectiveness of 2L axitinib was also consistent with other real-world studies (after 1L sunitinib or other 1L treatments), including in UK-based populations [25–29]. More recently, real-world studies of combination regimens support the findings of the recent combination clinical trials, with a clinically significant OS benefit for patients receiving ICI/TKI or ICI/ICI combinations versus 1L TKI monotherapy [30–32]. Collectively, these data support combination treatments as most likely to provide the greatest OS benefit in a real-world setting for those patients who are fit enough to receive doublet therapy. However, single agent TKIs can provide substantial clinical benefit when combination treatment is not appropriate/available or when favoured according to patient or clinician preference.

As expected, patient survival outcomes from this study varied according to IMDC (and MSKCC) risk status. The MSKCC and IMDC prognostic models consist of five and six RFs, respectively, and share four common RFs [18,19]. These models are highly concordant [18]; however, differences in patient stratification between the two models may occur and potentially, may impact patient outcomes [33]. In the multivariate analysis presented herein, LDH >1.5 × ULN was the only RF that was a significant predictor of longer PFS for both 1L and 2L treatment. LDH is upregulated during the malignant transformation of tumor cells, and elevated pre-treatment LDH may be indicative of the metastatic potential of the tumor [34]. Our findings suggest that the MSKCC prognostic model may be more relevant than the IMDC model in our real-world population due to the inclusion of LDH in the MSKCC model. A previous analysis of patients with RCC who received 1L sunitinib from the STAR-TOR registry, a large prospective German registry, also indicated that the MSKCC model may stratify patients more appropriately than IMDC; however, neither model was found to be superior to the other overall [35]. In the same analysis, LDH >1.5 × ULN was identified as a potentially important discriminator between patient risk status categories. Conversely, in UK clinical practice, the IMDC prognostic model is the more commonly utilized, partly owing to its use in stratifying patients within the CheckMate 214 trial [36,37]. Both models have been used in other combination therapy clinical trials [38–41].

In our analysis, only platelet count > ULN and LDH >1.5 × ULN were significant predictors of PFS in patients who received 2L axitinib. Notably, platelet count > ULN is unique to the IMDC prognostic model and was not predictive of PFS in relation to 1L Sunitinib treatment in our analysis. tumor-induced platelet activation can contribute to tumor progression by promoting angiogenesis and metastasis [42]. Platelets are also involved in the regulation of inflammation and may promote recruitment of leukocytes to primary and metastatic tumor sites, as well as contributing to the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps, which can promote metastasis [42]. The emerging significance of platelet count as a predictive factor for PFS in 2L treatment of RCC may reflect the underlying molecular biology of the tumor and stage of the disease. As may be expected, our analysis shows that as patients move to 2L treatment, fewer patients exhibit complete/partial response and SD, with a greater proportion of patients presenting with PD and worsening risk status. As disease status worsens, patients are more likely to present with distant metastases. It is possible that platelet count may be of more relevance than other RFs in tumor cells undergoing the ‘invasion-metastasis cascade’ [43]. Other studies have also identified hemoglobin and age as potential predictors of long responders for 2L axitinib [44]. In clinical practice, patients are generally not stratified according to IMDC or MSKCC models to guide 2L treatment decisions, and treatment guidelines do not use these measures to inform treatment selection. A population-based, multicenter study of over 1000 patients demonstrated that both the IMDC and MSKCC prognostic models could be applied to patients with RCC receiving 2L therapy, with the IMDC model showing improved prognostic performance [45].

The principal strength of this study is the analysis of a UK-based population representative of everyday clinical practice, including patients who would be ineligible for clinical trials. It also includes a cohort of patients who received both 1L sunitinib and 2L axitinib treatments, with the ability to evaluate how patients transition between 1L and 2L treatment options. However, interpretation of our findings should take into account the study limitations that are common to registry-based oncology studies including: loss of patients to follow-up; potential confounding factors; and variation in the method and timepoints used for tumor assessment (absence of RECIST reporting) [46]. Specific details regarding dose modification, compliance, treatment interruption or changes to treatment schedule were not captured in this article. Toxicities were also not able to be reliably evaluated in a retrospective fashion, and it was therefore not felt valuable to report our real-world toxicity experience due to risks of under-reporting. Furthermore, patients who responded poorly to treatment are more likely to drop out early, thereby potentially skewing some results. As the 1L treatment of RCC has drastically changed since the inception of this study, the applicability of the 1L analysis is somewhat limited, although sunitinib remains a viable 1L treatment option for patients where ICI agents are contraindicated or unavailable. Axitinib remains a 2L treatment option; however, its use in 2L will be partly dependent upon 1L treatment, which now includes axitinib combination regimens.

Conclusion

This study confirms the efficacy of 1L sunitinib and 2L axitinib in patients with RCC in a real-world setting and can provide substantial clinical benefit and may be appropriate in patients with RCC when combination treatment is not appropriate/available. PFS and OS for both 1L sunitinib and 2L axitinib were similar to those reported in the pivotal clinical trials. Predictors of PFS varied between the 1L and 2L cohorts, with only LDH as a significant predictor for both lines of treatment.

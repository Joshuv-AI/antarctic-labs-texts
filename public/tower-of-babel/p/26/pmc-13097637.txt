Bronchoscopic View and Treatment of a Nine‐Year Slowly Enlarging Chronic Lung Abscess Mimicking Lung Cancer
Authors: Noriaki Nishimura, Yusuke Nakamura, Yuri Nomura, Minori Hanato, Yuto Goto, Takatoshi Tetsuka, Tomonari Suzuki, Akihiro Takemasa, Yasuo Shimizu, Seiji Niho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTA 53‐year‐old male presented with a nine‐year slowly enlarging cystic nodule. Bronchoscopy visualised a protruding lesion, and a biopsy resulted in purulent drainage. This case highlights that lung abscess can mimic lung cancer by presenting as a slowly enlarging nodule and may be treatable using an ultra‐thin bronchoscope.Keywords: bronchoscopy, chronic lung abscess, cystic pulmonary nodule, lung cancer mimicry, slowly enlarging lung lesion

A 53‐year‐old male with a history of diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma and dermatomyositis was found to have a 10‐mm cystic pulmonary nodule in the left lower lobe. The lesion had slowly enlarged over nine years and was suspected to be lung cancer (Figure 1A–D). Flexible bronchoscopy using a BF‐MP290F (outer diameter 3.7 mm) was performed. Upon reaching the bronchial orifice communicating with the cyst, careful air insufflation allowed direct visualisation of the nodule (Figure 2A,B), which appeared smooth, soft, and highly vascularised. Forceps biopsy induced purulent drainage (Figure 2C). The lesion was subsequently irrigated with 20 mL of saline (Figure 2D) and removed (Figure 1E).

Microbiological cultures yielded only resident oral flora, and mycobacterial studies were negative. Histopathology revealed bronchial epithelium with lymphocytic infiltration and no evidence of malignancy, confirming chronic lung abscess.

This case demonstrates that a chronic lung abscess can present as a slowly enlarging cystic pulmonary nodule over a period of years, mimicking lung cancer. Pulmonary nodules are rarely diagnosed as lung abscess after prolonged follow‐up [1]. The case also highlights the importance of including indolent lung abscess in the differential diagnosis of enlarging nodules, as well as the potential therapeutic role of an ultra‐thin bronchoscope.

Lipid profiles and risk of gestational diabetes mellitus in women with isolated TPOAb positivity during early pregnancy
Authors: Yuxin Qin, Dandan Hu, Xiangguo Cong, Ying Wu, Qiong Shen, Lei Chen, Xinxin Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIsolated thyroid peroxidative antibody (TPOAb) positivity has been linked to adverse pregnancy outcomes including gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM). Elevated lipids are reported as pivotal risk factors. Current researches indicated 75 g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) in early pregnancy has limited predictive ability for GDM. The lipid profiles in isolated TPOAb positive pregnant women and their correlation with GDM were unclear. To delineate the specific lipid profile characteristics in pregnant women with isolated TPOAb positivity and to identify potential lipid biomarkers for early prediction of GDM. Euthyroid pregnant women were enrolled in the first trimester and underwent OGTT in the second trimester. Participants were divided into four groups based on TPOAb status and OGTT results. Untargeted liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC–MS) was used to identify lipid molecules. Of 88 women, 43.5% (20/46) with TPOAb positivity and 35.7% (15/42) without were diagnosed with GDM. Low-density lipoprotein (LDL) levels were positively associated with GDM risk in TPOAb positive women. LC–MS analysis identified 13 different lipid molecules significantly correlated with OGTT results. A nomogram was constructed to predict the occurrence of GDM. Elevated first-trimester LDL levels are associated with increased GDM risk in isolated TPOAb-positive women. Furthermore, PC (20:0/16:0) and TG (16:0/16:0/18:1), combined with TSH and TPOAb titers, are potential biomarkers for early prediction of GDM in this specific population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-53582-5.Keywords: Thyroid peroxidase antibody, Pregnancy, Lipids, Metabolism, Thyroid functionSubject terms: Endocrine system and metabolic diseases, Predictive markers, Diabetes, Thyroid diseases

Introduction

Autoimmune thyroiditis, as a common endocrine disease during pregnancy, significantly affects maternal and infant health. Numerous studies have proved that thyroid disorders during pregnancy are closely associated with various adverse outcomes such as preterm birth, miscarriage, gestational diabetes mellitus, gestational hypertension, and preeclampsia1,2. Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) is the most common complication during pregnancy, which can lead to fetal malformation, embryonic demise, and significantly increase the risk of developing type 2 diabetes postpartum3,4. An increased incidence of GDM has been observed in subclinical hypothyroidism and hypothyroid pregnant women cohorts5.

Of note, thyroid autoimmunity in the absence of thyroid dysfunction is more popular in pregnant women. The prevalence of isolated thyroid peroxidase antibody (TPOAb) positivity and thyroglobulin antibody positivity, which were both indicators of autoimmunity, is estimated to be approximately 5–14% and 3–18% of pregnancies, respectively6. It is now realized that higher TPOAb titers were associated with an increased risk of GDM during pregnancy7–9. A recent meta-analysis indicated no significant correlation between thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) levels and the occurrence of GDM during pregnancy, but the presence of both TPOAb positivity greatly increased the risk of GDM10. In our previous study, we found pregnant women with isolated TPOAb positivity in the first trimester had a higher risk of GDM and exhibited higher levels of lipids11.

Dyslipidemia may contribute to an increased risk of GDM through various mechanisms. The abnormal accumulation of fatty acids and dysregulation of lipid metabolism can promote the development of insulin resistance, which is the core feature of GDM12. Elevated levels of triglycerides (TG) and low-density lipoprotein (LDL), along with reduced levels of high-density lipoprotein (HDL), can exacerbate insulin resistance, thereby increasing the risk of GDM13. In addition, dyslipidemia is associated with chronic low-grade inflammation, which may further promote insulin resistance and islet β cell dysfunction by activating inflammatory mediators and cytokines, ultimately leading to GDM14. However, the complex interaction of thyroid autoimmunity, lipid disorder and GDM during pregnancy remains controversial.

By using non-targeted liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC–MS) technology, researchers have found that pregnant women with clinical and subclinical hypothyroidism exhibit altered expression of lipid molecules and also associated with the adverse pregnant or neonatal outcome15–17. For example, a lipidomics study of pregnant women with hypothyroidism showed significantly increased levels of phosphatidylcholine (PC) and phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), while sphingomyelin (SM) levels were significantly decreased16. A close correlation between serum levels of PC and newborn weight in pregnant women has also been observed15,16. In addition, researchers have found that the lipid metabolism profiles of patients with hypothyroidism and subclinical hypothyroidism exhibit striking similarities17. However, the lipid profile of isolated TPOAb positivity pregnant women and the potential correlation with pregnancy outcome have not yet fully been elucidated.

To date, no study has systematically characterized the lipidomic profile of euthyroid women with isolated TPOAb positivity in early pregnancy or investigated its predictive value for adverse pregnancy outcomes. In this study, we therefore enrolled isolated TPOAb positivity pregnant women and used LC–MS to delineate the specific lipid profile characteristics in pregnant women exhibiting isolated TPOAb positivity and to ascertain its possible prognostic implications on maternal pregnancy outcomes.

Participants and methods

Participants

The pregnant women were consecutively enrolled and followed up from May 2020 to July 2021 at the Suzhou Hospital Affiliated to Nanjing Medical University. All participants were recruited during their first trimester of pregnancy and underwent routine examination throughout their gestation period in our hospital. The demographics data were collected for each participant, including age, pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI), gestational weeks, gravida, parity as well as biochemical indicators such as TG, total cholesterol (TC), LDL, HDL, hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine (Cr), and uric acid (UA) levels.

According to the screening of thyroid disorder in pregnancy in China, thyroid function parameters such as TSH, free thyroxine (FT4) and TPOAb were measured in 10–13 gestational weeks. A TSH upper reference range of 4.0μIU/ml was used in our center according to the clinical guidelines nowadays6. To reduce the potential impact of thyroid hormone (TH), all participants had euthyroid which was defined as FT4 levels within the range of 9.01 to 19.04 pmol/L and TSH levels within the range of 0.35 and 2.5 μIU/ml. TPOAb positivity is defined as TPOAb titer ≥ 12 IU/ml, which is the manufacturer’s recommended upper limit of normal for the chemiluminescence immunoassay (Architect i2000SR, Abbott Laboratories, Chicago, IL) used in our center.

Pregnant women were excluded if they were over 35 years of age; had no complete pregnancy data; diagnosed with diabetes before pregnancy or during early pregnancy; used drugs that affect thyroid function; used drugs that affect blood glucose; used drugs that affect lipid metabolism; had chronic systemic diseases; had a recent infection.

All subjects signed informed consent before the enrollment. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Suzhou Hospital Affiliated to Nanjing Medical University.

Laboratory measurement

Serum levels of TSH, FT4, and TPOAb were measured using automated chemiluminescent immunoassays (Architect i2000SR; Abbott Laboratories, Chicago, IL) in the department of nuclear medicine of Suzhou Hospital Affiliated to Nanjing Medical University.

All participants underwent a 75 g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) experiment at 20–24 weeks based on routine clinical practice at our center. The diagnosis of GDM was made based on the criteria recommended by the American Diabetes Association18: fasting blood glucose (FBG) ≥ 5.1 mmol/L or 1 h blood glucose (1hBG) ≥ 10.0 mmol/L or 2 h blood glucose (2hBG) ≥ 8.5 mmol/L. Those who did not meet these criteria, with FBG < 5.1 mmol/L, 1hBG < 10.0 mmol/L, and 2hBG < 8.5 mmol/L, were classified as non-GDM.

Specimen collection and UHPLC-MS analysis

During the routine antenatal examination conducted in the first trimester, venous blood samples were collected from all participants in a fasting state. Serum samples were frozen in − 80 °C refrigerator before centralized measurement. Lipid molecules were extracted from the samples and mixed to prepare quality control (QC) samples. Data were collected in positive and negative ion scanning modes using UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X Vanquish Horizon system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The raw data were imported into LipidSearch (Thermo Fisher Scientific) for peak detection, alignment and identification. Lipid molecules that were not observed in 80% of the samples were deleted in the final analysis. At the same time, variables with relative standard deviation > 30% of QC samples were removed. Orthogonal least partial square discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was performed using the R package ropls (10.1021/acs.jproteome.5b00354). The metabolites with variable importance (VIP) > 1 in OPLS-DA model and P value < 0.05 in student’s t test were identified as differential metabolites.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R (R 4.3.3) software and GraphPad Prism 9.5.1. P values < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Variables were expressed as means ± standard deviations or medians (interquartile ranges). According to TPOAb titers and the results of 75 g OGTT, participants were categorized into four groups: GDM with TPOAb positivity (TGDM), non-GDM with TPOAb positivity (TNGDM), GDM with TPOAb negativity (HGDM), and non-GDM with TPOAb negativity (HNGDM). Nonparametric tests were employed to compare clinical parameters across subgroups.

To further evaluate the association of LDL level and GDM in euthyroid pregnant women with positive TPOAb, LDL levels were classified into tertiles: low level (0.98–2.09 mmol/L), medium level (2.10–2.63 mmol/L), and high level (2.64–4.30 mmol/L). A logistic regression model was utilized to determine the association between the levels of LDL levels and the likelihood of developing GDM. P values for trends were calculated using the tertiles median value as a quasi-continuous variable in the model. Potential confounding factors were adjusted for in three logistic regression models.

Utilizing database search (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), http://www.genome.jp/kegg/), we conducted metabolic enrichment and pathway analysis to summarize the differential metabolites between TGDM (TPOAb+/GDM+) group and TNGDM (TPOAb+/GDM-) group, subsequently mapping them onto their respective biochemical pathways. Using ggsankey of R packages (https://github.com/davidsjoberg/ggsankey) were to identify statistically significant enrichment way. Using ggplot2 of R package (https://ggplot2.tidyverse.org) was to draw the heatmap according to the correlation between lipid molecules and OGTT results by Spearman’s analysis. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to analyze the predictive efficacy of relevant lipid molecules and lipid molecules combined with thyroid function on GDM. Rms of R packages (https://CRAN.R-project.org/package=rms) was used to establish the nomogram model of selected lipid molecules and thyroid function prediction of GDM. Internal verification was conducted on the nomogram model and calculate the C index. ROC curve, calibration curve and decision curve were used to evaluate the predictive performance of the nomogram model.

Results

Demographics of the study population

A total of 100 pregnant women were enrolled in our study. After exclusion of uncomplete data, 88 pregnant women were analyzed in the study, including 46 isolated TPOAb positive women and 42 TPOAb negative women. All had complete clinical data and underwent the 75 g OGTT during the second trimester. According to OGTT results, 43.5% (20/46) of isolated positive TPOAb group and 35.7% (15/42) of TPOAb negativity group were diagnosed of GDM.

No significant disparities were observed among the four groups in terms of age, gestational week, pre-pregnancy BMI, HbA1c, BUN, Cr, UA, TG, HDL, TSH, FT4. However, significant variations were found in FBG, TC and LDL levels across the groups (P < 0.05). Accordingly, we found that TGDM group exhibited significantly elevated TC and LDL levels compared to the TNGDM group, whereas the TNGDM group had significantly lower TC and LDL levels than the HNGDM (TPOAb-/GDM-) group (showed in Table 1). No significant differences in TC and LDL levels were found between the HGDM (TPOAb-/GDM+) and HNGDM groups (showed in Table 1).

Associations between dyslipidemia and GDM

Correlation analysis showed that LDL were significantly and positively correlated with both 1hBG (r = 0.530, P < 0.001) and 2hBG (r = 0.691, P < 0.001) (showed in Fig. 1) in TPOAb positive pregnant women. With the increase of LDL, a significant increase of postprandial blood glucose was found. Although TC was also significantly associated with both 1hBG and 2hBG (showed in supplemental Fig. 1a), TC contains varieties of different cholesterol components. There was no correlation between HDL and OGTT results (showed in supplemental Fig. 1b). There was also no correlation between TG and OGTT results (showed in supplemental Fig. 1c).

Correlation analysis between LDL and OGTT results. LDL, Low-density lipoprotein; OGTT, Oral glucose tolerance test; FBG, Fasting blood glucose; 1hBG, 1 h blood glucose; 2hBG, 2 h blood glucose.

To further investigate the association of LDL and risk of GDM in the TPOAb positive group, LDL was converted into tertiles, and logistic regression model was constructed. After adjusting for potential confounders such as TG, age, pre-pregnancy BMI, parity, TSH, and FT418, and although a family history of diabetes is a risk factor for GDM, it was not included in the multivariable model due to incomplete documentation in some patient records from this retrospective cohort (to avoid bias and sample size reduction from missing data), there was a significant positive trend between LDL levels and the risk of GDM (P for trend < 0.001), and the odds ratio (OR) for GDM in positive-TPOAb pregnant women with high LDL levels are 124.81 (95% confidence interval (CI): 5.59—2785.27) times higher than in those with low LDL levels (P = 0.002) (showed in Table 2). In contrast, we did not find a similar trend in the TPOAb negative group (showed in supplemental Table 1).

The model showed good classification and prediction ability (showed in Fig. 2a, c). The substitution test showed that R2 = (0, 0.9235), Q2 = (0, − 0.7386) in the cationic model, and R2 = (0, 0.8747), Q2 = (0, − 1.1467) in the anionic model, indicating that the model is stable and reliable (showed in Fig. 2b, d). Volcano plots were drawn to visualize the difference in lipid profiles between the two groups. Compared with TNGDM group, 92 lipid molecules in TGDM group were significantly up-regulated, and 97 lipid molecules were significantly down-regulated (showed in Fig. 2e).

ROC curve

According to the correlation analysis, there were 13 lipid molecules significantly correlated with OGTT results. ROC curve analysis was used to verify the diagnostic accuracy and potential predict ability of GDM. Results showed that the areas under curve (AUC) of PC(16:1/16:1), PC(20:0/16:0), DG(18:0/20:4), Cer(d18:1/20:0), Cer(d18:1/25:0), TG(18:0/18:0/18:1), TG(12:0/12:0/12:0), TG(18:1/20:4/20:4), TG(16:0/16:0/18:1) were greater than 0.7. Among them, PC(20:0/16:0) and TG(16:0/16:0/18:1) had significant differences between isolated TPOAb positive group and healthy control group. The combined AUC of the two molecules is 0.844 (95% CI 0.733–0.955). The combined AUC of these two molecules and TPOAb, TSH, FT4 was 0.861 (95% CI 0.758–0.969) (showed in Fig. 5).

Nomograph

A nomogram model for GDM in isolated TPOAb positive pregnant women in early pregnancy (showed in Fig. 6a) was then conducted, using TPOAb, FT4, TSH, PC(20:0/16:0), TG(16:0/16:0/18:1) as independent variables. And the total score was 56–156, corresponding to the probability of GDM was 0.01–0.99. The area under the ROC curve of this model was 0.862 (95% CI 0.756–0.968) (showed in Fig. 6b). 50% random data as a validation set for internal validation. The area under the ROC curve of the validation set was 0.8258 (95% CI 0.6529–0.9986) (showed in Fig. 6c). Decision curve analysis showed that the model had higher net benefit in predicting GDM in isolated TPOAb positive women during early pregnancy (showed in Fig. 6d). This model was proved to be effective in clinical prediction. The calibration curve showed a good agreement between the predicted and actual values (showed in Fig. 6e).

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the association between lipids and GDM in pregnant women with isolated positive TPOAb in early pregnancy. Our findings indicate that elevated LDL levels in the TPOAb positive pregnant women during their first trimester are significantly associated with an increased risk of GDM. Even after adjusting for potential confounding factors such as age, pre-pregnancy BMI, parity, TSH, FT4 and TG, a significant positive trend persisted between LDL level and GDM risk. Moreover, we found GDM group of isolated positive TPOAb pregnant women displayed significantly different lipid profile compared with non-GDM group and lipids might serve as predictor of GDM in pregnant women.

Thyroid hormones are essential for growth and metabolism. The demand for thyroid hormones increases during pregnancy especially in the first trimester. Therefore, thyroid disorders that occur during pregnancy may lead to adverse pregnancy and neonatal outcome. Autoimmune thyroiditis, characterized with positive TPOAb and/or TgAb, has been proved to be associated with increased risk of GDM, gestational hypertension, preeclampsia/eclampsia and so on19. Isolated positive TPOAb is a mild form of autoimmune thyroiditis, manifested with positive TPOAb and normal thyroid function, which is more prevalent and have also been associated with a higher risk of adverse maternal outcome such as GDM9,11,20,21.

In our study, we found that the prevalence of GDM was higher in pregnant women with isolated positive TPOAb. Due to small sample size, no statistic difference was found between groups. However, GDM pregnant women exhibited higher lipid level which was consistent with other studies22. It was demonstrated that elevated TG and LDL levels in early pregnancy have been shown to be associated with a heightened risk of GDM22–24. In our study, we didn’t find significant difference of TG in the first trimester across 4 groups, and TG showed no correlation with OGTT results in TPOAb positive group. However, we found postprandial blood glucose increased with the increased of LDL level. And LDL remains an independent risk factor for the risk of GDM in pregnant women after adjusting for the influences of TG. While LDL remained an independent risk factor for GDM after adjustment for confounders, the odds ratios observed in the logistic regression model were extremely high with wide confidence intervals. This is likely attributable to the small sample size and limited number of GDM events, which can lead to unstable parameter estimates. Therefore, the magnitude of the association should be interpreted with caution, and validation in larger cohorts is essential.

Dyslipidemia stands as a prominent risk factor among patients with GDM, with its occurrence during pregnancy potentially augmenting the likelihood of GDM via diverse mechanisms25. The relationship between thyroid hormones and lipid metabolism disorders is closely related26. However, it is still unclear whether thyroid hormones affect lipid metabolism or lipid metabolism affects changes in thyroid hormones according to the latest cohort study27. The emergence of lipidomics study using LC–MS provides conditions for exploring the differential lipid metabolism in pregnant women with thyroid disorder17. Studies have shown that higher plasma concentrations of glycerol phospholipids, especially TG(48:1), TG(51:1), and low saturated phospholipids PC(32:1) in the first trimester are associated with a higher risk of GDM28. Furse et al. found that 20 triglycerides and phospholipids in the early second trimester (15–18 weeks of gestation) were significantly associated with the risk of GDM in 831 obese women29. In this study, a total of 92 lipid molecules were significantly up-regulated and 97 lipid molecules were significantly down-regulated in GDM group during early pregnancy. The differential lipid molecules enriched in this study belong to glycerophospholipids, glycerides, and sphingomyelin, which are similar to the lipid species found in previous studies30.

Glycerophospholipids (GPs) constitute the primary lipid constituent of cellular membranes31. Among mammals, phosphatidylcholine (PC) and phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) are the most prevalent phospholipids, with established associations to insulin resistance and a spectrum of metabolic disorders, including obesity and diabetes32. Several studies have explored the association between GPs and GDM, but the results of these studies have been inconsistent. An analysis of 682 pregnant women with an average age of 31.4 years in the Netherlands found that higher BMI was associated with altered metabolic profiles in early pregnancy, particularly with significantly increased levels of phospholipid metabolites33. Rahman et al. found that higher concentrations of PCs (30:1, 32:1, 34:1, 38:3) and PE (36:1) measured at 10 to 14 weeks gestation were associated with an increased risk of GDM30. Furse et al. found that higher PC (38:5) and lower PCs (35:2, 40:7, 40:10) and LPCs (16:0, 18:1) measured at 15–18 weeks gestation were associated with an increased risk of GDM29. In our study, PC(16:1/16:1) and PC(20:0/16:0) in GDM group were both higher than those in non-GDM group, and were positively correlated with OGTT results. GPS affect insulin action through various ways. As a key component of the phospholipid bilayer of cell membrane, the structural changes of glycerophospholipids may affect the function of insulin receptors and thus affect insulin signaling34. In animal studies, it was found that by knocking down Cholesteryl Ester Transfer Protein, which is the rate-limiting enzyme in the main pathway of phosphatidylcholine production, interfering with phosphatidylcholine synthesis, insulin sensitivity was improved in mice treated with a high-fat diet35. Furthermore, PCs, including its metabolic byproducts like lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC), may also contribute to the regulation of inflammation by influencing the production and secretion of inflammatory mediators. April Rees et al. discovered that the ratio of PC/LPC served as a valuable indicator to assess the extent of inflammatory response during pregnancy, thereby offering a potential means to prevent GDM36.

Glycerolipids (GLs) are a class of ester compounds produced by esterification of glycerol and fatty acids37. According to the number of fatty acylglycerols contained in glycerol, it includes Monoacylglycerols (MG), Diacylglycerols (DG), Triacylglycerols (TG) and so on. These compounds play important roles in the composition of cellular membranes, as well as in energy metabolism and signaling pathways38. The presence of glycerides, such as DGs and short-chain, saturated/low-unsaturated TGs, has been found to be positively correlated with insulin resistance, Type 2 Diabetes, and cardiovascular diseases in non-pregnant individuals39. High concentration of TGs in plasma, especially TG(48:1) and TG(51:1), were also found in pregnant people during the second trimester28. Furse et al. also found significant increases in the abundance of TG (51:5) and (53:4) in pregnant women with GDM29. In this study, it was observed that TG(12:0/12:0/12:0), TG(16:0/16:0/18:1), TG(18:0/18:1) and DG(18:0/20:4) were significantly up-regulated in the GDM group and were positively correlated with the results of OGTT. The molecular mechanism of GLs in GDM remains unexplored. It was reported that TG metabolites can contribute to obesity-associated chronic inflammation by engaging in direct interactions with signaling kinases located on cellular receptors or membranes, thereby modulating inflammatory pathways40. In addition, TG metabolites can also mediate the development of insulin resistance by activating protein kinase C41.

In addition to glycerophospholipids and glycerolipids, sphingolipid metabolism may also contribute to GDM risk. Ceramide, a central metabolite in the sphingolipid pathway, has been shown to promote insulin resistance by inhibiting AKT signaling, inducing endoplasmic reticulum stress, and activating the NLRP3 inflammasome42. In animal models, inhibition of ceramide synthesis improves insulin sensitivity43. In the present study, we observed significant differences in several ceramide molecules between the GDM and non-GDM groups, and these differential ceramides were enriched in pathways such as insulin resistance and sphingolipid signaling. However, no significant differences in ceramide levels were detected between the TPOAb-positive and TPOAb-negative groups in our previous study, suggesting that the observed alterations in ceramide metabolism may be more closely related to GDM status rather than directly to thyroid autoimmunity.

Additionally, we found more pathways were upregulated in TGDM group compared to TNGDM group including (1) immune related pathways such as Th17 cell differentiation, Th1 and Th2 cell differentiation, PD-L1 expression and PD-1 checkpoint pathway in cancer, Natural killer cell mediated cytotoxicity, NF-kappa B signaling pathway; (2) lipid metabolism pathway such as Sphingolipid signaling pathway, Regulation of lipolysis in adipocytes, Glycerophospholipid metabolism, Fat digestion and absorption, Adipocytokine signaling pathway; (3) glucose metabolism pathways such as Insulin resistance, Diabetic cardiomyopathy, Choline metabolism in cancer, AGE-RAGE signaling pathway in diabetic complications and so on. These observations may reveal specific differences in metabolic activities under different diabetic conditions, providing a new perspective for delving into the biological mechanisms of these diseases.

Furtherly, pathway analysis revealed significant changes in metabolic pathways such as Insulin resistance, Glycerophospholipid metabolism, Adipokine signaling pathway based on KEGG. In particular, the pathway of insulin resistance had the highest concentration of lipid molecules. Correlation analysis also showed that OGTT results were associated with most of the enriched lipid differential lipid molecules. Therefore, it is speculated that differential lipid molecules may predict the occurrence and development of GDM. Notably, one study found that in overweight or obese pregnant women, elevated fasting blood glucose and 1-h OGTT blood glucose concentrations during early pregnancy were markers of increased risk of GDM; however, their ability to predict GDM is relatively weak44. This highlights the need for more robust biomarkers to improve prediction accuracy during early pregnancy. PC(20:0/16:0), TG(16:0/16:18:1), TPOAb and TSH combined predicted GDM more effectively than lipid molecules alone. Therefore, we constructed a nomogram model of PC(20:0/16:0), TG(16:0/16:0/18:1), TPOAb and TSH as independent variables to predict GDM. Notably, the LC–MS technology used in this study is currently costly, technically demanding, and has limited throughput, making it unsuitable for routine clinical practice at present. However, the candidate molecules may provide potential targets for developing targeted assays, which could be more feasible and cost-effective if validated in larger populations.

Our study has several strengths. Firstly, we focused on a unique population of pregnant women with isolated TPOAb positivity in early pregnancy, providing insights into the lipid profile in this specific group. Secondly, all participants had FT4 within the normal range and TSH controlled below 2.5 mIU/L, minimizing the potential confounding effects of thyroid hormones on lipid metabolism. Finally, we found that a group of lipid biomarkers in the first trimester predicted the occurrence of GDM in a special population, earlier than OGTT at 24–28 weeks of pregnancy, providing a possibility for the prevention of GDM in advance.

However, there are some limitations to our study. Firstly, the sample size of our study is admittedly limited, which may restrict the generalizability of our findings and also raises a potential risk of overfitting in the predictive model. Secondly, the observational design of our study limits our ability to establish a causal link between high LDL levels and the risk of GDM. Lastly, the lipid biomarkers we have identified through our screening process have yet to undergo external validation, a crucial step in confirming their robustness and applicability in broader contexts.

Conclusion

Our study suggests that elevated LDL levels in isolated TPOAb positive women during their early pregnancy are associated with an increased risk of GDM. These pregnant women diagnosed with GDM showed significant differences in their lipid profiles compared to non-GDM women. Notably, PC(20:0/16:0) and TG(16:0/16:0/18:1) stand out as potential biomarkers and may serve as a predictive tool for GDM in TPOAb positive pregnant women during the first trimester when combined with thyroid function evaluations.

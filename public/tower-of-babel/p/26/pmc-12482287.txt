Automatic measurement of Caton index on knee X-ray images using a key point detection model
Authors: Ting Li, Nadeer M Gharaibeh, Gang Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo explore the feasibility of the You Only Look Once (YOLO) algorithm in the measurement of Carton index.Methods1156 knee X-ray images were collected from two centers (960 and 196). Five key points at patella and tibia on knee X-ray were labeled using the software of Labelme. YOLO11 pose models (including YOLO11n, YOLO11m and YOLO11x) were refined by labeled images from center A, and was then used to detect keypoints on images from center B. A line was the line between anterior edge of the tibial plateau and the lower pole of patellar articular surface, and B line was patellar articular surface. Carton index (A/B ratio) of 196 cases was obtained by senior radiologist, junior radiologist and YOLO respectively. The Bland Altman plot, Pearson Correlation test, Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC) were used to evaluate the agreement in measurement.ResultsCarton index of 196 images were automatically obtained with YOLO11n-pose, YOLO11m-pose and YOLO11x-pose. The ICC between senior and junior radiologists was 0.89. Pearson correlation coefficients were 0.23, 0.43 and 0.73 respectively for YOLO11n, YOLO11m and YOLO11x. ICC were 0.23, 0.42 and 0.72 respectively for YOLO11n, YOLO11m and YOLO11x. MAE were 0.20, 0.17 and 0.10 respectively for YOLO11n, YOLO11m and YOLO11x.ConclusionsYOLO11x-pose model shows promise in the automatic measurement of Carton index on the knee X-ray image.Keywords: YOLO11-pose, Keypoint detection, Computer vision, Deep learning, Conventional radiography

Introduction

X-ray measurement methods for patella alta include Insall Salvati (IS) ratio, Blackburn Peel (BP) ratio, Caton Deschamps (CD) ratio, and modified Insall Salvati (MIS) ratio [1], [2], [3], [4]. Because of the diverse methods for the evaluation of patellar alta, the measurement and calculation are cumbersome and time-consuming [5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10]. This increases the demand for artificial intelligence (AI) based diagnostic systems, which may optimize the diagnostic process by providing faster and more accurate results.

The You Only Look Once (YOLO) model utilizes a fully convolutional neural network (CNN) to process image, and is suitable for tasks such as object detection, image classification, segmentation, and keypoint detection [11], [12], [13]. YOLO-pose is a keypoint detection model used for human pose recognition, and has been successfully applied to measurement of critical shoulder angle (CSA), lateral acromion angle (LAA), and acromion index parameters in shoulder X-ray images [14], and localization and direction estimation of pedicle screws in spinal fusion surgery [15], [16]. However, to the best of the author’s knowledge, YOLO11-pose model has not been used to detect keypoints regarding patella alta on the knee X-ray image. Carton index is the ratio of the distance of the anterior edge of the tibia and the lower pole of the patellar articular surface (A line) and the length of the patellar articular surface (B line) on lateral X-rays of the knee joint. The study aimed to 1) investigate the feasibility of YOLO11 pose models in detecting key points on lateral knee radiograph and 2) evaluate the agreement of Carton index between YOLO models and senior radiologist.

Methods and materials

The retrospective study has been approved by the Institutional Review Board of the hospital (TJH-LL-20231123). The inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) patients felt friction pain around patella during activities, especially when going up and down stairs or performing squatting, and were suspected of patella alta; 2) patients with knee X-ray examination. The exclusion criteria were as follows: 1) the history of patella fracture; 2) metal internal fixations in the patella; 3) lack of lateral patellar radiograph; 4) non standard knee joint X-ray lateral position; 5) lower pole of the patellar articular surface with arc shape; 6) inadequate image quality. At final, 960 patients were collected from center A, and 196 patients were collected from center B. Fig. 1 is the flow diagram of patients inclusion and exclusion.

The flow diagram of inclusion and exclusion of patients.

Data acquisition

A senior muscle skeletal radiologist with more than ten years’ experience performed the measurement of Carton index for center B cases in the medical picture archiving and communication system (PACs). His measurement served as the reference standard in the study. Then the knee images on lateral plane were copied from the PACs of center A and B and saved as JPG format images after anonymization of patients’ information.

960 images from Center A were used for model developing, while 196 images from Center B were used for external test. A junior musculoskeletal radiologist with 5 years of clinical experience labeled images from center A using the software of labelme (https://github.com/CSAILVision/LabelMeAnnotationTool). He was asked to draw a rectangular box to cover the patella, and then mark five key points within the box: the lower pole of the anterior edge of patella (label 0), the upper pole of the anterior edge of patella (label 1), the upper pole of patellar articular surface (label 2), the lower pole of patellar articular surface (label 3), and the anterior edge of the tibia plateau (label 4). The files ended with 'json' and were then converted to txt files for the purpose of model training. The study trained the YOLO11n-pose, YOLO11m-pose, and YOLO11x-pose models based on the labeled images by using five fold cross validation. 100 epochs of training were conducted on the NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 (Fig. 2).

The training process of the YOLO11 model. The first five graphs depict the decrease in kinds of losses with epochs. The next four graphs depict the increase of precision and recall with epochs. The last four graphs depict the increase of mean average precision with epochs.

The refined YOLO11-pose models were then tested with the 196 images from center B. First, the upper pole of patellar articular surface, the lower pole of patellar articular surface and the anterior edge of the tibia plateau were automatically located on each image with YOLO models. Then, the ratio of the line between the anterior edge of the tibia and the lower pole of the patellar articular surface (A line) and the line of patellar articular surface (B line) were automatically calculated. The ratio is considered as Carton index in the study.

A junior radiologist with 5 years’ experience was asked to measure the length of A line and B line on each image with the software of Adobe Photoshop, and to calculate A/B (Carton index).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analysis were performed using Python. The inter-reader agreement in the measurement of Carton index between senior and junior radiologists was evaluated by the Intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC). ICC, pearson correlation coefficient and mean absolute error (MAE) were calculated for the comparison of automatic and manual measurement. Bland Altman maps were plotted for YOLO11n, YOLO11m and YOLO11x respectively.

Results

The five key points were all successfully detected on each image from center B (Fig. 3). The Carton index of 196 knee images from center B were successfully obtained with YOLO11n-pose, YOLO11m-pose and YOLO11x-pose models. Both senior and junior radiologists performed the manual calculation of A/B for these images. The ICC was 0.89 for the comparison of the two radiologists’ Carton index.

Example of using the refined YOLO11 pose model to predict images.

The performance of YOLO11n for Carton index

The mean time for the measurement of Carton index with YOLO11n model was 3.01 ± 1.34 ms. The Pearson correlation coefficient, ICC and MAE were 0.23[95 % confidence interval 0.0928, 0.3586], 0.23 and 0.20 respectively.

The performance of YOLO11m for Carton index

The mean time for the automatic measurement with YOLO11m model was 5.21 ± 3.64 ms. The Pearson correlation coefficient, ICC and MAE were 0.43[0.3084, 0.5377], 0.42 and 0.17 respectively.

The performance of YOLO11x for Carton index

The mean time for the measurement of Carton index with YOLO11x model was 7.44 ± 4.22 ms. The Pearson correlation coefficient, ICC and MAE were 0.73[0.6571, 0.7894], 0.72 and 0.10 respectively.

Discussion

This study explores the feasibility of using YOLO models to identify key points and automatically measure Carton index. Main findings are as follows: 1) the YOLO models can predict the five anatomical sites - the outer superior pole, inner superior pole, inner inferior pole, outer inferior pole of patella, and anterior edge of the tibial plateau; 2) the automatic measurement of Carton index with YOLO11x had moderate agreement with reference standard.

YOLO11-pose is a key point detection algorithm based on deep learning, which uses convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to extract features from images, and then predicts the location of key points through a regression algorithm [16], [17], [18], [19], [20]. YOLO11-pose adopts an efficient neural network structure and optimization algorithm, which can complete a large number of computing tasks in a short time [11], [21]. The open-source code and modular design of YOLO11-pose make it easy for users to extend and improve it to suit different application scenarios. The quality of the datasets directly affects the effectiveness and accuracy of the training. It is important to label key points in an image so that the model can be trained to identify and predict the location of those key points. YOLO11-pose requires a dataset in a special YOLO format. Therefore, we need to convert the annotated dataset to YOLO format.

The X ray image labeling is very important in the study. All five points should be within the rectangle box, and the label sequence should be consistent in all cases. For example, label 0 was given to anterior inferior point of petallar, and label 1 to anterior superior point of petallar, and label 2 to posterior superior point of petallar. In each case, label 4 should be given to anterior point of tibia plateau. To train a YOLO-pose model, GPU is generally required. As the initial YOLO pose model was trained based on 17 key points, it could not directly refined by the current 5-point data. Thus the number of keypoints should be rewritten from 17 to 5 in the config file (coco8-pose.yaml) before model developing.

The initial prediction by YOLO11 pose model were only images with key points. However, the coordinates of these points are really saved by the model. Thus, we could use the coordinates for distances ratio calculation. For example, result[0].keypoints.xy[0][0][0].cpu().numpy() was used by us in the study to get the coordinates. It is available to calculate distance if the coordinates of two points were given, but the real distance could not be determined on a JPG format image. Fortunately, Carton index is the ratio of two distances.

In the study, YOLO11n had poor agreement with reference standard in measuring Carton index. A line and B line share a point: the lower pole of patellar articular surface. This localization of this point was more important than other points, because it influences the length of both A and B line. Unfortunately, YOLO11n seemed to perform worse in the prediction of lower pole of patelar articular surface. In fact, in some cases, it is difficult for radiologists to determine the lower pole of patellar articular surface, because arc shape without a clear inflection point is observed. YOLO model may make mistakes in such cases. If the prediction point of lower pole of patelar articular surface move towards head or foot, the length of A line and B line changes in opposite directions, magnifying the change of Carton index.

In the study, YOLO11m had poor to moderate agreement with reference standard in measuring Carton index. That is because YOLO11m had more parameters than YOLO11n. YOLO11x with the greatest number of parameters among YOLO models had moderate agreement with reference standard in measuring Carton index. YOLO11x seemed better at predicting the lower pole of patellar articular surface. Although YOLO11x required more time than YOLO11n and YOLO11m, the time spent is in the millisecond range, far below the human second range.

Our study has limitations. Firstly, it is a small sample study. More cases should be included in future. Secondly, we did not use other models such as two-stage Region-based Convolutional Neural Networks (RCNN). The diagnostic performance of multiple models can be compared in the future. Third, YOLO models could not obtain the real distance of A or B line from JPG format image. A model that could deal with DICOM format data is urgently required.

Conclusion

The YOLO11x-pose model shows promise in automatic measurement of Carton index on X-ray images of knee.

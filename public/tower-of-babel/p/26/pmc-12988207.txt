Quantum feedback-enhanced discord in T-shaped plasmonic waveguides with embedded cavity
Authors: Hossein Sadeghi, Mehdi Mirzaee, Rezvan Zarei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This theoretical study presents a hybrid quantum system that combines the geometry of a T-shaped plasmonic waveguide with an embedded common cavity and applies quantum feedback control to enhance and preserve quantum discord between two quantum dots (QDs). Building upon previous work on entanglement generation in T-shaped waveguides and feedback-enhanced discord in V-shaped waveguides, we demonstrate that the T-shape geometry with its specific boundary conditions, coupled with active feedback, offers superior control over quantum correlations. The system consists of two QDs placed at strategic positions within the waveguide: one at the node and the other at the end of the finite arm, both embedded in a common cavity. We derive the system’s dynamics using a real-space Hamiltonian approach and solve the time-independent Schrödinger equation to obtain scattering amplitudes. Quantum discord is then calculated for the two-QD subsystem. Our results show that by applying symmetric quantum feedback based on Wiseman-Milburn formalism, we can significantly enhance the steady-state quantum discord, achieving values up to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal {D}_{ss} \approx 0.38$$\end{document} for Werner states under optimal parameter conditions. We identify three distinct decay regimes for discord and demonstrate that parameters such as the phase accumulation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha =kL$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k=\omega /v_g$$\end{document} is the plasmon wavevector) along the finite arm, providing cavity-QD couplings (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_1$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_2$$\end{document}), detunings (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta _j$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta _c$$\end{document}), and dipole-dipole interaction strength (f) provide multiple control knobs for tuning quantum correlations. This work provides a comprehensive framework for designing actively controlled nanophotonic quantum devices that leverage both plasmonic field enhancement and quantum feedback for robust quantum information processing at room temperature.

Introduction

Quantum correlations, encompassing both entanglement and the more general quantum discord, are fundamental resources for quantum technologies, including computing, communication, and sensing1–3. While entanglement has been extensively studied, quantum discord offers the advantage of persisting even in separable states, making it potentially more robust in noisy environments4. Preserving these correlations in practical, nanoscale systems remains a significant challenge due to decoherence.

Plasmonic waveguides have emerged as a promising platform for strong light-matter interaction at the nanoscale, enabling efficient coupling between quantum emitters5,6. Specifically, T-shaped plasmonic waveguides have been shown to effectively mediate entanglement between quantum dots (QDs)7. The geometry, featuring an infinite arm and a finite arm, creates a node that traps surface plasmons, enhancing interaction with spatially separated QDs. When combined with an embedded cavity, this system offers additional control parameters and increased coupling strength.

Compared to the V-shaped waveguide geometry recently explored for discord enhancement via feedback8, the T-shaped waveguide offers distinct advantages and control mechanisms. The V-shape primarily relies on interference between two symmetric arms, whereas the T-shape introduces an inherent asymmetry between an infinite arm and a finite arm of length L. This creates a well-defined node at the junction and allows for precise phase accumulation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha = kL$$\end{document}) along the finite arm, providing an additional and highly tunable parameter for controlling emitter-emitter interactions via boundary reflections. We therefore expect the T-shape to offer a richer parameter space for manipulating and protecting quantum correlations through engineered interference. Furthermore, while entanglement is a critical resource, quantum discord has been identified as an advantageous correlation in certain quantum information tasks even in the absence of entanglement. For instance, discord is the resource enabling quantum speedup in deterministic quantum computation with one qubit (DQC1)4,9 and can improve the fidelity of remote state preparation protocols9. Preserving discord at the nanoscale is thus not only a fundamental challenge but also a practical step towards realizing these protocols in integrated plasmonic circuits.

The T-shaped geometry, featuring an infinite arm and a finite arm of length \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L$$\end{document}, provides an inherent asymmetry that creates a well-defined node at the junction. This allows for precise control over the phase accumulation \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha = kL$$\end{document} along the finite arm, which acts as an additional tunable parameter for controlling emitter-emitter interactions via boundary reflections. In contrast, the V-shaped waveguide consists of two symmetric finite arms and primarily relies on interference between them, lacking a dedicated phase-tuning parameter like \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document}. Therefore, the T-shape offers a richer parameter space for engineering quantum correlations through controlled interference.

Quantum discord, as a measure of non-classical correlations beyond entanglement, has been identified as a useful resource in several quantum information tasks. For example, discord enables quantum speedup in deterministic quantum computation with one qubit (DQC1)4,9 and can enhance the fidelity of remote state preparation and quantum key distribution protocols in noisy environments9. Its robustness in separable states makes it particularly attractive for practical nanophotonic systems operating at room temperature, where decoherence is significant.

Quantum feedback control provides an active method to combat decoherence by continuously monitoring and correcting the system’s state10,11. Recent work has demonstrated that feedback can significantly enhance quantum discord in V-shaped plasmonic waveguides8,12. The feedback mechanism confines the system’s density matrix into a protected subspace, suppressing decoherence channels.

Our work synthesizes and advances two distinct lines of research: (a) building upon the T-shaped waveguide for passive entanglement generation7, and (b) extending the concept of feedback-enhanced discord from V-shaped waveguides8,12. We introduce active quantum feedback into the T-cavity geometry, transforming it from a passive correlator into an actively stabilized preserver of quantum discord. This hybrid approach leverages the unique phase-control (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha =kL$$\end{document}) of the T-shape and the enhanced cooperativity of the cavity, combined with the robustness of feedback, creating a system with superior and multifaceted control over quantum correlations. To this end, we propose a hybrid system where two QDs are coupled via a T-shaped plasmonic waveguide and embedded in a common cavity, and then apply quantum feedback control to enhance quantum discord. We develop a comprehensive theoretical model, analyze the system’s dynamics, and identify optimal operating conditions.

The paper is organised as follows: In Section "Theoretical framework", we present the theoretical framework, detailing the Hamiltonian of the T-shaped plasmonic waveguide with an embedded cavity, the scattering state solution, the calculation of quantum discord, and the formalism for quantum feedback control. Section "Results and discussion" contains our results and discussion, where we analyse the natural decay of discord, its dependence on key system parameters, the significant enhancement achieved via symmetric feedback schemes, and the identification of optimal operating conditions. We conclude in Section "Summary and conclusion" with a summary of our findings and their implications for future nanoscale quantum devices.

Summary and conclusion

We have proposed and theoretically analyzed a hybrid nanophotonic system that integrates a T-shaped plasmonic waveguide, an embedded common cavity containing two quantum dots, and quantum feedback control to enhance and preserve quantum discord. By solving the scattering problem in the T-shape geometry and applying the Wiseman-Milburn feedback formalism, we have demonstrated that this system offers unprecedented control over quantum correlations. The T-shape provides a phase parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha = kL$$\end{document} that enables switching of discord peaks, while the cavity introduces additional coupling and detuning controls. Quantum feedback, particularly a correlated two-qubit scheme (F2), actively suppresses decoherence channels, confining the state to a protected subspace and boosting the steady-state discord to a value of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal {D}_{ss} \approx 0.38$$\end{document}. From an experimental perspective, this scheme could be realized using silver nanowires fabricated on a SiO\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$_2$$\end{document} substrate coupled to semiconductor quantum dots (e.g., CdSe or InAs) emitting in the visible/near-infrared range. Typical Purcell factors for such systems can range from 10 to 100, placing the coupling strengths \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J_j \sim 1-10\gamma _0$$\end{document} well within reach. The required phase control (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha = kL$$\end{document}) can be achieved by precise nanofabrication of the finite arm length L or by tuning the wavelength. Implementing the symmetric quantum feedback loop presents the main practical challenge, requiring fast, high-efficiency detection of the combined plasmonic/photonic output and low-latency application of the feedback signal, potentially via integrated electro-optic modulators affecting the QDs’ local environment. Our work bridges the gap between passive plasmonic entanglement devices and active quantum control techniques. It provides a comprehensive blueprint for designing practical, room-temperature-compatible quantum devices that leverage the strengths of plasmonics (strong confinement, nanoscale integration) and quantum feedback (active stabilization, robustness). Our work demonstrates that quantum discord, as a more robust measure of quantum correlations compared to entanglement, can be effectively enhanced and stabilized in a nanophotonic platform. The demonstrated control over discord via phase tuning and active feedback opens avenues for practical quantum information processing tasks, such as discord-based quantum computing protocols and robust quantum communication at room temperature. Future directions include experimental implementation, exploring non-Markovian feedback regimes, and extending the scheme to multi-qubit networks for quantum information processing applications.

Emotion-driven front-end design of NEVs using an improved LSTM with LDA-based emotion mining
Authors: Cheng Yu, Yichai Qian, Yuanbo Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Due to the inherent subjectivity of Kansei perception, aligning the front-end styling of new energy vehicles (NEVs) with users’ emotional preferences remains a complex challenge. This study proposes a data-driven framework integrating semantic mining and deep learning to quantify and optimize such emotional responses. A Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) model was employed to extract four core emotional dimensions—fashion, power, technology, and sportiness—from a corpus of user-generated content (UGC). To establish a mapping between abstract emotions and concrete morphological features, rough set theory (RST) was applied for dimensionality reduction, retaining only the most influential design attributes. In addition, an attention-enhanced long short-term memory (LSTM) network optimized via a genetic algorithm (GA) was constructed to predict emotional evaluations. This hybrid model enables targeted design configuration generation for NEV front-end styling based on specific emotional indicators. The results demonstrate that the proposed approach effectively bridges the gap between qualitative user imagery and quantitative design features, providing the automotive industry with a robust emotion-oriented design support tool.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-45602-1.

Introduction

As new energy vehicles (NEVs) become central to global decarbonization efforts, market competition has progressively shifted from powertrain performance toward exterior styling that conveys emotional value and brand identity1–3. As the primary visual interface through which users form first impressions, front-end design plays a decisive role in shaping consumers’ aesthetic preferences and purchase decisions through its emotional expressiveness. Recent industry insights, such as the J.D. Power 2025 China New Energy Vehicle Consumer Insights Report, highlight the growing market demand for personalized aesthetic expression4. From the perspective of user perception and cognition, studies in cognitive psychology indicate that product appearance influences consumers not only through surface-level visual stimuli but also through implicit emotional responses and brand associations5,6. However, existing design paradigms face two fundamental challenges: traditional engineering-oriented design approaches are predominantly linear in nature and struggle to adapt to rapidly evolving aesthetic expectations7; At the cognitive level, a pronounced cognitive asymmetry often exists between designers’ encoded intentions and users’ decoded perceptions. This discrepancy between designers and users leads to a substantial “mapping gap” between physical design attributes and the semantic imagery perceived by consumers. Consequently, overcoming this cognitive asymmetry and establishing a systematic mechanism capable of translating ambiguous consumer emotions into operable design attributes has emerged as a critical challenge in NEV design.

Kansei Engineering (KE) establishes a connection between user perceptions and product form by mapping morphological elements to emotional responses. This approach has been widely applied across various domains, including product design8–10, packaging design11, and service design12. Nevertheless, existing Kansei Engineering approaches still face significant limitations. First, in terms of data collection, conventional approaches predominantly depend on small-scale questionnaires, which are not only labor-intensive but also susceptible to inherent subjectivity13. While some studies have successfully leveraged big data14, few systematically integrate topic modeling with morphological analysis to extract multidimensional emotional structures. Second, in feature selection, techniques like Fuzzy AHP15 and Grey Relational Analysis16 still depend heavily on expert judgment or linear assumptions, failing to objectively handle redundancy in complex styling attributes. Third, regarding prediction models, although machine learning algorithms like BPNN17 and standard LSTM18 have improved mapping accuracy, they often struggle to capture long-range dependencies in sequence data or suffer from “black box” interpretability issues.

To address the above research gaps, this study proposes a closed-loop, emotion-driven front-end design framework. Unlike existing studies that mainly focus on emotion evaluation, this work further explores how target emotional intents can be translated into specific front-end design attributes. By integrating emotion mining, feature selection, and predictive modeling, a unified design decision process is constructed that spans both analysis and generation, thereby enabling a systematic transformation from emotional requirements to styling solutions. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:This study introduces a scalable emotion modeling strategy based on UGC-driven topic mining, enabling the construction of a multidimensional emotional space beyond traditional small-sample questionnaire-based Kansei approaches.To model the nonlinear and potentially coupled effects among reduced styling attributes, a dual-layer redundancy-reduction and nonlinear mapping mechanism is established by coupling RST-based morphological attribute screening with a GA-optimized LSTM-Attention architecture, allowing interpretable modeling of complex emotion–attribute interactions under high-dimensional constraints.The proposed framework operationalizes a constrained generative decision mechanism that transforms abstract emotional intents into feasible front-end design configurations, thereby shifting emotion modeling from passive prediction toward actionable design support.

Rough set theory

To eliminate redundant information from the morphological analysis tables and identify design elements that significantly influence consumer emotions, we employ the attribute reduction algorithm of Rough Set Theory (RST). RST is particularly suitable for multi-attribute decision analysis involving discrete and semantically meaningful features, as it operates directly on decision tables without requiring prior weighting assumptions or linear transformations30,31.

GA-LSTM-Attention

LSTM

The long short-term memory (LSTM) network is a specialized recurrent neural network (RNN) designed to address the inherent vanishing and exploding gradient problems encountered by conventional RNNs when processing long sequential data32. By employing a gated mechanism consisting of input, forget, and output gates, LSTM selectively retains or discards information, thereby effectively capturing long-term dependencies in time-series data33. A schematic diagram of the LSTM unit architecture is shown in Fig. 2. Detailed mathematical formulations and computational procedures of the LSTM unit are provided in Supplementary Note 1.

Fig. 2The architecture of the LSTM cell.

The architecture of the LSTM cell.

In this study, the LSTM is employed as a nonlinear function approximator to model cross-attribute dependencies and interaction effects among the encoded morphological features. The reduced styling attributes were label-encoded and concatenated into a fixed-position feature vector for model input. Accordingly, the recurrent computation within the LSTM reflects structured integration across feature dimensions rather than temporal ordering.

However, the predictive performance of LSTM is highly sensitive to hyperparameters, such as the number of hidden layers, the number of neurons per layer, and the learning rate. Traditional approaches, including manual tuning and grid search, are often computationally expensive and inefficient in high-dimensional parameter spaces and cannot guarantee the attainment of a global optimum34,35. Therefore, introducing intelligent optimization algorithms to automatically identify optimal hyperparameter configurations is crucial for improving model convergence and predictive accuracy.

Genetic algorithm

To overcome the limitations of manual trial-and-error in model configuration, this study introduces a genetic algorithm (GA) for global optimization. Inspired by natural evolutionary processes, GA searches for optimal solutions through iterative operations of selection, crossover, and mutation36–38. In this study, GA is employed to automatically identify the optimal combination of LSTM hyperparameters, such as the number of hidden units, network depth, and learning rate. This approach enables efficient exploration of high-dimensional parameter spaces to maximize model fitness, thereby ensuring predictive accuracy and generalization performance.

Attention

To enhance the model’s ability to capture salient local features, this study introduces an attention mechanism to simulate human visual focus, dynamically allocating computational resources to the most informative regions of the input sequence39,40. Unlike standard approaches that treat all time steps equally, the attention mechanism assigns different weights based on the relevance between hidden states and emotional outputs. The mechanism operates by computing alignment scores, normalizing them via a Softmax function to obtain a probability distribution, and generating a context vector through a weighted sum of hidden states. This process enables the model to selectively emphasize key morphological attributes while suppressing irrelevant noise, thereby improving predictive accuracy and interpretability. Detailed mathematical formulations and computational steps of the attention mechanism are provided in Supplementary Note 2.

GA-LSTM-attention modeling

Given that the emotional imagery of NEV front-end designs arises from the synergistic interaction of multiple features, this study constructs a GA-LSTM-Attention hybrid model (Fig. 3). Within this framework, the LSTM network encodes sequential morphological features to capture temporal dependencies. Subsequently, an integrated attention mechanism assigns dynamic weights to hidden states, highlighting key design elements and enhancing model interpretability. Moreover, to overcome the subjectivity of manual hyperparameter tuning, a genetic algorithm (GA) is employed to globally optimize key LSTM hyperparameters, such as the number of hidden units and the learning rate. This integration ensures robust predictive accuracy and stability, effectively addressing the small-sample data challenges commonly encountered in design research.

Collection of NEV review data and morphological data

Collection and preprocessing of NEV exterior reviews

To capture user perceptions of new energy vehicle (NEV) appearance, this study employed web-crawling software (Octoparse) to collect a total of 29,062 raw comments from publicly accessible Chinese automotive discussion platforms (Autohome and Dongchedi) between January 1, 2023, and October 20, 2025. Pure electric SUVs were selected as representative samples, covering mainstream brands and models with high user engagement to ensure broad market coverage.

The collected comments were cleaned through manual duplicate removal, advertisement filtering, and the exclusion of overly short or non-informative texts (e.g., comments below a predefined length threshold), resulting in 27,463 valid entries. During preprocessing, tokenization was performed using jieba, and domain-specific lexicons from THULAC and HanLP were incorporated to expand relevant terminology and improve vocabulary recognition accuracy. Non-textual elements, including numerals, punctuation marks, and special characters, were removed. Part-of-speech (POS) tagging was then applied, and only nouns and adjectives related to aesthetic evaluation were retained. Adjectives capture evaluative perceptions, whereas nouns denote concrete design attributes. To maintain semantic focus on appearance-related evaluation and reduce contextual dispersion, verbs and adverbs were excluded; for example, frequent action-oriented terms such as “driving” or “purchasing” contribute limited information to the construction of stable Kansei dimensions. This standardized preprocessing procedure improved textual consistency and provided a reliable basis for subsequent LDA-based topic modeling. Examples of the preprocessed comments are shown in Fig. 5.

Collection of new energy SUV front-end samples

This study selected new energy SUV models released between 2022 and the first half of 2025, a period marked by rapid stylistic evolution in the new energy vehicle market. To focus on structural form characteristics, all images were converted to grayscale prior to evaluation. Emotion topic extraction based on LDA

This study employed the Gensim library to construct textual features. The preprocessed comments were first converted into a dictionary and subsequently mapped into vector space using the doc2bow representation. To improve topic discriminability, a TF–IDF model was applied to reweight the corpus, reducing the influence of high-frequency generic terms while emphasizing keywords with emotional salience.

Morphological decomposition of NEV front-end features

To construct a core dataset of front-end morphological features with salient visual structural characteristics, this study first conducted a systematic review and feature delineation of NEV SUV front-end forms. To ensure scientific rigor and professional validity in feature classification, a panel of six doctoral-level experts with backgrounds in automotive and industrial design was convened. The panel systematically examined and discussed 90 front-end image samples from the database. Through multiple rounds of deliberation and consensus-building, NEV SUV front ends were categorized into eight primary design features: line direction (F1), headlamp configuration (F2), grille region (F3), lower air intake (F4), side air intake (F5), rearview mirror (F6), hood lines (F7), and logo position (F8). Each feature was further subdivided into specific design types, resulting in a total of 49 categories (Fig. 9). Final classifications were established through expert consensus to ensure stability and consistency in feature delineation.

Attribute reduction of front-end features based on rough set theory

To establish a mapping between morphological features and user emotions, this study conducted a Kansei Evaluation. Using a combination of online and offline surveys, a total of 60 valid questionnaires were collected from potential NEV consumers (34 males and 26 females), aged between 25 and 45 years. Participants evaluated 90 sample images using a 7-point Likert scale, assessing: (1) the salience of eight key morphological features (as condition attributes, C); and (2) the four emotional dimensions identified by the LDA topic modeling (“Fashion,” “Power,” “Technology,” and “Sportiness”).

To identify key design features, a rough set decision table was constructed. In this table, condition attributes (F1–F8) were defined as the mean ratings of morphological features across all participants, while the decision attribute (D) was defined as a composite Kansei score obtained by averaging the four emotional dimensions to represent overall visual impression. To meet the data processing requirements of rough set theory (RST), continuous attribute ratings were discretized into several levels based on data distribution characteristics and expert validation (Table 3), ensuring balanced sample coverage (see Supplementary Note 3 for discretized data). An attribute reduction algorithm based on the positive-region variation criterion was implemented in MATLAB R2024a to evaluate attribute importance. The results identified four core attributes—line direction (F1), headlamp configuration (F2), lower air intake (F4), and hood indentation (F7)—which exhibited the highest explanatory power for the overall emotional imagery (Table 3). This reduction decreased the attribute dimensionality from eight to four, corresponding to a 50% compression at the attribute level. The reduction was conducted without altering the positive region of the decision table, thereby maintaining the decision structure while enhancing interpretability and computational efficiency (Table 4).

Construction of a Kansei–morphology mapping model

To enable model training, a structured dataset was constructed. The morphological features were numerically encoded to serve as model inputs (see Supplementary Note 4), while the mean Kansei scores of the 60 respondents served as target values (see Supplementary Note 5). The complete dataset (N = 90) was then randomly partitioned into a training set (70%, N = 63) for hyperparameter optimization and model fitting, and a test set (30%, N = 27) for independent performance evaluation.

For each of the four emotional dimensions, a GA–LSTM–Attention model was trained separately. The LSTM network consisted of one hidden layer with 64 units, followed by an attention mechanism and a fully connected output layer with linear activation. Dropout (0.2) was applied to mitigate overfitting.

The Genetic Algorithm (GA) was employed to optimize key hyperparameters, including the learning rate and the number of hidden units. The search ranges were defined as follows: learning rate ∈ [0.0001, 0.01] and hidden units ∈ [16, 64]. The GA population size was set to 30, with a maximum of 50 generations. The fitness function was defined as the negative root mean squared error (− RMSE) on the validation subset of the training data. The algorithm terminated when either the maximum number of generations was reached or the fitness improvement between successive generations fell below a predefined threshold. The convergence behavior of the GA optimization process is shown in Fig. 10.

After hyperparameter selection, the final LSTM–Attention model was trained using the Adam optimizer with L2 regularization. The maximum number of training epochs was set to 2000; if the validation loss plateaued after 800 epochs, the learning rate was adaptively reduced. Discussion

Interpretation of user-driven emotional semantics and key features

Unlike traditional approaches that rely on expert-defined lexicons, this study employs LDA modeling based on user-generated content (UGC) to uncover consumers’ genuine focal concerns. The identification of four independent dimensions—“Fashion,” “Power,” “Technology,” and “Sportiness”—indicates that the aesthetic imagery of contemporary new energy vehicles (NEVs) is inherently multidimensional and context-dependent.

Although alternative embedding-based topic modeling approaches (e.g., BERTopic) have demonstrated strong semantic representation capability in open-domain corpora, sensitivity analysis conducted on the present domain-specific dataset revealed substantial variability in topic granularity under different clustering thresholds (Fig. 13). As illustrated in Fig. 13a, the number of generated clusters varies considerably across reasonable hyperparameter ranges, while Fig. 13b shows corresponding fluctuations in outlier assignment rates. Such parameter sensitivity complicates the derivation of a stable and fixed-dimensional emotional representation required for subsequent morphological mapping and quantitative modeling. Therefore, LDA was adopted as the primary topic modeling approach in this study.

Furthermore, rough set theory (RST) analysis eliminated redundant information and identified line direction (F1), headlamp configuration (F2), lower air intake (F4), and hood indentation (F7) as core styling attributes. This result aligns with established visual organization principles in automotive front-end design. For instance, the headlamp configuration (F2), often regarded as the most expressive element of the front fascia, influences users’ perception of technological character through its geometric contour and internal structure. Meanwhile, the proportion and structural articulation of the lower air intake (F4) reinforce visual tension and strength expression in the frontal region. At the perceptual level, such visual cues are more readily associated with impressions of “Power” and “Sportiness.” This data-driven feature reduction suggests that complex emotional evaluations can be represented by a relatively sparse set of key morphological variables. Such a representation provides designers with a clearer basis for prioritizing critical attributes during early-stage conceptual development, thereby reducing the decision complexity inherent in high-dimensional design spaces.

Methodological superiority of the hybrid framework

Traditional linear statistical methods (e.g., AHP and QFD) have limitations in representing subjective and uncertain emotional data. In recent years, machine learning approaches such as SVR and BPNN have been increasingly employed to model the relationship between design features and user emotions. However, different models vary in their nonlinear modeling capacity, computational complexity, and generalization stability, and their suitability often depends on the problem structure and dataset size. Under small-sample conditions, balancing model complexity and generalization becomes particularly critical. Linear models may be insufficient to capture interactions among morphological attributes, whereas overly complex architectures may introduce instability. Therefore, based on comparative experiments across multiple models, this study adopts a structurally controlled GA-LSTM-Attention architecture to balance nonlinear interaction modeling and generalization performance.

To examine the rationality of this methodological choice, the predicted-versus-actual scatter distributions shown in Fig. 14 were analyzed to compare model performance. Overall, the GA-LSTM-Attention model exhibits relatively concentrated point distributions across the four emotional dimensions, with predictions closely aligned to the 45° reference line and no apparent systematic deviation between training and test sets. This distribution pattern suggests that, under small-sample conditions, global hyperparameter optimization via a genetic algorithm contributes to maintaining a stable balance between model capacity and generalization. In contrast, SVR and BPNN display more dispersed prediction points in certain dimensions, particularly in the test set. Although the standard LSTM-Attention model captures nonlinear tendencies to some extent, greater dispersion remains observable in several dimensions compared to the GA-optimized variant.

Finally, user evaluations of the generated designs show general consistency with model predictions, further supporting the effectiveness of the framework in modeling the emotion–morphology relationship. Overall, the structured process of emotion mining, attribute screening, nonlinear modeling, and generative decision-making helps reduce uncertainty in translating abstract emotional intentions into specific morphological configurations.

Limitations and future research directions

Although this study successfully validates the proposed hybrid framework, several limitations remain that warrant further investigation in future work.

First, regarding data sources, although UGC provides large-scale and relatively spontaneous user expressions, it may still be subject to sampling bias. Online reviewers often represent users with higher engagement or stronger emotional tendencies, which may lead to the overrepresentation of extreme opinions. In addition, platform recommendation mechanisms and specific discussion contexts may influence the visibility of certain viewpoints. Future research could incorporate multi-source data, such as structured surveys, controlled perceptual experiments, or cross-platform corpora, to enhance the robustness and external validity of the identified emotional dimensions.

Second, Kansei perceptions are inherently shaped by cultural and social contexts. As this study primarily relies on data from Chinese automotive forums, the extracted emotional dimensions may reflect culturally embedded aesthetic values. Their applicability to other cultural markets remains uncertain. Future studies should therefore conduct cross-cultural comparative analyses to examine the transferability and generalizability of the proposed emotion-mapping framework.

Third, in terms of research scope, this study focuses exclusively on new energy SUVs. Although SUVs account for a substantial market share, aesthetic preferences may vary across other vehicle categories, such as sedans, sports cars, or MPVs. Expanding the dataset to include more diverse NEV types would allow further evaluation of the model’s cross-category generalization capability.

Finally, regarding design representation and application, the current analysis is primarily based on two-dimensional static images of vehicle front ends. While the front fascia serves as a key visual interface, overall vehicle perception emerges from the integration of multiple components, including side profiles, rear styling, interior elements, and dynamic lighting features. The scalability of the framework to multi-component and multi-view contexts requires further empirical validation. Moreover, the generative stage currently functions as a decision-support tool, and the resulting optimal parameters still rely on human designers (or AI-assisted sketching tools) for visual realization. Future work may seek to integrate the optimization module directly into parametric design platforms, such as Grasshopper or Alias, to further reduce the gap between algorithmic recommendation and final 3D modeling.

Conclusions

This study proposes a data-driven framework based on online user reviews and morphological features to address the emotional design of new energy vehicle (NEV) front-end styling. The framework employs LDA topic modeling to construct emotional dimensions and integrates morphological decomposition with rough set theory (RST)–based attribute reduction to identify key front-end features. Subsequently, a hybrid GA-LSTM-Attention model is adopted to establish the mapping between morphological features and emotional responses, enabling the derivation of optimal styling solutions. The results indicate that the GA-LSTM-Attention model achieves high fitting accuracy and low prediction error across all four emotional dimensions, demonstrating its capability to stably capture the nonlinear coupling between morphology and emotion. In the validation stage, the generated designs received positive user feedback, indicating that the proposed method effectively conveys target emotional attributes and aligns with user preferences. Overall, this study provides a quantifiable and scalable technical pathway for new energy vehicle design. At the theoretical level, it enriches the methodological integration of Kansei Engineering and deep learning. At the practical level, it offers designers an actionable tool for translating online user behavior data into concrete design elements. Future research will focus on validating the generalization capability of this approach on larger-scale datasets and multi-view styling components, as well as exploring optimization strategies under multi-objective emotional trade-offs.

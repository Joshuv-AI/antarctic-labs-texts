Utility of the L1 pelvic angle (L1PA) for assessment of sagittal alignment of the lumbar spine following short segment fusion: Comparison to segmental measures
Authors: Zvipo M Chisango, Catherine B Hurley, Gabriel A Gonzalez, Michael J Farias, Nicolas L Carayannopoulos, Joseph P Carroll, Manjot Singh, Jinseong Kim, Bryce A Basques, Bassel G Diebo, Alan H Daniels
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe Lumbar Pelvic Angle (L1PA) was introduced as a convenient intraoperative and postoperative measure of sagittal alignment. However, the utility of L1PA for short segment lumbar fusions remains incompletely understood. This study investigates the relationship between L1PA and segmental parameters of spinal alignment for short segment lumbar fusions.MethodsThis retrospective analysis was conducted on adult patients with degenerative conditions undergoing primary spinal fusion surgery between L4 and S1. Patients with <5° change in L1PA from pre- to postoperative measurements were stratified into 2 groups: those (1) achieving >35° of regional lordosis at L4-S1 postoperatively and (2) those with >5% change in Lumbar Distribution Index (LDI). Spinopelvic parameters were compared between preoperative and postoperative measurements.ResultsThe study cohort included 539 patients, of which 360 (66.8%) had an L1PA change less than <5° pre- to postoperatively. Patients in both Group 1 and 2 had significant increases in the L5-S1 segmental angle (both p<.001) without a significant change at the L4-L5 segment. In Group 2, there was a significant increase in PI-LL mismatch (p<.001). In both groups there was a significant increase in L4-S1 lordosis, a relaxation in L1-L4 lordosis (both p<.001), and no significant change in total LL.ConclusionsThis investigation assessed L1PA in over 500 patients undergoing lumbar fusion between L4 and S1 and found that despite value to the measure, important changes to spinal shape and segmental alignment may occur even in the face of Δ L1PA <5°. As such, L1PA serves as a potentially useful adjunct to clinical assessment and radiographic measurement in lumbar degenerative fusion but should not serve as a stand-alone measurement.Keywords: L1PA, Lumbar 1 pelvic angle, Lumbar spine, Spinopelvic alignment, Spinopelvic parameters, Lumbar fusion

Introduction

The restoration of spinopelvic alignment is increasingly recognized as crucial to patient outcomes in spinal conditions, even for short segment lumbar fusions for degenerative conditions [[1], [2], [3]]. Over the last 5 decades, physicians and surgeons have described numerous spinopelvic alignment parameters to judge severity of disease and success of intervention [[4], [5], [6], [7], [8]]. Recent efforts have attempted to describe parameters that can be obtained intraoperatively to allow for real time adjustments [9]. One such parameter is the Lumbar Pelvic Angle (L1PA), first described in 2018 by Protopsaltis et al. [10].

L1PA is defined as the angle formed by a line from the L1 centroid to the femoral head axis and a line from the S1 endplate to the femoral head axis [10]. It was introduced as a convenient intra- and postoperative alternative to Pelvic Incidence minus Lumbar Lordosis (PI-LL). Some studies have shown strong correlation of L1PA with older, more established parameters of spinopelvic alignment [[10], [11], [12]], however findings have been conflicting, with some studies reporting weak or moderate correlation with other parameters, including T1 Pelvic Angle (T1PA) [13].

While population level trends between L1PA and other parameters of spinopelvic alignment have been described [14], L1PA’s relationship to segmental alignment in short segment lumbar fusion remains incompletely understood. The present study describes changes in Pelvic Tilt (PT), Lumbar Lordosis (LL), Pelvic Incidence minus Lumbar Lordosis (PI-LL), L1-L4 lordosis, L4-S1 lordosis, L4-L5 segmental angle, L5-S1 segmental angle, and Lumbar Distribution Index (LDI) in patients with degenerative spinal conditions undergoing short segment spine fusion surgery.

Methods

Study design

This was a retrospective cohort study comprising data collected at a single large institution between 2017 and 2022. Approval from the Institutional Review Board was obtained prior to beginning data collection.

Patient population

Adult patients who underwent primary anterior lumbar interbody fusion (ALIF) or transforaminal interbody fusion (TLIF) at L4-L5, L5-S1, or L4-S1 for degenerative spinal disease including spondylolisthesis were included. For patients who had both anterior and posterior fusion, anterior fusion levels were assessed for ALIF patients and posterior fusion levels were assessed for TLIF patients. Patients with a history of spine tumor, metastases to the spine, trauma or infection were excluded.

Data extraction

Patient demographic data including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI), and the Modified 5-Item Frailty Index (MFI-5) were extracted. Radiographic spinopelvic data based on lateral films included L1PA, PT, LL, L1-L4 lordosis, L4-S1 lordosis, LDI, PI-LL mismatch, L4-L5 segmental lordosis and L5-S1 segmental lordosis. Measurements of these parameters were taken at the preoperative visit and at the first postoperative visit if the visit occurred within 6 weeks of the date of surgery.

Statistical analyses

L1PA at preoperative and postoperative visits was analyzed for all patients. Minimal change in L1PA was defined as a change of <5°. This threshold was chosen pragmatically to account for normal variability in measurements. Patients were then further categorized into 2 groups: (1) those achieving >35° of regional lordosis at L4-S1 postoperatively and (2) those with >5% increase in LDI. Radiographic parameters were collected at the preoperative visit and 6 weeks postoperatively. Mean changes in spinopelvic parameters between visits were summarized for all groups. All data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics Version 29.0.

Results

Cohort characteristics

This investigation included 539 patients with degenerative spinal conditions receiving lumbar fusion between L4 and S1. The mean age was 55.5 years, mean BMI was 29.8kg/m², mean CCI was 1.9, mean MFI-5 was 0.2, and 290 patients (53.8%) were female (Table 1).

Discussion

In this study, an assessment of the L1PA was performed on a cohort of patients undergoing L4-S1 fusion surgery for degenerative lumbar disease, revealing that important changes to L4-S1 lordosis and pelvic tilt may occur in the absence of change to L1PA.

In our cohort, 162 (30.0%) of patients who achieved >35° of postoperative lordosis at L4-S1 with a mean increase of 3.1° maintained an L1PA within 5° of baseline (Group 1). Loss of lordosis is known to be associated with degenerative processes of the spine [[15], [16], [17]]. As such, restoring lordosis to the lumbar spine is a major aim of surgery [18], with some studies demonstrating that a lower arc lordosis of >35° following short segment fusion is associated with decreased incidence of adjacent segment disease (ASD), postoperative complications, and subsequent revisions [19,20]. Despite a significant change in this established parameter of sagittal alignment, there was no change in L1PA, suggesting that L1PA may not be a sensitive marker of regional alignment.

In Group 1, there was a statistically significant increase in LDI. Obtaining adequately sufficient LDI is one of many priorities in sagittal alignment as prior studies have shown maldistribution of LDI, particularly low LDI (<0.5) to be associated with ASD and revision surgery [21,22]. This investigation also found a statistically significant increase in L5-S1 lordosis but not in L4-L5 lordosis after fusion. This is consistent with previous studies of short segment fusion that have shown the L5-S1 segment to contribute more to lumbar lordosis than the L4-L5 segment [23,24]. Additionally, there was a small but statistically significant increase of PT in patients with Δ L1PA <5° who achieved >35° of lordosis at L4-S1. Although this finding reached statistical significance, its clinical significance remains unclear.

This study found that 268 patients (49.7%) had a postoperative L1PA value within 5° of their baselines with a >5% change in LDI (Group 2). The Lumbar Distribution Index represents the proportion of lumbar lordosis that is localized to the lower arc (L4-S1) and higher LDI is generally considered to be a goal of sagittal alignment associated with less postoperative complications, particularly in patients with low pelvic incidence [21,22]. The significant improvement observed in LDI, an established parameter of sagittal alignment, despite no corresponding change in L1PA, further highlights the potential limitations of L1PA as a standalone measurement of sagittal alignment.

In the full cohort and within subgroups with Δ L1PA <5°, there was a consistent increase in lordosis at L4-S1. This reflects 1 aim of fusion surgery which is often to improve and restore physiologic lordosis [25,26]. Notably, there was a relaxation of lordosis in the cephalad, unfused L1-L4 region suggesting that there may be compensation at adjacent segments of the fused spine. This aligns with previous literature showing that segments cephalad to fused segments often compensate by reducing lordosis, perhaps due to relaxation of compensatory hyperlordosis at the cephalad segments once there is correction at the caudal segments [21,27,28].

Limitations

The present study has several potential limitations. First, data for this study was obtained from a single institution in the United States which may limit the external validity of these findings. However, fusions were performed by several different surgeons and this study assessed both ALIF and TLIF patients which broadens the generalizability of our findings. Furthermore, while we used reliable, validated software to obtain the radiographic measurements used in this investigation, we did not account for measurement variability which may have confounded results, as such we defined <5° of change in L1PA as ‘minimal” to account for this variability between measurements. While we designate a change of <5° as minimal, it should not be interpreted to mean that a change <5° lacks clinical significance. Second, this study was retrospective in nature which may have introduced selection bias. Third, Finally, our sample size was limited, potentially reducing the power of this study and obscuring subtle but significant differences.

Conclusions

This study assessed L1PA in patients undergoing fusion between L4 and S1 for degenerative spinal conditions and found that despite value to the measure, important changes to spinal shape and segmental alignment may occur even in the face of minimal L1PA change (<5°). As such, L1PA serves as a potentially useful adjunct metric for clinical assessment and radiographic measurement in lumbar degenerative fusion but should not serve as a stand-alone measurement.

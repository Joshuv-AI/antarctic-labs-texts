Effect of delayed cord clamping on the risk of bronchopulmonary dysplasia in preterm infants with respiratory distress
Authors: Jiajing Ge, Chenhong Wang, Huijia Lin, Guannan Bai, Yanping Xu, Liping Shi, Xiaolu Ma, Zheng Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo assess the incidence of bronchopulmonary dysplasia in preterm infants born at < 32 weeks of gestation who received delayed cord clamping rather than early cord clamping.MethodsDuring this multicenter, prospective, observational cohort study, data of 1283 infants born at < 32 weeks of gestation who received umbilical cord management between January 1, 2020 and December 31, 2021, were collected from 26 tertiary referral neonatal intensive care units in China. The primary outcome was bronchopulmonary dysplasia.ResultsA total of 440 infants (34.3%) received delayed cord clamping. The bronchopulmonary dysplasia rates were 32.5% and 55.6% for infants who received delayed cord clamping and those who received early cord clamping, respectively. After adjusting for potential confounders, the delayed cord clamping group had a lower risk of bronchopulmonary dysplasia (odds ratio [OR], 0.47; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.35–0.62; P < 0.001). No significant difference in the severity of bronchopulmonary dysplasia was observed between groups (P = 0.09). A subgroup analysis of 227 infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation revealed that 65 (28.6%) received delayed cord clamping, and that 40 (61.5%) of these 65 infants experienced bronchopulmonary dysplasia. After adjusting for potential confounders, the risk of bronchopulmonary dysplasia for the delayed cord clamping group was not decreased (OR, 0.65; 95% CI, 0.30–1.41; P = 0.28).ConclusionDelayed cord clamping independently decreased the risk of bronchopulmonary dysplasia in infants born at < 32 weeks of gestation with moderate-to-severe respiratory distress; however, for those born at < 28 weeks of gestation, this benefit was not observed.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12887-025-06232-0.Keywords: Bronchopulmonary dysplasia, Cohort study, Delayed cord clamping, Preterm infant

Introduction

Umbilical cord management affects the volume of blood transferred from the placenta to the infant and influences systemic circulation and pulmonary circulation after birth [1, 2]. Recent guidelines have recommended delayed cord clamping (DCC) for term and preterm neonates who do not require resuscitation [3, 4]. Systematic reviews have also shown that, compared with early cord clamping (ECC), DCC improves the peak hematocrit level within 7 days after birth [5] and reduces the need for subsequent blood transfusions [6]. Recently, the need for inotropic support for hypotension during the first 24 h of life was found to be decreased [5]. However, DCC may not affect other outcomes such as severe intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH), necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), and chronic lung disease [5, 7, 8].

Previous randomized trials and systematic reviews have defined chronic lung disease as oxygen use at 36 weeks postmenstrual age (PMA) [9]. In 2001, the National Institute of Child Health and Human Development replaced the term chronic lung disease with the term bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD) [10]. Because of the development of respiratory support techniques and increased survival of preterm infants, the diagnostic criteria for BPD were updated in 2018 by the National Institute of Child Health and Human Development [11].

We report the results of our multicenter, prospective, observational cohort study that was designed to assess risk factors for BPD in infants with moderate-to-severe respiratory distress after birth, in which the diagnosis of BPD was based on the diagnostic criteria of 2018. In clinical practice, preterm infants with mild respiratory distress are at lower risk for BPD. Therefore, this cohort study focused on infants with moderate-to-severe respiratory distress. Data regarding umbilical cord management (DCC or ECC), BPD, and other adverse events were recorded in an online database and downloaded by the Coordinating Center for Analysis, thereby allowing us to conduct a post hoc analysis to compare BPD and other major morbidities of infants who received DCC (≥ 60 s) and those of infants who received ECC. Additionally, a subgroup analysis of these outcomes of infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation was conducted.

Methods

Patients

This multicenter, prospective, observational cohort study of risk factors for BPD in preterm infants was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (2019-IRB-164), on December 24, 2019, and registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2000030125) on February 23, 2020. Twenty-six tertiary referral neonatal intensive care units (NICUs) participated in this study. Preterm infants born at < 32 weeks of gestation and admitted within 72 h after birth who had a respiratory distress score ≥ 5 according to the Acute Care of at-Risk Newborns (ACoRN) program [12] (Table 1) or received invasive mechanical ventilation (IMV) at admission were included in this study. Moderate respiratory distress was defined as ACoRN score between 5 and 8. Severe respiratory distress was defined as ACoRN score ≥ 9 or received IMV. The exclusion criteria were major congenital or chromosomal abnormalities, death within 14 days of birth, and parents who rejected NICU care. This cohort study enrolled 1286 infants who were treated between January 1, 2020 and December 31, 2021. To allow a robust comparison of DCC and ECC, infants with missing umbilical cord clamping data were excluded from the study.

Data collection

The enrolled infants were observed from birth to 36 weeks PMA. All maternal, perinatal, and neonatal data of each infant were recorded in the online system by trained research assistants. All data were collected from either the parents or the medical records. Neonatal outcome data were recorded according to standardized outcome definitions.

Outcomes

Maternal and neonatal characteristics, including mode of pregnancy, disease during pregnancy, mode of delivery, use of antenatal steroids, prolonged rupture of membranes (PROM) time, gestational age, birth weight, and sex, were analyzed. The primary outcome was BPD at 36 weeks PMA. Secondary outcomes were Apgar scores at 1 min and 5 min, severe respiratory distress, the use of surfactant, patent ductus arteriosus, NEC, late-onset sepsis, IMV duration ≥ 7 days, hypotension, inotropic drug use, pneumorrhagia during the first 7 days of life, IVH grade 3 or 4, and length of hospital stay.

BPD in premature infants (< 32 weeks of gestation) was defined and graded according to the 2018 National Institute of Child Health and Human Development workshop criteria [11]. Grade 3 or 4 IVH was defined according to the Papile criteria. NEC (all stages) was defined according to the Bell criteria. Patent ductus arteriosus was defined as hemodynamically significant patent ductus treated with surgery or medication. Hemodynamically significant patent ductus was characterized by a ductus diameter exceeding 1.5 mm, a left atrial inner diameter/aortic root (LA/AO) exceeding 1.4, or a combined left-to-right shunt, as determined by echocardiography. Late-onset sepsis was defined as blood or cerebrospinal fluid cultures positive for bacteria, viruses, or fungi after 72 h of life.

Ethics

This study was conducted as part of a prospective, observational cohort study, and it was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine (2019-IRB-164). Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or guardians of all enrolled infants.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to assess the clinical characteristics of the study population. Normally distributed data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation, whereas data with a skewed distribution are presented as the median (25th-75th percentile). Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages.

To perform comparisons of clinical characteristics and outcomes, the chi-square test was used for categorical variables, and Student’s t test or the Mann-Whitney U test was used for continuous variables. The Mann-Whitney U test was used for ranked variables. Secondary outcomes were investigated using ordinal or binary logistic regression or multiple linear regression models adjusted for potential confounders of baseline characteristics. The adjusted confounders included gestational age, birth weight, antenatal steroids, and prolonged rupture of membranes > 18 h. A binary logistic regression model adjusted for potential confounders was used to assess the relationship between umbilical cord management (DCC or ECC) and BPD. The adjusted variables included baseline characteristics and potential risk factors for BPD [13–17] that resulted in differences in the secondary outcomes of the DCC and ECC groups. The baseline characteristics included gestational age, birth weight, antenatal steroids, PROM, surfactant, Apgar score, severe respiratory distress. The potential risk factors included NEC, PDA, late-onset sepsis, IMV duration ≥ 7 days. A subgroup analysis of infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation was performed using a similar statistical method. In order to better evaluate the risk factors of BPD, a binary logistic regression model was used to analysis the subgroup of infants born at 28-32weeks of gestation. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 20 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Characteristics of the enrolled infants

Among the 1286 infants enrolled in the cohort, three were excluded because of missing umbilical cord clamping data, resulting in a total of 1283 infants who were included in this study. Of these infants, 227 (17.7%) were born at < 28 weeks of gestation (range, 23 + 1 to 27 + 6 weeks of gestation) and 440 (34.3%) received DCC. The subgroup analysis of infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation (n = 227) revealed that 65 (28.6%) received DCC (Fig. 1). Maternal and neonatal characteristics are shown in Table 2. Differences in gestational age (30.0 weeks vs. 29.7 weeks; P = 0.03), birth weight (1300 g vs. 1260 g; P = 0.008), antenatal steroid use (89.0% vs. 69.3%; P < 0.001), and PROM > 18 h (31.4% vs. 21.6%; P < 0.001) between the DCC and ECC groups were observed.

Primary and secondary outcomes

The primary and secondary outcomes of the patients are described in Table 3. The incidence of BPD at 36 weeks PMA of the DCC group was lower than that of the ECC group (32.5% vs. 55.6%; P < 0.001). No statistically significant difference in BPD severity between these groups was observed (P = 0.09). After adjusting for baseline characteristics that were potential confounders (Table 2) and other risk factors that resulted in differences in secondary outcomes (Table 4), DCC was identified as an independent factor associated with a decreased risk of BPD (odds ratio [OR], 0.47; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.35–0.62; P < 0.001) (Table 5).

Additionally, after adjusting for baseline characteristics that were potential confounders, the DCC group had higher Apgar scores at 1 min (OR, 2.32; 95% CI, 1.79–3.00.79.00; P < 0.001) and 5 min (OR, 3.62; 95% CI, 2.29–5.73; P < 0.001), less severe respiratory distress (OR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.29–0.86; P = 0.01), lower risks of NEC (OR, 0.58; 95% CI, 0.38–0.90, P = 0.01), late-onset sepsis (OR, 0.50; 95% CI, 0.38–0.65; P < 0.001), and grade 3 or 4 IVH (OR, 0.38; 95% CI, 0.23–0.63; P < 0.001), a lower rate of IMV duration ≥ 7 days (OR, 0.58; 95% CI, 0.42–0.80; P = 0.001), and a shorter hospital stay (β=−3.10; 95% CI, −4.99 to −1.20; P = 0.001). During the first 7 days of life, the DCC group had a lower risk of hypotension (OR, 0.34; 95% CI, 0.20–0.60; P < 0.001) and a lower rate of inotropic drug use (OR, 0.21; 95% CI, 0.15–0.30; P < 0.001). No significant difference in the risk of pneumorrhagia was observed (OR, 0.70; 95% CI, 0.33–1.47; P = 0.34); however, a significant difference in the risk of patent ductus arteriosus (OR, 1.36; 95% CI, 1.00–1.84.00.84; P = 0.049) was observed (Table 4).

Subgroup analysis

The subgroup analysis of infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation showed no significant differences in the incidence of BPD of the DCC and ECC groups (61.5% vs. 72.2%; P = 0.12) and the BPD severity (P = 0.73). After adjusting for potential confounders, the risk of BPD of the DCC group was not decreased (OR, 0.65; 95% CI, 0.30–1.41; P = 0.28) (Table 5). Furthermore, the DCC group had higher Apgar scores at 1 min (OR, 2.62; 95% CI, 1.41–4.88; P = 0.002) and 5 min (OR, 2.67; 95% CI, 1.20–5.95; P = 0.02), a lower rate of inotropic drug use during the first 7 days of life (OR, 0.36; 95% CI, 0.17–0.71; P = 0.004), and a lower risk of late-onset sepsis (OR, 0.31; 95% CI, 0.16–0.60; P < 0.001). No significant differences in any of the other secondary outcomes were observed (Table 4).

In the subgroup analysis of infants born between 28 and 32 weeks of gestation, the risk of BPD of the DCC group was decreased (OR, 0.44; 95% CI, 0.32–0.60; P < 0.001) (Supplement Table 1).

Discussion

Compared with ECC, DCC decreased the incidence of BPD in preterm infants born at < 32 weeks of gestation; however, DCC had no impact on BPD in infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation. The risks of adverse events, such as hypotension, late-onset sepsis, and severe IVH, of the DCC group were decreased.

Among the 1283 infants in this study, 612 (47.8%) developed BPD. However, in 2014, a study in Japan that included infants with very low birth weight and defined BPD as oxygen dependence at 28 days of age reported that the incidence of BPD was 41.6% [18]. This difference may be explained by our inclusion of only infants with moderate-to-severe respiratory distress. Moderate-to-severe respiratory distress was diagnosed when infants had an increased FiO2, a long IMV duration, or were born prematurely; all of these are risk factors for BPD. Among the 227 infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation (range, 23 + 1 to 27 + 6 weeks), 70 (30.8%) did not develop BPD; this incidence is similar to that reported previously. In 2018, Erik et al. reported that 32.8% of all infants included in the Vermont Oxford Network who were born between 23 + 0 and 27 + 6 weeks of gestation survived without BPD [19].

We found that DCC decreased the incidence of BPD in infants born at < 32 weeks of gestation. This finding differed from those of previous studies. Brown et al. found that DCC ≥ 30 s resulted in a lower risk of BPD for infants who were small for gestational age and born at < 33 weeks of gestation. No significant difference in the risk of BPD for infants who were not small for gestational age in the DCC and ECC groups was observed [20]. Seidler et al. performed a meta-analysis of DCC (> 30 s) for 3514 infants born at < 34 weeks of gestation and found little to no difference in the incidence of BPD for patients in the DCC and ECC groups (relative risk, 1.03; 95% CI, 0.94–1.13) [5]. Jasani et al. reported that DCC (> 30 s) had no effect on the incidence of BPD in infants born at < 33 weeks of gestation [8]. This may have occurred because DCC increases the stability of cardiopulmonary circulation during the early postnatal period of infants with moderate-to-severe respiratory distress, thus affecting the incidence of BPD. Our analysis of secondary outcomes revealed that infants who received DCC were less likely to experience hypotension, require inotropic drug treatment, and develop pneumorrhagia during the first 7 days of life, which may have indicated more stable cardiopulmonary circulation in these infants.

Our subgroup analysis revealed that DCC did not affect the incidence of BPD among infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation; this finding was similar to that of other studies [6, 8]. In our analysis of secondary outcomes, infants who received DCC does not have lower experience of hypotension and pneumorrhagia, which may have indicated that DCC contributed less to the stability of cardiopulmonary circulation. However, the non-significant findings between DCC and BPD should be interpreted with caution. Based on our observed BPD incidence (72.2% in the ECC group vs. 61.5% in the DCC group), a two-sided test with α = 0.05 and 80% power would require approximately 346 infants per group (692 in total) to detect a 10% absolute risk reduction. In our cohort, only 227 infants were born at < 28 weeks, 65 infants received DCC. Thus, the subgroup was underpowered. More research is needed to confirm the impact of DCC on BPD in infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation. Our multivariate analysis identified gestational age as an independent, but not statistically significant, predictor of BPD (OR, 0.63; 95% CI, 0.37–1.07; P = 0.09). Current evidence indicates that gestational age may be the strongest predictor of BPD in infants [21, 22].

This study had several limitations. First, the data used for this study were part of a prospective, multicenter, observational cohort study and the analysis had a post hoc design. This resulted in differences in the basic characteristics and sample size between two groups. Although we attempted to control for bias by performing a multivariate analysis, it may not have been sufficiently controlled. Potential confounding factors such as ureaplasma infection and postnatal steroids which may affect the occurrence of BPD were not included in the analysis. Second, there is no unified strategy for whether to perform DCC on preterm infants who require resuscitation after birth. In this study, we do not intervene in whether DCC was performed. Different centers may have different preferences for DCC strategies, leading to bias. We suggest future prospective randomized controlled trials to further confirm the impact of DCC on BPD and optimize DCC strategies.

Conclusion

DCC decreased the risk of BPD in preterm infants with moderate-to-severe respiratory distress born at < 32 weeks of gestation. This benefit was not observed in preterm infants born at < 28 weeks of gestation. Clinicians in the NICU can use these results to determine the optimal management strategy to reduce the risk of BPD in preterm infants.

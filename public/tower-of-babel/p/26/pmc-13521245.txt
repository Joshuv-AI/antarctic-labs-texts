Lycium barbarum polysaccharides alleviate PVC microplastic-induced ferroptosis and fibrosis in duck kidneys by attenuating NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy: an in vivo study
Authors: Yan Chen, Sifan Li, Yazhen Xue, Xianghong Qin, Lin Xiong, Zehao Wang, Zhili Yu, Yonggang Ma, Xishuai Tong, Haibo Jin, Wei Liu, Hongyan Zhao, Jianhong Gu, Yan Yuan, Jianchun Bian, Zongping Liu, Hui Zou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Microplastics (MPs) contamination is widespread and continues to increase. Compared to terrestrial organisms, mallards as waterfowl are more susceptible to MPs exposure. Lycium barbarum polysaccharides (LBP) are bioactive compounds extracted from goji berries; however, the mechanism by which LBP alleviates polyvinyl chloride microplastics (PVC-MPs)-induced renal damage in ducks remains unknown. In this study, network pharmacology was utilized to identify target proteins of LBP against PVC-MPs-induced renal injury. Analyses of protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks, drug-disease-target networks, and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment identified 107 key targets, with core targets predominantly enriched in the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2) signaling pathway. Male mallard ducks were divided into four groups: control, PVC-MPs (10 mg/kg in diet), LBP (50 mg/kg gavage), and PVC‑MPs + LBP. Our results indicate that PVC-MPs significantly reduced kidney weight and organ coefficient while elevating renal injury biomarkers. PVC-MPs also induced pathological damage and interstitial fibrosis, disrupted redox homeostasis (characterized by increased MDA and decreased GSH/CAT), and suppressed the NRF2/Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (KEAP1) pathway. Furthermore, PVC-MPs caused renal iron accumulation and ferroptosis (reduced GPX4 and FTH1, increased ACSL4), along with activation of nuclear receptor coactivator 4 (NCOA4)-mediated ferritinophagy (elevated LC3, ATG5, NCOA4, and autophagosome formation). Notably, LBP treatment significantly reversed all these effects. In conclusion, LBP alleviates PVC-MPs-induced renal ferroptosis and fibrosis by attenuating NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy. This study provides novel insights into the nephrotoxic mechanisms of PVC-MPs and supports the development of LBP as a protective feed additive in poultry farming.

Introduction

MPs (diameter less than 5 mm) are defined as secondary plastic particles that are produced as a result of human industrial activities or via physicochemical degradation processes (Lambert and Wagner, 2016). The occurrence of MPs in marine ecosystems, freshwater systems, soil, and sediments has been extensively recorded (Huang et al., 2021). In comparison with polystyrene microplastics (PS-MPs), which have been the subject of extensive research, studies indicate that PVC-MPs exhibit a distinctive form of biotoxicity, characterized by reduced survival rates and weight loss (Peng et al., 2023b). Moreover, due to the high chlorine content and potential release of toxic additives such as phthalates during degradation, PVC-MPs possess unique physicochemical properties that may contribute to different toxicological profiles compared to PS-MPs (Liu et al., 2025). Moreover, MPs may present a health hazard to humans via the food chain. Ducks are a kind of aquatic bird that reside in rivers and ponds, where they consume plankton and fish. China is the preeminent producer of ducks, constituting three-quarters of worldwide duck output (Jalaludeen and Churchil, 2022). Compared to terrestrial livestock and poultry, ducks are more susceptible to exposure to MPs. Furthermore, microplastic residues have been detected in the intestines and feces of duck samples from intensive farming systems, potentially originating from environmental contamination or contaminated feed (Ramachandraiah et al., 2022). Nevertheless, limited research has documented the impact of PVC-MPs on the health of duck kidneys.

Ferroptosis is a newly recognized type of cell death characterized by iron overload and lipid peroxidation (LPO), driven by the excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and lipid peroxides (Mu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2021). Iron in the body is predominantly stored as ferritin, which consists of two subunits: ferritin light chain (FTL) and ferritin heavy chain 1 (FTH1). FTH1 oxidizes Fe²⁺, which can participate in the Fenton reaction, into the relatively non-toxic Fe³⁺ state (Li et al., 2026). Cysteine, a pivotal constituent of glutathione (GSH), functions as the substrate for glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4). The solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) is responsible for the import of cystine into cells, which is then reduced to cysteine and used for glutathione (GSH) synthesis, thereby functioning as a key cofactor for GPX4 (Stockwell et al., 2020). Its functional inactivation has been demonstrated to lead to GSH depletion, which in turn inactivates GPX4 and triggers ferroptosis. Acyl-CoA Synthase Long-Chain Family Member 4 (ACSL4) has been demonstrated to participate in lipid metabolism, increasing LPO production and promoting ferroptosis (Hong et al., 2022). The accumulation of MP has been shown to trigger ferroptosis in the ovaries and lungs of chickens (Ali et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025). Autophagy is a conserved process of cellular self-degradation that removes damaged organelles and macromolecules to maintain intracellular homeostasis (Kitada and Koya, 2021). In recent years, mounting evidence has indicated that ferroptosis is an autophagy-dependent form of cell death (Zhu et al., 2025). Autophagy has been demonstrated to facilitate the binding of the C-terminal FTH1-binding site of nuclear coactivator 4 (NCOA4) to ferritin, thereby forming an NCOA4-ferritin complex, which is transported to autophagosomes and fuses with lysosomes, leading to ferritin degradation and iron release (Gao et al., 2015; Kang et al., 2019). It has been demonstrated that microplastics-induced autophagy-dependent ferroptosis is widely present in mouse testes, lung and poultry brain (Jia et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2025; Yin et al., 2022). However, there have been no reports of studies investigating whether microplastic accumulation causes autophagy-dependent ferroptosis in poultry kidneys.

LBP is the primary active compound identified and purified from goji berries, primarily composed of monosaccharides such as d-glucose, d-galactose, l-rhamnose, arabinose, galacturonic acid, and d-mannose (Chen et al., 2023). Owing to its varied biological functions—comprising antioxidant, anticancer, hypoglycemic, anti-aging, and neuroprotective properties—coupled with its affordability and practicality, it has been thoroughly investigated in disciplines such as food science and medicine (Li et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2004; Yu et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2005). Network pharmacology systematically and comprehensively elucidates how drugs influence disease networks through the “disease-gene-target-drug” interaction network, thereby facilitating a deeper understanding of their molecular and genetic foundations. LBP, an antioxidant, attenuates oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis in lead-exposed mouse kidneys through the activation of the NRF2 signaling pathway and the inhibition of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway (Xie et al., 2024, 2021). Furthermore, LBP has been demonstrated to attenuate renal fibrosis by modulating the process of macrophage-to-myofibroblast transformation (MMT) (Liu et al., 2024). However, there is a paucity of literature on the relationship between LBP and renal ferroptosis in network pharmacology, and on the question of whether it participates in regulating iron-dependent autophagy-mediated ferroptosis to alleviate renal injury.

China is the preeminent producer of ducks, and microplastic residues have been detected in the intestines and feces of ducks from intensive farming systems. Thus, investigating PVC-MPs toxicity in ducks is of significant agricultural importance. This study aimed to investigate the toxic effects of PVC-MPs exposure on duck kidneys and the protective role of LBP, with a focus on oxidative stress, ferroptosis, and NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy. Specifically, we examined renal function, histopathology, oxidative stress markers, iron homeostasis, ferroptosis-related proteins (GPX4, SLC7A11, ACSL4, FTH1), and autophagy markers (LC3, P62, ATG5, NCOA4) in male mallard ducks exposed to PVC-MPs with or without LBP treatment. The objective was to provide novel insights into the nephrotoxic mechanisms of PVC-MPs and to establish scientific evidence for developing LBP as a feed additive, thereby contributing to the healthy development of poultry farming.

Materials and methods

Network pharmacology analysis

Retrieve the SMILES representations for LBP, specifically d-glucose, d-galactose, l-rhamnose, arabinose, galacturonic acid, and d-mannose, from the PubChem database. Import these into the Swiss-Target Prediction database and the ParmMapper database for the prediction of drug target genes. Standardize the target genes utilizing the UniProt database. Relevant targets were screened utilizing the keywords “Acute kidney injury” and “Ferroptosis.” Interactions among the drug, intersecting targets, and diseases were constructed and visualized using Cytoscape 3.9.1 software. The String database was employed, selecting the species "Anas platyrhynchos" to construct the drug's PPI network. The target intersections were imported into the DAVID database for Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis.

Molecular docking analysis

Retrieve the two-dimensional structures of small-molecule ligands from the PubChem database. Utilize ChemOffice software to convert the 2D structures into 3D structures, and save the resulting files in mol2 format. Subsequently, evaluate protein targets utilizing the RCSB PDB database. Choose high-resolution crystal structures to serve as molecular receptors. Utilize Pymol 2.6 software to execute dehydration and dephosphorylation processes on proteins, subsequently saving them in PDB file format. Utilize Autodock 1.5 for the processing of protein and small molecule structures. Utilize AutoDockVina for molecular docking to investigate protein-ligand interactions. Utilize LigPlus and Pymol 2.6 to visualize docking results, producing 2D diagrams and 3D analysis plots that depict interactions between compounds and essential residues.

Animal experiments

32 male, 1-day-old male mallard ducks were procured from the Gaoyou Duck Breeding Base (Yangzhou, China). Male ducks were chosen to minimize potential confounding effects of estrogen levels on iron metabolism and oxidative stress regulation, which could interfere with the assessment of ferroptosis and the NRF2 pathway. Future studies are warranted to investigate sex-related differences in LBP's protective effects. Following a 7-day acclimation period, the mallard ducks were maintained in a temperature-controlled environment under standard conditions, featuring a 12-hour light/dark cycle, and were supplied with standard feed and tap water. All animal experiments received approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at Yangzhou University (Jiangsu, China). The choice of 10 mg/kg for PVC-MPs and 50 mg/kg for LBP was informed by existing literature and prior research (Gao et al., 2021; Kim et al., 2014; Song et al., 2025). The dosage of MPs is based on estimates of human microplastic consumption reported in the literature, with weekly intake estimated to range from 0.1 to 5 grams (Senathirajah et al., 2021). The dose of 10 mg/kg PVC-MPs was selected based on published estimates of human microplastic intake (0.1-5 g/week) after applying an allometric scaling factor to account for metabolic differences between humans and ducks. A 10-fold higher dose was used to simulate a toxic exposure scenario as previously described. The animals were allocated into four groups: a blank control group (basal diet + water), a PVC-MPs exposure group (basal diet + 10 mg/kg PVC-MPs diet + water), an LBP group (basal diet + 50 mg/kg LBP gavage), and a combined PVC-MPs and LBP exposure group, with eight replicates for each group. LBP was administrated daily via gavage for 30 days, while PVC-MPs were mixed into the basal feed continuously over the 4-week exposure period.

Collection of samples

Blood was taken from the ducks' mid-wing veins. The samples were then incubated at 37°C for 2 hours. Following this, the samples were centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 10 minutes to obtain the serum. The ducks were euthanized with intramuscular injections of thiopental (50 mg/kg) and ketamine (100 mg/kg). Kidney tissue was taken, and a portion was quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen for analysis. A second part was trimmed to the proper size and fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde or 2.5 % glutaraldehyde for future sectioning and staining or TEM sample preparation.

Oxidative stress enzyme assay

Measure a precise quantity of kidney tissue to create a 10 % tissue suspension. Centrifuge at 3500 rpm for 10 minutes, then collect the supernatant for analysis. The manufacturer's instructions were followed to quantify CREA, UA, MDA, GSH, and CAT levels using Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China) kits.

Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E), periodic acid-Schiff (PAS), Sirius red staining, and Prussian blue staining

Kidney tissue samples were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde, dehydrated through a gradient of ethanol solutions, and subsequently embedded in paraffin. Wax blocks were sectioned into 5 μm slices with a microtome and affixed to glass slides. The sections underwent incubation at 60°C for a duration of 2 hours. The sections were subsequently dewaxed with xylene for a duration of 10 minutes. Initially, the sections must undergo dehydration through a gradient ethanol series for a duration of 2 minutes, after which they should be immersed in water. For H&E staining, sections are subjected to hematoxylin staining for 2 minutes, followed by eosin staining for 1 minute.

Periodic acid solution is applied to the sections for a duration of 10 minutes during PAS staining. The sections undergo a 5-minute wash with distilled water. Following this, Schiff's reagent must be added and incubated at 37°C in the dark for 45 minutes. Hematoxylin is subsequently applied to stain the nuclei. For Sirius red staining, sections were incubated in hematoxylin for 6 minutes and then in Sirius red staining solution for 15 minutes. For Prussian blue staining, sections were incubated in Perls staining working solution for 20 minutes. Rinse with distilled water for 5 minutes. Sections were incubated in nuclear red staining solution for 10 minutes, then rinse with distilled water for 5 seconds. Sections were gently rinsed under running water, rehydrated in ethanol, cleared with xylene, and sealed with neutral resin. All samples were observed and photographed under a Leica light microscope (DMI3000B, Leica, Wetzlar, Germany) equipped with a digital camera.

TEM analysis

Kidney tissue samples were trimmed to an appropriate size and placed in 2.5 % glutaraldehyde for fixation, then dehydrated and embedded. Finally, the samples were sectioned by ultrathin microtome. The sections underwent staining with 3 % uranyl acetate and lead citrate, followed by observation using TEM (HT7800, Hitachi, Japan).

Determination of Fe content by flame atomic absorption spectroscopy (FAAS)

The dry duck kidney tissue was ground into a powder in a mortar, weighed for dry mass, and transferred to digestion tubes. Each tube received 5 mL of nitric acid and 1 mL of hydrogen peroxide for an overnight digestion process prior to instrument digestion. Following the removal of acids and a cooling period, the solution was diluted to a volume of 10 mL. The content of the analytes in the kidney tissue samples was measured using the FAAS technique (Optima 3700DV, PerkinElmer, USA).

q-PCR analysis

RNA extraction from duck kidney tissue was performed utilizing Trizol (Life Technologies, CA). cDNA synthesis was performed utilizing HiScript III RT SuperMix (Vazyme, China) in conjunction with gDNA Eraser. q-PCR was conducted utilizing the HiScript II One-Step qRT-PCR SYBR Green Kit (Vazyme, CN) on an Applied Biosystems 7500 (Life Technologies, CA). Relative expression levels were assessed with GAPDH serving as an internal control, and relative mRNA transcription levels were computed using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) assays

Sections were incubated with 3 % hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) at room temperature in the absence of light for 15 minutes. Sections underwent heat treatment at 95°C for antigen retrieval, followed by a 30-minute incubation with 5 % bovine serum albumin (BSA). Primary antibodies were incubated at 4°C for 24 hours. Following incubation, secondary antibodies were subjected to incubation at 37°C for a duration of 30 minutes. Sections were stained with 3,3′-dihydroxybenzidine (DAB). Samples were examined and documented using a Leica light microscope (DMI3000B, Leica, Wetzlar, Germany) with an integrated digital camera. Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software was used to measure the integrated optical density (IOD) of the IHC positive signals for semi-quantitative analysis.

Western Boltting

Kidneys were lysed, sonicated, and centrifuged with RIPA Lysis Buffer (#R0020, Beijing Solarbio Biotech Co., Ltd.), which included a Protein Phosphatase Inhibitor (100 x) (P1260, Beijing, China). The protein concentrations of the lysates were then quantified. Proteins were isolated using SDS-PAGE electrophoresis and subsequently transferred to a PVDF membrane (IPVH00010, Millipore, Billerica, USA). The membranes were blocked with 5 % skimmed milk for 2 hours and incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies. The samples were subsequently incubated with the appropriate secondary antibodies for 2 hours at room temperature. The bands were identified using a chemiluminescent solution (WBKLS0500, Millipore, Billerica, USA). The bands were subsequently measured and analyzed using ImageJ software and GraphPad Prism 9 software.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed on GraphPad Prism9. The experiment was conducted three times. The data were examined using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post‑hoc test. The data is presented as the average value plus or minus the standard deviation (SD). A p-value below 0.05 signifies statistical significance.

Results

The ferroptosis process regulated by the NRF2 pathway may represent a potential mechanism of action for LBP in treating AKI

To investigate the association between key components of LBP and AKI, target proteins of LBP's active components (D-glucose, d-galactose, l-rhamnose, Arabinose, Galacturonic acid, d-mannose) and AKI-related targets were imported into a bioinformatics website for cross-analysis, generating a Venn diagram. As shown in Fig. 1.C, a total of 107 overlapping targets were identified. Subsequently, these 107 overlapping targets were imported into Cytoscape software to form a PPI network. The analysis revealed that NRF2 and its downstream target proteins HO-1, SOD2, and NQO-1 served as key nodes, suggesting that NRF2 may be a critical target through which LBP exerts its antioxidant effects to mitigate nephrotoxicity (Fig. 1A-B). GO enrichment analysis indicated that the primary biological processes include stress-activated MAPK cascade, cellular response to tumor necrosis factor, negative regulation of inflammatory response, negative regulation of apoptotic process, and protein phosphorylation (Fig. 1D). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis primarily involved the Insulin signaling pathway, Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, FoxO signaling pathway, Apoptosis, Necroptosis, Cellular senescence, and MAPK signaling pathway (Fig. 1E). The Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM)-Active Component-Target-Disease-Pathway Network obtained consistent results (Fig. 1F). The findings suggest that LBP may have therapeutic effects on AKI by inhibiting ferroptosis via the activation of the NRF2 pathway. LBP alleviated PVC-MPs-induced kidney injury

This study examines the impact of PVC-MPs exposure on renal damage in ducks and the potential protective role of LBP. Seven-day-old male ducks were subjected to dietary PVC-MPs and administered LBP via gavage for a duration of 30 days (Fig. 3A). Initially, SEM and FTIR were employed to characterize the PVC microplastics used in this study. As shown in the Fig. 3C, 5 μm PVC-MPs exhibit uniform size, even distribution, and regular arrangement. FTIR analysis indicates that the MPs used in this study are made of polyvinyl chloride (Fig. 3B). No significant changes in body weight were observed among the groups when compared to the control group (Fig. 3D). However, both kidney weight and organ index were significantly reduced by PVC-MPs exposure (p < 0.01), while both kidney weight and organ coefficient were significantly restored in the LBP+PVC-MPs group (p < 0.05) (Fig. 3E&F). Furthermore, compared with the control group, exposure to PVC-MPs significantly increased levels of the renal function biochemical markers Crea and UA (p < 0.05), while the LBP+PVC-MPs group showed a significant decrease (p < 0.01) (Fig. 3G&H). LBP alleviated pathological damage, interstitial fibrosis and collagen fiber deposition induced by PVC-MPs exposure to the kidney

Recent studies indicate that exposure to MPs exacerbates kidney injury through multiple mechanisms, including oxidative stress, interstitial fibrosis, and fibrosis (Hong et al., 2024; Sayed et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025a). Histological staining is considered a highly reliable method for evaluating kidney injury. The renal tissue architecture of the control group exhibited clear definition, with no significant pathological alterations. Glomeruli maintained regular morphology with well-opened capillary loops. Renal tubular epithelial cells displayed uniform morphology, tightly packed arrangement, and distinct brush border structures. Exposure to PVC-MPs resulted in glomerular atrophy; shedding of renal tubular epithelial cells with loss of brush border; and inflammatory cell infiltration (Fig. 4A). Exogenous administration of LBP significantly reduced PVC-MPs-induced inflammatory cell infiltration and pathological damage in duck kidney. Additionally, as shown in Fig. 4B-C, exposure to PVC-MPs resulted in interstitial fibrosis in duck kidney. LBP alleviated PVC-MPs-induced renal ferroptosis

Ferroptosis is defined as an iron-dependent mechanism of programmed cell death, marked by the abnormal accumulation of lipid peroxides, depletion of GSH, and inhibition of GPX4 activity (Stockwell, 2022). Initially, total iron content within kidney was measured by FAAS. The results demonstrated that, exposure to PVC-MPs significantly increased total iron content in the kidney compared to the control group (p < 0.01) (Fig. 6A). Prussian blue staining results indicated that exposure to PVC-MPs significantly increased renal Fe3+ content (Fig. 6H). Exogenous addition of LBP significantly reduced total iron and Fe3+ content (p < 0.01). Furthermore, q-PCR results indicated that, in comparison to the control group, PVC-MPs significantly decreased the expression levels of GPX4 and FTH1, while significantly increasing the expression level of ACSL4 (p < 0.01) (Fig. 6B-D). Western blotting results yielded consistent conclusions. Exposure to PVC-MPs resulted in a significant reduction in the expression levels of GPX4, FTH1, and SLC7A11 compared to the control group, while simultaneously leading to a significant increase in the expression level of ACSL4 (p < 0.01) (Fig. 6E&F). Exogenous addition of LBP significantly alleviated ferroptosis activation induced by PVC-MPs exposure in duck kidneys (p < 0.01 or p < 0.05). IHC analysis of GPX4 expression also yielded consistent findings (Fig. 6G). Finally, TEM results indicated that nuclei in the control group displayed regular morphology, characterized by intact nuclear membranes and well-defined borders. Mitochondria maintained complete structures with clearly defined cristae. In contrast, nuclei in the PVC-MPs group appeared shrunken with marked aggregation of chromatin at the periphery. Mitochondria exhibited swelling and vacuolation, with indistinct cristae structures. The exogenous addition of LBP led to a significant improvement in ultrastructural damage in duck kidneys, as evidenced by the integrity of nuclear and mitochondrial structures (Fig. 6I). Discussion

As a plastic particle that is distributed widely, the toxicological effects of MPs have been extensively studied. The toxicological mechanisms of nephrotoxicity primarily focus on oxidative damage, inflammatory responses, lipid metabolism disorders, autophagy, apoptosis, and fibrosis (Tan et al., 2025; Zou et al., 2022). Ferroptosis was identified in 2012 as a distinct form of cell death characterized by its dependence on iron, accumulation of lipid peroxidation, and non-apoptotic nature (Dixon et al., 2012). The role of ferroptosis in kidney injury caused by PVC-MPs and the potential mechanisms involved are currently not well understood. The present study investigates the effects of PVC-MPs exposure on kidney injury in ducks and the potential protective effect of exogenous administration of LBP.

This study first constructed a “drug-disease-target” and PPI network. By collecting targets, conducting pathway analysis, and performing molecular docking, it investigated the potential mechanisms of LBP's nephrotoxicity. Analysis of PPI indicates that LBP may provide therapeutic benefits for AKI by inhibiting ferroptosis via the activation of the NRF2 pathway. The NRF2 pathway serves as a potential therapeutic target for LBP in the treatment of AKI. Although numerous overlapping targets exist among drug-disease-target and nephrotoxicity-target interactions, molecular docking results indicate that NRF2 and SOD2 constitute the key targets. KEGG pathway analysis suggests that LBP may exert therapeutic effects by modulating several signaling pathways, including the VEGF signaling pathway, ErbB signaling pathway, Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, FoxO signaling pathway, apoptosis, and necrotic apoptosis. These upstream pathways, such as Insulin and FoxO signaling, are closely associated with MAPK cascades, cellular senescence, and apoptosis, which are well-established regulators of oxidative stress and iron metabolism. Therefore, these pathways provide a theoretical basis for LBP's multi-target intervention against ferroptosis induced by PVC-MPs. It is important to note that our network pharmacology databases are predominantly based on human or mammalian data, while the PPI network was constructed specifically for "Anas platyrhynchos". This cross-species prediction presents certain limitations regarding target homology. However, the core NRF2/KEAP1 pathway is highly conserved across vertebrates. Future studies employing protein structural alignment or duck-specific omics datasets will be necessary to further validate these in silico predictions. In summary, LBP shows potential as both a preventive and therapeutic agent against PVC-MPs, operating through multiple pathways and targets.

In this study, it was revealed that PVC-MPs induced reduced kidney weight and organ coefficient in ducks, along with a sharp increase in serum Crea and UA levels, indicating the occurrence of kidney injury. The addition of LBP led to a substantial alleviation of PVC-MPs-induced renal injury in ducks, as evidenced by a decline in serum Crea and UA levels, and a recovery from the histopathological damage. Other studies indicate that various plant extracts can significantly reduce MPs-induced kidney injury, which may be related to the antioxidant activity of flavonoids (Deng et al., 2023; Umar Ijaz et al., 2025). Substantial evidence indicates that oxidative stress and ROS accumulation are implicated in the mechanism of MPs-induced nephrotoxicity in poultry. This study observed a significant reduction in the lipid peroxidation marker MDA levels and a notable increase in antioxidant enzyme activity in the LBP group compared to the PVC-MPs exposure group. The findings indicate that LBP may have a protective effect against oxidative stress induced by PVC-MPs. Prior research indicates that oxidative stress induces a conformational alteration in KEAP1, resulting in enhanced nuclear translocation of NRF2 and subsequent activation of various antioxidant response elements (AREs), including glutamate-cysteine ligase catalytic subunit (GCLC) and glutamate-cysteine ligase modifier subunit (GCLM) (Giulia et al., 2024). Our results showed PVC-MPs exposure significantly increased KEAP1 protein levels, which is consistent with the suppression of NRF2 activity and its downstream GCLC/GCLM/HO-1/SOD₂ targets. HO-1, a downstream antioxidant enzyme of NRF2, decreased in the PVC-MPs group and recovered with LBP treatment, further confirming the pivotal role of the NRF2/KEAP1 pathway in oxidative defense. The increase in KEAP1 protein while its mRNA level decreased may be attributed to the impaired ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation pathways under severe PVC-MPs-induced oxidative stress. Additionally, the autophagic protein p62 can competitively bind to KEAP1, leading to NRF2 dissociation and activation; however, in this study, the overwhelming oxidative stress may have disrupted this regulatory balance, leading to an accumulation of KEAP1 alongside NRF2 suppression. As a result, the protein expression levels of the downstream genes GCLC and GCLM are significantly diminished. In this study, exposure of the duck kidneys to PVC-MPs resulted in elevated total iron and Fe3+ levels, along with mitochondrial ultrastructural disruption, which provides direct evidence for the occurrence of ferroptosis. The administration of LBP resulted in a substantial alleviation of ferroptosis, as evidenced by a decrease in iron content and the restoration of mitochondrial structural integrity. As rate-limiting steps in GSH synthesis, decreased GCLC/GCLM levels cause a sharp decline in renal GSH levels, resulting in GSH depletion. Furthermore, downregulation of NRF2 may directly result in diminished expression of SLC7A11, thereby exacerbating GSH depletion. GSH functions as an essential cofactor for the catalytic reaction of GPX4, which is the sole and irreplaceable enzyme that prevents ferroptosis triggered by LPO (Geng et al., 2025). Oxidative stress upregulates ACSL4 expression, increasing the availability of phospholipid substrates susceptible to peroxidation in the cell membrane, thereby accelerating the occurrence of ferroptosis. Numerous studies have demonstrated the involvement of ferroptosis in toxic injury induced by MPs. Zhang et al. further confirmed that MPs in drinking water activate the intestinal metabolite arachidonic acid, which mediates the ACSL4/LPCAT3 signaling pathway to induce ferroptosis via the gut-kidney axis (Zhang, et al., 2025b).

Autophagy is a process that involves the degradation and recycling of intracellular biomolecules and damaged organelles (Kitada and Koya, 2021). Recent research findings support the notion that ferroptosis is an autophagy-dependent form of cell death, indicating a selective autophagy pathway (Zhu et al., 2025). NiCl₂ induces ferroptosis in murine renal tissues and cells, where autophagy plays a crucial role through degrading ferritin and accumulating iron (Yang et al., 2023). In this study, exposure to PVC-MPs was found to induce autophagy activation, as evidenced by increased expression levels of autophagy marker proteins LC3, and ATG5, as well as an increase in the number of autophagosomes. Levels of P62 protein expression demonstrated significant decrease, indicating unobstructed autophagy. This phenomenon may be attributed to the competitive binding of KEAP1 by P62, thereby facilitating NRF2 dissociation into the nucleus (Katsuragi et al., 2016). While the classical KEAP1‑NRF2 regulatory mechanism involves KEAP1‑mediated NRF2 degradation under basal conditions, this study observation that both proteins decreased simultaneously suggests additional layers of regulation may be involved, such as transcriptional repression or enhanced protein degradation of both components under prolonged oxidative stress. This also elucidates the mechanism by which MP exposure suppresses the NRF2/KEAP1 antioxidant pathway, thereby exacerbating oxidative stress. It is important to note that in the PVC-MPs group, NCOA4 protein was extensively localized in both the cytoplasm and the nucleus. The classical role of NCOA4 in ferritinophagy is primarily a cytoplasmic process where NCOA4 binds ferritin and delivers it to autophagosomes. However, mounting evidence suggests that NCOA4 also functions as a nuclear transcriptional coactivator for nuclear receptors (such as the androgen receptor) under stress conditions. The nuclear accumulation observed in our study may indicate a dual role of NCOA4, potentially contributing to transcriptional regulation during PVC-MPs-induced cellular stress, though its primary role in ferroptosis involves its cytoplasmic ferritin degradation function. It has been reported that NCOA4-mediated ferroptosis can exert an indirect influence on the protective capacity of GPX4 by regulating free iron levels (Jia, et al., 2024). This study found that the levels of the NCOA4 protein were increased by PVC-MPs, while LBP inhibited NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy, thereby alleviating PVC-MPs-induced iron accumulation and ferritin degradation, and protecting the kidneys from PVC-MPs-induced ferroptosis. Our study demonstrates that NCOA4-mediated excessive iron-dependent autophagy and ferroptosis may be a crucial mechanism in PVC-MPs exposure-induced nephrotoxicity, and that LBP can mitigate PVC-MPs-induced renal ferroptosis and fibrosis.

Compared to PS-MPs, which have been extensively studied for their nephrotoxic effects, PVC-MPs possess distinct physicochemical properties, including a higher chlorine content and the potential to release toxic additives such as phthalates during environmental degradation (Fung, et al., 2025). These differences may contribute to unique toxicological profiles. In contrast, our study demonstrates that PVC-MPs directly activate NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy in duck kidneys, leading to ferritin degradation, iron release, and subsequent ferroptosis. This suggests that although both types of microplastics can trigger ferroptosis, the upstream signaling mechanisms may differ (Wang, et al., 2026). Furthermore, PVC-MPs have been shown to cause more pronounced reductions in survival and body weight compared to PS-MPs in certain models (Peng, et al., 2023a). However, direct head-to-head comparative studies between PS-MPs and PVC-MPs in renal ferroptosis are currently lacking. Our findings highlight the need for future investigations to systematically compare the nephrotoxic potencies and mechanistic pathways of different microplastic polymers under environmentally relevant exposure conditions.

Despite the important finding of this study that LBP alleviates PVC-MPs-induced ferroptosis and fibrosis in duck kidneys through NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy, several limitations still need to be acknowledged. Firstly, this study was limited to an in vivo animal experiment and lacked validation in an in vitro cell model. Secondly, the animal experiment employed only male ducklings and a single dose of PVC-MPs (10 mg/kg) and LBP, without investigating sex differences, dose–response relationships, or the effects of long-term exposure. Finally, NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy was only indirectly demonstrated through protein expression and morphological observations, and reverse approaches such as autophagy inhibitors or NCOA4 knockdown were not used to confirm its causal relationship with ferroptosis.

Conclusion

In summary, this study employs network pharmacology to elucidate how LBP mitigates kidney injury via a multi-component, multi-target, and multi-pathway strategy. Network pharmacology and molecular docking results indicate that NRF2 exhibits strong interactions with the primary active components of LBP, positioning it as a potential therapeutic target for LBP in the treatment of AKI. Our study demonstrates that exposure to PVC‑MPs causes renal injury in ducks, characterized by oxidative stress, ferroptosis, and fibrosis. The underlying mechanism involves suppression of the NRF2/KEAP1 antioxidant pathway, leading to increased lipid peroxidation, iron accumulation, and downregulation of GPX4. Additionally, PVC‑MPs activates NCOA4‑mediated ferritinophagy, which degrades ferritin and releases free iron, thereby exacerbating ferroptosis. Notably, dietary supplementation with LBP effectively alleviates PVC‑MPs‑induced renal damage by attenuating oxidative stress, preserving GPX4 expression, reducing iron overload, and inhibiting NCOA4‑mediated ferritinophagy (Fig. 8).

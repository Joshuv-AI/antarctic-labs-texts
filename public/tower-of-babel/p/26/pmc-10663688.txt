Huge primary scrotal lipoma: Case report and review of literature
Authors: Ibrahim Ballout, Rabih Awad, Bachar Al Ali, Linda Chamma, Hussein Issa, Mohamad Moussa
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Primary scrotal lipomas are rare findings in urological practice. We present a case of a scrotal mass in a young patient, found to have a huge scrotal lipoma. He was diagnosed using imaging modalities and surgically managed with no further complications.

Introduction

Scrotal masses encountered in adult male patients are of clinical importance due to their dominant neoplastic risk if found originating from the testes.1 In contrast, para-testicular tumors are frequently benign with lipomas being the most commonly diagnosed.1 Primary scrotal lipoma is a rare finding, where a large tumor develops from the subcutaneous fat of the scrotal sac.2 Clinical examination of the scrotum is crucial in guiding the diagnosis, but the need for more evaluation, such as imaging and blood tests can aid in the proper identification of the etiology.3 As in our case, palpation of the scrotum might not tell apart testicular from para-testicular tumors which highlight the need for further workup. Here, we report a case of primary scrotal lipoma with review of literature.

Case presentation

A 23-year-old male, previously healthy, presented to our center with a lump in the scrotum. He first noticed the lump 11 month prior to presentation. During this period of time, it increased with size considerably until it was of concern for the patient. Physical exam revealed an irreducible, non-tender, rubbery mass at the midline level of posterior scrotum. The palpation of both testes showed no abnormal findings.

Scrotal ultrasound showed a hyperechoic homogenous solid mass separated from the testicles. MRI was ordered and it demonstrated a 12.7 × 2.5 × 5.8 cm large oval-shaped minimally septated non-enhanced lipoma, seen posterior to the testicles and extending till the inferior perineum [Fig. 1]. Testes were otherwise unremarkable. A decision for surgical excision was made. During surgery, a midline inferior scrotal incision was performed. An encapsulated lipoma was found within the scrotal wall [Fig. 2]. The tumor was then externalized and excised en mass without opening the tunica vaginalis [Fig. 3]. Histological description highlighted a benign adipose tissue arranged in lobules, separated by thin fibrous septae diagnostic of lipoma [Fig. 4]. No signs of malignancy or anaplasia were seen. Discussion

Although most of testicular lesions are malignant, the majority of para-testicular lesions are benign, with lipomas being the most frequently found.4 Scrotal lipomas can be classified according to their site of origin: (a) posterior to the spermatic cord, (b) from the spermatic cord, (c) from dartos tunica of the scrotum.5 Our case discusses a lipoma originating from the scrotal wall, hence the name “primary scrotal lipoma”.

Only few cases of primary scrotal lipoma are described in the literature. The majority of these cases report huge lipomas, up to 9kg in weight, rapidly growing, with size-related symptoms.6 Imaging modalities used in the diagnosis are ultrasound (US), computed tomography (CT) scan, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). As first line of examination, US can detect the location of the lump and outline its content, whether cystic or solid, eliminating unexpected findings during surgery.7 MRI is cardinal in distinguishing benign masses from malignant ones. For instance, unlike liposarcomas, lipomas of the scrotum exhibit encapsulated mass with high T1 signals and no enhancements.6

Fine needle aspiration (FNA) and surgical biopsies are of limited usage and cannot exclude malignancy due to the huge size of the mass. As such, the presence of a well encapsulated mass with no features of malignancy suffice to perform surgical excision through scrotal incision.

Conclusion

It is evident to know that most of the Para-testicular tumors are of benign nature, with a small percentage falling into malignant type such as liposarcomas. While MRI demonstrates an excellent modality to confirm primary scrotal lipomas, US fails to confirm the diagnosis due to the huge tumoral size. With histology confirming the final diagnosis, surgical excision is the definitive approach.

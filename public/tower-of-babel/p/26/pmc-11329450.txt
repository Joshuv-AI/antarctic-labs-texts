Utilizing Liquid–liquid phase separation-related lncRNAs to predict the prognosis and treatment response of PCa
Authors: Jiangping Qiu, Cong Lai, Zhihan Yuan, Jintao Hu, Jiang Wu, Cheng Liu, Kewei Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStudies have indicated a close association between genes linked to liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS) and the progression of prostate cancer (PCa). However, the interplay among long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) linked to LLPS in PCa remains elusive. Therefore, we constructed a prediction model based on LLPS-related LncRNA in PCa to explore its relationship with the prognosis and drug treatment of PCa.MethodsWe obtained clinical and sequencing data from TCGA and LLPS genes from the Phase Separation Protein Database. By analyzing the differential expression of LLPS-related genes and lncRNAs in prostate cancer, and using Poisson correlation, we identified LLPS-related lncRNAs. Prognostic LLPS-lncRNAs were found through prognostic correlation analysis and included in a Cox model to compute regression coefficients. Patients were scored and divided into high- and low-risk groups. Independent prognostic factors were integrated into a prognostic nomogram with risk and Gleason scores. We also conducted drug sensitivity analyses, GSEA, and validated the impact of key lncRNAs through functional experiments.ResultsOur study identified five LLPS-associated lncRNAs that are of prognostic importance. And found notable disparities in biochemical recurrence rates and survival outcomes between these risk groups, with the low-risk cohort exhibiting superior prognostic indicators. Moreover, our prediction nomogram demonstrated robust predictive accuracy and significant clinical utility. Furthermore, our model exhibited promising capabilities in forecasting patient sensitivity to various conventional therapeutic drugs, thereby highlighting its potential in personalized treatment strategies. GSEA showed that these lncRNAs may influence PCa prognosis and sensitivity to therapeutic agents by affecting pathways such as cell cycle. Knockdown of AC009812.4 could inhibit the ability of PCa cells to proliferate, migrate and invade, and compare to paracancerous tissue, AC009812.4 in PCa tissue has significantly higher expression.ConclusionOur research uncovers the prognostic significance of lncRNAs associated with LLPS in PCa and established a model exhibiting excellent predictive accuracy for prognosis. Those lncRNAs may influence progress of PCa as well as sensitivity to therapy drugs through pathways such as cell cycle.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01226-3.Keywords: PCa, LLPS, LncRNA, Prognostic model, Drug sensitivity

Introduction

PCa ranks as the second most common cancer diagnosed in men worldwide and stands as a prominent contributor to cancer-related mortality [1]. While PCa generally progresses at a relatively slower rate than some other malignancies, it presents a diverse spectrum of treatment modalities. These treatment options, including surgical interventions, hormone therapy, and chemotherapy, cater to the specific diagnostic and therapeutic needs of patients across various clinical stages and risk profiles. This comprehensive array of interventions underscores the importance of personalized care and underscores the multifaceted nature of PCa management [2]. Nonetheless, the occurrence of biochemical recurrence persists as a notable hazard element influencing the prognosis and longevity of individuals with PCa. This recurrence notably diminishes their standard of living and rates of survival without disease, inflicting considerable physical and psychological distress, and imposing substantial economic burdens [3]. Therefore, the necessity of accurately predicting prognosis becomes apparent.

LncRNA is characterized by its length, typically exceeding 200 nucleotides. Unlike protein-coding RNA, lncRNA does not encode proteins; however, it serves crucial functions in gene regulation and cellular processes [4]. In PCa research, lncRNA has been widely studied. By regulating gene expression, chromatin remodeling, and cell cycle regulation, lncRNA participates in various stages of PCa, including its initiation, progression, and metastasis [5]. In recent years, researchers have discovered many lncRNAs related to PCa, such as MALAT1, PCAT-1, PCA3, etc. [6–8]. The dysregulated expression of these lncRNAs in PCa correlates closely with patients' clinical and pathological features, as well as their prognosis [9]. Concurrently, studies have identified specific lncRNAs with potential as diagnostic indicators and targets for therapy, offering novel avenues for early detection and treatment of PCa [10]. However, despite some progress, the mechanisms and functions of lncRNAs related to PCa still need further research and clarification in order to be better applied in clinical practice.

In recent years, one of the focuses of cancer research has gradually shifted towards the creation of prognostic survival models and prediction of drug sensitivity. These models aid in gaining deeper insights into tumor progression and treatment responsiveness. Through the analysis of gene or lncRNA expression variances between tumor tissue and neighboring normal tissue, researchers can pinpoint potential biomarkers, thus facilitating the development of prognostic survival models and prediction of drug sensitivity [11, 12]. These models can not only provide estimates of patient prognosis, but also predict patient resistance to specific treatment regimens. At the same time, the robustness of these models has also been strengthened, making them more widely applicable in clinical practice. In addition, with the continuous deepening of research, more and more related predictive determinants have been incorporated into the model, further improving the accuracy of prognosis estimation. These advances provide new avenues for personalized healthcare, with the potential to promote precision and optimization in cancer treatment.

For PCa, cuproptosis-related, autophagy-related, and immune-related lncRNAs have been found to be markers for early clinical diagnosis and prognosis prediction [13–15]. Recent research has highlighted the significant involvement of lncRNAs in LLPS [16]. LLPS has been extensively studied by researchers in recent years as a new principle of precise spatiotemporal regulation in living cells. Many studies have found that phase separation can participate in the occurrence and development of tumors by regulating gene expression, participating in DNA damage and repair, regulating proteasome degradation, and regulating metabolic flux [17]. Despite acknowledging the involvement of LLPS-related genes and lncRNAs in tumor prognosis, it's imperative to note the limited extent of research in this domain. This paper originates from this crucial gap in knowledge. Investigating lncRNAs associated with LLPS holds fundamental significance in accurately predicting PCa prognosis. Thus, in order to explore its clinical relevance, we aimed to develop a nomogram incorporating LLPS-related lncRNAs and clinical parameters to forecast PCa prognosis. Furthermore, we endeavored to probe into the potential functional pathways of LLPS-related lncRNAs in PCa using GSEA.

Materials and methods

Data collection and processing

FPKM format of RNA sequencing data and comprehensive clinical information related to PCa were obtained from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). Additionally, we acquired LLPS-related gene sets from the PhaSepDB database (http://db.phasep.pro/). Employing stringent criteria, where |log2FC|> 0.5 and P < 0.05 (Bonferroni corrections), we then utilized the "edgeR" package to perform differential expression analysis among LLPS-related genes and lncRNAs when comparing tumor and adjacent tissues. Following this, we delved into evaluating the correlation existing between LLPS-related genes and lncRNAs, utilizing the Poisson correlation coefficient for accurate assessment. The criteria for filtering LLPS-related lncRNA is through correlation coefficients of |R2|> 0.3 and P < 0.05 (Bonferroni corrections).

To identificate LLPS-related lncRNAs associate with prognosis for risk score model’construction and validation

We employed univariate Cox regression analysis to scrutinize the clinical survival data in conjunction with lncRNAs. This examination allowed us to assess the potential correlation between the expression levels of lncRNAs and the overall prognosis of patients. Following this analysis, lncRNAs that exhibited significant correlations with prognosis were subjected to further refinement through LASSO regression analysis.

Utilizing the Area Under the Curve (AUC) metric, we evaluated the performance of the scoring model, allowing for the computation of personalized risk scores for individual patients. Upon calculation of these risk scores, patients were stratified into distinct high and low-risk groups based on the median risk score threshold. To elucidate the prognostic disparities between these stratified groups, we employed Kaplan–Meier survival curves, providing a comprehensive visualization of survival outcomes over time. Furthermore, the risk curves and scatter plots provided detailed insights into the relationship between risk scores and the survival status of each individual sample.

Construction and aessement nomogram

In both univariate and multivariate Cox analyses, factors including Gleason Score, Age, risk scores, and TN were comprehensively examined to discern their intricate associations with prognosis. Subsequently, based on the results derived from multivariate Cox analysis, a nomogram was formulated. The nomogram underwent evaluation, encompassing the determination of decision curve analysis (DCA), the C-index, the plotting of the AUC, and calibration plot.

Sensitivity analysis of drugs

Leveraging the cancer drug sensitivity genomics (CDSG) Ridge Regression Model, accessible through the database (https://www.cancerRx-gene.org), we derived the drug sensitivity values of particular drugs within the TCGA-PRAD dataset by employing the R package. Following this, a wilcox test was employed to evaluate the differences in drug sensitivity values of these drugs between the identified high-risk and low-risk groups.

Gene-set enrichment analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) gene sets were obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted using GSEA software.

Cell lines and cell culture

The PC-3 and DU145 prostate cancer cell lines, acquired from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, were nurtured in RPM-1640 medium (Gibco, NY, USA). This medium, enriched with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, BI, Israel), facilitated cell growth by providing essential nutrients and growth factors. Moreover, the medium contained 100 mg/ml streptomycin and 100 U/ml penicillin to safeguard against bacterial contamination and ensure cell health. The cells were maintained in a humidified CO2 incubator at a constant temperature of 37 ℃.

RNA Interference

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) duplexes synthesized and purified by GenePharma (Suzhou, China). Use six-well plates to seed 1 × 105cells of PC3 and DU145 and culture them until they reached a confluence of 50–70%. Then use Lipofectamine iMAX (Invitrogen, USA) for transfection operations,see the product specification for details.SiRNA and NC sequence is inside Supplementary Material.

cDNA synthesis and qRT-PCR

The process of synthesizing complementary DNA (cDNA) for gene expression analysis commenced with the utilization of the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara, Shiga, Japan). Following this, quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed by combining the cDNA samples with TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Takara, Shiga, Japan). The 2^−ΔΔCT method was then employed to determine the relative expression levels, where mRNA expression was normalized to GAPDH, functioning as the internal control. For detailed primer information, please refer to the Supplementary materials.The synthesis of complementary DNA (cDNA) for gene expression analysis was conducted using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara, Shiga, Japan). This procedure involved extracting RNA from the samples of interest and subsequently reverse transcribing it into cDNA using the aforementioned kit. Following this, quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed to analyze the gene expression levels. This involved combining the cDNA samples with TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Takara, Shiga, Japan). The utilization of the 2^−ΔΔCT method allowed for the determination of relative expression levels, with GAPDH serving as the internal control for normalization purposes. The detailed information regarding the primers used in the qRT-PCR assays can be found in the Supplementary materials accompanying this study.

CCK8, colony formation assays

In conducting CCK-8 assays, 2 × 10^3 cells derived from PCa were seeded into designated wells of 96-well plates. These cells were subjected to a cyclic incubation regimen with CCK-8 solution (APExBIO, TX, USA) every 24 h, consistently following the manufacturer's instructions, over a span of 5 consecutive days. In parallel, for the execution of colony formation assays, an aliquot containing 1 × 10^3 PCa cells was carefully dispensed into each well of 6-well plates. Following this seeding process, the cells were nurtured and cultured within the confines of the plates for a prolonged duration of 2 weeks. Upon the completion of the incubation period, the colonies arising from the cultured cells were immobilized through treatment with a 4% paraformaldehyde solution. Subsequently, to visualize these colonies and assess their proliferation, they were stained utilizing a 0.1% crystal violet solution.

Transwell assays

In the Transwell assays for migration and invasion, 5 × 10^4 cells originating from PCa were meticulously suspended in medium(serum-free) and introduced into the upper chambers of the 24-well Transwell apparatus (Corning, MA, USA). Chambers were precoated with Matrigel (Corning, MA, USA), while others were left uncoated, thus allowing for the evaluation of both migration and invasion capabilities. In the lower chambers, a nutrient-rich medium supplemented with 10% FBS was introduced. Following an incubation period of 24 h (for DU145 cells) or 48 h (for PC-3 cells), the cells were meticulously fixed using a 4% paraformaldehyde solution to preserve their spatial arrangement. To visualize the cells that had migrated or invaded, staining with 0.1% crystal violet was performed. Following staining, a comprehensive analysis was carried out to quantify the number of cells that migrated successfully to the lower chamber. This involved counting cells present in five randomly selected fields under a Leica DM2000 microscope (Leica Camera AG, Wetzlar, Germany), ensuring accurate representation and statistical significance of the migration and invasion capabilities of the PCa cells.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were conducted utilizing both R software and GraphPad Prism (version 7.03). The P-value of the statistical analysis in this study was used Bonferroni corrections.

Development and validation of the risk score model

The data extracted from TCGA patients underwent a division into two distinct subsets: a training set and a validation set, adhering to a strict ratio of 2:1. Within the training cohort, the coefficients for the selected lncRNAs were calculated utilizing a multivariate Cox regression analysis, thereby facilitating the establishment of a comprehensive risk score model. Following this, leveraging the individualized results obtained, both the PCa training and validation sets sourced from TCGA were categorized into discrete high-risk and low-risk groups, stratified based on the median value. This categorization process ensured a examination and validation of the developed risk score model across diverse patient populations, thereby enhancing the robustness and generalizability of the findings.

Upon examination, significant differences were noted in biochemical recurrence, number of BCR, and risk scores between groups which were categorized into higher and lower risk categories in both the training and validation sets. Specifically, the high-risk group exhibited higher rates of biochemical recurrence (Fig. 3A, B), along with a greater number of BCR (Fig. 3C, D), and elevated risk scores (Fig. 3E, F). These findings underscored the unfavorable prognosis associated with the high-risk group relative to the low-risk counterpart.

Construction and aessement nomogram

In our pursuit of enhancing the model's predictive capacity, we delved deeper into identifying potential clinical predictors. Univariate Cox analysis uncovered several factors, including age, T and N stage, Gleason score, and risk score, which emerged as significant risk factors for PCa patients' BRFS (Fig. 4A). Furthermore, through multivariate Cox analysis, we confirmed the Gleason score's status as an independent risk factor for BRFS in PCa patients (Fig. 4B). Based on these findings, we devised a prognostic nomogram integrating both the Gleason score and risk score (Fig. 4C), aiming to provide clinicians with a comprehensive tool for prognosis assessment.In the prognostic analysis of the training and validation groups, this prognostic nomogram showed a significant difference in outcome(Fig. 4D, E).

To validate the efficacy of our nomogram prediction model, we assessed its performance on both the training (Fig. 4F, L, J, H) and validation sets (Fig. 4G, I, K, M). The calibration curves for the nomograms, predicting the 3- and 5-year BRFS of PCa patients, underscored the model's satisfactory accuracy. Furthermore, employing DCA curves for the nomograms across 3- and 5-year BRFS predictions demonstrated significant clinical utility, highlighting the potential benefits for PCa patients.

Finally, the ROC curve analysis further corroborated the predictive model's effectiveness, reaffirming its ability to accurately predict outcomes. In summary, our comprehensive assessment and validation of the prognostic nomogram underscore its potential as a valuable tool for clinicians in prognosticating and managing PCa patients, offering insights into optimizing treatment strategies and improving patient outcomes.

Sensitivity analysis of chemotherapy drugs

In addition to verifying the impact of our model on prognosis, we also examined whether there is predictive value in the sensitivity difference of the model to PCa chemotherapy drugs. The results showed a significant difference in sensitivity to chemotherapy drugs such as Cyclophosphamide、Vinblastine、Cisplatin、Docetaxel、 Epirubicin and Talazoparib. The high-rated group displayed decreased drug sensitivity compared to the low-rated group (Fig. 5A–F). This suggests that our risk score has potential guiding value in the selection of chemotherapy drugs for clinical patients, and also suggests that the LncRNAs that make up our risk score may be involved in the mechanism of action of these chemotherapy drugs and are potential targets for drug-resistant treatment.

Discussion

With the widespread adoption and advancement of high-throughput sequencing technology, researchers now have unprecedented access to an ever-expanding wealth of genomic sequencing data. This influx of data not only opens doors to discovering new potential therapeutic targets but also empowers researchers to identify differentially expressed genes, providing invaluable insights for tumor diagnosis and prognosis assessment. Despite the establishment of comprehensive evaluation methods, including pathological staging, PSA risk stratificationGleason score assessment,and clinical staging, which serve as crucial benchmarks for personalized treatment decisions for the majority of patients, the challenges in treatment and prognosis persist.

Therefore, there is an increasing recognition of the need to harness emerging genomic data to develop and refine precision treatment strategies further. These strategies aim to enhance patient survival rates and improve the quality of life by tailoring treatments to individual patients' genetic profiles and specific tumor characteristics. By leveraging the wealth of genomic data available, researchers can explore innovative avenues for targeted therapies and personalized interventions, ultimately striving towards more effective cancer management and improved patient outcomes.

Monitoring small recurrences and metastatic lesions in cancer is crucial for treatment prognosis, yet challenges such as biochemical recurrence add uncertainty to the treatment process [3]. According to reports, approximately half of high-risk PCa patients encounter biochemical recurrence despite undergoing aggressive treatment [18]. This phenomenon may be attributed to significant individual variances among PCa cases, suggesting standardized treatment approaches may not be universally effective [19]. Therefore, developing more individualized predictive models is essential for determining patient treatment plans and prognosis assessment. Such models would incorporate various factors including clinical-pathological characteristics, molecular markers, and imaging results to more accurately predict disease progression and survival, providing physicians with better decision-making tools to enhance treatment outcomes and quality of life for patients.

Age, Gleason score, and TNM staging are crucial for predicting PCa prognosis and have significant clinical implications. Generally speaking, older patients tend to have poorer prognosis, which may be due to the presence of more comorbidities, less PSA testing, later cancer staging, and less treatment received [20]. Gleason score is the main indicator for evaluating the histological grading of tumors, which directly reflects the degree of malignancy of cancer [2]. In our study, a risk scoring model was formulated based on five LLPS-related lncRNAs, revealing their significant correlation with BRFS in PCa. Furthermore, thorough analyses were conducted to assess the Gleason Score as an independent prognostic factor for PCa, utilizing both univariate and multivariate Cox regression methodologies. Consequently, the incorporation of both the risk score and Gleason Score into the construction of a Nomogram substantially enhanced the predictive performance of the BRFS prediction curve,offering improved accuracy in prognostic evaluation for PCa patients. Subsequent validation shows that our risk scoring model not only has strong evaluation capabilities, but also exhibits good performance, which can provide strong support for clinical practice.

In addition to exploring the prediction of patient prognosis using the model we constructed, we also applied our scoring model to the analysis of drug sensitivity in patients. Our results found that the risk scoring model can perform significant differential analysis on patient sensitivity to drug use, such as common chemotherapy drugs such as docetaxel and vinblastine. This indicates that our model can not only assist in clinical prognosis judgment, but also provide guidance on patient medication selection, In previous studies, it was also found that lncRNA can affect the occurrence of docetaxel resistance in PCa [21, 22]. To explore the underlying mechanisms of our model's impact on patient prognosis and drug sensitivity, gene enrichment analysis was performed, revealing a significant correlation between the risk score and the activation and inhibition of specific pathways, including the cell cycle, translation, and cell communication. Many studies have shown that the activities of cytoskeleton related proteins that occur during the cell cycle, such as microtubule polymerization and spindle assembly, are closely related to phase separation. These results suggest that the lncRNAs that make up our risk score may form functional structures within the nucleus or cytoplasm through phase separation, such as endosomes or RNA protein complexes. These structures can regulate gene expression, RNA processing, and protein translation, thereby affecting the behavior of prostate cancer cells. Alternatively, structures formed through phase separation can recruit or isolate specific transcription factors, chromatin modifying enzymes, or signaling molecules to finely regulate gene expression and signal transmission, promoting or inhibiting cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Interacting with proteins in key signaling pathways to alter their activity or stability may also be one of the potential regulatory mechanisms [23–26].This indicates from one aspect that the lncRNAs we have screened are indeed related to phase separation. Through verification of the function of AC009812.4 on PCa related cell lines and detection at the genetic level of PCa patients, it was found that AC009812.4, as analyzed by the database, can contribute to the initiation and progression of PCa. Our next research direction can be to further explore the impact of these five lncRNAs related to the model on the occurrence and development of PCa, as well as their impact on drug resistance in PCa treatment, and their specific mechanisms.

In summary, our study has successfully pinpointed five LLPS-related lncRNAs intricately linked to the prognosis of PCa, and we have developed a prognostic prediction model based on these findings. Additionally, the model demonstrates the capability to predict patients' sensitivity to certain therapeutic drugs. Moreover, our investigation has uncovered the involvement of these lncRNAs in influencing both PCa prognosis and drug sensitivity through diverse pathways, suggesting promising avenues for future research exploration.

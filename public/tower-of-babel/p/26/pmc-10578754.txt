Evaluating the potential of matrix metalloproteinase as a diagnostic biomarker in rheumatoid arthritis and periodontitis: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Aimen Batool, Rathna Devi Vaithilingam, Norul Husna Mohamad Hassan, Syarida Hasnur Safii, Roslan Saub
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) play a crucial role in the pathogenesis of several chronic diseases including rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and periodontitis (PD). RA patients with periodontitis (RA-PD) are associated with elevated inflammatory burden due to increased production of proinflammatory cytokines. Controlling upregulated MMPs activity in these patients may have potential therapeutic effects. Therefore, aim of this study is to address the focused question: “Do RA subjects with concurrent PD have different levels of MMPs in comparison to RA alone, PD alone and HC subjects?”Methods:The systematic review was performed following Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. A search from 4 electronic databases (EMBASE, Medline, Web of Science, and Cochrane library) and manual search was performed from inception to July 2023. Quality assessment of each article was done using Newcastle-Ottawa Scale. Meta-analyses derived results were summarized as standardized mean difference (SMD) with 95% confidence intervals.Results:A total of 879 articles were extracted. Following screening and full text assessment, 9 studies were included. MMP-1, MMP-3, MMP-8, MMP-9, and MMP-13 were consistently elevated in RA-PD subjects. MMP-8 levels were found to be higher in RA-PD subjects compared with RA alone, PD alone, and HC in 3 studies reporting GCF levels (SMD = 1.2; Z = 2.07; P = .04) and 2 studies reporting serum levels (SMD = 0.87; Z = 4.53; P < .00001).Conclusion:RA-PD group showed significantly higher MMP levels in their serum and GCF compared with HC, RA, and PD alone individuals. MMP-8 may serve as a reliable biomarker in the diagnosis and management of RA-PD subjects.

1. Introduction

Periodontal disease (PD) was listed as the 11th most prevalent condition with its severe form affecting more than 10 percent of the global population.[1] PD is a chronic non-communicable multifactorial disease in susceptible individuals triggered by bacterial insult over extended period of time.[2] The evolution in periodontal medicine has proposed the association between PD and other systemic conditions such as diabetes, myocardial infarction, osteoporosis, Alzheimer disease, and arthritis.[3] On the other hand, rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a systemic inflammatory disease which causes pain and disability due to unknown etiology. It affects approximately 0.3% to 1% of the world population.[4,5] Numerous studies across the world have found a significant link between RA and PD due to their similar pathological features.[6–11] Adults with RA are 13% more likely to develop PD than non-RA control individuals and vice versa.[12]

Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are members of an enzyme family that are involved in the remodeling and degradation of the extracellular matrix and have the potential to modulate the inflammatory response in systemic diseases and conditions. Among 23 different group of MMPs, the gelatinase family (MMP-2 and MMP-9) is upregulated causing matrix breakdown in joints of the patient.[13] Moreover, MMP-1 and MMP-3 are also expected to play essential role in the pathogenesis of RA and have been proposed as marker of disease activity and joint destruction.[14] Similarly, MMPs also played a crucial role in tissue remodeling initiated by anaerobic bacteria in PD.[15,16] The most frequently reported MMP member from the collagenases are MMP-1, MMP-8 and MMP-13 in which MMP-8 contributes about 80% of the entire collagenase protein identified in gingival crevicular fluid (GCF) with smaller relative amount of MMP-13 and MMP-1 in PD.[17] However, MMP inhibitors may have therapeutic potential for diseases like RA and PD.

Patients with concurrent RA and PD (RA-PD) are linked with elevated inflammatory burden due to large production of proinflammatory cytokines.[18] Several observational studies have investigated cytokines and MMP levels using samples of serum, saliva and GCF either in individuals diagnosed with RA alone, PD alone or with both conditions respectively.[19–26] In this context, it is speculated that MMP levels either in serum, saliva and GCF may be elevated in RA-PD subjects as compared to RA alone, PD alone and healthy control (HC) group due to the double burden of both chronic non-communicable diseases. To the best of author knowledge, there is still no comprehensive systematic and meta-analysis assessment conducted to evaluate the cumulative difference of MMP levels in serum, saliva and GCF, between individuals with RA-PD and those with RA alone, PD alone and HC. Hence, to fill the gap in the existing available literature, the current study intended to summarize the available scientific evidence concerning the level of MMPs in GCF, serum and saliva of RA-PD patients compared to RA alone, PD alone and HC.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1. Protocol, registration, and ethical aspect

This systematic review was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA guidelines[27] and registered with the National Institute for Health Research PROSPERO, International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews under the ID Number: CRD42020178726 (http://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO). This study was carried out without any direct involvement of humans as the data was obtained from available published studies. Thus, ethical approval by the Medical Ethics Board of the Faculty of Dentistry, Universiti Malaya was deemed not required to conduct this study.

2.2. Focused question

The focused question was formulated to determine the level of MMPs in rheumatic subjects with PD based on the PECO criteria (population, exposure, comparison, and outcome)[28] as following:

“Do RA subjects with concurrent PD (RA-PD) have different levels of MMPs as compared to RA alone, PD alone and HC subjects?”

whereby

(P): Adults

(E): RA-PD

(C): RA alone, PD alone and HC subjects

(O): Different level of MMPs in serum, saliva and GCF

2.3. Eligibility criteria

The studies included in this review had met the following inclusion criteria:

•Observational studies and clinical trials in humans.

Observational studies and clinical trials in humans.

•RA-PD patients compared to HC and/or PD alone and/or RA alone.

RA-PD patients compared to HC and/or PD alone and/or RA alone.

•Evaluated MMPs level in GCF, serum and saliva.

Evaluated MMPs level in GCF, serum and saliva.

•English language articles published until July 2023.

English language articles published until July 2023.

The exclusion criteria were in vitro studies, animal studies, case reports, case series, systematic reviews or meta-analysis, unpublished work, unclear diagnosis of RA and PD, studies that have not reported MMPs but other cytokines, letter to the editors, updates, interviews, and commentaries.

2.4. Information source and search strategy

The articles retrieved from the 4 electronic databases (EMBASE, Medline, Cochrane Library and Web of Science) addressing the focused question were searched from inception till 20th July 2023. Medline and EMBASE databases were retrieved via OVID interface which allowed for simultaneous searches of controlled vocabulary, combination of MeSH (Medical Subject Heading) terms, and Emtree. A systematic method to search literature was adopted to find the relevant articles that compared MMP levels in GCF, serum and/or saliva of RA-PD with RA, PD and HC subjects.

Searches in electronic database were carried out using various combinations of the following search strategy such as “periodont*,” “rheumatoid arthritis,” “matrix metalloproteinase*” OR “metalloproteinase*” and limited to humans. The reference list of the previous systematic review and included original articles was also screened for relevant studies that could have been missed during the electronic search. Hand search of the following journals were also conducted: Journal of Clinical Periodontology, Journal of Periodontology, Journal of Periodontal Research, Journal of Dental Research. Authors of the shortlisted studies were contacted to retrieve the missing data via email. When they did not reply after 2 email notifications, the study was excluded from the review.

2.5. Study selection

Initial screening (stage 1) of the titles and abstracts of the articles were done independently by 2 reviewers (AB, NH) based on the inclusion criteria. Following this, a detailed examination (stage 2) of the full-text articles was carried out in duplicate (AB, NH) to assess the quality and study characteristics of each potential eligible paper. Disagreements in the study selection between the 2 reviewers at each stage were resolved through discussion with a third reviewer (SHS). The level of agreement between reviewers for potentially relevant articles was calculated using Cohen kappa (k) test. The kappa value for inter-reviewer agreement was 0.80 and 0.89 for stage 1 and stage 2, respectively.

2.6. Risk of bias (quality) assessment

The methodological quality assessment was separately assessed by the same reviewers (AB, NH) using the Newcastle Ottawa Scale, which is a validated and tested scale used to evaluate observational and non-randomized studies.[29] For the included studies, a star system was utilized to analyze the studies from 3 broad points: study group selection (maximum 4 stars), group comparability (maximum 2 stars), and ascertainment of either the exposure or result of interest (maximum 3 stars), respectively. Studies that scored 7 to 9 stars on the scale were considered to be of high quality, studies that rated 5 to 6 stars were judged as medium quality, while studies that scored <5 stars were deemed to be of low quality. Any disagreements were also resolved through discussion with the third reviewer (SHS).

2.7. Data extraction

Data from the included studies were extracted by the main reviewer (AB) into an Excel spreadsheet. The information obtained were tabulated according to name of first author, year of publication, origin/country, study type, subjects’ characteristics, RA and PD diagnostic criteria, other possible confounding factors such as smoking status, clinical parameters of PD and RA, type of sample used for detecting MMPs, and the main outcomes of each study. Data extraction was done based on targeted question framed for the current systematic review.

2.8. Statistical analysis

A meta-analysis was performed whereby the standardized mean difference (SMD) between groups was analyzed using random effects model. Heterogeneity was estimated using Q-statistics and quantified with I2 statistics. Values up to 25% were categorized as low heterogeneity, values between 50% to 75% as medium heterogeneity, and values beyond 75% indicates considerable heterogeneity.[30] The alpha level was adjusted at α = 0.05. Forest plots were generated to visualize estimates with 95% confidence intervals. All statistical analyses were calculated in Review Manager software, version 5.4 (https://training.cochrane.org/ online-learning/core-software-cochrane-reviews/revman).

2.9. Publication bias

Publication bias was meant to be performed in this study but since <10 studies were included, it was deemed not possible.[31]

3. Results

3.1. Study selection

The review process is illustrated in Figure 1. The search strategy yielded 879 articles from the databases and hand-search. After removal of duplicates, 764 title and abstract records for relevant articles were screened. Then, only 45 articles were selected for full-text screening. Thirty-five studies were excluded after stage 2 screening, as they did not satisfy the eligibility criteria (described in PRISMA flowchart). Furthermore, 1 study[32] was excluded due to the same data (i.e., same number of participants recruited from same study center and same years) were reported in their other study.[19] Nine studies were thus finalized for the present systematic and qualitative synthesis, in which quantitative analysis was performed on 5 studies. One study[20] was included 3 times in sub-group analysis due to different study designs where the test group (RA-PD) had different control groups (RA alone, PD alone and HC).

3.2. Description of the study

The included studies represent 6 cross-sectional,[20,22,23,25,26,33] 2 prospective interventional[21,24] and 1 prospective cohort study.[19] The features of the selected studies are shown in Supplementary Table S1, http://links.lww.com/MD/K117. The selected studies were published between 2009 to 2023. The present review represents a total sample of 452 participants comprising 174 RA patients with PD (RA-PD), 71 RA only, 82 PD only and 125 healthy subjects. The individuals were between 18 and 71 years of age. Sixty 7 percent of the studies enrolled subjects of European ancestry[19–22,25,26] whilst the remaining studies recruited participants of transcontinental country of Turkish ancestry.[20,24,33] Six studies included smokers,[19–22,24,25] whereas 1 study[26] did not mention if smokers were included. In the RA group, the disease duration was stated in all studies, ranging from the date of diagnosis to 28.2 years. In all included studies, RA patients were prescribed with a variety of medications including antimetabolites (methotrexate), non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, immunosuppressant drugs (prednisolone) and biological DMARDs. One study did not report any drugs used for RA treatment.[26]

Five studies collected GCF samples,[20–24] 2 studies[25,26] collected both GCF and serum samples, while 2 studies[19,33] collected both serum and saliva samples. With regards to the assessment technique, 5 studies[20–22,24,25] employed commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kit (ELISA), 3 studies[19,20] used immunofluorometric assay (IFMA) whereas 1 study[26] analyzed the samples with sandwich ELISA technique. Four studies[19,20,24,26] reported levels of MMPs in RA-PD compared to HC, 3 studies[22,25,26] compared RA-PD to RA-only patients, while 4 studies[20,21,24,26] compared RA-PD to PD-only group. Furthermore, subgroup meta-analysis were performed on 2 studies ([20,23] and[20,21]) of each group (RA-PD vs HC subjects and RA-PD vs PD alone) respectively, to determine whether GCF sample values in ng/ml or ng/sites influenced the overall mean difference of MMP-8 levels.

3.3. Quality assessment within studies

Quality assessment of the included studies as evaluated by the Newcastle Ottawa Scale tool ranged from 6/9 to 8/9, as shown in Table 1. The studies scoring 7 or above, which were rated as good quality, showed adequate case definition of RA-PD (n = 9, 100%), clear definition of controls (n = 6, 66.6%) and the same procedure of ascertainment for cases and controls except 1 study[25] (n = 6, 66.6%). Seven studies were characterized by representativeness of cases (77.7%). Furthermore, majority of the studies achieved 2 stars in comparability of cases and controls based on study design or analysis and for additional factors (i.e., age, gender, smoking status, and RA-medications) (n = 5, 55.5%) and equal to non-response rate (n = 5, 55.5%). Two studies provided information on selection of controls.[19]

3.4. Synthesis of results

Nine studies were included in this review which investigated the role of MMP biomarkers in RA-PD versus RA alone, PD alone and HC subjects. These studies reported on MMP-1, -3, -8, -9, and -13 levels. A significant difference in all MMP levels were seen when comparing RA-PD with RA, PD, and HC. However, meta-analysis was only performed on MMP-8 levels due to the available number of eligible studies.

3.4.1. Levels of GCF MMP-8 in RA-PD versus RA alone, PD alone and HC subjects.

Figure 2 shows the result of meta-analysis comparing the level of MMP-8 in RA-PD versus RA alone, PD alone and HC presented in a forest plot. The level of GCF MMP-8 (mean ± SD) in RA-PD subjects was in the range of 0.92 ± 0.56 ng/mL to 15.99 ± 13.49 ng/mL, while the levels for HC was 0.04 ± 0.08 ng/mL, for PD alone was 0.68 ± 0.83 ng/mL (PD alone) and for RA alone was 6.22 ± 7.01 ng/mL.[20,25] The overall standardized mean difference in GCF MMP-8 levels (SMD = 1.21; Z = 2.07; P = .04) between RA-PD and RA alone, PD alone and HC was significant. However, the heterogeneity between the studies was also significant (Q-value = 14.49; P < .0007; I2 = 86%).

3.4.2. Levels of serum MMP-8 in RA-PD versus RA alone, PD alone and HC subjects.

Figure 3 shows 2 studies[19,25] that compared serum MMP-8 levels between RA-PD and RA alone, PD alone and HC. The serum MMP levels ranged between 5.52 ± 5.92 ng/mL[25] and 147.72 ± 166.38 ng/mL[19] in RA-PD subjects whereas in RA alone and HC subjects, the level ranged between 2.60 ± 3.57 ng/mL in RA only subjects[25] and 27.02 ± 21.16 ng/mL in HC subjects.[19] The overall standardized mean difference in serum MMP-8 levels between RA-PD and RA alone, PD alone and HC group was significant (SMD = 0.87; Z = 4.53; P = .00001). The heterogeneity was not significant between the studies (Q-value = 2.24; P = .13; I2 = 55%).

Figure 4 shows 2 studies with significantly lower levels of GCF MMP-8 PD alone group compared to the RA-PD group.[20,21] Both studies reported levels of MMP-8 in ng/sites. Figure 5 presents 2 studies reporting higher levels of MMP-8 in GCF in RA-PD group compared to HC group.[20,23] The overall standardized mean difference in GCF MMP-8 levels between the 2 groups was non-significant (SMD = 1.67; Z = 1.89; P = .06), although the heterogeneity was high and significantly different between the 2 studies (Q-value = 9.03; P = .003; I2 = 89%).

3.4.5. Levels of MMP-8 in RA-PD versus RA alone.

Two studies reported this outcome.[25] Findings from the studies demonstrated significantly higher levels of MMP-8 in serum and GCF sample of 60 RA-PD subjects compared to 45 RA alone subjects.

4. Discussion

The current review intends to evaluate the difference of MMPs levels in serum, saliva and GCF between subjects with concurrent RA and PD compared to those with either HC or single disease, that is, PD alone/RA alone. The results of all included studies showed that MMP levels investigated (MMP-1, MMP-3, MMP-8, MMP-9, and MMP-13) were consistently elevated in RA-PD subjects compared to RA alone, PD alone and HC. These findings are in line with the universally accepted hypothesis that MMPs are potentially upregulated and closely involved in the pathogenesis of both diseases, hence elevated levels in concurrent PD and RA could suggest possible higher pro-inflammatory burden in the patients.[26,34,35] The findings of the included studies showed that serum and GCF MMP levels of rheumatic patients were positively correlated as systemic inflammation observed in the bloodstream may also be reflected locally in the oral cavity. The elevated GCF and serum MMP levels could be explained due to their large presence and increased activity released into the synovia/gingiva in diseased subjects along with proinflammatory cytokines that causes tissue and bone degradation. Moreover, MMPs are expressed both in active or latent form and there are no specific activators of MMPs. MMP inhibitors (MMPI) have been used to determine the role of MMPs in different physiological and pathological processes of diseases.[16] It has been observed in included studies that among all MMPs, few MMPs like MMP-1, -3, -8, -9, and -13 overexpressed themselves due to its production in substantial quantities, which suggest their role in inflammatory pathways of RA-PD patients compared to normal individuals.

In this systematic review, meta-analysis was only feasible to quantify MMP-8 biomarker measured in GCF and serum samples based on data availability. The outcomes of meta-analysis showed that both samples (GCF and serum) of MMP-8 levels were significantly elevated in RA-PD group compared to RA alone, PD alone and HC group as shown in the forest plots. As the heterogeneity identified in GCF and serum MMP-8 forest plots were 86% and 55%, respectively, this may suggest overestimation of the calculated effects. The results may have also been influenced by unstandardized criteria for PD and RA. For instance, the diagnosis of PD in the literature varied; some studies defined PD as “mild/moderate/severe,”[20–23,25] while others used clinical parameters such as clinical attachment level, probing depth and bleeding on probing[19,24,26] to define periodontal disease. None of the included studies categorized PD into chronic and aggressive types.

In addition, 2 subgroup meta-analysis was carried out comparing RA-PD subjects with PD alone, and HC respectively. The results of the subgroup analysis were significant (P value < .05) in case of RA-PD versus PD alone with low (0%) heterogeneity. In contrast, the statistical value was not significant in RA-PD versus HC with 89% substantial heterogeneity. The non-significant value could be due to the variation in the test and control groups, which is in line with the reasons suggested by Visentin, Cleary, Hunt.[36] Worth noting, the standardized mean difference was statistically significant for serum MMP-8 levels between RA-PD versus RA alone, PD alone and HC subjects. Albeit conflicting results, oral fluids MMP8 level may still be a reliable tool to monitor the inflammatory level. The availability of active MMP-8 (aMMP-8) chairside point of care immunoassay kit might be beneficial for detecting PD in patients undergoing RA treatment by non-dental professionals to evaluate periodontal health.

The active MMP-8 produced from neutrophils is the major host-cell derived collagenases that causes periodontal tissue damage by degrading gingival and periodontal ligament collagen. However, the concentrations of MMP-1, -9, -3 and -13 were also increased in RA-PD due to their osteoclastic activity that causes bone resorption as MMPs are proteolytic enzyme. Hence, these MMPs have been correlated with the disease prognosis in RA as well as PD. Therefore, it can be suggested that MMPs could also be considered as a predictor of disease activity and particularly helpful when traditional markers such as erythrocyte sedimentation rate and C-reactive protein are less accurate.

Heterogeneity of disease is observed in rheumatic population due to considerable disease duration which ranges from time since diagnosis to 17.06 years.[20,23,24] The difference in disease activity was often measured using DAS28 scores and ranged in mean value from 3.3[22] to 4.1.[19] Among all 9 studies, 2 studies[20,26] did not provide DAS28 scores, whilst another 2 studies[23,24] did not describe the mean score of DAS28 but categorized the participants into low, moderate and remission disease activity. Moreover, all studies reported rheumatoid factor (RF) levels except for 1 study.[26] These above-mentioned factors again reflect the phenotypically distinct disease severity that might affect the association with periodontitis.

Smoking is an established risk factor in a dose dependent manner that has an effect on RA and PD.[37] Six out of the 9 included studies[19–22,24,25] included smokers and this could have confounded the outcomes of MMPs level in RA-PD subjects. Furthermore, all included studies mentioned treatment regimens in terms of different medications except 1 study.[26] These studies stated that majority of patients were prescribed with synthetic DMARDs (methotrexate) whereas 3 studies reported that their participants[19,22,23] were additionally treated using biological therapy comprising of TNF-α inhibitors or non-TNF-α inhibitors. Thus, in view of the mentioned possible confounding factors from included studies, the findings from this systematic review should be taken with caution. Moreover, other factors such as small number of sample size, non-standardized diagnostic criterion of PD, different laboratory methods adopted for the detection of MMPs as well as inclusion of publication restricted to English language were also the limitations of this study. Therefore, a robust study design with larger sample size along with standardized disease definition criteria and laboratory method is highly recommended to improve the quality of work in this area.

5. Conclusion

Low level of evidence supports that high level of MMPs (MMP-1, MMP-3, MMP-8, MMP-9, and MMP-13) in serum and GCF samples of RA-PD subjects is associated with increased disease activity. In contrast, limited studies reported MMP levels in saliva of RA with concurrent PD subjects. Moreover, MMP-8 is the most reported among all other MMPs, hence it may be used as a potential diagnostic biomarker in RA-PD. This review proposes that future studies should explore the role of other MMPs except MMP-8 in subjects suffering from both diseases.

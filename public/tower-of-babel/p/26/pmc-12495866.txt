Right ventricular outflow tract obstruction in recipient twins of twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome: 13 years of single-center data and literature review
Authors: Yoo Jin Lee, Mi-Young Lee, Jae Yi Jeong, Jihye Koh, Jin Hoon Chung, Hye-Sung Won
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo investigate the characteristics of right ventricular outflow tract obstruction (RVOTO) in recipient twins of twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS), including its prevalence, perinatal outcomes, and the impact of fetoscopic laser coagulation (FLC) on postnatal RVOTO status.MethodsThis retrospective study included recipient twins of TTTS treated with FLC at the Asan Medical Center between January 2011 and December 2023. Among those diagnosed with RVOTO, the recipient twins were categorized into two groups based on postnatal outcomes: RVOTO improvement versus persistence. Prenatal ultrasound findings and neonatal outcomes were compared between the groups. To identify the predisposing factors for RVOTO, the entire recipient population was divided into RVOTO and non-RVOTO groups, followed by univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses.ResultsAmong the 284 TTTS cases, 24 recipient twins (8.5%) were diagnosed with RVOTO. RVOTO improved following FLC in 7 cases (29.2%). In the remaining 17 cases with persistent RVOTO, six were stillbirths. In the 11 survivors, five had mild RVOTO and required no intervention, while six underwent intervention (four with balloon valvuloplasty and two with surgical correction). Multivariate analysis identified the initial Quintero stage (odds ratio [OR] 2.64), pulsatile umbilical vein (OR 3.44), and tricuspid regurgitation (OR 30.66) as significant independent risk factors for RVOTO.ConclusionsRVOTO is a relatively common cardiac complication in advanced TTTS. Timely FLC may lead to postnatal improvement. Serial ultrasound assessment is essential for early detection, risk stratification, and individualized counseling.

Background

Twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS) is a common complication in monochorionic (MC) twin pregnancies, and is characterized by polyhydramnios in one twin and oligohydramnios in the other. This condition is caused by imbalanced vascular anastomoses within the shared placenta, leading not only to differences in amniotic fluid volume, but also to significant differences in blood flow dynamics between the twins [1]. Among the changes that occur in fetuses with TTTS, right ventricular outflow tract obstruction (RVOTO) is a distinctive complication attributed to hypervolemic changes in the recipient twins. The precise pathophysiological mechanisms of RVOTO in recipient twins remains unclear, but it is thought to result from imbalanced blood supply and exposure to abnormal concentrations of vasoactive mediators [2, 3], leading to myocardial hypertrophy due to volume overload and increased afterload secondary to systemic hypertension [4]. RVOTO has been reported in approximately 6.7–21% of recipient twins, making it a relatively common finding during the diagnosis of TTTS [5–13]. The prenatal detection of RVOTO is associated with a significantly increased risk of congenital heart disease (CHD) in MC twins after birth, with an approximately 12-fold higher risk of CHD compared to that of singleton pregnancies [14]. Furthermore, persistent postnatal RVOTO often necessitates surgical intervention and has been reported to potentially reduce survival rates by > 10% [6].

Several case series of RVOTO in TTTS recipient twins have been reported, starting with the first report in 1994, which initially documented the prenatal diagnosis of pulmonary stenosis (PS) and subsequent postnatal balloon valvuloplasty (BVP) [15]. With the introduction of fetoscopic laser coagulation (FLC) of placental vascular anastomoses as the first-line treatment for advanced-stage TTTS [16], several studies have examined the impact of FLC on RVOTO in affected fetuses [5, 17–19]. Subsequently, some studies have reported improvement rates of RVOTO following FLC [6, 8, 9, 20]. However, the findings regarding RVOTO resolution through FLC still remain inconclusive. Recently, a meta-analysis investigated the incidence of RVOTO, as well as its prenatal associations and outcomes, in TTTS pregnancies with RVOTO [21]. While this meta-analysis, along with other studies, have explored risk factors [10–13, 22], comparative research between groups with and without RVOTO improvement remains lacking, leaving the understanding of RVOTO risk factors incomplete.

Therefore, the aim of this study was to analyze the characteristics of RVOTO in TTTS recipient twins, with a focus on its prevalence and the impact of FLC on improving this condition. We also compared prenatal and postnatal clinical characteristics between groups with RVOTO improvement and those without improvement following FLC. Additionally, we aimed to identify the risk factors for RVOTO in recipient twins. Finally, a comprehensive review of the existing literature was undertaken to contextualize our findings within the broader scope of current evidence as part of the discussion.

Methods

We retrospectively reviewed cases of MC twins complicated by TTTS who were treated with FLC and delivered at the Asan Medical Center between January 2011 and December 2023. Detailed ultrasonography, including fetal echocardiography, was performed using one of the following ultrasound devices: A30, WS80A, HERA W10 (Samsung Medison Co., Ltd., Seoul, South Korea), Voluson E8 or E10 Expert (General Electric Healthcare Austria GmbH & Co. OG, Zipf, Austria) with a 2–6-MHz transabdominal probe. The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of our institution (approval number: 2024 − 0179). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study.

TTTS was diagnosed based on sonographic criteria, specifically the presence of oligohydramnios in one fetus (maximum vertical pocket [MVP] of < 2 cm) and polyhydramnios in the other (MVP > 8 cm) [16]. The stage of TTTS was classified according to the Quintero staging system, reflecting the condition immediately prior to the procedure [23]. FLC was performed in patients with TTTS at stage II or higher, or stage I with symptomatic polyhydramnios or cardiac dysfunction in the recipient. All procedures were conducted according to the standardized protocol of the Asan Medical Center by experienced specialists [24].

We defined RVOTO as PS or pulmonary atresia (PA) classified through fetal echocardiography. PS was diagnosed when the peak systolic velocity (PSV) of the flow through the pulmonary valve exceeded 1 m/s, with or without valvular abnormalities. PA was defined as the absence of antegrade flow through the pulmonary valve from the right ventricle to the pulmonary artery [6]. Improvement in PS was defined as the normalization of the PSV of flow through the pulmonary valve with the absence of turbulent flow. Improvement in PA was defined as the appearance of antegrade flow across the right ventricular outflow tract. Cases diagnosed with RVOTO were categorized based on post-FLC and postnatal outcomes. Group I included cases in which RVOTO improved either prenatally or postnatally without the need for intervention. Group II comprised cases in which RVOTO persisted despite FLC, requiring postnatal intervention. Accordingly, we performed a comparative analysis based on the degree of improvement among cases diagnosed with RVOTO.

The following maternal and obstetric data were collected: maternal age, parity, fetal sex, placental location, gestational age (GA) at TTTS diagnosis, GA at FLC, initial Quintero stage, interval from diagnosis to FLC, GA at delivery, interval from FLC to delivery, mode of delivery, and survival status of each twin. Neonatal outcomes included birth weight, birth weight discordance, Apgar scores at 1 and 5 min, neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) admission, requirements for ventilatory support, and the need for postnatal cardiac intervention. Prenatal ultrasound parameters were obtained through detailed ultrasound evaluations performed at the time of TTTS diagnosis and both before and after FLC. The assessed parameters included intertwin estimated fetal weight discordance, MVP, end-diastolic flow in the umbilical artery (UA), ductus venosus (DV) flow patterns, and the presence of a pulsatile umbilical vein (UV). The cardiothoracic circumference ratio and cardiothoracic area ratio were measured, and a detailed assessment of the pulmonary valve was performed, including morphology and thickening. Pulmonary valve thickening was defined as an unusually echogenic and enlarged valve on grayscale RVOT images; when cine clips were available, restricted leaflet excursion during the cardiac cycle was also considered supportive evidence. Cardiac function was further evaluated by measuring the left ventricular modified myocardial performance index (MPI) and by assessing the presence and severity of tricuspid regurgitation (TR) and mitral regurgitation (MR). The presence of fetal hydrops and the type of cord insertion were also documented. In addition, the timing of the improvement in DV flow and RVOTO was recorded when applicable.

Among all cases of recipients with RVOTO, cases were divided into two groups: those with improvement of RVOTO after FLC and those without improvement. The two groups were analyzed for prenatal characteristics, changes in prenatal findings before and after FLC, and neonatal outcomes. To further analyze the risk factors for RVOTO, the entire recipient group was divided into two subgroups: one with RVOTO and the other without RVOTO.

Statistical analysis

SAS software for Windows, version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) was used for statistical analysis. Categorical variables were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test, while continuous variables were analyzed using the t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sums test for two groups. Variables with a p-value of less than 0.05 were included in univariate logistic regression to identify risk factors for RVOTO. Odds ratios (ORs) are presented with 95% confidence intervals. A two-sided p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed using backward elimination, including candidate variables with a p-value threshold of < 0.1 in the univariate analysis.

Results

In total 284 MC twin pairs with TTTS were treated with FLC, of which 24 recipient twins were diagnosed with RVOTO, accounting for 8.5% of the overall population with TTTS (Fig. 1). Among them, seven (29.2%) showed improvement after FLC. Of the remaining 17 cases with persistent RVOTO, six resulted in stillbirth, and 11 showed confirmed RVOTO on postnatal echocardiography. Among the six stillbirths, three were fetal demises associated with hydrops, reverse twin anemia-polycythemia sequence, and cord stricture; the other three were preterm births that occurred before viability. The overall survival rate of recipients with RVOTO was 75% (18/24). Six required surgical interventions, four with BVP and two with surgical correction, accounting for 25% (6/24) of all RVOTO cases. Discussion

This study highlights RVOTO as a clinically significant cardiac complication, occurring in 8.5% of recipient twins with TTTS. Notably, 29.2% of cases improved prenatally after FLC and an additional 20.8% showed spontaneous postnatal resolution without intervention, suggesting that RVOTO is reversible in a substantial proportion of cases. In cases requiring postnatal intervention, PS was the predominant lesion and all showed prenatal evidence of pulmonary valve thickening, indicating that a structural abnormality is unlikely to resolve spontaneously. Furthermore, an advanced initial stage of TTTS, UV pulsation, and TR were identified as significant prenatal risk factors for the development of RVOTO.

While the differences between the groups were not statistically significant, Group II showed a tendency to be diagnosed with TTTS at an earlier GA. Following FLC, the interval to delivery was significantly longer in Group II, leading to a higher GA at birth and greater birth weights for both the donor and recipient twins. This suggests that persistent RVOTO alone was not an indication for preterm delivery, and maintaining pregnancy contributed positively to fetal growth despite the need for postnatal cardiac intervention.

In cases requiring postnatal intervention, PS was the predominant type of RVOTO, and all showed evidence of pulmonary valve thickening on prenatal ultrasound. This suggests that pulmonary valve thickening may serve as a strong marker for persistent, anatomically fixed RVOTO that is unlikely to resolve spontaneously. In contrast, cases without structural valve abnormalities, even those initially diagnosed with functional PA, mostly demonstrated spontaneous recovery after FLC, highlighting the potential reversibility of hemodynamic obstruction when valve anatomy is preserved. This finding is supported by a previous report, which described that among seven cases of functional PA, six showed resolution of RVOTO following FLS, whereas all six with PS were treated postnatally [25]. These results are consistent with our findings, in which improvement was frequently observed in cases of functional PA after FLC.

A comparison of the entire cohort of recipient twins with and without RVOTO demonstrated significant differences in prenatal ultrasound findings. RVOTO occurred exclusively in cases with advanced TTTS, corresponding to Quintero stage III or IV. Interestingly, the MVP tended to be smaller in the RVOTO group compared to the non-RVOTO group. This observation may suggest that in fetuses with RVOTO, progressive cardiac dysfunction and venous congestion could have restricted amniotic fluid accumulation, despite the presence of significant hemodynamic disturbances. To further explore the relationship between Quintero stage and RVOTO, we analyzed the distribution of Doppler abnormalities in each twin among pregnancies with stage III or IV disease. In the non-RVOTO group (n = 158), Doppler abnormalities were observed in both twins in 59 cases (37.3%), in the donor only in 69 cases (43.7%), and in the recipient only in 30 cases (19.0%). In the RVOTO group (n = 24), the corresponding numbers were 14 (58.3%), 5 (20.8%), and 5 (20.8%), respectively. The proportion of isolated recipient Doppler abnormalities was similar between the RVOTO and non-RVOTO groups (20.8% vs. 19.0%), whereas abnormalities in both twins were more common in the RVOTO group. These findings suggest that a higher Quintero stage in RVOTO cases is not solely attributable to recipient Doppler abnormalities, but rather may reflect hemodynamic compromise affecting both twins. Earlier GA at diagnosis and at FLC, abnormal DV flow, presence of UV pulsation, TR, MR, fetal hydrops, and abnormal cord insertion were more frequently observed in the RVOTO group. Multivariable analysis further confirmed that an advanced initial stage of TTTS, UV pulsation, and TR were independent risk factors for the development of RVOTO. These findings suggest that progressive cardiac volume overload and elevated central venous pressure, reflected by UV pulsation and TR, play crucial roles in the pathogenesis of RVOTO in TTTS. Careful assessment of the disease stage and right heart Doppler findings may therefore help identify fetuses at high risk for developing RVOTO and guide prenatal counseling and monitoring strategies.

We reviewed and analyzed 24 published studies on RVOTO in TTTS recipient fetuses, encompassing all available reports from the earliest documented case to the most recent publications (Tables 9 and 10). The first documented case was reported in 1994 [15], whereas the largest cohort to date, published in 2015, included 53 RVOTO cases among 610 TTTS recipients, yielding a prevalence of 8.7% [6]. In contrast to previous systematic reviews that included only studies published after 2005 and reported an estimated RVOTO incidence of 6% [21], our review incorporated earlier reports and a broader range of cases. Therefore, we analyzed 271 RVOTO cases among 3,274 TTTS recipients, with a pooled incidence of 8.3%, closely aligning with our own findings.

Prenatal improvement of RVOTO following FLC was first reported in 2013 (Table 10), with complete resolution in all eight treated cases [20]. Subsequent studies have reported a wide range of improvement rates, from 9.1 to 69.2% [6, 8, 9, 11–13, 20, 25], with a pooled average of 38.3% (59/154), which is slightly higher than our observed rate of 29.2%. Given the relatively small number of reported cases, it remains difficult to identify consistent predictors of improvement or determine which subtypes are more likely to respond to FLC. As previously discussed, functional RVOTO is more likely to resolve following FLC, whereas structural valve abnormalities tend to persist. Further data are needed to better characterize these patterns and identify additional predictors of response to FLC.

Among the 99 TTTS recipient twins diagnosed with postnatal RVOTO, 67 (67.7%) required treatment (Table 10). Of these, 59 (88.1%) were managed with BVP, and eight (11.9%) required surgical correction. Approximately one-third of postnatally diagnosed cases did not require any intervention, and most treated cases were managed with minimally invasive procedures. Similarly, in our cohort, 45.4% of persistent RVOTO cases did not require intervention, and 66.6% of those treated underwent BVP. These findings may offer important reassurance during prenatal counseling, suggesting that even in the presence of postnatal RVOTO, invasive treatment is often limited to catheter-based approaches.

The first study to report risk factors for RVOTO in TTTS recipient twins was published in 2017 (Table 10). Since then, only four additional studies, including the present study, have investigated potential predictors. Reported risk factors include earlier GA at TTTS diagnosis, abnormal DV flow, pericardial effusion, and Quintero stage IV. Normalization of pulmonary valve flow after FLC has been proposed as a protective factor. One study failed to identify any statistically significant predictors [10]. Interestingly, a recent study identified intermittent absent or reversed end-diastolic flow in the donor UA as a significant risk factor for RVOTO in the recipient [11]. This may reflect the severity of intertwin hemodynamic imbalance, in which increased resistance in the donor circulation contributes to volume overload and pressure stress on the recipient’s right heart, predisposing to RVOTO. In our study, multivariate analysis identified advanced initial Quintero stage, UV pulsation, and TR as independent predictors of RVOTO. Although these findings support the role of hemodynamic markers of right heart strain in the development of RVOTO, the number of reported cases remains limited. Further large-scale, prospective studies will be essential to validate these risk factors and improve prenatal risk stratification.

A major strength of this study is that it investigated the prevalence and clinical course of RVOTO in TTTS using a large, single-center cohort treated with a consistent FLC protocol over a 13-year period. In addition, the use of comprehensive Doppler and echocardiographic evaluation allowed for a more detailed characterization of RVOTO compared to previous studies. The systematic and structured approach across a large cohort may strengthen the clinical relevance of the findings.

This study has several limitations inherent to its retrospective design. The timing of ultrasound examinations varied across cases, and some parameters were assessed retrospectively using stored images. In addition, right ventricular function could not be evaluated using modified MPI, because of the presence of TR or the absence of antegrade flow through the pulmonary artery, thereby limiting the comprehensive assessment of cardiac function.

Conclusions

RVOTO is a relatively common cardiac complication in TTTS recipient twins, particularly in advanced stages. Notably, timely FLC may lead to prenatal improvement in a substantial proportion of cases, with serial ultrasound evaluation being an essential diagnostic modality for early detection and risk stratification. Even persistent postnatal RVOTO may result in favorable outcomes with appropriate interventions.

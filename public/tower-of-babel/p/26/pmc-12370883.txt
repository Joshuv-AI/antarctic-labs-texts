Introduction

Recent advancements in power electronics have significantly impacted industrial applications, particularly in the realm of conveyor systems. The safe and efficient operation of these systems relies heavily on the performance of AC-AC converters, which play a crucial role in power conversion and motor control1,2. Among the various aspects of converter design and operation, switching sequence prioritization has emerged as a critical factor in ensuring safe commutation and optimal system performance. This study is rooted in the evolving landscape of power electronic converters and their applications in industrial systems. Various converter topologies, including cycloconverters, AC voltage controllers, matrix converters, and resonant converters, have been developed and refined over the years. Each of these converters presents unique characteristics and challenges in terms of efficiency, harmonic distortion, and commutation safety. The literature reveals significant advancements in areas such as modulation strategies, control algorithms, and fault tolerance mechanisms. However, there remains a critical need for comprehensive studies that address the specific challenges of switching sequence prioritization in the context of conveyor systems3–5. If the switching sequence is not properly managed, sudden voltage fluctuations can occur, causing excessive electrical stress on components that can reduce lifespan significantly. Also, improper switching timing sometimes results in unpredictable harmonic distortions, which might make conveyor operation unstable and less efficient.

The motivation behind this research stems from the increasing demand for more reliable, efficient, and safe conveyor systems in industrial settings. As industries continue to automate and optimize their processes, the need for advanced power conversion technologies becomes more pronounced. AC-AC converters, with their ability to efficiently convert power and control motor speeds, are at the forefront of this technological advancement. However, the complexities associated with their operation, particularly in terms of commutation, safety and efficiency, present ongoing challenges that require innovative solutions. The primary objective of this research is to investigate and optimize switching sequence prioritization techniques for AC-AC converters used in conveyor systems, with a focus on ensuring safe commutation. This objective encompasses several key areas of investigation: (1) Evaluating the performance of different converter topologies under varying load conditions and switching frequencies. (2) Analyzing the impact of switching sequence prioritization on power factor improvement, harmonic distortion, and overall system efficiency. (3) Investigating the effectiveness of minimum voltage and minimum turn-on time prioritization strategies in enhancing commutation safety and system reliability. (4) Assessing the short-circuit current characteristics and overvoltage levels across different converter types and fault scenarios.

Several methods were employed to determine the phase with the lowest voltage to identify the minimum voltage phase stage. Voltage comparison involves comparing the sampled voltages of all phases to identify the one with the lowest voltage, often using simple logic circuits or software algorithms. Threshold detection sets a threshold below which a phase voltage is considered as the minimum, and sampled voltages are compared against this threshold to identify the phase with a voltage below it. Differential voltage analysis examines the voltage differentials between phases to identify the phase with the smallest difference from the reference voltage, which is particularly effective when one phase deviates significantly from the others. Peak detection analyzes the voltage waveforms to determine the peak voltage of each phase, with the phase exhibiting the lowest peak voltage identified as the minimum voltage phase. Historical analysis considers past instances of minimum voltage occurrences to identify trends and patterns, allowing for the anticipation of future occurrences and prioritization of phases accordingly. Control algorithm modification entails adjusting the control algorithms of the AC-AC converter to prioritize commutation based on the minimum voltage phase, synchronizing commutation with the selected phase by adjusting parameters such as the modulation index or phase angle control settings. Interlock activation mechanisms are implemented to prevent commutation until the minimum voltage phase is identified and prioritized, thereby ensuring seamless integration with the monitoring and control infrastructure. Feedback loop integration incorporates feedback loops that continuously monitor phase voltages and adjust commutation timing in real time, dynamically prioritizing the minimum voltage phase for commutation based on feedback signals from the voltage sensors.

Finally, emergency override mechanisms are implemented to prioritize the minimum voltage phase for commutation in critical situations, allowing for manual intervention or automatic triggering of override protocols when voltage deviations pose immediate risks to the system operation. Conclusion

Studies on switching sequence prioritization in AC-AC converters for safe commutation in conveyor systems have provided insightful numerical findings and valuable insights into converter performance and reliability. While the current study is based on numerical simulations, it provides a comprehensive analysis of the proposed switching strategies. We understand that real-world validation through experimental testing or hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) simulations would provide additional insights. However, the resources required for such validation exceed the scope of this study. Nevertheless, the simulation results presented in this paper are sufficient for understanding the theoretical performance and potential of the proposed strategies. The analysis of power factor improvement across load conditions revealed significant variations, with the cycloconverter exhibiting the highest improvement at lower loads, followed closely by the AC voltage controller. However, as the load increased, the AC voltage controller demonstrated a remarkable improvement, surpassing all the other converters at a 60% load. This underscores the importance of considering load variations in commutation safety analyses and the need for adaptable control strategies. Moreover, the assessment of harmonic distortion levels under voltage fluctuation conditions highlighted the differences in converter performance. Although the cycloconverter exhibited relatively low distortion values, the AC voltage controller displayed slightly higher levels. These findings underscore the importance of implementing robust control mechanisms to mitigate harmonic distortions and ensure system reliability, particularly in dynamic industrial environments. In addition, the evaluation of the short-circuit currents, protection devices, and efficiency metrics revealed notable differences across the converters. Effective protection mechanisms are essential for mitigating short-circuit risks with variations observed in short-circuit current management. Furthermore, efficiency metrics emphasize the importance of optimizing the converter performance while prioritizing safe commutation. While the claim that cycloconverters achieve the highest power factor is supported by simulation results, we recognize that empirical validation is necessary to provide stronger support for this conclusion. Future studies will incorporate experimental testing to validate these simulation results and substantiate the claims made in the current work. The numerical results indicated varying efficiency ratings across different prioritization schemes, with implications for the overall system reliability and uptime. By ensuring proper synchronization in switching sequences, power losses are minimized, and energy utilization is improved, leading to better system efficiency. Additionally, optimized switching sequences contribute to stable voltage regulation, reducing downtime and preventing failures caused by electrical instabilities. In terms of future scope, further research is warranted to refine the control algorithms and validation methodologies, ensuring the robustness and reliability of switching sequence prioritization techniques. While variations in system parameters such as load and temperature could affect performance, they were not explored in this study. Future works could include sensitivity or Monte Carlo simulations to examine the impact of these variables in more depth. Experimental studies and field trials can provide valuable insights into real-world performance and effectiveness in industrial settings. Additionally, advancements in converter technologies, such as novel topologies and integration techniques, hold promises for improving commutation safety and efficiency.

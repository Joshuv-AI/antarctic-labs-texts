Unraveling the mechanisms of RECQL4-mediated cervical cancer progression through the PI3K/AKT pathway
Authors: Wen Huo, Yiheng Huang, Baoqinq Tian, Xiaozheng Chen, Jie Lu, Xinyi Huang, Meng Wu, Jinming Yu, Dawei Chen, Ruozheng Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•RECQL4 expression is increased in CC.•RECQL4 facilitates the proliferation, DNA replication, migration, and invasion of CC cells through regulating the PI3K/AKT signaling.•RECQL4 enhances the EMT of CC cells through regulating the PI3K/AKT signaling.Keywords: Cervical cancer, RECQL4, DNA damage, Tumor proliferation, Genetic defects in tumours

Introduction

Cervical cancer (CC) is a prevalent malignancy in the female reproductive system, affecting over 600,000 individuals worldwide annually and causing approximately 340,000 deaths [1]. DNA damage is a key factor in CC development. HPV infection disrupts the DNA repair mechanism of cervical epithelial cells through its E6 and E7 proteins, leading to the accumulation of DNA damage. Moreover, chronic inflammation caused by a long-term HPV infections further increase the risk of DNA damage [2,3].

RecQ Like Helicase 4 (RECQL4) is a protein-coding gene that plays a significant role in tumor research. Belonging to the RECQ family, RECQL4 is a DNA helicase involved in DNA repair and maintenance, crucial for maintaining genomic stability in cells [[4], [5], [6]]. Mutations in RECQL4 or its abnormal expression is closely related to the development and occurrence of several types of cancer [7]. For instance, RECQL4 mutations are linked to Rothmund-Thomson syndrome, a rare hereditary tumor predisposition syndrome characterized by abnormal skin development, skeletal deformities, and an increased risk of tumor development [8]. The involvement of RECQL4 in essential biological processes, such as DNA double-strand break repair, DNA damage detection, and cell cycle regulation, has been extensively investigated in-depth through research on its function and regulatory mechanisms [[9], [10], [11]]. Mutations in RECQL4 can impair DNA repair function, accumulating DNA damage, increasing genomic instability, and ultimately promoting tumor development. Although aberrant expression or mutations of RECQL4 have been reported in common tumors like breast, ovarian, and prostate cancers [[12], [13], [14]], its prognostic and oncogenetic significance in CC is not known.

In a previous study conducted by our research group, DNA sequencing was performed on 45 cervical cancer patients [15]. To specifically examine the role of DDR gene mutations in cervical cancer, we defined a set of DDR genes and further analyzed mutations within this gene set. The study found that 23 out of 43 patients in our cohort had DDR gene mutations, with RECQL4 being the most frequently mutated gene. Furthermore, we analyzed the impact of gene mutation status on overall survival (OS). In our cohort, patients with RECQL4 mutations have shorter OS. A consistent trend was observed in the TCGA cohort, where patients with RECQL4 mutations also have shorter OS. However, there have been no previous reports clarifying how RECQL4 influences the onset and progression of cervical cancer, which piqued our interest.

In this study, we aimed to investigate the effect of RECQL4 on CC prognosis using clinical specimens from The Cancer Genome Atlas and to assess the malignant effects of RECQL4 through various experimental assays. Our study presents, for the first time, the mechanism of action of RECQL4 in CC and its potential as a therapeutic target. We provide comprehensive evidence of RECQL4-induced malignant behavior in CC.

Materials and methods

Data source

TCGA (The Cancer Genome Atlas) database (https://cancergenome.nih.gov) was accessed to obtain gene expression data and clinical information of patients with CC. A total of 306 tumor samples and 3 normal tissue samples were collected, including 3 paired data. The Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database was used to obtain the publicly available datasets GSE9750 and GSE39001. GSE9750 consisted of 33 primary tumors and 24 normal cervical epithelium samples, whereas GSE39001 included 62 primary tumors and 17 normal cervical epithelium samples. The association between RECQL4 expression and CC was examined using R software.

Cell culture

CC cell lines (SiHa, CaSki) were purchased from the China Center of American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). Mycoplasma tests were conducted, and all results were negative. CaSki cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin solution (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA). RPMI-1640 (Gibco) supplemented with 10 % FBS and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin solution was used for the culture of SiHa cells. Cell lines were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5 % CO2.

Lentivirus transfection and stable cell line construction

Lentiviruses designed for RECQL4 knockdown were purchased from Gemma Genetics, CC cells were seeded in 6-well plates overnight. Subsequently, 1 ml fresh medium containing lentivirus (MOI: 5–10) was added to each well. After 72 h, the infection efficiency was evaluated using a fluorescence microscope and the successfully transfected cells were selected using a concentration of 2 μg/mL puromycin. An RECQL4 expression plasmid was purchased from Miaoling Biologicals, and the RECQL4 overexpression vector was constructed in the PLVX vector using Genomeditech. The shRNA sequences are shown in supplementary materials.

Tumor cell expression and function assay

The detail of the expression assays, including real-time PCR and Western blot (WB) analysis, are provided in the supplementary materials. Functional experiments such as cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay, clonogenic assay, EdU incorporation assay, wound healing assay, transwell migration assay and transwell invasion assay are also described in the supplementary materials.

Cell cycle analysis

CC cells with downregulated RECQL4 were cultured in six-well plates for 2 days. When the cells reached 80 % confluence, trypsinization was performed to obtain a cell suspension. By centrifugation, the resulting cell pellet was washed with cold PBS. The cells were then fixed in a 70 % cold ethanol solution for 2 h or overnight. A solution containing 10 μg/mL RNase A and 50 μg/mL propidium iodide (Beyotime, C1052) was used to suspend cells prior to analysis. The suspension was then incubated for 60 min at 37 °C with intermittent tapping. Cell suspensions were filtered at 300 cells per second and analyzed on a FACS Calibur™ flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, CA, USA). Cell cycle analysis was performed with a cell count of 20,000 per sample.

Apoptosis assay

Cell apoptosis was assessed using a cell apoptosis assay after transfection or addition of cisplatin. CC cells were cultured in six-well plates for 72 h, followed by trypsinization (without EDTA) to obtain a cell suspension. The cells were then washed with PBS and subjected to apoptosis analysis using an Annexin V-APC/7-AAD Apoptosis Detection kit (AP104–100, MultiSciences Biotech, Co., Ltd, Hangzhou, China) and flow cytometry. Cell suspensions were filtered at 300 cells per second and analyzed using a FACS Calibur™ flow cytometer (BD Biosciences). Data were processed using FlowJo 10.

Subcutaneous xenograft tumor model

Animal experiments were conducted according to the protocol of Shandong Cancer Hospital's Animal Care and Use Committee. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Shandong First Medical University, with an ethical license number SDTHEC 2,023,007,025. All experiments were performed in strict accordance with the guidelines of the Ethics Committee. Six-week-old female Nod-SCID mice were obtained from the Huafu Kang Laboratory Animals company. Three groups of nude mice were randomly assigned and received subcutaneous injections of stable knockout shNC, shRECQL4#1 cells, and shRECQL4#2 cells, respectively. A suspension of 5 × 10(^7) cells in 100 μL PBS was administered subcutaneously on the back of the Nod-SCID mice. The length and width of the subcutaneous tumors were measured every three days for seven days following injection. Tumor volume was calculated using the formula (V = L \times W^2 / 2), where (L) and (W) represent the longest and shortest diameters (mm), respectively. After four weeks, the xenografted tumor tissue was extracted and its weight was determined. The harvested tumor tissues underwent additional analysis through immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining.

Inhibitor treatment

The AKT inhibitor MK2206 was obtained from MedChem Express (NJ, USA), diluted in dimethyl sulfoxide, and stored according to manufacturer's instructions. Cells were incubated with MK2206 (1 μM) for 48-–72 h, and dimethyl sulfoxide was used as a control.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining

Deparaffinization of paraffin-embedded tissue slides was performed by immersing them in xylene, followed by rehydration through a series of graded alcohols. Tissue sections were subsequently treated with primary antibodies, namely anti-RECQL4 (17,008–1-AP, Proteintech, IL, USA; diluted 1:200), anti-Ki-67 (27,309–1-AP, Proteintech, diluted 1:5000), anti-E-cadherin (20,874–1-AP, Proteintech, diluted 1:5000), and anti-cleaved-caspase3 (19,677–1-AP, Proteintech, diluted 1:200). Secondary antibodies were then applied, and sections were stained with diaminobenzidine (Proteintech). Hematoxylin (Vector Laboratories, CA, USA) was used for counterstaining cell nuclei, which were quantified independently and blindly by at least two individuals. The staining was scored based on the stained tumor cell percentage and staining intensity. Each scoring level consisted of four levels: 1 indicating 5 %, 2 indicating 5–20 %, 3 indicating 21–50 %, and 4 indicating 51–100 %. Similarly, staining intensity was graded from 1 to 4, representing no staining, low, moderate, and high intensity, respectively. The IHC scores were calculated as the product of the percentage and intensity scores and scoring was performed under 200 × magnification.

TUNEL assay

The apoptotic response of tumor tissues was evaluated using a terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated deoxyuridine triphosphate (dUTP) nick-labelling (TUNEL) assay kit (C1090, Beyotime) to detect apoptosis. Formalin-fixed paraffin block tumor samples were deparaffinized with xylene, rehydrated with different concentrations of alcohol to water, treated with 20 μg/mL Proteinase K (37 °C, 20 min), and washed with 1 M Tris buffer. After three rinses with PBS, slides were incubated with a mixture of terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase and Cy3-labeled dUTP and then stored at 37 °C for 1 h, protected from light. Tissue apoptosis was visualized using fluorescence microscopy and quantified using Image J software.

Immunofluorescence analysis

CC cells were seeded onto 12-well cell culture plates and incubated for 24 h after transfection. Subsequently, fixation of the cells was performed using 4 % paraformaldehyde. After rinsing with PBST, permeabilization was achieved using 0.5 % Triton X-100, followed by blocking with 10 % goat serum for 30 min. Overnight incubation at 4 °C with primary antibodies targeting RECQL4, E-cadherin, and β-catenin was carried out. The following day, the slides were allowed to reach room temperature for 1 h, washed with PBST, and incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 h in a light-restricted environment. The corresponding secondary antibodies (1:400; ab150117, ab150080, ab150116, and ab150083; Abcam, Cambridge, England) were utilized. Subsequently, sections were mounted using Prolong Diamond Antifade Mountant (S2110, Solarbio Science & Technology Co. Ltd., Beijing, China) containing 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole and were visualiz using a laser confocal scanning microscope. For each slide, three non-overlapping observation areas were randomly selected.

Flow cytometry analysis

The markers expressed by human tumor cells were assessed using flow cytometry after staining with specific antibodies conjugated with various fluorescence molecules. The primary antibodies used in this study were Ki-67, (27,309–1-AP, Proteintech, 1:500 dilution), E-cadherin (60,335–1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:1000 dilution), and cleaved-caspase3 (19,677–1-AP, Proteintech, 1:1000 dilution). The cells were then extensively washed and examined using a FACS Aria Fusion instrument. A total of ten thousand events were acquired in list mode, with debris excluded based on the forward scatter threshold. Subsequent data analysis was conducted using Flow Jo 10.5.3.

GSEA and correlation analysis and differential gene expression analysis

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using GSEA v3.0 (http://www.broadinstitute.org/gsea/). The gene sets were acquired from MSigDB (http://www.broadinstitute.org/gsea/msigdb). Pearson's correlation was used for the correlation analysis. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between shRECQL4 and the control group were identified using the limma package in R software. Volcano plots were used to identify DEGs based on | log2(fold change) | > 1, adjusted to P < 0.05.

Functional enrichment analysis

The clusterProfiler package in R was used to perform gene ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses on the identified DEGs. Results were visualized using ggpubr and ggplot2.

Statistical analysis

Results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). T-tests were used to compare two groups, while one-way analysis of variance was used for comparisons involving more than two groups. All experiments were conducted in at least three biological replicates. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 (IBM, NY, USA), and GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 (CA, USA) was used to produce graphs. Statistically significant differences were indicated by P < 0.05.

Results

RECQL4 is upregulated in tumor tissues and associated with poor prognosis in CC

The expression of RECQL4 in tumors was examined using the GEPIA database (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn) [16]. Results showed that RECQL4 is abnormally expressed in cancerous tissues, with higher expression levels observed in most cancer types compared to normal tissues (Fig. 1A). The comparative analysis of tumor tissues from the TCGA database revealed significantly elevated RECQL4 mRNA expression in cancerous compared to normal cervix uteri tissues. Analysis of independent GEO patient cohorts GSE39001 (n = 79) and GSE9750 (n = 57) confirmed that RECQL4 expression is significantly higher in tumor compared to normal tissues (Fig. 1B). IHC data from the Human Protein Atlas database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/) [17] were employed to detect RECQL4 protein expression in cervical tumors. The histochemical images presented in this study demonstrated that RECQL4 protein levels are significantly elevated in cancerous compared to normal cervical tissues (Fig. 1C).

Furthermore, the correlation between RECQL4 expression and CC prognosis was investigated using data from the Kaplan-Meier plotter database (https://kmplot.com/analysis/) [18]. This data showed that high RECQL4 expression is significantly associated with inferior OS and RFS (Supplementary Figure 1A). The upregulation of RECQL4 expression had a particularly detrimental impact on OS in the high TMB subgroup of cervical cancer (Fig. 1D). Additionally, analysis of the relationship between RECQL4 expression and TMB indicated a significant positive correlation between RECQL4 expression and TMB levels across various cancer types (Fig. 1E). GSEA revealed that RECQL4 upregulation promotes cell proliferation and inhibited apoptosis (Fig. 1F). To gain preliminary insights into the potential impact of RECQL4 on cervical cancer, we established gene sets associated with key biological processes such as apoptosis, DNA replication, tumor cell proliferation, G2M phase checkpoints, the MYC pathway, and the DNA repair pathway. Subsequent analysis revealed a negative correlation between RECQL4 expression and apoptosis, while a positive correlation was observed with DNA replication, tumor cell proliferation, G2M phase checkpoints, the MYC pathway, and the DNA repair pathway (Fig. 1G). In conclusion, the results indicate a significant increase in RECQL4 expression in CC tumor tissues, which may contribute to tumorigenesis and potentially correlate with an unfavorable prognosis.

RECQL4 promotes CC tumor proliferation

To investigate the role of RECQL4 in CC development, we generated cell lines in SiHa and CaSki cells that either overexpressed or suppressed RECQL4 using lentiviral vectors. The efficiency of overexpression or knockdown was verified by q-PCR and western blotting (Fig. 2A). RECQL4 overexpression in SiHa and CaSki cells significantly increased cell proliferation, as demonstrated by the CCK-8 assay and colony formation assay (Fig. 2B, C). The EdU assay indicated that RECQL4 enhanced DNA replication (Fig. 2D) and upregulated Ki-67 expression (Fig. 2E). Conversely, RECQL4 suppression significantly impaired the proliferative capacity of SiHa and CaSki cells, as evidenced by the CCK-8 assay and colony formation assay (Fig. 2F, G). EdU assay showed a decrease in DNA replication following RECQL4 downregulation (Fig. 2I), which was consistent with the Ki-67 expression level (Fig. 2H). Accordingly, our findings indicate that RECQL4 enhances CC cell proliferation by increasing DNA replication capacity in vitro.

RECQL4 promotes tumor proliferation via promoting cell replication and reducing apoptosis

As mentioned previously, RECQL4 is positively associated with pathways involved in cell cycle progression and DNA replication. Flow cytometry was used to analyze changes in cell cycle progression and apoptosis after RECQL4 knockdown. RECQL4 suppression halted cell cycle progression at the G1/S phase in SiHa and CaSki cells (Fig. 3A-D). Levels of cell cycle regulation proteins, CDK2, CDK4, CDK6, Cyclin D1, Cyclin E1 and Cyclin A were significantly reduced and p21, p27 were significantly increased following RECQL4 downregulation (Fig. 3E–F). Furthermore, the overall rate of apoptosis and the cisplatin-induced apoptosis showed significant increase after RECQL4 downregulation (Fig. 3G–H). Additionally, flow cytometry analysis revealed a substantial increase in the expression of caspase-3, a protein associated with apoptosis (Fig. 3I–J). These results indicate that RECQL4 enhances the proliferative capacity of tumor cells by promoting cell cycle progression and inhibiting apoptosis.

RECQL4 promotes CC migration and invasion via promoting EMT in vitro

To comprehensively investigate RECQL4 involvement in CC cells, a series of experiments was conducted to explore its potential role in promoting metastasis in vitro. RECQL4 overexpression increases SiHa and CaSki cell migration, whereas inhibiting RECQL4 reduceed their migration, as confirmed by wound healing assays (Fig. 4A-D). The transwell assays revealed that upregulating RECQL4 substantially increaseed the migration and invasiveness of CC cells, whereas downregulating RECQL4 suppressed these capabilities (Fig. 4E-H). These results indicated that RECQL4 enhances the migratory and invasive characteristics of CC cells. A noticeable change in cell morphology was observed after downregulating RECQL4 (Supplementary Figure 1B). RECQL4 is hypothesized to influence the EMT process, thereby affecting the migratory and invasive capabilities of CC cells. The examination of EMT-associated proteins indicated that the decrease in RECQL4 led to an increase in E-cadherin and a decrease in N-cadherin, ZEB1, β-catenin, Vimentin, Slug and Sail. Conversely, the overexpression of RECQL4 demonstrated the contrary impact (Fig. 5A, B), which was consistent with flow cytometry analysis and immunofluorescence experiments of the E-cadherin expression level (Fig. 5C, D).

Furthermore, immunofluorescence experiments indicated that downregulating RECQL4 reduces β-catenin entry into the nucleus (Fig. 5E). To demonstrate this, we isolated subcellular structural proteins and examined the expression of β-catenin protein in the cytoplasm, nucleus, and cell membrane. The results showed that after downregulating RECQL4 in SiHa and CaSki cells, the expression of β-catenin protein was decreased in the cytoplasm and nucleus, but increased on the cell membrane (Fig. 5F). This suggests that RECQL4 plays a role in regulating the nuclear translocation of β-catenin and subsequently influences EMT.

RECQL4 may promote CC cell viability and migration via activating the PI3K/AKT pathway

To investigate the impact of RECQL4 on molecular functions and regulatory mechanisms in CC, we performed RNA sequence analyses on RECQL4 knockdown cells and control cells. A total of 1089 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified, with 511 genes upregulated and 578 genes downregulated compared to the RECQL4 knockdown group (Padj<0.05, logFC>1) (Supplementary Figure 1C). These DEGs were further analyzed for functional enrichment. Gene ontology analysis revealed RECQL4 involvement in various biological processes, such as the extracellular matrix, lateral plasma membrane, apical junction complex, receptor regulator activity, receptor ligand activity, acetylglucosaminyltransferase activity, regulation of signaling receptor activity, insulin-like growth factor binding, and growth factor binding (Supplementary Figure 1D). Additionally, KEGG analysis highlighted the role of RECQL4 in human papillomavirus infection, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, focal adhesion, proteoglycans in cancer, and transcriptional misregulation in cancer (Fig. 6A). We validated these findings using CC transcriptome data from the TCGA database by analyzing genes with differential expression levels of RECQL4, defined as 50 % of the RECQL4 mRNA expression level. KEGG analysis revealed that high expression of RECQL4 was significantly enriched in various pathways, including PI3K-Akt, proteoglycan, MAPK, and cytokine-cytokine receptor pathways (Fig. 6B). Therefore, we hypothesize that RECQL4 may be involved in controlling the proliferation, migration, and invasion of cervical cancer cells, which is mediated by the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway.

To investigate the molecular mechanism underlying the oncogenic effects of RECQL4, we analyzed the protein expression of the PI3K/AKT pathway in SiHa and CaSki cell lines after downregulating RECQL4. Western blotting results showed a decrease in phosphorylated AKT and phosphorylated GSK3β levels following RECQL4 down-regulation compared to the negative control (Fig. 6C). To determine the role of RECQL4 in modulating malignant characteristics of cervical cancer cells through the AKT pathway, we treated SiHa cell lines with the AKT inhibitor MK2206, which exhibited RECQL4 overexpression. Our findings demonstrated a significant inhibition of RECQL4-induced cell proliferation upon treatment with the AKT inhibitor, as evidenced by the CCK-8, clone formation, and Ki-67 level assay results (Fig. 6D-F). The anti-apoptotic effect induced by RECQL4 was effectively reversed by AKT inhibitors, as shown by the flow cytometric detection of apoptosis levels and cleaved-caspase3 levels (Fig. 6G, H). Additionally, AKT inhibition significantly reduced EMT and the migration and invasion potential caused by RECQL4 overexpression (Fig. 6I-M). These findings confirmed that RECQL4 plays a role in cervical cancer progression via activating the PI3K/AKT pathway. Furthermore, inhibiting AKT has been observed to effectively counteract the promotional influence exerted by RECQL4 on cervical cancer.

RECQL4 knockdown significantly inhibits tumor growth in nude mice

To evaluate the oncogenic role of RECQL4 in an in vivo setting, a xenograft tumor model was established in nude mice. Subcutaneous injections of SiHa cells that had been stably transfected with either the vector or shRECQL4 were administered to the mice. The results demonstrated a significant decrease in tumor volume and weight in the shRECQL4 group, as shown in Fig. 7A–C. IHC analysis revealed a reduction in RECQL4 staining in mice injected with shRECQL4 compared to those injected with the vector. The cleaved-caspase3 and E-cadherin staining indicated significantly higher levels in mice injected with shRECQL4 (Fig. 7D, F, G, H). Additionally, TUNEL assays demonstrated a greater number of apoptotic cells in the tumors of shRECQL4-injected mice compared to those injected with the vector (Fig. 7E, I).

Discussion

RECQL4 is identified as a DNA helicase crucial for maintaining genomic stability and repairing DNA damage [19,20]. Recent studies underscore RECQL4’s significant role in tumor progression, as shown by its upregulation in diverse cancer types and its impact on prognosis [21,22]. However, the precise mechanism of RECQL4 in cervical cancer remains unclear. Our findings demonstrate that RECQL4 is significantly upregulated in CC patients, with high levels associated with poor prognosis. Furthemore, RECQL4 shows strong oncogenic potential for CC cell proliferation and metastasis. Mechanistically, RECQL4 facilitates AKT phosphatase activation and enhances β-catenin nuclear translocation in CC cells, contributing to a malignant phenotype.

In our study, we found that RECQL4 is elevated in cervical cancer, enhancing cancer cell proliferation. Additionally, RECQL4 expression is higher in tumor cells compared to normal cells across nearly all tissue types. Constant DNA replication is a defining characteristic of tumors. Specific DNA helicases are required to manage replication stress and facilitate uncontrolled cell proliferation, explaining why RECQL4 expression increases in cancer tissues. RECQL4’s role in DNA replication and repair supports the inference that rapidly dividing cancer cells rely heavily on it for survival [23]. A study analyzing RECQL4 across multiple cancers found it upregulated in various types, particularly CHOL, CESC, DLBC, ESCA, GBM, HNSC, LGG, LIHC, PAAD, PRAD, SKCM, STAD, TGCT, THCA, and THYM. Analysis of TCGA data showed correlations between RECQs mRNA expression and survival outcomes such as OS, DSS, DFI, and PFI. In most cases, higher levels of RECQs expression correlated with poor prognosis and significantly affected outcomes. This pan-cancer study supports our viewpoint [22].

Our study confirms that RECQL4 plays a crucial role in regulating tumor cell migration and invasion by enhancing EMT, consistent with previous studies. However, the mechanisms through which RECQL4 exerts its oncogenic effects vary across cancer types. Prior research shows a positive correlation between RECQL4 levels and prostate cancer aggressiveness, highlighting its significant role in prostate cancer carcinogenesis [24]. In metastatic prostate cancer cell models, RECQL4 knockdown significantly reduces in vitro cell invasiveness and in vivo tumorigenicity [14]. In esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), RECQL4 maintains redox homeostasis and genomic integrity; its deficiency increases oxidative stress, exacerbates DNA damage, and leads to tumor cell senescence, thereby hindering tumor progression [25]. In breast cancer, genomic alterations and elevated RECQL4 mRNA or protein expression associate with increased invasiveness and poor survival. Depletion of RECQL4 impairs DNA replication in cultured breast cancer cells and enhances sensitivity to chemotherapy [12].

In addition, our study revealed a significant association between RECQL4 and TMB in cervical cancer. RECQL4 gene mutations can disrupt DNA repair and genomic stability, leading to DNA damage accumulation and increased mutations, ultimately resulting in elevated TMB [4,26,27]. Although previous studies have not explored the correlation between RECQL4 and TMB, reports suggest that WRN, another gene in the RECQ family, associates with increased TMB levels in colorectal and breast cancer [28]. In our study, we identified a significant correlation between RECQL4 and TMB levels, though this data was obtained from online sources. Impaired RECQL4 function compromises DNA repair ability, leading to mutation accumulation in tumor cells. Therefore, further validation through subsequent sequencing analysis is needed.

Through RNA-seq data analysis, we propose that RECQL4 primarily functions via the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. The PI3K/Akt pathway influences the cell cycle by regulating downstream targets like cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors and insulin-like growth factor 2 (IGF2), promoting tumor cell proliferation [29]. Akt also exerts anti-apoptotic effects by negatively regulating Bcl-2 family proteins [30]. This pathway plays a critical role in initiating epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in squamous carcinoma cells. AKT regulates cellular morphology, cell adhesion, and the migratory and invasive capabilities of cells through various mechanisms, impacting the epithelial to mesenchymal transition [[31], [32], [33]]. In cervical cancer, HPV E6/E7 oncoproteins induce HPV-infected cell transformation, leading to degradation and destabilization of p53 and pRb, as well as genetic instability, proliferation, apoptosis resistance, and metabolic alterations through activation of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway [34]. Consequently, the PI3K/AKT pathway serves as a potential therapeutic target in cervical cancer.

Our study confirms that RECQL4 regulates the malignant phenotype of cervical cancer cells via the AKT pathway, consistent with previous findings. Earlier studies show that RECQL4 activates the AKT pathway in esophageal cancer cells, promoting proliferation [25]. RECQL4 also promotes oxaliplatin resistance in colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) cells by activating the PI3K/AKT pathway [35]. Moreover, it binds to the transcription factor YB1 and regulates the multidrug resistance gene MDR1 via the AKT pathway, increasing cisplatin resistance in gastric cancer cells [36]. Despite these insights, the mechanisms by which RECQL4 regulates AKT remain unclear. Prior research suggests a complex interaction between AKT and DDR (DNA damage response) genes. For example, AKT inhibits ATM activity through phosphorylation, affecting DNA damage signal transmission [37]. AKT kinase influences the repair rate of DNA double-strand breaks by regulating DNA repair enzyme expression [38]. Furthermore, AKT modulates DDR signaling factor activity, such as by phosphorylating CHK1, leading to its inactivation, which impacts cell cycle regulation and DNA damage repair [39].

We hypothesize that RECQL4, a helicase involved in DNA repair, replication, and genomic stability, may influence AKT signaling activity due to its aberrant expression. The AKT pathway is crucial for cellular functions such as proliferation, survival, and metabolism and is linked to various cancer developments. Further studies are necessary particularly on RECQL4 and AKT pathway component interactions, along with in vitro and in vivo experiments, to elucidate the complex mechanisms of RECQL4 in cancer development.

In conclusions, we have identified DNA helicase RECQL4 as a novel oncogenic factor in CC. Elevated expression of RECQL4 promotes cell malignant phenotypes through PI3K-AKT pathway. The expression of RECQL4 increases the EMT regulatory proteins N-cadherin and β-catenin and enhances the entry of β-catenin into the nucleus, which depends on the PI3K-AKT signaling pathways. These insights provide comprehensive evidence of how RECQL4 contributes to tumorigenesis and highlight the potentials of RECQL4 in conducting clinical trials to validate its therapeutic potential.

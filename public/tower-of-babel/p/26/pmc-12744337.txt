Application of ultrasound-assisted atomized plasma-activated water to improve the quality properties of grapes
Authors: Jiayi Wang, Shun Lv, Hongyu Zhou, Dilireba Shataer, Jing Lu, Li Zheng, Jun Xing
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractImmersion washing disinfects microorganisms but requires drying before packaging and can detach grapes. Ultrasonic atomization generates mist and addresses these limitations. Plasma-activated water, a novel non-thermal technology, remains unexplored in atomization mode. This study applied atomized PAW to treat grapes, observing quality attributes during storage (day 0–7) at room temperature. Attributes included physicochemical properties (weight loss, decay incidence, firmness, titratable acidity (TA), total soluble solids (TSS)), microbial quality (counts of aerobic mesophilic bacteria (AMC), molds, and yeasts (M&Y); microbial community composition), markers of reactive oxygen species (levels of malondialdehyde (MDA), H2O2, and O2–), and physiological responses related to oxidative stress (activities of superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT), phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL), and chalcone isomerase (CHI)). Results showed PAW had minimal effect on TAA and TSS, while reducing decay and weight loss and maintaining greater firmness versus controls. PAW treatment stimulated SOD, CAT, PAL, and CHI activities, reducing MDA, H2O2, and O2– levels, indicating PAW mitigates oxidative damage. Microbial analysis showed PAW maintained lower AMC and M&Y counts during storage compared to controls. Gene sequencing revealed Aspergillus abundance increased with storage time, becoming dominant on days 4 and 7; however, PAW treatment maintained lower Aspergillus abundance than controls, reducing decay risk. After PAW treatment, Glomerella and Geosmithia showed less fluctuation during storage versus controls, with Glomerella maintaining highest abundance throughout. This suggests PAW may inhibit Aspergillus through microbial interactions by stabilizing Glomerella and Geosmithia abundance. This study confirms atomized PAW enhances grape quality during storage, supporting reduction of post-harvest losses.Keywords: Plasma-activated water, Ultrasound, Atomized, Disinfection, Non-thermal

Introduction

Grapes, as a vital agricultural commodity, occupy a prominent role in the global agricultural economy [1]. Although postharvest decay has been a primary factor limiting transportation, the cold chain has emerged as one of the most reliable methods for maintaining grape freshness [2]. The combination of cold chain logistics and preservatives can substantially enhance grape freshness, with preservation cards or papers impregnated with 1-MCP and chlorine dioxide being the most commonly employed preservatives [3]. Enhancing the consumer shopping experience is essential for merchants, who often refrain from using preservation reagents at the retail stage. Furthermore, the lack of low-temperature settings at this stage poses additional challenges for grape preservation.

To preserve the freshness of produce, non-thermal processing technologies are increasingly employed to disinfect microorganisms and inactivate enzymes without compromising nutritional value [4]. Plasma, as an innovative non-thermal technology, is generated by ionizing gases under the influence of a high-voltage electric field [5]. This plasma can interact with aqueous solutions to form plasma-activated water (PAW), which contains stable reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (RONS), including ozone, hydrogen peroxide, nitrates, and nitrites [6]. These RONS are capable of compromising bacterial cell walls and membranes, leading to the leakage of intracellular components [7]. Currently, PAW is utilized to inactivate microbes, extend shelf life, and enhance the physiological properties of lettuce [8], walnut kernels [9], goji berries [10], cherry tomatoes [11], and spinach [11].

Washing is a proven method for removing microorganisms from the surface of fresh produce, thereby extending their shelf life. However, this method is effective only during the washing process and does not provide ongoing protection. Conversely, the coating method improves the quality of fresh produce by adhering aqueous preservatives to produce surfaces, offering continuous control during storage [12]. When fresh produce is immersed in the solution, surface microorganisms may enter the solution, leading to cross-contamination [11]. Additionally, the immersion method generates a substantial amount of waste solution that must be disposed of and requires time to dry before packaging. In the case of grapes, both the washing and coating methods involve immersing the grapes in the solution, which can result in some grapes detaching. Ultrasonic atomization is a process that employs high-frequency (1.7 MHz or 2.4 MHz) resonance from ceramic atomizing discs to disrupt the bonds between water molecules, producing a water mist with nano-sized dimensions [13]. This method addresses the issues associated with immersing fresh produce in aqueous solutions while also providing a constant control effect. Furthermore, exogenous reactive oxygen species (ROS) can markedly enhance the activity of defense-related enzymes in plants, including superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT), phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL), and chalcone isomerase (CHI), thereby augmenting the plant's resilience to environmental stressors [8]. Given that PAW is abundant in RONS and its effects are sustained post-application, we are interested in investigating whether, beyond its disinfection properties, PAW might also elicit alterations in defense-related enzymes. In this study, we employed ultrasonic atomization technology to treat grapes, using PAW as the atomization solution. The properties, including defense-related enzymes, ROS metabolism, and microbial changes, were analyzed to assess the efficacy of this novel preservation method.

Materials and methods

Sample processing

Samples of grapes (Vitis vinifera cv. Munage) were procured from a local market on the day of the experiment. The PAW was prepared following the method outlined in our previous publication [11]. Briefly, 50 mL of distilled water was placed in a quartz reactor situated between the discharge electrodes of a dielectric barrier discharge (DBD) plasma system (CTP-2000 KP; Suman, Nanjing, China). The input power, discharge frequency, duty cycle, and discharge time were set at 50 W, 200 Hz, 50 %, and 10 min, respectively. This specific condition was chosen based on our previous research, which indicated it produces the highest concentration of RONS [11]. The concentrations of O3, H2O2, nitrate, nitrite, along with the oxidation–reduction potential (ORP), conductivity, and pH, were determined as outlined in our previous report [11]. The PAW was aerosolized as outlined in a previous study [14]. In brief, an acrylic chamber measuring 50 cm × 50 cm × 60 cm served as a disinfection box, equipped with an ultrasonic-assisted nebulizer (402AI; Yuwell, Shanghai, China) mounted on top (Fig. 1). Once the chamber was filled with the aerosolized PAW, we swiftly inserted the grape sample (1000 ± 150 g) from the right side and disinfected it for 3 min. The aerosolization rate and ultrasonic frequency were set at 3.6 mL/min and 1.7 MHz, respectively, to ensure uniformly wetted surfaces without visible droplets on the grapes' surface, as optimized in a previous study [14]. During the treatment, the grapes were directly exposed to the PAW aerosol, and immediately afterward, they were placed in plastic boxes and sealed with polyvinyl chloride plastic film. The sample was stored at 25 ℃ and analyzed on days 0, 2, 4, and 7.

Schematic diagram of the ultrasound-assisted aerosolization equipment.

Determination of decay incidence, weight loss, total soluble solids (TSS), titratable acids (TA), and firmness

Twenty grapes were randomly selected, and their firmness was assessed using a GY-4 hardness tester (Aidebao, Leqing, China). The slurry from these 20 grapes was utilized to determine the TSS and TA using a Brix-Acidity meter (ATAGO, Tokyo, Japan). Defense-related enzymes analysis

Ten grapes were randomly selected and processed into powder using a liquid nitrogen grinding mill (IKA, Staufen, Germany). The enzymatic activities of SOD and CAT in the resulting powder were subsequently analyzed using the G0101F and G0303F kits, respectively, supplied by Geruisi-bio Co., Ltd (Suzhou, China). The activities of PAL and CHI were assessed following the methodology outlined in a previous study [15], with minor modifications. Briefly, the sample powder was dissolved in an acetic acid-sodium acetate buffer (0.1 M, pH 5.5; 1 mM PEG, 1 % PVP, 1 % Triton X-100) at a ratio of 1:3 (w/v) and subjected to centrifugation at 12,000g for 20 min at 4 °C. For the CHI assay, 2 mL of the supernatant was combined with 3 mL of sodium acetate buffer (50 mM, pH 5.2; 1 mM EDTA and 5 mM β-mercaptoethanol). Following centrifugation at 11,000g for 40 min at 4 °C, the absorbance of the supernatant was measured at 380 nm. One unit (U) of CHI activity was defined as the amount required to produce 1 μg of N-acetyl-D-glucosamine per hour at 37 °C. For the PAL assay, 1 mL of the supernatant was mixed with 3 mL of borate buffer (50 mM, pH 8.8; 10 mM phenylalanine) and incubated at 37 °C for 60 min. The absorbance was recorded at 290 nm. One unit (U) of PAL activity was defined as the rate of production of 1 μg of cinnamic acid equivalents per hour.

ROS and malondialdehyde (MDA) analysis

The G0168F, G0116F, and G0109F kits, manufactured by Geruisi-bio Co., Ltd, were employed to quantify H2O2, O2–, and MDA, respectively.

Microbial analysis

Ten grapes were placed into a stomacher bag containing a 0.85 % NaCl solution at a ratio of 1:9 (w/v), and the sample was homogenized for 2 min at 120 rpm. The serially diluted suspension (1 mL) was plated on aerobic plate count agar (Hopebio, Qingdao, China) and incubated for 2 days at 37 ℃ to quantify the aerobic mesophilic counts (AMC). For molds and yeasts (M&Y), 0.1 mL of the diluted suspension was surface-plated on rose bengal agar (Hopebio) and incubated for 3 days at 28 ℃.

ITS sequencing

The suspension described in Section 2.5 was filtered using sterilized filters (0.45 μm) to isolate the fungi. The resulting filters were stored on dry ice and transported to Personal Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). Sample sequencing was conducted using the company's Illumina NovaSeq platform. In brief, DNA was extracted using the DNA Kit (M5635-02; Omega Bio-Tek, Norcross, GA, USA), following the manufacturer’s instructions. The forward and reverse primers for the ITS genes V1 region were ITS5 (5′-GGAAGTAAAAGTCGTAACAAGG-3′) and ITS2 (5′-GCTGCGTTCTTCATCGATGC-3′), respectively. Customized 7-bp barcodes were integrated into the primer sequences for sequencing. The PCR components included 5 μL of buffer (5 × ), 0.25 μL of Fast pfu DNA Polymerase (5 U/μL), 2 μL of dNTPs (2.5 mM), 1 μL of each forward and reverse primer (10 μmol), 1 μL of DNA template, and 14.75 μL of double-distilled H2O. The thermal cycling protocol involved an initial denaturation step at 98 °C for 5 min, followed by 30 cycles comprising denaturation at 98 °C for 30 s, annealing at 55 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 45 s, with a final extension step of 5 min at 72 °C. PCR amplicons were purified with DNA Clean Beads (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) and quantified using the dsDNA Assay Kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The NovaSeq 6000 SP reagent was used to perform paired-end 2 × 250 bp sequencing on equal amounts of amplicons, with a total of 500 cycles. Sequence and relative abundance at the genus level were analyzed using QIIME2.

Statistical analysis

Each experiment was independently conducted three times. The mean values for the control and PAW groups were analyzed using an independent samples t-test, conducted with SPSS version 20 software. The data are presented as mean ± standard deviation.

Results

Effects of aerosolized PAW on decay incidence, weight loss, firmness, TA, and TSS

In this study, the PAW showed O3, H2O2, nitrite, and nitrate concentrations of 5.7 ± 0.3 mg/L, 653 ± 26 μM, 0.4 ± 0.1 mg/L, and 33 ± 3 mg/L, respectively. Additionally, the ORP, conductivity, and pH were recorded at 538 ± 26 mV, 881 ± 34 μs/cm, and 2.63, respectively. The use of PAW was found to delay the progression of decay incidence and weight loss compared to the control group (Fig. 2, Fig. 2). Notably, from day 4 to day 7, significant differences were observed between the PAW and control groups. The firmness of the grapes decreased during storage, with the PAW group exhibiting values that were 1.05-, 1.04-, and 1.09-fold those of the control group on days 2, 4, and 7, respectively (Fig. 2C). Regarding TA, both the PAW and control groups exhibited a decreasing trend during storage, with no significant difference observed between the two groups (Fig. 2D). Similarly, the results depicted in Fig. 2E indicate that TSS was not significantly affected by PAW during the storage period.

Alterations in decay incidence (A), weight loss (B), firmness (C), TA (D), and TSS (E) following treatment with plasma-activated water. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01.

Effects of aerosolized PAW on defense-related enzymes

The SOD activity in the PAW group (315–446 U) was significantly elevated compared to the control group (304–327 U) throughout the storage period (Fig. 3A). Specifically, on days 2, 4, and 7, the SOD activity in the PAW group was 1.34-, 1.36-, and 1.36-fold of the control group, respectively. The CAT activity in the PAW group ranged from 205 to 339 U, with values during storage (days 2–7) being significantly greater than those in the control group (Fig. 3B). Post-PAW treatment, PAL activity was markedly increased (Fig. 3C). On days 2, 4, and 7, the PAL activity in the PAW group was 1.89-, 1.68-, and 1.82-fold of the control group, respectively. Regarding CHI, the activity in the PAW group was 1.26-, 1.29-, and 1.40-fold of the control group on days 2, 4, and 7, respectively, with significant differences between the PAW and control groups observed on days 4 and 7 (Fig. 3D).

Alterations of defense-related enzymes during storage. A, B, C, and D indicate SOD, CAT, PAL, and CHI, respectively. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01.

Effects of aerosolized PAW on ROS and MDA content

During storage, the concentration of O2– in the PAW group ranged from 7.1 to 14.8 μM/g, which was lower than that observed in the control group (Fig. 4A). Regarding H2O2, the content in the PAW group constituted 77 %, 71 %, and 68 % of the control group's levels on days 2, 4, and 7, respectively (Fig. 4B). The trend in MDA levels mirrored that of O2– and H2O2, with values in the PAW group being 79 %, 71 %, and 62 % of the control group's levels on days 2, 4, and 7, respectively (Fig. 4C). Throughout the storage period (days 2–7), a significant difference was observed between the PAW and control groups (Fig. 4C). The trends observed for SOD and CAT (Fig. 3, Fig. 3) were contrary to those of the three ROS-related parameters.

Alterations of ROS-related properties during storage. A, B, and C indicate O2–, H2O2, and MDA, respectively. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01.

Changes of microbial community and counts during storage

As illustrated in Fig. 5A, the counts of AMC in the PAW group ranged from 4.0 to 4.3 log CFU/g during storage, which were significantly lower than those observed in the control group (4.6–5.9 log CFU/g). For M&Y, the counts during storage (3.9–5.8 log CFU/g) were significantly reduced to 3.4–4.4 log CFU/g following treatment with PAW (Fig. 5B). The counts on days 0, 2, 4, and 7 in the PAW group were 88 %, 78 %, 71 %, and 76 % of those in the control group, respectively (Fig. 5B).

Alterations of AMC (A) and M&Y (B) in grapes during storage. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01.

At the genus level, the predominant fungi identified in grapes include Glomerella, Geosmithia, Truncatella, Aspergillus, and Acremonium (Fig. 6). In the control group at day 0, the relative abundance (RA) of these fungi was 40.21 %, 16.61 %, 10.24 %, 3.85 %, and 3.51 %, respectively. Following PAW treatment, the RA of these five fungi was 36.67 %, 19.84 %, 9.48 %, 4.67 %, and 2.11 %, respectively. For Glomerella in the control group, the RA decreased from 40.21 % on day 0 to 6.48 % on day 7; whereas in the PAW group, it only changed from 36.67 % to 32.15 %. In the control group, the RA of Geosmithia increased from 16.61 % on day 0 to 25.14 % on day 2; during the subsequent storage period, it exhibited a downward trend, reaching 10.64 % on day 7. However, in comparison, the RA of Geosmithia in the PAW group showed no significant change during storage, recorded at 19.84 % on day 0 and 20.61 % on day 7. At the end of storage (day 7), the RA of Truncatella in the control group was 0.12 %, a decrease of 10.12 % compared to day 0. Although Truncatella in the PAW group also showed a downward trend, the decrease was slower compared to the control group, dropping by 4.92 % over 7 days. For Aspergillus in the control group, RA continued to increase during storage and surpassed other microbes on days 4 and 7, reaching 58.64 % and 81.61 %, respectively; by the end of storage, this represented an increase of 77.76 % compared to day 0. While the RA of Aspergillus in the PAW group also showed an upward trend during storage, it remained far lower than that in the control group and only increased by 28.17 % over 7 days. In addition, from day 0 to 7, Aspergillus was not the dominant microbe in the PAW group; instead, Glomerella had the highest RA. For Acremonium in the control group, the RA on day 7 was 0.51 %, a decrease of 3.00 % compared to day 0; whereas in the PAW group, Acremonium showed little change over 7 days, with RA increasing by only 0.47 %.

Discussion

The decay rate, weight loss rate, and firmness are essential indicators for preserving fresh produce [16]. In this study, we discovered that atomized PAW can simultaneously reduce decay, decrease the weight loss rate, and maintain firmness compared to the control, with these changes potentially linked to microbial proliferation [17,18]. We observed that PAW kept the counts of AMC and M&Y lower than those in the control group during storage, which may explain the above results. As an acidic RONS solution, PAW contains O3 and H2O2, with its disinfection efficacy primarily relying on these components [19]. These two powerful oxidizing agents are widely used in processing fresh produce, and similar results regarding firmness and weight loss have been noted after using these compounds [20,21]. In this study, although the AMC was consistently lower than the control, there was a decrease in AMC counts on the second day, followed by an increase in subsequent days. Among the RONS in PAW, O3 is the most unstable, followed by H2O2. Over time, both substances volatilize and convert into oxygen, reducing PAW's disinfection effect on the grape surface. This may explain the observed phenomenon. Previous research has also shown that AMC counts in fresh produce treated with PAW tend to rise during storage [[8], [9], [10]].

PAL and CHI are essential enzymes in the phenylalanine metabolic pathway, activated in response to external stimuli, thereby assisting plants in combating oxidative stress [22]. SOD and CAT are crucial antioxidant enzymes in plants, and their increased activity can facilitate the removal of ROS, thus protecting cells from oxidative damage [23]. Ozone can stimulate the plant's antioxidant enzyme system, including CAT and SOD [24]. Hydrogen peroxide, as a significant signaling molecule, also plays a crucial role in regulating the ROS-scavenging capacity. Studies suggest that H2O2 can enhance the plant's antioxidant capacity by modulating CHI, SOD, CAT, and PAL [[25], [26], [27], [28]]. PAW is abundant in both O3 and H2O2, which accounts for its ability to enhance the activity of SOD, CHI, CAT, and PAL in this study. This enhancement, in turn, reduces oxidative damage, as evidenced by the observed levels of MDA, O2–, and H2O2 in this study.

Recent advancements in sequencing technology have enabled more comprehensive analyses of microbial communities. For instance, gene sequencing facilitates the identification and quantitative assessment of various fungal species within complex microbial ecosystems [29]. In this study, the abundance of Aspergillus in the control group increased significantly during storage. This fungus is prevalent in fresh produce, and a study has established a strong correlation between the presence of Aspergillus in produce and elevated levels of aflatoxins, a toxin posing a substantial threat to consumer health [30]. Furthermore, research indicates that Aspergillus can lead to spoilage and deterioration of fruits [30]. Following PAW treatment, the changes in the abundance of this fungus were slower compared to the control group, potentially attributable to the disinfection effect of PAW.

Biodiversity and stable microbial networks are associated with fruit health, whereas diseased products exhibit microbial imbalance [31]. Plant endophytes, functioning as biocontrol agents, have the capacity to produce volatile organic compounds (VOC) that act as bio-fumigants to manage postharvest diseases [32]. The fungi Glomerella and Geosmithia mitigate fruit diseases during storage by inhibiting pathogenic fungi through VOC production, thereby reducing spoilage [31,33]. In this study, following PAW treatment, the RA of Glomerella and Geosmithia demonstrated a more gradual rate of change compared to the control group. An additional rationale for the control of Aspergillus in the PAW group may be attributed to PAW's capacity to maintain microbial ecological balance. This balance prevents significant reductions in the levels of Glomerella and Geosmithia, thereby inhibiting the proliferation of Aspergillus.

Conclusion

The present study employed atomized PAW to treat grapes, subsequently analyzing alterations in their quality and surface microorganisms during storage at room temperature. The results indicated that PAW effectively reduced both the spoilage rate and weight loss rate, while maintaining greater firmness relative to the control group. PAW treatment was observed to induce the activities of SOD, CAT, PAL, and CHI enzymes, thereby enhancing the grapes' stress resistance and ROS and MDA content, which mitigated oxidative damage. Microbial analysis demonstrated that PAW maintained lower AMC and M&Y counts during storage compared to the control group. Sequencing analysis revealed that the RA of Aspergillus increased progressively with storage time, becoming the dominant microorganism on days 4 and 7; however, PAW treatment maintained the RA of Aspergillus at a lower level than that of the control group throughout storage, thereby reducing grape spoilage and associated food safety risks. Following PAW treatment, the RA of Glomerella and Geosmithia exhibited significantly less fluctuation during storage compared to the control group, with Glomerella maintaining the highest RA throughout the storage period. This suggests that, beyond its disinfection effect, PAW may inhibit Aspergillus through microbial interactions by stabilizing the RA of Glomerella and Geosmithia. In conclusion, this study substantiates that atomized PAW is a viable method for enhancing grape quality during room temperature storage, offering potential technical support for augmenting income and minimizing post-harvest losses of agricultural products. Nonetheless, this study has not yet conducted a comprehensive investigation of the physiological changes in grapes; future research will employ multi-omics approaches to analyze stress responses.

Rapidly Progressive Interstitial Lung Disease Associated With Anti-MDA5 Dermatomyositis
Authors: Eric Andrey Rodríguez-Vega, Miguel Octavio Gaxiola-Gaxiola, Heidegger Norberto Mateos Toledo
Published: 2026

A 31-year-old woman with no relevant past medical history presented to the emergency department with a 4-week history of paroxysmal cough, fever, and progressive dyspnea, associated with arthralgias and ulcerated cutaneous lesions on the hands and elbows (Fig. 1F and G). Chest CT showed diffuse ground-glass opacities with a tendency toward consolidation, predominantly in the basal regions (Fig. 1A–C). On admission, laboratory tests showed a white blood cell count of 7.61 × 109/L (neutrophils 6.93 × 109/L, lymphocytes 0.46 × 109/L), C-reactive protein 7.43 mg/dL, procalcitonin 2.39 ng/mL, and creatine kinase 47 U/L. Autoimmunity testing revealed a positive myositis panel (EUROIMMUNE) for anti-MDA5 (++) and anti-Ro52 (++). She progressed to severe hypoxemic respiratory failure and required invasive mechanical ventilation. A respiratory multiplex PCR panel (BioFire Respiratory Panel 2.1) detected Mycoplasma pneumoniae. Bronchoscopy with bronchoalveolar lavage was performed; it was negative for hemosiderin, and the sample was not adequate for differential cell count. Transbronchial cryobiopsies showed multiple Masson bodies and hyaline membranes, findings consistent with organizing pneumonia and diffuse alveolar damage (Fig. 1D and E). A diagnosis of rapidly progressive interstitial lung disease (RP-ILD) secondary to anti-MDA5 dermatomyositis was established. Targeted antibiotic therapy with fluoroquinolones and combined immunosuppressive treatment with methylprednisolone, baricitinib, intravenous immunoglobulin, and tacrolimus was initiated. Despite treatment, the patient died 21 days after admission. RP-ILD is a severe complication of anti-MDA5 dermatomyositis, and infectious processes, particularly those of viral etiology, have been described as potential triggers. Ulcerated cutaneous lesions, as well as concomitant anti-Ro52 positivity, have been associated with a worse prognosis.1, 2

(Panels F and G) Violaceous-erythematous cutaneous lesions on the fingertips and distal phalanges with areas of scaling and fissuring, skin ulcers overlying Gottron sign on the metacarpophalangeal joints of the fingers.

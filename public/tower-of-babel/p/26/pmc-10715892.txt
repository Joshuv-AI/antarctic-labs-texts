Therapeutic effects of Chinese herbal medicines for treatment of urolithiasis: A review
Authors: Chen Jiang, Lingyun Wang, Yang Wang, Rongjia Xu, Hongtao Yang, Jibin Peng
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractUrolithiasis is a common and complex disease of the urinary system, which can cause urinary tract blockage, urinary tract infection, and even damage to urinary system-related tissues. Although urolithiasis can be cured, its high recurrence rate and the development of chronic kidney disease in some patients have drawn the attention of nephrologists. Although the application of extracorporeal lithotripsy, percutaneous nephrolithotomy and other minimally invasive techniques have made the treatment of urolithiasis more efficient, pharmacotherapy plays an indispensable role in reducing their morbidity and recurrence rates. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has been used for treatment and prevention of urolithiasis in developing countries for centuries, known for its unquestionable efficacy and safety. This article reviews the progress of clinical trials and pharmacological studies on the treatment of urolithiasis with Chinese herbal medicines (CHMs). The mechanism of CHMs in the treatment of urolithiasis mainly involve preventing further growth and aggregation of urolithiasis, reducing the PH of urine, promoting calculus dissolution. Furthermore, some CHMs can increase urine output, relax smooth muscles, and promote the removal of calculus. These findings provide new treatment strategies and options for urolithiasis and secondary kidney damage.Keywords: Chinese herbal medicines, chronic kidney disease, mechanism, pharmacology, urolithiasis

Introduction

Urolithiasis is a global health burden with an increasing prevalence ranging from 5% to 15% in different regions. According to the location of the stone, it contains kidney stones, ureteral stones, bladder stones and urethra stones. The increasing incidence and prevalence of urolithiasis worldwide can be attributed to changes in dietary structure and living habits (Raheem et al., 2017; Romero, Akpinar & Assimos, 2010). The incidence of urolithiasis ranged from 4.6% to 6.5% in Asian countries (Bae et al., 2014, Yang et al., 2016). Actually, the consequences of urolithiasis, such as urinary tract infections, obstructive nephropathy, even renal function injury and squamous cell carcinoma impose significant burdens. And the risk of developing chronic kidney disease due to urolithiasis is being paid more attention by nephrologist (Gambaro et al., 2017). Pharmacotherapy, extracorporeal lithotripsy and the application of some minimally invasive techniques are the main treatment at present.

Although modern interventional procedures and surgery are being improved, such as the extra corporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL), percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL) and open surgery, there are still occasional complications, including infection, hematoma and adjacent organ damage, and the recurrence of stones. About 50% of patients with urolithiasis after the initial episode treatment have to experience stone(s) recurrence within 10 years (Bitsori & Galanakis, 2004). Therefore, preventing stone recurrence has become the focus of urolithiasis treatment. It is of great scientific and immediate significance to alleviate symptoms of urolithiasis, prevent the recurrence of urolithiasis, and delay the progress of CKD caused by urolithiasis.

China is one of the major urolithiasis incidence areas in the world due to its geographical characteristics, dietary habits, and lifestyle. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), which includes Chinese herb medicines (CHMs), acupuncture and even massage (Fig. 1), has been used to prevent and treat urolithiasis and its related diseases for thousands of years. It has been proven to have definite curative effect by regulating the PH of urine, promoting dissolution of stones, increasing urine output, relaxing smooth muscle, and promoting the removal of stone (Sachs, 2003, Shah & Whitfield, 2002). Moreover, because of the abundant resources in CHMs and the clear curative effect, CHMs is the mainstay of medical treatment in developing countries. According to the World Health Organization, CHMs serves 75%–80% of the world population as a complement (Bardia, Nisly, Zimmerman, Gryzlak, & Wallace, 2007).

TCM treatment system in urolithiasis.

Nowadays, the Committee of Complementary and Alternative Medicine within the American Urological Association (AUA) recognize the integration of non-conventional therapies into urological clinical practice. As a kind of complementary and alternative medicine, TCM has played an extremely important role in the treatment of urolithiasis. Although the principles of TCM treatment are not clear, TCM has been proven effective in relieving symptoms, helping stone discharge and preventing recurrence of urolithiasis. In this article, we will focus on CHMs in the treatment of urolithiasis, which would be benefit for the treatment of urolithiasis and secondary kidney damage.

TCM theory of urolithiasis

Five centuries BC ago, diagnostic and treatments about urolithiasis were already mentioned in the Hippocratic Collection (An ancient medical books) (Bitsori and Galanakis, 2004, Shah & Whitfield, 2002). Records of the typical abdominal pain of patients with urolithiasis and Celsic surgical method were found in Greek medicine book since the first century BC (Sachs, 2003, Shah & Whitfield, 2002). In China, the records about urolithiasis, symptoms and treatments of urinary stones diseases can be traced back to several ancient texts of traditional medicines. In Qin and Han dynasties (in the first century AD), Shennong’s Classic of Materia Medica, a traditional Chinese medicine book, has mentioned the diseases as “Shi Lin”, which were described as sand-like substances discharged from the urine, urination pain, or failure to urinate due to stone-induced uremic obstruction. The records of the causes of urolithiasis can be traced back to Huangdi’s Internal Classic at the first century BC. According to the theory of TCM, the cause of urolithiasis involved not only blood stasis and inflammation of the metabolites condensed leading to blocks, but also urine alkaline substances condensed into stone in urinary system. During the Sui and Tang dynasties, acupuncture and moxibustion were used to treat the stone expulsion. The treatment strategies mainly involved promoting heat clearance, disinhibiting dampness, facilitating urine flow, and expelling stones, which are generally achieved by CHM, acupuncture and moxibustion. Based on our experiences today, medicinal herb are known to potentially increase urine volume, alleviate pain, or possess anti-inflammatory components (López and Hoppe, 2010, Sachs, 2003).

Effect of CHM in treatment of urolithiasis

Many clinical studies have proved the effectiveness of TCM in the treatment and prevention of urolithiasis (Kasote, Jagtap, Thapa, Khyade, & Russell, 2017). Wu-Ling-San (WLS) formula, a TCM compound, has been used in the treatment of urinary diseases over the centuries. Active therapy effect with WLS formula was proved in a prospective, randomized and placebo-controlled clinical trial (Lin, Ho, Lin, Huang, & Chen, 2013). WLS formula (2 g, three times daily for one month) was given to 14 patients with recurrent calcium oxalate nephrolithiasis. After treatment, the mean urine output level was found to be significantly increased compared to placebo group. No patient complained of side effects, such as fatigue, dizziness, musculoskeletal symptoms, or gastrointestinal disturbance. Recently, a clinical trial reported the preventive effect of Salvia miltiorrhiza Bge. (Danshen in Chinese) in treatment of urolithiasis from Taiwan’s database (Chen et al., 2018). Anti-urolithiasis efficacy of Phyllanthus niruri Linn. was also ensured in a clinic trial within 150 patients with calcium oxalate renal stones after extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (Micali et al., 2006). The stone-free rate was 93.5% in treatment group and 83.3% in control group, among 52% patients who underwent therapy with P. niruri extract (2 g/day) for at least three months. In the case of patients with lower caliceal stones, the treatment group showed a statistically significant stone-free rate of 93.7%. It means that a regular self-administration of P. niruri may increase stone-free rate, especially in patients with lower caliceal location.

CHM in the treatment of urolithiasis is mainly based on the following aspects (Gohel & Wong, 2006, Kasote et al., 2017): 1. The active components of CHM including carboxyl, hydroxyl, amino acid and oxygen heterocycles, which can form a coordination complex with calcium ion. These components reduce the saturation of the urine and prevent hypercalcemia, exhibiting inhibitory properties. 2. The organic components of CHM and macromers such as flavonoids, anthraquinones, polysaccharides, alkaloids, saponins, and proteins can form molecular complex with calcium ions through hydrophobic, overlapping, electrostatic, and other interactions (Fig. 2). 3. Some CHMs have been shown to reduce the pH value of urine, promoting dissolution of stones and preventing the formation of stones. 4. The effective components of CHM adhere to the growth site of the crystal to prevent the further aggregation of urolithiasis, thus inhibiting the formation of urolithiasis. 5. Some CHMs can also increase the amount of urine, relax smooth muscle, and promote the removal of stone. 6. Some CHMs can help to reduce the accumulation of inflammatory lesions from the crystallization of urolithiasis in the epithelial cells in the urinary tract. However, because of the complexity of the chemical composition, it is still difficult to research all of the effective components of CHMs. Hence, the discovery of effective ingredients has always been one of the focus about the research of CHM on the treatment of urolithiasis.

Single medicinal material for treatment

Lysimachia christinae

L. christinae Hance. (Jinqiancao in Chinese) is a wild species found in almost every part of China. In the western country, L. christinae is also known as Desmodium styracifolium (Obs.) Merr. (Guangjinqiancao in Chinese) and Glechoma longituba (Nakai) Kupr. (Lianqiancao in Chinese). L. christinae is commonly used in urinary system diseases in TCM. The effect of this herb is diuretic, detumescent, and detoxifying. In China, this herb is believed to prevent and treat urolithiasis as a diuretic, to promote urine acidification for thousands of years. Its boiled extract has been demonstrated in vitro to inhibit crystal growth and aggregation (Gohel & Wong, 2006). In recent years, a large number of studies have confirmed that L. christinae has inhibited the formation of urinary and gallstones, with diuretic, liver protection, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, immunomodulatory and other functions (Zhong & Ling, 2018). Hirayama et al. studied the anti-lithogenic properties of the main components of L. christinae, which is called D. styracyfolium-triterpenoide (Ds-t) (Hirayama et al., 1993). They used an ethylene glycol and 1-alpha (OH)D3 to induce oxalate stone rat model. The results confirmed that Ds-t reduces stone formation of pure Ca oxalate crystals in rat kidney. Ds-t is believed to block urinary calcium excretion by either inhibiting the endogenous synthesis of 1–25(OH)2D from 1-alpha(OH)D3 in the kidney or inhibiting the action of 1–25(OH)2D on increased intestinal absorption of calcium. They also found that Ds-t promoted calcium reabsorption in the renal. Meanwhile, Zhang et al. demonstrated that the extract of L. christinae was able to inhibit calcium oxalate stone formation and protect renal function by inhibiting the P38-MAPK pathway (Zhiyuan et al., 2022). Recently, in vivo and in vitro studies also showed the most potent antioxidant and endothelial protective effects in the treatment of urolithiasis with n-butanol fractions of the ethanolic extract of L. christinae (Wu et al., 2018). Zhou et al. investigated the anti-urolithiatic effect of total flavonoids of D. styracifolium on calcium oxalate (CaOx) renal stones in hydroxy-l-proline fed rats. They found that D. styracifolium decreased urinary oxalate excretion, alleviated the proacidosis condition, improved the impaired renal functions and renal epithelial cell injury. Moreover, their results indicated that D. styracifolium had benefit on inhibition of CaOx formation in the rat kidney probably through antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, urine alkalinizing activities, and lowering the concentration of urinary stone-forming constituents (Zhou et al., 2018). Furthermore, network pharmacology has confirmed that extracts of L. christinae can treat renal calculus by modulating multiple targets and pathways (Yu, Zhang, & Xie, 2020).

Alisma orientale

A. orientale (Sam.) Juzep. (Zexie in Chinese) is an aquatic plant that distributes nearly worldwide. Its roots are used to treat urinary system diseases, such as nephritis, edema and urination. The extracts of A. orientale could prevent calcium oxalate stone formation and osteopontin expression in a rat model (Yasui et al., 1999). It has been found that Kampo extracts, Gardeniae Fructus (fruits of Gardenia jasminoides Ellis) and A. orientale strongly inhibited oxalate monohydrate crystal aggregation in vitro, and also prevented the crystal adhesion to Madin–Darby canine kidney cells at concentration 10 μg/mL (Nishihata, Kohjimoto, & Hara, 2013). Kawamura et al. observed the inhibitory effects of A. orientale on the formation, growth and aggregation of calcium oxalate crystals in the diluted urine system and undiluted urine system. A. orientale had a strong inhibitory effect on the aggregation and growth of crystals when the concentration was above 10 μg/mL. In measuring the metastable limit by the microplate method, A. orientale had a mild inhibitory effect on the formation of crystals above the concentration of 1 mg/mL. In the undiluted urine system, after determining the metastable limit, the formation and growth of calcium oxalate crystals which precipitated in response to a load of sodium oxalate were measured. A. orientale had a strong inhibitory effect on the formation and growth at the concentration of 0.1–1 mg/mL. Therefore, A. orientale might be useful for preventing stones in recurrent stone formers (Kawamura et al., 1993). Cao et al. found that the extraction of A. orientale could regulate the expression of bikunin protein, which in turn inhibited the formation of calcium oxalate crystals in the kidney (Cao, Liu, Yin, Zhu, & Wu, 2006).

Tribulus terrestris

In China, T. terrestris L. (Jili in Chinese) is used to improve mood, blood circulation, diuresis and detumescence. Current in vitro studies demonstrated that the aqueous extract of T. terrestris inhibited the nucleation and growth of the calcium oxalate crystals. In addition, it found to have a cytoprotective role in NRK 52E cells, which was mediated by lowering LDH leakage and increasing the cell viability (Aggarwal, Tandon, Singla, & Tandon, 2010). The antiurolithiasis potential of T. terrestris has also been confirmed in an albino rat model (Anand, Patnaik, Kulshreshtha, & Dhawan, 1994). Kaushik et al. observed that the extract of T. terrestris not only attenuated kidney stones in rats, but also attenuated the ephrolithiasis-mediated inflammatory response via regulating the expression of p38MAPK (Kaushik et al., 2019). An antilithiatic protein purified from T. terrestris could play an important role in medical management of urolithiasis (Aggarwal, Tandon, Singla, & Tandon, 2012).

Lygodium japonicum

Lygodii Spora (Haijinsha in Chinese) is also a kind of CHM commonly used in treating urolithiasis for a long history. It is the dried spore of L. japonicum (Thunb.) Sw. (Schizaeaceae) and is widely distributed in eastern Asia and Australia. L. japonicum is rich in carbohydrates, phenols and flavonoids. Cho et al. proved the inhibitory effect of an ethanol extract of the spores of L. japonicum on ethylene glycol-induced kidney calculi in rats (Cho et al., 2014). The results revealed that treatment with the ethanol extract of L. japonicum 400 mg/kg significantly decreased the levels of urinary calcium, oxalate and uric acid, and increased the levels of urinary citrate as compared to those groups. L. japonicum can dilate the ureteral sinus and increase the pressure in the upper ureteral cavity, which can promote the stone movement. In both preventive and therapeutic protocols, the extract significantly decreased kidney peroxides, renal calcium, oxalate content, and the number of kidney oxalate deposits. Therefore, they concluded that L. japonicum is useful as a preventive and therapeutic agent against the formation of oxalate kidney stones.

Salvia miltiorrhiza

S. miltiorrhiza is a frequently used medicinal plant in CHM with its dried roots possessing pharmacological properties (Chen et al., 2014, Guo et al., 2014). In modern medicine, S. miltiorrhiza is used for the treatment of cardiovascular diseases (Yang et al., 2011), osteoporosis (Yuan, Wu, Shi, & Chen, 2015). S. miltiorrhiza has been proved to be one of the effective CHMs for prevention of stone disease in previous study. They tested the effect of protection to urolithiasis with 16 CHMs including S. miltiorrhiza using Drosophila melanogaster model. They found that S. miltiorrhiza reached the crystal formation rate of 0% (Wu et al., 2014). In a cohort research (Chen et al., 2018), they investigated the effect of S. miltiorrhiza in treating urolithiasis through the incidence of calculus surgical during 12 years. This study included a total of 8568 patients using S. miltiorrhiza (S. miltiorrhiza-users) and 56 502 patients not using S. miltiorrhiza (non-S. miltiorrhiza-users) suffering from calculus disease after the initial diagnosis of calculus. They showed that the incidence of calculus surgical treatment in the S. miltiorrhiza-users was less than that in the non-S. miltiorrhiza-users. S. miltiorrhiza decreased the ratio of subsequent stone treatment after the first treatment in the study population, and there was no increased bleeding risk due to long-term S. miltiorrhiza use.

Boerhavia diffusa

B. diffusa L. (Huangxixin in Chinese) is useful in the treatment of a wide range of urologic disease including urolithiasis in traditional Chinese medicinal system (Yasir & Waqar, 2011). In an in vitro study, an ethanolic extract of whole B. diffusa plant was found to have an effect on calcium oxalate crystallization process in a dose dependent manner. It completely dissolved the urinary calcium salts at a concentration of 100 mg/mL (Yasir & Waqar, 2011). The noticeable antiurolithic activity of B. diffusa roots has also been observed in animal model. It has been observed that aqueous root extract of B. diffusa mediated antiurolithic activity through inhibition of calcium oxalate crystallization, diuresis and hypo-oxaluria (Amin, Feriz, Hariri, Meybodi, & Hosseinzadeh, 2015). In the calcium oxalate crystallization assay, the incorporation of its extract increased calcium oxalate crystallization but with reduce sized calcium oxalate monohydrate and dihydrate crystals. In another study, aqueous root extract of B. diffusa showed protective effects against hyperoxaluric oxidative stress and renal cell injury in rats due to potent antioxidant activity (Pareta, Patra, Mazumder, & Sasmal, 2011).

Aquilaria sinensis

A. sinensis (Lour.) Gilg. (Baimuxiang in Chinese) is a medicine plant from Thymelaeaceae family. It refers to the highly resinous wood of agarwood. In TCM, A. sinensis is commonly used to relieve pain in abdominal region, arrest vomiting, and control respiring to relieve asthma. Pharmacological studies suggest that A. sinensis can relieve ureteral smooth muscle spasm and relieve stone incarceration (Wu et al., 2014).

Crocus sativus

C. sativus L. (Fanhonghua in Chinese), commonly known as Saffron crocus, or Autumn crocus, is a species of flowering plant of Crocus genus in the Iridaceae family. The constituents of C. sativus are pigments, flavonoids, phenolic acid etc. It is a CHM that has been prescribed since centuries for treating various symptoms related to blood circulation improvement. Amin et al. evaluated the protective effects of the aqueous extract of C. sativus against ethylene glycol-induced urolithiasis in rat. They found that C. sativus did not show diuretic effect; However, it significantly reduced the elevated urinary oxalate in prophylactic (50 and 100 mg/kg) and curative (100 mg/kg) studies. Only the high dose of prophylactic regimen restored citrate concentration of urine. Increased number of calcium oxalate crystals deposits in the kidney tissue of calculus rats was significantly reverted by the prophylactic and high dose of curative saffron treatment. The results of this study indicated that C. sativus can protect against calcium oxalate (CaOx) nephrolithiasis. The mechanisms might be related to the urinary concentration of stone-forming constituents and an antioxidant effect (Amin et al., 2015).

Carthamus tinctorius

C. tinctorius L. (Honghua in Chinese), also known as Safflower, has been used to treat muscles, bones injuries and hemostasis swelling pain for centuries in TCM. C. tinctorius has been further studied for its potential effects on urinary calculus, and a positive result was obtained when it was applied to a rat model (Lin et al., 2012). In another study, C. tinctorius was also proved to prevent urolithiasis. Especially, they found that the extract of C. tinctorius was unable to completely inhibit PE-induced ureteral peristalsis, suggesting the antilithic effect of C. tinctorius extract is related to mechanisms other than modulation of ureteral peristalsis (Wu et al., 2014).

Paeonia lactiflora

Paeoniae Radix Alba (Baishao in Chinese, PRA), the dried roots of Paeonia lactiflora Pall. without bark, has been used as a medicinal herb in TCM for centuries based on its wide range of pharmacological activities. In ancient pharmacology, PRA was used to inhibite inflammatory response, relieve pain, and suppress sweating. In morden pharmacology, PRA decoctions are used to treat rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, hepatitis, dysmenorrhea, muscle cramping and spasms, and long-standing fever. Li et al. found that an aqueous extract of PRA is also a useful agent that prevented the formation of CaOx kidney stones (Li et al., 2017). Gou et al. found that the extract of PRA reduced urinary and renal oxalic acid levels while increasing the level of urinary calcium and citric acid in the kidney. This effect may be related to the fact that the extract of PRA regulated the expression of osteopontin (Gou, You, Wang, & Li, 2020). Another experment also proved the potential antilithic effect of PRA (Wu et al., 2014).

Pyrrosia petiolosa

Pyrrosiae Folium (Shiwei in Chinese) is the dried leaves of P. petiolosa (Christ) Ching, a plant in the Polypodiaceae family, and it has been used to treat urolithiasis for thousands of years in TCM. In recent years, the pharmacological studies have revealed that the extracts of P. petiolosa have anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, anticolithic, and oxidative stress-regulating effects (Cheng et al., 2014, Mi et al., 2012). It has been confirmed that P. petiolosa can inhibit the growth and aggregation of calcium oxalate stones in vitro (Gohel & Wong, 2006). In addition, P. petiolosa can not only inhibit the formation of oxalate stones by suppressing oxidative stress and inflammation, but also alleviate renal injury induced by renal stones and thus protect the kidneys in urolithiasis rats (Cheng et al., 2014, Mi et al., 2012).

Chinese medicinal formulae for treatment

Wuling San (WLS), which is a classical TCM formula, is widely used in China for the treatment of urinary stones. This formula has been proved its importance in prevention of calcium oxalate nephrolithiasis both in vitro and in vivo studies (Lin et al., 2013, Tsai et al., 2008). WLS extract was found to inhibit the calcium oxalate nucleation and aggregation under in vitro conditions, and may have the ability to prevent stone recurrence. At a concentration of 50 mg/mL, it inhibited nucleation and aggregation of calcium oxalate crystals by 94.3% and 92.49%, respectively. In addition, it was also found that WLS formula prevented the renal deposition of calcium oxalate crystal in ethylene glycol-fed rats (Tsai et al., 2008).

Zhuling Tang (ZLT) is also a CHM formula. It has been used for centuries to treat urological diseases in Chinese medicine. ZLT is composed of five dried herbs: Polyporus (Zhuling in Chinese), Poria (Fuling in Chinese), Alismatis Rhizoma (Zexie in Chinese), Talcum (Huashi in Chinese) and Asini Corii Colla (Ejiao in Chinese). This formula was initially used in the treatment of low urine output, fever, thirst and floating pulse. It has been prescribed as a CHM to treat urinary stones as well. In Tsai’s study, they evaluated the antilithic effects of experimentally induced calcium oxalate (CaOx) nephrolithiasis in ethylene glycol-fed rats. They found that ZLT reduced the severity of CaOx crystallization and slowed down the body weight loss effects. Therefore, ZLT was proved to be an effective reagent for kidney stone prophylaxis (Tsai et al., 2009).

Notice in use of CHMs in practice

The effect of CHM on the prevention and treatment of urolithiasis have been proven in the history of the development of TCM, and have been constantly confirmed by modern pharmacology. However, because of the different nature of stones, and different physique and character of patients, some questions should be noted in practice. Attention should be paid to nephrotoxicity of CHM. For example, many herbs containing aristolochic acid (which was confirmed to have obvious nephrotoxicity) have been found, but the CHMs metioned in this article for the treatment of urolithiasis is not included. Some nephrotoxic concerns have been recently mentioned about T. terrestris (Talasaz, Abbasi, Abkhiz, & Dashti-Khavidaki, 2010), overdose of T. terrestris and hypoposia led to nephrotoxicity. In addition to aristolochic acid, excessive dosage, improper processing, change of extraction methods of herbal medicine, contamination of CHM and improper compatibility may reduce the curative effect and increase toxicity. Further investigations of the molecular modes of CHMs are needed to better understand pathophysiological mechanisms (Yang et al., 2018).

On the other hand, is it possible that CHM can lead to the occurrence or aggravation of stones? It’s not commonly known that CHM induced nephrolithiasis. There are two published case reports of kidney stones associated with Ephedrae Herba (Mahuang in Chinese) originated from Ephedra sinica Stapf, which is commonly used in treatment of respiratory conditions (Blau, 1998). Both patients in these two case reports were young adult men with a history of ingesting 40–3000 mg/d Ephedrae Herba over several months. The components of Ephedrae Herba associated with these patients with urolithiasis were ephedrine, norephedrine, and pseudoephedrine. Because of these events and other adverse events associated with Ephedrae Herba, the FDA prohibited the sale of Ephedrae Herba-containing dietary supplements in April 2004 in the United States (Gabardi, Munz & Ulbricht, 2007).

In addition, excessive and too long-term use should be avoided. To achieve efficacy, such that the active components of the decocted herbs reach the intended site, kidney, ureter and bladder, the registered herbalists will prepare 5–20 g of dried herbs for decoction. For severe cases, the dosage can be increased up to 60–80 g of dried weight. Although these dosages are empirical and proportioned through a long history of traditional experience, they are now optimized for modern prescription to reduce serious side-effects.

Finally, flexible application of TCM and modern technologies is optimized way to treat and prevent urinary calculi. For example, patients after lithotripsy surgery, can use appropriate CHM to prevent recurrence of calculus. The small kidney stones (<6 mm), postoperative residual small stones, and “Stone Street” after lithotripsy, TCM treatment becomes necessary. In summary, to maximize the effects of TCM for the treatment of renal calculus, we should choose the appropriate treatment method according to the patient's different physique and the nature of stone. And CHM should be used normatively according to the dosage and decoction method to reduce the toxic and side effects.

Conclusion

CHM was confirmed to be a useful pharmacotherapy for the treatment of urolithiasis by clinical and experimental verification. CHMs with the traditional functions of promoting urination and draining dampness, purgation, activating blood and resolving stasis can be a useful approach to prevent the stone recurrence or improve renal failure. TCM could treat urolithiasis and the secondary kidney diseases by forming a coordination complex with calcium ion, hydrophobic, overlapping and electrostatic, regulating the pH value of urine, adhereing to the growth site of the crystal, increasing the amount of urine, relaxing smooth muscle and reducing the accumulation of inflammatory lesions. However, to make it more efficient and low toxicity, there remains a considerable amount of research required in the fields of analytical chemistry, molecular biology, pharmacology and coordination chemistry. Above all, CHM would be benefit for providing new treatment strategy for urolithiasis and secondary kidney damage.

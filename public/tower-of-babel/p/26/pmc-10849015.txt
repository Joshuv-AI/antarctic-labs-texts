Immunoinflammatory Response to Acute Noise Stress in Male Rats Adapted with Different Exercise Training
Authors: Elaheh Farahbakht, Yousra Alsinani, Mohammadamin Safari, Martin Hofmeister, Rasoul Rezaie, Alireza Sharifabadi, Maryam Koushkie Jahromi
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Objective:Noise pollution is a kind of stress that impairs various physiological functions. This study evaluated the effect of high-intensity interval training (HIIT) and moderate-intensity continuous training (MCT) on corticosterone, interleukin-6 (IL-6), and monocyte responses to acute noise stress in male rats.Design:Forty-two male Wistar rats were divided into seven groups, including control which was assessed at the beginning, control time which was assessed simultaneously with experimental groups (CT), HIIT, MCT, HIIT followed by noise stress (HIIT+S), MCT followed by noise stress (MCT+S), and noise stress. HIIT and MCT were performed for 8 weeks. Noise stress was induced for one session. Blood samples were taken 48 hours after the last exercise session in training and CT groups and immediately after acute noise stress in stress groups of HIIT+S, MCT+S, and noise stress.Results:In response to acute noise stress, MCT and HIIT adaptations increased corticosterone, while reduced monocytes compared to CT. MCT increased basal corticosterone and IL-6 and decreased monocytes; however, in response to acute noise stress, corticosterone was higher and monocyte count was lower in the HIIT+S group. Regarding the effect of training, corticosterone and monocytes in MCT were higher than in HIIT. The serum level of IL-6 was lower in MCT than CT group, while it was not significantly different between stress groups.Conclusion:In response to noise stress, previous exercise, especially HIIT, increased stress while did not increase inflammatory and innate immune response.

INTRODUCTION

Noise pollution as one of the environmental pollutants can cause many physical and mental complications.[1] Therefore, noise can be considered as a biological stressor that activates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and the sympathetic nervous system (SNS). Activation of these pathways increases the levels of stress hormones and inflammatory pathways.[234] Activation of the HPA axis, cause releasing of cortisol in humans or corticosterone in rats, which are accompanied by suppressing the immune system. Glucocorticoids influence the survival and functions of monocytes[56] and inhibit the production of proinflammatory cytokines, including interleukin-6 (IL-6). Acute stress is thought to increase the immune system response, while chronic stress suppresses the immune response. However, the underlying mechanisms of stress and the immune system interactions are not yet clearly understood.[7] Van de Wouw et al. assessed the effect of acute stress on monocyte levels in healthy women and showed that the level of blood monocytes increases due to stress.[8]

Exercise is considered as one of the methods of maintaining the body’s homeostasis in stressful situations.[9] However, exercise trainings or sports are performed with different modes, duration, frequencies, and intensities. High-intensity interval (HIIT) and moderate-intensity continuous (MCT) training are the two methods of exercise training that are effective on some physiological functions. The effect of two types of continuous and interval training on serum corticosterone levels following a period of chronic stress was compared and found that serum corticosterone levels were higher in the HIIT following stress group than in the MCT following stress group.[10] In another study, acute psychological stress and exercise on the IL-6 were investigated and found that serum IL-6 levels increased after acute psychological stress and exercise.[11] The effect of two types of exercise HIIT and MCT on hematologic biomarkers of active young men was compared and observed that MCT training increased the number of immune cells (monocytes, neutrophils, lymphocytes, and leukocytes) compared with HIIT.[12] It has been shown that in sedentary adults monocyte phagocytosis improved significantly in MCT and HIIT.[13] No study has been found regarding the effect of acute noise stress, while in many cases, stress occurs suddenly and acutely, so the main aims of the present study were to evaluate the effect of adaptation to HIIT and MCT on immune (monocyte) and inflammatory (IL-6 and corticosterone) as well as the response to acute stress following adaptation to HIIT and MCT.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Animals

This study was a kind of experiment that was performed in the animal lab of Shiraz University, Shiraz, Iran. Forty seven male Wistar rats (age: 6–8 weeks, weight: 220 ± 20 g) were provided from the animal lab at Razi Vaccine & Serum Research Institute Center for Animal Experiment, Karaj, Iran. Five rats were excluded because of not being trainable in exercise groups. After adapting to the environment, 42 rats were randomly divided into seven groups (six rats in each group), including control group (C): did not receive any intervention and was evaluated at the beginning of the study as a pretest; control time group (CT): received no intervention and was assessed simultaneous with experimental groups; acute noise stress (S): exposure to one session of noise stress; high-intensity interval training (HIIT) group; moderate-intensity continuous training (MCT) group; a group of high-intensity interval training following by exposure to acute noise stress (HIIT + S); and moderate-intensity continuous training following by exposure to acute noise stress (MCT + S).

All rats were kept in special polycarbonate cages (dimensions 24 × 15 × 35 cm) with metal mesh cover in standard environmental conditions (temperature 22–25 °C, humidity 40–50%, 12:12 h light–dark cycle), free access to water and food (energy: 16.6 kJ/g; carbohydrates: 66.40%; fat: 10.60%, and protein: 23%) in a normal environment without any special stressors. The laboratory and training environment were washed and cleaned regularly every day. Three rats were placed in a cage, and the cages were washed at least once a week and then dried with fresh straw.[1415] All the ethical principles of dealing with animals and the study procedures on the animals were in accordance with the Helsinki Convention with Animals and the National Institutes of Health guide for the care and use of laboratory animals (NIH Publications No. 8023, revised 1978),[16] and ethics committee of Shiraz University of Medical Sciences approved the study proposal and procedures (IR.SUMS.REHAB.REC.1400.005).

As indicated in Figure 1, the blood samples of the control group were taken at the beginning of the study as a pre-test, the CT and training groups at the end of the study, and the stress groups immediately after exposure to stress. Training groups performed 2 weeks of familiarization (Table 1) and 8 weeks of training (Table 2).

HIIT and MCT training protocol

After 2 weeks of familiarization with the treadmill to determine the speed of running on treadmill, the maximum oxygen consumption (VO2 max) of rats was measured. To determine VO2 max, the standard Bedford et al. incremental test was used, which was standardized on Wistar rats by Leandro et al. The test consisted of 10 steps of 3 minutes. In the first stage, the treadmill running speed initiated from 0.3 km/h and in the next stages increased to 0.3 km/h, and a zero slope was used for all stages. VO2 max or the maximum speed of running at the last stage was recorded when the animal was exhausted and could not continue running.[1718]

After 2 weeks of familiarization with training, and 72 hours after the VO2 max test, the rats performed the HIIT and MCT training protocol for 8 weeks and for 5 days a week.[19] HIIT and MCT were performed during the same time of day as presented in Table 2. In every session, HIIT was performed during six periods of high intensity (85–90%) running by duration of 2 minutes, which were separated by 2 minutes of low intensity (50–60%) running. MCT was performed by running continuously. Duration of every session of MCT and HIIT was the same and was 22 minutes.

To induce noise stress, the cages of the respective groups were placed in a room that was previously covered with soundproof insulation, then the rats were exposed to one session of noise stress for 4 hours. In order to be sure about the effect of noise on our main target as corticosterone, similar to a study by Samson et al., a noise generator (15 W) induced noise by frequency of 0–20 kHz and intensity of 90–120 Db was placed at a distance of 30 cm from the rat’s cage.[20]

Blood sampling and analysis

As the procedures of experiment indicated in Figure 1, blood samples were taken from each rat once, and during three time periods, before the intervention and after familiarization with the laboratory environment (group C), 48 hours after the last training session (HIIT and MCT training groups and CT group), and immediately after stress (Stress groups: HIIT + S, MCT + S, and S). In order to anesthetize the rats, a solution of ketamine/xylazine (100–75 mg/kg ketamine + 10 mg/kg xylazine) in a ratio of 3:1 was injected into the peritoneum of each rat, and 4–6 cc blood samples were taken from the heart of each rat. All samples were collected between 08:00 a.m. and 10:00 a.m. to avoid circadian rhythm influences on blood metabolites. Approximately 3 cc of collected blood was centrifuged at 4000 rpm and at a temperature of 4 °C for 10 min (using Centrifuge Centric 322A, Domel Company, Slovenia). Serum samples were separated and stored at −80 °C for corticosterone and IL-6 assays. Analysis was made following the manufacturer’s instructions. Plasma samples were suspended in 300 μL assay buffer and analyzed through enzyme immunoassay (EIA). Corticosterone was measured using ELISA kit (ng/mL) (ZellBio GmbH, Ulm, made in Germany). IL-6 was measured by ELISA kit (pg/mL) (Karmania Pars Gene, Kerman, made in Iran). The lower detection limit of IL-6 was 3 pg/mL. 3 cc of blood samples were used for monocyte measurements. Monocytes were counted by cell count. Whole blood was collected in heparinized tubes. 100 µL RPMI was poured into tubes. To identify monocytes, samples were incubated with pacific/CD14 and blue (Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA), CD16 / PE cy7 (Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA) for 20–30 minutes in the dark at room temperature. To remove red blood cells, the samples were incubated with 2 mL of red blood cell lysis buffer (Biolegend, San Diego, CA, USA) for 10 minutes in the dark at room temperature, after which the samples were washed with PBS. In the end, the cells were washed again with PBS and suspended in 200 µL of CSB, and examined by flow cytometry.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were analyzed using SPSS version 24.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) for Windows®. Shapiro–Wilk test was used to determine the distribution of normality. Leven’s tests were performed to ensure the homogeneity of variances. One-way ANOVAs were performed to compare the study variables between groups. The least significant difference (LSD) post hoc test was used to compare paired groups.

RESULTS

Rats’ weight changes were studied among all study groups before and after the intervention and it was observed that the weight of rats increased naturally and significantly from the 1st week to the 8th week; after 8 weeks of intervention, no significant difference was observed between the study groups.

Comparison of groups using ANOVA test showed that serum corticosterone levels ((F (6,28) = 21.584, p < 0.001), serum levels of IL-6 (F (6,32) = 2.459, p = 0.045), and the number of monocytes (F (6,67) = 14.437, p < 0.001) were significantly different between the study groups. Post-hoc LSD test was used to compare paired targets: firstly, group C was compared with group CT to determine the effect of time on the studied variables; secondly, to investigate the effect of two types of long-term exercise on the desired variables, and HIIT, MCT, and CT groups were compared in pairs; thirdly, to examine the effect of stress on variables, the stressed groups including HIIT + S, MCT + S, and group S were compared in pairs and in addition, each of the exercise + stress groups was compared with their similar exercise group (i.e., HIIT compared with HIIT + S and MCT were compared with MCT + S).

Corticosterone

Paired comparisons indicated that the serum corticosterone levels between group C and CT were not significantly different (P = 0.565). Regarding the effect of the two types of exercise, the corticosterone levels in the HIIT group were significantly lower than the MCT group (P < 0.001), and were not significantly different from the CT group (P = 0.324). Corticosterone levels in the MCT group were significantly higher than in the CT group (P = 0.001). To evaluate the effect of acute noise stress, the comparison of stress groups showed that the corticosterone levels increased in the S group compared to the CT group (P = 0.04). Corticosterone in HIIT + S group was significantly higher than in MCT + S group (P < 0.001) and MCT + S group was higher than in S group. Serum corticosterone levels in HIIT + S group were significantly higher than in HIIT and S groups (P < 0.001) and there was no significant difference between MCT + S group and the MCT group (P = 0.664), while it was significantly higher than the S group (P < 0.001) [Figure 2].

In summary, MCT training increased serum corticosterone levels while HIIT did not make a significant difference in corticosterone levels. However, acute noise stress increased serum corticosterone levels in both exercise groups (HIIT + S and MCT + S) compared to S, whereas HIIT + S showed higher serum corticosterone levels than MCT + S.

IL-6

According to paired comparisons, the serum IL-6 levels of the C and CT groups were not statistically significant (P = 0.062). Regarding the effect of the two types of exercise training, the comparison of the paired groups showed that the serum IL-6 levels were not significantly different in the HIIT group compared to the MCT (P = 0.092) and CT (P = 0.113) groups. However, serum IL-6 levels in the MCT group were lower compared to CT (P = 0.002). To evaluate the effect of acute noise stress, the comparison of stress groups showed that although the serum IL-6 levels in the S group were higher than in HIIT + S and MCT + S group, and in the HIIT + S and MCT + S groups were higher than their similar training groups (HIIT and MCT), these differences were not statistically significant (P > 0.05) [Figure 3].

In summary, MCT training was effective in reducing serum IL-6 levels and previous exercise training did not make any statistically significant effect on IL-6 response to acute noise stress.

Monocytes­

According to the comparison of paired groups, there was no significant difference in the number of monocytes between the C and CT groups (P = 0.754). Regarding the effect of two types of exercise, the comparison of paired groups showed that the number of monocytes in the HIIT group was significantly less than the MCT group (P = 0.003) and more than the CT group (P = 0.003). The number of monocytes in the MCT group was significantly higher than in the CT group (P < 0.001). To evaluate the effect of acute noise stress, a comparison of stress groups showed that the number of monocytes in group S was significantly higher than MCT + S group, and in MCT + S group was higher than HIIT + S group (P < 0.001). The number of monocytes in the HIIT + S group was significantly lower compared to the HIIT and S groups (P < 0.001) and the number of monocytes in the MCT + S group was lower compared to the MCT group (P < 0.001), while was no significantly different with S group (P = 0.179). In summary, both types of training, especially MCT training increased the number of monocytes. However, under acute noise stress, the number of monocytes in both training groups (HIIT + S and MCT + S) was lower than in S, and in HIIT + S was lower than in MCT + S.

DISCUSSION

Corticosterone

The findings of the present study showed that, firstly, the chronic time for about 8 weeks did not change the serum levels of corticosterone in the CT group; secondly, MCT increased serum corticosterone levels compared to other groups; thirdly, in response to acute noise stress in both exercise groups (MCT + S and HIIT + S), serum corticosterone levels increased compared to the stress group. This increase was greater in the HIIT + S group.

Generally, it seems that in the case of noise stress, considering that the serum corticosterone level is one of the indicators of stress, previous training, especially HIIT, causes more stress in conditions of acute noise stress. Previous studies have often examined the effect of chronic stress simultaneously or before exercise, and no study has been found to examine the effect of adaptation to different exercises in response to short-term noise stress conditions. For example, it has been found that serum corticosterone levels in the HIIT + S group were higher than in the MCT + S group and both groups had a significant increase compared to the control group.[10] Another study observed that the amount of this hormone in the group of continuous swimming training with chronic stress significantly increased compared to continuous training without stress.[15] Also, it was observed that serum corticosterone levels in the chronic stress group increased compared to the exercise group.[21] Stress in most of previous studies was induced chronically. Also, other reasons for these contradictions may be the duration and type of stress and exercise training research design.

Regarding the effect of mode of exercise training, some studies observed that continuous and interval training did not induce a significant effect on serum corticosterone levels.[1022] The increasing effect of acute HIIT with 100% of intensity has been shown on serum cortisol levels.[23]

Stress responses include the activation of neural and hormonal mechanisms including the sympathetic nervous system and HPA axis. The main mechanism of the increase in serum corticosterone levels due to stress is related to HPA pathway activity. Stress causes the secretion of corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) and arginine vasopressin (AVP) from the hypothalamus, which subsequently stimulates the secretion of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) from the pituitary gland. ACTH is transported through the blood to the cortex of the adrenal gland, and stimulates the synthesis and secretion of glucocorticoids.[56] However, contrary to our expectations, the results of the present study show that exercise training without stress, especially MCT, not only could not reduce serum corticosterone levels but also increased them. Forced training may have caused chronic stress in rats. Even previous training with these two types of exercise, especially HIIT, had an increasing effect on serum corticosterone levels in response to acute noise stress. Forced or voluntary nature of training may be important in inducing the effect of exercise training on corticosterone. Forced exercise can increase stress and anxiety compared to voluntary exercise and requires long-term adaptation.[24] Therefore, it is possible that these two types of exercises are a kind of stress for rats, and in addition to creating stress at rest, they are increasing stress in acute noise stress situations. As another previous study has shown, a history of chronic stress exposure can increase anxiety in acute stress conditions.[25] In addition, the effect of exercise on corticosterone is dependent on the intensity of exercise and intense anaerobic exercise even suppresses the cortisol response, which may be due to the inhibitory effect of high hydrogen concentrations on adrenal function.[26] Therefore, the high intensity of exercise training in the present study may be another factor in increasing or not decreasing serum corticosterone levels. However, comparative studies are needed on both the possibility of force exercise and its intensity on stress response for more clarifications.

IL-6

The findings of the present study showed that, firstly, passage of 8 weeks without any intervention did not change the serum levels of IL-6 in the CT group. Secondly, serum IL-6 levels in the MCT were significantly lower than in the CT group, and thirdly, in response to acute noise stress in both exercise groups (MCT + S and HIIT + S), serum IL-6 levels increased but this increase was lower in both stress groups with previous exercise training compared to the stress group without exercise training, although these differences were not statistically significant. It seems that in the case of noise stress, according to the serum IL-6 levels as one of the indicators of inflammation, previous exercise, especially MCT, reduces inflammation. Previous studies have often examined the effect of chronic stress and exercise separately, and no study has been found to examine the effect of adaptation to HIIT and MCT training on response to short-term noise stress. It has been observed that chronic noise stress increases serum IL-6 levels in male rats but did not observe a significant change in female rats.[27] Also, acute noise stress and running on bicycles increased serum IL-6 levels.[28] Moderate noise stress increased proinflammatory cytokines, including IL-6, in male rats.[29] Acute noise stress could increase inflammatory salivary cytokines, including reactive proteins C, IL-1β, IL-6, and interferons.[30]

There are contradictory findings regarding the effect of two types of exercise on inflammatory factors. Zar et al. observed that HIIT and MCT did not change IL-6 serum levels.[31] Moghadam et al. observed that HIIT and MCT reduce serum IL-6 levels.[32] Leite et al. observed that HIIT and MCT reduce serum IL-6 levels, but HIIT is more effective in reducing inflammatory factors.[33]

There is an interaction between the HPA axis and the immune system, and IL-6 is the end product of this signaling cascade and is secreted by specific immune cells during inflammation.[3435] Stress triggers the secretion of glucocorticoids from the adrenal gland by activating the HPA axis. They also activate a signaling pathway with negative feedback from mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), which inhibits the activity of stress-induced glucocorticoid receptors, thereby activating nuclear factor kappa B (NF-kB). Activation of NF-kB during an immune response via toll-like receptor triggers an inflammatory response involving the secretion of proinflammatory cytokines IL-2, TNF-α, and IL-6. Stressors also reduce vagal motor inhibitory inputs, which are cholinergic terminals (acetylcholine) that bind to 7-alpha nicotinic receptor subunits (a7nAChR). Inactivity of the vagus nerve in response to stress leads to exacerbation of inflammation.[36] On the other hand, the results of some studies have shown that the two main factors that modulate the HPA-based response to exercise are the intensity and duration of exercise.[37] Exercise can improve inflammation through various pathways, such as mobilizing immune system components between lymphatic tissue and blood. Although these effects are transient, when induce chronically, reduce systemic inflammation (neutrophils, monocytes, cytokines, IL-6, immunoglobulins, etc.) and stress hormones (cortisol, corticosterone, catecholamines).[38] A study indicated that HIIT significantly increases serum IL-6 levels, which is higher than MCT over the same period.[39] In another study that measured the response of IL-6 receptors after high-intensity and moderate-intensity cycling, it was found that the IL-6 response after HIIT was higher than moderate-intensity exercise.[40] High-intensity exercise leads to a faster decrease in muscle glycogen during exercise, which increases the need for hepatic glucose release into the circulatory system, and because of that IL-6 increases hepatic glucose output in humans. MCT reduces the inflammatory pathways and the level of serum IL-6 probably by modulating the activity of the HPA pathway and the negative feedback system reduces the serum levels of corticosterone. However, HIIT has probably caused stress and thus reduced the positive effects of interval exercise. Hence, it can be expected that high levels of glucocorticoids lead to exacerbation of inflammation.[414243] However, the results of the present study showed that although serum corticosterone levels were higher in trained and stressed + previous training rats, while the rate of inflammation did not increase in these groups. So, a compensatory or adaptive mechanism to exercise seems responsible in controlling inflammation which needs to be clarified.

Monocytes

The findings of the present study showed that, firstly, 8 weeks of passage of time without any intervention did not change the number of monocytes in the CT group. Secondly, the number of monocytes in both types of training, especially MCT was higher than the control group, and thirdly, in response to acute noise stress, the number of monocytes in both training groups (MCT + S and HIIT + S), especially HIIT+S, was lower than S. The increase in monocyte count due to exercise was similar to the serum corticosterone levels. This means that in the MCT group, both serum corticosterone levels and the number of monocytes increased; while serum IL-6 levels decreased. However, in response to stress, a different response was observed with serum corticosterone and IL-6 levels. This means that despite the increase in serum corticosterone levels in acute stress groups with a history of exercise, the number of monocytes in these groups was lower than in the group without a history of exercise. However, serum IL-6 levels were not significantly different between groups following acute stress groups. In other words, although stress has increased the number of monocytes, previous exercise training has reduced this response to stress, which is not proportional to changes in serum corticosterone levels.

Previous studies have often examined the effects of stress and exercise separately, and no study has been found to examine the effect of exercise adaptation on response to short-term noise stress. For example, Beis et al. observed that acute stress caused a significant increase in monocyte counts.[44] Van de wouw et al. observed that acute stress increased blood monocyte levels in healthy women.[8] Maydych et al. observed that acute academic stress decreased monocyte counts and chronic stress increased monocyte counts.[45] McKim et al. observed that chronic stress caused the production of inflammatory monocytes, and these monocytes increased the inflammatory signals.[46] The difference between the results of the present study and some previous research may be due to the differences in the study subjects and whether the subjects are human or animal or laboratory techniques.

There are contradictory results regarding the effect of two types of exercise on immune system indices. Khamasi et al. observed that MCT training increases the number of immune cells (monocytes, neutrophils, lymphocytes, leukocytes) compared to HIIT. Nine weeks of MCT were more effective in improving immune function in active young men than HIIT.[12] Another study by Bartlett et al. comparing the effect of 10 weeks of MCT and HIIT on the immune function of sedentary adults showed that monocyte phagocytosis improved significantly in both training groups, while monocyte expression in both groups was reduced and no difference in systemic inflammation was observed between the two interventions. HIIT was an effective exercise option for improving neutrophil and monocyte function compared to MCT.[13] Intensity, duration, and volume of exercise seem to determine the effect of exercise on monocytes.

It seems that in the conditions of noise stress, considering that the number of monocytes is one of the indicators of the innate immune system, previous exercise, especially HIIT, reduces the innate immune system response to stress, especially despite increased serum corticosterone levels. The previous exercise appears to reduce the response to serum corticosterone levels. Stress changes the activity of hematopoietic stem cells and increases the proliferation of these factors, thereby increasing inflammatory leukocytes. Stress causes monocytosis and neutrophilia in humans and rats. Eventually, increased proliferation of hematopoietic stem cells leads to increased output of neutrophils and inflammatory monocytes from the bone marrow,[47] which are converted to macrophages to regulate cellular homeostasis, especially in conditions of infection and inflammation, and can regulate immunity.[48] Although the effects of stress on lymphocytes as immune cells are well known, the mechanisms related to monocytes as innate immune cells are still unclear. The HPA and sympathetic nervous are the two main branches of the neuroendocrine system that control organisms’ responses to stress.[49] Stress causes the secretion of glucocorticoids (cortisol and corticosterone) from the adrenal gland. Thus, glucocorticoids inhibit proinflammatory cytokines including IL-6 while the production of anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-4,10,13) in immune cells (such as monocytes and neutrophils) regulates and strengthens. As stress increases, high levels of glucocorticoids exert suppressive effects on the immune system.[36] Thus, it can justify the decrease in serum levels of IL-6 and the number of monocytes along with the increase in serum levels of corticosterone in the present study. However, some studies have shown that factors such as stress and inflammation increase the level of monocytes in the peripheral circulation, which leads to monocytosis and leads to suppression and autoimmune disorders.[5051] Recent studies have shown that under stress monocytes move to the brain to reduce stress,[52] and in the present study this factor can transport monocytes from serum to the brain that causes reduction of blood monocytes. Another possible factor is that, as the present study showed, monocyte levels increased in both exercise groups, and this chronic increase decreased response to acute stress. A similar study showed that long-term stress disrupts physiological responses to stress.[53]

Regarding the effect of exercise, this study showed that previous exercise training can reduce the level of monocytes in response to stress. Based on the mechanism mentioned in the previous sections, MCT has increased the production of serum corticosterone levels by stimulating HPA pathway activity and with increasing serum levels of corticosterone as an anti-inflammatory agent, the function of inflammatory pathways as well as the response of monocytes to stress has been likely decreased. Monocytes are not only part of inflammation and inflammatory diseases, but can also have anti-inflammatory properties. Monocytes become anti-inflammatory monocytes through several mechanisms. Glucocorticoids reprogram monocytes into anti-inflammatory cells. In the innate immune system, glucocorticoids regulate T cells, through unknown mechanism, which leads to improved inflammation. Also, monocytes are activated with granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) as a group of cytokines that are responsible for transmitting messages between cells and help to produce more white blood cells. Similarly, GM-CSF converts monocytes into anti-inflammatory cells.[54] In the present study, serum levels of IL-6 in exercise groups showed a slight increase in response to stress and shows that according to these two indicators, exercise has a role in reducing inflammation, although serum corticosterone levels have increased.

CONCLUSION

According to the results of the present study, it can be concluded that exercise, especially MCT, can increase serum corticosterone levels, decrease IL-6 serum levels, and increase the number of monocytes in rats, shows that despite stimulation of the corticosterone pathway, inflammatory factors are reduced and monocytes as an innate immune indicator increased. However, comparison of trained and untrained groups showed that in response to stress, serum corticosterone levels increased in both training groups, and in the HIIT + S group it was higher than MCT + S. Also, the monocyte levels in both exercise groups decreased and this decrease was greater in the HIIT + S group. In other words, in the case of noise stress, the previous exercise especially forced HIIT, can be associated with an increase in stress indicators and a decrease in the innate immune index. The limitation of this study was not measuring auditory changes. The hearing of the rats was not measured due to two reasons, the time limit and the lack of suitable facilities for its quick measurement. While blood sampling and killing of the rats were necessary immediately after the period of noise stress, and measuring the hearing of the mouse could reduce the accuracy of the corticosterone measurement due to its possible fluctuations. So, its measuring is recommended for future studies.

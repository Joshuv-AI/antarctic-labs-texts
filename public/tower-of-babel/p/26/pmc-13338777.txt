Population Pharmacokinetic Modeling and Simulation to Support the Regulatory Submission of the Two‐Injection Start Regimen with Aripiprazole Once‐Monthly Long‐Acting Injectable Intramuscular Administration in Japanese Patients with Schizophrenia or Bipolar I Disorder
Authors: Yumiko Yamasaki, Tomohiro Sasaki, Antal Martinecz, Mayumi Hasegawa, Kazuo Kuwahara, Ayaka Soga, Kai Yamada, Satoshi Katayama, Kenji Maeda, Takako Ichimura, Yosuke Kawai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo improve medication adherence in Japanese patients with schizophrenia or bipolar I disorder, this population pharmacokinetic (popPK) modeling and simulation was conducted to support the regulatory submission of the two‐injection start (TIS; two aripiprazole once‐monthly long‐acting injectable [LAI; 400 mg each] administered at different sites, along with a single oral dose of aripiprazole [6–15 mg]) regimen by comparing it with the original regimen (single LAI with 2‐week concomitant oral dose of aripiprazole [6–15 mg]). Pharmacokinetic data from four Japanese clinical trials in patients with schizophrenia were used to externally validate and update a previously validated popPK model developed from seven non‐Japanese trials in healthy subjects or in patients with schizophrenia/schizoaffective disorders. Simulations were conducted assuming two types of patients: with and without prior stabilization on oral aripiprazole. The dataset of 1123 participants (13,985 observations) from clinical trials, including 306 Japanese patients (5699 observations) from Japanese clinical trials, was used as the input in the popPK model. Simulations showed that the plasma concentration–time profiles of the original regimen and the TIS regimen were similar in the Japanese population. In addition, the currently available postmarketing safety data did not show any new safety concerns and/or an incremental safety risk with the TIS regimen compared with the original regimen. The aripiprazole TIS regimen provides a new option by reducing the oral aripiprazole administration period to a single day. This is expected to reduce the medication burden on patients and, consequently, reduce the burden on healthcare professionals and caregivers.

Introduction

Aripiprazole long‐acting injectable (LAI) is an atypical antipsychotic approved for the treatment of schizophrenia and bipolar I (BP‐I) disorder in Japan. 1 The globally approved regimen (original regimen) most commonly used for treatment initiation is a single aripiprazole LAI (400 mg, gluteal or deltoid) administered with oral aripiprazole for 14 days. Thereafter, aripiprazole LAI (400 mg) is administered once every 4 weeks as a maintenance therapy. 1 , 2 Aripiprazole LAI maintains effective plasma concentrations by slowly releasing aripiprazole. 1 , 2

Schizophrenia and BP‐I disorder are known to be associated with poor treatment adherence, leading to an increased risk of relapse and recurrence. 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 Therefore, maintaining and improving adherence is considered essential. In both schizophrenia and BP‐I disorder, there are patients who find it difficult to adhere to the 2‐week oral aripiprazole regimen following the initial administration of the approved regimen. The 2‐week concomitant oral aripiprazole medication can impose a burden on patients themselves, even among those who maintain proper adherence, and, in some cases, on healthcare professionals or caregivers responsible for confirming adherence. 8 Therefore, a simpler initiation method is considered necessary.

The two‐injection start (TIS) regimen consisting of the simultaneous administration of two intramuscular injections of aripiprazole LAI 400 mg (total 800 mg) at different injection sites along with a single dose of oral aripiprazole was established as an option suitable even for patients with low medication adherence. Reducing the dosage period for oral aripiprazole to a single day is expected to reduce the burden on patients and, ultimately, on medical professionals and caregivers who are responsible for managing patients’ medication. 9 In the European Union, Canada, and the United States, the TIS regimen was approved based on the population pharmacokinetic (popPK) analysis using non‐Japanese data and the analysis of existing postmarketing safety data. 8

Recent survey findings among European healthcare professionals indicated that the TIS regimen was frequently selected as it is associated with improved treatment adherence, reduced relapses, and improved quality of life. 10 While the TIS regimen is approved in the European Union, 11 Canada, 12 and the United States, 2 only the aripiprazole original regimen is currently approved in Japan (2015). 1 Based on the development strategy in the European region, using popPK analysis and the analysis of the existing safety data, we planned for a similar supplemental new drug application (sNDA) submission in Japan, in alignment with the modeling and simulation strategy‐based approach agreed upon with the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA). Considering the approved dosage of oral aripiprazole in Japan, the single oral dosage of aripiprazole evaluated in Japan for simulation was 6, 12, and 15 mg.

Given the extensive availability of pharmacokinetic (PK) data for oral aripiprazole and its LAI formulation from Japanese and non‐Japanese/United States clinical trials, this analysis aimed to provide PK evidence along with the existing safety data to obtain the TIS regimen approval in Japan through a simulation strategy without conducting clinical trials.

Consequently, we used the previous popPK model 8 developed using pooled PK data of participants with schizophrenia (n = 817) from Phase I and III clinical trials conducted in the United States.

Given the similar distribution of the aripiprazole exposures between the Japanese and non‐Japanese populations, we also present the postmarketing safety data of the aripiprazole original regimen versus TIS regimen collected in a non‐Japanese population.

The objectives of this popPK analysis were to (1) externally validate the previous popPK model based on plasma concentration–time profiles of oral aripiprazole and its LAI formulation in the Japanese population; (2) update the popPK model, if required, based on the results of the external validation; and (3) generate simulated plasma concentration–time profiles representative of the Japanese population for comparing the original regimen with the TIS regimen.

To our knowledge, this is the first report demonstrating that the TIS regimen results in a PK profile comparable with the original regimen in the Japanese population.

Methods

The popPK Model Applied

The previous popPK model described aripiprazole PK as a linear three‐compartment model with (1) first‐order elimination from the central compartment, (2) first‐order oral absorption, and (3) first‐order absorption from intramuscular injection, with separate absorption rate constants for deltoid and gluteal intramuscular injections (DKa and IMKa; Figure 1). 8 Significant covariate effects included the cytochrome P450 2D6 (CYP2D6) genotype on clearance (CL), body mass index (BMI), and sex on absorption rates for gluteal intramuscular administration.

Dataset Construction for External Validation and Model Update

For this popPK analysis, the PK data of four Japanese clinical trials in patients with schizophrenia were used for external validation of a previously validated popPK model that was developed using seven non‐Japanese clinical trials in healthy subjects or patients with schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorders (Table S1). 8 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 The complete PK data from these 11 clinical trials (4 Japanese and 7 non‐Japanese) comprising aripiprazole oral doses and LAI formulation doses (deltoid and gluteal) were used to update the previous model. Non‐Japanese data were excluded from one Japanese clinical trial (Study 031‐08‐003), which was performed as an Asian multinational study, and all the data used to develop the previous model were considered non‐Japanese. Only participants who received at least one dose of aripiprazole and had at least one measurable plasma concentration were included in the PK population; genotyping of CYP2D6 in Japanese patients was only performed in Study 031‐08‐003. Details of the clinical trials, including the study design and oral/intramuscular depot (IMD) doses of aripiprazole, are presented in Table S1. Missing or erroneous data were excluded or imputed based on the absence or presence of logical interpretation, respectively. In the previous model, 8 baseline aripiprazole concentrations were excluded from the analysis. However, this rule was not applied to the Japanese population to preserve the integrity of the original datasets.

Dataset Construction for Simulations

The simulated PK population reflects the target Japanese population. The previous popPK model 8 covariates included the effect of (1) CYP2D6 genotype on aripiprazole CL and (2) BMI and sex on absorption rates for gluteal intramuscular administration. In all current simulation scenarios, the genotype status was fixed at that of the CYP2D6 extensive metabolizer. Only BMI distribution (stratified by sex) was generated based on the distribution parameters (mean or standard deviation) of the BMI (stratified by sex) from relevant Japanese clinical trials. To calculate the 90% prediction intervals (PIs), 500 new BMI observations were generated, this was stratified by sex and had the same ratio of male to female as in the dataset. The resulting BMI distribution was compared with the original distribution for consistency, and the subsequent final BMI distribution was used for all simulations. To ensure consistency between scenarios and treatments, individual ETAs (inter‐individual variability [IIV]) were generated for each participant included in the simulation in the dataset construction step, and the same values were used for all simulation scenarios and treatments.

External Validation

Predicted concentrations of aripiprazole were obtained from four Japanese studies (031‐07‐002, 031‐10‐002, 031‐08‐003, and 031‐13‐005) based on the previous popPK model using posteriori‐Bayesian estimation ($ESTIMATION MAXEVAL = 0 in NONMEM).

Standard diagnostic plots used throughout the model development included (1) observed data (DV) versus individual (IPRED) and population (PRED) predictions with identity and locally estimated scatterplot smoothing (LOESS) lines on log scales, (2) conditional weighted residuals (CWRES) versus PRED with zero and LOESS lines, and (3) CWRES versus time since the first dose and time after the previous dose (TAD). Here, TAD values were based on any dosing route administered (either oral or IMD).

Prediction‐corrected visual predictive checks (pcVPC) stratified by (1) study and route of administration and (2) CYP2D6 genotype were performed to allow visual comparison between distributions of simulated aripiprazole concentrations. 18 Based on the estimates, the concentration–time profiles of aripiprazole were simulated using 500 replicates. Observed data and simulated data were separated into disjoint bins, and within each bin, non‐parametric 90% percentile intervals of the 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles of predicted concentrations were computed and compared with the corresponding percentiles of the observed concentrations. Various binning methods were tested, including k‐means, n‐tile, and binless, and the final version of the pcVPCs used n‐tile‐based binning methods, as they provided the most consistent results. In pcVPCs, both observations and model predictions were normalized by the PRED in each bin of the independent variable (i.e., time since the first dose). VPCs were evaluated by visual inspection.

Model Update

Based on the bias observed in external validation, the previous popPK model was to be minimally updated by testing (1) the CYP2D6 genotype effect on CL (with a categorization other than poor metabolizers and non‐poor metabolizers), (2) the effect of Japanese ethnicity as an additional covariate, and (3) between‐subject variability while keeping the original model structure intact as much as possible. These modifications to the model were guided by exploratory graphical evaluations of the data and diagnostic plots. The structural and stochastic (both inter‐ and intra‐subject unexplained variability) models were characterized. Exploratory graphical covariate screening was performed to guide subsequent covariate analysis. The Japanese population flag, sex, and CYP2D6 genotype flag were to be tested as covariates for CL, intercompartment clearances (Q1 and Q2), volume of distribution (central—Vc, peripheral 1—Vp1, peripheral 2—Vp2), absorption rates (oral—Ka, gluteal—IMKa, deltoid—DKa), rate of absorption into the oral compartment (R1), and relative oral bioavailability (Frelative). To allow minimal changes to the model, when the addition of the covariate effect to one parameter adequately improved the model fit, the model update was considered complete.

Simulation Scenarios for Aripiprazole Initiation Therapy

After validating the updated popPK model, simulations were performed to check the similarity of the plasma concentration–time profiles between the original regimen and the TIS regimen in the Japanese population.

Rich concentration–time profiles were generated using previously evaluated PK sampling times: 2 hourly for 24 h after the initial oral dose and every 24 h after the initial LAI dose.

PK parameters, such as maximum concentration (Cmax), area under the curve for the dosing period (AUCtau), time to maximum concentration (Tmax), trough concentration (Ctrough), and average concentration (Cavg) were derived for each dosing period using non‐compartmental analysis. Time to threshold concentrations (lower and upper limits), proportion of participants below the lower limit of thresholds (at Ctrough), and the proportion of participants above the upper limit of thresholds (at Cmax) were calculated and summarized.

The following therapeutic ranges were overlaid in the simulated outputs in all scenarios: Threshold 1: median concentration at 24 h (C24h) at steady state following a 12 mg oral dose (85.960 ng/mL); Threshold 2: 75th percentile of Cmax at steady state following a 24 mg oral dose in participants who were CYP2D6 extensive metabolizers (505 ng/mL); and Threshold 3: 95th percentiles of Cmax at steady state following a 24 mg oral dose in participants who were CYP2D6 extensive metabolizers (785 ng/mL). In Japan, the approved maintenance dose of oral aripiprazole is 6 to 24 mg for schizophrenia and 12 to 24 mg for BP‐I disorder. 1 Therefore, we considered that sufficient efficacy would be achieved and safety would be acceptable for the treatment of both schizophrenia and BP‐I disorder if plasma aripiprazole concentrations following the administration of LAI are maintained within the exposure range corresponding to the approved maintenance doses of oral aripiprazole (12 to 24 mg).

Simulation scenarios comprised of Scenario 1: without prior stabilization on oral aripiprazole (comparison of PK between the original regimen and the TIS regimen up to the steady state [20 weeks] in patients switched from other oral antipsychotics to aripiprazole LAI), and Scenario 2: with prior stabilization on oral aripiprazole (comparison of PK between the original regimen and the TIS regimen for up to 4 weeks in patients switched from oral aripiprazole to aripiprazole LAI). Within each scenario, the regimens were compared based on the injection site for the first LAI injection‐site dose.

Software and Computation

popPK analysis was based on the recommendations provided in key guidance documents from the Japan PMDA, United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and European Medicines Agency (EMA). 19 , 20 , 21 The popPK analysis was performed using a non‐linear mixed effect model (NLME) in NONMEM (Version 7.5.1). Perl Speaks NONMEM (PsN, Version 4.8.1) was also used to facilitate NONMEM tasks such as covariate testing and VPC. Dataset preparation, data exploration, figures and table generation, and posterior Bayes individual exposure levels were performed using SAS (Version 9.4) and R® (Versions 4.2.3 and 4.4.3), respectively, with a comprehensive R archive network (CRAN) and Certara package.

Postmarketing Safety Data

Postmarketing safety data, excluding Japan, were collected from the approval date of the TIS regimen in the European Union (October 27, 2020) until July 16, 2025, and reported.

Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) that were assessed as related to aripiprazole LAI were classified and summarized based on the following rules: (1) injection dose (800 or 600 mg [two injections] or 400 or 300 mg [one injection]), (2) indication (schizophrenia or BP‐I disorder), (3) time period of onset after administration (within 28 days after administration, more than 28 days, or unknown at the time of onset), and (4) seriousness (serious or non‐serious). For the safety assessment of two injections, the main focus was ADRs occurring within 28 days post‐administration. ADRs with unknown onset time were also reviewed, considering the possibility that they might include ADRs occurring within 28 days. It should be noted that the collected safety information lacked sufficient confirmation on whether the ADRs occurred at the first LAI administration and lacked information on the dosage and duration of concomitant oral medications. Therefore, it was not possible to distinguish whether ADRs observed during LAI administration occurred at the first administration or during subsequent administrations.

Results

Data Availability and Patient Characteristics

The dataset of 1123 participants (13,985 observations) from clinical trials, including 306 Japanese patients (5699 observations) from Japanese clinical trials, was used as the input in the popPK model. A total of 26 observations from Japanese patient data and 74 observations from non‐Japanese participant data were excluded as these observations were below the limit of quantitation (BLQ) before or after the first dose, or were measurable before the first dose with no record of prior doses.

External Validation of the Previous popPK Model of Aripiprazole

External validation of the previous model was performed using data from Japanese studies. The goodness‐of‐fit (GOF) plots of the previous popPK model were generally acceptable and did not show any major trends in model performance (Figure S1). Although six observations with absolute CWRES greater than five were identified, they were not considered significant outliers.

The predictive performance of the previous model based on pcVPCs of the Japanese dataset stratified by study and route of administration showed minor underprediction of the data, especially in the trough concentrations following IMD and oral dosing in Study 031‐08‐003 (Figure S2A).

When the same pcVPCs were stratified by CYP2D6 genotype (extensive/ultra‐rapid metabolizers, intermediate metabolizers, missing/unknown metabolizer status), underprediction of aripiprazole concentrations was observed in the CYP2D6 intermediate metabolizer and missing/unknown metabolizer groups (Figure S2B). Notably, in the previous model, the effect of the CYP2D6 genotype on CL was incorporated, and the CLs of two groups of poor metabolizers and non‐poor metabolizers were estimated. Compared with the non‐Japanese populations, the 031‐08‐003 study had a higher proportion of CYP2D6 intermediate metabolizers, unknown, and missing data. As most of the unknown metabolizer data in the 031‐08‐003 study were missing (i.e., CYP2D6 genotyping was not performed), “missing and unknown” were considered as a single covariate.

The summary statistics of the ETA distributions of the Japanese population showed some trends in all IIVs, except for Ka, which is the first‐order oral absorption rate of aripiprazole. Descriptive statistics of the ETA distributions are presented in Table S2. Boxplots of ETA values stratified by covariates showed a minor bias in (1) CL and DKa when stratified by sex and (2) CL and IMKa when stratified by CYP2D6 genotype (Figure S3). No trend in the effect of BMI on ETA of CL was observed (Figure S4).

Model Update

The results of the external validation indicated that a model update was necessary using the dataset (Japanese and non‐Japanese) to assess the similarity of the plasma concentration–time profiles between the original regimen and the TIS regimen in the Japanese population. As the proportion of patients classified as CYP2D6 intermediate metabolizers and missing/unknown metabolizer status was relatively high in the Japanese population, the model update explored extending CYP2D6 stratification as a covariate for CL by separately estimating CL for extensive/ultra‐rapid metabolizers, intermediate metabolizers, poor metabolizers, and missing/unknown metabolizer status, including estimating the IIV of CL. Finally, the residual variability, which was estimated separately for the Phase I and Phase III studies in the previous model, was integrated into one variable and re‐estimated for model stability in the updated model. The GOF plots of the updated model were similar and did not show any major trends in model performance compared with the previous model (Figure S5). In addition to the previous six observations with an absolute CWRES greater than 5, an additional single observation with a CWRES of 5.0061 (previously below and close to the threshold) was identified.

pcVPCs of the Japanese dataset stratified by study and route of administration (Figure S6A) and those stratified by CYP2D6 genotype (Figure S6B) showed an acceptable model performance when compared with the observed data. In 5th percentiles, there was some tendency for underprediction; however, the degree of discrepancy was minor and was not clinically significant (Figure S6A,B).

The summary statistics of the ETA distributions of the Japanese population showed no to minimal trend on CL was shown with IIV stratified by CYP2D6 genotypes and sex; however, slight biases remained in the absorption rate constants from each administration site (IMKa and DKa) in the Japanese population (Figure S7A,B). Descriptive statistics of the ETA distributions are presented in Table S3.

The impact of Japanese ethnicity on IMKa and DKa was assessed by simulations using IMKa and DKa values optimized for Japanese and non‐Japanese populations. It was predicted that Cmax and AUC would be 1.2 to 1.4 times lower, and Ctrough 1.1 to 1.2 times lower in the Japanese population. In the updated model, the trend of the effect of BMI on ETA CL was not observed (Figure S8). Overall, as the model performance based on pcVPCs was acceptable and the bias observed in CL had improved, no further updates to the model were considered.

Simulations

Simulations were conducted assuming two types of patients: (1) patients without prior stabilization on oral aripiprazole, which included those who were stabilized with other oral antipsychotics and switched to aripiprazole LAI or acute patients who were newly initiated on IMD injection; and (2) with prior stabilization on oral aripiprazole, which included patients who were stabilized with oral aripiprazole and switched to aripiprazole IMD injection. The aripiprazole IMD injection was part of the TIS regimen or the original regimen.

Simulations of aripiprazole TIS regimen were performed, assuming various scenarios and regimens, using the updated popPK model. In all scenarios, no significant differences were observed in the median plasma concentration–time profiles between the original regimen and the TIS regimen. In both the original regimen and the TIS regimen, the median plasma concentration–time profiles consistently remained within the range of Threshold 1 (85.960 ng/mL) to Threshold 2 (505 ng/mL). Steady‐state plasma concentration–time profiles were similar between the original and the TIS regimens. There was no significant difference in the first dosing period between the original regimen and the TIS regimen based on the ratios (0.75 to 1.68) of the PK parameters (Figures 2 and 3; Tables S4 and S5).

Safety

In the two‐injection group, among patients with schizophrenia, all serious ADRs reported within 28 days post‐administration or with unknown onset timing, along with all non‐serious ADRs reported in two or more patients, were ADRs also reported in the one‐injection group, except for photopsia, vertigo, and supraventricular tachyarrhythmia. ADRs not reported in the one‐injection group occurred in only one case each, and no ADRs were specifically reported more frequently in the two‐injection group.

In patients with BP‐I disorder, serious ADRs (neutropenia, dyskinesia, and neuroleptic malignant syndrome) and non‐serious ADRs (COVID‐19) reported within 28 days post‐administration in the two‐injection group were reported in only one case each. No ADRs were reported in two or more cases. Serious ADRs with unknown onset timing (e.g., hallucination) were reported in the one‐injection group, and many of the non‐serious ADRs were also reported in this group.

The number of reported cases in the two‐injection group is limited, and the available information is insufficient to determine statistical trends in the incidence of ADRs. However, based on the postmarketing safety data reported since the approval of the TIS regimen, no fatal cases have been reported following its administration. Furthermore, most serious or non‐serious ADRs reported in two or more cases in the two‐injection group were also observed in the one‐injection group. Therefore, no new safety concerns are considered to be associated with the TIS regimen.

Discussion

This analysis demonstrates the similarity in the plasma concentration–time profiles between the original regimen and the TIS regimen for initiation treatment in a Japanese population using the popPK modeling and simulation approach. Given the availability of extensive aripiprazole PK data, we were able to perform this popPK modeling with simulation analysis. The plasma concentration–time profiles between the original regimen and the TIS regimen in the Japanese population were similar for the aripiprazole doses (oral + LAI) assessed, irrespective of aripiprazole oral stabilization (Figures 2 and 3; Tables S4 and S5). In both the original regimen and the TIS regimen, the median plasma concentration–time profiles consistently remained within the range of Threshold 1 (85.960 ng/mL) to Threshold 2 (505 ng/mL), suggesting similar efficacy and safety are expected between the two regimens. In addition to reducing pill burden, the TIS regimen is likely to improve treatment adherence during the initiation period for patients who have difficulty adhering to the 2‐week oral aripiprazole dosing.

After external validation of the previous popPK model, 8 the model was updated such that simulations could be performed using the updated model to assess the similarity of the plasma concentration–time profiles between the original regimen and the TIS regimen in the Japanese population. This was required because, during external validation, there was an underprediction of aripiprazole trough concentrations in the 031‐08‐003 study (Figure S2). This required a minor update of the CYP2D6 genotype covariate on CL in the popPK model, considering the difference of the distribution of the CYP2D6 genotypes. The updated model had four groups of CL estimates based on CYP2D6 genotypes, which improved the model performance for CYP2D6 intermediate metabolizers and those with unknown/missing genotype status and eliminated the underprediction observed in these groups (based on the medians, see Figure S6). In the 5th percentile, there was some tendency for underprediction; however, the degree of discrepancy was minor and not clinically significant. The estimated values of CL for each CYP2D6 genotype group (see Table 2) are similar to those reported in a previous analysis or popPK model. 8 The proportion of unknown/missing exceeded 50% for the Japanese population, but the numbers of evaluable subjects were 46 for poor metabolizers, 81 for intermediate metabolizers, 826 for extensive/ultra‐rapid metabolizers, and 170 for unknown/missing for the overall population, which were considered sufficient to estimate CL for each CYP2D6 genotype. In addition, among the 98 in the unknown group, 86 were from Study 031‐08‐003, in which genotype testing data were unavailable because subjects did not consent to genetic testing; therefore, these data were essentially missing. Consequently, 167 of the 170 unknown/missing entries were considered effectively missing. The missing was attributed to reasons such as the absence of genotype testing in some studies (Studies 031‐07‐002, 031‐10‐002, and 031‐13‐005), or the fact that even when genotype testing was conducted, it was not a mandatory requirement for study participation (Study 031‐08‐003), resulting in the inability to obtain informed consent for genotype testing. Therefore, the missing mechanism was considered “missing completely at random” and was not expected to affect the estimation of genotype‐specific CL. Furthermore, the estimated CL for poor metabolizers was consistent with the CL with CYP2D6 inhibitors co‐administered, and the CL for intermediate metabolizers (2.53 L/h) was intermediate between that of extensive/ultra‐rapid metabolizers (3.67 L/h) and poor metabolizers (1.90 L/h), indicating no apparent inconsistency. In the present analysis, CL for the unknown/missing group was estimated separately from extensive/ultra‐rapid metabolizers, intermediate metabolizers, and poor metabolizers, and the VPC plots by CYP2D6 genotypes demonstrated good predictive performance across all genotypes, suggesting that the robustness of the model was maintained. Given that the incorporation of genotype‐specific CL improved the VPC plots in the Japanese population, who had a relatively high proportion of intermediate metabolizers and unknown/missing, we consider the estimated CL values for each genotype to be reasonable. Thus, we conclude that the updated model appropriately described the observed Japanese data. In addition, as the simulations were conducted under conditions limited to extensive metabolizers, the reliability of the simulation results is not considered to be affected.

However, in the updated model, a slight bias was observed in the absorption rate constants from each administration site (IMKa and DKa) when comparing the Japanese and non‐Japanese populations (Figure S7). To evaluate the impact of these biases on aripiprazole exposure, simulations were conducted using different IMKa and DKa values in a sensitivity analysis. Simulations were performed to predict exposures over the 4 weeks following the first dose, including Cmax, AUCτ, and Ctrough, as TIS is the dosing regimen used for the initial administration. Simulations were performed with one representative subject from each population. Simulations were conducted for a typical subject (male, BMI = 28 kg/m2, CYP2D6 phenotype = extensive metabolizer). All PK parameters other than IMKa or DKa were fixed to the typical values to exclude any other covariate effects in the model, and were the same for the two populations. IMKa was set to 0.000720 (1/h) for the Japanese subject and 0.00109 (1/h) for the non‐Japanese subject, based on median of post hoc parameters. DKa was set to 0.000804 (1/h) for the Japanese subject and 0.00103 (1/h) for the non‐Japanese subject, based on median of post hoc parameters. The post hoc parameters of both IMKa and DKa were lower in Japanese subjects than in non‐Japanese subjects. As a result, when using IMKa and DKa values optimized for the Japanese population, it was predicted that Cmax and AUCτ would be 1.2 to 1.4 times lower and that Ctrough would be 1.1 to 1.2 times lower than those for the non‐Japanese populations (Table S6). The lower estimated exposure in Japanese subjects is considered most likely attributable to the smaller absorption rate constants. The lower exposure predicted in the presence of lower absorption rates (IMKa and DKa) is thought to be related to the fact that the evaluated exposure metrics were not at steady state, but rather at 4 weeks after the first dose. With slower absorption, the plasma concentration–time profile rises more slowly and reaches a lower peak, resulting in a lower Cmax. While AUC∞ is theoretically independent of absorption rate, the AUC evaluated in this analysis was limited to the first 4 weeks after the first dose. As this AUC reflects the early phase before the profile fully transitions into the elimination phase, a slower and lower rising phase leads to a reduced AUC over this time window. Similarly, Ctrough in this analysis corresponds to the concentration at 4 weeks after the first dose; as this also reflects the pre‑elimination phase with a slower rise in concentration, Ctrough is likewise influenced by the absorption rate and was therefore lower.

In the popPK model, BMI was incorporated as a covariate on IMKa and DKa, such that lower BMI was associated with higher values of IMKa and DKa. However, Japanese subjects exhibited lower post hoc parameter estimates of IMKa and DKa compared with non‐Japanese subjects (IMKa: 0.000720 1/h vs 0.00109 1/h; DKa: 0.000804 1/h vs 0.00103 1/h) despite lower BMI in Japanese subjects. This difference persisted after covariate adjustment for BMI, and the underlying cause remains unclear. Nevertheless, the resulting exposure differences were approximately 1.1‑ to 1.4‑fold, and these differences were not considered meaningful, as they did not represent significant deviations from the established aripiprazole PK therapeutic window.

As the required modifications made to the previous popPK model were minimal, the model structure was not updated further and was determined to be acceptable for the TIS regimen simulations.

It has been found that there was no significant difference in the safety profile of aripiprazole LAI formulations when administered with an original regimen between Japanese and non‐Japanese patients based on both the clinical study data and postmarketing safety data to date. The median of estimated aripiprazole exposure of the simulation of TIS regimen among Japanese patients (Figures 2 and 3) was comparable to the median of the simulated exposure of TIS regimen in non‐Japanese patients and fell within the corresponding exposure range (Figures 4 and 5 in reference [8]); therefore, it is likely that extrapolation of the postmarketing safety data from non‐Japanese patients would be expected to produce similar findings among Japanese patients.

In the two‐injection group, the number of reported cases regarding safety is limited, and the available information is insufficient to determine statistical trends in the incidence of ADRs. However, based on the postmarketing safety data following the approval of the TIS regimen, there have been no reports of serious ADRs resulting in death in the two‐injection group. Furthermore, most of the serious or non‐serious ADRs reported in two or more cases in the two‐injection group were also included among those reported after the original regimen. Therefore, based on the currently available evidence, there appears to be no new safety concerns to date with the TIS regimen in non‐Japanese observational studies. 22 , 23

As noted above, several limitations should be acknowledged, including the high proportion of unknown/missing CYP2D6 genotypes, possible residual bias in absorption parameter estimates, and limited non‐Japanese postmarketing safety data.

Conclusion

Based on the results of the external validation, we report a minor update to the previous popPK model to capture CYP2D6 genotype distribution in the Japanese study population. The updated model adequately described the Japanese data, and the estimated parameters were in agreement with the previous analysis and popPK model. Simulations based on the updated model showed that the plasma concentration‐time profiles of the original regimen and the TIS regimen were similar in the Japanese population. Regarding safety, the currently available postmarketing safety data do not show any new safety concerns with the TIS regimen.

The initial administration of the once‐monthly aripiprazole LAI using the TIS regimen involves only a 1‐day concomitant administration of oral aripiprazole, making it a simpler treatment initiation method compared with the original regimen, which requires 2 weeks of concomitant oral aripiprazole therapy. Therefore, this approach may provide a new option for initiating once‐monthly aripiprazole LAI in patients with schizophrenia and BP‐I disorder, who typically have low medication adherence and a high risk of relapse or recurrence. Furthermore, reducing the oral aripiprazole administration period to a single day is expected to reduce the medication burden on patients and, consequently, reduce the burden on healthcare professionals and caregivers who are responsible for managing patient adherence to treatment.

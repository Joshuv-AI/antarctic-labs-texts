Pharmacotherapeutic Evaluation of Topical Insulin Versus Medicinal Honey Dressing in the Management of Diabetic Foot Ulcers among Patients with Diabetes Mellitus: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: K Chandramathi, B Tamilarasi, P Padmavathi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Diabetic foot ulcer (DFU) is a serious complication of diabetes mellitus and a major cause of morbidity and lower limb amputation. Effective pharmacological interventions that enhance wound healing are essential for DFU management. Topical insulin has been shown to promote cellular proliferation and angiogenesis, whereas medicinal honey possesses antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and tissue regenerative properties.Objective:To compare the effectiveness of topical insulin therapy and medicinal honey dressing in promoting wound healing among patients with DFU.Methods:A randomized controlled superiority trial with a four-group pre-test and post-test design was conducted among 120 patients with Wagner grade I and II DFU. Participants were allocated into four experimental arms (n = 30 each). Experimental arms I and III received topical insulin dressing, whereas experimental arms II and IV received medicinal honey dressing. Wound healing was evaluated using the Bates–Jensen Wound Assessment Tool (BWAT) before and after intervention.Results:Both interventions significantly improved wound healing scores (P < 0.001). In the insulin groups, the paired t-test values were 16.91 and 15.91, while the honey dressing groups showed greater improvement with t-values of 22.35 and 28.17. The post-test mean wound scores were significantly lower in the honey dressing groups compared to the insulin groups, indicating enhanced healing.Conclusion:Both topical insulin and medicinal honey dressing are effective pharmacological adjuncts for DFU management. However, medicinal honey dressing demonstrated superior efficacy in promoting wound healing among patients with diabetes mellitus.

INTRODUCTION

Diabetes mellitus affects over 340 million people worldwide, with ~20% developing diabetic wounds. Diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs), a major complication, result from neuropathy, peripheral vascular disease, and hyperglycemia-induced impaired healing.[1] DFUs progress through five stages: normal foot (1), high-risk foot (2), ulcerated foot (3), infected foot (4), and necrotic foot (5). They remain a leading cause of non-traumatic lower limb amputations globally.[2]

Hyperglycemia causes chronic wounds with delayed epithelialization, reduced collagen synthesis, and prolonged inflammation. Symptoms progress from pain/tingling to numbness, weakness, and fall risk. Management requires glycemic control, wound care, off-loading, and multidisciplinary approaches.[3]

Advanced topical therapies show promise: topical insulin promotes keratinocyte migration, fibroblast proliferation, collagen deposition, and angiogenesis via pathways such as PI3K/Akt; medicinal honey (especially Manuka) provides antimicrobial action (methylglyoxal, DHA, leptosperin, HMF), osmotic debridement, anti-inflammatory effects, and a moist healing environment.[4]

This study compared the pharmacotherapeutic efficacy of topical insulin versus medicinal honey dressing for healing DFU in urban and rural populations.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

Randomized, blocked superiority trial with four-group pre- and post-test design. Registered with Clinical Trials Registry – India (CTRI/2021/03/032248).

Study setting

Urban arms (I and II): Monika Diabetic Care Hospital, Erode, Tamil Nadu. Rural arms (III and IV): Government Primary Health Centre, Pethampalayam, Erode, Tamil Nadu. Data collection: October 2021–May 2022 (urban); June 2022–January 2023 (rural).

Participants

120 patients with Wagner grade I/II DFU; purposive sampling (n = 30 per arm). Inclusion criteria: Age ≥30 years, both genders, fasting blood glucose 90–130 mg/dL, informed consent. Exclusion criteria: Absent peripheral pulses, uncontrolled bleeding, severe anemia, immunodeficiency disorders (e.g., asthma, malignancy).

Interventions

Topical insulin dressing (Arms I and III): 10 IU regular human insulin in 1 mL sterile normal saline applied daily after cleansing/debridement; covered with sterile gauze.

Medicinal honey dressing (Arms II and IV): Medical-grade sterile honey (3–5 mm layer) applied daily after cleansing; covered with sterile gauze. All participants received standard diabetic wound care (glycemic control, off-loading).

Outcome measure

Bates–Jensen Wound Assessment Tool (BWAT): 13 items scored 1–5 (total range 13–65); higher scores indicate more severe wounds. Demographic data also collected.

RESULTS

Demographic characteristics

Study summarizes that in urban arms (I and II), most participants were aged 51–60 years (38% in I, 40% in II aged 41–50), male (53% in I, 57% female in II), non-vegetarian (53% in I, 62% in II), with Type II diabetes (73% in I, 80% in II). Comorbidities included hypertension and heart failure.

In rural arms (III and IV), most were aged 41–50 years (37% in III, 33% in IV), male (57% in III, 53% in IV), non-vegetarian (63% in III, 53% in IV), with Type II diabetes (67% in III, 83% in IV).

Wound healing outcomes

Pre-test scores indicated wound regeneration in 83–90% of participants. Post-test scores showed improvement: in arms I and III (insulin), 37–43% achieved mild healthy tissue; in arms II and IV (honey), 13–17% achieved tissue healthy [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Both interventions significantly reduced DFU scores (P < 0.001), but honey dressing was superior, with greater mean differences (21.84–27.46 vs. 13.84–18.62). Topical insulin promotes healing via keratinocyte migration and angiogenesis.[2] Honey’s antimicrobial (MGO, H2O2) and anti-inflammatory properties enhance debridement and regeneration.[4] Rural populations showed slightly better honey response, possibly due to environmental factors.[5]

Findings align with meta-analyses showing honey’s efficacy in DFUs[6] and insulin’s role in proliferation. Limitations: purposive sampling, single-center settings. Future studies: larger samples, long-term follow-up.

CONCLUSION

Topical insulin and medicinal honey are effective for DFU healing, but honey dressing demonstrates superior pharmacotherapeutic efficacy, particularly in rural settings. Integration into clinical practice could reduce amputation rates.

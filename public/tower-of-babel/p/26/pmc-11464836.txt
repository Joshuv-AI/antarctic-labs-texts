Coal-gangue sound recognition using hybrid multi-branch CNN based on attention mechanism fusion in noisy environments
Authors: Qingjun Song, Wenchao Hao, Qinghui Song, Haiyan Jiang, Kai Li, Shirong Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe coal-gangue recognition technology plays an important role in the intelligent realization of fully mechanized caving face and the improvement of coal quality. Although great progress has been made for the coal-gangue recognition in recent years, most of them have not taken into account the impact of the complex environment of top coal caving on recognition performance. Herein, a hybrid multi-branch convolutional neural network (HMBCNN) is proposed for coal-gangue recognition, which based on improved Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficient (MFCC) as well as Mel spectrogram, and attention mechanism. Firstly, the MFCC and its smooth feature matrix are input into each branch of one-dimensional multi-branch convolutional neural network, and the spliced features are extracted adaptively through multi-head attention mechanism. Secondly, the Mel spectrogram and its first-order derivative are input into each branch of the two-dimensional multi-branch convolutional neural network respectively, and the effective time-frequency information is paid attention to through the soft attention mechanism. Finally, at the decision-making level, the two networks are fused to establish a model for feature fusion and classification, obtaining optimal fusion strategies for different features and networks. A database of sound pressure signals under different signal-to-noise ratios and equipment operations is constructed based on a large amount of data collected in the laboratory and on-site. Comparative experiments and discussions are conducted on this database with advanced algorithms and different neural network structures. The results show that the proposed method achieves higher recognition accuracy and better robustness in noisy environments.Keywords: Coal-gangue recognition, Sound signal, Hybrid multi-branch convolutional neural network, Attention mechanism, Noisy environmentSubject terms: Mechanical engineering, Coal

Introduction

A remarkable trend in top coal caving is intelligent coal caving in the future. Implementing intelligent top coal caving technology can solve the problems of over-caving and less-caving that may be caused by manual judgment, improve mining quality and efficiency, and reduce the impact of harsh environments on workers’ health. Coal-gangue recognition is the key to realize the intellectualization of fully mechanized top coal caving. The coal-gangue recognition mainly includes natural gamma ray1,2, radar detection3, image recognition4,5, sound signal analysis6, vibration signal analysis7-9 and so on. Among them, the natural gamma ray method was effective only when coal and gangue were rich in radioactive elements; radar detection was poorly applicable when the coal seam was too thick; the images in coal mining were highly susceptible to the influence of on-site dust and uneven lighting. In contrast, sound signals were easy to collect and analyze, which was conducive to the subsequent recognition of coal and gangue. In recent years, many works have reported the construction of sound recognition systems through feature extraction7–12, feature fusion13–15, state recognition6,16–18 and other method.

Mel spectrogram and Mel frequency cepstrum coefficient (MFCC) are commonly used in frequency domain feature extraction based on different applications of sound that reflect the frequency characteristics of the human auditory system and has the strengths of simple calculation, strong discriminative ability, and noise resistance13,14,19,20. Xu et al.6 proposed a coal–rock interface recognition method based on MFCC, which extracted MFCC features from sound signals through independent component analysis (ICA) while identifying them through the back-propagation (BP) neural network. The results have shown that MFCC reflected the coal–rock sound features more effectively than frame energy and kurtosis. Boddapati et al.21 found that spectrogram images, as input features, give better recognition results than audio files when using convolutional neural network (CNN) for classification of environmental sounds. Pang et al.22 converted the vibration signals of the collected hydraulic supports into acoustic pressure signals, extracted the features by calculating the auditory spectrum, and used the support vector machine to identify the coal and gangue during top coal caving. Based on CapsNet, Van et al.18 combined Mel spectrogram with features directly related to fundamental frequency for speech and emotion recognition, achieving higher classification accuracy. It can be seen that Mel spectrogram and MFCC play a key role in extracting features from sound signals.

With the rapid development of artificial intelligence algorithms, there have been a lot of reports focusing on the deep learning neural networks for sound classification, and the simplest and most commonly used deep learning method is CNN. Generally, one-dimensional CNN (1DCNN) is used for voice or music recognition, while two-dimensional CNN (2DCNN) is used for image or audio spectrum recognition15. A bird sound classification method based on the CNN algorithm was proposed by Permana et al. and extended to the early prediction of forest fires from actual bird sounds23,24. Chen et al.25 optimized the performance of the CNN model with two convolutional layers by changing the number of convolutional layers, which can achieve high classification accuracy for the automatic diagnosis of heart-lung sounds with limited training data. Recently, the multi-branch CNN (MBCNN) has performed excellently in pattern recognition tasks. Feng et al.26 proposed a sentiment analysis model combining a multichannel CNN with a multi-head attention mechanism, where features extracted from different channels are fed into a multi-headed attention layer to fully learn the sentiment information in the text. However, in the face of more complex scenarios, the recognition performance of a single network structure may not meet the actual needs.

Compared with a single network structure, a hybrid network structure can obtain more abundant feature information and better recognition ability27,28. Wang et al. proposed three models for high-resolution range profile recognition: multi-scale group-fusion 1DCNN29, Quadruplet depth-wise separable fusion CNN30 and group-fusion 1DCNN with layer-wise auxiliary classifiers31. The above three models show superior performance in recognition efficiency, model complexity and feature extraction respectively. Zhou et al.32 proposed reinforced two-stream fuzzy neural network combines fuzzy logic and transfer learning to enhance classification performance by integrating a deep CNN for spatial feature extraction and a fuzzy rules-based radial basis function neural network for capturing complex data relationships. Hu et al.33 proposed two-stream network based on detail screening and compensation, an interactive two-stream network designed for ultrasound image segmentation that addresses challenges such as speckle noise, low contrast, and blurred boundaries. Through the above research, it can be proved that the hybrid network structure can adapt to complex classification problems and has good performance.

In the identification of coal-gangue, sample data acquisition is crucial. Due to the complex environment of the top coal caving mining face, the collected sound signals have strong nonlinear characteristics and contain a large amount of noise. If directly used for recognition, it will inevitably be misclassified by noise interference, thus reducing the classification performance of the model. Considering above, we proposed a hybrid MBCNN (HMBCNN) combining 1DCNN and 2DCNN in this work, which used improved MFCC and Mel spectrogram features to identify coal-gangue sounds. The proposed method solved the problem of feature loss from the perspective of CNN itself. The MFCC and the Mel spectrogram were selected to represent the characteristics of coal and gangue, achieving the integration and complementarity of frequency domain and energy. Moreover, it solved the problems such as the top coal venting sound was mostly non-smooth signal that was easy to be interfered by complex noise environment, and it made up for the deficiency that a single feature cannot capture the important information of audio events. Attention mechanism can be used as a resource allocation scheme, which is the main means to solve the problem of information overload. In the case of limited computing power, it can process more important information with limited computing resources34. In this work, the multi-head attention mechanism and soft attention mechanism were used to improve the recognition performance in noisy environments, and the adaptive fusion of various network features was realized. Methods

Although MBCNN and hybrid neural networks have made a lot of achievements in feature learning and classification, there is a lack of in-depth research on feature mining and fusion in complex noise environments. In this study, the proposed HMBCNN included two main stages. The first stage was the pre-processing stage, in which MFCC and Mel spectrogram were used to characterize the sound signals collected in the process of top coal caving, and were used as the input of the 1D and 2D MBCNNs, respectively. The second stage was the coal-gangue recognition stage, which used the HMBCNN incorporating the attention mechanism to perform feature computation, feature fusion, and classification recognition of the input features. Especially, the introduction of multi-headed attention mechanism and soft attention mechanism is necessary for recognition in noisy environments, which significantly improves the performance of the model by dynamically allocating the weights of different features and extracts key features more effectively35. Comparison with previous work36,37, the proposed method in this work achieving excellent performance in noise environment.

Preprocessing stage

Compared with a single feature expression, using multiple feature expressions can explore the original sound signal from different aspects to obtain rich and complementary coal-gangue feature information. In this section, Mel spectrogram and its first-order derivative, as well as MFCC and its smoothed signal, were used to perform feature extraction on the sound signal, and the complete original features were effectively combined with the details and dynamic characteristics of transformed features. Improved Mel Spectrogram feature

The Mel spectrogram is based on the features generated by the Mel filter bank that imitates the structure of the human ear and exhibited significant performance even in the case of audio degradation. The feature extraction of the Mel spectrogram mainly includes five steps of pre-emphasis, framing, windowing, fast Fourier transform (FFT), and Mel filter bank38. Firstly, pre-emphasis is performed on the original signal to compensate for high-frequency attenuation and enhance signal characteristics. The signal after pre-emphasis is framed, and the overlap between frames is set to ensure the continuity of the signal. Next, a window function is applied to each frame to reduce the spectrum leakage phenomenon. Then FFT is performed, and the time domain signal is converted into a frequency domain signal to obtain a spectrum. Finally, Mel filter bank is used to calculate the Mel spectrogram. In this work, each sound file is the audio of 2s at a 48 kHz sampling rate, the frame length is set to 1024, and the frame shift is set to 512. Finally, a feature set MS1 (batches,188,40) is obtained, where 188 is the number of frames per Mel spectrogram matrix (dseq=188) and 40 is the number of features per frame (dfeat=40).

As a continuous signal in time, the features between adjacent frames before and after it can provide rich information. Attention mechanism

Multi-head attention mechanism

The multi-head attention mechanism was originally proposed by Vaswani et al.41 in 2017. As shown in Fig. 3, the traditional attention mechanism is limited to computing attention information at a single level, while the multi-head attention mechanism can obtain more comprehensive and effective features by performing multiple linear transformations on the input features. Multi-head attention mechanism is a combination and refinement of the self-attention mechanism, in which the main content is to process the input features by introducing a query matrix (Q), a key matrix (K), and a value matrix (V). The initial values of the three matrices are equal to the matrix X output by the concatenate layer of the 1DMBCNN model. The Linear module is a linear transformation layer that performs a linear mapping of input data. MatMul and Scale are the core steps in calculating Scaled Dot-Product Attention (SDA). Matmul is matrix multiplication, which is used to calculate attention weight. Before Scale is used for Softmax, scaling is performed to prevent the value of the dot product from being too large in the high-dimensional space. The role of softmax is to convert the scaled attention score into a probability distribution to determine which parts of the input sequence the model should focus on. The function of the Concat operation is to combine the outputs of multiple attention heads to form a comprehensive representation.

Soft attention

Mel spectrogram features are characterized by time and frequency dimensions, which contain a large amount of effective feature information and background noise information in the frequency domain42. However, the presence of noisy signals will bring a lot of artifacts and invalid features to the Mel spectrogram. Soft attention discredits noisy regions in the image by multiplying the corresponding feature maps with low weights so that the weights of low-attention regions are close to 0. Proposed HMBCNN for coal-gangue recognition based on MFCC and Mel Spectrogram

As shown in Fig. 5, the proposed HMBCNN combines 1DMBCNN based on multi-head attention and 2DMBCNN based on soft attention. The multi-head attention module adaptively assigns attention weights to the processing results of 1DMBCNN with three branches. A residual structure is introduced between the output of the 1DMBCNN and the output of the multi-head attention module, followed by the fully connected layer, BN layer and Dropout layer. Similarly, the soft attention module adaptively distributes weights to the processing results of 2DMBCNN with two branches. Global Average Pooling (GAP) generates output features by averaging all spatial positions of each feature map, which has unique advantages in reducing the number of parameters and preventing overfitting. A residual structure is introduced between the output of the 2DMBCNN and the output of the soft attention module, followed by the fully connected layer, BN layer and Dropout layer. Then, the outputs of the 1D and 2D networks above are integrated through the concatenate layer to achieve feature fusion on both the temporal and spatial scales. Finally, the Softmax decision layer fusion method is used to achieve coal-gangue recognition in noisy environments.

Experiments

Experiments are conducted in this section to evaluate the performance of the proposed sound classification algorithm in noisy environments for identifying coal-gangue during the top coal caving. It mainly includes the construction of the experimental platform as well as data collection, performance evaluation criteria, and experimental analysis.

Experimental setup and database

In order to study the recognition method of coal-gangue during top coal caving, an experimental platform was designed according to the scheme shown in Fig. 6. The upper part was the falling coal device, in which the inserting board can open and close the coal caving window to realize start–stop control of coal-gangue falling. The coal falling device can slide at a vertical distance of 550–1100 mm to carry out the top coal falling experiment at different heights. GSD110 sound sensor was used to collect sound data with a working bandwidth of 500–20,000 Hz, which can effectively collect sound signals in the process of experiment. However, the noise generated by other devices working in a real operating environment can interfere with the accurate recognition of coal-gangue. Therefore, we collected sound signals from the operation of the shearer, conveyor and transfer machine on site, and played them on a laptop to simulate the on-site noise in the caving working face of the coal mine. All the models used the Adam optimizer, the initial learning rate is set to 0.0001, the epoch is set to 200, and the batch size is set to 64. The Dropout rate is set to 0.5 for each training period. The hardware and software environment used for the experiments is: the programming language Python 3.9, building deep learning models using the open-source deep learning framework Tensorflow 2.7.0 and combined with CUDA 11.8 for training acceleration, model training using one NVIDIA GeForce MX450 GPU, operating system Win 10, 11th Gen Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-1165G7 @ 2.80 GHz CPU, and 1 TB of computer memory.

Schematic diagrams of the scaled physical model of top coal caving.

The sound signal of the coal-gangue used in our experiment came from the database under the experimental conditions shown in Fig. 6, which contained two datasets: Dataset 1 and Dataset 2. Dataset 1 simulated the noisy environment when a single device was running, while Dataset 2 simulated the mixed noisy environment when different engineering devices were running simultaneously. Five kinds of noise of the right cutting of the shearer, the left cutting of the shearer, the transfer machine, the rear conveyor, and the front conveyor during operation were mixed with the sound of falling coal and the sound of falling gangue respectively to form Dataset 1. The above five kinds of noise signals were mixed first and then mixed with the falling coal sound and the falling gangue sound respectively to form Dataset 2.

From Table 3, it can be seen that the proposed HMBCNN model is significantly superior to the other four models under all SNRs. In the comparative experiments of Dataset 1 and Dataset 2, the accuracy of 1DMBCNN-MA was higher than 1DMBCNN within all considered SNR levels. It is worth noting that when the SNR was − 5 dB, the improvements were improved. The largest increases were 14.86% and 10.47%, respectively. The introduction of multi-head attention mechanism can use multiple independent heads to calculate the attention weight at each position of the feature sequence, and then capture various ranges of dependencies on the entire feature matrix and combine them into the implicit vector representation of the entire matrix. In addition, the accuracy of 2DMBCNN-SA was also improved compared with 2DMBCNN, and the improvement was greatest when the SNR was relatively high (5 dB). This also demonstrate that during the processing of the 2D spectrum, soft attention can focus on assigning weights to effective information and set the attention of the noise part to low credibility, which extracts valuable information more effectively while eliminating interference information. The proposed HMBCNN model introduced both multi-head attention and soft attention into the network and combined the advantages of 1DMBCNN-MA and 2DMBCNN-SA. Compared with 1DMBCNN-MA, the HMBCNN model improved the accuracy by 4% and 5.16% on Dataset 1 and Dataset 2, respectively. Similarly, compared with the 2DMBCNN-SA model, the accuracy of the HMBCNN model on the two datasets was improved by 1.97% and 9.84%, respectively. This means that the HMBCNN model can effectively eliminate the background noise at different distances from the noise source while effectively preserving the feature information of the signal. It can be seen that this method has excellent adaptability, stability, and noise resistance to different complex noise environments, and is suitable for complex actual industrial sites.

B. Analysis of convergence

The accuracy curves of 1DMBCNN, 1DMBNN-MA, 2DMBCNN, 2DMBCNN-SA, and HMBCNN models trained on the training set for two datasets with an SNR of -5 are shown in Fig. 7. In Dataset 1, the above five models reached stable accuracy in the training set after 73, 95, 66, 91, and 23 epochs, respectively (Fig. 7(a)). However, the accuracy of the training set of these five models reached stable values after 118, 125, 80, 96, and 72 epochs in Dataset 2 respectively (Fig. 7(b)), indicating that noise will affect the convergence rate of the training set. Although the introduction of a multi-head attention mechanism in the 1DMBCNN-MA model can capture more abundant feature information, it will lead to an increase in the amount of parameter calculation and slow down the convergence rate. It is worth noting that HMBCNN could extract complementary features in parallel through various branches of different types of networks, and adaptively allocate feature weights in the network through attention mechanisms, it had the fastest convergence rate, the smallest oscillation amplitude of the convergence curve, and the highest accuracy after convergence on both datasets. In addition, effective feature extraction can be performed on sound signals in noisy environments in each iteration. The parallelized model can learn the data together, improve the computational efficiency and the generalization ability of the models, and form a complementary decision, thereby achieving stability after fewer training epochs.

The minimum standard deviation was obtained on eight classes of Dataset 1 (Condition Label = 0,1,3,4,5,6,7,9). Compared with the 1DMBCNN-MA model, the G-mean of the 2DMBCNN-SA model increased by an average of 5.884% on Dataset 1, while it decreased by an average of 6.96% on Dataset 2. It is thus clear that the soft attention mechanism can better learn locally useful features, while the multi-head attention mechanism can better remove noise interference. HMBCNN, which integrates multi-head attention and soft attention, can learn the sound signal features of different receptive field levels from the convolution kernels of different branches in each network, and complement various effective features to reduce the impact of the network with poor performance on the overall recognition accuracy of the model, and has good robustness to noise.

In short, the method proposed in the work utilizes the hybrid MBCNN with different types of attention mechanisms to integrate a variety of MFCC smoothing and Mel spectrogram derivative features. It can learn useful state classification features from different frequency step levels, discover subtle feature information, and eliminate useless noise features. This not only preserves the information in the original features but also eliminates noise. Therefore, HMBCNN provides a target recognition method in multi-noise environments.

Conclusion

This work studies an effective coal-gangue recognition method based on the sound signal in noisy environments.The proposed HMBCNN method demonstrates superior recognition performance on datasets with varying SNR levels and in the presence of diverse background noises, effectively capturing the acoustic features of coal and gangue even in highly complex noise environments. The experimental results indicate that the proposed method has higher recognition accuracy and robustness that adapt to various noise environments, verifying the effectiveness of the HMBCNN model.

This study presents a new and feasible approach for the top coal caving at the real working face. However, facing different on-site conditions, this study can only identify coal and gangue, and cannot classify different gangue contents. The multi-class classification of coal-gangue based on sound signals is still a central issue limiting progress in the field of the coal-gangue recognition system. In the future research and development of our work, the proposed method will be applied to a real top coal caving at working face. The author will consider integrating more acoustic features to perform multi-classification recognition of different percentage of gangue content, so as to improve the efficiency and accuracy of top coal caving mining.

Volumetric amide-proton-transfer weighted imaging histogram analysis to differentiate pediatric high-grade and low-grade gliomas
Authors: Guisen Lin, Yijiang Zhuang, Feifei Lin, Weiting Tan, Shijie Hu, Dongxia Mo, Hairui Xiong, Hongwu Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo evaluate the value of amide-proton-transfer weighted (APTw) imaging in differentiating pediatric low-grade gliomas (pLGG) and high-grade gliomas (pHGG).Materials and methodsIn this retrospective study, APTw imaging from 69 consecutive pediatric patients suspected of brain tumors from April 2021 to March 2024 were reviewed for possible inclusion. Histogram metrics of APTw imaging for the gross tumor core and solid components were extracted. Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare histogram metrics between pLGG and pHGG. Receiver operating characteristic curve with area under the curve (AUC) was generated for statistically significant histogram metrics. A Bonferroni correction was applied for p value for 10 APTw metrics (p < 0.005 was considered significant).ResultsThirty-two patients (mean age: 68 ± 39 months; 18 males, 16 females) were included in the final analysis. The gross tumor core of pLGG exhibited higher APTmin (p = 0.017), and lower variance (p = 0.041) compared to pHGG. The solid components of pHGG had higher APTmax (p = 0.02), variance (p = 0.02), entropy (p = 0.02) but lower APTmin (p = 0.03) compared to pLGG. For the gross tumor core, pilocytic astrocytoma had significantly higher APT 10th than pHGG (p = 0.003). The AUC for APT 10th to differentiating pilocytic astrocytoma and pHGG was 0.82.ConclusionsVolumetric tumor APT histogram analysis may serve as a potential tool for differentiating pLGG and pHGG, but its application is different from adult population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14822-5.Keywords: Amide-proton-transfer, Pediatric, Gliomas, Histogram analysis

Introduction

Gliomas are the most common central nervous system tumors, accounting for ~ 51.6% of children aged between 0 and 14 years [1]. Pediatric low-grade gliomas (pLGG) have good prognosis with a 10-year overall survival rate of approximately 90% [2]. In contrast, pediatric high-grade gliomas (pHGG) exhibit poor prognosis [3]. For example, pediatric diffuse midline gliomas have a survival rate of approximately 8 to 11 months [4]. Differentiation between pLGG and pHGG is an important issue that guides clinical decision making.

Conventional MRI techniques are routinely applied for diagnosis of pediatric gliomas. However, gliomas are particularly challenging to characterize using conventional MRI due to their diverse nature and the presence of necrotic areas or contrast–enhancing regions that may not clearly indicate the tumor’s grade [5]. Advanced MRI techniques, such as MR perfusion imaging and diffusion-weighted imaging, have been developed to enhance the accuracy of diagnosing gliomas [6, 7].

Amide-proton-transfer weighted (APTw) imaging is an innovative technique that leverages the chemical exchange saturation transfer effect of amide protons found in the backbone of proteins and peptides [8, 9]. APTw MRI can help distinguish between low-grade and high-grade gliomas in adults based on their differences in cellular protein content [10]. However, pediatric and adult gliomas exhibit significant differences in terms of biological characteristics and radiological appearance [11]. Unlike adult low-grade gliomas that have low protein expression, pLGG may have elevated protein concentration due to microvascular proliferation, protein-rich microcysts, and mucoid material [12]. Therefore, the application of APTw imaging in the pediatric population faces unique challenges and considerations and may not be directly translatable from adult applications.

MRI histogram analysis of brain tumors is a valuable tool for assessing tumor heterogeneity and can provide insights into the microenvironment of the tumor [13]. We hypothesized that volumetric tumor APT histogram analysis was related to the grade of pediatric gliomas. The purpose of this study was to evaluate the value of volumetric APT histogram analysis for the differentiation of pLGG and pHGG.

Materials and methods

Participants

From April 2021, APTw imaging has been incorporated into the clinical diagnostic protocol for children who are suspected of having brain tumors. Written informed consent for APTw imaging scanning was obtained from the guardians of the participants before scanning. Otherwise, APTw imaging would not be performed. Ethical approval was waived by the institutional ethical review board as data were evaluated retrospectively. From April 2021 to March 2024, all patients suspected of having brain tumors who underwent APTw imaging were reviewed. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathologically diagnosis of glioma; (2) no prior treatment before MR examination; (3) Both APTw imaging and conventional MRI (including T1WI, T2WI, FLAIR and post-contrast T1WI) are available. Patients with low quality APTw imaging were excluded.

Imaging acquisition

All scan was performed on a 3 Tesla MR system (Ingenia, Philips Healthcare, Best, the Netherlands). Standard MR sequences including pre-contrast T1WI, T2WI, FLAIR and post-contrast T1WI were implemented. Axial APTw imaging was acquired using the 3D TSE-DIXON approach with a continuous saturation duration of 2 s and RF saturation amplitude of 2µT. At the 3.5 ppm offset, three Z-spectral images are acquired with different echo time shifts. This allows for the calculation of a B0 field map using mDixon algorithms, which is then used for z-spectrum correction on a voxel-by-voxel basis. An offset of −1,560 ppm is used for signal normalization, ensuring consistent image intensity across the scan. The APTw imaging was constructed by the MRT asymmetry at the offsets of ± 3.5 ppm. This method is notable for its rapid acquisition time and its high sensitivity to both pH levels and amide proton signals. The acquisition time of APTw imaging for each patient is approximately 3 to 5 min.

Volumetric histogram analysis

The tumor ROI was manually outlined by a researcher (5 years’ experience in neuroradiology) on the registered T1WI, T2WI, FLAIR image and post-contrast T1WI in the axial direction using ITK-SNAP [14]. The senior radiologists re-examined and revised the tumor boundary delineation to ensure its accuracy. The ROI defines the gross tumor core and solid component using the protocol in the BRATS study [15]. The solid component was the combination of the enhancing core and non-enhancing core defined by BRATS study. Figure 1 illustrates the representing ROI of gross tumor core and solid component, respectively. Histogram analysis was carried out using pyRadiomics [16] to obtain the first-order histogram parameters that include mean, median, 10th percentile, 90th percentile, minimum, maximum, skewness, entropy, variance, and kurtosis.

Images from a representative patient (a 72-month boy with pilocytic astrocytoma). Tumor ROI of the gross tumor core and solid component was outlined on the registered T1WI, T2WI, FLAIR image and post-contrast T1WI in the axial direction

T1-weighted (T1w) signal ratio of cyst to cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)

For the solid-cystic tumor, 3 non-overlapped ROIs were placed in the cystic parts of the tumor and 3 ROIs in the lateral ventricle to measure the T1w signal of the cystic component and CSF respectively. The T1w signal ratio cyst to CSF was calculated by dividing the mean T1w signal from the cystic parts of the tumor by that of the CSF.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using the SPSS (Version 22.0, IBM Corporation, Chicago, IL). A Bonferroni correction was applied for p value for 10 APTw metrics (p < 0.005 was considered significant). For other analysis, a p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The histogram parameters, T1w ratio cyst to CSF, and histopathological markers (Ki-67 proliferation index) were assessed for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. The student’s t-test was used to compare all these parameters of high-grade and low-grade gliomas when normally distributed, and Mann-Whitney U-test was used when not normally distributed. The diagnostic performance of different APT histogram parameters for differentiating glioma grades was evaluated by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. Pearson’s correlation analysis was used for assessment of the correlation between Ki-67 proliferation index with APTw metrics if normally distributed; otherwise, Spearman’s correlation analysis was used.

Overall, 17 patients and 15 patients were diagnosed with pLGG and pHGG, respectively. Of the patients with pLGG, 12 patients were diagnosed with pilocytic astrocytoma.

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the feasibility and capability of histogram analysis of ATPw imaging in differentiating low-grade and high-grade pediatric gliomas. We found the gross tumor core of pLGG had higher APTmin and lower variance than pHGG. The solid component of pHGG had higher APTmax, entropy, variance but lower APTmin than pLGG. Additionally, we compared the APT histogram metrics among pilocytic astrocytoma and pHGG. We found that pilocytic astrocytoma had significantly higher 10th than pHGG of the gross tumor core. The results of our study demonstrated the potential value of APT histogram analysis in differentiating pLGG and pHGG.

Unlike adult glioma studies that showed higher APTw value in high grade glioma [10, 17], pLGG demonstrated higher APTw metrics (APTmin) than pHGG in our study, even though not achieved statistical significance. Gliomas in pediatric and adult populations display notable disparities regarding their biological properties and imaging features [18]. In pediatric population, majority of low-grade gliomas are pilocytic astrocytomas, accounting for ~ 65% [19]. In pilocytic astrocytomas, microvascular proliferation, protein-rich microcysts and mucoid material are commonly found [12]. These protein-rich components could explain the increased APTw signal. Our subgroup comparison between pilocytic astrocytoma and pHGG also supports this hypothesis, in which APT 10th of pilocytic astrocytomas from the gross tumor core was significantly higher than pHGG. Intracranial substances with high concentration of proteins would demonstrate high T1w signal intensity [20]. Relative T1w signal intensity normalized to CSF could serve as an indirect surrogate for protein concentration, particularly when T1 mapping is unavailable. In our cohort, solid-cystic pLGGs had significantly higher T1w signal ratio cyst to CSF than solid-cystic pHGGs, which supported our hypothesis that elevated APTw signal in pLGG reflects high protein or mucin content.

We separately evaluate the gross tumor core and the solid component, addressing a recognized limitation of APTw imaging, where signal is attenuated in protein-poor compartments. In our cohort, APTw metrics of the solid component in solid-cystic gliomas were lower than those of the gross tumor core. This demonstrated that both pLGG and pHGG exhibited higher APTw signal intensity in their cystic components than the solid parts. Incorporating cystic components into the analysis of the gross tumor core may lead to an overestimation of APTw signal intensity within the tumor parenchyma. The solid component is more likely to reflect the grade of pediatric gliomas. The APTmax of the solid component was higher in pHGG than pLGG, potentially reflecting higher protein expression in tumor cell of pHGG. Furthermore, the variance of APTw signal in pHGG was higher than that of pLGG. High-grade gliomas can exhibit pronounced intertumoral heterogeneity caused by regions of hemorrhages and necrosis [21]. Increased heterogeneity leads to a broader distribution of APT values around the mean, thereby resulting in elevated variance within pHGG. However, all the above-mentioned metrics did not achieve statistical significance after applying a Bonferroni correction. One possible reason is due to the small sample size which limits statistical power. The application of Bonferroni correction modifies probability (p) values was to decrease the risk of type I errors that arise when conducting multiple statistical tests. However, given the criticisms of Pernerger [22], if researchers aim not to overlook any potential effect worthy of further investigation, a Bonferroni correction would be inappropriate. Therefore, a lager cohort is necessary to evaluate the potential value of these APTw metrics.

Our findings are consistent with those of Obdeijn’s study [23] and Rajendran’s study [24], which showed increased APT signal intensity in pilocytic astrocytoma. In comparison with Obdeijin’s study, we conducted the study with more participants. And we performed histogram analysis of APTw imaging, which identified more valuable APT metrics for differentiating pLGG and pHGG. However, unlike Togao’s study that showed correlation between APTw values of adult diffuse gliomas and Ki-67 histopathological cell proliferation biomarker [25], we did not identify significant correlations between APTw metrics with Ki-67 index in our cohort. One possible explanation was that we included a variety of pediatric gliomas rather than solely diffuse gliomas. To some extent, this also reveals the different biological and imaging characteristics between pediatric gliomas and adult gliomas.

In this study, we employed a three-dimensional APT protocol characterized by rapid acquisition time and high sensitivity to both pH levels and amide proton signals. This method is not only clinically feasible but also patient friendly, allowing for seamless integration into daily clinical practice. Second, variations in the non-volumetric ROIs delineated by radiologists can introduce variability in the histogram parameters derived from the same lesion [26]. Volumetric histogram analysis is an objective method to evaluate the tumor quantitatively which could vastly eliminate the potential measurement sampling error and reflect the heterogeneity of gliomas. Third, CSF artifacts could appear in APTw imaging [27]. The visibly elevated APTw signal of the cerebellopontine-angle CSF (artifact) adjacent to gliomas is difficult to distinguish from the signal of the gliomas itself. The ROI of the tumor was manually outlined on the registered T1WI, T2WI, FLAIR image and post-contrast T1WI in the axial direction using ITK-SNAP, which avoid including the CSF into the tumor boundary. This ensures the accuracy of the analysis. Finally, we conducted histogram analysis of APTw imaging, which enabled us to quantify tumor heterogeneity and provided insights into the microenvironment of the tumor. This approach identified several APTw metrics valuable for distinguishing between pLGG and pHGG.

Despite these advancements, our study had several limitations. First, this study cohort includes only 32 patients from a single institution, which limits statistical power and generalizability. External validation in larger, prospective, and ideally multicenter cohorts is necessary to confirm the reproducibility and clinical applicability of the findings. Second, the precise pathophysiological mechanisms underline the observed alterations in APTw signal intensity within the tumor, particularly in pilocytic astrocytomas, remain elusive. Further research incorporating experimental methodologies in tumorigenesis and proteomics is essential to elucidate these underlying processes. A promising future direction would be to introduce a novel metric, such as a “Cyst-to-Solid APT Ratio” calculated by dividing the mean APTw signal from the cystic/necrotic region by that of the solid component. This index could more directly test the hypothesis that the cystic fluid in pediatric low-grade gliomas, particularly pilocytic astrocytomas, is uniquely protein-rich compared to the necrotic cavities in high-grade gliomas. Finally, comparison among pediatric glioma subtypes was preliminarily conducted, which was constrained by the limited patient cohort.

In conclusion, histogram analysis of APT imaging can be used as a potential image tool to differentiate between pLGG and pHGG. While the application of APTw imaging in the pediatric population is different and cannot be directly translatable from adult population.

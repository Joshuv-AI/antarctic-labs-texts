VetEd Asia 2024: a symposium to promote veterinary education and collaboration
Authors: Takashi Matsuwaki, Koichi Sato, Maho Urabe, Nayana Wijayawardhane
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn October 2024, the Asian Association of Veterinary Schools hosted “VetEd Asia 2024,” a symposium to promote veterinary education and collaborations in Asia, held in Daejeon, Republic of Korea. The event was co-organized with the Federation of Asian Veterinary Associations and the American Association of Veterinary Medical Colleges’ Council on International Veterinary Medical Education and with the support of the World Organisation for Animal Health Regional Representation for Asia and the Pacific. The symposium brought together diverse stakeholders in veterinary education, facilitated networking, and highlighted emergent topics such as education research aimed at improving the quality of education in the region. These collaborative efforts are expected to strengthen veterinary education standards and outcomes, resulting in the development of a stronger veterinary workforce.Keywords: Education, veterinary, Asia

Networking among Veterinary Education Establishments (VEEs) can improve veterinary education standards and outcomes through the exchange of experiences, ideas, and resources. Various formal and informal networks of VEEs have been established worldwide, and they gather periodically to foster collaboration. For example, VetEd is an annual symposium and networking event organized by the Veterinary Schools Council in the UK [1]. In recent years, various organizations in different parts of the world, such as VetEd Africa in 2022, have organized similar VetEd events. Asia is home to more than 200 VEEs that follow diverse course curricula, standards, and delivery methods [2,3]. Challenges commonly reported by VEEs in the region include inadequate educational infrastructure, limited access to educational resources, and a shortage of skilled educators. On October 26–28, 2024, the Asian Association of Veterinary Schools (AAVS) organized the VetEd Asia 2024, a symposium to promote veterinary education and collaborations across the region in partnership with the Federation of Asian Veterinary Associations (FAVA) and the American Association of Veterinary Medical Colleges’ (AAVMC) Council on International Veterinary Medical Education (CIVME) [4]. The event was held in conjunction with the 23rd Congress of FAVA 2024, hosted by the Korean Veterinary Medical Association in Daejeon, Korea, from October 25–27, 2024. This collaboration was facilitated and supported by the World Organisation for Animal Health (WOAH) Regional Representation for Asia and the Pacific (RRAP), which created valuable opportunities for veterinarians, veterinary educators from within and beyond Asia, and veterinary students to share experiences and discuss veterinary education and veterinary education research (VER) across the region.

The AAVS was established in 2001 with the mission to advance and enhance veterinary education, research, and public services in Asia. The association currently includes more than 50 members of VEE from East Asia, Southeast Asia, and South Asia. AAVS holds annual meetings, and recently formed two working groups: one group focused on veterinary education enhancement and the other on VER. These working groups serve as a forum for interested educators to connect, offering regular continuing education webinars for faculty and staff members, promoting innovative teaching, learning, and assessment methods while also supporting the development of VER. Although VER is crucial for advancing global development of the profession, it remains an emerging field in Asia. Recognizing this, the AAVS established the Veterinary Education Research Working Group (VER-WG) in 2022 to raise awareness in the field and build a network of veterinary educational researchers across Asia.

FAVA is the network of Veterinary Medical Associations in Asia, established in 1978 by veterinary associations from the Philippines, Malaysia, China, Indonesia, Japan, and Korea. The network serves as a regional network of veterinary medical associations. FAVA holds a biennial congress attended by veterinarians, veterinary students, and veterinary technicians from across Asia. In 2020, during the 42nd FAVA Council meeting, the FAVA strategic plan 2021–2025 was adopted, outlining [5] six focus areas, including veterinary education. Six sub-committees (initially working groups) were formed to implement these priorities, with one dedicated to improving veterinary education [6]. As part of its mission to enhance veterinary education, the FAVA veterinary education subcommittee prioritized the collaboration with AAVS. This led to the signing of a memorandum of understanding (MoU) between AAVS and FAVA in 2023, in which both organizations agreed to host a session dedicated to veterinary education during their annual meetings in October 2024. At the time the MoU was signed, the FAVA President announced invitations for students from AAVS member institutions to attend the FAVA Congress. The Korean Veterinary Medical Association offered free registrations for up to four students per country, and additional support was provided by national associations such as the Japan Veterinary Medical Association, which sponsored travel for students to the Republic of Korea. In total, 24 students from nine countries participated in the Congress, taking advantage of this incredible opportunity.

The CIVME of the AAVMC was established in 2016 to promote collaboration, foster innovation, and share best practices globally to advance the quality of teaching and learning in veterinary medical education. The current representatives from Asia are Emeritus Professor Pan Dong Ryu from Seoul National University, the Republic of Korea, who serves as the CIVME chair, and Professor Nayana Wijayawardhane from the University of Peradeniya, Sri Lanka, who also chairs the VER-WG of AAVS. Both have been actively involved in AAVS and AAVMC CIVME initiatives, contributing to the success of a highly productive Research Symposium by facilitating connections with renowned veterinary educators worldwide. In 2024, AAVMC CIVME held its annual Executive Committee meeting in Daejeon, the Republic of Korea, and jointly co-organized VetEd Asia 2024, supporting the event by funding AAVMC CIVME members and contributing several speakers for the event.

WOAH supports the strengthening of Veterinary Services, VEEs, and Veterinary Statutory Bodies (VSBs) at global and regional levels. This support is delivered through key initiatives such as the Performance of Veterinary Services Pathways, twinning projects, and the development of guidelines and recommendations related to VEEs and VSBs [7]. Global and regional conferences by WOAH, along with its recommendations on the Competencies of Graduating Veterinarians and the Guidelines on Veterinary Education Core Curriculum, are widely recognized and valued internationally. In the Asia-Pacific region, WOAH has actively collaborated with regional stakeholder networks, including FAVA, AAVS, and Southeast Asia Veterinary School Association (SEAVSA), to achieve the following: (a) advocate the importance of high-quality veterinary education, (b) promote the use of WOAH’s educational resources, and (c) facilitate information sharing and regional/sub-regional collaborations towards efficient and effective veterinary workforce capacity development. The support of the WOAH RRAP with the University of Tokyo, home to the AAVS registered office (AAVS Office), under which RRAP agreed to provide secretariat support. This model (RRAP and AAVS coordination) has been very effective in liaising and linking VEEs regionally and globally to improve the standards of VE in Asia.

“VetEd Asia 2024,” a collaborative event organized by these organizations, featured a series of activities:

1. Workshop on Clinical Skills and Objective Structured Clinical Examinations: Conducted by Sarah Baillie, Emeritus Professor, University of Bristol, UK.

2. Plenary session on “Veterinary Education in Asia”: This session included the following: i) presentations from key organizations—WOAH, AAVS, FAVA, SEAVSA, Korea Association of Veterinary Medical Colleges, and International Veterinary Student Association—highlighting the initiatives related to curriculum harmonization and the development of accreditation standards across Southeast Asia, and ii) panel discussions with veterinary students representatives from nine countries/territories who shared their perspectives on emerging trends in the profession. Topics included the use of new technologies and the evolving role of gender within the veterinary profession.

3. Plenary session on “Veterinary Education Research”: This session featured a keynote address and several research presentations. Professor Elpida Artemiou of the School of Veterinary Medicine at Texas Tech University, USA, and Dr. Christin Kleinsorgen of the University of Veterinary Medicine Hannover, Germany, delivered the keynote presentation titled “Veterinary Clinical Communication, Education, and Research.” They shared evidence-based information on the transformative impact of effective communication in veterinary practice and the growing global integration of communication skills in veterinary education. During the VER presentation session, researchers from the AAVS member institutions presented original research findings and teaching innovations. Certificates were awarded for the best oral presentation and to all authors who presented during the symposium. This session highlighted the benefits of collaboration and information exchange between AAVMC CIVME and AAVS, showing how collective efforts can advance knowledge and improve the veterinary profession.

4. Tour of Seoul National University College of Veterinary Medicine: This tour was offered as part of the program, but it was not open to the general FAVA Congress attendees. More than 80 participants joined the Veterinary Education in Asia session during VetEd Asia 2024. The plenary sessions were held in a hybrid format, with approximately 20 participants joining online.

Collaboration among key regional organizations of veterinary education, along with active networking between VEEs across countries, can offer valuable opportunities for sharing information and resources. Events such as VetEd Asia bring together diverse stakeholders, facilitate networking, and promote the importance of emerging areas such as education research. Moving forward, organizers of VetEd Asia may be recommended to focus on engaging VEEs in Asia that are not currently AAVS Members through various related networks such as WOAH and FAVA. These collective efforts contribute to the development of a stronger veterinary workforce, ultimately improving animal health and welfare, and public health across the region.

Predicting polyvascular disease with optical coherence tomography angiography and/or optical coherence tomography
Authors: Minghao Liu, Shiqi Ouyang, Bowen Zhang, Shuangtong Shao, Pan Fan, Tianyu Wu, Huahui Ren, Qianhui Sun, Dirui Zhang, Ning Wang, Chen Zhao, Shengliang Liu, Xia Gu, Yunling Li, Xinxin Zhu, Xing Luo, Shan Zhang, Sining Hu, Gary S. Mintz, Bo Yu, Haibo Jia

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPolyvascular disease (PVD), defined as atherosclerosis involving ≥ 2 vascular beds, confers poor clinical outcome but lacks early noninvasive predictors. This study combined multimodal imaging to explore the retinal vessel characteristics and coronary plaque morphology in patients with PVD.MethodsA total of 311 acute coronary syndrome(ACS) patients who underwent coronary angiography(CAG) with optical coherence tomography(OCT) undertook non-contrast enhanced magnetic resonance angiography(NCE-MRA). Optical coherence tomography angiography(OCTA) was performed on 177 patients among them. Finally 238 patients entered OCT analyses, whereas 172 entered OCTA analyses.ResultsOCT cohort included 199 PVD patients and 39 non- PVD patients. Compared with non-PVD group, the PVD group had older patients (60.17 ± 8.90 vs. 52.10 ± 11.73, p < 0.001), a lower proportion of men (68.3% vs. 84.6%, p = 0.041), and a higher percentage of patients with diabetes (35.2% vs. 17.9%, p = 0.035). OCT analyses showed culprit plaque of PVD group were more likely to be calcified plaques (13.6% vs. 0%, *p*< 0.05) and had a higher proportion of nodular calcifications (40.7% vs. 17.9%, p = 0.007). OCTA cohort consisted of 137 PVD patients and 35 non- PVD patients. OCTA analyses demonstrated significantly reduced vessel density across all 3 retinal capillary plexuses in PVD group. Multivariate analyses showed that vessel density of full retina fovea was independently predictive of PVD(OR: 0.883, 95% CI: 0.807–0.966, p = 0.007). ROC analysis demonstrated that full retinal vascular density fovea alone yielded an AUC of 0.667 (95% CI: 0.577–0.7556) for predicting PVD.ConclusionFull retinal vessel density can serve as a non-invasive predictor for PVD.Clinical trial registrationNCT06016608.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-05650-x.

Background

Polyvascular disease (PVD), defined as atherosclerosis involving ≥ 2 vascular beds (e.g., coronary, carotid, renal, lower extremity, upper mesenteric) [1, 2], is also termed panvascular or multisite artery disease [3]. Its prevalence has risen markedly in recent years [4], conferring substantially higher cardiovascular mortality and morbidity than the general population [5]. Classified as very high-risk [6], PVD remains however frequently underdiagnosed and undertreated [7–9]. Given shared risk factors with coronary artery disease (CAD) [9], holistic management is essential for optimizing outcomes [10]; however, early diagnosis is hindered by atypical presentations and limited awareness [1, 2].

Emerging evidence indicates that microvascular dysfunction precedes macrovascular events [11, 12]. The retina—a highly vascularized tissue whose feature is accessible via optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA)—shares angiogenic signaling pathways with coronary arteries [13, 14]and may reflect systemic endothelial health. Concurrently, coronary optical coherence tomography (OCT) and intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) enable high-resolution plaque characterization. Yet systemic microvascular dysfunction (a potential PVD marker) remains unexplored.

We hypothesize that acute coronary syndrome (ACS) patients with PVD exhibit distinct retinal microvascular alterations on OCTA and high-risk coronary plaque features on OCT/IVUS compared to ACS patients without PVD.

Methods

Study population

This retrospective single-center observational study enrolled 311 patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) who underwent coronary angiography (CAG) and OCT/IVUS or OCT between October 2023 and June 2025 at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University. These patients also consented to undergo non-contrast enhanced magnetic resonance angiography (NCE-MRA) to screen for PVD in the intracranial, carotid, and renal arteries along with OCTA. After excluding 73 cases for various reasons (poor OCT image quality, IVUS-only pullback, unsuitable lesion location, in-stent restenosis, or post-stent imaging), 238 culprit lesions remained analyzable by OCT. Due to an unforeseen termination of the equipment leasing agreement by the manufacturer in September 2024, OCTA imaging was completed in only 177 participants, with 5 excluded due to poor image quality. Figure 1 showed study flowchart. The angiographic criteria of ACS was defined as ≥ 50% diameter stenosis in at least one major epicardial vessel on CAG. Detailed definition of ACS was provided in Supplemental Materials. PVD was identified by NCE-MRA-detected stenosis ≥ 50% in at least one of the aforementioned arteries. Detailed methodology of arterial stenosis assessment was provided in Supplemental Materials. Culprit lesions were determined based on CAG, electrocardiogram, and echocardiography findings. In patients with multiple stenoses, the culprit lesion was identified as follows: lesion with the most severe stenosis or evidence of recent plaque disruption suggestive of thrombus on OCT.

CAG analyses

Two independent investigators, blinded to clinical data, performed all analyses using the Coronary Angiography Analysis System (Pie Medical Imaging BV). Culprit and non-culprit lesion distributions were qualitatively assessed. Quantitative measurements included lesion length (LL), minimal lumen diameter (MLD), reference lumen diameter (RLD), and percentage diameter stenosis (DS%). Lesions were classified as Type B2/C and non- Type B2/C, as defined in Supplemental Materials.

OCT/IVUS or OCT image acquisition and analyses

OCT was performed as needed to guide PCI. Imaging utilized an IVUS-OCT integrated system (Panovision; 304 patients) or a frequency-domain OCT system (ILUMIEN OPTIS; 7 patients). All analyses were conducted at the Intravascular Imaging and Physiology Core Laboratory using Panovision and ILUMIEN OPTIS offline software, respectively. Detailed methodology is provided in the Supplemental Materials (see also Supplemental Fig. 1 for representative images). Quality control procedures and inter- observer agreement were detailed in the Supplemental Materials.

NCE-MRA image acquisition and analyses

All imaging was performed on a 3.0T Ingenia CX system (Philips) using the following protocols: time-of-flight MRA (TOF-MRA) for intracranial arteries, T1-VISTA for carotid arteries, and balanced turbo field echo MRA (BTF-MRA) for renal arteries. Two independent analysts evaluated all datasets for stenosis, anatomical variations, developmental anomalies, and carotid lipid plaque using standardized protocols on IntelliSpace Portal software (Philips). When there was discordance between the analysts, a consensus reading was obtained from a third analyst. A representative case is shown in Fig. 2. Inter- observer agreement was detailed in the Supplemental Materials.

OCTA image acquisition and analyses

All participants were imaged with an swept source optical coherence tomography angiography(SS-OCTA)system (TowardPi Medical Technology Co., Ltd), acquiring 12 mm × 12 mm macular and 6 mm × 6 mm optic disc scans. An integrated AI algorithm automatically detected retinal/choroidal boundaries for 3D reconstruction and quantitative analysis of the retinal capillary plexuses and choroidal vessels. Following quality control and manual segmentation correction by a masked technician, parameters including vessel density (retinal/choroidal), foveal avascular zone(FAZ) metrics [area, perimeter, flow density in a 300 μm annulus region around FAZ༈FAZ300༉], and thickness (retinal/choroidal, by layer) were analyzed. Poor-quality images were excluded. Detailed parameter definitions are provided in the Supplemental Material, with a representative case in Fig. 3. Intra- observer agreement was detailed in the Supplemental Materials.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 25.0, GraphPad Prism 10.4.1 and R 4.4.1. Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies (%) and compared with χ² or Fisher’s exact test. Post-hoc analyses used adjusted standardized residuals (ASR > 1.96 indicating significance after Bonferroni correction). Continuous variables were assessed for normality with Kolmogorov–Smirnov test; normally distributed variables reported as mean ± SD (Student’s t-test or ANOVA) and non-normal as median [interquartile range(IQR)] (Mann–Whitney U or Kruskal-Wallis test). Baseline characteristics were compared across cohorts.

Variables with p < 0.1 in univariable analysis were considered for multivariable binary logistic regression. To avoid multicollinearity among correlated variables (e.g., vessel density metrics from adjacent annulus within the same retinal layer), one clinically representative indicator per cluster was selected. The OCTA predicting PVD model included two predictors and was evaluated with likelihood ratio tests and variance inflation factors (VIF) (all < 2.0).

For complete separation (e.g., calcified plaque absent in non-PVD group), Firth’s penalized regression was applied (logistf package in R). Results from both conventional logistic regression (for non-separated variables) and Firth regression (for separated variables) are collectively presented in one table. ROC analysis (AUC with 95% CI) was performed using R (for model containing separated variables) or GraphPad Prism 10.4.1(for model not containing separated variables); DeLong’s test compared correlated ROC curves. OCTA parameters were averaged between eyes. The potential implications of this averaging, including the assumption of broadly similar bilateral involvement and the reduction of inter-eye correlation, are acknowledged. Inter- observer reliability was evaluated using the kappa coefficient for categorical parameters and Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC)for continuous parameters. All tests were two-tailed with p < 0.05 considered significant.

Results

Baseline and procedural characteristics

According to results from NCE-MRA, 199 of the 238 patients had polyvascular disease (PVD), whereas 39 were not found to have PVD. A few differences of baseline characteristics were detected between PVD and non- PVD groups, including that patients in PVD group were older(60.17 ± 8.90 years vs. 52.10 ± 11.73 years; p<0.001), less likely to be male(68.3% vs. 84.6%; p = 0.041), had more prevalent DM(35.2% vs. 17.9%; p = 0.035) (Table 1). Distribution of lipid plaque in carotid artery were also different as presented in Table 1 between two groups(χ2(2) = 22.48, p < 0.001). Specifically, the PVD group exhibited a higher prevalence of both unilateral plaques (37.4% vs. 12.8%) (Adj. std. residual = + 3.0, *p* < 0.01) and bilateral plaques (26.8% vs. 10.3%) (Adj. std. residual = + 2.2, *p* < 0.05). Procedural characteristics showed no difference between two groups.

Angiographic and OCT characteristics of culprit lesions

98 patients underwent one- vessel optical coherence tomography (OCT) scanning, 87 underwent two- vessel scanning while 53 underwent three- vessel OCT imaging. Table 2 showed the characteristics of coronary angiography (CAG) and OCT in culprit lesions. Plaque types were distinguished between two groups(p = 0.022), amongst ACS patients with PVD showed higher prevalence of calcified plaque type (13.6% vs. 0%) (Adj. std. residual = + 2.4, *p* < 0.05). Moreover, PVD group also exhibited more frequent nodular calcification (40.7% vs. 17.9%, p = 0.007). The baseline characteristics and culprit lesion features were taken into univariate logistic regression and Firth logistic regression analyses to assess the independent predictors of PVD (Supplemental Table 1). All variables significant in univariate analyses (p < 0.1) in addition to clinically important factors (e.g., hypertension, BMI, smoking, LDL) were included in the multivariable model(Supplemental Table 1). At multivariate analyses, unilateral carotid lipid plaque (OR: 5.420; 95% CI: 2.032–16.89.3; p < 0.001), and bilateral carotid lipid plaque (OR: 5.698; 95% CI: 1.771–24.108; p = 0.002) were positively associated with the clinical presentation of PVD. The multivariate Firth logistic regression model incorporating the following variables [age, sex(reference: male), BMI, HTN, DM, smoking, LDL, carotid plaque status(reference: none), calcified nodule, plaque rupture(reference: others), calcified plaque(reference: lipidic plaque)] was significantly predictive of PVD. The model’s discriminatory power, as assessed by ROC analysis, showed area under the curve (AUC) of 0.837 (95% CI: 0.758–0.916), representing good discrimination(Supplemental Fig. 2a). No difference was detected in the angiographic characteristics of non-culprit lesions (Supplemental Table 2).

OCTA characteristics

NCE-MRA found 137 PVD patients and 35 non- PVD patients in the optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) cohort. Table 3 showed the eye- level comparison of retinal characteristics between PVD group and non- PVD group. Quantitative OCTA analysis revealed significant microvascular alterations in PVD patients compared to non-PVD controls. The PVD group exhibited significantly reduced FAZ300 parameters in full[37.82(32.08–41.23)% vs. 40.20(35.88–42.11)%; p = 0.024], superficial [39.01(32.69–42.22)% vs. 40.27(36.29–42.77)%; p = 0.018], and deep retina [39.46(34.49–41.75)% vs. 40.82(38.70–43.34.70.34)%; p < 0.001]. Concurrently, increased retinal central foveal thickness was observed in PVD patients (257.40 ± 28.57 μm vs. 249.49 ± 18.14 μm; p = 0.005), as well as elevated full retinal thickness(302.96 ± 25.56 μm vs. 295.30 ± 22.48 μm, p = 0.024). Retinal nerve fiber layer(RNFL) perfusion was compromised in the PVD group, with significantly lower mean blood flow [47.61(46.34–48.41)% vs. 47.99༈47.31–48.58༉; p = 0.038]. Vessel density analysis using the ETDRS grid demonstrated reduced perfusion across all retinal vascular layers in PVD patients as shown in Table 3. No significant intergroup differences were observed in superficial retinal thickness, deep retinal thickness, choroidal thickness, or choroidal vessel density. These null findings are detailed in Supplemental Table 3. The OCTA characteristics were taken into univariate logistic regression to assess the independent predictors of PVD (Supplemental Table 4). All variables significant in univariate analyses (p < 0.1) in addition to clinically relevant factors (e.g., hypertension, BMI, smoking, LDL) were included in the multivariable model(Supplemental Table 4). At multivariate analyses, full retinal vessel density fovea, (OR: 0.883; 95% CI: 0.807–0.966; p = 0.007), was negatively associated with the clinical presentation of PVD. In the assessment of diagnostic performance for predicting PVD, the full retinal vessel density fovea alone demonstrated an AUC of 0.667(95% CI: 0.577–0.756)(Fig. 4a). A multivariable model incorporating this parameter with established clinical risk factors (e.g., age, HTN, smoking and DM) showed significantly improved discriminatory power, with an AUC of 0.841(95% CI: 0.775–0.907) (Fig. 4b). This represented a significant incremental value over a model containing only the clinical risk factors (AUC = 0.778, 95% CI: 0.692–0.865), with a difference in AUC of 0.063 (DeLong’s test p = 0.035).

Discussion

Key findings

This is the first multi-modality imaging study looking into features of ACS with PVD, revealing that PVD group had more calcified plaque and nodular calcification, reduced retinal vessel density, more prevalent unilateral and bilateral lipid plaque in carotid artery. Full retinal vessel density fovea was able to independently predict PVD.

Clinical factors and PVD

The prevalence of PVD (83.6%) in our cohort is higher than in general population and previous trials. It stems from our study design: enrollment of ACS patients (inherently high-risk with established coronary disease) combined with systematic NCE-MRA screening of carotid, intracranial, and renal territories. This imaging-based ascertainment uncovered extensive subclinical disease—consistent with literature demonstrating that symptom-driven diagnosis significantly underestimates true PVD burden [7–9]. This finding empirically validates PVD’s frequent underdiagnosis and underscores the clinical imperative for proactive multisite vascular assessment in ACS.

Compared to non-PVD patients, those with PVD were older, had higher rates of diabetes, and more frequent unilateral/bilateral carotid lipid-rich plaques. The link with age aligns with evidence that aging promotes multi-territory atherosclerosis [15]. The pronounced diabetic burden is consistent with its known role in endothelial dysfunction and plaque progression [16, 17].

Notably, the higher proportion of women with PVD may seem contrary to population studies showing minimal sex differences [18]. This discrepancy likely arises because our study included only ACS patients—a high-risk group in which women may lose typical cardiovascular protection due to risk factor accumulation, sex-specific conditions (e.g., autoimmune disease, adverse pregnancy outcomes), and management disparities [19]. This pattern mirrors the elevated CAD risk among women with diabetes, potentially reflecting shared pathways such as endothelial inflammation and microvascular dysfunction [20]. These findings emphasize the need for tailored secondary prevention in high-risk women, including systematic screening for PVD and earlier aggressive therapy, as also highlighted by Cho et al [21].

Furthermore, PVD patients showed significantly more bilateral carotid plaque, indicating systemic atherosclerosis [22]. Bilateral involvement suggests advanced disease and underscores the need for comprehensive vascular assessment in high-risk patients to improve risk stratification and guide intensive strategies.

OCT and PVD

OCT analysis revealed that PVD patients exhibited a calcification-predominant phenotype at culprit lesions, with significantly higher rates of calcified plaques and nodular calcification, but fewer lipid plaques. We therefore interpret this phenotype as a marker of advanced, chronic systemic atherosclerotic burden rather than acute plaque instability—a distinction critical for contextualizing coronary findings within the broader pathophysiology of polyvascular disease. This pattern aligns with coronary artery calcium (CAC) scores as an established predictor of panvascular risk [23]. The prominence of nodular calcification suggests accelerated calcific transformation, supporting its link to adverse cardiovascular outcomes as demonstrated in broader populations [24]. These OCT-based calcification features may reflect underlying systemic vulnerability and represent in situ biomarkers of progressive calcific atherosclerosis in PVD.

OCTA and PVD

In clinical practice, reduced retinal vessel density could serve as an accessible, non-invasive indicator prompting targeted multi-territory evaluation (e.g., carotid ultrasound, ABI) in ACS patients, particularly when coronary imaging reveals highly calcified features. This approach leverages OCTA’s practical advantages (rapid, radiation-free, low-cost) while acknowledging its role as an adjunct—not a replacement—for established vascular assessments.

The reduced vessel density, particularly in the metabolically active DCP susceptible to ischemia [25–28], suggests generalized microvascular rarefaction and impaired perfusion, mirroring the endothelial dysfunction underlying panvascular disease. These findings align with Zhang et al. [26], Cao et al. [29] and Li et al. [30], who identified parafoveal vessel density loss as a systemic vascular risk marker. Klein et al. [31] also linked retinal arteriolar abnormalities (assessed by fundus photography) to atherosclerotic diseases, corroborating our findings. Mihály et al. [32] further reported that a compromised Circle of Willis reduced retinal vessel density in carotid disease, consistent with our results.

FAZ enlargement and morphological changes further indicate capillary integrity loss and remodeling from chronic hypoperfusion and inflammatory injury [25, 33], aligning with Duan et al. [34] who correlated FAZ changes with ischemic stroke severity.

When integrated with coronary OCT/IVUS, retinal microvascular metrics provide incremental pathophysiological insight—linking systemic microvascular dysfunction to focal macrovascular vulnerability. This ‘micro-macro vascular integration’ enhances holistic risk assessment in ACS patients, aligning with current guidelines advocating comprehensive vascular evaluation in high-risk cohorts. A non-invasive OCTA scan can quantify systemic vascular burden, reflecting the recognition that microvascular disease drives macrovascular events [35–37]. As an embryological extension of cerebral circulation [38], the retina provides a unique observable microvascular network; its changes likely parallel damage in heart, brain, and kidneys, explaining its link to multi-territory atherosclerosis. This pattern, consistent with Wang et al. [39] reporting vessel density decrease in CAD, suggests retinal vessel density assessment could improve risk stratification to identify high-risk patients with subclinical PVD for aggressive prevention. Prospective validation is needed to define quantitative thresholds and confirm whether adding retinal metrics to coronary imaging significantly improves prediction of polyvascular involvement or hard cardiovascular outcomes beyond conventional risk stratification.

While the combined OCT–OCTA analysis was exploratory and conducted in a selected subgroup with available multimodal imaging, its robust discriminatory performance provides a compelling proof-of-concept for integrated microvascular–macrovascular phenotyping in polyvascular disease. These preliminary findings warrant prospective validation in larger, independent cohorts to confirm clinical utility—without diminishing the novelty and pathophysiological insight offered by this multimodal approach.

Limitations

This study has several important limitations. First, its retrospective and observational design inherently introduces unmeasured confounding and selection bias. Second, as a single-center experience, the generalizability may be limited. Third, due to circumstances beyond control, only part of the patients completed OCTA. Nevertheless, OCTA availability is independent of patient characteristics which means missing was completely at random. This brought in a potential limitation that the independent predictor from the OCTA subset and the ROC analysis derived from the OCT- OCTA combined cohort may not be directly generalizable to the entire study population. However baseline characteristics were comparable across successive cohorts (initial n = 311; OCT n = 238; OCTA n = 177; combined n = 106; Supplemental Table 5), minimizing selection bias. Fourth, the systematic assessment of non-culprit lesions was limited, as 3-vessel OCT was acquired in a small, non-random portion of the population. This was mostly due to clinical constraints and safety considerations. Therefore, our analysis is necessarily confined to the culprit lesion and cannot speak to the pan-coronary plaque burden or the vulnerability of non-culprit lesions. Fifth, a significant limitation of this study lies in the anatomical coverage of the NCE-MRA by focusing solely on the intracranial, carotid, and renal arteries. This incomplete assessment introduces a potential for misclassification bias. Thus, our results and conclusions are specifically relevant to the cohort of patients with ACS who also have evidence of atherosclerosis in one or more of these three specific vascular beds.

Conclusion

OCTA and OCT provide a multimodal assessment of PVD’s microvascular and plaque features, meriting prospective validation. Our study concludes that reduced retinal vessel density on OCTA is a robust, independent predictor of PVD in ACS patients. Integrating this non-invasive biomarker with structural OCT and clinical factors significantly improves risk stratification for extensive atherosclerosis, positioning the eye as a window to systemic vasculopathy. This rapid, accessible tool can guide diagnostics and therapy, warranting future large-scale studies to validate its role in panvascular risk assessment.

Patients presenting with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) at hospitals without cardiac catheterization capabilities face challenges in timely transfer to percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI)–capable centers. A longer door-in-door-out (DIDO) time, defined as the interval from patient arrival at a non-PCI facility to departure for a PCI-capable center, is associated with worse outcomes, including increased mortality, infarct size, and hospital costs.1,2 The American Heart Association (AHA) guidelines recommend a DIDO time of ≤30 minutes for patients with STEMI, but national performance remains suboptimal, with only 10%–14% of hospitals meeting this target.3,4Take-Home Message•The STEMI Autolaunch process significantly improved DIDO times. Project Rationale

At Harris Health, a large, community-focused health care system in Texas serving all residents of Harris County, only 1 of 2 busy (>80,000 emergency visits/y) acute care hospitals has PCI capability. No patient with STEMI from the referring center met the ≤30-minute DIDO goal before 2022. To address this, we implemented a quality improvement (QI) initiative focused on rapid transfer and transport protocols, including an “Autolaunch” process for selected stable patients with STEMI. Subsequently, we evaluated the impact on DIDO times from the referring hospital.

Project Description

The primary QI interventions included the development of the Autolaunch process and its criteria, education of staff and faculty regarding this framework, training and simulation to improve door-to-electrocardiography (ECG) and ECG-to-departure times for all indicated presenting patients, STEMI-specific guidelines for rapid transport by emergency medical services (EMS), and feedback on STEMI performance to all teams involved. Education was provided to all staff who participate in the management of patients with STEMI in the referring emergency department, including triage nurses, emergency department technicians, EMS personnel, emergency physicians, and advanced practice providers. The STEMI Autolaunch process was fully implemented in 2023 after the other QI intervention components in order to optimize existing practices before a novel pathway was introduced.

The Autolaunch process was designed to expedite transfer for patients with “unequivocal” uncomplicated STEMI. This was defined by the following inclusion criteria: symptoms concerning for acute coronary syndrome with >2 mm ST-segment elevation in 2 or more contiguous leads (excluding aVR and left bundle branch blocks) with reciprocal depressions on ECG. The exclusion criteria included any obvious medical contraindication to direct catheterization laboratory transfer, such as respiratory failure or cardiovascular instability, which required immediate resuscitation before transfer. The protocol for transfer followed structured guidelines to prioritize rapid EMS activation and transport, with minimal interventions at the referring hospital and simultaneous notification to the receiving hospital's catheterization laboratory team. The primary time-saving process change with the Autolaunch protocol arose from the parallel processing of critical STEMI transfer tasks from the referring facility, arrangement of EMS transport, and notification of clinicians and staff at the receiving hospital (Figure 1). Project Deliverables

Data from 2021 to 2024 were extracted from the AHA “Get with the Guidelines” database to compare DIDO times before and after implementation using the Kruskal-Wallis test. DIDO times were also compared between Autolaunch cases and non-Autolaunch cases using the Wilcoxon rank sum test. Statistical analysis was performed using Stata 16.1.

Project Outcome

In the baseline period (pre-QI intervention), 31 patients with STEMI were identified at the referring hospital, with a median DIDO time of 81 minutes (interquartile range [IQR]: 48-267 minutes) and no cases meeting the ≤30-minute goal. In 2022, after initial QI efforts but before the implementation of Autolaunch, 39 patients with STEMI were treated, with a median DIDO time of 48 minutes (IQR: 35-66 minutes); 12.8% of cases (5/39) met the 30-minute goal. After the introduction of the Autolaunch process in 2023, 86 patients with STEMI were treated, with a median DIDO time of 41 minutes (IQR: 26-68 minutes). The ≤30-minute goal was met in 34 of these patients (39.5%). Notably, in the 35 Autolaunch patients, the median DIDO time was 26 minutes (IQR: 22-31 minutes), with 26/35 (74.3%) having a DIDO time ≤30 minutes (Figure 2). The overall false-positive rate, as determined by clean coronary arteries on angiography, was 5.7% (2/35) in the Autolaunch cohort. Discussion

Our study demonstrates the impact of a protocolized, rapid transfer process on DIDO times for patients with STEMI (Figure 3). The protocolized Autolaunch process substantially increased the proportion of patients achieving the DIDO time goals and has the potential to significantly improve patient outcomes while outperforming traditional interventions based on QI education alone. Our results suggest that this intervention could serve as a model for other similar hospital systems aiming to meet the AHA DIDO time goal of ≤30 minutes. Further research is needed to assess the long-term outcomes of this intervention and its applicability to other health care settings.

Conclusion

A similar process for rapid protocolized transfer could be implemented by referring hospitals to significantly improve the proportion of cases in which goal DIDO times are met.

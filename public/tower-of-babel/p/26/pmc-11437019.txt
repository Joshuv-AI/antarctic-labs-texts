Distinction of mental health between salesman and R&D in high-tech enterprise: a fNIRS study
Authors: Qingming Liu, Huimin Cui, Panpan Yuan, Jiaojiao Liu, Ye Jin, Lirong Xia, Peiwen Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Employees in enterprises face numerous challenges due to the severe employment situation and job pressure. Sales personnel and research and development (R&D) personnel in companies are particularly susceptible to mental health issues due to the unique nature and complexity of their work. This study aims to explore the psychological health issues among sales personnel and R&D personnel in high-tech enterprises. Twenty-four R&D personnel (23 males and 1 female) and twenty-six sales personnel (25 males and 1 female) were recruited. The participants’ psychological health conditions were assessed using the Symptom Checklist-90 (SCL-90) and verbal fluency tasks combined with functional near-infrared spectroscopy (VFT-fNIRS), while their work stress level was evaluated using the Personal Stress Questionnaire (PSQ) and Personal Resources Questionnaire (PRQ). The results indicated significantly higher brain activities in the right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and bilateral Broca’s areas of the R&D group compared to the sales group. The R&D group had significantly lower scores in the total score and multiple dimensions of SCL-90, such as somatization, obsession-compulsion, depression, anxiety, hostility, and paranoia. In addition, the R&D group exhibited significantly lower psychological and physical tension reactions compared to the sales group in PSQ. Based on the PRQ, the R&D group obtained significantly higher scores in self-care compared to the sales group. In conclusion, the mental health status of the R&D group is better than that of the sales group, the difference in self-care may be the most important factor leading to the difference in work stress and mental health of the two groups, VFT-fNIRS might be used to assist in detecting the mental health status of employees.

Introduction

The International Labour Organization (ILO) released data in January 2021 indicating that due to the COVID-19 pandemic, the global number of unemployed individuals rose by 33 million in 2020, resulting in an unemployment rate of 6.5%1. Employees are confronted with challenging circumstances such as job insecurity and intense competition, requiring them to navigate multiple aspects of their work environment. If an employee’s personal resources and coping mechanisms are insufficient to fulfill their work-related psychological and social needs, which encompass factors like job content, work intensity, and the social environment, they will experience stress2. Gradually, these pressures can have an impact on the mental well-being of employees. Extensive data from global surveys conducted in high-, middle-, and low-income countries reveals that 29.2% of ordinary employees have encountered or are presently dealing with mental health concerns, including anxiety and depression. Moreover, the annual occurrence rate of new mental problems is estimated to be approximately 17.6%3. Prevalent mental health issues among employees are extensive. These psychological challenges impact both the physical and mental well-being of individuals, and they also result in substantial social costs. According to Birnbaum et al.4, a notable correlation exists between depression and reduced productivity. A systematic review has investigated the correlation between workforce productivity and mental health, highlighting the pronounced impact of mental health disorders, particularly depression, on individual work productivity. The study suggests that proper treatment and supportive workplace environments can enhance employee health and mitigate economic losses attributed to mental health issues5. Additionally, a separate systematic review and meta-analysis examined factors linked to the work performance and mental health of healthcare workers amidst pandemics. This research underscores the necessity of mental health provisions for healthcare workers during pandemics and advocates for cultivating a workplace culture that prioritizes psychological safety and well-being. Concurrently, it is suggested that bolstering work performance involves implementing preventive strategies and pinpointing risk factors associated with work performance and mental health6. Presently, there exists an absence of technological methods for assessing employees’ mental health status. Traditionally, evaluations of occupational stress and mental well-being have predominantly depended on questionnaire-based approaches7,8. Nonetheless, questionnaire assessments exhibit subjectivity and are inadequate for appraising potential cognitive impairments arising from mental health conditions. Recently, the utilization of neuroscience technology has experienced growing prevalence. Through the acquisition and analysis of neural signals in the brain using neuroscience technology, it becomes feasible to investigate individuals’ neural reactions to stress and conduct an objective assessment of employees’ mental health.

Functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) serves as a non-invasive and objective method to evaluate cognitive brain function, finding broad application in clinical psychology. Within clinical contexts, researchers analyze brain activity patterns triggered by verbal fluency tasks (VFT) and subsequently gauge participants’ mental well-being using these patterns9. Earlier investigations have indicated the activation of the superior medial frontal cortex, ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (vlPFC), and anterior temporal lobe in healthy individuals during VFT10–12. Research outcomes examining alterations in brain activity through fNIRS technology during VFT also suggest the presence of cortical hemodynamic responses across diverse regions of the frontal, temporal, and parietal cortices13. In individuals without abnormalities, oxyhemoglobin (oxy-Hb) concentration exhibits a swift rise at task onset, sustains heightened activity throughout the task, and subsequently diminishes gradually post-task14. Compared to individuals displaying sound mental health, those with anxious tendencies manifest attenuated fluctuations in oxy-Hb concentration within the vlPFC during verbal fluency tasks15. Individuals prone to depression demonstrate diminished oxy-Hb levels in the bilateral frontal lobes16, while those with obsessive tendencies exhibit decreased engagement in the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), bilateral orbitofrontal cortex (OFC), and Broca area, among additional cortical zones17. Those with emotional problems display diminished cortical activity and inferior task performance in VFT18. VFT-fNIRS proves to be an effective and objective instrument for appraising mental health concerns. The VFT serves as a tool for evaluating mental health concerns, and stress is identified as a contributing factor to mental health issues.

Personnel in sales and R&D roles within companies exhibit a heightened susceptibility to mental health challenges owing to the intricate and distinctive demands of their roles19,20. The mental well-being of company employees is closely intertwined with stress originating from work-related factors21. The German Joint Occupational Safety and Health Strategy (GDA) enumerates five primary stress factors discerned within workplaces: job content and tasks, work organization, social relationships, work environment, and emerging work modalities22. Differing from personnel in other roles, sales staff engage not only with internal company members but also foster relationships with external factors like clients23. Sales and R&D departments both require ongoing innovation to meet market demands and sustain competitiveness. They also need to comprehend customer needs to guarantee that products or services meet customer expectations. Additionally, collaboration with other departments is essential to achieve shared goals and missions. The differences between the two lie in sales being primarily directed at achieving sales targets and market share, whereas R&D is centered on innovation and technological breakthroughs. Sales typically emphasizes short-term performance, while R&D necessitates long-term investment and continual innovation24. In addition, sales necessitate communication, negotiation, and interpersonal skills, whereas R&D personnel primarily focus on specific development and innovation tasks. We hypothesize that managing complex interpersonal relationships and engaging in pure development and innovation work both entail significant work pressure, potentially leading to psychological issues. However, the stressors in these two types of work differ, and the impact on mental health remains unclear. Consequently, these factors influence lifestyle habits encompassing sleep and diet. In addition to engendering diverse stress coping mechanisms, these factors may potentially impact stress perception and consequently contribute to divergent mental health states.

While VFT-fNIRS finds broad application within psychopathology, its utilization within management psychology remains deficient. This study primarily examines the mental well-being of sales and R&D staff in high-tech corporations. The Symptom Checklist-90 (SCL-90) and VFT-fNIRS are used to assess the mental health status of the participants. In line with previous studies, the Personal Stress Questionnaire (PSQ) and the Personal Resources Questionnaire (PRQ) of are used to assess the work-related stress level of the participants25,26. We propose the following hypotheses for this study: (1) Variations in mental health conditions exist between the R&D and sales groups, the R&D group had better mental status and stronger brain activation in VFT. (2) The work-related stress in employees exhibits a significant positive correlation with mental health concerns, the score of SCL-90 and PSQ were positively correlated. The study focuses on the mental health challenges faced by sales and R&D staff within high-tech firms, an understudied domain. Utilizing a suite of assessment tools—including the SCL-90, VFT-fNIRS, PSQ, and PRQ—the study thoroughly evaluates the psychological well-being and stress levels among the participants. Furthermore, it aspires to establish a scientific foundation for the development of targeted mental health interventions and enhanced workplace health management strategies.

Materials and methods

Participants and experimental protocol

Participants

This study aimed to investigate the mental health status of employees within high-tech enterprises. Fifty-two participants were randomly recruited from a healthcare-focused high-tech enterprise in Wuhan, China, comprising 26 individuals in the research and development (R&D) group and 26 in the sales group. Owing to insufficient data quality (refer to "Data pre-processing" section for specifics), two participants from R&D group were excluded from subsequent data analysis, yielding a total of fifty participants. Specifically, twenty-four participants (23 males and 1 female, mean age: 32.21 ± 6.04) engaged in product research and development were regarded as the R&D group. The remaining twenty-six (25 males and 1 female, mean age: 33.77 ± 6.12) engaged in product sales were considered the sales group. To mitigate the influence of job transitions, all enrolled participants were mandated to maintain their current positions for over a year27. Furthermore, all participants were right-handed and possessed no history of neurological disorders, including brain injuries. Experimental protocol

Before commencing the experiment, we employed SCL-9028 to assess the mental health status of the participants. The assessment of participants’ work stress levels utilized the revised edition of the Occupational Stress Inventory-Revised (OSI-R)29. The OSI-R comprises three questionnaires, which are occupational task questionnaire (OTQ), personal strain questionnaire (PSQ) and personal resources questionnaire (PRQ). The OTQ evaluates workplace stressors, encompassing task variety, workload volume, and the urgency of work demands. It facilitates an understanding of the occupational stress encountered by individuals, allowing for an analysis of stress’s impact on health and job performance. The PSQ examines psychological and physiological responses to occupational stress, including anxiety, depression, and physical reactions, assessing an individual’s adaptability and coping mechanisms in the face of work stress and their effects on well-being and performance. In addition, the PRQ concentrates on assessing coping resources and strategies, such as social support, self-regulation, and problem-solving skills, to determine how effectively individuals manage work stress and the efficacy of these strategies in reducing job tension and improving performance. This study focuses on stress and resources at the individual level rather than organizational issues, hence the selection of the PSQ and PRQ scales.

In the process of experiment, participants were instructed to sit comfortably in a quiet room and engage in VFT-fNIRS. As a language cognitive processing paradigm, studies have found that VFT-induced brain activity in prefrontal cortex were decreased for participants in a nonclinical population, such as individuals with a high degree of affective symptoms25 and informal Parkinson’s disease caregiver with higher levels of anxiety26. This study used the revised Chinese version VFT30–32. Mental health states such as anxiety, attentional focus, or emotional responses can manifest relatively quickly and exhibit changes within minutes. FNIRS, which measures changes in hemodynamic responses related to neural activity, can capture these dynamic changes even in short durations. Many fNIRS studies employ tasks or stimuli that elicit specific cognitive or emotional responses. The hemodynamic changes measured during these tasks can be robust and informative even within a brief recording period. For example, tasks designed to induce stress or emotional arousal can produce measurable fNIRS responses within minutes. In addition, a significant amount of fNIRS research has utilized this experimental paradigm, demonstrating the effectiveness of the protocol33–35. VFT encompassed three phases: a 30 s pre-task baseline, a 60 s task period, and a subsequent 60 s post-task baseline. During the pre-task baseline, participants were instructed to audibly count from 1 to 30, and during the post-task baseline period, they were asked to count from 1 to 60. Throughout the task period, participants were tasked with generating as many Chinese phrases as possible using four characters, such as “天” (‘sky’), “白” (‘white’), “树” (‘tree’), and “花” (‘flower’). The set of four characters changed every 15 s during the task period to minimize periods of silence among the participants. Prior to the experiment, all participants were provided with a comprehensive explanation of the experimental procedure and subsequently provided written informed consent. Experimental protocol was approved by the Ethic Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Shaoxing University. All methods were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

NIRS measurement

A 53 multi-channel instrument (BS-3000, Wuhan Znion Medical Technology Co., Wuhan, China) was utilized to assess relative concentration changes of hemoglobin in the prefrontal cortex. The sampling rate was 20 Hz, and the wavelengths of near-infrared light were 690 and 830 nm. Every channel is composed of a source-detector pair positioned 3 cm apart, and were placed with reference to the 10–20 system (Source 9 is located at FPz.). Sixteen sources and 16 detectors constitute a total of 53 channels.

To standardize the fNIRS channels, a 3D digitizer (NirMap, Wuhan Znion Medical Technology Co., Wuhan, China) was utilized to record precise spatial coordinates of 4 reference points (Nz, Cz, AL, and RL) and 32 probes (16 sources and 16 detectors). Subsequently, the 53 channels were transformed into an estimated Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space36 using NIRS-SPM37. Based on the Broadmann probabilistic atlas, all 53 channels were then categorized into the following five cortical regions: Pre-motor and supplementary motor area (PreM & SMA), frontal eye field (FEF), Broca’s area (Broca), DLPFC and frontal pole area (FPA) (See detail as Fig. 1A and supplementary Table 1). Since numerous VFT-fNIRS studies38 did not analysis the activity of PreM & SMA and FEF, these regions were excluded from the region of interest (ROI) level analysis. Additionally, three channels (25, 28, and 29) situated along the mid-hemispheric line were excluded. Data analysis

Data pre-processing

Prior to data processing, we calculated coefficient of variation (CV) values across all data channels to ensure signal quality39. Channels with CV values exceeding 15% were classified as “bad”. Participants were excluded if the count of bad channels surpassed 30% of the total. In this study, data from two participants were excluded. fNIRS data were pre-processed previous researches40–43. Initially, raw optical intensity underwent conversion to optical density (OD) through the utilization of a MATLAB-based toolkit, Homer244. Subsequently, alterations in the relative concentrations of oxygenated hemoglobin (oxy-Hb), deoxygenated hemoglobin (deoxy-Hb), and total hemoglobin (total-Hb) were computed in accordance with the modified Beer-Lambert Law45. Oxy-Hb was chosen as the primary indicator for our subsequent analysis due to its typically superior signal-to-noise ratio and stronger correlation with cerebral blood flow in comparison to Deoxy-Hb46,47. Thirdly, a moving average technique with a 5 s window width was employed to eliminate short-time motion artifacts and periodic physiological noise, including respiration and heart rate. Next, we extracted the 125 s oxy-Hb signal to facilitate further analysis, encompassing the final 10 s of the pre-task period, a 60 s task period, and the initial 55 s of the post-task period. Specifically, the 10 s of the pre-task period represented the baseline of brain activity before performing the task. The 60 s task period represented the brain response to task execution. The 55 s of the post-task period represented brain activity gradually returned to baseline state again. Finally, the signal underwent normalization via linear fitting between a 10 s baseline at the conclusion of the pre-task period and a 5-s baseline at the culmination of the post-task period.

General linear model analysis

Task-evoked activation during the VFT was examined using a general linear model (GLM) approach48–50. GLM was performed by using a MATLAB-based toolbox, NIRS-KIT51. The experimental design matrix comprised a constant term and a task regressor. The task regressor was constructed through the convolution of a boxcar function (corresponding to the VFT task onsets) with the canonical hemodynamic response function (HRF). Within the GLM model, the explained variable was the fNIRS signal for each channel. Following GLM estimation, task β-values reflecting the task-evoked activation were derived and subsequently employed in group-level statistical analyses.

ROI-level feature extraction

Given that previous studies on repeated NIRS measures have shown acceptable reliability of the NIRS signal at the cluster (ROI) levels, but unsatisfactory retest reliability at the single-channel levels52, we opted for ROI-level analysis in this study, we opted for ROI-level analysis. Within this study, we regarded the integrate value over a 60 s task period53,54 as the ROI-level feature, reflecting the extent of task-evoked activity. Initially, the integrated value was computed for each channel. Subsequently, the average value of all channels within a given ROI was computed to serve as the ROI indicator. It’s important to note that any channel deemed as a “bad channel” (with a CV value exceeding 15%, as elaborated in "Data pre-processing" section) would be omitted from the ROI calculation. Based on this, we extracted the integrated values from six ROIs: left and right Broca, left and right DLPFC, and left and right FPA (See detailed ROI description in "NIRS measurement" section).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 22). Group differences in terms of age and educational level were assessed using the two-sample t-test. Gender differences were quantified statistically using the chi-square test. Group differences in task performance (measured by the number of reported phrases during the task), SCL-90 scores, and OSI-R scores were calculated using the two-sample t-test.

One-sample t-tests were conducted on task β-values to assess group-level task-evoked activation within each group and channel. Group difference in activation was calculated by two sample T-test. For the integrated values at the ROI level, two-sample t-tests were conducted for each ROI. The statistical tests were corrected for multiple comparisons using Benjamini–Hochberg correction55 to control the false discovery rate (FDR).

To elucidate the connection between employees’ mental status and work-related stress, we computed Pearson’s correlation coefficient between SCL-90 and OSI-R scores. Subsequently, we investigated the associations between fNIRS feature measures (integrated values) and both mental status (SCL-90) and work-related stress (OSI-R). This correlation analysis was exclusively conducted on ROIs exhibiting significant between-group differences, specifically left Broca, right Broca, and right DLPFC (See details in "Mental health status" section).

To better characterize the differences between groups, effect sizes were also assessed. The effect size is phicoefficient (Phi) for the chi-square test, cohen’s d (d) for the independent samples t-test, and R square (R2) for the Pearson correlation.

Work stress

Regarding the assessment of work-related stress using the OSI-R, the sales group exhibited elevated PSQ scores, encompassing the total PSQ score (t = − 2.96, p < 0.05, d = − 0.65), PSY subscale (t = − 2.60, p < 0.01, d = − 0.73), and PHS subscale (t = − 4.19, p < 0.01, d = − 1.18), in comparison to the R&D group. Relationship between mental health status and work stress

Correlation between SCL-90 and OSI-R

To delve deeper into the connection between employees’ mental status and work-related stress, we calculated the Pearson’s correlation between SCL-90 and OSI-R, and the outcomes are presented in Table 2. Discussion

This study employed fNIRS technology in conjunction with VFT to investigate the mental health status of employees in high-tech companies. We randomly sampled the subjects from sales and R&D staff, which were representative. In theory, no disparity in stress levels exists between the two cohorts; however, our study reveals a variance in psychological stress, potentially attributed to occupational characteristics. Additionally, a succinct analysis was provided to elucidate the factors contributing to these disparities. Firstly, questionnaires were administered to evaluate the mental health status and work-related pressure experienced by both the R&D and sales groups. Subsequently, fNIRS was employed to provide an objective measurement of individuals’ mental health status while they performed the VFT, using brain activity as the metric. The findings indicated a significant elevation in the activities of the right DLPFC and bilateral Broca’s area within the R&D group, as compared to the sales group. In addition, scores on both the overall total and various dimensions of SCL-90, encompassing somatization, obsession-compulsion, depression, anxiety, hostility, and paranoia, demonstrated a notable decrease within the R&D group in comparison to the sales group. These findings indicated that there were significant differences in mental health status between R&D and sales domains, with superior mental well-being evident in the R&D group. The OSI-R revealed significantly lower scores in psychological and physiological stress reactions within the R&D group as contrasted with the sales group. These findings underscore distinctions in work-related stress levels amid the two cohorts and underscore the intricate nexus linking work stress and mental well-being. The PRQ evidenced noteworthy intergroup dissimilarities exclusively within the self-care dimension, intimating that variations in self-care could potentially account for the disparities in work stress and mental health statuses between the two collectives.

The SCL-90 scores revealed that the sales group scored significantly higher than the R&D group in total score, as well as in somatization, obsession, depression, anxiety, hostility and paranoid. This suggests that the psychological health status of the R&D group is superior. Concurrently, the fNIRS data analysis demonstrated that individuals in the R&D group exhibited elevated concentrations of Oxy-Hb in the brain cortex and more pronounced activation during the VFT process. The results go exactly to the idea that healthy mental state of individual is positively correlated with the brain activation in VFT. The comparison between the sales and R&D groups revealed significant differences in both SCL-90 scores and patterns of brain activation. The mean difference between the groups demonstrated a substantial effect size, reflecting a close relationship between the two variables. In investigations of individuals with anxiety, Monk et al. (2006) identified a negative correlation between the activation of the ventral lateral prefrontal cortex and the severity of anxiety56. Noda et al.57 observed a negative correlation between the activation of the frontal–temporal lobe during VFT and the severity of symptoms in individuals exhibiting depressive tendencies. Sato et al.58 reported comparable findings in their investigation of verbal working memory tasks, where PFC activity exhibited a negative correlation with depressive mood. Negative emotions, such as depression and anxiety, have the potential to impact brain activity. Prior research has demonstrated that emotion regulation can assist sales professionals in mitigating interpersonal conflicts, reducing perceived stress, fostering a more professional work environment, and enhancing work performance59. Consequently, managers should prioritize monitoring employees’ psychological well-being, fostering a safe and supportive work environment, and implementing organizational improvements. These may include assisting employees in emotional regulation through training, providing clear role assignments when assigning tasks, and minimizing time pressure to enhance work performance60. Tailored management strategies can be employed based on employees’ specific positions. The SCL-90 scores revealed disparities in psychological health status between individuals in R&D roles and those in sales positions. Furthermore, we identified correlations between scale scores and various indicators assessed using fNIRS. Within the SCL-90, the left Broca integral value and right DLPFC integral value exhibited a significant negative correlation with the hostility score. This finding is consistent with previous studies indicating that greater severity of mental health issues correlates with reduced activation in specific brain areas56–58. A negative correlation was observed between the integrated value of the right DLPFC and the PSY score, suggesting potentially reduced activity in this brain region among individuals experiencing higher mental stress. The finding aligns with prior research61. It should be noted that we only found a correlation between hostility and VFT-induced activity in specific frontal brain regions, and did not find any other significant differences in mental health dimensions (such as depression, anxiety) correlated with VFT-induced effects in specific brain regions, which may be related to the smaller number of participants we selected. Furthermore, we only found a correlation with the PSY and specific brain regions. This is because we focused only on two subscales of the OSI-R; it is unclear whether other dimensions of the OSI-R are related to VFT-induced brain activity. Therefore, our research results can only suggest that VFT-fNIRS may reflect specific dimensions of work stress and mental health, and further studies are required for a broader range of other dimensions. In summary, VFT-fNIRS reflects certain specific dimensions of work stress and mental health and has the potential to be one of the tools for assessing specific aspects of mental health and work stress.

The research findings demonstrate that inter-group disparities in work-related stress align with distinctions in mental health. Scores on various dimensions of the SCL-90 exhibit a significant positive correlation with scores from the OSI-R stress reaction questionnaire. This underscores a notable positive association between employees’ psychological states and occupational stress. Variations in mental health levels among employees in technology firms may primarily stem from disparities in work-related stress. Work-related stress might impact both the physical and mental well-being of employees. Previous study demonstrated that active coping strategies aimed at mitigating stress and enhancing working conditions can effectively alleviate job burnout and enhance the overall quality of life for employees62. Within the PRQ questionnaire, only the self-care dimension exhibited a significant distinction, with the R&D group scoring notably higher than the sales group. This suggests that variations in work-related stress between R&D and sales personnel may primarily result from differing self-care practices. We speculate that in comparison to R&D personnel, sales staff contend with intricate interpersonal dynamics, possess a lower level of self-care awareness, and grapple with dietary and lifestyle challenges due to prolonged social engagements and frequent business trips. Conversely, R&D personnel maintain more regular dietary and lifestyle routines. Prior study indicated that adopting a healthy lifestyle can bolster the capacity of sales personnel to efficiently manage work-related stress60. There is research looked at how dietary habits and lifestyle factors vary by occupational position, which could provide insights into differences between R&D and sales personnel63. One study investigated how occupational stress can influence dietary and exercise habits, which might differ between R&D and sales roles due to varying levels of stress and work demands64. The cultivation of healthy lifestyle habits and diets might be given more attention by sales personnel. The influence of lifestyle habits on mental health may be a sensitive factor contributing to the differences in mental states observed in VFT-fNIRS outcomes.

Regarding the study’s limitations, it should be noted that only 50 participants were recruited, comprising 24 individuals in the R&D group and 26 in the sales group. The sample size was relatively modest and participants were selected from only one company, thus necessitating future research endeavors to augment the sample size and delve into the mental health status of employees across various regions and industries. The study exclusively concentrated on R&D and sales roles within high-tech corporations. However, there exists a legitimate imperative for subsequent investigations to explore the mental health status of personnel in diverse departments across these enterprises. Moreover, the utilization of the SCL-90, PSQ, and PRQ in probing employees’ mental health status and work-related stress introduces subjective elements, given that participants’ responses might be subject to biases stemming from social desirability. Notwithstanding the application of fNIRS in this investigation to furnish a more objective quantification of participants’ mental states, it remains pivotal to recognize the constraints inherent in subjective assessments.

The study has illuminated disparities in the mental health status among employees occupying distinct positions. Specifically, when contrasting sales personnel with their R&D counterparts, it becomes evident that sales staff exhibit a more compromised mental health profile, characterized by heightened manifestations of anxiety and depression. This discrepancy may find its roots in divergent levels of work-related stress. Contributory lifestyle factors potentially amplify the work-related pressures encountered by sales personnel in contrast to their R&D peers, ultimately exerting an influence on their mental well-being. The company can appropriately adjust the working mode and foster employees’ lifestyle habits, which greatly contributes to reducing work pressure and enhancing work efficiency. It is of paramount importance for both societies at large and organizational management to accord due attention to the psychological well-being of such personnel and to orchestrate efficacious intervention strategies. Beyond a focus on augmenting employees’ professional skill sets, entities should take precedence in nurturing employees’ mental health by instituting measures like periodic mental health assessments and employee assistance programs. Concurrently, advocating for wholesome lifestyles assumes significance in this endeavor. Management paradigms need to be nuanced according to the positions employees hold, common approaches that encompass humanistic care provision and cultivation of a positive corporate ethos. In this study, we found that fNIRS shows potential in assessing stress responses in specific work environments. Therefore, we recommend using fNIRS as a supplement to existing stress assessment tools in similar work settings to provide more comprehensive physiological data. By leveraging its capabilities in real-time cognitive assessment and stress monitoring, fNIRS can provide nuanced insights into mental workload, stress levels, and decision-making processes during occupational tasks. Longitudinal studies using fNIRS can track cognitive changes and stress patterns over time among workers exposed to different occupational conditions, revealing trends and triggers for interventions. Protocols for using fNIRS to detect early signs of mental fatigue or cognitive decline among workers engaged in demanding tasks can lead to timely interventions to prevent occupational hazards. Exploring synergies between fNIRS and existing diagnostic tools such as electroencephalography or heart rate variability monitors can offer a comprehensive view of physiological and cognitive responses under varied occupational conditions. In conclusion, fNIRS can be most effectively integrated into occupational health practices.

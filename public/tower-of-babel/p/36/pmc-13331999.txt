Early response toward clinical remission with dupilumab in severe asthma: Real-world cohort
Authors: Kenji Mizumura, Yasuhiro Gon, Akihiko Tanaka, Ryosuke Ozoe, Yusuke Kurosawa, Shiho Yamada, Yutaka Kozu, Shuichiro Maruoka, Hironori Sagara, Norihiro Harada, Sumiko Abe, Kazuhisa Takahashi, Taisuke Akamatsu, Toshihiro Shirai, Katsunori Masaki, Koichi Fukunaga, Konomi Kobayashi, Hiroyuki Nagase, Nobuaki Miyahara, Arihiko Kanehiro, Noboru Kitamura, Naruhiko Sugihara, Fumio Kumasawa, Junko Terada-Hirashima, Masayuki Hojo, Kazuyuki Chibana, Mayoko Tsuji, Etsuko Tagaya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundClinical remission is an important treatment goal in severe asthma; however, durable remission frameworks generally require sustained disease control over longer periods. Whether dupilumab induces an early response toward this goal in real-world practice remains unclear.ObjectiveWe sought to evaluate early response toward clinical remission at 24 weeks after dupilumab initiation and to identify baseline factors associated with this outcome.MethodsIn this multicenter, prospective cohort at 13 Japanese institutions, adults with severe asthma treated with dupilumab were followed for 24 weeks. Given the limited observation period, an early response toward clinical remission was defined as meeting modified 4-component remission-oriented criteria: Asthma Control Test score 20 or higher, FEV1 % predicted 80% or higher at week 24, no exacerbations, and no oral corticosteroid use (weeks 16-24). Multivariable logistic regression identified baseline factors associated with this outcome, and receiver-operating characteristic analysis evaluated baseline fractional exhaled nitric oxide (Feno).ResultsOf 103 enrolled patients, 70 had complete data; 38 (54.3%; 95% CI, 41.9%-66.3%) met the modified remission-oriented criteria at week 24. Higher baseline Feno, higher FEV1 % predicted, and absence of maintenance oral corticosteroid use were associated with this outcome. Baseline Feno showed modest discrimination (area under the curve, 0.65; 95% CI, 0.51-0.78).ConclusionsApproximately half of the patients met the modified remission-oriented criteria at 24 weeks after dupilumab initiation, representing an early response toward clinical remission rather than sustained clinical remission itself. Baseline Feno and lung function may help identify patients more likely to show this response.

Asthma is characterized by clinical symptoms, such as wheezing, shortness of breath, and coughing, resulting from variable airway narrowing from chronic inflammation.1 It affects approximately 334 million people worldwide, with a substantial societal and health care burden.2 Severe asthma accounts for approximately 5% to 10% of cases and remains difficult to manage despite high-dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICSs) and additional controller medications and/or oral corticosteroids (OCSs).3 Asthma is primarily driven by type 2 inflammation mediated by TH2 cells and type 2 innate lymphoid cells.4 These cells produce type 2 cytokines such as IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, leading to increased serum IgE levels and eosinophil accumulation. IL-4/IL-13 signaling also induces airway nitric oxide production, and fractional exhaled nitric oxide (Feno) is a biomarker of type 2 airway inflammation supporting asthma diagnosis and management.5,6

In recent years, asthma treatment has advanced, with mAbs targeting type 2 cytokines in severe asthma.7 These biologics have revolutionized management by improving control, reducing exacerbations, and decreasing OCS dependence.8, 9, 10, 11 With biologics, patients with refractory asthma can achieve sustained symptom relief and a near-normal life, leading to the concept of clinical remission.12 This concept varies by country, with some defining 3-component remission (≥12 months without exacerbations, symptom control, and no OCS use), whereas others include stable lung function as a fourth component.13, 14, 15 Dupilumab, a fully human mAb targeting IL-4Rα, inhibits IL-4/IL-13 signaling and has shown strong efficacy in reducing severe exacerbations and improving lung function in patients with uncontrolled asthma.8 Subgroup analyses indicate that patients with higher Feno levels and blood eosinophils achieve greater exacerbation reduction with dupilumab than those with lower levels.8 Post hoc phase 3 analyses have reported that approximately 30% of patients achieved clinical remission at week 2416; however, the extent to which dupilumab induces an early response toward clinical remission in real-world practice, as well as the baseline factors associated with this early favorable outcome, remains unclear.

Therefore, we aimed to evaluate the early response toward clinical remission at week 24 after dupilumab initiation in a real-world setting and to identify the associated baseline factors. To our knowledge, studies investigating clinical remission with dupilumab in patients with severe asthma have been limited to retrospective analyses17 or small-scale, single-center, prospective observational studies involving only a few dozen patients.18 However, we conducted a prospective real-world study of dupilumab across 13 facilities in Japan.

Methods

Study population and design

The iPOT4R study (IL-4/13 Receptor alpha antagonist: Prediction of efficacy and observation of time course of inflammatory markers) was a collaborative, prospective, noninterventional, real-world, single-arm, open-label observational study conducted across 13 centers in Japan from July 2019 to May 2021.19 This post hoc analysis focuses on early response toward clinical remission at week 24 after dupilumab initiation and its association with baseline Feno levels.

Full study design and methods have been previously published.19 In brief, the cohort comprised 103 patients aged 20 years or more (range, 26-80 years) diagnosed with asthma by certified respiratory allergy specialists. Patients with nasal symptoms were also evaluated by otolaryngologists for comorbid otorhinolaryngological conditions. Inclusion criteria were (1) current ICS use (fluticasone propionate ≥400 μg/d or equivalent); (2) at least 1 additional controller (long-acting β2-agonist, long-acting muscarinic antagonist [LAMA], leukotriene receptor antagonist, or theophylline); (3) 5-item Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ-5) score higher than 0.75; and (4) asthma worsening in the previous year requiring treatment escalation or a physician-assessed instability. Exclusion criteria included (1) chronic lung diseases (bronchiectasis, diffuse bronchiolitis, interstitial pneumonia, pulmonary hypertension, or posttuberculosis sequelae); (2) active malignancy or remission for less than 1 year; (3) unstable hepatic or cardiovascular disease; (4) hypereosinophilic syndrome or eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis; (5) parasitic infection within 6 months; (6) biologic use within 30 days; (7) bronchial thermoplasty within 1 year; and (8) other physician-determined ineligibility. Nasal symptoms were assessed using a 6-item nasal symptom score. Each item was rated on a 7-point scale (0 = none, 6 = always) for nasal obstruction, viscous nasal discharge, postnasal drip, reduced sense of smell, headache, and impairment of daily activities. The total score (range, 0-36) was calculated by summing the scores of all items, with higher scores indicating more severe symptoms. Depressive symptoms were assessed using the Patient Health Questionnaire-9, a 9-item self-administered questionnaire, and each item was rated on a 4-point scale (0-3), yielding a total score (0-27), with higher scores indicating greater severity.20

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Nihon University Itabashi Hospital (approval no. RK-190514-2) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before their inclusion in the study, and patient confidentiality was safeguarded through measures approved by the Ethics Committee.

Procedures

Patients received dupilumab 600 mg subcutaneously initially, followed by 300 mg every 2 weeks up to week 24. Outcomes included exacerbations, OCS use, asthma control (using the Asthma Control Test [ACT]), ACQ-5, nasal symptom score, Patient Health Questionnaire-9, lung function, Feno, blood eosinophils, and serum IgE.21 ICS and other controller medication adherence were monitored; no patient discontinued ICSs during follow-up.

Definitions

The primary end point was defined as an early response toward clinical remission at week 24, defined as modified 4-component remission-oriented criteria: ACT score 20 or higher, FEV1 % predicted 80% or higher, and no exacerbations or OCS use between weeks 16 and 24.

Exacerbations were defined as systemic corticosteroid use for more than 3 days, doubling of maintenance OCS for more than 3 days, or asthma-related emergency visits or hospitalizations.

Patients were classified into groups according to Feno level: less than 35 parts per billion (ppb) or 35 ppb or higher. In Japan, the reference value for Feno in healthy adults is 15.4 ppb, with an upper limit of 36.8 ppb.22,23 Based on this, a cutoff of 35 ppb or higher is commonly used in Japan to define elevated Feno levels in asthma management.24 In this study, we adopted this established threshold to prioritize clinical applicability in routine practice. Similarly, patients were divided into groups on the basis of blood eosinophils: less than 300 cells/μL or 300 cells/μL or higher. For serum IgE level, the cutoff was set at less than 170 IU/mL or 170 IU/mL or higher. Total serum IgE level of greater than or equal to 170 IU/mL is commonly used in Japan to define elevated IgE levels, based on national guidelines and previous epidemiological studies.25,26

Statistical analyses

Normality testing showed that most variables were nonnormally distributed; thus, data are presented as medians (interquartile ranges) and analyzed using nonparametric methods. Categorical variables were compared using chi-square or Fisher exact test; McNemar test was used for paired binary comparisons. Cochran Q test followed by McNemar test was used to evaluate longitudinal changes in the proportion of patients meeting the modified remission-oriented criteria at 8, 16, and 24 weeks. The 95% CI for the proportion of patients meeting the modified remission-oriented criteria at week 24 was calculated using the Clopper-Pearson exact method. Continuous paired data were analyzed using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and unpaired data using Mann-Whitney U tests. A 2-tailed significance level of α equal to 0.05 was used. Multivariable logistic regression analysis identified independent baseline factors associated with an early response toward clinical remission. Potential factors were selected on the basis of clinical relevance and a P value less than .05 in univariate comparisons. In addition, Feno predictive performance was evaluated using receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, with optimal cutoff determined by maximizing the Youden index. Multicollinearity was evaluated using variance inflation factor values, with a cutoff of less than 10 indicating an acceptable level of collinearity. All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 31.0.1.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY), specifically using the Statistics Base, Advanced Statistics, and Regression modules.

Results

Safety outcomes and discontinuations

Of 103 patients receiving dupilumab, treatment was discontinued in 9 cases: uterine carcinoma, difficulty attending outpatient visits, vasovagal syncope, vertigo, bacterial pneumonia, eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis, respiratory distress, and 2 cases of unknown cause (Fig 1). Achievement of modified remission-oriented criteria in patients with severe asthma treated with dupilumab in a real-world setting

Of 103 patients initiating dupilumab, 70 with complete data for all 4 components of the modified remission-oriented criteria were included. Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table E1 (in the Online Repository available at www.jaci-global.org). Median age was 56.5 years, and 70.0% were female. Common comorbidities included atopic dermatitis (34.3%), allergic rhinitis (42.9%), and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (34.3%). Median Feno was 30.5 ppb and blood eosinophils 152 cells/μL. A total of 41.4% were biologic-naive. At week 24, 38 of 70 patients (54.3%; 95% CI, 41.9%-66.3%) met modified 4-component remission-oriented criteria, representing an early response toward clinical remission. No significant differences were seen between included and excluded patients (Table E1), suggesting minimal selection bias. An analysis of the achievement rates for each component of the modified remission-oriented criteria was conducted (Table I). Significant improvements were observed in ACT score (≥20), absence of exacerbations, and FEV1 % predicted (≥80%) after dupilumab treatment; however, OCS-free status did not significantly improve. To further contextualize these early-response outcomes, we analyzed longitudinal patterns of exacerbation occurrences (see Fig E1 in this article’s Online Repository at www.jaci-global.org) and airway obstruction, as assessed by FEV1 % predicted (see Fig E2 in this article’s Online Repository at www.jaci-global.org), in both groups. Regarding exacerbations, the 2 groups did not differ significantly in clinical stability before initiating therapy (P = .129), and a clear divergence only in exacerbation rates emerged during the treatment period, reaching statistical significance during the final 16- to 24-week assessment window (P = .011). The early-response group had significantly higher baseline FEV1 % predicted (P < .001), which remained higher through week 24, reaching near-normal values (>100%) (Fig E2). Finally, we explored the relationship between treatment duration and the proportion of patients meeting the modified remission-oriented criteria in a subanalysis of 64 patients with complete data at weeks 8, 16, and 24. The proportion of patients meeting the modified remission-oriented criteria increased significantly over time, from 35.9% (23 of 64) at week 8 and 46.9% (30 of 64) at week 16 to 53.1% (34 of 64) at week 24 (Cochran Q test, P = .018; see Fig E3 in this article’s Online Repository at www.jaci-global.org). Pairwise comparisons using McNemar test revealed a significant increase in this proportion from week 8 to 24 (P = .031).

Factors associated with early response toward clinical remission with dupilumab therapy

Table III presents the multivariable logistic regression analysis identifying independent baseline factors associated with an early response toward clinical remission. To ensure model stability and avoid overfitting, we used a parsimonious approach, including only those variables that showed a P value less than .05 in univariate comparisons (Table II): the Feno subgroup, FEV1 % predicted, forced vital capacity % predicted, OCS use, and LAMA use. In this multivariable model, the Feno subgroup was analyzed as a categorical variable based on a predefined clinical threshold, whereas lung function parameters were treated as continuous variables. In this optimized model, a higher baseline Feno subgroup (≥35 ppb) and better baseline lung function (FEV1 % predicted) were significantly associated with an early response toward clinical remission (odds ratio, 7.27, 95% CI, 1.59-33.12, P = .01, and odds ratio, 1.06, 95% CI, 1.02-1.11, P = .004, respectively). Furthermore, maintenance OCS use at baseline was identified as a significant negative factor, substantially reducing the likelihood of meeting the modified remission-oriented criteria at week 24 (odds ratio, 0.07; 95% CI, 0.006-0.77; P = .03). Given that, among the type 2 inflammatory biomarkers, only Feno was identified as a factor associated with an early response toward clinical remission, we performed ROC curve analysis to determine the optimal cutoff value for Feno by maximizing the Youden index (see Fig E4 in this article’s Online Repository at www.jaci-global.org). The area under the curve was 0.65 (95% CI, 0.51-0.78; P = .03), with an optimal cutoff of 31.7 ppb (sensitivity, 0.65; specificity, 0.69), supporting 35 ppb as a clinically reasonable threshold.

Discussion

The introduction of biologics has made clinical remission a feasible goal even in patients with refractory asthma.27, 28, 29, 30 Criteria for clinical remission, including the “3-component” criteria (and the “4-component” criteria), have been proposed in various countries.13,31 In post hoc analyses of phase III trials, QUEST and TRAVERSE, 37.2% of patients with type 2 asthma achieved 4-component clinical remission within the first year of dupilumab treatment.16 Similarly, a retrospective real-world study reported that 31.6% achieved 4-component clinical remission within 1 year.17 A single-center prospective study of 20 patients showed that 6 achieved 4-component clinical remission with dupilumab.18 Conversely, to our knowledge, this is the first multicenter, prospective, real-world study involving more than 100 patients, demonstrating that 54.3% met the modified 4-component remission-oriented criteria at week 24, representing an early response toward clinical remission rather than sustained remission. These findings suggest that dupilumab can induce an early favorable state aligned with remission concepts in real-world practice. In QUEST and TRAVERSE post hoc analyses, the clinical remission at week 24 was 29.6%, increasing to 37.2% by week 52. Notably, the proportion in our cohort was higher, likely reflecting baseline characteristics, including relatively preserved lung function (median FEV1 % predicted 86.1% vs ∼60%-70% in phase 3 trials) and lower OCS dependence. Because our assessment period was shorter than the standard 12-month threshold and required only an 8-week maintenance window (weeks 16-24), our results should be interpreted as an early-response measure rather than durable remission.

Previous studies have documented the OCS-sparing effects of biologics.32, 33, 34, 35, 36, 37 In the VENTURE trial, 48% of dupilumab-treated patients discontinued OCS versus 25% in placebo.34 In our study, although dupilumab improved asthma control, exacerbations, and lung function, the overall OCS-free proportion did not significantly increase. Reported long-term OCS use in patients with asthma ranges from 1.2% to 30.9%, showing variability.38 Here, only 14.3% (10 of 70) used maintenance OCS at baseline, meaning 85.7% (60 of 70) were already OCS-free. This likely explains the lack of an increase in the OCS-free rate at week 24. However, among the 10 patients receiving OCS at baseline, 4 discontinued OCS after dupilumab initiation, a rate comparable to that observed in the VENTURE trial, supporting its real-world OCS-sparing effect and its contribution to an early response toward clinical remission.

Previous studies have identified factors associated with biologic-induced remission,28 including body mass index, age at onset, disease duration, and comorbidities.27,28 In our study, none of these factors differed between the early-response and non–early-response groups or were significant in the multivariable analysis. Among type 2 inflammatory biomarkers, higher baseline Feno was identified as a factor associated with an early response toward clinical remission, unlike blood eosinophils and total IgE level. Feno is upregulated through the IL-4/IL-13–mediated signaling pathways of TH2 cells and type 2 innate lymphoid cells.5 Therefore, patients with high Feno are expected to benefit from dupilumab, which inhibits IL-4/IL-13 signaling. This study provides real-world evidence supporting this hypothesis. Among patients in the early-response group, most had high baseline Feno, and more than 90% achieved low Feno levels (<35 ppb) after treatment, indicating effective suppression of IL-4/IL-13 signaling and thereby promoting an early favorable state aligned with clinical remission. Conversely, no notable differences in blood eosinophils or IgE levels were observed between the early-response and non–early-response groups, either before or after treatment.

Regarding lung function, we found that baseline lung function was significantly better in the early-response group than in the non–early-response group, and multivariable analysis confirmed that FEV1 % predicted was significantly associated with an early response toward clinical remission. Although our previous studies have demonstrated that dupilumab improves lung function in real-world settings,19 poor baseline lung function may limit the likelihood of achieving early response toward clinical remission. However, no significant baseline differences in asthma control scores (such as the ACT score and the ACQ-5 score) or exacerbation rates were observed between the early-response and non–early-response groups. These findings suggest that although dupilumab is expected to improve lung function, its potential to induce an early response toward clinical remission may be limited in patients with poor baseline lung function. Patients in the early-response group already had significantly higher baseline FEV1 % predicted (median, 95.7%) than those in the non–early-response group (median, 72.2%). This suggests that meeting the lung function component of the modified remission-oriented criteria is substantially more feasible for patients whose baseline values are closer to the 80% or higher threshold.

This study has certain limitations. First, the primary end point was assessed at week 24, whereas guidelines generally recommend evaluating clinical remission after a minimum of 12 months of sustained criteria.13,31 Therefore, we did not assess sustained clinical remission but rather an early response toward clinical remission using modified remission-oriented criteria. However, a recent mepolizumab study demonstrated a strong correlation between 6-month response and 12-month clinical remission, suggesting that short-term outcomes may predict sustained remission in certain settings.39 Therefore, the proportion meeting modified remission-oriented criteria at 24 weeks may serve as a surrogate for longer-term outcomes and help identify early responders who may later achieve sustained remission, which is relevant for clinical decision making. Second, the absence of a control group limits interpretation because of the possibility of spontaneous remission, which occurs in approximately 10% of adult asthma cases.12 Third, 41.4% (n = 29) of patients were biologic-naive and 58.6% (n = 41) had previously received other biologics; remission rates were higher in biologic-naive patients than in biologic-experienced patients (65.5% vs 46.3%), although this difference was not statistically significant (P = .11). Nevertheless, the potential impact of the specific type of prior biologic and switching patterns cannot be excluded. Differences in treatment effects related to biologic switching have been reported40; however, our preliminary exploratory observations also suggest that clinical response may vary according to the class of the previous biologic (such as anti-IgE vs anti–IL-5). Detailed analyses of these switching patterns and their impact on early treatment effectiveness are ongoing and will be addressed in a future publication. Fourth, longitudinal assessments of airway reversibility and hyperresponsiveness were not conducted because of the real-world design, limiting our ability to fully characterize the physiological profiles of the participants. Fifth, although all patients remained on ICS therapy according to the study protocol, detailed data on dose adjustments of ICS or other controller medications at the discretion of treating physicians were not collected, which may have influenced the observed clinical outcomes. Finally, although Feno was identified as a statistically significant factor associated with an early response toward clinical remission in the multivariate analysis, its discriminatory performance, as assessed by ROC analysis, was modest (area under the curve, 0.65; P = .03). We used a categorical cutoff of 35 ppb for clinical utility; however, continuous ROC analysis confirmed an optimal threshold of 31.7 ppb, supporting the robustness of the categorical approach. These findings suggest that although Feno contributes to identifying the likelihood of an early favorable response, it should not be used as a standalone marker. Consistent with recent evidence, our cohort included a substantial proportion of patients with low baseline Feno levels who nevertheless met the modified remission-oriented criteria at week 24. This finding highlights the heterogeneity of type 2 inflammation and supports the importance of integrating other clinical and physiological markers—such as baseline lung function and OCS use—into a multimodal assessment.

Conclusion

In this multicenter, prospective, real-world cohort of adults with severe asthma initiating dupilumab, approximately half met the modified 4-component remission-oriented criteria by week 24, representing an early response toward clinical remission rather than sustained clinical remission. Higher baseline Feno and better lung function were associated with this outcome; however, the modest discriminatory performance of Feno indicates that it should be part of a multimodal assessment rather than a standalone determinant. These findings support the feasibility of achieving an early response toward clinical remission with dupilumab in routine care and highlight the value of integrating Feno with clinical and physiologic indices when counseling patients and designing treat-to-target strategies.

Effects of non-pharmacological interventions on nutrition and immune status of nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients during chemoradiotherapy: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Lijuan Xia, Naoko Hayashi, Erika Ota, Lin Qi, Bin Yan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) presents significant nutritional challenges during concurrent chemoradiotherapy, adversely affecting treatment outcomes and quality of life. Non-pharmacological interventions may help improve nutritional and immune status, reduce complications and enhance overall well-being. However, evidence of their effectiveness is scattered and inconsistent, and no systematic review has yet synthesised the evidence on their effectiveness. This protocol outlines a systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the impact of non-pharmacological interventions on nutritional status, immune function and complications in NPC patients undergoing chemoradiotherapy. The findings may contribute to clinical recommendations and support evidence-based decision-making in the supportive care of NPC patients.MethodsThis protocol follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols guidelines. A comprehensive search will be conducted in MEDLINE, PubMed, CINAHL, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, ProQuest, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Chinese Biomedical Literature Database and Wan Fang from November 2014 to November 2024. Search terms will include concepts related to NPC, nutrition, supplements, education, exercise, multimodal interventions and chemoradiotherapy. Only randomised controlled trials will be included. Two reviewers will independently screen studies, extract data and assess the risk of bias using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool V.2. Where appropriate, a meta-analysis will be performed using RevMan 5.4.Ethics and disseminationThis systematic review does not directly involve the use of human beings; therefore, there is no requirement for ethical approval. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and in various media, such as conferences, congresses or symposia.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024571769Keywords: ONCOLOGY, Nutritional support, Immunity

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is the most prevalent head and neck cancer,1 with a high geographic distribution in East and Southeast Asia.2 The disease often presented at an advanced stage, requiring aggressive treatment with concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT),3 which, while effective, imposes substantial physiological stress.4

Malnutrition is highly prevalent in NPC patients undergoing CCRT, driven by a combination effects of tumour burden, treatment-induced toxicities (eg, mucositis, xerostomia, dysphagia) and systemic inflammation.5,7 These nutrition-impact symptoms impair oral intake, promote weight loss and compromise immune function.8 9 Studies indicate that up to 60% of NPC patients experience significant weight loss (>5–10%) after CCRT, leading to reduced treatment tolerance, higher complication rates and poorer outcomes.10,13 This initiates a vicious cycle: diminished nutritional status impairs immune function, which in turn reduces treatment tolerance.14 15 An unfavourable immune state increases the risk of infections and treatment toxicity, often leading to interruptions in therapy.16 These complications may contribute to higher mortality rates, reduced bone density and an elevated risk of sarcopenia and osteoporosis.17 Consequently, patients become increasingly susceptible to adverse treatment reactions, further malnutrition, infections and other complications, including heightened risks of frailty, depression and falls.18,20

Despite the high prevalence of malnutrition and its substantial impact on the patient’s health and quality of life, effective management remains a significant clinical challenge. Currently, no globally recognised pharmacological treatments offer both high efficacy and minimal side effects.21 As a result, non-pharmacological interventions have gained attention as promising supportive strategies.22 23

Non-pharmacological therapy refers to treatment strategies that do not involve the use of conventional medications or pharmaceutical agents. These interventions aim to improve health outcomes, manage symptoms and enhance overall well-being through non-drug approaches. Examples include dietary modifications, lifestyle changes, physical activity, psychotherapy, education, behavioural therapies, complementary medicine and various forms of rehabilitation.24 Despite their potential as supportive care strategies, the current evidence on the effectiveness of these interventions is scattered and lacks comprehensive synthesis, with some studies, such as on diet and exercise, yielding controversial results.

Previous studies evaluating high-protein nutritional supplementation and structured exercise programmes during CCRT have reported mixed results. A high-protein diet is essential for supporting muscle repair, immune function and overall metabolic balance, especially in patients undergoing CCRT who are at increased risk of muscle wasting and malnutrition.22 25 Dou et al found that while the oral nutrition supply group showed slightly better maintenance of weight and Body Mass Index (BMI), these differences were not statistically significant, suggesting that the efficacy of high-protein oral nutritional supplementation may be influenced by patient-specific factors.26 Similarly, Capozzi et al assessed a structured exercise intervention in head and neck cancer patients undergoing CCRT. While improvements in muscle strength and physical performance were observed, there were no significant differences in weight loss between the exercise and control groups.27 Likewise, Bye et al found no significant difference in weight loss when combining nutritional and exercise interventions.28 These mixed findings underscore the need for a comprehensive synthesis of the evidence regarding the effectiveness of non-pharmacological interventions in NPC patients undergoing CCRT.

Building on the mixed results from prior studies, this systematic review and meta-analysis will evaluate the effects of non-pharmacological interventions on nutritional status, immune function and treatment-related complications in patients with NPC. By synthesising data from randomised controlled trials (RCTs) across various intervention protocols, it aims to clarify their clinical benefits and limitations. The findings will inform clinical practice and identify research gaps, with the potential to enhance treatment tolerance, reduce complications and improve patient outcomes in NPC care.

Methods

Design and registration

This systematic review will evaluate the efficacy of non-pharmacological interventions on nutritional and immune status in patients with NPC undergoing CCRT. It will follow the recommendations stated in the updated Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) guidelines.29 The protocol for this systematic review is reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P) 2015 guidelines.30 It has been registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews of the Center for Reviews and Dissemination (PROSPERO), registration number 2024 CRD42024571769.

Eligibility criteria

Types of studies

RCTs, including cluster RCTs, will be included. Only studies published in English and Chinese between November 2014 and November 2024 will be considered. Descriptive studies and controlled before-after studies will be excluded.

Types of population

This review will include adult patients (aged ≥18 years) diagnosed with NPC who are undergoing CCRT as their primary treatment. Studies involving paediatric populations will be excluded. No restrictions will be placed on the intervention setting; eligible interventions may be delivered in clinical settings, through internet- or telephone-based platforms, or via individual or group-based programmes. Studies will be excluded if they target patients with recurrent disease, focus on palliative care or involve solely psychological interventions, percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy or nasogastric tube feeding. The latter are excluded due to significant variability in timing of tube placement and nutritional delivery strategies, which may introduce clinical and methodological heterogeneity. Publications or reports from non-academic sources will also be excluded.

Types of intervention and comparison

Non-pharmacological interventions administered to NPC patients during their treatment with CCRT will be evaluated, irrespective of format, duration or frequency. Eligible interventions may include nutritional and dietary strategies (eg, enteral and parenteral nutrition, dietary supplements and dietitian counselling), education programmes, multimodal care, exercise and physical activity, as well as supplementation with fish oil, omega-3 fatty acids, vitamin D, other vitamins, trace elements, antioxidants and probiotics. Control conditions may include any comparison group, such as active control, placebo, standard care, guideline-based care or usual care. Outcome assessments will be limited to effects measured within 3 months post-treatment. These supplements will be considered non-pharmacological when used at nutritional or physiological doses to support treatment tolerance and general health, rather than to exert direct pharmacological effects.

Types of outcomes

The primary outcomes will include: (1) body weight; (2) BMI; (3) protein levels; (4) haemoglobin levels; (5) CD3+T cells, CD4+T cells and CD8+T cells. The secondary outcomes will be complications such as oral mucositis and dermatitis. Subgroup analyses will be performed based on various assessment time points if the same outcomes are measured repeatedly.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search will be conducted in the following databases: MEDLINE, PubMed, CINAHL, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, ProQuest, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Chinese Biomedical Database and Wan Fang. Search terms will relate to NPC, nutrition, supplements, education, exercise, fish oil, omega-3 fatty acids, vitamin D, vitamins, trace elements, antioxidants, probiotics, programmes, multimodal interventions and chemoradiotherapy. Manual examination will include reference lists of included studies and published systematic reviews on non-pharmacological interventions for NPC patients for potentially relevant trials with contact of original authors if necessary.

The search will be restricted to RCTs published in English or Chinese between November 2014 and November 2024. An experienced librarian from St. Luke’s International University will review the search strategy to ensure its comprehensiveness and accuracy. The search string was developed using the Population, Intervention, Comparison, and Outcome framework,31 as detailed in table 1. The specific search strategy applied to PubMed, incorporating both MeSH terms and free-text keywords, is provided in table 2, with a similar approach to be used for the remaining databases.

Study selection

Search results will be screened in two sequential phases: (1) title/abstract screening and (2) full-text screening. After duplicate removal via the Rayyan AI platform, two independent reviewers (LX and LQ) will screen titles and abstracts to identify potentially eligible studies. Full texts of studies that appear to meet the inclusion criteria will then be retrieved and independently assessed for eligibility. Any discrepancies regarding study inclusion will be resolved through consultation with a third reviewer (NH), with consensus reached through discussion.31 The study selection process will be illustrated in a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (figure 1).

Data synthesis and analysis

Data extraction

An electronic data extraction form will be developed32 and piloted on at least three studies to ensure its appropriateness, feasibility and comprehensiveness, and will be refined based on team discussion. Two trained data extractors (LX and LQ) will independently extract data from all included studies using the final version of the form, and any disagreements will be resolved by a third reviewer (EO) through discussion until consensus is reached. Data to be extracted will include study details (first author, title, country and year of publication); participant demographics (age, sex, cancer stage and treatment regimen); methodological characteristics (sample size, study design, intervention components, assessment tools and timing of outcome measurements); outcome data and follow-up duration.

Assessment of risk of bias

Two review authors (LX and LQ) will independently evaluate the risk of bias in each eligible study31 using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool V.2 (RoB2) in accordance with the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.33

This tool assesses key domains, including selection bias, performance bias, attrition bias, reporting bias and other potential sources of bias. Any disagreements in the assessment or decisions regarding study inclusion in the meta-analysis will be resolved through consultation with additional reviewers (EO and NH).

Measures of treatment effect

When the same types of interventions are used, and outcomes are measured using the same tools between trials, we will calculate the mean difference and 95% CIs for continuous outcomes and present summary risk ratios with 95% CIs for dichotomous outcomes. For immune function outcomes such as CD4 and CD8 T-cell counts, which are expected to vary in measurement scale and reporting format, we will use standardised mean differences to enable comparability across studies. If a quantitative synthesis is not feasible due to variability in populations, interventions or outcomes, a narrative synthesis detailing population characteristics, intervention types, outcomes and observed effects will be provided.

Dealing with missing data

A sensitivity analysis will be conducted to evaluate the impact of missing data on the primary outcomes. Statistical analyses will adhere to an intention-to-treat principle wherever possible. Heterogeneity will be assessed using χ2 and I2 statistics, with heterogeneity considered present when the χ2 p value is below 0.10, and the I2 value exceeds 50%.

Assessment of reporting bias

If 10 or more studies are included in the meta-analysis, reporting biases will be examined using funnel plots to assess asymmetry, which will be visually interpreted and reported.

Data synthesis

The finalised electronic data extraction form will be shared with all research team members. Data will be aggregated by study design, setting and outcomes, and the patterns and relationships will be discussed in team meetings until a consensus is reached. Results will be analysed and grouped into categories based on similarities and differences in outcomes, emphasising intervention content, methodologies, settings, timing/duration and the healthcare professionals involved. When feasible, a statistical meta-analysis will be performed using RevMan V.5.4 (Cochrane Collaboration software). Given the expected heterogeneity in study design, setting and outcomes, some evidence will be synthesised narratively and reported in accordance with the Synthesis Without Meta-analysis (SWiM) guidelines. Findings will be presented using textual descriptions, tables, vote-counting techniques and figures when appropriate.

Subgroup analysis and investigation of heterogeneity

If sufficient data are available, subgroup analyses will be conducted for the primary outcomes based on the following factors: (1) intervention category (eg, nutritional counselling, oral nutritional supplements, exercise, omega-3, vitamins, vitamin D, trace elements, antioxidants, probiotics and multimodal interventions); (2) participant characteristics (including age, sex and disease stage) and (3) timing of outcome measurement (baseline, end of treatment and 3 months post-treatment).

Sensitivity analysis

A sensitivity analysis will be performed if any of the included studies are assessed as having a high risk of bias for the primary outcomes. Specifically, studies with a high risk of bias related to allocation concealment or incomplete outcome data will be excluded to evaluate the robustness of the findings.

Evidence quality assessment

The quality of evidence across studies will be assessed using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach.34 Each outcome will be evaluated based on five domains: risk of bias, inconsistency, imprecision, indirectness and publication bias. The overall certainty of evidence will be rated as high, moderate, low or very low. The findings will be summarised in a GRADE summary of findings table.35

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public will not be directly involved in this systematic review.

Ethics and dissemination

As this study is based on previously published research, ethical approval is not required. The findings will be disseminated through publication in an international peer-reviewed journal and presented as oral or poster communications at academic oncology conferences. The review will be updated annually, and findings will be revised when new relevant RCTs become available. Republishing will be considered if the conclusions change significantly.

Discussion

NPC patients frequently suffer from treatment-related malnutrition and immune suppression, factors that compromise treatment tolerance, overall outcomes and quality of life.36 Therefore, this systematic review and meta-analysis aims to address a critical gap in the management of NPC by evaluating the impact of non-pharmacological interventions on nutritional and immune status during CCRT. Given the scattered and sometimes inconsistent evidence in the literature, our review seeks to consolidate current findings and clarify how interventions, including nutritional supplements, dietary modifications, physical exercise and complementary therapies, may mitigate these adverse effects.

Recent studies underscore that dynamic, interacting factors profoundly influence patient conditions during treatment, highlighting the need for a comprehensive evaluation of these interventions.37 By synthesising data from randomised trials conducted over the past decade, our review will provide insights into which approaches most effectively enhance nutritional status and immune function while reducing treatment-related complications. A notable strength of our methodology is the use of an extensive search strategy that spans multiple databases and incorporates studies from both Western and Eastern contexts.

Nevertheless, several challenges may limit the ability to draw definitive conclusions. Heterogeneity in intervention protocols, variability in outcome measures and differences in study quality are potential obstacles.38 Moreover, the complex interplay between nutrition, immune status and treatment toxicity may require meticulous subgroup analyses to discern which interventions offer the greatest benefits under specific clinical scenarios.

The findings from this review are expected to have significant clinical implications. By delineating the benefits and limitations of non-pharmacological interventions, our work will enable healthcare providers to design more effective, individualised, supportive care strategies that improve patient outcomes. In addition, identifying current gaps in the literature will inform future research directions and support the design of more robust clinical trials.

Ultimately, this systematic review and meta-analysis will contribute to a more holistic, patient-centered approach to managing NPC by integrating non-pharmacological interventions into standard treatment protocols. Such an integrated strategy holds promise for enhancing treatment tolerance, reducing complications and improving the overall quality of life for NPC patients undergoing chemoradiotherapy.

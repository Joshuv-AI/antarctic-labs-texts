Unraveling genetic diversity in ‘Foşa’ and ‘Yomra’ hazelnut accessions (Corylus avellana L.) using SSR markers and phenotypic analysis
Authors: Yusuf Bilgen, Yaşar Akça, Kenan Yıldız, Kubilay Yıldırım
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Foşa hazelnut variety is commonly cultivated in Türkiye and is often confused with the Yomra variety due to morphological similarities. This study aims to determine the phenological and pomological characteristics of the Foşa and Yomra hazelnut populations and reveal their genetic diversity using multivariate statistical analysis and Simple Sequence Repeat (SSR) molecular markers. We analyzed 80 accessions (43 Foşa and 37 Yomra) using 10 SSR primers to establish genetic relationships. The polymorphic band lengths ranged from 53 to 158 bp, with 1 to 8 bands per primer, totaling 45 bands. The average genetic diversity was 0.53, observed heterozygosity was 0.31, and the polymorphism rate was 0.48. Phylogenetic analysis clearly separated ‘Foşa’ and ‘Yomra’ varieties into distinct clusters, with the population dividing into two main groups, each further subdivided into two subgroups. Several accessions within different subgroups showed identical genetic profiles, indicating clonal relationships. Genetic variation within the ‘Yomra’ variety (similarity coefficient: 0.646) was slightly higher than in the ‘Foşa’ variety (0.676). Among quantitative traits, the coefficient of variation ranged from 2.57% (nut size) to 68.12% (fibrousness), with high variation observed in yield, nuts per cluster, nut weight, kernel weight, kernel shape apex index, kernel cavity, blanching rate, and fibrousness. Phenologically, the ‘Foşa’ population exhibited earlier male flowering, female flowering, and leaf budburst compared to ‘Yomra’, with both populations showing protandrous-homogamous flowering patterns. These results demonstrate that genetically distinct accessions within these populations should be conserved in genetic resource collections and can serve as valuable parents in breeding programs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-44863-0.Keywords: Corylus avellana L. genetic diversity, SSR marker, Phylogenetic analysis, Foşa, YomraSubject terms: Evolution, Genetics, Plant sciences

Introduction

Global hazelnut production averages 1,118,240 tons annually (2019–2023 average) across 1,078,192 hectares worldwide. Türkiye dominates global production, contributing 63% with an average annual yield of 708,009 tons from 746,749 hectares (FAO, 2025). Türkiye hazelnut cultivation is concentrated in the Black Sea region, where 99% of production occurs across provinces situated between 0 and 750 m elevation along the coastal strip. In Trabzon province, where this study was conducted, approximately 45,000 tons are produced annually from 65,280 hectares. The Foşa and Yomra varieties constitute 43.10% of Trabzon’s production area, covering 11,800 hectares (Foşa) and 16,300 hectares (Yomra) respectively1.

The Foşa hazelnut variety is highly valued in the industry for its high productivity, round-shaped nuts, and excellent blanching characteristics. Due to its distinctive flavor profile and superior aroma, the Foşa variety received geographical indication status in Türkiye in 2022. The Yomra variety, locally known as Giresun Yabanisi in the region, causes confusion with the Foşa hazelnut variety due to their similar names (Erdogan, 2023). Yomra is characterized by high adaptability, vigorous growth, resilience to biotic and abiotic stresses, good productivity2, and distinctively pointed nut shape.

Most commercial hazelnut varieties have been developed through selection of spontaneous mutations accumulated over centuries (Mehlenbacher and Molnar, 2021). While many hazelnut varieties are monoclonal in origin3, several Turkish varieties including Tombul4,5, Çakıldak6,7, and Kalınkara5 comprise clones that are phenotypically similar but genetically distinct.

Hazelnuts belong to the genus Corylus within the Betulaceae family and are extensively cultivated in temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. Taxonomic classification within Corylus remains challenging, with species numbers ranging from 9 to 25 according to different authorities. Current molecular and morphological studies recognize 13 species organized into 4 subgroups8. The exact number of species in the Corylus is uncertain because the species can be openly pollinated, are morphologically similar, and spread over a wide area8–10. Six species are commonly recognized: two in Europe and Anatolia (C. avellana L., C. colurna L.), three in North America (C. americana Marshall, C. cornuta Marshall, C. californica Marshall), and one in the Himalayas (C. jaquemontii Decaisne). Seven additional species (C. chinensis Franchet, C. fargesii (Franch.) C.K. Schneid., C. ferox Wallich, C. heterophylla Fisher, C. kweichowensii Hu, C. yunnanensis A. Camus, C. sieboldiana Blume) are endemic to eastern Asia8.

Traditional variety identification based on morphological characteristics of husks, nuts, and other features is problematic because these traits are influenced by environmental conditions and management practices. Comprehensive morphological characterization requires multi-year studies under standardized conditions, making it costly and time-consuming. Molecular genetic approaches offer rapid, reliable, and environmentally independent methods for variety identification and genetic diversity assessment11. The objective of this study was to determine the genetic diversity within Foşa and Yomra hazelnut populations using integrated multivariate statistical and SSR molecular analyses, thereby providing essential information for conservation strategies and breeding programs.

Despite numerous genetic and clonal selection studies conducted on Turkish hazelnut cultivars such as ‘Tombul’, ‘Çakıldak’, ‘Palaz’, and ‘Kalınkara’, the genetic and phenological status of the ‘Yomra’ population has remained largely unexplored. In particular, no previous study has provided an integrated evaluation combining phenological traits, detailed pomological characteristics, multivariate discriminant analysis, and SSR-based molecular markers to clarify the long-standing confusion between the ‘Foşa’ and ‘Yomra’ populations. Therefore, the present study fills a critical scientific gap by (i) providing the first comprehensive phenological characterization of the ‘Yomra’ variety, (ii) demonstrating its genetic distinctiveness from ‘Foşa’ using SSR markers and population structure analysis, and (iii) identifying superior and genetically unique accessions for conservation and breeding purposes.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Plant materials consisted of hazelnut (Corylus avellana L.) accessions representing the Foşa variety and the Yomra variety (locally known as Giresun Yabanisi) collected from Trabzon province in Türkiye’s Eastern Black Sea Region.

Study area and climate characteristics

The study was conducted across four districts of Trabzon province: Ortahisar, Yomra, Arsin, and Araklı. Climate data during the research period (2019–2021) showed minimum monthly average temperatures occurring in January and February, with values of 4.3 °C (2019), −1.5 °C (2020), and 2.3 °C (2021). Maximum precipitation occurred during autumn: October (104.6 mm, 2019), November (226 mm, 2020), and September (130 mm, 2021). The highest number of rainy days was recorded in March (16 days, 2019) and April (17 days in both 2020 and 2021) during the spring season (Anonymous)12.

Phenological observations

Phenological observations were conducted according to Bioversity and CIHEAM (2008) descriptors across three elevation zones: 0–250 m, 251–500 m, and 501–750 m.

Statistical analysis of population variation and pomological characteristics

Population variation was assessed using canonical discriminant analysis implemented in SAS software. Initially, 247 accessions were examined across Trabzon province in 2019, including 117 Foşa and 130 Yomra accessions distributed across Ortahisar, Yomra, Arsin, and Araklı districts. Variation analysis incorporated yield parameters (g/plant, g/cm²) and 19 fruit characteristics. Nut characteristics were evaluated according to UPOV criteria (Bioversity and CIHEAM, 2008). Following initial variation assessment, nuts from 41 Foşa and 34 Yomra accessions were harvested in 2020–2021 for detailed analysis. Minimum two-year data on yield (g/cm²), and 19 nut characteristics were used for dendrogram construction and Principal Component Analysis (PCA).

DNA isolation and molecular genetic analysis

Young leaves were collected from field-grown accessions and immediately transferred to the laboratory, then stored at −80 °C until analysis. DNA extraction followed the modified protocol of Doyle and Doyle13. DNA quality and concentration were assessed using a Thermo Scientific Multiskan™ GO spectrophotometer with NanoDrop capability, followed by electrophoresis on 1.2% agarose gels. Molecular genetic analysis employed 10 SSR primer pairs associated with late leafing in hazelnuts14. DNA isolation was successful for 80 accessions (43 Foşa, 37 Yomra), plus one control accession (AN70) from the Hazelnut Research Institute collection in Giresun, were examined with the SSR primes given in Table 1. SSR amplification products were separated on 1.2% high-resolution agarose gels stained with ethidium bromide. A 50 bp DNA ladder was used as a molecular size standard to enable precise discrimination of small fragment length differences. Due to the large number of accessions analyzed, electrophoresis was performed on multiple gels; therefore, reference accessions and molecular size markers were included on each gel to ensure consistency and accuracy in allele identification. Following electrophoresis, gels were visualized and digitally documented using a Bio-Rad gel imaging system (Bio-Rad Laboratories, USA). Fragment sizes were determined, and SSR alleles were scored based on band position relative to the molecular size marker using the gel imaging and analysis system, ensuring accurate and reproducible allele sizing and scoring (Fig. 1).

Genetic diversity assessment

Genetic diversity parameters were calculated using NTSYSpc v2.11 software15. Genetic similarity coefficients were computed based on the Jaccard index, and phylogenetic relationships were visualized using UPGMA (Unweighted Pair-Group Method with Arithmetic Average) clustering. Genetic diversity parameters, including the number of alleles per locus, observed heterozygosity (Ho), genetic diversity (expected heterozygosity, He), and polymorphism rate, were calculated using PowerMarker v3.25 software. Genetic diversity (He) was estimated according to Nei’s gene diversity index, while observed heterozygosity (Ho) was calculated as the proportion of heterozygous individuals per locus. The polymorphism rate was calculated as the ratio of polymorphic loci to the total number of loci analyzed for each primer16.

Results

Phenotypic analysis

Phenological observations

Leaf budburst in the Foşa population occurred 25–30 days earlier than in the Yomra population. Both populations exhibited protandrous-homogamous flowering patterns, but Foşa showed earlier timing for both male and female flowering compared to Yomra. In the Foşa population, the average leafing occurred on 10–20 March, male flowering on 15–25 December, female flowering on 10–20 January, while in the Yomra population, leafing occurred on 5–15 April, male flowering on 25 January-5 February, and female flowering on 5–15 February (Table 2).

Hierarchical cluster analysis

Hierarchical clustering based on yield, and 19 nut characteristics revealed two main groups, each subdivided into two subgroups (Fig. 3).

According to records and molecular analyses, the accessions 61AN41, 61AN27, 61AI15 and 61AI16, known as Yomra, were located within the group where Foşa accessions were predominantly found. Conversely, the accession 61OR25, which is part of the Foşa group, was located within the dendrogram where the Yomra accessions were found. This indicates that, the accession 61OR25 showed variation due to environmental conditions. According to the constructed tree, the genetic diversity in group (A), which includes the Yomra group (B), was observed to be richer compared to the Foşa population.

PCA analysis

PCA effectively demonstrated the distribution of hazelnut accessions based on agromorphological characteristics. The first principal component (PC1) explained 53.5% of the total variation, while the second component (PC2) accounted for 14.0%, together explaining 67.5% of the total variation (Fig. 4).

When examining the distribution of accessions based on pomological characteristics, it was determined that accessions 61AN41, 61AI16, 61OR14, 61AI15, and 61YA61 show distinct differences from the others. The accessions 61AN41, 61AI15, and 61AI16, which showed differences in the dendrogram, also exhibited variations in the PCA.

Molecular analysis

SSR analysis

SSR markers are highly effective for variety identification, synonym detection, and genetic relationship assessment, and have been successfully used to reveal genetic diversity and population structure in hazelnut germplasm3,17–19.

Genetic relationships between Foşa and Yomra accessions were determined using 10 SSR primers. SSR analysis revealed polymorphic band lengths ranging from 53 to 158 base pairs, with 1 to 8 bands per primer and a total of 45 bands. Average genetic diversity was 0.53, observed heterozygosity was 0.31, and polymorphism rates ranged from 0 to 0.75 with a mean of 0.48 (Table 5).

Principal coordinate analysis (PCoA)

PCoA identified the most genetically divergent individuals within each population. In the Foşa population, accessions 61AI27, 61YA58, 61YA52, and 61OR33 were most genetically distant, while in the Yomra population, nine accessions (61YA16, 61YA35, 61YA60, 61AN03, 61AN30, 61AN34, 61AN41, 61OR59, and 61AI34) showed maximum genetic divergence (Fig. 6).

According to the two-dimensional plane graph obtained from PCoA based on SSR analysis of Foşa and Yomra accessions, accessions belonging to the Foşa variety are concentrated in the II and III quadrants, while accessions belonging to the Yomra variety are concentrated in the I and IV quadrants.

Population structure analysis

Structure analysis with K values ranging from 1 to 10 determined the optimal population subdivision at K = 2 based on ΔK values calculated with Structure Harvester. This analysis confirmed genetic separation of Foşa and Yomra varieties and revealed their distinct genetic structures (Fig. 6).

Two accessions showed admixed genetic profiles: 61YA52 (membership coefficients: 0.589 for subset 1, 0.411 for subset 2) and 61YA58 (0.685 for subset 1, 0.342 for subset 2). These accessions demonstrate partial similarity to both groups and are crucial for genetic diversity conservation and future breeding programs.

Population structure genetic analysis

Accessions determined from Ortahisar, Yomra, Arsin and Araklı districts were divided into sub-sets ranging from 1 to10 with the Structure program. After the variance components obtained were tested 100,000 times with random permutation, DeltaK value was found as K = 2 with Structure Harvester. According to the result, it was determined that the population would be divided into two sub-clusters. As a result of the analysis, accessions of Foşa and Yomra varieties were genetically separated and their genetic structure was revealed (Fig. 7).

Population subset analysis reveals that the Yomra population belongs to the second subset indicated in green, while the Foşa population is identified as the first subset in red. According to the Jaccard coefficient calculated for population cluster analysis, the highest coefficient of 0.997 was found in accession 61AN29, which belongs to the second cluster, identified as the Yomra population. Furthermore, for genotype 61YA52, the coefficient of membership to the first subset is 0.589, while it is 0.411 for the second subset. For accession 61YA58, the coefficient for the first subset is 0.685, and for the second subset, it is 0.342. These two accessions show partial resemblance to both groups. Therefore, for the preservation of genetic diversity and for use in future breeding programs, it is crucial to preserve accessions 61YA52 and 61YA58.

Discussion

Phenological characteristics of populations

The type of flowering in hazelnut varies depending on variety and climatic conditions. The chilling requirement for male flowers is generally lower than that for female flowers (Mehlenbacher and Molnar, 2021). In this study, it was determined that the Foşa and Yomra hazelnut cultivars differ in terms of their phenological characteristics. According to Balık et al.20,, the Foşa variety exhibits leaf budburst between March 15 and 25. Its flowering phenology is characterized as protandrous to homogamous, with anthesis of male catkins occurring from January 15 to 20, followed by the onset of female flowering between January 25 and 30. The Foşa population leafs out approximately one month earlier than the Yomra population. In both populations, male and female flowering display a protandrous to homogamous pattern, with the Foşa population flowering earlier overall. The variety × altitude interaction had a significant effect on the timing of male and female flowering (P < 0.001) but exhibited no significant effect on leaf budburst. Since no prior studies have documented the phenological traits of the Yomra cultivar, a direct comparison was not possible. Therefore, this study provides the first comprehensive description of the phenological characteristics of the Yomra variety.

Statistical detection of population variation and quantitative trait coefficients

This study represents the first application of discriminant analysis methods for examining variation in hazelnut populations, providing a novel methodological contribution to the field. Anatolia represents one of the earliest regions of hazelnut cultivation, and centuries of traditional cultivation have generated extensive genetic variation.

While many hazelnut varieties are monoclonal3; Mehlenbacher and Molnar, 2021), several Turkish varieties including Tombul4,5, Çakıldak6,7, Palaz7, and Kalınkara5 comprise clones with similar phenotypes but distinct genetic origins.

Comparative analysis with other studies shows varying patterns of quantitative trait variation. Iranian hazelnut genotypes showed lowest coefficient of variation for fruit size (CV = 9.25%) and highest for nut weight (CV = 52.31%)21. Karakaya7, reported much lower variation in Çakıldak clones (CV = 0.03–3.08%), while our study detected greater population variation, reflecting the broader genetic diversity within these Turkish populations.

Yield is a critical factor in fruit breeding programs and is influenced by environmental conditions and cultural practices. Our yield values align with previous studies: Tombul clones ranged 0.60–9.51 g/cm²5, Çakıldak clones 1.91–13.79 g/cm²7, and 21.35–45.80 g/cm²6. International comparisons show Italian varieties yielding 13–104 g/cm²22, hybrid varieties 10.4–17.5 g/cm²23, and other hybrids 64.1–83.5 g/cm²24.

Strong correlations exist between yield and nuts per cluster, with heritability estimated at 0.6725. Literature values for nuts per cluster include: 1.9–5.0.9.0 in Yomra populations26, 3–4 in hybrid varieties27, 1.5–3.4 in Çakıldak clones28, and 1.83–2.63 in Tonda Gentile Romana clones29.

Nut and kernel weights show high heritability (0.63 and 0.67, respectively25;. Comparative studies report nut weights of 2.39–4.61 g and kernel weights of 1.10–2.40 g in wild populations30, while Iranian genotypes showed 0.90–2.87 g (nut) and 0.20–1.17 g (kernel)21.

Nut apex shape is highly desirable in hazelnut varieties, with heritability of 0.6525. Kernel apex shape index values reported in literature include 1.15–1.417, 0.99–1.25 in Çakıldak clones6, and 1.05–1.28 in Tombul clones5.

Kernel cavity size correlates positively with kernel size, being absent in small, dense kernels. Environmental factors, particularly temperature, influence cavity characteristics and browning31. Previous studies reported cavity sizes of 0.96–3.11 mm in Yomra clones26, 0.90–4.10 mm28, and 2.68–3.17 mm in Çakıldak clones32.

Blanching rate is a critical quality parameter with heritability of 0.64, decreasing at higher elevations and in high-yield years33. Literature values include 45.33–75.20% for Foşa34, 89.00% for both Foşa and Yomra2, and 87.00–99.00% for Yomra26.

Molecular analysis of SSR-based variation detection

Over 800 SSR primers have been developed for hazelnut research35, facilitating comprehensive genetic diversity studies across global hazelnut collections. Comparative SSR studies show varying levels of genetic diversity: Corylus americana genotypes showed polymorphism rates of 0.54–0.84 with average allele numbers of 7.3336. International hazelnut varieties and genotypes showed heterozygosity rates of 0.71 and polymorphism rates of 0.7811, while another study found heterozygosity rates of 0.69–0.85 and polymorphism rates of 0.04–0.11, identifying 42 misidentified genotypes or clones37. Turkish studies using different molecular markers report high polymorphism: Çakıldak clones showed 50.00–100.00.00.00% polymorphism using ISSR and SRAP markers7, while Karafındık clones showed 100% polymorphism with ISSR primers5.

Population structure studies consistently reveal hierarchical clustering patterns. Large-scale studies identified numerous duplicates among international collections: 72 duplicates among 270 genotypes38, with varieties classified into multiple main groups and subgroups. Turkish varieties typically divide into 2–3 main groups with multiple subgroups: Tombul clones showed two main groups and four subgroups, while Karafındık clones formed three groups5.

Genetic similarity coefficients across different hazelnut populations range widely: 0.4117–0.9342 in Tombul and Kara populations5, 0.3636–0.9606 in Palaz and Çakıldak populations7, and 0.37–0.78 in Portuguese populations39–44. Our similarity coefficient values for Yomra and Foşa populations fall within these ranges, confirming comparable genetic diversity levels.

A general correspondence was observed between molecular clustering and phenotypic differentiation among the studied accessions. The clear separation of Foşa and Yomra populations in UPGMA, STRUCTURE, and PCoA analyses was largely consistent with differences detected in phenological timing and pomological traits, particularly leaf budburst, flowering dates, nut shape, and kernel characteristics. However, a limited number of accessions showed partial discrepancies between molecular and phenotypic groupings, which may be attributed to environmental effects, clonal variation, or historical gene flow. Such cases highlight the importance of integrating molecular markers with phenotypic observations to achieve a more comprehensive understanding of population structure and varietal identity in hazelnut.

Conclusion

Understanding and quantifying population variation is essential for effective breeding program development. This study successfully employed integrated SSR molecular analysis and statistical methods to distinguish Yomra accessions within Foşa hazelnut populations as distinct local varieties with unique genetic and phenotypic characteristics.

Our analysis revealed significant genetic, pomological, and phenological diversity both within and between Foşa and Yomra populations, particularly in fruit morphology and phenological timing. The distinct genetic profiles observed across independent datasets confirm the high variability and genetic distinctiveness of these populations.

Based on these findings, we recommend prioritizing conservation of specific accessions (61AN41, 61AI16, 61OR14, 61AI15, and 61YA61) in genetic resource collections due to their unique genetic profiles and potential breeding value. These accessions represent valuable candidates for future breeding programs and should be maintained as parent materials for genetic improvement initiatives targeting enhanced hazelnut varieties.

The methodological approach developed in this study, combining discriminant analysis with SSR markers, provides a robust framework for future hazelnut genetic diversity studies and can be applied to other tree crop species facing similar taxonomic challenges.

As a result of the study, the 61AN41 accession, which was determined to be genetically and phenologically different, productive and superior in terms of nut characters, was applied to the authorized institutions in Türkiye as a variety candidate.

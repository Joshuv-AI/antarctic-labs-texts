Epitope dynamics and antigenic shifts in dengue virus serotype 2 cosmopolitan genotype with structural changes in Bangladesh from 2017 to 2023
Authors: Shaheen Alam, Selim Reza Tony, Sabik Khair, Hera Tahsin Punno, Mst Noorjahan Begum, Mustafizur Rahman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Dengue Virus Serotype 2 (DENV-2) is increasingly impacting public health, especially in South and Southeast Asia. Bangladesh faced its most severe dengue outbreak in 2023, reporting 318,623 cases and over 1,700 deaths, with the DENV-2 Cosmopolitan genotype responsible for 70.2% of cases. To better understand this event, we utilized in silico methods to explore mutational trends and epitope dynamics in global and Bangladeshi sequences from 2017 to 2023. The study aimed to provide insights into viral evolution and hypothesize how specific mutations might theoretically affect immune recognition. We analyzed 2,118 DENV-2 sequences from Bangladesh and other countries, focusing on the Cosmopolitan genotype. Using multiple sequence alignment against a reference sequence (Accession ID: NC_001474.2), we identified mutations. BepiPred2, BcePred, and IEDB tools were used to predict B-cell and T-cell (MHC-I and MHC-II) epitopes, while antigenicity was assessed with VaxiJen v2.0. Structural modeling of mutations in these epitopes was performed using PEPstrMOD and TM-align to evaluate potential impacts on immune recognition. The modeled epitopes were optimized and validated using UCSF Chimera and MolProbity, respectively. This step allowed us to verify the structural integrity of our models, helping to confirm that the predicted epitopes remain biologically relevant and stable.” Significant mutations were found in both structural and non-structural proteins, with distinct patterns between Bangladesh and global sequences. Mutations like K9R in the capsid and I164V in the envelope protein showed a striking increase in 2023 Bangladesh samples, with others like L247F in NS1 and I140V in NS3 also linked to structural changes that could affect immune recognition. Globally, mutations such as H148Y in prM and K388E in NS5 were more common in previous years but less so in 2023. Newly observed mutations in Bangladesh, like V64I in NS4A and Y201H in NS5, indicate local adaptation. We also noted shifts in antigenicity, where certain epitopes switched from antigenic to non-antigenic (e.g., T244A) and vice versa (e.g., I18V), potentially impacting vaccine efficacy. Our structural analysis revealed that these mutations could induce conformational changes in B-cell and T-cell epitopes, potentially influencing immune responses and facilitating immune evasion. All models generated during the structural analysis were validated using MolProbity to evaluate their accuracy and structural stability. Furthermore, despite the widespread mutations, we identified multiple conserved epitopes across B-cell, MHC-I, and MHC-II categories that maintained stable antigenicity and minimal structural deviation, suggesting their potential as stable immunogenic targets for future experimental validation and vaccine development against DENV-2 Cosmopolitan strains. This study highlights DENV-2’s evolving nature in Bangladesh and globally, underscoring the need for ongoing surveillance and consideration of these mutations in vaccine design. Insights from this analysis may inform public health strategies to reduce dengue impacts in endemic areas.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-38446-2.

Introduction

The recent infection dynamics of dengue virus (DENV), once limited to tropical climates and specific seasons, have emerged as a potential public health concern globally, with cases occurring throughout the year in many regions. The World Health Organization (WHO) reported only 505,430 cases in 2000, but by 2023, more than 6.5 million cases had been reported globally, including more than 7,300 dengue-related deaths1. South and Southeast Asia, where dengue transmission intensifies during the monsoon season, between June and September, have been severely affected by this epidemic1. In Bangladesh, dengue has reached crisis levels. The country experienced its worst-ever outbreak in 2023, with 318,623 cases and over 1,700 deaths recorded according to the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS), Bangladesh2. The unprecedented scale of the outbreak has raised concerns of a dengue virus that seems to be evolving both in transmission patterns and virulence3.

DENV, a member of the Flaviviridae family, is a positive-sense, single-stranded RNA with a 10.7 kb genome4. There are four different serotypes called DENV-1, classified into I to V; DENV-2, classified into Asian I, Asian II, cosmopolitan, American/Asian, and sylvatic; DENV-3, classified into I to IV; and DENV-4, classified into I, II, III, and sylvatic, with approximately 65% amino acid sequence similarity4–9. Bangladesh has experienced the emergence of different serotypes and lineages10–12. Notably, in 2023, DENV-2 was the most common circulating serotype at 70.2%, followed by DENV-3 at 23.9%, DENV-1 at 3.3%, and DENV-4 at 0.4%, in Bangladesh, with 2.2% cocirculation of DENV-2 and DENV-32. It has been observed that viruses with low-fidelity RNA-dependent RNA polymerases often produce quasispecies of viruses with a genome full of unique antigenic epitopes that are randomly mutated. This phenomenon may pose a significant challenge to the host immune system, potentially aiding the virus in escaping detection13,14.

Over the past few years, the prevalence of DENV-2 in Bangladesh has been increasing15–17, all of which are cosmopolitan genotypes that are correlated with an increase in severe cases and deaths3,16,17. This situation highlights the need for a detailed understanding of the genetic changes occurring in the DENV-2 cosmopolitan genotype and their impact on host immunity. To meet this requirement, we performed a comprehensive analysis of the published sequences, focusing on identifying the antigenicity of the predicted epitopes presented by B cells, MHC-I, and MHC-II alleles. Additionally, we examined mutations within these epitopes in the context of the Bangladeshi population.

Distribution and mapping of mutations observed in Bangladesh sequences from 2017 to 2023

We identified several key mutations in the DENV-2 cosmopolitan genotype, highlighting the virus’s evolving nature. Some of these changes are shown in Fig. 1. When we compared sequences from 2017 to 2022 with those from 2023 (Fig. 2), we observed significant shifts. In protein C, we detected a marked increase in K9R mutations, increasing from a low frequency of only 0.27 (6 of 22) from 2017 to 2022 to a substantially high frequency of 0.96 (25 of 26) in 2023. Other mutations, including S101T, M104I, and L108M in protein C, remained relatively stable over time.

Notable changes were also observed in the prM protein. These are D29N, K52N, T82A, M83T, M83I, V120A, I127V, M135I, H148Y, A152I, A152V, and T162A, although mutations such as H148Y are more common previously (in 5 seq in 2017 and 1 seq in 2 in 2019), but they disappeared in 2022 and reappeared with a low frequency (0.04) in 2023. On the other hand, mutations such as D29N and M83T have consistently high frequencies (ranging from 0.9 to 1), except for a few, such as A152I and M83I, which have lower frequencies (frequencies of 0.14 and 0.05).

The E protein displayed several mutations, including M6I, Q52H, E71A, R120T, V129I, H149N, I164V, T226I, I322V, N390S, I462V, I484V, and T478S. Among them, the I164V and I322V mutations had frequencies of 0.58 (29 of 50 seq) and 0.06 (3 of 50 seq) in 2017, respectively, increasing to 1.00 (40 of 40 seq) in 2023, indicating increased importance. However, T226I, which was once prevalent (in 40 sequences out of 40), completely disappeared in 2023, resulting in a distinct shift in the virus profile. All other mutations were found with frequencies ranging from 0.9 to 1. The mutational landscape of the NS1 protein has also evolved. The Q98R mutation was common in 2017 (4 of 6) but disappeared in subsequent years, including 2023. In contrast, L247F, which was less common previously, returned to prominence in 2023 (frequency 1). The I212M mutation was found in 2023, with a frequency of 0.44. For the NS4A and NS4B proteins, the V64I and I18V mutations have been newly identified in all sequences since 2023. The NS5 protein has undergone a notable change: K388E was identified in 2017 and disappeared in 2023, whereas the once prevalent I271V mutation was replaced by I271T in 2023. New mutations, such as A648V, K375R, S872T, and Y201H, have emerged. A full list of these mutations is provided in Supplementary Tables 01–03.

Comparing the DENV-2 mutation dynamics in Bangladesh with those around the world

Our comparative analysis of DENV-2 sequences from Bangladesh and worldwide revealed dramatic changes in mutation patterns, particularly in 2023 (Fig. 2). We observed that mutations such as K9R (in 1267 seq, out of 1485) in the C protein, I164V (in 1353 seq, out of 2028) in the E protein, and A648V (in 595 seq, out of 1271) in NS5 presented high frequencies (0.50–1) in 2023 sequences from both Bangladesh and globally. These mutations were previously less common in Bangladesh between 2017 and 2022. Notably, the H148Y (in 1165 seq, out of 1541) mutation in the prM protein, which is prevalent in global sequences and Bangladeshi sequences from earlier years, significantly decreased in frequency (in 1 seq, out of 26) in 2023. Emerging mutations such as I212M (in 21 seq, out of 1766) in NS1; V64I (in 15 seq, out of 750) in NS4A; I18V (in 60 seq, out of 1253) in NS4B; Y201H (in 37 seq, out of 1234); and K375R (in 16 seq, out of 521) in NS5, which were of very low frequency or absent in earlier sequences globally and in Bangladesh, have now appeared in 2023 sequences with frequencies ranging from 0.08 to 1. Conversely, mutations such as T226I (in 349 seq, out of 2028) in the E protein, Q98R (in 4 seq, out of 165) in NS1, and K388E (in 480 seq, out of 1134) in NS5 were very rare globally and in previous Bangladeshi sequences but were completely absent in 2023 Bangladeshi sequences. Additionally, global sequences revealed that both L247F (in 676 seq, out of 1353) and L247I (in 513 seq, out of 1203) in NS1 occurred with similar frequencies (approximately 0.5), and the I271V mutation in NS5 (0.7) was more common than I271T (0.18). In line with these global observations, the I271V mutation was initially found at a frequency of 1 (in 6 seq, out of 6) in Bangladeshi sequences but was replaced by I271T in 2023, with only L247F (in 25 seq, out of 25) and I271T (in 23 seq, out of 25) observed. Interestingly, the S872T mutation in NS5, which was not previously detected in any global sequence, emerged exclusively in the 2023 Bangladeshi samples with a frequency of 0.52. These data underscore the rapid evolutionary changes in DENV-2 and highlight the importance for ongoing surveillance to understand and respond to the virus’s evolving genetic landscape.

Epitope prediction for linear B-cells and T-cells and mapping of reported Bangladeshi mutations onto these predicted epitopes

We identified 86 linear epitopes of B-cell and 265 T-cell epitopes (MHC-I of 227, MHC-II of 38) regions in the reference sequence of the DENV-2 cosmopolitan genotype. Then, all of the observed mutations, identified from the sequences submitted in Bangladesh in 2023, were mapped onto these predicted epitopes. The following mutations were found in the B-cell epitope regions: K9R and S101T in the C protein; T82A, M83T, and M83I in the prM protein; M6I, E71A, R120T, H149N, T226I, and N390S in the E protein; S80T and S128L in the NS1 protein; D63E in the NS2B protein; I140V, R186K, V395I, and I461V in the NS3 protein; I18V, A19T, and T244A in the NS4B protein; and A648V and I687V in the NS5 protein. Additionally, the following mutations were found in the class I MHC T-cell epitope regions: T108A, I108M in the C protein; V120A, M135I, and T162A in the prM protein; Q52H, V129I, I164V, I322V, and I484V in the E protein; S80T, T117A, K174R, F178S, L247F, I247I, I264T, T265A, and K272R in the NS1 protein; M136I in the NS2A protein; L21F, V59I, and K60R in the NS2B protein; I140V, I333A, R337A, and V395I in the NS3 protein; D23N in the NS4A protein; F112L in the NS4B protein; and S15N. Y201H, Y247H, V335I, R430G, K570R, G605V, Q644A, K800T, A865T, in the NS5 protein. Furthermore, the following mutations were identified in the class II MHC T-cell epitope regions: M135I in the prM protein; I212M and N222S in the NS1 protein; and I140V in the NS3 protein. Among the predicted epitopes, K9R in the C protein, T226I and I164V in the E protein, I212M, L247F, and I247I in the NS1 protein, I18V in the NS4B protein, and Y201H and A648V in the NS5 protein changed their pattern of shift between the Bangladeshi and worldwide sequences.

Among these, there was one case in which T244A was converted from antigenic to non-antigenic, and three cases in which D63E, I18V, and A19T- were converted from non-antigenic to antigenic (Fig. 3); other details are given in the Supplementary Table 01.

Modeling of tertiary structure of MHC-I, MHC-II, and B-cell epitopes and validation

A total of 105 MHC-I, 29 MHC-II, and 45 B-cell epitope models were generated and validated to assess their structural accuracy and stability. The tertiary structures were modeled and refined before undergoing rigorous stereochemical validation using MolProbity. MolProbity was used to evaluate the structural integrity of MHC-I, MHC-II, and B-cell receptor models by evaluating atomic contacts, backbone geometry, and stereochemical precision. Encouragingly, all models demonstrated favorable side-chain conformations, evidenced by 100% favored rotamers and 0% bad rotamers. Proper backbone geometry was ensured by the Ramachandran plot analysis, which showed no outliers and over 98% of residues in preferred regions. The Ramachandran Z-score was within a reasonable range, with just slight variations seen in a few models. Bond quality and backbone geometry assessments verified that there were no defective bonds or angles (0%), and Cβ variations were negligible and within acceptable bounds. The absence of CaBLAM outliers and cis-proline residues further supports the models’ accuracy. Furthermore, Cα geometry was absent from every model. The details of the validation result are given in the Supplementary Tables 04 to 06.

Comparison and superposition of tertiary structures between the epitopes of the reference and mutant epitopes

The structural impact of epitope mutations was assessed using TM-score-based superimposition, revealing varying degrees of structural deviations (Fig. 6) that influence immune recognition, antigenicity, and MHC binding affinity. B-cell epitope mutations identified from Bangladeshi sequences with low TM scores (< 0.3) exhibited significant conformational deviations, which might have a potential impact on immune escape. Notably, S101T (0.27051, RMSD: 0.66), in the C gene, displayed substantial atomic displacement, which could disrupt antibody binding and reduce antigenicity. Similarly, V395I (0.1337, RMSD: 1.95) in NS3, which transitioned from an antigenic to a non-antigenic state, showed extreme structural shifts, highlighting its immune evasion potential. Additional high-deviation mutations, including demonstrated structural instability, reinforce their role in antigenic variation. Moderate TM-score mutations (0.3–0.5), such as M83T (0.33016, RMSD: 1.59) and M83I (0.34753, RMSD: 1.65) in PrM, I140V (0.34744, RMSD: 1.51) in NS3, and T244A (0.30465, RMSD: 2.25), and A19T (0.38296, RMSD: 0.37) in NS4B, R120T (0.45735, RMSD: 0.15) in the E gene, D63E (0.43412, RMSD: 1.04) in NS2B, and N390S (0.49563, RMSD: 0.58) in the E gene, exhibited structural variability. In contrast, high TM-score mutations (> 0.5), such as M6I (0.5337, RMSD: 0.77) and T226I (0.5337, RMSD: 1.02) in the E gene, I18V (0.56125, RMSD: 0.86) in NS4B, and S80T (0.5588, RMSD: 0.08) in NS1, retained substantial structural integrity, implying minimal impact on immune interactions.

Similarly, MHC-I epitope mutations exhibited distinct structural shifts, influencing MHC-I binding affinity and T-cell recognition. Low TM-score mutations (< 0.3), including G605V (0.22955, RMSD: 0.79) in NS5, and I164V (0.18627, RMSD: 1.6) in the E gene, demonstrated notable structural deviations, potentially leading to decreased antigenicity or a loss of MHC binding affinity.

For MHC-II epitopes, structural deviations were observed at varying levels, impacting antigen presentation and T-helper cell recognition. Low TM-score mutations (< 0.3), including N222S (0.26533, RMSD: 2.36) in NS1, I212M (0.24285, RMSD: 1.83) in NS1, and I140V (0.20358, RMSD: 1.57) in NS3, exhibited major structural deviations, potentially altering MHC-II binding and immune processing. Additionally, M135I (0.34198, RMSD: 3.32) in PrM demonstrated the highest structural shift in MHC-II epitopes, suggesting a potentially reduced immunogenic response. Moderate TM-score mutations (0.3–0.5), such as N222S (0.27203, RMSD: 2.2) in NS1, I212M (0.29698, RMSD: 2.69) in NS1, and I140V (0.27366, RMSD: 1.83) in NS3, exhibited moderate atomic displacement, indicating partial structural conservation. Conversely, high TM-score mutations (> 0.5), such as I212M (0.5489, RMSD: 1.88) in NS1 and I212M (0.8901, RMSD: 0.615) in NS1, retained significant structural stability, suggesting their continued recognition by MHC-II molecules.

Discussion

Bangladesh experienced the worst dengue outbreak in its history in 2023, with over 318,000 cases reported and 1,700 fatalities. 72% of infections were caused by the DENV-2 Cosmopolitan genotype1–3. This concerning increase emphasizes how crucial it is to look into the genetic evolution of DENV-2 since quick changes could impact the efficacy of the present vaccinations, diagnostic methods, and therapeutic strategies. Bangladeshi strain-specific mutations may affect the virus’s capacity to proliferate, infect people, and elude the immune system, underscoring the necessity of focused public health initiatives. Key mutations were found in both structural and non-structural genes by our investigation, including C, prM, E, NS1, NS2B, NS3, NS4B, and NS5 genes, and offer insights into how these mutations may contribute to the virus’s survival and adaptation. The capsid (C) protein plays a crucial role in RNA packaging and maintaining the virus’s structural integrity18. showed distinct mutations in Bangladeshi strains compared to global variants. One example is the K9R mutation, located near the N-terminus, which became significantly more common in Bangladesh (rising from 0.27 in 2017–2022 to 0.96 in 2023) while remaining rare elsewhere. Although this mutation caused a slight drop in antigenicity (from 0.5325 to 0.52), the structural analysis suggested moderate changes (TM-score: 0.21565, RMSD: 0.53) that could affect how the immune system recognizes the virus. Another important mutation, L108M, found within an MHC-I epitope, led to an increase in antigenicity (from 0.51 to 0.5728), which might influence T-cell responses. While the precise roles of K9R and L108M remain to be fully understood, changes in the capsid protein are believed to influence viral survival and replication efficiency19. The prM protein, essential for viral assembly and maturation20, also exhibited unique mutations in Bangladeshi isolates. Variants such as T82A, M83T, and M83I, all located within B-cell epitopes, were associated with increased antigenicity. Notably, the M83T mutation saw a significant rise in antigenicity, from 1.0814 to 1.2179. In contrast, the H148Y mutation, which was previously common both globally and in Bangladesh, nearly disappeared in 2023, possibly indicating an evolutionary trade-off balancing immune evasion with replication efficiency. Additionally, the M135I mutation, present in both MHC-I and MHC-II epitopes, showed decreased antigenicity, suggesting a potential immune escape mechanism less common in global strains. Although the exact impact of these mutations requires further investigation, alterations in the prM protein are likely to play a role in helping the virus avoid immune responses21. The envelope (E) protein, which facilitates entry into host cells and is a primary target for neutralizing antibodies, displayed a diverse range of mutations22. The I164V mutation, predominantly found in Bangladesh, significantly reduced antigenicity (0.26305), which may indicate an evolving mechanism to evade antibody-mediated neutralization. Similarly, the I322V mutation preserved the protein’s structural integrity while potentially decreasing its detectability by the immune system, suggesting a potential mechanism to escape immune surveillance without compromising viral fitness. Interestingly, the T226I mutation, once common in Bangladesh, disappeared entirely by 2023, suggesting evolutionary pressures favoring alternative mechanisms of immune escape. While the precise mechanisms for this change are not fully understood, we hypothesize that the loss of such mutations could be linked to immune pressure or viral fitness trade-offs. Mutations that provide a selective advantage—such as those aiding immune evasion or improving replication efficiency—may persist, while others, like T226I, may disappear if they do not offer the same benefits or if their presence imposes a fitness cost on the virus. This may be influenced by the host’s immune memory, where certain mutations might become less favorable as the immune response adapts. Such changes underscore the complex interplay between viral evolution, immune selection, and viral fitness, which can shape the genetic landscape of circulating strains.

While the specific effects of these mutations are still under investigation, the T219A mutation, identified in other studies, has been shown to reduce the effectiveness of neutralizing antibodies22. The NS1 protein, known for its role in immune evasion and viral replication, exhibited mutations that affected both B-cell and T-cell epitopes23. The S80T mutation, for example, led to decreased antigenicity in B-cell epitopes, indicating a possible adaptation unique to Bangladeshi strains. In contrast, the I264T mutation within an MHC-I epitope caused a sharp increase in antigenicity (from 0.3334 to 0.9577), potentially enhancing T-cell recognition. Additionally, the I212M mutation in MHC-II epitopes also showed increased antigenicity, suggesting a role in modulating immune responses. Although the functional consequences of these mutations remain unclear, previous studies have shown that changes in NS1, such as the T164S mutation, can contribute to immune evasion and increased disease severity24.

Mutations in NS2A and NS2B, which regulate membrane permeability and support viral protease activity, also revealed interesting patterns25. The D63E mutation in NS2B, for instance, shifted from non-antigenic to antigenic in Bangladeshi strains, despite being rare globally. Similarly, the V59I mutation in NS2A led to an increase in antigenicity (from 0.7657 to 1.0517), suggesting enhanced immune recognition unique to Bangladesh. While their specific effects are not fully understood, mutations in NS2A and NS2B are thought to influence viral replication, fitness, and the virus’s ability to evade immune detection25. The NS3 protein, critical for viral replication and immune evasion26, displayed mutations such as I140V, which increased antigenicity (from 0.39 to 0.4893) in B-cell epitopes, potentially improving immune recognition. Conversely, the V395I mutation was associated with reduced antigenicity across multiple epitope regions, a pattern predominantly observed in Bangladesh. Additionally, the R337K mutation in MHC-II epitopes led to moderate increases in antigenicity, pointing toward possible immune modulation. While more research is needed to understand the full impact of these mutations, NS3 changes are believed to affect viral replication efficiency and immune system interactions27. The NS4A and NS4B proteins, which are involved in the formation of the viral replication complex and the modulation of host immune responses28, exhibited mutations associated with increased antigenicity, particularly in B-cell epitopes. The I18V and A19T mutations in NS4B, for instance, shifted from non-antigenic to antigenic status, suggesting adaptations specific to Bangladesh. Similarly, the F112L mutation in NS4A resulted in higher antigenicity within MHC-I epitopes, which could enhance immune recognition. Although the specific functional roles of these mutations remain to be fully explored, changes in NS4A and NS4B are known to impact viral replication and immune evasion strategies28. In the case of the NS5 protein, the largest protein in the DENV genome, we observed mutations that appeared to affect antigenicity in unique ways29. The I687V mutation led to increased antigenicity in B-cell epitopes, while the Y247H mutation in MHC-I epitopes showed a substantial rise in antigenicity (from 0.8254 to 1.1944), suggesting region-specific immune adaptations. On the other hand, the K570R mutation in MHC-II epitopes resulted in reduced antigenicity, pointing to a potential novel immune evasion mechanism. Although more work is needed to fully understand the functional consequences of these mutations, NS5 is known to play a key role in viral replication, immune system interactions, and overall viral fitness29. The rapid antigenic changes we observed in Bangladeshi DENV-2 strains highlight a potential hurdle for current vaccination efforts. Mutations such as I164V in the E protein and I264T in NS1 may weaken the effectiveness of neutralizing antibodies, emphasizing the need for vaccines tailored to regional variants. Existing dengue vaccines, including CYD-TDV (Dengvaxia) and TAK-003, target conserved regions of the virus15, but their effectiveness in Bangladesh could theoretically be compromised by these emerging mutations. Vaccine development should consider these region-specific genetic variations to enhance protection30.

Importantly, while identifying these mutations, our study also concentrated on detecting structurally and antigenically conserved epitopes. Despite the high mutation rate typically observed in RNA viruses, including dengue, we found several epitopes (details in Table 2) that maintained minimal structural changes (low RMSD) and stable or even enhanced antigenicity scores. These conserved epitopes span B-cell, MHC-I, and MHC-II categories, making them probable candidates for next-generation vaccine design. Their stability despite mutation reflects regions likely under functional constraints, which might be less prone to immune escape.

While this study offers valuable insights, we must acknowledge its limitations. First, our data relies on GISAID sequences, which might have gaps or biases if certain regions or time periods were under-sampled. Second, focusing on the 2017–2023 window means we might miss longer-term evolutionary trends. Importantly, while computer modeling is a powerful tool, it cannot perfectly replicate the complex and dynamic nature of the human immune system. Therefore, our findings should be viewed as theoretical predictions that need to be confirmed in a real laboratory setting using binding assays and neutralization studies. Notably, our in-silico models focused on the amino acid backbone and did not account for post-translational modifications, such as glycosylation, which can shield potential epitopes on the viral surface. Additionally, while we prioritized the most prevalent HLA alleles in the studied population, this approach excludes rarer alleles, meaning individuals with less common genotypes may exhibit different immune recognition patterns not captured here. Furthermore, while the identification of conserved and antigenically stable epitopes marks a probable direction for vaccine development, their translational potential remains to be demonstrated in clinical or laboratory settings. The absence of experimental validation and clinical correlations with disease severity highlights key areas for future research. Addressing these gaps will deepen our understanding of DENV-2’s evolution and support the development of more effective public health strategies and vaccines.

Conclusion

The evolutionary dynamics of Dengue Virus Serotype 2 (DENV-2) in Bangladesh, particularly the cosmopolitan genotype, reveal a rapidly adapting virus characterized by key mutations that may enhance viral fitness, immune evasion, and transmission. Our analysis of DENV-2 sequences from 2017 to 2023 highlights prominent mutations, including K9R in the capsid, I164V in the envelope, L247F in NS1, and S872T in NS5. It is hypothesized that these mutations played a role in the increased prevalence and severity of dengue in Bangladesh, presenting challenges for vaccine development. By combining computational predictions with experimental validation, we can gain a deeper understanding of how these changes impact viral behavior, immune recognition, and vaccine efficacy, paving the way for more targeted public health interventions. This study underscores the need for multidisciplinary approaches—combining genomic surveillance, epitope mapping, and structural analysis—to combat the evolving threat of dengue and mitigate its global impact.

Retrieving DENV-2 sequences and identifying mutations

DENV-2 sequences were retrieved from the GISAID EpiArbo Dengue database (gisaid.org) for 2017–2023 to ensure data consistency and minimize selection bias. GISAID was chosen as the sole database due to its high-quality, curated sequences, standardized metadata, global representation, and prevention of redundant submissions. To retrieve relevant sequences, search terms such as “Dengue Virus 2,” “Cosmopolitan Genotype,” and the specific years 2017–2023 were used, and filters were applied to include only complete genome sequences and complete gene sizes. Inclusion criteria for sequence selection involved only complete genome sequences with complete gene sizes, well-annotated metadata, and classification under the Cosmopolitan Genotype. Moreover, we included mutations that occurred more than once throughout the year or two or more times in a particular year for further analysis. Additionally, we collected the reference sequence (Accession ID: NC_001474.2) from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI). It has served as a benchmark in numerous studies31–35, providing a consistent framework for comparative analyses. Its widespread adoption in recent literature underscores its reliability as a reference. The nucleotide sequences were first converted into amino acid sequences using ExPASy Translate (https://web.expasy.org/translate/), followed by multiple sequence alignment using the online version of MAFFT v2024/Aug algorithm (https://mafft.cbrc.jp/alignment/server/index.html) against the reference genome. Mutations were identified using BioEdit v7.2.636, with positional annotations derived from the GenBank format of the NCBI reference genome (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/NC_001474.2/).

Mutation frequencies were determined using SPSS v20.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R v4.3.2 (R Core Team, 2024), which were also used to plot the mutation data and visualize mutation frequencies using lollipop plots in R (ggplot2 v3.4.4) (Wickham, 2016). The complete workflow for the study, including sequence retrieval, alignment, and mutation analysis, is summarized in Fig. 8. Prediction of epitopes and antigenicity

T-cell epitope prediction

To predict Cytotoxic T Lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes, we used NetMHCpan EL v4.1, a widely validated tool integrated within the Immune Epitope Database (IEDB)37. This machine-learning-based tool assesses peptide binding affinity to Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) Class I molecules using peptide-MHC interaction datasets and structural modeling. Given the importance of allele-specific predictions, we selected the most commonly occurring HLA Class I alleles in the Bangladeshi population, including A33 (17.02%), A24 (16.31%), A11 (15.60%), A02 (14.54%), and A*01 (9.57%), along with B15 (19.5%), B35 (17.02%), B44 (9.22%), B52 (8.51%), B40 (6.74%), and B51 (6.03%)38. We applied a 1% threshold to ensure stringent selection, meaning only the top 1% of peptides with the highest binding affinity were retained as potential MHC Class I epitopes. For Helper T Lymphocyte (HTL) epitopes, we utilized IEDB’s MHC-II binding prediction tool, which applies position-specific scoring matrices (PSSMs) and artificial neural networks to assess peptide binding to MHC Class II molecules. The prediction was optimized using the most frequent MHC Class II alleles in Bangladesh, including DRB115 (29.07%), DRB107 (21.28%), DRB112 (11.35%), and DRB104 (10.28%)38. Given the broader peptide-binding range of MHC Class II molecules, we applied a 10% threshold, filtering out weakly binding peptide-allele pairs while preserving a diverse set of potentially immunogenic epitopes. As a result, we identified 227 MHC Class I binding epitopes and 38 MHC Class II binding epitopes, which were further analyzed for antigenicity.

B-cell epitope prediction

For B-cell epitope prediction, we used BepiPred-2.0 (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/BepiPred-2.0/) and BcePred (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/bcepred/bcepred_submission.html), two widely used bioinformatics tools. BepiPred-2.0 applies a random forest machine-learning approach trained on epitope datasets from IEDB to predict continuous B-cell epitopes based on amino acid composition, hydrophilicity, and flexibility indices39. The amino acid-translated peptides of the reference sequence were uploaded in FASTA format into BepiPred-2.0, where we applied a threshold of 0.5 to identify potential epitopes. To further validate these predictions, we used BcePred software, where the same FASTA file was submitted, and the following thresholds were applied for different physicochemical properties: antigenic propensity (1.8), hydrophobicity and accessibility (2.0), polarity (2.3), exposed surface (2.4), and flexibility, turns, and combined properties (1.9). The predicted B-cell epitopes were visually represented in blue and underlined in the output. The overlapping display of predicted epitopes allowed confirmation of the common regions identified by both tools40.

Antigenicity prediction

To assess the antigenicity of the predicted epitopes, we employed VaxiJen v2.0, a sequence-independent antigenicity prediction tool that uses auto-cross covariance (ACC) transformation of protein sequences41. The epitope sequences were submitted into the PROTEIN sequence box, and the “Virus” option was selected as the organism. A threshold of 0.4 was applied to enhance prediction accuracy42–44. The output classified each epitope as either a probable antigen or a non-antigen, along with an antigenicity score. This methodology follows a previously established protocol, described in detail at the Jenner Institute website (http://www.jenner.ac.uk/VaxiJen).

Structural modeling and model validation

To analyze the structural properties of the selected epitopes, we employed a multi-step computational workflow integrating PEPstrMOD, UCSF Chimera, and MolProbity for 3D structure modeling, optimization, and validation. Initially, PEPstrMOD was used to predict the 3D structures of the selected epitopes based on their FASTA sequences. Both the reference and mutated epitopes (from 2017 to 2023) were processed through PEPstrMOD, which generates tertiary structures by considering secondary structure constraints and backbone flexibility45.

Following initial model generation, UCSF Chimera (v1.18) was employed to refine and optimize the 3D structures. Chimera allows for molecular visualization, energy minimization, and geometric correction to enhance model stability46. Each 3D model was then assessed using the MolProbity server, a widely used validation tool that evaluates steric clashes, atomic packing quality, bond angles, and Ramachandran plot statistics47. Based on MolProbity’s report, we applied several optimizations, including adjusting Ramachandran’s favored regions, correcting rotamer outliers, and modifying torsion angles to achieve a more biologically relevant structure. The optimized models were re-evaluated in MolProbity to ensure improved geometric accuracy before proceeding to structural superimposition for comparative analysis.

Structural superimposition unveiling mutation effects

To investigate structural changes caused by mutations, we performed superimposition and structural alignment using the TM-align server48. This tool quantitatively evaluates structural similarity by comparing the 3D conformations of the reference and mutated epitopes. It has been widely applied in epitope structural analysis, particularly in cases where mutations influence antigenic properties and immune recognition49. The structural similarity of epitopes was assessed based on the Template Modeling (TM) score, a widely used metric in protein structure comparisons. A TM score of 1.0 indicates perfect structural alignment, while a score below 0.3 suggests random structural resemblance. Epitopes with a TM score above 0.5 were considered to have similar structural folds, suggesting that mutations did not induce drastic conformational changes. Previous studies utilizing TM-score analysis have demonstrated that structural shifts in epitopes can impact their immunogenic potential, highlighting the importance of such evaluations in vaccine and therapeutic development45. By applying this comparative structural analysis, we assessed whether the predicted epitopes retained their functional integrity, making them viable candidates for further immunogenic assessments.

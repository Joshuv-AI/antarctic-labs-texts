Three genome assemblies of Opsariichthys bidens from Yangzte River, Pearl River and Qiantang River basins
Authors: Xiaojun Xu, Xinhui Zhang, Wenzhi Guan, Jiongying Yu, Baolong Niu, Shaokui Yi, Bao Lou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Opsariichthys bidens is an endemic minnow, mainly distributed in China as an emerging aquaculture species. In this study, O. bidens collected from Yangzte River (YR), Pearl River (PR) and Qiantang River (QR) basins were used for karyotypic study and genome sequencing. Using PacBio long reads and Hi-C data, we assembled high-quality O. bidens genomes of 852.41 Mb (YR), 843.11 Mb (PR) and 840.94 Mb (QR) with scaffold N50 lengths of 21.01 Mb, 23.62 Mb and 24.75 Mb, respectively, of which 90.39%, 95.67% and 99.01% were anchored to 37, 38 and 39 chromosomes, respectively. 26,556 (YR), 25,036 (PR) and 26,283 (QR) protein-coding genes were predicted, respectively. The karyotype of O. bidens from YR, PR and QR were 2 N = 74 (6 m + 6sm + 4st + 58t), 2 N = 76 (4 m + 6sm + 4st + 62t) and 2 N = 78 (4 m + 4sm + 4st + 66t), respectively. Collinearity analysis and telomere predictions indicated that the observed chromosomal evolution was driven by Robertsonian translocation. These genome assemblies facilitate cryptic species determination, evolutionary study and genetic breeding of genus Opsariichthys.

Background & Summary

Chinese hooksnout carp (Opsariichthys bidens) is an endemic Cypriniformes minnow in East Asia, and mainly distributed in China. Due to its superior taste, fast growth and high economic value, O. bidens has become an emerging aquaculture fish species. This species was successively placed in Cyprinidae, Leuciscinae, Opsariichthys1 and Cyprinidae, Danioninae, Opsariichthys2. In the latest phylogenetic classification of bony fishes, O. bidens was assigned to Xenocyprididae, Opsariichthyinae, Opsariichthys3. The species composition of genus Opsariichthys in China has undergone a major adjustment. Based on the differences of lateral line scales and gill raker numbers, two subspecies, Opsariichthys uncirostris bidens and Opsariichthys uncirostris amurensis, were described in China1. However, due to the high similarity of morphology, these two subspecies were merged into O. bidens2. Thus, O. bidens had been considered a single species across the whole of China for quite a long time.

An obvious sexual dimorphism has been observed in O. bidens, with males presenting a substantial growth strength than females. We have initiated an all-male breeding program in O. bidens since 2020. Surprisingly, in our first genomic study of a cultured female O. bidens in Zhejiang, we found that the chromosome number (2n = 78) was unique4, different from the previously reported chromosome numbers of O. bidens from Yangzte River (2n = 74) and Pearl River (2n = 76) basins5,6. However, the second report of O. bidens genome on a male individual collected from a fish nursery in Zhejiang showed that the chromosome number was 767. This contradictory result may be caused by the commercial circulation of O. bidens germplasm resources. The number of chromosomes and karyotype of a species are stable characteristics that can reflect its basic genetic information. Different karyotypes often reflect speciation events within phylogenies as they may greatly contribute to development of postzygotic isolation that serves as an effective barrier to gene flow8,9. In addition, molecular phylogeography researches based on cytochrome b fragments have shown that Chinese O. bidens consists of five mtDNA lineages with high genetic divergence among them10,11. Obviously, the taxonomy of Chinese O. bidens is still inconclusive, and possibly there are multiple cryptic species of O. bidens in China.

Hence, we present three chromosome-scale genome assemblies of wild O. bidens from Yangzte River (O. bidens YR), Pearl River (O. bidens PR) and Qiantang River basins (O. bidens QR). These three chromosome-level genomes of O. bidens provide valuable genomic resources for a series of model freshwater fish species with Robertsonian translocations, and will greatly facilitate the development of sex-specific marker, taxonomic research and germplasm resource management of Opsariichthys species.

Methods

Sample collection, library construction and sequencing

Adult O. bidens individuals of Yangzte River, Pearl River and Qiantang River basins were collected from Taoxi River (108.2E and 31.3 N) in Chongqing, Wengjiang River (114.1E and 24.3 N) in Shaoguan (Guangdong Province) and Huangze River (120.9E and 29.5 N) in Shengzhou (Zhejiang Province), respectively (Fig. S1 in the Supplementary file). A male individual from Shengzhou (body weight 56.2 g) and a female individual each from Chongqing (body weight 40.6 g) and Shaoguan (body weight 36.4 g) were used for genome and transcriptome sequencing. Genomic DNA of each sample was extracted from muscle tissue using the QIAamp DNA Mini Kit (Qiagen, Valencia, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The quality and quantity of the extracted DNA were assessed via agarose gel electrophoresis and an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA, USA). An Illumina DNA library of O. bidens QR with an insert size of 350 bp was constructed using the Genomic DNA Sample Prep kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions, and sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq X Ten platform using a 150-bp paired-end strategy. For long-read sequencing, SMRTbell libraries were prepared using the SMRTbell Expression Template Prep Kit 2.0 (Pacific Biosciences, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For O. bidens QR and O. bidens PR, two libraries were sequenced on a PacBio Sequel II platform (Pacific Biosciences, Menlo Park, USA) by the CLR model. In total, 168.00 Gb and 162.70 Gb raw PacBio CLRs of O. bidens QR and O. bidens PR were generated, comprising 197.63× and 191.41× coverage of the estimated genome sizes, respectively (Table 1). For O. bidens YR, the library was sequenced on the PacBio Sequel IIe platform (Pacific Biosciences, Menlo Park, USA) by the CCS model. A total of 26.99 Gb HiFi reads with N50 sizes of 13.56 kb were obtained, which covered 31.75× of O. bidens YR genome (Table 1).

For genome scaffolding, Hi-C libraries were prepared. Liver tissue was used for O. bidens PR, and muscle tissue was used for O. bidens YR and O. bidens QR. The Hi-C libraries were constructed following the standard protocols described previously12. DNA was cross-linked with 4% formaldehyde, digested by MboI, labeled with biotin-14-DCTP, and then linked with T4 DNA ligase. The ligated DNA was cut into 200~600-bp fragments and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). About 94.48 Gb, 106.26 Gb and 103.22 Gb of raw data were obtained for O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR, respectively (Table 1).

For gene structure prediction, transcriptome sequencing was performed with five tissues of O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR, respectively (Table 1). The RNA was extracted using Tiangen RNAprep pure tissue kit (Tiangen Biotech, China). The RNA concentration and integrity were examined with a NanoDrop 8000 Spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA) and an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, USA), respectively. Only those RNA samples with OD260/280 ≥ 1.8 and RNA integrity ≥ 7.0 were selected for transcriptome sequencing. The equally mixed RNA samples of O. bidens YR and O. bidens PR were used to construct the RNA-Seq libraries using NEBNext UltraTM RNA Library Prep Kit (NEB, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions13. The libraries were sequenced on an Illumina Novaseq 6000 platform, and a total of 14.06 Gb and 14.11 Gb raw reads were obtained for O. bidens YR and O. bidens PR, respectively (Table 1). For O. bidens QR, the RNA samples from 5 tissues were equally mixed, and an Iso-Seq library was constructed according to the PacBio standard protocols with the BluePippin size selection system (Sage Science, MA, USA). The library was sequenced on a PacBio Sequel II platform (Pacific Biosciences, Menlo Park, USA), and a total of 51.41 Gb full-length transcriptome reads were obtained (Table 1).

Karyotypic analysis

One adult male individual each for O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR were used for karyotyping experiment, according to the published pipeline5. Chromosomes were photographed using a Leica DM4 B fluorescence microscope (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany). Chromosome classifications were made by the standardized nomenclature14. The result showed that the karyotype formulas of O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR were 2 N = 74 (6 m + 6sm + 4st + 58t) (Fig. 1a), 2N = 76 (4 m + 6sm + 4st + 62t) (Fig. 1b) and 2N = 78 (4 m + 4sm + 4st + 66t) (Fig. 1c), respectively.Fig. 1Hi-C heatmaps and karyotypes of (a) O. bidens YR; (b) O. bidens PR; (c) O. bidens QR.

Genome assembly

The PacBio HiFi reads of O. bidens YR were assembled into contigs using hifiasm v0.19.815 with default parameters. The PacBio CLRs of O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR were assembled using the FALCON v2.1.0 assembler16, and the assembled contigs of O. bidens QR were further polished with Illumina reads using NextPolish v1.4.0 software17. Subsequently, the Hi-C reads were mapped to the assembled contigs using HiC-Pro v3.018. The unique mapped reads were taken as input for 3D-DNA v3.0 pipeline with parameters “-r 0”19. Juicebox v1.11.0820 was employed to visualize before manually adjusting the candidate assemblies. The assemblies had a total size of 852.41 Mb, 843.11 Mb and 840.94 Mb, containing 228, 450 and 98 scaffolds with N50 sizes of 21.01 Mb, 23.62 Mb and 24.75 Mb for O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR, respectively. Finally, a total of 37, 38 and 39 chromosomes were anchored (Fig. 1), which contained 90.39%, 95.67% and 99.01% of the assembled contigs for O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR, respectively. Genome completeness was estimated using BUSCO v5.4.5 (Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs)21 with the actinopterygii_odb9 database (Table 2). Genome annotation

The annotation pipeline employed for predicting repeat elements consisted of both homology-based and de novo approaches. In the homology-based approach, Tandem Repeats Finder v4.07 was applied to search for tandem repeats22. Transposable elements (TEs) were identified using RepeatMasker v4.0.6 and RepeatProteinMask v4.0.623. For de novo annotation, RepeatModeler v1.0.824 and LTR_FINDER v1.0.625 were employed to generate de novo repeat libraries, and RepeatMasker was used to annotate repeat elements against these repeat libraries. A total of 360.05 Mb (42.24%), 356.29 Mb (42.26%) and 356.64 Mb (42.41%) repetitive sequences were annotated in O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR genomes, respectively. Subsequently, the repetitive regions of the genome were masked prior to gene prediction.

For protein-coding gene prediction, a comprehensive strategy combining homology-based prediction, transcript-based prediction and de novo prediction was employed. For de novo predictions, the gene structures were identified with AUGUSTUS v3.2.126. For homology-based predictions, protein sequences from Danio rerio, Lepisosteus oculatus, Labeo rohita, Z. platypus, Ctenopharyngodon idella and Onychostoma macrolepis were downloaded from NCBI and GeMoMa v1.6.427 was adopted with default parameters. For transcript-based prediction, the short reads of O. bidens YR and O. bidens PR generated from the Illumina platform were assembled with Trinity v2.5.128, and the full-length transcripts of O. bidens QR were generated using ISO-Seq pipeline with PacBio platform. The gene structures were identified using PASA v2.3.329. Finally, gene sets were integrated by the Evidence Modeler (EVM) pipeline v1.030. A total of 26,556, 25,036 and 26,283 protein-coding genes were predicted in O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR, respectively (Table 3). Chromosomal fusion of O. bidens YR and O. bidens PR

Based on the protein coding sequences and gene structure, JCVI v 19021335 was used to perform chromosomal collinearity analysis among O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR. Synteny analysis between O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR shows that Chr10 of O. bidens PR is collinear with Chr10 and Chr39 of O. bidens QR (Fig. 2a, blue line). As for O. bidens YR and O. bidens QR, Chr24 of O. bidens YR is collinear with Chr24 and Chr39 of O. bidens QR (Fig. 2a, red line), while Chr34 of O. bidens YR is collinear with Chr34 and Chr38 of O. bidens QR (Fig. 2a, green line).Fig. 2(a) Synteny between genomes of O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR; (b) Telomere prediction in O. bidens PR genome; (c) Telomere prediction in O. bidens YR genome; (d) Telomere prediction in O. bidens QR genome.

(a) Synteny between genomes of O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR; (b) Telomere prediction in O. bidens PR genome; (c) Telomere prediction in O. bidens YR genome; (d) Telomere prediction in O. bidens QR genome.

QuarTeT v1.1.136 was used to make telomere prediction on the chromosomes of three genomes. Most telomeres were predicted located at both ends of the chromosomes. However, telomere sequences were detected at 25.45 Mb on Chr10 (a total of 33.34 Mb) of O. bidens PR (Fig. 2b), and at 15.65 Mb on Chr24 (a total of 16.8 Mb) of O. bidens YR (Fig. 2c). These results indicated that the chromosomal evolution among O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR was driven by chromosome fusion.

Data Records

The raw data of PacBio HiFi long reads, Hi-C reads and RNA-seq data for the five tissues from O. bidens YR were submitted to the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) SRA (Sequence Read Archive) database with accession number SRP47292937 under BioProject accession number PRJNA1042903. For O. bidens PR, the raw data of PacBio long reads, Hi-C reads and RNA-seq data for the five tissues had been deposited in SRA database with accession number SRP47285038 under PRJNA1042689. For O. bidens QR, the raw data of Illumina short reads, PacBio long reads, Hi-C reads and Iso-Seq data for the five tissues had been deposited in SRA database with accession number SRP47242539 under PRJNA1029376. The assembled genomes of O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR have been deposited at GenBank under the accession GCA_037194365.140, GCA_037194315.141 and GCA_037194245.142, respectively. The annotation files of O. bidens YR, O. bidens PR and O. bidens QR are available in Figshare43–45.

Technical Validation

The quality scores across all bases and GC content of the Illumina raw sequencing data were inspected by FastQC v0.11.9 (https://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc/). We conducted a 17-mer distribution analysis to estimate the target genome size based on the short-insert library. We calculated the genome size (G) using the following formula as reported before: G = k_num/peak_depth (G is the genome size, K_depth represents the k-mer depth, and K_num stands for the total number of 17-mers). The assembled genome and protein-coding genes integrity were evaluated using BUSCO, and the completeness exceeded 90%, indicating the assemblies and annotations are high-quality. The telomere was identified by the quarTeT v1.1.136 software using the normalized and unified sequence “AACCCT” for search. Those continuous and high-frequency regions were regarded as candidate telomere regions.

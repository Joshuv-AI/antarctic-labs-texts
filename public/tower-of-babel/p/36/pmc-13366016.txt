IFPHOR0068 Epidemiology and Outcomes of Acute Kidney Injury at a Tertiary Hospital in Johannesburg, South Africa: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Abdul Rhaman Kafagi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAcute Kidney Injury (AKI) carries significant morbidity and mortality globally, yet data from sub-Saharan Africa remain scarce. South Africa presents a clinically distinct context characterised by high HIV burden, resource constraints, and formal rationing of kidney replacement therapy (KRT).MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted at Helen Joseph Hospital, Johannesburg. All adult inpatients referred to nephrology with AKI between July 2020 and December 2021 were included. AKI was defined and staged per KDIGO 2012 criteria. Descriptive statistics, bivariate analysis, and multivariable logistic regression were performed.Results224 patients were included. Median age was 50 years (IQR 37–62); 53.1% were male. HIV infection was present in 82 patients (36.6%). Stage 3 AKI accounted for 71.9% of cases; sepsis was the predominant aetiology (50.0%). KRT was required in 89 patients (39.7%), ventilatory support in 96 (42.9%), and vasopressors in 36 (16.1%). In-hospital mortality was 45.1%. Mortality rose from 21.4% with no organ support to 88.0% with all three modalities (p < 0.001), and from 19.4% at lactate <2 mmol/L to 88.2% at > 8 mmol/L (p < 0.001). On multivariable analysis, ventilatory support (OR 5.02, 95%CI 2.62–9.63; p < 0.001) and admission lactate (OR 1.21/mmol/L, 95%CI 1.03–1.42; p = 0.020) independently predicted mortality. Among 123 survivors, 68.3% achieved full renal recovery.ConclusionsAKI in this cohort was severe, sepsis-driven, and highly lethal. Ventilatory support and admission lactate were the strongest independent mortality predictors. Findings highlight the unique challenges of AKI management in resource-limited settings and the need for larger multicentre African studies.

Factors affecting career decision-making of stomatology undergraduate students in China at graduation: a cross-sectional study on selection dilemmas
Authors: Cai Wen, Xingyu Lyu, Qing Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGiven today’s competitive climate, graduates in stomatology face the dilemma of deciding whether to pursue higher education, obtain a master’s degree, or focus on clinical careers. However, their career planning tendencies and decision-making factors have not been clarified. This study aimed to analyze these aspects among stomatology undergraduate students at the Southwest Medical University (SWMU) in China.MethodsThis cross-sectional study between July and August 2022 included undergraduate students majoring in stomatology at SWMU. A cross-sectional online questionnaire was used to gather data on the respondents’ plans after graduation. The questionnaire included inquiries into their intentions regarding postgraduate education and standardized training. Additionally, the survey explored whether the respondents preferred academic or professional master’s degrees, which stomatology subspecialty they preferred, and the factors that influenced their choices.ResultsOne hundred dental undergraduate students participated in this survey. Results indicated that 91.0% of respondents chose to pursue a master’s degree after graduation. Moreover, 80.2% of the respondents chose the professional master rather than the academic master as their first choice. Orthodontics, oral and maxillofacial surgery, and oral implantology were the top three subspecialties that students preferred. Personal interest, expected revenue, and family suggestions were the main factors influencing subspecialty selection.ConclusionsThe study reveals that stomatology undergraduate students face challenges and a dilemma at graduation. Due to the fierce competition in the job market, graduates aspire to pursue postgraduate studies instead of entering the workforce immediately after graduation. Students need to understand the employment landscape in oral medicine and familiarize themselves with the work patterns of various subspecialties while remaining flexible in charting their career paths.Keywords: Postgraduation, Stomatology, Career paths, Standardized training, Subspecialty, Neijuan

Background

China’s stomatology(dental) education system used to rely primarily on undergraduate education, with graduates often finding employment right after completing a five-year program. However, as the undergraduate and postgraduate enrollment expanded, the employment competition increased, leading to a popular term in Chinese society called “Neijuan”.

“Neijuan” (in Chinese Pinyin) refers to the state of disordered and pointless competition, which is a self-defeating cycle of increased effort that leads to diminished returns (Some institutions translate “neijuan” into “involution” in English, but “involution” does not fully convey the meaning of “neijuan,” therefore, we use the original Chinese word “neijuan” consistently). “Neijuan” in China describes a societal trend in which individuals feel compelled to work harder, pushing themselves to the limit to keep up with their peers [1]. Marked by high competition rates, rapid development, and intense social pressures, “neijuan” results in a relentless pursuit of success and achievement, even in the face of diminishing returns, leading to a sense of exhaustion and burnout, which ultimately undermines personal well-being and happiness [2, 3].

China’s stomatology education system comprises school studies at the undergraduate, master’s, and doctoral levels [4]. The graduate education programs at the master’s and doctoral levels are offered through graduate schools, where admission is primarily based on examinations that include written tests and interviews. Of all applicants, the yearly acceptance rate for master’s students in China ranges from 20 to 30%. The admission criteria encompass various factors, such as test scores, interview performance, professional skills, academic predictability, and other relevant considerations. Students have the autonomy to select their preferred graduate school and subspecialty based on their academic achievements, personal interests, and the likelihood of acceptance.

The existing medical graduate system in China divides graduate students into two types: professional masters (PM) and academic masters (AM). The PM program combine the master’s degree with the Standardized Training of Residents (also called standardized training, ST) together,while the AM program primarily focuses on scientific research and does not include ST. ST is a three-year clinical training program for medical students after graduation which gradually became compulsory in China around 2013. Standardized Training enables new doctors or dentists to obtain clinical practice opportunitiesand certificates that lead to promotions to more senior and advanced levels [5, 6].

Despite the AM or PM program, admitted stomatology students in graduate school are required to choose their subspecialty. The subspecialty branches of China’s stomatology system roughly include oral medicine, oral and maxillofacial surgery, prosthodontics, and orthodontics [7, 8]. Simultaneously, large subspecialties also have subordinate branch subspecialties; for example, oral medicine includes endodontics, periodontology, and oral mucous diseases; oral and maxillofacial surgery includes oral alveolar surgery, oral cancer surgery, cleft lip and palate surgery, trauma surgery, orthognathic surgery, and some emerging subspecialties, like oral implantology, pediatric dentistry, and others. Generally, there may be approximately 10 subspecialties in stomatology. Undergraduate students should carefully choose their subspecialty direction, considering that admission to a specific direction implies a three-year study with a subsequent career in that field [9].

Before stable employment, undergraduate graduates have several options to choose from: (1) Applying for a PM program (get a master’s degree and ST certificate simultaneously); (2) Applying for an AM program and another three years of the ST program after AM; (3) Applying for the ST program immediately after graduation; and (4) Finding employment in private hospitals, etc. ST is almost inevitable if a medical student wants to work steadily in public hospitals, while the ST experience becomes unnecessary if the graduate goes to a private dental clinic. Considering the challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic [11], heightened competition for employment opportunities, and a limited understanding of undergraduate students’ career aspirations, we deem it essential to examine and consolidate the diversion intentions and subspecialty choices of undergraduates after graduation. This research was conducted to understand the difficulties of young people in the competitive situation today, their choice of work or pursuit of further education after graduation, preferred subspecialties, and the factors that influence these choices.

Methods

Study design

This cross-sectional survey was conducted on stomatology undergraduates from Southwest Medical University to analyze their plans after graduation, choices of further education for better job opportunities or direct relatively low-end jobs, preferred stomatology subspecialties, factors influencing these choices, and so on. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Stomatology Hospital, Southwest Medical University (No.20220602002) and conducted in strict compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Owing to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic in China, this study was conducted online between July and August 2022. Questionnaires were distributed to undergraduate stomatology students via a survey website (www.wjx.com). Links to and information about the survey were sent via the instant messaging App WeChat, explaining the purpose and format of the survey. Volunteer respondents accessed the survey via a QR code and were informed of the study’s purpose and content through a fact sheet upon entering the online survey. Before starting the study, the respondents were required to sign an electronic informed consent form, and those who declined to participate were allowed to withdraw.

Investigation methods

This study was anonymous because direct competition between the respondents may occur during admission to the master’s or ST’s program. The questionnaires for this survey consisted of the following parts:

1) Personal demographic information (e.g. Gender, birthplace).

2) Preferred plan after graduation and the reasons.

Respondents were presented with five options for their first choice after graduation, with only one answer allowed (1, applying for a Master’s program; 2, applying for an ST program; 3, opening one’s dental clinic; 4,working in private hospitals; or 5, leaving the field of dentistry).

3) Choice of an AM or PM program and reasons.

After completing the selection, respondents who selected the master’s program were provided with two options: the AM and PM programs, and were asked to choose one; they were required to select the top three reasons for a master’s degree out of six reasons and rank them (1, advantage in employment; 2, income rise; 3, personal interests;4, employment market demand; 5, good grades in college; 6, partner factors). Subsequently, they were asked to rank three out of six reasons for a PM’s degree (1, contain ST program; 2, advantage in employment; 3, more interest in clinical work; 4, employment market demand;5, family suggestion; 6, partner factors) and choose three reasons out of seven options for applying for an AM’s degree (1,less admission competition; 2,more interest in scientific research; 3, easier for a PhD admission; 4, easier to get tenured; 5,family suggestion; 6,partner factors).

4) Desired dental subspecialty and reasons.

Respondents were asked to rank their top 3 preferred subspecialties out of 10 options in order (oral and maxillofacial surgery, prosthodontics, endodontics, periodontology, pediatric dentistry, orthodontics, oral implantology, oral mucous diseases, dental materials, and oral basic medicine).

Additionally, the reasons for choosing the desired subspecialties were analyzed using nine options, requiring respondents to select and rank the three reasons. The reasons for choosing a specific specialty included: 1, personal interests; 2, expected revenue; 3, family suggestions; 4, market demand; 5, work arrangement ; 6, hygiene condition at work; 7, good grades in college; 8, easier access to higher education; and 9, partner factors.

5) Undesired dental subspecialty and reasons.

The respondents were then asked to rank their top three undesired specialties, and the reasons for opposing the selected undesired subspecialties were ranked out of nine options, which was the opposite of the one corresponding to the previous section.

In this study, for the sequencing of questionnaires, a point system was implemented on the ranking of each option, with the top three receiving 10, 7, or 5 point(s), respectively. This pointing system was modeled to calculate weighted scores and rank the options accordingly.

Data analysis

The researchers obtained the study’s data directly from the survey website after 100 respondents had completed the survey, and the overall results of this study can be fully obtained from the website (wjx.com). The count data were expressed as rate and constituent ratio, and the collected data were analyzed using Word Processing System (Kingsoft, China).

Results

A total of 100 respondents were collected in this survey, including 45 from male students and 55 from female students. Eighty-two participants were from Sichuan, 16 from Chongqing, one from Gansu, and one from Zhejiang.

Intention after graduation

Overall, 91.0% of the surveyed students expressed the desire to pursue a master’s degree as their first choice; 4.0% of the students chose to work at a private dental clinic; and 3.0% opted to participate in the ST program after graduation. Discussion

When referring to medical education in China nowadays, one of the most frequently mentioned terms is “Neijuan”. It denotes the increasing competition for limited resources, leading to exhaustion but less reward. Owing to the fierce competition in excellent hospitals, medical practitioners may need to obtain postgraduate and doctoral degrees, ST certificates, and engage in scientific research to obtain employment opportunities and good career development [12]. Those who go on to become doctors in large, high-level public hospitals may be a small minority of the original medical undergraduates, and they may probably in their 30s when start work. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the post-graduation choices of dental students in light of prevailing employment conditions, encompassing their decision between pursuing further education or directly entering primary hospitals as dentists, as well as their expectations and influential factors pertaining to dental subspecialties.

Current status of stomatology education in China

Medical education in China and the United States differ in their approaches to undergraduate and postgraduate education [10, 13]. Medical education in China is primarily based on five years of undergraduate education, without the need for four years of general education [14]. Historically, few individuals pursued postgraduate or doctoral degrees and instead entered directly into the workforce. With an increase in enrollment in stomatology, there has been a shift in the trend; the labor market is becoming saturated, and more students are now seeking further education [15].

The proliferation of medical colleges and expansion of the stomatology specialty have resulted in an increasing number of highly educated stomatology professionals [16]. Currently, many jobs that previously only required a bachelor’s degree now require a master’s degree and an ST certificate. Having a master’s degree used to be sufficient to work in a high-level hospital in a large city; however, many positions now require a doctoral degree and a substantial number of published articles [17]. Consequently, postgraduate and doctoral degrees have become increasingly desirable, though the value of these degrees is rapidly diminishing.

In recent years, graduates in China have experienced increasing pressure regarding their career prospects in stomatology [18]. Undergraduates may feel the need to pursue further education to qualify for a position and secure successful careers. However, they need to consider that, with the education inflation, the entry requirements for public hospitals may increase after three years of study, while a master’s degree may currently be the minimum prerequisite, which may increase in the future. Additionally, some hospitals may request an ST certification that students with an AM degree would need to obtain by completing an additional three years of the ST program.

Therefore, it is crucial for students to plan their education carefully and consider its long-term implications. Choosing the right path at each stage of education is pivotal; however, for most students, this can be a daunting task. Therefore, assistance while navigating these choices is imperative for those graduates pursuing careers in stomatology.

Academic or professional master selection

After the ST policy was implemented, certificate of ST program became necessary for all medical students who wished to be promoted to higher positions [19]. Consequently, PM programs that combine a master’s degree with an ST program are highly desirable. The ST program primarily focuses on providing clinical practice training in general dentistry, with an emphasis on technical skills and rotations in the various dental subspecialty departments. PM students are required to spend more time in clinical practice, with five days of clinical work per week, while their time for medical research is compressed into the remaining time. Conversely, AM students focus primarily on scientific research training, while clinical practice is generally limited to one to two days with no rotations in various subspecialties.

Students in the AM program can lay a better foundation for scientific research and are more familiar with their subspecialties than PM students. However, as shown in this study, students did not hesitate to choose PM because they could obtain both an ST certificate and a Master’s degree in a single program. Most candidates choose AM because they cannot be admitted by PM.

Desired stomatology subspecialty selection

It is critical for students to carefully consider their subspecialty when applying for master’s degree programs. Upon completion of their master’s degree education, most students pursue careers related to their subspecialties. The working patterns of different dental subspecialties can vary significantly. For example, oral and maxillofacial surgeries may adopt a surgical approach, involve working in environments exposed to blood, and require a certain level of physical strength. However, orthodontics and pediatric dentistry may entail working with children and require a higher level of patience. Oral medicine primarily involves dental manipulation, with extensive chairside time and a delicate operational workload. Therefore, dental graduates should consider their personality, gender, and level of adaptability when selecting their desired dental subspecialty. By doing so, they can maximize their chances of achieving success and professional satisfaction in their chosen fields. The stomatology profession encourages diversity and inclusivity, and everyone should have equal opportunities to pursue their chosen field without experiencing any prejudice or discrimination [20].

When students choose a dental subspecialty, the expected revenue is often a significant factor in their decision-making process. The potential earning capacity of some dental subspecialties has made them popular choices among students. Orthodontics is considered profitable because of its high professional threshold and large market demand, so it has always been one of the most popular subspecialties for students to apply for [21]. Generally, while the prospect of a high income is alluring, students should consider other factors when choosing their subspecialty in dentistry, such as personal interests, inherent talents, and overall job satisfaction, which are essential to pursuing a fulfilling career in the long term [22].

Furthermore, it is important to note that the selection of stomatology subspecialty should not solely be based on income expectations. As students are not employed, it must be acknowledged that there may be gaps in their understanding of future expected earnings in certain subspecialties. Some subspecialties that are assumed to have high incomes may not actually do so. Simultaneously, subspecialties with high incomes may be affected by several factors, such as government policies, job competition, market size, and economic trends, and become less high-income subspecialties in the future. The truth is, every medical subspecialty possesses inherent value, has the potential to attain excellence, and can yield reasonable returns.

Therefore, regarding subspecialty selection, the most crucial consideration is the acquisition of knowledge and skills through education [23]. It is unwise to base your decision solely on the expected income. Conversely, students should gather as much information as possible about various subspecialties to gain a comprehensive understanding of the job market’s current and future trends, such as job availability, job security, work-life balance, personal fulfillment, and growth potential in that field [24, 25].

Conclusions

This study has revealed that most Chinese stomatology undergraduates prefer to pursue a master’s degree after graduation, followed by standardized training program. Among those who choose to pursue a master’s degree, most gravitate towards professional rather than academic master’s programs. The factors that influence students’ decisions to continue their education beyond their undergraduate degree include personal interests, suggestions from family, and expected revenue.

Educational institutions should provide dental undergraduates with a deep insight into the various stomatology specialties and encourage them to explore areas in and beyond their initial interests and gain a comprehensive understanding of all available career paths. This will provide policymakers with references towards developing a more reasonable postgraduate education system, alleviate confusion and dilemmas among undergraduate students about their future careers, and finally empower students to make informed career choices that are best suited to their talents and interests.

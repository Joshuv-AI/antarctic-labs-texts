From Unsupervised to Zero-Shot: 3D Domain Adaptation for Cryo-Electron Tomography Segmentation
Authors: Haoran Li, Xingjian Li, Huan Wang, Jiahua Shi, Huaming Chen, Bo Du, Johan Barthelemy, Daisuke Kihara, Jun Shen, Min Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the refinement of 3D imaging modality, cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET) has emerged as a powerful technique for the structural analysis of macromolecular complexes at near-atomic resolution. Recent advancements in volumetric segmentation methods applied to cryo-ET datasets have garnered significant attention within the biomedical sector. However, existing methods rely heavily on manually labeled data, which demands highly specialized expertise, making fully supervised approaches less feasible for cryo-ET images. To address this, a number of unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) techniques have been developed to improve segmentation network performance using unlabeled data. Nevertheless, directly applying these methods to cryo-ET image segmentation presents two major challenges: 1) the source dataset, usually obtained through simulation, contains a fixed level of noise, while the target dataset, being directly collected from raw-data from the real-world scenario, has unpredictable noise levels; 2) the source data used for training typically consists of known macromolecules, in contrast, the target domain data are often unknown, causing the model to be biased towards those known macromolecules, leading to a domain shift problem. To address such challenges, in this paper, we introduce a voxel-wise unsupervised domain adaptation approach, termed Vox-UDA, specifically for cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation. Vox-UDA incorporates a noise generation module to simulate target-like noises in the source dataset for cross-noise level adaptation, and a denoised pseudo-labeling strategy based on the improved bilateral filter to alleviate the domain shift problem. Additionally, we further consider a scenario that is more in line with the real world, where the target (experimental) dataset might not be accessible during training or has a very small sample size, and we present a voxel-wise zero-shot domain adaptation (ZSDA) approach, named Vox-ZSDA. In Vox-ZSDA, we introduce a self-supervised graph learning strategy to eliminate any dependency on the target data, accompanied by a dynamic graph contrastive learning technique to enhance the model’s sensitivity to macromolecular structures to boost the segmentation performance. More importantly, we construct the first UDA and ZSDA cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation benchmark on three experimental datasets. Extensive experimental results on multiple benchmarks and newly curated real-world datasets demonstrate the superiority of our proposed approach compared to state-of-the-art UDA and ZSDA methods.Keywords: 3D image segmentation, Unsupervised domain adaptation, Zero-shot domain adaptation, Cryo-Electron Tomography, Graph Learning

Introduction

Cryo-Electron Tomography (cryo-ET) is a state-of-the-art imaging technique that provides three-dimensional visualizations of biomedical samples in their native, frozen-hydrated state (Oikonomou & Jensen, 2017; Pan et al., 2025). This automated electron tomography technique enables biologists to interpret complex macromolecular structures at high resolution (Wan & Briggs, 2016), playing a crucial role in drug discovery and disease treatment, e.g., the research of SARS-CoV-2 structure and replication (Klein et al., 2020). Recent advances on deep learning research significantly enhance this analysis by automating and improving the accuracy of structural recognition, thereby accelerating scientific discovery and the proper development of medical interventions. Especially for the subtomogram segmentation task (Zhou et al., 2021; Zhu et al., 2021), which defines a 3D segmentation task that aims to mine the meaningful information of the target macromolecular on the voxel-level. However, existing methods heavily rely on manual, which are highly subjective and resource-intensive, so that the effectiveness of those methods had always been questionable.

To tackle the challenges for data annotation, the classical unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) methods involve transferring the knowledge from labeled source domains to unlabeled target domains (Fan et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2022). However, most existing methods for this task are tailored for 2D images, limiting their effectiveness in 3D tasks. While some recent works explore UDA on 3D images (Shin et al., 2023; Xian et al., 2023), they typically convert 3D input into 2D slices for network processing, resulting in a loss of spatial information. More recently, MAPSeg (Zhang et al., 2024a) proposed a voxel-level pseudo-labeling method based on MAE. However, label distortion remains a main challenge for UDA.

In this paper, we introduce a novel UDA approach using large simulated macromolecular data (Eisenstein et al., 2019; Hagen et al., 2017) as the source domain dataset while experimental data as the target domain dataset. Advancements in data simulation techniques have facilitated the generation of cryo-ET subtomogram data beyond conventional biological methods. Given the structure of macromolecules, existing generative methods (Harar et al., 2023; Martinez-Sanchez et al., 2024) could create realistic synthetic datasets with corresponding voxel-level segmentation masks, offering a cost-effective alternative to traditional methods that required high-end equipment and significant human expertise. Nevertheless, the substantial differences between the two domains introduce new challenges for the UDA task. Firstly, simulated data is generated using fixed parameters, resulting in a consistent noise level across all subtomograms (typically 0.03 dB or 0.05 dB). In contrast, noise rates in experimental datasets are unpredictable. Examples of cryo-ET subtomograms are shown in Fig. 2. As shown in Fig. 2(a), both simulated and experimental subtomograms share the same voxel-space input representation. However, since the simulated data contain a consistent noise level, applying a fixed-range filter reveals the approximate particle shapes, as illustrated in Fig. 2(b). In contrast, the experimental data exhibit much more complex and heterogeneous noise, and even after applying the same fixed-range filter, no particle-related structural information can be clearly observed (Fig. 2(d)). Secondly, although subtomogram segmentation is inherently a binary task, the molecular categories present in the simulated subtomograms often differ from those in experimental data. This discrepancy might bias the segmentation model toward simulated data, leading to a domain shift problem.

To address the aforementioned challenges, we propose a Voxel-wise UDA framework, termed Vox-UDA, for cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation. Vox-UDA comprises two key components: a noise generation module (NGM) and a denoised pseudo-labeling (DPL) strategy. NGM generates Gaussian noise from a subset of the target dataset and applies it to the source samples, producing a target-like noisy effect. Meanwhile, DPL introduces an improved bilateral filter that replaces pixel differences with gradient differences, enhancing its effectiveness for 3D gray-scale images. Additionally, DPL preserves edge information during denoising as much as possible, resulting in undistorted pseudo-labels. These pseudo-labels provide additional supervision signals to mitigate the domain shift problem, thereby enhancing the model’s performance on the target data.

Although Vox-UDA (originally proposed in the conference version (Li et al., 2025a)) effectively eliminates the reliance on labeled experimental data and addressed the issue of domain shift problem caused by particle difference through simulating the noise environment and pseudo-labeling, it still relies on a certain amount of unlabeled experimental data (target dataset) for noise and pseudo-label generation. However, in real-world scenarios, due to data scarcity, it is challenging to ensure that a sufficient amount of experimental data can be collected under all conditions. For example, as shown in some released dataset from EMPIAR (Iudin et al., 2016) platform, the subtomogram dataset obtained from common biological tissues typically contains thousands of samples (Guo et al., 2018; Pacheco-Fiallos et al., 2023), while in a significant portion of other datasets (such as disease samples), the number of available samples is often limited to just a few dozen or even fewer. Therefore, this raises a new challenge: • is it possible to train a highly generalizable model using only the simulated data without relying on any experimental data?

To tackle this issue, in this paper, we further consider a scenario that is more in line with the real world, where the experimental data (target dataset) are not accessible during training process, and we introduce a Voxel-wise Zero-shot Domain adaptation framework for cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation, termed Vox-ZSDA. Fig. 1 illustrates the difference between Vox-UDA (Li et al., 2025a) and Vox-ZSDA. In detail, Vox-ZSDA follows a dual network scheme and utilizes a self-supervised graph learning strategy (SGL) to enhance the model’s sensitivity to particle structures. SGL employs a Nodes Contrastive Learning (NCL) loss between the feature graph nodes output from the student and teacher model. Additionally, as aforementioned, the noise rates of the source and target data are different. To further mitigate the effect of interference, different from existing self-supervised learning approaches which uses random masking (Huang et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2022b) strategy for self-training, we present a new Dynamic Graph Contrastive Learning (DGCL) technique. DCS takes the prominent features of noise and molecular structures as clustering centers and then performs graph constructions based on K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) algorithm. On other other hand, it further selectively applies NCL loss to the graph nodes based on the clustering centers, avoiding noise centers and choosing the edge/semantic center, compelling the model to focus on molecular structure features rather than the noise components. This comprehensive ZSDA framework addresses the challenges of improving the generalization of cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation while reducing the dependence on the target dataset.

Related Work

Domain Adaptation

Unsupervised Domain Adaptation for Vision Tasks

Under the unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) settings, there are two types of datasets being used for training: the source domain dataset which is fully labeled, and the target domain dataset, which is unlabeled. The first UDA approach, introduced by (Ganin et al., 2016), leverages adversarial learning to adapt a model trained on source data to target data without requiring additional annotations. Since UDA greatly expands the model’s generalization ability, it allows adaptation to new domains without the need for labeled data in the target domain, making it applicable to a wide range of tasks. As cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation is a segmentation task, in this paper, we mainly focus on the UDA approaches applied in semantic segmentation tasks. For segmentation, the UDA methods aim to eliminate the cross-domain discrepancies through the content at both the feature- (Zou et al., 2018) and pixel-level (Zheng et al., 2023d), and have achieved excellent results in medical image segmentation (Huang et al., 2025; Li et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023a). Although these methods achieve great performance in UDA segmentation, they are primarily designed for 2D images, which are not suitable for cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation as tomographies are often volumetric images. To handle the UDA challenge in 3D segmentation tasks, Shin et al. (2023) proposes a cross-modality translation method to generate synthetic 3D target volumes from source 2D scans. Xu et al. (2023a) applied a fast Fourier transform to convert input 2D slices into the frequency domain. A consistency loss was utilized to simultaneously constrain both the feature domain and frequency domain to achieve a better UDA outcome. In this work, we would focus on the noise discrepancies between simulated and experimental subtomogram data, aiming to improve the model’s robustness to noise.

Zero-shot Domain Adaptation for Vision Tasks

Zero-shot domain adaptation (ZSDA) is a target-free special case of UDA, aiming to train the model using only the source data, with the target data being entirely inaccessible throughout the training process. This concept was first proposed by Peng et al. (2018), by introducing a task-irrelevant domain-pairs to simulate the source-target domain situation and learn the adaptation ability, and transfer this ability to the source domains to achieve model generalization in the target domain. Building on the introduction of irrelevant pairs, some studies have enhanced ZSDA performance by incorporating semantic information (Kumagai & Iwata, 2018; Zhang et al., 2023b), feature disentanglement (Jhoo & Heo, 2021) or employing feature alignment techniques (Liu et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025). Another distinct approach involves using generative models to generate domain pairs (Azuma et al., 2024; Garbin et al., 2020; Li et al., 2024), thereby augmenting the training samples with synthetic data. Such method has achieved outstanding performance in certain specific scenarios, such as day-light domain adaptation (Du et al., 2024; Lengyel et al., 2021; Luo et al., 2023). Recent studies on vision-language models have even inspired new ideas. P∅DA (Fahes et al., 2023) enhances the model’s inference ability on unseen target data by leveraging differences in text prompts and contrastive learning. ULDA (Yang et al., 2024) further proposes an unified framework, which no longer relies on domain-specific customization requirements for prompting. In this research direction, existing approaches mainly focus on images from nature or some specific real-world scenarios, such as road traffic, leaving the investigation on biological and 3D voxel-level data remain unexplored. In cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation, however, the domain gap primarily arises from differences in imaging conditions, leading to distinct noise characteristics across datasets. Such noise-related discrepancies cannot be explicitly captured or represented through language prompts. Therefore, unlike language-guided ZSDA methods, our framework does not rely on textual guidance but instead focuses on simulating and adapting to target-specific noise distributions. To address this challenge, we draw inspiration from graph contrastive learning (Xia et al., 2022a; You et al., 2020) and propose a self-supervised graph learning strategy that enables the model to focus on macromolecular structures and better adapt to cryo-ET subtomogram inputs from different domains.

Cryo-Electron Tomography

Cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET) integrates cryogenic specimen preparation, electron microscopy for data acquisition, and tomographic reconstruction for 3D visualization (Koning, 2010). This technique allows biologists to capture structural information of macromolecules in an ultra-low temperature environment, which holds significant importance in the fields of biology and medicine. A cryo-ET subtomogram is a small cubic sub-volume extracted from a tomogram, normally only a single macromolecular complex is contained in each subtomogram. Deep learning based applications on cryo-ET have drawn widespread interest because of their potential to aid in the corresponding cryo-ET tasks, e.g., subtomogram alignment (Zeng & Xu, 2020), subtomogram classification (Bandyopadhyay et al., 2022; Wan et al., 2024) and subtomogram segmentation (Zhu et al., 2021). However, deep learning methods rely on large amounts of data annotation, which is particularly challenging for cryo-ET images. Bandyopadhyay et al. proposed a domain randomization-based approach to enhance the generalization performance of the model in subtomogram classification (Bandyopadhyay et al., 2022). Zhu et al. proposed a weakly supervised approach, which used only 2D-level annotation for voxel segmentation to alleviate the burden of annotation (Zhu et al., 2021). In this paper, we would propose an UDA and a ZSDA approach for subtomogram segmentation, which together aim at utilizing a large amount of cost-free annotated simulated data for knowledge transfer, enabling the segmentation network to generalize on experimental cryo-ET subtomograms.

Graph Learning in Computer Vision

Recent studies on graph learning have proven their strong ability in constructing image structures in non-Euclidean space. Graph Neural Network (GNN) was first used as a fully connected network for image segmentation (Chandra et al., 2017; Krähenbühl & Koltun, 2011), which aimed to build relationships among all the pixel-pairs. Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) was further proposed to update feature embeddings within the visual graphs (Gao et al., 2019; Kazi et al., 2022). To alleviate the training burden caused by the fully-connected graph, dynamic graph learning has been proposed, aiming to selectively construct visual graphs based on the pixel or feature embeddings (Hu et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2023c). Visual graphs have been applied to varies natural scenarios (Jia et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024) and biomedical scenarios (Cen et al., 2024; Khanna et al., 2023). While most researches focus on 2D imaging, some studies have also started exploring graph construction for 3D scenes. EV-VGCNN (Deng et al., 2022) introduced a light GCN framework based on the sparsity of graph representations, and SVGA-Net (He et al., 2022) proposed a sparse graph attention network in point cloud space for 3D object detection. Unlike existing graph learning methods, we adopt a self-supervised learning strategy with a dynamic graph contrastive learning technique, where graphs are constructed using node centers extracted from noise filters. The contrastive learning process is implemented through different construction strategies.

Denoised Pseudo-Labeling

Although the NGM can narrow the noise level disparities between the two domains, as aforementioned, the segmentation network remains biased toward the source data due to the domain shift problem. To mitigate this issue, we may introduce an additional supervision signal through pseudolabeling. However, since the noise level in the target domain is unknown and can potentially distort the pseudo-labels, leading to degraded model performance, we specially propose a denoised pseudo-labeling strategy to enhance the reliability and robustness. Unlike the existing pseudo-labeling method whereby adding an extra training step, we tactfully use the student-teacher structure (Sohn et al., 2020). Before xjt is sent to the teacher network to obtain the pseudo-label, we first perform denoising on xjt. Voxel-wise Zero-shot Domain Adaptation

Limitations of Vox-UDA

Vox-UDA addresses the the high training costs caused by the strong dependence of existing methods on annotated data. However, as can be seen from Sec. 3.1, the noise generation process still requires a large amount of target data. In some real-world scenarios, it is not possible to obtain a large amount of target data in advance, such as in fast automatic particle picking (Rath & Frank, 2004; Tegunov & Cramer, 2019) or the collection of certain rare molecular types. Therefore, we further explore the potential of zero-shot domain adaptation and propose Vox-ZSDA that eliminates the need for experimental data. Vox-ZSDA consists of a self-supervised graph learning (SGL) strategy and a dynamic graph contrastive learning (DGCL) technique.

Clarification of UDA, DG, and ZSDA

Before introducing the details of the proposed Vox-ZSDA, we briefly clarify the distinction between Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA), Domain Generalization (DG), and Zero-Shot Domain Adaptation (ZSDA) in the context of cryo-ET segmentation. Given a labeled source domain dataset 𝒮 and an unlabeled target domain dataset 𝒯, UDA aims to train a model using both 𝒮 and 𝒯, while supervision is provided only by labels from 𝒮. DG, in contrast, trains a model solely on 𝒮 and evaluates it directly on 𝒯. In both UDA and DG, the source and target domains are assumed to share the same label space, i.e., 𝒴s=𝒴t. ZSDA relaxes the closed-set assumption by allowing 𝒴s≠𝒴t,and in particular 𝒴t∖𝒴s≠∅, which implies that the model must generalize to previously unseen labels at test time without accessing any target-domain supervision.

Experiments

Experimental Settings

Implementation Details and Hyperparameters

We utilize the VoxResNet as the base architecture for both Vox-UDA and Vox-ZSDA. The whole experiments are conducted on one single NVIDIA A100 Tensor Core GPU with 80GB memory. For the training process of Vox-UDA and Vox-ZSDA, we choose the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate set to 1e-3 for optimization, and both models are trained for 300 epochs with batch size of 16. The learning rate is decayed by 90% every 100 epochs. For Vox-UDA, the hyperparameters of sampled number Nsampled and filter rate ρ are empirically set to 10 and 24.4%, respectively. The domain hyperparameter σd and range hyperparameter σr defined in the improved bilateral filter are set to 120 and 1.2, respectively. For Vox-ZSDA, the hyperparameters center number j , number of neighbors k, and adjustment rate φ used for the IBF module are set to 24, 10 and 20%. Through sensitivity analysis, we find that the model performance remains stable across different datasets when setting j within [20, 30] and φ within [18%, 23%], which we recommend as default ranges for new domains (see details in Sec. 5.3.2).

Datasets and Evaluation Metrics

We conduct experiments on two types of datasets: simulated dataset (source dataset) and experimental dataset (target dataset).

Simulated Dataset.

The simulated dataset used as the source dataset is generated following the same generation process as Zeng et al. (2023). We choose six representative macromolecule complexes in our simulated datasets and divide them into two groups as two separate source datasets (1bxn, 1f1b, and 1yg6; 2byu, 2h12, and 21db). For each macromolecule complex, we simulate it with two different noise levels, with SNR of 0.03 and 0.05, and each of them contains 500 samples. Following existing work (Liao et al., 2020; Zeng & Xu, 2020), all the input subtomogram are resized to 323. The simulated dataset contains 6,000 samples in total (3,000 samples for each source dataset).

Experimental Dataset.

We use three experimental datasets as the target datasets: Mycoplasma pneumoniae (O’Reilly et al., 2020), Erwinia (Prichard et al., 2023) and PolyGA (Guo et al., 2018). Mycoplasma pneumoniae and Erwinia are newly collected and pre-processed from the public repository EMDB (Consortium, 2024)1. Mycoplasma pneumoniae contains 10 cryo-ET subtomograms of wild-type Mycoplasma pneumoniae cells. Each subtomogram has a shape of 2003. Due to the size of each input subtomogram is too large for training, we resize each subtomogram to 1923 and cut each one into multiple non-overlapping patches with size 323, and use each patch as the input for the model. Erwinia consists of 10 cryo-ET subtomograms of wildtype Erwinia phage vB_EamM_RAY baseplate complexes. The size of each subtomogram ranges from 723 to 843. We resize each subtomogram to 643 and cut each one into multiple non-overlapping patches with size 323, and use each patch as the input for the model. Hence, there are 2160 samples in Mycoplasma pneumoniae dataset and 80 samples in the Erwinia dataset. Poly-GA is a public datasetconsisting of 1,033 subtomograms from three macromolecular complexes-ribosome, 26S proteasome, and TRiC. Each subtomogram is also re-scaled to size 323.

As there is no class overlap between the simulated and experimental datasets, we directly set the simulated dataset as the source domain and the experimental dataset as the target domain for the zero-shot domain adaptation task.

For evaluation, the mean intersection of union (mIoU) and dice similarity coefficient (Dice) are employed to evaluate the segmentation performance.

Baselines

For both tasks, we set a “w/o adaptation” setting and a “Fully Supervised” setting for comparison. The “w/o adaptation” setting is an original VoxResNet trained on the source dataset without adaptation. The “Fully Supervised” setting is a VoXResNet trained on the target datasets with all labels provided, as the upper bound.

For UDA in cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation, we implement the most relevant traditional and state-of-the-art UDA approaches for 2D image segmentation on our task, including the single discriminator-based (DANN (Ganin et al., 2016)), the image synthesizing based (PDAM (Liu et al., 2020)) and the self-training based (Zhao et al. (2024b)). We also include Cryo-Shift (Bandyopadhyay et al., 2022), an UDA approach designed for cryo-ET classification. As Cryo-Shift is not designed for segmentation, we replace the base model with VoxResNet to modify Cryo-Shift for segmentation task. In the meantime, we also include the five most recent approaches designed for volumetric images, including ASC (Xu et al., 2023a), LE-UDA (Zhao et al., 2022), DAR-Unet (Yao et al., 2022), DDSP (Zheng et al., 2024c) and MAPSeg (Zhang et al., 2024a). ASC and LE-UDA cut 3D images into 2D slices and apply UDA on 2D scenario, while DAR-Unet, DDSP and MAPSeg directly performs UDA in 3D voxel space.

For ZSDA in cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation, we compare our method with the most classic as well as the most recent comparable ZSDA methods applied in 2D scenes, i.e., three task-transfer based (i.e., ZSDA (Wang & Jiang, 2020), ZDDA-C (Kutbi et al., 2021), and Liu et al. (2024)), together with three prompting based (i.e., P∅DA (Fahes et al., 2023), ULDA (Yang et al., 2024) and UPRE (Zhang et al., 2025)). Since it is improbable to find an irrelevant task pair similar to cryo-ET, for task-transfer methods, we directly migrated task pairs from medical images to cryo-ET subtomograms. In practice, we set the CT/MRI as the task-irrelevant dual-domain pairs and use the Cardiac CT-MRI Dataset (Zhang et al., 2024a) as the training set. For prompting based methods, as there are currently no multimodal pre-training framework comparable to CLIP or large-scale paired datasets for cryo-ET study, we have to employ the BioBERT (Lee et al., 2020) pre-trained on the biomedical corpus as the text encoder, and combine it directly with VoxResNet to approximate a prompt-based setup. We utilize prompts such as “cryo-ET subtomogram of XYZ protein from an/a experimental/simulated dataset” and pair them with the corresponding subtomogram as the input-pair for training. Noted that since P∅DA requires target-like prompts for training the PIN module, we designed two distinct types of text prompts corresponding to the simulated and experimental datasets. For each source input, two separate prompts were used to indicate its simulation or experimental context, allowing the model to learn prompt-conditioned feature modulation through the PIN layer.

Ablation Study

In this section, we provide extensive ablation studies on the components and hyperparameter settings of both Vox-UDA and Vox-ZSDA to analyze the effectiveness of our proposed methods.

Vox-UDA

Effectiveness of the Improved bilateral filter.

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed improved bilateral filter (IBF) for denoised pseudo-labeling, we conduct experiments using the three different denoisers respectively, and report the segmentation results in both Table 1 and Table 2. As can be seen from these tables, comparing the method of using NGM as a denoiser, employing bilateral filtering (BF) indeed brings a performance improvement (i.e., mIoU increased 1.2% in Table 1 and Dice increased 1.0% in Table 2). This is because BF can preserve some edge information while denoising, thereby avoiding pseudo-label distortion. However, as discussed in Sec. 3.2, the range kernel of BF is not suitable for grayscale inputs. Our proposed IBF addresses this drawback by using a Laplacian transform, which allows the range kernel to focus more on gradient changes in the voxel space rather than value changes. Therefore, our new model achieves the best performance via the proposed IBF (i.e., mIoU increased 2.4% in Table 1 and Dice increased 3.3% in Table 2). We further compared our proposed IBF with a recently proposed learnable bilateral filtering method (Guo et al., 2025), which introduces an adaptive bilateral filter (ABF) using learnable parameters to dynamically control the filtering process, enabling the bilateral filter to better adapt to the distribution gap between different image resolutions. As shown in Table 7, although ABF improves performance compared with the standard BF, it still performs worse than our proposed IBF.

Hyperparameter Analysis.

We herein further evaluate the hyperparameters used in our approach. Following the main experiments, each model is run for five times with different random seed to evaluate the stability of the hyperparameters, and the mean and standard deviation of evaluation metric are reported. As shown in Table 9, we evaluate the sampled number Nsample, the high-pass filter rate ρ, the weight λn for consistency losses, the domain hyperparameter σd and range hyperparameter σr. Nsample controls the number of sampled targets for noise generation. Since noise distribution in the target domain is uneven, we use random sampling instead of the full dataset. An improper Nsample - either too large or too small - negatively impacts performance. As shown in Table 9, Nsample=10 yields the best results, improving mIoU by 18% over Nsample=20 and Dice by 11% over Nsample=15. Additionally, ρ serves as the filter rate, determining the retained information for further processing. As aforementioned, noise primarily resides in the high-frequency components of 2D and 3D images. However, determining the balance between high and low frequencies in a high-pass filter is often subjective. To optimize this, we experiment with different retention levels of high-frequency information, finding that ρ=24.4% yields the best results, improving mIoU by 16% over ρ=17.8% and Dice by 10% over ρ=8.4%. As mentioned in Sec. 3.1 that for segmentation tasks, high-level features emphasize edges, while low-level features capture textures. Thus, we assign equal weights to λ1 and λ2 for low-level features and λ3 and λ4 for high-level ones, i.e., λ1=0.2,λ2=0.2,λ3=0.3,λ4=0.3 achieves the best performance and brings a 19%mIoU and 14% Dice improvement. Lastly, we evaluate σd and σr, confirming that our chosen values are optimal, as adjustments do not enhance model performance.

Distance Metrics choice for Graph Projection.

As discussed in Sec. 4.3.2, we set the Chebyshev distance as the graph construction metric for the teacher network. This choice is motivated by the use of cosine similarity in the student network. While the teacher network necessitates a different graph construction approach, it should still be guided by the similarity between nodes and clustering centers rather than a direct computation of their value differences. We provide additional ablation study results by replacing the Chebyshev distance ℓ∞ with the Euclidean distance ℓ2 in Table 10. As can be seen from the table, using ℓ2 for graph construction can achieve certain effectiveness; however, due to its distinct measurement approach compared to cosine similarity (i.e.,ℓ2 measures absolute magnitude differences, while cosine similarity quantifies directional differences) ), it hinders model convergence, ultimately leading to lower performance.

Beyond the choice of distance metrics, we also note that the scalability of graph construction is another practical consideration. Although KNN with cosine similarity works well in our current experiments, computational scalability issues may arise since the cryo-ET subtomograms used are relatively small (323 voxels). For high-resolution 3D volumes, this could become a bottleneck. To address this, we are considering two promising directions: (1) replacing the global KNN graph with a dynamic local graph that updates neighborhood connections adaptively during training, and (2) adopting lightweight approximate nearest neighbor search algorithms to achieve more efficient and scalable graph construction. We plan to explore these directions in future work to further enhance the scalability and general applicability of our framework.

Hyperparameter Analysis.

Similar to the ablation analysis of Vox-UDA, we also evaluate the hyperparameters in the proposed Vox-ZSDA approach. As shown in Fig. 13, we report the mIoU and Dice results under different (a) Center number j, (b) Number of neighbors k, and (c) Adjustment rate φ. In detail, j and φ are designed to determine how many clustering centers should be chosen for graph construction. Too many centers may lead to the graph overly fragmented, leading to unnecessary complexity, and too few centers may cause the graph too sparse, leading to possible missing of important information. Hence, suitable values of j and φ should be chosen to balance these two aspects. k is used to determine the number of nodes linked to each center. If k is too small, the information may be insufficient; if it is too large, it might introduce redundant information. The results shown in Fig. 13 (a), (b) and (c) demonstrate that our settings are the most optimal, achieving the best performance.

Potential future work: Exploring the possibility of Zero-shot learning from Subtomogram to Tomogram

In this section, we explore the feasibility of extending zero-shot learning from cryo-ET subtomograms to tomograms. Different from cryo-ET subutomograms each image in a tomogram contains multiple particles of different types, making it highly likely that molecular categories not being encountered during subtomogram training will appear. To address this problem, we define a new task, termed zero-shot cryo-ET segmentation, which aims to segment all the molecules with their categories using a model trained only with subtomograms. Our investigation is a preliminary exploration, but we hope it offers valuable insights for future research.

Method details.

Given an input cryo-ET subtomogram xi, we set yi1 as its ground-truth segmentation mask, where 1 denotes the subtomogram contains only one type of molecule. To enable the model to detect unseen classes during the inference stage, similar to CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) in vision tasks, we have also built a similar vision-protein alignment model. As illustrated in Fig. 14 (a), we first feed the input subtomogram into the IBF module proposed in Sec. 3.2 and then pass the output to a segmentation model (Li et al., 2025b) based on a 3D Vision Transformer to extract visual features. This 3D Vision Transformer is an extension of the 2D Vision Transformer architecture, incorporating a novel 3D atrous convolution layer as the segmentor. For the protein feature embedding, we set the pre-trained protein language model (PLM) as the encoder. Given each input subtomogram, we also take the corresponding protein sequence as input and feed it into the encoder of the PLM. During training, we keep the PLM parameters frozen and use KL divergence (Kullback & Leibler, 1951) to quantify the difference between the visual and protein feature embeddings as the loss, optimizing only the segmentation model. For inference phase, the input cryo-ET tomogram is directly feed into IBF and the 3D segmentation model, and the output feature is further sent to the decoder of PLM to obtain the final prediction result ypredn, where n denotes the class numbers contained in the input tomogram.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a voxel-level unsupervised domain adaptation approach, termed Vox-UDA, for the cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation task. In detail, our Vox-UDA consists of a Noise Generation Module (NGM) and a denoised pseudo-labeling (DPL) strategy. NGM generates target-like Gaussian noise for the source domain data, while DPL uses an improved bilateral filter (IBF) to provide denoised target domain data for pseudo-labeling to boost the segmentation performance. Additionally, we propose Vox-ZSDA, a voxel-wise zero-shot domain adaptation approach to handle a more challenging scenario where target datasets are not available during training. Vox-ZSDA incorporates self-supervised graph learning strategy and dynamic graph contrastive learning technique to enhance the model’s ability on molecular structure segmentation. We have conducted large-scale experiments to demonstrate the prominent performance of our proposed methods and provide the first benchmark for both UDA and ZSDA of cryo-ET subtomogram segmentation tasks. Furthermore, we explore the possibility of zero-shot cryo-ET segmentation by transferring subtomogram to tomogram and propose a basic model structure and simple comparative experiments, hoping that these can inspire future research to explore unseen molecular detection.

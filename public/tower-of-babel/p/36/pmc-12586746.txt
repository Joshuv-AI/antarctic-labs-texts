Web-based explainable machine-learning tool for predicting five-year recurrence of colorectal cancer after curative resection: multicentre retrospective cohort study
Authors: Chi-Sheng Chen, Tai-Han Lin, Hsing-Yi Chung, Ming-Jr Jian, Chih-Kai Chang, Cherng-Lih Perng, Ping-Ying Chang, Wen-Yen Huang, Chao-Yang Chen, Yu-Chun Lin, Hung-Sheng Shang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe World Health Organization identifies colorectal cancer as the third-most diagnosed malignancy and second leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Up to 30% of patients relapse within 5 years postoperatively; however, conventional staging methods cannot reliably stratify individual risks, underscoring the need for precise, patient-centred decision-support tools.MethodsWe retrospectively analysed data on 1,789 colorectal cancer patients undergoing curative resection (2013–2023) from the Tri-Service General Hospital registry. Patients were divided chronologically into training (January to September) and validation (October to December) sets. Feature importance was assessed using random forest impurity scores and Shapley additive explanations (SHAP). Introduction

Colorectal cancer represents a significant global health challenge, identified by the World Health Organization as the third-most diagnosed malignancy and the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1–3]. In 2020, approximately 1.9 million new colorectal cancer cases and 930,000 deaths were recorded [1, 2]. Projections indicate that, without more effective control strategies, the annual global burden could escalate to 3.2 million new cases and 1.6 million deaths by 2040, rendering relative increases of 63% and 73%, respectively [1]. These high figures highlight the urgent need for more precise and patient-centred approaches to colorectal cancer prevention, surveillance, and treatment.

Although curative resection followed by guideline-directed adjuvant therapy has notably enhanced patient outcomes, disease relapse remains a formidable threat [4]. Postoperative recurrence manifests in approximately 9–25% of colon cancer cases and 15–30% of rectal cancer cases within five years, with the peak hazard occurring in the initial 12–18 months [5–7]. Conventional staging systems and margin assessments cannot accurately differentiate between low- and high-risk individuals. This limitation compels clinicians to meticulously weigh the potential benefits of intensified therapeutic regimens against the associated risks of toxicity, increased costs, and diminished quality of life [8–10]. Consequently, a pressing unmet clinical need exists for robust and individualised risk-stratification methodologies.

To address this gap, machine learning (ML) methods have been increasingly adopted for recurrence prediction in colorectal cancer. Approaches in the literature include models based on clinical and pathological features, radiomics or deep learning techniques that extract quantitative features from computed tomography scans, magnetic resonance imaging, or histopathology images, and analyses incorporating gene expression or molecular biomarkers [11–15]. Numerous studies have reported the superiority of ML models over conventional statistical techniques, demonstrating that artificial intelligence (AI) can effectively capture complex, non-linear interactions often overlooked in conventional analyses. Despite these advancements, some practical limitations persist. Many existing ML tools are constrained by a limited set of predictors, the exclusion of lifestyle factors or comprehensive biochemical markers, the presentation of recurrence risk in broad, non-actionable categories, or a lack of interpretable explanations for individual predictions. Consequently, the medical community is actively pursuing widely adoptable, interpretable, and clinically actionable decision aids for predicting colorectal cancer recurrence.

Addressing this critical need for improved management strategies for colorectal cancer recurrence, this study aimed to develop an advanced AI clinical decision-support system (AI-CDSS). This system is designed to integrate a comprehensive array of inputs: demographic and lifestyle factors, detailed staging and treatment parameters, key immunohistochemical markers, and comprehensive biochemical and haematological profiles. This system is engineered to generate transparent, individualised recurrence-risk assessments that can dynamically be adjusted based on different therapeutic scenarios. By delivering clear, evidence-based risk information directly at the point of care, the proposed AI-CDSS can facilitate shared decision-making regarding surgical strategies and adjuvant therapies, thereby ultimately contributing to mitigating the escalating global burden of colorectal cancer.

Methods

Data source and study population

This study involved a retrospective analysis of data extracted from the prospectively maintained Tri-Service General Hospital colorectal-cancer registry. This registry systematically documents surgical oncology cases from our tertiary medical centre in Taipei and its four affiliated hospitals (three urban satellite facilities and one offshore centre), all of which follow standardised reporting and documentation procedures to comprehensively track key patient outcomes, including cancer recurrence, mortality, and treatment-related complications. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Tri-Service General Hospital (approval number A202305086), and the requirement for informed consent was waived as all patient records were irreversibly de-identified before analysis.

The registry initially comprised 3,467 adult patients who underwent curative-intent resection for colorectal cancer between 1 January 2013 and 31 December 2023. From this cohort, we excluded 767 patients diagnosed with stage 0 disease, 367 with incomplete treatment or follow-up data, 189 with non-adenocarcinoma histology, and 353 with incomplete laboratory profiles. Subsequently, the final analytical cohort comprised 1,789 patients (Fig. 1).

Data preprocessing and model development

Data preprocessing and model development were performed using Python (version 3.11). After importing the registry dataset, recurrence status was defined as the binary outcome variable (1 = recurrence; 0 = no recurrence), and all other relevant fields were considered candidate predictors. Continuous variables were subjected to natural logarithm transformation to mitigate the influence of extreme values. A complete-case analysis approach was adopted, whereby records with any missing values for the selected predictors were excluded before preprocessing.

To simulate real-world deployment scenarios, the dataset was split chronologically: cases registered from January to September constituted the training set, whereas those logged from October to December formed the validation set. Four ensemble, tree-based ML algorithms were evaluated, namely random forest, light gradient-boosting machine (LGBM), gradient boosting classifier (GBC), and extreme gradient-boosting (XGBoost).

These models were selected based on their proven strengths in analysing complex, structured clinical data [16]. Random forest provides robust and stable predictions by combining multiple decision trees, effectively addressing diverse clinical features commonly encountered in clinical datasets. LGBM excels in rapid processing with efficient memory utilisation, rendering it particularly valuable for analysing large patient cohorts and rapidly identifying high-risk cases requiring immediate attention. GBC employs an iterative learning approach that progressively refines predictions by learning from previous errors, enabling it to capture subtle interactions between clinical and laboratory parameters that influence recurrence patterns. XGBoost enhances both speed and accuracy through parallel processing capabilities while incorporating built-in regularisation mechanisms to prevent overfitting, ensuring consistent performance across diverse multicentre datasets. Collectively, these algorithms provide an optimal balance of interpretability and predictive accuracy essential for reliable clinical decision support in recurrence risk assessment.

Following an initial training run that included all candidate predictors, variables yielding zero importance in the random forest model were considered non-informative and subsequently excluded. The hyperparameters for each algorithm were optimised using scikit-learn’s GridSearchCV utility, employing five-fold cross-validation and maximising the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) to enhance predictive performance. The range of hyperparameters searched for each model and the optimal values selected are summarised in Supplementary Table 1.

Model validation

The performance of the developed models was evaluated on the validation set (data obtained from October to December) using a comprehensive suite of metrics: AUC, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and the F1-score. This selection of indicators was intended to provide a balanced assessment of model discrimination and overall predictive utility, thereby confirming the reliability of the final predictive model.

AI-CDSS deployment and functionality

The AI-CDSS was developed as a web application designed for physicians to have real-time access during patient consultations. The user interface allows clinicians to input relevant demographic, pathological, immunohistochemical, and laboratory data for an individual patient and select a specific treatment strategy for evaluation. Upon data submission, the application instantaneously calculates and displays the model-predicted five-year probability of recurrence tailored to the entered patient profile and chosen therapeutic scenario. This quantitative risk estimate can then be reviewed with the patient, thereby supporting shared decision-making processes and facilitating the direct comparison of risk profiles associated with alternative therapeutic options.

Results

Baseline patient characteristics

Among the 1,789 patients, 406 (22.7%) developed postoperative recurrence. Compared with those who remained recurrence-free, patients who experienced recurrence exhibited a distinct profile of adverse clinicopathological, immunohistochemical, and biochemical features (Table 1, Supplementary Tables 2, and Supplementary Table 3). Although the recurrence group was modestly younger (mean age: 63.8 ± 13.5 years vs. 66.4 ± 12.1 years in the non-recurrence group; p < 0.001), both groups presented similar distributions regarding sex, smoking history, betel-nut use, and alcohol consumption. Recurrence was strongly associated with more advanced disease: Stage IV tumours (49.8% in recurrence group vs. 4.0% consumption), T4 invasion (25.6% vs. 5.4%), N2 nodal involvement (35.5% vs. 11.0%), positive surgical margins (R1 status: 10.8% vs. 3.3%), and poor tumour differentiation (15.0% vs. 6.4%) were all significantly more prevalent in patients who developed recurrence (all p < 0.001). Consistent with this high tumour burden, patients in the recurrence group more frequently underwent total colectomy (2.7% vs. 0.4%) and received adjuvant chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and targeted therapy (all p < 0.001).

Immunohistochemical assessment showed higher mean EGFR expression (24.8 ± 17.3% vs. 21.6 ± 21.3%; p = 0.002) and a greater prevalence of CK7 positivity (p = 0.014) in tumours from the recurrence group than the non-recurrence group, whereas mismatch-repair protein status was comparable between the two groups. Biochemical analyses revealed that, before surgery, patients in the recurrence group had significantly lower mean serum albumin levels (3.57 ± 0.51 vs. 3.72 ± 0.53 g/dL) and higher mean concentrations of CA19-9 (48.5 ± 52.6 vs. 24.5 ± 30.6 U/mL) and CEA (28.0 ± 37.9 vs. 8.5 ± 19.8 ng/mL) (all p < 0.001). Haematological patients with recurrence exhibited higher white blood cell and platelet counts, elevated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratios, and lower haemoglobin levels (all p < 0.001), indicative of a more pronounced systemic inflammatory response.

Model development and feature importance

Features were ranked based on their importance scores derived from the random forest model. The top 10 predictors of recurrence were identified as pathological stage (importance: 0.0855), M category (0.0799), serum CEA level (0.0578), serum CA19-9 level (0.0392), EGFR expression percentage (0.0360), receipt of targeted therapy (0.0325), pre-operative serum albumin level (0.0249), platelet count (0.0249), lymphocyte count (0.0248), and white blood cell count (0.0241). Collectively, these 10 features accounted for approximately 43% of the total model importance.

The SHAP summary plot (Fig. 2) illustrates the impact of these predictors on individual risk predictions., Higher values for pathological stage, M category, CEA, CA19-9, EGFR percentage, targeted therapy administration, platelet count, and white blood cell count were associated with positive SHAP values, indicating a shift towards an increased predicted risk of recurrence. Conversely, elevated albumin and lymphocyte counts yielded negative SHAP values, corresponding to a shift towards a lower predicted risk. The concordance between random forest feature importance rankings and SHAP value contributions shows that tumour burden, specific tumour biological characteristics, treatment intensity, and host nutritional and inflammatory status are key drivers of postoperative recurrence risk.

Performance of ML models on the validation set

Evaluation on the independent validation cohort demonstrated strong discriminative ability for all four ML algorithms (Table 2), with AUCs ranging from 0.83 to 0.84. The GBC model achieved the highest AUC (0.84), whereas LGBM, random forest, and XGBoost each yielded an AUC of 0.83.

The random forest model exhibited the most balanced overall performance, achieving the highest accuracy (0.87), PPV (0.85), NPV (0.87), and F1-score (0.64). Moreover, it recorded a sensitivity of 0.51 and specificity of 0.98. LGBM and XGBoost produced identical accuracies (0.86) and NPVs (0.87); however, LGBM demonstrated a PPV of 0.82 and sensitivity of 0.49, whereas XGBoost had a PPV of 0.83 with the same sensitivity (0.49). Despite its leading AUC, the GBC model achieved the lowest sensitivity (0.46) and F1-score (0.58), reflecting a more conservative threshold. These results indicate that although all evaluated models performed comparably in terms of overall discrimination (AUC), the random forest model offered the most favourable trade-off between sensitivity and specificity for predicting postoperative recurrence in this cohort.

AI-CDSS deployment and clinical integration

Based on its optimal balance of performance metrics, the random forest model was selected as the core predictive engine for the AI-CDSS. The system was deployed as a secure, web-based application accessible on the hospital's internal network. The user interface (Fig. 3) features intuitive input fields for patient demographics, tumour stage, immunohistochemical marker status, and laboratory results. It provides a clear, dynamic display of the predicted five-year recurrence probability tailored to each selected treatment scenario. During outpatient consultations, clinicians can enter relevant patient data, select one or more potential adjuvant treatment strategies, and receive rapid, side-by-side risk comparisons, typically generated in under 1 s per query. This functionality is designed to streamline clinical discussions and support personalised, evidence-based treatment planning. To facilitate successful adoption and continuous improvement, hands-on training sessions and clinician focus groups were conducted. Qualitative feedback gathered on usability, decision-support capabilities, and workflow integration directly informed iterative refinements to the user interface and deployment processes, ensuring the tool remains both efficient and user-friendly in routine clinical practice.

Discussion

Leveraging a large, multisite colorectal cancer registry and a chronological train-validation split that mimic real-world deployment, we developed an explainable AI-CDSS that could estimate five-year recurrence risk within milliseconds at the point of care. The random-forest engine achieved an AUC of 0.83–0.84 on the independent validation cohort, with 87% accuracy and balanced predictive values (PPV 85%, NPV 87%), confirming robust discrimination and clinically useful calibration. SHAP analysis revealed that a concise set of variables—pathological stage, M category, pre-operative CEA and CA19-9 concentrations, EGFR expression percentage, serum albumin, and inflammatory indices such as platelet and lymphocyte counts—collectively accounted for approximately 43% of overall model importance, underscoring the intertwined contributions of tumour burden, biology, nutrition, and host immunity to postoperative relapse. By translating these feature contributions into intuitive web-based AI-CDSS, the system offers transparent, scenario-specific explanations that strengthen clinician trust and enable nuanced, patient-centred counselling regarding adjuvant therapy intensity and surveillance planning.

Postoperative recurrence reflects a complex interplay between the intrinsic aggressiveness of the tumour and the patient’s biological factors [17–19]. Advanced pathological stage and metastatic status indicate substantial tumour burden and occult spread beyond the surgical field; such tumours typically exhibit elevated CEA and CA19-9 levels, which enhance cell adhesion and modulate immune responses, thereby favouring relapse [18–20]. Over-expression of EGFR amplifies proliferative signalling and reduces sensitivity to cytotoxic therapy, further increasing recurrence risk [21]. On the host side, low preoperative serum albumin, elevated neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, and thrombocytosis are associated with impaired antitumor immunity and predict worse clinical outcomes in colorectal cancer patients [22]. Moreover, the frequent use of targeted biologics in these patients signals an inherently aggressive disease course [18]. By integrating staging data, immunohistochemical markers, and routine laboratory results, the model captures complex, non-linear relationships that conventional staging overlooks, enabling more precise stratification of recurrence risk.

This study rests on four main strengths: First, the model was built and examined using data obtained from a prospective registry that spans five hospitals, yielding a large, well-documented patient group. Second, the data were split by date for training and validation, a setup that mirrors real-world use and minimises bias. Third, the model operated on a broad mix of inputs—including basic patient details, tumour stage, marker status, and routine blood tests—thus, it could capture the many factors linked to recurrence. Finally, we used SHAP values to show how each input shapes the prediction, which helps clinicians understand and trust the tool. Together, these steps provide a solid and transparent base for estimating postoperative recurrence risk.

However, this study has some limitations. First, because we relied on past medical records and excluded cases with missing information, unrecorded factors may still have influenced the findings. Second, the registry does not include lifestyle habits, genetic data, or complications that arose after surgery, so the model could not use these possible predictors. Third, cancer care changes over time, implying that the model will need regular retraining to keep pace with new treatments and guidelines. Finally, although our model achieved high specificity and overall accuracy, its sensitivity was moderate, correctly identifying approximately half of the recurrence cases in the validation cohort. This reflects our approach to balancing predictive performance while minimising false positives that could cause patient distress and unnecessary interventions. Threshold selection should be tailored to the disease stage. As Taiwan’s screening program identifies many patients at early stages, high specificity may be preferred to avoid unnecessary interventions for these patients, while advanced-stage patients may require thresholds favouring sensitivity to ensure recurrences are not missed.

Future studies will encompass several complementary directions. A prospective randomised controlled trial should clarify the influence of the tool on clinical decision-making and patient outcomes. A periodic model refinement that incorporates threshold optimisation, ensemble methods, genomic data, radiomic features, and longitudinal laboratory results is required to preserve predictive accuracy as treatment standards evolve. Developing a patient-facing interface could translate risk estimates into individualised survivorship plans and improve engagement in follow-up care. Finally, compared with conventional monitoring strategies, a rigorous health-economic analysis should determine the cost-effectiveness of AI-guided surveillance.

Conclusions

This study reported the development and validation of an AI-CDSS for predicting five-year colorectal cancer recurrence with strong discrimination (validation AUCs: 0.83–0.84), high accuracy (87%), PPV (85%), and NPV (87%). Deployed as a user-friendly web-based application, it delivers personalised risk estimates in under 1 s via an intuitive interface, which was iteratively refined based on clinician feedback. This tool was designed to enhance patient care by facilitating evidence-based discussions and treatment planning grounded in local population data.

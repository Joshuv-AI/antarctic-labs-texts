Case report: Two cases of ileo-ileal and colo-colic intussusceptions revealing Vanek's tumor
Authors: C Rhoul, N Khomsi, R Arena, H Braham, L Charre, A Police
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIntestinal obstruction represents a surgical emergency requiring urgent diagnosis and treatment. Vanek's tumor or inflammatory fibroid polyp (IFP) is a rare benign gastrointestinal tumor. This tumor is responsible for intestinal obstructions and rarely bleeding.Case reportWe report two cases of Vanek's tumor. The first was a 58-year-old woman with intestinal obstruction confirmed in computed tomography scan, the patient had an emergency laparotomy with bowel resection, the histopathological examination showed a polyp measuring 3.5 cm. The postoperative course was simple with no medico-surgical complications. The second case is a 22-year-old man with no significant pathological history who presented with colocolic intussusception and who underwent a left colectomy.DiscussionVanek's tumor is a rare tumor of the digestive tract. It is a benign tumor with no malignant potential, which can be asymptomatic or complicated by intestinal intussusception. Treatment is based on endoscopic resection when the tumor is small, or surgical resection in the case of complications.ConclusionIntestinal intussusception is a rare cause of intestinal obstruction in adults. IFP is a rare, benign tumor, which preferentially affects the stomach and ileum, and it can be responsible for serious complications.Keywords: Intestinal intussusception, Vanek's tumor, Inflammatory fibroid polyp, Surgical treatment

Introduction

Intestinal intussusception in adults is related to an organic origin in 70–90 % of cases [1,2]. Indeed, in small bowel intestinal intussusception is associated with benign tumors, such as Vanek's tumor, while malignant pathology is the primary cause in colon [3]. Inflammatory fibroid polyp (IFP) or Vanek's tumor is a rare benign that can affect gastro-intestinal tract [4]. There is considerable debate over the responsible cause of the development of this tumor; some physical, chemical, and metabolic elements are implicated [[3], [4], [5]]. They are typically found accidentally during the histological evaluation of an operative specimen following surgery of bowel obstruction or intussusception [6]. We present two cases who had intestinal intussusception as a result of Vanek's tumor.

Case presentation 1

A 57-year-old woman with history of with history of depression for 06 years and previous laparoscopic appendectomy 20 years ago with no personnel or familial history of chronic inflammatory bowel disease or malignant neoplasia of the digestive tract, who presented to emergency department with diffuse abdominal pain evolving for 04 days with nausea and vomiting associated with cessation of feces and gas. She was hemodynamically stable and the abdomen was soft with tenderness in the left iliac fossa without palpable abdominal mass. She had an elevated white cell count (15,800/µl) and C-reactive protein (51 mg/l). A computed tomography (CT) of the abdomen showed a dilatation of stomach and small bowel with intaluminal obstructive masse in the distal ileum (Fig. 1). The patient underwent an exploratory laparotomy, which revealed an ileo-ileal intussusception 10 cm from the ileo-caecal valve, with no digestive perforation and a small serosanguinous peritoneal effusion. A 25-cm segmental resection of the ileum, including the intussusception, was performed with end-to-end bowel anastomosis Anatomopathological examination revealed a 3.5 cm polyp composed of spindle cells arranged on a fibrous background rich in vessels and associated with inflammatory cells, notably eosinpphils. These spindle cells have an eosinophilic cytoplasm with an oval nucleus with clear chromatin. This histological description corresponds to the inflammatory fibroid polyp or Vanek's tumor. The post-operative course was marked by the resumption of intestinal transit 48 h after surgery, with a favorable clinical outcome. The patient was subsequently discharged on the 4th post-operative day. At the 4-week follow-up, the patient had fully recovered with no particular complaints.

Case presentation 2

A 22-year-old patient, with no notable personal or family medical, especially chronic inflammatory bowel disease or malignant neoplasia of the digestive tract, or surgical history, consulted for constipation that had been evolving for seven days. Clinical examination revealed tenderness in the left iliac fossa, with no defensiveness or obvious mass. Biological tests showed a moderate inflammatory syndrome (leukocytes: 11600/µl; haemoglobin: 12.6 g/dl; VGM: 80 fl; CRP: 62 mg/l). Abdomino-pelvic CT showed a left colo-colic intussusception with an intraluminal mass (Fig. 2). Rectosigmoidoscopy revealed a sub-mucosal, obstructive, intra-luminal lesion, not formally characterized. An oncologic left colectomy with laparoscopic colorectal anastomosis was performed. Pathological examination showed a lesion extending to all layers of the digestive wall (Fig. 3: a,b), with a proliferation of spindle cells and concentric collagen deposits in favour of a 10 cm PFI, without mutation of the KIT, PDGFRA or BRAF genes (Fig. 4: a,b). Immunohistochemistry was negative for anti-C-kit antibody and anti-DOG-1 antibody, and positive for anti-CD34 antibody (Fig. 4: c). The work has been reported in line with the SCARE criteria [7].

Discussion

Vanek's tumor was reported for the first time by Josef Vanek in 1949 [8]. Present throughout the gastrointestinal tract, primarily in the gastric antrum followed by ileum, and infrequently in the duodenum and jejunum [9]. It accounts for 0.1 % to 3 % of gastric polyps [10]. However; the ileal tract is where these tumors most frequently result in intussusception. [11]. This tumor predominantly affects women, with a median age of 50 years, but a wide age range was noted (4-84ans) [12,13].

IFPs are typically asymptomatic, meaning they may go unnoticed for a long period or be discovered by accident during laparotomies or endoscopic procedures [14]. The location and size of the tumor determine the clinical manifestations when such polyps become symptomatic [6]. Intussusception was observed in more than half of the patients with small bowel IFTs [15]. In some cases, digestive bleeding secondary to these tumors was noted [16].

CT-scan is more efficient for the diagnosis of intestinal intussusception [17]. It allows to identify the origin of the obstruction and possible complications, especially ischaemia and perforation [18]. The characteristics that can be found here include target, sausage and reniform pattern with an outer intussuscipiens and a central intussusceptum separated by mesenteric fat [19].

Macroscopically it appears like a perpendicular or sessile lesion measuring 0.2 to 20 cm in diameter and protruding into the lumen of the bowel [20]. Histologically these tumors grows from the submucosa and it consists of connective tissue, vessels and a predominantly eosinophilic inflammatory reaction [3]. On immunohistochemistry, tumor cells express CD34, but this positivity is absent in the intestinal form. Most of the time immunostains for KIT, DOG-1, S100 and EMA are negative [3].

IFP must be distinguished from other spindle-shaped tumors, in particular gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) and inflammatory myofibroblastic tumors [3,21]. GISTs often affect the stomach and give a polypoid appearance, while in the intestine they present as an invagination [3]. On immunohistochemical analysis, GISTs are generally positive for CD117 and DOG, which is not the case for IFP [21]. Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumors express ALK, desmin and keratin while IFPs do not [21]. It should be noted that GISTs and inflammatory myofibroblastic tumors have malignant potential, whereas IFPs are purely benign tumors [21].

Most of these tumors can be resected endoscopically, particularly in the case of small tumor [22,23]. In the case of large IFPs causing intestinal intussusception, they are resected either by laparoscopic exploration or by laparotomy [3]. Reduction of intestinal intussusception prior to resection is permitted only if there are no signs of intestinal ischemia or malignance [24]. However, for ileocolic and colocolic intussusception, resection without reduction is recommended [25,26]. The recurrence of this tumor is very rare [3].

Conclusion

The ileum is the preferred site for intestinal intussusception, often secondary to a benign tumor such as Vanek's tumor. The exact pathogenesis is still unknown but certain genetic and environmental factors have been described. Treatment is based on endoscopic resection if the tumor is discovered by incidentally, or surgery if it is complicated by intussusception.

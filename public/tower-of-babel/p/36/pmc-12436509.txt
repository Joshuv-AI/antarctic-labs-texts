Clinical parameters-based machine learning models for predicting intraoperative hemodynamic instability in hypertensive pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas patients
Authors: Houming Zhao, Lu Tang, Zhuoran Li, Xintao Li, Tongyu Jia, Jin Luo, Yujie Dong, Shangwei Li, Xin Ma, Peng Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo create machine learning (ML) models based on inflammatory markers and coagulation parameters for predicting intraoperative hemodynamic Instability (HI) in sustained hypertensive patients with pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas (PPGLs).Methods197 sustained hypertensive PPGLs patients who underwent laparoscopic or robotic-assisted surgeries were included. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression (LR) analyses were conducted to identify the independent risk factors for HI. Various ML methods were employed to construct predictive models, including random forest (RF) and support vector machine (SVM). The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, decision curve analysis (DCA), calibration curve, and Hosmer-Lemeshow test were employed to assess the performance of the ML models. The SHapley Additive explanation (SHAP) method was used to explain the model by prioritizing feature importance based on their contribution to the prediction.ResultsThe univariate and multivariate analyses revealed that the white blood cell-to-lymphocyte ratio (WLR), neutrophil-to-platelet Ratio (NPR), international normalized ratio (INR), and other clinical parameters were independent risk factors for HI (P < 0.05). The RF model exhibited the best predictive performance, with an AUC of 0.854 on the training set and 0.812 on the test set. The calibration plot and Hosmer-Lemeshow test showed the model had excellent concordance. DCA demonstrated that the predictive model was clinically practical and effective. The SHAP method identified WLR as the most critical factor contributing to the prediction.ConclusionIn patients with hypertensive PPGLs, inflammatory, coagulation, and other clinical parameters are correlated with a high risk of intraoperative HI. ML models have a good predictive ability for HI in patients with sustained hypertensive PPGLs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00345-025-05890-0.Keywords: Pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas, Hemodynamic instability, Machine learning, Predictive model

Introduction

Pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas (PPGLs) are endocrine tumors originating from neural crest-derived cells of the adrenal medulla or the sympathetic or parasympathetic (anterior thoracic and head and neck) paraganglia [1]. It is characterized by the excessive secretion of catecholamines and can cause life-threatening systemic effects such as stroke, heart attack, and multiple organ failure [2]. Surgical excision is recognized as the definitive therapeutic approach for managing PPGLs. Nevertheless, the anesthetic and surgical provocations may result in uncontrolled catecholamine release, which can incite intraoperative hemodynamic instability (HI), culminating in the onset of life-threatening complications [3, 4]. Compared to patients with PPGLs who have normotensive profiles, those with sustained hypertension demonstrate more prominent clinical manifestations, including headaches and palpitations, and possess higher levels of catecholamines within their systems [5]. Additionally, sustained hypertensive patients with PPGLs experience a substantially increased risk of surgical risks, exhibiting a threefold higher risk of intraoperative hypertensive emergencies and hypotensive episodes compared to normotensive individuals with PPGLs [6]. Therefore, constructing an intraoperative HI risk prediction model specifically for sustained hypertensive PPGLs patients holds greater significance.

Catecholamines secreted by PPGLs can induce metabolic alterations within the body. These biogenic amines possess the capability to potentiate inflammatory responses, exert effects on the coagulation system, and affect the structure and function of red blood cells [7–9]. However, the predictive efficacy of clinical laboratory indices such as inflammatory markers, coagulation parameters, and erythrocyte morphological indices for intraoperative HI in hypertensive PPGLs remains unclear. As an essential branch of artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML) methods can effectively analyze potential associations and rules in datasets by building adaptive algorithm models, and show better predictive effects [10]. Consequently, this research aims to investigate the predictive significance of these parameters for intraoperative HI in patients with sustained hypertensive PPGLs and to construct predictive models using ML methods for assessing the risk of HI.

Methods

Study population

Data were collected retrospectively from patients who underwent surgery for pheochromocytoma or paraganglioma at the First Medical Center, Chinese PLA General Hospital, from January 2012 to June 2021 in the present study. The inclusion criteria are as follows: [1] postoperative pathological confirmation of PPGLs; [2] medical history of sustained hypertension; [3] unilateral pheochromocytoma or paraganglioma; [4] laparoscopic surgery or robotic surgery; [5] no severe inflammation, infection, or blood disease. Patients who met any of the subsequent conditions were excluded from the follow-up analysis: [1] bilateral pheochromocytoma; [2] ectopic paraganglioma (bladder, pelvic, etc.); [3] recurrent PPGLs; [4] incomplete medical data. The detailed inclusion and exclusion process was illustrated in the Supplementary Fig. 1.

Patients with severe inflammation, active infection, or hematologic disorders were excluded during the study design phase to control for potential confounders that could affect inflammatory markers. A total of 197 patients were eventually included in this study. The patients were randomly divided into a training set and a test set at a ratio of 7:3. The cases in the training set were used to optimize the core parameters for building the predictive model, while the test set served as an independent sample set to evaluate the model’s generalization ability. Informed consent was obtained from all eligible patients, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Chinese PLA General Hospital (registration no.S2024-231-01).

Data collection

We collected routine examination data after admission, including demographic status, clinical symptoms, medical history, laboratory results obtained one week before the operation, tumor status, surgical records, intraoperative hemodynamic status, and postoperative pathological data. Tumor status covers its maximum diameters, anatomical localization, and pathological classification. Surgical methods encompass laparoscopic surgery and robotic surgery through the transperitoneal or retroperitoneal approach. The clinical symptoms include symptoms associated with elevated catecholamine levels that were present in patients before consultation. These encompassed headache, palpitations, excessive sweating, dizziness, and irritability. The length of clinical symptom refers to the duration (in months) from the onset of the aforementioned clinical symptom to the time the patient undergoes surgical intervention. Sustained hypertension was defined as (a) documented office systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) ≥ 90 mmHg on ≥ 2 separate outpatient visits, or (b) 24-h ambulatory SBP/DBP ≥ 130/80 mmHg [11].

SBP and DBP at presentation denote the initial systolic and diastolic pressure at the time of admission. Preoperative SBP and DBP represent the systolic and diastolic pressure measured in patients before surgery. Drug preparation refers to adrenergic alpha-receptor blockers, including phenoxybenzamine or terazosin. Duration of drug preparation refers to the number of preoperative alpha-receptor blocker administration days for drug preparation, and patients are typically required to engage in at least two weeks of drug preparation before surgery. Urinary catecholamine levels, including norepinephrine, epinephrine, and dopamine, were evaluated via high-performance liquid chromatography. The levels of blood catecholamine metabolites, methoxyadrenaline and methoxynorepinephrine, were quantified through high-performance liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry.

Laboratory results included white blood cell-to-lymphocyte ratio (WLR), neutrophil-to-platelet Ratio (NPR), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), platelet-to lymphocyte ratio (PLR), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), prothrombin time (PT), international normalized ratio (INR), thrombin time (TT), hematocrit (HCT), mean corpuscular volume (MCV), red cell distribution width (RDW), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), platelet count, white blood cell count, neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, monocyte count. The systemic inflammatory markers were calculated as follows: SII = (neutrophils*platelets)/lymphocytes, PLR = platelets/lymphocytes, MLR = lymphocytes/monocytes, WLR = white blood cell/lymphocyte, NPR = neutrophils/platelets. All laboratory indicators were measured within one week before surgery, using the most recent preoperative laboratory results.

A consistent, experienced team managed anesthesia to ensure stability and comparability in blood pressure (BP) management. Anesthesiologists closely monitored intraoperative BP and administered appropriate medications in response to fluctuations. The commonly used antihypertensive drugs include the vasodilator sodium nitroprusside, the alpha-adrenergic receptor blocker phentolamine, and the calcium channel blocker nicardipine. The widely used hypertensive drugs were mainly norepinephrine. BP and heart rate (HR) were digitally recorded in an automated, continuous fashion every five minutes through a catheter placed within the radial artery. HI events were the cumulative intraoperative frequency and duration of the following features [12, 13]: SBP ≥ 200 mmHg, SBP ≥ 180 mmHg, SBP ≥ 160 mmHg, SBP < 90 mmHg, DBP < 50 mmHg, mean arterial pressure (MAP) < 60 mmHg, HR ≥ 110 bpm, and HR ≤ 50 bpm. This study defined HI as an SBP ≥ 180 mmHg or MAP < 60 mmHg.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are expressed as frequency and percentage, and the significance was determined using χ²; if the theoretical count of a variable is less than 10, Fisher’s exact test was used. Validate the normality of continuous data using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Mean ± standard deviation is used to express normally distributed quantitative variables, and the significance was determined by employing Student’s t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables are represented using the median and the interquartile range (IQR), and the significance was determined using the Kruskal-Wallis rank sum test. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and calculation of the Youden Index were conducted for the target variables to determine their optimal cut-off points, which were then used to categorize the variables into a categorical format (Supplementary Table 1).

Univariate logistic regression (LR) analysis was performed on the inflammatory markers, coagulation markers, and erythrocyte morphological indices. Variables with P ≤ 0.05 from the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate LR analysis, and variables with P ≤ 0.05 in the multivariate LR were identified as the independent predictive factors of HI. Based on these variables, ML methods were used to construct predictive models, including Naïve Bayes (NB), Decision Tree (DT), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), AdaBoost, Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Support Vector Machine (SVM), and LR. 5-fold cross-validation was implemented for model selection and hyperparameter tuning to mitigate the risk of overfitting during model training.

The accuracy and discriminative ability were assessed by calculating the area under the ROC curve, the calibration curve, and the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to evaluate the clinical utility of prediction models by calculating the net benefits at different threshold probabilities in clinical decision-making. Sensitivity analysis was conducted using an external validation cohort, and a modified RF model incorporating continuous predictors instead of the original dichotomized variables to validate the model’s robustness and portability. The external validation cohort was established utilizing data from the Third Medical Center of PLA General Hospital and the Air Force Medical Center. This cohort comprised 37 consecutive cases from January 2012 to June 2025, rigorously selected according to the predefined inclusion/exclusion criteria. The Shapley Additive explanation (SHAP) method explained the model by prioritizing feature importance based on its contribution to the prediction. Analyses were performed using the Python software scikit-learn package (version 1.7.1), and the global random seed was set to 0. P ≤ 0.05 was defined as a statistical difference in this study.

Results

Characteristics of participants

After screening by the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 68 patients with HI and 129 patients without HI were included in the two cohorts. Table 1) displays the baseline characteristics of the patients. Patients with or without HI showed no notable distinctions in terms of sex, BMI, diabetes medical history, tumor size, tumor location, surgery technique (laparoscopic or robotic), surgery approach (transperitoneal or retroperitoneal), clinical symptom, drug preparation, SBP, and DBP at presentation, as well as preoperative SBP and DBP. The HI cohort had a longer duration of clinical symptoms, but the difference did not reach statistical significance. The study participants had a mean age of 51.01 ± 12.98 years in the HI cohort and 46.27 ± 13.22 years in the non-HI cohort. Patients with HI were significantly older than those without HI (P = 0.017). In terms of fluid catecholamine concentrations, the HI group exhibited increased concentrations of urinary adrenaline and noradrenaline compared to the non-HI group. However, these differences did not reach statistical significance. Conversely, the non-HI group exhibited significantly higher urinary dopamine levels (P = 0.027).

Univariate and multivariate LR analyses

Table 2 illustrates the relationship between inflammatory, coagulation, and erythrocyte markers and HI. The univariate LR analysis revealed that WLR, NPR, APTT, INR, MCV, age, and ALP were predictive factors for HI, whereas other variables showed no statistical difference (P > 0.05). After including these variables from univariate analysis into multivariate LR analysis, it was found that WLR (OR, 2.69; 95% CI, 1.09–6.63; P = 0.032), NPR (OR, 3.27; 95% CI, 1.21–8.82; P = 0.019), INR (OR, 4.28; 95% CI, 1.60–11. 46; P = 0.004), MCV (OR, 2.83; 95% CI, 1.16–6.91; P = 0.022), age (OR, 5.69; 95% CI, 1.93–16.82; P = 0.002), ALP (OR, 2.57; 95% CI, 1.02–6.44; P = 0.044) were identified as independent risk factors for HI.

ML models to predict the probability of HI

Furthermore, six independent risk factors for HI were included in the multivariate LR model as part of the ML-based predictive models. ML methods were used to construct predictive models, including NB, DT, LightGBM, AdaBoost, MLP, SVM, and LR (Fig. 1A and B). Among the models, the RF model exhibited the best predictive performance, with an AUC of 0.854 (95% CI: 0.789–0.918) on the training set and an AUC of 0.812 (95% CI: 0.699–0.924) on the test set. The detailed performance of several ML predictive models was shown in the Supplementary Table 2. The calibration curve demonstrated the excellent consistency between the predictions and actual observations (Fig. 1C and D). The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test indicated that the RF model in both the training and test sets demonstrated a good fit (P-value > 0.05). Furthermore, DCA showed that the RF model is effective in clinical practice (Fig. 1E and F).

In the sensitivity analysis, an external validation cohort comprised 37 patients was established. The Random Forest model demonstrated robust performance in this independent validation set, achieving an AUC of 0.807 (95% CI: 0.666–0.948) (Supplementary Tables 2 and 3). The RF model achieved an AUC of 0.861 (95% CI 0.800–0.922) in the training set and 0.836 (95% CI 0.674–0.885) in the test set using the continuous predictors (Supplementary Table 4). The result showed a modest improvement over the original dichotomised model. We retained the dichotomised variable approach for the primary model construction to prioritize bedside simplicity and facilitate clinical implementation. The results confirmed the model’s robust predictive performance and favorable portability.

The SHAP analysis elucidated the RF model’s feature importance, with WLR emerging as the most critical factor for prediction (Fig. 2). The analysis revealed that WLR, age, and NPR were the key variables affecting the model’s outcomes. Elevated WLR and NPR, markers of significant inflammatory or stress responses, correlated positively with SHAP values, indicating a heightened probability of HI.

Discussion

With the widespread use of advanced imaging technology and clinical awareness, PPGLs are increasingly being identified, prompting a greater focus on their diagnosis, management, and underlying pathophysiological mechanisms [14]. Intraoperative HI continues to be one of the most significant surgical and anesthetic challenges in surgery for PPGLs, and it is also receiving increasing recognition for its critical implications in patient management [15]. Previous studies have identified tumor size, surgical approach, and clinical symptoms as independent predictors of intraoperative hemodynamic fluctuations in patients with PPGLs [16–18].

Currently, inflammatory markers such as the SII and WLR, alongside coagulation marker INR, have been widely incorporated into the development of clinical predictive models. These markers have demonstrated efficacy in predicting the outcomes of various malignancies, including lung cancer, renal cancer, and colorectal cancer [19–21]. Additionally, inflammatory markers are closely linked with shifts in human metabolic and endocrine functions and are also linked to increased risk of hypertension and related cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality [22–24]. PPGLs, particularly those accompanied by sustained hypertension, represent a category of endocrine-metabolic disorders that may exhibit significant correlations with these markers. However, the predictive utility of these indices for intraoperative HI, particularly in patients with sustained hypertensive PPGLs, remains unclear.

Existing research has confirmed tumor size as an independent risk factor for HI, with larger tumors being associated with an increased risk of HI [16, 25]. However, in our cohort of patients with hypertensive PPGLs, no correlation was found between tumor size and the risk of HI. A study has demonstrated that there is no significant difference in tumor size between hypertensive PPGLs patients and those with normotension, yet the catecholamine secretion is markedly higher in the hypertensive group [26]. This suggests that in patients with hypertensive PPGLs, the adrenomedullary cells have a more substantial capacity to secrete catecholamines, which is not determined by tumor size. Therefore, in hypertensive PPGLs, the risk of HI and the volume of catecholamines secreted may be dictated by the biological function of the cells rather than the tumor size.

Intriguingly, the HI group in this study exhibited higher urinary norepinephrine and epinephrine levels than the non-HI group, which showed notably lower urinary dopamine levels. Previous research has demonstrated that hypertensive patients possess lower urinary dopamine concentrations than those with normal BP, and our results are consistent with this conclusion [6, 27]. Existing study also indicates that nearly all urinary dopamine originates from the renal uptake and decarboxylation of circulating L-DOPA, rendering it unsuitable for assessing tumor-derived dopamine production [28]. On the other hand, dopamine serves as a precursor to norepinephrine and epinephrine, and this process is catalyzed by the enzymes dopamine β-hydroxylase and phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase. Additionally, increased activity of dopamine β-hydroxylase and phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase was observed in hypertensive individuals [29]. Therefore, we hypothesize that in hypertensive individuals, dopamine may be more extensively converted into the more potent pressor agents, norepinephrine and epinephrine, potentially leading to more HI in patients with PPGLs. This needs further experimental validation.

In this study, considering the minimally invasive characteristic of laparoscopic and robot-assisted surgical approaches that can mitigate the impacts of surgical trauma on intraoperative hemodynamic variability in patients with PPGLs, we elected to exclude cases undergoing open surgery. Our study revealed that the surgery technique (laparoscopic versus robotic) and surgery approach (transperitoneal versus retroperitoneal) had no impact on the risk of HI in individuals with hypertensive PPGLs. The clinical presentation of PPGLs and the time from symptom onset to surgery (length of clinical symptom), measured in months as a continuous variable, were not determinants of HI risk.

Catecholamines exhibit a close relationship with inflammatory responses. These biogenic amines are identified as potent inflammatory activators of macrophages, upregulating NF-κB and further downstream cytokine production in these cells [30]. Additionally, studies have indicated that catecholamines can induce inflammatory responses in human hepatocytes [31]. PPGLs have also been found to secrete interleukin-6 (IL-6), which can further contribute to developing systemic inflammatory response syndrome [32, 33]. Furthermore, inflammatory markers were also found to be independent prognostic factors for PPGLs [34]. Regarding coagulation, catecholamines can trigger monocyte-endothelial adhesion, endothelial damage, and platelet aggregation and activation, subsequently leading to a hypercoagulable state in patients [35, 36]. Owing to the catecholamines produced by PPGLs, which induce inflammatory responses and cause a hypercoagulable state in the body, markers of inflammation and thrombin potentially serve as effective predictors of HI in patients with PPGLs, particularly those with hypertension. Consequently, we developed this research. It is worth noting that in our pre-data analysis, these markers were not significantly predictive of HI in the overall population of PPGLs patients and those without hypertension. However, it is conceivable that the heightened levels of catecholamines and more substantial endocrine impact in patients with hypertensive PPGLs could account for the notable predictive capability of these indicators for HI within this subgroup.

In order to enhance model transparency, we employed SHAP analysis. SHAP, grounded in game theory, quantifies the contribution of each feature to individual predictions, offering high interpretability and broad applicability. This analysis clearly illustrates the direction (positive or negative influence) and magnitude of each feature’s impact on the model’s output and the prediction trends across varying values of each feature. The results of SHAP analysis showed that WLR, age, and NPR were identified as key factors influencing the model’s predictions. An elevated WLR signals systemic inflammatory activation. This change contributes to a dynamic interplay among cellular proliferation, apoptosis, migration, inflammatory signaling, and extracellular matrix reorganization, all critical mechanisms of vascular remodeling [37, 38]. In parallel, coagulation factors function as signaling molecules that activate protease-activated receptors (PARs) ubiquitously expressed within the vasculature. PAR-mediated signalling modulates vascular smooth-muscle cell (VSMC) repair programmes and can precipitate pathological vascular remodelling, amplifying catecholamine-induced vasomotor lability [39]. Additionally, advanced age also contributed to higher HI risk, likely due to age-related physiological changes. The impact of MCV and ALP on HI risk was less clear-cut and may depend on various pathophysiological factors.

This study has demonstrated the predictive role of inflammatory and coagulation markers in the intraoperative HI of patients with sustained hypertensive PPGLs. Furthermore, intraoperative HI serves as an independent risk factor for postoperative cardiovascular events in patients with PPGLs [40, 41]. Additionally, inflammatory markers were also found to correlate with an increased incidence of cardiovascular events [42]. Therefore, we intend to examine further the predictive capacity of inflammatory and coagulation markers regarding cardiovascular events following PPGLs resection to enhance personalized therapeutic approaches and offer customized treatment strategies. Our study presents an innovative approach, as we have not encountered any research that predicts intraoperative HI in the pivotal patient population with sustained hypertensive PPGLs.

Based on the distribution of model-predicted probabilities and clinical feasibility, we stratified patients into three tiers: low risk (< 10%), intermediate risk (10–30%), and high risk (> 30%). We recommend standard intraoperative, non-invasive BP monitoring for low-risk patients and a basic vasoactive medication protocol. Intermediate-risk patients warrant increased non-invasive monitoring frequency and preparation of multiple vasoactive agents. In high-risk patients, invasive arterial pressure monitoring should be employed intraoperatively, accompanied by comprehensive preoperative optimization, meticulous surgical and anesthetic planning, and preparation for ICU admission. DCA demonstrated significantly higher net benefit for our model compared to both “aggressive monitoring for all patients” and “minimal monitoring for all patients” at threshold probabilities of 10–30%. At thresholds > 30%, net benefit plateaued, indicating robust clinical value for intensive interventions in intermediate and high risk status. By guiding targeted preventive and therapeutic strategies, it may significantly reduce complications from severe hypotension (organ hypoperfusion causing myocardial ischemia, stroke, or acute kidney injury) or hypertensive crises (heart failure, intracranial hemorrhage), thereby enhancing surgical safety and improving patient outcomes.

However, our study also has its limitations. Firstly, although the data from multiple centers were included, the overall sample size was still relatively small. Limited sample size predisposed our model to overfitting and parameter estimation bias. Additionally, as a retrospective study, selection bias inevitably exists, and there is a lack of prospective validation. In the future, a larger-scale multicenter prospective study is required to substantiate these findings.

Conclusion

In patients with sustained hypertensive PPGLs, elevated inflammatory and coagulation markers are associated with a high risk of intraoperative HI. ML models are successfully developed based on inflammatory markers and clinical parameters to predict HI effectively. This model can enhance the management of hypertensive PPGLs, assisting surgeons and anesthesiologists in focusing intraoperative hemodynamics on patients at high risk of HI and preventing the incidence of perioperative HI and associated complications.

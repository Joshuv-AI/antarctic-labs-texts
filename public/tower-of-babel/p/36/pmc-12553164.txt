RBM15 is a novel prognostic biomarker and correlates with immune cell infiltration in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Mingsheng Ma, Wei Wang, Li Li, Xiaoyan Wang, Qiubo Huang, Chen Zhou, Lianhua Ye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsThis study aimed to evaluate RBM15 gene expression and the potential effects as a biomarker in LUAD progression.Materials and methodsThe RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data and clinical data of patients with LUAD were acquired from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) databases. Kaplan-Meier (K-M) curves were generated to investigate the relationship between RBM15 and the prognosis of patients with LUAD. Gene Ontology (GO) and Reactome enrichment analyses were performed using the “cluster Profiler” R package. Finally, the Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER)database and CIBERSORT algorithm were used to assess the correlations between RBM15 expression and immune infiltration in LUAD.ResultsRBM15 was upregulated in tumor tissue, and it was regarded as an independent prognostic factor in LUAD. The genes co-expressed with RBM15 were closely related to cell cycle checkpoints and M phase signaling pathways. Furthermore, there was a significant correlation between RBM15 gene expression and immune infiltration in LUAD.ConclusionOur data suggested that RBM15 is critical in LUAD progression, is associated with tumor immune infiltration and served as a valuable potential diagnostic biomarker in patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-025-03585-6.Keywords: RBM15, Tumor infiltration, Prognosis, Lung adenocarcinoma, Lymphocytes introduction

Introduction

Lung cancer is a common malignancy, which accounts for a significant proportion of cancer-related deaths globally [1]. Based on histopathological features, 85% of the patients are categorized as non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [2]. LUAD is a common subtype of NSCLC, accounting for approximately 40% of all cases [3]. Despite receiving immunotherapychemotherapy and radiotherapy, the patients of 5-year survival rate with LUAD is still below 20%. Moreover, LUAD metastasis and recurrence are prevalent even when the tumor is diagnosed early. Various genomic studies have found some genetic changes in LUAD, which may be associated with its progression [4]. In addition, the tumor microenvironment (TME) largely affects the prognosis of tumor, and immune cells usually penetrate the TME, then interact with tumor cells in this compartment. Immunotherapy of the interaction between immune cells and tumor cells has shown encouraging results in some cancer patients [5]. Therefore, it is vital to identify additional prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for LUAD [6, 7].

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) RNA methylation is the most typical and extensive post-transcriptional modification of RNAs, which is a key gene regulatory mechanisms involved in cancer biology [8]. RBM15 has been found to be an important part of the m6A methyltransferase complex [9]. and can encode an RNA binding protein that regulates Notch, Wnt, and other signaling pathways. RBM15 plays a critical role in cell growth and apoptosis through these signaling pathways. In recent years, research on RBM15 has gradually increased, and it has been found that RBM15 is closely linked with the occurrence and development of many diseases [10]. For example, RBM15 is involved in RNA nuclear export, which is significantly associated with the tumor status in squamous cell carcinoma of the cervix and adenocarcinoma of the cervix (CESC) [11]. Additionally, the expression of specific m6A modulators (RBM15B and ZC3H13) is correlated with the expression of various immune gene biomarkers and the level of immune cell infiltration [12, 13]. Previous studies have suggested a close association between RBM15 and lung cancer, confirming that RBM15 is up-regulated in LUAD tissues and cell [14].However, the role and mechanism of RBM15 in LUAD infiltrating immune remain unclear.

Therefore, this article aims to excavate the expression and function of RBM15, its prognostic, and the relationship of tumor infiltrating immune cells. The results showed that RBM15 is important in LUAD, which provides reference biomarkers for predicting the prognosis of tumor patients. This article provides more insights into the molecular mechanism of LUAD, and it helps to develop more effective personalized treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

Dataset source

The RNA-seq data and corresponding clinical data of patients with LUAD were acquired from the TCGA database (https://www.cancer.gov/) and the TCGA-LUAD dataset is used for the training set. The GSE10072 [15], GSE31210 [16], and GSE43458 [17] were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) and they were used as a validation set.

Survival analysis

The cutoff value of RBM15 was determined using the surv_cutpoint function of Survminer software package. According to the cutoff value, patients with LUAD were classified into high expression and low expression groups. The differences in overall survival (OS) and disease-free intervals (DFI) between the two groups were compared using K-M analysis and log-rank test (P < 0.05).

Relationship of RBM15 expression with clinicopathological features

The RBM15 expression data and clinical information of patients with LUAD in TCGA database were obtained from UCSC Xena (http://xena.ucsc.edu/) [18, 19]. After deleting the incomplete cases, the significance of associations between RBM15 expression and clinical features was evaluated using the Chisquare test, and the relationship was further verified in the GSE10072 dataset. The differences between two expression groups in different clinical features (age, sex, stage, and smoking status) were determined using the Wilcoxon test. At the same time, the clinicopathological features were performed on the 80 lung tumor patients from January 2016 and December 2016.

Identification of co-expressed genes of RBM15 in TCGA

The genes co-expressed were screened using cBioPortal according to the criteria of |correlation coefficient|>0.4 and P-value < 0.05. The expression of the top 20 genes that were positively or negatively correlated with RBM15 were shown in a heatmap, which was generated using the “pheatmap” R package (version 1.0.12). Genes that were co-expressed with RBM15 were subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and Reactome enrichment analyses using the “clusterProfiler” R package (version 3.16.1) [20]. A protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of co-expressed genes was established using the STRING database (https://string-db.org/cgi/input.pl). Set the interaction score greater than 4.0 [21].

Correlation between RBM15 expression and immune cell infiltration

The abundance of six immune cells was evaluated using the TIMER (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/) database [22]. The correlations between RBM15 expression and immune cells were demonstrated using the gene module of TIMER database, ssGSEA and ESTIMATE algorithm. The immune activity of 28 tumor lymphocytes was scored by SSGSEA in each sample of TCGA data set [23]. The ESTIMATE algorithm immune score (ImmuneScore) matrix score (StromalScore) and their combined ESTIMATE score and tumor purity (TumorPurity) were used ESTIMATE algorithm.Subsequently, the relationship between RBM15 expression and the genetic markers of immune cells in LUAD were investigated using Spearman’s correlation analysis.

Immunohistochemical staining

Immunohistochemical staining was performed on the LUAD tissue, which consisted of 80 lung tumor tissues from January 2016 and December 2016. The protocol for the present study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of The Third Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University (Kunming, China) (KYCS202130) and was performed in accordance with Declaration of Helsinki. All patients included in the present study previously provided written informed consent. Those tissue were dewaxed, rehydrated and incubated with polyclone rabbit anti-human RBM15 (dilution 1:200, Bioss, bs-15019R) at 4 °C after epitope retrieval until overnight with H2O2 and non-specific antigens blocking treatment. Afterward, the results of the staining were observed under a microscope.

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses in our analyses were executed by using the R package. The diagnostic value of RBM15 in LUAD was evaluated by Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were employed to investigate whether RBM15 was an independent indicator for the prognosis of LUAD patient. P values less than 0.05 were regarded to be statistically significant.

Results

RBM15 expression was upregulated and influence prognosis in LUAD

The RNA-seq data was applied to estimate the RBM15 expression in tumor and normal tissues from the TCGA database. Compared with the corresponding non-carcinoma tissues, RBM15 expression was remarkably higher in LUAD (p < 0.001) (Fig. 1A). However, RBM15 expression in kidney renal papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP; p < 0.001), kidney chromophobe (KICH; p < 0.001), and thyroid carcinoma (THCA; p < 0.001) was considerably lower than that in the corresponding non-cancerous tissues (Fig. 1A). Additionally, significantly higher levels of RBM15 were observed in LUAD tissues than in normal tissues from GSE10072 (p = 0.015; Fig. 1B), GSE31210 (p = 1.4e-05; Fig. 1C) datasets. These results indicate that RBM15 is overexpressed in LUAD.To demonstrate the value of RBM15 in the prognosis of patients with LUAD, we compared the survival differences between the high and low expression groups of RBM15. The K-M curve indicated that LUAD patients with higher expression of RBM15 showed worse DFI (p = 1.26e-01and HR = 1.396; Fig. 1D) and OS (p = 1.50e-02 and HR = 1.445; Fig. 1E).RBM15 was essential for tumor development and could be identified as a potential prognostic marker for LUAD. Further, stage T1, Stage (I-II), and smoking in the high RBM15 expression group were significantly lower than in those with low RBM15 expression (p = 1.20e-04 and HR = 2.615; Fig. 1F, p = 3.16e-03 and HR = 1.725; Fig. 1G, p = 1.73e-04 and HR = 1.692; Fig. 1H). RBM15 was essential for tumor development and could be identified as a potential prognostic marker for LUAD. We used Prognoscan database to verify the prognostic effect of RBM15,the GSE31210 database showed that the high and low expression of RBM15 was significantly different in recurrence free survival and the survival time of patients with low expression of RBM15 was higher than that of patients with high expression of RBM15(Supplementary Table 1).

Identification of co-expressed key candidate genes from the RBM15 interaction network

Key candidate co-expressed genes with RBM15 from TCGA-LUAD were screened to further explore the RBM15. The results indicate that a total of 50 genes were obtained according to the screening criteria (| r | >0.4 and p < 0.05) and the top 20 node representing genes that were related with RBM15 in Supplementary Fig. 1 A and Table 3. GO enrichment analysis demonstrated that these related genes were significantly enriched in the “regulation of mitotic cell cycle phase transition” and “regulation of cell cycle phase transition” (Supplementary Fig. 1B). A PPI network was carried out based on these related genes using the STRING database (Supplementary Fig. 1 C). Six co-expressed key candidate genes were identified in the most crucial modules obtained using MCODE, including Topoisomerase IIβ-binding protein 1(TOPBP1), Cell division cycle 7(CDC7), Centromere protein F(CENPF), Centromere protein E (CENPE), Claspin gene(CLSPN), and Mini-chromosome maintenance (MCM8) (Supplementary Fig. 1D). The clustered heatmap showed that compared with the normal controls, the expression levels of six core genes in tumor tissues was higher, and these genes were positively correlated with the expression level of RBM15 (Supplementary Fig. 1E). Furthermore, it was found that CENPF, CENPE, CLSPN, and MCM8 were associated with the OS of LUAD, and the high expression groups had worse OS compared to that in the low expression groups (p = 0.00079 and HR = 2; Supplementary Fig. 1 F). These results further indicate that TOPBP1, CDC7, CENPF, MCM8, CLSPN can be used as candidate target genes for RBM15 by PPI network and MCODE.

Correlation of RBM15 expression with immune cell infiltration

The infiltration levels of six immune cells (CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, neutrophils, macrophages, B cells, and DCs) were assessed using TIMER. DCs were most abundant in the TME in LUAD (Fig. 3A). The immune cell infiltration abundances were inconsistent for LUAD, but the relationships were all positively correlated. (Fig. 3B). Then, we assessed the association between RBM15 expression and immune infiltration in LUAD. Consequently, RBM15 expression was positively correlated with the infiltration levels of B cells (p = 9.56e-04 and r = 0.15), CD4 + T cells (p = 8.54e-06 and r = 0.201), CD8 + T cells (p = 4.99e-04 and r = 0.157), neutrophils (p = 3.82e-07 and r = 0.229), and DCs (p = 5.19e-03and r = 0.126) in LUAD (Fig. 3C). Except for CD4 + T cells, the other immune cells exhibited significant differences in tumor and normal patients among the six types of immune cells (Fig. 3D). Further, there were considerable differences in the immune cell infiltration levels between the high expression and low expression groups of RBM15, and RBM15 expression was positively related with the B cell infiltration degrees for LUAD (Fig. 3E). Finally, after correcting for tumor purity, we demonstrated the relationship between RBM15 expression and genetic markers (complement component 3a receptor 1(C3AR1), chemokine receptor 8(CCR8), CD163, CD19, CD209, CD68, and CD84) and revealed that RBM15 expression was substantially associated with 16 markers in LUAD (Table 4). These results indicate that RBM15 expression was closely related with the infiltration levels of immune cells in LUAD.

Discussion

RBM15 belongs to SPEN (dividing ends) family members, which regulates the formation of X-inactive specific transcript(XIST) lncRNA m6A in human cells, and the mechanisms might be associated with its biding to RNA through interacting with spliceosome, and its binding to m6A methylated complex [24]. Currently published studies demonstrated that RBM15 was associated with the progression of hepatic cancer [24], colorectal cancer [25], leukemia [26], and laryngeal squamous carcinoma [27], while its relation with LUAD remained unclear. Given this situation, we further analyzed LUAD, and tested the expression of RBM15 mRNA. Moreover, we explored not only the diagnostic performance of RBM15, but the relationship between its expression and prognosis and immune infiltration.

TCGA data were used to estimate the expression of RBM15 mRNA in different types of cancer. The results indicated that the expression of RBM 15 mRNA increased remarkably in multiple cancers, including LUAD. However, RBM15 was down-regulated in other types of cancer, including in KIRP, KIRP and THCA, which might be due to the differences in the potential pathogenetic mechanisms of these cancers. Additionally, through the analyses for data sets from different databases, we fully demonstrated the over-expression of RBM15 in LUAD, compared with non-cancer tissues. The importance of RBM15 as biomarker for LUAD diagnosis and prognosis has never been assessed before. TCGA data revealed that the over-expression of RBM15 was associated with poor OS and PFI, and TCGA-based and clinical tumor patients multivariate COX regression and showed that RBM15 expression was the one of independent predictor for OS in LUAD. On the other hand, ROC curve indicated that RBM15 expression was of great value for diagnosing LUAD. Immunohistochemistry staining found that the expression level of RBM15 was significantly elevated in lung tumor tissues compared to paired tumor adjacent tissues. In general, these findings strongly supported RBM15 expression as a biomarker for LUAD diagnosis and prognosis.

Recent studies identified several potential mechanisms regarding the association of RBM15 over-expression with malignancy prognosis. Carcinogenesis refers to the un-controll able proliferation of cancer cells, and the abnormal expression of cell cycle-related proteins could contribute to cancer invasion, metastasis, inducible resistance, and anti-apoptosis. Multiple cell cycle-related proteins have been the targets for cancer treatment [28]. RBM15 up-regulation could lead to cancer progression, and might be associated with the poor prognosis of cancer [25]. In our study, we found that TOPBP1, CDC7, CENPF, MCM8, CLSPN can be used as candidate target genes for RBM15 by PPI network. These candidate target genes of RBM15 were mostly associated with cell cycle signaling pathways [29–33]. According to our enrichment analysis, RBM15 was associated with cell cycle signaling pathways, suggesting that RBM15 might interact with TOPBP1, CDC7, CENPF, MCM8, CLSPN affect the prognosis of LUAD through affecting cell cycle signaling pathways.TIMER method, ssGSEA and ESTIMATE algorithm were applied for detecting the relationship between RBM15 expression and cancer immune cell infiltration, which indicated that RBM15 was positively related with the infiltration degree of B cells, CD4 + T cells in LUAD. In addition, cancer purity was adjusted to explore the correlation between RBM15 and various immune cell genetic markers, and we found that RBM15 expression was remarkable correlated with 16 immune cell markers respectively in LUAD. Recently, Jiang et al. suggested that with the expression of RBM15 could improve prognosis of hepatocellular carcinoma(HCC)and correlated with immunotherapy response [34]. In our study, we found that both B cells and neutrophils were significantly different in the disease/normal group and in the RBM15 high/low expression group in LUAD.These findings provided robust evidence for the involvement of RBM15 in cancer immune infiltration in LUAD. Immunotherapy has reformed the treatment for many advanced malignancies [35]. The immune response in cancer site is predisposed by TME-infiltrated immune cells [36], and RBM15 might plays a crucial role in recruiting infiltration immune cells and regulating immune response, so as to affect the prognosis in LUAD.

This study deepens our understanding of the relationship between RBM15 and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), yet several limitations remain. Initially, we conducted bioinformatics analyses based on TCGA and GEO databases, identifying RBM15 as highly expressed in LUAD with potential prognostic value. To validate these findings, we further performed immunohistochemical (IHC) staining on 80 paired LUAD and adjacent non-tumor tissues and conducted univariate and survival analyses using clinical data, which confirmed the differential expression and prognostic relevance of RBM15, thus strengthening the clinical reliability of our results. Although this particular study did not include in vitro or in vivo functional assays, our team has conducted parallel research demonstrating that RBM15 promotes proliferation, migration, and invasion of LUAD cells, likely through the regulation of m6A-related pathways [37]. In the future, we aim to explore the specific mechanisms by which RBM15 modulates the tumor immune microenvironment, including its effects on immune cell function and its downstream targets. Furthermore, while several datasets (including TCGA-LUAD) suggest a strong correlation between RBM15 expression and immune cell infiltration, the relatively small sample sizes in some cohorts and the inconsistent expression patterns between tumor and normal tissues indicate that larger, multi-center clinical studies are still needed to comprehensively assess the robustness and generalizability of RBM15 as a prognostic biomarker in LUAD.

In conclusion, our study suggested that RBM15 could be applied as a biomarker for diagnosis and prognosis in LUAD. Additionally, we found several RBM15-related pathways such as cell cycle, DNA replication, RNA interference and ATPase activity. RBM15 expression is associated with the infiltration degree of different immune cells in cancer site. This study provided reference for the further exploration of new cancer immunotherapy.

Global trends, sex- and country-specific disparities in nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) mortality: A comprehensive analysis of the Global Burden of Disease data across 204 countries (1990–2023)
Authors: Ibrahim Khalil, Sajjad Ghanim Al-Badri, Md. Imran Hossain, Athar Ud din, Mahfuja Jahan Ima, Mohd Turzo Rahman, Mst. Mahmuda Akter, Lota Halder, Arindam Das Joy, Sunjida Amin Promi, Md Abu Sayed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (now termed metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease [MASLD]) is a leading cause of liver-related mortality worldwide, linked to the rising prevalence of metabolic syndrome. Despite prior analyses using earlier Global Burden of Disease (GBD) iterations, comprehensive trends incorporating post-2020 data remain limited. This study provides the first analysis utilizing the latest GBD 2023 release, examining global, sex-, and country-specific disparities in MASLD age-standardized mortality rates (ASMR) from 1990 to 2023 across 204 countries, highlighting the impact of recent events like the COVID-19 pandemic on metabolic health. MASLD ASMR data were extracted from the GBD 2023 database, defined by International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision codes. Estimated annual percentage changes (EAPC) in ASMR were calculated using logarithmic linear regression. Analyses were stratified by sex and country, with 95% confidence intervals derived from the standard error of β. Globally, the mean EAPC was 0.152% (range: −3.079% in the Republic of Korea to 4.143% in Kazakhstan) for both sexes, 0.020% for females (−3.630% in Bermuda to 4.612% in the Russian Federation), and 0.291% for males (−2.970% in the Republic of Korea to 4.277% in Turkmenistan). Increases predominated in 111 countries (54.4%), with significance in 94; declines in 93, significant in 78. Pronounced rises occurred in Eastern Europe/Central Asia (e.g., Kazakhstan, Russian Federation) and the Middle East (e.g., Oman), while declines were evident in East Asia (e.g., the Republic of Korea) and Southern Europe (e.g., Italy). Males showed higher EAPC in 119 countries, with a mean sex difference of 0.271%; notable disparities included Cuba (males 3.296% vs females −0.172%). Country-specific trends revealed vulnerabilities in low- and middle-income countries, with mixed patterns in Africa. This inaugural GBD 2023 analysis reveals a modest global rise in MASLD mortality, with regional surges in transitioning economies and male-predominant disparities. Urgent, tailored interventions targeting metabolic risks are essential to mitigate this escalating burden.

1. Introduction

Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (now termed metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease [MASLD] to better reflect its metabolic underpinnings) represents a spectrum of liver conditions characterized by excessive fat accumulation in hepatocytes, unrelated to significant alcohol consumption or other secondary causes.[1,2] This condition ranges from simple steatosis to nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), fibrosis, cirrhosis, and ultimately hepatocellular carcinoma, posing a substantial threat to global public health.[3,4] Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is intimately linked to metabolic syndrome, including obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), dyslipidemia, and hypertension, which are escalating worldwide due to urbanization, sedentary lifestyles, and dietary shifts toward high-calorie, processed foods.[5,6] The global prevalence of NAFLD has been estimated to affect approximately 25% to 30% of the adult population, with projections indicating a continued rise, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) undergoing rapid epidemiological transitions.[7,8]

The epidemiology of NAFLD has evolved dramatically over the past 3 decades. Early studies, primarily from high-income countries (HICs) like the United States and Europe, highlighted its association with obesity epidemics, but recent data reveal a shifting burden toward Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East, where urbanization and Westernized diets are accelerating metabolic disorders.[9,10] For instance, systematic analysis of GBD 2017 data has reported that sub-Saharan Africa had the highest mortality rates of cirrhosis in the world.[11,12] These disparities underscore the influence of socioeconomic development, genetic factors, and environmental exposures on disease distribution.[13,14] Moreover, NAFLD is not merely a hepatic disorder; it contributes significantly to extrahepatic complications, including cardiovascular disease, which remains the leading cause of death among affected individuals, followed by liver-related mortality.[15,16]

Mortality attributable to NAFLD has garnered increasing attention as the disease progresses to advanced stages. Global Burden of Disease (GBD) studies have documented a steady increase in NAFLD-related deaths, with age-standardized mortality rates (ASMR) rising in parallel with incidence and prevalence.[17,18] From 1990 to 2019, the GBD 2019 study reported a 25% increase in NAFLD prevalence globally, with particularly sharp rises in regions like East Asia and North Africa. The highest NAFLD prevalence rate was observed in Latin America (44.4%), followed by North Africa and the Middle East (36.5%), South Asia (33.8%), South-East Asia (33.1%), North America (31.2%), East Asia (29.7%), Asia Pacific (28.0%), and Western Europe (25.1%). Projections suggest that by 2030, the number of individuals with NAFLD could increase significantly, leading to a substantial uptick in liver-related deaths and decompensated events.[19–21] These trends are exacerbated by the aging global population, as NAFLD prevalence peaks in the 40 to 60 age group, coinciding with peak productivity years and imposing economic burdens through healthcare costs and lost productivity.[22,23]

Sex-specific disparities in NAFLD epidemiology add another layer of complexity. Historically, NAFLD was considered more prevalent in men, attributed to differences in body fat distribution, hormonal influences (e.g., protective effects of estrogen in premenopausal women), and lifestyle factors.[24,25] However, emerging data indicate shifting patterns, with postmenopausal women experiencing accelerated disease progression due to estrogen decline, potentially narrowing or reversing the sex gap in older cohorts.[26,27] A study found a higher pooled prevalence in men than women, but women exhibited higher risks of progression to advanced fibrosis.[28] Country-specific variations further highlight inequalities; in LMICs, limited access to diagnostic tools like transient elastography or magnetic resonance imaging may underdiagnose NAFLD in women, who often face barriers to healthcare due to sociocultural factors.[29,30]

The rationale for this study stems from the need to comprehensively map global, sex-, and country-specific trends in NAFLD mortality using the most recent GBD data spanning 1990 to 2023. Previous analyses, such as those from GBD 2017 and 2019, have provided snapshots but lack the granularity of post-2020 data, which captures the potential impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic on metabolic health, including weight gain from lockdowns and delayed care for chronic conditions.[31,32] By calculating estimated annual percentage changes (EAPC) in ASMR, this analysis aims to identify high-burden regions, quantify disparities, and inform targeted interventions. Understanding these trends is crucial for policymakers, as NAFLD is projected to become the leading indication for liver transplantation in many countries by 2030, straining healthcare systems already burdened by viral hepatitides and alcohol-related liver disease.[33,34]

In summary, this study leverages GBD 2023 data to elucidate temporal trends in NAFLD mortality, emphasizing sex and geographic disparities. By doing so, it contributes to the growing body of evidence on NAFLD as an emerging global epidemic, calling for integrated strategies that address upstream determinants like obesity and diabetes while enhancing surveillance and early detection in vulnerable populations.[35]

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data source

This study utilized data from the GBD Study 2023, coordinated by the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation at the University of Washington.[36] To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to employ the GBD 2023 dataset for analyzing NAFLD mortality trends. The GBD framework provides comprehensive, comparable estimates of health loss due to diseases, injuries, and risk factors across 204 countries and territories, stratified by age, sex, year, and location.[37] For NAFLD, defined using International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision codes K75.8 (NASH) and K76.0 (fatty change of liver, not elsewhere classified), the GBD incorporates a wide array of data sources, including vital registration systems, verbal autopsy surveys, cancer registries, hospital discharge records, and literature reviews.[36–38] These inputs are processed through sophisticated modeling techniques to address inconsistencies, incompleteness, and biases in raw data, ensuring robust estimates even in regions with sparse primary data.[39]

Mortality estimates were derived using underlying-cause coding within the GBD framework (Cause of Death Ensemble model), with NAFLD/MASLD-specific mortality extracted after accounting for competing liver etiologies through the standard GBD hierarchical redistribution and modeling processes. The analysis used point estimates of ASMR for EAPC calculations, while 95% uncertainty intervals incorporated the full distribution from 1000 posterior draws where relevant for robustness checks. ASMRs are standardized to the GBD world population standard.[40,41] Data extraction specifically focused on NAFLD ASMR from 1990 to 2023, covering the full temporal span available in the GBD 2023 release. This period includes pre- and post-COVID-19 eras, allowing assessment of potential pandemic-related perturbations in trends.[42] The analysis encompassed estimates for both sexes combined, as well as sex-stratified (male and female) data. Country-level aggregation was maintained without subnational breakdown, consistent with GBD’s emphasis on national-level policy relevance.[43] All estimates were accompanied by 95% confidence intervals (CIs), computed via 1000 draws from the posterior distribution of each modeling step, incorporating uncertainties from data inputs, model selection, and non-sampling errors.[44]

2.2. Calculation of EAPC

To assess temporal changes in NAFLD ASMR, we calculated the EAPC using a logarithmic linear regression model, a method widely employed in epidemiological trend analyses to quantify relative changes over time.[45] The EAPC was derived from the formula: EAPC = 100 × (exp(β) − 1), where β represents the slope coefficient obtained from the regression model ln(ASMR) = α + β × calendar year + ε, with α as the intercept and ε as the error term.[46] This approach assumes a log-linear relationship, providing an average annual percentage change that accounts for the compounding nature of rates over the study period. Positive EAPC values signify increasing trends, negative values indicate decreases, and values approximating zero suggest temporal stability.[47] The 95% CIs for EAPC were estimated from the standard error of β, using the formula: 95% CI = 100 × (exp(β ± 1.96 × SE(β)) − 1), where SE(β) is the standard error.[48] Statistical significance was determined if the CI did not cross zero, indicating a reliable directional trend.

EAPC computations were performed independently for both sexes combined, females, and males to delineate sex-specific trajectories. This stratification is critical given documented differences in NAFLD pathogenesis and outcomes between sexes.[49] Sensitivity analyses explored nonlinear models (e.g., joinpoint regression) to detect potential inflection points, such as around 2020 due to COVID-19, but linear assumptions held robustly across most countries.[50]

2.3. Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were employed to summarize global, regional, and national EAPC distributions, including means, medians, ranges, and interquartile ranges for continuous variables, and frequencies for categorical ones (e.g., number of countries with positive/negative EAPC).[51] Heterogeneity was qualitatively evaluated by categorizing countries into GBD super-regions (e.g., Central Europe, Eastern Europe, and Central Asia; high-income Asia Pacific; Latin America and Caribbean) and socio-demographic index quintiles, allowing identification of patterns linked to development levels.[52] Sex disparities were quantified as the absolute difference in EAPC between males and females for each country, with summary measures including mean differences and rankings of the largest gaps to highlight priority areas.[53] GBD super-regions and socio-demographic index quintiles were used for qualitative pattern evaluation as described in the Results. Sensitivity analyses explored nonlinear models (e.g., joinpoint regression) to detect potential inflection points, such as around 2020 due to COVID-19; results confirmed that linear assumptions held robustly across most countries, with no major inflection points altering overall EAPC interpretations.

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.4.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing; https://www.R-project.org), leveraging packages such as “tidyverse” for data wrangling and visualization, “dplyr” for summarization, “ggplot2” for graphical representations, and “lm” for linear regressions in EAPC calculations.[54] Custom scripts ensured reproducibility, with code available upon request. Sensitivity checks involved excluding data from 2020 to 2023 to assess COVID-19 impacts, revealing minimal alterations in overall trends, suggesting resilience in GBD modeling.[55] This is an ecological study using aggregated mortality data without individual-level linkage to metabolic risk factors. No adjustments for multiple comparisons were applied, as the analysis was exploratory. The absence of multiple-comparison adjustment is noted as a limitation of this exploratory approach. Ethical approvals were not required, as the study relied on de-identified, publicly accessible GBD data, exempting it from institutional review board approval. Nonetheless, all data utilization conformed to the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation’s terms of use, including appropriate attribution and adherence to dissemination guidelines that promote open access while protecting data provenance.[56]

Drawing from the GBD 2023 systematic analysis spanning 1990 to 2023, this examination reveals a complex tapestry of mortality trends across 204 countries and territories. The ASMR for MASLD, quantified through EAPC, highlight not only global patterns but also stark regional, national, and sex-specific disparities (Figs. 1–10).

Globally, the mean EAPC for both sexes combined stands at a modest 0.152% (Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content 1), indicating a slight upward trajectory in MASLD-related deaths over the 33-year period. This average masks considerable variation, with the most dramatic decline observed in the Republic of Korea at −3.079% (95% CI = −3.209 to −2.948) and the sharpest rise in Kazakhstan at 4.143% (95% CI = 3.682 to 4.606).

When stratified by sex, females exhibit a near-flat mean EAPC of 0.020%, ranging from a steep drop of −3.630% (95% CI = −4.017 to −3.242) in Bermuda to a pronounced increase of 4.612% (95% CI = 3.992 to 5.235) in the Russian Federation. In contrast, males show a higher mean EAPC of 0.291%, with extremes from −2.970% (95% CI = −3.048 to −2.891) in the Republic of Korea to 4.277% (95% CI = 3.995 to 4.560) in Turkmenistan. These figures underscore a subtle but persistent global rise in MASLD mortality, with males generally experiencing more accelerated increases than females. This sex difference may reflect variations in metabolic risk factors, healthcare-seeking behavior, and hormonal influences.

3.2. Distribution of increasing and decreasing trends

At the country level, the distribution of trends paints a heterogeneous picture. A total of 111 nations (54.4%) reported positive EAPC values for both sexes, signaling growing mortality burdens, while 93 countries showed declines. For females, declines slightly dominated (107 countries) over increases (97 countries). Males showed higher EAPC in 119 countries, with a mean sex difference of 0.271%; notable disparities included Cuba (males 3.296% vs females −0.172%).

Statistical significance further refines this landscape, with 94 countries demonstrating significant rises and 78 verifying notable decreases for both sexes (where 95% CIs excluded zero). This bifurcation often aligns with socioeconomic development levels: rapid increases tend to cluster in transitioning economies, whereas declines are more common in high-income regions, possibly due to stronger public health infrastructure and early detection programs.

3.3. Regional patterns of increasing trends

Increasing trends in MASLD mortality were most pronounced in Eastern Europe, Central Asia, the Middle East, and selected parts of North America and Latin America.

Kazakhstan recorded the highest EAPC globally at 4.143% (95% CI = 3.682 to 4.606) for both sexes, followed closely by neighboring Turkmenistan at 3.531% (95% CI = 3.270 to 3.791). The Russian Federation also showed a steep rise at 4.120% (95% CI = 3.525 to 4.718), with a notable sex disparity – females experienced a higher EAPC (4.612%, 95% CI = 3.992 to 5.235) than males (3.497%, 95% CI = 2.935 to 4.063). Lithuania reported an EAPC of 3.644% (95% CI = 2.782 to 4.513), substantially higher than the United Kingdom’s 2.938% (95% CI = 2.486 to 3.392).

In the Middle East, Oman demonstrated a marked increase at 2.876% (95% CI = 2.671 to 3.081), while Saudi Arabia showed a more moderate rise at 1.755% (95% CI = 1.576 to 1.933), largely driven by females (2.317%, 95% CI = 2.125 to 2.509). In North America, Canada recorded an EAPC of 2.818% (95% CI = 2.663 to 2.974), with females again showing a higher rate (3.041%) than males (2.613%). The United States exhibited a more modest increase at 1.420% (95% CI = 1.236 to 1.603).

Latin America displayed nuanced rising patterns. Nicaragua (2.545%) and Paraguay (2.262%) showed consistent increases, predominantly among males. Cuba presented an interesting contrast, with an overall EAPC of 1.661% that masked a sharp male-specific rise (3.296%, 95% CI = 2.836 to 3.759) against a slight decline among females (−0.172%). In Oceania, Australia (1.668%) and New Zealand (1.048%) showed moderate increases, while smaller island nations such as Tuvalu exhibited male-dominant trends.

3.4. Regional patterns of declining trends

In contrast, declining trends in MASLD mortality were prominent in East Asia, Southern Europe, and parts of the Caribbean and sub-Saharan Africa. The Republic of Korea led with the steepest decline globally at −3.079% (95% CI = −3.209 to −2.948), followed by Italy at −2.861% (95% CI = −2.989 to −2.732). Bermuda recorded a substantial decrease of −2.356%, particularly pronounced among females (−3.630%). Other notable declines were observed in Japan (−1.998%), Portugal (−2.004%), China (−1.482%), and Spain (−1.775%), with females often benefiting more in several European countries.

In South Asia, Bangladesh showed a decline of −1.525%. Several small nations and territories, including San Marino, Luxembourg, and Singapore, also demonstrated reductions. In sub-Saharan Africa, Burkina Faso recorded one of the largest declines at −2.073% (95% CI = −2.179 to −1.966). These downward trends are potentially linked to improvements in healthcare access, lifestyle interventions, and public health policies, although competing causes of mortality in low-resource settings may also influence observed patterns.

3.5. Sex-specific disparities

Sex disparities were evident across most regions, with males exhibiting a mean EAPC 0.271% higher than females globally. This male predominance was particularly striking in countries such as Cuba (males 3.296% vs females −0.172%) and Belize (males 1.754% vs females 0.151%). However, the pattern was not universal. In the Russian Federation and Benin, females showed higher EAPC values, suggesting context-specific factors including hormonal changes, differences in healthcare utilization, and varying exposure to metabolic risks. Detailed country-level EAPC values for both sexes, males, and females are presented in Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content 1.

3.6. Summary of key findings

Overall, this GBD 2023-based analysis reveals a modest global increase in MASLD mortality between 1990 and 2023, characterized by substantial heterogeneity across regions and sexes. Rising burdens are concentrated in countries undergoing rapid socioeconomic transitions, particularly in Eastern Europe, Central Asia, and the Middle East. Conversely, several HICs and selected East Asian nations have experienced declining trends. Males generally demonstrated faster increases in mortality rates compared to females, although important exceptions highlight the need for a nuanced, sex-specific understanding of MASLD epidemiology.

These patterns underscore the evolving nature of MASLD as a global health challenge. While the overall rise remains relatively modest, the pronounced increases in certain transitioning economies signal the need for urgent public health attention. Tailored interventions focusing on healthy diets, physical activity, early screening, and management of metabolic risk factors will be essential, particularly in addressing observed sex disparities, to mitigate the future burden of this preventable disease.

4. Discussion

The findings from this comprehensive analysis of GBD 2023 data from 1990 to 2023 reveal a nuanced and multifaceted global picture of NAFLD mortality trends, characterized by a modest overall increase in ASMR, marked regional and national heterogeneity, and notable sex-specific disparities.[36] With a mean EAPC of 0.152% for both sexes combined, the data indicate that NAFLD-related deaths are rising globally (Figs. 1–10), albeit at a pace slower than some earlier projections based on prevalence surges.[57] This upward trajectory aligns closely with prior epidemiological studies and systematic reviews, which have consistently documented the escalating burden of NAFLD amid the intertwined global pandemics of obesity, T2DM, and metabolic syndrome.[1,58] For instance, a GBD study estimated that NAFLD prevalence has doubled in some regions since the early 2000s, reaching over 30% globally by 2017, driven by metabolic risk factors that directly accelerate liver-related mortality.[11] The modest global EAPC observed here may reflect offsetting effects: rapid increases in LMICs balanced by stabilizations or declines in HICs where interventions like lifestyle modifications and pharmacological advances (e.g., glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists) are more accessible.[59,60]

Regionally, the most pronounced increases in NAFLD ASMR were observed in Eastern Europe and Central Asia, exemplified by Kazakhstan (EAPC 4.143%, 95% CI = 3.682 to 4.606) and the Russian Federation (4.120%, 95% CI = 3.525 to 4.718), regions undergoing rapid socioeconomic transitions.[61] These trends may be attributable to dietary Westernization, increased consumption of ultra-processed foods high in fructose and saturated fats, and rising alcohol co-consumption, despite NAFLD’s nominal exclusion of alcohol etiology.[62,63] National surveys in these areas have reported obesity rates doubling since 1990, with concurrent surges in T2DM prevalence exceeding 50% in some cohorts, as corroborated by GBD 2019 ancillary data.[19,64] Similarly, Middle Eastern nations such as Oman (2.876%, 95% CI = 2.671 to 3.081) and Saudi Arabia (1.755%, 95% CI = 1.576 to 1.933) exhibit high EAPC values, likely linked to high sedentary behavior rates (over 70% in urban populations) and dietary shifts toward energy-dense meals, as evidenced by prospective cohort studies.[65,66] In North America and Western Europe, increases like those in Canada (2.818%, 95% CI = 2.663 to 2.974) and the United Kingdom (2.938%, 95% CI = 2.486 to 3.392) underscore the persistent challenge even in HICs, where aging populations and post-COVID metabolic disruptions (e.g., weight gain from lockdowns) may exacerbate trends.[42,67]

Conversely, declines were prominent in East Asia and Southern Europe, with the Republic of Korea showing the steepest drop (−3.079%, 95% CI = −3.209 to −2.948) and Italy following closely (−2.861%, 95% CI = −2.989 to −2.732).[68] These reductions suggest the efficacy of targeted public health measures, including widespread metabolic screening programs, promotion of traditional low-glycemic diets, and early intervention for at-risk groups.[69,70] Japan’s decline (−1.998%, 95% CI = −2.183 to −1.814) aligns with national initiatives like the “Health Japan 21” campaign, which has reduced refined sugar intake and improved NAFLD outcomes, as demonstrated in longitudinal studies.[71] In sub-Saharan Africa, mixed trends – such as Ethiopia’s modest increase (0.413%, 95% CI = 0.343 to 0.484) versus Burkina Faso’s sharp decline (−2.073%, 95% CI = −2.179 to −1.966) – highlight diagnostic and reporting challenges, where NAFLD is often underrecognized amid competing infectious liver diseases like hepatitis B and C.[72,73] This underestimation is compounded by limited access to imaging modalities, leading to potential biases in GBD modeling.[74]

Sex-specific analyses revealed notable disparities, with males displaying higher EAPC values than females in the majority of countries, yielding a mean difference of 0.271%.[75] This male predominance is evident in contexts like Cuba (males 3.296%, 95% CI = 2.836 to 3.759 vs females −0.172%, 95% CI = −0.551 to 0.208), potentially due to greater male exposure to visceral obesity, insulin resistance, and occupational hepatotoxins, as supported by meta-analyses showing 19% to 44% higher NAFLD prevalence in men.[24,76] Hormonal mechanisms further explain these patterns; estrogen’s protective role against fibrosis in premenopausal women diminishes postmenopause, accelerating progression in older female cohorts.[26,77] However, in select countries like the Russian Federation (females 4.612%, 95% CI = 3.992 to 5.235 vs males 3.497%, 95% CI = 2.935 to 4.063) and Saudi Arabia (females 2.317%, 95% CI = 2.125 to 2.509 vs males 1.432%, 95% CI = 1.245 to 1.620), females exhibited higher EAPC, possibly reflecting gender-specific barriers to care or higher postmenopausal risks in aging populations.[78,79] These findings resonate with a 2020 study, which reported lower NAFLD risk in women but higher fibrosis progression once diagnosed.[28,80] Such disparities underscore the need for sex-stratified approaches in NAFLD management, incorporating gender-sensitive screening and therapies.[81]

Country-specific insights illuminate vulnerabilities, particularly in LMICs where data scarcity likely underestimates true burdens.[82] For instance, in Latin America, nations like Nicaragua (2.545%, 95% CI = 2.349 to 2.742) and Paraguay (2.262%, 95% CI = 2.121 to 2.404) face economic transitions fueling metabolic epidemics, with obesity rates surpassing 30% and limited access to antifibrotic agents.[83,84] In contrast, small island nations like Bermuda (−2.356%, 95% CI = −2.738 to −1.971) show declines, possibly due to robust healthcare systems and tourism-driven health awareness.[85] African trends, with mixed signals (e.g., South Africa’s increase at 0.885%, 95% CI = 0.616 to 1.154), highlight the dual burden of communicable and noncommunicable diseases, where NAFLD coexists with HIV and tuberculosis, complicating attribution.[86,87]

Comparatively, our EAPC estimates extend and refine those from earlier GBD iterations (e.g., GBD 2019), incorporating 2020 to 2023 data to capture post-COVID effects like increased sedentary behavior and delayed diagnostics, which may have amplified mortality in vulnerable groups.[88,89] Declines in HICs suggest mitigating impacts from therapeutic advances, including semaglutide and bariatric surgery, with randomized trials reporting NASH resolution rates up to 60% to 80%.[90,91] However, the slower global rise than anticipated may indicate underreporting in LMICs or model adjustments in GBD 2023.[92]

The implications are profound: rising NAFLD mortality signals an urgent need for multisectoral responses, integrating obesity prevention, universal screening, and equitable access to emerging therapies like resmetirom.[93,94] Policy frameworks, such as those advocated by the World Health Organization, should prioritize LMICs to avert projected escalations.[95]

Overall, these trends affirm NAFLD’s evolution from a niche “Western” ailment to a ubiquitous global challenge, necessitating collaborative, evidence-based strategies to mitigate its socioeconomic toll.[96,97]

4.1. Study limitations

While the GBD 2023 framework offers unparalleled global coverage and methodological rigor, several limitations must be acknowledged.[36] First, GBD estimates heavily rely on statistical modeling to impute missing data, particularly in LMICs where vital registration systems cover <50% of deaths (e.g., sub-Saharan Africa), potentially leading to underestimation of NAFLD mortality due to misclassification with undifferentiated liver diseases or underreporting amid competing causes like infectious hepatitides.[98,99] Second, NAFLD ascertainment in GBD is predicated on International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision codes, which lack granularity to differentiate simple steatosis from progressive NASH or cirrhosis without histological confirmation, risking trend inflation in regions with improved diagnostic coding or deflation where biopsies are rare.[100,101] Third, the analysis does not explicitly disentangle comorbidities such as COVID-19, which may have indirectly amplified NAFLD deaths through metabolic exacerbations like pandemic-related weight gain and healthcare disruptions.[88,89,102,103] Fourth, the EAPC metric assumes log-linear trends, which may mask nonlinear dynamics, such as abrupt shifts post-2020, although sensitivity analyses confirmed robustness.[104] Fifth, sex disaggregation could be biased by gender inequities in healthcare access, with women in patriarchal societies potentially underdiagnosed due to stigma or resource constraints, skewing female EAPC downward.[105,106] Finally, while GBD incorporates uncertainty intervals, these may not fully capture systematic biases from data quality variations across countries.[107]

4.2. Future directions

Future research should prioritize enhancing primary data collection in underrepresented LMICs through multinational prospective cohorts, leveraging noninvasive diagnostics like FibroScan or controlled attenuation parameter to improve NAFLD ascertainment.[108,109] Interventional randomized controlled trials targeting sex-specific risks such as estrogen-modulating therapies for postmenopausal women or androgen-focused approaches for men are warranted to address disparities.[110,111] Globally, integrating NAFLD screening into existing T2DM and cardiovascular disease programs could facilitate early detection and cost-effective management.[112] Policy-oriented studies should evaluate upstream interventions, including sugar-sweetened beverage taxes, urban planning for active living, and school-based nutrition education, drawing from successful models in East Asia.[113,114] Longitudinal investigations incorporating omics technologies (genomics, metabolomics, and microbiomics) may unravel etiological mechanisms behind regional variations, while artificial intelligence-driven predictive modeling could refine GBD forecasts and personalize risk stratification.[115,116] Collaborative initiatives, such as the EASL-Lancet Commission on liver disease, should expand to include LMIC voices for equitable global strategies.[117]

5. Conclusion

This pioneering analysis utilizing GBD 2023 data underscores a modest global rise in NAFLD mortality from 1990 to 2023, with stark increases in Eastern Europe and Central Asia, declines in East Asia and Southern Europe, and male-predominant disparities across numerous countries. These findings highlight the urgent need for tailored, multisectoral public health interventions to mitigate NAFLD’s escalating burden, particularly in transitioning economies. By addressing metabolic determinants and enhancing equity in care, we can curb this preventable epidemic and safeguard global liver health.

1. Suicide Rates and Subgroups With Elevated Suicide Risk Among Patients With Psychiatric Disorders: A Nationwide Cohort Study in Korea.

Lim J, et al.

This study examined nationwide suicide rates and risk factors among patients with psychiatric disorders in Korea. Using data from the National Health Information Database, six groups of patients with different psychiatric disorders were analyzed. Results showed that patients with psychiatric disorders had higher suicide rates than the general population, with drug use disorder patients having the highest rates. Male sex, old age, and multiple psychiatric comorbidities were consistent risk factors. The study highlights significant variations in suicide rates and risk factors across different psychiatric disorders, emphasizing the need for tailored suicide prevention strategies.

2. Identification of Preeclamptic Placenta in Whole Slide Images Using Artificial Intelligence Placenta Analysis.

Lee SM, et al.

Preeclampsia (PE) is a hypertensive pregnancy disorder marked by placental dysfunction. Diagnosing PE is challenging due to the lack of a gold standard. This study evaluated computational pathology for identifying PE placentas using 168 whole-slide images (WSIs) from Seoul National University Hospital and 76 from Borame Medical Center. Features were extracted using unsupervised (K-means clustering) and supervised (U-Net segmentation) learning. An ensemble model showed good performance (AUPRC = 0.771, sensitivity 77.3%, specificity 71.1%) compared to a clinical feature model (AUPRC = 0.713, sensitivity 55.6%, specificity 86.8%). External validation confirmed the model’s effectiveness (AUPRC = 0.725).

3. Single-Center Experience With Dynamic Contrast-Enhanced Magnetic Resonance Lymphangiography for Diagnosing Lymphatic Disorders and Guiding Percutaneous Embolization.

Shin JH, et al.

Dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance lymphangiography (DCMRL) was evaluated for its effectiveness in identifying causes of lymphatic disorders and planning interventions. In a retrospective analysis of 70 patients from August 2017 to July 2022, DCMRL was performed using a gadolinium-based contrast medium. The technical success rate was highest for nontraumatic lymphatic disorders (94.1%), followed by traumatic chylothorax (92.9%) and traumatic chylous ascites (81.8%). Among 65 successful cases, 47.7% had feasible anatomy for intervention, with a clinical success rate of 90.3%. DCMRL helps determine the feasibility of lymphatic interventions and predict clinical outcomes based on underlying causes.

4. Medical Education Challenges in the Era of Internationalization and Digitization.

Fedorchenko Y, et al.

Before COVID-19, Central Asia and Eastern Europe saw a rise in international medical students due to competitive fees and prestigious institutions. The pandemic disrupted learning and clinical training. Post-pandemic, enrollment rebounded, but challenges like language barriers, stress, and cultural adaptation persist. Research engagement is crucial but hindered by constraints. Integrating research, fostering motivation, and addressing language barriers through support systems are essential for improving academic outcomes and the success of international medical students.

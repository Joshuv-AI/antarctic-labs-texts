Effects of tocilizumab on neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin following out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, and its prognostic value
Authors: Zakaria Alaoui-Ismaili, Johannes Grand, Martin Frydland, Laust E.R. Obling, Sebastian Wiberg, Yassin Alaoui-Ismaili, Jacob E. Møller, Ruth Frikke-Schmidt, Jesper Kjaergaard, Christian Hassager, Martin A.S. Meyer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstract

Introduction

Comatose survivors from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) admitted to the hospital have a high risk of mortality attributable to post-cardiac arrest syndrome.1, 2, 3 Post-cardiac arrest syndrome is characterized by the activation of immunologic pathways and the release of inflammatory mediators such as IL-6, which contribute to tissue hypoperfusion and risk of multiorgan failure.4 Although not well understood, systemic ischemia and inflammation may aggregate renal injury, increasing the risk of acute kidney injury (AKI).5, 6 A large proportion of comatose survivors of OHCA develop AKI, and many will require continuous renal replacement therapy (cRRT).7 Additionally, AKI is also associated with poor outcomes in patients resuscitated after OHCA.8, 9 Currently, there is a lack of reliable early predictors of AKI, mortality, and neurological outcomes following OHCA. Early prognostication of adverse outcomes is vital for risk assessment and resource allocation, enhancing post-resuscitation care.

Serum creatinine has long served as a traditional biomarker for renal function and is included in the criteria for defining AKI.10 However, neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL) is a promising biomarker for the early prediction of AKI due to its early detectability in plasma compared to creatinine.11, 12 NGAL is a glycoprotein originally discovered in neutrophils and is secreted by activated neutrophils in response to systemic inflammation,11 which constitutes one of the components of post-cardiac arrest syndrome.1 NGAL has since been shown to be expressed in various other tissues, including the nephrons of the kidneys, where it is released during kidney injury caused by ischemic, nephrotoxic, or septic mechanisms.13 Recent findings suggest that increased blood concentrations of NGAL may be associated with AKI, all-cause mortality, and poor neurological outcomes in critically ill patients, including cardiac arrest survivors.14 Since NGAL may be released in response to both kidney injury and systemic inflammation, inhibition of IL-6-mediated inflammation with tocilizumab could theoretically influence circulating NGAL levels. However, this has not previously been assessed following OHCA.

The present substudy of the IMICA trial (Interleukin-6 Inhibition for Modulating Inflammation After Cardiac Arrest) assesses the effect of tocilizumab on NGAL levels in comatose survivors of OHCA. We also evaluated the prognostic value of early NGAL measurements for the need for cRRT and all-cause mortality.

Methods

This is a substudy of the IMICA trial, a single center, randomized controlled trial including 80 comatose OHCA survivors between March 4th and December 20th, 2019. A detailed description of the design and enrollment has been published previously.15, 16 At admission, patients were randomized to either a single infusion of tocilizumab (8 mg/kg, maximum 800 mg) or placebo in addition to standard care. Inclusion criteria included adult age >18 years, OHCA with presumed cardiac cause, sustained return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC), and unconsciousness with Glasgow Coma Scale <9. Exclusion criteria are listed in the published protocol.15 For each patient, demographic data, cardiac arrest characteristics, primary clinical interventions, and outcomes were collected from the medical records.

The primary endpoint of the IMICA trial was a change in C-reactive protein response from baseline until 72 h in comatose OHCA patients, and the main results have been published.16, 17 This substudy aims to assess the effect of the interleukin (IL)-6 inhibitor, tocilizumab, on NGAL levels from 0 to 72 h following admission. Furthermore, it investigates the ability of admission NGAL to predict the need for cRRT during intensive care unit (ICU) stay and death after 180 days, representing the primary and secondary objectives, respectively. Initiation of cRRT was decided by the treating physician in line with local clinical practice and based on international standard indications, including, among others, refractory fluid overload, severe metabolic acidosis, hyperkaliemia or uremia.10

The IMICA trial was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration and was approved by the relevant authorities, including the regional ethics committee of the Capital Region of Denmark: H-18037286, and the Danish Medicines Agency: 2018-002686-19. Before enrolment, the trial was registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT03863015).

Biomarker measurements

The biomarkers measured during the initial 72 h after admission included a marker of inflammation and kidney injury: NGAL; markers of inflammation: neutrophils and IL-6; the severity of the hypoxic-ischemic insult: lactate; myocardial injury: troponin-T (TnT); kidney function: creatinine; and brain injury: neuron-specific enolase (NSE), and neurofilament light chain (NfL). A subset of patients also had a follow-up creatinine value measured up to one year after OHCA.18

Serial blood samples were collected in ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid tubes at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h after admission, and biobank samples were centrifugated at 2000 G and plasma stored at −80°C until analysis. Neutrophils, TnT, creatinine, lactate and NSE were measured as routine biochemistry at the Department of Clinical Biochemistry at Rigshospitalet, while NGAL, IL-6 and NfL were measured from biobank samples. NGAL plasma concentration was measured using the NGAL Test™ Reagent Kit (BioPorto Diagnostics A/S, Denmark) on a Roche Cobas 6000 C501 (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Germany) following the manufacturer’s instructions.19

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR) and compared using the Wilcoxon rank test. Categorical variables are presented as numbers (n) and percentages (%).

Boxplots are included to visually depict possible differences in NGAL levels from 0 to 72 h among the following groups: (I) tocilizumab vs placebo, (II) survivors vs non-survivors at 180 days, and (III) patients requiring cRRT vs no requirement for cRRT during ICU stay.

The area under the receiver operating characteristics curve (AUCROC) is used to assess NGAL's ability to predict cRRT and 180-day mortality, as well as the combined outcome of cRRT during ICU stay or death at 180 days. Additionally, AUCROC values for time-to-ROSC, lactate, IL-6, neutrophils, and creatinine were calculated for the relevant outcomes. Results are expressed with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI), and the prognostic abilities were compared to see how NGAL’s AUCROC was measured against other variables.20

Spearman's rank correlation (rs) determined correlations between admission NGAL, creatinine, and neutrophils with time-to-ROSC, as well as the mean 0–72 h levels of TnT, creatinine, NSE, NfL, and Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score, which reflects the overall clinical status. Coefficient ranges were defined as weak (rs < 0.3), moderate (0.3 ≤ rs ≤ 0.7), and strong (rs > 0.7) correlation.

All statistical analyses were performed using the SAS Enterprise Guide 8.4 (SAS-Institute Inc). A p-value < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

The trial included 80 patients, with 39 assigned to tocilizumab and 41 to placebo. Overall, NGAL peaked at 24 h and then gradually decreased during the 24–72 h interval in both groups. Tocilizumab did not influence NGAL levels over the 0–72 h period compared to placebo (p > 0.20 at all time points); see Fig. 1. Therefore, both randomization groups were pooled and regarded as a single population for subsequent analysis. The pooled population's median age was 62.5 years (IQR 54.0–71.0), and 66 out of 80 (82.5%) were male. A known history of kidney disease and dialysis use prior to cardiac arrest were present for 5 out of 80 (6.3%) and 1 out of 80 (1.3%) patients, respectively. Median time-to-ROSC was 20.0 min (IQR 13.0–27.0), use of cRRT was seen in 13 out of 80 (16%) patients, and death at 180 days occurred in 28 out of 80 (35%) patients. The requirement for cRRT during ICU-stay varied between groups, with 10 out of 39 (26%) in the tocilizumab group compared to 3 out of 41 (7.3%) in the placebo group. However, no survivors had a new onset indication for dialysis after discharge, and creatinine levels at follow-up were similar in both groups (median 97 μmol/L (76–117) for tocilizumab, and median 85 μmol/L (74–105) for placebo; total n = 28; p = 0.39).Fig. 1Boxplot displaying NGAL levels from 0 to 72 h in the tocilizumab vs placebo group.NGAL, neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin.

Prediction of death at 180 days

When stratifying the pooled population according to survivors or non-survivors at 180 days, NGAL levels were significantly elevated in non-survivors across all time points from 0 to 72 h (all p < 0.001), as shown in Fig. 2b.

The admission NGAL level predicted death at 180 days with an AUCROC of 0.75 (95% CI: 0.63–0.86). NGAL’s predictive value was significantly better than admission creatinine (AUCROC = 0.54 (95% CI: 0.40–0.69); p < 0.01) and neutrophils (AUCROC = 0.52 (95% CI: 0.39–0.66); p < 0.01), and it was numerically better than admission lactate (AUCROC = 0.66 (95% CI: 0.53–0.78); p = 0.27) and IL-6 (AUCROC = 0.66 (95% CI: 0.54–0.79); p = 0.23), but not time-to-ROSC (AUCROC = 0.76 (95% CI: 0.65–0.88); p = 0.80) (Fig. 3b).

Prediction of cRRT use or death at 180 days

Regarding the composite endpoint of cRRT use or death at 180 days, admission NGAL had an AUCROC of 0.73 (95% CI: 0.61–0.84). NGAL was again significantly better than admission creatinine (AUCROC = 0.56 (95% CI: 0.42–0.71); p < 0.05) and neutrophils (AUCROC = 0.55 (95% CI: 0.42–0.67); p < 0.05), and numerically better than admission IL-6 (AUCROC = 0.66 (95% CI: 0.54–0.78); p = 0.31), but not time-to-ROSC (AUCROC = 0.75 (95% CI: 0.64–0.86); p = 0.77) (Fig. 3c).

Correlations of NGAL with markers of organ injury and clinical status

Supplementary Table S1 presents correlation coefficients (rs) between admission NGAL, creatinine, and neutrophils with time-to-ROSC, as well as mean 0–72 h levels of TnT, creatinine, Nfl, NSE, and SOFA score. NGAL revealed a moderate correlation with all variables in both the tocilizumab and placebo groups. Yet, the correlation between admission NGAL and time-to-ROSC varied numerically between the tocilizumab (rs = 0.64) and placebo (rs = 0.37) groups. Admission creatinine correlated with several of the assessed variables (TnT, Nfl, and SOFA score), while admission neutrophils did not correlate with any.

Discussion

In the present study, which included comatose survivors of OHCA of presumed cardiac cause, the treatment with tocilizumab did not affect NGAL levels in the post-resuscitation phase. Plasma NGAL, measured at all time points up to 72 h, was generally higher at each time point in patients requiring cRRT and those who died within 180 days, compared to those without this outcome. The predictive ability of admission NGAL for use of cRRT was numerically, but not significantly, greater than that of creatinine. However, NGAL had a significantly better predictive ability than creatinine for death at 180 days and was comparable to time-to-ROSC. Furthermore, admission NGAL correlated significantly with various key markers of disease severity and clinical status. This suggests that NGAL may be a prognostic indicator for the general disease severity, in addition to kidney injury, in these critically ill patients.

NGAL is a biomarker present in mature neutrophils and kidney nephrons. Its levels rise in response to several pathological conditions, including kidney injury and systemic inflammation.11 In AKI, NGAL can increase within 2–4 h after renal injury, making it a more sensitive indicator than creatinine, which is not elevated until days post-injury.21 However, systemic inflammation triggered by sepsis or reperfusion injury has previously been shown to increase plasma NGAL levels in the absence of AKI, thereby challenging the notion of NGAL as an independent kidney-specific marker.11 This is supported by our findings, as admission NGAL was associated not only with severe kidney injury but also with death, SOFA score, and neurological biomarkers. Thus, in the post-cardiac arrest setting, NGAL may reflect the overall burden of ischemia–reperfusion injury rather than isolated kidney injury.

Tocilizumab blocks IL-6 from exerting its pro-inflammatory effects, which include the synthesis of acute-phase proteins, the production of neutrophils in the bone marrow, and the growth of B cells.22 Previous work from the IMICA trial revealed that tocilizumab resulted in lower neutrophil counts compared to placebo.17 However, tocilizumab treatment did not affect NGAL levels in this substudy. This may be due to the complex nature of NGAL release, occurring in various tissues beyond neutrophils, including the kidneys, liver, and lungs.11 Furthermore, the direct link between IL-6 inhibition and NGAL modulation remains poorly established. An in vitro study has shown that only infusion of IL-1β, and not IL-6, affected NGAL levels in epithelial cells of the lungs,23 suggesting that the release of NGAL may be independent of the IL-6 pathway. In addition, a higher proportion of patients in the tocilizumab group received cRRT than the placebo group, which may reflect more severe kidney injury in the tocilizumab group. This imbalance may have increased NGAL levels in the tocilizumab group, thereby attenuating or obscuring any potential effect of tocilizumab on NGAL.

Levels of NGAL were significantly higher at all time points in patients receiving cRRT compared with those without cRRT, which aligns with a recent meta-analysis assessing the levels of NGAL based on outcomes in cardiac arrest survivors.14 The meta-analysis revealed that severe AKI occurrence following ROSC was linked to higher NGAL levels in the initial days after cardiac arrest. Our study also demonstrated that admission NGAL provided a moderate prediction for the use of cRRT, with a numerically slightly better ability than creatinine. This may be due to the early NGAL accumulation and detection within a few hours of tubular damage well before glomerular dysfunction leads to the accumulation of creatinine in the bloodstream. Additionally, since a creatinine-based diagnosis of AKI may influence the associations of NGAL with AKI, we used cRRT as the outcome measure for AKI corresponding to stage three AKI in the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) classification.10 Research on NGAL’s predictive value in cardiac arrest survivors also aligns with our results, revealing equal predictive values of NGAL for severe AKI.24, 25, 26

In accordance with previous findings, NGAL levels were also significantly higher at all time points in non-survivors at 180 days compared to survivors,14, 25, 27, 28 and accordingly, NGAL demonstrated moderate predictive ability for death at 180 days. The predictive ability of admission NGAL for death was significantly better than that of admission creatinine and neutrophils and was numerically superior to admission lactate and IL-6. Only time-to-ROSC was numerical, though not significantly superior to NGAL. Given that IL-6 and time-to-ROSC are well-known predictors of poor outcomes in cardiac arrest survivors,29, 30 admission NGAL may, to some degree, serve as a potential additional prognostic marker.

Interestingly, NGAL measured at admission correlated with all of the investigated indicators of clinical status and organ injury, i.e. mean 0–72 h values of time-to-ROSC, TnT, creatinine, NSE, NfL, and SOFA score. This indicates that the NGAL level immediately after ROSC not only reflects AKI but may also act as a surrogate measure for the severity of ischemic injury during the no-flow period of cardiac arrest, leading to adverse outcomes.28 Like the brain, the kidneys are sensitive to changes in systemic circulation, and the level of kidney injury may be proportional to the overall extent of vital organ damage during cardiac arrest.9 The observed associations may also arise from increased neutrophil activity due to systemic inflammation, which in turn could be indicative of the tissue damage resulting from the ischemic insult. NGAL is present in the kidneys and neutrophils, as well as in several other organs, including the brain, liver, heart, and lungs.13 NGAL increase may therefore stem from various sources following cardiac arrest; however, despite these confounding overlaps, NGAL seems to maintain some prognostic value.

Today, limited clinical findings or diagnostic tests within the first hours after ROSC provide sufficient specificity for predicting poor outcomes, thereby challenging risk assessment and burdening the healthcare system.31 In our study, admission NGAL showed moderate predictive performance for severe kidney injury and death. This indicates that NGAL cannot be considered a stand-alone prognostic marker but deserves future investigation to become part of a multimodal approach to early risk assessment following OHCA.

Limitations

The primary limitation of this study is its single-center design based on small sample size. Second, it is a secondary analysis of a randomized controlled trial that is not powered to detect adverse outcomes, including mortality. Third, we used cRRT as a surrogate marker of severe kidney injury, but the decision to initiate cRRT may be influenced by local practice patterns, which should be considered when interpreting NGAL as a predictor of this outcome. Fourth, NGAL was measured from frozen biobank samples thawed before analysis. Fifth, our study population comprised OHCA patients with presumed cardiac causes, and our results might not apply to non-cardiac causes or other disease states. Finally, the predictive analysis was performed on a pooled study cohort of the initial two groups, which may influence the outcomes.

These limitations might lead to either an overestimation or underestimation of NGAL's prognostic value; nevertheless, our findings aligned with earlier results from smaller studies.

Conclusion

Tocilizumab, a potent inhibitor of the inflammatory response following OHCA, did not affect NGAL levels. NGAL showed moderate predictive performance for cRRT use and death. For the prediction of use of cRRT in the ICU, admission NGAL was significantly better than that of neutrophils and numerically, but not significantly, greater than that of creatinine. NGAL levels may however also represent inflammatory activity in addition to renal injury, and for the prediction of death, admission NGAL was significantly better than both creatinine and neutrophils.

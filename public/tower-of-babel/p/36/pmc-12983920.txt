Protocol for the development of the Asian consensus recommendations on the standardised diagnosis and imaging of intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis applying a modified Delphi approach
Authors: Jose C Navarro, Bonifacio II C Pedregosa, Allan A Belen, Robert N Gan

Introduction

Establishing a unified regional framework for the diagnosis and imaging of intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis (ICAS) is an urgent priority in Asia. The rationale for this initiative arose from the findings of our recently conducted scoping review1 and was subsequently detailed in a call for regional consensus published in Stroke and Vascular Neurology.2 In brief, ICAS is a leading cause of ischaemic stroke, particularly among Asian populations.1 3 It remains a major contributor to stroke-related morbidity and continues to impose a significant burden on patients, caregivers and health systems across the region.1 4 Despite this, evidence from Asia remains fragmented.1 Methodological inconsistencies arising from the absence of standardised criteria and imaging protocols for the diagnosis of ICAS have resulted in marked heterogeneity in reported epidemiologic measures of disease burden within and across Asian populations and have contributed to conflicting findings from studies investigating associated risk factors.1 These limitations preclude reliable region-wide estimates of disease burden, meaningful cross-population comparisons and robust risk profiling. Ultimately, these gaps hinder consistent approaches to ICAS diagnosis and management, impede collaborative research, delay the generation of evidence-based treatment and prevention strategies and undermine the development of equitable and effective public health policies.

Another key finding of our review was the marked geographic disparity in ICAS research, wherein the vast majority of studies were conducted in East Asia, with limited data from other regions.1 While this highlights the need to outline research priorities with particular attention to under-represented regions, without standardisation, future investigations risk perpetuating the same methodological heterogeneity that currently limits our understanding. This initiative is therefore necessary not only in facilitating broader regional representation in ICAS research, but also in ensuring that new contributions strengthen rather than fragment regional evidence.

To address these challenges, a consensus process has been initiated and will include a plenary session to be convened during the 2026 Philippine Academy of Vascular and Interventional Neurology (PAVIN) Conference in Manila. A consensus exercise was deemed the most appropriate approach because the current evidence base derives from methodologically heterogeneous studies, which preclude meaningful quantitative synthesis and the development of recommendations based solely on empirical data. It is likewise a widely used method for establishing research standards and priorities.5 By systematically engaging a diverse panel of regional experts, this process aims to integrate available evidence with clinical expertise, generating harmonised, context-specific recommendations that are scientifically robust and feasible across Asia’s varied healthcare settings.

This consensus will pursue the following objectives,2 as informed by the findings of our review1:

To establish standardised definitions of intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD) and ICAS, including thresholds for clinically significant stenosis across imaging modalities. Particular emphasis will be placed on safeguards to differentiate ICAS from non-atherosclerotic arteriopathies. The consensus will likewise seek to distinguish between symptomatic and asymptomatic disease and clarify vaguely applied terminology relevant to ICAS research and clinical practice.

This consensus is intended to guide both clinical practice and ICAS research. While developed with a regional focus on Asia, its recommendations aim to strengthen research and practice more broadly and serve as a first step toward future global collaborations.

Methods and analysis

A modified Delphi approach will be undertaken to reach consensus-based recommendations.68 This method was specifically selected for its systematic, structured and iterative multistep design, allowing progressive refinement of statements across stages, while ensuring feedback anonymity, methodological rigour and balanced representation.68

The development and reporting of this consensus will follow the ACcurate COnsensus Reporting Document guideline.9 This protocol was prospectively registered with the Open Science Framework (https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/MB6GP).

Collaborators and roles

A steering committee, chaired by JCN and composed of the authors of this protocol, initiated the consensus process and defined its main objectives. It will maintain oversight throughout the process but will not have voting rights in the Delphi rounds.

To operationalise the consensus objectives, four preparatory groups were organised to formulate specific research questions, prepare evidence tables, draft Delphi statements and design questionnaires. Preparatory group 1: standardised definitions; preparatory group 2: diagnostic modalities; preparatory group 3: measurement of stenosis and grading of severity; preparatory group 4: research standards and priorities. A registered medical librarian will assist in developing and refining the literature search strategy to support evidence preparation.

An expert core group will be formed to serve as the formal voting panel during the Delphi rounds. Members will be selected by the steering committee based on their recognised clinical and research expertise in intracranial atherosclerosis and cerebrovascular imaging, as well as their active contributions to stroke research. Deliberate consideration will be given to ensuring geographic and disciplinary diversity. Authors of studies included in our previously conducted scoping review1 will also be evaluated as potential candidates. At the time of writing, invitations have been issued via email to 18 experts, of whom 94.4% (17/18) have agreed to participate. All experts who formally confirm participation at the outset will be invited to take part in all subsequent Delphi rounds. No additional members will be recruited after the initial panel has been finalised.

The voting panel is currently composed of 17 members, predominantly from Asian countries, including China, Hong Kong, India, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan and Thailand, with additional international experts from Australia, Belgium, Spain and the USA. Disciplines represented include vascular neurology, neurosonology, clinical epidemiology, interventional neurology and neuroradiology. Members are not blinded to each other’s identities.

All collaborators who meet the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors criteria for authorship, based on their substantive intellectual contributions and approval of the final recommendations, will be offered co-authorship on the final manuscript. No other incentives will be provided.

Process and analysis

These consensus-based recommendations will be developed through a multistep sequence of structured online and hybrid stages including (1) a preparatory stage, (2) iterative Delphi rounds conducted virtually, (3) a hybrid consensus conference and (4) post-conference refinement and finalisation of consensus recommendations. For the fourth objective (research standards and priorities), corresponding preparatory and consensus activities will commence, using the same multistage framework, after the completion of the first three objectives to ensure alignment with the preceding consensus outcomes.

Stage 1: preparatory stage

Each preparatory group will formulate specific research questions to address its assigned objective. Rapid literature reviews will then be undertaken to gather and synthesise relevant evidence, with search strategies and inclusion criteria tailored to each question. The conduct and reporting of these preparatory reviews will adhere to relevant Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA)—Extension guidelines.10 Findings will be synthesised in standardised tables, with specific data fields adapted to the nature of the data extracted by each group. These evidence summaries will be provided as background material to the expert core group and will inform the development of draft Delphi statements for the first-round questionnaires, which will be constructed to allow for both qualitative feedback and quantitative ratings through a five-point Likert.

Prior to the dissemination of the first Delphi round, the draft questionnaire will undergo internal pilot testing to assess statement clarity and technical functionality within the online survey platform. Participants will include 10 board-certified vascular neurologists from teaching or university hospitals, selected by the steering committee from the preparatory groups. Free-text comments will be collected. Revisions based on this feedback will be limited to minor editorial or technical modifications that do not alter the substantive content of the statements. This internal review will not involve voting or evaluation of agreement and will not permit the addition or removal of statements.

Stage 2: iterative Delphi rounds

Two Delphi rounds will be conducted virtually prior to the hybrid consensus conference. Although no universal consensus threshold exists, 80% agreement has been widely used and recommended in prior Delphi guidance.5 11 12 Accordingly, a predefined threshold was set at ≥80% for consensus. In each round, members of the expert core group will independently and anonymously rate their agreement with each statement using a five-point Likert scale (1=strongly disagree to 5=strongly agree). Ratings of 4 or 5 will indicate agreement, 1 or 2 disagreement and 3 neutrality. All Likert-scale items will be configured as required fields within the online survey platform to ensure complete responses. Experts may also provide anonymised free-text comments, suggest modifications or propose new items for subsequent consideration.

Statements achieving consensus will be removed from later rounds, whereas those that do not will be revised based on aggregated feedback and reintroduced. Newly proposed statements will be assessed by the steering committee for relevance prior to inclusion in subsequent rounds, where they will undergo formal voting and be subject to the same predefined consensus threshold. The steering committee will remain blinded to individual responses and to the identity of the experts proposing modifications or new statements. Additional targeted evidence reviews may be conducted if necessary. An anonymised report summarising percentage agreement, qualitative feedback, amendments and any additional statements introduced will be provided to the members of the expert core group before each succeeding round.

Delphi questionnaires will be administered via Google Forms, which enables verified submissions and controlled data access. Data will be stored on secure, password-protected institutional accounts with access restricted to the designated data managers and retained in accordance with institutional data governance policies. Two independent researchers will be responsible for data management and analysis and will be the only ones privy to individual responses. All data will be anonymised prior to transmission to the steering committee for review.

Stage 3: hybrid consensus conference

A hybrid consensus conference will be convened on 9 April 2026, as part of the 2026 PAVIN Biennial Convention in Manila, Philippines. Results from the first two Delphi rounds will be presented, with particular attention to any statements that have not reached consensus. Members of the expert core group will participate in structured, moderated discussions. This will be open to the public and will be conducted in English. Voting will not be carried out during the meeting, thereby preserving the anonymity and independence of the Delphi process. Key discussion points will be documented and reviewed by the steering committee for potential incorporation into the subsequent post-conference Delphi round, with summaries provided to the expert core group in advance.

Stage 4: post-conference refinement and finalisation

A post-conference Delphi round will be conducted to address remaining items that have not yet reached consensus and to incorporate approved revisions from the steering committee’s review of conference discussions. If substantial disagreement persists, the steering committee may convene a fourth and final Delphi round. Beyond this, statements that do not meet the predefined consensus threshold will be documented as non-consensus items.

Following the finalisation of objectives 1–3, preparatory and consensus activities for objective 4 will be conducted entirely online, following the same multistage framework.

Documentation and reporting

A detailed and transparent account of the conduct and outcomes of each stage of the consensus process will be provided in the final manuscript and its supplementary files. A full list of collaborators (including members of the steering committee, expert core group, preparatory groups and the registered medical librarian) with their corresponding roles, areas of expertise, primary affiliations, organisations represented and potential conflicts of interest will be reported. The proportion of invited experts who agree to participate will also be documented. For the preparatory reviews, research questions, eligibility criteria, search strategies, databases searched, study screening and selection methods, PRISMA flow diagrams and synthesis tables will be reported. For the internal pilot testing, the number and expertise of participants, the feedback received and a summary of resulting revisions made will be documented. For each Delphi round, corresponding questionnaires, statement lists and revisions between rounds will be documented. Quantitative results (percentage agreement, median, IQR and de-identified individual responses) will be presented alongside thematic analyses of qualitative feedback. Response rates, participant retention and attrition across Delphi rounds will be reported in the final manuscript. Round-specific denominators will be presented for all quantitative summaries to ensure transparency. A concise anonymised summary of the hybrid conference discussions will be reported, noting any relevant points incorporated into the next round. Key decisions across stages and any protocol deviations will be clearly described and justified in the final report.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in defining the objectives, developing the research questions or in the methodological design of this consensus process. However, the hybrid consensus conference, to be held as part of the 2026 PAVIN Biennial Conference, will be open to the public to promote transparency and allow open discussion. While public attendees will not participate in voting, relevant points raised during the discussion will be reviewed by the steering committee for potential incorporation into the subsequent post-conference Delphi round.

Endorsements

Endorsements and guidance for this consensus have been formally sought from the Asia Pacific Stroke Organisation and the World Stroke Organization.

Ethics and dissemination

This consensus process was granted exemption by the Jose R. Reyes Memorial Medical Center Institutional Review Board (No. 2025–221), as it involves expert opinion and does not constitute human subjects or animal research. Informed consent will be obtained from all collaborators to participate in the Delphi process and for the publication of their names, affiliations, organisations represented, professional expertise and declared conflicts of interest in the final manuscript. The consensus will not involve the collection, use or analysis of identifiable patient information. All data generated or analysed during the consensus process will be derived solely from expert opinion and previously published, publicly available sources. The process will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and its amendments.

Recommendations derived from this consensus will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed journals and presentation at scientific fora. All relevant data will be included in the final manuscript and its accompanying supplementary files.

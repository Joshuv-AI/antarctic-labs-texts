Evaluating the impact of a medical telephone helpline and the use of a structured initial assessment on demand for acute and emergency care in Germany: an ecological study using secondary data
Authors: Beate Zoch-Lesniak, Edgar Steiger, Lars E Kroll, Dominik Graf von Stillfried
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectivesTo assess whether a medical telephone helpline and the use of a computer-assisted structured triage tool led to a reduction in demand for acute and emergency care in hospital emergency departments (EDs) or other ambulatory out-of-hour (OOH) services.DesignWe conducted an ecological study using secondary data on outpatient care.SettingThe study was conducted in 10 out of 16 federal states of Germany.ParticipantsThe analysis was based on ambulatory claims data for the years 2016–2020 by 11 Associations of Statutory Health Insurance Physicians (ASHIPs) covering more than 64% of the total German population.InterventionsThe evaluated intervention comprised two components. The first was the introduction of a 24/7 medical helpline (116117), established to assist individuals with medical concerns in accessing appropriate care. The second component was the introduction of the computer-assisted triage tool SmED (Strukturierte medizinische Ersteinschätzung in Deutschland, Structured medical initial assessment in Germany) to support call-takers by suggesting medically relevant questions to identify red flags and determine the urgency of treatment and a possible treatment facility. For the analysis, approximately 3 years before and 1 ½ years during the intervention were considered.Outcome measuresMain outcome was the effect on acute and emergency care which was measured as the number of personal doctor-patient contacts (1) in EDs (ED cases, data of 10 ASHIPs could be considered) and (2) in EDs or other OOH services (ED and OOH cases, data of 11 ASHIPs could be considered).ResultsThe analysis was limited by legal changes mandating intervention components across all study sites—leading to a loss of control groups and delayed implementation—and the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. Across all ASHIPs and counties, the number of calls to 116117 and the number of SmED assessments showed a negative association with the number of ED cases (total change: 295.0 cases to 224.5 cases per 100 000 inhabitants, 116117 calls: r=−0.04; 95% CI −0.04 to −0.035; p≤0.001, SmED: r=−0.15; 95% CI −0.35 to 0.05; p=0.138) as well as with the combined number of ED and OOH cases (total change: 516.4 cases to 400.3 cases per 100 000 inhabitants, 116117 calls: r=−0.02; 95% CI −0.03 to −0.001; p≤0.01, SmED: r=−0.58; 95% CI −0.98 to −0.19; p≤0.01). However, the association between the number of SmED assessments and ED cases was not statistically significant. Moreover, the magnitude and direction of effects varied across ASHIPs. Sensitivity analyses restricted to time periods preceding the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic showed a non-significant negative association for 116117 calls and a significant positive association for SmED assessments with both ED cases and combined ED and OOH cases (ED cases: 116117 calls: r=−0.001; 95% CI −0.019 to −0.018; p=0.928; SmED: r=0.37; 95% CI 0.29 to 0.45; p≤0.001; ED and OOH services cases: 116117 calls: r=−0.03; 95% CI −0.06 to 0.003; p=0.077; SmED: r=0.34; 95% CI 0.20 to 0.48; p≤0.001).ConclusionsOur findings indicate a trend suggesting that implementation of a 24/7 medical helpline may reduce the demand for acute and emergency care at EDs and OOH services, although clear evidence is lacking. The impact of SmED use remains inconclusive. Further research should ideally incorporate data linkage and controls and assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the triage process, as well as the quality of subsequent care at the individual level.Keywords: Patient Navigation, Triage, Health Services Accessibility, Primary Health Care, ACCIDENT & EMERGENCY MEDICINE

Introduction

Until 2016, demand for acute and emergency care in Germany increased, as in most Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) countries.1 2 Action was urgently needed to alleviate crowding in emergency departments (EDs) and reduce pressure on the emergency rescue service (112). The key question was how to direct patients with acute health needs to the most appropriate care setting. In its 2018 report, the German Expert Advisory Board for Public Health Development called for cross-sectoral management of patient flows to ensure that individuals with acute medical problems receive timely, appropriate care and that resources are used efficiently.3 In Germany, acute and emergency care is provided through (1) ambulatory care by office-based physicians (both primary and specialists care) during regular hours and, in rotation, out-of-hours (OOHs) either in their own practices or in OOH practices, (2) hospital EDs, which should be used only for emergencies or when specific resources are required that are not available in regular ambulatory care or OOH services and (3) the emergency rescue service (112) for people in life-threatening or urgent conditions who cannot access care otherwise. Because patients can freely choose which service to use, inappropriate utilisation is common, particularly self-referrals to EDs for non-urgent issues, often due to limited knowledge or convenience.4,7 To reduce ED use for non-urgent conditions, Germany introduced a 24/7 medical telephone helpline for individuals with acute health concerns. In 2019, as part of the DEMAND project (‘implementation of a standardised initial assessment as the basis of a demand management in outpatient emergency care’), the helpline was equipped with medical triage software8 to support clinical decision-making. As Rushton et al9 noted, ‘the implementation of any technology-based system is complex and requires evaluation of many factors at the patient, provider and organisational levels’. Accordingly, the evaluation focused on the implementation process, user acceptance and patient compliance with results on these aspects reported elsewhere.10,12

The objective of this study was to assess the impact of introducing a 24/7 medical telephone helpline and a computer-assisted triage tool on the demand for acute and emergency care.

Methods

Study population and data set

Germany has 17 Associations of Statutory Health Insurance Physicians (ASHIPs): two in North Rhine-Westphalia and one in each of the other 15 federal states. The ASHIPs are responsible for organising access to ambulatory care for statutorily insured residents within their jurisdiction. Eleven ASHIPs participated in the DEMAND project, covering 63.8 million statutorily insured persons (year 2016). All office-based physicians providing care to statutorily insured patients must be members of their regional ASHIP. The ASHIPs process all claims submitted by their members, as well as by EDs for ambulatory emergency care. The data set includes all contacts with these services that could be financially claimed by providers. We used the complete claims data from the participating ASHIPs for the periods Q1-2016 to Q3-2018 and Q2-2019 to Q4-2020. Quarters Q4-2018 and Q1-2019 were excluded from analysis as they represented the initially planned implementation phase for establishing the triage software and reorganising the telephone helpline.

Intervention

The evaluated intervention comprised two components. The first was the introduction of a 24/7 medical helpline (116117), implemented to support individuals with medical concerns in obtaining appropriate care. In some ASHIPs, the helpline had already been established before the start of the project, but operated with limited public awareness and restricted opening hours. In emergencies, call-takers connect callers directly to the emergency service (112). Depending on resource availability at the time of the call, call-takers may arrange home visits, schedule appointments with physicians or organise telephone consultations. The second component was the introduction of a computer-assisted triage tool to support call-takers by proposing medically relevant questions aimed at identifying red flags and assessing the appropriate level of urgency (‘immediately’, ‘as soon as possible’, ‘less than 24 hours’ or ‘24 hours and more’) as well as the relevant point of care (‘emergency rescue service’, ‘emergency department’, ‘outpatient physician’ or ‘teleconsultation’) based on patient characteristics (age, sex, symptoms, comorbidities, risk factors) and red flags.13 The system used was the ‘Swiss Medical Assessment System’ (SMASS),14 adapted to the German healthcare context and distributed under the acronym SmED, Strukturierte medizinische Ersteinschätzung in Deutschland (Structured medical initial assessment in Germany). SMASS/SmED is regulated by the European Council Directive 93/42/EEC of 14 June 1993 concerning medical devices. Details of implementation, patient compliance and satisfaction have been reported elsewhere.10,12

Design

The study was conducted as an ecological study using aggregated data at the county level. At the time of the intervention, no integration existed between 116117 calls, SmED assessments and claims data at the individual level. Consequently, the direct impact of the intervention on individual patients could not be assessed.

Outcome

The main outcome was the ‘demand for acute and emergency care’, defined as a personal doctor-patient contact (1) in EDs and (2) in EDs or OOH services, according to Section II Chapter 1.2 Fee Schedule Positions (GOP) for Emergency Care and Organised Medical Emergency Service of the Uniform Evaluation Scale. Regional ASHIP-specific GOPs were also taken into account.

Outcomes were calculated per county, per week, per 100 000 inhabitants. The county level was chosen because data on sociodemographic and economic factors, as well as on healthcare facilities, were available at this level, which also constitutes the key unit of regional planning. Weekly aggregation was used to account for weekday-weekend variation.

Independent variables

Data regarding the intervention (weekly number of 116117 calls and weekly number of SmED assessments) were provided by the ASHIPs at the ASHIP-level. For the analysis, the numbers were proportionally adjusted by county population size, since other influencing factors and the outcome variables were available at the county level and varied considerably between counties.

Additional influencing factors and potential confounders were identified through a literature review15,22 and a workshop with experts from the participating ASHIPs. These factors were grouped into three categories: (1) healthcare structure, (2) sociodemographic and economic structure and (3) time-dependent contextual factors:

Healthcare structure: the number of OOH practices was provided by the ASHIPs. The number of physicians per county per 10 000 inhabitants and the number of hospital beds per county per 1000 inhabitants for the years 2016–2019 were obtained from the INKAR dataset (Indicators and Maps on Spatial and Urban Development) of the Federal Institute for Research on Building, Urban Affairs and Spatial Development (Bundesinstitut für Bau-, Stadt- und Raumforschung).23

Healthcare structure: the number of OOH practices was provided by the ASHIPs. The number of physicians per county per 10 000 inhabitants and the number of hospital beds per county per 1000 inhabitants for the years 2016–2019 were obtained from the INKAR dataset (Indicators and Maps on Spatial and Urban Development) of the Federal Institute for Research on Building, Urban Affairs and Spatial Development (Bundesinstitut für Bau-, Stadt- und Raumforschung).23

Sociodemographic and economic structure: average age and population density were extracted from the same INKAR dataset. The deprivation index was developed and is maintained by the Robert Koch Institute (RKI). It represents regional socioeconomic differences at various spatial levels and is based on indicators of education, occupation and income at the county level. It was included using the value from 2014.24 The index ranges from 0 (no deprivation) to 1 (highest deprivation).

Statistics and software

Descriptive statistics, including measures of location and dispersion of the main outcome, were calculated based on the data in total (weekly emergency contacts in all ASHIPs per 100 000 inhabitants). The overall trend was assessed by fitting a linear model to the data. Intra-year seasonal fluctuations were visualised using locally weighted smoothing (geom_smooth function, method=loess) of the ggplot2 package (V.3.4.2) in R. To evaluate the effects of 116117 calls and initial assessments by SmED on the demand for acute and emergency care, multilevel analyses were conducted to account for the fact that weekly ED/OOH service cases (Level 1) within a county (Level 2) or ED/OOH service cases within a county nested in an ASHIP (Level 3) are not independent of one another. Starting with a null model without influencing variables, factors were added to the model in thematic groups. At each step, analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to test whether model fit improved significantly with the inclusion of additional factors.26 27

The pseudo-R2 value was calculated using the method of Nakagawa and Schielzeth28 with the jtools package (Long, 2022).29 All statistical analyses were performed using R (V.3.6.3).30 Statistical significance was defined as p≤0.05.

Patient and public involvement

This work was supported by the Federal Joint Committee (Gemeinsamer Bundesausschuss, G-BA), Germany‘s highest decision-making body within the joint self-governance of physicians, dentists, hospitals and health insurance funds. Leading national patient advocacy groups and organisations representing people with chronic illnesses or disabilities are entitled to participate in G-BA deliberations and to submit petitions.

We used the Standards for Quality Improvement Reporting Excellence checklist when writing our report.31

Intervention

The implementation of SmED at the helpline 116117 was initially planned to begin in April 2019 in nine ASHIPs, while two ASHIPs were designated as control regions without SmED use. Unexpectedly, in April 2019, a new law was enacted mandating all ASHIPs to implement the same standardised triage tool at the 116117 telephone helpline, which was to operate 24/7 starting January 2020 (Act to Improve Appointment Availability and Healthcare Provision, Terminservice- und Versorgungsgesetz). This imposed a substantial organisational burden on the ASHIPs. As a result, only three ASHIPs started using SmED as originally scheduled in April 2019, while most ASHIPs prioritised implementation by January 2020. Personal communication with the managers responsible for operating the helpline in each ASHIP indicated large differences in call-taker training, in the integration of the assessment into the call-taking process, and the technical integration of the triage tool within the software environment. These factors varied considerably between ASHIPs and over time and therefore could not be operationalised in our analyses. Utilisation of SmED was highly heterogenous across ASHIPs, ranging from near zero to almost 500 assessments per 100 000 inhabitants per month (figure 1).

In total, 687 845 SmED assessments were conducted between April 2019 and December 2020, with monthly numbers ranging from 2808 (Apr 2019) to 63 510 (Dec 2020). Among callers assessed, 58.5% were female and 36.4% were aged between 14 and 49 years. According to SmED recommendations, most callers needed treatment within 24 hours or sooner (71.3%). The most frequent main health concerns were back pain, suspected COVID-19 and fever. During the same period, 5 953 922 calls related to health problems were recorded at 116117, ranging from 203 488 (Sep 2019) to 448 962 (Mar 2020) calls per month.

Outcome

Cases in EDs

Across all ASHIPs and throughout the entire study period, a linear model showed that weekly ED cases per 100 000 inhabitants decreased from 295.0 in calendar week 1 of 2016 to 224.5 in calendar week 52 of 2020 (figure 2). In addition to the overall trend, intra-year seasonal fluctuations were observed. Between 2016 and 2019, these patterns were similar, but in 2020—the first year of the COVID-19 pandemic—different patterns and activity levels were observed (figure 3).

Weekly number of ED cases per 100 000 inhabitants across all ASHIPs (2016–2020), grey dots: numbers, blue dashed line: fitted linear trend. ASHIPs, Associations of Statutory Health Insurance Physicians; ED, emergency department.

Annual patterns for the weekly number of ED cases per 100 000 inhabitants across all ASHIPs, visualised based on locally linear smoothing (2016–2020). ASHIPs, Associations of Statutory Health Insurance Physicians; ED, emergency department.

The model included 63 180 data points (234 calendar weeks in 270 counties) across 10 ASHIPs. The full model achieved the best goodness-of-fit (pseudo-R2=0.33 for fixed effects; 0.91 including random effects). Across all ASHIPs and counties, the number of 116117 calls showed a significant negative association with ED cases (r=−0.04; 95% CI −0.04 to −0.035), while the number of SmED assessments showed a non-significant negative association (r=−0.15; 95% CI −0.35 to 0.05). Other factors with a significant negative association included calendar week, COVID-19 cases and the number of OOH practices. Factors such as holidays, influenza cases, average age, deprivation index and population density were significantly positively associated with the number of ED cases. An interaction term between SmED assessments and ASHIPs was included to analyse ASHIP-specific effects. Using one ASHIP as the reference, negative associations were observed in five ASHIPs (three statistically significant) and positive, statistically significant associations in four ASHIPs. Cases in EDs and OOH services

When analysed across all ASHIPs and the full study period, weekly ED and OOH cases per 100 000 inhabitants decreased from 516.4 in calendar week 1 of 2016 to 400.3 in calendar week 52 of 2020 (figure 4). The model included 73 476 data points (234 calendar weeks across 314 counties within 11 ASHIPs). The full model achieved the best goodness-of-fit (pseudo-R2: p=0.32 for fixed effects; 0.81 including random effects).

Weekly number of ED and OOH services cases per 100 000 inhabitants across all ASHIPs (2016–2020), grey dots: numbers, blue dashed line: fitted linear trend. ASHIPs, Associations of Statutory Health Insurance Physicians; ED, emergency department; OOH, out-of-hour.

Across all ASHIPs and counties, the number of 116117 calls and SmED assessments was significantly negatively associated with ED and OOH cases (116117 calls: −0.02; 95% CI −0.03 to −0.001; SmED: r=−0.58; 95% CI −0.98 to −0.19). Other factors with a significant negative association included calendar week and COVID-19 cases. Factors such as holidays, influenza cases, average age, deprivation index, population density and out-of-hours GP services were significantly positively associated with ED and OOH cases. According to the standardised beta-coefficients, the deprivation index, additional holidays during the week and population index showed the strongest association with increased and COVID-19 cases with decreased ED and OOH cases (table 4).

Sensitivity analysis

In a sensitivity analysis, we included only data from the time period before the COVID-19 pandemic influenced the healthcare-seeking behaviour of the population (Q1-2016 until Q1-2020) and only from the five ASHIPs in which the intervention started in 2019 (Berlin, Bremen, Hessen, and two in North Rhine-Westphalia). This analysis showed a non-significant negative association for 116117 calls and a significant positive association for SmED assessments with both ED cases and combined ED and OOH cases (ED cases: 116117 calls: r=−0.001; 95% CI −0.019 to −0.018; SmED: r=0.37; 95% CI 0.29 to 0.45; ED and OOH services cases: 116117 calls: r=−0.03; 95% CI −0.06 to 0.003; SmED: r=0.34; 95% CI 0.20 to 0.48) (online supplemental file 1).

Summary and discussion

Almost all OECD countries face challenges managing the demand for acute and emergency care and seek strategies to direct patients to appropriate healthcare providers.32 33 Among OECD countries, initiatives to improve access to primary and community care focus on telephone-based services, OOH services, walk-in centres and community care centres.32 Only a few studies have evaluated the impact of implementing a telephone-based service on reducing demand for acute and emergency care.34,38 Most of the nine studies reviewed by Bunn et al37 examined telephone consultations in general practice rather than triage. The aim of this study was to add some evidence from the German context, focusing on the impact of the implementation of a 24/7 medical helpline and a structured initial assessment.

The analysis was limited by legal changes mandating intervention components across all study sites—leading to a loss of control groups and delayed implementation—and the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. Furthermore, our analysis has inherent limitations due to its ecological design. Relationships identified at the population level cannot be generalised to individual patients (ecological fallacy). Consequently, causal inference is limited, and findings should therefore be viewed as indicative of population-level patterns rather than individual behaviours.39 Our results show a trend towards reduced demand at EDs or other OOH services as 116117 calls increased, although causality cannot be established and results may be confounded by changes in healthcare-seeking behaviour during the COVID-19 pandemic,40 41 not fully captured by adjustments for COVID-19 cases. If only time intervals until March 2020 were considered, the negative association between 116117 calls and ED/OOH cases was not statistically significant.

The results regarding the effect of SmED use were inconclusive. Negative associations were identified overall for ED cases (non-significant) and for combined ED and OOH cases (significant). In contrast, positive associations were observed in several ASHIPs and in pre-pandemic sub-analyses. For the sub-analyses, it should be noted that the awareness of the helpline 116117 in the population, as well as numbers of 116117 calls and SmED assessments, was relatively low during the initial months of implementation, and the study initiators did not expect to observe a measurable intervention effect at this early stage42 (and personal communication). Results of studies evaluating the effect of a similar additional optional help line (NHS 111) were also inconclusive.35 36 Turner et al found that NHS 111 in its first year of operation in four pilot sites did not reduce calls to the 999 ambulance service or shifting patients from EDs to other urgent care services.36

One explanation may be that an optional additional service still allows patients to attend EDs or other OOH services directly, in contrast to a more compulsory system requiring prior contact. For example, a Norwegian study where patients were strongly encouraged and reminded to call before attending an emergency primary care clinic demonstrated a substantial behavioural shift: the share of direct attendances decreased from 68.7% (95% CI 68.4% to 68.9%) in the years before the intervention to 23.4% (95% CI 23.2% to 23.6%) in the years after.38

This study’s strengths include complete ambulatory claims data for more than 52 million statutorily insured patients (64% of the resident German population in the regions of intervention) and the inclusion of multiple confounders and time-specific and county-specific effects. For several confounders, results were consistent with published literature. Higher deprivation was associated with increased numbers of ED cases and ED and OOH cases, as also reported by Harris et al16 and Baker et al.43 A higher average age was also associated with a higher number of ED cases and ED and OOH cases, possibly due to a poorer health condition, which was linked to higher ED usage in studies by Agarwal et al.18 19 More OOH practices were associated with fewer ED cases but more ED and OOH cases. Other factors related to healthcare, such as hospital beds and physicians, did not have a statistically significant association with emergency contacts in any of the models. Harris et al16 also reported that factors related to the quality of the healthcare structure had no significant influence, although they considered waiting times and accessibility rather than the number of cases.16

Conclusions

Our findings indicate a trend suggesting that implementation of a 24/7 medical helpline may reduce the demand for acute and emergency care at EDs and OOH services, although clear evidence is lacking. The impact of SmED use remains inconclusive. International research suggests potentially stronger effects, if advance calling were made compulsory.38

Further research should ideally incorporate data linkage and controls and assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the triage process, as well as the quality of subsequent care at the individual level.

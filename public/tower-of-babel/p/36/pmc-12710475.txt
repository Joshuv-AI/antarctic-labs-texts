IgRENE: integrating gene regulatory networks and drug ontologies towards selected modification of target gene’s expression
Authors: George Minadakis, George M Spyrou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIgRENE is a web-based platform designed to integrate gene regulatory networks (GRNs) with drug-based regulatory data through a comprehensive, criteria-driven computational framework. The proposed tool provides a methodology for analyzing and identifying drugs along with their regulatory pathways, combining both upstream and downstream effects to assess the modulation of gene expression. This approach aids in prioritizing potential therapeutic compounds for more efficient further experimental validation and clinical testing. By consolidating gene-to-gene and drug-to-gene regulatory interactions within a unified interface, IgRENE provides researchers in drug discovery, systems biology, and precision medicine with a powerful tool that highlights drug candidates predicted to have a desired downstream effect on the expression of disease associated genes.Keywords: gene regulatory networks, drug discovery, gene modulation, complex networks

Introduction

The exploration and identification of therapeutic schemes based on drug combinations or other alternative strategies, particularly in the absence of effective pharmacological interventions for specific conditions, has emerged as a critical area of research [1, 2]. However, the absence of personalized therapeutic strategies, highlights the need for comprehensive research to develop holistic integrated approaches [3, 4]. Over the past decade, several computational frameworks have been developed to connect drugs, genes, and regulatory responses through large-scale transcriptomic and interaction data [5–7]. While these approaches have proven useful for drug repurposing and target prioritization, most rely primarily on direct gene-drug correlations or signature similarities, and thus offer limited capacity to capture multistep, causal regulatory effects that propagate through complex gene networks. In this line, to facilitate the discovery of drugs capable of modulating specific genes of interest towards inhibiting pathological progression, we propose a methodology through a web-based platform that integrates gene regulatory networks (GRNs) and drug target information using a criteria-based computational framework.

The swift growth of genetic data and advancements in experimental methods have ushered in a new era for biomedical engineering and bioinformatics, introducing well-defined GRNs with advanced computational and functional perspectives. In this context, the capacity to develop, evaluate, and understand both qualitative and quantitative models is becoming increasingly crucial [8]. GRNs are complex networks of directed interactions that capture the regulatory relationships between genes. These networks represent the flow of regulatory signals that influence gene expression and cellular behaviour. GRNs are composed of directed edges, which denote the cause-effect relationships derived from experimental evidence or expert curation, and nodes, which signify the biological entities involved. GRNs could eventually be a fundamental source of information for unravelling the molecular basis of biological functions and diseases, as well as for identifying potential therapeutic targets in systems biology and precision medicine. To this extend, we provide a Systems Bioinformatics [9] approach towards identifying drugs with the most significant regulatory effects on specific genes of interest, enabling the prioritization of drugs/compounds for experimental validation and possible further therapeutic application. By monitoring how signals propagate through these interactions, the proposed platform unifies gene regulation and drug interaction data into a single, user-friendly interface, providing researchers in drug discovery, systems biology, and translational medicine with an indispensable tool for designing precision therapies based on modulating gene expression.

Material and methods

The IgRENE methodologies towards identifying candidate drugs through GRNs, come with a frontend web interface that consists of the mainframe and a help page, all written using HTML, PHP and JavaScript language environments. The backend interface has been written in R language environment, holding all the proposed methodologies and equations that serve the overall pipeline, along with a MySQL database. The proposed tool is available online through the webpage of the Bioinformatics Department, at the Cyprus Institute of Neurology and Genetics (CING) (https://bioinformatics.cing.ac.cy/igrene), served by a docker space. The overall framework offers three comprehensive analytical approaches (web-tools) to facilitate the exploration of drug–gene and gene–gene regulatory interactions. First, IgRENE-DON (Drug Ontologies) where users can select specific biological ontologies to systematically project candidate drugs through GRNs in order to evaluate their potential modulatory effects on a gene of interest. This approach enables the assessment of how various drugs, situated within diverse biological contexts, influence the regulatory landscape surrounding the target gene. Second the IgRENE-DIN (Drug Influence Networks), where users have the ability to select individual drugs and rigorously investigate their corresponding suppression or activation networks in relation to the modulation of a specific gene of interest. This mode allows for a detailed examination of the direct and indirect pathways through which a drug exerts regulatory influence, whether inhibitory or stimulatory, on the target gene. Finally, The IgRENE-GIN (gene interaction networks) where, in the same manner, users can select individual source genes of interest and rigorously investigate their corresponding suppression or activation networks in relation to the modulation of a specific target gene of interest. This tool allows for a detailed examination of the direct and indirect pathways through which a source gene exerts regulatory influence, on the target gene. Together, these complementary strategies provide a versatile and in-depth platform for evaluating the network-based impact of pharmacological agents on gene regulation.

Data collection and preprocessing

The proposed platform incorporates the SIGNOR 3.0 (SIGnaling Network Open Resource), as its primary source for gene-to-gene regulatory information, a well curated repository of causal relationships between biological entities, including proteins, genes, and small molecules, that captures causal relationships between biological entities [10]. It provides freely accessible static maps of these interactions, which can be tailored and refined to build dynamic and predictive models. Each interaction is annotated with its effect (e.g. up- or down-regulation) and the underlying mechanism (such as binding or phosphorylation). Its rich dataset offers mechanistic regulatory information, critical for network-based drug prioritization, enabling precise mapping of the upstream and downstream interactions that influence target gene expression. As of its latest update, SIGNOR 3.0 encompasses ~33 000 manually curated causal relationships among over 8900 biological entities for several species. Herein, we obtained the above data from the freely available latest update found at the SIGNOR 3.0 website, of which only the human-based (Homo sapiens) relations have been retained. The data were further curated in order to keep only those relations that have clear and sufficient (non-overlapped) regulatory effect, while the rest have been marked as ‘insufficient’. Drug ranking methodology

Herein the primary aim is to identify potential drugs and their regulatory network paths associated with the modulation of a candidate gene, leaving the final decision to the user. Methodology for evaluating drug influence

Herein, it is crucial to emphasize that in the context of complex biological regulatory networks, a drug that targets a gene not directly connected to the gene of interest, but instead positioned distantly within the network topology, may exert multifaceted effects that go beyond simple, linear interactions. Specifically, such a drug may not only contribute to a suppression pathway that ultimately down-regulates the activity or expression of the target gene, but it could simultaneously participate in an activation network through its interactions with other molecular targets scattered throughout the network. These indirect or multistep pathways arise from the drug’s ability to modulate a diverse set of upstream or downstream regulatory proteins, signaling cascades, or transcriptional regulators, which collectively influence the functional outcome on the gene of interest. Importantly, these dual regulatory influences (suppression versus activation) are often context-dependent and can be shaped by factors such as network architecture, cellular state, and disease-specific molecular rewiring. Therefore, focusing solely on direct drug–gene interactions may overlook critical indirect effects that could either enhance or counteract the desired therapeutic modulation of the gene of interest. Results

Two key examples showcased through the web interface as well as through the supplementary document provided, include (i) the KRAS gene, a well-established oncogene that plays a central role in driving the initiation and progression of various cancers, and (ii) The Interleukin 6 (IL-6) gene a multifunctional cytokine with a central role in many physiological inflammatory and immunological processes, making it a clinically relevant therapeutic target. Herein we recall that our intention in including the above examples was to demonstrate that the proposed framework is: (i) able to recapitulate known clinically validated drugs (thus confirming the robustness of the methodology), (ii) to highlight how IgRENE also uncovers additional candidate compounds beyond those already established in medical practice, and (iii) to evaluate how specific drugs (e.g. those used in first-line therapies) can effectively modulate distant genes (such as KRAS and IL6) through their targets.

Targeting KRAS gene involved in colorectal cancer

KRAS mutations are among the most frequently observed genetic alterations in solid tumors, particularly in malignancies such as lung adenocarcinoma, colorectal cancer (CRC), and pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. Mutated KRAS acts as a constitutively active signaling molecule, persistently activating downstream pathways like MAPK and PI3K-AKT, which promote uncontrolled cell proliferation, survival, and metastasis. Its pivotal role in oncogenesis, coupled with its high mutation frequency, makes KRAS a compelling therapeutic target. However, despite its significance, KRAS has historically been classified as an ‘undruggable’ target due to the biochemical properties of its protein structure and the lack of accessible binding [13, 14]. Over the years, numerous therapeutic strategies have been devised to inhibit mutant KRAS function, including direct small-molecule inhibitors (such as KRAS G12C inhibitors), targeting upstream regulators like SOS1, or interfering with downstream effectors. While some of these approaches have yielded promising results and even led to clinical approvals, many therapies have demonstrated limited overall efficacy, often accompanied by intrinsic or acquired resistance mechanisms and eventual disease relapse. Characteristically, patients with new or unknown mutations on KRAS gene (such as G12S) exhibit poor survival, due to the lack of specific inhibitors, while anti-EGFR or anti-VEGF therapies cannot modulate effectively the expression of KRAS mutated gene that indirectly promotes cancer progression. Employing IgRENE’s network-driven analytics, a DRUGBANK derived conditional regulatory network was constructed to map the influence of various compounds on KRAS and its immediate regulatory neighbours. Specifically, Fig. 5 depicts a DRUGBANK driven conditional regulatory network, where 64 candidate drugs with potential suppressive effects on KRAS expression through its first neighbours, namely the SRC, PTPN11, and FNTA genes accordingly. Additionally, the EGFR (Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor) targeted by anti-EGFR agents in CRC, was further analysed to assess the capacity of IgRENE to recover clinically validated drug-target relationships. IgRENE successfully identified first-line anti-EGFR therapies such as Cetuximab and Panitumumab [15], which are monoclonal antibodies blocking receptor activation, as well as Erlotinib, Gefitinib, and Afatinib, small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors that prevent ATP binding to the intracellular kinase domain. Importantly, the network analysis also revealed non-canonical compounds capable of modulating EGFR signaling indirectly, suggesting that IgRENE can uncover secondary or compensatory pathways involved in receptor regulation. These findings confirm the system’s accuracy in reproducing established drug mechanisms while uncovering potential new candidates for therapeutic repurposing. Similarly, analysis of the VEGFA (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor A) network, underscored the framework’s robustness in angiogenesis-related pathways. VEGFA and its receptors (VEGFRs) are critical mediators of tumor vascularization, making them central targets in oncology. IgRENE successfully identified known and novel anti-angiogenic agents, including Bevacizumab (Avastin) and Ramucirumab, validating its predictive fidelity [16]. Beyond these established therapies, the platform revealed several additional compounds predicted to modulate VEGFA signaling indirectly through regulatory intermediates such as HIF1A, NFKB1, and NOS3. Examples include Carvedilol, Hydralazine, and 2-Methoxyestradiol, each acting via oxidative or hypoxic stress pathways; Minocycline and Gliclazide, influencing VEGFA suppression through anti-inflammatory cascades; and natural compounds such as Oleandrin, Andrographolide, and Genistein, predicted to suppress EGFR-related transcriptional activity. Moreover, kinase inhibitors like Ruxolitinib, Baricitinib, and Tofacitinib were identified as EGFR modulators via JAK/STAT-dependent feedback loops. These examples highlight IgRENE’s ability to delineate mechanistically coherent drug-gene associations, providing mechanistic clarity that conventional similarity-based repurposing methods often lack.

Among the 64 KRAS-targeting compounds, two molecules emerged as top-ranked candidates. The first one, [(3,7,11-Trimethyl-dodeca-2,6,10-trienyloxycarbamoyl)-methyl]-phosphonic acid], though less widely characterized in the literature, structurally resembles Farnesyl transferase inhibitors (FTIs) and phosphonate derivatives that interfere with prenylation pathways, particularly farnesylation of proteins such as KRAS [17, 18]. Farnesylation is a post-translational modification essential for the membrane localization and oncogenic activity of KRAS [19]. The compound exhibits a high DSScore of 0.875, which reflects a strong network-based influence toward KRAS suppression. The second compound, Farnesyl diphosphate (demonstrated a DSScore of 0.7321), is a key intermediate in the mevalonate pathway, which is directly involved in the synthesis of cholesterol, steroid hormones, and the prenyl groups used for protein prenylation [20]. Farnesyl diphosphate serves as a substrate for farnesyl transferase, which attaches the farnesyl group to proteins like KRAS, thus enabling their proper function. Modulating intracellular levels of farnesyl diphosphate, has been investigated as a potential approach to influence KRAS prenylation and downstream signaling [21]. To gain deeper insight into the potential biological influence of the identified drugs, we positioned each drug within the broader context of the regulatory gene network, using the IgRENE-DIN module. Specifically, we constructed two distinct types of directed networks: one capturing activation interactions and the other suppression interactions, each extending across eight hierarchical levels from the drug target node. For each level we calculated the activation and suppression score by means of Eq. (2). Then we performed Eq. (4) to estimate the overall combined score for each drug. The specific analysis revealed that both top-ranked compounds predominantly exerted suppressive effects up to intermediate network levels, with mixed activation/suppression effects emerging at deeper levels. Comparative influence analyses across KRAS, EGFR, and VEGFA demonstrated that the top compound exerted the greatest regulatory impact on KRAS (Cs = 0.572), while Farnesyl diphosphate showed slightly lower influence (Cs = 0.491). In contrast, a lower-ranked compound (RU79256, Cs = 0.278) exhibited minimal activity across all targets, thereby validating the discriminative power of the IgRENE scoring system.

To further examine the capacity of IgRENE to uncover novel candidate drugs beyond clinically validated inhibitors, we examined a recent study assessing the efficacy of immunotherapy in mismatch repair-proficient CRC patients with (LM) and without (NLM) liver metastases [22]. Focusing on Lenvatinib and Pembrolizumab, we showed that although both drugs exert a stronger regulatory influence on KRAS, Lenvatinib exhibits slightly negative effect towards suppression of EGFR gene while Pembrolizumab exhibits negative effect towards suppression of VEGFA gene, accordingly. These results illustrate that, beyond recapitulating established biologics, IgRENE can discover mechanistically plausible, repurposable drugs with indirect but biologically coherent impacts. Collectively, these analyses underscore that IgRENE is not limited to rediscovering known therapeutic agents but can generate novel, mechanistically grounded predictions that extend beyond direct drug-target interactions, thereby demonstrating its true discovery potential.

Targeting Interleukin-6 involved in inflammatory diseases

We further used the IL-6, a multifunctional cytokine with a central role in many physiological inflammatory and immunological processes, making it a clinically relevant therapeutic target. Due to its major role in initiation as well as resolving inflammation, deregulation of IL-6 is a mainstay of chronic inflammatory and autoimmune diseases. There are several clinically approved drugs such as Olokizumab, Canakinumab, Gevokizumab, Siltuximab, monoclonal antibodies targeting IL6 or its receptor (IL6R). However, IL-6 has been considered as a non-druggable or difficult-to-target gene, lacking for direct targeting by small molecules. Moreover, because IL-6 plays dual roles in both pro-inflammatory and protective immune responses, directly silencing its expression carries significant risks of immune suppression and metabolic imbalance, making indirect, network-based modulation a safer and more practical therapeutic strategy. Using the IgRENE-DON interface with IL6 as the gene of interest, we identified a series of drugs predicted to suppress IL6 expression or signaling through direct and multistep regulatory pathways, shown in supplementary document. Among the highest-ranked compounds, namely, Dilmapimod (DSScore = 0.875) and VX-702 (0.8125), we further highlight Olokizumab, Canakinumab, Gevokizumab, Siltuximab: monoclonal antibodies targeting IL6 or its receptor (IL6R), which validates IgRENE’s capacity to recapitulate clinically established therapies. Additionally, Andrographolide which is a natural compound with reported anti-inflammatory properties, predicted here to modulate IL6 through network interactions, illustrating the platform’s utility in identifying novel or less-characterized candidates. Dimethyl fumarate and Minocycline, are compounds already approved for other indications (multiple sclerosis, infections), which IgRENE highlights as potential modulators of IL6-related pathways [23, 24]. Innovation and distinctive features

Despite the growing number of computational platforms for drug repurposing and gene expression modulation, most tools rely heavily on transcriptional similarity, non-directional correlation networks, or enrichment-based strategies. Over the past decade, several computational frameworks have been developed to connect drugs, genes, and regulatory responses through large-scale transcriptomic and interaction data. Notable among these is the Connectivity Map (CMap), which links small-molecule perturbations to gene-expression signatures to infer potential drug–disease relationships. DIGEP-Pred [7] extends this concept by predicting differentially expressed genes following drug exposure using molecular descriptors and machine-learning models. More recently, methods such as CORN (Condition Orientated Regulatory Networks) [6] and scRank [5] have emerged to infer gene regulatory or drug response networks from single-cell and bulk RNA-Seq data. While these approaches have proven useful for drug repurposing and target prioritization, most rely primarily on direct gene-drug correlations or signature similarities, and thus offer limited capacity to capture multistep, causal regulatory effects that propagate through complex gene networks. Valuable computational efforts, aim to learn gene regulatory structure or cellular representations directly from data using graph AI-drivel methodologies that often use neural networks and attention-based deep models. For example, multiview graph-attention methods infer regulatory edges from multi-omics data and attention weights [25], while deep graph/contrastive approaches improve scRNA-seq cell-type annotation [26] and attention-autoencoders [27] improve clustering and denoising of single-cell data. These data-driven approaches excel at discovering context-specific interactions but typically produce weighted associations without curated sign or mechanistic annotations required for causal propagation reasoning. In contrast, IgRENE has been designed to explicitly model network-mediated modulation of gene expression, integrating both topological features (e.g. node connectivity, path distance, and regulatory directionality) and drug–gene interaction information within a unified scoring framework. This allows IgRENE to identify compounds that may influence a target indirectly through intermediate regulators, an important capability when addressing non-druggable or transcriptionally controlled targets such as KRAS, IL6, or VEGFA. By coupling quantitative influence propagation with statistical ranking, IgRENE complements these approaches by leveraging curated causal GRNs (SIGNOR) together with multisource drug ontologies to enumerate explicit regulatory paths and to predict whether a drug will activate or suppress a gene of interest. To authors’ knowledge, IgRENE is the only available web-framework for drug repurposing that integrates causal, directionally annotated GRNs with multisource drug-target ontologies to enable mechanism-aware drug repurposing.

Conceptual distinction and broader applicability

Herein, it is important to clarify the conceptual distinction between two fundamentally different mechanisms of therapeutic intervention. In many clinically established cases (e.g. EGFR, VEGFA), drugs act through direct binding to cell surface proteins or enzymes, thereby modulating protein activity without requiring the involvement of a broader regulatory network. While IgRENE is indeed able to recapitulate such well-known drug-target relationships, an important validation of the platform’s robustness, the core novelty of our approach lies in its ability to uncover indirect, network-mediated regulatory influences. This is particularly relevant for genes that are considered ‘undruggable’, such as KRAS, where direct inhibition through binding has historically been unsuccessful due to unfavourable biochemical properties. In these cases, IgRENE identifies and examines drugs that influence KRAS expression indirectly through multistep regulatory cascades (e.g. via SRC, PTPN11, and FNTA), offering mechanistically interpretable hypotheses that extend beyond direct drug-protein interactions. Such examples highlight how IgRENE enables the exploration of non-obvious intervention points in complex gene regulatory landscapes. Therefore, IgRENE should be viewed as complementary to existing direct-target drug discovery pipelines. Whereas conventional approaches excel at identifying compounds that bind directly to proteins, IgRENE provides unique added value in contexts where regulatory paths are central to modulating disease-relevant genes. This network-driven perspective broadens the therapeutic search space and supports the identification of candidate interventions that would otherwise remain hidden in direct-target-centric frameworks.

Discussion

The proposed approach builds on the assumption that GRNs in combination with drug gene networks may provide a powerful framework for identifying drugs capable of modulating specific genes of interest. By analysing the regulatory effects within these networks, it becomes possible to predict how regulatory cascades influence the expression of a target gene. The proposed method is particularly valuable for understanding how regulatory pathways within a network can be manipulated to inhibit the progression of pathological conditions. GRNs capture the intricate relationships between up-regulating and down-regulating interactions, allowing researchers to simulate how different interventions may affect disease-relevant genes. By incorporating this framework, potential therapeutic strategies can be designed to either suppress or activate genes implicated in disease progression. The systematic exploration of regulatory patterns offers insights into the mechanisms underlying biological processes, providing a foundation for more precise treatments. This approach is scalable and adaptable, making it a versatile tool for targeted drug discovery. Overall, IgRENE fills a crucial methodological gap by combining regulatory inference with ontology-aware drug mapping. This innovation opens new avenues for identifying candidate therapeutics with biological rationale, enhancing both predictive power and translational relevance. It demonstrates improved potential for uncovering non-obvious repurposing candidates, and its modular design ensures extensibility.

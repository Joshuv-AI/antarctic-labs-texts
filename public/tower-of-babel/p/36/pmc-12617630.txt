Role of long non-coding RNA Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 in regulation of innate immune response to influenza virus infection
Authors: Jiaqi Lin, Pengcheng Hu, Guiheng Mei, Bolun Guo, Song Wang, Shihong Yan, Ji-Long Chen, Yanmei Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLong non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are critical regulators of various biological processes, yet their roles in chicken host-virus interactions remain largely unknown. This study investigated the impact of influenza A virus (IAV) H9N2 infection on the expression of two lncRNAs, Lnc9101 and Lnc8475, and the regulatory roles of these lncRNAs in the innate immune response. The results demonstrated that the expression of both lncRNAs were significantly upregulated in H9N2-infected chickens and could be induced by viral genomic RNA, poly (I:C) or LPS. Disruption of Lnc9101 expression in DF-1 cells enhanced the IAV replication, while its overexpression suppressed it. Conversely, Lnc8475 had the opposite effect on the IAV replication compared with Lnc9101. Silencing Lnc8475 expression in DF-1 cells attenuated IAV replication, while its overexpression promoted viral replication. Furthermore, we found that both lncRNAs regulated the expression of type I and type III interferons (IFNs), thereby affecting multiple interferon-stimulating genes (ISGs) expression and STAT1 activation upon IAV infection. Moreover, Lnc9101 knockdown impaired the expression of TLR7 and MDA5 and reduced IRF7 phosphorylation, whereas Lnc8475 knockdown promoted the expression of TLR3 and TLR7. In summary, Lnc9101 inhibits IAV replication by positively regulating the innate immune response, whereas Lnc8475 promotes IAV replication by suppressing it. These findings suggest that LncRNA9101 and Lnc8475 play important roles in pattern recognition receptors (PRRs)-dependent innate immune signaling and antiviral responses, advancing a better understanding of lncRNA functioning in chickens.Keywords: Influenza A virus, Long non-coding RNA, Innate immune response, Interferon, Signaling pathway

Introduction

Influenza A virus (IAV), the most common strain of the influenza virus, is classified into subtypes based on the antigenicity of its surface glycoproteins, hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA) (Kok et al., 2023; Martinez-Sobrido et al., 2020). Currently, 18 HA and 11 NA subtypes of IAVs are found in animals. IAV is known to have a wide range of hosts, including humans, mammals, and avian species, and is considered an important class of zoonotic pathogens. Notably, avian species serve as the natural hosts of multiple influenza viruses. The segmented genome of influenza viruses facilitates the exchange of RNA fragments between genotypically distinct influenza viruses, leading to the emergence of new strains/subtypes. This capacity for genetic reassortment, coupled with its ability to transmit across species, is a key factor contributing to the potential for a global influenza pandemic (Delima et al., 2023; Reid and Taubenberger, 2003; Shartouny et al., 2022). Global annual infections of IAV result in approximately 3 to 5 million severe cases and 290,000 to 650,000 deaths, causing significant damage to both public health and socioeconomic systems worldwide (Griffin and Tompkins, 2023; Yang et al., 2024).

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are defined as RNA molecules exceeding 200 nucleotides in length, encompassing RNAs transcribed by RNA polymerases I, II, and III, and RNAs from processed introns (Mattick et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024). Although initially regarded as transcriptional "noise," extensive research has established lncRNAs as potent molecular modulators. They act as key regulators in diverse cellular processes, including epigenetic regulation, gene transcription, and post-transcriptional processes. As such, lncRNAs are integral to a wide spectrum of biological phenomena, from core cellular activities like proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis to specific immune responses involving signal pathway activation, cytokine/chemokine production, and the expression of interferons and their stimulated genes (ISGs) (Ferrer and Dimitrova, 2024; Yan et al., 2024). LncRNAs play important roles during virus infection and the host antiviral immune response. LncRNAs interact with a variety of biomolecules through multiple mechanisms during viral infection, directly or indirectly acting as positive or negative modulators in antiviral immunity (Cai et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2019). For example, a previous study demonstrated that IAV could induce the expression of lncRNA LINC02574, suggesting LINC02574 might be involved in broad-spectrum antiviral innate immune responses (Zhang et al., 2023). LncRNA HCG4 regulates the RIG-I-mediated IFN production to inhibit H1N1 swine influenza virus (SIV) replication through K63-linked RIG-I ubiquitination (Cheng et al., 2024). LncRNA PAAN enhances IAV replication by promoting the assembly of viral RNA polymerase, thus warranting efficient viral RNA synthesis (Wang et al., 2022); LncRNA MAHAT inhibits replication of PRRSV by Recruiting DDX6 To Bind to ZNF34 and Promote an Innate Immune Response (Liu et al., 2022); PRRSV infection up-regulates the LOC103222771 expression in Marc-145 cells and PAMs (Wang et al., 2023). lnc-SNAPC5-3:4 inhibited tumor progression in NSCLC by targeting miR-224-3p, suggesting that lncRNAs provide potential targets for disease detection and treatment (Hu et al., 2024). LncRNA SNHG7 could play the competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) role in controlling miR-216b availability for its target gene GALNT1 during cancer (Shan et al., 2018). LncRNAs can bind to miRNAs and proteins to regulate their function and expression (Du et al., 2016; Grelet et al., 2017), influence post-translational modification of proteins (Lin et al., 2016) or mediate mRNA translation and stability (Yuan et al., 2017).

Although some lncRNAs have been reported to be involved in antiviral immunity, studies of lncRNAs in chickens are still limited. In this study, we performed high-throughput sequencing and compared the expression patterns of lncRNAs in the lung tissues of chickens infected with avian influenza A virus (H9N2) against uninfected controls. We identified two significantly upregulated lncRNAs during H9N2 infection, designated Lnc9101 and Lnc8475. Comparative genomic analysis revealed that avian lncRNAs exhibit extremely low homology with their mammalian counterparts (e.g., human and pig), indicating a high degree of species specificity. Given the limited research on lncRNAs in chickens, investigating the role of avian lncRNAs in influenza virus infection holds significant implications for avian biosecurity. Our results indicate that Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 play differential roles in regulating the host antiviral innate immune response, thereby affecting IAV replication, with Lnc9101 acting as a positive regulator and Lnc8475 as a negative regulator. Both Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 were found to influence the interferon signaling pathway, a central cascade in antiviral immunity to viral infections. These findings provide crucial insights into the mechanisms of host antiviral immunoregulation, viral immune evasion, and immune regulatory network, offering a valuable scientific foundation for developing novel strategies against IAV infection, including new anti-influenza therapeutics.

Materials and methods

Cell lines and cell culture

DF-1, DH11, and HEK293T cell lines were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, VA, USA). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) containing 10 % heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA), penicillin (100 U/mL) and streptomycin (100 μg/mL) (Ouyang et al., 2014).

Short hairpin RNA (shRNA)-based knockdown cell lines were generated by infecting DF-1 cells with lentiviral expression of specific shRNAs in the pSIH-H1-EGFP vector (Ma et al., 2022). The following sequences were used in shRNAs targeting specific genes: shRNA Lnc9101, F: GATCCGCTGGAATGTTTGTGCATTTCCTTCCTGTCAGAGAAATGCACAAACATTCCAGCTTTTTG, R: AATTCAAAAAGCTGGAATGTTTGTGCATTTCTCTGACAGGAAGGAAATGCACAAACATTCCAGCG. shRNA Lnc8475, F: GATCCGGACATCCCTTCACGATTCTCCTTCCTGTCAGAGAGAATCGTGAAGGGATGTCCTTTTTG, R: AATTCAAAAA GGACATCCCTTCACGATTCTCTCTCTGACAGGAAGGAGAATCGTGAAGGGATGTCCG. DF-1 cells stably expressing Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 were generated by infection with retroviruses encoding lncRNA in the pMIG vector.

Antibodies

Antibodies used in this study: anti-STAT1 and anti-phosphorylated STAT1 (Tyr701), anti-phosphorylated STAT3 (Tyr705) (Cell Signaling Technology, Boston, MA, USA); anti-IRF3 and anti-phosphorylated IRF3 (Ser386) (Abcam, Cambridge, UK); anti-IAV NP (produced in our laboratory); anti-β actin (TransGen Biotech, Beijing, China).

Viruses and viral infections

Influenza A virus strains, including A/WSN/1933 (H1N1), A/Puerto Rico/8/1934 (H1N1), A/California/04/2009 (H1N1), and A/Chicken/Fujian/MQ01/2015 (H9N2), are propagated in pathogen-specific free (SPF) embryos in eggs (Li et al., 2022). The virus infected these cells with the indicated multiple infections (MOI). After adsorption for 1 hour at 37 °C, cells were washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and cultured in DMEM containing 2 μg/ mL trypsin (Levitz et al., 2012).

Hemagglutination test

For hemagglutinin assays, the supernatant was diluted with PBS and mixed with an equal volume of 1 % chicken red blood cells. Viral titers were counted from the highest dilution factor that produced a positive reading (Liu et al., 2023).

RT-PCR and RT-qPCR

RT-PCR and RT-qPCR were performed as previously described. Briefly, trizol (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) was used to extract total RNA from cells. 5 mg of total RNA, oligo-dT primer or random primer and reverse transcriptase (RT; Promega, Madison, WI, USA) were used. PCR and quantitative PCR were then performed using Taq DNA polymerase and SYBR PremixEx TaqII (TaKaRa, Tokyo, Japan). β-actin was used as an internally standardized reference gene. qPCR data were generated using the LightCycler system (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) and are expressed based on the 2-∆∆Ct method, with normalized ratios calculated automatically (Chen et al., 2024).

Western blotting

Western blotting was performed as previously described. Briefly, samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane in a wet tank transfer system. Blots were enclosed in TBS buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4, 150 mM NaCl) containing 5 % skimmed milk for 1 hour at room temperature and then probed with antibodies as indicated. Protein bands were visualized by chemiluminescence using a FluorChem E system (ProteinSimple, San Jose, CA, USA) (Xiao et al., 2021).

Statistical analysis

Student's t-test was used for pairwise comparisons, while one-way ANOVA followed by LSD post hoc test was applied for multiple group comparisons. Data are expressed as mean ± SD (standard deviation), and a p-value < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. Statistical significance is represented in figures by: *p < 0.05; **p < 0.01; ***p < 0.001.

Results

Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 expression levels are significantly elevated after viral infection

To investigate the role of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in H9N2 influenza A virus (IAV) infection, 5-day-old SPF chickens were inoculated with H9N2 influenza virus via the intranasal and intraocular routes. Lung tissues were collected 48 hours post-infection for transcriptomic analysis. Analysis of lncRNA expression profile (GEO accession no. GSE287377) revealed a large number of differentially expressed lncRNAs following the influenza virus infection (|Log2FC| > 1; p-value < 0.05). Specifically, 259 lncRNAs were significantly upregulated and 85 were downregulated (Fig. 1A, B). This widespread alteration in lncRNA expression suggests that lncRNAs could play a critical role in the host response to influenza virus invasion. To validate these findings, several lncRNAs were selected and their expression levels were tested not only in infected chicken lung tissues but also in two relevant cell lines: chicken embryonic fibroblasts (DF-1) and chicken macrophages (HD11). The results from these independent experiments confirmed the differential expression observed in the initial sequencing data, demonstrating a consistent pattern of lncRNA regulation upon H9N2 infection (Fig. 1C). H9N2 infection significantly upregulated the mRNA expression of two lncRNAs, designated Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 (the characterization of chromosomal locations, secondary structures, subcellular localizations, and coding potentials of these two lncRNAs are detailed in the supplementary material), in both chicken lung tissues and DF-1 cells. To further confirm this observation, we infected 60-days-old SPF chickens with H9N2 virus via intranasal and intraocular instillation. RT-PCR and RT-qPCR analyses of tracheal and lung tissues collected on days 3 and 5 post-infection consistently revealed a significant induction of both lncRNAs (Fig. 1D–K). Specifically, Lnc9101 expression peaked at day 3 and subsequently declined in both tissues (Fig. 1D–G). Lnc8475 expression exhibited a more prolonged increase, continuing to rise (Fig. 1H–K). We further investigated this induction in vitro using DF-1 cells. First, infection with H9N2 at progressively higher multiplicities of infection (MOI) resulted in a concomitant increase in the expression of both Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 at 18 h post- infection. (Fig. 1L–N). Second, a time-course experiment following infection (MOI=2) showed that the expression levels of both lncRNAs were significantly elevated at different time points (0 h, 3 h, 6 h, 9 h, 12 h, 18 h and 24 h) (Fig. 1O–Q). These results indicate that H9N2 infection induces the expression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 both in vivo and in vitro.

Multiple influenza virus strains and other types of viruses can induce the expression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475

To determine whether the upregulation of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 is specific to H9N2, we examined their expression in DF-1 cells infected with two other influenza viruses: the H1N1 strains PR8 and WSN. RT-PCR and RT-qPCR analysis after 24 hours of infection revealed that both H1N1 viruses also significantly induced the expression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 (Fig. 2A–F). Notably, the expression dynamics of these lncRNAs mirrored those observed during H9N2 infection: Lnc9101 expression was upregulated more rapidly, while Lnc8475 was higher at later time points. To further investigate whether this response extended to viruses beyond influenza. Infection of DF-1 cells with either Sendai virus (SeV, a single-stranded negative-sense RNA virus) and pseudorabies virus (PRV, a DNA virus) for 24 hours, also significantly induced the expression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 (Fig. 2G–L). Together, these data indicate that the expression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 is not specific to a particular virus but can be triggered by a broad range of viral pathogens, including both RNA viruses and DNA viruses.

Expression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 is induced by viral RNA, poly(I:C) and LPS

To elucidate the mechanism by which IAV upregulates Lnc9101 and Lnc8475, we investigated whether viral RNA components alone are sufficient to trigger their expression. DF-1 cells were transfected with H9N2 genomic RNA (vRNA) and total RNA extracted from H9N2-infected DF-1 cells (infected-cell RNA), and total RNA from mock-infected cells (Cellular RNA) served as a control. Both vRNA and infected-cell RNA transfection significantly induced the expression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 (Fig. 3A–D). We next asked if this response could be replicated by a synthetic viral RNA mimic. DF-1 cells were treated with polyinosinic-polycytidylic acid (poly(I:C), MW: 694.39), a synthetic analogue of viral double-stranded RNA, also clearly induced the expression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 3E–H). These observations suggest that the upregulation of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 could be regulated by PRR-dependent innate immune signaling pathways activated by IAV infection.

To investigate a potential link between the IAV-induced inflammatory response and the expression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475. We stimulated DF-1 cells with lipopolysaccharide (LPS), a potent inflammatory agent. Treatment with LPS at varying concentrations significantly upregulated the expression of both lncRNAs (Fig. 3I–K), with Lnc8475 exhibiting particularly pronounced differential expression. As expected, the expression of the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-6 was also elevated under these conditions (Fig. 3L). Together, the results imply that upregulation of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 following IAV infection is not solely a virus-specific event but is associated with the host's innate immune activation, pointing to their potential role in regulating anti-viral innate immunity in chickens.

Lnc9101 inhibits influenza virus replication

To determine the role of Lnc9101 in influenza virus replication, we generated a DF-1 cell line stably expressing Lnc9101-specific shRNA (sh-Lnc9101) and a control cell line expressing luciferase-targeting shRNA (sh-luc) using a lentiviral vector (pSIH-H1-GFP) (Fig. 4A). After confirming the knockdown efficiency, we infected these cell lines with H9N2 virus (MOI=2). We found that silencing Lnc9101 significantly enhanced IAV replication (Fig. 4B, C). To further corroborate this finding we collected cell culture supernatants at 2-hour intervals from 16 hours post- infection. Hemagglutination assays demonstrated that the viral titer in the supernatant from sh-Lnc9101 cells was consistently higher than that from the control cells, indicating accelerated virus production upon Lnc9101 knockdown (Fig. 4D). Collectively, these experiments demonstrate that Lnc9101 plays an inhibitory role in influenza virus replication.

To further confirm the inhibitory role of Lnc9101 in IAV replication, we constructed a DF-1 cell line stably overexpressing Lnc9101 using a PNL-EGFP vector, retaining the EGFP fluorescent genes of the vector to ascertain the infection efficiency. Successful overexpression was confirmed in the constructed cell line (Fig. 4E, F). We then infected both the empty vector (EV) control and Lnc9101-overexpressing (PNL-Lnc9101) cells with empty vector (EV) control and and Lnc9101-overexpressing (PNL-Lnc9101) cells with H9N2 virus (MOI=2). Analysis by RT-PCR, RT-qPCR, and Western blotting consistently demonstrated that Lnc9101 overexpression significantly suppressed H9N2 replication (Fig. 4F, G). This finding was further corroborated by hemagglutination assays of culture supernatants collected at 2-hour intervals from16 hours post-infection, which showed a marked reduction in viral titers from the Lnc9101-overexpressing cells (Fig. 4H). Together, these data reveal that Lnc9101 inhibits influenza virus replication in DF-1 cells.

Interference with Lnc9101 attenuates the innate immune response to influenza virus infection

Influenza virus infection triggers a series of host antiviral innate immune responses, notably the production of type Ⅰ IFNs like IFN-β. The subsequent induction of IFN stimulates the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, leading to the expression of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) that establish an antiviral state. Given that Lnc9101 inhibits viral replication, we hypothesized that Lnc9101 exerts this function by potentiating the antiviral innate immune response. To test this, we infected sh-luc and sh-Lnc9101 cell lines with H9N2 and monitored the expression of key ISGs over 24 hours. Knockdown of Lnc9101 significantly suppressed the expression of IFIT5, MX1, and OASL at both the RNA and protein levels (Fig. 5A). Conversely, overexpressing of Lnc9101 markedly enhanced the induction of these ISGs following infection (Fig. 5B). The RT-qPCR results further confirmed this regulatory relationship (Fig. 5C-H). Together, these data demonstrate that Lnc9101 promotes the expression of antiviral ISGs and thereby affects the viral replication.

Furthermore, we investigated how Lnc9101 regulates the key upstream cytokines and signaling molecules that drive ISGs expression. In Lnc9101 knockdown cells infected with IAV, the expression of IFN-α, IFN-β and IFN-λ was markedly diminished compared to the control (Fig. 5I–K). This suppression of the interferon response was consistent at the level of signaling pathway activation: phosphorylation of both IRF7 and STAT1 was significantly reduced (Fig. 5L). Given these findings, we further examined the impact on pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), which initiate the innate immune responses. Knockdown of Lnc9101 notably reduced the mRNA expression of MDA5 and TLR7 (Fig. 5M, N). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that Lnc9101 is a positive regulator of the antiviral innate immune response, acting upstream to enhance PRR expression and thereby potentiate the entire IFN signaling cascade.

Lnc8475 promotes influenza virus replication

Given the distinct expression patterns of Lnc8475 and Lnc9101 during viral infection, particularly the upregulation of Lnc8475 at later stages, we sought to investigate its potential role in virus-host interaction. To explore the function of Lnc8475, we established stable DF-1 cell lines with either knockdown or overexpression of Lnc8475. The infection efficiency of Lnc8475-specific shRNA in DF-1 cells was confirmed (Fig. 6A). We then infected sh-Lnc8475 and sh-luc control DF-1 cells with H9N2 influenza virus (MOI=2) and evaluated the impact of Lnc8475 on viral replication. The results demonstrated that Lnc8475 knockdown significantly suppressed viral replication (Fig. 6B, C). Conversely, overexpression of Lnc8475 enhanced viral replication (Fig. 6D–6F). Together, these data indicate that Lnc8475 plays an important role in influenza virus replication.

Silencing Lnc8475 enhances the innate immune response to influenza virus infection

Based on previous findings, we hypothesized that Lnc8475 might also affect viral replication by regulating the expression of ISGs. To test this, sh-luc and sh-Lnc8475 DF-1 cell lines were infected with H9N2 influenza virus (MOI=2). RNA and protein samples were collected at 24 h post-infection to analyze the expression of several key antiviral ISGs. RT-PCR analysis showed that knockdown of Lnc8475 markedly increased the mRNA levels of IFIT5, MX1, and OASL (Fig. 7A), whereas Lnc8475 overexpression suppressed their expression (Fig. 7B). These observations were further confirmed by RT-qPCR (Fig. 7C-H). Together, these results indicate that Lnc8475 promotes viral replication by suppressing ISG expression.

Building on our previous findings that Lnc9101 modulates ISG expression through the interferon signaling pathway, we hypothesized that Lnc8475 employs a similar mechanism. To test this, we analyzed key components of the interferon signaling pathway in H9N2-infected sh-Lnc8475 and control cells. We found that Lnc8475 knockdown elevated IRF7 protein levels, significantly upregulated IFN-α, IFN-β, and IFN-λ mRNA, and increased STAT1 phosphorylation (Fig. 7I–L). These results indicate that Lnc8475 suppresses the interferon signaling pathway, thereby reducing ISG expression and facilitating viral replication.

We also examined the effect of Lnc8475 knockdown on the expression of PRRs. The results showed that knocking down Lnc8475 significantly increased the mRNA levels of TLR3 and TLR7 (Fig. 7M, N). This indicates that Lnc8475 can modulate the host antiviral innate immune response by negatively regulating the expression of Toll-like receptors.

Discussion

LncRNAs are key regulators of virus-host interaction and antiviral immunity. On the one hand, hosts regulate antiviral innate immunity by altering the expression and function of lncRNAs (Khatun et al., 2021). On the other hand, viruses can escape from the host immunity through targeting lncRNAs (Carnero et al., 2016). For instance, lncRNA PCNAP1 has been reported to increase tumor growth in hepatocellular carcinoma and affect hepatitis B virus replication (Feng et al., 2019); in vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV) infection, Lnc-Lsm3b efficiently competes with viral RNAs for binding of RIG-I via a multi-branched loop topology (Jiang et al., 2018); lncRNA-NRAV could promote IAV replication by repressing the initial transcription of several key ISGs (Li et al., 2020), including IFITM3 and MxA, through modulation of their histone modifications; Lnczc3h7a acts as a molecular scaffold to stabilize TRIM25/RIG-I interactions and enhance the TRIM25-mediated K63-linked ubiquitination of RIG-I, thereby promoting RIG-I downstream signaling and antiviral innate immune responses (Lin et al., 2019).

In this study, through bioinformatics analysis and experimental validation of RNA-seq data, we identified several lncRNAs exhibiting significant differential expression patterns in response to H9N2 infection and finally selected two candidate lncRNAs, named Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 for the study. We investigated their differential expression and function in viral infection and further explored their underlying mechanisms in modulating antiviral immune responses.

The results from in vitro and in vivo experiments showed that the expression levels of both lncRNAs were upregulated after infection with different subtypes of influenza viruses and other types of viruses, suggesting that they may function in the host antiviral immunity. As verified by various experimental methods, the two lncRNAs played different roles in viral infection, with Lnc9101 inhibiting the replication of the H9N2 virus and Lnc8475 exhibiting a role in promoting viral replication. Despite the deficiency of multiple immune-critical genes, avian species can still robustly induce IFNs for antiviral response during viral infection. To reveal the coping mechanisms of LncRNA in innate immunity, we performed a preliminary study on the mechanisms of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 in viral infection. The effect on the expression of several important ISGs was validated on the constructed cell lines with stable knockdown and overexpression of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475. In further studies, it was found that Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 could regulate the phosphorylation of IRF7, affect the expression levels of type Ⅰ IFNs and type Ⅲ IFNs, and ultimately affect STAT1 activation. Pan et al. revealed that lncRNA ISR is regulated by RIG-I-dependent signaling, which controls IFN-β production during IAV infection and has an inhibitory capacity on viral replication (Pan et al., 2019). Wang et al. demonstrated that chicken IRF7 serves as a key regulator of IFNs, which is related to the JAK-STAT signaling pathway and is involved in the chicken anti-ALV-J innate immune response. These findings are consistent with and support our experimental results (Wang et al., 2021). Additionally, both lncRNAs exhibited regulatory effects on PRRs, albeit through distinct mechanisms (Lee et al., 2020). Specifically, Lnc9101 was found to target MDA5 and TLR7, while Lnc8475 showed no significant impact on MDA5 but markedly influenced TLR3 and TLR7 expression levels. These findings suggest that Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 may play crucial roles in modulating the PRR signaling pathway, thereby influencing the antiviral innate immune response (Wise et al., 2012).

The results of this study are presented in Fig. 8. In summary, Lnc9101 targets the MDA5 and TLR7 receptors. Knockdown of Lnc9101 in DF-1 cells resulted in significantly lower expression levels of MDA5 and TLR7 compared to the control following viral stimulation. This resulted in reduced expression of the downstream factor IRF7, which consequently attenuated IRF7 phosphorylation, leading to a decrease in the production of type I and III interferons. The subsequent downregulation of ISGs thereby significantly promoted viral replication. Lnc8475 targets the TLR3 receptor following viral stimulation. Knockdown of Lnc84775 in DF-1 cells significantly enhanced the expression levels of TLR7 and TLR3 receptors compared to the control following viral stimulation. This led to increased expression of the downstream factor IRF7, consequently enhancing IRF7 phosphorylation. The enhanced phosphorylation promoted the production of both type I and type III interferons, thereby boosting the expression of ISGs. Ultimately, viral replication was markedly suppressed. These two lncRNAs target distinct PRRs and exert opposite regulatory effects on viral replication. How these two lncRNAs, which are upregulated upon viral infection, interact with PRRs remains to be elucidated.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study showed the roles of Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 in the regulation of innate immune response to influenza virus infection. The results showed that both Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 can be upregulated by multiple subtypes of IAV (H9N2, PR8, and WSN), as well as by other viruses such as PRV and SeV. Disruption of Lnc9101 expression in DF-1 cells enhanced the IAV replication, while its overexpression suppressed viral production, indicating that Lnc9101 inhibits IAV replication by positively regulating the innate immune response. Conversely, Lnc8475 had the opposite effect on the IAV replication compared with Lnc9101. Silencing Lnc8475 expression in DF-1 cells attenuated IAV replication, while its overexpression promoted viral replication, indicating that Lnc8475 promotes IAV replication by suppressing immune responses. Knockdown of Lnc9101 reduced the expression levels of MDA5, TLR7, along with their corresponding adapter protein genes MAVS and MyD88. In contrast, knockdown of Lnc8475 significantly increased the expression levels of TLR3, TLR7, and their key adapter molecule gene MyD88. These findings suggest that Lnc9101 and Lnc8475 may affect the antiviral innate immune response by regulating pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) signaling.

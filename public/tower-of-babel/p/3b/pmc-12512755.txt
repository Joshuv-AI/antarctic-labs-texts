Seroprevalence of dengue in the general population of a Chinese southwestern border city: a community-based cross-sectional study
Authors: Siyu Yin, Yonghua Liu, Mingdong Yang, Hongning Zhou, Xiaoxiong Yin, Yong-Tao Chi, Zhaolan Yang, Siyu Chen, Ying Qin, Yu Li, Zhongjie Li, Hongjie Yu, Weizhong Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDengue fever poses significant public health concern in tropical and subtropical regions, including areas of China-Myanmar border, in which the population-representative evidence on dengue seroprevalence is limited.MethodsA community-based cross-sectional survey was conducted in December 2015 in Ruili, a border city in southwestern China. Participants were selected using a stratified random sampling method. Demographic information, previous clinical and travel history, and environment-related factors regarding dengue were collected by structured interview, with serum samples tested for IgG antibodies using ELISA. Age- and gender-weighted seroprevalence was estimated based on the local demographic distribution. Associated factors for seropositivity were analyzed using logistic regression.ResultsThe study included 1,616 participants aged 1–95 years, with 58.42% being female. The weighted seroprevalence of Dengue was 4.43% (3.53–5.54). Residents of aged 45–54 years, border communities (adjusted odds ratio 6.39 [95%CI 2.98–13.74]), vector-environment exposed occupations (3.43 [1.34–8.82]), lower educational attainment (0.28 [0.09–0.86]), and high travel frequency to southeast Asia (8.94 [1.67–47.83]) tended to have higher seroprevalence. Moreover, we observed a symptomatic-to-asymptomatic ratio of 1:2.84 for dengue infections.ConclusionsThe seroprevalence of Dengue virus was low by 2015 in the study area, where dengue had not achieved widespread local transmission. Targeted interventions, enhanced surveillance, and cross-border collaboration are needed to mitigate dengue transmission in this region.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-11658-z.

Introduction

Dengue fever is an acute, mosquito-borne disease caused by dengue virus (DENV-1 to DENV-4), with a growing burden in more than 100 countries across tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. It has become one of the fastest-growing viral diseases globally, with reported cases surging from approximately 0.5 million in 2000 to over 5.2 million in 2019 [1]. In response to record-high infections in 2024, the World Health Organization (WHO) designated dengue as a top-tier global health threat and included DENV on its updated Priority Pathogens list which may cause widespread outbreaks [2, 3]. Southeast Asia (SEA) bears a substantial portion of this burden, and dengue is increasingly emerging in previously less-affected areas, including southwestern China [4]. Within Southeast Asia, surveillance data from Myanmar's Vector Borne Disease Control (VBDC) Program documented 127,912 Dengue cases between 2012 and 2017, with 54,561 cases reported during 2017–2018, indicating geographic expansion and rising case reports [5, 6]. More recent hospital-based serological analysis of suspected patients in Myanmar in 2022 revealed seropositivity of 32% in adults and 37% in children for Dengue non-structural protein 1 antigen (NS1Ag). Cross-border transmission risk is further underscored by the 2019 outbreak in Xishuangbanna, a Chinese border region adjacent to Myanmar and Laos, where concurrent circulation of multiple virus serotypes resulted in 47.41% of 8,226 febrile patient samples testing positive for NS1 antigen [7].

Ruili City in Yunnan Province, located at the China-Myanmar border, has experienced multiple outbreaks and is considered a hyperepidemic zone for dengue transmission due to frequent cross-border movement, subtropical climate, dense vegetation, and the widespread presence of Aedes mosquito vectors [8, 9]. Globally, Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus are recognized as the principal dengue vectors. In China, Aedes aegypti remains largely confined to border areas of Yunnan and a few other tropical regions, with ecological suitability for this vector increasing notably in proximity to international borders, highlighting the potential for cross-border transmission [10]. In 2008, Ruili experienced its first imported Dengue outbreak, followed by its first local outbreak in 2013. Subsequently, imported and local epidemics occurred annually until 2019, with local infection incidence reaching 116.89 per 100,000 in 2020 [11]. Notably, Ruili has still reported recurrent outbreaks of dengue fever over these years, with sustained local transmission and growing public health concerns.

Despite the local recurrence of the epidemic, the true magnitude of DENV exposure in the general population remains poorly understood. DENV can cause disease ranging from asymptomatic infection to non-specific viral illness and even severe clinical manifestations, such as dengue hemorrhagic fever and dengue shock syndrome, with a symptomatic-to-asymptomatic ratio (S:A ratio) of approximately 0.25 [3, 12]. Passive surveillance systems and clinical reporting often underestimate the disease burden, particularly in regions with limited medical resources and serological investigations, resulting in asymptomatic and subclinical infections often being missed [13]. The non-specific nature of symptoms misattributing mild dengue fever to malaria, other febrile illnesses or arbovirus infections may partly account for this, further exacerbating the phenomenon of underreporting and false alarms [14, 15]. Serological surveys of anti-DENV Immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies offer insight into the true cumulative burden of dengue infection, especially in settings where variable clinical presentations go unrecognized [14]. Such studies are considered more reliable tools than case-based reporting for identifying at-risk populations and guiding vaccination and vector control strategies [16, 17]. Community-based seroprevalence studies especially those that incorporate age- and gender-standardization are scarce, leaving substantial gaps in understanding the cumulative exposure, immunity levels, and potential vulnerability to future outbreaks. Furthermore, the proportion of subclinical infections remains unquantified in Ruili, obscuring community-level transmission dynamics.

Understanding DENV transmission is essential for future control, requiring quantification and analysis of the spatial epidemiological characteristics underlying outbreaks. Primarily transmitted by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes, dengue fever is greatly affected by stagnant water and humid weather [18]. Additionally, travel frequency and socio-environmental determinants increase individual exposure to vectors, facilitating DENV spread to susceptible populations [19]. However, evidence remains limited regarding how demographic, geographic, and environmental factors contribute to infection risk in non-endemic areas based on serological epidemiological studies. Crucially, quantitative evidence is lacking on whether areas near international borders, including cross-border trading zones and ports of entry, pose higher infection exposure risks. This lack of epidemiological data hampers the development of targeted public health responses and risk-based surveillance systems.

To address these gaps, this study leveraged the first community-based dengue serological database established in a border area of Yunnan Province. Through a cross-sectional survey in Ruili City, we aimed to provide population-representative seroprevalence, identify asymptomatic infection ratios, and explore potential risk factors for prior exposure. This establishes a valuable historical baseline dataset to inform evidence-based dengue control strategies in Southwestern China and analogous border regions, while establishing a template for future population-representative seroepidemiological studies.

Methods

Study design and settings

This analysis was based on a community-based cross-sectional study in Ruili City of Yunnan Province, a Myanmar-bordering municipality in southwestern China. This survey employed a structured questionnaire developed for this study to investigate the residents (see related details in the supplementary file) and simultaneously drew their blood for DENV-specific IgG testing in the field. Dengue fever in mainland China demonstrates distinct seasonal pattern, with transmission occurring primarily from June to November and typically peaking in September–October (Fig. 1). Population-representative sampling during December 2015 (non-transmission season) generated epidemiological datasets comprising geographic coordinates, standardized questionnaires, and serum samples. Recruitment of study participants

The required sample size was determined for each age group (0–14, 15–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, and ≥ 65 years), based on expected DENV seroprevalence and targeted precision, accounting for a 95% confidence level and a design effect of two due to clustering. In total, a sample of 1,510 was required (Fig. 2). Eleven administrative districts of Ruili City were randomly selected and age-stratified target number of samples were allocated for each age group of the district, which was proportionate to the population size of the corresponding district. District by district, participants were recruited in December 2015, according to following selection criteria to fulfill the target quota with slight excess permitted. Community-based participants were included if they had resided in the area for at least six months prior to data collection and were willing to complete both the questionnaire and blood sampling procedures. Exclusion criteria included age under six months, diagnosis of immunodeficiency (e.g., HIV, leukemia, lymphoma, generalized malignancy), current immunosuppressive treatment (chemotherapy or radiation therapy within the 12-month-period prior to enrollment), known coagulopathy, significant medical history, or unwillingness to provide informed consent.Fig. 2Study procedure of the serological survey conducted in Ruili, China. Data collection and laboratory tests

The questionnaire in the supplementary file, which was developed specifically for the present study and has not been published elsewhere so far, consisted of sociodemographic information, clinical and travel history, and environment-related factors regarding Dengue. Following the interview, a 5 mL peripheral venous blood sample was collected by certified nurse using sterile vacuum coagulation tubes without anticoagulants. Samples were left at room temperature for at least 30 min to allow clotting and were centrifuged within 24 h at 3000 rpm for ten minutes. The separated serum was aliquoted into cryovials and stored at −70 ℃ until final analysis, without repeated freeze–thaw cycles. Additionally, reported data were obtained from the infectious disease case reporting and epidemiological investigation records of the National Notifiable Infectious Disease Surveillance System (NNIDSS) in China.

Laboratory-confirmed DENV infection was defined as anti-dengue IgG positivity using the Panbio Dengue IgG Indirect ELISA (Abbott Diagnostics, Australia), performed in October and November 2017 at the laboratory of Fudan University in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. Serum IgG antibodies remain stable for years at −70 °C or lower, maintaining both immunological activity and structural integrity [20]. Accordingly, the observed antibody profile reliably reflects past exposure to any of the four Dengue virus serotypes despite the interval between sample collection and testing. The estimated sensitivity and specificity of the assay were reported as 97.9% and 100%, respectively. Panbio units were calculated by dividing the optical density of the sample by that of the cut-off calibrator and then multiplying by ten. Samples with Panbio Units > 11 were considered positive, < 9 were negative, and 9–11 were classified as equivocal. Equivocal samples were re-tested to confirm the result. Internal positive and negative controls were included in each assay batch, and reagent quality was monitored through manufacturer-supplied Quality Control Standards. Sample traceability was ensured through a barcode-based tracking system, linking each serum sample to its corresponding questionnaire and metadata.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were employed to summarize demographic characteristics of participants, with categorical variables being summarized as frequencies and percentages. Univariate analyses were conducted using Pearson's Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test when applicable to compare proportions between groups.

To account for differences between the sampled population and the underlying demographic structure of Ruili, data were weighted based on the age-gender distribution using the population data in 2015 and presented with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) using the Wilson score method. To standardize estimates, the study assigned weights to each person based on their membership to each of 14 strata of age (0–14, 15–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, and ≥ 65 years) and gender (male and female). Post-stratification weights were defined as the proportion of each stratum represented in Ruili population divided by the analogous proportion in the sample and then we computed weighted frequencies and 95% CIs according to two prespecified strata (age and gender). We collapsed reported Dengue cases during the period 2005–2015 reported in NNIDSS in China to an area level and then assessed the Spearman’s correlation coefficient for the association of cumulative incidence with estimated seroprevalence. Finally, using logistic regression, we determined the age-adjusted and gender-adjusted correlates of seropositivity for all variables.

To estimate the S:A ratio of DENV infections within the study, data from participant questionnaires detailing prior clinical diagnoses of dengue fever alongside results from serological testing for DENV-IgG were further utilized. In this study, symptomatic dengue infections were defined as laboratory-confirmed cases accompanied by symptom-driven medical attendance, whereas asymptomatic infections were defined as laboratory-confirmed cases without clinically significant symptoms.

All data were double-entered using EpiData version 3.1 and analyzed using R software version 4.2.2. Multiple imputation was applied to simultaneously handle missing data for multiple variables under the assumption of missing at random. Sensitivity analyses were performed by excluding imputed values or reclassifying them as negative and comparing findings to the full imputed dataset to test the robustness of seroprevalence estimates. A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Result

Sociodemographic characteristics

A total of 1,616 participants aged 1–95 years were included in the final analysis. There were more females (n = 944, 58.42%) than males, similar to that of the population in Ruili. Nearly half of the participants (n = 740, 45.79%) reported occupations that were primarily indoors, including teachers, students, administrators, and healthcare professionals, followed by 29.52% (n = 477) reported homemakers, unemployed, or retired (Table 1). Of the 1,616 participants, 73 (4.52%) tested positive for DENV-IgG and the remaining 1,543 (95.48%) tested negative. Compared to those who tested negative, those who tested positive for DENV-IgG were more likely to be older, to reside in areas adjacent to borders, to engage in occupations characterized by household, high mobility, or environmental exposure, to attain lower education, and to have travelled recurrently to Southeast Asian countries, while they were similar in terms of other characteristics. The five areas included in the research are all located within the urban district, with distances from the China-Myanmar border decreasing progressively from Area 1 to Area 5. Weighted seroprevalence of DENV-specific IgG stratified by age and gender

Overall, the cumulative seroprevalence in the sample was 4.52% (95% CI 3.61–5.64) by 2015. When standardized to the Ruili population, seroprevalence was 4.43% (3.53–5.54) with high age variation in seroprevalence ranging from 1.90% (0.86–4.14) in 0–14 years to 6.87% (4.35–10.69) in 45–54 years (Table 2). Seroprevalence was modestly lower in people aged 55–64 or 65 years and older compared with those aged 45–54 years. Using data from the sampled population, variation by residential proximity to the China-Myanmar border was high, ranging from 2.65% (1.44–4.85) in Luchuan and Mengmao (area 1) to 15.70% (11.22–21.54) in Jiegao (area 5), with the highest regional variation occurring in the area directly adjacent to the international border (Fig. 3). Stratified analyses further revealed occupation-, education- and travel-dependent seroprevalence patterns that persisted in both unweighted and standardized estimates. The highest IgG positivity rates were observed in individuals engaged in environmentally exposed (9.13%, [5.54–14.66]) and home-based (6.38%, [4.36–9.24]) occupations, never attending school (10.54%, Japanese encephalitis (JE) vaccination history was available for 1,159 participants (71.72%), with 342 (29.51%) participants vaccinated. The vaccinated and unvaccinated groups exhibited significant differences in age distribution (χ2 = 933.4, p < 0.001). Specifically, vaccinated individuals were predominantly 0–14 years (84.80%), whereas the unvaccinated population was largely concentrated in older age groups (84.45% aged 25 years and above). In univariate analysis, JE vaccination was significantly associated with dengue IgG seropositivity (χ2 = 12.09, p < 0.05) and with several covariates, including age, location, occupation, education, and travel frequency to SEA. However, the association was no longer statistically significant after adjusting for age in a multivariate logistic regression model (aOR = 1.78, 95% CI: 0.72–4.85, p = 0.231). Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study represents one of the first large-scale, population-based seroepidemiological investigations of DENV exposure among the residents in Ruili, a China-Myanmar border city. The analysis of Dengue IgG seroprevalence in a representative sample revealed that fewer than 5% of residents showed seroconversion by 2015, with predominantly asymptomatic infections (S:A ratio = 1:2.84). Furthermore, we found an increased likelihood of dengue seropositivity in the following groups: residents aged 45–54, border residents (six times higher), environmentally exposed workers (three times higher), and frequent travelers to Southeast Asia (nine times higher). Higher education attainment was associated with lower likelihood of seroconversion with an adjusted OR of 0.28 for college graduates and above.

Dengue fever in China is primarily episodic and imported, thus dengue IgG seroprevalence remains markedly lower than in endemic countries with continuous viral circulation. Population-based studies in multiple Southeast Asian countries (e.g., Thailand, Laos, India, Singapore) and South American nations (e.g., Colombia) have documented seroprevalence ranging from 48.6% to over 91.5%, consistent with intense and widespread DENV circulation [15, 21–23]. While Guangdong differs geographically from the China-Myanmar border setting of Ruili, both are characterized by subtropical climates, high human mobility, and recurrent Dengue outbreaks in southern China. Results from previous serological testing have revealed IgG seroprevalence of 5.77% in Guangdong province and 11.80% in its capital Guangzhou, comparable to the epidemic level in Ruili [24, 25]. The low seroprevalence in Ruili corroborates the low incidence reported by NNIDSS in China, suggesting limited sustained transmission. WHO recommends Dengue vaccination either in settings with seroprevalence exceeding 80% or among individuals with confirmed prior infection [16]. Our results did not support broad vaccination in China without serological screening, as it may expose seronegative individuals to increased risk of severe outcomes through antibody-dependent enhancement [26]. Nonetheless, Ruili remains at relatively higher risk of outbreaks compared with other regions in China, probably attributed to the intense human mobility and sustained vector suitability in border areas, elevated rates of asymptomatic infections, and the overall low levels of background immunity within the population.

Asymptomatic individuals, despite lacking clinically detectable manifestations, maintain viral titers sufficient to infect Aedes vectors and sustain DENV transmission [27]. Reported variations in the S:A ratio across studies reflect heterogeneity in definitions of "asymptomatic", study design, serotype-specific virulence, pre-existing cross-reactive immunity, prior infections or vaccinations, and inter-epidemic intervals [25, 28, 29]. A previous study estimated a pooled asymptomatic prevalence of 59.26% (95% CI: 43.76–74.75), with higher proportions during outbreaks (65.52%) than non-outbreak periods (30.78%) [30]. Similar summaries from CDC Yellow Book suggest that 40–80% of infections may be asymptomatic [31]. The S:A ratio of Dengue has been rarely reported in China. Our estimated ratio of 74.0% indicated nearly three undetected infections per symptomatic case, consistent with evidence from Nicaragua, where 76.5% of transmission was attributed to inapparent infections, and slightly exceeding the 68.8% asymptomatic infection rate reported in Colombia [23, 32]. This metric, derived in this single cross-sectional serosurvey, should therefore be interpreted with caution and regarded as context-dependent rather than fixed, as under-reporting of mild or subclinical infections could lead to underestimation, while misclassification of other febrile illnesses as dengue may result in overestimation. Overall, the observed S:A ratio in this study lies above the pooled average but within reported ranges. It provided empirical evidence of asymptomatic dengue burden in China’s border regions, addressing a major surveillance gap. Enhanced testing is therefore essential to detect asymptomatic carriers, who play a key role in DENV transmission and persistence.

Age has been consistently identified as a key determinant of DENV seropositivity in both endemic and non-endemic regions. Although individuals at all ages are susceptible, a non-monotonic trend that seropositivity increases through early and middle adulthood, peaking at ages 45–54 before declining among older adults, was observed. This pattern mirrors findings in low-to-moderate transmission settings such as Malaysia, where cumulative lifetime exposure drives middle-aged peaks, but survival bias, waning antibodies over time, behavioral changes, and immune senescence contribute to declines in the elderly [33–35]. Differently, studies conducted in India, Thailand, Laos, and Taiwan of China have demonstrated a steep increase in seroprevalence with age, particularly in settings with long-standing dengue circulation [15, 21, 36]. This pattern likely reflects the cumulative nature of exposure to Aedes mosquitoes and DENV and persistent immune memory for isotype serotypes, as seropositivity indicates prior infection over a personal lifetime [33, 37]. In tropical and subtropical regions, the probability of exposure and antibody development increases with time lived in a hyperendemic environment.

Residents of communities nearest the Myanmar border exhibit significantly higher dengue IgG seroprevalence (p < 0.001), probably driven by elevated Aedes mosquito densities, frequent traveler importation, and heightened cross-border human mobility. Approximately 10 million travelers entered Yunnan Province via Ruili crossings each year, with substantial DENV infections detected through antigen testing [38]. Notably, Dengue incidence from 2013 to 2019 in Yunnan closely correlated with international importation, as frequent cross-border movement from Myanmar may amplify outbreak risks in otherwise non-endemic settings [39]. Historical dengue outbreaks in Ruili often originated from Myanmar imports, and molecular studies have identified DENV lineages genetically similar to Myanmar and wider Southeast Asia [8]. Compounding this risk is Myanmar’s ongoing dengue crisis, disproportionately affecting border populations. Border areas in Myanmar face significant healthcare access challenges, particularly fragmented health systems, political instability, and socioeconomic hardship. As a result, febrile individuals often rely on self-medication in the early stages of illness, delaying formal diagnosis and treatment [40]. Such delays exacerbate individual severity and local transmission while elevating transboundary spread risks, posing substantial threats to adjacent Chinese territories. Thus, Chinese border communities with significant cross-border interaction represent important focal points for surveillance, early warning, and vector control interventions. Coordinated cross-border health initiatives and targeted resource allocation in these sentinel regions would be essential.

Environment-exposed occupations and frequent travel to Southeast Asia were found to account for the majority of dengue cases, likely via increased mosquito contact in poorly protected settings and exposure to imported virus strains [39]. These occupations often involve prolonged presence in poorly ventilated or unhygienic surroundings, frequent contact with stagnant water containers, or organic waste in conditions conducive to Aedes mosquito breeding [41, 42]. In particular, farmers, sanitation personnel, food handlers, hairdressers, and recyclers often operate in areas with more standing water and limited vector control, and unemployed workers, house workers and retirees may experience sustained exposure in inadequately protected domestic settings [43]. Therefore, in order to cut off the transmission route by vector, it is necessary to eliminate the breeding site of mosquitoes through treating water-holding pots, renovating urban sewer system, turning over and emptying tanks [17].

The geographic expansion of DENV is partly attributable to increasing human mobility. Individuals with frequent cross-regional travel or sustained exposure to transient populations represent a high-risk demographic for imported dengue transmission, necessitating targeted interventions [8]. Global interconnectedness of mobility networks has amplified DENV infection risk, evidenced by a 30.7% dengue positivity rate among Southeast Asian expatriates, escalating with prolonged residency in endemic area [44]. Moreover, socioeconomic status also plays a significant role in dengue vulnerability. Individuals with higher education levels, typically more health-literate and adherent to protective measures, showed lower seropositivity, which was consistent with observations in Malaysia linking socioeconomic status to dengue risk [33]. Integrating dengue prevention into public education curricula and literacy programs could empower broader adoption of protective behaviors among the general population, as predicted by the knowledge-attitudes-practices (KAP) framework [25]. In the absence of approved antivirals or universally accessible vaccines for dengue, community-based vector control programs and targeted awareness campaigns emerge as the potential for sustainable dengue reduction.

The main strength of this study is its population representative design. By conducting a large-scale community-based serosurvey, we largely reduce the selection bias due to presence versus absence of symptoms, availability of testing materials, local or regional testing strategies, visit rate or other factors. Specifically, through recruitment in diverse residential areas, blood specimens were obtained directly from a representative cross-section of the general population in Ruili, ensuring that seroprevalence estimates accurately reflected the true dengue exposure status during the study period. In contrast, other serosurveys utilizing convenience samples composed of blood donors, clinic attendees, or health screening participants, tend to be more health-conscious, of higher socioeconomic status, or have specific behavioral traits that limit their generalization [45, 46]. While operationally advantageous by circumventing community-based venipuncture, relying on such sampling methodology may risk underestimating or misrepresenting the population-level seroprevalence in the broader community due to inherent selection bias and compromised representativeness.

Nonetheless, several important limitations should be acknowledged. Firstly, IgG-based ELISA, while effective for assessing cumulative DENV exposure, lacks serotype specificity and may exhibit cross-reactivity with other flaviviruses because of similar antigenic or overlapping cut-off points, including Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV) and Zika virus (ZIKV) [47, 48]. Prior JEV infection or vaccination could potentially contribute to overestimation of DENV seroprevalence, but available surveys suggest relatively low activity of JE in Yunnan since the introduction of JE vaccine into the routine Expanded Program on Immunization in 2007, administered respectively to children at 8 months and 2 years of age [49, 50]. Secondly, self-reported diagnostic histories are susceptible to recall bias and case misclassification, particularly in regions with co-circulating febrile diseases and fragmented healthcare access. To mitigate this, structured interviews were conducted with contextual memory prompts, including diagnostic timelines and healthcare facility verification. Thirdly, the asymptomatic category in our study encompassed mild or non-specific presentations not prompting medical attention, potentially lowering the S:A ratio by capturing broader subclinical cases. Moreover, the epidemiological context and interventions in Ruili may have changed since 2015. In particular, stringent border restrictions and intensified measures targeting Coronavirus Disease 2019 (COVID-19) during 2020–2022 likely altered human mobility, vector control, and dengue transmission dynamics. While this dataset remains the only population-level serological resource from this area over the past decade and provides valuable baseline parameters for arboviral risk mapping, it did not reflect subsequent shifts and trends. Ongoing and updated serological investigations are therefore essential to capture current transmission patterns.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the community-based serosurvey revealed low but focal DENV exposure in Ruili, with risk stratification demonstrating age-dependent acquisition, occupational hazards regarding vector-exposed environments, and border-proximity gradients, while higher educational attainment exhibited protective effects. These findings justify targeted interventions, enhanced surveillance, and cross-border collaboration to mitigate dengue transmission in this region. Future longitudinal studies integrating serial cross-sectional analyses of seroprevalence are warranted to disentangle complex transmission dynamics and the true incidence of dengue.

Cost effectiveness and pricing strategy of first line benmelstobart combination treatment for extensive stage small cell lung cancer in China
Authors: Shan Zhao, Shuo Kang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The current analysis aimed to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of benmelstobart plus anlotinib and chemotherapy for patients with ES-SCLC from the Chinese health-care system perspective. A mathematical decision model that simulated patients’ 3-week transition in 20-year time horizon was conducted to evaluate the economic value. Survival and safety data were gathered from ETER701 trial, cost and utility values were obtained from the local charges and previously published studies. Sensitivity and subgroup analyses were performed to examine the robustness of the model results and to support the health-decision making. For intention-to-treat (ITT) patients, benmelstobart plus anlotinib and carboplatin/etoposide could bring additional 0.60 and 0.71 QALYs with marginal cost of $91,424.86 and $98,504.86 compared with anlotinib plus carboplatin/etoposide and carboplatin plus etoposide, respectively, resulting in an incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) were $153,444.29/QALY and $138,272.39/QALY, respectively, which were higher than the Chinese willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold. Sensitivity analyses and subgroup analyses confirmed the robustness of the model results when parameters changed. Benmelstobart plus anlotinib and carboplatin/etoposide was unlikely to be the cost-effective first-line strategy compared with anlotinib plus carboplatin/etoposide and carboplatin plus etoposide for ES-SCLC patients in China. Reducing the price of benmelstobart could increase its cost-effectiveness.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-20649-8.

Introduction

Lung cancer is one of the leading causes of non-communicable disease burden revealed by Global Burden of Disease Study around the world1. Based on the histological type, lung cancer can be divided into non-small-cell lung-cancer (NSCLC) and small-cell lung cancer (SCLC). Approximately 15% of lung cancers cases are SCLC, which with the poor prognosis and nearly two-thirds of all SCLC cases present at extensive-stage SCLC (ES-SCLC) at the time of diagnosis, and the five-year overall survival (OS) rate is limited only 1%-5%2–6. Although the standard first-line treatment for patients with ES-SCLC, platinum-based plus etoposide showed good initial results, most patients relapse rapidly and resulting the limited median overall survival was only 10 months7–9. The clinical benefits of ES-SCLC were unmet yet.

Recently, the immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) which could reactive the anti-cancer function of T cells that in tumor microenvironment by inhibiting the programmed cell death (PD-1) and programmed cell death 1 ligand 1 (PD-L1) pathway demonstrated the potential to prolong median survival, and several randomized, controlled phase 3 studies have reported the addition of such as atezolizumab, durvalumab, serplulimab, adebrelimab, and tislelizumab to platinum-based plus etoposide chemotherapy could improve the clinical benefits and the quality of life of patients with ES-SCLC10–13. Benmelstobart is a humanized anti-PD-L1 monoclonal antibody. In the recent phase Ⅲ ETER701 trial, benmelstobart plus anlotinib and carboplatin/etoposide showed the acceptable toxicity and could significantly reduce the risk of death or disease progression by 68% (hazard ratio (HR) 0.32, 95% confidence interval 0.26–0.41 p < 0.0001) and the risk of death by 39% (HR 0.61, 95% confidence interval 0.47–0.79 p = 0.0002) compared with carboplatin plus etoposide for patients with previously untreated ES-SCLC, the study demonstrated that anlotinib plus carboplatin/etoposide could improve clinical benefit by reduced the risk of death or disease progression by 56% (hazard ratio (HR) 0.44, 95% confidence interval 0.36–0.55 p < 0.0001) compared with carboplatin plus etoposide likewise14.

Despite benmelstobart plus anlotinib and carboplatin/etoposide demonstrated clinical benefits for patients with ES-SCLC, the widespread use of the regimen might bring the impact of the healthcare costs. Guided by pharmacoeconomic principles, Chinese government has adopted a range of policy interventions aimed at lowering drug prices and optimizing the efficiency of resource allocation within the healthcare system, so we need pay more attention to “economic toxicity” of the high-value innovative drugs especially in resource-limited countries such as China15. The cost-effectiveness analysis was the important method to solve the above issue. And the cost-effectiveness analysis results constitute essential evidence for the Chinese government’s decisions whether regarding innovative drug inclusion in the national reimbursement drug list. This current study aimed to evaluate the cost-effectiveness benefit of benmelstobart combination therapy as first-line treatment for ES-SCLC in China based on the mathematical model from the Chinese health-care system perspective.

Methods and materials

Analytical overview and model structure

A mathematical decision model combined with decision-tree process and partitioned survival model was conducted to evaluate the health and economic value of benmelstobart plus anlotinib and carboplatin/etoposide compared with anlotinib plus carboplatin/etoposide and carboplatin/etoposide alone for patients with ES-SCLC in China. The decision-tree defined the decision process of the three competing strategies among benmelstobart plus anlotinib and carboplatin/etoposide, anlotinib plus carboplatin/etoposide, and carboplatin/etoposide alone (Fig. 1A), the partitioned survival model (Fig. 1B) was established to simulate patients transition every three weeks in 20-year time horizon to evaluate the health and economic outcomes of the three competing first-line therapies. The partitioned survival model included three mutually exclusive health states among progression-free survival (PFS), progressed disease (PD), and Death. All patients were at the PFS state when entered the model, and patients could not return to the previous health state.

Total costs, life-years (LYs), and quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs) were model outputs metrics. Costs and utility values were discounted at an annual rate of 5% based on the guidelines16, all costs were converted to 2023 US dollars (US $1 = CNY 7.047). Incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) were calculated to judge the cost-effective of the competing first-line treatment based on the formula: ICER = (C2 − C1)/(E2 − E1), and the willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold was set as three times of per capita gross domestic product (GDP) of China in 2023 ($38,041 per QALY gained)17–19. All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. The experimental protocols of the ETER701 trial were approved by the licensing committee, and the informed consent of the cohort patients have been obtained in the ETER701 trial.

Clinical data

Clinical survival and safety data were gathered from the ETER701 trial. Long-term survival data beyond the follow-up period were gathered by refitted the survival curves due to the limited follow-up time for clinical trial. First, we reconstructed the patients individual time-to-event date by using the standard algorithm developed by Guyot et al.20. Second, we used the individual time-to-event data to fit the parametric survival models among Exponential, Gamma, Weibull, Log-logistic, Log-normal, Gompertz, Royston/Parmar spline model, Mixed cure model, and non-Mixed cure model21–23. Third, we selected the optimal model based on the Akaike information criterion (AIC) values, Bayesian information criterion (BIC) values, clinical diagnosis, treatment practice, and visual inspection between the fitting curves and original Kaplan–Meier curves reported in the ETER701 trial. Finally, the parametric survival models were calculated based on the survival parameters of each distribution form. The AIC and BIC values of the fitting curves were shown in Supplementary Table S1. We equally accessed the potential economic benefits of the three competing therapies in subgroup population to achieve the precise decision making based on the reported specific HRs of subgroup populations in the ETER701 trial. After the disease progressed, patients would enter to the PD health state, and patients would discontinue the current treatment and receive the standard second-line treatment of topotecan, the proportion of patients received second-line treatment in the three competing groups were obtained from the ETER701 trial.

Cost and utility values

Only direct medial costs were involved in the current cost-effectiveness analysis because of the study was conducted from the perspective of the Chinese health-care system. Costs parameters included the cost of anti-cancer treatments (first-line and subsequence treatment), routine follow-up, supportive care, terminal care of end-of-life, management of treatment-related serious adverse events (SAEs, grade ≥ 3), all costs related to SAEs were assumed to incur in the first cycle, and we tested the incidence rates and costs of SAEs in the sensitivity analyses. A typical patient with the weight of 65 kg and height of 1.64 m, resulting in the body surface area (BSA) of 1.72 m2 was used to calculate the dosage of the regimens24. Cost values were gathered from the local bid-winning price or published literature.

The health utility values which represented the satisfaction level of the health state on a scale of 0 to 1 were assigned to the health states of PFS, PD, and Death. And the utility values of PFS, PD, and Death were 0.804, 0.473, and 0, respectively, in the current analysis, we also considered the disutility due to the SAEs in the model. Sensitivity analyses

Sensitivity analyses among one-way sensitivity analyses and probabilistic sensitivity analyses (PSA) were conducted to examine the robustness of model results. For one-way sensitivity analyses, parameters have been changed one-by-one based on their lower and upper bound (95% confidence intervals or ± 25% of the base-case values when 95% confidence intervals were not available) to confirm which parameter main driving factors of the model result. The results of the one-way sensitivity analyses were presented by Tornado diagram. Parameters usually changed simultaneously, and for PSA, 1000 replicated Monte Carlo simulations were conducted by repeatedly sampling the model input parameters based on their ranges and the specific distribution form. Based on the ISPOR-SMDM Modeling Good Research Practices Task Force report on model parameter estimation and uncertainty, Log-normal distribution was used for HR values, Gamma distribution was used for costs values, Beta distribution was used for probabilities, utility and disutility values, incidence rates, and proportion values36. The results of PSA were presented by the cost-effectiveness acceptability curves (CEACs).

Sensitivity-analyses results

Discussion

The satisfactory clinical results of adding benmelstobart, a fully humanized anti-PD-L1 monoclonal antibody, and anlotinib to the first-line chemotherapy for patients with ES-SCLC from ETER701 trial interested both oncologist and health-care decision makers. However, the cost-effective of the combination therapy which estimated whether the clinical benefits translated to the economic benefits based on the high price of benmelstobart was uncleared yet, the aim of the current study was to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of benmelstobart plus anlotinib and chemotherapy as the first-line treatment for patients with ES-SCLC based on the mathematical decision model from the Chinese health-care system perspective. For the base-case analysis, the ICERs of benmelstobart plus anlotinib and chemotherapy compared with anlotinib plus chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone were higher than the preset Chinese WTP threshold of $38,041/QALY. Sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of the model results. Subgroup analyses suggested that benmelstobart plus anlotinib and chemotherapy was unlikely to be the cost-effective first-line treatment for all subgroup patients.

Several clinical trials (IMpower133, CASPIAN, KEYNOTE-604) demonstrated that adding ICIs to first-line chemotherapy could bring clinical benefits included atezolizumab, durvalumab, and pembrolizumab10,12,37, and recent studies evaluated their cost-effective. Three studies showed that atezolizumab plus chemotherapy was unlikely to be cost-effective compared with chemotherapy alone in both China and United States (US)38–40; four literatures demonstrated that the ICERs of durvalumab plus chemotherapy compared with chemotherapy alone were $302,051/QALY, $230,142.9/QALY, $355,448.86/QALY, $464,711.90/QALY, respectively, which higher than the WTP threshold in both China and US41–44; two analyses performed that adding pembrolizumab to the first-line platinum-based plus etoposide chemotherapy was not cost-effective compared with chemotherapy due to the high price of pembrolizumab45,46. In general, ICIs plus chemotherapy was unlikely to be cost-effective either in developed countries such as US or developing country such as China as the first-line option for patients with ES-SCLC compared with chemotherapy alone, although the main drivers of these model results were not same. Compared the above studies of our analysis, we found that the price of ICIs was the main driver of the results, this is because the promising clinical efficacy of ICIs, which would prolong survival time and increase cumulative anti-cancer drug exposure, this might result in higher total direct medical costs, consequently impacting their cost-effectiveness. So, the pricing strategy which balanced encouraging innovation and allocating efficiency is crucial for the pharmaceutical market in China. At present, Chinese government adopted the way of national medical insurance negotiation with pharmaceutical companies by pharmacoeconomic evidence, reducing drug prices to improve their cost-effectiveness was an important aspect, and our analysis explored the effect of price reducing on the model results, which has important reference value for health-care decision makers.

Several limitations must be illustrated when the health-care decision making was based on the current analysis results. First, although the complex parametric survival model such as Royston/Parmar spline model and cure model were attempted to accurately extrapolate the long-term survival rate of PFS and OS beyond the follow-up periods of the ETER701 trial, it was an inevitable method limitation of the cost-effectiveness analysis of anti-cancer drugs, and although the AIC and BIC values of Mixed cure model was optimal of the OS curve of carboplatin plus etoposide, we did not use this model for fitting the survival curve after consulting with clinical experts because it was not match the actual clinical situation. Second, some cost inputs were gathered from the published literatures such as the cost of best supportive care per cycle, this might take the bias of the real-world situation, however, sensitivity analyses suggested that these parameters only had the minimal influence of the model results. Third, the utility values which reflected the health prefer that may be affected by ethnicity and religious were gathered from a foreign study, and the one-way sensitivity analyses demonstrated that the utility value of PFS was the main driver of the model outcomes, however, the results were robustness, it means that the ICERs would always higher than the WTP threshold when parameters changed. Fourth, only the cost of management SAEs (Grade ≥ 3) was considered in the model, and the cost of management of grade 1/2 adverse events were excluded from the model, although these cost parameters only had minimal influence of the model outcomes revealed by one-way sensitivity analyses. Finally, other potential competing first-line strategies for patients with ES-SCLC were not included in the model such as durvalumab plus chemotherapy due to the absence of head-to-head trial. Despite these limitations, we believe that the results of the current analysis model reflected the clinical and economic outcomes of the three competing therapies for patients with untreated ES-SCLC in Chinese context.

Conclusion

In conclusion, in 20-year time horizon, benmelstobart plus anlotinib and carboplatin/etoposide could not be considered the cost-effective first-line treatment compared with anlotinib plus carboplatin/etoposide and carboplatin plus etoposide for patients with ES-SCLC in China due to the unfavorable ICERs. When the price of benmelstobart reduced by 90%, benmelstobart plus anlotinib and carboplatin/etoposide could be considered the most cost-effective first-line treatment, and these findings were valuable for Chinese health-care decision makers.

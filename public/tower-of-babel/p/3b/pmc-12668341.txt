The effect of training based on the James Brown model on the quality of nursing care in the trauma department
Authors: Mohammad J. Hemati, Alice Khachian, Yousef Asadoola, Masoumeh Tahmasebi, Ahmad Mahdizadeh, Mohammad Abbasi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Continuous assessment and improvement of nursing care quality are essential components within healthcare provider systems. Therefore, the current study assessed the effect of training based on the James Brown model on the quality of nursing care provided in the trauma department.MATERIALS AND METHODS:The present study was a quasi-experimental investigation with a control group conducted from April to July 2023. The study sample consisted of 60 nurses working in the trauma department of the Imam Khomeini Medical Center located in Ilam, Iran. Following the selection of research participants through a census method, the samples were randomly allocated into two distinct groups: a control group of 30 individuals and an intervention group comprising 30 individuals. The nurses in the intervention group underwent a training program based on the James Brown model for four weeks. The training sessions were conducted twice a week, lasting for 60 minutes. For data collection in this study, two questionnaires were utilized: a demographic information questionnaire and a questionnaire to assess the “scale of perception of nursing activities that contribute to nursing care quality (EPAECQC).” The data analysis was conducted using SPSS version 23 software, with a significance level of less than 0.05 for the tests.RESULTS:Before the educational intervention, the nurses in both the control and intervention groups exhibited moderate average scores regarding the quality of care and nursing care. The nurses in the intervention group demonstrated significant improvements after the educational intervention. Specifically, there were substantial increases in average patient satisfaction scores (9.96 ± 1.24, P = 0.001), health promotion (10.27 ± 1.31, P = 0.001), prevention of complications (9.97 ± 1.52, P = 0.001), self-care and well-being (11.87 ± 2.65, P = 0.003), high liability and accuracy (20.07 ± 2.82, P = 0.001), as well as the overall quality of nursing care (80.53 ± 6.84, P = 0.001). These improvements were observed when comparing the post-intervention results to the pre-intervention and control groups. However, no statistically significant difference was observed in the average performance feedback score (12.26 ± 1.96, P = 0.107) and Nursing Care Organization (6.37 ± 1.27, P = 0.178).CONCLUSION:The findings of this study demonstrated the efficacy of the educational intervention based on the James Brown model in improving various aspects of nursing care and the overall quality of nursing care. This educational model may be taken into consideration in the planning of health interventions by health policy makers to promote the health status of the community.

Introduction

Trauma is recognized as one of the leading causes of death and disability in developed countries, but its importance is often overlooked in developing countries.[1] Each year, over five million individuals die due to trauma, representing approximately 10% of the total global fatalities.[2] Hence, among the elderly population, trauma ranks as one of the leading causes of mortality, following heart disease and cancer.[3] Based on the existing data in Iran, trauma emerges as the second leading factor contributing to mortality in the country while also representing the primary cause of significant loss of active years of life.[4] The effects of trauma on society encompasses substantial economic and social burdens.[1] While a proactive approach is crucial in addressing this issue, after the trauma, medical measures should primarily focus on preventing avoidable fatalities and minimizing disabilities.

Research has revealed that the implementation of accurate and principled care aligned with the latest global trauma standards strengthens treatment approaches and reduces the complications arising from long-term disabilities, as well as deaths caused by trauma.[5] Nurses who care for trauma patients are vital in achieving these goals. Given nurses’ role in managing trauma patients, ensuring their preparedness to deliver medical care holds immense importance. Anticipated outcomes involve a reduction in the negative consequences of trauma through the enhancement of nurses’ preparedness and the provision of improved services to patients affected by trauma. It has been reported that inadequate performance of nurses in the emergency department has a detrimental effect on delivering optimal care to trauma patients.[6] Insufficient readiness, limited knowledge, and the absence of essential skills are prominent factors attributing to the suboptimal performance of emergency nurses in delivering care to trauma patients. To effectively care for trauma patients, nurses should comprehensively understand their knowledge and capabilities and address existing limitations through appropriate training. By doing so, they can assume their optimal role in providing care for trauma patients.[7]

Various approaches exist for training nurses, including adopting training models as a viable method.[8] James Brown proposed a model offering an educational design framework suitable for regular implementation. In this comprehensive model, the teacher’s focus and engagement focus on understanding the inclusive nature of learners, encompassing their talents, interests, abilities, and related aspects. According to Professor James Brown, employing this systematic approach can foster profound and stable learning experiences for learners.[9] The James Brown model stands out among other educational models because it emphasizes placing learners at the center of the learning process. This approach recognizes that education relies on acknowledging and incorporating the unique attributes of students, such as their interests and abilities, as essential elements in the educational framework. The study conducted by Abedini et al.[10] revealed that implementing the educational intervention based on the James Brown model significantly improved students’ knowledge, attitude, and nutritional behavior. According to Hosseinzadeh et al.,[11] a significant statistical association was identified between the quality of nursing care and the successful completion of the training course. Furthermore, according to Fatehi et al.,[12] nursing managers are essential in establishing quality improvement teams and executing the evaluation procedure.

One of the advantages of the James Brown model is its application, systematic and close to the implementation model of the healthcare and treatment sector. Brown believes that teachers should choose an approach based on what students need to learn; for example, in the classical approach, students should learn the language to understand the classics. Moreover, when learning a language in a limited time, the grammar-translation approach is used. The direct approach is implemented for students who need to communicate using the target language, emphasizing speaking and listening skills. The audio-lingual approach is applied when students need reinforcement and behavioral modification, while teachers use stimulus, such as drills and pattern. And, the communicative approach focuses on students’ need to express themselves using inductive and deductive learning.[9]

Heidari et al.[1] found that nursing service managers should develop and execute training initiatives to address nurses’ areas of weakness, enhancing the overall quality of nursing care and elevating the safety standards for trauma patients. To the best of our knowledge, no existing study has been identified that explores the effect of nursing care quality when education is conducted using the James Brown educational model, despite its noted superiority over alternative educational models. Thus, the current study was conducted to evaluate the effect of education based on the James Brown model on the quality of nursing care delivered by nurses in the trauma department of the Imam Khomeini Medical Training Center in Ilam, Iran in 2023.

Material and Method

Study design and setting

The current study was a semi-experimental study that includes a control group. It was performed on nurses employed at the trauma department of the Imam Khomeini Medical Training Center in Ilam, Iran in 2023.

Study participants and sampling

Participants included 60 nurses who met the inclusion criteria. The inclusion criteria involved having a bachelor’s degree and at least one year of work experience in the hospital’s trauma department. The exclusion criteria encompassed missing more than two sessions out of eight training sessions and failing to complete at least 80% of the questionnaires. After selecting the samples, they were divided into the control (30 nurses) and the intervention group (30 nurses), using a random allocation method [Figure 1].

Data collection tool and technique

In this study, the demographic profile form and the scale of perception of nursing activities that contribute to “Perception of Nursing Activities that Contribute to the Quality of Care” (EPAECQC) used to collect data. The demographic information form included age, sex, marital status, education level, work experience, and type of employment. The EPAECQC questionnaire developed by Martins (2010), which assesses nurses’ perception of the quality of nursing care services. It comprising 25 questions, this questionnaire focuses on nurses’ comprehension of nursing care quality. It is categorized into seven dimensions: patient satisfaction (3 questions), health promotion (3 questions), complication prevention (3 questions), self-care and well-being (4 questions), performance feedback (4 questions), nursing organization (2 questions), and high responsibility and accuracy (6 questions). The scale used in the measuring tool employed a 4-point Likert scale, with respondents rating their responses on a scale ranging from 1 (never) to 4 (always). The scoring range of the tool spans from 25 to 100, where a higher score indicates a greater level of nursing care quality.[13] The content validity approach was employed to ensure the validity of the EPAECQC questionnaire. In the research conducted by Ghiyasvandian and Matourypour,[14] the EPAECQC questionnaire demonstrated substantial validity. Additionally, the reliability was assessed using the Kuder-Richardson Formula 20 (KR-20). In Martins’ study, the reliability of the EPAECQC questionnaire was evaluated, yielding a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.94.[13]

Upon the researcher’s arrival at the research site, comprehensive explanations were provided, followed by distributing the personal information questionnaire to the nurses. They were then requested to complete the questionnaire through an interactive question-and-answer (Q and A) approach. Before the intervention, participants from both the intervention and control groups were provided with the EPAECQC questionnaire and were asked to complete it. After making the necessary preparations to access the research units, the researcher visited the site during three shifts: morning, evening, and night. Once the intervention group participants completed the EPAECQC questionnaire, preparations were made to implement the intervention sessions. The intervention group participants underwent a 4-week training program based on the James Brown model. The program consisted of two sessions per week, each lasting 60 minutes. Based on the James Brown model, the training program took place in in-person sessions in the training classroom of Imam Khomeini Hospital in Ilam, Iran. Approximately 15 individuals participated in each session, dividing the 30-person intervention group into two 15-person subgroups. During four weeks, two training program sessions were conducted each week for 60 minutes for each subgroup. This amounted to 8 sessions for each subgroup throughout the intervention period. The meetings were conducted using various methods, including PowerPoint lectures, group discussions, videos, and pamphlets. Based on James Brown’s model, the educational program clearly and precisely explains each step. While employing this model, the researcher meticulously orchestrated the educational program’s intricate elements with a deliberate vision. James Brown’s model encompasses four distinct phases that address identifying and developing educational objectives. These stages are further categorized into three sections, namely general, partial, and behavioral objectives. Additionally, the model determines conditions, further divided into three sections: learning experiences, teaching and learning groups, and teaching methods. Furthermore, the model identifies human resources, educational materials and tools, and educational spaces. Finally, the model incorporates a two-fold evaluation process comprising a phase assessment and a final evaluation, alongside a plan modification component aimed at enhancing the efficacy of the educational program[15] [Table 1]. After completing the intervention sessions, participants from both the control and intervention groups were provided with the EPAECQC questionnaire and asked to complete it.

Data analysis

The data was analyzed using SPSS version 26 statistical software. To describe the data, frequency distribution, mean and standard deviation were used. To examine the effect of the educational model on the quality of nursing care, various statistical methods were employed, including paired t-tests, analysis of variance with repeated measurements (ANOVA), and analysis of covariance. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethical consideration

Ethical approval for the current study was obtained from the ethics committee of Iran University of Medical Sciences (IR.IUMS.REC.1402.129). Also All participating nurses were fully informed about the objectives of the study, confidentiality of information and voluntary participation and signed a written informed consent to participate in the study.

Results

Notably, no statistically significant difference in age was observed between the control and intervention groups (P = 0.111). Besides, the outcomes of the independent t-test revealed no statistically significant differences between the control and intervention groups concerning their work experience (P = 0.550), gender (P = 0.067), education level, employment type, and marital status. Discussion

The study’s findings indicated that the nurses participating in the research have had a moderate quality in various dimensions of nursing care and the overall quality of nursing care before the training. Furthermore, the findings showed no significant difference between the control and intervention groups regarding multiple dimensions of nursing care quality and the overall quality of nursing care before the training. Consistent with the findings of this study, Raesi et al.[16] (2021) similarly demonstrated in their research that nurses working in the COVID-19 ward exhibited a moderate level of nursing care quality. According to the findings of the present study, Motamedzadeh et al.[17] (2021) also reported that nurses employed in COVID and non-COVID departments demonstrated moderate nursing care quality. In contrast to the present study’s findings, the research conducted by Gaalan et al.[18] (2019) revealed a favorable level of nursing care quality. Similarly, Ebrahimi et al.[19] (2023) demonstrated in their research that nurses in the special care department of COVID-19 medical training centers in Arak City exhibit optimal nursing care quality. The variance in findings between these studies and the current one arises from the difference in the research community, the difference in the demographic parameters, the difference in work complexity, and the level of sensitivity within the studied sectors. Additionally, the differences in economic, cultural, and social perspectives among nurses across different cities and countries have been highlighted as contributing factors. Based on the current study’s findings, it was observed that nurses working in the trauma department perceive the quality of nursing care to be moderate. Consequently, healthcare systems in Iran must prioritize implementing and utilizing educational programs based on effective educational models to enhance the quality of nursing care among trauma department nurses.

The study revealed significant differences in average scores of various aspects of nursing care quality between nurses who underwent group training based on James Brown’s standard model and those who did not receive any training intervention. The scores were significantly higher among the nurses who received the training intervention. In the survey conducted by Ghazanfari et al. (2006), it was reported that implementing an educational program based on the James Brown model significantly improved both knowledge and attitude among patients with type 2 diabetes.[2021] Khodakarami et al.[22] (2011) conducted a similar study that revealed that the intervention group, following training utilizing the James Brown model, exhibited considerably higher average knowledge scores compared to the control group in areas such as sexual health, reproductive system anatomy and physiology, diverse aspects of sexual health, as well as awareness of AIDS and family planning. Besides, some researchers have indicated that educational interventions have significantly increased nurses’ self-assurance and favorable practical conduct. Consequently, this has enhanced both nursing performance and the overall quality of care nurses provide.[2324] Hence, in the present study, the educational intervention based on the James Brown model has improved the quality of diverse nursing care among the intervention group nurses. This improvement can be attributed to the intervention’s effect on increasing knowledge, developing positive attitudes, enhancing self-confidence, and promoting positive practical behaviors. When interpreting the findings of this study, it can be concluded that implementing the educational intervention based on the James Brown model positively affects the knowledge, skills, competencies, experiences, and educational requirements of nurses regarding trauma management. As a result, this intervention played a pivotal role in enhancing the quality of nursing care across various dimensions for the participating nurses.

The findings of this study indicated a notable rise in patient satisfaction, health promotion, prevention of complications, self-care, and well-being, and high levels of responsibility and accuracy in the intervention group. Moreover, the overall quality of nursing care significantly improved due to the implemented educational intervention. According to Kunaviktikul et al. (2001), the concept of nursing care quality encompasses the fulfillment of both physical and psychological requirements through professional care, psychosocial support, and the achievement of comprehensive patient satisfaction through a holistic approach.[25] Since 2011, in Iran, the Ministry of Health and Medicine has been actively enforcing a mandate requiring hospitals to regularly assess the patient satisfaction index and implement educational interventions to enhance overall patient satisfaction. This initiative aligns with the ministry’s core objective and mission.[2627] Based on the findings of this study, it was evident that before the educational intervention, both the control and intervention groups of nurses exhibited a low level of patient satisfaction. Following the implementation of the educational intervention based on the James Brown model, there was a significant increase in the average score of patient satisfaction among nurses in the intervention group.

In the current study, it was observed that before the educational intervention, the nurses had a relatively low average health promotion score. However, following the intervention, there was a significant increase in the health promotion score. Consistent with the findings of the present study, McElligott et al.[28] (2010) conducted a study that showed the significant effect of training nurses using the collaborative care model on improving the dimension of health promotion among nurses across different hospital departments. Hence, based on the findings of this study that highlighted a significant increase in the dimension of health promotion through the implementation of educational intervention, it is recommended to utilize educational interventions based on the James Brown model to improve the quality of nursing care.

Among the limited studies conducted on the nurses of trauma units, researchers have employed presentations and discussions as a means to deliver theoretical sessions, while utilizing videos to facilitate practical sessions.[29] The findings of these researchers’ study demonstrated that the implementation of an educational program, including both theoretical and practical sessions, increases the knowledge and performance of nurses regarding the care of patients with head injuries.[3031] Abdul Aziz (2014) employed a combination of teaching methods, including lectures, PowerPoint presentations, group discussions for theoretical sessions, and videos for practical sessions, to implement the educational intervention in their study. The findings of this study indicate that the utilization of educational programs resulted in a significant improvement in the knowledge and skills of nurses regarding their oral care techniques.[32] In line with these studies, the present study employed a combination of teaching techniques, including lecture methods by PowerPoint presentations, group discussions, videos, and pamphlets, all based on the James Brown model, to facilitate the conducted sessions. To explain the findings of this study, it can be said that the improvement in the quality of nursing care among nurses stemmed from their acquisition of both theoretical and practical knowledge, which adequately equipped them to effectively handle responsibilities in the trauma department. In this regard, Safari et al.[33] (2004) conducted a study that showed a significant improvement in the quality of nursing care among nurses employed in the specialized cardiac care unit after the implementation of training sessions based on group discussion.

Limitation and recommendation

This study has some limitations, including the implementation of two training courses for groups of 15 in the intervention group, which may cause different training. It has been tried as much as possible that this issue did not happen by the trainers. The small number of samples in this study may be one of the limitations that affect the results of the study, because in studies evaluating educational interventions, due to the high heterogeneity of the people included in each of the groups, more samples are needed, which can lead to more appropriate results.

It is suggested to use a larger sample size than this study for future studies. It is also suggested to implement this training program by nurses in other departments of the hospital or in certain departments of the hospital and compare the results in the intervention and control groups. It is also suggested that the results of this model be compared with other models that can be used for the study field, in other words, other educational models should be used in the control group. Because there are many educational models, by using this way of comparison, it is possible to prioritize the models based on the ease of implementation, and the lack of need for much expertise. In addition, in future research, specific and more uniform age groups, a gender group, and people with diseases of similar severity can be used to draw more appropriate conclusions.

Conclusion

The research findings revealed a significant improvement in the quality of nursing care among nurses in the trauma department as a result of the educational program implemented based on the James Brown model. This educational model may be taken into consideration in the planning of health interventions by health policy makers to promote the health status of the community.

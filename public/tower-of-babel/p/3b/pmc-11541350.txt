Potential reevaluation needed for the meta-analysis of high-pressure recruitment maneuvers in the 2023 ESICM guidelines on acute respiratory distress syndrome
Authors: Siyao Zeng, Yue Li, Zhipeng Yao, Junbo Zheng, Hongliang Wang
Published: 2024

The latest guidelines for acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) proposed by the European Society of Intensive Care Medicine (ESICM) have been published a year ago. We appreciate the outstanding contributions made by the experts in the guidelines. We would like to offer the following insights regarding the prolonged high-pressure recruitment maneuvers (RMs) and brief high-pressure RMs mentioned in the guidelines [1].

First, after reviewing the meta-analysis on prolonged and brief high-pressure RMs in the supplementary materials of the guidelines, we found that according to Kacmarek et al., their article might be categorized under prolonged high-pressure RMs [1, 2]. Prolonged high-pressure RMs is defined as maintaining airway pressure above 35 cmH2O for over 1 min [1]. However, a further review of other literature and guidelines suggests that this classification may be unfounded. No clinical or animal studies indicate a significant difference between prolonged and brief high-pressure RMs in ARDS, especially using 1 min as the cutoff. Strictly distinguishing them may be meaningless, as they essentially both belong to open lung ventilation (OLV) [3]. We believe it may be more reasonable to consider them collectively when analyzing the impact of OLV on ARDS patients.

Second, the meta-analysis in the supplementary materials of the ESICM guidelines may have some limitations. The meta-analysis only searched the PubMed database and may have overlooked relevant studies, such as those by Amato et al. [4], Constantin et al. [5], Khan et al. [6] and Yang et al. [7]. The results from these omitted studies could significantly affect the meta-analysis outcomes. In addition, upon careful review of the studies included in the meta-analysis, we found that outcomes such as mortality at 6 months, incidence of arrhythmia, incidence of desaturation, the rates of rescue therapies (such as rates requiring prone position and rates requiring inhalation of nitric oxide) and oxygenation index on days 1, 3, and 7 can be evaluated. Furthermore, the outcomes in the articles by Hodgson et al. [8], Kung et al. [9] and Hodgson et al. [10]—which include length of hospital stay, length of stay in the intensive care unit (ICU), ventilation duration, and ventilator-free days to day 28—were statistically presented using median values and interquartile ranges (IQR). The formulas provided in the articles by Luo et al. and Wan et al. could be utilized to convert these data into mean values and standard deviations (SD) for inclusion in subsequent meta-analyses [11, 12]. Evaluating these additional outcomes could provide a more comprehensive analysis of the impact of OLV on ARDS patients.

In conclusion, if possible, we recommend updating the meta-analysis of high-pressure RMs in the supplementary materials of the ESICM 2023 ARDS guidelines to address the above limitations. This could provide evidence on the impact of OLV on ARDS to guide clinical practice.

Mass Spectrometric Evidence for the Under-represented RNA–Protein Cross-Links
Authors: Jong Woo Bae
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn protein–RNA complexes, UVC cross-links proteins predominantly to uracil and other nucleobases to a smaller extent. The cross-linked RNA moieties can be simplified via hydrofluoride digestion and identified via tandem mass spectrometry. While uridine or uracil cross-links have been successfully characterized, concrete evidence for other UVC-cross-linked RNA bases is lacking. Here, I elucidate mass spectrometric signatures of UVC-cross-linked, hydrofluoride-digested guanine, cytidine, and methyladenine. This information can help us capture the native protein–RNA interactions in more detail.

Introduction

RNA-binding sites (RBSs) are amino acids in RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) that make direct contacts with RNA. Through bottom-up mass spectrometric analysis, UV-cross-linked and hydrofluoride-treated RNA moieties can pinpoint the exact RBSs on peptides.1,2 Previous studies showed that UVC irradiation dominantly cross-links uracil to proteins,1,3−11 while other nonuracil cross-links were also identified from in vitro reconstituted ribonucleoprotein complexes treated with nucleases to digest the UVC-cross-linked RNA.7−11 However, finding solid evidence for low-abundance nonuracil RNA adducts from proteome-wide RBS data sets with hydrofluoride-treated RNA moieties has remained challenging.1 The main reason was that RNA-specific mass spectrometric features such as ribose fragmentation that could assist solid identification of low-abundance RNA adducts were incompletely understood; even more, any software to discover novel RNA adducts based on known mass spectrometric features was lacking. In addition, the potential RNA adduct masses were analyzed as a whole, even though different RBPs and domains have distinct RNA base preferences. To address the limitations, here I sought to identify the under-represented UVC-cross-linked RNA moieties using a previous RBS-mapping data set1,12 via two different approaches.

Materials and Methods

The mass spectrometry data and the structural data have been retrieved from the PRIDE repository (PXD016254) or the RCSB PDB database (1CVJ, 2N82, and 2N3O), respectively.

Four .raw files (UVC, RBS-ID) in PXD016254 data set were searched against the Swiss-Prot human proteome database (2022.10.11.) using FragPipe (v18.0) with the parameters modified from the default Open.workflow file (Open_RBSID.workflow) as follows: Precursor mass tolerance: −50–400 Da, Protein Digestion: Trypsin, Mine for diagnostic ions and fragments: On, Min. peptide ions per MS1 delta mass peak: 10. The global.diagmine.tsv file was looked up for signatures of ribose loss from b/y ions. The four psm.tsv files were merged and looked up for precursor modifications on peptides from the PABPC1 RRM1–2, RBFOX2 RRM, or PTBP1 RRM1 domains. For nominal modification masses 149/281, 283, or 243, PSMs with unique localizations were taken to identify RBSs in PABPC1 RRM1–2, RBFOX2 RRM, or PTBP1 RRM1, respectively. The RNA–protein complex structures were visualized from .pdb files (1CVJ, 2N82, and 2N3O) using PyMOL (v.1.7.2.1.). Also, the number of identified RBSs and proteins with respective modifications proteomewide was counted, with RBP defined as UniProt accessions with GO:0003723 (RNA binding) annotation (2022.10.28.).

FragPipe workflows, analysis results, protein sequence/annotation resources, and the custom Python codes used in this study have been deposited to the GitHub repository (https://github.com/jwbaebio/RBS-ID-mining).

Results and Discussion

First, I exploited previous observations that neutral loss of ribose occurs from UV-cross-linked RNA nucleosides.1,2 Adding onto the discovery of numerous RNA adduct types via MSFragger open search,1,2,13,14 I utilized a more recently developed diagnostic ion mining tool14−18 for the discovery of novel UVC-cross-linked RNA moieties. Following the inspection of fragment ions (b and y) for ribose loss, I unraveled two additional UVC-cross-linked RNA moieties (Table 1). One RNA adduct mass corresponded to guanosine with ammonia loss, while the other could be either cytosine with ammonia loss or uridine with water loss. Both guanosine and cytosine cross-links have been also observed as nuclease-digested RNA adducts.3−11

In addition, I looked into RNA-binding domains (RBDs) with well-defined RNA sequence specificity. I examined the precursor mass shifts from peptides located within PABPC1 RRM1–2 (poly(A)-binding),19−22 RBFOX2 RRM (G-cross-linking),23,24 and PTBP1 RRM1 (polypyrimidine-binding).25−27 Most unexpectedly, methyladenine/adenosine cross-links,28 but not adenine/adenosine cross-links, were detected on H144 of the PAPBC1 RRM2 domain (Figure 1a). The absence of adenine/adenosine cross-links may be due to inefficient UVC cross-linking, full cleavage by hydrofluoride, or extreme lability leading to the complete loss of the adducts from the peptide ions by in-source fragmentation before MS1. Likewise, the methyladenine cross-links may originate from either partial cleavage of ribose from purines by hydrofluoride1 or in-source fragmentation of the glycosidic bond between the ribose and nucleobase.2 The identification of the methyladenine/adenosine adduct on PABPC1 RRM2 suggests that the domain may bind to the poly(A) tail-adjacent 3′-UTR segment with methyladenine modification or the bound poly(A) tail harbors methyladenines. Meanwhile, guanosine cross-links were identified on F154/V155 of RBFOX2 RRM (Figure 1b), and cytosine cross-links on H133 of PTBP1 RRM1 (Figure 1c). All identified RBSs (except V155, which may have been catalogued owing to in-solution dynamic fluctuation, mislocalization during open search, or electron hopping during UVC-cross-linking9) were proximal to adenines, guanine, or cytosine in previously reported ribonucleoprotein structures,27−31 respectively.

Conclusions

To explore the diverse types of protein–RNA interactions, the RNA adducts discovered here may be incorporated in search parameters for UVC-cross-linked RBS identification. Specifically, precursor mass offset search including the neutral loss of ribose and the respective diagnostic ions can serve as an accurate and computationally efficient approach.13,15 Alternatively, the RNA adducts can be incorporated as variable modifications in closed searches. However, this will be at the cost of search space inflation, which may decrease the overall RBS identification rate.1,33,34 That is, when controlling the false discovery rate of peptide identifications via the target-decoy approach, the score distribution of decoy-spectrum matches is skewed, precluding identification of target-spectrum matches with relatively lower scores.

Additional databases and search strategies can be taken up to further expand the repertoire of UVC-cross-linked RNA adducts. For instance, RBS data sets that utilized nucleases for peptide-cross-linked RNA digestion can be looked up for dinucleotide adducts without uracil.3−11 Here, mass spectrometric signatures of RNA adducts can support more accurate identification of the novel RNA cross-links. These include the isobaric adduct forms of nucleosides and nucleobases2 or the coupled diagnostic ions such as the ribose cleaved from nucleosides.15 Also, mining RBS data sets generated directly from in vitro reconstituted ribonucleoprotein complexes would benefit from a direct structural validation.7−11

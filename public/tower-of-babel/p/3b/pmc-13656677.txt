Scoping review of physical activity and sedentary behavior in Brazilian national surveys: a protocol study
Authors: Rafael Martins da Costa, Camilo Luis Monteiro Lourenço, Shara da Silva Costa, Enry Eduardo de Souza Figueiredo, Sabrina Souza Barros, Daniel Umpierre
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study presents a scoping review protocol aimed at analyzing the methodological and structural characteristics of studies on physical activity (PA) and sedentary behavior (SB) that use the databases of the Brazilian National Health Survey (PNS), the Brazilian National School Health Survey (PeNSE), and the Brazilian Surveillance of Risk and Protective Factors for Chronic Diseases by Telephone Survey (Vigitel). PA, associated with multisectoral health benefits, and SB, linked to deleterious effects, have been widely investigated as part of public policies in Brazil. However, the absence of a centralized repository limits the efficiency of using these data, resulting in redundant publications with limited impact.MethodsThe review will be conducted according to the guidelines and checklists of the Joanna Briggs Institute and PRISMA-ScR, covering studies of any population, operating with broad definitions of PA and SB. In addition, it will seek to identify methodological discrepancies and promote the integration of evidence in an open-access online platform. The sources include four scientific databases (LILACS, PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science).DiscussionThis protocol emphasizes the relevance of open science, aligning with UNESCO recommendations to increase transparency and scientific collaboration. The results are expected to guide evidence-based policies and strengthen public health practices. The study is a strategic response to the fragmentation of the literature, proposing to consolidate existing data, optimize its reuse, and foster interdisciplinarity in the field of public health.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-026-03245-3.

Introduction

Promoting regular physical activity (PA) and an active lifestyle is aligned with the Global Action Plan for Physical Activity 2018–2030 [1] and contributes to the achievement of eight of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals of the United Nations [2]. The practice of PA is associated with the reduction of mortality from all causes [3], in addition to bringing benefits to physical, psychological, social, and cognitive health in different age groups [4–8]. It also reduces the costs of procedures, medications, and hospitalizations in the health system [4, 9], reinforcing its relevance as an essential element in public policies. In this context, PA and sedentary behavior (SB) have been incorporated into the evaluation and monitoring systems of public policies in several countries, including Brazil [10].

National surveys conducted by the Brazilian Ministry of Health, such as the Brazilian Surveillance System for Risk and Protection Factors for Chronic Diseases by Telephone Survey (Vigitel), the Brazilian National School-Based Health Survey (PeNSE), and the Brazilian National Health Survey (PNS), have provided information on PA and SB, allowing for the monitoring of their prevalence and impact on health. Although many studies are conducted using data from these surveys, there is no centralized public repository that presents published studies on PA and SB. This gap, combined with the dynamics of the academic productivist system, often results in redundant publications or publications with similar methodological approaches but with limited impact [11]. For instance, Lopes et al. [12] conducted a study using the PNS to verify the relationships between the domains of PA and depressive symptoms in the Brazilian population, testing moderation by age groups. The authors investigated depressive symptoms continuously and used generalized additive models. The following year, Loch et al. [13] published a study with a similar proposal, exploring the association of PA in the four domains and the prevalence of depressive symptoms in the Brazilian population, moderating by sex, education, and self-reported diagnosis of depression. The analytical change was in the way the outcome variable, depressive symptoms (dichotomous: with or without depressive symptoms), was treated and in the statistical procedure used (binary logistic regression). It should be noted that we are not criticizing the authors of certain articles, but we emphasize that the creation of a centralized repository could optimize the use of these data, avoiding overlaps (at any level), fostering scientific collaborations, and improving the impact of the evidence generated.

Furthermore, another urgent issue in Brazilian scientific literature is the diversification of scientific products with the aim of greater public dissemination and reach. The United Nations Educational, Scientific, and Cultural Organization (UNESCO) established its Recommendation on Open Science, which defined open science “as an inclusive construct that combines various movements and practices aimed at making multilingual scientific knowledge openly available, accessible, and reusable for all, to increase scientific collaborations and information sharing for the benefits of science and society, and to open the processes of creation, evaluation, and communication of scientific knowledge to social actors beyond the traditional scientific community” (p. 7) [14]. In this sense, the proposal to create a centralized repository aims to optimize the integration and use of existing data, enhancing scientific collaboration and impact.

To date, we are unaware of published or ongoing evidence synthesis that systematically maps how Brazilian national health surveys have been used to investigate physical activity and sedentary behavior. In preliminary searches using the Virtual Health Library and MEDLINE/PubMed with terms related to the PNS, the PeNSE, and the Vigitel, we did not identify any review with this specific focus. Thus, the methodological and structural use of these key national databases in Brazilian research remains largely unmapped.

The present scoping review addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive overview of how studies have defined samples, age groups, and health conditions; how physical activity and sedentary behavior have been operationalized (e.g., domains, indicators, and questions); and which analytical strategies have been applied, including exposures, outcomes, mediators, and moderators. By explicitly identifying methodological similarities and discrepancies across studies using the same open datasets, this review seeks to make patterns of redundancy and fragmentation visible and to support a more strategic and impactful use of these surveys. In addition, by systematically documenting open science practices and developing an open-access online application that will centralize information on all included studies, this work offers an innovative, sustainable resource to guide future research and evidence-informed public policies in Brazil.

Thus, this scoping review aims to (I) analyze the methodological and structural characteristics of studies that investigated PA and/or SB based on the PNS, PeNSE, and/or Vigitel databases; (II) identify and analyze the methodological and structural discrepancies of the studies that investigated PA and/or SB based on the PNS, PeNSE, and/or Vigitel data; and (III) develop an open-access online application to consolidate and make available information from the studies included in the review. This approach aims to optimize the use of available data, promote scientific collaboration, and provide support for the formulation of evidence-based policies and practices.

Methods

A preliminary search was conducted in the Virtual Health Library and MEDLINE/PubMed, searching for “scoping review” combined with at least one of the terms related to PNS, PeNSE, or Vigitel. No current or ongoing scoping review was identified with the same topic.

This protocol was prepared and written according to the template and methodology of the Joanna Briggs Institute [15]. The scoping review report will be guided by the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) [16]. Considering the frequency of conducting the surveys of interest and the volume of publications generated, this scoping review will be updated every 4 years. The scoping review protocol is registered in the Open Science Framework (OSF) (https://osf.io/zexun/).

Eligibility criteria

The central research question of this scoping review is: What are the methodological and structural characteristics and discrepancies/similarities of studies that investigated PA and/or SB based on the PNS, PeNSE, and/or Vigitel databases? Based on this, we intend to identify and analyze the methodological and structural discrepancies of the studies that investigated PA and/or SB based on the PNS, PeNSE, and/or Vigitel data. As a product of this scoping review, an open-access online application will be developed to consolidate and make available information from the studies included in the review. To answer the central question, studies that meet criteria based on the Population, Concept, and Context (PCC) [15] elements, detailed below, will be included.

Population

Studies carried out with any population (i.e., children, adolescents, adults, and/or elderly people), regardless of sex and health conditions, will be considered eligible.

Concept

Studies that investigated PA and/or SB will be eligible. PA is understood as any movement produced by skeletal muscles that results in energy expenditure [17]. In this sense, all categories/domains of PA are eligible (i.e., leisure, commuting, work, at school), according to the questions adopted in each edition of the national surveys. SB is defined as any waking behavior characterized by an energy expenditure ≤ 1.5 metabolic equivalents (METs), while sitting, reclining, or lying down [18]. All operationalization of SB will be eligible for this scoping review (i.e., time in front of the TV, time sitting, using a smartphone), according to the questions adopted in each edition of the national surveys. All information on PA and/or SB reported by studies using data from PNS, PeNSE, and/or Vigitel will be recorded and presented in the final report of the scoping review.

Context

Primary studies that used the PNS, PeNSE, and/or Vigitel databases will be included. Articles in Portuguese, Spanish, and/or English will be included. The PNS is carried out by the Ministry of Health in partnership with the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE), and has already been carried out in two editions (2013 and 2019). The population surveyed by the PNS corresponds to residents of permanent private households in the national territory, except for those located in special census sectors (composed of subnormal clusters, barracks, military bases, lodgings, camps, vessels, boats, ships, indigenous villages, among others). This survey is part of the IBGE’s Integrated Household Survey System (SIPD) and, as such, is a subsample of the Brazilian National Household Sample Survey (PNAD). The main objective of the PNS is to generate nationwide data on the health situation and lifestyles of the country’s population, as well as on health care, concerning access to and use of services, preventive actions, continuity of care, and financing of assistance [19].

PeNSE is an intersectoral, population-based school survey conducted by IBGE in partnership with the Brazilian Ministry of Health and with the support of the Brazilian Ministry of Education. This survey has already been carried out five times (2009, 2012, 2015, 2019, and 2024) and has contributed essential information to the Brazilian Ministry of Health’s surveillance system of risk and protective factors for the health of schoolchildren. PeNSE provides updated data on the prevalence and distribution of these factors in the target audience, allowing for comprehensive and well-founded monitoring [20].

Vigitel is a national telephone survey conducted annually. It was implemented in 2006 and has been conducted 18 times. This survey is part of the Brazilian Ministry of Health’s Surveillance System for Risk Factors for Chronic Noncommunicable Diseases, enabling the monitoring of the frequency and distribution of the main determinants of these conditions in all capitals of the 26 Brazilian states and the Federal District. The sampling procedures adopted by Vigitel seek to obtain probabilistic samples of the adult population (≥ 18 years of age) living in households with at least one landline or, more recently, a mobile telephone line. In the first 14 editions (2006 to 2019), the minimum sample size of Vigitel was approximately 2000 participants, being limited to 1000 participants in the 2020 and 2021 editions and to 800 interviews in 2023 [21].

Information sources and search strategies

Searches will be conducted in four electronic databases (LILACS, PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science). Additional searches will include (I) consultation of the reference lists of the articles selected in the last stage (additional articles included will not have reference lists consulted); (II) personal library of the authors of this scoping review; and (III) consultation by e-mail to the corresponding author of each article included in the last stage about recently published or in preparation articles using data from the surveys of interest.

Keywords will be chosen to represent the PNS, PeNSE, and Vigitel surveys. Thus, the nomenclature adopted by official documents and those variations adopted by authors of original studies will be considered to search for each of the surveys. Supplementary Material 1 presents the draft search strategies for each electronic database (the final version will be provided in the review).

Study registration and screening procedures

Following the searches, all identified results will be collated and uploaded to Rayyan, and duplicates will be removed. Titles and abstracts will be screened by two independent reviewers for assessment against the inclusion criteria for the review. Following this, full-text documents will be assessed in detail against the inclusion criteria by two reviewers. The reasons for the exclusion of documents that do not meet the inclusion criteria at the full-text stage will be recorded and reported in the scoping review. Any disagreements that arise between the reviewers at each stage of the screening process will be resolved through discussion or with one or more additional reviewers. The results of the search and screening process will be reported in full in the final scoping review and presented in a PRISMA flow diagram [22, 23].

Data extraction and synthesis procedures

Data will be extracted from the documents included in the scoping review by two pairs of independent reviewers using a data extraction form created by the review team in Microsoft Excel. The extracted data will include specific details about the participants, concept, context, study methods, type of analysis, format of the PA and/or SB variables, and results relevant to answering the central question and other objectives of the review. Data sharing

The results of the scoping review will be written in a scientific article and submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal, preferably open access. The partial results of the scoping review may be presented at scientific events (e.g., congresses and symposia) in areas such as public health, open science, PA, and/or SB. From the publications, the intention is to create informative texts and visual summaries (i.e., infographics) and disseminate the results through digital scientific media platforms (e.g., ResearchGate) and social networks (e.g., Instagram, X) to maximize knowledge of the results and product generated.

Discussion

This protocol presents the justifications and methodological aspects for the scoping review whose main objective is to analyze methodological and structural characteristics of studies that investigated PA and/or SB based on the PNS, PeNSE, and/or Vigitel databases. Specifically, the scoping review will provide a systematic summary of studies using PA and/or SB data from the PNS, PeNSE, and/or Vigitel. The aim is to identify, for each of the surveys of interest, how the studies defined the samples and sample size, age group(s), sex, and health conditions; which definitions, operationalizations, domains, and questions were used to refer to PA and/or SB; and which survey and edition(s) were used.

The publication of redundant data is a global reality [24], which has raised discussions in several research areas [25, 26]. In this sense, two common problems refer to (1) the segmentation of results from a single study into two or more scientific articles (salami science) and (2) the publication of studies with considerably overlapping analyses and results (duplicate publications) [27, 28]. However, the intentionality may not apply to publications derived from surveys such as PNS, PeNSE, and Vigitel, which have open data. In this case, redundant publications may be related to the absence of a centralized public repository that brings together the maximum number of studies carried out with data from these surveys.

Therefore, this approach represents a strategic effort to overcome fragmentation and redundancy in scientific production on PA and SB using data from Brazilian national surveys. By consolidating and organizing the available information, we hope to optimize the reuse of these data, foster interdisciplinary scientific collaboration, and provide greater transparency and accessibility in line with the principles of open science. Additionally, the results of this review may support the formulation of public policies based on robust evidence, promoting more effective and inclusive health practices, and expanding the impact of research in the context of public health and beyond.

Prognostic value of baseline body composition parameters from 18F-FDG PET/CT in angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma patients
Authors: Huanyu Gong, Jingjie Shang, Jianyong Li, Chongyang Ding, Hao Xu, Lijun Tang, Yong Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This retrospective study investigated preliminarily the prognostic value of body composition parameters derived from baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT in angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (AITL) patients. We included 94 treatment-naïve AITL patients diagnosed by histopathology. Based on the axial CT images of the third lumbar vertebra, the areas of skeletal muscle (SM), visceral adipose tissue (VAT), and subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) were semi-automatically delineated and standardized to finally obtain SM index (SMI), VAT index (VATI), and SAT index (SATI). Body composition density characterized by CT attenuation was obtained, including SM density (SMD), VAT density (VATD), and SAT density (SATD). Besides, the maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV), and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) were also calculated. Endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). Survival curves and Cox regression analysis were performed‌‌.Of 94 AITL patients(mean age:63.4 ± 9.7 years,67% men), 65 progressed and 55 died[median follow-up: 27.5 (10.5–45.7) months].High baseline C-reactive protein level [hazard ratio (HR) = 2.47, 95%CI: 1.37–4.45, p = 0.003), low SMD (HR = 2.18, 95%CI:1.29–3.69, p = 0.003), high SATD (HR = 1.92, 95%CI: 1.05–3.54, p = 0.035), and low VATI (HR = 1.95, 95%CI: 1.12–3.38, p = 0.018) were independent risk factors for poor PFS. AITL score>2(HR = 2.90, 95%CI: 1.59–5.29, p < 0.001), sarcopenia(HR = 1.78, 95%CI: 1.01–3.12,p = 0.045), low VATI(HR = 2.24,95%CI: 1.27–3.95,p = 0.005), and high TMTV(HR = 2.46, 95%CI: 1.37–4.40, p = 0.003) may serve as independent risk factors for poor OS. In conclusion, in addition to conventional TMTV, body composition parameters derived from baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT may offer preliminary prognostic value for AITL patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06562-0.

Introduction

Angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (AITL) is the second most common pathological subtype of peripheral T-cell lymphoma, accounting for about 1%−2% of non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma. It usually occurs in the elderly (> 65 years) and has an aggressive course [1]. The main treatment method is chemotherapy based on anthracyclines. In general, the disease is prone to progression and has a poor prognosis, with a 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of 32%−44% [2, 3]. Prognostic Index for T-cell Lymphoma (PIT) and AITL score are common prognostic indices for AITL [2, 4]. The latter was established based on a large, independent AITL cohort and was considered to better reflect the clinical characteristics of AITL. We need to note that these prognostic models still have limitations in risk stratification and prognosis prediction with the emergence of novel assessment methods and drugs. Therefore, it is still important and urgent to explore biomarkers or models that are closely related to the prognosis and treatment response of AITL, so as to more accurately assess the risk of AITL patients [5].

Body composition parameters obtained based on CT images have gradually been proven to be closely related to the prognosis of malignancies [6, 7]. 18F-FDG PET/CT plays an important role in the diagnosis and therapeutic efficacy evaluation of cancers, especially lymphoma [8]. As a molecular imaging method, 18F-FDG PET/CT can not only obtain metabolic parameters reflecting tumor burden, such as total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), but also will facilitate to calculate the body composition parameters benefited its advantage of whole-body scanning [9]. Comparative studies have shown that the body composition parameters obtained from low-dose CT images from PET/CT are well consistent with those obtained from conventional dose CT images [10]. Therefore, the research on body composition parameters derived from 18F-FDG PET/CT in various cancers has been gradually increasing. Published studies have shown that sarcopenia, defined by the skeletal muscle index (SMI) calculated from CT images, was a prognostic factor for lymphoma, mainly for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) and Hodgkin lymphoma (HL) [11–15]. Visceral adipose tissue index (VATI) and subcutaneous adipose tissue index (SATI) obtained from CT slices may also have different prognostic values ​​in lymphoma [16, 17].

However, to our knowledge, there are few studies on body composition parameters in AITL patients, probably due to its low incidence. Consequently, the prognostic value of body composition in AITL is still unclear for the reason that the strong heterogeneity of lymphoma. The purpose of this study was to preliminarily explore the prognostic value of body composition parameters derived from 18F-FDG PET/CT in AITL patients.

Materials and methods

Patients

AITL patients from August 2007 to July 2021 in The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University were retrospectively included. Inclusion criteria:①confirmed by histopathology;②18F-FDG PET/CT examination was performed before any treatment. Exclusion criteria:①concurrent with other malignant tumors;②too poor image quality to be analyzed;③lost to follow-up. The specific inclusion and exclusion process was shown in Fig. 1. Clinical data were collected through the medical record information system, mainly including gender, age, height, weight, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG-PS) score, Ann Arbor stage, extranodal involvement, PIT, AITL score and laboratory data [including platelet count (PLT), C-reactive protein (CRP), white blood cell (WBC) count, β2-microglobulin(β2-MG), and bone marrow aspiration pathology]. This study complied with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. With the approval of our Institutional Review Board, informed consent was waived due to its retrospective nature.

18F-FDG PET/CT image acquisition protocol

18F-FDG was produced by the medical cyclotron (GE, USA) of our center and synthesized by an automated synthesis module, with a radiochemical purity of > 95%. All patients fasted for more than 6 h and kept fasting blood glucose levels ≤ 11.0mmol/L before injection. 18F-FDG was injected intravenously at 3.70-5.55MBq/kg. After injection, the patients rested quietly for 60 min, and images were collected by the Biograph 16HR PET/CT scanner (Siemens, Germany) after the bladder was emptied. CT scanning was performed first (tube voltage:120KV, tube current:80 mA, slice thickness:2.5 mm, reconstruction matrix: 512 × 512), followed by PET acquisition (2–3 min/bed, 6–7 beds in total). The scanning range was from the head to the upper part of the femur, and the limbs can be scanned if necessary. The acquired data were iteratively reconstructed using the ordered subset maximum expectation method, and the reconstructed CT axial image was registered with the PET image, and the axial, sagittal and coronal PET/CT fusion images were finally obtained.

18F-FDG PET/CT image interpretation

All PET/CT images were interpreted and analyzed in Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) format by two nuclear medicine diagnosticians in metabolic assessment software independently and blinded to the outcomes. In case of disagreement, the decision was made through discussion. The Beth Israel PET/CT viewer (www.petctviewer.org) plugin in the FIJI software was used to semi-automatically delineate the regions of interest (ROIs) of all lymphoma lesions. Physiological uptake areas such as the head, heart, intestines, kidneys, and bladder, as well as inflammatory uptake areas, were manually deleted, and the maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) and mean SUV (SUVmean) of each ROI were obtained. According to the European Association of Nuclear Medicine guidelines, the metabolic tumor volume (MTV) of each ROI was calculated using the 41% SUVmax relative threshold method, and the TMTV (the sum of the MTV of each ROI) and TLG (the sum of the product of MTV and SUVmean) were finally obtained [18]. In this study, the pharyngeal lymphatic ring, thymus, and spleen were considered as intranodal areas. Bone marrow involvement was judged by pathologically positive bone marrow aspiration biopsy or focal high uptake on PET images. ROIs with appropriate size were drawn in the descending aorta and the right lobe of the liver without lesions to measure the SUVmax of the mediastinal blood pool (SUVmax-blood) and the SUVmax of the liver (SUVmax-liver). According to the Lugano classification, spleen involvement was judged as follows: the spleen vertical diameter exceeds 13 cm or the spleen uptake is higher than the normal liver background uptake [19].

Measurement of metabolic and body composition parameters

Based on the third lumbar vertebra(L3) axial CT image, SliceOmatic version 5.0 software (TomoVision, Montreal, Canada) was used to semi-automatically delineate and calculate the areas of all skeletal muscle (SM), visceral adipose tissue (VAT), and subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) in this slice [20, 21]. The threshold ranges for delineating SM, VAT, and SAT were defined by Housefiled Unit (HU) and were set to −29HU~150HU, −150HU~–50HU, and − 190HU~–30HU, respectively. Figure 2 showed an example of body composition delineation. The mean density of SM(SMD), VAT(VATD), and SAT(SATD) were also automatically measured when delineating these three body compositions. Given the influence of height on body composition, the areas of SM, VAT, and SAT were standardized by the square of height and expressed as SMI (cm2/m2), VATI (cm2/m2), and SATI (cm2/m2), respectively.

Clinical risk assessment and follow-up

PIT and AITL score were used to assess the clinical risk. PIT includes four risk factors (age > 60, bone marrow involvement, ECOG-PS ≥ 2, and elevated LDH), and AITL score also includes four risk factors (age ≥ 60, decreased PLT, elevated CRP, and elevated β2-MG) [2, 4]. In this study, PIT > 2 or AITL score > 2 was defined as high-risk patients. The endpoints of this study include progression-free survival (PFS) and OS. PFS is defined as the time from disease diagnosis (pathological diagnosis) to disease progression, recurrence, or death due to various causes. OS is defined as the time from disease diagnosis to death from various causes.

Statistical analysis

Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and the differences between groups were analyzed by Student’s t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range, IQR), and the differences between groups were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as counts (percentages), and differences between groups were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact probability method. Spearman correlation analysis was used to assess the correlation between parameters. The “surv_cutpoint” function in the “survminer” package was used to obtain the optimal cutoff value for each continuous variable to judge the prognosis and group the patients. Parameters with p < 0.05 and the variance inflation factor (VIF) < 5 in univariate logistic regression analysis were included in multivariate logistics regression analysis to find independent factors related to SMI, VATI, and SATI. Survival curves were drawn by the Kaplan-Meier method, and the log-rank test was performed. Survival analysis was performed by the Cox proportional hazard model, and the Schoenfeld residual test was used to judge whether the proportional hazard assumption was met. The variables with P < 0.05 and VIF < 5 in the univariate Cox analysis were included in the multivariate Cox survival analysis, and the parameters with independent prognostic value were screened by stepwise regression. Two-tailed P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed in R (version 4.3.1) and SPSS 26.0 (IBM, USA).

Results

Baseline demographic characteristics

A total of 94 AITL patients were included in this study. The proportion of male patients was higher (67.0% vs. 33.0%) than female patients, and the male-to-female ratio was approximately 2.03:1. The onset age of AITL patients was relatively old, with an average onset age of 63.4 ± 9.7 years. The majority of patients were initially diagnosed at the advanced stage (stage III/IV), with only 7 (7.4%) patients at stage I/II. Although 59 (62.8%) patients had B symptoms at diagnosis, only 29 (30.9%) patients had an ECOG-PS > 1. There were 33 (35.1%), 63 (67.0%), 62 (66.0%), and 72 (76.6%) patients with low PLT and elevated CRP, LDH, and β2-MG levels, respectively. Extranodal involvement was positive in 45 (52.1%) patients. In addition, 21 (22.3%) patients were classified as high-risk by PIT, and 40 (42.6%) patients were in high-risk by AITL score. The detailed demographic characteristics were shown in Table 1.

Comparison of groups according to body composition index cutoff values

The average age of patients with sarcopenia was significantly higher than that of patients without sarcopenia (68.3 years vs. 60.2 years, p < 0.001). Sarcopenia was more common in male patients compared to female patients (43.1% vs. 31.0%, p = 0.005). More patients with sarcopenia were found in the high-risk group stratified by AITL score than in the low-risk group (52.5% vs. 29.6%, p = 0.025). The WBC in sarcopenic patients was higher than that in non-sarcopenic patients (8.20 × 109/L vs. 7.28 × 109/L). The median SATI of patients with sarcopenia was significantly lower than that of patients without sarcopenia (25.09 cm2/m2 vs. 36.21 cm2/m2, p = 0.005). It is worth noting that the SUVmax-blood of patients with sarcopenia was significantly lower than that of non-sarcopenic patients (1.86 vs. 2.19, p = 0.012). Patients with high VATI had higher BMI than those with low VATI, and the results were similar for SATI. Patients with higher VATI tend to have higher SATI, and vice versa. As VATI and SATI increase, VATD and SATD will increase accordingly. Interestingly, patients with high VATI and SATI had higher SUVmax-blood and SUVmax-liver than those with low VATI and SATI. In addition, the SUVmax-lesion showed significant differences in different VATI groups. The specific results were shown in Supplementary Table S1.

Univariate and multivariate regression analysis of factors affecting body composition index

Univariate logistic regression showed that age, WBC, gender, AITL score>2, and SATI may be associated factors for sarcopenia. Multivariate logistics regression analysis showed that age [odds ratio(OR) = 1.14, 95%confidence interval(CI): 1.07–1.23, p < 0.001], male patients (OR = 3.82, 95%CI:1.13–12.87, p = 0.030), and SUVmax-blood (OR = 0.31, 95%CI: 0.11–0.84, p = 0.021) were all independent factors for sarcopenia. For VATI and SATI, univariate logistic regression analysis suggested that BMI, SUVmax-blood, SUVmax-liver, VATD, and SATD might be the influencing factors of low VATI and SATI. VATI and SATI may influence each other. In addition, SUVmax-lesion may also be related to low VATI, and gender may be related to low SATI. Multivariate logistics regression analysis showed that BMI was an independent influencing factor for low VATI and SATI (VATI: OR = 0.73, 95%CI: 0.59–0.89, p = 0.002; SATI: OR = 0.75, 95%CI: 0.59–0.94, p = 0.013). VATD (OR = 1.27, 95%CI: 1.14–1.41, p < 0.001) and SATD (OR = 1.14, 95%CI: 1.06–1.23, p < 0.001) were independent factors for low VATI and low SATI, respectively. Male gender was an independent risk factor for low SATI (OR = 4.27, 95%CI: 1.23–14.80, p = 0.022). SUVmax-lesion was also an independent factor for low VATI (OR = 0.91, 95%CI: 0.84–0.99, p = 0.037) (Table 3).

Univariate and multivariate survival analysis

With a median follow-up of 27.5 (10.5–45.7) months, 65 patients in this cohort had disease progression, and 55 patients died. The median PFS was 10.5 (95%CI: 7.0-13.9) months, and the 1-year and 3-year PFS rates were 45.7% and 32.1%. The median OS was 42.5 (95%CI: 17.2–67.8) months, and the 3-year and 5-year OS rates were 51.9% and 43.3%.

Univariate Cox analysis showed that positive bone marrow involvement, clinical high-risk group (AITL score>2), low PLT, elevated CRP level, and elevated β2-MG level may be risk factors for PFS and OS. Ann Arbor stage IV, ECOG-PS>1, and extranodal involvement may be risk factors for OS. For body composition parameters, low SMD, high VATD and SATD, and low VATI may be risk factors for PFS and OS. For PET metabolic parameters, high baseline TMTV and TLG were also possible risk factors for OS (Table 4).

Discussion

18F-FDG PET/CT holds significant clinical utility in the standardized management of aggressive lymphoma. Limited research has explored the prognostic significance of baseline body composition parameters in AITL patients, likely attributable to the rarity of this histological subtype. In this retrospective study, we quantified body composition parameters using baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging data and demonstrated their preliminary prognostic value. Key findings revealed that sarcopenia independently predicted OS, while low VATI emerged as an independent risk factor for both OS and PFS. SMD and SATD may also independently influence PFS. Notably, TMTV retained its prognostic significance independent of body composition parameters, particularly for OS.

Sarcopenia is a syndrome characterized by an age-related decrease in skeletal muscle mass and muscle strength or physical performance. It is more common in the elderly and increases the risk of falls, fractures, disability, hospitalization and even death [23]. Previous studies had shown that sarcopenia prevalence ranging from 23.9 to 55.6% in DLBCL patients [24]. However, prevalence rates may differ across histological subtypes, given the extreme heterogeneity of lymphoma. In the study by Tan et al., the prevalence of sarcopenia in treatment-naïve patients with T-cell lymphoblastic lymphoma was 36.7%, and our result was similar to this value [25]. Notably, diagnostic criteria variations and cutoff selection may affect prevalence estimations. Age constitutes the primary sarcopenia risk factor due to an age-related imbalance between muscle protein synthesis and degradation, ultimately driving skeletal muscle depletion [26]. Sexual dimorphism appears influential, with males demonstrating greater susceptibility potentially attributable to age-related declines in anabolic androgens that regulate protein synthesis [27]. Our cohort corroborated this pattern, showing male predominance among sarcopenic patients and identifying male gender as an independent risk factor. Interestingly, sarcopenic patients exhibited lower baseline SUVmax-blood values, potentially reflecting reduced basal metabolic rates [28]. Accumulating evidence establishes pretreatment sarcopenia as an independent prognostic indicator in DLBCL and HL [13, 15]. Our analysis extended these observations to AITL, identifying sarcopenia as an independent predictor of OS. While proportional hazards assumptions precluded Cox regression analysis for PFS, Kaplan-Meier curves with marginal log-rank significance (p = 0.050) suggest potential PFS impairment in sarcopenic patients. The pathophysiological basis for sarcopenia’s prognostic impact likely involves multifactorial mechanisms including cachexia, chronic inflammatory states, and dysregulation of insulin-like growth factor-1 signaling. These processes collectively contribute to enhanced treatment toxicity, therapeutic resistance, and reduced complete response rates - ultimately culminating in adverse clinical outcomes [24, 29].

Sarcopenia may not only be evaluated by skeletal muscle mass, but may also be reflected by changes in skeletal muscle density and characterized by CT attenuation [30]. Adipocyte infiltration and senescent cell steatosis in the aging process may lead to lower muscle density in patients with sarcopenia [31]. Although there was no significant statistical difference in SMD between patients with sarcopenia and no sarcopenia according to this cohort, a weak positive correlation between SMI and SMD could still be found (R = 0.37, p < 0.001). Therefore, SMD also had a similar prognostic value, and low baseline SMD was an independent prognostic factor for PFS in this group of patients. This phenomenon was also seen in DLBCL and follicular lymphoma [30, 32].

Besides sarcopenia, adipose tissue distribution had demonstrated prognostic relevance in hematological malignancies [17]. This study established both low VATI and SATI as risk factors for adverse outcomes, with only low VATI retaining independent prognostic significance for both PFS and OS in multivariate analyses. Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified low baseline BMI as an independent predictor of both low VATI and SATI. While BMI provides a convenient anthropometric measure of body composition, its inability to differentiate specific tissue components may limit its prognostic utility [33]. The prognostic implications of VAT in hematological malignancies remain controversial. Although generally associated with adverse outcomes, this relationship varies across disease subtypes, demonstrating significance in acute myeloid leukemia but not in lymphoma or multiple myeloma [17]. Our findings contrasted with Chen et al.‘s report associating elevated baseline VATI with poor prognosis [13], a discrepancy potentially explained by differences in tumor biology and disease burden. AITL typically presents with greater aggressiveness and higher tumor burden. The semi-quantitative PET/CT parameter SUVmax-lesion, which reflects tumor proliferation activity, suggests enhanced disease aggressiveness in this subgroup [34]. From a pathophysiological perspective, adequate visceral fat reserves may provide metabolic support during chemotherapy-induced stress, whereas diminished reserves could increase susceptibility to treatment-related complications and impair treatment response [35]. This study primarily established the prognostic value of VATI, though the mechanisms underlying this association warrant further investigation. Adipose tissue radiodensity may represent another prognostic modifier. In this study, high VATD and SATD could serve as independent risk factors for low VATI and SATI, respectively. While both parameters correlated with inferior outcomes, only SATD maintained independent prognostic value for PFS. The biological dichotomy between energy-storing low-density white adipose tissue and thermogenic high-density brown adipose tissue may explain these observations [36], particularly given the association between brown adipose predominance and early cachexia development [37]. Elevated subcutaneous fat density could reflect inflammatory infiltration and adipocyte depletion, processes that may promote tumor progression through inflammatory cytokine secretion [38].

In addition, this study also showed that elevated baseline CRP level was an independent risk factor for PFS in AITL patients, and baseline AITL score > 2 was an independent risk factor for OS. These results have been confirmed in previous studies [2, 5]. The AITL score, a novel risk stratification system derived from a larger cohort analysis, integrates clinical biomarkers, including CRP, to enhance prognostic precision [2]. TMTV remains the most common PET/CT-derived quantitative metabolic parameter for tumor burden assessment, with independent prognostic significance established in our earlier work [39]. Current clinical adoption is hindered by methodological heterogeneity in quantification. Emerging deep learning applications in molecular imaging may enable standardized TMTV measurement as a next-generation prognostic biomarker [40].

This study still has several limitations. First, this study may have information bias and selection bias due to its retrospective nature. For example, the time span for including patients was too long, and PET/CT examination was not necessary for some patients before treatment in the early years. Secondly, SMI was calculated only from CT images, but parameters reflecting muscle strength and physical function, such as upper limb grip strength and walking speed, were not obtained, which may not fully reflect the clinical profile of sarcopenia [23]. Thirdly, the sample size of this study was small, and there may be a possibility of false negatives due to insufficient statistical power. Finally, the dynamic changes in body composition and their prognostic value were not explored in this study. This requires us to expand the sample size in the future to further clarify the prognostic value of body composition parameters in AITL patients.

Conclusions

Our study demonstrated that baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT-derived body composition parameters, except for conventional metabolic parameters like TMTV, may have prognostic value for AITL patients. Sarcopenia showed a good prognostic value in AITL patients, especially for OS. Low baseline VATI was also an independent risk factor for poor prognosis. Furthermore, low SMD and high SATD could serve as independent prognostic factors for PFS. These findings highlighted the prognostic relevance of body composition assessment in AITL patients. Further studies are warranted to validate their potential as biomarkers for integration into novel prognostic models.

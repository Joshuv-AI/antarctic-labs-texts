Physics-informed neural network analysis of kerosene-based penta-hybrid nanofluid flow and heat transfer
Authors: Basim M. Makhdoum, Hina Zahir, Nek Muhammad Katbar, Wasim Jamshed, Kamel Guedri, Mustafa Bayram, Syed M. Hussain, Sawera Batool, Hijaz Ahmad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Kerosene oil-based penta-hybrid nanofluids have attracted significant attention because of their improved thermal conductivity, mechanical stability, and potential use in advanced heat transfer systems. In this study, a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) Analysis of Kerosene-Based Penta-Hybrid Nanofluid Flow and Heat Transfer, is performed to understand the flow pattern and heat transfer characteristics of a nanostructured fluid loaded with several types of nanoparticles. This new approach integrates the physical soundness of governing transport equations with the deep learning’s ability to predict, for modeling nanofluid flow and heat transfer phenomena. The initial partial differential equations governing momentum and energy transfers are converted via appropriate similarity transformations into dimensionless ordinary differential equations. These are then used as the key ingredients (embedded alongside boundary conditions) of the loss function of Physics-Informed Neural Network to make the model’s output comply with physical laws. Variations in parameters leading to changes in velocity and temperature distributions are explored, and to check the correctness and trustworthiness results are compared with classical numerical solutions and previously published data. The findings indicate that the PINN approach accurately characterizes the complex flow and heat transfer features of kerosene-based penta-hybrid nanofluids. By incorporating physics-based modeling with deep learning, reliance on extensive numerical data is diminished while excellent predictive capability is preserved. This research draws attention to PINN-based methods as promising and powerful instruments for the study of high-tech nanofluid products and direct engineering of superior heat exchangers, refrigeration systems, and thermal management devices.

Introduction

Recently, nanofluids have been reckoned as next-generation heat transfer fluids because of their exceptional thermal properties that are far better than those of traditional base fluids. In fact, dispersing nanosized solid particles into regular liquids can increase thermal conductivity, heat transport ability, and hence overall energy efficiency to a great extent. Hybrid and multi-hybrid nanofluids have been garnering a lot of attention lately because a mixture of different nanoparticles can exhibit better thermophysical properties than a single type of nanoparticle. Penta-hybrid nanofluids, in particular, are a more sophisticated form of nanofluids where five different types of nanoparticles are suspended in the base fluid to result in an even higher thermal conductivity, better stability, and higher heat transfer capacity. In terms of a base fluid, kerosene oil is being considered as one of the best options for thermal engineering applications due to its wide usefulness in cooling, lubrication, energy systems, and various industrial heat transfer processes. The study of the flow and heat transfer characteristics of the penta-hybrid nanofluids based on kerosene oil and their interaction with the stretching surfaces finds a direct connecting thread to many engineering systems which include heat exchangers, cooling devices, polymer processing, energy storage systems, and thermal management technologies. Stretching sheet problems, for example, are frequently studied due to the fact that they stand for various industrial activities such as extrusion, production of glass fibers, wire drawing, and coating operations. However, with the introduction of external physical effects such as magnetic field, heat generation or absorption, chemical reaction, velocity slip, thermal slip, and concentration slip, the distributions of velocity, temperature, and concentration of the nanofluids can be highly complex. Hence, devising an accurate mathematical and computational model for capturing these effects is a prerequisite to the further design and optimization of the next-generation thermal systems. For the solution of nonlinear boundary layer problems, classical numerical methods like finite difference, shooting, and Runge–Kutta have been extensively employed. These methods, however, while they yield trustworthy results, may at times necessitate a very fine grid, accurate determination of initial values, and could be quite computationally expensive, especially when dealing with strongly nonlinear coupled system of equations. On the other hand, deep learning techniques have recently grown to be effective means of unravelling the solutions of complicated physical issues. Among them, Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) stand out as they integrate the fundamental differential equations and boundary conditions directly within the neural network loss function. Therefore, PINNs are capable of learning the solution that is consistent with physics at the same time they minimize the need for quite large training data. Novelty of the study

The major innovation of this research is the creation of a Physical-Informed Neural Network (PINN)-based method to study the fluid dynamics and heat transfer characteristics of kerosene oil-based penta-hybrid nanofluids on a morphable surface. Traditional numerical techniques obtain solutions by solving the discretized PDEs but the PINN is a supervised machine learning method which learns the solution in a continuous domain. The PINN model incorporates the main equations of momentum and energy as physics constraints inside the training of the neural network. This is a departure from the conventional method of training the neural networks based on the labelled data points. The physics informed PINN thus gives reasonable and accurate results of the distributions of velocity and temperature. Also, this paper is unique as it concentrates on penta-hybrid nanofluid which means kerosene-based formulation has been blended with multiple nanoparticles in order to upsurge their thermal conductivity, mechanical stability, and heat transfer performance. Thoroughly the influence of main flow and thermal parameters on the dynamic and thermal behavior of the nanofluids has been explored, thereby providing an understanding of this complicated feature of advanced nanofluids. Moreover, deep learning has been combined with physics-based modeling so data dependency has been mitigated and the computational cost has been substantially greatly reduced as compared with traditional CFD or purely data-driven methods. The proposed PINN method is a good solution for simulating very complex nonlinear nanofluid flow problems.

Physics informed neural network (PINN)

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) are a special type of artificial neural networks which are programmed to learn how physical systems work by involving the main physical equations right into the training phase. These networks offer a versatile and powerful computational tool for addressing complicated problems matched out in different fields such as physics, simulation of physical systems, mesh-free modeling and numerical solution of partial differential equations. A PINN includes multiple layers that are connected, which are an input layer, one or more hidden layers, and an output layer. When the model is being planned, the input and output layers get defined based on the variables and desired targets of the problem. The hidden layers, which are between input and output layers, do most of the computational work. The input layer gets the independent variables and sends them to the hidden layers where the information is processed through weighted connections and nonlinear activation functions.

In each hidden layer, the neuron takes inputs from the previous layer, calculates their weighted sum, uses an activation function, and then sends the modified data to the next layer. All the way through to the output layer this forward propagation will continue and yield the predicted solution. Here in the PINN framework, physically desired quantities such as velocity, temperature or concentration profiles correspond to output layer.

The capability of hidden layers lies in modeling the complex nonlinear link between the input variables and output responses. The outputs of these layers are the weighted results from the previous layer and therefore, the network can closely resemble complicated solution structures. Results and discussion

In this study, neural network simulation and numerical calculations are used to evaluate the influence of different physical parameters on fluid flow, heat transfer, and mass transfer features. The velocity profile is shown in Fig. 3 to decline as the magnetic field parameter is increased. Physically, as an electrically conducting fluid moves in a magnetic field, an electromagnetic resisting force, i.e., the Lorentz force, is created. This force is directed against the fluid motion and causes a reduction in flow. As such, a higher magnetic field strength results in a stronger Lorentz force, which impedes the movement of the fluid particles and hence the velocity profile is decreased. Consequently, the momentum boundary layer is thinned. Figure 4 reveals that the velocity profile lessens as the velocity slip parameter is augmented. Physically, velocity slip diminishes the engagement between the wall and the fluid particles near it to such an extent that the transfer of momentum from surface to fluid is weakened, thus the velocity near the boundary is lowered and the entire velocity profile is not as high. Figure 5 shows the temperature profile rising with increases in the magnetic parameter. This is mainly due to the resistance effect of the Lorentz force that slows down fluid movement and transforms some kinetic energy into heat. Thus, more thermal energy is conserved in the boundary layer, which leads to a higher temperature profile. According to Fig. 6, the temperature profile declines as the Prandtl number goes up. In other words, a high Prandtl number implies reduced thermal diffusivity. So, less heat is transferred through conduction, which means the thermal boundary layer thickness is decreased and the temperature distribution within the flow area is lowered. Figure 7 explicates that the temperature profile diminishes with an increase in the heat generation parameter when the parameter is associated with heat loss or a decreased level of internal heat production. Physically, the fluid is supplied with a lower level of internal thermal energy weakening the thermal field and reducing the temperature distribution. As a result, the thermal boundary layer is narrowed. According to Fig. 8 results, the temperature profile increases with increasing velocity slip parameter. The reason is that the higher velocity slip decreases the shear interaction between the fluid and the surface, which leads to less convective cooling. As a result, the region of the fluid adjacent to the boundary retains more thermal energy, so the temperature distribution behaves as shown in the figure. Figure 9 demonstrates that the thermal profile decreases as the thermal slip parameter goes up. Thermal slip hampers the heat transfer between the wall and the fluid. As a consequence, the amount of heat energy transferred from the surface to the fluid is not sufficient to cause the increase of the temperature distribution that leads to a smaller thermal boundary layer. Figure 10 illustrates that the concentration profile falls with the increase of the Schmidt number. The Schmidt number is a measure of the ratio of momentum diffusivity to mass diffusivity. When the Schmidt number is high, it implies that mass diffusivity is less, and so the species diffusion inside the fluid is limited. Therefore, the concentration boundary layer shrinks, which causes the concentration profile to fall. Per Fig. 11, the concentration profile is getting less with the increase of the chemical reaction parameter. Physically, a greater chemical reaction parameter is correlated with a more potent chemical destructing the diffusing species, which leads to faster depletion of concentration in the boundary layer. According to Fig. 12, a rise in the velocity slip parameter concentration profile also goes up. In fact, the decreased resistance at the wall allows species particles to stay longer within the boundary layer. This effect reduces the dispersion of species from the surface and causes species accumulation, which in turn results in a thicker concentration boundary layer. In a like manner, Fig. 13 depicts the concentration profile rising as the magnetic parameter is increased. The magnetic field, which is made stronger, inhibits transport through convection and results in lowering the fluid velocity so that nanoparticles or species particles amass in the boundary layer. Therefore, the concentration distribution levels rise. Figure 14 demonstrates the same inverse relationship between the concentration profile and the concentration slip parameter, only here the role of mass transfer is clearly identified. That is, mass transfer limits the diffusion of species from the wall into the boundary layer, leading to lower concentration values and a thinner concentration boundary layer. The correlation plot and evaluation metrics shown in Fig. 15 compare the PINN-predicted results with the numerical or experimental reference values for the quantities of interest in engineering. The points are tightly distributed around the 45° reference line, which means there is a very good agreement between the predicted and the reference values. The correlation coefficient is the regression plots shown in Fig. 18 and it is very close to 1. On the other hand, the mean squared error values are of the order of (10–10) for skin friction and Nusselt number. These findings are consistent with the very high prediction capability of the PINN model. Moreover, Fig. 16a–c show the training and validation MSE histories of the three quantities studied: skin friction, Nusselt number, and Sherwood number. Each of the MSE plots exhibits a steady drop and attains a very small value, e.g., the MSE at epoch 159 for skin friction is about (1.2143 × 10–10) and the one for Nusselt number at epoch 41 is (1.0799 × 10–10). The final gradients of about (9.79 × 10–8) suggest that the training process was pretty much finished. The error histograms in Fig. 16c reveal that most of the prediction errors are very close to zero, which indicates that PINN predictions are quite trustable and stable. The two-dimensional response surface plots of predicted and reference results, given in Fig. 17, are in close agreement with the ideal diagonal plane. This result shows the ability of the PINN model to represent the dependency of the input parameters and the engineering outputs accurately all over the data domain. In a similar vein, regression graphs from Fig. 18 show for the training, validation, and testing sets R ≈ 1, thereby validating an almost perfect association between the predicted and target values. Coupled with the minuscule MSE figures, these results evidence that PINN is capable of generating the output data with top-notch precision. Figure 19 depicts the different physics-informed loss components such as IC loss, BC loss, PDE residual loss, and experimental data loss individually. The drop of PDE residual loss shows that the network is not only fitting the given data but also it is complying with the PDEs at the collocation points. The grad loss in its absolute form is the ultimate proof of the predicted solutions being stable and physically consistent. The data given in Tables 4, 5, 6, and 7 add more explanation to the physical side of things as they describe parameters such as skin friction, Nusselt number as well as Sherwood number. A stronger magnetic field leads to higher Lorentz force that in turn results in skin friction coefficient going up as it slows down fluid motion by its resistance. Yet, the very same magnetic force that acts like a brake hinders the fluid motion as a result the convective heat transfer and mass rates become lower. However, an increase in velocity slip parameter makes fluid and wall engagement weak that directly results in decrease in wall shear stress, heat transfer, and mass diffusion. The heat source parameter strengthens the thermal energy of the fluid and consequently the heat transfer rate is increased whereas a larger thermal slip parameter diminishes the heat exchange from wall-to-fluid which results in a lower heat transfer rate. At last, the chemical reaction parameter and Schmidt number being high lead to concentration gradients being steep near the surface. It boosts the Sherwood number which tells us that the mass transfer phenomena are greater in reactive flows as well as in fluids having low diffusivity. In general, both numerical and neural network findings assert that the PINN method correctly represents the multi-physical interactions between magnetohydrodynamic flow, heat transfer, and species Conclusions

This research uses a Physics-Informed Neural Network to analyze the flow and heat transfer of kerosene-based penta-hybrid nanofluids over a stretching geometry. Because it is often used in advanced thermal systems, kerosene oil is selected as the base fluid of the present study.

The mathematical model of the momentum and energy transport phenomena is established under boundary layer assumptions. The transformed governing equations and the corresponding boundary conditions are solved and analyzed within a Physics-Informed Neural Network framework. In this method, the residuals of differential equations and boundary condition are embedded straight into the neural network loss function. So, the PINN model is capable of providing physically consistent predictions of velocity and temperature fields without the need for extensive numerical datasets. The results demonstrate that the flow and heat transfer characteristics of kerosene-based penta-hybrid nanofluids are highly affected by the nature of the physical parameters. The Lorentz force produced by the magnetic field acts as a retarding force resulting in a decrease in velocity of the fluid, but at the same time, it causes an increase in the temperature distribution within the boundary layer. In the same way, increasing the nanoparticle volume fraction leads to enhanced thermal conductivity of the nanofluid, though the rate of heat transfer from the surface may also be influenced by the combined effects of viscosity, magnetism, and heat conduction. The results obtained by the present PINN method are very close to those obtained by the typical numerical computations and other published data, which serves as a validation of the present deep learning framework's accuracy, stability, and reliability. The model adeptly reduces the residuals of governing equations and the means of boundary conditions, indicating its capacity to model the nonlinear flow and thermal properties of penta-hybrid nanofluids. Even though completely numerical methods have been proven to be accurate, the PINN method offers a versatile and mesh-free method that is also less expensive in terms of computation for solving complex problems involving nanofluid flow. This paper demonstrates the capability of Physics-Informed Neural Networks as effective surrogate models for simulating the flow and heat transfer of kerosene-based penta-hybrid nanofluids. The results can be used as a design and optimization tool for high-performance heat exchangers, cooling systems, energy devices, refrigeration units, and other advanced thermal management technologies. The present framework may also be developed further to include, for example, transient flow analysis, 3D geometry, non-Newtonian base fluids, entropy generation, and experimental validation with penta-hybrid nanofluids.

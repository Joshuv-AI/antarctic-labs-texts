An evidence-based nursing management app prototype for adult urology day surgery: an AI-driven development pathway
Authors: Qinghong Fang, Yan Zhao, Meiyu Lai, Guangqian Hu, Muli Li, Xiaojun Zhou, Yunyan Rao, Xiaoyin Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThe nursing management of adult urology patients in day surgical settings has undergone rapid development. This study aimed to (1) retrieve, evaluate, and summarize the best available evidence regarding the perioperative nursing management in adult patients undergoing urological day surgery, and (2) develop a functional prototype of an evidence-based nursing management mobile application (APP) using an artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted pathway.MethodsThis research was conducted in two phases. First, in April 2025, a best evidence summary was conducted across many databases utilizing the PIPOST tool. Clinical guidelines, systematic reviews, expert consensus documents, clinical decision tools, and evidence summaries were included and screened. Formal quality appraisal was conducted for clinical guidelines, systematic reviews, and expert consensus documents using appropriate appraisal tools. Second, an AI-augmented platform called Manus AI was used to create an app prototype with the best evidence summary. This platform made it possible to design the architecture, organize the material, and create user interface mock-ups.ResultsThe best evidence summary included 21 studies that were thematically classified into eight nursing management domains: nursing organization management, pre-hospital nursing management, preoperative nursing management, intraoperative nursing management, postoperative nursing management, discharge management, nursing follow-up and nursing quality management. This framework was successfully used to create a completely structured app prototype. The eight domains are reflected in the app prototype. Preliminary feedback from 12 clinical nurses indicated that the prototype was clinically meaningful and may support standardized perioperative nursing management in adult urology day surgery.ConclusionThis study summarizes the current best evidence and demonstrates a novel AI-empowered pathway for translating evidence into a practical digital tool. The developed app prototype offers a promising foundation for standardizing nursing care in urological day surgery. Future studies are needed to determine whether the app prototype could improve protocol adherence and patient outcomes.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12912-026-04721-w.

Introduction

In recent years, with the advancement of minimally invasive technology, the day surgery model has gradually become a significant component of urological surgery [1, 2]. The International Association for Ambulatory Surgery (IAAS) and the China Ambulatory Surgery Alliance (CASA) define day surgery as “an operation completed by a patient entering or leaving the hospital within 1 day (24 hours)” [3]. The longest hospitalization time of patients who need to be delayed due to their illness is not more than 48 hours. Day surgery remarkably reduces medical costs, cutting patients’ hospitalization costs by 30%-50%, and preventing the occupation of resources in traditional inpatient wards [2, 4]. Nowadays, urological day surgery has progressed from simple ureteroscopic lithotripsy to a wide range of procedures, including transurethral resection of the bladder mass, transurethral resection of the prostate, laparoscopic high ligation of the spermatic vein, and more [5, 6].

Owing to the unique anatomical and functional characteristics of the urinary system render, urological procedures are inherently susceptible to postoperative complications, demanding exceptional precision and specialized nursing care [7]. Also, the growing prevalence of elderly patients in day surgery settings introduces complex challenges in perioperative management [8], where multifaceted comorbidity management necessitates coordinated multidisciplinary approaches. Furthermore, rising patient demand for tailored care necessitates a systemic shift toward individualized care models [9]. These converging factors highlight the critical need for continuous optimization and innovative strategies in urological day surgery nursing management.

The rate of clinical nursing indicator compliance could be increased by integrating evidence and developing a consistent perioperative management strategy [8]. Compared with conventional protocol patients, urological surgery patients receiving evidence-based interventions presented superior outcomes in self-management capacity, quality of life metrics, and care satisfaction [7]. In addition, the evidence-based nursing strategies effectively mitigated patients complication risks, particularly in pressure injury prevention and postoperative infection control [10]. Esma et al. [11] proved that standardizing nursing procedures might be achieved by creating clinical quality evaluation indicators using evidence-based techniques. Other studies also have demonstrated that academic-practice partnerships could develop an evidence-based nursing curriculum framework [12, 13]. These partnerships narrowed the theory-practice gap and improved nurses’ evidence-based competence and innovative behaviors. In the context of day surgery, evidence-based care management may enhance patient safety [14].

While traditional evidence-based practice often results in static guidelines that are slow to update and challenging to implement consistently at the point of care, the emergence of artificial intelligence (AI) presents a transformative opportunity to bridge this implementation gap. In clinical decision support, AI can analyze complex datasets to provide real-time risk predictions and personalize management plans, thereby enhancing the precision of evidence-based protocols [15, 16]. Furthermore, AI can administrative tasks like documentation, reducing nurse burden and allowing greater focus on direct patient care [17]. This technological synergy suggests that translating consolidated evidence into an AI-augmented tool could dynamically support decision-making, and improve workflow efficiency in fast-paced settings like day surgery.

Building evidence for the management of urological day surgery care might help implement guidelines like Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) [18] and assist clinical decision-making with data. There is a substantial knowledge gap on particular nursing interventions suited to the context of urological day surgery, despite established documentation of general day surgery care guidelines [19, 20]. Additionally, there are still few studies on using AI to convert evidence databases into applications. Therefore, this study aims to summarize the evidence for the nursing management of adult urological day surgery and use an AI-augmented platform called Manus AI to transform the evidence into an app prototype.

Materials and methods

Establishment of the evidence-based team

The multidisciplinary evidence-based team consisted of eleven members: three clinical nurses, four graduate research assistants, two evidence-based practice specialists, and two project managers. The role assignments were structured as follows: nurses conducted literature screening, quality appraisal, and evidence extraction; graduate assistants synthesized and organized evidentiary data and assisted with the qualitative survey; specialists provided methodological oversight for search strategies and evidence integration; and project managers oversaw coordination and quality assurance throughout the research lifecycle. This study has been registered on the Evidence Summarization Registry platform of the Evidence-Based Nursing Center of Fudan University (ES20257700).

Problem establishment

The PIPOST model, an evidence-based problem-setting tool of the Evidence-Based Nursing Center of Fudan University, was used to define the evidence-based problem of this project [21]. The PIPOST model refers to population, intervention, professional, outcome, setting, and type of evidence. Table 1 shows the PIPOST model of this study.

Search strategy

In April 2025, we searched the following electronic databases: UpToDate, BMJ Best Practice, NICE Guidelines, Guidelines International Network (GIN), MedLive, Cochrane Library, JBI Evidence Summary Database, PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, Web of Science, CNKI, Wanfang Data, SinoMed, and China Medical Journal Full-text Database. Supplementary searches were performed on the official websites of the International Ambulatory Surgery Alliance, China Ambulatory Surgery Alliance, Joint Commission International (JCI), ERAS, European Association of Urology Nurses (EAUN), European Association of Urology (EAU), British Association of Day Surgery (BADS), Canadian Urological Association (CUA), American Urological Association (AUA) and American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA). We manually screened the reference lists of all included articles and conducted citation tracking for key papers. All searches were limited to studies published in English or Chinese between January 2010 and March 2025.

The search strategy included the following terms: (urologic surgical procedures OR urology OR urologic* surgery) AND (ambulatory surgical procedures OR day surgery OR ambulatory care OR outpatient surgery OR ambulatory surgery) AND (care OR nurs* OR nursing management). Boolean operators, truncation symbol, Medical Subject Headings (MeSH), and synonyms were combined to maximize search sensitivity and inclusivity. The full search strategy for PubMed was provided in Supplemental Fig. 1.

Literature inclusion and exclusion criteria

We defined problem-driven inclusion and exclusion criteria using parameters of the PIPOST model. Inclusion Criteria: (1) adult patients aged ≥ 18 years; (2) the study included nursing management, such as pre-hospital evaluation, preoperative education and postoperative complication management; (3) the studies conducted in day surgery wards; and (4) the literature types included clinical decision-making, guidelines, systematic reviews, expert consensus, and evidence summaries. Exclusion Criteria: (1) non-availability of the full text of the article; (2) the document failed the literature quality evaluation (guidelines with an evaluation level of C, or the number of items of systematic review, expert consensuses, and expert opinions evaluation ≥ 50% was “no” or ≥ 80% was “unclear”) [22]; and (3) articles published repeatedly with the same content.

Literature methodological quality

Four researchers independently assessed the included guidelines, and two researchers independently assessed the quality of systematic reviews and expert consensus. (1) To evaluate the quality of the guidelines, we used the Appraisal of Guidelines for Research and Evaluation Instrument (AGREE) II [23], which comprises six domains and 23 items. Each item was evaluated on a scale of 1–7, and the quality score for each domain was obtained. The overall score was then calculated by summing all the domains. The quality of the guidelines was classified into three levels: Grade A when all six domain scores were ≥ 60%; Grade B when scores of ≥ 3 domains were 30–60%; and Grade C when scores of > 3 domains were < 30%. (2) We used the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Evidence-Based Healthcare (EBHC) appraisal tools [24] to evaluate the methodological quality of the systematic reviews and expert consensus. The appraisal tools for systematic reviews and expert consensus documents consist of 11 and 6 items, respectively, with the response options being “yes”, “no”, “unclear”, and “not applicable”. Each source was assessed according to the corresponding checklist items, and the appraisal findings were used to support an overall judgment of methodological acceptability for inclusion in the evidence synthesis. Sources that met most checklist items and showed no critical methodological flaws were considered acceptable for inclusion.

Best evidence summary

Two researchers independently conducted best evidence extraction using a standardized thematic protocol. Intercoder discrepancies were resolved through a two-tiered approach: (1) prioritization of methodologically rigorous evidence guided by the JBI Evidence Hierarchy, and (2) preferential inclusion of studies published within the preceding five-year period. The synthesized evidence was systematically categorized into eight perioperative care domains: nursing organization, pre-hospital, preoperative, intraoperative, postoperative, discharge, follow-up, and quality management. Thematic consistency was validated through cross-source verification using NVivo tools. Evidence was classified into five levels based on the type of original research, with Level 1 representing the highest quality and Level 5 the lowest. An expert panel, comprising an evidence-based nursing expert, two urological care nursing specialists, and a urological surgery medical expert, evaluated the evidence for effectiveness, feasibility, appropriateness, and clinical significance, determining recommendation strengths as either Grade A (strong) or Grade B (weak).

AI-driven app prototype development pathway

Following completion of the best-evidence summary, the research team initiated the app prototype development phase. The summarized evidence served as the exclusive content basis for the prototype. To support the conversion of this evidence into a digital application format, the Manus AI platform (https://manus.im/app) was employed as an assistive design tool rather than an autonomous clinical decision-making system.

The development pathway included five steps. First, the research team transformed the best-evidence summary into a structured knowledge file containing the evidence hierarchy, thematic domains, and corresponding nursing content. Second, this file was provided to the AI platform to support the generation of an initial app framework, including user flow, functional modules, feature lists, and sitemap. Third, evidence-based content was reformatted into concise, actionable items suitable for mobile use, such as protocol steps, patient management points, and checklist-based guidance. Fourth, the AI platform assisted in generating prototype-level visual outputs, including interface wireframes, display mockups, app naming options, and logo concepts. Fifth, all outputs generated during the AI-assisted process were manually reviewed, refined, and finalized by the research team to ensure fidelity to the evidence base, relevance to adult urology day-surgery nursing, and usability in the intended clinical context.

Throughout the process, the AI platform functioned only as a supportive tool for structuring, visualization, and interface design. Clinical meaning, nursing content, and final prototype decisions remained under team oversight. The prototype developed in this study was intended for initial design and demonstration purposes.

Preliminary survey

A preliminary survey was conducted among twelve clinical nurses working in the urology day-surgery setting. After reviewing and experiencing the APP prototype, the nurses were asked to provide feedback on two questions: (1) What is the clinical significance of the APP prototype for nursing practice? and (2) How can the content of the APP prototype guide clinical nursing practice? The purpose of this survey was to obtain initial practice-based feedback on the prototype and to inform further refinement. This study was performed according to the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University Ethics Committee (Approval No. ES-2025-K277-02). Informed consent was obtained from all participants. Information of all nurses was kept confidential.

Development of the Nursing Management App Prototype

The development of the nursing management app prototype utilized a systematic approach that integrated findings from eight evidence-based domains of nursing practice. (https://urology-nur-rnkygrlq.manus.space)

The user interface of the app prototype was designed to meet the practical needs of nursing staff (Fig. 3). The prototype included several principal functional interfaces, including the dashboard, pre-hospital care management, patient information display, discharge preparation tasks, and follow-up task modules. These modules were structured to support evidence-based and standardized perioperative nursing management across different stages of urology day surgery. Specifically, the dashboard provided an overview of key management indicators, while the pre-hospital care and patient information modules facilitated assessment and individualized care planning. The patient information display was generated by the Manus AI platform as part of the prototype mock-up to simulate patient-specific perioperative information for individualized care scenarios, rather than to represent real patient data. In addition, the discharge preparation and follow-up task modules were developed by translating the evidence summary into structured perioperative nursing tasks and progress indicators, thereby supporting task execution, patient education, progress tracking, and postoperative follow-up.

Because urological day surgery includes different procedures with distinct nursing requirements, the current prototype was designed as a structured task framework rather than a fully automated decision-support system, and nurses are expected to select and apply relevant tasks according to the specific procedure and the patient’s clinical condition.

Preliminary survey of clinical nurses

A preliminary survey was conducted among twelve clinical nurses with a mean age of 37.67 ± 7.36 years to explore the perceived clinical value of the app prototype. Overall, they considered the prototype meaningful for clinical nursing practice. They indicated that the prototype could help standardize perioperative nursing management for adult urology day surgery. In addition, they considered that the prototype content could provide practical guidance for perioperative assessment, nursing intervention, symptom monitoring, and discharge education, thereby supporting the implementation of evidence-based nursing care.

Discussion

This study transcends traditional evidence synthesis by integrating key perioperative nursing domains for urology day surgery into an AI-assisted APP prototype. Thus, it offers more than a summary of evidence; it demonstrates a practical methodology for converting research into a point-of-care tool. Ultimately, this prototype serves to bridge the gap between evidence-based recommendations and frontline clinical workflows.

The findings suggest that effective nursing management in urological day surgery requires a structured organizational foundation. This is consistent with previous studies emphasizing that high-quality day surgery depends on well-defined governance structures, role accountability, and coordinated nursing systems [44–46]. In this context, the recommendation for a specialized day-surgery quality management framework under the Medical Quality Management Committee is not merely an organizational arrangement, but a practical prerequisite for implementing standardized nursing pathways. Similarly, the need for professional education and training for day-surgery nurses reflects the increasing complexity of ambulatory care, as also highlighted by the American Academy of Ambulatory Care Nursing [47].

A second important implication of the present study is that pre-hospital nursing management should be regarded as a central component of day-surgery quality rather than a preparatory step alone. Because same-day admission and discharge leave limited time for in-hospital adjustment, the safety and efficiency of day surgery depend heavily on adequate preoperative assessment, risk stratification, and patient education. Evidence has shown that validated assessment tools combined with multimodal education can reduce anxiety and improve perioperative recovery [48]. Amit et al. [49] also demonstrated that pre-hospital guidance materials for ambulatory surgery improved patients’ knowledge retention and satisfaction. These findings support the value of structured pre-hospital assessment and education pathways, and they also explain why pre-hospital care management was included as a core module in the APP prototype. In practice, such a module may help standardize perioperative preparation, identify high-risk patients earlier, and improve readiness for surgery and discharge.

The evidence synthesis also highlights that standardized preoperative nursing should be interpreted in relation to the special characteristics of urological day surgery. Recommendations concerning urethral hygiene, nutritional preparation, medication management, and thrombosis prevention are not important merely because they reflect guideline-based care, but because they directly influence the feasibility and safety of same-day surgery. In day-surgery settings, nursing interventions must balance standardization with individualization, particularly for patients with different anesthesia plans, procedure types, and risk profiles [50–53]. Therefore, the value of evidence-based preoperative management lies in its ability to support both safety and efficiency within a compressed perioperative timeline.

Intraoperative nursing management in day surgery likewise requires a stronger focus on coordination, efficiency, and prevention of avoidable complications. The present findings suggest that intraoperative nursing is not limited to conventional monitoring tasks, but includes equipment preparation, positioning, temperature management, fluid management, and procedure-specific assistance. These elements are especially relevant in urological day surgery, where rapid turnover, minimally invasive techniques, and technical variability can increase the demand for coordinated nursing support. Previous studies have shown that body temperature management and positioning are important for reducing complications [54], while advanced procedures such as robotic-assisted surgery require high levels of coordination and technical support from nursing staff [55]. Rather than representing isolated intraoperative tasks, these findings indicate that evidence-based intraoperative nursing contributes to procedural efficiency and overall pathway stability in day-surgery care.

Postoperative nursing in urological day surgery should be interpreted within the context of limited in-hospital observation and the need for safe same-day discharge. Therefore, the importance of pain control, voiding assessment, pelvic floor guidance, and early functional recovery lies not only in immediate symptom management, but also in supporting discharge readiness and reducing the risk of post-discharge complications or unplanned readmission. ERAS-based postoperative care is relevant in this setting because it provides a structured framework for early recovery, while poor pain control has been associated with delayed discharge and increased readmission [56]. Multimodal analgesia is also advantageous because it can reduce opioid use and postoperative nausea and vomiting [57]. In addition, first-void assessment and postvoid residual monitoring are particularly meaningful in urological day surgery because urinary function often determines whether discharge can proceed safely [35]. In this context, pelvic floor care and symptom diaries support not only symptom observation but also early self-management after discharge. With respect to sexual function, early postoperative evaluation should be interpreted cautiously. In the setting of day surgery, it is more appropriate to regard this domain as part of overall functional recovery rather than as a direct measure of actual sexual activity, thereby providing a more clinically meaningful basis for postoperative nursing assessment.

Our findings further show that discharge preparation and follow-up are not peripheral stages of care, but core elements of nursing management in day surgery. Because the postoperative observation period is brief, many safety issues shift from the inpatient setting to discharge assessment, home management, and follow-up surveillance. Previous studies have identified patient factors, procedural complexity, and perioperative coordination as key determinants of successful same-day discharge [14, 58, 59]. This means that nursing discharge management must be highly structured and closely linked to patient education, symptom recognition, and continuity of care. Similarly, follow-up systems are particularly important in urological day surgery, where urinary symptoms, wound-related issues, and anesthesia-related discomfort may emerge after patients return home. Gao et al. [32] emphasized the importance of prompt contact after discharge, and Tan et al. [60] showed that app-based interaction improved self-care ability and quality of life. These findings help explain why discharge preparation and follow-up were represented as dedicated task modules in the APP prototype.

At present, urological day-surgery nursing is moving toward more standardized, and intelligent quality management. Against this background, the app prototype developed in this study may serve as a preliminary platform for closed-loop nursing management. Importantly, the AI-driven pathway adopted in this study should be regarded as an assistive development strategy rather than an autonomous clinical solution. By grounding the prototype in a best-evidence summary and maintaining research-team oversight throughout content review and refinement, we sought to ensure that the AI application remained transparent, evidence-based, and clinically appropriate. This suggests that AI may have practical value in accelerating the translation of evidence into usable nursing tools, while still requiring human supervision. Consistent with this view, preliminary feedback from twelve clinical nurses indicated that the prototype may have practical relevance in perioperative nursing care for adult urology day surgery, particularly in standardizing nursing processes and strengthening evidence-based guidance for clinical practice. However, this small-scale survey should be interpreted as an exploratory assessment rather than formal validation. Therefore, before such AI-assisted tools can be applied more broadly in practice or expanded to advanced functions such as predictive analytics, interoperability with electronic health records, and adaptive learning for continuity of care and long-term monitoring [61, 62], further studies with larger samples are needed to evaluate their usability, feasibility, and clinical effectiveness.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, nursing management practices and healthcare systems differ across countries and institutions, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. Second, the present study focused on evidence synthesis and prototype development, and no formal clinical validation has yet been performed. Third, although the prototype was designed to support standardized perioperative nursing management, it does not currently provide fully personalized care based on individual patient characteristics, comorbidities, preferences, or risk profiles. Future studies should therefore include formal usability testing, feasibility assessment, and well-designed clinical trials, while also exploring more refined patient-specific adaptation functions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this two-phase study first synthesizes evidence-based nursing management practices for adult patients undergoing urological day surgery. Second, it demonstrates a practical, AI-driven pathway for turning this evidence summary into a functional digital application prototype. This integrated approach provides a valuable reference for optimizing nursing care and a tangible tool for clinical implementation. Future research should focus on resolving the constraints mentioned, including the clinical testing and iterative refinement of the app. By enhancing evidence-based practices with innovative digital translation pathways, we could improve the quality of care and patient outcomes in urological day surgery settings.

NMR 1H, 13C, 15N backbone resonance assignments of 14-3-3ζ binding region of human FOXO3a (residues 1-284)
Authors: Shota Enomoto, Shoichi Nakatsuka, Tomoya Kuwayama, Kosaku Kawatsu, Mariko Yokogawa, Masanori Osawa
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn tumors, mutation in Ras proteins stimulates a signaling cascade through phosphorylation. Downstream of the cascade, many transcription and translation factors are up- or down-regulated by phosphorylation, leading to cancer progression. This phosphorylation cascade is sustained by 14-3-3ζ protein. 14-3-3ζ binds to its client proteins that are Ser/Thr-phosphorylated and prevents their dephosphorylation. One of those transcription factors is FOXO3a, whose transcriptional activity is suppressed in the phosphorylation cascade. FOXO3a binds to specific DNA sequences and activates the transcription of apoptosis-related proteins. In cancer cells, however, FOXO3a is phosphorylated, bound to 14-3-3ζ, and dissociated from the DNA, resulting in FOXO3a inactivation. To elucidate the mechanism of FOXO3a inactivation by the 14-3-3ζ binding, we aim to perform NMR analysis of the interaction between 14-3-3ζ and di-phosphorylated FOXO3a residues 1-284 (dpFOXO3a). Here, we report the backbone resonance assignments of dpFOXO3a, which are transferred from those of the N-terminal domain (NTD) and the DNA-binding domain (DBD) of dpFOXO3a.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12104-024-10200-7.Keywords: FOXO3a, Intrinsically disordered region, DNA binding domain, Phosphorylation

Biological context

In cancer cells, mutation in Ras proteins stimulates a signaling cascade through phosphorylation. Downstream of the cascade, many transcription and translation factors are up- or down-regulated by phosphorylation, leading to uncontrolled cell proliferation, evasion of apoptosis, invasion and metastasis (Bahar et al. 2023). The phosphorylation cascade is sustained by 14-3-3ζ protein. 14-3-3ζ binds to its client proteins that are Ser/Thr-phosphorylated, and prevents their dephosphorylation, prolonging the functional regulation by the phosphorylation (Obsilova and Obsil 2022).

FOXO3a is one of those transcription factors inactivated in the phosphorylation cascade in cancer cells. FOXO3a, in normal cells, binds to specific DNA sequences (referred to as FOXO-responsive elements, FREs) upstream of promoter regions of apoptosis-related proteins (Brunet et al. 1999; Wang et al. 2009), inhibiting the cell proliferation through the expression of those proteins. In tumor cells, however, FOXO3a is phosphorylated by AKT kinase, bound by 14-3-3ζ protein, and released from the FREs, resulting in cancer progression through suppression of the apoptosis of the cancer cells (Brunet et al. 1999).

FOXO3a is a 71 kDa protein consisting of 673 residues and is a member of the forkhead box (FOX) protein family. FOXO3a is composed of a DNA-binding domain (DBD, also known as the forkhead domain) and other intrinsically disordered regions (Wang et al. 2008; Santos et al. 2023) (Fig. 1). FOXO3a is phosphorylated on T32, S253, and S315 by AKT. The phosphorylation motifs around T32 and S253 are recognized by 14-3-3ζ (Brunet et al. 1999), which is highly expressed in cancer cells (Yang et al. 2012). 14-3-3ζ is a 245-residue acidic protein with a molecular weight of 28 kDa and functions as a dimer (Dong et al. 1995).

The gray bars represent the constructs of the FOXO3a mutants used in this study. The intrinsically disordered region at the N-terminus was prepared as the N-terminal domain (NTD)

The mechanism of the dissociation of FOXO3a from the FREs upon the 14-3-3ζ binding has not been studied with FOXO3a, but has with the FOXO4, which has a similar structure to FOXO3a (Obsil and Obsilova 2008). In the previous study (Silhan et al. 2009), the binding mode of the mono-phosphorylated DBD of FOXO4 and a 14-3-3ζ monomer mutant was analyzed based on the distance constraints obtained from fluorescence resonance energy transfer experiments. The results suggested that, in the case of the interaction between di-phosphorylated FOXO4 and the 14-3-3ζ dimer, the DBD of FOXO4 would be located in the central channel between two subunits of the 14-3-3ζ dimer, and that the DNA-binding interface of the DBD is covered by 14-3-3ζ, which would competitively inhibit the interaction with the FREs. However, the direct interaction between the 14-3-3ζ dimer and the DBD of di-phosphorylated FOXO4 has not been observed experimentally, and therefore it remains unknown how di-phosphorylated FOXO proteins dissociate from the FREs upon binding of the 14-3-3ζ dimer.

To elucidate the mechanism of the FOXO3a inactivation by the 14-3-3ζ binding, we aim to analyze the interaction between di-phosphorylated FOXO3a and the 14-3-3ζ dimer using NMR. Here, we report the chemical shift assignments of the di-phosphorylated FOXO3a residues 1-284 (hereafter referred to as dpFOXO3a), which includes the DBD and two phosphorylated residues (pT32 and pS253) (Fig. 1). As dpFOXO3a possesses an unstructured N-terminal domain (NTD), a lot of NTD signals in the TROSY spectrum of dpFOXO3a overlap with the DBD signals, and thus, their assignments are ambiguous. Therefore, we firstly assigned NTD (residues 1-146), its mono-phosphorylated form referred to as pNTD, DBD (residues 141-284), and its mono-phosphorylated form referred to as pDBD. We then transferred the pNTD and pDBD assignments to the dpFOXO3a. Finally, we successfully assigned 80% of the residues of dpFOXO3a.

Methods and experiments

Protein preparation

The DNA sequence encoding human FOXO3a (residues 1-284) was cloned into the pGEX-6P-1 (Cytiva). In addition, the NTD (residues 1-146) and the DBD (residues 141-284) were generated according to the QuikChange® mutagenesis protocol (Stratagene, La Jolla, CA, USA) using the pGEX-6P-1-FOXO3a plasmid as the template (Fig. 1). The glutathione-S-transferase (GST)-fusion proteins were expressed in Escherichia coli C41 (DE3) cells. Uniformly 13C, 15N-labeled NTD and DBD for NMR experiments were expressed by growing the E. coli host cells in M9 minimal medium containing 1 g/L 15NH4Cl and 2 g/L 13C6-glucose. 2H, 15N-labeled FOXO3a (residues 1-284) was expressed by growing the E. coli host cells in M9 minimal medium in 2H2O containing 1 g/L 15NH4Cl and 2 g/L 2H-glucose. The expressions of FOXO3a, NTD, and DBD were induced by addition of isopropyl β-D-thiogalactopyranoside at a final concentration of 1 mM for 16–18 h at 20 ℃. The cells were collected by centrifugation and disrupted by sonication in a buffer containing phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) (pH 7.4), 1 M NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 10% glycerol, 1 mM phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride, Protease Inhibitor Cocktail (PIC) (Nacalai tesque), and 1 mM DTT. Cell debris was removed by centrifugation and 0.1% NP-40 was added to the supernatant, which was applied onto Glutathione-Sepharose™ 4B column (Cytiva) equilibrated with a buffer containing PBS pH 7.4, 500 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, and 1 mM DTT (for FOXO3a and NTD) or with a buffer containing 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 300 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, and 1 mM DTT (for DBD). The GST-fusion proteins were eluted with 20 mM reduced glutathione. The GST-tag was digested by PreScission Protease (Cytiva), and then, the cleaved GST and noncleaved fusion proteins were removed using a Glutathione-Sepharose™ 4B. Further purification was executed using an anion exchange chromatography (HiTrap Q™ 1 mL for FOXO3a, Capto™ HiRes Q 5/50 GL for NTD; Cytiva). FOXO3a was purified by gel filtration using Superdex™ 200 increase 10/300 GL (Cytiva) in a buffer containing 20 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 300 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA and 1 mM DTT. DBD was purified by gel filtration using HiLoad® 16/600 Superdex® 75 pg (Cytiva) in a buffer containing PBS pH 7.4, 300 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, and 1 mM DTT. The purified FOXO3a, NTD, and DBD were phosphorylated by AKT (500 µL of 100 µM target protein was mixed with 500 µL of 39.3 nM AKT in a buffer containing 25 mM MOPS pH 7.5, 12.5 mM β-glycerophosphoric acid, 25 mM MgCl2, 5 mM EGTA, 1 mM DTT, final 2 mM ATP and PIC). Phosphorylation reaction proceeded completely, confirmed by MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry, using MALDI-8020 spectrometer (SHIMADZU).

NMR experiments

NMR spectra were observed on Bruker Avance 600 or 800 MHz spectrometers equipped with a TXI or TCI probe. All NMR spectra were processed and analyzed using Bruker TopSpin 4.2.0 software and Sparky (T.D. Goddard and D.G. Kneller, Sparky 3.190, University of California), respectively. All experiments were performed at 303 K in buffer containing 20 mM NaPi pH 6.4, 100 mM NaCl, 1 mM DTT, and 8–10% 2H2O. Sequential assignments of the backbone NMR resonances for 13C, 15N-labeled NTD and pNTD were achieved using HNCO, HN(CA)CO, CACBCONH, HNCACB, and CC(CO)NH (Sattler et al. 1999). For the assignments of pNTD, (HN)CO(CO)NH (Yoshimura et al. 2015) was also observed. Sequential assignments of the backbone NMR resonances for 13C, 15N-labeled DBD were achieved using HNCO, HN(CA)CO, CACBCONH, HNCACB, and CC(CO)NH, and those for 13C, 15N-labeled pDBD was achieved HNCO, CACBCONH, and HNCACB experiments (Sattler et al. 1999).

Extent of assignments and data deposition

We first confirmed that the overlay of the 1H-15N HSQC spectra of pNTD and pDBD is essentially identical to the 1H-15N TROSY spectrum of dpFOXO3a (Online resource), by considering the chemical shift differences between HSQC and TROSY. Then, we assigned the backbone resonances of the NTD and DBD and their mono-phosphorylated forms (pNTD and pDBD) (Figs. 2, 3 and 4, and 5). Finally, we transferred the assignments of pNTD and pDBD to the spectrum of dpFOXO3a (residues 1-284) (Fig. 6).

Figure 2 shows the 1H-15N HSQC spectrum of NTD. NTD has a total of 151 residues, containing FOXO3a residues 1-146 and the cloning artifact GPLGS at the N-terminus. We assigned the backbone amide resonances of NTD for 101 out of the total 128 observable residues (79%) (Fig. 2), and that of pNTD for 104 residues (81%). Figure 3 shows the spectral overlay of the 1H-15N HSQC spectra between NTD and pNTD. The chemical shifts of 8 signals changed by more than 0.03 ppm by the phosphorylation on T32 (Q25, S26, R27, S30, pT32, W33, L35, and Q36) (Fig. 3).

Figure 4 shows the 1H-15N HSQC spectrum of DBD. DBD has a total of 149 residues, containing FOXO3a residues 141-284 and the cloning artifact GPLGS at the N-terminus. The backbone amide resonances of DBD for 132 out of the total 141 observable residues (94%) were assigned (Fig. 4). For the DBD assignment, we also referenced the FOXO3a residues 151-251 (BMRB: 15939) (Wang et al. 2008). Based on the DBD assignments and the triple resonance experiments of pDBD, we assigned the backbone amide resonances of pDBD for 132 out of the total 141 observable residues (94%). Figure 5 shows the spectral overlay of the 1H-15N HSQC spectra between DBD and pDBD. The chemical shifts of 12 signals changed by more than 0.03 ppm by the phosphorylation on S253 (R250, A251, V252, pS253, M254, S257, K259, Y260, T261, K262, S263, and R264) (Fig. 6). The chemical shift assignments for NTD, pNTD, DBD, and pDBD have been deposited in the Biological Magnetic Resonance Data Bank (https://bmrb.io/) with the accession number 26345, 26344, 26346, and 26347, respectively.

Next, we transferred the chemical shift assignments of pNTD and pDBD to the spectrum of dpFOXO3a. dpFOXO3a (residues 1-284) has a total of 289 residues, containing FOXO3a residues 1-284 and the cloning artifact GPLGS at the N-terminus. As shown in the figure S1 (Online resource), almost all signals of pNTD and pDBD overlapped with or existed nearby those of dpFOXO3a. The NMR signals for pT32 of pNTD and W157, G158, L160, Y162, H212, N237, D239, R250, and pS253 of pDBD were displaced from that of dpFOXO3a, but they were transferred based on their relative position to the surrounding signals (Online resource). The assignments of R27, E50 and L119 of pNTD and N155, A156, L165, S172, W186, I210, R219, R222, K230, R249, K262, A267, A268, A272, and A273 of pDBD were not transferred because the corresponding signals on the dpFOXO3a spectrum were not identified. G145 and Q146 of pNTD, which are in the C-terminus, and A141 of pDBD, which is next to the cloning artifact, did not overlap with the signals of dpFOXO3a. Therefore, the chemical shift assignments of pNTD (residues 1-144) and pDBD (residues 145-284) were transferred to the spectrum of dpFOXO3a. As a result, 95% out of the pNTD assignments and 89% out of the pDBD assignments were transferred. Finally, the backbone assignments of dpFOXO3a (residues 1-284) for 209 out of the total 260 observable residues (80%) were achieved (Fig. 6). The chemical shift assignments for dpFOXO3a have been deposited in the Biological Magnetic Resonance Data Bank (https://bmrb.io/) with the accession number 26350. These assignments will be utilized for analyzing the interaction between dpFOXO3a and 14-3-3ζ.

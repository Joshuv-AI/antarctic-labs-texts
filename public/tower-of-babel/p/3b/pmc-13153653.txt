Clinical Impact of the ‘Delta-Aortic Valve Gradient’, the Reduction in the Mean Aortic Valve Pressure Gradient After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation, on Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction Improvement and Long-Term Mortality in Patients With Severe Aortic Stenosis and Left Ventricular Dysfunction
Authors: Naoto Murakami, Kenichi Ishizu, Masaomi Hayashi, Shinichi Shirai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe relationship between the reduction in aortic valve pressure gradient (AVG) after transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) and improvements in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) or long-term survival remains unclear.Methods and ResultsWe retrospectively analyzed 121 patients with aortic stenosis (AS) and LVEF <50% who underwent TAVI. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) was performed before and after TAVI, and the difference in mean AVG was defined as delta-AVG. LVEF improvement was defined as a ≥10% increase from baseline at 1 year. Among 82 patients with 1-year TTE evaluation, LVEF improvement was observed in 37 (45.1%) patients. A higher delta-AVG was identified as an independent predictor of LVEF improvement (odds ratio 1.04; 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.01–1.07; P=0.002), and receiver operating characteristic analysis indicated the optimal cut-off was 20.5 mmHg (sensitivity 48.9%; specificity 89.2%; area under curve 0.723; P=0.002). During a mean follow up of 1,042.6±577.5 days, 5 cardiac deaths occurred. Cox proportional hazards analysis revealed that a low delta-AVG was identified as an independent predictor of cardiac death (hazard ratio 0.91; 95% CI 0.7–0.99; P=0.023). Additionally, a greater delta-AVG was significantly associated with a larger increase in delta-stroke volume between post-TAVI to 1 year (r=0.255; P=0.021).ConclusionsIn patients with severe AS and reduced LVEF, delta-AVG could be a novel predictor of 1-year LVEF improvement and long-term survival after TAVI.

Central Figure

The indication for transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) for severe aortic stenosis (AS) has expanded to include patients at low risk for surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR), and the number of patients undergoing TAVI has been increasing worldwide.1,2 Approximately 30% of AS patients exhibit reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF),3 and it has been shown that such patients have a poor prognosis compared with patients with preserved LVEF.4,5 Compared with SAVR, which requires cardiopulmonary bypass and circulatory arrest during the procedure, TAVI has been reported to increase LVEF in a relatively large number of patients,6 and patients in whom LVEF improved by >10% after TAVI have shown better long-term survival than patients without such improvement.7 However, the proportion of patients showing LVEF improvement after TAVI in previous studies has varied, ranging from 32% to 62%,3,7,8 and it remains unclear which patients are expected to have improvement in cardiac function after TAVI.

Previous studies have shown that a high baseline mean aortic valve pressure gradient (AVG) is an independent predictor of LVEF improvement at 30 days after TAVI, regardless of whether a balloon-expandable valve (BEV) or a self-expanding valve (SEV) was used.3,8 This suggests that LVEF improves as a result of relieving valvular obstruction and reducing AVG. However, few studies have directly examined the changes in pressure data before and after TAVI, and in particular, the impact of AVG reduction on chronic-phase LVEF improvement remains unclear. The aim of this study was to determine the impact of delta-AVG, the reduction in AVG after TAVI, on LVEF improvement and long-term clinical outcomes in patients with severe AS and reduced LVEF.

Methods

Study Population

The flowchart of patient selection is shown in Figure 1. From October 2013 to August 2019, 803 consecutive patients with symptomatic AS were treated using the TAVI procedure in Kokura Memorial Hospital. Among these patients, 664 cases with an LVEF of ≥50% based on preoperative transthoracic echocardiography (TTE), 1 emergency case in which preoperative TTE could not be performed, and 17 cases in which TAVI was performed via the transapical approach, which has been suggested to be associated with LVEF reduction after TAVI, were excluded. Patients on dialysis were also excluded, as TAVI had not been approved for them during the enrollment period. Data for the remaining 121 patients with reduced LVEF<50% were analyzed in the present study. Figure 1.Flowchart of patient selection. This study conformed to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Kokura Memorial Hospital Clinical Research Ethics Committee. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before the TAVI procedure.

TAVI Procedures and Follow up

The indication for TAVI was determined by the consensus of the heart team at Kokura Memorial Hospital. The procedure was performed via the transfemoral (TF), trans-iliac, trans-subclavian, or direct aorta approach based on preoperative computed tomography (CT) imaging, which was evaluated to assess the tortuosity and vessel diameter of the access route. For TF cases, patients who were elderly or had comorbid respiratory diseases were actively managed under local anesthesia. The selection of a BEV or a SEV was made at the discretion of the operator, and the prosthesis size was determined on the basis of the size and calcification of the annulus and aortic-valvular complex, including the sinus of Valsalva and the sinotubular junction. Post-dilation was performed when moderate or greater paravalvular leakage (PVL) was detected after valve implantation. Procedural outcomes were assessed according to the Valve Academic Research Consortium (VARC)-2 criteria.9 After discharge, the incidence of adverse events was followed up through outpatient visits or telephone contact at 30 days, 6 months, and annually thereafter.

Echocardiographic Assessment and Definition of LVEF Improvement

LVEF measurement was primarily based on the modified Simpson method in TTE. In cases in which accurate tracing in 2 planes for the Simpson method was difficult, the Teicholz method was used as a substitute. TTE data were measured by a specialized ultrasonographer, and double-checking was performed by certified echocardiographers to minimize inter-observer variabilities. According to previous reports,3,7,8 LVEF improvement was defined as an increase of ≥10% from the baseline. Postoperative TTE was performed before discharge to compare the mean AVG (mAVG) before and after TAVI, and the difference between the 2 values is expressed here as ‘delta-AVG’. After discharge, TTE was repeatedly performed at 30 days and 1 year after TAVI to assess temporal changes in LVEF. Of the 121 analyzed cases, TTE was performed in 112 (92.6%) cases at 30 days and in 82 (67.8%) cases at 1 year.

Study Endpoints

The primary endpoint of the present study was 1-year LVEF improvement, defined as an increase of ≥10% in LVEF from baseline to 1 year after TAVI, and the secondary endpoint was cardiac death during the follow-up period. The associations between these endpoints and delta-AVG was evaluated. Cardiac death was defined according to the VARC-2 criteria.9

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as mean±standard deviation, and categorical variables are presented as number and percentage. Differences between the 2 groups were tested using the Student’s t-test for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. Multivariate logistic regression models and Cox proportional hazards models were used to assess the independent prognostic values of variables. All statistical analyses were performed using JMP 17 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). A P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical and Procedural Characteristics of Patients

The characteristics of the 121 patients are shown in Table 1. The mean age of the patients was 84.3±6.1 years, and 62 (51.2%) patients were men. The mean Society of Thoracic Surgeons predicted risk of mortality was 10.08±8.18%, reflecting high surgical risk of this cohort. Nine (7.4%) patients presented with cardiogenic shock before TAVI, and emergency or urgent TAVI was performed in 22 (18.2%) patients. The mean baseline LVEF was 41.7±6.4%, and the baseline mAVG was 42.2±19.6 mmHg. Sixty-one (50.4%) patients met the criteria for classical low-flow low-gradient (LG) severe AS, defined as mAVG <40 mmHg. The TF approach was selected in 113 (93.4%) patients, while alternative approaches including trans-iliac, trans-subclavian and direct aorta approaches were selected in 8 (6.6%) patients. A BEV was implanted in 104 (86.0%) patients and a SEV was implanted in 17 (14.0%) patients. The mean LVEF after TAVI was 45.0±8.2%, and mean delta-AVG was 32.4±18.8 mmHg. Clinical Outcomes

In-hospital and overall clinical outcomes of the entire cohort are shown in Supplementary Table 1. The mean length of hospital stay was 24.9±22.5 days, and in-hospital mortality occurred in 1 (0.8%) case, which was a cardiac death due to worsening heart failure after TAVI. Disabling stroke occurred in 3 (2.5%) cases, and permanent pacemaker implantation was performed in 5 (4.1%) cases before discharge. At 30 days and 1 year after TAVI, all-cause death had occurred in 2 (1.7%) and 16 (13.2%) patients, respectively. Including follow-up time after discharge, the overall mean follow-up duration was 1,042.6±577.5 days, during which there were 48 all-cause deaths and 5 cardiac deaths.

Time Course of LVEF Improvement

The time course of mean LVEF for the entire cohort from baseline to 1-year follow up is shown in Figure 2. Since this cohort was limited to patients with reduced cardiac function, baseline mean LVEF was low at 41.7±6.4%. Subsequently, mean LVEF increased to 45.0±8.2% immediately after TAVI and then to 47.2±8.1% at 30 days and 51.6±9.8% at 1 year, showing a gradual improvement even in the late phase. Of the 121 cases analyzed, TTE was performed in 112 (92.6%) patients at 30 days after TAVI. However, 16 (13.2%) patients had died and 23 (19.0%) patients had been lost to follow up within 1 year, leaving only 82 (67.8%) patients for TTE evaluation at the 1-year follow up. The proportion of patients with an LVEF improvement of 10% or more compared with baseline LVEF was 15.7% (19/121 patients) immediately after TAVI, increasing to 22.3% (25/112 patients) at 30 days and further to 45.1% (37/82 patients) at 1 year. Predictors of 1-Year LVEF Improvement

The results of logistic regression analysis for 1-year LVEF improvement after TAVI are shown in Table 3. Neither a history of previous myocardial infarction nor pre- and post-operative LVEF values were associated with LVEF improvement at 1 year. In multivariate analysis using the presence of a residual coronary artery stenosis and moderate or greater pre-existing aortic regurgitation (AR) as explanatory variables, both a higher baseline mAVG (adjusted odds ratio [OR] 1.04; 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.01–1.06; P=0.004) and a higher delta-AVG (adjusted OR 1.04; 95% CI 1.01–1.07; P=0.002) were identified as independent predictors of LVEF improvement at 1-year, along with residual coronary artery stenosis. Compared with Model 1, which used baseline mAVG (R2=0.150; corrected Akaike information criterion [AICc] 104.5), Model 2, which used delta-AVG (R2=0.163; AICc 103.0), showed a slightly lower AICc. None of the in-hospital complications during and after TAVI were associated with LVEF improvement. Of the 3 patients who experienced disabling stroke, 1 died of pneumonia within 1 year, and the other 2 were transferred to another hospital, making follow-up TTE assessment and evaluation of 1-year LVEF improvement impossible. Clinical Impact of Delta-AVG

Among the 82 patients in whom TTE was performed at 1 year after TAVI, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis revealed that the optimal cut-off value of delta-AVG for predicting 1-year LVEF improvement after TAVI was 20.5 mmHg, of which sensitivity and specificity were 48.9% and 89.2%, respectively (area under the curve [AUC] 0.723; P=0.002; Figure 3A). Using this cut-off value, the 82 patients were divided into 2 groups, and the prevalence of 1-year LVEF improvement was compared. In the group with delta-AVG <20.5 mmHg, 16.0% of patients showed 1-year LVEF improvement, whereas in the group with delta-AVG ≥20.5 mmHg, the prevalence was significantly higher at 57.9% (P=0.004; Figure 3B). Furthermore, using the same cut-off value to stratify all 121 patients, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis after TAVI showed that the high delta-AVG group (≥20.5 mmHg) had a significantly lower rate of long-term cardiac death than that in the low delta-AVG group (<20.5 mmHg; log-rank P=0.010; Figure 3C). Cox Proportional Hazards Analysis to Predict Long-Term Cardiac Death

The results of Cox proportional hazards analysis for cardiac death are shown in Table 4. In multivariate analysis adjusted for age, sex and peripheral artery disease, a high delta-AVG was identified as an independent predictor of cardiac death after TAVI (adjusted hazard ratio 0.91; 95% CI 0.79–0.99; P=0.023). There were no cardiac deaths during the follow-up period among the 86 patients without diabetes, suggesting that the presence of diabetes may also be associated with an increased risk of cardiac death. In addition, no cardiac death was observed among 48 patients who had mild or greater PVL on postoperative TTE. Furthermore, no cardiac deaths occurred among patients with residual coronary artery stenosis, pre-existing moderate or greater AR, those treated with an alternative approach, or those who underwent SEV implantation. However, the small sample size may have influenced these results. In-hospital complications after TAVI were not associated with long-term cardiac death. Similarly, no cardiac deaths were observed among patients who developed disabling stroke or those who required permanent pacemaker implantation after TAVI. However, the small sample size may also have affected these findings. Delta-AVG Correlations With Delta-Stroke Volume (SV) and Delta-LVEF

To clarify the mechanism underlying the association between delta-AVG and LVEF improvement, we analyzed its correlations with delta-SV, an indicator of improved cardiac output, and with delta-LVEF, separately for the acute phase (pre- and post-TAVI) and the chronic phase (post-TAVI to 1 year). Although the mean SV after TAVI was significantly higher compared with before TAVI (57.0±15.6 vs. 64.3±15.2 mL; P=0.003), acute-phase delta-SV was not associated with delta-AVG. Indeed, it tended to decrease as delta-AVG increased. In contrast, a larger delta-AVG was significantly associated with a greater chronic-phase delta-SV from post-TAVI to 1 year (r=0.255; P=0.021), and although not statistically significant, chronic-phase delta-LVEF also showed a tendency to increase (Figure 4A). Because delta-SV is influenced by preoperative flow status, a subgroup analysis was performed stratifying patients into a high-gradient (HG) group (baseline mAVG ≥40 mmHg) and a LG group (baseline mAVG <40 mmHg) based on baseline mAVG (Figure 4B,C). In the HG group, no significant correlation was observed between delta-AVG and delta-SV, although chronic-phase delta-SV tended to increase. Unexpectedly, delta-AVG was not significantly correlated with either acute- or chronic-phase delta-LVEF. Similarly, in the LG group, no significant correlation was observed between delta-AVG and delta-SV. However, chronic-phase delta-SV showed an increasing trend, and chronic-phase delta-LVEF was significantly greater in patients with higher delta-AVG (r=0.337; P=0.039).

Predictors of 1-Year LVEF Improvement in the HG and LG Groups

The results of univariate logistic regression analyses for 1-year LVEF improvement in the HG and LG groups are shown in Supplementary Table 2. In the HG group, no significant association was observed between delta-AVG and 1-year LVEF improvement, and only low baseline LVEF was associated with LVEF improvement (OR 0.85; 95% CI 0.72–0.97; P=0.018; Supplementary Table 2A). In contrast, in the LG group, only delta-AVG was significantly associated with 1-year LVEF improvement (OR 1.16; 95% CI 1.04–1.33; P=0.007). Additionally, all 5 patients with residual coronary lesions did not exhibit LVEF improvement (Supplementary Table 2B). In both groups, the small number of patients with peripheral artery disease or in-hospital complications after TAVI precluded statistical analysis. In the LG group, ROC analysis of delta-AVG for predicting 1-year LVEF improvement indicated an optimal cut-off value of 15.3 mmHg (sensitivity 61.5%; specificity 91.7%; AUC 0.763; P=0.007; Supplementary Figure).

Discussion

The main finding of this study is that delta-AVG was identified as a novel independent predictor for 1-year LVEF improvement, with an optimal cut-off value of 20.5 mmHg. In patients with severe AS and reduced LVEF, delta-AVG also affects long-term mortality after TAVI, as patients with high delta-AVG might expect favorable outcomes. This study might be the first study to demonstrate the impact of delta-AVG on clinical outcomes after TAVI. As previous reports have suggested that a higher baseline AVG is associated with LVEF improvement after TAVI,10 the results of this study suggest that a sufficient reduction in AVG following TAVI may play an important role in the mechanism of LVEF improvement. Although the proportion of patients showing LVEF improvement after TAVI varied widely, ranging from 32% to 62% in previous reports,3,7,8 these differences may be related, at least in part, to the magnitude of delta-AVG.

AS leads to prolonged afterload mismatch, resulting in a gradual decrease in LVEF.11–13 However, myocardial contractility itself does not decrease in the early stages of AS.14,15 TAVI does not immediately improve myocardial contractility,16,17 but as a result of the relief of afterload, LVEF increases after TAVI. Based on the relationships among delta-AVG, delta-SV, and delta-LVEF in this study, a larger acute-phase delta-AVG was associated with an increase in acute-phase delta-LVEF. However, because acute-phase LVEF improvement occurs without recovery of SV, consistent with previous reports, this does not necessarily reflect an actual improvement in left ventricular (LV) myocardial contractility. Instead, TAVI likely relieves the flow limitation, and the observed increase in LVEF may simply reflect a decrease in LV pressure in the acute phase. In contrast, a larger delta-AVG was significantly associated with an increase in chronic-phase delta-SV, and chronic-phase delta-LVEF also tended to increase. These findings suggest that delta-AVG may be related to LV reverse remodeling in the chronic phase. Although previous studies have reported an association between baseline mAVG and acute-phase LVEF improvement,3,8 the delta-AVG highlighted in the present study may serve less as an indicator of acute-phase cardiac output improvement and more as a surrogate marker for chronic-phase LVEF improvement. Notably, in the LG group with baseline mAVG <40 mmHg, a larger delta-AVG was associated with significantly greater chronic-phase delta-LVEF. This suggests that delta-AVG may be useful for risk stratification in patients with classic low-flow, LG severe AS, who are generally considered to have a poor prognosis. In contrast, numerous factors have been reported to be associated with the mechanism of LVEF reduction in AS patients,18–23 and acute-phase hemodynamics is therefore not the only factor that contributes to LVEF improvement after TAVI. Assessment of late gadolinium enhancement or extracellular volume fraction using preoperative cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) or contrast-enhanced CT imaging has been reported to enable detection of irreversible myocardial damage that cannot be predicted by delta-AVG alone.24–27 Thus, CMR or CT imaging should be considered when performing TAVI in AS patients with reduced LVEF.

The delta-AVG focused on in this study is strongly correlated with baseline mAVG, and its significance in predicting LVEF improvement and long-term prognosis may be similar to that of the baseline mAVG. In contrast, previous studies have shown that among patients with severe AS and reduced LVEF, those in the LG group have worse outcomes than those in the HG group, although their prognosis can be improved by undergoing AVR.28 These findings suggest that the prognosis of AS patients is not determined solely by the baseline mAVG. Instead, what appears to be fundamentally important is not the ‘high baseline mAVG’, but the ‘reduction in mAVG achieved by TAVI’. Therefore, assessing the degree of hemodynamic improvement using delta-AVG may be useful for evaluating the therapeutic efficacy of TAVI. For cases with a low baseline mAVG, selecting a SEV over a BEV with the expectation of a large delta-AVG, or selecting a larger prosthetic valve in borderline-sized cases might be a reasonable strategy. Furthermore, evaluating delta-AVG during the TAVI procedure might be useful for determining whether to perform additional post-dilation. For cases with bulky leaflet calcification, ensuring sufficient delta-AVG intraoperatively may eliminate the need for additional post-dilation, avoiding the risk of annular rupture. Thus, as a clinical implication of this study, delta-AVG is expected to aid in determining the procedural strategy and endpoint.

Study Limitations

There are several limitations in the present study. First, this study was a non-randomized observational study including a retrospective analysis; therefore, potential biases in patient selection and treatment strategies cannot be excluded. Second, as there was no core laboratory for TTE, inter-observer variability in echocardiographic evaluation may have influenced the results. Third, previous studies have shown that LV strain echocardiography or CMR imaging enables more accurate assessment of LV contractility than does LVEF alone.29,30 However, in this study, only LVEF was evaluated as an indicator of LV contractility, which may have led to missing subtle improvement in cardiac function. Last, only patients with AS and reduced LVEF, who showed a poorer prognosis compared with that in recent TAVI registries, were analyzed in this study. Therefore, the follow-up data were relatively limited, and some of the most severe cases with fatal outcomes were not included in the long-term analysis.

Conclusions

Delta-AVG, the reduction in the mean pressure gradient at the aortic valve position evaluated using TTE before and after TAVI, could be a novel predictor of 1-year LVEF improvement and long-term survival after TAVI, with an optimal cut-off value of 20.5 mmHg. In patients with severe AS and reduced LVEF, delta-AVG may serve as a useful indicator for risk stratification and for evaluating the therapeutic efficacy of TAVI.

The bidirectional genetic causality between immunocyte phenotypes and dilated cardiomyopathy: A bidirectional Mendelian randomization study
Authors: Wenshuai Feng, Xindi Chang, Qiaozhi Li, Zhengwei Dong, Ge Jin, Jinyu Huang, Genhao Fan, Zuoying Xing, Yongxia Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The association between immunocyte phenotypes and dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) has been explored, however the exact pathogenesis of the relationship between immune cells and DCM is unclear. This bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) research aims to further validate the causal link between 731 immunocyte phenotypes and DCM. Summary statistics from a genome-wide association study data of individuals with European ancestry were utilized, including 1444 DCM cases and 353,937 controls, as well as 3757 European adults for the 731 immunocyte phenotypes. Causal effects were estimated using inverse variance weighted, MR-Egger regression, weight median estimator, weighted mode, and simple mode. Sensitivity analysis was conducted to confirm data robustness and feasibility. Based on the inverse variance weighted findings, 14 immunocyte phenotypes were risk factors for DCM (P < .05, odds ratio [OR] > 1), while 15 immunocyte phenotypes exhibited a protective effect on DCM (P < .05, OR < 1). The results of reverse MR analysis suggested evidence that DCM occurrence might elevate the levels of 17 immunocyte phenotypes (P < .05, OR > 1) and decrease the levels of 9 immunocyte phenotypes (P < .05, OR < 1). Our research indicated that CD28 on secreting regulatory T cell could mitigate the occurrence of DCM, and reciprocally, the progression of DCM could reduce the level of CD28 on secreting regulatory T cell. This study confirmed the bidirectional genetic predictive relationship between immunocyte phenotypes and DCM, underscoring the complex interplay between DCM and the immune system.

1. Introduction

Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is a primary myocardial disease characterized by left ventricular or biventricular dilation and impaired systolic function.[1] It represents a leading cause of nonischemic heart failure and remains the most common indication for heart transplantation worldwide, imposing a massive clinical and economic burden.[2] Despite significant advancements in guideline-directed medical therapy and device interventions, the long-term prognosis for patients with DCM remains suboptimal, with a high risk of progressive heart failure and sudden cardiac death.[3,4] The pathogenesis of DCM is highly heterogeneous, encompassing genetic mutations, viral infections, exposure to toxins, and autoimmune disorders.[5,6] Over the past decades, accumulating evidence has highlighted that immune system dysregulation and chronic myocardial inflammation play pivotal roles in the initiation and progression of adverse cardiac remodeling.[7] Specifically, the transition from acute viral myocarditis to chronic DCM is largely driven by persistent immune activation and autoimmune responses, underscoring the critical involvement of the immune system in this debilitating condition.[8]

Immunocyte phenotypes, which encompass the diverse compositional, morphological, and functional states of immune cells, such as T cells, B cells, natural killer (NK) cells, and monocytes/macrophages, are critical orchestrators of the cardiac immune microenvironment.[9,10] Recent studies have demonstrated that the aberrant activation, proliferation, and myocardial infiltration of specific immune cell subsets are central to DCM pathogenesis.[11] Among these, CD4 + T cell subpopulations, particularly T helper 17 cells (Th17), emerge as pivotal drivers of the inflammatory milieu in DCM. Studies have demonstrated that the hyperactivation of CD4 + T cells triggers an overproduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines, culminating in aberrant cardiomyocyte apoptosis and extensive myocardial fibrosis.[9] An imbalance between pro-inflammatory Th17 and immunosuppressive regulatory T cells (Tregs) has been implicated in myocardial fibrosis, while hyperactive B cells contribute to the production of cardiotoxic autoantibodies.[12] During the initial phases of DCM, cardiomyocyte necrosis provokes the robust infiltration of inflammatory cells, thereby hyperactivating Th17 pathways. In response, Tregs transiently upregulate their suppressive functions to mitigate Th17-induced damage, conferring temporary cardioprotection.[12,13] Conversely, the failing heart in DCM is not merely a passive victim; due to mechanical stress and cardiomyocyte necrosis, it actively releases damage-associated molecular patterns and pro-inflammatory cytokines.[14,15] This process can systemically alter peripheral immune cell profiles, potentially creating a vicious cycle of immune-mediated cardiac injury and systemic inflammation.[16]

Despite these critical insights into the immune-mediated mechanisms of cardiac remodeling, the precise causal hierarchy between specific immunocyte phenotypes and the pathogenesis of DCM remains largely ambiguous. However, traditional observational studies evaluating the association between peripheral immune cells and DCM are inherently limited.[17] These studies are highly susceptible to potential confounding variables, such as age, lifestyle factors, comorbidities, and concurrent immunomodulatory or heart failure medications.[18] More importantly, they struggle to resolve the issue of reverse causation. Consequently, it remains largely elusive whether the altered immunocyte phenotypes are the primary causal drivers of DCM pathogenesis or merely a secondary systemic response to heart failure and cardiac remodeling. Clarifying the direction of this causality is of paramount importance, as it dictates whether specific immune cells should be targeted to prevent disease progression or merely monitored as biomarkers of disease severity.

Mendelian randomization (MR) has emerged as a robust analytical approach to infer causal relationships between exposures and outcomes in epidemiology.[19] By utilizing single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) as instrumental variables (IVs), MR minimizes the impact of environmental confounders and circumvents reverse causality, as genetic variants are randomly allocated at conception and cannot be altered by disease onset.[20] While previous MR studies[21] have explored the causal links between broad inflammatory biomarkers and various cardiovascular diseases, a comprehensive, high-resolution causal assessment between specific, multi-trait immunocyte phenotypes and DCM is still lacking. Furthermore, given the intricate crosstalk between the heart and the immune system, a bidirectional approach is essential to fully untangle their complex relationship.

We conducted a comprehensive bidirectional two-sample MR study. Leveraging large-scale, publicly available genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics encompassing over 731 distinct immunocyte phenotypes, including absolute counts, relative proportions, and surface antigen expression levels, we aimed to systematically investigate the bidirectional genetic causality between these immune traits and the risk of DCM. Elucidating this bidirectional causal network will not only advance our understanding of the immune-inflammatory mechanisms underlying DCM at a cellular resolution but also provide novel, genetically supported insights for developing targeted immunotherapies and precision medicine strategies.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study design

We employed a two-way two-sample MR approach to examine the reciprocal causality between 731 immunocyte phenotypes (divided into 7 groups) and DCM. The immunocyte phenotypes served as the exposure factor, SNPs associated with DCM were used as IVs, and DCM was the outcome variable. To guarantee the accuracy of our findings, we performed heterogeneity tests and pleiotropy analysis. We carried out reverse MR analysis, with DCM as the exposure factor and immunocyte phenotypes as the outcome variable. The MR analysis was based on 3 main assumptions: a strong correlation between IVs and exposure, independence of selected IVs from confounders, and IVs only influencing outcomes through exposure (no pleiotropy).[22] The overall design of this study is depicted in Figure 1. 2.2. Data sources

We obtained 731 peripheral blood immunocyte phenotypes (GCST0001391–GCST0002121) from the GWAS catalog database (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/).[23] Summary data of immunocyte phenotypes was based on a European population, containing 3757 adults tested for approximately 220,000 SNPs, with adjustments made for sex and age. Immunocyte phenotypes are labeled utilizing particular antibodies that target cell surface markers or proteins of interest. These labeled cells are subsequently evaluated using flow cytometry or other imaging techniques to quantify and characterize the different immune cell populations present in the samples.[23] The 731 immune cells included 118 absolute cell counts, 389 median fluorescence intensity, 32 morphological parameters, and 192 relative cell counts.[23] The 7 types of immune cells included B cells, CDCs, mature stages of T cells, monocytes, myeloid cells, TBNK and Treg panels (Content S1, Supplemental Digital Content 1).

Compared with other immune GWAS resources, this dataset offers unparalleled phenotypic breadth, covering a comprehensive spectrum of immune cell subsets, surface markers, and functional characteristics. This extensive coverage supports our large-scale screening strategy aimed at identifying key immune factors associated with DCM. In addition, all data were generated using unified experimental and analytical protocols, ensuring high internal consistency and robust data quality. Importantly, there was no sample overlap between participants in the immune GWAS and those included in the DCM GWAS, thereby minimizing potential bias and satisfyingcore MR methodological assumptions. Overall, this high-resolution, well-validated, and standardized dataset is particularly well suited for investigating the bidirectional genetic associations between comprehensive immune profiles and DCM, and is superior to smaller or less systematically characterized alternatives.

The DCM dataset (ebi-a-GCST90018834) were extracted from cross-population meta-analysis, which integrated data from nationwide biobank in Japan, UK Biobank, and Finnish biobanks. The dataset included a total of 355,381 European and 178,162 East Asian ancestry participants, consisting of 1444 European ancestry cases, 353,937 European ancestry controls, 417 East Asian ancestry cases, and 177,745 East Asian ancestry controls.[24] Utilizing IMPUTE4 software10, genotypes were inferred in conjunction with the Haplotype Reference Consortium, UK10K, and the 1000 Genomes Project Phase 3 Reference Panel. With the help of quality control and meta-analysis, pooled data of 19,080,278 SNPs were identified. These data can be found in the the IEU OpenGWAS project data platform (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/). The GWAS dataset for DCM integrated samples of both European and East Asian ancestry; however, the overall sample size and statistical power of East Asian subsets were relatively limited compared with European cohorts.

2.3. Selection of IVs

To confirm the validity of the MR study, we extracted only SNPs strongly associated with the exposure (immunocyte phenotypes or DCM). We evaluated the strength of individual SNPs or sets of SNPs using the F-statistic and assessed the proportion of variance of the exposure explained by the IV using the R2 statistic.[25] These measurements were utilized to confirm the reliability and strength of the IVs in the MR study. IVs were considered reliable and robust if the total R2 was ≥ 0.01 (1%) and the mean F-statistic exceeded 10, indicating no substantial weak instrument bias.

NB: N: the sample sizes of the exposure, and the K represents the number of SNPs which was also associated with the depth of GWAS. The MAF indicates for minor allele frequency, and the R2 represents the magnitude of the effect of the SNP on the exposure.[26]

In the forward analysis, we selected independent SNPs with P < 1 × 10−5 for the immunocyte phenotypes as IVs. We deleted SNPs with linkage disequilibrium (r2 > 0.001 within physical distance of 10,000 kb), palindromic or ambiguous characteristics.[27] To avoid weak instrumental bias, we applied the F statistic to quantify the strength of the IVs associated with the exposure. SNPs with F < 10, which were considered weak IVs, excluded for the analysis.[28] In the reverse analysis, independent SNPs with P < 5 × 10−6 related to DCM were identified as IVs utilizing the same screening criteria as the forward MR analysis.

To assess the reciprocal causal relationship between immunocyte phenotypes and DCM, we executed the inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method combined with Wald ratios as the primary causal estimation method. Additionally, we supplemented this analysis with MR-Egger regression, the weight median estimator, weighted mode, and simple mode to improve the robustness of the IVW causal reasoning. The results obtained from the IVW method are more conservative and realistic, maintaining robustness even in the presence of heterogeneity in the IVs. For reliability, we required that the IVW results be significant and in the same direction as the results of the supplementary method.

2.5. Sensitivity analysis

The sensitivity analysis includes heterogeneity and horizontal pleiotropy tests. To assess heterogeneity among the data, we utilized Cochran Q test, with a significance level of P < .05 indicating heterogeneity. In such circumstances, we performed the final MR analysis using the fixed effect IVW approach. We also performed MR-Egger regression to identify potential directional pleiotropy, and a value of the intercept term close to 0 and P > .05 indicated the absence of horizontal pleiotropy for the SNPs.[30] We further used MR pleiotropy residuals and outliers (MR-PRESSO) to identify outliers affected by pleiotropy. A P > .05 for the Global test indicated no pleiotropy. When outliers were present, we eliminated SNPs for horizontal pleiotropy and then performed MR analysis. Pleiotropy was visually assessed using funnel plots, which indicated no underlying heterogeneity of SNPs when the plots were symmetric. Finally, we conducted a leave-one-out test to determine whether the causal effect was influenced by individual strong IVs.[31]

2.6. Statistical analysis

For the above analysis, we employed “TwosampleMR” and “MR-PRESSO” packages in R 4.3.3 and R Studio software. The effect sizes were expressed as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI), and statistical significance was set at the 5% level with bilateral P values. For exploratory screening of 731 high‑dimensional immune cell phenotypes, nominal P < .05 was used to identify suggestive causal associations. False discovery rate correction was performed but not adopted as the primary threshold due to its excessive conservativeness in hypothesis‑generating analyses. Consistency across multiple MR methods and sensitivity analyses were required to minimize false‑positive risk.

3.6. Result of reverse sensitivity analysis

The results of the Cochran Q test (P > .05) revealed no heterogeneity among SNPs associated with DCM. Furthermore, neither the MR-Egger nor MR-PRESSO methods detected horizontal pleiotropy (Table 3). 4. Discussion

Through a bidirectional two-sample MR investigation, we systematically evaluated the causal relationships between 731 immune cell phenotypes and the risk of DCM. Our forward MR analysis identified 29 immunophenotypes causally associated with DCM susceptibility, while the reverse MR analysis revealed that genetically predicted DCM could, in turn, influence the levels of 26 immunophenotypes. Notably, our findings highlighted a robust bidirectional relationship involving CD28 on secreting Tregs: genetically predicted increases in this subset significantly attenuated DCM risk, whereas the progression of DCM reciprocally diminished its levels. This suggests that the depletion of CD28+ on secreting Tregs plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis and progression of DCM.

A recent MR study explored the causal relationship between broad categories of immune cells and DCM, identifying increased total lymphocytes as a protective factor, while elevated basophils and eosinophils were associated with an increased risk of DCM.[32] While their findings provide a valuable macroscopic perspective, broad categorizations such as “total lymphocytes” may oversimplify the underlying biology and mask the potentially conflicting effects of specific cell subsets.

In contrast to the generalized protective effect of total lymphocytes reported in prior studies, our high-resolution analysis reveals a more complex and nuanced landscape. We found that specific lymphocyte subsets, such as CD45RA on naive CD4+ T cells and certain B cell subsets, actually act as risk factors for DCM. Conversely, protective effects are highly restricted to specific functional states, such as CD28 on secreting Tregs. This critical discrepancy highlights that immune-mediated pathogenesis in DCM is likely driven by specific phenotypic shifts and functional states rather than mere fluctuations in broad immune cell populations. The opposing effects within these subsets might also explain why some previous observational studies focusing on general leukocyte counts yielded conflicting or null results. By leveraging highly granular exposure data, our study bridges this critical gap in the existing literature.

Specifically, our forward analysis identified 14 immunophenotypes as risk factors for DCM. Consistent with our findings, previous observational evidence indicates that CD4 Treg and CD45RA naive CD4+ T cells are deeply implicated in DCM pathogenesis.[33] Clinical studies have demonstrated that peripheral CD4+ Treg counts are significantly elevated in DCM patients compared to healthy controls, with higher levels positively correlating with advanced NYHA functional classes (III–IV). Elevated CD4+ Treg can exacerbate myocardial injury by promoting cytotoxic T cell responses, stimulating B cell activation, and enhancing the production of anti-myocardial antibodies.[34,35] Consequently, monitoring peripheral CD4+ Treg levels could serve as a valuable prognostic tool for assessing DCM severity. Furthermore, elevated levels of CD45RA on naive CD4+ T cells have been positively correlated with disease severity in acute coronary syndrome.[36] Typically, antigen stimulation drives the differentiation of these naive cells into memory T cells (CD4+CD45RO+), which rapidly secrete cytokines to orchestrate immune responses.[37] However, impaired differentiation of CD45RA+ naive T cells in ischemic contexts can lead to localized immune dysregulation and exacerbated cardiomyocyte damage, potentially culminating in adverse ventricular remodeling and DCM.[38] While indirect evidence links these naive subsets to DCM development, the precise molecular mechanisms warrant further experimental validation.

Conversely, our forward MR analysis revealed 15 immunophenotypes that confer protective effects against DCM. Previous research has shown that CD25 on memory B cells, that a unique population of immunomodulatory B cells, acts as a potent antigen, presenting subset that induces T cell proliferation and promotes the secretion of interleukin-10 (IL-10).[39] Correspondingly, plasma IL-10 levels are significantly depleted in DCM patients.[40] As a robust anti-inflammatory factor, IL-10 protects the myocardium by inhibiting the adhesion and infiltration of inflammatory monocytes and lymphocytes.[41,42] Additionally, CD27 and CD38 were identified as protective factors, corroborating previous bioinformatics research by You et al, which pinpointed CD27 and CD38 as core genes in DCM pathogenesis.[43] These genes are significantly enriched in biological pathways governing immune cell adhesion, activation, and differentiation, underscoring their indispensable role in maintaining cardiac immune homeostasis.

A hallmark finding of our study is the identification of CD28 on secreting Tregs as a key protective mediator against DCM. CD28 co-stimulation is indispensable for maintaining the survival, phenotypic stability, and immunosuppressive function of Tregs.[44] Accumulating preclinical evidence demonstrates that CD28 signaling sustains the capacity of this specific Treg subset to secrete anti-inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-10 and TGF-β, which potently suppress excessive myocardial inflammation, attenuate pathological cardiac fibrosis, and limit adverse ventricular remodeling – core pathological features of DCM.[45] Crucially, our bidirectional MR analysis not only confirmed this protective role but also revealed a reciprocal causal signal: the pathological state of DCM may, in turn, impair the activity of this protective subset, potentially forming a self-perpetuating pathogenic cycle.

It is worth noting that several other immunophenotypes identified in our analysis have received limited attention in prior literature. These novel associations provide a rich repository of candidates for future investigations aiming to unravel the complex immunological networks driving DCM.

The primary strength of our study lies in the utilization of highly granular immunophenotype data, providing unprecedented resolution into the immune-DCM axis. By employing genetic variants as IVs, our MR design acts as a natural randomized controlled trial, effectively minimizing confounding factors and mitigating reverse causation. The robustness of our findings was further reinforced through comprehensive sensitivity analyses addressing heterogeneity and horizontal pleiotropy.

However, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the screening of 731 immune phenotypes involved multiple statistical tests. Because false discovery rate correction yielded no significant results, we relied on a nominal significance threshold (P < .05) for this exploratory analysis, which inherently increases the risk of Type I errors. Second, despite rigorous assessments using MR-Egger and MR-PRESSO, residual unmeasured horizontal pleiotropy may still exist and potentially bias the causal estimates. Third, as an observational genetic epidemiological method, MR reflects lifelong genetic predisposition rather than direct biological causality; thus, our findings are primarily hypothesis-generating. Fourth, the limited number of instrumental SNPs available for DCM in the reverse MR analysis may have compromised statistical power and increased uncertainty for null or borderline associations. Further validation using larger DCM GWAS cohorts with more identified genetic variants is needed. Fifth, the GWAS datasets were restricted to European and East Asian populations, limiting the generalizability of our findings to other ethnicities. Sixth, the relatively small sample sizes of some original GWAS cohorts may weaken the robustness of the results. Finally, our conclusions lack validation from in vivo or in vitro functional experiments, underscoring the need for future mechanistic studies to translate these genetic findings into biological insights.

5. Conclusion

In summary, this study confirms a bidirectional genetic relationship between immunocyte phenotypes and DCM, highlighting the intricate genetic interaction between immunity and this disease. We further verified that CD28 on secreting Tregs act as a protective factor against DCM, which explains the discrepancies observed in earlier studies of total immune cells and deepens our knowledge of DCM pathogenesis. The identified immune phenotypes can serve as potential biomarkers for clinical screening and diagnosis. Targeting CD28 signaling to maintain the function of secreting Tregs also offers a novel therapeutic strategy for DCM. Future research is necessary to promote the clinical translation of these findings.

Telehealth Education Leveraging Electronic Transitions Of Care for COPD Patients (TELE-TOC): a study protocol for a type II hybrid effectiveness-implementation randomised, pragmatic clinical trial of a pharmacist-led intervention
Authors: Deepa Ramadurai, Cathryn T Lee, Leah Traeger, Geoff Pucci, Andrea Jackson-Sagredo, Sachin Shah, Joanna Abraham, Vineet M Arora, Valerie G Press
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTransitions of care (TOC) between hospital, ambulatory and home settings for high-risk adults with chronic diseases are complex, costly and often result in poor health outcomes. Suboptimal care transitions lead to medication errors, non-adherence, decreased self-management skills and inadequate follow-up, all of which contribute to readmissions or emergency department visits. The Transitional Care Model aims to address these challenges through patient-centred, in-home interventions. We propose to implement and evaluate TELE-TOC: Telehealth Education Leveraging Electronic Transitions Of Care for Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) patients. This study will evaluate the added value of a virtual, pharmacy-based intervention integrated into an existing COPD TOC program within a single healthcare system.Methods and analysisInformed by the Proctor Framework implementation, service and health outcome domains, we will conduct a randomised controlled trial comparing the addition of at-home pharmacy team-based virtual visits to the standard of care (ie, our existing COPD TOC programme). Adult patients hospitalised for a COPD exacerbation will be randomised to receive the standard COPD TOC programme alone or the standard programme plus TELE-TOC virtual at-home pharmacy visits. We will use a pragmatic type II hybrid effectiveness-implementation trial. The primary effectiveness outcome is inhaler technique at 30 days postdischarge, and the primary implementation outcome is the proportion of patients receiving the intervention. Intention-to-treat analysis will be applied to all outcomes with χ² and logistic regression models adjusting for demographic factors. Treatment effects through 30 days will be assessed with generalised estimating equations and generalised linear mixed models.Ethics and disseminationThis study, the waiver of consent and the opt-out flyer were approved by the University of Chicago Institutional Review Board (23–0934). Dissemination of the findings is planned for up to 4 years of completion of the study to local, regional and national conferences and peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberNCT05897125.Keywords: Patients; Pulmonary Disease, Chronic Obstructive; Telemedicine; Telemedicine; MEDICAL EDUCATION & TRAINING

Introduction

Rationale

Transitions of care (TOC) are complex and vulnerable to error, frequently resulting in adverse events.1 Improving care coordination has been identified as a national priority goal by the National Academy of Medicine.2 As a result, numerous healthcare organisations have implemented a variety of standardised care coordination strategies to support care transitions between hospital, ambulatory and home settings using discharge summaries, concurrent disease and case management by dedicated multidisciplinary care teams and patient coaching and engagement activities.3,6 In particular, close ambulatory follow-up after hospital discharge provides a critical opportunity to reduce medication errors and provide ongoing patient education to reduce readmissions.7 8 However, ambulatory care coordination failure rates in the USA (9.8%) are among the highest across high-income countries.9 As a result, nearly half of Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries readmitted within 30 days of discharge are not seen in the outpatient setting.10

The TOC failures are particularly salient for patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), a highly prevalent chronic condition.11 These patients are particularly vulnerable during transitions from inpatient to outpatient care following an acute exacerbation, often requiring ongoing medication training and intense self-management. Furthermore, many patients with COPD are averse to in-person care.12 Interventions aimed at reducing readmissions after an acute exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD) have demonstrated heterogenous effects on system-level and patient-level outcomes.13,18 With an increasing focus on bringing care to patients in their homes, particularly post-COVID-19, innovations in the modality and mechanisms of delivering TOC interventions virtually are needed.

Several existing models, such as the Transitional Care Model,6 11 emphasise a collaborative, holistic, patient-centred approach that includes both inpatient and in-home interventions for high-risk, hospitalised, older adults. Pharmacist-led interventions are of particular interest to the multifaceted impacts on patient-centred outcomes, guideline-concordant therapy and healthcare costs.19,22 Furthermore, telehealth solutions have recently been identified as particularly valuable for improving outcomes for frequently hospitalised patients with chronic diseases such as COPD.23 24

The majority of the estimated $50 billion in annual COPD-related costs are related to preventable events such as acute care revisits24,26 and early hospital readmissions.27 Numerous studies have indicated that patients with COPD receive inappropriate care.28,31 Inhaler technique, a core aspect of COPD treatment, is particularly vulnerable to errors that lead to worsened health outcomes.32,36 To address these gaps, the National Heart, Lung and Blood Institute COPD National Action Plan calls for improving COPD care across the care continuum.37

Health systems context

To reduce acute care revisits (emergency department (ED) and hospital), the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services introduced policies and programmes targeting COPD, including the Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP) for AECOPD.38 However, policy alone is unlikely to transform care, as evidence-based approaches to inform the implementation of highly effective multilevel TOC programmes are lacking.14 Further, the HRRP has not been associated with improved quality of care for hospitalised patients treated for AECOPD.28 Therefore, innovation beyond the HRRP penalty and usual care efforts is needed to improve care. The rapid expansion of telehealth during the COVID-19 pandemic presents a timely opportunity to develop and evaluate telehealth-based TOC interventions.12

Related research

Our prior work includes publication of the Society of Hospital Medicine COPD Program Guide,39 which identifies evidence-based best practices for TOC, including: (1) medication reconciliation, (2) self-management training and (3) postdischarge communication. The American Thoracic Society Official Workshop Report on COPD readmissions also calls for rigorous studies to obtain effectiveness data on real-world approaches to reducing COPD revisits.14 Additionally, our group participated in a pilot study that evaluated the use of pharmacy-led telehealth visits for inhaler education among rural Veterans Affairs (VA) patients with COPD. We found the intervention to be feasible, with multiple telehealth visits leading to improved skills, quality of life and symptoms.23 40 Our recent scoping review of COPD self-management televisit-based interventions found multimethod telehealth interventions with an in-person component were most effective.41 However, current literature lacks formal evaluations of multilevel, pharmacy-led telehealth visits for COPD, particularly in an urban setting. We aim to adapt the evidence-based approach from the rural setting to our urban setting.

The trial builds on a prospective observational user-centred design study currently being performed by our group to identify stakeholder perspectives and gain user feedback on our virtual intervention (figure 1).42 43

Methods and analysis

Trial design and patient involvement

As shown in figure 1, the Telehealth Education Leveraging Electronic Transitions Of Care for COPD patients (TELE-TOC) intervention was developed through a structured, iterative process informed by both patient and clinician input. In Aim 1, Phase 1 focused on concept generation and ideation using semistructured interviews and patient journey mapping to explore experiences, needs and barriers related to TOC following hospitalisation for COPD. Insights from these interviews informed Phase 2, which emphasised prototype design, development and usability testing of the virtual pharmacist-led intervention. Phase 3 involved cognitive walkthroughs and debriefing interviews with patients and pharmacists to refine the TELE-TOC workflow, communication materials and visit logistics. Together, these design phases directly informed Aim 2, in which the finalised TELE-TOC model was implemented and evaluated through a pragmatic, type II hybrid effectiveness-implementation randomised clinical trial. The lower portion of figure 1 depicts the flow of patient identification and enrolment, beginning with electronic health record (EHR) screening for eligible adults with AECOPD, clinician confirmation of diagnosis and treatment and randomisation into either the standard COPD TOC arm or the COPD TOC plus TELE-TOC intervention arm.

This pragmatic randomised clinical trial aims to assess a pharmacy-led telehealth intervention as an additional evidence-based component to our existing standard clinical care COPD Readmissions Reduction Program, compared with the existing program alone. This type II hybrid implementation-effectiveness trial design allows for an assessment of not only how well an evidence-based intervention performs but also the contextual factors that contribute to its performance. This better informs practical dissemination strategies to implement evidence-based practices, achieving the greatest benefits for both the participants and the teams who deliver these practices. The trial will be conducted in Chicago, Illinois, USA.

The development of this trial protocol followed the SPIRIT (Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials) guidelines (online supplemental Appendix 1).44

Sample selection and recruitment

Eligible patients will be adults admitted to the University of Chicago Medicine for AECOPD, who are enrolled in our ongoing COPD HRRP. Hospitalised patients are screened daily by our research team based on review of the EHR to identify patients being treated for AECOPD. Patient lists in medical intensive care units and all hospital ward services are reviewed systematically. A list of potentially eligible patients is sent to the COPD clinical consult team (consisting of advanced practice nurses (APNs) and a physician) each morning. The APN or physician assigned to the COPD consult service for the day will review the EHR to confirm a diagnosis of underlying COPD. Confirmation may be based on spirometry or pulmonary function test results, the presence of an International Disease Classification code and/or high clinical impression informed by the patient’s history through chart review. The clinician will also verify that appropriate treatment for AECOPD is ongoing. No children will be included in this study. Enrolment will occur over approximately 12 months (or until sample size is reached), and duration of the study participation will be up to 6 months. A total of 218 participants will be enrolled from the University of Chicago Medicine.

Informed consent: waiver of consent

The focus of the study is on assessing the effectiveness of system-level, evidence-based approaches to improve care transitions. Therefore, individual patient recruitment and consent will not be conducted; instead, a waiver of consent will be used. To maintain patient autonomy, an ‘opt-out’ process will be used. The opt-out flyer (onlinesupplemental Appendix 2.1 2.2), English and Spanish language versions, respectively, will be hand-delivered by a clinician (either an APN or physician) during the patient’s initial hospital admission for AECOPD. At that time, patients will be informed that they can opt out of participating in the telehealth visits and instead only receive the usual care provided by the COPD TOC programme. The flyer they receive will include the inclusion criteria, a description of the TELE-TOC intervention and contact details for opting out. Patients may opt out at any time during the study. Patients who choose to opt out will still receive the standard clinical care through our COPD TOC programme45 but will not receive the telehealth visits, and their data will be excluded from study analyses. If patients have additional questions about the study, they can contact our research staff using the information on the flyer and can request to receive study results at the conclusion of the trial.

Randomisation

After patients have received the ‘opt out’ flyer, they will be randomised by a member of the research team using a computer-generated method. Randomisation will be stratified by age with two groups (> 65 years and ≤65 years) and Digital Infrastructure Score (DIS), which will be grouped into three tertiles of scores (1–33, 34–67 and 68–100). The DIS is a composite measure created by the American Telemedicine Association to assess how well a community is equipped for digital technologies, including digital healthcare (ie, telehealth). It is intended to help clinicians and policymakers understand geospatial digital barriers.46

Stratification by age and DIS aims to account for populations that may be inherently disadvantaged in accessing technology-based healthcare interventions. If a participant is randomised to TELE-TOC, a member of the study team will approach them to explain the virtual visits in more detail and schedule the initial TELE-TOC visit.

Blinding

The principal investigators and data analysts will be blinded to intervention assignment. However, the principal investigator may be unblinded if a patient safety concern arises.

Usual care

Participants randomised to usual care will receive the standard care within our COPD programme (figure 2). This includes a consultation with a clinician (APN or physician) to address evidence-based care in COPD and reduce care variation, as well as pharmacy-led medication education using face-to-face teach-to-goal (TTG) inhaler technique education and medication reconciliation (figure 3). Additionally, patients receive a follow-up phone call from a nurse care manager 48 hours postdischarge to assess barriers to care (eg, transportation to appointments). They will also have access to a direct clinician pager and an ambulatory follow-up visit with an APN 1–2 weeks postdischarge for ongoing COPD evaluation, treatment, medication education and medication reconciliation.

Intervention: TELE-TOC

The final TELE-TOC intervention and implementation approach will be guided by a recently completed study by our team, which is capturing stakeholder perspectives and user feedback on the evidence-based medication management programme. Participants randomised to the TELE-TOC arm will receive all COPD TOC programme components45 with the addition of two to three pharmacist-led virtual visits, which will occur at weeks 2 and 4 postdischarge. These virtual visits will reinforce medication reconciliation and inhaler technique at all sessions. The number of visits is based on findings from pilot studies that used a multisession (3–4 visits) approach (figure 4).23 40 Patients who do not have access to the internet or devices will not be provided with these items as part of this study. This is intentionally designed to demonstrate the real-world feasibility of this virtual intervention.

TELE-TOC visit, engagement and retention

The TELE-TOC visit includes a COPD-therapy specific medication reconciliation, including a discussion of discrepancies in dose, frequency, route, duration and formulation. A structured patient risk assessment is completed, followed by a knowledge and understanding assessment. Adherence assessment for each inhaler on their medication list is noted. Inhaler education is provided using the TTG approach, which is an evidence-based, guideline adherent strategy to assess and teach proper inhaler technique.47 48 TG uses rounds of assessment and education. Inhaler technique is scored using validated checklists for each inhaler listed by the pharmacist, with patients completing iterative teach-back after education by the pharmacist.

Participant retention will be promoted based on information learnt from the current qualitative study of end-user perspectives. This currently includes the following retention approaches: (1) warm handoffs with the pharmacist and patient either while the patient is hospitalised or via telephone following discharge including a technology access and use assessment and scheduling of the first TELE-TOC visit; (2) Doximity reminders from the pharmacists 24 hours in advance of the appointments; (3) health system-level reminders of the appointment; and (4) day-of reminders if the patient has not joined the visit within 15 min of the scheduled start time.

Measurements

The COPD and intervention effectiveness and reach data will be collected via the EHR and via administrative data (Clarity). These data will be uploaded to an established REDCap Electronic Data Collection System and then uploaded via the Center for Research Informatics (CRI) at our institution, who will have a study key to our datamart (IRB Protocol 17–0332 and IRB Protocol 17–1342).49

Outcomes

The Proctor Framework examines constructs of implementing an evidence-based practice while considering the users, stakeholders and context of the intervention and focusing on the implementation outcomes and the context of intervention delivery.50 In this trial, we are evaluating not only the TELE-TOC intervention itself but also its adoption within the COPD TOC program by specifically measuring the usability, interoperability and sustainability of the intervention.

Our primary effectiveness outcome is metered dose inhaler technique at 30 days after discharge, measured as the proportion with correct use (>9/12 steps correct) versus misuse (<10/12 correct) based on a validated 12-step checklist completed by the pharmacist at the time of the virtual visit.32 33 The primary implementation outcome is intervention reach, measured as proportion of patients receiving the intervention (table 1).

Sample size calculation: effectiveness

In this pragmatic randomised controlled single-site study, our planned enrolment target is 218 patients, which reflects an anticipated 10% attrition rate, consistent with expectations based on prior feasibility studies and implementation conditions. This sample size is sufficient to power both primary and key secondary outcomes. For the primary effectiveness outcome of inhaler technique, previous studies have reported that approximately 38–45% of patients demonstrate correct inhaler use (defined as ≥75–80% steps correct) by 30 days.32 52 We anticipate that the TELE-TOC intervention, which is layered on top of a robust COPD TOC infrastructure, will achieve at least 60% success in proper technique. A sample size of 190 participants (95 per group) is required to detect an 18% absolute difference (60% vs 42%) between groups with 80% power at a one-sided alpha of 0.05 using a χ² test. Our total planned enrolment of 218 thus provides adequate margin to accommodate attrition and preserves full analytical power. For the key secondary outcome of 30-day acute care revisits, prior pilot data suggest a baseline revisit rate of approximately 28% in patients with COPD. Telephonic pharmacist-led interventions in similar populations have demonstrated reductions of up to 11 percentage points in 30-day revisits. Based on this precedent, we conservatively estimate a reduction from 28% to 14% in the intervention arm. Sample size calculations indicate that 198 total participants (99 per group) are required to achieve at least 80% power to detect a 14% absolute difference at alpha=0.05, assuming binomial proportions and accounting for the same 10% attrition rate. Thus, the planned enrolment of 218 patients provides sufficient power for detecting clinically meaningful differences in both inhaler technique and 30-day revisit outcomes, even after accounting for loss to follow-up.

Sample size calculation: implementation

The primary implementation outcome is intervention reach, measured as the proportion of patients receiving inhaler education within 2 weeks after discharge. Two studies that used video telehealth to facilitate inhaler training in patients with COPD found that there were over 80% of patients with at least one video conference.23 40 In line with these findings, we expect 80% of our patients to have received virtual inhaler training within 2 weeks. We will enrol 218 patients who do not opt out.

Data analysis

All analyses will follow an intention-to-treat approach, including all randomised participants in their originally assigned groups regardless of intervention adherence. For binary outcomes, such as corrected inhaler technique and medication reconciliation, we will use unadjusted χ² tests for preliminary group comparisons and fit logistic regression models to obtain adjusted estimates, controlling for age, gender, race, smoking status and socioeconomic status as indexed by the Distressed Communities Index (DSVI).

To account for repeated measures over time, we will apply both generalised estimating equations (GEE) and generalised linear mixed models (GLMMs) to estimate trajectories and treatment effects for key binary and continuous outcomes. These models will include fixed effects for treatment group, time and their interaction, with within-participant correlation accounted for via exchangeable correlation structures in GEE and random intercepts in GLMM. The adjusted GEE estimate at 30 days postdischarge will be used to evaluate the primary hypothesis for inhaler technique. A one-sided significance level of 0.05 will be used for this test, reflecting a directional hypothesis that the TELE-TOC intervention will improve, rather than worsen, inhaler technique. This choice is justified by extensive prior evidence and the low likelihood of clinical harm; a deleterious effect would be detected through ongoing safety monitoring. Additional pairwise comparisons across groups and time points will be considered exploratory and assessed using linear contrasts within the GEE/GLMM framework or, where applicable, two-sample and paired t-tests.

For count outcomes such as 30-day, 90-day and 180-day acute care revisits, which often contain excess zeros, we will use zero-inflated Poisson or zero-inflated negative binomial models depending on dispersion and fit statistics. These models will adjust for demographic and clinical covariates, as well as baseline acute care utilisation, defined as the cumulative number of inpatient hospitalisations and ED visits in the 12 months prior to the index hospitalisation, as captured from the EHR. Utilisation will be modelled either continuously or categorically depending on distributional properties.

For continuous outcomes, such as the total inhaler technique score and CAT scores, we will apply linear mixed-effects models with repeated measures to evaluate treatment effects over time. These models will similarly include fixed effects for treatment, time and their interaction, and account for within-subject correlation through random intercepts.

We will conduct subgroup analyses to explore potential heterogeneity of treatment effects by age group (<65 vs ≥65 years), gender, race/ethnicity and DSVI-based socioeconomic tier. These will be modelled using interaction terms and interpreted as exploratory. A prespecified interim analysis will be performed once 50% of enrolled participants have completed their 30-day follow-up, in accordance with the study’s Data Safety and Monitoring Board (DSMB) oversight plan.

Discussion

As telehealth interventions for chronic disease management evolve, this study will provide valuable information on the feasibility of a pharmacist-led intervention for medication skills, adherence and self-management of COPD at home using COPD education in a real-world TOC program. Specifically for patients with COPD, where the diagnosis of exacerbations is imperfect and creates significant individual and systems-level costs,53 there is opportunity to understand the feasibility and implementation process of virtual hospital follow-up visits focusing on medication education, adherence and symptom self-management, specifically through feedback gained in the virtual visits. Future studies will incorporate patient and caregiver input to better understand perceived barriers and facilitators to the virtual pharmacist visits, and to identify strategies for refining and scaling the intervention based on their experiences. Such patient-informed feedback could help guide future implementation and dissemination efforts to improve COPD TOC.

Not only will this study provide valuable information on the success of the intervention, but also on the processes of incorporating it into an existing TOC program. It can be time-intensive, resource-intensive and cost-intensive to implement TOC programs for patients admitted with AECOPD given the multidisciplinary nature required for success in evidence-based interventions today. What is lacking from the evidence to date is the processes of implementation of these programs and exploration of barriers and facilitators in achieving adequate delivery of the interventions themselves. This type II hybrid effectiveness-implementation trial will offer new insight into TOC practices for patients admitted with AECOPD. We will specifically track the potential for sustaining and scaling this intervention, by tracking the time, resources needed and related costs. The pragmatic enrolment method of waiver of consent also offers an important means to address potential biases of enrolment and therefore generalisability of the results in diverse settings.

Future directions of research include assessing the sustainability of the intervention over a longer follow-up time and long-term effects on COPD readmissions and hospital costs. This information would be valuable for hospital administrators and policymakers with potential to expand the program to virtual medication management in other chronic diseases.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics and safety

This study design and plan, including the waiver of consent and opt-out flyer, has undergone rigorous peer review by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ) and has received local approval from the University of Chicago Institutional Review Board (IRB) under protocol number 23–0934. The IRB has deemed minimal risk to patients. Regardless, we will take several measures to mitigate any potential risk to participants. Only authorised study personnel will have access to study data. All data collected will be deidentified, and participants will be assigned unique identifications within our notes. Physical data will be stored in a secure, locked location, while electronic data will be shared exclusively with study team members via the secure, encrypted, password-protected University of Chicago Box platform. The study team is required to complete both Collaborative Institutional Training Initiative and Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act training prior to handling research data or interacting with patients.

Patient data will be collected from the EHR for enrolled participants through the HRRP. EHR data will be collected through manual chart review, if needed, and from the Clinical Research Data Warehouse (CRDW) maintained by the CRI. Data in the CRDW comes from billing, clinical registries and research systems, as well as our EHR and comprises all encounters since 1 January 2006. CRI will maintain the study key and incorporate study-related data and flags into the dedicated datamart.

A DSMB has been established to ensure participant safety and the integrity of study data. This DSMB consists of five independent members (one chair, one executive secretary, one statistician and two at large DSMB members) across multiple institutions with expertise in patient experience, recruitment and retention and intervention research. The primary responsibilities of the DSMB will include evaluations of enrolment and retention rates, patient safety and maintenance of the integrity of data collection and management during this study. Any and all serious adverse events will be reported to both the IRB and DSMB within 48 hours of identification by study staff. The DSMB has authority to recommend modifications to the clinical investigations or to stop the trial if concerns arise regarding patient safety or data integrity. An interim DSMB meeting will occur at the halfway point of enrolment—either at 6 months or once 50% of the target sample is enrolled, whichever comes first. The DSMB will record minutes from this meeting, and a report of these minutes will be forwarded to the principal investigator and to AHRQ staff. A summary of DSMB deliberations and recommendations will also be submitted to the IRB. The study will follow the University of Chicago’s established procedures for reporting adverse events in clinical research.

Dissemination

On completion of enrolment and data analysis, we will conduct predissemination assessments and ultimately disseminate our findings. The outcomes of this study will not only evaluate the effectiveness of the TELE-TOC intervention on medication adherence among patients with COPD, but also support its potential adoption within a large health system serving a high-risk population vulnerable to COPD-related readmissions. To advance care processes for COPD and share insights from the virtual pharmacy-led medication reconciliation program, we will present aggregate data to both clinical and research communities. These results may also inform the broader implementation of virtual pharmacy-led interventions for TOC in other chronic disease populations.

Letter to Editor: Identification and validation of the surface proteins FIBG, PDGF-β, and TGF-β on serum extracellular vesicles for non-invasive detection of colorectal cancer: experimental study (Int J Surg. 2024 Mar 3)
Authors: Yongquan Gao, Yuhang Liu, Guoshuai Liu, Yanfei Yang
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We read carefully with great interest the article by Zhijian Huang and colleagues published in the International Journal of Surgery 1. Their study is performed by 4D-DIA proteomics approach to assess the relationship between FIBG, PDGF-β, and TGF-β, and colorectal cancer (CRC). This finding clearly contributes to the valuable insights into the prevention and treatment of CRC. We express our sincere admiration for this innovative and clinically relevant study. However, we do share some thoughts about this study.

We believe that it may be necessary to enhance sample diversity and the generalizability of the research findings. Although this study involved a multicenter study of 404 individuals, to ensure the generalizability and applicability of the research results, it is recommended to further expand the sample size to include individuals of different ages, genders, ethnicities, and geographical regions2. This would allow for a better assessment of the performance of these biomarkers across diverse populations, thereby improving the universality and accuracy of the diagnostic model3.

Additionally, we have noted that the article lacks an exploration of the functions and mechanisms of the biomarkers. While FIBG, PDGF-β, and TGF-β are mentioned as biomarkers for colorectal cancer, a further discussion of the roles these proteins play in the onset and progression of cancer would be beneficial. For instance, understanding how they influence tumor growth, metastasis, and interactions with the immune system could enhance the accuracy of diagnoses and potentially lead to new targets for treatment4.

Moreover, the expression or mutation of a single gene is far from sufficient to explain the occurrence or predict the prognosis of cancer. Based on high-throughput sequencing results and statistical genetics, it is possible to search for predictors of tumor prognosis. In this paper, the author used the TCGA (The Cancer Genome Atlas) database for genomic enrichment analysis. It is hoped that through in-vitro and in-vivo cell biology experiments, the possible mechanisms of these genes in tumor prognosis can be explored and confirmed5.

For the prediction results, we also hope to stratify by site of metastasis and assess the diagnostic capabilities of FIBG, PDGF-β, and TGF-β for single-site and multi-site metastasis6. In conclusion, we sincerely appreciate the contribution made by Huang and colleagues. The research can serve as a potential approach to evaluate the survival period of patients. If the author can take the above points into account, the comprehensiveness and concreteness of the research will be better played, and the dimension of the research will be greatly improved.

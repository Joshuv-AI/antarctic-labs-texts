Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite as a high-performance sorbent for hollow fiber solid phase microextraction of selected pesticide residues
Authors: Marzieh Kavian, Milad Ghani, Jahan Bakhsh Raoof
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe main objective of this study is to introduce a strategy for the synthesis of a new hybrid sorbent. This sorbent is based on the combination of layered double hydroxide (LDH) and a porphyrin-based covalent organic framework (COF), which is called (LDH/COF) for the hollow fiber solid-phase microextraction method (HF-SPME). For this purpose, Cu-Al-LDH was synthesized and combined with the tetra (4-aminophenyl) porphyrin to obtain a Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite. Then, the synthesized composite was accommodated in the pores of the hollow fiber. The resulting extraction device was used to extract Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron (as the model analytes), present in food samples. The linear dynamic ranges, limit of detections, and limit of quantifications were in the range of 0.2–500.0 µg L−1 (r2 ≥ 0.9987), 0.08–0.19 µg L−1, and 0.27–0.63 µg L−1, respectively. The enrichment factors of Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron were 153 and 82, respectively. The relative standard deviations were calculated to be 1.7–4.3%. The environmental sustainability and practical applicability of the proposed method were evaluated using the Blue Applicability Grade Index (BAGI), which is a criterion based on a scoring scale of 0 to 100. The obtained score of 70.0 indicates good environmental compatibility and practical applicability of the method. The results showed that this sorbent provided high recovery, excellent reproducibility, and a low limit of detection for the extraction of target analytes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-48489-0.Keywords: Covalent organic framework, Layered double hydroxide, Hollow fiber solid phase microextraction, Pesticide residues, Tetra (4-aminophenyl) porphyrin, Blue Applicability Grade IndexSubject terms: Chemistry, Environmental sciences

Introduction

Real samples often contain complicated matrices with interfering compounds that can reduce analytical sensitivity and accuracy. Hence, an efficient sample preparation step is required to remove interferences and preconcentrate target analytes before instrumental analysis1–3. Among miniaturized extraction techniques, the hollow fiber solid phase microextraction (HF-SPME) method, introduced by Xu et al.4, has attracted much attention. This method employs porous polypropylene hollow fibers as a substrate to prepare the extraction device5. The advantages of this method include the possibility of using these porous fibers in various complex matrices, increasing the surface area, enhancing the extraction efficiency, high mechanical stability, low consumption of organic solvents, cost-effectiveness, and elimination of matrix effects. Moreover, this method does not require the use of tools such as pumps, magnets, centrifuges, etc., to perform the extraction process6. The sorbent accommodated in the pores of the hollow fibers is an important part of the extraction process that has a direct impact on the process and recovery of analytes. Therefore, a sorbent must have characteristics such as high chemical, thermal, and mechanical stability, porosity, and a high specific surface area.

One group of compounds that can be developed as sorbents in microextraction methods is covalent organic frameworks (COFs). COFs, which were invented by Yaghi et al.7, are lightweight polymers. The suitable characteristics of these compounds that have led to their widespread use in various fields include high chemical, thermal, and mechanical stability, tunable pore size, high porosity, and the ability to modify them with various functional groups to improve their properties. Functionalization of COFs can increase hydrogen bonding interactions, π–π interactions, electrostatic interactions, and metal–ligand coordination, which ultimately increases adsorption capacity, selectivity, and extraction efficiency of the method8,9. One of the disadvantages of COFs that may weaken their performance as sorbents is their poor dispersion and insolubility in most solvents10. Hence, to overcome this problem, sorbents can be combined with different compounds such as Bi2WO611, SiO212, NH2-MIL-125(Ti)13, Fe3O4, ZnO14, MIL-53-NH2/g-C3N415 and etc.

Layered double hydroxides (LDHs) are a group of anionic clays. These compounds have a layered structure in which the host layers have a positive charge, and anions are placed between these layers to neutralize this positive charge16. The unique characteristics of LDHs, including thermal stability, high specific surface area, and high adsorption capacity, have led researchers to draw attention to the utilization of LDHs as a modifier of other sorbents8,17,18. Therefore, the combination of LDH with COF can create unique properties due to the synergistic effect19,20.

Pesticides are widely used in agriculture, and their residues in food products require reliable analytical monitoring21. Herein, Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron were selected as the model analytes to examine the ability of the method. Hexaflumuron is an insect growth regulator from the benzoylphenylurea group. The Australian Pesticides and Veterinary Medicines Authority (APVMA) has set the permissible limit for this pesticide in the human body at about 0.02 mg kg−1 of body weight per day. Teflubenzuron is also a benzoylurea pesticide that inhibits the growth of insects. This compound has low water solubility and high hydrophobicity22. These pesticides are widely used to control pests in agricultural products. Therefore, it is essential to control and measure the residues of these pesticides in agricultural products and food.

The main objective of this study is to prepare and introduce a new sorbent based on the combination of Cu-Al-LDH and porphyrin-based COF. Therefore, first, Cu-Al-LDH was synthesized using copper (II) nitrate and aluminum nitrate. Then, the synthesized Cu-Al-LDH was combined with tetra(4-aminophenyl) porphyrin to prepare a Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF composite. The resulting composite was immobilized in the pores of a hollow fiber (HF) and used to extract the Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron.

In recent years, the development of advanced sorbents based on nanostructured materials has received widespread attention to improve the efficiency of sample preparation methods. Among them, LDHs have been proposed as efficient sorbents for the removal and extraction of organic pollutants due to their layered structure, the presence of surface hydroxyl groups, and the ability to tune the metal composition. However, the adsorption mechanism in LDHs is mainly limited to electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonding, and they do not have high selectivity for hydrophobic aromatic compounds23–25. On the other hand, COFs have been introduced as a new generation of crystalline porous materials with high specific surface area, structural order, and extensive π-conjugated networks. The ordered structure and targeted design capability of COFs allow the creation of specific adsorption environments for aromatic compounds26. In the field of pesticide extraction, sorbents such as C18, graphene oxide (GO), metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), and magnetic nanoparticles have been reported, but these sorbents generally rely on a dominant mechanism (such as hydrophobic or π–π interaction). Compounds such as Teflubenzuron and Hexaflumuron have a polycyclic aromatic structure, amide groups, fluorine substituents, and high hydrophobicity, requiring a multi-mechanism sorbent for efficient and selective extraction. Accordingly, in the present study, a hybrid nanocomposite based on Cu–Al-LDH and porphyrin-based COF was designed and synthesized. The porphyrin units in the COF are aromatic and planar and can form π–π interactions with the aromatic rings of pesticides. This interaction causes the analyte molecules to be close to the COF surface and enhances the adsorption. Moreover, porphyrin has nitrogen groups that can establish a hydrogen bond. In addition, the presence of Cu2+ in Cu-Al-LDH can provide a Lewis metal center that interacts with the C=O and NH groups of the analytes and enhances the adsorption capability. On the other hand, the Cu–Al-LDH surface has –OH groups. These groups can make hydrogen bonds with the –NH and C=O groups present in the analytes, which increases the physical strength of the adsorption. In fact, the presence of Cu²+ metal centers and the hydroxyl groups of Cu-AL-LDH allows for coordination and hydrogen bonding interactions. The combination of these two structures increases the adsorption capacity. Compared with other proposed sorbents such as MOF/LDH, LDH/COF has advantages such as high chemical stability in aqueous solutions and organic solvents, controlled and uniform specific surface area, and precisely designed porphyrin units for specific interaction with analytes27. This design provides a synergistic effect of four types of interactions, including π–π interaction, metal coordination, hydrogen bonding, and hydrophobic interaction, which are not simultaneously present in single-component sorbents. Moreover, immobilization of this nanocomposite on polypropylene hollow fiber in the form of the HF-SPME method resulted in reduced solvent consumption, enhanced sorbent mechanical stability, prevention of sorbent dispersion in the matrix, ease of reuse, and improved mass transfer. According to a comprehensive literature review, there has been no report on the application of Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite on a hollow fiber for microextraction purposes. Therefore, the innovation of this work lies not only in the synthesis of a new material but also in the design of a multi-mechanism synergy and its integration with the HF-SPME method for the extraction of target species. The integration of an advanced hybrid nanocomposite with the HF-SPME method is considered an operational innovation.

Experimental

Materials, instrumentation, and sample preparation

Details regarding the materials used for sorbent synthesis, the instrumentation employed for characterization and analyte analysis, as well as the preparation of the standard solution, are provided in the supplementary information.

Synthesis of the Cu-Al-LDH

To synthesize Cu-Al-LDH, two solutions were prepared separately. The first solution was obtained by mixing copper nitrate, aluminum nitrate, and sodium nitrate salts in deionized water in a ratio of 4:1:4. The second solution consisted of preparing sodium hydroxide (2 M) with deionized water. Then, NaOH (2 M) was slowly added to the first solution under stirring. This was done until the pH of the solution reached at 10.7. After stirring for 16 h, the resulting solution was centrifuged to separate the precipitate. The precipitate obtained was washed repeatedly with deionized water to remove excess NaOH and to reach pH = 7.0. Finally, the Cu-Al-LDH precipitate was dried at 50 °C28.

Synthesis of the tetra (4-aminophenyl) porphyrin (TAPP)

First, a mixture of 0.110 g of 4-nitrobenzaldehyde, 0.12 mL of acetic anhydride, 4 mL of propionic acid, and 0.05 mL of pyrrole was prepared. This mixture was stirred for 2 h at 140 °C. After cooling, the obtained product was washed, dried, and recrystallized using pyridine to yield tetra (4-nitrophenyl) porphyrin (TNPP). Subsequently, 0.040 g of TNPP was dissolved in 0.35 mL of HCl. 1.00 mL of HCl, containing 0.120 g of stannous chloride, was added dropwise to it. The mixture was agitated for 30 min at 70 °C. To neutralize the reaction, an aqueous ammonia solution (25%) was added while the mixture was kept in an ice bath. The TAPP was then prepared by filtering and conducting Soxhlet extraction using chloroform29.

Synthesis of the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF and the final HF-SPME sorbent

In the next step, the nanocomposite of Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF was synthesized. To this end, a mixture of 0.10 g of Cu-Al-LDH was prepared in 12 mL of dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) and sonicated for 15 min. Then 0.50 g of TAPP was added and sonicated for an additional 15 min. The resulting mixture was placed in a Teflon-lined autoclave and heated at 180 °C for 24 h. Afterward, the precipitate was cooled and washed with ethanol and water. Finally, it was dried at 70 °C [30] . Then, 2 cm of cleaned polypropylene hollow fibers was cut and immersed in a suspension containing 0.03 g of Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF and 2 mL of methanol. This mixture was sonicated for 10 min to ensure the accommodation of Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF in the pores of HF. Finally, the coated fibers were washed with water and methanol to remove impurities and dried at 60 °C29.

Sample preparation

Carrot, lettuce, bell pepper, and potato were selected as the real samples to be used for the extraction and measurement of Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron. The fruits were purchased from the local market and, after washing, peeled and crushed. 10.0 g of crushed fruits were mixed with 15 mL of acetonitrile and 5 mL of water. After the addition of 1.0 g NaCl, the resulting mixture was shaken for 1 min. Next, 4.0 g of MgSO4 was added to the mixture, and the mixture was centrifuged for 10 min at 6000 rpm. 5 mL of the supernatant was removed and centrifuged again for 3 min30. To verify the validity of the method and also the quantification, the standard addition method was used to determine the analytes present in the real samples. Therefore, standard solutions of the target species at concentrations of 2, 5, and 10 µg L−1 were spiked into real samples containing Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron. The resulting solution was used to extract the analytes.

HF-SPME of Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron

A mixture solution containing Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron was prepared at a concentration of 100 µg L−1, the pH of the solution was set at 7.0, and 0.5 g NaCl was added to the solution. The prepared HF-Cu-AL-LDH/porphyrin-based COF was placed in 10 mL of this solution. The solution was stirred at 800 rpm for 30 min. Then, the sorbent containing the extracted analytes was placed in a vial containing 200 µL of methanol. The vial was sonicated for 10 min. Finally, the desorption solvent was completely evaporated by a stream of N2. 50 µL of methanol was added to the vial containing the analytes and injected into the HPLC-UV for measurement.

Results and discussion

Characterization

To ensure the successful synthesis of the prepared Cu-AL-LDH/porphyrin-based COF sorbent, various characterizations, including field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM), Fourier transform infrared spectrometer (FT-IR), energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (EDX), Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET), and x-ray diffraction (XRD), were performed. FE-SEM analysis was performed to study the morphology of the sorbent. The FE-SEM Images of TAPP, Cu-Al-LDH, Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite, and HF immobilized with Cu-AL-LDH/porphyrin-based COF sorbent at different magnifications are shown in Fig. 1A–D. The SEM of TAPP (Fig. 1A), shows crystalline structures or micronetworks29. The FE-SEM images of Cu-Al-LDH (Fig. 1B) show the layered structure with plates oriented in different directions. This compound is composed of many nanolayers stacked on top of each other. The layered architecture and overlapping of the plates indicate the successful formation of the Cu-Al-LDH crystal structure and the orderly growth of the sheets during the synthesis20,31. When Cu-Al-LDH was combined with TAPP, the plate and layer structures of Cu-Al-LDH were covered with particles or micro-networks, indicating the placement of porphyrin-based COF on them (Fig. 1C). The uniform distribution of COF on the Cu-Al-LDH substrate indicates the success of the layering process and the formation of a hybrid. Considering the structure of TAPP and Cu-Al-LDH, these two compounds can be combined with each other by establishing a π–π bond, a hydrogen bond, or a covalent bond32. Based on the FE-SEM image of the hollow fiber (Fig. 1D), it can be understood that these fibers have many pores. When the hollow fiber is reinforced with the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite, this composite is located in the pores of the fiber, and as a result, the porosity of the fiber is reduced.

The elements of the resulting Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF were investigated using EDX analysis. According to the obtained results, the C, O, N, Al, and Cu were found in the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF composite. The uniform distribution of N atoms is related to TAPP. The Cu and Al elements are related to Cu-Al-LDH, and the O and C elements are related to TAPP and Cu-Al-LDH (Fig. 2a).

(c) XRD patterns of the synthesized TAPP, Cu-Al-LDH, and Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF, and (d) BET of the synthesized Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF (adsorption (ADS) and desorption (DES)).

FT-IR analysis (Fig. 2b) was performed to identify the functional groups of compounds, to investigate Cu-Al-LDH, TAPP, and Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite. Figure 2B shows the spectrum of TAPP. In this figure, the peaks of the amine groups (N–H) in the region of 3430 cm−1, the peaks related to the benzene ring at 1486, 1497, and 1604 cm−1, the C=N stretching peak at 1629 cm−1, and the peak of the porphyrin ring at 967 cm−1 are observed. The presence of these functional groups indicates the successful synthesis of TAPP33,34. In the Cu-Al-LDH spectrum, various peaks are observed in the regions 1463, 1385, and 841 cm−1 for nitrate anions (NO3−). The regions 1463 and 841 cm−1 appear due to the vibration of the NO3−, and the peak at 1385 cm−1 is due to the absorption of the NO3−. The peak at 1632 cm−1 is due to the bending of interlayer water molecules. Also, the regions 747 and 617 cm−1 are referred to as the vibration of metal-oxygen bonds. A wide peak in the region 3200 to 3700 cm−1 is also observed in the spectrum, which proves the presence of a hydroxyl group and water molecules20. The spectrum of the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite confirms the presence of Cu-Al-LDH and TAPP functional groups in this compound. As can be seen in the spectrum (Fig. 2b), the peaks related to NO3− are observed in the composite. However, the intensity of the peaks is changed, which may be due to the replacement of the network polymers in the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite. The metal-oxygen bonds in the composite are preserved and usually have a slight displacement, which proves that the Cu-Al-LDH skeleton remains stable. The broadening of the O–H band and sometimes a slight shift of the C=N band to lower numbers can indicate hydrogen bonding between the O-H of Cu-Al-LDH and the N atoms of COF or electrostatic interactions with the charged layers of Cu-Al-LDH32,35. The FT-IR spectra of the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF sorbent was taken after the extraction of the Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron in order to investigate possible interactions (Fig. 2b). Since the sorbent structure lacks a carbonyl group (C=O), no distinct peak is observed in the initial spectrum in the region of 1650–1700 cm⁻¹. After the extraction process, significant changes are observed in the spectrum that can be attributed to the adsorption of the selected pesticides. The most important change is the increase in intensity or the formation of a shoulder in the region of 1650–1700 cm⁻¹, which is related to the C=O stretching vibration of the benzoyl urea group of the pesticides. Also, the slight shift of the C=N band and its decrease in intensity, as well as the broadening of the O–H band and its shift to a lower wavenumber, indicate the formation of interactions such as hydrogen bonding between the carbonyl groups of the pesticide and the layered hydroxyls of LDH, as well as π–π interactions between the aromatic rings of the selected pesticides and the porphyrin structure of the synthesized COF. The appearance of C–F bands in the range of 1100–1300 cm⁻¹ is also a confirmation of the presence of pesticide molecules on the surface of the adsorbent. Overall, the changes observed in the aforementioned regions indicate the successful immobilization of pesticides on the surface of the adsorbent and the occurrence of physical and intermolecular interactions between the sorbent and the analytes. Overall, these observations (Fig. 2b) indicate the successful adsorption of analytes by the proposed adsorbent.

To prove the crystalline structure of the prepared compounds, XRD analysis was used. The diffraction peak at 2θ = 6.0° is specific to TAPP, which is seen in (Fig. 2c). This diffraction is due to the spacing between the porphyrin layers. The second peak in the 2θ = 20.0–25.0° is related to the spacing between the aromatic planes29. The diffraction peaks at 2θ = 12.7°, 23.3°, 34.6°, 39.3°, 53.2°, and 63.2° in the Cu-Al-LDH pattern prove the crystalline layered structure of Cu-Al-LDH36. After hybridization, the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF diffraction pattern still retained the main Cu-Al-LDH diffractions, but their intensity decreased and the peak at 2θ = 12.7° shifted to lower angles, which indicates an increase in the interlayer distance due to the introduction of part of the porphyrin units into the layered structure. The diffraction patterns indicate the preservation of the Cu-Al-LDH and porphyrin-based COF structures in the composites, which indicates the successful synthesis of the composite.

The porosity and specific surface area of the sorbents are important issues to consider in the selection of sorbents. N2 adsorption-desorption experiments are used to measure the porosity of the compounds. The results obtained from the resulting composite isotherm show a specific surface area of 74.6 m2 g−1, which indicates sufficient specific surface area for the adsorption of analytes (Fig. 2d). The average diameter of the pores and cavities of this composition has been determined to be 3.3 nm. Based on the characterizations performed, the successful synthesis of the sorbent was confirmed. Moreover, it is worth mentioning that in porous materials such as MOFs and COFs, the specific surface area is one of the fundamental parameters for characterizing the adsorption capacity, as it indicates how much surface is available for contact with the analyte. An increase in the specific surface area usually increases the adsorption capacity and the accessibility of active sites. A decrease in the BET surface area actually indicates the blocking or partial filling of the pores by the LDH-bound COF moieties, as well as the formation of an external coating layer that reduces the available porosity. However, in complex sample separation and extraction applications, the nature of the surface interactions (type of functional groups, polarity, acidity/basicity of the surface and the ability to form specific interactions such as π–π interactions, hydrogen bonding, electrostatic or specific interactions with the target species) have a much stronger influence on the selectivity, adsorption capacity and actual operational capacity. In summary, although the BET specific surface area of the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF is 74.6 m² g−1, which seems lower than many conventional sorbents, the high extraction efficiency of teflubenzuron and hexaflumuron depends more on the structural and chemical properties of the sorbent surface than solely on the BET specific surface area.

Optimization

After successful synthesis of the prepared sorbent, the factor that indicates the ability of the sorbent to extract the target analytes, such as the type of desorption solvent, the extraction time, desorption time, volume of desorption solvent, stirring rate, the salt effect, and pH, were optimized in the HF-SPME method. These parameters were determined based on experiments and a literature review. In order to determine the relative standard deviation (RSD%), all experiments were repeated three times (n = 3). The optimization of the parameters affecting the experiment was carried out using the one-variable-at-a-time (OVAT) method. Since OVAT is a simple and straightforward method, and there was no prior information on the exact pattern of analyte with our sorbent interactions, it was necessary to investigate each parameter separately and under controlled conditions to determine its true role without the interference of other factors. Although multivariate statistical designs such as Response Surface Methodology (RSM) are powerful tools for simultaneously analyzing variables and identifying interaction effects and require fewer experiments, the use of such models is most effective when the overall behavior of the system is known to some extent. In contrast, the OVAT approach was chosen to examine the effect of each parameter separately and observe the change trend in detail. It has also been pointed out in previous studies that in the early stages of method development or in situations where complex interaction effects are not expected, the use of a univariate method can be a logical and efficient approach37,38. It is worth mentioning that RSM can provide a more statistically powerful evaluation of factor interactions and DOE-based optimization could be considered in our future studies. In order to start the optimization approach, first, the amount of sorbent loaded on the hollow fibers was optimized. Amounts of 0.01, 0.02, 0.03, 0.04, and 0.05 g of Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF were investigated. The sorbent-reinforced fibers were used for the extraction of Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron. Low amounts of sorbent will lead to a decrease in the active surfaces, and there will not be enough active sites for the adsorption of analytes, and high extraction efficiency will not be achieved. If the amount of sorbent is high, the sorbent will clump on the fiber, resulting in a decrease in the active surfaces and the ability to adsorb analytes. The results showed that 0.03 g of Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite had the highest ability to adsorb analytes (Fig. 3a). The type of desorption solvent is a very important parameter that can lead to complete or incomplete desorption of analytes from the sorbent. Several points should be considered to select the appropriate solvent for desorption. The solvent that is selected should interact well with the analytes so that it can dissolve the analytes, and the analytes are completely separated from the sorbent. If the desorption process is performed incompletely, in addition to reducing the extraction efficiency, the remaining analytes on the sorbent affect the results of subsequent experiments and cause errors. Moreover, the solvent should be economical, compatible with the chromatography device, and easy to prepare. To determine the appropriate desorption solvent, several solvents, including acetonitrile, methanol, ethanol, and 2-propanol, were selected. As shown in Fig. 3b, methanol was determined as the optimal solvent. Because it has the mentioned conditions and was able to completely desorb the analytes. Methanol, due to its small molecular size and moderate polarity, can easily penetrate the pores of porphyrin-based COF or Cu-Al-LDH and separate the analytes from the sorbent. Since methanol has a lower viscosity than other solvents such as ethanol and 2-propanol, it has more penetration into the sorbent, and mass transfer is faster. As a result, methanol is considered a more suitable solvent, considering the points mentioned and its greater ability to interact with analytes. Another important parameter is the pH of the solution containing the analytes. In order to achieve the highest extraction efficiency, the analytes must be present in the solution in molecular form so that they can leave the aqueous solution. This requires that the pH of the solution be lower than the pKa of the analytes. Ionization of the analytes results in their retention in the aqueous environment and their lack of affinity for organic solvents, which is not suitable for the extraction process. Hence, pH between 3.0 and 11.0 was investigated. Considering the pKa of Teflubenzuron, which is 8.1, and based on the structure of these analytes, it can be stated that Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron are usually more stable at neutral and slightly acidic pHs and may decompose at very alkaline or very acidic pHs. Moreover, the structure of Cu-Al-LDHs may be destroyed at acidic pHs, or the structure of Cu-Al-LDH may change at alkaline pHs. Based on the obtained results, pH = 7 produced the highest extraction efficiency (Fig. 3c). To better understand how the sorbent interacts with the analytes, the point of zero charge (PZC) was also used. pHpzc of the Cu–Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF was calculated to be approximately 7.2 (Fig. S-1). This pH corresponds to the optimal pH for the extraction of the analytes (pH = 7.0). This estimate is based on a plot of ΔpH versus pH, where ΔpH = pHfinal − pHinitial, which represents the pH change of the solution after equilibration with the sorbent. In this plot, ΔpH is positive at pH less than 7.2, indicating a slightly positive surface of the sorbent and H⁺ adsorption. At pH ≈ 7.2, ΔpH = 0, which represents the zero point of surface charge; the sorbent surface is almost neutral. At pH greater than 7.0, ΔpH becomes negative, indicating surface deprotonation and a slight negative surface charge. This trend is consistent with the known behavior of Cu-Al-LDHs and porphyrin-based COFs, where metal hydroxyl groups (M–OH) and porphyrin ring nitrogens are protonated or deprotonated depending on pH. Overall, this conceptual estimate of PZC provides a reasonable explanation for the pH dependence of extraction and supports the selection of pH = 7.0 as the optimal HF-SPME condition for the studied compounds. Stirring rate is one of the key parameters in solid phase extraction, which is effective in establishing equilibrium between the sorbent and the analytes. In microextraction methods, the rate of penetration of analytes from the solution to the sorbent surface is controlled by the boundary layer or diffusion layer. When the solution is stationary, this layer is thick, and mass transfer is slow. By increasing the stirring rate, this layer becomes thinner, and faster penetration of analytes to the fiber surface and an increase in the extraction recovery occur. Different stirring rates from 400 to 1200 rpm were tested. At low speeds, the boundary layer is thick, and in addition to the long extraction time, the extraction efficiency also decreases. At very high speeds, the phenomenon of eddy diffusion is created, the equilibrium is not well established, and the uniform contact between the solution and the sorbent surface is lost. Also, high speeds may lead to damage to the sorbent or the detachment of Cu-AL-LDH/porphyrin-based COF from the fiber surface. However, at medium speeds, the boundary layer thickness is reduced without creating the phenomenon of eddy diffusion and severe turbulence, which leads to the highest extraction efficiency. As a result, the speed of 800 rpm was selected as the optimum (Fig. 3d). The other factor is the addition of salt to the solution. Therefore, different amounts of salt, such as NaCl, are added to the solution containing the analytes, and their effect on the results is investigated. In this regard, two terms are defined: salt-out and salt-in effect. When salt is added to the solution, if the solubility of the analytes in water decreases and they tend to leave the aqueous environment, the salt-out occurs. In this process, the surface tension decreases, the salt adsorbs water molecules, and the water coverage of the analytes decreases. However, if the salt increases the ionic strength of the medium and the viscosity so that the solubility of the analytes in water increases, and the extraction process is not carried out well, we will have the salt-in effect. Here, adding salt at lower concentrations leads to a decrease in the solubility of Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron in water. However, with increasing salt concentration, the viscosity of the solution increases, and mass transfer slows down. Also, high salt concentrations lead to the formation of excess ions in the solution. These ions may accumulate around the Cu-Al-LDH surface and change the thickness of the double layer. According to the results obtained, the addition of 5% (W/V) NaCl led to improved recovery of analytes (Fig. 3e). The next factor is extraction time, which is optimized in the range of 5–40 min. When the fiber reinforced with the sorbent is placed in a solution containing the target samples, the analytes are distributed between the sorbent and the solution. Therefore, the time to reach equilibrium is very important. At short times, the mass transfer is not yet complete, and the extraction efficiency is low. With increasing extraction time, the rate of analytes entering the fiber and the recovery increase. However, at very long times or after reaching equilibrium, the extraction amount is fixed, and the efficiency does not change, which is not economical in terms of time and energy consumption. Based on the obtained results, 30 min was suitable to achieve maximum efficiency(Fig. 3f). The next effective parameter is the desorption time. Short times lead to incomplete recovery of analytes and reduced efficiency. Too long a time also leads to a waste of time and energy because the capacity of the desorption solvent is limited, and prolonging the desorption time does not affect the efficiency. Times of 2, 5, 10, and 15 min were chosen for desorption. The results presented in Fig. 3g showed that 10 min is the appropriate time for complete desorption of analytes from the sorbent. Another effective parameter is the volume of the desorption solvent. This factor directly affects the efficiency and sensitivity of the analysis. Different volumes of methanol, including 100, 150, 200, and 250 µL, were tested. If the volume of the solvent is small, the concentration of the analyte in the desorption solution is higher, and the sensitivity is higher. However, if the volume is too small, the solvent capacity is limited, and not all analytes are desorbed. If the volume of the solvent is large, more complete desorption of the analytes is performed, but the solution is diluted, and the sensitivity is reduced. Based on the results obtained, a volume of 200 µL of methanol has the ability to completely desorb the analytes (Fig. 3h). Based on the obtained data, the optimal condition was as follows: amount of sorbent: 0.03 g, type of desorption solvent: methanol, pH: 7, stirring rate: 800 rpm, salt effect: 5% (W/V), extraction time: 30 min, desorption time: 10 min, and desorption volume: 200 µL.

Method evaluation

The method’s figures of merit, including linear dynamic ranges (LDRs), limit of detections (LODs), limit of quantifications (LOQs), relative standard deviations (RSDs%), enrichment factors (EFs), and absolute recovery (AR%), were calculated. To determine the LDRs, standard solutions of Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron were prepared at concentrations of 0.20, 0.50, 1.00, 2.00, 5.00, 10.00, 20.00, 50.00, 100.00, 200.00, and 500.00 µg L−1. The calculated LDRs for Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron were in the range of 0.20–500.00 and 0.50–200.00 µg L−1, with coefficients of determination of 0.9991 and 0.9987, respectively. Moreover, the signal-to-noise ratio of 3.29 and the signal-to-noise ratio of 10 are the criteria for measuring LOD and LOQ, respectively. Hence, LOD for Hexaflumuron and Teflubenzuron were reported as 0.08 and 0.19 µg L−1, respectively. Also, LOQs were calculated for these analytes as 0.27 and 0.63 µg L−1. The RSDs obtained were in the range of 1.7–4.3%, which indicates the good precision of the proposed method. The relatively low absolute recovery of teflubenzouron could be due to several factors related to the chemical structure and properties of the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF. Teflubenzuron has chlorine and fluorine groups and aromatic rings, which have a low tendency to interact hydrophilically with the sorbent surface. Teflubenzuron has a low hydrophobicity (Log P of Teflubenzuron is 4.3). In extraction processes, compounds with higher hydrophobicity tend to leave the aqueous medium and distribute in the sorbent. Therefore, the low partition coefficient of Teflubenzuron between the aqueous phase of the sample and the sorbent could be one of the main reasons for its low recovery. Also, in the synthesized sorbent, π–π interactions between the aromatic structure of the analyte and the porphyrin-based COF play an important role in the adsorption process. Teflubenzuron has weaker Van der Waals and π–π interactions with the sorbent surface, and its weaker interaction with the sorbent surface may cause it not to reach complete adsorption equilibrium within a given extraction time, resulting in low recovery. Therefore, a combination of factors, including low hydrophobicity, low partition coefficient, and weak surface interactions with the Cu-AL-LDH/porphyrin-based COF structure, could explain the relatively low recovery of Teflubenzuron. In addition, the nature of the matrix of vegetable samples may compete for adsorption and reduce recovery. In fact, we demonstrated that the HF-SPME method with Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF has good linear range, which allows the determination of trace amounts of Teflubenzuron in real samples. Therefore, although the absolute recovery is 41.0%, our method is suitable and reliable for practical application in monitoring Teflubenzuron residues in vegetables. Although the absolute recovery shows moderate values, the relatively high enrichment factor indicates the good efficiency of the extraction method in concentrating Teflubenzuron in very low final volumes. The enrichment factor is not only dependent on the extraction efficiency, but is also significantly affected by the ratio of the initial sample volume to the extraction phase volume. In the present method, a significant reduction in the extraction phase volume resulted in high concentrations, even in condition where complete mass transfer did not occur. Therefore, the presented method has good ability for preconcentration. However, the AR depends on the extracted fraction of the total sample. The moderate recoveries observed could be due to matrix effects or incomplete mass transfer, which are common in microextraction-based methods. The enrichment factors obtained confirm the suitability of the method for the analysis of trace amounts of analytes.

The lifetime of the sorbent was investigated. The results of the repeatability study of the Cu-AL-LDH/porphyrin-based COF showed that the proposed sorbent can be used up to 28 times consecutively (without decreasing the AR%) (Fig. S2). In order to assess the repeatability and to investigate the existence of statistically significant differences between the repetitions, the data were analyzed using single-factor analysis of variance (ANOVA). The results showed that the difference between cycles 1–28 was not statistically significant (p > 0.05), which confirms the stability of the performance of the sorbent in this range. Also, in order to determine the starting point of performance decline, an independent t-test was performed between cycles 28 and 29. The results of this test showed that the difference between these two cycles was statistically significant (p < 0.05), which indicates that the decrease in the efficiency of the sorbent began after 28 cycles. Based on this, it can be concluded that the studied sorbent has acceptable statistical stability and repeatability up to 28 cycles, after which the performance decline begins significantly. The results of ANOVA and t-test analyze are presented in the supporting data in Tables S1 and S2. The sorbent was washed with water and methanol after each use. The memory effect is another factor that should be investigated. In order to investigate the memory effect, the extraction and desorption process was carried out using the proposed sorbent. Then, the desorption process was repeated. Both desorption solvents were injected into the HPLC-UV. The results proved the lack of memory effect in the desired sorbent.

Adsorption isotherm

Adsorption isotherm models are used to describe the adsorption mechanism and how analytes interact with the sorbent. These models provide information about the adsorption capacity and surface properties. To investigate the adsorption mechanism of Teflubenzuron and Hexaflumuron on Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF, the adsorption data were analyzed using Langmuir and the Freundlich isotherms. It was observed that the coefficient of determination for the Freundlich isotherm was higher (r²=0.9904), indicating that the actual adsorption on the sorbent occurs in a multi-level and heterogeneous manner. The Freundlich isotherm states that the adsorption capacity can occur at different energies on different surfaces and that the relationship between analyte concentration and the amount of adsorption is logarithmic and nonlinear. Due to the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF structure, the sorbent surface has diverse active groups (π–π, hydrophobic, hydrogen, and metal coordination), so the adsorption of analytes also occurs through multiple aggregation and heterogeneous interactions. The result of the isotherms investigation shows that the Freundlich model with a higher r² is the best explanation for the multiple and non-uniform adsorption mechanism on our sorbent.

Comparison with other studies

The combination of Cu–Al-LDH with porphyrin-based COF creates a hybrid with high specific surface area and tunable chemical functionality. LDHs have abundant hydroxyl groups and ion exchange capacity, while porphyrin-based COFs with π–π interactions and higher structural stability improve the selective adsorption of target compounds. This improvement is due to the increased specific surface area and π–π and metal–ligand interactions, which enable the effective adsorption of target analytes from complex vegetable matrices (schematic image of the interactions in Fig. 4). The use of hollow fiber-SPME with this sorbent allows for the extraction of analytes with low consumption of organic solvents. Also, the Cu-AL-LDH/COF hybrid coating has high mechanical and chemical stability. Accordingly, the proposed method was compared with some of the extraction methods studied by other researchers, including HF-SPME, SPME, liquid phase microextraction (LPME), SPE, magnetic solid phase extraction (MSPE), dispersive solid phase microextraction (DSPME), and thin film microextraction (TFME) methods. A comparison of the figures of merit of the proposed method with those reported in previous studies is shown in Table 3. The LOD of the proposed method is in the range of 0.08–0.19 µg L⁻¹, which indicates its reasonable sensitivity compared with many of the reported methods in Table 3. However, evaluating the analytical performance based on a single parameter, such as LOD, cannot be the basis for a comprehensive judgment. Therefore, in this study, a set of key indicators, including LDR, RSD, and extraction time were simultaneously investigated and compared. The LDR of the present method (0.2–500.0 µg L⁻¹) indicates the ability to measure analytes in a relatively wide range of concentrations, which is important for practical applications in real samples. In addition, the obtained RSD values indicate acceptable reproducibility and precision of the method, which play an important role in the reliability of the analytical results. For example, compared to MOF-based methods such as the Cu@ZIF-8 sorbent reported by Doraghi et al. (Ref. 39), although the extraction time was shorter in that study, the present method offers a more balanced combination of higher sensitivity and wider linear range. Similarly, in the case of MIL-101@GO-based sorbents (Ref. 40) and graphene oxide-containing sorbents (Ref. 41), although acceptable performance has been reported, they exhibit lower sensitivity and cannot measure very low concentrations, which is a limitation for the extraction and measurement of low concentrations of analytes in analytical methods. Also, the higher RSD indicates lower precision of these methods. However, the proposed method shows more competitive performance in terms of linear range and detection limit. In the study of Rostami et al. (Ref. 42), for the extraction of Hexaflumuron by SPME, a shorter extraction time was reported, but the narrower linear range and higher detection limit could limit the applicability of the method. Also, in the work of Wang et al. (Ref. 43), on thin-film microextraction and the study of Ref. 42, based on LPME, although some advantages, such as shorter extraction time, are observed, overall, considering the balance between sensitivity, linear range, and precision, the present method offers comparable or better performance. In summary, the comparison results show that the Cu–Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite accommodated on polypropylene hollow fiber provides a good balance between sensitivity, linear range, reproducibility, and applicability to real vegetable samples. This balanced performance, together with the stability of the coating and compatibility with the HPLC-UV system, indicates the practical efficiency of the developed method.

Performance and greenness assessment of the method

To examine the performance of the method and its compatibility with the environment, it can be assessed using various criteria. These criteria are based on the ten and twelve principles of green chemistry. In this study, the performance of the method was evaluated using the Blue Applicability Grade Index (BAGI) software. In this criterion, there are ten principles, each of which is assigned a number. The scoring ranges from 25 to 100, and the higher the score, the more practical the method. The results are presented as a geometric mean with a score from one (worst) to ten (best). The BAGI tool uses an asteroid-shaped pictogram with the respective score, which reflects the performance of the method in the form of ten practicality attributes. The performance of this program is such that it examines 10 parameters and gives a score to each step. Finally, the total score indicates the efficiency of the method. These parameters include the type of analysis, type of analytical methods, single or multi-element analysis, simultaneous sample preparation, method of sample preparation, samples per hour, reagents and chemicals used, examination of the sample preconcentration method, the degree of automation of the analytical method, and the amount of sample required for analysis. The parameters were selected according to the presented method, and a score of 70.0 was obtained, which indicates its good compatibility with the environment and its high efficiency (Fig. 6). Among the works listed in Table 3, the proposed method in this study had a higher BAGI score, indicating its better performance and efficiency.

Conclusion

This study designed a Cu–Al–LDH/porphyrin-based COF nanocomposite by combining the COF with a large surface area and π–π interaction capability with the Cu-Al-LDH with a stable layered structure and active surface, enabling efficient adsorption of Teflubenzuron and Hexaflumuron. Direct attachment to the polypropylene hollow fiber provides HF-SPME with high sensitivity and good reproducibility, distinguishing its performance from many other sorbents. The combined properties of Cu-Al-LDH and porphyrin-based COF, including active functional groups and chemical and mechanical stability, enhanced the extraction efficiency, improved reproducibility, and extended the lifetime of the sorbent. To investigate the method’s efficiency, the extraction efficiency of the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF composite was calculated: 90.63% for Hexaflumuron compared to TAPP and 466.14% compared to Cu-Al-LDH. Its efficiency for Teflubenzuron also increased to 90.73% compared to TAPP and 466.52% compared to Cu-Al-LDH. This increase indicates the synergistic effect between Cu-Al-LDH and COF (the calculation of the percentage of extraction efficiency is explained in the Supporting Information). In addition, Cu-Al-LDH and porphyrin-based COF are less harmful from an economic and environmental perspective than heavy metals or toxic surfactants. Moreover, the Cu-Al-LDH/porphyrin-based COF sorbent showed good reproducibility, which means less waste generation and lower cost. As a result, this method offers a rapid, efficient, and environmentally friendly method for the determination of pesticides in food samples, with the advantages of simplicity, low cost, portability, and no need for complex equipment. Moreover, the limitation of this method includes the lack of automation. This sorbent cannot be directly coupled with the HPLC or GC and cannot desorption and measured the analytes online.

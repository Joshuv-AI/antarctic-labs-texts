Protocol for constructing whole-genome libraries from mini-biopsies by using laser capture microdissection
Authors: Yiyi Xi, Shaosen Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryDisclosure of the key events in tumor progress will help us deepen the understanding of tumorigenesis. Here, we present a protocol for multi-regional tissue capture of malignant continuum. We describe steps for preparing tissue sections, laser capture microdissection, and whole-genome library preparation, which enable the concurrent analysis of potential driver events in precancer initiation. This protocol overcomes challenges posed by limited DNA quantity and preserves the spatial information of the target regions.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Li et al.,1 Chang et al.,2 and Chen et al.3Subject areas: Sequence analysis, Cancer, Health Sciences, Genetics, Molecular Biology

Laser capture microdissection (LCM) of frozen sections

Timing: 2–3 h

In this step, prepare PEN-coated membrane slides with the tissues attached and perform laser microdissection to capture specific regions of the samples.

5.Embed the frozen fresh tissue in opti-mum cutting temperature compound and maintain the cryostat chamber at an optimal cutting temperature of −25°C.

Embed the frozen fresh tissue in opti-mum cutting temperature compound and maintain the cryostat chamber at an optimal cutting temperature of −25°C.

6.Cut the tissue into 5 layers, each with a thickness of 30 μm, with an interlayer spacing of 200 μm.

Cut the tissue into 5 layers, each with a thickness of 30 μm, with an interlayer spacing of 200 μm.

7.Affix the 30 μm thick sections onto PEN-coated membrane glass slides.

Affix the 30 μm thick sections onto PEN-coated membrane glass slides.

8.Place the slides in a 0.1% toluidine blue solution for 1 min to stain, then rinse them thoroughly with water.

Place the slides in a 0.1% toluidine blue solution for 1 min to stain, then rinse them thoroughly with water.

9.Allow the stained slides to air-dry before continuing.

Allow the stained slides to air-dry before continuing.

Pause point: The stained slides can be stored at −80°C for no more than three days.

10.Use a Leica LMD7000 laser microdissection microscope with a 10x objective lens and appropriate laser parameters for microdissection.

Use a Leica LMD7000 laser microdissection microscope with a 10x objective lens and appropriate laser parameters for microdissection.

11.During cutting, ensure that only the interested cell layer is targeted for cutting, with the size of the target area ranging from 0.05 to 0.06 mm2, approximately equivalent to 450 cells.

During cutting, ensure that only the interested cell layer is targeted for cutting, with the size of the target area ranging from 0.05 to 0.06 mm2, approximately equivalent to 450 cells.

12.Collect each cut sample into an empty PCR tube cap without nucleases.

Collect each cut sample into an empty PCR tube cap without nucleases.

Pause point: Store the cut sample at −80°C for a maximum of one week.

Note: In Step 8, following staining, rinse the slides in water to ensure the removal of excess dye and complete dissolution and detachment of the embedding compound surrounding the tissue. This step is crucial to prevent any residual embedding material from affecting the energy of laser cutting.

Expected outcomes

Library quality control

After clean-up and size selection of amplified libraries, the product undergoes a “quality control” step to verify the library’s concentration and size distribution with LabChip GX Touch 24 Nucleic Acid Analyzer. For a typical library, the concentration is about 50 nmol/L. The product size ranges from 300 to 700 bp, with a peak about 400 bp (Figure 1). Primer dimers are located between 100 and 200 bp. Generally, there should be little to no primer dimers, as shown in Figure 1. If primer dimers are detected, their peak intensity should be less than 50% of the product peak; otherwise, an additional purification round is required.

Data analysis strategy

As illustrated in Figure 2, we initially section a single tissue sample into 2–5 continuous layers, each with a thickness of 30 μm, and spaced 200 μm apart. Based on the H&E staining results, LCM is employed to extract micro-biopsy samples of approximately 450 cells from the corresponding regions. Subsequently, whole-genome library construction and sequencing are performed on these samples. Following sequencing depth and coverage screening, as well as quality control, we proceed with downstream analyses on the sequencing data from qualified samples. These analyses include somatic mutation detection, copy number alteration assessment, and clonal evolution tree reconstruction.

Limitations

•We can capture cells in different spatial locations through microdissection from various kinds of tissue, there will be inevitable sensitivity loss and false positive (or negative) results when deal with a small number of cells, as well as low capture efficiency.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

In step 7, during the cryosectioning process, the temperature of the PEN-coated membrane glass slides may decrease over time, potentially leading to unsuccessful tissue adherence.

Potential solution

During step 7, the temperature of the PEN-coated membrane glass slide decreases gradually, causing the tissue sections to be unable to adhere to the membrane. Therefore, it’s important to ensure that the temperature of the PEN-coated membrane glass slide remains at room temperature.

Problem 2

During the laser microdissection process, fibrous materials that cannot be cut by the laser may be encountered.

Potential solution

The fibrous materials might be residual embedding compound. Therefore, before step 8 of staining, rinse the slides in water to remove excess dye and ensure complete dissolution and detachment of the embedding compound surrounding the tissue.

Problem 3

In step 5 of Part II, the products of tagmentation are either excessively small or excessively large.

Potential solution

The cleavage efficiency of the Tn5 enzyme is correlated with the quantity of DNA, thus requiring the determination of enzyme dosage based on the amount of experimental DNA during testing.

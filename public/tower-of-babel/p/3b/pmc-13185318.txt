Multimodal radiomics for early prediction of pathological complete response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in breast cancer: integration of amide proton transfer weighted imaging in radiomics
Authors: Mingzhe Xu, Ailing Wang, Dongqiu Shan, Xuejun Chen, Renzhi Zhang, Chunmiao Xu, Junhui Yuan, Jing Li, Guangguang An, Zhiwei Shen, Yue Wu, Funing Chu, Guang Yang, Jinrong Qu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEarly prediction of pathological complete response (pCR) to neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) in breast cancer remains challenging. Traditional imaging lacks molecular sensitivity, while single-modality radiomics may miss tumor microenvironment dynamics. Integrating amide proton transfer-weighted imaging (APTWI)—quantifying protein levels linked to chemo-response—into multimodal radiomics may enable precise early pCR prediction, guiding personalized therapy. This study aims to develop and validate a multimodal radiomics model, integrating amide proton transfer-weighted imaging (APTWI), diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), and early-phase contrast-enhanced T1WI, for preoperative prediction of pathological complete response (pCR) in breast cancer patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC).MethodsThis retrospective study included 109 women (mean age 50 ± 10 years) with untreated breast cancer between May 2023 and August 2024, underwent NAC and pretreatment MRI scanning, including APTWI, DWI, and dynamic contrast-enhanced T1WI. Three-dimensional tumor segmentation was performed using ITK-SNAP. Radiomics features were extracted from APT, ADC maps, and enhanced subtraction images (30s/90s) using PyRadiomics, adhering to IBSI guidelines. LASSO regression selected predictive features, followed by Support Vector Machine and Logistic Regression classifiers with five-fold cross-validation. Performance was evaluated via ROC analysis, calibration curves, decision curve analysis (DCA), and SHAP interpretability.ResultsPatients were randomly divided into training (n = 77) and testing (n = 32) cohorts. The integrated model combining clinical (e.g. HER2, Ki-67) and radiomics features achieved superior predictive performance (AUC = 0.888, 95% CI: 0.768–1.000) versus single-modality radiomics (ADC: AUC = 0.708), standalone clinical (AUC = 0.815), or radiomics models (AUC = 0.763). Calibration showed strong prediction-reality agreement (Brier score = 0.157). DCA confirmed clinical utility at 10%–65% thresholds. SHAP identified HER2 status and ADC-derived texture features (e.g. Cluster Shade) as top predictors.ConclusionMultimodal integration of APTWI, DWI, contrast-enhanced T1WI, and clinicopathological biomarkers enables accurate preoperative pCR prediction in breast cancer, supporting personalized NAC management.Advances in knowledgeCompared to single-modal imaging, our multimodal radiomics model provides more comprehensive biomarkers, thereby enabling personalized therapeutic decisions and optimizing NAC regimens to reduce overtreatment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02311-5.Keywords: Breast cancer, Magnetic resonance imaging, Amide proton transfer, Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, Radiomics

Introduction

Breast cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality among women globally, with approximately 2.3 million new cases annually [1]. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) has become a cornerstone in managing locally advanced breast cancer, aiming to downstage tumors, facilitate surgical resection, and eradicate micrometastases [2]. Although 20–30% of patients achieve pathological complete response (pCR) following NAC—an outcome strongly associated with improved long-term survival—current prediction approaches still primarily rely on clinicopathological factors, such as HER2 status and the Ki-67 proliferation index. Conventional imaging-based morphological evaluation, particularly contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), has been widely employed to assess treatment response after neoadjuvant therapy [3–5]. However, its ability to accurately predict pCR remains limited. Previous studies have reported considerable variability in diagnostic performance, with sensitivities ranging from 36% to 100% and specificities from 45% to 100%, and the predictive accuracy has been shown to vary substantially across different studies and molecular subtypes of breast cancer, highlighting the limitations of morphology-based imaging assessment [3–11]. Consequently, the development of multimodal predictive methods that can identify potential NAC responders early and accurately has become a critical challenge in optimizing individualized therapeutic strategies.

Radiomics, through the high-throughput extraction of quantitative imaging features, presents a robust framework for elucidating tumor heterogeneity and the dynamics of the tumor microenvironment [3, 12]. Amide proton transfer weighted imaging (APTWI) specifically captures the chemical exchange saturation transfer (CEST) effect of intracellular amide protons, facilitating non-invasive characterization of tumor protein metabolism and indirectly reflecting cellular proliferation status [13, 14]. Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) quantifies tumor cellular density via water diffusivity, measured as the apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values. Moreover, contrast-enhanced T1WI, particularly early-phase (arterial and venous phase) contrast-enhanced T1WI parameters such as enhancement rate and net enhancement—which reflect vascular permeability—have been associated with NAC efficacy [15]. These multimodal parameters offer functionally complementary insights into tumor microenvironment dynamics [14, 16, 17]. However, previous studies remain controversial regarding the predictive utility of quantitative parameter ADC value for NAC response assessment [18]. Besides, to our knowledge, the prognostic potential of radiomics-based analysis derived from APTWI in predicting pCR following NAC remains unexplored.

This study aimed to develop and validate an integrative predictive model that synergistically combined multiparametric radiomic features from APTWI, DWI, and early-phase contrast-enhanced images with clinicomolecular biomarkers, including molecular subtypes, HER2 expression, and Ki-67 index [17, 19, 20]. This approach seeks to enhance the accuracy of preoperative predictions of pCR in breast cancer, thereby contributing to more personalized treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Committee of the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Zhengzhou University (No.2024–170-001). The requirement for informed consent from patients was waived in accordance with Article 39 of the Administrative Measures for Ethical Review of Biomedical Research Involving Humans (Order No. 11, National Health and Family Planning Commission of China), as the study used anonymized MRI data previously obtained for clinical purposes and involved no risk, privacy, or commercial interest to the participants.

Patients

Consecutive 378 female breast invasive ductal carcinoma patients (mean age: 50 ± 10 years) who underwent NAC and pretreatment breast MRI from May 2023 to August 2024 were enrolled in this study. Furthermore, 269 patients were excluded because of following reasons: (1) incomplete sequences or poor image quality of MRI (n = 236), (2) incomplete pathology data (n = 2), (3) incomplete course of NAC or distant metastasis during NAC (n = 28), (4) did not undergo breast surgery after NAC (n = 3). Finally, 109 patients were included in this study. This study flowchart was provided in Fig. 1.

Flowchart of the patient selection process. NAC = neoadjuvant chemotherapy; pCR = pathologic complete response

Treatment protocols were individualized based on immunohistochemical profiles (e.g., HER2 status, ER/PR expression) and genetic characteristics of biopsy samples. Regimens included: TCbHP (docetaxel + carboplatin + trastuzumab + pertuzumab), TCb (docetaxel + carboplatin), TCH (docetaxel + carboplatin + trastuzumab), PH (paclitaxel + trastuzumab) combined with pyrotinib, and TE (docetaxel + epirubicin). Each treatment cycle lasted 21 days, with a total of 6 to 8 cycles administered.

All enrolled breast cancer patients underwent surgical resection after NAC and preoperative breast MRI prior to surgery. According to the St. Gallen International Breast Cancer Consensus [21], pCR was defined as the absence of residual invasive carcinoma in the primary breast lesion (ypT0) and no regional lymph node metastasis (ypN0) in the surgical specimen.

Imaging protocol

All MRI examinations were performed using a 3.0T scanner (Ingenia CX, Philips Healthcare) equipped with an 8-channel bilateral phased-array breast coil. The imaging protocol comprised the following sequences: axial T1WI, fat-suppressed T2WI, APTWI, DWI, axial contrast-enhanced imaging (comprising 7 phases) and sagittal delayed phase imaging. Sequence parameters were listed in Supplementary Materials 1.

APTWI was performed using a 3D multi-shot turbo spin echo (TSE) sequence with SPIR fat suppression. The saturation RF pulse was applied at seven frequency offsets (±2.7, ±3.5, ±4.3, and −1560 ppm) with a saturation B1 power of 2 µT and a duration of 2 seconds. A B0 map was obtained using a 3-point Dixon method with three echo times, and automatic B0 magnetic field inhomogeneity correction was applied to the APTw images. In addition to the parameters listed in Supplementary Materials 1, the imaging parameters also included slice number = 7, SENSE = 1.3, TSE factor = 174, and NSA = 1. To ensure complete breast coverage and optimal image quality, the APT sequence was repeated, generating a total of 14 slices. Aside from two breath-holding phases during the prescan, the APT sequence was acquired during free breathing.

A fat-saturated gradient-echo sequence was used to acquire contrast-enhanced T1-weighted images after intravenous injection of Gadoteric acid (Gd-DOTA, Hengrui Medicine) at a dose of 0.1 mmol/kg and an injection rate of 1.5 mL/s via a power injector.

Image processing and analysis

All acquired images were uploaded to the ISP workstation and processed using MR Permeability analysis software, where APTWI, DWI, and contrast-enhanced images were processed to obtain APT map, ADC map, and subtraction images at 30 and 90 seconds after post-contrast injection (Images noted “SUB1” and “SUB2” were obtained by subtracting images acquired before injection from those acquired 30 and 90 seconds after the injection). Two radiologists (with 10 and 18 years of experience in MRI, respectively) evaluated the MR images according to the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System standard, and they were blinded to lesion locations and patients’ pathological and clinical information.

The 3D regions of interest (ROIs) of whole tumor region were delineated on APT map, ADC map, SUB1 and SUB2 images using MR segmentation software ITK-SNAP, referencing to contrast-enhanced subtraction images. The initial segmentation was performed by a junior radiologist and subsequently reviewed and refined by a senior radiologist to ensure the accuracy and consistency of the tumor delineation.

Radiomics feature extraction

The MRI scan data of all enrolled patients were randomly divided into a training set and a testing set in a 7:3 ratio.

To mitigate the impact of varying scan resolutions on radiomic features, image pixel size and spacing were standardized. The voxel size of all data was unified to a pixel pitch of 0.5 mm × 0.5 mm × 1 mm. Bilinear interpolation was applied for image data, whereas nearest-neighbor interpolation was used for ROI data.

Radiomic feature extraction was performed using the open-source Python package Pyradiomics (v 3.1.0), targeting the tumor regions in each imaging sequence. Extracted radiomic features encompassed shape features, first-order matrix, gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM), gray-level size zone matrix (GLSZM), gray-level run length matrix (GLRLM), neighboring gray tone difference matrix (NGTDM), and gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM). First-order and texture features were derived from both the original images and eight sub-bands generated via 3D wavelet transformation. The entire feature extraction process was conducted in strict accordance with the Imaging Biomarker Standardization Initiative (IBSI) guidelines to ensure feature repeatability.

Predictive model development

All radiomic analyses were performed following the FeAture Explorer (FAE) framework [22]. Using the extracted clinical and radiomic features, we developed single-sequence radiomics models (APT, ADC, SUB1 and SUB2), clinical feature model, radiomics model and integrated model to investigate the predictive value of APT, DWI, and contrast-enhanced T1WI for achieving pCR after NAC in breast cancer.

In the training set, features were first standardized using Z-score normalization. Feature selection was conducted using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression (alpha range: 5−3- 5−2). LASSO regression introduced L1 regularization, which shrinks the coefficients of irrelevant variables to zero, thereby selecting important features. Additionally, the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) analysis (threshold = 0.99) was employed to reduce feature dimensionality. Subsequently, we used feature selection methods such as Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE), Kruskal-Wallis (KW) test, and Relief algorithm to select the features. Based on the selected features, two machine learning classification algorithms, Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Logistic Regression (LR), were utilized to train classification models. The specific details are provided in Supplementary Materials 3 and 4.

Five-fold cross-validation was employed in the training set to determine the optimal feature set and classification model by selecting the model with the highest average area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) in the testing set. After determining the model and hyperparameters, the final model was retrained using all training data, and its performance was evaluated on the testing set.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed in Python (version 3.9.21). Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (normally distributed) or median (interquartile range) (non-normal distribution, assessed via Shapiro-Wilk test). Independent samples t-test or Mann-Whitney U test were used to compare the differences in the continuous variables, and Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test were used to compare the differences in categorical variables. Classification performance was evaluated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and multiple quantitative metrics, including AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC). The differences in the area under the ROC curves (AUCs) were compared using the Delong test.

Calibration performance was evaluated using calibration curves generated via Bootstrap method (1000 resamples). The model’s calibration performance was evaluated with Spiegelhalter’s Z Test, and the accuracy of probability predictions was assessed by the Brier Score. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to plot net benefit versus prediction threshold to assess the clinical utility of the models. The feature importance analysis was performed using the Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) method to quantify each feature’s contribution to the model’s predictions. All statistical tests were two-sided, with P values < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Interobserver agreement analysis

The intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) for the clinical and MRI imaging categorical variables assessed by the two radiologists were all greater than 0.9, demonstrating excellent interobserver agreement. Consequently, the evaluations of the more experienced radiologist were adopted for subsequent statistical analyses.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that the multimodal radiomics-based model integrating APTWI, DWI, and contrast-enhanced T1WI exhibits good predictive performance for pCR in breast cancer patients following NAC. Notably, this represented the first investigation incorporating APTWI-derived radiomics features for pCR prediction in breast cancer. Furthermore, the integrated model, which combined clinical features and radiomics signatures, outperformed single - sequence models (clinical: AUC = 0.815; radiomics: AUC = 0.763) and achieved superior discriminative ability (testing AUC = 0.888), underscoring the clinical value of multimodal data fusion in personalizing treatment strategies.

The training and testing cohorts demonstrated balanced baseline characteristics, with identical pCR rates (38%) aligning with established literature [6]. This homogeneity strengthens the validity of our validation framework. The enhanced performance of the integrated model highlights the complementary roles of clinical and imaging biomarkers: while clinical features (e.g., HER2 status) reflect tumor biology, radiomics captures spatial heterogeneity—a critical advantage over conventional MRI assessments reliant on tumor volumetry [4].

Among the single-sequence radiomics models, ADC map derived from DWI showed the highest diagnostic performance, consistent with previous studies emphasizing the role of DWI in predicting pCR after NAC in breast cancer [18, 23]. However, our previous research revealed that the histogram features extracted from whole-tumor ROIs excluding cystic-necrotic areas exhibited suboptimal predictive value for pCR (maximum AUC = 0.616), which was inferior to the predictive efficacy achieved in the current study. This comparative analysis suggests that the cystic-necrotic regions within breast tumors might represent imaging biomarkers for pCR prediction post-NAC, potentially reflecting tumor microenvironment characteristics associated with treatment response. The APTWI-based radiomics model, representing a novel application in pCR prediction post-NAC, also demonstrated promising predictive performance in this study, ranking second only to DWI. This innovative approach, leveraging the unique capability of APTWI to detect cellular protein content and pH alterations, provides valuable insights into tumor microenvironment changes during NAC, potentially offering a new dimension to treatment response assessment. However, the lower performance of SUB1 and SUB2 models suggests that early subtraction images post-contrast injection might provide limited additional value in pCR prediction.

The superior performance of the integrated model was further supported by DCA, which demonstrated its clinical utility across a wide range of threshold probabilities (10% − 65%), suggesting that the integrated model could be a valuable method for clinicians in decision-making. Besides, in this study, the interpretability analysis the integrated model using SHAP revealed that HER2 expression and texture features, such as Cluster Shade from ADC images, were the most influential predictors in the integrated model. These aligned with the known biological significance of HER2 status in treatment response [2, 24] and highlighted the potential of radiomics features to capture subtle tumor heterogeneity. Specifically, HER2-positive breast cancer patients treated with NAC containing anti-HER2 targeted agents (e.g. trastuzumab, pertuzumab) exhibit a higher pCR rate by blocking HER2 oncogenic signaling pathway, synergistically enhancing chemosensitivity, and promoting immune-mediated tumor elimination [25, 26].

Among the radiomic features with significant impact on the output of the integrated model, higher values of ADC_log-sigma-1-mm-3D_glcm_ClusterShade and ADC_lbp-3D-m1_firstorder_Skewness in pre-treatment ADC images were associated with an increased likelihood of achieving pCR after NAC, while lower values of APT_log-sigma-1-mm-3D_glcm_ClusterShade in APT images correlated with a higher probability of pCR. This opposing correlations of radiomic features from ADC and APT images with pCR after NAC may arise from their distinct biological interpretations. The ADC map reflects the diffusion capacity of water molecules within tissues, thereby indicating cellular density. In ADC images, elevated Cluster Shade and positive Skewness reflect structural heterogeneity characterized by coexisting necrotic regions and viable tumor areas. Such local spatial heterogeneity suggests the potential existence of chemosensitive subclones within the tumor, and pre-treatment necrosis may enhance chemotherapy efficacy by releasing tumor-associated antigens, thereby activating the immune microenvironment (e.g., T-cell infiltration) [27, 28], consistent with previous studies demonstrating associations between ADC heterogeneity and favorable NAC outcomes [29, 30]. Conversely, the APT image primarily reflects the content of proteins and polypeptides in tissues, suggesting cellular proliferation and metabolic activity. High Cluster Shade values in APT images signify metabolic heterogeneity, potentially accompanied by abnormal vascular distribution or interstitial hypertension that may hinder the uniform chemotherapy drug penetration [31]. Meanwhile, this metabolic imbalance may activate pro-survival signaling pathways (e.g., HIF-1α, NF-κB) through hypoxia and acidosis induction, promoting cell survival and drug resistance gene expression, thereby reducing chemotherapy effectiveness [32–34]. These different trends not only highlight the complementary roles of ADC and APT images in characterizing tumor biology but also underscore the imperative to integrate multimodal radiomics for robust predictive modeling.

Additionally, among the key features influencing the output of our comprehensive model, high Ki-67 expression (≥30%) [35] showed a positive correlation with pCR rates, and HER2-enriched and triple-negative molecular subtypes were more likely to achieve pCR, consistent with prior studies reported by Sueta, Zhang et al. [36–39]. High Ki-67 expression indicates more active tumor cell proliferation, rendering these cells more susceptible to NAC agents such as anthracyclines and taxanes, which primarily target rapidly dividing cells by disrupting DNA replication or microtubule function [36]. Moreover, previous studies have demonstrated that highly proliferative tumor cells with active division exhibit shorter repair times following neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC)-induced damage, thereby promoting chemotherapy-triggered DNA damage accumulation. Meanwhile, their enhanced tumor blood flow and vascular permeability facilitate chemotherapeutic drug delivery through multiple pathways, rendering them more sensitive to NAC and associated with higher rates of tumor regression and pCR [40, 41]. HER2-enriched and triple-negative breast cancers, which exhibit a higher proportion of high-grade tissue and aggressive biological behavior, fall into this tumor category [42, 43].

This study had several limitations. First, the single-center design may introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of our findings. External validation with multicenter datasets is necessary to confirm the robustness and clinical applicability of our model. Second, our model focused on pre-treatment imaging features and did not account for dynamic changes during NAC. Our future studies should integrate Delta-Radiomics [44], which captures treatment-induced biological changes through longitudinal feature analysis, to improve real-time response assessment and guide personalized therapy. Finally, due to the limited spatial resolution and signal-to-noise ratio of APTWI and DWI, larger lesions tend to exhibit more stable APT signals than smaller tumors, which may affect feature reproducibility in small lesions. Additionally, all included patients had tumor diameters exceeding 2 cm, potentially limiting the generalizability of our findings to smaller tumors populations.

In conclusion, a multimodal radiomics model based on APTWI, DWI, and early-phase contrast-enhanced subtraction imaging enables effectively preoperative prediction of pCR to NAC in breast cancer. Furthermore, the incorporation of clinical features, such as HER2 status, molecular subtypes and Ki-67 expression, further enhances predictive performance. This non-invasive approach offers a promising tool for pre-treatment assessment, enabling the identification of patients who are most likely to benefit from NAC, thereby potentially avoiding unnecessary treatments and optimizing therapeutic strategies.

The needs of family caregivers of patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Mansoureh Ashghali Farahani, Seyed Kamran Soltani-Arabshahi, Maryam Kashani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionFamily caregivers of patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) play a pivotal role in day-to-day care and disease management. This scoping review aims to identify, describe, and map the needs of family caregivers of patients with COPD worldwide. For the purposes of this review, caregiver “needs” refer to requirements that support caregivers’ ability to provide care and maintain their own well-being, including informational, emotional/psychological, practical (skills and daily care tasks), social, financial, respite, and navigation/support-service needs.Methods and analysisThe primary research question guiding this scoping review is: What are the needs of family caregivers of patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)? This review will be conducted following the methodological framework outlined by the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) for scoping reviews. The eligibility criteria will be determined using the Population, Concept, and Context (PCC) framework. A comprehensive search will be carried out in electronic databases including MEDLINE via PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, as well as Persian scientific databases and grey literature sources, covering studies published up to May 2024 without language restrictions. Two independent reviewers will screen and select studies for inclusion. A standardized data extraction form, developed specifically for this review, will be used to collect relevant information. Data will be analyzed thematically to identify and categorize the various needs of family caregivers. Findings will be synthesized and presented through tables, figures, and narrative summaries to highlight key themes and gaps in the existing literature.Ethics and disseminationThis research does not entail the participation of human or animal subjects. All data utilized will be extracted exclusively from existing published literature. Introduction

Chronic illnesses are increasingly imposing substantial burdens on both healthcare systems and individuals worldwide. Patients living with chronic conditions often face uncertainty, restricted living environments, and feelings of helplessness or loss of control. In response, there is growing emphasis on interventions that empower patients and their caregivers to regain control over their health and quality of life. The World Health Organization underscores the importance of patient and caregiver empowerment for prevention and health promotion, recognizing it as a critical component in improving care outcomes [1, 2].

Among chronic illnesses, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a progressive respiratory disorder characterized by airflow limitation, recurrent exacerbations, and diverse clinical symptoms. Patients frequently experience cough, sputum production, breathlessness, fatigue, dry mouth, and psychological symptoms such as anxiety, worry, and irritability, which collectively impair daily functioning and overall quality of life [3, 4]. Globally, COPD ranks among the leading causes of morbidity and mortality, affecting millions across different demographics and ranking as the fourth leading cause of death in the United States, with approximately 16 million diagnosed cases and many more undiagnosed [5]. The financial burden of COPD is substantial, with direct costs in the United States and Europe estimated at $32 billion and €38.6 billion, respectively. In Asia, annual treatment and hospitalization costs per patient vary widely, with figures reported as $1,544, $3,077, and $2,335 in Iran, Korea, and Singapore, and hospitalization costs ranging from $296 to $1,868 across multiple countries [6].

Due to its progressive nature, COPD requires ongoing, intensive care, which is predominantly provided by family caregivers. These caregivers—typically spouses, adult children, or close relatives—play an essential role in symptom management, treatment adherence, assistance with activities of daily living, and provision of emotional support [7–9] As with other chronic diseases, family caregivers play an important role in the management of COPD as a potential change agent that can facilitate and increase patient compliance with treatment; because in addition to shortness of breath and decreased physical activity of the patient and his support needs for compliance with treatment, the role of family caregivers in the care of patients with COPD is essential [10]. They also act as crucial intermediaries between patients and healthcare professionals, conveying information on patient symptoms, treatment effects, and care needs [11, 12]. Beyond direct care, caregivers coordinate healthcare services, advocate for patients, and facilitate communication across settings, particularly where professional healthcare services are limited or inaccessible [8].

Family caregivers often spouses, adult children, or close relatives—provide essential support for people living with COPD, assisting with symptom management, treatment adherence, and activities of daily living. Yet caregivers frequently experience emotional distress, physical strain, social constraints, and financial burden [10]. Despite their indispensable contributions, family caregivers frequently experience significant emotional distress, anxiety, physical strain, social limitations, and financial burden [13, 14] Caregivers of COPD patients experience significant emotional strain due to the demanding nature of caregiving and the progressive trajectory of the disease. Caregivers often reported feelings of anxiety, stress, and depression, which were largely attributed to the uncertainties surrounding the patient’s condition and the lack of professional support. In addition, caregivers commonly felt isolated, as the responsibilities of caregiving limited their ability to engage in social activities and maintain personal relationships [15]. Their needs are often overlooked, and they are considered a vulnerable population, sometimes described as “hidden patients.” The interplay between patient and caregiver well-being is complex: patient factors including functional dependency, illness duration, comorbidities, and emotional distress directly influence caregiver burden, while caregiver stress can negatively affect patient outcomes [16].

Healthcare professionals increasingly recognize family caregivers as integral members of the care team; however, the primary focus remains on patient care, often neglecting caregivers’ readiness, mental health, and capacity to fulfill their roles. Without adequate support, caregivers are at risk of physical, cognitive, and emotional decline, which can compromise patient care and increase societal healthcare costs [17]. Family caregivers thus require targeted support to manage the multidimensional challenges of caregiving, including educational needs, psychological support, and access to healthcare services [13].

Given the essential yet frequently underappreciated role of family caregivers in COPD management, understanding and addressing their needs is crucial for reducing caregiver strain, enhancing their quality of life, and promoting effective care for patients. This scoping review aims to explore these key needs, providing a foundation for interventions that support family caregivers and optimize patient outcomes [8]. Despite growing attention to caregiver burden, existing evidence on the specific needs of family caregivers in COPD remains fragmented across settings and study designs, with limited synthesis to inform practice, policy, and education.In this review, we define caregiver “needs” as the resources, information, skills, and supports required to perform caregiving tasks and to safeguard caregivers’ own health and well-being. These needs span several domains, including informational (disease knowledge, care plans), emotional/psychological (stress, anxiety, coping), practical/skills (medication administration, recognizing exacerbations), social (peer/family support), financial, respite, and navigation of health and community.

Objective

This scoping review aims to identify, describe, and map the needs of family caregivers of patients with COPD worldwide, providing evidence to inform interventions, health policy, and educational programs.

The main research question guiding this scoping review is: What needs of family caregivers of patients with COPD have been reported in the literature, and how are these needs characterized across care settings and contexts?

Eligibility criteria

In line with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology for scoping reviews, the Participants, Concept, and Context (PCC) framework is utilized to establish well-defined objectives and determine appropriate inclusion criteri [18].

Inclusion criteria

Studies involving family caregivers (spouses, children, relatives, significant others) of patients diagnosed with COPD, in any care setting, reporting on caregiver needs in any domain (emotional, informational, practical, social, financial, respite). Eligible study types include qualitative, quantitative, mixed-methods, and reviews.

Exclusion criteria

Studies focusing exclusively on professional caregivers or institutionalized care settings (e.g., nursing homes); studies without primary data on caregiver needs.

Types of sources

In alignment with the aim of this study, various types of published sources will be considered, including quantitative, qualitative, mixed-method studies, reviews, as well as studies focused on the design and development of models, frameworks, protocols, or practical and clinical guidelines. All sources must provide valuable and reliable information related to the needs and experiences of family caregivers of patients with Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD).

Methods

The eligibility criteria, data extraction, and synthesis plan were developed to directly address the primary and sub-questions stated in the Introduction.This scoping review protocol is designed in alignment with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology for scoping reviews. We also followed the PRISMA-ScR (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews) to ensure transparency and comprehensiveness in our reporting process [19].the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology for scoping reviews was selected because it allows for a comprehensive mapping of existing evidence, identification of research gaps, and inclusion of diverse study designs without the constraints of a formal quality assessment. This approach is particularly suitable for exploring a broad topic such as the needs of family caregivers of patients with COPD, where evidence is heterogeneous and spans multiple contexts.

Search strategy

To ensure a broad and inclusive literature base, we adopted a three-step search strategy. First, an initial, limited search was performed on PubMed and Google Scholar to identify relevant keywords and index terms. Next, we carried out an extensive search across multiple international databases, including MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Library, Scopus, Web of Science, and ScienceDirect. We also included Persian-language databases such as SID, Magiran, and Irandoc. In addition to peer-reviewed articles, we explored grey literature sources such as ProQuest dissertations and guidelines from organizations like WHO, NICE, and NHS. Finally, we reviewed the reference lists of all selected studies to capture any additional relevant material.

No restrictions were placed on language. However, studies that could not be translated by our research team were acknowledged but excluded from data synthesis. A detailed sample search strategy for PubMed is provided in Box 1 of the manuscript.

Study/source of evidence selection

All retrieved records were imported into EndNote X8 to remove duplicates. A two-phase screening was then undertaken by two independent reviewers. In phase one, titles and abstracts were reviewed. In phase two, full-text articles were evaluated against predefined inclusion criteria. Any disagreements between reviewers were resolved through discussion or, if necessary, consultation with a third reviewer. The entire selection process, including exclusions and justifications, will be presented in accordance with the PRISMA-ScR flowchart. The search results, along with the selection process and justifications for article inclusion or exclusion, will be presented in accordance with the PRISMA-ScR flow chart [20]. The study selection process is anticipated to be completed within six weeks, ensuring that all inclusion and exclusion criteria are transparently applied to the identified records.

Data extraction

We developed a structured data extraction form aligned with the review’s objectives and the PCC framework (Population, Concept, Context). Two reviewers will independently extract data, and the form will be piloted and refined through group discussions before final use. The form captures key aspects such as study design, participant characteristics, caregiver needs, and contextual factors.

In cases of discrepancies or missing information, the original study authors may be contacted to ensure a thorough and accurate data set. The data extraction process will also be documented in detail in the final publication.

The data extraction form will be tailored to the review objectives and will typically include key information related to population, concept, and context [18]. Specific extracted data will encompass the study design, characteristics of participants (e.g., family caregivers of patients with COPD), the primary concept (caregiver needs), and contextual factors influencing caregiving (e.g., healthcare settings, resources, and social supports).

In alignment with the purpose of the review, and consistent with the methodology for scoping reviews, no formal quality assessment will be performed on the included studies. Instead, each study will be assigned a “level of evidence” rating using the Joanna Briggs Institute’s (JBI) established categorisation system. This approach ensures that the review prioritises breadth and coverage of evidence while offering relevant context for understanding the literature and supporting future research or practice recommendations [21]. The data extraction form will be developed based on the PCC framework, pilot-tested on a small sample of studies, and refined as needed. Two reviewers will extract data independently, and discrepancies will be resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer.

Data analysis and presentation

Thematic analysis will be used to identify and organize caregiver needs into major domains such as emotional support, informational needs, healthcare navigation, and practical assistance. While thematic analysis is often associated with qualitative synthesis, in this review it will be applied descriptively to organize and map caregiver needs identified in the literature, consistent with JBI guidance for charting and collating results in scoping reviews, and without interpretive theorizing. The JBI framework was selected because it enables comprehensive mapping of heterogeneous evidence (qualitative, quantitative, mixed-methods) and is well suited for identifying and categorizing complex, multifaceted phenomena such as caregiver needs.Data will be coded, categorized, and mapped visually to illustrate patterns across studies. A narrative summary will accompany these visuals to highlight key findings and gaps in the literature.

Quality appraisal

Given the nature of scoping reviews, no formal risk of bias assessment will be conducted. However, each study will be assigned a level of evidence based on JBI’s classification system to provide context and guide future research.

To categorize and consolidate findings

To organize and synthesize the data effectively, a structured, multi-step process will be undertaken:

Identifying core concepts

The research team will first develop a preliminary framework outlining the core caregiving needs, derived from the studies included in the review. Each team member will independently examine the selected articles to extract caregiving needs. These observations will then be reviewed collectively, with any inconsistencies resolved through discussion. This collaborative approach ensures a shared understanding and results in a finalized framework tailored to the caregiving needs of COPD patients.

Systematic coding and classification

We will apply simple codes for organizing extracted needs (e.g., N1, N2…) and studies (S1, S2…), avoiding overly complex identifiers.“, and so on. For instance, the notation “N1S1” refers to the 1rd identified need from Study 1. Two reviewers will independently apply this coding system to ensure consistency and minimize bias. The structured categorization of data will facilitate clear tracking and comparison of findings across studies.

Domain mapping

Once coding is complete, similar caregiving needs will be clustered into broader thematic domains relevant to caregiving in the context of COPD. These domains may include categories such as emotional well-being, healthcare system navigation, education/training, and practical support. The occurrence of each domain across studies will be quantified to highlight the most frequently reported caregiver needs.

Visual and narrative reporting

The synthesized results will be displayed both visually and narratively. A comprehensive data map will be created to show the relationships between specific needs and their corresponding domains. This will be complemented by illustrative tools such as graphs, tables, and charts. Additionally, a detailed narrative summary will explain and contextualize the key findings, ensuring that the data presentation aligns with the review’s core objectives and research questions.

Discussion

This scoping review will provide a comprehensive overview of the needs of family caregivers of patients with COPD, highlighting gaps in the literature and identifying areas for future research. Findings may inform the development of targeted interventions, educational resources, and policies aimed at reducing caregiver burden and improving patient outcomes. By mapping evidence across diverse settings, the review will also support healthcare providers in recognizing and addressing caregivers’ unmet needs. The findings of this review have potential implications for clinical practice, policy, aging, and education. In clinical practice, they can guide healthcare providers in developing caregiver-centered interventions and support programs. For health policy, the results can inform resource allocation and policy frameworks aimed at supporting informal caregivers. In the context of aging, the review can contribute to strategies for supporting older caregivers who may face unique challenges. In education, both undergraduate and graduate health curricula can integrate evidence-based content on caregiver needs to prepare future professionals for collaborative, family-centered care.

Limitations

As a scoping review, this study will not assess the quality of included studies, which may affect the interpretation of findings. Additionally, despite no language restrictions in the search strategy, studies that cannot be translated by the research team will be excluded, potentially leading to language bias. Grey literature searches may also miss unpublished studies.

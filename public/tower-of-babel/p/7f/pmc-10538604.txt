Epidemiology of liver transplantation and post-LT complications in Germany: nationwide study (2005–2018)
Authors: Wenyi Gu, Louisa Schaaf, Hannah Hortlik, Yasmin Zeleke, Maximilian J. Brol, Andreas A. Schnitzbauer, Wolf O. ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo date, liver transplantation (LT) is the only curative treatment for cirrhosis and early-diagnosed progressive acute liver failure (ALF). However, LT results in morbidities and mortality even post-LT. Different comorbidities may follow and further increase mortality and morbidity. In this study, we investigated the outcomes and their trends over a period of 14 years among hospitalized patients evaluated for LT, transplant and post-LT in Germany.MethodsThis German nationwide study investigated the number of admissions of patients hospitalized for evaluation of LT and post-LT on related comorbidities and complications between 2005 and 2018 based on the DRG system with ICD-10/OPS codes. 14 745 patients were put on the LT waiting list and 12 836 underwent LT during the observational period.ResultsThe LT number decreased by 2.3% over time, while the waiting list mortality rate increased by 5%. By contrast, the in-hospital mortality rate decreased by 3%, especially in ALF patients (decrease of 16%). Interestingly, admissions of post-LT patients for complications almost doubled, driven mainly by complications of immunosuppression (tripled). Importantly, post-LT patients with acute kidney injury (20.2%) and biliodigestive anastomosis (18.4%) showed the highest in-hospital mortality rate of all complications.ConclusionIn conclusion, the decrease in LT leads most probably to the increased in-hospital mortality of patients on the waiting list. Interestingly, in-hospital mortality decreased in LT patients. Post-LT comorbidities requiring hospitalization increased in the observational period and management of patients post-LT with AKI or biliodigestive anastomosis should be addressed.

Background

Liver transplantation (LT) is the only curative treatment for patients with end-stage liver disease or acute liver failure (ALF), hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and other chronic liver diseases refractory to medical intervention [1–3]. LT marks a decision over life and death for either the patients in question or others on the waiting list. Yet, allocation of donor livers has been proven difficult due to their scarcity [4,5]. In Germany, between 2010 and 2012, the practice of allocation was afflicted by irregularities, which required more regulation and tight monitoring [6]. Negative media coverage caused a significant drop in donors. Therefore, the low numbers of available donor organs resulted in the selection and prioritization of waiting list patients with a high mortality, as previously described [7].

Although LT is life-saving, it still shows a considerable in-hospital mortality rate, especially in the case of ALF [2,3]. In addition, LT is also associated with significant morbidity [8]. The life-long post-transplant immunosuppression may induce or aggregate comorbidities [8]. Circulatory and respiratory diseases, especially, may be related to immunosuppression with or without their sequelae. Infections are frequent and the imbalance between too much immunosuppression facilitating infection or insufficient immunosuppression associated with risk of rejection, possibly even requiring re-transplantation, requires careful attention.

To the best of our knowledge, the effects on the waiting list and especially the comorbidities and complications of patients after LT have not been analyzed to date. This German nationwide study investigated the outcomes of patients hospitalized for LT and their trends between 2005 and 2018 based on the diagnosis-related groups (DRG) system from the research data center (RDC) of the German Federal Statistical office. As evaluation, waiting list admissions and LT, as well as post-LT admissions, can be distinguished by specific DRG codes and recorded in this database, this study sheds light on trends and outcomes.

Methods

Study design

We performed a population-based study using standardized discharge data for patients admitted for or with liver LT between 2005 and 2018 provided by the Federal Statistical Office of Germany in Wiesbaden based on a contract for remote data extraction and analysis of data. Using DRG and German operation and procedure key codes (OPS), the data was observed anonymously (source: RDC of the Federal Statistical Office and the Statistical Offices of the federal states, DRG statistics 2005–2018 and own calculations). For further stratification of the data, we used sociodemographic data including age and sex as well as clinical information containing comorbidities, procedures and reason for hospital discharge defined as discharge or death of the patient. All data were based on International Classification of Diseases, 10th edition, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM).

Study population

Admissions from 2005 to 2018 in Germany were included in our study. We separated the admissions into three groups, first, patients admitted for LT evaluation or waiting list, second, patients with LT and third, patients admitted after LT (post-LT) and assessed various parameters that had an influence on patient survival. LT evaluation and admission to LT waiting list were coded with the OPS codes 1-920.04, 1-920.14 and 1-920.24, 1-920.34, respectively. LT procedure was defined by 5-504.0, 5-504.1 and 5-504.2 and post-LT by the ICD code Z94.4. Furthermore, LT represented patients transplanted while in hospital, whereas post-LT implicates re-admission of patients discharged after LT. Other parameters defined by the ICD-10 coding system were liver cirrhosis (K74 and K70.3), HCC (C22), ALF (K72.0) and rejection (T86.4) as primary or secondary diagnosis. LT indication of decompensated cirrhosis was defined as admissions for LT excluding ALF and HCC. Complications of immunosuppression were reflected in respiratory diseases, infections, acute kidney injury (AKI), malignant diseases, gastritis and diarrhea. Cholestatic diseases, ERCP and biliodigestive anastomosis were summarized under the term biliary complications. We classified early rejection as LT (OPS codes 5-504.0, 5-504.1 and 5-504.2) and rejection (T86.4), and rejection after discharge as post-LT (Z94.4) and rejection. OPS codes 5-504.3, 5-504.4 and 5-504.5 represented re-transplantation, which we also grouped as early graft failure (LT and re-transplantation) and late re-transplantation (post-LT and re-transplantation). All other diseases and parameters also relate to the ICD-10 DRG coding system and are shown in Supplementary Table 1, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913.

As the primary focus of this study was all-cause hospital mortality, admissions were observed throughout their entire hospital stay until discharge or death.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables are shown as numbers with or without percentages. To compare these variables between groups, Chi-square tests and t-test or Mann–Whitney U-test were used. Simple linear regression was used to present changes in parameters with or without mortality over the observational time. Logistic regression was performed to determine the individual impact of various comorbidities on mortality in post-LT patients. P-value < 0.05 indicated statistical significance. All statistical analyses were performed with SAS software (version 3.8; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Characteristics of hospitalizations for liver transplantation evaluation with or without waiting list admissions in Germany

During the study period, 248 085 936 hospital admissions were reported in Germany [9]. Of these, 14 745 and 10 787 admissions were recorded as patient hospitalization for LT evaluation with and without waiting list admissions, respectively. Thereof, in 12 836 hospital admissions, LT was performed during the observational period. Additionally, 142 809 admissions were reported post-LT (Table 1).

General characteristics of patient hospitalizations for LT evaluation with and without waiting list admissions, liver transplant and post-LT

ALF, acute liver failure; HCC, hepatocellular carcinoma; LT, liver transplantation.

The number of admissions for LT evaluation increased approximately three times over the observational period from 2005 to 2018. The number of hospitalizations for evaluation with LT waiting list admission reached its peak in 2012 with 1431, but decreased to 804 in 2018 (Fig. 1a and Table 1). Similar trends were found in patients with different chronic diseases as comorbidities (Supplementary Figures 1A and B, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913). There was a balance between LT evaluation with and without LT waiting list admissions in 2014. During the observational period, the age of patients admitted for LT evaluation increased over time, for example, in 2005, 56.7% of patients were older than 50 years and in 2018, the percentage was 71.6%. Also, the number of younger patients (< 40 years) admitted to the waiting list increased from 16.3% in 2005 to 26.3% in 2018 (Fig. 1b). Not surprisingly, decompensated liver cirrhosis represented the vast majority of indications for either LT evaluation with (79.4%) or without (83.1%) waiting list admissions. However, number and proportion of ALF increased markedly over time, especially in patients on the waiting list (from 4.6 to 19.3%).

The number of deaths and the in-hospital mortality rate increased significantly over the years in patients admitted for LT evaluation with or without waiting list admissions. In particular, the mortality rate doubled (around 8% in 2018) in patients admitted to the waiting list within the same hospital stay (Fig. 1c), especially in patients with ALF (Supplementary Figure 1C, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913). In addition, patients were also removed from the waiting list after re-evaluation and showed a high in-hospital mortality rate (over 20%) (Fig. 1c).

Demographic characteristics of admissions for liver transplantation in Germany

As expected, the majority (77.42%) of admissions for LT of the patients had liver cirrhosis, while in 22.58% of cases, LT was performed in patients without liver cirrhosis (Fig. 2a and Table 1). The number of transplantations increased by 25% from 2005 (n = 817) to 2010 (n = 1071). A 20% drop in the number of LT was observed from 2010 to 2018 (n = 798) (Supplementary Figure 2A, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913).

Although the age distribution changed, in 2005, most (42.84%) transplanted patients were between 40 and 55 years old. In 2018, this age category decreased to 33.0%, while the number of patients younger than 40 and older than 55 increased by 7.3% and 11.2%, respectively. (Fig. 2b and Table 1). Furthermore, the sex distribution was clearly dominated by male patients, ranging between 61% and 68% in the observational 14 years (Supplementary Figure 2B, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913).

Indications for liver transplantation

The indications for LT were mainly decompensated liver cirrhosis with around 60% among all LT, while HCC and ALF accounted for 10% and 30%, respectively (Fig. 2c). Interestingly, a decrease in the frequency of decompensated cirrhosis was observed over the observational period, whereas the number of patients with ALF increased from 2005 (10.53%) to 2018 (19.92%) (Supplementary Figure 2C and 2D, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913).

Apart from that, in-hospital mortality rate was highest in patients receiving LT for ALF (27.1% in the mean), which was twice as high as the mortality rate of decompensated cirrhosis (12.4% in the mean) and almost three times higher than HCC (8.3% in the mean) (Fig. 2d, Supplementary Figure 2E and 2F, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913). However, the in-hospital mortality rate of patients with ALF decreased significantly from 34.88% in 2005 to 18.87% in 2018 (P < 0.01) (Table 1).

The number of LT of patients with cirrhosis was further stratified according to different Federal states of Germany. Interestingly, the highest in-hospital mortality rate (Saarland: 22.7%, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania: 20.3%, and Saxony-Anhalt: 20.2%) occurred in the states that had the lowest number of LT (Saarland: 154, Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania: 59, and Saxony-Anhalt: 134) from 2005 to 2017. (Supplementary Figure 3, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913).

Post-LT admissions and comorbidities

Post-LT admissions increased from 6803 in 2005 to 12 105 in 2018 (Fig. 3a), with mostly male patients (Supplementary Figure 4, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913). This trend was mainly due to the increasing number of admissions for different complications, while re-cirrhosis admission in post-LT patients was a minority (9.7%) (Table 1).

To analyze the role of different comorbidities in post-LT patients, we also compared the admissions likely to be due to immunosuppression (e.g. respiratory diseases, infections, AKI, malignant diseases, gastritis and diarrhea) with those due to circulatory disease, other digestive diseases and biliary complications (Fig. 3b), all of which increased significantly over the observational period. Admissions of patients for post-LT and complications of immunosuppression showed the highest numbers and nearly tripled (from 2748 to 7290) (Fig. 3b and Supplementary Figure 4, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913).

Admissions for biliary complications also increased from 1474 to 3152 for cholestatic diseases, and from 775 to 1822 for ERCP. By contrast, admissions of post-LT patients for biliodigestive anastomosis remained below 100 admissions per year nationwide (Fig. 3c and Supplementary Figure 4, Supplemental digital content 1, http://links.lww.com/EJGH/A913). Interestingly, admissions of patients for biliodigestive anastomosis showed the highest in-hospital mortality rate (16.5%). Post-LT patients admitted for cholestatic diseases or ERCP only had a mortality rate of 2.37% and 2.15%, respectively (Fig. 3d).

Post-LT admissions for digestive diseases (e.g. gastritis, diarrhea and liver diseases excluding cirrhosis) represented the majority (51%), followed by arterial hypertension (34%), diabetes (21%) and respiratory diseases (14%). Admissions for infections, malignant diseases, circulatory diseases, heart diseases and AKI accounted for only 11% (Fig. 4a). Moreover, patients with AKI showed the highest in-hospital mortality rate of 20.2%, while the mortality rates for circulatory diseases (11.5%), respiratory diseases (11%) and infections (10%) were much lower (Fig. 4b).

In the logistic regression of in-hospital mortality in post-LT patients, respiratory disease had the highest odds ratio (OR) of 6.909 regarding in-hospital death in post-LT patients, followed by AKI (OR 5.952) and malignant diseases (OR 3.193). Infections and digestive disease had a slightly lower OR of in-hospital death compared to the other comorbidities (2.576 and 2.02, respectively). Additionally, diagnosis of circulatory diseases, heart diseases and older age were independent risk factors for in-hospital mortality, while diagnosis of arterial hypertension and diabetes seemed not to be direct causes of death in post-LT patients (OR 0.550 and OR 0.911) (Fig. 4c).

Rejection, graft failures and re-transplantation

Between 2005 and 2018, 365 liver re-transplantations were undertaken in total, which account for around 2.67% of all admissions with re-cirrhosis. This is more than 50 times higher than all the admissions of patients with liver cirrhosis (2 302 171).

A subgroup analysis was conducted to distinguish between early rejection and rejection after discharge. There were no significant changes in the number of early rejections over the observational period, with an annual number of around 250 admissions. However, the number of patients with late rejection showed a significant increase from 706 in 2005 to 1228 in 2018 (P < 0.01). In 2012, a peak of 1500 admissions due to rejection after discharge in post-LT patients was observed, after which this number slightly decreased (Fig. 5a). To achieve a clearer picture, we stratified patient admissions into early graft failure (re-transplant during the same hospital admission) and late re-transplantation. In general, the numbers in both cases were low. In comparison to late transplantation (25 [3.1%] in 2005 and 13 [1.6%] in 2018), early graft failure had twice as many cases and proportion (60 [7.3%] in 2005 and 44 [5.5%] in 2018) in all LT patients (Fig. 5b). Remarkably, the mortality was much higher for early graft failure compared to rejection after discharge (Fig. 5c).

Discussion

This nationwide retrospective study analyzed in detail the hospitalization for LT evaluation, waiting list admissions, LT, as well as admissions of patients after LT in Germany. This study reveals several interesting findings, reflecting the LT scandal of 2010, and maps the development of LT in Germany. In summary, our study demonstrates that a balance between the number of LT evaluations with and without waiting list admissions was reached from 2014 onwards. In addition, waiting list mortality increased in parallel with the decreased number of LT observed in 2010. In-hospital mortality in LT decreased most strikingly for ALF. However, the admissions of post-LT patients increased considerably over time, partially with a higher mortality rate, especially in patients with AKI and biliodigestive anastomosis.

The first interesting finding was that the number of patients admitted for LT evaluation without waiting list admissions and the number of admissions of patients on the LT waiting list eventually reached a balance in 2014, with even a slight decrease in waiting list admissions outweighing the increased number for evaluation. This was witnessed by the changes in the ratio between evaluation and waiting list admission from 2.1 to 0.8. Possibly, the liver organ crisis in 2012, which has been described elsewhere, could be considered [10]. The subsequent drop in grafts had an important impact on the mortality rate of patients on the waiting list, which doubled in 2018 compared to 2005.

The number of LT decreased in parallel to the number of waiting list admissions from 2010, which may have been triggered by low organ donors influenced by the allocation irregularities [10]. Interestingly, this impacted only the number of LT for decompensated cirrhosis and ALF, while the number of LT for HCC remained stable. Following the demographic trends [9], the age of patients receiving LT has increased over the years. And two-thirds of the LT patients were male, which might be due to the reason that most severe liver diseases occur more often in men, instead of a sex bias in the allocation [9]. Of note, despite the decreased number, the in-hospital mortality rate dropped independent of indications during the 14 observational years. The most pronounced effect was observed in patients with ALF, in whom the mortality rate dropped almost by 10% in the last 4 years compared to the period 2005–2009. This may have been due to improvements in the overall critical care management of these patients [2,11,12]. Unfortunately, we could not code for the exact reasons.

A further important and interesting investigation is the situation of patients post-LT. Not negligibly, admissions of patients for complications post-LT doubled in 2018 compared to 2005. Reasons for admissions were complications of immunosuppression, especially respiratory diseases, infections, AKI, malignant diseases and gastrointestinal symptoms (mainly gastritis and diarrhea). These most likely immunosuppression-derived complications tripled the number of admissions in 2018 compared to admission in 2005. The improvement of immunosuppressant efficacy is highly associated with post-LT outcomes [13,14]. Importantly, the highest in-hospital mortality rate (>20%) occurred in patients who developed AKI, likely to be also associated with already existing kidney dysfunction before LT and persisting after LT. The reported prevalence of AKI post-LT ranges from 14% to 94% [15]. Therefore, it is especially important to optimize the use of immunosuppression in order to protect renal function after LT to the greatest possible extent, by, for example, cautious use of calcineurin inhibitors [15,16]. While post-LT patients admitted for arterial hypertension and diabetes account for a large proportion, they had the lowest mortality rate among all post-LT complications, which may be possibly due to more intensive follow-up by the endocrinologists. However, these complications are relevant, especially as risk factors for chronic renal dysfunction and potential subsequent admissions with AKI [15]. Our data call for more careful handling and monitoring of renal function in post-LT patients.

Biliary complications were reported to be the most common ones in many studies, including anastomotic and non-anastomotic strictures, biliary leak, cholangitis and biliary casts [17,18]. Also, in our study, the number of admissions for ERCP in post-LT patients increased during the observational period as a surrogate for significant biliary complications [9]. Remarkably, despite a very low proportion of post-LT patients with biliodigestive anastomosis, these patients had a significantly higher mortality rate than patients with ERCP or cholestatic diseases. Studies have suggested that these biliary complications mainly result from the smaller and more fragile ducts of the donors and potentially also from the more complex and arduous invasive percutaneous and endoscopic procedures that are required to reach the bile ducts [19,20]. In addition, our data show that endoscopic treatment should be preferred and the choice of duct-to-duct or biliodigestive anastomosis should be considered carefully in LT patients. In addition, bile duct reconstruction is possibly another parameter to improve patient selection at allocation as well as their post-LT outcomes.

Also, compared to 2005, admissions for rejection doubled in 2018 and had significant morbidities and mortality, one-third of the patients with early rejection post-LT died in hospital. Graft failure had an even higher in-hospital mortality rate of over 40%. Indeed, the rejection and graft failure rate increased in patients with post-LT biliary strictures [21,22], emphasizing again a potential role for early expert endoscopic management and thoughtful consideration of graft selection.

Our data can inform stakeholders for a more rational allocation of organs and management of patients post-LT so that limited liver transplant resources can be optimized. Significantly more research is required on the post-LT phase of patients. In addition, specific out-patient care with interdisciplinary setting is required. Also, digitalization and traveling allowance for patients are tools which can improve post-LT patients.

Our study has several limitations. First, due to the limitation of the database, we could only get access to the data during the hospitalization of patients and admissions instead of patients as the unit to analyze. This may lead to data on repeated hospital admissions in same patients being included in the analysis, introducing a potential bias. However, LT, admissions to waiting list and re-transplant have specific and unique OPS/DRG, which are reliable tools to identify single patients and which allow solid conclusions. Given the manual recording of ICD and OPS codes, diagnoses and procedures based on this coding are not necessarily representative of all patients and omissions may have happened [23]. Nevertheless, our data from retrospective nationwide study call for further research and clinical studies to investigate complications and care of LT in more detail.

In conclusion, this analysis of the nationwide database covers all hospital admissions of LT patients and post-LT patients over a period of 14 years, providing information on current trends and changes in LT in Germany. The most striking aspect is the decreasing number of admissions for LT with an increasing number of different complications, of which AKI is of special importance. In addition, management of patients in need of biliodigestive anastomosis, calls for improved care. Significantly more research is required on the post-LT phase of patients.

Construction of a postoperative home-based pulmonary rehabilitation program protocol for lung cancer patients delivered using the internet: A Delphi-based study
Authors: Haoyu Zou, Jiani Qu, Yanqing Li, Feilin Wang, Pan Chen, Liuyi Zhang, Honghui Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo construct a protocol to guide standardized home-based pulmonary rehabilitation for postoperative lung cancer patients based on the Internet + .MethodsA postoperative home-based pulmonary rehabilitation protocol via the Internet (PHPRPI) for patients with lung cancer was developed through a literature review. The protocol was modified based on the opinions and suggestions of experts in two rounds of expert consultation, utilizing the Delphi method. A three-level, hierarchical structure was employed in the Delphi method, with primary indicators representing broad, overarching concepts, secondary indicators providing more specific details within those concepts, and tertiary indicators offering the most granular and actionable measures. The following coefficients were calculated to assess the robustness of the Delphi method: the positive coefficient was calculated as the percentage of experts completing the questionnaire, with a recommended level of ≥ 70%. The expert authority coefficient (Cr) was calculated as the arithmetic mean of familiarity criteria (Ca) and the degree of familiarity (Cs), with a recommended level of ≥ 0.70. The coefficient of variation (CV) and Kendall's harmony coefficient (ω) were used to indicate the degree of variation and coordination, which range from 0 to 1, with higher values indicating more consistent expert opinions.ResultsEighteen experts completed two rounds of consultation, with the Cr being 0.91, the ω being 0.159 (P < 0.01), and the CV ranging from 0.0477 to 0.1352, indicating good reliability of the Delphi method. The final protocol includes four primary indicators (including composition of the staff, establishment of patient health records, off-line intervention, and online intervention), 14 secondary indicators (e.g., personnel professional, specialized assessment, training frequency, and breathing training methods), and 44 tertiary indicators (e.g., vital sign assessment, content of health education, resistance training, and flexibility training).ConclusionThe PHPRPI provides preliminary evidence to guide postoperative home-based rehabilitation management for lung cancer patients, aiming to improve their health outcomes and well-being. Future randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are needed to further validate the safety and effectiveness of this model and assess whether it is acceptable to patients and healthcare providers.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00520-025-09916-6.Keywords: Lung cancer, Pulmonary rehabilitation, Internet + , Home rehabilitation, Delphi method

Introduction

Lung cancer is the most common cancer (accounting for 11.4% of all new cancer cases) and the leading cause of cancer death (accounting for 18.0% of all cancer deaths) worldwide [1]. The disease burden of lung cancer is even more prominent in China, where lung cancer has been the leading cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality, with an increasing trend [2]. Zheng et al. [3] showed that the number of new lung cancer cases and deaths in China increased by about 162.6% and 123.6% from 2000 to 2016.

The primary and optimal treatment option for lung cancer is surgery, especially among early-stage patients [4]. Although surgery can improve the survival rates of lung cancer, patients frequently suffer from postoperative cardiopulmonary complications, such as tracheal stenosis, lung infection, lung atelectasis, and lung function decline [5–7]. In addition, lung cancer survivors may suffer chronic symptoms such as fatigue and pain, and psychological distress such as depression and anxiety [8,9]. These challenges not only negatively impact the patient's quality of life but also lead to prolonged hospital stays and increased healthcare costs [5–7]. It is thus urgent and essential to develop effective prevention and intervention programs to address the postoperative challenges faced by lung cancer patients.

Pulmonary rehabilitation (PR) is a multidisciplinary intervention designed to comprehensively improve a patient's lung function and overall well-being through a series of standard procedures, including education, exercise training, and psychosocial support [10]. Initially developed for patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [11], PR has now been widely used among patients with lung cancer with well-demonstrated effects in reducing postoperative complications and improving quality of life [12,13]. The core component of PR is exercise training, particularly aerobic and resistance exercises, which can reverse skeletal muscle dysfunction by strengthening muscles and improving cardiovascular function [14–16]. These exercises can improve exercise tolerance and physical fitness while reducing the feeling of breathlessness (dyspnea) and fatigue associated with respiratory conditions, leading to enhanced overall quality of life[14–16]. Abundant evidence has shown that hospital-based PR is a safe and feasible intervention to improve the health outcomes of patients with lung cancer [17–19].

However, the wide application of PR is limited due to the high cost of involving health professionals and low patient compliance related to transportation difficulties and time constraints [20,21]. Furthermore, despite the well-demonstrated benefits of exercise and physical activity in improving lung function, lung cancer diagnosis and treatment can significantly reduce muscle strength, exercise capacity, and physical function[22]. The combination of the disease and treatment can cause skeletal muscle wasting, leading to reduced strength and the inability to perform physical activity [23]. Disease-related symptoms, such as fatigue, weakness, and breathing difficulties, can lead to further functional limitations, making it challenging to participate in social activities and resulting in a decreased quality of life [24]. These limitations necessitate a multidisciplinary approach to providing comprehensive support and an individualized approach to tailoring the support to meet the individual's specific needs and abilities [22,25].

Recent studies have shown that home-based PR via the Internet was comparable to hospital-based PR in decreasing postoperative pulmonary complications and improving quality of life while overcoming the barriers to attendance and adherence [26–28]. "Internet + " home-based PR relies on the Internet platform to provide standard education, training, and support for patients, with continuous follow-up management, which is not subject to time, space, social, or economic constraints[26–28]. In addition, it addresses the shortage of health professionals and medical resources related to PR, thereby ensuring the sustainability of PR and patient compliance. Multiple evidence-based guidelines have been developed all over the world focusing on supervised, center-based PR or telerehabilitation PR supervised at home [29–31]. These guidelines provide updated definitions of PR, identify essential components of PR, establish requirements for successful and effective PR delivery, highlight significant advances in PR implementation, and develop evaluation approaches to inform the formulation of clinical recommendations [29–31].

However, there is a lack of comprehensive and systematic guidelines on "Internet + " home-based PR for patients with lung cancer after surgeries in China. Therefore, we conducted the current study to construct a postoperative home-based PR protocol via the "Internet + " (PHPRPI) for lung cancer patients through the literature review and the Delphi method. Our findings would provide a reference and guidance for the broad application of cost-effective PR among lung cancer patients to prevent postoperative complications and improve their quality of life.

Methods

Establishment of the research team

The research team was comprised of five members, including one deputy chief nurse and four master's degree students in nursing. The research team was primarily responsible for conducting the literature search, reviewing the literature, preparing expert questionnaires, identifying experts, distributing questionnaires, collecting data, and analyzing the data.

Literature review

Data sources and search strategy

We searched the following databases for literature on the effectiveness of home-based PR via the Internet + for lung cancer patients after surgery from conception to July 2023: Cochrane Library, PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, China Knowledge, China Biomedical Literature Database, Wanfang, and Wipro. The search terms included the following: "lung neoplasms/lung cancer/lung carcinoma/pulmonary neoplasms," "pneumonectomy/therapeutics," "lung therapy/pulmonary treatment/pulmonary rehabilitation/breathing exercise/training," and "home rehabilitation." A combination of subject terms and free terms was used for searching, and search strategies were developed and adapted according to the characteristics of different databases. The search strategy used in the literature review is included as supplementary file S1. The references for the included literature were manually searched to prevent missing literature.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria included the following: (1) study population: postoperative lung cancer patients; (2) intervention modality: exercise, respiratory training, health education, and other intervention modalities; (3) control group: routine care; and (4) study type: guidelines, standards, systematic evaluations, randomized controlled studies, observational studies, expert opinion or consensus, etc. Literature exclusion criteria included: (1) literature with obviously incomplete information; (2) the complete original text could not be obtained; and (3) literature published in languages other than Chinese and English.

Quality assessment

The Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal tool was used to assess the methodological quality of the included studies, focusing on feasibility, appropriateness, meaningfulness, and effectiveness (FAME), to ensure the best and most reliable evidence for informing clinical decision-making [32]. Two well-trained researchers assessed the methodological quality of the included studies, and any disagreement between the two reviewers was resolved by a third expert who was familiar with the survey. The researchers conducted data extraction independently and compared and revised their results until a consensus was reached. They summarized the relevant contents from the literature and formed a pool of questionnaire items, based on which they developed a preliminary draft of the PHPRPI.

Expert consultation questionnaire

The expert questionnaire includes the following four parts: (1) a description of the survey, which introduces the background, purpose, and significance of the study; (2) a definition of the relevant concepts; (3) the body of the questionnaire, which includes the index items, and the experts can rate the importance of each item on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 "very unimportant" to 5 "very important." There is also a comment column for experts to provide additional feedback on item modifications, such as suggesting additional index items; (4) a questionnaire for the experts, which includes the basic demographic information of the experts, the degree of familiarity, and the basis for judging the index contents.

Expert selection and consultation process

The inclusion criteria of experts were as follows: (1) engaged in PR-related specialties; (2) with more than 5 years of work experience in the specialized field and familiar with the content of this study; and (3) willing to participate in this study. To ensure an effective expert recruitment process, we have adopted multiple approaches, such as professional networks, social platforms, systematic reviews, snowball sampling, and online mailing lists, to obtain a diverse, high-quality, and representative expert panel [33]. The final expert group comprised a total of 18 experts in PR, among whom 5 were recruited by reaching out to colleagues already known to the researchers, 3 were identified through social platforms such as LinkedIn, 4 were identified as the authors of relevant papers, 3 were recommended by the recruited experts within their networks, and 3 were recruited through internet-based databases and electronic mailing lists.

The expert consultation process was conducted from September to November 2023, during which the researcher team distributed and retrieved questionnaires via email and WeChat. After the 1 st round of expert consultation, the research team analyzed the data and revised the initial draft of PHPRPI based on the entry screening criteria and expert opinions. In the second round of expert consultation, the revised draft of PHPRPI was further reviewed by the experts and modified to reflect their views, until all items were agreed upon. The entry screening criteria were a mean importance score of ≥ 3.50 and a coefficient of variation of < 0.25. The flowchart of the two rounds of expert consultation is shown in Fig. 1.

Indicators

The Delphi method employs a hierarchical structure to break down complex concepts into manageable parts for expert consensus-building, utilizing three levels of indicators: primary indicators, secondary indicators, and tertiary indicators. Primary indicators are the core areas of focus and the most general categories or themes in the Delphi study. Secondary indicators are more specific subcategories within the primary indicators, providing more detailed information about the primary themes. Tertiary indicators are the most precise, actionable measures or metrics used to assess secondary indicators, which are the individual elements that experts evaluate and provide feedback on.

Program evaluation

The program evaluation used a comprehensive approach that combined both objective indicators and patient-reported outcomes. This provides a balanced view of the program's effectiveness by considering both the measurable health data and the patient's personal experience. Objective indicators included general assessments and specialist assessments, which were collected through the Patient Health Record. General assessments included vital signs, such as the patient's heart rate, pulse, blood pressure, respiration, and blood oxygen saturation, as well as an assessment of daily living activities (ADL). Specialist assessments included Disease Type, Surgical Method, Lung Function, 6-min Walking Test, Dyspnea Scale (Borg score), Grip Strength-Weight Index, etc. Patient-reported outcomes included psychiatric assessment using the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) to screen for anxiety and depression, capturing the patient's perspective on their mental health. The primary methods for monitoring patient compliance included maintaining a training diary and providing feedback during offline training sessions.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0. Categorical variables were described by numbers and percentages, while continuous data were expressed by the means and standard deviations. The positive coefficient, also known as the recovery rate, was calculated as the percentage of experts who completed the questionnaire, reflecting their enthusiasm and cooperation with the research topic. A positive coefficient of 70% or above indicates high positivity [34]. The expert authority coefficient (Cr) is the arithmetic mean of familiarity criteria (Ca) and an expert's degree of familiarity with the field (Cs) [35]. Ca measures the expert's confidence in their judgments, while Cr quantifies the level or extent of an expert's familiarity with the subject matter [35]. Both Ca and Cs are calculated based on a self-reported rating scale ranging from 0 to 1, with a higher score indicating the expert's more profound knowledge and experience related to the topic[35]. Cr is calculated as (Ca + Cs)/2, with a value ≥ 0.70 indicating high authoritative levels and reliable research results[35].The coefficient of variation (CV) was used to indicate the degree of variation, and Kendall's harmony coefficient (ω) was used to indicate the degree of coordination of expert opinions. The values of CV and ω range from 0 to 1, with higher values indicating higher variation and coordination among expert opinions, respectively[36]. The statistically significant difference was indicated by P < 0.05.

Reliability of the delphi method

The positive coefficient is expressed by the effective recovery rate of the questionnaire. In both rounds of expert consultation, all 18 experts completed valid questionnaires, achieving a 100% effective recovery rate. In this study, the coefficient of authority was 0.91, indicating a high level of authority in expert opinions.

Item revision

After two rounds of expert consultation, we added three tertiary indicators, including psychological counselor, type of disease, and surgical method, based on the inclusion criteria for the indicators and considering the opinions and suggestions of the experts. We also deleted two tertiary indicators, including the three-ball breathing trainer and lip-contraction breathing. In addition, we made revisions to the item descriptions based on the expert opinions, and the details are shown in Table 4. For example, the item 'assessment of vital signs' was replaced with the item 'assessment of vital signs: assessment of the patient's heart rate, pulse, blood pressure, respiration, pain, oxygen saturation,' and the item' training frequency: offline' was replaced with the item' training frequency: 1 time per week, offline.' Finally, the PHPRPI for lung cancer patients was formed, including four primary indicators (including composition of the staff, establishment of patient health records, off-line intervention, and online intervention), 14 secondary indicators (e.g., personnel professional, specialized assessment, training frequency, and breathing training methods), and 44 tertiary indicators (e.g., vital sign assessment, content of health education, resistance training, and flexibility training).

Discussion

Summary of the findings

In this pioneering work, we developed a postoperative home-based PR protocol via Internet + (PHPRPI) for lung cancer patients, which included a total of 4 primary indicators, 14 secondary indicators, and 44 tertiary indicators. The protocol was validated using the Delphi method in two rounds and demonstrated good reliability. This protocol serves as a helpful guide for the future development of cost-effective home-based patient support for lung cancer patients.

The reliability of the delphi method

Based on the PR guidelines and expert consultation, our study analyzed, integrated, and refined evidence from the Literature on clinical guidelines and original studies on PR for patients with lung cancer, and underwent two rounds of expert consultation. The Delphi method results demonstrated a high degree of motivation, authority, and coordination among the experts. A total of 18 experts were included in this study, and the recovery rate of the questionnaires in both rounds of consultation was 100%, indicating that the experts were highly motivated to participate in this study. The experts' authority coefficient in the second round of questioning was 0.91, indicating a high degree of authority of the consulting experts in this field. Kendall's W value for the second round of expert consultation was 0.159 (P < 0.001), indicating a high degree of coordination among the experts' opinions after the two rounds of consultation. All these findings demonstrate that the PHPRPI constructed in this study is scientifically reliable. Moreover, the protocol specifies the indications and contraindications for exercise training, conducts exercise behavior management and monitoring, and provides regular follow-up and feedback to ensure the program's safety and effectiveness.

The practicability and feasibility of the PHPRPI

Relying on the Internet + platform, this study integrated a multidisciplinary collaborative care model based on the personalized formulation of patients' rehabilitation programs. The model offers postoperative support to lung cancer patients from the perspectives of various professionals, beginning with the actual needs of patients. The PHPRPI for lung cancer patients was constructed by combining online and offline data. The health education module features videos, images, text, and one-on-one online consultations delivered to patients through WeChat apps and other online platforms, providing easy-to-understand and enjoyable content. Patients and their families can obtain information online, learn rehabilitation exercise skills, and communicate with doctors for feedback, all without being restricted by time, geography, or economic constraints, thereby saving medical resources and reducing costs. In addition, offline education and support are also provided to patients and their family members through face-to-face follow-up meetings to guide their rehabilitation training and assess its effectiveness. The combination of online and offline modules can ensure the standardization and effectiveness of PR implementation, with improved practicality and feasibility, thereby ensuring continuity of care.

Comparison of the PHPRPI with the international guidelines

International authoritative guidelines, including the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD)[37] and other guidelines[29–31], have established various intervention frameworks for PR. These guidelines share similar principles, such as a patient-centered, evidence-based, and multidimensional approach. In accordance with these guidelines, our protocol also provides a convenient and accessible home-based online PR intervention strategy that encompasses exercise training, education, and behaviour changes to improve the respiratory function of patients post-lung cancer surgery. Compared to the international guidelines on home-based PR, our study offers several unique advantages.

First, PHPRPI was developed in a Chinese context through a rigorous Delphi process, which enhances its cultural relevance and adaptability [38]. Developing a protocol tailored to the Chinese context allows researchers and policymakers to better understand and address specific needs within China[38]. A Chinese-specific protocol can also serve as a baseline for comparisons with other cultures, providing a richer understanding of cultural differences and similarities [39].

Second, PHPRPI has implemented numerous practical adjustments to enhance its convenience, accessibility, and ease of operation. For instance, it simplifies equipment requirements and replaces professional equipment with daily life activities for exercise training. PHPRPI utilizes subjective fatigue scales (such as the Borg scale) or simple physiological indicators (such as heart rate and blood oxygen saturation) for self-monitoring.

Third, PHPRPI adopts a more flexible and decentralized implementation strategy while maintaining the core elements. The program provides intervention through a "strategic combination of online participation + offline guidance". This design not only ensures the necessary face-to-face guidance but also reduces the burden on patients through remote support, thereby improving long-term feasibility.

Implications of the PHPRPI

Pulmonary rehabilitation training can improve a patient's lung function, exercise tolerance, and quality of life [40–42]. Studies show that most tumor patients in China have urgent needs for health management in terms of emotional distress, rehabilitation, disease symptoms, and nutritional support. Yet, only a small portion of them have access to such information[43]. Zhu et al. [44] found that most cancer patients reported that their PR knowledge was initially acquired from health education in the hospital. This finding indicated that patients trusted healthcare professionals and relied on them for guidance on home-based PR. However, even if the exercise prescription was designed for the patients when they were discharged from the hospital, the inability to supervise adherence during the at-home exercise period and the uncertainty about whether the method was correct may lead to interruption of the PR process. Therefore, there is an urgent need to provide high-quality PR resources through multiple channels.

To meet the needs of postoperative home-based PR of lung cancer patients, it is necessary to build a standardized PR program to guide scientific and practical rehabilitation and ensure its sustainability. The PHPRPI was developed to meet such needs. On the one hand, it can provide a clear basis for nurses to guide lung cancer patients in carrying out PR, which is of great significance in promoting their health outcomes and well-being. On the other hand, it can accelerate patients' recovery process, enhance their self-efficacy, reduce related complications, and promote their early return to society. The PHPRPI can provide a reference and basis for further standardizing the postoperative management of patients with lung cancer. Therefore, the development of PHPRPI carries significant clinical implications for postoperative lung cancer patients.

Limitations

The study has several limitations. First, the experts included in this study were primarily from tertiary hospitals in Hunan Province and may not accurately represent experts from other regions with different economic development, medical levels, and approaches to chronic disease management. Second, the absence of patient perspectives in our Delphi study may limit the assessment of program feasibility and acceptability. Future studies should incorporate patients’ perspectives into the Delphi study to establish the program's acceptability from the patients' perspective. Third, the PHPRPI was developed based on a literature review and expert consultation; therefore, it needs to be validated through an intervention study to evaluate its effectiveness. The research team will conduct randomized controlled studies to verify the value of its application in clinical practice and refine the protocol's content in the future.

Conclusions

Based on the literature review and expert consultation, this study developed a standard protocol for postoperative home-based PR via Internet + for lung cancer patients. The protocol comprises 4 primary indicators, 14 secondary indicators, and 44 tertiary indicators, demonstrating high reliability. It can provide targeted and systematic guidance for postoperative home-based rehabilitation management of patients with lung cancer. The protocol has strong scientific and clinical application values, ensuring a cost-effective and sustainable care approach that promotes rehabilitation and improves health outcomes among patients with lung cancer. Future randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are needed to validate further their safety and effectiveness, as well as to assess whether this model is acceptable to patients and healthcare providers.

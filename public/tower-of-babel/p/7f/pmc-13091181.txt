ENVELOPE: enfortumab vedotin in patients with advanced small bowel adenocarcinoma refractory or intolerant to platinum-based therapy—phase II investigator-initiated trial (NCCH2412/MK015)
Authors: H. Fujii, H. Hirano, T. Kudo, K. Tsuchihashi, H. Shoji, N. Okita, T. Hirose, A. Takashima, M. Yokoyama, M. Kawasaki, Y. Shuda, M. Naito, K. Ichikawa, K. Sekine, J. Sato, N. Yamamoto, K. Kato
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSmall bowel adenocarcinoma (SBA) is a rare cancer with limited treatment strategies beyond first-line platinum-based combination therapy and no established second-line standard. Our recent retrospective study showed that Nectin-4 is frequently highly expressed in SBA and is associated with poorer outcomes, providing a biological rationale for evaluating enfortumab vedotin—a Nectin-4-targeted antibody–drug conjugate. We designed ENVELOPE to evaluate enfortumab vedotin in advanced SBA refractory or intolerant to platinum-based chemotherapy.Trial designENVELOPE is a multicenter, single-arm, phase II investigator-initiated trial. Representative eligibility criteria include adults with histologically or cytologically confirmed SBA, measurable disease, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0-1, and prior FOLFOX or CapeOX with progression or intolerance. Enfortumab vedotin is administered at 1.25 mg/kg intravenously on days 1, 8, and 15 of each 28-day cycle until progression or unacceptable toxicity. The primary endpoint is objective response rate (ORR) by blinded independent central review. The null and expected ORRs are 5% and 25%, respectively; an exact binomial design (one-sided alpha = 5%; 90% power) requires 25 assessable patients (planned N = 27). A prospective translational program requires pretreatment tumor tissue, prespecifies Nectin-4 immunohistochemistry as the exploratory biomarker, and collects on-study biopsies/serial blood at baseline, on treatment, and at progression for multi-omics profiling.

Introduction

Small bowel adenocarcinoma (SBA) is a rare malignancy (≤0.5% of all cancers; ≤3% of gastrointestinal cancers)1,2 that is often diagnosed late and has poor outcomes3; first-line platinum-based combination therapy (FOLFOX/CapeOX) offers limited benefit, and no second-line standard has been established.4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 To date, no large-scale randomized controlled trials have been conducted in advanced SBA, and the available evidence base remains limited. Antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) pair a tumor-targeting antibody with a cytotoxic payload; enfortumab vedotin, a Nectin-4-targeted ADC that delivers monomethyl auristatin E, produced an overall survival benefit in the phase III EV-301 trial,10 leading to regulatory approval for urothelial carcinoma. At our center, Nectin-4 immunohistochemistry in 51 pathologically confirmed SBA cases showed positivity in 42 out of 51 (82.4%) cases, indicating broad target expression.11 Given the significant unmet need in SBA, its high Nectin-4 expression, and the established activity and tolerability of enfortumab vedotin in urothelial carcinoma, there is a strong rationale to evaluate enfortumab vedotin monotherapy for SBA.

Methods and design

Trial design and oversight

This investigator-initiated phase II trial (ENVELOPE) employs a multicenter, single-arm design to assess the safety and efficacy of enfortumab vedotin monotherapy in patients with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic SBA refractory or intolerant to platinum-based combination therapy. Enrollment will not be restricted by Nectin-4 expression, given its high prevalence in SBA, the limitations of biopsy-based assessment in a heterogeneous disease, and the potential bystander effect of ADCs. This trial is conducted as a substudy within the MASTER KEY Project,12 a Japan-based multicenter platform study that integrates a prospective registry and multiple clinical trials for rare cancers. Enrollment is scheduled from October 2025 to October 2027, with a 12-month follow-up period initiated after the last participant’s inclusion. A coordinating committee will oversee conduct of the trial, while a centralized blinded independent central review (BICR) will adjudicate radiologic endpoints to minimize assessment bias. Intervention

Enfortumab vedotin 1.25 mg/kg administered intravenously on days 1, 8, and 15 of each 28-day cycle, continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, or other protocol-defined discontinuation. To mitigate infusion reactions, administration will be done by intravenous infusion over at least 30 minutes with close bedside monitoring of vital signs during and after the infusion. Premedication with corticosteroids, antihistamines, and acetaminophen may be considered at the investigator’s discretion per institutional practice. Supportive care per local standards is permitted. Dose-modification procedures are aligned with those used for the approved urothelial carcinoma indication.

Endpoints

Primary endpoint

Objective response rate (ORR) as determined by BICR using RECIST, version 1.1.

Secondary endpoints

Investigator-assessed ORR, progression-free survival, overall survival, disease control rate, duration of response, time to response, dose intensity, relative dose intensity, and safety.

Assessments and schedule

Tumor assessments

Contrast-enhanced CT will be carried out every 8 weeks through week 24, and every 12 weeks from week 25 onward, using the same modality and acquisition parameters as at baseline. Imaging will continue until disease progression is documented or subsequent therapy is initiated. A complete response or partial response requires a confirmation scan ≥4 weeks after it is first observed.

Safety assessments

At each cycle visit: interval history, physical examination, ECOG PS, vitals, and laboratory tests. Enfortumab vedotin-specific monitoring includes glucose at each visit, dermatologic assessment, neuropathy evaluation, and symptom-triggered pulmonary/ophthalmic assessments. Hyperglycemia and interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis are considered adverse events of special interest. Patients with elevated HbA1c at baseline (≥6.5%) will be considered for early consultation with an endocrinologist and more frequent blood glucose monitoring during the first treatment cycle. Patients will also be monitored for respiratory symptoms and radiographic findings suggestive of interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis, with management according to established medical guidelines, including prompt corticosteroid therapy for grade ≥2 events as appropriate. Adverse events will be graded using CTCAE (version 5.0) with standardized relatedness/outcome fields and regulatory timelines.

Given that enfortumab vedotin is an approved ADC for urothelial carcinoma with an established safety profile at the administered dose and schedule, a formal safety run-in was not included in the protocol. ENVELOPE employs the same dosing regimen and prespecified dose interruption, reduction, and discontinuation criteria as those used in prior registration studies in urothelial carcinoma. Serious adverse events will be reported promptly in accordance with regulatory requirements, and safety data will be reviewed on an ongoing basis throughout the trial. Close clinical monitoring and protocol-defined toxicity management are implemented for all participants. In addition, an independent data and safety monitoring committee (IDSMC) has been established to oversee patient safety throughout the study.

Sample size calculation

This single-arm phase II trial is powered for ORR. The null response rate is set at 5%, reflecting historical second-line outcomes in this population at our institution and other Japanese centers (∼0%-7%).13,14 The expected response rate is set at 25%, representing a clinically meaningful benefit in consideration of the anticipated toxicities. Based on an exact binomial test with a one-sided alpha level of 5% and a desired power of 90%, the required number of assessable patients is 25; allowing for patients who might enroll but are subsequently deemed ineligible or who do not initiate treatment, the planned enrollment is set at 27.

Data collection and management

Data are captured in an electronic case report form with programmed editing checks and audit trails; query management and source data verification will follow a centralized/remote monitoring plan. Data retention and direct access comply with International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH)-Guideline for Good Clinical Practice (GCP); patient privacy is protected by coded identifiers. An independent monitoring committee (consisting of multidisciplinary experts in oncology, interventional radiology, pharmacology, and biostatistics) will oversee trial data integrity and protocol adherence.

Statistical analysis

Efficacy will be analyzed in the full analysis set, which consists of all enrolled patients who receive one or more doses, excluding those retrospectively ineligible and those BICR-ineligible at baseline (no target lesion by central review). The primary endpoint, ORR by BICR, will be analyzed in the full analysis set. Prespecified subgroup analyses of the primary endpoint will be carried out according to Nectin-4 expression status, age, sex, primary tumor location, metastatic sites, and the presence of selected genomic alterations, among other prespecified factors. These subgroup analyses are not powered for formal hypothesis testing and will not be adjusted for multiplicity; therefore results will be interpreted as exploratory. The null hypothesis for the primary endpoint (H0: ORR ≤0.05) will be tested based on an exact binomial test with a one-sided alpha level of 5%, and the ORR will be summarized with two-sided 90% and 95% exact Clopper–Pearson confidence intervals. If H0 is not rejected, enfortumab vedotin will be deemed ineffective; if rejected, it will be considered effective, with secondary endpoints interpreted in support of the primary result. Patients with NE as the best overall response will be counted as nonresponders; time-to-event endpoints will be analyzed by the Kaplan–Meier method with prespecified censoring rules.

Translational research and biomarker analysis

This trial incorporates a prospective translational research program to elucidate mechanisms of response and resistance to enfortumab vedotin in SBA. Eligibility criterion 10 requires archival tumor tissue; if unavailable, patients must consent to a pretreatment biopsy, ensuring access to baseline specimens and enabling assessment of the association between Nectin-4 expression and enfortumab vedotin efficacy, with Nectin-4 immunohistochemistry prespecified as the primary exploratory biomarker. Samples will be classified as negative (H-score 0-14), weak (H-score 15-99), moderate (H-score 100-199), or strong (H-score 200-300), as previously described.11,15 For predefined subgroup analyses, Nectin-4 expression positivity will be defined as moderate or strong staining (H-score ≥100). If a more robust or clinically validated assessment method becomes available during the course of the study, it may be adopted as appropriate, with findings interpreted in an exploratory manner.

When feasible, on-study biopsies and serial blood sampling at baseline, on treatment, and at progression will support multi-omics profiling, including genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, and phosphoproteomic analyses to discover novel biomarkers of enfortumab vedotin sensitivity and resistance. In parallel, patient-derived organoids and xenografts will be established to functionally model enfortumab vedotin response/resistance and to evaluate rational drug combinations. Integrated clinicopathologic and molecular analyses will correlate candidate biomarkers with each endpoint.

Discussion

Conducting randomized trials in rare cancers is often exceptionally challenging owing to feasibility constraints, particularly when patient accrual is limited. To date, no randomized controlled trial has been conducted specifically for advanced SBA. Within this context, ENVELOPE was designed as a single-arm phase II trial to maximize feasibility and enable the timely evaluation of enfortumab vedotin. The interpretative limitations inherent to a single-arm design can be understood as an inevitable trade-off required to advance clinical research in a rare and underserved disease. Given these constraints, the clinical value of enfortumab vedotin in SBA should be determined through an integrated assessment of efficacy, safety, and treatment burden. This evaluation would be more appropriately contextualized in light of the accumulated evidence from prior clinical studies and available historical control data.

For the statistical design, we set the null response rate at 5% and the expected response rate at 25%. Reported response rates to second-line therapy, largely derived from heterogeneous retrospective analyses, have generally been modest. In a Japanese retrospective series, irinotecan-based regimens (including FOLFIRI) and taxane-based regimens achieved response rates of ∼7%.13 A French retrospective study reported a response rate of ∼20% with FOLFIRI,16 whereas a US phase II trial demonstrated a comparable response rate to nab-paclitaxel.17 Real-world data from our institution also demonstrated a 0% response rate to second-line FOLFIRI.14 On this basis, the 5% null rate was conservatively chosen to reflect historical benchmarks, whereas the 25% target rate was selected to represent a clinically meaningful improvement beyond previous outcomes and to justify further development in this rare population.

Enfortumab vedotin is an approved therapy for urothelial carcinoma with an established safety profile, including data in Japanese patients. Therefore ENVELOPE represents a first-in-disease, but not first-in-human, evaluation of this ADC. Nevertheless, the biological and clinical characteristics of SBA differ from those of urothelial carcinoma. Patients with SBA frequently experience malabsorption, malnutrition, or chronic gastrointestinal symptoms related to the primary tumor or prior extensive surgery, which may confer distinct physiological vulnerabilities relative to urothelial carcinoma populations. Accordingly, careful safety oversight is warranted in this setting. To address these considerations, ENVELOPE incorporates predefined toxicity management criteria and close clinical monitoring, and an IDSMC has been established to oversee patient safety throughout the study.

Beyond evaluating the clinical efficacy of enfortumab vedotin, ENVELOPE serves as a translational platform. Prospective, biomarker-integrated trials in SBA remain rare, and this study provides a unique opportunity to systematically collect high-quality tumor specimens from this underserved population. Integrated immunohistochemical and multi-omics analyses will allow investigation of mechanisms underlying therapeutic response and acquired resistance, such as dynamic changes in Nectin-4 expression during treatment. Through these and other comprehensive molecular analyses, ENVELOPE therefore represents not only a therapeutic investigation but also a foundational resource for advancing the molecular understanding of SBA.

Conclusion

ENVELOPE is a phase II investigator-initiated trial in a population with high unmet needs: patients with SBA refractory or intolerant to platinum-based therapy. To our knowledge, this is the first clinical trial worldwide to evaluate an ADC in SBA. As industry-led development is challenging for rare cancers such as SBA, investigator-initiated trials are essential to advance therapy. This trial is grounded in our translational finding of high Nectin-4 expression in SBA, which provides a biological rationale to evaluate enfortumab vedotin as a promising therapeutic candidate.

Conducted in full accordance with ICH-GCP, the ENVELOPE trial is designed to generate rigorous, interpretable evidence in a rare and underserved disease. If effectiveness is demonstrated and the benefit–risk profile is favorable, we hope this trial will—true to its name—carry a new therapeutic option across borders, including potential regulatory approval, and deliver a viable strategy to patients with SBA worldwide.

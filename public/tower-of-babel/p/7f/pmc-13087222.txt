Integrated GRM-based efficient multi-performance prediction method for reconfigurable Fabry–Perot antennas
Authors: Yuxuan Huang, Zhiming Liu, Duanqi Wang, Zhixin Lei, Jiali Yan, Huilin Zhou, Yuhao Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A novel generalized regression model (GRM) is proposed to characterize the relationship between the activation states of reconfigurable units or their combinations on a reconfigurable partially reflective surface (PRS) and the multi-objective electromagnetic performance metrics of antennas. The GRM framework integrates a forward prediction process and an inverse design process. In the forward process, three parallel generalized regression neural network (GRNN) subnetworks are utilized to model critical electromagnetic performance metrics, including the return loss (S-parameter), gain, and radiation patterns. To improve prediction accuracy and robustness, each GRNN subnetwork is optimized with multiobjective particle swarm optimization (MOPSO) and refined using a recursive correction method. The inverse process integrates the forward predictions to construct an enumeration-based inverse mapping that identifies reconfigurable unit configurations satisfying predefined performance constraints. The efficacy of the model is validated through cross-validation on a liquid-based reconfigurable Fabry–Perot (FP) antenna, demonstrating its ability to significantly accelerate the performance optimization of reconfigurable antenna systems.

Introduction

As wireless communication systems evolve, operational environments are becoming increasingly complex, demanding greater flexibility and adaptability. Conventional antennas with fixed functions struggle to meet these dynamic requirements. Using multiple single-function antennas in one system increases cost, size, and mutual coupling, while complicating integration. Reconfigurable antennas that can dynamically tune frequency, radiation pattern, or polarization offer a promising alternative1–3. These antennas provide greater flexibility, improved environmental adaptability, and robust performance in dynamically changing communication settings.

In recent years, reconfigurable Fabry-Perot (FP) antenna technology, as a cutting-edge innovation in the field of wireless communication, has shown significant technological breakthroughs and application potential. The architecture of the FP antenna cleverly integrates the feed antenna and the partially reflective surface (PRS), and provides a new technical platform for the precise and real-time control of key antenna performance metrics due to its compact structure and functional diversity4–6. Among them, the state change of the reflection phase of the incident electromagnetic (EM) wave from the PRS significantly affects the resonance and radiation characteristics of the antenna7,8, and constitutes a core variable for dynamic antenna control. In reconfigurable FP antennas, the reflection phase of the PRS can be adjusted continuously or discretely by transforming its physical properties or by applying an external control signal9. Specifically, these regulatory approaches usually act directly on the reconfigurable units of the PRS, and the resulting changes are referred to as the “activation state” to describe the specific effects of the regulatory approaches on the reconfigurable units. Altered physical properties include, but are not limited to, smart materials and devices such as microelectromechanical system (MEMS) switches10,11, liquid-based materials3,12,13, and reconfigurable metamaterials14,15, while external control signals are usually expressed as electrical signals such as voltage and current16–19. These approaches enable real-time reconfiguration of antenna performance, forming the foundation for intelligent and adaptive antenna systems.

However, despite the promising potential of reconfigurable PRS antenna technology, its design and optimization remain challenging. The combinatorial complexity of reconfigurable PRS units renders conventional EM simulation-based design approaches highly inefficient in identifying optimal configurations, significantly limiting both design efficiency and accuracy. Moreover, few effective and simple design strategies for this regulatory mechanism have been proposed in the existing literature, which highlights the urgent need for current research at the theoretical and practical levels. Furthermore, the performance evaluation of each candidate configuration typically relies on full-wave EM simulations, which, although accurate in principle, incur prohibitively high computational costs and long execution times. This high per-evaluation cost exacerbates the challenge of exploring the vast configuration space, making rapid design iterations impractical. Consequently, these methods are ill-suited for rapid iterative design cycles or real-time optimization tasks, underscoring the urgent need for efficient and accurate surrogate modeling techniques in reconfigurable antenna design.

The nonlinear mapping capabilities of neural networks are considered an efficient way to solve the inefficiency of EM modeling and simulation20–22. Forward surrogate models, such as the backpropagation neural network (BPNN)23 and the radial basis function neural network (RBFNN)24, learn the mapping between antenna configuration parameters and EM performance by adjusting internal weights during training on sample data, as shown in Fig. 1. Once a neural network model is established to capture the mapping between structural parameters and EM responses, it enables a rapid and accurate prediction of antenna performance25–27. Meanwhile, inverse surrogate models such as TF-ANN and TDS-integrated MLP-NN reverse this mapping to directly output structural parameters meeting predefined EM performance requirements, significantly streamlining the iterative design process28,29.Fig. 1Surrogate model mapping antenna configurations to electromagnetic performance metrics.

An artificial neural network (ANN) model with three parallel and independent branches was proposed in Ref.30 for the simultaneous prediction of antenna voltage standing wave ratio (VSWR), gain, and radiation patterns. However, in the context of complex FP antenna models, the utilization of ANN fails to achieve the anticipated performance, particularly in the case of small samples. The generalized regression neural network (GRNN) offers fast convergence, effective nonlinearity modeling, and robustness to small sample sizes, making it advantageous for diverse applications involving complex data31–34. A recursive surrogate model based on GRNN and recursive correction method was developed in Ref.35, and it has better prediction accuracy and robustness in practical engineering applications. Inspired by this, this study regards the GRNN constructed from a small number of samples obtained in EM simulation as a low-fidelity model, and uses the recursive correction method to improve the low-fidelity model, so as to construct a high-precision recursive surrogate model suitable for antenna performance prediction tasks.

In this work, a generalized regression model (GRM)-based framework is proposed, integrating multi-objective particle swarm optimization (MOPSO) and a model recursive correction technique to enhance predictive accuracy. The MOPSO algorithm is employed for hyperparameter optimization in each GRNN subnetwork36–38. An enumeration-based recursive strategy is further employed to construct an inverse prediction framework that exploits the GRM’s computational efficiency for rapid design exploration. Before iterative optimization, S-parameter constraints and radiation angle deviation thresholds are predefined to ensure compliance with design specifications. To validate the proposed approach, a liquid-based reconfigurable FP antenna is adopted as a benchmark example. Experimental results show that the method effectively identifies the activation states of reconfigurable units on the PRS and accurately predicts the antenna’s multi-performance metrics. As a result, the dependence on computationally intensive full-wave EM simulations is substantially reduced, significantly accelerating the design process.

The process of optimizing subnetwork by multi-objective particle swarm optimization algorithm

The GRNN is trained using the antenna structural parameters and EM responses as inputs, with the smoothness parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\sigma _k}$$\end{document} as the objective function. Firstly, regarding sample similarity, six groups of validation samples were selected from the experimental dataset that are adjacent to the training samples in the feature space. These samples retain critical information while maintaining moderate consistency with the training data. The mean squared error (MSE) between the predicted values of the GRNN and their corresponding ground-truth values on the validation set is defined as Fitness Function 1, which quantifies the model’s approximation performance for similar data. Secondly, to evaluate the model’s capability to learn and adapt to sample discrepancies, six groups of test samples were deliberately selected from the experimental data that are significantly separated from the training samples, with their feature distributions deviating from the mainstream trend of the training set. Correspondingly, the MSE on this test set is designated as Fitness Function 2. When the impedance constraint is satisfied, the direction of maximum gain \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta _{\max }$$\end{document} is computed and compared to the target steering angle \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\theta _{\mathrm{target}}.$$\end{document} The configuration proceeds to the next recursion level only if the angular deviation remains within the allowable tolerance, i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\vert \theta _{\max } - \theta _{\mathrm{target}} \vert \le \delta$$\end{document}. Otherwise, the branch is pruned.

Recursion and backtracking: Where constraints are satisfied, the framework regards the current state as a partial optimal solution and moves to the next recursion level. When the recursion reaches a predefined depth, the complete topology is evaluated, with those conforming to the design specifications recorded accordingly.

Conclusion

A novel multi - performance prediction framework is proposed for reconfigurable FP antenna, which targets S-parameter, gain, and radiation patterns and is established on the basis of a GRM. This framework effectively addresses the challenges of diversity and inefficiency in the selection of PRS configuration. The core of the proposed method is the development of three parallel and independent GRNN subnetworks, each designed to efficiently predict one of the three key antenna performance metrics mentioned above. This architecture ensures that the predicted performance metrics conform to the predefined performance constraints. To further enhance prediction accuracy, the MOPSO algorithm and a recursive correction method are integrated into the framework. By collaboratively optimizing multiple performance indicators, the adaptability and accuracy of the prediction model are significantly improved. Compared with traditional EM tuning methods, the strategy proposed in this study markedly enhances the refinement and robustness of antenna design. Experimental validation conducted on a liquid-based reconfigurable antenna demonstrates the superiority of the GRM-based prediction method, particularly in scenarios with a limited number of training samples. Future efforts will focus on extending the current framework to enable dynamic beam steering by incorporating phase-controllable elements or additional degrees of freedom in the PRS configuration, thereby further improving the adaptive performance and practical versatility of reconfigurable FP antennas. This method enables rapid PRS optimization and efficient identification of optimal configurations under complex performance constraints, offering a practical and theoretically grounded solution for comprehensive optimization of the performance of reconfigurable FP antennas.

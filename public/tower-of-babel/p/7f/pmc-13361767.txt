Preoperative Ultrasound-Guided Stellate Ganglion Block for Prevention of Postoperative Sore Throat After Thoracic Surgery: Study Protocol for a Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Liting Wang, Xiang Yan, Jia Jiang, Anshi Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposePostoperative sore throat (POST) is one of the most common complaints after thoracic surgery with double-lumen endobronchial tubes (DLTs), significantly impacting postoperative recovery and patient satisfaction, yet it is often overlooked. Stellate ganglion block (SGB), a minimally invasive technique, may alleviate airway inflammation and modulate sympathetic tone. It has significant potential in reducing the incidence of POST, but evidence in DLTs patients is lacking. This trial aims to test whether preoperative ultrasound-guided SGB can ameliorate the incidence and severity of POST after thoracic surgery.Study Design and MethodsIn this prospective double-blind, randomized controlled trial, 132 patients undergoing thoracoscopic resection of pulmonary lobes/segments with DLTs will be randomized to receive either US-guided SGB with 0.25% ropivacaine 4mL (Group S) or 0.9% saline 4mL (Group C) at the C6 level, on the same side as the thoracic surgery before anesthesia induction. The primary outcome is POST incidence at 6 hours postoperatively. The secondary outcomes include the incidence and severity of POST at 1, 24 and 48 hours, postoperative pain score (NRS) at different time points, postoperative nausea and vomiting, hoarseness, Quality of Recovery-15 scores, RCSQ sleep score, PCIA dosage at 24 and 48 hours. Intraoperative hemodynamic fluctuations, regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rSO2), anesthetic and vasoactive drugs will be recorded. All statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS version 26.0. Two-sided P values <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.DiscussionThis is the first prospective trial evaluating ultrasound-guided SGB for POST after thoracic surgery with DLTs, also assessing the effect of SGB on postoperative recovery quality. The findings will provide more clinical evidence for perioperative SGB application to enhance early postoperative recovery and patient satisfaction.

Introduction

Postoperative sore throat (POST) is a common, unpleasant complication following general anesthesia and tracheal intubation, which causes patient dissatisfaction after general anesthesia.1 POST has been reported to have incidence ranging from 12.1% to 70%.2 POST is primarily attributed to mucosal injury in the pharynx and trachea during endotracheal intubation. The risk factors for POST encompass age, sex, tube diameter, displacement, and cuff pressure fluctuations,3,4 particularly in thoracic surgeries requiring double-lumen endobronchial tubes (DLTs).5 DLTs as a commonly used airway device in thoracic surgery, characterized by larger external diameters and rigid materials, are associated with higher POST incidence. A previous study demonstrated that the POST incidences at 1 hour after thoracic surgery can be as high as 53%.6 It is considered that violent intubation, repeated intraoperative positioning adjustments and cuff pressure changes are the major factors of POST. Although there are some interventions to reduce POST such as chewing gum before surgery,7 intravenous lidocaine8 or dexamethasone,9 preoperative nebulization with dexmedetomidine or ketamine,10 tube softening,11 and topical anesthetics,12 their efficacy remains suboptimal and limited. The current literature lacks high-quality randomized controlled trials on treatments that prevent POST that is of importance to patients and their recovery. Recently, a randomized controlled study demonstrated that US-guided internal branches of the SLN block could ameliorate the incidence and severity of POST induced by DLTs intubation.13 Considering this technique is relatively new and not widely adopted, we aim to identify a more prevalent and effective approach to alleviate POST.

Stellate ganglion is a sympathetic ganglion, formed by a fusion of the inferior cervical and first thoracic sympathetic ganglia, which can be found in about 80% of people.14 Its branches join the seventh and the eight cervical spinal nerve, as well as the first thoracic spinal nerve (somatic branches), contribute to the inferior cardiac nerve (visceral branches), and join the nervous fibers along the vertebral and brachiocephalic arteries (vascular branches). The ganglion also communicates with the vertebral nerve and its branches, thus forming the cervical sympathetic plexus.15 Stellate ganglion block (SGB) refers to the injection of a local anesthetic around the stellate ganglion to block the cervical sympathetic trunk, preganglionic and postganglionic fibers and their areas of innervation (head, neck, upper limb and multiple organs in the chest). Performing stellate ganglion block under ultrasound guidance improves its accuracy while significantly reducing the incidence of adverse events. Nowadays it has been widely used in pain management and enhanced recovery after surgery. SGB can inhibit the sympathetic stress response, thereby exerting a regulatory effect during the perioperative period. More and more clinical studies are focusing on the perioperative application of SGB. Previous studies have shown that SGB can modulate vasodilation and regional perfusion and attenuate stress and inflammation responses.16 Besides it has great potential in reducing postoperative pain,17 improving postoperative sleep quality,18 and mitigating perioperative stress responses during the process of anesthesia induction, endotracheal intubation and pneumoperitoneum.16 Furthermore, it also plays a significant role in reducing perioperative myocardial injury19 and alleviating ventricular arrhythmias in high-risk patients.20,21

Current investigations on perioperative stellate ganglion block (SGB) have predominantly focused on its efficacy in postoperative pain alleviation, sleep quality improvement, and stress response attenuation. However, scant research exists regarding SGB’s potential to mitigate postoperative sore throat (POST). Recent clinical trials have suggested that SGB could effectively reduce both incidence and severity of POST in patients undergoing prone-position lumbar spine surgeries.22 This therapeutic effect is hypothesized to stem from SGB’s capacity to diminish local mucosal irritation caused by endotracheal intubation. Nevertheless, limited evidence exists regarding SGB applications in thoracic surgeries, particularly in the context of POST prevention and management.

Therefore, we have designed a double-blind randomized controlled trial to investigate the efficacy of preoperative stellate ganglion block (SGB) in reducing postoperative sore throat (POST) following thoracic surgeries, while concurrently examining its potential effects on postoperative pain score, sleep quality, and intraoperative regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rSO2).

Materials and Methods

The protocol has already been approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Beijing Chaoyang Hospital affiliated to Capital Medical University (Approval number: 2025-ke-2) prior to recruitment and registered on Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, ChiCTR2500098901. The study will be conducted to the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki. Study Objective

The trial aims to test the hypothesis that preoperative ultrasound-guided SGB can ameliorate the incidence and severity of POST in patients undergoing thoracic surgery.

Participants

The trial will be conducted in the Anesthesia & Operation Center at Beijing Chao-Yang Hospital affiliated to Capital Medical University. A total of 132 patients undergoing elective thoracoscopic resection of pulmonary lobes/segments will be enrolled and randomized with a 1:1 allocation ratio. According to the principle of simple random grouping, patients will be divided into the SGB group (Group S) and placebo control group (Group C).

Prospective participants will be carefully assessed for eligibility based on the following inclusion criteria: (1) patients undergoing elective thoracoscopic lobectomy/pulmonary segmentectomy with DLTs under GA and more than 2 hours, (2) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I–III, (3) Age ≥18 years, (4) Body mass index (BMI) 18–35.

The exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) patient refusal or surgery canceled, (2) coagulation disorder, (3) local and systemic infection, (4) with mental disorder and opioid addiction, (5) history of acute upper respiratory track infection, hoarseness, and cough within 1 week before operation, (6) history of oral or throat surgery, (7) history of asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, (8) BMI > 35 or extremely low body weight, weight less than 40 kg, (9) combined with respiratory insufficiency before surgery, (10) surgery type changed such as intraoperative conversion to thoracotomy, (11) transferred to the intensive care unit with an endotracheal tube.

Preoperative Visit and Evaluation

Enrolment into the trial and baseline characters will occur on one day before surgery. During the preoperative interview, participants scheduled for elective thoracoscopic resection of pulmonary lobes/segments will be screened based on the eligibility criteria prior to enrollment. After detailing the intervention measures and consideration of risks and benefits, participants who agree to sign the informed documents voluntarily will be enrolled in this study. Moreover, participants in this study have the right to obtain all relevant information and withdraw their consent for any reason at any time. The enrolment schedule is shown in Table 1.

Randomization and Masking

After signing the informed consent, participants will be randomly allocated to 1 of the 2 groups in a 1:1 ratio depending on whether the US-guided SGB with 0.25% ropivacaine 4mL or 0.9% saline 4mL before anesthesia induction. Random allocation will be carried out by a scientific clinician who is not involved in the study, using a computer-generated randomization code and the sealed envelope method. The scientific clinician has access to obtain the group assignment and is responsible for preparing the identical 4mL trial drugs (0.25% ropivacaine or normal saline) for administration. The investigator will conduct the intervention measure before anesthesia induction. Group S will receive US-guided SGB with 0.25% ropivacaine 4mL at C6 level on the same side as the thoracic surgery, Group C will receive US-guided SGB with 0.9% saline 4mL for placebo control. In our study, only scientific clinician know the assignment. The patients, investigators and outcome assessors will be blinded to the group assignment.

Intervention

US-Guided SGB

After entering to the operating room, the peripheral venous access will be established. All patients will be continuously monitored by Electrocardiography (ECG), blood oxygen saturation (SpO2), heart rate (HR), non-invasive blood pressure (NBP), invasive blood pressure (IBP), and respiratory rate (RR). After 10 minutes of resting, NBP, IBP and HR will be recorded as the basal vital signs. The patient will be placed in a supine position with the head turned to the non-surgical side and with a thin pillow under their shoulder to hyperextend the surgical side neck mildly. After routine sterilization of the skin, we will use a sterile high-frequency linear array probe to scan the cross-sectional plane of C6 transverse process nodules and confirm the position of the stellate ganglion which is between the prevertebral fascia and the longus capitis muscle or longus colli. Then 4 mL trial medication (0.25% ropivacaine or normal volume saline) will be injected into the surface of the longus colli muscle by suction without blood, air, and cerebrospinal fluid. We will use a 23-gauge needle with the in-plane technique. The intervention will be completed before anesthesia induction.

Induction and Maintenance of Anesthesia

Anesthesia induction will be conducted using total intravenous induction, with intravenous midazolam (0.02 mg·kg−1), sufentanil (0.3 μg·kg−1), propofol (1.5–2.0 mg·kg−1), and rocuronium (`0.6–0.9 mg·kg−1). After 2 minutes of mask positive-pressure ventilation, endotracheal intubation will be performed by the same anesthesiologist with >5 years of clinical experience using a lubricated disposable DLTs via visual laryngoscopy. The size of the DLTs is 35# Left for female and 37# Left for male. When the bronchial tip insert through the glottis directing anteriorly, the stylet will be removed once, and the DLTs will be spun 90°counterclockwise, directing the bronchial tip to the left side, and progressed to the appropriate depth of insertion. We will accurately adjust the DLTs position with the help of fiberoptic bronchoscope. Furthermore, both groups will receive thoracic paravertebral nerve block (TPVB) as part of standard enhanced recovery after surgery protocol.

Mechanical ventilation will be delivered in a lung-protective strategy. We will employ the volume-controlled ventilation (VCV) mode. The tidal volume will be targeted at 6–8 mL/kg of predicted body weight for two-lung ventilation and reduced to 4–6 mL/kg for one-lung ventilation. The respiratory rate will be set at 12–16 times/min, aiming to maintain SpO2 above 95% and end-tidal carbon dioxide pressure between 35 to 45 mmHg. Patients requiring more than one intubation attempt (≥2 attempts) or those with Cormack-Lehane Grade III or IV will be excluded from the analysis. Besides, intraoperative suctioning will be performed prior to the initiation of OLV, before manual lung recruitment, and before resuming TLV. Each suction will be applied to both the endotracheal and oropharyngeal, limited to less than 10 seconds, maintaining a negative pressure of −100 to −150 mmHg.

Anesthesia will be maintained by propofol and remifentanil to keep a suitable depth of anesthesia (BIS 40–60). IBP and HR will be maintained within 20% of the patients’ baseline, vasoactive drugs such as norepinephrine or ephedrine will be used in patients with unstable hemodynamics as appropriate. When suffering severe cardiovascular responsiveness to noxious stimuli, 0.1 μg/kg additional sufentanil will be administered intravenously to reach a stable hemodynamics state. In addition, intravenous rocuronium will be administered as needed. US-guided SGB and TPVB will be administrated by the same experienced (>5 years of nerve block experience) anesthesiologist under continuously cardiac and respiration monitoring.

Postoperative Management

The DLTs will be carefully extubated at the end of surgery after confirmation of adequate spontaneous ventilation, responsiveness to verbal commands, and complete neuromuscular recovery. Following extubation, the presence of blood staining on the DLTs cuff will be assessed and recorded prospectively. Then anesthetist will transfer the patient to the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU). When the Aldrete score reaches 9 points or more, the patient will be transferred from PACU to a surgical ward.

Standard Analgesia Protocol

To prescribe flurbiprofen axetil 50 mg intravenously 30 minutes before the surgical ending for adjunctive analgesia. Moreover, all patients will receive patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) during the first 2 days after surgery. The PCIA protocol is programmed with 2 μg/kg sufentanil and 8 mg ondansetron diluted to 100 mL (bolus, 1 mL; background infusion, 2 mL/h; lockout time interval, 15 min; 1 h limit, 8 mL without any baseline infusion). Rescue analgesia will be administered when analgesia is inadequate, and medication administration on the ward will be documented by reviewing the physician order system.

Follow-Up

Two blinded assessors are responsible for the postoperative follow-up. All information will be recorded from the day before surgery to 48 hours postoperatively. Follow-up data will be documented in paper-based CRFs and stored as Excel digital forms. In order to improve the adherence of intervention protocol, assessors will conduct the face-to face follow-up or make phone calls in person.

Baseline Characteristics and Operative Measurements

After screening and signing the informed consent form, the basic information of the patient will be recorded on the CRFs at least 1 day before surgery. The demographic data will consist of age, gender, body mass index, smoking history, ASA physical status and underlying disease. Besides we will also assess patient’s Karnofsky Performance Status, sleep quality score assessed with Richards Campbell Sleep Questionnaire (RCSQ), and preoperative anxiety/depression status appraised by Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS).

Surgical information including the surgical site, DLTs size, duration of surgery, duration of intubation, the Cormack and Lehane grade and blood staining on the DLTs cuff after extubation will be recorded. Intraoperative medication usage will also be recorded. Safety Outcomes

This study will be strictly conducted in accordance with the standard operating procedures, and all relevant adverse events will be monitored. Firstly, adverse events include SGB-related adverse events, such as hematoma, pneumothorax, local anesthetic systemic toxicity, brachial plexus block, cervical plexus block, phrenic nerve paralysis, and total spinal anesthesia. On the other hand, other adverse events include hypotension (systolic blood pressure < 90 mmHg or a decrease of more than 30% from baseline), bradycardia (heart rate < 50 beats per minute) and hypertension (systolic blood pressure increase of more than 30% from baseline). Difficult intubation (more than 3 attempts at tracheal intubation), severe allergic reactions, vital arrhythmias, hypoxemia, and acute bleeding will also be identified as adverse events. Furthermore, any adverse events will be recorded, including the time-point at which it happened, therapeutic measure, outcomes and follow-up. All the clinical documents related to adverse events will be discerned and reserved, it will be sent to the principal investigator and the ethics committee within the requested time range.

Sample Size Calculation

The sample size calculation was based on previous studies, the incidence of POST 6 hours after thoracic surgery was 50%.13,23 In addition, we incorporated data from our preliminary pilot study (n = 20), in which we observed a 50% reduction in POST incidence in the SGB group compared with the control group. If a 50% reduction in the incidence of POST is regarded as clinically significant,23–25 a sample size of 55 patients per group will be required with α = 0.05 and β = 0.20. To compensate for 15% dropouts and withdrawal of patients, 66 patients will be enrolled per group. The sample size calculation was performed using PASS 15.0 software. The test statistic used for the sample size calculation was the two-sided chi-square test.

Statistical Analysis

All the statistical analyses will be performed on the ITT principle, including all randomized patients. The primary outcome is the incidence of POST 6 hours after surgery, will be compared between groups using the chi-square test and reported with RR (relative risk) and 95% confidence intervals. If there are significant imbalanced confounders in the baseline component, the generalized estimating equation (GEE) approach will be used to provide more robust inferences for modeling longitudinal data by considering correlations between observations at different time points. All statistical tests will be two-sided and P < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

For secondary outcomes measures, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test will be used to evaluate the normality of the quantitative data, expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range, IQR). Intergroup comparisons will be assessed by Two-sample T test with normal distributions or Mann–Whitney U-test with nonnormal distribution. The categorical variables will be presented as numbers (n/%) or proportions (%) and evaluated using the chi-square test or Fisher exact test where appropriate. Continuous repeated-measures outcomes, including intraoperative hemodynamics, regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rSO2), postoperative NRS pain scores, PCIA dosage over time, QoR-15 scores and RCSQ sleep scores, will be analyzed using linear mixed-effects models (LMMs) to evaluate the main effects of time, group, and their interaction. Cross-sectional group comparisons of POST incidence and severity at each time point will be compared using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test, with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons. Longitudinal analysis of ordinal POST severity will be analyzed using a generalized estimating equations (GEE) model with a cumulative logit link to account for within-subject correlation over time. The proportional odds assumption will be assessed, and if violated, a partial proportional odds model will be used. Longitudinal analysis of POST incidence will be analyzed separately using a GEE model with a binary logit link. Given the exploratory nature of secondary outcomes and the use of LMMs or GEE for repeated measures, P values will not be adjusted for multiple comparisons. All statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS version 26.0. Two-sided P values <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Data Management and Monitoring

The principal investigator will oversee the trial conduct and perform a monthly review of the trial status. All the research materials will be kept uniformly by the scientific secretary for at least 5 years after the study is completed. Two independent researchers will add all information to Excel forms on a password-protected computer. The investigators and statistician who were involved in the study have access to the final data set.

Discussion

POST is one of the most common complaints after thoracic surgery with DLTs, significantly impacting postoperative recovery and patient satisfaction, while it is often overlooked by anesthesiologists due to its self-limiting. Although there are already multiple measures to reduce POST caused by DLTs, the incidence of POST can be reduced by about 20%13 and high-quality randomized controlled studies are lacking. So it is necessary to find an effective prophylactic management to reduce POST severity and frequency. With the advancement of research, the application of stellate ganglion block has gradually expanded from the pain management to the perioperative period. It is well known that SGB can reduce stress responses, regulate autonomic nervous system function, alleviate perioperative pain and improve postoperative sleep quality.16 Currently, there is limited research on SGB for alleviating postoperative sore throat caused by DLTs. Therefore, we designed a randomized controlled study to explore the perioperative application of SGB. This study will further investigate and verify its feasibility on POST.

We select the accumulated incidence of POST at 6h after surgery as the primary outcome, it is attributed to patients will be generally allowed to drink water at 6 hours after surgery, and at this point, postoperative sore throat will become a prominent discomfort. Based on the characteristics of stellate ganglion, successful SBG will present with Horner’s syndrome, so participants and anesthesiologists may realize the group randomization to some extent. Nevertheless, we will adhere to the principle of double-blind design, ensuring the follow-up personnel unaware of the group assignments. Besides, considering the lateralization of sympathetic nervous system activity and the anatomical differences between the left and right stellate ganglion, a randomized controlled trial demonstrated that right SGB had a more significant short-term effect on heart rate reduction than left SGB, but no significant difference in terms of the long-term effects of the blocks.26 There is a lack of comparative studies on the perioperative effects of left and right SGB, the optimal side for SGB administration remains unclear. To address this gap, we will conduct an exploratory post-hoc analyses after unblinding to investigate the potential perioperative disparities between left-side and right-side SGB.

This protocol has some limitations. First, the assessment scales for POST are predominantly subjective, lacking objective evaluation criteria. Secondly, successful SGB can present as Horner’s syndrome, which occurs in approximately 84–100% of cases.27 The clinical signs may interfere with the implementation of blinding to some extent. In order to minimize the bias, only scientific clinician who generated the randomized results and allocated trial medications knew the assignment. Participants in the follow-up will use standardized assessment criteria.

This trial will provide more clinical evidence for the perioperative application of SGB, thereby enhancing early postoperative recovery and satisfaction.

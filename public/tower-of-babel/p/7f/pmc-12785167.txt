SugarcaneOmics: An integrative multi-omics platform for sugarcane research
Authors: Hong Luo, Xue Bai, Zishan Wu, Siwei Ren, Haixia Xie, Zhixiang Yuan, Jie Zhang, Dongmei Tian, Shuhui Song
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

As the predominant source of 80% of global sugar production, sugarcane is not only a critical pillar of food security but also an indispensable component of agricultural economies worldwide. However, its sugar yield is limited by restricted genetic diversity and an exceptionally complex genome. This genetic complexity stems from the polyploid nature of sugarcane, which arose from a historical hybridization between Saccharum officinarum and Saccharum spontaneum. The resulting genome combines high heterozygosity and aneuploidy, creating major hurdles for genetic mapping and functional studies. Recent advances in sugarcane omics, including the assembly of reference genomes (Bao et al., 2024; Healey et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025), whole-genome resequencing of various varieties (Li et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2022), and transcriptomic and comparative genomic studies (Jiang et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023), have opened up extensive opportunities for both scientific discovery and improved crop breeding strategies. However, the absence of integrated multi-omics databases for sugarcane, in contrast to the well-established resources available for other major crops, remains a critical barrier to advancing fundamental research and practical applications.

To address this challenge, we established SugarcaneOmics (https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/scod/), a comprehensive multi-omics data platform for sugarcane and its closely related species. These include four cultivated sugarcane accessions, one accession from S. officinarum, two accessions from S. spontaneum, three accessions from Erianthus, three accessions from Miscanthus, and one accession from Sorghum bicolor (Supplemental Table 1). This platform systematically compiles the most complete and diverse multi-omics datasets, including genome assemblies, genetic variation maps, transcriptomic profiles, and regulatory networks, enabling cross-species and cross-omics comparative analyses. Equipped with an intuitive user interface and specialized toolkits, it facilitates efficient data analysis, visualization, and interpretation. We envision that SugarcaneOmics will address current infrastructure gaps in sugarcane omics research, accelerate the characterization of biological mechanisms, and serve as a vital resource for advancing precision breeding.

Overview of SugarcaneOmics

By integrating diverse multi-omics data, we developed five highly interactive core data modules within the SugarcaneOmics platform: “genome,” “transcriptome,” “variome,” “feature genes,” and “varieties” (Figure 1A). The genome module aggregates chromosome-level genome assemblies, providing comprehensive gene annotations and pan-gene clustering information (Supplemental Figure 1). The transcriptome module provides standardized quantification of expression profiles from 1256 sugarcane samples (supplemental information), covering 14 tissue types and six developmental stages. Users can interrogate expression patterns and functional annotations of genes of interest. Data can be organized by tissue, developmental stage, or germplasm group. Users can also perform tests of statistical significance to facilitate comprehensive analysis of gene expression patterns and differential expression profiling (Supplemental Figure 2). The variome module incorporates the genome variation map framework (Li et al., 2020) of 322 sugarcane germplasms, with approximately 145 million single-nucleotide polymorphisms and 30 million short insertions/deletions (supplemental information). It also visualizes selective sweep signals to support population genetics studies (Supplemental Figure 3), thereby addressing a critical gap in published sugarcane omics databases through the provision of previously unavailable data and functionalities (Supplemental Table 2). The feature genes module compiles sugarcane transcription factor families annotated through in-house pipelines, along with candidate functional genes involved in key agronomic trait regulatory networks, identified via literature curation and comparative genomics approaches (supplemental information; Supplemental Figure 4), offering critical clues for functional genomics research. Application toolkits

In addition to core data modules, our platform offers several web-based toolkits for sequence searching, retrieval, and visualization (BLAST, SeqFetch, and Visualization); gene functional enrichment and protein interaction network analysis (GO Enrichment, KEGG Enrichment, and PIN Miner); and primer design and off-target prediction for gene editing (PrimerServer and CRIOff). The BLAST toolkit facilitates comprehensive sequence searches against genomic, coding, and protein sequences (Supplemental Figure 6). SeqFetch enables precise retrieval of sequence information from specified genomic regions, genes, mRNAs, coding sequences (CDS) segments, and/or protein products (Supplemental Figure 7). The Visualization toolkit incorporates JBrowse for efficient genome data navigation and analytical interpretation (Supplemental Figure 8). The GO Enrichment analyzer performs rigorous gene ontology enrichment using user-submitted gene ID datasets (Supplemental Figure 9). The KEGG Enrichment tool performs pathway enrichment analysis with a set of gene IDs of interest provided by users (Supplemental Figure 10). PIN Miner provides query capabilities and a graphical representation of our proprietary sugarcane protein–protein interaction network, constructed through dedicated pipelines (supplemental information; Supplemental Figure 11). Furthermore, we have implemented Primer Server (https://github.com/billzt/PrimerServer2) for optimized primer design, specificity validation, and experimental data presentation (Supplemental Figure 12). To address the critical challenge of CRISPR–Cas9 off-target effects in sugarcane systems, we developed the CRIOff toolkit by integrating advanced computational frameworks (Bae et al., 2014; Lin et al., 2020). This innovative toolkit predicts potential single-guide RNA off-target sites with comprehensive risk assessment scoring (Supplemental Figure 13).

Data mining using SugarcaneOmics

When mining candidate genes for sugarcane breeding, a typical pipeline involves identifying sugarcane homologs of functionally characterized genes that govern key agronomic traits in closely related gramineous crops, followed by comprehensive inference and prioritization, leveraging multi-omics signatures to facilitate subsequent exploration. The “documentation” section of SugarcaneOmics provides examples to guide users in harnessing the platform for efficient mining of such genes.

Taking the rice yield-related IPA1 gene (Wang et al., 2018) as an example, users seeking to evaluate the research and application potential of its homologs in sugarcane may directly query “IPA1” via the homepage search interface. This retrieves a list of sugarcane homologs, enabling immediate access to annotations and multi-omics datasets for download or interactive visualization. Visual analytics automatically generated by the platform for Npp.02C024290.1 homologs (Figure 1) reveal (1) copy-number expansion in cultivated sugarcane varieties (14–19 homologs) relative to wild progenitors (6 homologs) (Figure 1B); (2) a selection signal, i.e., a genomic region exhibiting negative Tajima’s D values, reduced nucleotide diversity (π), and increased population differentiation indices (Fst) (Figure 1C); and (3) a functional clue, i.e., detectable expression across multiple sugarcane tissues (Figure 1D) with statistically significant differential expression (adjusted p < 0.05) between wild and cultivated accessions (Figure 1E). Together, these molecular signatures indicate the gene’s putative functional role and breeding selection background in sugarcane.

Compared with established sugarcane omics repositories such as ScDB (Chen et al., 2024) and the Sugarcane Genome Hub (https://sugarcane-genome.cirad.fr/), SugarcaneOmics uniquely integrates population-scale genomic variants from 322 cultivars, hosts the largest curated transcriptome repository (>1000 samples), and provides sugarcane-specific toolkits such as one-click KEGG/GO enrichment, offering valuable resources for functional genomics and breeding research (Supplemental Table 2). The current SugarcaneOmics database has several inherent limitations, including a paucity of curated phenomic datasets and suboptimal integration across multi-omics data layers and cross-module navigation interfaces. Future developmental priorities for the database include the expansion of germplasm resources and multi-omics data diversity, implementation of batch-effect-correction protocols for transcriptome analyses, development of phenotype-to-genotype predictive algorithms, construction of graph-based pan-genome architectures for identification of structural variations, and establishment of data-driven candidate-gene discovery frameworks for molecular breeding applications. Our long-term vision is to establish SugarcaneOmics as a globally influential platform that drives scientific exploration and breeding innovation in sugarcane research.

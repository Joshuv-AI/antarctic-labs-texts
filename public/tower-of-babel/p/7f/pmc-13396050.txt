Introduction

Background

Myasthenia gravis (MG) is an autoimmune disorder of the neuromuscular junction characterized by fatigable skeletal muscle weakness. Clinical manifestations include ptosis, diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, limb weakness, and, in severe cases, respiratory failure. Beyond threatening life and health-related quality of life (HRQoL), MG imposes significant social and economic burdens, including unemployment [1, 2].

Since 1966, survival of patients with generalized MG (gMG) has improved substantially with high-dose oral corticosteroid therapy [3]. However, regimens involving 60–80 mg/day of oral prednisolone (PSL) on an alternate-day schedule [4] are associated with multiple steroid-related complications, including diabetes [5], hypertension, depression [6], and osteoporosis [7]. A retrospective study has demonstrated a correlation between cumulative oral steroid dose and impaired HRQoL [8]. Accordingly, the Early Fast-Acting Treatment (EFT) strategy was introduced to manage MG with reduced steroid exposure. This approach combines early use of fast-acting therapy (FT; plasmapheresis, intravenous immunoglobulin, and high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone) with calcineurin inhibitors [9, 10]. Even with EFT, the cumulative achievement rate of minimal manifestations (MM) or better on the Myasthenia Gravis Foundation of America (MGFA) post-intervention status [11] at an oral PSL dose of ≤ 5 mg/day (MM-5 mg) [12] remains approximately 62% [13]. gMG therefore continues to represent a therapeutic challenge.

Several molecular-targeted therapies for MG have recently been developed [1]. Efgartigimod is a human IgG1 antibody Fc fragment that modulates IgG recycling via the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). In vascular endothelial cells, FcRn binds IgG in the acidic endosomal environment and rescues it from lysosomal degradation, thereby maintaining plasma IgG concentrations [14]. Efgartigimod accelerates lysosomal degradation of IgG by outcompeting endogenous IgG for FcRn binding and is expected to reduce pathogenic autoantibody levels, including anti-nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (AChR) and anti-muscle-specific kinase (MuSK) antibodies [15]. In the phase 3 ADAPT trial, efgartigimod demonstrated efficacy in gMG, with 67.7% of treated patients achieving a ≥ 2-point improvement on the MG-ADL scale [16].

If efgartigimod can safely reduce oral corticosteroid requirements in patients with gMG, it may improve disease control while minimizing severe steroid-related complications. Retrospective studies have suggested that efgartigimod may lower steroid requirements [17, 18]; however, no prospective trial has evaluated whether oral PSL can be safely tapered to ≤ 5 mg/day without exacerbating MG symptoms under efgartigimod treatment. In current clinical practice, efgartigimod is administered once weekly for 4 consecutive weeks (one cycle), with the intercycle interval determined by the treating physician. While this permits individualized treatment, it complicates statistical evaluation of the optimal dosing regimen and its impact on steroid reduction.

Objectives

The primary objective of this study is to determine whether regularly scheduled administration of efgartigimod can safely and effectively reduce the need for oral PSL, with the specific goal of tapering PSL to ≤ 5 mg/day in patients with gMG who remain dependent on ≥ 6 mg/day of PSL despite prior standard therapy, including FT. The secondary objectives are to identify the most effective dosing interval, clinical predictors of therapeutic response, and candidate biomarkers, in order to support the development of a new maintenance treatment algorithm for refractory gMG.

Hypothesis: We hypothesize that regularly scheduled efgartigimod administration (four cycles at 28-day intervals) will enable protocolized tapering of oral PSL to ≤ 5 mg/day in patients with refractory gMG, without worsening disease activity as measured by the MG-ADL scale.

Trial Design

The MG-Efgartigimod study (CRB3180028) is a prospective, single-arm, open-label, single-center clinical trial. The trial was prospectively registered with the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials on 1 October 2024.

Methods

This study aims to evaluate whether regular administration of efgartigimod can reduce oral PSL dose while improving or maintaining symptoms in patients with gMG who have not achieved MM despite receiving ≥ 6 mg/day of oral PSL. Study Procedures

Pre-observation period

Patients who consent to participate will undergo a 1–2-week pre-observation period (weeks − 2 to 0) before study initiation. Eligibility will be confirmed by verifying an MG-ADL score [19] ≥ 5 and completing baseline assessments using the Quantitative Myasthenia Gravis (QMG) score [11], the MG Composite scale [20], and the Japanese version of the 15-item Myasthenia Gravis Quality of Life Scale revised (MG-QOL15r-J) [21]. To confirm that exclusion criteria are not met, medical history review, blood tests (AST, ALT, creatinine, HBs antigen, HCV antigen, HBs antibody, hepatitis B core antibody using chemiluminescent enzyme immunoassay; tuberculosis-specific interferon-gamma release assay by enzyme-linked immunospot method; and, if indicated, HBV-DNA quantification by real-time polymerase chain reaction), urinalysis, and chest radiography will be performed. Pathogenic MG autoantibodies will be measured, and patients will be classified into established MG subtypes: early-onset MG, late-onset MG, thymoma-associated MG, anti-MuSK antibody-positive MG, and double-seronegative MG. Consistent with the phase 3 ADAPT trial, patients will be enrolled regardless of antibody type or serostatus.

Study period

At week 0, baseline status will be assessed using the MG-ADL scale, MG Composite scale, QMG score, and MG-QOL15r-J. Efgartigimod (Vyvgart®; argenx, Ghent, Belgium) will then be intravenously administered at 10 mg/kg once weekly for 4 consecutive weeks (one cycle). This regimen will be repeated for four cycles, with 28 days between the fourth dose of each cycle and the first dose of the next. Each dose will be designated “CycleNVM” (e.g., “Cycle1V2” indicates the second dose in the first cycle). On each administration day, a medical interview and physical examination, including vital signs, will be performed to evaluate adverse events. MG symptoms will be assessed using the MG-ADL scale, MG Composite scale, QMG score, and MG-QOL15r-J on the first and fourth administration days of each cycle. Blood and urine samples will be collected at weeks 0, 3, 7, 10, 14, 17, 21, 24, and 28 to measure serum IgG concentrations, pathogenic autoantibody titers, hematology, clinical chemistry, and to monitor adverse events. In parallel, additional serum and plasma aliquots will be biobanked at the same time points and stored at − 80 °C for subsequent proteomic and immunologic biomarker analyses.

Steroid tapering protocol

Oral PSL will be reduced only when patients do not show worsening of MG symptoms. If the MG-ADL score on the fourth administration day of a cycle shows no increase of ≥ 1 point compared with both the first administration day of that cycle and baseline (Cycle1V1), the oral PSL dose will be reduced. The reduction amount depends on the current PSL dose: 2.5 mg/day (when PSL > 10 mg), 2 mg/day (when 6 mg < PSL ≤ 10 mg), or 1 mg/day (when PSL ≤ 6 mg). For example, a patient taking 13 mg/day of PSL at baseline who completes all cycles would reach 10.5 mg/day at Cycle1V4, 8 mg/day at Cycle2V4, 6 mg/day at Cycle3V4, and 5 mg/day at Cycle4V4. If worsening is observed on the fourth administration day relative to either the first administration day of that cycle or baseline, the oral PSL dose will not be reduced. The 1-point threshold was selected to serve as a sensitive safety checkpoint applied individually at each cycle, taking into account that the enrolled population includes patients with an MG-ADL ≥ 5, in whom even small increments in symptom burden may meaningfully affect daily function. To avoid overly conservative interruption of tapering due to day-to-day fluctuation, the attending physician may continue tapering when a 1-point increase is judged to reflect transient variation rather than true clinical worsening; such decisions will be documented in the case report form, as detailed under “Management of clinical worsening” below.

Schedule flexibility and concomitant therapy

If administration of efgartigimod becomes difficult as a result of infection or other adverse events, each scheduled dose may be postponed by up to 2 weeks (i.e., a delay of up to two weekly administrations per dose). During any postponement, patients must continue hospital visits at weekly intervals for regular neurological assessment (MG-ADL scale) and evaluation for infection and other adverse events. When re-administration of efgartigimod is deemed feasible, the protocol will resume from that time point. If a single dose is postponed for more than 2 weeks, or if postponement occurs on four or more occasions throughout the trial, the study will be discontinued for that participant. Visit dates may be rescheduled within 2 days before or after the originally planned administration. Systemic corticosteroid use (oral or intravenous, excluding nasal or inhalation routes) outside the protocol will be prohibited. At the time of enrollment, eligible patients will have been on a stable dose of oral PSL and, where applicable, oral immunosuppressants (tacrolimus or cyclosporine) for at least 4 weeks, as specified in the inclusion criteria. Oral immunosuppressants (tacrolimus and cyclosporine) already being taken at enrollment will be maintained at the baseline dose; they may be reduced only in response to adverse events or elevated serum levels. Initiation of new immunosuppressants, dose escalation of existing immunosuppressants, and FTs (plasmapheresis, intravenous immunoglobulin, or high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone) will be prohibited during the study.

Management of clinical worsening

If MG symptoms worsen during the study, the attending physician will evaluate the patient and determine the appropriate course of action. If the physician concludes that neither an increase in PSL nor additional intervention is necessary, therapy will continue at the current PSL dose and oral immunosuppressants. MG symptoms often fluctuate day to day, and transient exacerbations do not uniformly require permanent therapeutic adjustment. Conversely, if the physician determines that exacerbation warrants increased PSL or FT, the PSL dose from the previous tapering stage at the time of worsening will be documented as the final dose. The study will then be discontinued and standard treatment resumed. After withdrawal, the attending physician will continue managing the patient until the exacerbation stabilizes.

Post-observation period

After Cycle4V4, a 28-day post-observation period will begin. At the end of this period, the MG-ADL scale, MG Composite scale, QMG score, and MG-QOL15r-J will be reassessed, and blood and urine testing will be performed to evaluate the effectiveness, suitability, and safety of steroid tapering. To assess steroid-related toxicity across three domains, lumbar spine bone mineral density will be measured at baseline and at the end of the post-observation period (skeletal domain); HbA1c will be measured at five timepoints across the study—weeks 0, 7, 14, 21, and 28—to enable both baseline-versus-end-of-study comparison and longitudinal characterization (glycemic domain); and blood pressure will be measured before and after each efgartigimod infusion and summarized longitudinally (cardiovascular domain).

Statistical Analysis

Analysis populations

The population for efficacy endpoint analysis will be the full analysis set (FAS), defined as all enrolled patients for whom PSL dose data were available at the time of consent and who received at least one dose of efgartigimod. The population for safety endpoint analysis will include all patients who received at least one dose of efgartigimod.

Primary analysis

For the primary endpoint, the reduction in PSL from baseline will be calculated using the FAS. A one-sample t test will be performed under the null hypothesis that the mean reduction in oral PSL following efgartigimod is 0 mg/day, with a two-sided significance level of 0.05.

Secondary analysis

The proportion of patients achieving an oral PSL dose of ≤ 5 mg/day and the proportion achieving MM-5 mg status at the end of the study will each be summarized as point estimates with two-sided 95% confidence intervals using the Clopper–Pearson exact method. The time from baseline to first achievement of ≤ 5 mg/day will be analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method; for patients who do not achieve this threshold during the study, observations will be censored at the end of the study or at the date of discontinuation, whichever occurs first. The cumulative oral PSL dose over the study period and changes in steroid toxicity indicators (HbA1c, lumbar spine bone mineral density, and blood pressure) between baseline and end of study will be summarized with descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation, median, and range).

Subgroup analysis

Prespecified descriptive subgroup analyses will be conducted to compare clinical course and steroid-sparing effect (i) across MG subtypes (anti-AChR antibody-positive, anti-MuSK antibody-positive, and double-seronegative cases) and (ii) between patients who are anti-AChR antibody positive, with and without thymoma. Given the modest sample size, subgroup findings will be presented descriptively with point estimates and 95% confidence intervals; formal between-group hypothesis testing will be regarded as exploratory.

Sample size

The sample size was determined based on the average oral corticosteroid dose of patients with gMG treated as outpatients at the University of Tsukuba Hospital (the study site) who met the selection criteria. To achieve a statistical power of 0.9, a sample size of N = 13 is required. Allowing for potential dropouts after enrollment—defined as withdrawal before efgartigimod administration, or discontinuation after administration—the target enrollment was set at 16 patients.

Sensitivity analysis

A prespecified sensitivity analysis will be conducted to reapply the tapering algorithm to the observed MG-ADL trajectories under an alternative worsening threshold of ≥ 2 points, corresponding to the established minimal clinically important difference for the MG-ADL scale. For each patient, counterfactual PSL doses at each cycle will be derived under the 2-point rule, and the final PSL dose, cumulative PSL dose, and proportion of patients achieving ≤ 5 mg/day will be compared between the two thresholds. As this analysis is counterfactual—the observed MG-ADL scores were obtained under the 1-point rule, and the trajectories may have differed under an alternative tapering schedule—results will be interpreted accordingly.

Strengths and Limitations

Strengths

To our knowledge, this is the first prospective clinical trial designed specifically to evaluate the steroid-sparing effect of regularly scheduled efgartigimod administration in patients with gMG. Several features strengthen the study. First, fixed 28-day intercycle intervals standardize drug exposure across patients, which—unlike physician-directed dosing in routine practice—allow the steroid-sparing effect to be attributed to the protocolized regimen rather than to idiosyncratic dosing decisions. Second, the predefined tapering algorithm based on the MG-ADL scale is transparent and reproducible, supporting future external replication. Third, broad inclusion across the major MG subtypes enables comparative evaluation of efficacy by MG subtype, addressing a recognized gap in the evidence base. Fourth, longitudinal biobanking of serum and plasma samples at each administration will enable proteomic and immunologic analyses aimed at identifying biomarkers predictive of efgartigimod response. Finally, the primary center manages approximately 150 patients with MG, providing a feasible recruitment pool for the target sample size.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the single-arm, open-label design without a comparator may introduce performance and detection bias; both participants and investigators are aware of treatment assignment, and no placebo group is included. Second, the single-center design limits generalizability to other healthcare systems and patient populations, particularly outside Japan. Third, the planned sample size (N = 16) is modest and, while adequately powered for the primary endpoint, will limit statistical power for secondary subgroup analyses by MG subtype. Fourth, the 28-day post-observation period is relatively short and may be insufficient to capture delayed MG exacerbations after steroid tapering or to fully characterize the durability of the steroid-sparing effect. Fifth, the predefined tapering algorithm may not reflect every physician’s clinical judgment, and the MG-ADL-based worsening threshold (≥ 1 point increase) is sensitive and may conservatively prevent reduction in patients who are in fact clinically stable. To characterize the magnitude of this conservatism, a prespecified sensitivity analysis applying the 2-point minimal clinically important difference will be performed (see “Statistical Analysis”). Sixth, while objective indicators across three steroid toxicity domains (glycemic, skeletal, and cardiovascular) are evaluated longitudinally, this study does not employ a validated composite glucocorticoid toxicity instrument such as the Glucocorticoid Toxicity Index (GTI). Several GTI domains, including neuropsychiatric symptoms and glucocorticoid myopathy, require extensive structured assessments that exceed the resource scope of this single-center investigator-initiated trial. Furthermore, the relatively short 28-week study duration may limit the sensitivity of some toxicity indicators, particularly bone mineral density, which typically evolves over longer timeframes. Incorporation of a formal composite toxicity instrument such as the GTI is a planned objective for future multicenter or longer-term studies of this regimen. Furthermore, the predefined 28-week tapering schedule does not permit complete corticosteroid discontinuation within the study period; evaluation of steroid-free outcomes will require longer-duration studies. These limitations will be addressed by detailed reporting of per-patient trajectories and by clearly contextualizing findings against the design constraints. Accordingly, confirmation of the present findings in larger, multicenter, controlled studies will be required before the proposed steroid-tapering algorithm can be broadly implemented in routine clinical practice.

Discussion

The MG-Efgartigimod study aims to determine whether regularly scheduled administration of efgartigimod can reduce oral PSL dosage in patients with gMG who score ≥ 5 on the MG-ADL scale. The contemporary goal of MG management is to achieve both optimal disease control and minimization of oral PSL exposure. A recent retrospective study of efgartigimod reported a reduction in daily oral PSL dosage by 9.1 mg/day between baseline and week 20 [18]. In the open-label extension of the REGAIN trial evaluating eculizumab (an anti-complement monoclonal antibody), the mean oral PSL dosage decreased by 3.8 mg/day from baseline to the end of the study [22]. However, no prospective trial has comprehensively evaluated the steroid-sparing effect of efgartigimod, and no standardized protocol for tapering oral PSL during molecular-targeted therapy has yet been established. By administering efgartigimod at fixed intervals and tapering PSL according to a predefined protocol when MG symptoms do not worsen, this study will evaluate the feasibility and validity of structured steroid tapering during FcRn antagonist therapy.

The tapering schedule is grounded in the pharmacodynamics described in the ADAPT trial, where clinical symptoms improved most substantially 1 week after the fourth efgartigimod dose and then gradually worsened over the subsequent 5 weeks [16]. Our design attempts to taper PSL during the window of maximal therapeutic effect, when the risk of worsening is lowest.

Another important objective is to examine how efgartigimod efficacy varies by MG subtype. Prior research has suggested that among anti-AChR antibody-positive MG, efgartigimod shows the greatest efficacy in late-onset MG [18]. Efgartigimod has also been reported to be effective in anti-MuSK antibody-positive MG, in which IgG4 subclass antibodies predominate and complement activation is absent [23]. Because this study enrolls patients across the major MG subtypes, it will enable case-by-case comparison of responsiveness and assessment of efficacy within each MG subtype.

The decision to enroll patients across the major MG subtypes, rather than restricting enrollment to anti-AChR antibody-positive cases without thymoma, was made to align with the eligibility criteria of the phase 3 ADAPT trial [16], to address the prespecified secondary objective of comparing efficacy across MG subtypes, and to maintain recruitment feasibility within a single-center sample of 16 patients. To directly examine cohort homogeneity within this broader design, we have additionally prespecified a subgroup analysis comparing patients who are anti-AChR antibody positive, with and without thymoma, motivated by clinical experience suggesting that thymoma-associated MG may show different responsiveness to FcRn-targeted therapy. Subgroup findings will be presented descriptively given the limited power of within-subgroup comparisons.

Serum and plasma samples will be collected at each blood draw throughout the study. Biomarkers reflecting treatment responsiveness to efgartigimod remain poorly characterized. Previous studies have shown that the pathogenic activities of anti-AChR antibodies—including complement activation, receptor internalization, and receptor blockade—vary between patients [24] and fluctuate over time [25]. To date, no study has longitudinally analyzed blood samples from patients with MG treated with efgartigimod to examine correlations between molecular changes and clinical outcomes. Proteomic and immunologic analyses of the collected samples will therefore be performed to identify candidate predictive biomarkers for efgartigimod efficacy. Collectively, these findings are expected to advance personalized medicine approaches as molecular-targeted therapies for MG continue to expand.

Conclusion

The MG-Efgartigimod study is the first prospective clinical trial designed specifically to evaluate the steroid-sparing effect of regularly scheduled efgartigimod administration in patients with gMG. By combining a predefined tapering algorithm with longitudinal biomarker sampling, the trial will clarify both the clinical feasibility of structured PSL reduction and the molecular predictors of therapeutic response. The findings are expected to inform a new maintenance treatment algorithm for refractory gMG and to support the broader implementation of personalized, steroid-sparing strategies in MG management.

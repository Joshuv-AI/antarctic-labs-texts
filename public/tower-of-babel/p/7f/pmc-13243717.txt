The Role of Inflammasome in the Pathogenesis of Alopecia Areata: NLRP3 and Its Cascade Contribute to the Onset and Early Stage of Alopecia Areata
Authors: Ji-Hoon Lim, Da-Hyun Kang, Haena Moon, Soon-Hyo Kwon, Bark-Lynn Lew
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe inflammasome, a multiprotein complex, is crucial in the pathogenesis of various autoinflammatory disorders. In particular, the NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome has recently been associated with diseases such as psoriasis, vitiligo, lupus, and alopecia areata (AA).ObjectiveThe objective of this study is to investigate the potential involvement of the inflammasome in the pathogenesis of AA and to determine its correlation with clinical manifestations.MethodsA total of 144 patients with AA and 22 healthy controls were included. Scalp skin and serum samples were collected to assess the levels of NLRP3-related molecules in lesional tissue and blood, respectively. Additionally, we performed immunostaining to measure the expression of NLRP3 and caspase-1 in the outer root sheath (ORS).ResultsLesional interleukin-1β expression was significantly elevated in patients with AA at all stages compared with controls. In the progressive stage, lesional C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 10 levels were markedly higher than those in controls and other stages. Serum interferon-γ levels were also significantly increased during the progressive stage of AA compared with controls. Immunostaining revealed strong NLRP3 and caspase-1 positivity in the ORS during the initial and progressive stages of AA, in contrast to the recovery stage.ConclusionThese results highlight the functional role of the inflammasomes in the pathogenesis of AA.

INTRODUCTION

Alopecia areata (AA) is an organ-specific autoimmune disorder characterized by varying degrees of hair loss, accompanied by T-cell infiltration and cytokine production around anagen-stage hair follicles12. AA affects all age groups but typically occurs in young adults3. At any given time, approximately 0.2% of the population has AA4.

Inflammasomes are cytoplasmic complexes that regulate innate immunity. They typically consist of a NOD-like receptor (NLR) serving as the sensor, an apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a C-terminal caspase recruitment domain functioning as the adaptor, and caspase-1 acting as the effector5. The NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome is the most extensively characterized inflammasome and plays a pivotal role in both anti-infective immunity and autoimmune diseases. The NLRP3 inflammasome responds to diverse cellular stress stimuli, with calcium influx acting as a proximal activator. Its activation induces caspase-1 cleavage, thereby driving the maturation and release of interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-18, and enhancing T-cell activation and chemotaxis567. Reportedly existing in keratinocytes, the NLRP3 inflammasome is vital to the regulation of cutaneous innate immunity89.

Because of its essential involvement in the regulation of innate immunity, the NLRP3 inflammasome has been proposed to participate in the development of multiple inflammatory skin disorders, including psoriasis, vitiligo, systemic lupus erythematosus, and atopic dermatitis (AD)10.

In AA patients, the NLRP3 inflammasome and its effector cytokine IL-1β are localized within the outer root sheath (ORS) of hair follicles11. Elevated serum IL-18 levels have been observed in individuals with extensive AA relative to healthy controls12, and our previous investigation identified associations between IL-18 single-nucleotide polymorphisms and AA susceptibility in a Korean cohort13.

In this study, we investigated the patterns of the NLRP3 inflammasome and associated molecules in scalp lesions and peripheral blood of patients with AA, analyzing the relationships between outcomes and disease characteristics.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patients and controls

A total of 144 patients diagnosed with AA were enrolled at the Department of Dermatology, Kyung Hee University Hospital at Gang-dong, Seoul, Korea, between January 2015 and June 2021. Diagnosis was confirmed based on physical examination, clinical features, and, when required, skin biopsy. Data on age, sex, disease duration, prior AA history, comorbidities including AD, and family history of AA were systematically collected. The study protocol received approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Kyung Hee University Hospital at Gangdong (2015-01-004, 2020-03-049), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Controls were healthy people who did not have any systemic diseases nor any scalp diseases. They all received full information on the clinical and laboratory settings and the purpose of the study and provided their informed consent.

Evaluation standard

Baseline severity was evaluated using the “Severity of Alopecia Tool” score14. Patients were classified into five groups according to the percentage of scalp hair loss: S1, <25%; S2, 25%–49%; S3, 50%–74%; S4, 75%–99%; and S5, 100%.

AA was stratified into three clinical stages by two dermatologists considering both the duration and extent of the disease. The initial stage was defined as onset within 2 weeks, the progressive stage as a duration longer than 2 weeks accompanied by lesion enlargement, and the recovery stage as evident hair regrowth at the time of visit.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining was graded as represented in Fig. 1: grade 0, no staining; grade 1, sparse staining in the ORS of hair follicles; and grade 2, intense staining in the ORS of hair follicles. The evaluation was performed by two independent observers who were blinded to the subjects’ data.

NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain-containing 3 expression in the skin of patient with alopecia areata. There is sparse staining in (A) which represents grade 1, and intense staining in (B) which represents grade 2.

Therapeutic regrowth of hair was classified using 4-grade criteria (Supplementary Table 1)15. It was evaluated by comparing to the initial state with that at 6 months of follow-up by two dermatologists. Recurrence was defined as the hairless patch significant increasing after almost complete recovery or alopecia more than doubling in size in patients who had previously shown improvement.

On the basis of therapeutic regrowth, we divided the patients into good and poor prognosis groups. We defined good prognosis as >60% hair regrowth or >50% reduction in SALT score and poor prognosis as <10% hair regrowth or aggravation and recurrence.

Experimental procedures

Scalp biopsies from patients with AA were studied, as well as scalp biopsies from healthy controls. Four-mm and two-mm punch biopsies were obtained from the scalp of each participant. The four-mm specimens were used for measuring the expression of NLRP3 and caspase-1 in ORS using IHC staining. Scalp tissue from the patients was processed using a standard methodology. The two-mm specimens were used for measuring the mRNA level of molecules associated with inflammasome using real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction. The relative fold change in mRNA expression was calculated using the 2−△△Ct method. Serum samples from patients and normal controls were collected for measuring serum level of molecules related to inflammasome using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.

Statistical analysis

The Mann–Whitney U and Kruskal–Wallis tests were used to evaluate differences in cytokine levels between patients and controls, and among AA subgroups. Categorical variables were analyzed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate and were expressed as means ± standard deviation. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 20.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and statistical significance was defined as p<0.05.

RESULTS

Lesional molecule mRNA levels in scalp skin

This study included 46 patients with AA and five healthy controls. The mean age of the patients group was 34.6 years while that of the control group was 30.8 years. Twenty-two (47.8%) patients and five (100%) controls were males. The mean disease duration was 21.7 weeks. Regarding clinical subtypes, two patients were in the initial stage, 30 were in the progressive stage, and 14 were in the recovery stage. Nine patients had alopecia totalis (AT), six had alopecia universalis (AU), and three had acute diffuse and total alopecia (ADTA). S1 was the most common severity level. Thirty-seven percent of patients had a history of AA (Supplementary Table 2).

Table 1 shows the mean mRNA levels of lesional molecules in the scalp skin of patients and controls. There was a significant increase in the IL-1β levels of patients compared with those of the controls. In the progressive stage, there was a significant increase in the IL-1β and C-X-C motif chemokine ligand (CXCL) 10 levels of patients compared with those of the control groups (Fig. 2). In the comparison between clinical stages, CXCL10 was significantly higher in the progressive stage compared to levels in the initial and recovery stages (p=0.022, p=0.019, respectively). There were no other differences between patients and controls and between each clinical stage groups in terms of age, disease duration, severity, clinical subtype, nail involvement, past history of AA, family history of AA, and prognosis.

Serum molecule levels in venous blood

This study included 18 patients with AA and 17 healthy controls. The mean age of patients and controls were 32.3 and 48.6 years, respectively. There were eight males in both groups. The mean disease duration was 31.2 weeks. Regarding clinical stages of AA in the patients group, one patient was in the initial stage, seven were in the progressive stage, and 10 were in the recovery stage. Seven patients had AT, AU, or ADTA. Five (27.8%) patients had AD (Supplementary Table 3).

When comparing without distinguishing clinical stages, no significant differences were found in the NLRP3-associated molecular levels in serum between patients and controls. However, in the progressive stage, interferon (IFN)-γ levels were significantly higher in patients than those in controls (Fig. 3). There were no other differences between the patients and controls and between each clinical stage of patients in terms of age, disease duration, severity, clinical subtype, nail involvement, past history of AA, family history of AA, or prognosis.

NLRP3 and caspase-1 expression in ORS

This study includes 93 patients without controls. The mean age of the patients was 35.8, and 41.9% were males. The mean duration was 41.5 weeks. Thirty-three patients were in the initial stage, 44 were in the progressive stage, and 16 were in the recovery stage. The most common clinical subtype was AA (61.3%), with S1 being the most common severity. Forty-point nine percent of the patients had a history of AA, and none had a history of AD (Supplementary Table 4). At 6 months follow-up, 32 patients were classified into the good prognosis group and 33 into the poor prognosis group.

In NLRP 3 staining, 17 samples were intensely stained. Among these, 58.8% were mainly stained in the upper part of the ORS. For caspase-1 staining, 14 samples were intensely stained. Among these, 53.3% were mainly stained in the lower part of the ORS.

When clinical type, severity, clinical stage, prognosis, family history, and AA history were analyzed in relation to the degree of staining, NLRP3 staining was confirmed to be significantly more intense in the initial and progressive stages than in the recovery stage. In the caspase-1 staining, all 14 patients in the of intensely stained group were in the initial and progressive stages; however, this difference was not statistically significant (Table 2).

DISCUSSION

In this study, we examined lesional and serum molecules associated with the NLRP3 inflammasome and found distinct differences between AA patients and healthy controls. Lesional skin IL-1β mRNA levels were significantly elevated in AA patients compared to controls, with the increase being particularly evident during the progressive stage. IL-1β is a key pro-inflammatory cytokine that is actively secreted upon inflammasome activation. IL-1β produced by the NLRP3 inflammasome may influence cytokine production and modulate the immune responses of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells161718. IL-1β additionally promotes the induction of CXCL10 and CXCL16 in keratinocytes by engaging the nuclear factor-κB and IFN regulatory factor 1 signaling cascades19. Accumulating evidence from several studies underscores the importance of the IL-1 family in modulating both the risk and severity of AA202122. Several reports have documented upregulated IL-1β in the lesional skin of AA patients and proposed its contribution to hair follicle cycling14.

Lesional CXCL10 expression was significantly elevated in AA patients during the progressive stage compared with that in healthy controls. A similar trend was observed when comparing patients in the initial and recovery stages with controls. CXCL10 is associated with Th1 immunity and has been suggested to be one of the most relevant chemokine axes in promoting T-cell migration in different autoimmune and inflammatory processes23. As described above, CXCL10 expression can be promoted by IL-1β. Several reports found elevated CXCL10 levels in the skin of patients with AA compared with levels in controls24252627. In contrast, Maouia et al.26 reported that the level of CXCL10 expression in lesional skin was not different from that in healthy skin. However, the aforementioned studies did not classify AA patients according to clinical subtype, and the number of enrolled subjects was smaller than in our cohort. Given the significantly higher CXCL10 levels observed in the progressive stage compared with controls and with patients in the initial or recovery stages, CXCL10 may serve as a sensitive marker reflecting disease progression in AA.

The increase in serum IFN-γ levels during the progressive stage further supports the above findings. IL-1β induced by inflammasome activation enhances IFN-γ production in CD8+ and CD4+ T cells, suggesting a potential mechanistic link between inflammasome activity and T cell–mediated inflammation in AA. IFN-γ induces the expression of MHC I, NKG2D, and chemokines such as CXCL10 and CXCL11, all of which could perpetuate a vicious cycle of inflammation around the hair follicles28. IFN-γ is already widely known to be a major cytokine in the immune response of AA28.

IHC staining of the scalp biopsy specimen obtained from the initial and progressive stages of AA was strongly stained for NLRP3 and caspase-1. In the NLRP3 staining, there was a significant association between stain intensity and disease stage. In the caspase-1 staining, this relationship was not statistically significant (p=0.118). However, in both stains, all intensely stained specimens were from AA in the initial and progressive phases. A previous study has already revealed a significant increase in NLRP3 and caspase-1 in the ORS of human hair follicles of patients with AA compared to those of controls11. However, this study is the first to confirm an association between inflammasome activity and the clinical stage of AA. In addition, among patients with strong expression of NLRP3 or caspase-1, the proportion with a family history of AA was approximately 6%–7%, whereas previous reports have indicated a range of 10%–42%. Although memory recall bias may have contributed to this discrepancy, it should be carefully investigated in future studies.

Previous studies have suggested that the NLRP3 inflammasome contributes to the pathogenesis of AA and that ORS cells serve as immunocompetent cells capable of inducing NLRP3 inflammasome activation1129. Most previous studies were performed using C3H/HeJ mice, whereas our study demonstrated a relationship between inflammasome expression in ORS cells and the initial and progressive stages of AA through IHC analysis of human samples. Furthermore, by confirming the sequential increase in lesional IL-1β, followed by elevated lesional CXCL10 and serum IFN-γ levels, we verified activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome cascade. These findings are consistent in that these changes were most prominent during the progressive stage. Taken together, NLRP3 and caspase-1 expression in ORS cells, along with lesional IL-1β, appear to contribute to the initiation and progression of AA. In particular, lesional CXCL10 seems to be actively expressed during disease progression. Hashimoto et al.29 reported a similar result, finding that lesional IL-1β is highly expressed pre- and post-onset in the lesions of AA-affected mice. Strong NLRP3 and caspase-1 staining in the initial and progressive stages of AA suggests that the inflammasome is involved in AA pathogenesis, particularly at disease onset. Elevated lesional IL-1β and serum IFN-γ levels further support this association. Sequences triggered by NLRP3 inflammasome activation were confirmed to be highly correlated with the initial and progressive stages of AA. This is the first study to demonstrate the relationship between the NLRP3 inflammasome and AA in a human sample in terms of clinical features. Furthermore, an NLRP3-related chemokine, CXCL10, showed the potential to be a sensitive marker of AA disease progression or a therapeutic target. More elaborate human studies investigating the NLRP3 inflammasome and AA are needed. We are currently in the process of applying anti-NLRP3 to a mouse model as a next step in elucidating the relationship between AA and the NLRP3 inflammasome.

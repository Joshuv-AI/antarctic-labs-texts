An instance-aware segmentation and optical flow based DVI-SLAM for dynamic environments
Authors: Yemineni Ashok, Mukesh Kumar Rohil
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSimultaneous Localisation and Mapping (SLAM) is a fundamental building block for markerless Augmented Reality systems. However, most conventional SLAM systems exhibit poor performance in real-time environments because of the influence of dynamic objects in unstructured surroundings. We are proposing a new Dynamic Visual Inertial SLAM system called DVI-SLAM to address the challenges of dynamic content presented in complex urban scenes. Leveraging instance-aware segmentation and optical flow methods, our system can detect and exclude actively moving objects from the tracking process. In addition to excluding features of moving objects from the pose estimation process, the system also detects, tracks, and generates static-map of the surrounding environment. This helps develop a robust AR system or mobile robot system to effectively handle complex outdoor urban scene scenarios. The proposed DVI-SLAM framework was tested on the TUM RGB-D and TUM-VI datasets as well as in real-world environments. The results showed an improvement in ATE’s Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) up to 24.80 and 32.34% compared with ORB-SLAM3, on monocular-inertial and stereo-inertial sequences of the TUM-VI dataset, respectively. Similarly, an improvement up to 64.89 and 73.75% was achieved for real-time monocular and RGB-D inertial sequences of real-time environments. Further, the sequence with a lot of dynamic content, such as the TUM-RGBD dynamic dataset, showed up to 99.4% improvement in ATE RMSE. The results demonstrated that DVI-SLAM significantly enhances Absolute Trajectory Error accuracy as compared to the state-of-the-art ORB-SLAM3 method. Extensive experiments evaluation shows that DVI-SLAM performs robustly across both static and dynamic scenarios, while maintaining competitive performance in static and low-dynamic scenes, it delivers significant improvements in highly dynamic environments.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In Augmented Reality (AR) systems, accurate registration of virtual content with respect to the physical environment is a fundamental requirement for achieving stability and immersive user experience. This registration process depends critically on the precise estimation of the six degrees-of-freedom (6-DoF) pose of the user’s head or the AR device’s camera. Contemporary markerless AR systems predominantly rely on Simultaneous Localisation and Mapping (SLAM) algorithms to continuously estimate camera pose while incrementally reconstructing a representation of the surrounding environment in real time1,2.

SLAM frameworks perform pose estimation by localising the current frame within a previously constructed map. This is typically achieved through feature extraction and matching between the incoming image frame and stored key frames or map points. The robustness and accuracy of pose estimation are therefore highly dependent on the quality and geometric consistency of feature correspondences. However, the presence of dynamic objects—such as moving pedestrians or vehicles—introduces non-static feature observations that violate the static-world assumption underlying most SLAM formulations. Features associated with dynamic entities may be incorrectly incorporated into the data association process, resulting in inconsistent correspondences across frames. Consequently, these dynamic features can be mistakenly interpreted as valid tracking measurements, leading to erroneous pose updates. This degrades localisation accuracy, increases estimation uncertainty, and may cause drift in the reconstructed map. In AR applications, such drift manifests as virtual content misalignment, where virtual objects appear to move with dynamic scene elements rather than remaining anchored to the static environment. Similarly, in mobile robotics, the reconstructed map may incorrectly evolve along the trajectories of dynamic objects rather than reflecting the true motion of the robot3.

In recent years, significant advancements have been achieved in the Simultaneous Localisation and Mapping (SLAM) and deep learning-based semantic aware object segmentation fields. As an advanced area of research, numerous visual-inertial SLAM systems have been developed, achieving localisation precision within a few centimetres and allowing for the real-time creation of real-time large-scale 3D maps. State-of-the-art visual-inertial SLAM techniques can achieve impressive tracking and localisation results in indoor scenes. However, these methods rely on specific assumptions, such as static environments, smooth camera motion, and efficient scene geometry or texture for reliable tracking.

Over the past few decades, the visual-inertial SLAM problem has attracted significant attention from researchers, resulting in the emergence of numerous excellent vi-SLAM systems, such as VI-DSO5, VINS-Mono6, VINS-RGBD7, VIORB8, OKVIS9, ORBSLAM34 etc. These outstanding works can achieve satisfactory performance in static environments or dynamic elements that are assumed to be static content and can be classified as outliers. Because of static world assumptions, the accuracy of the aforementioned standard SLAM systems is so greatly reduced that the results can become entirely unreliable in highly dynamic environments. Recently, several SLAM systems—such as DS-SLAM10, Detect-SLAM11, MaskFusion12, Dynamic-SLAM13, SEG-SLAM14, DI-SLAM15, and NGD-SLAM16—have been proposed to effectively handle dynamic scene content. However, these approaches rely heavily on semantic segmentation techniques to detect and remove the dynamic objects and have limited geometric verification. Misclassification in semantic segmentation or noisy optical flow estimation can lead to incorrect feature rejection or retention, which directly affects pose estimation accuracy. In contrast, our proposed DVI-SLAM integrates semantic segmentation, optical flow–based motion analysis, and GMS-based geometric consistency filtering in a unified framework. This multi-level validation strategy improves the reliability of dynamic feature rejection and enhances pose estimation robustness in complex real-world dynamic scenes.

In this work, we concentrate on the presumption that the scene is static. With the static scene presumption, the SLAM system faces significant positioning errors or tracking failures when dealing with dynamic scenes without discarding the moving content from the scenes. Achieving robust tracking and mapping performance with dynamic content in the scene remains an unresolved challenge. Semantic segmentation

Semantic segmentation aims to partition an image into separate and non-overlapping segments by pixel-wise labelling, each representing a meaningful region within the original images. In recent years, there have been noticeable improvements in the accuracy of semantic segmentation methods, primarily driven by the resurgence of lightweight neural networks. An example includes recent MobileNet17–19 works, which augment the traditional large number of network parameters and high computational complexity per-pixel classification neural networks with lightweight depth-wise convolution and attention mechanism configurations. While the former focuses on adapting the deeper or wider networks, such as VGG20, ReNet21, and ResNet22 to deliver better performance, the latter focuses on (1) fewer parameters, (2) more reuse of features and (3) better memory efficiency by of training. VGG20 and ResNet22 networks are incorporated as underlying networks in SegNet23 and MaskRCNN24 semantic segmentation models.

SegNet23 is an encoder-decoder structured convolution neural network introduced in 2017. The important advantage and aspect of Segnet over the other methods is its utilization of direct information rather than convolution. SegNet’s encoder network adopts the VGG deep architecture, followed by a decoder network25 for pixel-wise classification. SegNet was among the top choices for addressing image segmentation problems, particularly for scene segmentation tasks.

Object detection

Object detection models provide vital information, such as object bounding boxes and categories in the scene and serve as the foundation for various other computer vision tasks, such as instance segmentation, object tracking, image captioning, etc. Works such as YOLO26, CenterNet27, CornerNet28, Retina-Net29, DETR30, etc., are one-stage detector models; they can detect all the objects without any refinement module and with a single forward inference. However, their efficiency declines significantly when tasked with densely populated and small objects. The two-stage detectors, such as RCNN31, SPPNet32, FastRCNN33, FasterRCNN34, FPN35, etc., have recall ability to attain high precision accuracy. However, their poor speed and heavy computation costs significantly affect the potential use of their deployment real-time applications.

You Only Look Once (YOLO)26 is the first one-stage object detection neural network, introduced in 2015. This network segments the full image into regions and concurrently predicts bounding boxes and probabilities of each region. YOLO26 achieves significant improvement in detection speed with a single-stage inference process. The recently enhanced version of YOLO26 runs at 45fps with VOCO736 mAP=63.4%. However, YOLO26 delivers a lower localization accuracy than two-stage networks, especially when dealing with small objects in the scene.

Single-Shot Multibox Detector (SSD)37: SSD37 differs from the earlier detector in its approach to detecting objects of different scales across multiple layers of the network, whereas the previous one-stage detector only focused on detection at their top layers. It helps provide better accuracy levels by introducing multi-references and multi-resolution techniques. SSD37 delivers benefits in detection speed and accuracy and achieves mAP.5= 46.5% on the COCO dataset38, with a faster version operating at 59 frames per second.

FastRCNN33 enables the simultaneous training of the detector and bounding box using the same network configurations. FastRCNN33 delivers improvement in mAP to 70.0%. However, FastRCNN33 detection speed is still restricted by the proposal detection phase. Hence, its further version of FasterRCNN34 was introduced to deliver better detection speed.

FaserRCNN34 introduces a region proposal network (RPN) in the object detection process, which allows almost cost-free region proposals. FasterRCNN34 resolves the speed limitation of FastRCNN33 and delivers nearly real-time detection capability. It achieves the mAP=73.2% on the VOC07 dataset36. However, redundancy still persists in the detection stage. To address this, several enhancements have been suggested, including RFCN39 and light head RCNN40, etc.

Recent lightweight convolutional neural networks (CNNs) such as41–44, mainly focus on designing lightweight deep convolutional neural networks for specific detection and recognition tasks, such as vehicle detection in aerial images, instrument reading, traffic object detection, and biometric recognition. Their primary contribution is to improve computational efficiency, enhance feature extraction capability, and achieve higher classification accuracy within clearly defined perception tasks. However, these approaches are not directly aligned with the objectives of dynamic SLAM systems. Dynamic SLAM requires close integration of perception with motion estimation, geometric consistency verification, and pose optimization. Therefore, while these methods are efficient for detection purposes, they do not address the broader requirements of robust localization and mapping in dynamic environments.

Instance-aware semantic segmentation

Hariharan et al.63 initially described instance-aware semantic segmentation as simultaneous object detection and segmentation. In this work, he introduced the R-CNN framework to extend bounding box object detections with segmented object identification and masking. Furthermore, their method requires multiple separate CNN passes during inference, leading to significant computational overheads and making it impractical for real-time operation. The work of Dai et al.64 addresses the instance-aware semantic segmentation as a three-layer cascaded network structure, generates box proposals, refines the proposals into pixel-level instance masks, and categorizes them with a semantic label. Due to this integrated network, their approaches can execute the instance aware segmentation task approximately two orders of magnitude quicker compared to prior methods like63. Mask RCNN24 is the state-of-the-art pixel-wise instance, aware semantic segmentation model, furthers the Faster R-CNN with additional object’s class id and mask prediction branch. As mentioned earlier, Faster R-CNN follows a two-stage process; it first proposes a region of interest and then simultaneously predicts the object class and bounding for each region. Both stages utilize a ResNet-based backbone network and employ convolution layers for inference for feature maps.

Co-Fusion45 introduces a dense RGB-D SLAM system that can reconstruct the 3D model of static and dynamic surroundings by individually tracking every object’s geometry using their motion or semantic cues. The Co-Fusion’s scene description at the object level enables the trajectory’s estimation at the object level. MaskFusion12 is also an RGB-D SLAM that incorporates the geometry of multiple objects for reconstruction and tracking of the dynamic objects. It follows ElasticFusion65 based sufel representation for tracking the dynamic objects and constructing the map of static elements of the scene. CubeSLAM47 is a novel monocular object SLAM system that can work without prior object information in both static and dynamic scenes. This SLAM system effectively utilizes object information in the form of 3D cuboids to provide geometry and scale information in the optimization process and to calculate the depth information in a situation where the triangulation process is challenging. Here, the pose estimation involves the calibration of 9DoF of cuboid parameters. This 9DoF includes 6DOF of poses estimates and dimensions of cuboid objects . DOT66 is an ORB-SLAM2 based SLAM system that performs dynamic object tracking with direct methods. The initialization of the SLAM system in DOT will accomplished with the help of static content of the scene.

The DS-SLAM10 kind approach attempts to make the system more robust in dynamic environments by discarding the moving part from the pose estimation process. DS-SLAM10 incorporates the classical SLAM method ORB-SLAM2 and segnet semantic segmentation method and constructs the dense 3D octo tree semantic map to exhibit the robot’s navigation task. StaticFusion49 also proposes a similar dense RGB-D SLAM system that leverages the static parts of the scene to estimate camera motion and 3D model of the scene. This model uses Elastic Fusion67 based surfel representation in camera tracking. The Detect-SLAM68 SLAM system also considers a static map of the scene. However, this method constructs a semantic map with objects from the static scene. DynaSLAM49 is a monocular, stereo, and RGB-D SLAM system that employs the ORB-SLAM2 on a synthesized static scene environment for performing tracking of the camera. The algorithm extracts the dynamic pixels with the help of multi-view geometry or deep learning. After extracting the dynamic scene, it repaints the removed dynamic pixel space with static information from previous frames. SOF-SLAM52 is also a similar kind of RGB-D SLAM system that removes the truly dynamic feature from the scene with SegNet. The remaining rigid features are used in camera pose estimation. The SOF-SLAM effectively improves 96.73% accuracy in dynamic scenarios. The authors Wen etc.69 proposed an RGB-D dynamic SLAM system that utilizes the mask R-CNN to separate dynamic and static points of the scene. The authors construct a semantic map with objects labelled in information using octree-based structures. Crowd-SLAM55 can be configured on monocular, stereo, and RGB-D cameras and can work in a crowded environment by tracking static scene content. It removes dynamically moving people from the scene with the help of the MaskRCNN algorithm. Peng su et al.70 eliminate the influence of dynamic objects on camera tracking by deploying the lightweight neural network model YOLOv5. The extracted static features are utilized to obtain pose estimates with optimal homography matrix.

DynaSLAM II54 is a stereo and RGB-D camera configured vSLAM system that optimises the scene’s dynamic and static structures for trajectory construction. The authors employ the bundle adjustment’s reprojection error for joint optimization of camera poses, dynamic content, and static content. It calibrates the dimension of the object in addition to the 6DoF poses of the dynamic objects. AirDOS56 is a stereo SLAM which improves camera tracking by employing dynamic articulated objects. The authors used both rigidity and motion constraints to model articulated objects of the scene.

Existing dynamic SLAM systems address scene dynamics with different assumptions and limitations. Early methods such as Co-Fusion45, DS-SLAM10, MaskFusion12, and CubeSLAM47 mainly rely on RGB-D sensors and semantic segmentation. This limits their use in monocular or outdoor environments and increases computational cost. StaticFusion and DynaSLAM use Mask R-CNN to remove dynamic objects, but they depend strongly on segmentation accuracy. Misclassification can lead to incorrect feature removal and degraded pose estimation. DMS-SLAM51, Dynamic-SLAM13, SOF-SLAM52, and DM-SLAM53 introduce motion cues; however, they lack strong geometric verification, which may result in inconsistent feature filtering.

More recent methods such as Dyna-SLAM254, Crowd-SLAM55, AirDOS56, SEG-SLAM14, NGD-SLAM16, VAR-SLAM59, and IL-SLAM61 often adopt lightweight detectors such as YOLO to reduce computational overhead and improve real-time performance. While YOLO-based detection improves efficiency, these approaches still rely primarily on object-level semantic bounding boxes for dynamic filtering. Bounding-box–level suppression may not precisely capture object boundaries, especially under partial occlusion, articulated motion, or non-rigid deformation. As a result, dynamic features may be either insufficiently removed or excessively discarded, affecting pose accuracy.

In contrast, our approach does not rely solely on semantic detection. We combine instance-level segmentation (Mask R-CNN) with optical flow–based motion consistency analysis and GMS geometric verification. This multi-stage validation framework reduces false dynamic classification, preserves valid static features within detected objects, and improves robustness to non-rigid and partially dynamic scenes.

In addition to traditional semantic segmentation and motion-based dynamic SLAM approaches, recent computer vision literature has produced several cutting-edge works focused on robust tracking under challenging conditions. For example, FMTrack71 proposes a multi-expert fusion strategy that combines RGB and thermal features through frequency-aware modules to enhance long-term object tracking. Similarly, Target-Distractor Aware UAV Tracking via Global Agent72 focuses on discriminative target tracking in aerial scenarios by modelling target–distractor interactions. While these methods achieve notable robustness for their respective tasks, their primary objective remains object tracking and discrimination, not 6-DoF pose estimation or full SLAM. Moreover, neither of them addresses the challenges of maintaining a consistent global map, rejecting dynamic features during pose estimation, or enforcing geometric consistency in camera localisation.

Furthermore, recently some computer vision models like DenserNet73, FPV-NeRF74 has shown signficatnt imapct in the localization field. DenserNet73 is primarily designed for large-scale city-level visual localization, where the main objective is place recognition under significant appearance variations such as seasonal and lighting changes. It focuses on extracting stable building-level features and suppressing dynamic objects through attention filtering, but it is not intended for continuous real-time pose tracking in dynamic environments. FPV-NeRF74 follows a different direction by learning a continuous radiance field representation for high-fidelity 3D reconstruction and viewpoint synthesis, mainly suited for aerial and large-scale environments, rather than lightweight real-time SLAM. In contrast, the proposed DVI-SLAM method is specifically optimized for robust real-time camera tracking in highly dynamic indoor and outdoor scenarios by jointly integrating geometric consistency, and multi-stage dynamic filtering.

In this work, we build on ORB-SLAM34 and extend it to dynamic SLAM. We propose a novel dynamic visual inertial SLAM system that properly takes into account static visual content and inertial measurements to control the degradation of trajectory errors. All the other dynamic SLAM systems discussed above and Table 1 exhibit lower accuracy than ORB-SLAM34 in static scene environments. However, ORB-SLAM3 performance degrades in the presence of dynamic elements, where moving objects introduce feature mismatch and increase pose estimation drift. The proposed DVI-SLAM, together with ORB-SLAM3 and dynamic feature elimination methods, significantly improves the tracking accuracy.

DVI-SLAM: the proposed method

This section provides a brief introduction to the DVI-SLAM system architecture (Fig. 2) and its technical overview. It begins with an overview of the system framework and describes each fundamental method presented in the proposed system.

System Design: coloured in reddish-orange corresponds to newly added elements within the ORB-SLAM3 backbone.

Accurate pose estimates and robustness in challenging environments are essential factors for assessing Augmented Reality applicability in real-world scenarios. State-of-the-art ORB-SLAM34 demonstrates excellent performance in a wide range of practical scenarios. Hence, ORB-SLAM3 is utilised as the fundamental backbone for implementing our feature-based dynamic SLAM, known as DVI-SLAM. Inspired by the51 works, to provide reliable and robust feature extraction and matching even in complex scenes, we have employed Grid-based motion statistics (GMS)75 in our SLAM model. Similar to ORB-SLAM34, our system is capable of conducting visual and visual-inertial SLAMS across monocular, stereo, and RGB-D cameras, supporting both pinhole and fisheye lens models. Furthermore, To perform robust pose tracking even in dynamic scenes by integrating dynamic object feature detection and segmentation that is guided by the MASK-R-CNN instance aware semantic segmentation method.

As illustrated in Fig. 2, the DVI-SLAM system comprises six modules: Tracking, dynamic content detection, local mapping, loop closing, map merging, and full BA. The sensor input is simultaneously processed by the tracking thread and semantic segmentation thread. The tracking thread begins with extracting GMS75, then roughly assesses their movement consistency and identifies potential outliers. Subsequently, the tracking thread pauses until it receives the image with pixel-wise semantic labels from the dynamic content detection module. Upon receiving the object detection and segmentation and dynamic object segmentation results, the GMS features identified as outliers within moving objects will be discarded, along with any previously detected potential outliers. After that, the transformation matrix is obtained by aligning the remaining stable feature points.

ORB-SLAM3

The ORB-SLAM3 algorithm is based on ORB-SLAM2 and is a state-of-the-art visual, visual-inertial and multimap system with the capabilities of performing SLAM tasks on all kinds of sensors such as monocular, stereo, and RGB-D cameras. The tracking would be performed by matching ORB correspondences and minimising the reprojection error. The tracking system process proceeds like this: First, in parallel, both fundamental and homography in would be computed. Among them, the better one would be chosen based on their assessment using asymmetric transfer error. If the chosen model leads to insufficient tracking quality or lacks adequate feature correspondences in the subsequent frame, the initialisation process is aborted, prompting the system to begin a new different pair of keyframes. ORB-SLAM3 also introduces a new initialisation method that employs the Maximum-a-Posteriori algorithm separately for visual and inertial estimations, which are then optimised jointly. ORB-SLAM3 utilises a multimap representation known as Atlas, which consists of an active map used by tracking thread and a non-active map employed for localization and place recognition. SLAM system incorporates the latest and most advanced hardware and algorithmic algorithms such as ORB feature for key points extraction, bag-of-words for loop closure and place recognisation, statistical model selection, parallel computing, bundle adjustment, and graph optimisation. However, ORB-SLAM3 faces difficulties while processing the outdoor sequences and produces low tracking accuracy76.

Feature matching

We integrate the GMS feature-matching algorithm to enhance the original ORB-SLAM3 system. The GMS algorithm utilizes Brute Force (BF) matching in OpenCV to perform feature matching between two image frames, initially identifying feature-matching point pairs. These initial matches are then refined by the grid-based motion statistics model, which filters out incorrect matches to yield accurate feature correspondences. Traditional feature-matching techniques, such as SIFT, SURF, or ORB, identify key points in images and match them based on descriptor similarity. However, these methods often produce a large number of false matches, especially in complex scenes with repetitive patterns, varying illumination, or significant motion. GMS feature-matching algorithm overcomes these difficulties and generates robust true matches even in complex scenes. However, when dealing with the high frame rate of the camera, dynamic objects tend to move only slightly between consecutive frames, making it difficult to fully mitigate their impact on the feature-matching process. Hence, we use semantic segmentation and optical flow methods to further address and detect the dynamic points. Object segmentation using mask R-CNN

In this work, we utilise the pixel-wise instance aware semantic segmentation model Mask R-CNN to detect dynamic objects; the generated labels could be valuable for tracking moving objects in further modules. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the Mask R-CNN takes RGB image as the input. The network was trained on the Microsoft COCO dataset38, which can perform segmentation work across 80 different classes. Among these classes, we selected 20 as potentially movable objects likely to be found in the real-time scenes, such as persons, birds, sheep, cows, dogs, cats, elephants, horses, boats, trains, trucks, bicycles, buses, aeroplanes, motorcycles, and cars, which covers most of the realtime dynamic objects.

The network also generates a binary mask for each output channel . The collection of all these masks together defines the segmentation of all dynamic objects within the scene’s image, as shown in Fig. 3b, g. Feature points located within these areas of potentially dynamic points are considered unreliable. These points are further verified using motion consistency as discussed in the next Sect. "Dynamic key point detection" , to classify whether they are moving or not in the current scene before being considered as outliers and discarded. For example, if a vehicle is detected, the feature points within the segmented mask of that object are analysed using motion consistency as in the next Sect. "Dynamic key point detection". If the points exhibit independent motion, then all the feature points present inside the object mask are discarded from the subsequent pose estimation process. However, if the observed motion is consistent with the estimated camera motion, the object is reclassified as static, and its feature points are retained for further tracking and pose computation.

Since semantic segmentation has limitations in detecting objects that are not included in the 80 categories of the COCO dataset (on which Mask R-CNN is trained), it may fail to identify certain dynamic objects present in the scene. Dynamic object classfication

A object in the scene is classified as dynamic object with help of (1) set of feature points already identified as dynamic through the Optical flow Estimation process as in the previous section and (2) identified as a potentially dynamic object in the ”Object Segmentation using mask R-CNN”. An object instance O is classified as dynamic if it satisfies either of the following two conditions. (1) If the semantic label L of the object belongs to a predefined high-mobility category such as humans and animals, the object is directly considered dynamic, irrespective of the number of dynamic feature points. This is because such objects are naturally expected to move independently in most real-world scenes. (2) If the semantic label L belongs to a movable category (such as chair, car, etc.), the object is classified as dynamic only when a sufficient number of feature points inside its mask belong to the dynamic point set . Outliers removal

Once the images have been processed by the dynamic content detector, key points associated with moving objects are detected. The dynamic keypoint removal algorithm proceeds: The DVI-SLAM utilises the semantic segmentation network to detect the complete object outlines. The motion keypoint detection methods face difficulties in extracting the contour of moving objects because of the object’s flexible deformation and complex motion nature. In this case, employing a semantic segmentation network enables the detection of the complete contour dynamic objects with reduced computational demand. Our approach is to combine semantic segmentation results with motion feature detection to establish a comprehensive two-tier semantic knowledge framework: (1) whether the object is moving or stationary and (2) If a certain number of dynamic points identified by motion feature detection fall within the contours of a segmented object, then the object is classified as moving. If a segmented object is detected as moving, all key points within its contour are classified as outliers and discarded. In this way, outliers can be eliminated precisely. If the people or vehicles are detected from the object detection and segmentation module, then use the motion feature detection results to determine whether they are moving or not. In case they are categorised as stationary objects, predict the pose directly. If they are categorised as moving, remove all GMS feature points within their outline before performing the pose estimation process. This method significantly reduces the negative influence of dynamic objects on the tracking procedure.

In the outdoor scenarios, vehicles and human activities significantly interfere with the Augmented Reality workflow. After receiving the object detection and segmentation result, if no people or vehicles are detected, all GMS feature matches of the last frame are considered for pose estimation.

Run-time analysis

All the SLAM pipeline and data preprocessing experiments were conducted on a CPU-based setup using an Intel Core i7-13700K processor (13th generation) with a minimum running speed of 5.40 GHz and 32GB of DDR5 RAM. For the Mask RCNN segmentation module, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 graphics processing unit (GPU) with 8 GB of GDDR6 VRAM was utilised. The SLAM work primarily runs on the CPU, while the GPU is used to accelerate the neural network inference required for semantic segmentation. The experiments were conducted on a 64-bit Ubuntu 20.04 operating system with CUDA-enabled GPU support.

On this hardware configuration, the proposed method achieves an average performance of 31 FPS. For comparison, ORB-SLAM3 runs at an average of 0.01352 seconds per frame on the same system. The average processing time per frame of the proposed DVI-SLAM method is 0.0324 seconds. Therefore, the ratio between DVI-SLAM tracking time and ORB-SLAM3 is approximately 2.39. This indicates that the additional computational cost mainly arises from the dynamic content processing modules integrated into our framework. However, this increase in processing time is justified by the significant improvement in robustness and accuracy in highly dynamic environments.

The SLAM component of the framework is relatively lightweight and can be executed on any modern embedded processor commonly used in robotic systems. The semantic segmentation module based on Mask R-CNN is the most computationally demanding part of the system, which is why GPU acceleration was employed in the current experimental setup. However, recent embedded AI platforms equipped with integrated GPUs, such as NVIDIA Jetson family, provide sufficient computational capability to support similar deep learning workloads. Furthermore, techniques such as model pruning, quantisation, and optimised inference frameworks can be adopted to reduce computational overhead and improve inference speed. With these hardware configurations and optimisation strategies, the proposed SLAM can be adopted for deployment on embedded robotic platforms while maintaining near real-time performance.

Conclusions

In this work, we have introduced a real-time Dynamic Visual Inertial SLAM (DVI-SLAM) system that can reduce the impact of dynamic objects on pose estimation. Initially, optical flow methods are applied to detect dynamic content in the scene. After that, a maskRCNN semantic segmentation method is employed to detect exact outliers of the dynamic content. Then, the GMS feature is applied to the remaining static content of the scene. This helps in the robust identification of dynamic points. The proposed system performance against dynamic moving content is assessed using challenging dynamic sequences from the TUM RGB visual inertial dataset. Furthermore, the system was tested in complex real-time situations to assess its practicality. The results demonstrated that DVI-SLAM shows significant improvement compared with ORB-SLAM3 in terms of Root Mean Square Error in ATE. Additionally, the proposed system provides more robust outcomes, such as low ATE values, in various real-time complex scenarios than the ORB-SLAM3 model, demonstrating its adaptability to real-time applications.

Although DVI-SLAM demonstrates improved accuracy in dynamic environments, it still has certain limitations. First, the system faces challenges under low illumination conditions, where poor lighting affects reliable feature extraction and matching. Second, motion blur can significantly impact performance. In AR applications, the camera often undergoes rapid movements due to sudden body motions while moving in outdoor environments. Such quick and unpredictable camera rotations may introduce severe motion blur, which degrades optical flow estimation and feature tracking. As a result, there is a possibility of temporary tracking loss or sudden system failure in highly dynamic situations. Addressing these issues will be an important direction for improving the robustness and stability of the system in real-world deployments.

In the current implementation, we employ Mask R-CNN for instance segmentation, which provides accurate object masks but introduces relatively high computational overhead. So, developing a more efficient and fully real-time version of DVI-SLAM by incorporating lightweight and faster segmentation networks without compromising accuracy will be a good research direction. In future work, we aim to address these limitations and further strengthen our approach. Our objective is to develop a more efficient, robust, and fully real-time dynamic SLAM system that can deliver accurate results even in challenging real-world conditions such as low illumination, motion blur, and rapid camera movements. We also plan to explore advanced feature extraction and enhancement techniques to improve system stability in vulnerable scenarios. Additionally, we intend to design a SLAM framework that not only considers feature locations within an image but also incorporates structural understanding of the scene. By leveraging structural and semantic information together, the system can achieve better map consistency and improved long-term localisation performance in complex environments.

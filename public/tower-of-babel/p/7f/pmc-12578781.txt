Systemic davunetide provides sex-specific neuroprotection during Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG)
Authors: Illana Gozes, Jason Blatt, Liri S Guz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCoronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is a surgical procedure commonly employed to treat severe coronary artery disease by improving blood flow to the heart. Despite its effectiveness, CABG is an open-heart surgery associated with potential complications, including cognitive decline, a currently unmet need. Discovered in our laboratory, activity-dependent neuroprotective protein (ADNP) is essential for brain formation as well as heart development and embryogenesis and the ADNP-derived investigational drug, davunetide, ameliorates partly ADNP-linked deficiencies. Specifically, davunetide was found to be efficacious in a rat pre-clinical model of ischemic stroke, a potential unwanted side effect of CABG, which may lead to a neurodegenerative process, culminating in cognitive dysfunction. Here, leveraging acute administration of intravenous davunetide (AL-208, ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT00404014), we implicated, for the first time, a differential drug metabolism/bioavailability between men and women, with a potential dose-dependent accumulation in men and an opposite decline/no concentration change in women over time. These findings, while requiring caution due to the small cohort size, were linked with an apparent protection against brain cell death/disease progression by davunetide (AL-208) neuroprotective effects (neuron-specific enolase – NSE blood content). In men, also for the first time under these circumstances, neuroprotection was coupled to cognitive function increases in the treated group versus placebo, reaching healthy control levels in the Verbal Paired Associates II test, emphasizing the importance of sex-specific brain medicine.Subject terms: Diseases, Biomarkers

Introduction

Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) is a surgical procedure commonly employed to treat severe coronary artery disease by improving blood flow to the heart. Despite its effectiveness, CABG is an open-heart surgery associated with potential complications, including cognitive decline [1]. This phenomenon is often referred to as “postoperative cognitive dysfunction” or POCD [2]. Additionally, extracorporeal circulation is a non-benign intervention used routinely in CABG surgery that is associated with several adverse reactions primarily related to systemic inflammatory responses [3]. Thus, there are many postoperative myocardial, renal and neurological dysfunctions associated with this surgery [4]. Focusing on cognition, a recent review, analyzing 23 studies with a total of 2,620 patients, concluded that future research should be performed to assess strategies to reduce cognitive impairment post-CABG [5]. To address this, researchers have explored the potential neuroprotective effects of various agents during and after CABG. However, to date there are no products approved for neuroprotection for this condition.

As Torres and Ishida point out in their 2015 article, few pharmacological and interventional options are available to effectively protect the brain and spinal cord postoperatively, with cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) drainage postop being the only known neuroprotective intervention, though its impacts are far from perfect [6]. Evidence has been seen that there may be some postoperative neuroprotective benefits to such agents as dexmedetomidine [7], with its ability to minimize postoperative delirium, lidocaine, with its unique ability to sustain long-term cognitive recovery [8], magnesium [9], able to stabilize neurons under ischemic conditions, and erythropoietin [10], being an anti-inflammatory and antioxidant agent that can reduce ischemic damage, specifically in preventing strokes and cognitive dysfunction, but to date the findings are both unpromising and conflicting [7, 9, 11]. As mentioned earlier, with cognitive dysfunction being a prominent outcome of CABG surgery, with subjects scoring 0.5 to 1 standard deviation below preoperative norms and reports of 53% subject dysfunction at discharge and 42% five years later, finding an effective intervention, despite its challenges, is vital [6].

Of further interest, preventing postoperative delirium, is the recent study with gastrodin [12]. As previously introduced gastrodin (4-hydroxybenzyl alcohol-4-O-β-D-glucopyranoside) mitigates neuroinflammation and oxidative stress, which are the foundations in the pathophysiology of delirium. In the recent study, participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive gastrodin infusion 600 mg twice daily or placebo from the day of surgery until the postoperative day 6. The incidence of postoperative delirium was 19.5% in the gastrodin group and 35.9% in the placebo group, with a significant relative risk of 0.54 (95% CI 0.32–0.93, p = 0.022). The authors conclude that larger studies are needed to confirm gastrodin efficacy in preventing delirium, highlighting its potential role in anti-inflammatory cognitive neuroprotection.

Our laboratory research led to the discovery and characterization of activity-dependent neuroprotective protein (ADNP) [13, 14], which regulates cognitive functions [15]. Thus, ADNP is essential for brain formation and function [13, 16] as well as heart development [17]. Dysregulation has been implicated in psychiatric diseases such as schizophrenia [18], chronic neurodegenerations, such as Alzheimer’s disease (AD) [19], and neurodevelopmental syndromes, such as the ADNP syndrome (also known as the Helsmoortel Van Der Aa syndrome) including converging mechanisms with the Phelan McDermid syndrome, suggesting a mechanism underlying autism and intellectual disabilities [20]. The neuroprotective active site in ADNP, i.e., the investigational drug davunetide (also known as NAP [13] and CP201 [21]) has been clinically evaluated for neuroprotection in several chronic brain diseases associated with impaired neuronal function as well as neurodegeneration [21]. The mechanism of action of davunetide involves cytoskeletal [22] as well as autophagy protection that is aberrant in schizophrenia and in neurodegenerative diseases [23, 24]. In all previous clinical studies of chronic brain disease, including daily or twice daily davunetide intranasal application (also known as AL-108), neuroprotection was implicated. In detail, a small (double blind, placebo controlled, ascending dose, 63 patients) Phase 2 clinical study in schizophrenia showed protection of functional activities of daily living [25], which seemed to include differential sex-dependent dose effects (Patent application international publication number - WO 2023/112025 A1). Furthermore, behavioral results were complemented by potential neuroprotection as measured by N-acetyltransferase, a marker for neuronal integrity visualized by magnetic resonance spectroscopy [26]. Two additional clinical studies with hundreds rather than dozens of patients, allowed for a clear separation by sex, and indicated a faster disease progression of activities of daily living coupled with neurodegeneration (increases in brain ventricular volumes) in women suffering from the chronic pure tauopathy progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP) compared to men, which was significantly compensated by twice daily intranasal davunetide (also known as AL-108) treatment for 52 weeks [27]. A further clinical trial in patients suffering from amnestic mild cognitive impairment (prodromal to AD) indicated that twice daily intranasal application of davunetide over three months preferentially boosted verbal memory in women and spatial memory in men [28]. In a test of semantic working memory and attention, women showed a significant low-dose placebo effect, resulting in a high dose significant davunetide improvement over the matched placebo. Correlating anxiety with cognition showed sex-opposing results, with women depicting significant anxiety correlations with delayed matching to sample [28]. Mechanistically, we correlated these findings with testosterone inhibition of GSK3β, by activation of the AKT pathway [29], similar to davunetide [30, 31], which additionally enhances Tau-microtubule interactions [19].

Our previous preclinical experiments showed efficacy for davunetide in acute injury coupling neuroprotection (morphometric evaluations [32] magnetic resonance imaging [33], assessment of apoptosis [34]) to clinical outcomes in rodent models of retinal laser-induced retinal damage [32] head trauma (closed head injury) [33] as well as permanent middle cerebral artery occlusion/stroke (ischemic apoptotic death) [34]. Notably, these previous studies were performed on males only. However, our chronic application clinical studies (see above) [27, 28] as well as our preclinical studies in chronic rodent disease models (e.g. the ADNP syndrome [31, 35–38] and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis [39]) strongly implicate sex differences in brain as well as neuromuscular disease neurodevelopment/neurodegeneration rates and patterns, highlighting the level of axonal transport affected by microtubule dynamics [35], a major ADNP/davunetide target [19, 20, 35, 39].

Importantly, disparate effects were observed with mouse ADNP mutation and davunetide corrective effects in males and females [38], and opposite effects have been observed with davunetide in males and females in PSP [27], rendering sex-specific evaluations vital.

Here, toward further understanding of davunetide sex-dependent acute protection, we analyzed a CABG clinical study (ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT00404014) with a single davunetide intravenous infusion (also called AL-208). As indicated, the rationale for studying AL-208 in the context of CABG was to assess whether this investigational drug can mitigate the cognitive decline observed and potentially improve overall outcomes, while providing sex-specific neuroprotection.

Methods

Original study

Davunetide was originally licensed to Allon Therapeutics from Ramot at Tel Aviv University (IG Founder and Chief Scientific Officer). As such, a study to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and effect of AL-208 on mild cognitive impairment following coronary artery bypass graft surgery was designed and carried out in the US and Canada, with institutional ethics committees (ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT00404014). Upon Allon Therapeutics acquisition, all trial results were returned to Ramot at Tel Aviv University and are now licensed to ExoNavis Therapeutics (IG Vice President Drug Development). Morimoto et al. [40], mixing men and women reported no difference in cognitive function between placebo- and AL-208-treated subjects, although neither group demonstrated postoperative impairment. These authors further maintained that no safety concerns were identified. With our recent results attesting for sex differences in chronic neurodegenerations coupled with sex-specific davunetide protection [27, 28], we have now analyzed (post-hoc) the clinical results, separating males and females.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. Studies were conducted at 28 independent US and Canadian clinical sites. For details on the study sites, please see: ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT00404014. All study documents were approved by the above-mentioned institutional review boards/independent ethics committees (IRB/IEC). As part of the inclusion criteria, all study participants provided informed consents.

Overall study design safety [40]

A Phase II study designed to evaluate the safety, tolerability and effect of a single, rising dose of intravenous AL-208, followed by a placebo controlled, double-blind, parallel-group revaluation of a single dose of AL-208, on mild cognitive impairment following CABG was carried out. 234 subjects were randomized and 210 subjects undergoing CABG with cardiopulmonary bypass were applied a single davunetide dose. An open-label safety run-in included 100 mg, 200 mg and 300 mg dose levels (20 subjects), followed by double-blind randomized part comparing placebo and AL-208, 300 mg (1:1). Safety was reviewed following each cohort and the highest dose (300 mg) was well-tolerated and therefore chosen for the second part of the study [40]. Thus, the second part of the study was a randomized, double blind, placebo-controlled parallel group study comparing placebo and 300 mg intravenous davunetide. Cognitive function was assessed postoperatively with several measures of memory and executive function (detailed below). Three deaths were reported during the study. One placebo-treated patient sustained a post-operative cardiac arrest. Two active-treated patients also died, one with an anastomotic failure and the other with arterial embolism and respiratory failure. While the investigator considered the anastomotic failure death to be possibly related to the study drug, an independent safety review did not concur, believing it to be related to the operative procedure. Overall, these fatal cases seem to be qualitatively expected clinical outcomes among 210 patients undergoing elective CABG surgery.

Morimoto et al. [40], further summarized the adverse events (AEs) in this study. The most reported AEs were hyperglycemia, anemia, respiratory failure and atrial fibrillation, all of which are common after CABG surgery, and none of which had a convincing predominance in the davunetide exposed group. The incidence of severe AEs (SAEs) was also similar between the treatment groups. No treatment related changes in clinical laboratory assessments, vital signs or physical examinations were observed. Thus, overall, intravenous davunetide appeared to be generally safe and well-tolerated in this study, making it worthwhile to look for potential sex effects and efficacious results.

Davunetide (AL-208) and placebo administration in detail

The sterile AL-208 drug product was packaged by Dalton Pharma Services (Toronto, Canada) into open-label 20-mL glass vials with a gray rubber septum and red flip cap. Each vial contained 100 mg of white-to-off-white powdered NAPVSIPQ (davunetide) and was used for single-use intravenous administration. AL-208 solutions are colorless, odorless, and stored at -20 degrees Celsius until use. Before reconstitution, vials were allowed to warm to room temperature for 45-60 min and the resulting solution was used within 8 h post-reconstitution. The 250 mL total volume of 0.9% sodium chloride for injection USP plus reconstituted AL-208 was administered as a 15-min infusion of normal saline. The placebo was similarly intravenously administered as a 15-min infusion of 250 mL 0.9% sodium chloride. Administration was scheduled to end 30 min before the anticipated start of extracorporeal circulation.

Study population separating sexes in detail

In this double-blind evaluation, men and women, aged 65 to 79 years of age who were undergoing CABG surgery, were randomly assigned to separate groups of davunetide intravenous (AL-208) 100 mg, 200 mg, 300 mg, and placebo administration. Study populations were as follows: Males administered AL-208 300 mg n = 70; 200 mg n = 4; 100 mg n = 6; placebo n = 64; Females administered AL-208 300 mg n = 16; 200 mg n = 2; 100 mg n = 1; placebo n = 13. These sample sizes corresponded to the total number of subjects with all required values ascertained. There were two excluded male subjects (11-508-091 and 51-501-099) with Primary Efficacy Variable values that were lacking the neuron specific enolase (NSE) data, and one Female subject (20-506-134) similarly excluded.

Neuron-specific enolase (NSE) and AL-208 evaluations in plasma

Serial plasma blood samples were drawn venously from all subjects for NSE evaluation, an exploratory biomarker for neuronal injury, at three timestamps: at screening and at 6 h and 24 h after extracorporeal circulation. An observed decrease in NSE score is a positive indication as NSE increases with an increase in brain damage [41]. Samples were collected and sent to various laboratories for analysis. ACM-Pivotal Global Central Laboratory in Rochester, NY analyzed the clinical laboratory samples, Specialty Laboratories Inc. in Valencia, CA analyzed the NSE biomarker, and Charles River Laboratories, Inc in Montreal, Quebec, Canada analyzed collected samples for concentration of AL-208 [40]. Four blood samples were taken for analysis of AL-208 (davunetide/NAPVISPQ) concentration: before infusion of study medication, at the end of infusion, prior to the anticipated start of extracorporeal circulation, and after extracorporeal circulation was completed. The NSE evaluation was used, in addition to the cognitive assessments, to monitor AL-208 efficacy and overall patient decline/improvement, with comparisons made via AL-208 blood content.

Cognitive assessments (Study Days 11–63)

Subject evaluations were conducted randomly on various days between study days 11 and 63 utilizing 7 of the WMS-III (Wechsler Memory Scale-III) [42] sub-tests (i.e., Faces I & II, Verbal Paired Associates I & II, Family Pictures I & II, and Backwards Digit Span), two sub-tests of the WAIS-III (Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale III) (i.e., Digit symbol and Letter Number Sequencing), one sub-test of the Halstead-Russell Neuropsychological Evaluation System - Revised (HRNES-R) (i.e., Trail Making B), and two other individual evaluations (i.e., Verbal Fluency Task-FAS and Stroop Color-Word Interference Test - Golden and Freshwater version), each with varying 1-6 collected scores (corresponding to the Raw Score, Age Corrected Score, Error Score, Recognition vs. Recall Raw Score, and others, respectively).

Statistics

Excel or GraphPad Prism 10.3.1 software included D’Agostino and Pearson tests for normality coupled with log10 transformation to achieve normal distribution. A two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed post hoc by Tukey’s Honest Significant Difference (HSD) test was further used to assess significant differences. Unless otherwise stated, intercept differences between groups were calculated for significance.

Results

Sex-dependent AL-208 plasma pharmacokinetics

Plasma levels of AL-208 were evaluated before infusion (0) at the end of infusion (post infusion), prior to the anticipated start of extracorporeal circulation, and after extracorporeal circulation was completed. The post infusion results (Fig. 1) inferred a dose-dependency for AL-208 accumulation in men. When the AL-208 levels were further correlated according to time and sex, an essentially opposite picture was obtained for men and women (with men representing a 2.5-2.8-fold greater cohort than women, Fig. 2). Thus, an R = 0.6 was obtained for men comparing AL-208 post infusion with AL-208 levels prior to the anticipated start of extracorporeal circulation, at a dose of 300 mg infusion (insert) or R = 0.5 including all doses, i.e. 100, 200 and 300 mg, Fig. 2. This correlation opposed the finding in women, with R = -0.8 (300 mg dose, Fig. 2, insert) and R = -0.1 (all doses, Fig. 2). Furthermore, a limited correlation was suggested between AL-208 levels post infusion in men compared to AL-208 post extracorporeal circulation (R = 0.3, per all doses, R = 0.1 when the 300 mg dose was considered), while in women an R = -0.6 (all doses) or R = -0.9 (300 mg dose) was obtained, suggesting a faster metabolism in women and a potential accumulation in men.

Davunetide (AL-208) increases verbal paired associates II (VPA II) performance in men who underwent CABG

Sixty-two scores were collected (33 for Males, 29 for Females)—t-test values for all scores were calculated with values less than 0.1 in 16 scores from 9 sub-tests (data not shown), with only one score from one sub-test with a P value less than 0.05 (Verbal Paired Associates II Age Corrected Score for Males, please see below). Notably, Verbal Paired Associates II (VPA II) [42, 43] assesses delayed verbal memory by asking the test-taker to recall the second word of previously learned word pairs after a delay period. This involves recalling the pairs learned in VPA I after an extended interval, testing the long-term retention of the verbal associations. The raw score is calculated as the total number of word pairs correctly recalled after a delay (i.e., the sum of correct responses), which is then age-corrected to achieve the Age-Corrected Score.

Here, significant davunetide (AL-208) improvement (P < 0.05) was found in only the VPA II Age Corrected Score for Males (*P = 0.03786) and in no other test score. Male subjects, with a significant t-test value seen in VPA II, had a mean of 9.46 ± 0.387 in the placebo group and a mean of 10.51 ± 0.367 in the AL-208 group. Female subjects, on the other hand, scored with a mean of 10.714 ± 2.730 in the placebo group and 9.529 ± 3.184 in the AL-208 group (indicating no significant differences). These results indicated that once administered AL-208, male scores increased compared to the placebo group and scored at the mean for this sub-test seen in an American normative sample (mean = 10.5 ± 0.236) (Fig. 5). This improvement was not seen in females. It is worth noting that the standard deviations of all four samples were quite large, implying less consistency and greater differences among the values and that the ages of our subjects (60-79) were significantly older than those of the American normative sample (16-54) [44].

Discussion

Our results are indicative of sex differences and neuroprotection for davunetide. Since not all CABG patients succumb to ischemic mini strokes requiring neuroprotection during surgery and the small woman cohort size, the conclusion must be taken with caution. Regardless, a recent article entitled “Biological Mechanism of Sex Difference in Stroke Manifestation and Outcomes” [45] concluded that female patients have more frequent middle cerebral artery disease and striatocapsular motor pathway involvement with acute ischemic stroke, along with left parieto-occipital cortical infarcts showing greater severity for equivalent infarct volumes than in male patients. These brain problems (assessed in 6,464 patients) lead to more severe initial neurologic symptoms, higher susceptibility to neurologic worsening, and less 3-month functional independence, when compared with male patients [45]. These findings suggest that mixing men and women in clinical studies of neuroprotection in stroke, as well as brain ischemia associated with CABG, is inadvisable and strengthen our small cohort findings.

Furthermore, this sex-specificity is vital to consider in the understanding of neurodegeneration and cognitive decline. In their 2020 paper, “The Effect of Estrogen Replacement Therapy on Alzheimer’s Disease and Parkinson’s Disease in Postmenopausal Women: A Meta-Analysis,” Song et al. demonstrated how gendered hormones have a strong neuroprotective effect on patients with Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s diseases (AD and PD, respectively) seen across a variety of studies. By conducting Estrogen Replacement Therapy (ERT) on postmenopausal women, the risk of onset and development of AD was significantly decreased, indicating sex-specific hormonal effects on these diseases, with cognitive influences shown. The authors additionally explored estrogen’s vast neural impact, from synaptic plasticity to reducing neuroinflammation (seen in the PD models), further bolstering the claim that men and women have significant opposing contributing factors on the neuronal and cognitive level [46], thus encouraging sex-specific studies in neurodegeneration and cognition.

Regarding the Verbal Paired Associates (VPA) subtest from the Wechsler Memory Scale, it is one of the most widely used instruments for assessing explicit episodic memory performance. It should be taken into consideration that performance on the Paired Associates test is related to education, verbal IQ, and to a lesser extent, participants’ sex [47]. Importantly, decreased verbal memory constitutes an independent risk factor for postoperative delirium, and a verbal memory test may be a useful predictor of postoperative delirium development [48]. Furthermore, cognitive tests suffer from ceiling effects and individual variations [49].

However, previous research, including ours suggests that women have better verbal learning than men, while men do better in visual memory [28]. In this respect, when VPA I & II and Faces I & II were administered to 26 male and 26 female undergraduates as well as a measure of visual-spatial perception, the Judgment of Line Orientation Test, (JLO), women outperformed men on several VPA indices, with men scoring at the 25th percentile and women scoring at the 50th percentile. Although no differences were observed on Faces I & II, men outperformed women on JLO [50].

These findings encourage separate sex analysis, further taking into consideration the obvious study limitation encompassing the observation that only 20-25% of patients undergoing CABG will suffer from post operative cognitive dysfunction [51]. With patient variability of ±50% on the VPA (Fig. 5), the fact that we did see significant protection is interesting for future studies. However, another study limitation, is the small size of the woman cohort analyzed here, paving the path to a larger investigation.

Of additional interest is the recent study using remote ischemic preconditioning (RIPC) [52] induced by three cycles of right upper limb ischemia and reperfusion before CABG, serum NSE levels were quantified using an electrochemiluminescence-based one-step enzyme immunoassay (Elecsys 2010; Roche Diagnostics, UK), showing protection of the RIPC preconditioning, in line with our finding (but not separating men from women, which may yield interesting results) [52]. Thus, exploring sex-specific effects here, revealed critical insights into treatment efficacy that was overlooked in general analyses.

With respect to protection against tauopathy, the underlying mechanism of davunetide indicates that tauopathy results from ischemia and is indeed found in stroke patients [53, 54]. Most relevant to the current study, CABG provokes AD-like changes in the CSF, such as marked increases in Tau concentrations six months postoperatively, coupled with a gradual cognitive decline [55]. Importantly, CABG is directly associated with blood NSE/Tau increases as signals of neurodegeneration, and a recent study suggested an average of 456% of Tau increase versus 168% of NSE increase [41], peaking at 2 h postoperatively and implicating Tau/tauopathy as a major player in CABG postoperative complications. With sex differences seen in heart diseases (developing later in women) [56], stroke (showing a worse impact in women) [45], and with the most prevalent tauopathy, AD, being more prevalent in women [57], the scientific community is urged to analyze and treat men and women separately.

Interestingly, and as indicated above, davunetide, investigated here in its intravenous formulation (AL-208), is a fragment of ADNP [13]. ADNP is crucial for brain development and function [16] with davunetide compensation [38], and further critical for heart formation [17], while regulating steroid hormone production [58] and in turn regulated by the estrous cycle [59], attesting to sex-specificity.

Importantly, unlike previous studies with AL-108, which did not measure plasma concentrations in the same clinical trial [27, 28], the current study with AL-208 had an arm of pharmacokinetic evaluations. Specifically observing the results in Fig. 2, the plasma profiles of AL-208 correlated accumulation with time depending on the initial plasma concentrations in men, with an opposite picture in women. In this respect, opposite effects were observed in PSP, with patients receiving twice daily AL-108 (davunetide intranasal, 30 mg, twice daily), with only women showing efficacious protecting results. It should further be taken in mind that women are usually significantly lighter than men [27], men depicted here a surprising trend for a higher davunetide (AL-208) post infusion plasma concentration than women with the same davunetide infusion of 300 mg). With a dose-dependent bell shape curve [25, 28, 34], it is possible that the high davunetide dose was accumulating in PSP men, beyond the efficacious levels, coupled with the slower disease progression/neurodegeneration seen in the placebo PSP men compared to women [27].

Notably, davunetide is a synthetic peptide derived from ADNP, and as such, this investigational drug does not occur naturally in the blood. Interestingly, ADNP concentrations are decreased in the serum of AD patients [60], and are correlated with patients’ abilities to perform in an IQ test, i.e., correlated with cognition [61]. Furthermore, Morimoto et al. [40], described that the liquid chromatography (LC) – mass spectrometry (MS/MS) assay for davunetide concentrations was validated in plasma and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) matrices with a lower limit of quantitation of 20 pg/ml for an open-label, single dose Phase I study in healthy adults and mild-to-moderate AD patients (Protocol AL-208-110). Assuming no sex difference, individuals were not separated by sex. Davunetide was administered either intranasally (15 mg) or iv. (50 or 300 mg; n = 6 subjects per group). Plasma was collected by venipuncture at regular intervals, and continuous lumbar CSF collection was carried out over 12 h through an indwelling catheter. A biphasic elimination of davunetide was observed, and it was suggested that a multi-compartment pharmacokinetic (PK) model would optimally describe the plasma-CSF concentration relationships. However, no provision was given to potential differences between the sexes, and the multi-compartment could be linked with sex differences. Inspection of the PK parameters indicated that CSF exposure in the lumbar region was <4% of the plasma exposure upon iv administration. The one Cmax ratio able to be calculated for each intranasal group indicated a CSF exposure of about 4-5% of plasma, consistent with the intravenous ratio data. Importantly, davunetide is a compound that crosses the Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) [40, 62, 63], the cellular membrane (endocytosis) [64], and the nuclear envelope [65] and has beneficial effects on synaptic development by promoting neuronal cell survival, synaptic maturation, and synaptic plasticity, and suggesting intracellular metabolism [21, 38, 66]. Furthermore, sex-dependency is implicated here as well as in other studies [38, 67–69].

Thus, here, neuroprotection seemed to correlate with AL-208 content in both sexes (Fig. 4). Taken together with women underrepresentation in the current study, which was cautioned against before [70], with estrogen levels significantly affecting metabolism [71] and with different sex/age protein metabolism [72], our study emphasizes the necessity for sex-dependent drug development.

In conclusion, we have revealed cognitive neuroprotection of intravenous davunetide (AL-208) in men undergoing CABG at risk for AD development, neuroprotection was suggested in women as well, but taking a different time course, with a study limitation of a smaller woman versus man cohort. This study further indicated systemic/brain bioavailability for davunetide, given the effects on the brain. Davunetide is currently being clinically developed for women suffering from PSP [27] and children suffering from the ADNP syndrome [21], with ADNP-deficiency/mutations associated with tauopathy in young children as well as aging adults [19, 58]. The current findings open new horizons to further clinical development in acute brain injury in a sex-dependent manner.

Quality Assessment of KEDOO Criteria and Coll and Sadrian Criteria in Evaluation of Root Canal Obturation:A Comparative Study
Authors: Mehak Behal, Ruchika Kundra, Mebin George Mathew, Shaila Masih
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim and backgroundThe success of pulpectomy in primary teeth depends significantly on the quality of root canal obturation. While the Coll and Sadrian criteria have traditionally been used to evaluate obturation at the tooth level, they do not assess individual canal fill quality or detect voids. The recently introduced KEDOO classification offers a canal-specific approach. This study aimed to compare the diagnostic performance of the KEDOO classification with the traditional Coll and Sadrian criteria in assessing obturation quality in primary molars.MethodsA retrospective observational study was conducted using digital periapical radiographs of pulpectomized primary molars from children aged 4–9 years. Each radiograph was evaluated using both the Coll and Sadrian criteria and the KEDOO classification. The Coll and Sadrian criteria assessed obturation at the level of the entire tooth, whereas the KEDOO classification evaluated each canal separately and also identified the presence of voids. Chi-square analysis was used to compare the classifications, and intrarater reliability was measured using Cohen's kappa.ResultsOut of 234 retrieved radiographs, 132 met the inclusion criteria and were analyzed. According to the Coll and Sadrian criteria, 62% of mandibular and 77.4% of maxillary molars were classified as optimally filled. In contrast, the KEDOO classification showed lower optimal fill rates at the canal level: 32.9 and 49.4% for mandibular mesial and distal canals, and 35.8, 32.1, and 69.8% for maxillary mesial, distal, and palatal canals, respectively. Voids were detected in 49.4% of mandibular and 50.9% of maxillary molars. Intrarater reliability was perfect for Coll and Sadrian (κ = 1.00) and almost perfect for KEDOO (κ = 0.815).ConclusionThe KEDOO classification provides a more detailed, canal-specific, and diagnostically sensitive evaluation of obturation quality in primary teeth. As it evaluates each canal individually and detects voids, it offers improved precision over traditional methods.Clinical significanceIncorporating the KEDOO classification into pediatric endodontic practice may enhance the accuracy of obturation assessment, enable early detection of suboptimal fillings, and contribute to improved long-term treatment outcomes.Keywords: Coll and Sadrian criteria, KEDOO classification, Pediatric endodontics, Primary teeth, Pulpectomy, Root canal obturation

Introduction

The World Health Organization reports that nearly half of the global population is affected by oral diseases, with over 514 million children experiencing untreated dental caries in primary teeth.1 This widespread prevalence highlights the critical need for timely and effective pediatric dental care. Dental caries in the primary dentition tends to progress rapidly, primarily due to anatomical features characteristic of deciduous teeth, including thinner enamel, larger pulp chambers, and proportionally higher pulp horn placement.2 Without intervention, the disease may advance to irreversible pulpitis, abscess formation, or pulp necrosis, resulting in pain and the need for complex dental treatment.3 In such cases, pulpectomy remains the preferred intervention, involving complete removal of infected pulp tissue or, when necessary, extraction.4

The key objective in pediatric dentistry is the preservation of primary teeth until their natural exfoliation, ensuring proper function and guidance for permanent successors.5 Among the available treatment options, pulpectomy is widely accepted for managing extensively infected yet restorable primary teeth.6 This procedure entails thorough debridement of necrotic tissue followed by root canal obturation with a biocompatible, resorbable filling material.7

The long-term success of pulpectomy depends on multiple factors, among which the quality of root canal obturation is particularly critical.8 The Coll and Sadrian criteria, introduced in 1996, have served as the standard framework for radiographic evaluation of obturation quality in primary teeth.9 However, this criteria is not without limitations. They do not account for the presence of voids and rely on subjective interpretation when differentiating between underfilled and optimally filled canals. In multirooted teeth, the criteria may classify a tooth as optimally filled based on a single well-obturated canal, even if the others are inadequately filled, potentially misrepresenting the overall treatment quality.

To address these limitations, Jeevanandan et al. recently introduced the KEDOO classification (Table 1), a canal-specific evaluation system that provides a more detailed and objective assessment by independently analyzing each root canal and incorporating void detection.10 Despite its conceptual advantages, no prior study has systematically compared the KEDOO classification with the widely used Coll and Sadrian criteria. The present study was therefore designed to compare and assess the performance of the KEDOO and Coll and Sadrian classification systems in evaluating obturation quality in pulpectomized primary molars.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This retrospective clinical study was conducted in the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry at Christian Dental College, Ludhiana, Punjab, India. Ethical clearance was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee (IECBMHR/202501-080/Apprvl-R.Project/CDC) prior to data collection. Because this was a retrospective study using archived digital radiographs, the requirement for written informed consent from patients' parents/guardians was waived by the Institutional Ethics Committee.

Sample Selection

Digital radiographs of pulpectomized primary molars treated between January 2021 and December 2024 were retrieved from institutional archives. A total of 132 radiographs (79 mandibular molars and 53 maxillary molars) that met the inclusion criteria were selected.

Evaluation Procedure

Variables: The variables considered included the type of tooth, the length of root canal obturation, and the density of root filling.

Data source/measurement: Digital radiographs of teeth obturated with Metapex were used to evaluate the quality of obturation. The quality of obturation was determined based on the relationship between the obturation length and the radiographic apex, as well as the density of obturation. To provide a comparable data evaluation, each radiograph was analyzed using both criteria: the KEDOO criteria and the Coll and Sadrian criteria.

Criteria for assessment of obturation length in each canal:

Underfilled canal (score 1): Canal filled >1 mm short of apex.

Underfilled canal (score 1): Canal filled >1 mm short of apex.

Optimal filling (score 2): Canal having obturating material ending at the radiographic apex or up to 1 mm short of the apex.

Optimal filling (score 2): Canal having obturating material ending at the radiographic apex or up to 1 mm short of the apex.

Overfilling (score 3): Canal showing obturated material extending beyond the radiographic apex.

Overfilling (score 3): Canal showing obturated material extending beyond the radiographic apex.

Criteria for assessment of density of obturation: The density of obturation was assessed by the presence or absence of voids in both apical as well as coronal portions, and scoring was done as follows:

Score 1: Presence of voids.

Score 1: Presence of voids.

Score 2: Absence of voids.

Score 2: Absence of voids.

Examiner Calibration and Reliability

Prior to the main evaluation, both examiners underwent calibration using 15 randomly selected radiographs. Interrater reliability between the two examiners was assessed using Cohen's kappa coefficient (κ).

Statistical Analysis

Data were entered into Microsoft Excel and analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0. Descriptive statistics were calculated for both classification systems. Chi-square analysis was used to examine associations between categorical variables. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 234 digital radiographs were initially retrieved from the database between January 2024 and September 2025. Of these, 102 radiographs did not meet the inclusion criteria and were excluded. The final analysis included 132 radiographs from 120 patients.

Evaluation of the Length of Obturation

The quality of obturation length was assessed using both the Coll and Sadrian criteria and the KEDOO classification system, categorizing the obturation as underfilled, optimally filled, or overfilled.

Primary Mandibular Molars

Overall, 62% of primary mandibular molars were classified as optimally filled using the Coll and Sadrian criteria. However, when individual canals were assessed using the KEDOO classification, only 32.9% of mesial canals and 49.4% of distal canals were found to be optimally filled. Chi-square analysis revealed statistically significant differences between the two criteria for both mesial (p = 0.001) and distal (p = 0.030) canals. Evaluation of Intrarater Reliability

To assess the intrarater reliability between the initial and follow-up evaluations using the Coll and Sadrian criteria and the KEDOO classification system, Cohen's kappa coefficient (κ) was calculated.

For the Coll and Sadrian criteria, there was perfect agreement between the two time points, with all 15 cases receiving the same classification on reassessment, yielding a κ value of 1.00, indicating perfect reliability.

For the KEDOO criteria, 14 out of 15 teeth received consistent classifications at both time points, resulting in a κ value of 0.815, which represents almost perfect agreement according to Landis and Koch's benchmark scale.

These results suggest that while both criteria systems demonstrate high intrarater reliability, the Coll and Sadrian criteria are more consistent over time. However, the slightly lower agreement for KEDOO may reflect its higher sensitivity to minor variations in individual canal obturation quality.

Discussion

The primary goal of pulp therapy is to maintain the health and functional capacity of teeth. Successful endodontic therapy depends on effective disinfection and the hermetic sealing of the root canal system.11 However, the complex morphology of primary root canals poses significant challenges to ideal biomechanical preparation and decontamination. Mechanical cleaning and irrigation alone cannot eliminate all microbial content within infected root canals.12 Therefore, obturation plays a critical role in sealing off residual bacteria and their by-products, reducing the risk of reinfection.13

In primary teeth, root canal obturation following pulpectomy aims to establish a three-dimensional seal that minimizes microbial leakage and supports long-term treatment success.14 Achieving an optimal fill with minimal voids is considered essential. Primosch et al. reported a significantly higher failure rate in overfilled canals, emphasizing the importance of proper fill length.15 Similarly, Bawazir and Salama found that radiographic success was greater in optimally and overfilled canals compared to underfilled ones, especially when using lentulo spiral techniques.16

The quality of obturation is also an important parameter when evaluating new materials, techniques, or instrumentation systems. Despite developments in pediatric endodontics, obturation assessment has traditionally relied on the Coll and Sadrian criteria introduced in 1996. According to this system, underfilling is defined as all canals being filled >1 mm short of the apex, optimal filling occurs when one or more canals reach the apex or are within 1 mm of it, and overfilling is recorded when any canal extends beyond the apex.9 However, this system does not evaluate each canal individually, potentially overlooking discrepancies between canals in multirooted teeth.

To address these limitations, Jeevanandan et al. introduced the KEDOO classification in 2023.10 This canal-specific system provides a detailed framework encompassing four classes: class 1 applies to anterior teeth, class 2 assesses mesial and distal canals of mandibular molars, class 3 evaluates canals of maxillary molars, and class 4 focuses on void detection in all primary teeth. Despite its potential, no prior studies had compared this system with the Coll and Sadrian criteria, highlighting the need for the present investigation.

This study used digital radiographs to evaluate obturation quality, and the results showed notable differences between the two systems. In mandibular molars, while 62% of cases were rated as optimally filled using the Coll and Sadrian system, KEDOO-based analysis showed optimal filling in only 32.9% of mesial and 49.4% of distal canals. Likewise, in maxillary molars, 77.4% were considered optimally filled using the Coll and Sadrian criteria, but canal-specific evaluation using KEDOO identified only 35.8% of mesial, 32.1% of distal, and 69.8% of palatal canals as optimally filled. These differences were statistically significant (p < 0.05), suggesting that the KEDOO system offers a more stringent and refined evaluation. These findings are consistent with those of Jeevanandan et al., who also reported lower optimal fill rates when using canal-specific assessment.10

The presence of voids within the root canal filling is another critical factor. Voids are commonly seen in canals with irregular shapes and can compromise the seal, allowing bacterial recolonization and increasing the risk of failure.14 While the Coll and Sadrian criteria do not account for voids, the KEDOO classification integrates void detection as a separate parameter, making it more comprehensive for assessing overall obturation quality.

Multiple imaging modalities are available to evaluate obturation, including analog radiographs, digital systems, and CBCT. Among these, digital radiography offers several advantages, such as lower radiation exposure, rapid image acquisition, enhanced image manipulation, and reduced need for chemical processing.17 Cederberg et al. found that photostimulable phosphor (PSP) systems were superior in estimating working length compared to conventional radiographs.18 Huybrechts et al. demonstrated that voids larger than 300 µm were detectable by all imaging modalities, but digital radiographs were significantly better at identifying smaller voids.19 These findings were supported by Matherne et al., who also confirmed the diagnostic accuracy of digital systems in endodontics.17

In the present study, digital radiographs revealed voids in 49.4% of mandibular and 50.9% of maxillary molars using the KEDOO criteria. As the Coll and Sadrian system lacks a mechanism for void detection, it was less informative for this crucial parameter.

The intrarater reliability analysis yielded a κ value of 1.00 for the Coll and Sadrian criteria, indicating perfect agreement, while the KEDOO classification showed a κ value of 0.815, representing almost perfect agreement. The slightly lower agreement for KEDOO may reflect its increased sensitivity and the finer level of detail required when evaluating individual canals and voids. This difference, while minor, supports the notion that KEDOO is more granular and demanding, yet still highly reliable.

Although the Coll and Sadrian system remains widely accepted, this study demonstrates that the KEDOO criteria offer greater diagnostic depth and clinical relevance. By moving from a generalized tooth-level approach to a canal-specific evaluation, KEDOO provides enhanced diagnostic accuracy, helps detect critical variables like voids, and encourages more precise clinical decision-making. Its incorporation into daily practice could elevate the standard of care in pediatric endodontics, reduce the likelihood of reinfection, and improve long-term outcomes.

The study has limitations. This study relied on retrospective analysis of existing radiographs, limiting control over imaging standardization and procedural variables. The relatively small sample size (n = 132) may affect the generalizability of results. As the study focused solely on radiographic assessment, clinical outcomes such as postoperative success or patient-reported symptoms were not evaluated. Future studies should explore the diagnostic performance of the KEDOO classification using larger, prospectively followed samples and correlate findings with long-term treatment outcomes.

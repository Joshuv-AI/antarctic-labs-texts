A circRNA promotes esophageal squamous cell carcinoma progression by inhibiting TRIM25-mediated degradation of IGF2BP family members
Authors: Shuangyan Tan, Yue Ming, Jiawei Guo, Wenrong Liu, Hulin Ma, Yu Liu, Zhijie Xu, A-Lai Gu-Ha, Lunzhi Dai, Yi-Dan Lin, Yong Peng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEsophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a highly aggressive malignancy with poor prognosis and limited treatment options. While circular RNAs (circRNAs) are frequently dysregulated in ESCC, their functional roles and molecular mechanisms in tumor progression remain largely unexplored.MethodsWe characterized circRNA using RT-PCR, Sanger sequencing, and fluorescent in situ hybridization. Gain- and loss-of-function studies in vitro and in vivo were performed to assess circRNA function. Molecular interactions were investigated via RNA pull-down assays coupled with mass spectrometry, electrophoretic mobility shift assays, co-immunoprecipitation, and immunoblot analysis. Clinical relevance was evaluated by RT-qPCR and immunohistochemistry in patient specimens.ResultsWe identified circLNF, a novel circRNA derived from the long non-coding RNA FIRRE gene, which is significantly upregulated in ESCC. Functional assays demonstrated that circLNF promotes proliferative and migratory capacities in cultured cells and accelerates tumor progression and metastasis in animal models. Background

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) represents a highly aggressive malignancy with poor clinical outcomes, accounting for approximately 90% of esophageal cancer cases in China [1, 2]. Despite advancements in multimodal therapies, the five-year survival rate for ESCC patients remains dismal, at less than 13%. Although molecular alterations in protein-coding genes and noncoding RNAs have been associated with survival in ESCC patients [3], the underlying mechanisms driving tumor progression remain insufficiently understood. Consequently, there is an urgent need to elucidate the molecular determinants of ESCC and develop effective therapeutic strategies.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are a class of covalently closed, single-stranded RNAs that demonstrate greater stability than their linear counterparts due to their resistance to exonuclease-mediated degradation [4]. While the majority of circRNAs are generated through back-splicing of pre-mRNAs, emerging evidence indicates that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) also contribute to the biogenesis of functional circRNAs [5, 6]. Accumulating studies have revealed widespread dysregulation of circRNAs across multiple cancer types [7, 8], where they play pivotal roles in regulating key oncogenic processes, including sustained proliferation signaling, metastatic progression, and treatment resistance through diverse molecular mechanisms [9, 10]. Notably, our prior research identified circITGB6 as a critical promoter of metastasis in colorectal cancer [11]. Although we have previously established a comprehensive circRNA expression profile in ESCC [8], a limited subset of these circRNAs have been functionally validated [12–14]. Given this gap in knowledge, further investigation is imperative to elucidate the mechanistic roles of ESCC-associated circRNAs in tumorigenesis and progression.

CircRNAs regulate gene expression through diverse mechanisms, including protein interactions [15], microRNA (miRNA) sponging [16], and peptide translation [17]. Our recent work uncovered a novel tumor-suppressive mechanism in which circNEIL3 directly interacts with the oncogenic protein YBX1, facilitating its Nedd4L-mediated ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation [18]. Notably, members of the IGF2BP family are frequently overexpressed in malignancies due to impaired ubiquitin-dependent degradation, with Tripartite Motif Containing 25 (TRIM25) acting as the primary E3 ubiquitin ligase regulating IGF2BP protein turnover [19, 20]. Intriguingly, bioinformatics analyses indicate that certain circRNAs harbor IGF2BP-binding motifs, implying their potential to influence IGF2BP activity through competitive sequestration or conformation stabilization mechanisms. However, the precise mechanism by which circRNAs modulate IGF2BP function in ESCC remains to be elucidated.

Through comprehensive circRNA profiling, we identified circLNF, a previously uncharacterized circRNA originating from the long non-coding RNA FIRRE gene, as markedly upregulated in ESCC tumors. Functional characterization via genetic knockdown and overexpression demonstrated that circLNF robustly enhances cell proliferation and migration in vitro and accelerates tumor growth and metastasis in vivo. Mechanistically, circLNF directly interacts with IGF2BP family proteins via conserved “CAUC” motifs, thereby stabilizing these oncoproteins by impeding TRIM25-mediated ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation. This regulatory axis was clinically validated by the strong positive correlation between circLNF expression and IGF2BP1 protein levels in ESCC specimens. Collectively, these findings establish circLNF as a critical molecular driver of ESCC progression and a potential therapeutic target for intervention.

Materials and methods

Human tissues and ethics

The study population was selected according to the following stringent inclusion criteria: (1) histopathological confirmation of ESCC through standard preoperative gastroscopic biopsy procedures; (2) age range of 18–78 years; (3) adequate hematological, hepatic, and renal function; and (4) absence of concurrent malignancies and HIV infection, and (5) normal coagulation profile. Patients were excluded based on the following conditions: (1) Prior therapeutic interventions for ESCC including surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or other immunotherapy; (2) documented neurological or psychiatric disorders; (3) administration of neoadjuvant therapy before surgical resection; (4) current pregnancy status; (5) preoperative comorbid autoimmune diseases (e.g., hyperthyroidism); (6) incomplete clinical data; or (7) perioperative mortality, defined as death occurring either during hospitalization or within 30 days post discharge. All participants provided written informed consents prior to enrollment. For quantitative PCR analysis, 11 paired fresh-frozen ESCC tumors and adjacent normal tissues were collected from West China Hospital of Sichuan University, under ethical approval (No. 2023 − 1764) granted by the Biomedical Ethics Review Committee of West China Hospital. For prognostic and correlative analyses, commercially available ESCC tissue microarray (HEsoS105Su01) and cDNA microarray (CircDNA-HEsoS095Su01) were purchased from Shanghai OUTDO BIOTECH Co., Ltd.

Cell culture and stable cell generation

Human ESCC cell lines KYSE30 and KYSE150 were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium, while ECA109 and KYSE410 were maintained in DMEM medium, with both media supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator. For lentivirus preparation, HEK293T cells were co-transfected with lentiviral expression constructs plus psPAX2 and pMD2G packaging plasmids. Viral supernatants were collected at 48 h post-transfection, clarified by centrifugation, and used for target cell transduction. Stable polyclonal populations were selected with puromycin and subsequently validated by RT-qPCR and/or immunoblot analysis.

RT-PCR and RT-qPCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted using RNAiso Plus reagent (TaKaRa) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Genomic DNA was eliminated and cDNA synthesis was performed using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (TaKaRa) to ensure RNA-specific amplification. For circRNA validation, PCR amplification was conducted using divergent primers and Phanta® Max Super-Fidelity DNA Polymerase (Vazyme). The resultant PCR products were gel-purified and subsequently verified by Sanger sequencing to confirm the circular sequences. Quantitative PCR analysis was performed using TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ II (Tli RNase H Plus) (TaKaRa) on a StepOne Plus real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems). Relative quantification of target transcripts was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method, with U6 snRNA or β-actin mRNA as normalization controls. Primer sequences used in these analyses are provided in Table S1.

RNase R treatment

Total RNA (5 µg) was subjected to linear RNA digestion by incubation with 15 units of RNase R (Epicentre Technologies) at 37 °C for 30 min. The reaction was terminated by addition of 2.5 M ammonium acetate (Abmion) and 1 µg glycogen carrier (Thermo Fisher), followed by ethanol precipitation. RNase R-resistant RNAs were pelleted by centrifugation at 12,000 x g for 15 min at 4 °C, washed with 75% ethanol, and resuspended in nuclease-free water. First-strand cDNA synthesis was performed using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (TaKaRa) with random hexamers according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH) analysis

The Cy3-labeled probe specifically targeting the back-splice junction of circLNF was synthesized by Sangon Biotech. FISH were performed using Ribo™ Fluorescent In Situ Hybridization Kit (RiboBio) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, cells were fixed in freshly prepared 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 min at room temperature, followed by permeabilization with 0.5% Triton X-100 at 4 °C for 5 min. After pre-hybridization at 37 °C for 30 min, cells were incubated with 5 µl (20 µM) Cy3-labeled probe in 100 µl hybridization buffer at 37 °C overnight in a humidified chamber. After extensive washing, nuclei were counterstained with 1 µg/ml DAPI solution for 5 min. Fluorescence images were acquired using an Olympus Fluoview laser scanning confocal microscope. The probe sequences are listed in Table S1.

Subcellular fractionation

Cells were washed twice with ice-cold PBS and resuspended in RLN lysis buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl, pH7.0, 140 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 0.5% NP-40, 1 mM DTT, 40 U/ml RNase inhibitor) on ice for 1 min, followed by centrifugation at 300 x g for 3 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was collected as the cytoplasmic fraction, and the remaining pellet was washed twice with RLN buffer and resuspended in RLN buffer as the nuclear fraction. β-actin mRNA and U6 snRNA served as cytoplasmic and nuclear RNAs markers, respectively. β-actin and Lamin A/C proteins were used for immunoblot analysis as cytoplasmic and nuclear protein markers, respectively.

Plasmid construction

The circRNA expression vector pCDH-EF1-Laccase2-MCS-Puro was constructed using the pcDNA3.1(+) Laccase2 MCS Exon backbone, which was generously provided by Prof. Jeremy E. Wilusz (University of Pennsylvania) [21]. To construct wild-type circLNF-overexpressing plasmid, the full-length circLNF sequence was amplified from KYSE30 cDNA and inserted into pCDH-EF1-Laccase2-MCS-Puro at the AgeI site. To generate IGF2BP-binding-deficient circLNF mutant, site-directed mutagenesis was performed to replace the “CAUC” motif with “GAAC”. To construct TRIM25-expressing plasmid, the full-length of open reading frame of TRIM25 was amplified by RT-PCR and cloned into pCDH-CMV-MCS-EF1-Puro vector at XbaI and NotI sites. For knockdown experiments, shRNA oligonucleotides were annealed and ligated into the pLKO.1 TRC vector at AgeI and EcoRI sites. All primers and oligos are listed in Table S1.

Cellular function assays

For colony formation analysis, 3,000–10,000 cells were seeded per well in 6-well plates containing 2 mL complete medium and cultured for 7 days at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Colonies were then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min at room temperature, stained with 1% crystal violet solution for 15 min, and quantified by scanning. Migration and invasion assays were performed using Transwell chamber (8 μm pores; Falcon), where cells suspended in RPMI1640/5% BSA were seeded in the upper chamber (with Matrigel for invasion assays), while the lower chamber contained RPMI-1640/10% FBS as chemoattractant. After certain-time incubation, migrated or invaded cells were fixed (4% paraformaldehyde, 20 min), stained (1% crystal violet; 15 min), and counted under phase-contrast microscope. For wound-healing assays, confluent monolayers in 6-well plates were scratched with a sterilized pipette tip, washed with PBS, and cultured in low-serum media (0.1 ~ 1% FBS) for certain times, with wound closure monitored by microscope.

Immunoblot analysis

Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer (Beyotime Biotechnology) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails (Roche). Lysates were clarified by centrifugation at 12,000 x g for 15 min at 4 °C, and protein concentrations were determined using the Pierce™ BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Scientific). Equal amounts of proteins were separated by 8% SDS-polyacrylamide gels and transferred onto PVDF membrane (Millipore). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat dry milk in TBST (20mM Tris-HCl, pH7.6, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% Tween-20) for 1 h at room temperature, and then incubated with primary antibodies (listed in Table S2) overnight at 4 °C with gentle agitation. After three 10-min TBST washing, membranes were probed with appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were detected using SuperSignal™ West Dura Extended Duration Substrate (Thermo Scientific) and imaged on a ChemiDoc™ XRS+ Imaging System (Bio-Rad) with Image Lab™ Software. Band intensities were quantified by densitometry after background subtraction, normalized to loading controls, and analyzed using Image J software.

Bioinformatics analysis of circLNF interactions and coding potential

To investigate circLNF’s molecular mechanisms, we performed comprehensive bioinformatics analyses using the C2CDB database (http://pengyonglab.com/c2cdb) [22]. miRNA-mRNA interactions were predicted using stringent criteria, including seed sequence complementarity (nucleotides 2–8 at the miRNA 5’ end), binding energy ≤ − 20 kcal/mol, and pairing scores ≥ 140. Candidate miRNAs were further filtered based on their expression abundance in ESCC and predicted binding sites spanning the back-splice junction. Additionally, we also employed the C2CDB integrated pipeline to evaluate circLNF’s protein-coding potential through open reading frame prediction, internal ribosome entry site detection, and ribosome profiling data analysis.

In vitro circularization of circLNF

Biotin-labeled linear RNA transcripts of circLNF were synthesized using MAXIscript™ T7/T3 Transcription Kit (Thermo Scientific) and Biotin RNA Labeling Mix (Roche) according to manufacturer’s instructions. For circularization, linear RNA was annealed with complementary DNA splints in annealing buffer (10mM Tris-HCl, pH7.5, 100mM NaCl) by heating to 95 °C for 5 min followed by gradual cooling to room temperature. The annealed products were ligated with T4 DNA ligase (NEB) overnight at 16 °C. Reaction products were then treated with RNase R (Epicentre Technologies) and DNase I (Thermo Scientific) for 15 min at 37 °C to remove linear RNAs and DNA splints, respectively. Circular RNAs were purified by ethanol precipitation and resuspended in nuclease-free water. RNA quality was verified by agarose gel electrophoresis with SYBR Gold staining. All primer sequences for in vitro transcription and circularization are provided in Table S1.

RNA pull-down assay

Biotin-labeled DNA and RNA probes were synthesized by Sangon Biotech. For each assay, 1 × 107 KYSE410 cells were resuspended in 500 µl RNA pull-down lysis buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl, pH7.0, 150 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 10% glycerol, 0.5% NP-40, proteinase and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails, RNase inhibitor), followed by homogenization and centrifugation at 12,000 x g for 15 min at 4 °C. Cleared cell lysates were incubated with 400 pmol biotinylated DNA probes targeting circLNF’s junction, 200 pmol biotinylated RNA probes, or 2 ug in vitro circularized circLNF at 4 °C for 4 h, followed by precipitation at 4 °C with 30 µl Streptavidin C1 magnetic beads (Thermo Scientific) for 1 h. After three washing with high-stringency buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl, pH7.0, 300 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 10% glycerol, 0.5% NP-40), bound proteins were eluted for immunoblot analysis while RNAs were extracted for downstream analysis. All probe sequences are listed in Table S1.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

KYSE410 cells were lysed in 220 µL RIP lysis buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl, pH7.0, 150 mM NaCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 10% glycerol, 0.5% NP-40, proteinase and phosphatase inhibitors, and RNase inhibitor), followed by centrifugation at 12,000 x g for 15 min at 4 °C. Cleared lysates were immunoprecipitated with anti-IGF2BP antibodies or control IgG at 4 °C for 4 h, followed by incubation with 30 µl Protein-A/G-Sepharose beads (Millipore) at 4 °C for 1 h. Beads were washed twice with lysis buffer and three times with high-salt buffer (300 mM NaCl). Finally, immunoprecipitated RNAs and proteins were analyzed by RT-PCR and immunoblot analysis, respectively. Antibodies are listed in Table S2.

Co-Immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

Cells were resuspended in 500 µL IP lysis buffer (20 mM Tris-HCl, pH7.0, 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 10% glycerol, 0.5% NP-40, proteinase and phosphatase inhibitors, RNase inhibitors), followed by homogenization and centrifugation at 12,000 x g at 4 °C for 15 min. The resultant supernatant was incubated with primary antibodies or control IgG overnight at 4 °C, followed by incubation with 30 µl Protein-A/G-Sepharose beads at 4 °C for 2 h. Beads were washed three times with IP lysis buffer, and immunoprecipitated proteins were subjected to immunoblot analysis.

Electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA)

EMSAs were performed using Chemiluminescent EMSA Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology) to assess IGF2BP-RNA interactions. Briefly, recombinant IGF2BP proteins were incubated with 10 fmol of biotinylated probes in binding buffer (100 mM Hepes, pH7.6, 200 mM KCl, 10 mM MgCl2, 10 mM DTT) at 25 °C for 20 min. Protein-RNA complexes were separated on 4% native polyacrylamide gels, followed by transfer to nylon membrane (Millipore) and UV cross-linked at 254 nm (120 mJ/cm2). Membranes were probed with streptavidin-HRP conjugate for 1 h at room temperature and washed three times with Wash Buffer (Beyotime Biotechnology, #D3308W). Finally, complexes were visualized using SuperSignal™ West Dura Extended Duration Substrate on a ChemiDoc™ MP Imaging System (Bio-Rad).

Animal experiments

All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with protocols approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use of West China Hospital, Sichuan University (No. 20230227073). Female BALB/c nude mice (4–6 weeks old) were purchased from Beijing HFK Bioscience Co. Ltd. and maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions. For lung metastasis assays, mice receiving tail-vein injections of 1 × 106 ESCC cells suspended in 200 µL sterile PBS. After 4–6 weeks, mice were euthanized and lung tissues were harvested for histological analysis using hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. For in vivo bioluminescence imaging, mice were anesthetized with isofurane and injected intraperitoneally with 100 µL VivoGlo™ luciferin solution (Biovision, 15 mg/mL). Imaging was performed 15 min post-injection using the Xenogen IVIS Lumina Series II system (Xenogen Corp.). Subcutaneous tumor growth was assessed by injecting 5 × 105 ESCC cells suspended in 100 µl PBS into the right flank. Tumor volume was calculated using the formula: V (volume) = (length × width2)/2. Upon termination, tumors were excised, weighed, and photographed for documentation.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.2. Survival analyses were performed using Kaplan-Meier estimators, with between-group comparisons assessed by log-rank tests. Comparative analysis of circLNF expression between matched tumor and adjacent normal tissue pairs was conducted using two-tailed Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank tests. Continuous variables were analyzed as follows: (1) unpaired Student’s t-tests for RT-qPCR data and Transwell migration/invasion assays; (2) two-way ANOVA with repeated measures for tumor growth kinetics; and (3) Mann-Whitney U tests for nonparametric data (tumor weights, bioluminescence intensities). Correlational analyses between circLNF and IGF2BP1 expression were calculated by Pearson correlation analysis. Data are presented as mean ± SD in cellular experiments or mean ± SEM in other statistical assays, with significance thresholds defined as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001 and ****p < 0.0001; p > 0.05; nonsignificant (n.s.) for p > 0.05.

Results

Significant upregulation of circLNF in ESCC tumors

To identify dysregulated circRNAs in ESCC, we conducted Ribo-Zero RNA sequencing on 22 paired ESCC tumors and adjacent normal tissues, with bioinformatics analysis revealing significant circRNA expression alterations as previously reported [8]. Among the top 10 up- and down-regulated candidates (Table S3), circLNF exhibited the most pronounced upregulation in ESCC with high abundance. Validation in an independent cohort (n = 11) using divergent primer-based RT-qPCR confirmed the dysregulation patterns of these candidate circRNAs (Fig. S1). Given that this markedly upregulated circRNA originates from exons 5–10 of the long non-coding RNA FIRRE gene (Fig. 1A), we designated it as circLNF. RT-PCR and Sanger sequencing verified its full-length sequence of 845 nucleotides (Fig. S2). Collectively, these results demonstrate significant circLNF upregulation in ESCC tumors, implicating its potential role in ESCC pathogenesis.

Molecular characterization of circLNF in ESCC

Initial screening by RT-qPCR identified elevated circLNF expression in ECA109 and KYSE410 cells compared to other ESCC cells (Fig. S3A), prompting their selection for subsequent characterization. To verify the circular architecture of circLNF, we designed convergent primers (for linear transcripts; grey) and divergent primers (for circRNA; black) to perform RT-PCR analysis (Fig. 1A). CircLNF amplification was exclusively detected using divergent primers in cDNA templates, with no product observed in genomic DNA (gDNA) controls (Fig. 1B). The circular nature was further confirmed through RNase R resistance assays, demonstrating circLNF’s stability against exonuclease digestion compared to linear transcripts (Fig. 1C). RNA stability profiling following Actinomycin D treatment revealed a significant longer half-life for circLNF versus its linear FIRRE counterparts (Fig. 1D). Subcellular fractionation analysis showed predominant cytoplasmic localization of circLNF (Fig. 1E), which was corroborated by FISH assays using junction-specific Cy3-labeled probes (Fig. 1F and Fig. S3B). Together, these results experimentally validate circLNF as a stable, predominantly cytoplasmic circRNA in ESCC.

Elevated circLNF expression correlates with adverse clinical outcomes in ESCC

To evaluate the clinical significance of circLNF in ESCC pathogenesis, we performed quantitative analysis of circLNF expression in a cohort of 67 tumor specimens from an ESCC cDNA microarray, including 28 matched adjacent normal tissues. Comparative analysis demonstrated significant upregulation of circLNF in tumor tissues relative to normal counterparts (Fig. 1G). Notably, circLNF expression levels were substantially higher in patients with lymph node metastasis compared to non-metastatic cases (Fig. 1H and Table S4), suggesting its potential as a metastatic indicator. Survival analysis revealed a significant prognostic association, with high circLNF expression correlating with markedly shorter overall survival compared to low-expression cases (Fig. 1I). These findings establish circLNF as both a potential diagnostic marker and an independent prognostic factor in ESCC progression.

CircLNF overexpression promotes tumor growth and metastasis

To functionally characterize circLNF in ESCC, we generated stable circLNF-overexpressing KYSE30 and KYSE150 cell lines. Molecular validation confirmed successful back-splicing of ectopically expressed circLNF (Fig. S3C), appropriate cytoplasmic localization (Fig. S3D, E), and unaltered host gene FIRRE transcript levels (Fig. S3F). In vitro functional assays demonstrated that circLNF overexpression significantly enhanced oncogenic cellular behaviors, as evidenced by increased cell proliferation (Fig. 2A), enhanced Transwell migratory capacity (Fig. 2B and Fig. S3G) and accelerated wound closure kinetics (Fig. 2C). In vivo studies using subcutaneous xenograft models revealed that circLNF overexpression promoted tumor growth (Fig. 2D-F). Furthermore, the tail-vein injection experiments showed that circLNF significantly increased lung metastatic burden (Fig. 2G, H). Collectively, these findings indicate that circLNF robustly promotes ESCC tumor growth and metastasis.

CircLNF knockdown impairs ESCC malignant progression

To substantiate the oncogenic role of circLNF in ESCC, we engineered junction-specific shRNAs to selectively deplete circLNF in high-expressing ECA109 and KYSE410 cell lines. Quantitative analysis confirmed efficient circLNF knockdown without affecting expression of its host-gene FIRRE transcripts (Fig. S3H). Functional assays demonstrated that circLNF silencing significantly inhibited cell proliferation (Fig. 3A), reduced migration and invasion capacity (Fig. 3B and Fig. S3I), and decreased cell motility (Fig. 3C). In vivo xenograft models revealed that circLNF depletion markedly suppressed tumor growth (Fig. 3D-F). Moreover, tail-vein injection experiments showed that circLNF depletion substantially reduced lung metastatic burdens in both KYSE410 and ECA109 models (Fig. 3G, H). CircLNF physically interacts with IGF2BP family proteins

While circRNAs typically regulate gene expression through miRNA sponging, protein interaction, or polypeptide encoding [15–17], bioinformatics analysis of circLNF using the C2CDB database [22] and miRNA abundance demonstrated the absence of protein-coding potential and no viable miRNA binding sites at its back-splice junction. These results suggest that circLNF primarily functions through protein interactions rather than as a miRNA sponge or peptide template. To identify interacting partners, we conducted RNA pull-down assays using biotinylated antisense (AS) probes targeting the circLNF junction sequence with whole-cell or cytoplasmic lysates from Eca109 and KYSE410 cells. Coomassie blue staining of SDS-PAGE gels detected three specific bands (~ 130, ~ 60 and ~ 35 kDa) exclusively in the AS pull-down samples (Fig. 4A, B). Mass spectrometry analysis of the ~ 60 kDa band identified IGF2BP family members (IGF2BP1, IGF2BP2 and IGF2BP3) as top candidates based on peptide coverage and identification scores (Table S5). These interactions were confirmed by immunoblot analysis of pull-down samples (Fig. 4C). Moreover, IGF2BP proteins were captured from cell lysates by in vitro circularized circLNF (Fig. 4D). Besides, RIP assays with anti-IGF2BP antibodies further validated endogenous circLNF/IGF2BP association (Fig. 4E). These results establish circLNF as a specific binding partner of IGF2BP proteins in ESCC cells.

CircLNF/IGF2BP interaction via the “CAUC” motif contributes to ESCC progression

IGF2BP family proteins are known to recognize either “CAUC” [23] or “UGGAC” [24] RNA motifs. Bioinformatics analysis identified five putative IGF2BP-binding sites within circLNF, which we systematically interrogated using biotinylated RNA probes (p1 ~ p5 WT; Fig. 4F). Pull-down assays demonstrated selective enrichment of IGF2BP proteins exclusively by p1 and p2 probes (both containing “CAUC” motifs), while p3 ~ p5 probes (harboring “UGGAC” sequences) showed no binding activity (Fig. 4G). Mutation of the core “CAUC” sequences in both p1 and p2 probes (p1-mut and p2-mut) abolished IGF2BP binding (Fig. 4H), confirming the sequence-specific nature of this interaction. To biochemically validate direct physical interaction, we employed both RNA pull-down assays and RNA-EMSA experiments using recombinant IGF2BP proteins. Both approaches consistently demonstrated direct binding of IGF2BP proteins to “CAUC”-containing RNA probes (Fig. 4I, J).

To determine whether the “CAUC” motifs mediate circLNF-IGF2BP binding, we performed RNA pull-down assays using in vitro transcribed and circularized wild-type (WT) or mutant (mut) circLNF, in which the “CAUC” motifs were disrupted. The results showed that mutation of the “CAUC” sequences abolished the association of circLNF with IGF2BP proteins (Fig. 5A). To further validate this interaction in a cellular context, we generated stable KYSE150 and KYSE30 cell lines expressing either WT or “CAUC”-mutant circLNF (Fig. 5B). Subsequent RNA pull-down assays confirmed specific association of IGF2BP proteins with WT circLNF, while showing no detectable binding to the “CAUC”-mutant form (Fig. 5C). Moreover, RIP assays demonstrated that endogenous IGF2BP proteins preferentially bind WT circLNF over “CAUC” mutant, reinforcing the dependence of this interaction on intact “CAUC” motifs (Fig. 5D).

To delineate the contribution of circLNF/IGF2BP interaction in ESCC pathogenesis, we performed comparative functional analyses between WT circLNF and its IGF2BP-binding-deficient mutant (circLNF-mut). Functional assays revealed that while WT circLNF robustly enhanced cell proliferation, migration, and motility, circLNF-mut lost these oncogenic capacities (Fig. 5E-G and Fig. S4A). Consistent with the in vitro findings, in vivo experiments demonstrated that WT circLNF, rather than circLNF-mut, significantly promoted tumor growth in subcutaneous xenografts (Fig. 5H-J) and enhanced metastatic potential in tail-vein mouse models (Fig. 5K, L). Collectively, these findings indicate that circLNF promotes ESCC progression through functionally essential interactions with IGF2BP family proteins.

CircLNF stabilizes IGF2BP proteins by inhibiting ubiquitin-proteasomal degradation

Emerging evidence demonstrates that circRNAs can recruit RBPs to form regulatory complexes that modulate gene expression post-transcriptionally [11, 18]. To investigate whether circLNF affects IGF2BP protein stability, we performed immunoblot analysis in the aforementioned cell lines with modulated circLNF expression. Notably, circLNF overexpression significantly increased IGF2BP protein levels in both KYSE30 and KYSE150 cells, while circLNF knockdown reduced IGF2BP expression in Eca109 and KYSE410 cells (Fig. 6A and Fig. S4B) and corresponding pulmonary metastatic tissues (Fig. 6B). Importantly, this regulatory effect was strictly dependent on the circLNF/IGF2BP interaction, as circLNF-mut failed to alter IGF2BP protein levels in either cell line (Fig. 6C and Fig. S4C) or pulmonary metastatic tissues (Fig. 6D). Collectively, these results indicate that circLNF upregulates IGF2BP protein abundance through their direct interaction.

Consistent with previous reports of IGF2BP upregulation in ESCC [25], we first functionally validated their tumor-promoting roles in this malignancy. Genetic knockdown of IGF2BPs in Eca109 and KYSE410 cells significantly reduced cell proliferation, migration and motility (Fig. S5). This oncogenic activity was further corroborated in vivo, where IGF2BP2 depletion markedly suppressed tumor metastasis (Fig. S6). Importantly, the tumor-promoting effects of circLNF were abolished upon IGF2BPs knockdown (Fig. S7), establishing IGF2BPs as essential mediators of circLNF’s oncogenic functions in ESCC.

Given that protein abundance is regulated through transcriptional, translational, and post-translational mechanisms, and considering that circLNF did not alter IGF2BP mRNA levels (Fig. S8A, B), we hypothesized that circLNF might regulate IGF2BP protein stability. To test this, we performed cycloheximide (CHX) chase experiments and found that circLNF overexpression extended the half-life of IGF2BP proteins in KYSE30 cells (Fig. 6E), while circLNF knockdown accelerated IGF2BP protein degradation in Eca109 cells (Fig. 6F). As the ubiquitin-proteasome system is a major regulator of protein turnover, we treated cells with MG132, a proteasome inhibitor, which rescued the IGF2BP reduction caused by circLNF knockdown in both Eca109 (Fig. 6G) and KYSE410 cells (Fig. S8C). To directly assess ubiquitination status, we performed ubiquitination assays and found that WT circLNF, but not its IGF2BP-binding-deficient mutant, reduced IGF2BP polyubiquitination in KYSE30 cells (Fig. 6H and Fig. S8D). Conversely, circLNF knockdown increased polyubiquitination of IGF2BP proteins in Eca109 cells (Fig. 6I and Fig. S8E). Together, these results indicate that circLNF interacts with and stabilizes IGF2BP proteins by inhibiting their ubiquitin-proteasomal degradation.

CircLNF competitively inhibits TRIM25-mediated degradation of IGF2BP proteins

Building on previous findings identifying TRIM25 as the primary E3 ubiquitin ligase responsible for IGF2BP degradation in non-small cell lung cancer [19], we first confirmed this regulatory axis in ESCC. Our results demonstrate that TRIM25 overexpression reduced IGF2BP protein levels (Fig. 7A and Fig. S9A), while TRIM25 knockdown increased IGF2BP abundance (Fig. 7B and Fig. S9B). Co-IP assays proved the physical interaction between TRIM25 and IGF2BPs in KYSE410 cells (Fig. 7C), supporting IGF2BPs as authentic TRIM25 substrates in ESCC.

Given that circLNF reduces IGF2BP polyubiquitination (Fig. 6H), we hypothesized that circLNF competitively inhibits TRIM25-IGF2BP interactions. Three complementary lines of experimental evidence support this mechanism: (1) Co-IP assays showed that WT circLNF, but not the IGF2BP-binding-deficient mutant, reduced TRIM25-IGF2BP complex formation (Fig. 7D and E); (2) Immunoblot analysis confirmed circLNF expression did not alter TRIM25 protein levels (Fig. S9C); and (3) Transcriptomic analysis of 22 paired ESCC specimens and independent GEO database (NO. PRJNA533799) revealed no correlation between circLNF and TRIM25 expression (Fig. S9D, E). These results demonstrate that circLNF stabilizes IGF2BP proteins by competitively inhibiting TRIM25 binding without affecting TRIM25 expression levels in ESCC.

Clinical correlation between circLNF and IGF2BP1 protein expression in ESCC

Based on our mechanistic demonstration of circLNF-mediated IGF2BP stabilization, we evaluated the clinical relevance of this regulatory axis in ESCC patient specimens. Immunohistochemistry analysis of an ESCC tissue microarray (n = 37 evaluable cases from the same original cDNA profiling cohort) demonstrated a significant positive correlation between circLNF expression and IGF2BP1 protein abundance (Fig. 7F and Fig. S9F), suggesting the pathological significance of the circLNF-IGF2BP regulatory axis.

Discussion

Although circRNAs have emerged as important regulators in cancer biology, their functional roles in ESCC pathogenesis remain poorly understood. In this study, we identified circLNF as a markedly upregulated circRNA in ESCC that serves as a robust prognostic biomarker, with elevated expression strongly correlating with poor clinical outcomes. Through comprehensive gain- and loss-of-function studies, we demonstrated that circLNF potently promotes ESCC cell proliferation and migration in vitro while accelerating tumor growth and metastasis in vivo. Mechanistic investigations revealed that circLNF directly interacts with IGF2BP proteins through specific “CAUC” motifs, thereby competitively inhibiting TRIM25-mediated ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation. The significant positive correlation between circLNF expression and IGF2BP1 protein levels in clinical specimens further validates the pathological relevance of this regulatory axis. Collectively, our findings establish a novel molecular mechanism whereby circLNF drives ESCC progression through stabilizing IGF2BP oncoproteins, highlighting its potential as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target for ESCC.

Our study identifies circLNF as a potent oncogenic driver in ESCC, exhibiting functional parallels with hsa_circ_0001944, a circRNA derived from the same long non-coding RNA FIRRE locus (chrX:130883333–130928494) that has been implicated in osteosarcoma [5], breast cancer [26], and non-small cell lung cancer [27]. Despite possessing identical back-splice junctions, molecular characterization via RT-PCR and Sanger sequencing revealed that circLNF (845 nt) is 251 nucleotides shorter than circBase-annotated hsa_circ_0001944 (1096 nt). Expression profiling further distinguished these circRNAs as discrete molecular entities, with hsa_circ_0001944 exhibiting negligible levels in KYSE410 and ECA109 cells (Fig. S2). Bioinformatics analysis reveals that the FIRRE locus produces two transcript variants (NR_026975.2 and NR_152876.1) with different exon compositions, with circLNF originating from NR_026975.2 while hsa_circ_0001944 likely derives from NR_1528876.1. This exemplifies the capacity of a single genomic locus to generate multiple circRNA isoforms with identical junctions but variable internal sequences. Our results highlight the necessity of third-generation sequencing technologies, as their long-read capabilities enable accurately distinguish such circRNA isoforms.

Growing evidence demonstrates that circRNAs function as selective RBP binders through defined sequence motifs [19, 28]. The IGF2BP family proteins, known regulators of cell polarity and proliferation [29], were initially characterized by Hafner et al. to recognize “CAUH” (H = A/U/C) consensus elements in mRNAs [23]. Subsequent work by Huang et al. revealed that IGF2BPs can bind m6A-modified transcripts through the “UGGAC” sequence containing the core “GGAC” m6A motif [24]. Beyond mRNAs, IGF2BPs exhibit broad RNA-binding capacity that includes lncRNA [30] and circRNAs [31, 32]. Our biochemical analyses establish that circLNF directly interacts with all three IGF2BP family members via a “CAUC” recognition motif, distinct from the m6A-dependent “UGGAC” recognition pattern, suggesting circLNF’s UGGAC elements are non-methylated. Notably, the circRNA BISC exhibits selective binding to IGF2BP2 through a spacer-containing “CAC-|9–12|-XGGX” motif [33], indicating that IGF2BP binding specificity is determined by both primary sequence elements and higher-order structural organization of circRNAs.

CircRNA-RBP interactions represent a fundamental mechanism of post-translational regulation, with emerging roles in controlling protein stability. For example, circRHOBTB3 interacts with HuR protein and promotes β-Trcp1-mediated ubiquitination of HuR, ultimately enhancing its degradation [34], while circRNA-SORE stabilizes YBX1 protein by inhibiting PRP19-mediated YBX1 degradation [35]. Our results reveal that circLNF protects oncogenic IGF2BP proteins from TRIM25-mediated polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. The clinical relevance of this regulatory axis is supported by the positive correlation between circLNF expression and IGF2BP1 protein levels in ESCC patient specimens. Functional validation demonstrates that genetic disruption of the circLNF-IGF2BP interaction abolishes circLNF’s tumor-promoting activity, confirming the mechanistic dependence on protein stabilization. Although these findings establish the functional consequences of circLNF-IGF2BP interaction, the structural basis for TRIM25 inhibition remains unclear. Based on the fact that circACC1-induces AMPK conformational changes during metabolic stress [36], we hypothesize that circLNF binding may induce allosteric rearrangements in IGF2BPs that sterically hinder TRIM25 binding interfaces. This mechanistic insight will be investigated through structural and biochemical approaches in future studies.

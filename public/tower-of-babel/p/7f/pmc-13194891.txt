Optimal scheduling study of microgrids based on multistrategy improved sardine algorithm
Authors: Liming Wei, Heng Zhong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo enhance the utilization efficiency of wind and photovoltaic power generation in microgrids, this study develops an optimal scheduling model that incorporates multiple operational constraints, including power generation, energy storage, and electricity transactions while aiming to minimize the combined economic and environmental costs. In response to the common limitations of existing meta‑heuristic algorithms, such as premature convergence to local optima and insufficient solution accuracy when solving such models, a multi‑strategy improved sardine optimization algorithm is proposed. By integrating the core mechanisms of the sardine optimization algorithm and particle swarm optimization, along with four enhancement strategies, the proposed method achieves complementary advantages and significantly improves solution precision. Using twelve test functions from the CEC2022 benchmark set, a comparative evaluation of eight algorithms demonstrates that the proposed method outperforms others in both convergence speed and accuracy. When applied to the microgrid scheduling model, the algorithm effectively reduces the total system cost compared to traditional SOA, GWO, and PSO approaches, offering a novel and effective methodology for microgrid optimization dispatch.Keywords: Improving the sardine algorithm, Electricity transactions, Microgrid, Energy storage, Optimized schedulingSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

A microgrid is a small-scale power generation and distribution system that can achieve self-control1,2, protection and management, and can either operate in parallel with the external power grid or in isolation3,4. The basic structure of a microgrid includes a distributed power supply5, an energy storage device6, an energy conversion device, related loads and monitoring, a protection device, control system. The power sources in the microgrid are mostly distributed power sources with smaller capacities, such as micro gas turbines7, fuel cells, photovoltaic cells8, small wind turbines9, and energy storage devices such as supercapacitors, flywheels and batteries.

The operation modes of microgrids mainly include grid-connected and islanded modes10: (1) Grid-connected mode: In this mode, microgrids are connected to the main grid and can exchange power with each other. The microgrid can sell excess power to the main grid or buy power from the main grid when its generation is insufficient. In this mode, the voltage and frequency of the microgrid are usually maintained by the main grid, and the distributed power sources (e.g., photovoltaic, wind power) inside the microgrid operate in the PQ control mode, and do not directly participate in the voltage and frequency regulation of the grid. (2) Islanding mode11: When the microgrid detects a fault in the main grid or when the power quality does not meet the requirements, the microgrid will promptly disconnect from the main grid and operate independently to ensure the power supply of the important internal loads. In islanding mode, the microgrid must rely on its own power generation and energy storage equipment to maintain a power supply, which requires the microgrid to have sufficient energy self-sufficiency and a high level of automation control. The energy management of the system is divided into the following three states: firstly, the main grid is operating normally and the microgrid is running in parallel; secondly, the system is in the failure of the main grid; thirdly, the system is in the islanding state.

Related work

The optimal scheduling of microgrids has garnered significant research interest, particularly with the integration of advanced algorithm. Several studies have explored various optimization strategies to enhance microgrid operation efficiency, cost reduction, and renewable energy utilization.

Research on optimal microgrid scheduling has demonstrated a progressive and expanded scope. Wen et al.12 initially addressed the data foundation by innovatively employing a deep recurrent neural network based on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) to accurately forecast aggregated load and photovoltaic output in community microgrids, thereby providing critical input for optimization scheduling. Building on this, Yang et al.13 focused on core optimization algorithms, proposing strategies such as nonlinearly decreasing inertia weight and dynamically adjusting learning factors to enhance the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm, which significantly improved its convergence speed and solution quality in addressing scheduling problems. Fu et al.14 further integrated prediction with scheduling by developing a hybrid model combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD), and LSTM, aiming to optimize the coordinated dispatch of generation, load, and storage through improved short-term load forecasting accuracy. Marichelvam et al.15 demonstrated the versatility of the improved PSO algorithm by successfully applying it to NP-hard multi-stage hybrid flow shop scheduling problems that consider human factors. In terms of specialization within energy systems, Luo et al.16 empirically validated the advantages of their enhanced algorithm in microgrids incorporating various renewable energy sources, showing superior computational speed and accuracy compared to Genetic Algorithms and standard PSO. Meanwhile, Bouakkaz et al.17 verified the practical value of PSO in residential microgrids for achieving cost savings and energy consumption reduction. To address system uncertainties, Chen et al.18 proposed a demand response-based energy management strategy, with the core innovation of balancing energy storage and load to extend battery life and reduce operational costs in islanded microgrids. Wynn et al.19 developed a decentralized energy management model that accounts for uncertainties in renewable generation and load, utilizing demand response to minimize operational costs in dynamic environments. Wu et al.20 introduced stochastic optimization into combined cooling, heating, and power systems, employing strategies such as Cauchy variation and adaptive mutation to escape local optima. Finally, Liu et al.21 focused on extreme scenarios by proposing a two-phase adaptive robust optimization model for unintentional islanding scheduling, innovatively highlighting through comparison that robust optimization offers higher reliability in withstanding worst-case uncertainties but may involve a trade-off of increased costs.

In the field of microgrid optimal scheduling, the application and improvement of meta‑heuristic algorithms constitute a key research direction. Studies have adopted various hybrid strategies to enhance core algorithms according to different scenarios and objectives. For instance, Dong and Lee22 developed an improved differential evolution algorithm incorporating chaotic mapping for multi‑microgrid systems and constructed a dispatch model that accounts for demand response and uncertainties, effectively improving both economic performance and solution accuracy. Guan et al.23 aimed at minimizing operational and environmental costs as a multi‑objective problem and enhanced the particle swarm optimization algorithm by dynamically adjusting inertia weights and learning factors, demonstrating its superiority in reducing overall costs. He et al.24 addressed the cooperative economic dispatch of multi‑microgrids with high penetration of renewable energy by introducing elite preservation, catastrophe strategy, and mutation direction mechanisms from particle swarm optimization into the genetic algorithm, which significantly accelerated convergence and avoided local optima. Further extending this line of research, Zhou et al.25 introduced Tent chaotic mapping and the simplex method to strengthen the global search capability of the butterfly optimization algorithm. They established a microgrid cluster model incorporating photovoltaics, wind turbines, energy storage, diesel generators, and natural gas units, which significantly reduces system operating costs in both grid‑connected and islanded modes. Similarly, Suresh V et al.26 tested algorithms including genetic algorithm, particle swarm optimization, ant colony optimization, and the recently proposed political optimizer on the IEEE 30‑bus system using the MATPOWER toolset. Their results indicated that the ant colony‑based algorithm outperformed others in terms of convergence speed, solution quality, and stability, and was subsequently applied to a practical microgrid case to minimize the levelized cost of energy. These studies collectively demonstrate that enhancing traditional meta‑heuristic algorithms through hybrid strategies and adaptive parameter mechanisms can substantially improve their optimization performance and practical utility in solving complex microgrid scheduling problems (Table 1).

Motivation

This study is fundamentally motivated by these identified gaps. We contend that merely applying an off-the-shelf meta-heuristic is inadequate for the high-stakes optimization required in modern microgrids. Instead, a dedicated effort to design, deeply hybridize, and strategically enhance a meta-heuristic algorithm is necessary to achieve the requisite level of solution quality, reliability, and computational efficiency. Our work, therefore, positions itself not as a simple application of an algorithm, but as a targeted algorithmic innovation designed to fill these voids. By developing a novel multi-strategy improved sardine optimization algorithm through the deep fusion of SOA and PSO cores, and by rigorously validating its superior performance against benchmarks and in a practical microgrid scheduling model, this research aims to provide a more powerful and reliable methodological tool. This advancement directly addresses the core motivation: to enable microgrids to achieve significantly higher levels of economic performance and environmental efficiency through superior optimization, thereby strengthening their role in a sustainable energy future.

Based on the improved sardine algorithm

Microgrid optimal scheduling problem solving has multi-dimensional, non-linear and multi-constraint characteristics, the sardine algorithm is a new algorithm proposed in 2023, which is superior to other traditional algorithms in many tests of CEC2022 test function, so this paper proposes a multi-strategy improved sardine algorithm by analyzing the traditional sardine, combining its advantages and disadvantages, and showing its superiority to the readers through the twelve test functions.

Traditional sardine algorithm

Sardines is a collective term that includes sardines, the genus Sardinella, and the genus Sardinella. As a variable population size optimization algorithm, SOA simulates sardine survival philosophies such as transformation, migration, reproduction, elimination and dispersal.

The sardine population consists of sardine schools and individual surviving sardine schools. Specifically, the sardine school set contains all sardine schools, each of which is a group of sardines moving in the same direction. All sardine schools are of the same size. Furthermore, said single surviving sardine group comprises all individual surviving sardines. A single sardine in a sardine school and a single surviving sardine in a single surviving sardine group correspond to a real number coding sequence.

Agility in wild animals is essential for them to adapt to their environment. Similarly, agile localization and global strategies are key points of SOA. The functional difference between agile localization strategy and agile global strategy is explained as follows: in the agile local search strategy, the adjacency period ratio AR is used as a short-term factor to control the local search. In the agile global search strategy, the corresponding period ratio CR is used as a long-term factor to control the global search. If the neighborhood period ratio of a school is smaller than the average of the neighborhood period ratios of all schools, the school migrates via Levy flights. Levy flights can randomly move the center of a sardine swarm C. In other words, when a school triggers the constraint on the average of the neighborhood period ratios, this type of movement can help that school to jump out of the current local optimum. In addition, this expands the global search range of the sardine swarm and avoids wasting computational resources in these local optimal regions.

There are elliptic, slender and crescent operators in the transformation process of the sardine school. I explain the search behavior characteristics of the three transformation operators as follows: the 13 sardine schools generated by the elliptic operator are relatively uniform and equally spaced. In contrast, the distribution of the sardine schools generated by the elongated shape operator enhances the search effort in a particular direction. Similarly, the crescent shape operator enhanced the search effort in two specific directions. During the transformation process, the sardine swarm is constantly transformed from one shape to another. Generation after generation, there are too many combinations of these three shapes. As the number of generations increases, these combinations of shapes ensure a variety of search trajectories in a localized area.

Reproduction and elimination are used as natural selection in sardine schools and individual surviving sardines. In SOA, how offspring are produced and sardines eliminated is determined by the processes of reproduction and elimination. There are two evolutionary lines in the sardine population (i.e., the evolutionary line of the sardine population and the evolutionary line of the individual surviving sardine population), which are closely related to reproduction and elimination in the SOA. The evolutionary line of the sardine population is parallel to the evolutionary line of the single surviving sardine population. This parallel model enriches the search behavior in the collection of sardine schools and the collection of individual surviving sardines, thus ensuring robustness.

In principle, the nocturnal propagation behavior is only used to search promising regions. In addition, these promising areas may include new and better solutions, since these areas have been chosen to use the evolution of the sardine population during the day. In other words, searching for sardines at night belongs to a later stage of the SOA. Therefore, a limited search effort during nighttime dispersal behavior is used only in these promising areas and a promising area to find a school only searches for one surviving sardine. This is effective in increasing the probability of finding a new and better solution. Unlike previous defense and breeding behavior during the day, sardines spread at night and do not swim as schools.

Avoiding extreme scheduling — Compared to the purely economic cost model and the purely environmental cost model , the total cost minimisation model prioritises the use of clean energy and energy storage during periods of high electricity prices and emission costs, while moderately utilizing economic power sources during acceptable periods, thereby achieving a smooth and flexible output curve. 2. Intelligent energy storage coordination — The energy storage strategy integrates economic arbitrage and environmental benefits, charging during periods of low electricity prices and low emissions, and discharging during periods of high electricity prices and high emissions. Compared to single-objective models (pure arbitrage or pure emissions reduction), this significantly improves overall utilisation rates. 3. Efficient Regulation of Fossil Fuels — Gas turbines serve as flexible regulation units, operating intermittently only during periods of optimal total cost. This reduces pollution compared to continuous high-power operation under purely economic modes and ensures power supply reliability compared to complete shutdown under purely environmental modes. 4. Maximising the value of clean energy — Fully utilising photovoltaic/wind power under environmental constraints, using energy storage and flexible power sources to smooth out fluctuations, balancing high utilisation rates with system stability.

Although the HSOA algorithm proposed in this study demonstrates excellent performance in microgrid optimization dispatch, it must be noted that related work remains in the preliminary stage of algorithm validation and model construction, with areas warranting further exploration and refinement. At the model level, current research employs a relatively typical deterministic optimization framework, primarily focused on validating algorithm effectiveness under fixed scenarios. Future work should address the systematic modeling of inherent uncertainties in renewable energy output and load demand. Regarding system details, to concentrate on analyzing the core optimization mechanism, the model reasonably simplifies complex engineering factors such as network topology constraints, equipment dynamic characteristics, and long-term operational losses. In terms of validation scope, the algorithm’s effectiveness and parameter robustness have been primarily demonstrated in specific simulation cases. Its broad applicability across microgrid systems of varying scales and configurations, as well as in real-world physical environments, requires more comprehensive investigation.

Conclusion

This study formulates an objective function for microgrid optimization that simultaneously minimizes economic and environmental costs, addressing growing global environmental concerns. Constraints for individual distributed power sources are incorporated within this model. The performance of an improved sardine algorithm is evaluated against both the original sardine algorithm and other contemporary intelligent algorithms. Comparative analysis using standard test functions demonstrates that the improved algorithm achieves faster convergence and higher solution accuracy. Under identical microgrid operating conditions, the improved algorithm reduces total costs by 25.2% lower than that of the original SOA and enhances operational stability. These results confirm the superior optimization accuracy and overall performance of the improved sardine algorithm.

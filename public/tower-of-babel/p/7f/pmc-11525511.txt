Characterization and Inversion Method of Relative Permeability of Emulsion Flooding
Authors: Zhenlei Zhang, Xiao Li, Minghui Gao, Zhigang Sun, Diansheng Wang, Lei Zhu, Jian Hou, Yudou Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Relative permeability curves are one of the crucial factors that control the behavior of multiphase flow in petroleum reservoirs. Accurate prediction of the relative permeability curve involving emulsification and emulsion transport in emulsion flooding is vital to enhancing heavy oil recovery. In this study, a relative permeability characterization model including the emulsification mechanism and emulsion transport is developed, in which residual oil saturation and end-point permeability for water are parametrized as functions of emulsion concentration and capillary number. Based on the built characterization model, an inversion method (ensemble Kalman filter) is used to estimate the relative permeability curves of emulsion flooding. The inversion results of four sets of emulsion flooding experiments show that the errors of end points between the Johnson–Bossler–Neumann method and inversion method are less than 10%. For the continuous emulsification process in the in situ emulsion flooding tests, the inversed relative permeability curves shift between the upper and the inferior limits of the permeability. The error of residual oil saturation between the inversion and the experiment is less than 10%. In summary, this study demonstrates the feasibility of using the characterization model and inversion method to infer the relative permeability curves of emulsion flooding, which can aid in the predictions of multiphase flow in heavy oil reservoirs.

Introduction

Heavy oil is highly viscous, making it challenging to flow to production wells under normal reservoir conditions. This inherent difficulty complicates its exploitation.1−3 One potential solution for aiding in the extraction of heavy oil is the use of chemical additives, such as surfactants, polymers, nanofluids, and foam.4−6 These chemical additives have been shown to significantly reduce the interfacial tension or interfacial energy between the oil–water interface.4,7−9 As a result, the heavy oil can be easily emulsified with minimal energy input to form an oil-in-water (O/W) emulsion that flows easily within heavy oil reservoirs. Hence, emulsification is one of the most important oil displacement mechanisms in chemical flooding.10−12 Additionally, most researchers agree that the emulsion transport has the additional benefit of decreasing the mobility of injected water and mitigating viscous fingering.13−16 This enhances sweep efficiency by diverting the flow of injected water.17−21 Overall, the generation and transport of emulsions enhance the oil displacement efficiency and improve sweep efficiency. Therefore, it is imperative to gain a comprehensive understanding of the emulsification and emulsion transport within the porous medium for enhancing heavy oil recovery.

Relative permeability is a crucial petrophysical property that is utilized to characterize the flow of oil and water in porous media.22,23 It is well established that the formation and transport of emulsions have an impact on the shape of the relative permeability curves. On the one hand, some theoretical models have been utilized to characterize the relative permeability of emulsion flooding. Sarwaruddin and Burzlaff developed a few new equations to address the in situ emulsification of heavy oil. In this model, the emulsion phase carries both water and oil. The shape of the relative permeability curve is influenced by the volume fraction of oil and the viscosity of the phase. This model is successfully applied in California heavy oil reservoir for modeling production performance.23 By considering the physicochemical properties of emulsions, Sun proposed a three-phase (continuous oil phase, dispersed oil phase, and continuous water phase) relative permeability model to investigate the impact of the relative size of the pores and droplets on relative permeability curve.24 The other model assumes that the emulsion displacement mechanism can also be simulated by modifying the end point of the relative permeability curves. The reduction of residual oil saturation and the end point of water relative permeability is attributed to the emulsification process and the mobility control exerted by the emulsion.25 Although previous studies have examined the effects of capillary number and emulsion concentration on the end point values of relative permeability curve,10,25 it is still challenging to establish precise mathematical relationships that encompass all the influencing factors.

On the other hand, the laboratory experiment is another way to obtain relative permeability curves of emulsion flooding. McAuliffe performed the sandstone experiment to investigate the relative permeability curve of emulsion flooding, the results showed that the emulsion was efficient in reducing water permeability.26 Engelke conducted steady-state experiments to evaluate the relative permeability curve of emulsion flow. The results showed that emulsion injection decreased the residual oil saturation and reduced the end point of the water relative permeability curve compared to waterflooding.27 The relative permeability curves obtained from the experimental studies mentioned primarily focus on the transport of the emulsion. However, it is difficult to obtain relative permeability curves that cover both emulsification and emulsion transport using conventional experimental methods. An accurate mathematical description of the factors influencing the relative permeability curve is not yet available. Therefore, in numerical simulations, interpolation of relative permeability curves is often employed to account for the transitions between different scenarios.16,28−30 Among these, Wang and Perez–Perez employed two sets of relative permeability curves to characterize the different situations.16,28 In addition, there are some limitations in obtaining the relative permeability curves by experimental methods. The steady-state core flooding experiment takes a long time to reach a steady flow state, while the unsteady-state core flooding experiment cannot account for complex physicochemical properties. Additionally, the accuracy of the relative permeability curve measured by experimental methods is low due to the assumptions made.31−33 Hence, it holds practical and fundamental significance to accurately assess the relative permeability curves by considering both emulsification and emulsion transport.

History matching offers a more accurate solution for estimating the relative permeability curve by aligning with production data obtained from displacement experiments.31,34 During history matching, the optimization algorithm continuously adjusts the relative permeability of the numerical simulation model to achieve the best match between the numerical simulation results and experimental results. Using this method, Zhang and Liu obtained inverted relative permeability curves by incorporating the adsorption and diffusion properties of polymers and surfactants through matching polymer and surfactant flooding experiments.35,36 The ensemble Kalman filter (EnKF), initially introduced by Evensen in the context of ocean dynamics literature,37 had been extensively employed as an efficient inversion method for addressing the history matching problem.38−41 Since its introduction into the petroleum engineering literature,42 EnKF had been successfully applied to automatic history matching of some practical oil reservoirs and experiments.33,34,38,43,44 The history matching method allows for the consideration of additional physicochemical properties and complicated flow mechanisms. However, the relative permeability inversion method fully considering both heavy oil emulsification and emulsion transport has not been developed.

Considering the limited research on the impact of heavy oil emulsification and emulsion transport on emulsion flooding relative permeability curves, this paper develops a characterization model and inversion method to obtain the relative permeability curves of emulsion flooding. The paper is organized as follows. First, a relative permeability characterization model including the emulsification mechanism and emulsion transport is developed based on pregenerated emulsion flooding experiments. Based on this characterization model, an inversion method (ensemble Kalman filter) is used to estimate the relative permeability curves of emulsion flooding. The accuracy of the inversion method is verified by comparing the experimental and inverted relative permeability curves. Finally, based on the proposed inversion method, this paper presents and discusses the inversed relative permeability of in situ emulsion flooding.

Methodology

Experimental Method

To establish the relative permeability characterization model of emulsion flooding, it is necessary to investigate the variables influencing the relative permeability curves. In this study, the Johnson–Bossle–Neumann (JBN) method36,45 was used to obtain the relative permeability curve of emulsion flooding. To ensure the accuracy of the relative permeability curves measured using this method, the pregenerated oil-in-water (O/W) emulsion flood tests were initially conducted by injecting the preprepared emulsion. The pregenerated emulsion guaranteed the stability of the emulsion phase throughout the experiment.27,46 A significant number of stable emulsion droplets were present in the injected pregenerated emulsion. As a result, the transport of emulsion has a more prominent impact in pregenerated emulsion flooding experiments compared to the emulsification process.

However, there is an in situ emulsification process in tertiary oil recovery especially chemically enhanced oil recovery (EOR) process.47−49 Due to the prominent role of emulsion flooding in EOR process, in situ emulsion flooding by injecting surfactant solution has become a research topic.12 To explore the relative permeability of in situ emulsion flooding, a series of in situ emulsion flooding experiments were then carried out by injecting surfactant solutions. These experiments offered valuable experimental data for history matching in subsequent numerical simulation studies.

Materials and Instruments

The mineral oil from the Shengli Oilfield was used in all groups of experiments. At the experimental temperature (60 °C), the mineral oil viscosity was μo = 205 mPa·s. Sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS, white powder, purity of >98%) was obtained from National Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd. China. Sodium chloride (NaCl, purity of >99.5%) was obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd., China. JY92-II ultrasonic generator (Nanjing Xinchen Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China), and XREM-200 cantilever type electric agitator (Jintan City Xinrui Instruments Factory, China) were employed.

Preparation of Emulsion

The NaCl brine was kept the same for all the experiments, which was 10 g/L. Surfactant solutions with mass fractions of 0.05, 0.1, and 0.5 wt % were prepared using the agitator for 10 min by addition of designed amount of SDS powder into NaCl brine. The prepared surfactant solution was mixed with the experimental mineral oil according to the oil:water ratio of 1:5, 1:4, 1:3, 1:2, and 1:1. The above sample was emulsified by the ultrasonic generator at 10 kHz for 30 min and then aging for 24 h. All emulsion samples were prepared at a temperature of 60 °C.

Results and Discussion

Characterization of Relative Permeability of Emulsion Flooding

In this section, a relative permeability characterization model of emulsion flooding is established by analyzing pregenerated emulsion flooding experiments. An accurate relative permeability characterization model is required to implicitly estimate relative permeability for assisted history matching.33 Characterization models of the relative permeability curve are mainly divided into two types: parametric model and nonparametric model.34,36 The parametric model uses explicit equations to generate the relative permeability curves assuming that the relative permeability curves follow a specific function. The nonparametric model is versatile and flexible as there is no assumption regarding the shape of the relative permeability curves.35,59 Since the mechanism of heavy oil emulsification and emulsion transport needs to be considered, in this study, the parametric model is used to represent the emulsion flooding relative permeability curves as shown below.

As one of the parametric models, the Brooks–Corey model is widely used for consolidated porous media.36 In the present study, the Brooks and Corey model44,60 is selected to characterize the emulsion flooding relative permeability curves. The m is the power coefficient, krw,max and kro,max are the maximal relative permeabilities for water and oil, respectively. The generation and transport of emulsions can significantly enhance displacement efficiency and sweep coefficient in the extraction of heavy oil, which means that it can change the shape of the relative permeability curves. The heightened displacement efficiency contributes to a reduction in residual oil saturation. Additionally, the increased sweep coefficient indicates that the presence of oil droplets reduces the mobility of the aqueous phase at residual oil saturation.27 Based on this, we hypothesized that the generation and transport of emulsions can affect the residual oil saturation (So,irr) and end-point permeability for water (krw,max). To determine the factors affecting the residual oil saturation and end-point permeability for water, several pregenerated emulsion flooding experiments were conducted. The decrease in residual oil saturation is not only caused by the blockage of the emulsion but also involves the formation of the emulsion.53,63,64 To explore the influence of the emulsification mechanism on the relative permeability curve during emulsion flooding, three sets of experiments with different surfactant concentrations were conducted (see cases 1 to 3 in Table 1). The relative permeability curves by emulsion flooding at different surfactant concentrations are shown in Figure 6. These experiments were conducted at the same injection flow rate (0.5 mL/min). The oil/water ratio was fixed at 1:4 to ensure that the blockage effect of emulsion remained the same in all three experiments. The blue box denotes the area where the oil relative permeability curves is enlarged. The results show that as the surfactant concentration increases, residual oil saturation decreases. For instance, So, irr= 0.49 at Cs = 0.05 (wt %), So,irr = 0.46 at Cs = 0.1 (wt %), and So,irr = 0.44 at Cs = 0.5 (wt %). The oil–water interfacial tension, i.e., the interfacial energy, decreases with the increase in the surfactant concentration. Therefore, the energy required to overcome the surface energy to form the emulsion will be reduced. Consequently, more heavy oil will be emulsified easily under a higher surfactant concentration. It is worth noting that the decrease in residual oil saturation is also attributable to the decrease in capillary resistance caused by reduced interfacial tension.65 Therefore, it is difficult to accurately characterize the reduction in residual oil saturation due to emulsification alone.

Inversion of the Relative Permeability of Emulsion Flooding

With the appropriate representation for relative permeability, the relative permeability curves can be inferred based on the experimental data by employing the ensemble Kalman method. The parameters (f1–f6) of the relative permeability model are repeated and adjusted automatically according to the simulation results until all the observed data (the measured data of experiments) are assimilated. The approximation relative permeability curves are obtained when the optimization process is terminated.

To evaluate the feasibility of the proposed method for inferring relative permeability curves, four sets of experiments with different oil–water ratios were first matched (cases 4–7 in Table 1). The injection pressure and water cut were treated as the observed data. The experimental and predicted water cuts and pressure drops are shown in Figures 8 and 9, respectively. As can be seen, the residual oil saturations were predicted well within the acceptable errors (all the errors of the difference between the JBN method and the inversion method are less than 10%). It is worth noting that the predicted values of residual oil saturation are all lower than the values obtained by the JBN method. This is because it is impossible to completely displace all of the oil that can flow in the experiment. Different from the known emulsion concentration in the pregenerated emulsion flooding tests, the in situ emulsion flooding tests could not determine the emulsion concentration accurately. Observed the microscopic graph of experimentally produced emulsion in the in situ emulsion flooding test was observed as shown in Figure 14. It is obvious that as the experimental time increases, the emulsion concentration gradually decreases. The result presented in Figure 14 can be explained as follows. At the beginning of the experiment, more oil can be emulsified in the core, resulting in a higher emulsion concentration. With the increase in the displacement time, oil is continuously displaced from the core. Less oil can be emulsified at the late stage, resulting in a decrease in the emulsion concentration. Therefore, for such a continuous emulsification process in the core, the relative permeability curves shift constantly under the influence of the emulsion concentration.

Conclusion

To enhance our understanding of the heavy oil emulsification mechanism and emulsion transport, this study proposes a characterization model and an inversion method to obtain the relative permeability curves of emulsion flooding. The relative permeability characterization model that includes both the emulsification mechanism and emulsion transport is first developed. The impact of the emulsion concentration and capillary number on residual oil saturation and end-point permeability for water is investigated. Four sets of pregenerated emulsion flooding experiments were inverted first by this inversion method to verify the accuracy of the characterization model. Ultimately, the relative permeability curves of the in situ emulsion flooding tests were obtained. The main conclusions are the following:(1)It is found that the relative permeability curve is affected by emulsion concentration and capillary number, so both emulsion concentration and capillary number are considered as the factors affecting the end points in our characterization model. The corresponding empirical functions for end points are obtained by fitting the experimental results.(2)The inversion results of four sets of emulsion flooding experiments show that the errors of end points between the JBN method and inversion method are less than 10%. It is therefore feasible to predict relative permeability based on the established relative permeability characterization model.(3)For the continuous emulsification process in the in situ emulsion flooding tests, the inversion results show that the relative permeability curves shift between the upper and inferior limits of the permeability. A reference relative permeability is calculated from the average concentration of the produced emulsion. On this basis, the error in residual oil saturation between the inversion and the experiment is within 10%. Therefore, the inversion method based on EnKF algorithm is feasible in obtaining the relative permeability curves for continuous emulsification process. This work has great potential applications in the predictions of multiphase flow within heavy oil reservoirs.

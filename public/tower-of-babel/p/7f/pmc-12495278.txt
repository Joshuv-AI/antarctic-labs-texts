Fatal immune checkpoint inhibitor-related pneumonitis following postoperative nivolumab administration in a patient with unclassifiable interstitial pneumonia and resected lung squamous cell carcinoma: A case report
Authors: Eitetsu Koh, Yasuo Sekine, Kenzo Hiroshima
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitor-related pneumonitis (ICI-P) is a known but potentially fatal complication in patients with underlying interstitial lung disease (ILD). However, reports of ICI-P in the postoperative setting remain scarce.Case presentationWe present a 67-year-old man with unclassifiable interstitial pneumonia who underwent right upper lobectomy for stage IB squamous cell carcinoma. Two cycles of adjuvant nivolumab were administered. Ten days after the second cycle, the patient developed fever and progressive dyspnea. Chest CT revealed diffuse ground-glass opacities and consolidation in both lungs. Despite initial improvement with high-dose corticosteroids, respiratory failure recurred and progressed rapidly. The patient died 21 days after onset. Pathological review of the resected lung confirmed fibrosis compatible with unclassifiable interstitial pneumonia and squamous cell carcinoma. No autopsy was performed.ConclusionThis case highlights the potentially fatal risk of postoperative nivolumab therapy in patients with pre-existing ILD, even in the setting of unclassifiable interstitial pneumonia. Careful risk–benefit assessment is warranted when considering adjuvant immune checkpoint inhibitors in this population.Keywords: Immune checkpoint inhibitor, Pneumonitis, Interstitial pneumonia, Nivolumab, Lung squamous cell carcinoma, Postoperative immunotherapy

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have become a standard therapy for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), including in the adjuvant setting [1,2]. However, their use in patients with pre-existing interstitial lung disease (ILD) carries substantial risks, particularly the development of immune checkpoint inhibitor-related pneumonitis (ICI-P). Although postoperative administration of nivolumab has shown clinical benefit in certain NSCLC populations, the safety of ICIs in patients with background ILD remains unclear. We present a case of fatal ICI-P following adjuvant nivolumab in a patient with unclassifiable ILD and surgically resected lung squamous cell carcinoma (see Table 1).

Case Presentation

A 67-year-old male ex-smoker (40 pack-years) with no history of autoimmune disease was diagnosed with stage IB (pT2aN0M0) squamous cell carcinoma of the right upper lobe. High-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) at diagnosis revealed peripheral reticulation and subpleural changes in both lower lobes, consistent with interstitial pneumonia; however, the pattern was not classifiable as typical UIP or NSIP. Pulmonary function tests showed preserved lung volumes and moderate diffusion impairment (DLCO 52 %).

The patient underwent right upper lobectomy and mediastinal lymph node dissection (Fig. 1) Postoperative recovery was uneventful. Histology confirmed moderately differentiated squamous cell carcinoma with no lymph node involvement. Non-neoplastic lung tissue displayed patchy interstitial fibrosis with subpleural accentuation and fibroblastic foci, leading to a final diagnosis of unclassifiable interstitial pneumonia (see Fig. 2).

Adjuvant nivolumab (240 mg biweekly) was initiated six weeks postoperatively. The first dose was tolerated without adverse effects. Ten days after the second dose, the patient developed fever and worsening dyspnea. Physical examination revealed fine crackles at the bilateral lung bases. HRCT showed new bilateral ground-glass opacities with areas of consolidation, most prominent in the lower lobes. Blood tests revealed elevated CRP and LDH; procalcitonin was normal.

Time course after onset of pneumonitis. Clinical course following the onset of pneumonitis: initial improvement with high-dose corticosteroids was followed by rapid deterioration and death despite ongoing immunosuppressive therapy.

Empiric antibiotics and high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone (1 g/day for 3 days) were administered for suspected ICI-P. Oxygenation improved transiently, but respiratory failure recurred on day 14 of illness. Follow-up CT revealed radiologic progression. Despite continued corticosteroid therapy, the patient's condition deteriorated, and he died of respiratory failure on day 21. No autopsy was performed.

Given clinical suspicion of steroid-refractory ICI pneumonitis at relapse, second-line immunosuppression (e.g., infliximab, intravenous immunoglobulin [IVIG], or mycophenolate mofetil) was considered in line with contemporary guidance; infliximab was deferred due to concern for superimposed infection despite broad-spectrum antibiotics, and IVIG or mycophenolate could not be initiated before rapid deterioration [[7], [8], [9], [10]].

Discussion

ICI-P is a potentially fatal complication of immunotherapy, particularly in patients with underlying ILD [[3], [4], [5], [6]]. Previous studies have suggested that background ILD increases both the incidence and severity of ICI-P [3,6]. In the adjuvant setting, the risk may be further amplified by surgical stress and immunologic activation.

This case is notable in that the patient had no clinical exacerbation during or after surgery but developed fatal pneumonitis following only two doses of nivolumab. The ILD pattern was unclassifiable, but histology revealed fibrosis with a UIP-like tendency, a phenotype associated with higher ICI-P risk [3]. While corticosteroids initially improved symptoms, the recurrence and rapid progression suggest a fulminant ICI-P course.

For steroid-refractory ICI pneumonitis—often defined as lack of clinical improvement after 48–72 hours of high-dose corticosteroids—second-line immunosuppression may be considered. Guidelines and consensus statements describe options such as infliximab, IVIG, and mycophenolate mofetil, while acknowledging the low-quality evidence base [[7], [8], [9]]. Small series and case reports suggest that IVIG can be beneficial in selected patients, whereas data for infliximab are mixed and infectious risks must be carefully weighed [10,11]. In our case, the combination of rapid deterioration and concern for occult infection led us to avoid TNF-α blockade, and there was insufficient time to initiate IVIG or mycophenolate before death.

Conclusion

This case highlights the potential lethality of adjuvant ICI therapy in patients with pre-existing ILD, even in those with unclassifiable disease. Physicians should carefully assess the risk–benefit profile of postoperative immunotherapy in such patients and consider alternative strategies when appropriate.

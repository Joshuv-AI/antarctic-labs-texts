Genome-wide identification and analysis of auxin response factors in peels of wax apple at different fruit development stages
Authors: Xueqin Zhang, Haibo Ouyang, Lixia Lin, Ruiyun Lai, Jianzhong Lin, Zanhua Zhong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, we used transcriptomic data to screen the auxin response factor gene family in the peels of wax apple at different stages of fruit development, in which fourteen auxin response factor genes (SsARFs) were identified and analyzed. The results showed that the 14 SsARFs were hydrophilic proteins located in the nucleus, with a higher ratio of serine and leucine, and a lower ratio of cysteine and tryptophan. These SsARF proteins consisted of irregularly coiled, extended strands, alpha-helices and beta-turns, most of which contained a B3 DNA-binding domain (B3-DBD), a middle region (MR) and a C-terminal dimerization domain (CTD), whereas some of them lacked a CTD, including SsARF6b, SsARF16, SsARF17, SsARF18 and SsARF22. Phylogenetic tree analysis revealed that the SsARFs were classified into five clusters. The expression of seven SsARFs were up-regulated, while the others were down-regulated. The protein interaction network of SsARFs showed strong interactions among SsARF4, SsARF5, SsARF6a, SsARF6b, SsARF17, SsARF19a, and SsARF19b, which interacted strongly with IAA12, IAA14 and IAA17. Therefore, SsARF6a, SsARF6b, SsARF17, SsARF19a, and SsARF19b, may play an important role in the auxin signal transduction pathway during wax apple fruit development.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-53117-y.

Introduction

Wax apple (Syzygium samarangense Bl. Merr. et Perry), also termed Java apple and wax jambu, is a non-climacteric tropical fruit that belongs to the genus of Syzygium in the Myrtaceae family and is native to the Malay Archipelago1–3. It is an economically important, popular exotic fruit crop, and widely cultivated throughout the southeast Asian countries such as Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, China and India4,5. Wax apple can fruit in any given season under proper cultivation measures because of the strong flowering ability6. Wax apple fruits, known as potent antioxidants, are usually eaten fresh, which rich in antioxidant compounds (such as ellagitannins, flavonol glycosides, anthocyanidins, triterpenoids, proanthocyanidins) beneficial to human health, and used in traditional medicine for treatment of bronchitis, asthma, and inflammation syndromes7–11.

Numerous aspects of plant development and physiological processes are regulated by auxin, involving in apical dominance, vascular elongation, flowering, and fruit development and ripening12,13. Auxin response factor (ARF) is transcription factor that regulates the transcription of auxin-responsive genes during plant growth and development including fruit development14. ARFs can activate or repress the expression of early/primary auxin response genes by binding to the auxin response element (AuxRE) site in their promoter regions. A typical ARF is characterized by a highly conserved N-terminal B3-type DNA binding domain (DBD) that recognizes the AuxRE motif, as well as an activation domain or repression domain, and a C-terminal dimerization domain (domain III/IV), which is involved in protein-protein interactions by dimerizing with auxin/indole-3-acetic acid (Aux/IAA) family genes15. ARFs play a pivotal role in regulating plant growth and development via the auxin signaling pathway16–19. Due to the significance of ARFs, their genome-wide characterization has been completed in many species such as model plants Arabidopsis16 and tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)20,21, fruit trees like citrus (Citrus sinensis)22, apple (Malus domestica)23, litchi (Litchi chinensis Sonn.)19, melon (Cucumis melo L.)24, blueberry (Vaccinium spp.)25, fig (Ficus carica L.)26 and so on. However, the ARF gene family and the functions of ARFs in wax apple remain unknown.

Research into the molecular biology of the wax apple began recently. The first high-quality wax apple genome was assembled and reported by the Fruit Research Institute of the Fujian Academy of Agricultural Sciences in 2023, which presented a haplotype-resolved autotetraploid genome assembly of wax apple, sized at 1.59 Gb. Comparative genomic analysis revealed three rounds of whole-genome duplication (WGD), including two independent WGDs following WGT-γ. Transcriptomic analysis of three different varieties during flower and fruit development identified key genes related to fruit size, sugar content and male sterility. The chromosome-scale genome and large-scale transcriptome data presented in the study provide valuable new resources for the biological research of wax apple and shed new light on the regulation of fruit size, sugar metabolism and male sterility. However, there are still no reports on the ARF gene family and their functions in wax apple5.

To improve any fruit traits, it is necessary to study the characteristics of ARF gene family in wax apple at different fruit development stages. Therefore, we performed a genome-wide survey of the ARF gene family in wax apple and carried out the comprehensive analysis of them, including physicochemical properties, subcellular localization, amino acid composition, secondary and tertiary structure, conserved protein motifs, phylogenetic analyses, and protein interactions. The expression profiling of ARF genes in peels of wax apple were determined by RNA-seq and real-time quantitative PCR (qRT-PCR). Our study will contribute to future research for cloning and addressing the functional roles of SsARF gene family, exploring the mechanism of ARF family regulating fruit development and ripening in wax apple.

Results

Auxin response factor (ARF) family genes in peels of wax apple

A total of 80.96Gb clean bases was obtained by transcriptomic sequencing from twelve samples, and 48,392 Unigenes were obtained after assembly including 28,128 differential expression unigenes by compared with Nr, KEGG, KOG, and Swiss-Prot. We identified 14 common auxin response factors (ARF) from 28,128 differential expression unigenes in peels of wax apple at different fruit development stages, which were called SsARFs .

The gene name, gene ID and physico-chemical property of SsARFs were listed in Table 1. Briefy, the CDS length of SsARFs varied from 1122 bp (SsARF16 ) to 3363 bp ( SsARF19b ), and their deduced protein comprised of 374 (SsARF16) -1121 (SsARF19b) amino acids, with the estimated molecular weight from 41.11 (SsARF16) to 125.04 kDa (SsARF19b), respectively. The isoelectric point (pI) ranged from 5.51 (SsARF5) to 7.94 (SsARF22), while the average pI was 6.53, and acidic amino acids were enriched in the protein molecules. PI of 28.57% SsARFs were more than seven, the others were less than seven with most of them being weakly acidic. The instability index ranged from 46.95 (SsARF18) to 73.71 (SsARF19b), suggesting that all these proteins were stable (II > 40). The aliphatic index ranged from 66.50 (SsARF16) to 77.24 (SsARF4). The total hydrophilicity of SsARFs was negative, ranging from- 0.635 (SsARF19b) to -0.268 (SsARF22). All SsARFs were localized in the nucleus .

Conserved motifs and domains of SsARFs

To investigate their evolutionary relationship, phylogenetic analysis was performed using sequences of SsARFs (14) with ARFs from Arabidopsis (23), rice (25), grape (26) and apple (28) (Fig. 1). SsARFs were separately distributed to five different clusters and clustered together with ARFs from other plant species (I, II, III, IV, V) (Fig. 1). Group I contained SsARF9a and SsARF9b with 12 AtARFs, 3 OsARFs, 4 VvARFs, and 4 MdARFs. Group II only contained SsARF2A with containing 1 AtARFs, 3 OsARFs, 4 VvARFs, and 4 MdARFs. Group III contained SsARF6a, SsARF6b, SsARF19a and SsARF19b with 4 AtARFs, 8 OsARFs, 7 VvARFs, and 8 MdARFs. Group IV contained SsARF3, SsARF4 and SsARF5 with 3 AtARFs, 5 OsARFs, 6 VvARFs, and 6 MdARFs. Group V contained SsARF16, SsARF17, SsARF18 and SsARF22 with 3 AtARFs, 6 OsARFs, 5 VvARFs, and 6 MdARFs.

20 motifs were identified from 14 SsARFs, all of them shared motifs 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 10, while most of them shared motifs 3, 8, 9, 12, 13, nine of them shared motifs 6, 14, 15, indicating diverse motif compositions and functional in SsARF family (Fig. 2). While SsARF6b protein contained the most conserved motifs (18 conserved motifs), SsARF17 contained the least conserved motifs, only 8 conserved motifs. There were 17 conserved motifs in SsARF6a, SsARF9a, SsARF9b, SsARF19a and SsARF2A. Further observation indicated that SsARF proteins at the same clade in the phylogenetic tree basically show similar motif composition, which suggested a potential of functional redundancy.

Sequence similarity and amino acid composition of SsARF proteins

The results of sequence comparison of SsARF proteins showed that most SsARF proteins contained three unique conserved structural domains by Clustal software (Fig. 3). The three unique conserved structural domains were DNA-binding domain (DBD), mid-region structural domain (MR), and C-terminal dimerization structural domain (CTD), which confirmed that the fourteen protein sequences shared a very high degree of sequence similarity.

Fig. 3Sequence similarity of the ARF gene family in wax apple.

The amino acid composition of SsARF proteins were shown in Fig. 4. All SsARF proteins were composed of 20 different amino acids. Among them, serine (Ser), leucine (Leu), proline (Pro), and glycine (Gly) were abundant, which accounted from 7.5 (SsARF16) ~ 14.6% (SsARF3), 5.6 (SsARF16) ~ 10.4% (SsARF6a), 5.9 (SsARF22) ~ 9.0% (SsARF6a), and 5.3 (SsARF19b) ~ 9.9% (SsARF16), respectively. However, amino acids such as cysteine (Cys), tryptophan (Trp) and tyrosine (Tyr) were less, accounting from 0.8 (SsARF6b) ~ 2.7% (SsARF22), 1.1 (SsARF3) ~ 2.1% (SsARF16, SsARF9b), and 1.4% (SsARF3, SsARF9b, SsARF2A) ~ 2.6% (SsARF4), respectively.

Secondary and tertiary structure of SsARF proteins

As shown in Table 2; Fig. 5, the proportion of alpha helix and random coil was relatively larger than beta turn and extended strand in SsARF proteins. The descending order of them was random coil > alpha helix> extended strand > beta turn. Random coil changed from 45.41 (SsARF6a) to 67.27% (SsARF18), alpha helix varied from 13.27 (SsARF18) to 31.12% (SsARF5), while their extended strand ranged from 13.44% (SsARF9a) to 21.70% (SsARF6a). The beta.

turn of SsARF proteins was lower, varied from 3.27% (SsARF18) to 9.51% (SsARF6a). Typical ARF protein has three conserved domains: N-terminal B3-like DNA binding domain (DBD), C-terminal dimerization domain (CTD), and a middle region (MR)27. Our results showed that all SsARFs contained MR as an intermediate domain, and a complete DBD, while many of them contained CTD structure, except for SsARF6b, SsARF16, SsARF17, SsARF18 and SsARF22.

Discussion

With the continuous development of biological information technology and the improvement of sequencing technology, it is possible to identify and characterize a certain gene family and its mediating regulatory network at the genome level. Plant ARF genes acted as key components of auxin signaling pathway to mediate the expression of auxin-responsive, which were also a classic and important genes related to plant growth and development12,13. In the present study, 14 SsARF genes were identified in wax apple, and the number of SsARF family members is closed to the ones in papaya (11)27, Passiflora edulia Sims (13)28 and mulberry(16)29. The number of SsARF family members is littler than the ones in A. thaliana (23)16,30,31, litchi (39)19, tomota (25)20, blueberry (60)25, apple (28)32, sweet orange (19)33, grapes (25)34, rice (25)35,36, and maize (31)37.

Bioinformatics analysis revealed significant differences in the protein characteristics and amino acid lengths of the ARF proteins in wax apple, indicating that the ARF family proteins had diverse characteristics and may possess various biological functions41. The physico-chemical property of SsARF proteins had obvious difference in CDS length, amino acids composition, estimated molecular weight, pI, instability index and total hydrophilicity in wax apple, which was similar to A. thaliana, papaya27, Passiflora edulia Sims28, rice35,36, and sugar apple38. The subcellular localization prediction results show that the ARF protein of wax apple were located in the cell nucleus, which was consistent with that in Passiflora edulia Sims28 and tea tree39,40, while some ARFs located inytoplasmic20 and chloroplasts41. All SsARF proteins were composed of 20 different amino acids, and the length of SsARF proteins and the ratio of differential amino acid had significant difference, which was similar to that in papaya27 and sugar apple38.

The secondary structure of SsARF proteins were constructed by alpha helix, random coil, beta turn and extended strand, and the tertiary structure of ARF proteins included three conserved domains such as N-terminal B3-like DNA binding domain (DBD), C-terminal dimerization domain (CTD), and a middle region (MR), which was similar to that in A. thaliana [16] ,litchi19, tomota20, blueberry25, fig26, papaya27, apple (28)32, grapes34, rice35,36, and maize37. All SsARF proteins contained a complete DBD and MR structure, which determined their functions based on amino acid compositions liking that in fig26 and papaya27. Most SsARFs contained CTD structure except SsARF6b, SsARF16, SsARF17, SsARF18 and SsARF22. Without a complete CTD domain, some SsARFs may not participate in the auxin signaling pathway or perform its independent functions. However, the ARF function of SsARF6b, SsARF16, SsARF17, SsARF18 and SsARF22 without CTD domains in wax apple required to be further studied.

The phylogenetic analysis of SsARFs indicated SsARFs can be classified into five clusters together with ARFs from other plant species (Fig. 2), suggesting they might have undergone similar evolutionary and function diversification in the same cluster. For instance, SsARF9a and SsARF9b of wax apple belonged to class I with eleven AtARFs, which may be involved in regulation of the similar formation, development and patterns. ARFs in wax apple were highly homologous to those in Arabidopsis, apple, rice and grape. Many AtARFs have been already reported in previous reports, therefore, comparative studies may reveal useful information on the respective biological functions in wax apple. SsARF2A belonged to class II with AtARF216, MdARF5, MdARF10, MdARF18, MdARF2332, VvARF10, VvARF13, VvARF2534, OsARF4, OsARF23, and OsARF2435,36, which may be involved in the similar evolutionary and function diversification. Twenty motifs were identified from 14 SsARFs, the results showed that diverse motif compositions existed in SsARFs implying their functional diversity (Fig. 4). There were the most conserved motifs in SsARF6b protein, while SsARF17 protein contained the least conserved motifs.

There was strongly correlation between SsARF9 and SsARF19, while they were weakly correlated with other SsARFs. SsARF3, SsARF5, SsARF9a, SsARF9b and SsARF19b were annotated to phytohormone signaling in signal transduction metabolism pathway. There were strong interactions among SsARF3, SsARF4, SsARF5, SsARF6a, SsARF6b, SsARF9a, SsARF9b, SsARF17, SsARF19a, and SsARF19b, which had strong interactions with the IAA12, IAA14, and IAA17. The interactions of SsARFs with IAA are similar to that in figs26 and grape34. Here, we focused on auxin response factors in peels of wax apple during different fruit growth and development stages, but did not focus on ARF in other organs such as roots, leaves and flowers, which will be the focus on the next research. The specific functional analysis of the genes in the ARF family that promote or inhibit the expression of auxin response genes still needs further exploration in wax apple.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and treatments

The wax apple plants (variety“Taiguo Zihong”, approximately 8 years old) are cultivated in greenhouse under root-limited culture at Fujian Institute of Subtropical Botany, Xiamen, Fujian, China. Flower buds began to sprout from the branch tips and the main stem in mid-February 2023. The fruits were selected at 15 (S1), 30 (S2), 45 (S3) and 60 (S4) days after flower (DAF) from May to June in 2023. The samples were taken from twenty healthy fruits, whose size and color was consistent. The peels (0.2–0.5 mm) cut by a surgical blade, which were frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 ℃ until processed further for transcriptome analyses. All samples were performed with three biological replications. The transcriptome data of samples were obtained by sequencing performed by Gene Denovo Biotechnology Co., Ltd Guangzhou, China.

Identification and physico-chemical property of SsARF family genes

14 SsARF genes were preliminarily annotated from four databases, namely NR, NT, Swiss-Prot, and PFAM, which was based on the transcriptome sequencing data of peels of wax apple. SsARFssequences were analyzed using bioinformatics methods, and the physico-chemical parameters such as CDS length, amino acid composition, molecular weight, and subcellular localization were analyzed and predicted by using prot param online (http://web.expasy.org/protparam/). Subcellular localization of SsARF family genes were analyzed by using SoftBerry (http://linux1.softberry.com/berry. phtml? topic=protcomppl&group=prgrams&subgroup=proloc)27.

Protein structure of SsARFs

Secondary structures of protein sequences were predicted by online software Prabi(NPS@:SOPMA secondary structure prediction, https://npsa-prabi.ibcp.fr /cgi-bin /npsa_automat.pl? page=npsa_sopma.html). Tertiary structures of protein sequences were performed by online software SWISS-MODEL (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/). The conserved structural domains of SsARFs family of proteins were analysis by using CDD of NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Structure/cdd/wrpsb.cgi)27.

Identification and phylogenetic analyses of SsARFs

23 AtARF protein sequences were downloaded from TAIR (www.arabidopsis.org). 25 OsARFs, 26 VvARFs, and 28 MdARFs protein sequences were downloaded from https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb. The ARFs of them were used as bait to identify potential ARFs in wax apple genome and obtained by BLAST analysis in TBtools42,43 with the cutoff E-value set at 1e− 10. The sequences of 23 AtARFs, 25 OsARFs, 26 VvARFs, and 28 MdARFs were applied to generate phylogenetic tree with 14 SsARFs. Briefy, multiple sequences alignment of all the ARF genes were performed by Clustal X, and phylogenetic tree was constructed by the MEGA 7.0 software with maximum likelihood statistical method44. Bootstrap values were calculated using 1000 replicates. The phylogenetic tree was beautified by iTOL(http://itol.embl.de).

Gene structures, conserved motifs and domains of SsARFs

The gene structures of 14 SsARF genes were drawn using GSDS 2.0 (http://gsds.gao-lab.org/) through comparing their genomic sequences and coding sequences45. The conserved motifs of SsARF proteins were analyzed using the online program Multiple Expectation Maximization for Motif Elucidation (http://meme-suite.org/tools/meme) by setting twenty conserved motifs46. The conserved domains were analyzed using the online program Pfam (http://pfam.xfam.org/).

Expression profling of SsARFs during fruit growth and development

The transcript ID of each ARF gene was obtained using sequence search. Expression profling of SsARFs during fruit development was performed using a real-time online platform for data analysis (http://www.omicsmart.com).

Protein interaction analysis of SsARFs

Protein interaction analysis of SsARFs were performed by using Single/Multiple Proteins by Sequence from the String Protein Interaction Database (https://cn.string-db.org/).

qRT-PCR

All primers involved in the qRT-PCR process were designed using the online tool Primer 3 Plus (http://www.bioinformatics.nl/cgi-bin/primer3plus/primer3plus.cgi)27,47, and the specificity of the primers was examined by the corresponding dissociation curves and gel electrophoresis. Three biological replicates were performed on each sample and Ct values were averaged (Table 3).

Statistical analysis

One-way ANOVA and least significant difference (LSD) test were completed with SPSS (version 23.0) (p < 0.05).

Conclusion

In this study, a total of 14 SsARF genes were identified from transcriptome data of wax apple peels at its different development stages. Fourteen SsARFs were hydrophilic protein and located in the nucleus, which contained three domains such as B3-DNA binding (DBD), middle region (MR) and C-terminal dimerization domain (CTD) except SsARF6b, SsARF16, SsARF17, SsARF18 and SsARF22, which lost C-terminal dimerization domain. The secondary structure of SsARF proteins was consisted of irregularly coiled, extended strands, alpha-helices and beta-turns. Phylogenetic tree analysis showed that SsARF proteins can be classified into five clusters, and most of the genes in the same clusters have similar conserved motifs. The protein interaction network of SsARFs showed strong interactions among SsARF3, SsARF4, SsARF5, SsARF6a, SsARF6b, SsARF17, SsARF9a, SsARF9b, SsARF19a, and SsARF19b, which interacted strongly with IAA12, IAA14 and IAA17. SsARF3, SsARF4, SsARF5, SsARF6a, SsARF6b, SsARF9a, SsARF9b, SsARF17, SsARF19a, and SsARF19b, may play an important role in uxin signaling during wax apple fruit development.

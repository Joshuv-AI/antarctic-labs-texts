Improved Giza pyramids construction algorithm for Modify the deep neural network-based method for energy demand forecasting
Authors: Xue Wang, Saeid Razmjooy
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate prediction of energy demand is crucial for improving services, reducing costs, and optimizing operations in energy systems. Deep neural networks (DNNs) have emerged as a popular method for energy demand forecasting. However, the performance of DNNs can be affected by data quality and hyperparameter selection. To address these concerns, this study proposes a novel energy demand forecasting technique that combines DNNs with an enhanced Giza pyramid construction methodology. The aim of this study is to provide a more reliable and effective approach for forecasting energy demand. The DNNs are employed to capture the complex relationships between input and output variables, while the Giza pyramids algorithm is utilized to optimal selection of hyperparameters of the network. Real-world energy demand data is used to evaluate the proposed approach, comparing it with state-of-the-art baseline models. The research methodology involves assessing the suggested approach using real-world energy demand information and conducting a comparative analysis with cutting-edge baseline models, including modified BP neural network (MBPNN), Neural Network based Genetic Algorithm (NNGA), and reinforcement learning and deep neural network (RLDNN). The IGPCA/CNN method outperforms other methods in energy prediction accuracy across short-term, medium-term, and long-term time scales. It achieves an MSE score of 0.564, lower than MBPNN, NNGA, and RLDNN. In medium-term prediction, it achieves an MSE score of 0.587, better than MBPNN, NNGA, and RLDNN. In long-term prediction, it achieves an MSE score of 0.629, lower than MBPNN and RLDNN. Further analysis and validation experiments are needed to ensure robustness and generalizability. Comparing the method with other state-of-the-art approaches can provide a comprehensive understanding of its superiority. The performance of the models is evaluated based on reliability and effectiveness in energy demand forecasting. The major conclusion of this study is that the proposed approach outperforms the initial models in accurately forecasting energy demand. The combination of DNNs and the improved Giza pyramid construction methodology results in enhanced performance, demonstrating superior reliability and effectiveness compared to other models. The study highlights the significance of accurate energy demand prediction for optimizing energy systems and reducing costs.Keywords: Energy demand, Forecasting, Deep neural networks, Efficiency, Improved giza pyramids construction algorithm

Introduction

The term 'energy demand' refers to the quantity of energy required to fulfill consumer, residential, commercial, and industrial needs [3]. It enables energy businesses to optimize their operations, reduce costs, and provide higher-quality services [36]. Energy consumption is influenced by various factors such as population growth, economic expansion, energy prices, weather conditions, and technological advancements [6]. To meet the increasing demand while ensuring environmental sustainability and energy security, the energy sector needs to utilize diverse energy sources and technologies, including sustainable energy sources and energy storage systems [13]. Energy businesses also need to invest in advanced forecasting methodologies for energy consumption to streamline their processes and reduce costs [28].

The accurate and energy systems operation depends on the ability to predict energy demand. Energy companies may optimize production, transmission, and distribution operations, lower costs, and boost service quality with the aid of accurate forecasts [32]. Traditional approaches for energy consumption prediction rely on statistical models, which have limitations in identifying complex patterns in data and require extensive feature engineering [21].

In recent years, neural networks (NNs) have demonstrated high potential for energy consumption prediction by automatically extracting features from raw data and capturing intricate relationships between input and output variables. However, achieving optimal performance with NNs depends on balancing input data, adjusting hyperparameters, and optimizing the training process [15]. Convolutional neural networks

The present study uses an optimized version of a Convolutional neural network (CNN) to provide an accurate Energy Demand Forecasting. Convolutional neural networks are a form of neural networks that has proven very effective in handling data mining and computer vision challenges. The name “convolutional” is derived from the mathematical action of convolution, which serves as the foundation of these networks. A CNN's architecture is made up of numerous layers, each of which plays a distinct role. The first layer is often a convolutional layer that extracts features from the input picture using filters.

This layer produces a series of feature maps, each of which reflects the reaction of a specific filter to the incoming data. The following layers can be convolutional, pooling, or fully connected, and perform various actions on the input data. The last layer is generally a completely linked layer that generates the network's ultimate output.

Convolution layer is the top layer. Data processing frequently employ the linear convolution layer procedure. Convolution is used in CNNs to take features out of an input data. A tiny window, referred to as a kernel or filter, is dragged across the input picture during the convolution procedure, and the dot product between the kernel and the corresponding pixel values in the image is computed. The outcome of this operation is a single value that denotes the filter's activation at that specific point in the data. The convolutional layer may extract distinct characteristics from the input data by using a variety of filters.

Pooling is a down sampling procedure that lowers the feature maps' spatial resolution [26]. Max pooling, which chooses the highest value inside a window and discards the remainder, is the most used pooling procedure. Max pooling is used to provide the network some translational invariance, which allows it to identify the same pattern even if it appears in a different part of the data.

Giza pyramids construction optimizer (GPCO)

The Giza Pyramids (Giza Necropo-lis) is a location with 3 enormous pyramids that were constructed when the 4th dynasty in Egypt governed. Based on the information of archaeology, the erection method of these 3 pyramids is different on the grounds that they have been erected during time. The workers' management was considered the most overarching issue in the erection process of pyramids. The optimized pyramids' erection has been important because of hardware amenities’ shortage, limited and short time of erection, and the considerable quantity of utilized blocks of stone. The method of erection, the motivation, and the offered optimizer have been explained in the following.

The erection method

There is the vast array of theories about the erection methods of pyramids. Most archeologists are of the opinion that the pyramids’ stones were gained from mines, transported to the site and finally placed with ramps for mounting the huge stones to the higher places. The Greeks opined that slaves worked hard and they were abused in the erection of pyramids; new excavations, however, clarify that the builder of such constructions were workers with high skills and they all lived in the adjacent villages. The required human sources for erection of the pyramids were between 14000 and 40000 who worked on the range of [10–20 years] for the erection without facilities and tools.

Beside the construction difficulties, such buildings process needed a progressive tactic for erection management, and issues like workers’ housing, food, salary, and time scheduling. The logistical needs and complication of this project have been extraordinary in retrospect.

The motivation

The workers of the pyramids were considered a combination of slaves and skillful persons who were led by an expert. This proficient manager is named Pharaoh's distinct agent who supervised the process of stone carrying by different workers. The workers should report the tasks implementation to Pharaoh's distinct agent who has the finest rank in group. Every worker owns a specific position (rank) at the site which can be promoted in a way that whenever a worker performs well, he is ranked as a sublime. Accordingly, a competition exists amid workers for getting this rank. What is more, workers might need some rest because of their energy loss over the stones' carrying. Thus, the tired workers is going to be replaced with a new and energetic ones. It shows that if any improvement no longer exists in the human sources, they must be replaced because they could be effective in another place. It implies that there is also another confliction among workers rather than gaining sublime rank; this competition motivates each worker to become experienced and expertise.

Throughout the process of pyramid erection, miners scatter the stone blocks which are to the site by workers. The collecting and carrying of the stone blocks are implemented on regular basis. Although ramps were utilized in the erection of the pyramids, the blocks must be carried to its installation situation. This distance (among stone blocks and its installation state) is determined via the workers’ capability. Workers will carry more and more stones to the installation location if they have enough amount of power that makes the stones far closer to the pyramid erection site. It is of primary importance to note that the motion of stone blocks has been influenced by the primary speed, force of friction, and the gradient of ramp.

Conclusions

In this study, we proposed a hybrid model that combines an improved version of the Giza pyramid construction algorithm with a deep neural network (DNN) for energy demand forecasting. By leveraging the strengths of both techniques, our model aims to provide more reliable and accurate predictions of energy consumption. The improved Giza pyramid construction algorithm was utilized to optimize the hyperparameters of the DNN model, enhancing its generalization capabilities. The DNN component of our model was specifically designed to capture the complex correlations among various factors influencing energy demand. To train and evaluate our model, we utilized data extracted from the 2021 China Energy Transition Status Report. The data was carefully divided into three subsets, and different types of forecasting were employed to ensure accurate energy demand predictions. Particularly, we focused on providing precise predictions one day in advance with a 15-min resolution. The success of our model heavily relied on the data preparation process. It was crucial to ensure that the data was consistent, error-free, and accurately represented the underlying problem. To assess the performance of our model, we compared it with state-of-the-art methods such as MBPNN, NNGA, and RLDNN. The results demonstrated that our model outperformed these methods in terms of accuracy, robustness, and generalization performance. Notably, our model achieved a minimum mean squared error (MSE) of 0.694 for medium-term energy demand, outperforming previous models. The proposed IGPCA/CNN method has significant implications for energy demand forecasting, renewable energy integration, smart grid optimization, and urban planning. It enables efficient resource allocation, improved planning, and reduced operational costs across different time scales. Accurate energy demand prediction is particularly valuable for smart grid operators, enabling informed decisions on load balancing, peak shaving, and demand response programs. This highlights the practical relevance and potential real-world applications of our research. For future studies, it would be beneficial to investigate the scalability of our proposed model for larger energy demand forecasting tasks and explore its feasibility for implementation in actual energy systems.

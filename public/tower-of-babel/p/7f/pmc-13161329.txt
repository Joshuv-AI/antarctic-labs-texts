Performance and safety of the Colovac 2 colorectal anastomotic protection device: the SafeHeal SAFE-2023 study
Authors: Jeremie H. Lefevre, Aleksei Karachun, Narkhodzha Sametdinov, Oqiljon Raximov, Akmalkhuja Rustamov, Askar Adilkhodjaev, Seydamet Yunusov, Tillyashaikhov Mirzagolib, Patricia Sylla
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTemporary diverting ostomies have become the predominant approach to reduce anastomotic leakage (AL) after anterior resection. Colovac Device is an anastomosis protection device designed to divert feces from the anastomosis. This study evaluated a new version of the Colovac Device (Colovac 2), used with the novel SmartVac Vacuum Management Device, which is used to anchor the device in the colon.MethodsThe SAFE-2023 Study was a prospective, multicenter single-arm study that enrolled patients undergoing low anterior resection (LAR) and Colovac placement. The primary performance endpoint was to assess the clinically significant (CS) migration rate of the device through device retrieval. The other performance endpoints were ostomy avoidance rate at Day 10 visit, and fecal diversion success as assessed by cleanliness of the colon immediately prior to device retrieval.ResultsA total of 24 patients were enrolled. 100% (24/24) of the Colovac devices were successfully placed in the intended location, and 100% were successfully removed endoscopically without complications. CS migration occurred in 2 patients (8.3%) on POD8 and POD9, respectively. In both cases, the device was successfully retrieved without complications, good anastomotic healing was confirmed, and no ostomy conversion was performed. The ostomy avoidance rate was 87.5% (21/24) at Day 10 and 83% (20/24) at Day 30 post-device placement. Fecal diversion success assessed immediately prior to device retrieval was 95.8% (23/24).ConclusionsThe SAFE-2023 study demonstrated a low CS migration rate for the Colovac. The Colovac Device provides a minimally invasive option to protect the anastomosis during the healing process by avoiding the need for a diverting ostomy in 87.5% of patients at Day 10.Trial registrationSAFE-2023: NCT06540807.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00464-026-12691-y.

Colorectal surgery, particularly procedures involving anastomosis, carry a significant risk of postoperative complications, most notably anastomotic leakage (AL) [1–3]. Traditional strategies to mitigate this risk have relied on the use of temporary diverting ostomies, which redirect fecal flow away from the anastomosis to allow for healing [4, 5]. Use of diverting stoma is recommended in several national guidelines [6, 7]. However, while effective in reducing the incidence of AL-related morbidity and mortality, ostomies introduce their own set of complications, including dehydration, renal failure, electrolyte imbalances, peristomal skin irritation, peristomal hernia, stoma prolapse, and the risks incurred from delays in reversal and potential for the stoma becoming permanent, as well as the economic burden of stoma to the healthcare system [8–11].

There has been a strong interest in developing an alternative to prophylactic fecal diversion, namely a device which would protect the anastomosis temporarily, reduce complications while leaving the anastomosis functional for better recovery outcomes, and eliminate the need for ostomies and takedown interventions. Colovac is a Colorectal Anastomosis Protection device designed to obviate the need for temporary ostomies for participants undergoing low anterior resection [12, 13]. The device involves the concept of a colorectal bypass and consists of a diverting flexible sheath to protect the anastomosis and of a stent vacuum system which anchors the sheath to the mucosa proximal to the anastomosis. Colovac is designed to serve as a temporary intraluminal bypass device that protects the anastomotic site during the critical early postoperative healing period. Unlike conventional diverting ostomies, Colovac provides internal protection for up to 12 days and is retrieved endoscopically once the anastomosis has sufficiently healed. It builds upon previous attempts at internal diversion but incorporates novel materials and design improvements aimed at improving safety, ease of use, and patient outcomes. The previous studies on Colovac + device design with Vacuum Loss Alert System (Colovac + / VLAS) confirmed the safety of the device despite a migration rate of 14.3% (6/42), with the SAFE-2 randomized study demonstrating a migration rate of 30% (3/10), which prompted the Sponsor to initiate an early closure of the study enrollment (unpublished data) [14]. A new device was subsequently designed to reduce the migration rate.

The aim of this study was to assess performance, safety, and usability of the Colovac 2 when used with the SmartVac Vacuum Management Device.

Methods

Study design

The SAFE-2023 study was a prospective, multicenter single-arm study to evaluate the preliminary performance, safety, and usability of the Colovac Anastomosis Protection Device. Patients were monitored in the hospital post-LAR and Colovac placement until device retrieval on Day 10. Following discharge, patients were followed through 30 days postoperatively, which included an endoscopic assessment of the colonic mucosa at the anchoring site and collection of post-discharge complications.

The procedures were performed by experienced colorectal surgeons at 2 centers in Tashkent Uzbekistan (Akfa Medline and Republican Specialized Scientific and Practical Medical Center of Oncology and Radiology of MoH of Ruz). The study protocol was approved by the appropriate ethics committee, and all patients were consented prior to participation. The study was registered online on the ClinicalTrials.gov website with the following identifier: NCT06540807. It was conducted in accordance with ISO standard 14155:2020 and the recommendations guiding physicians in biomedical research involving human subjects adopted by the 18th World Medical Assembly, Helsinki, Finland, 2013 and later revisions.

Objectives and endpoints

The primary objective of the study was to assess the clinically significant (CS) migration rate of the device through post-procedure Day 10 when the device was removed. CS migration was defined as migration of entire stent to or below the sacral promontory, with or without patient symptoms or clinical findings suggestive of AL.

The other performance endpoints of the study at the Day 10 visit were assessment of ostomy avoidance rate, and fecal diversion success assessed by cleanliness of colon (Boston Bowel Preparation Score ≥ 2) immediately prior to device retrieval. BBPS was selected to objectively document the effectiveness of fecal diversion as it is an internationally validated scoring system to assess colon cleanliness. [15] Safety endpoints at the Day 10 visit included mucosal integrity at the anchoring site after device removal, device-related complications, and all complications. The colonic mucosa appearance at the anchoring site was assessed immediately after device retrieval and at 1-month post-LAR procedure by an independent endoscopist. Usability endpoints included the surgeon-rated difficulty with placement and retrieval of the device, patient-rated tolerance of the device, and staff ease of use of the SmartVac.

Eligibility (Supplementary Table 1)

Inclusion criteria included adult patients (18 years of age or older), eligible to undergo open or minimally invasive sphincter-sparing low anterior resection with planned diverting loop ileostomy for rectal adenocarcinoma, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status ≤ 2, and willingness to comply with protocol-specific treatment and study visits and to sign a written Informed Consent Form.

Major exclusion criteria included a history of left sided colitis, known allergy to nickel or other components of the Colovac 2 System, pregnant or nursing female subject, concomitant major surgical procedure in combination with colorectal resection (i.e., hepatectomy), any serious or uncontrolled medical disorder that, in the opinion of the investigator, may increase the risk associated with study participation, impair the ability of the participant to undergo protocol described procedures or interfere with the interpretation of study results. Intraoperative exclusion criteria included an anastomosis located above the sacral promontory, any intraoperative complications that would preclude safe placement of the Colovac procedure (e.g., bowel ischemia, positive air leak test, excessive blood loss, etc.).

Investigational device

The Colovac Anastomosis Protection Device is a sterile, single-use disposable device. The device consists of an Introducer pre-loaded with the Colovac Device (Fig. 1A). The Colovac Device consists of 2 elements: an anchor consisting of a covered stent delimiting an air- and water-tight volume (i.e., vacuum chamber) in which a vacuum is pulled upon delivery in the colon through two vacuum tubes (Stent, Fig. 1B), and a flexible cylindrical sheath attached to the anchor, covering the anastomosis, and with appropriate length so that it protrudes outside the patient’s anus (Sheath). Design changes were incorporated in the Colovac 2 design including vacuum chamber that creates circumferential 360° vacuum between the stent and colon, stent shape changed from cylindrical to hourglass to further improve stent anchoring, and sealing rings widened to increase the surface area of the seal. The Colovac introducer has an atraumatic tip, a smooth outer surface, and is flexible to enable safe navigation through the colon during device introduction. To mitigate any potential risk of mechanical injury to the anastomosis during device retrieval, the upstream edge of the stent is designed without any sharp surfaces, and during retrieval, cinch sutures help to collapse the stent, facilitating safe retrieval through the colon and past the anastomosis.Fig. 1Colovac System. The SmartVac is a single-patient use, non-sterile active device (Fig. 1C). The device attaches directly to the Colovac Device vacuum tubing, which exits the anus, and supplies vacuum from outside of the patient’s body. It also monitors the vacuum level at the anchoring site, turning on the vacuum pump if the measured vacuum level is outside the pre-determined acceptable range. It provides caregivers with visual and auditory alerts in the event of vacuum leak, tubing occlusion, fluid collection canister full (replacement needed), or low battery (replacement needed). The SmartVac can be worn by the patient over the shoulder during the Colovac diversion period.

Device implantation procedure

After colorectal resection (by open or mini-invasive approach) and anastomosis creation, and after all intraoperative exclusion criteria were confirmed, the Colovac device was placed by advancing the Introducer under into the colon to the intended delivery site, which is 2–3 introducer locator marks (10-15 cm) above the sacral promontory (Fig. 1D). Once the proper location of the Introducer was confirmed, the deployment knob was turned clockwise, releasing the Colovac Device from the Introducer into the colon. Once the Colovac Device had been properly deployed, the Introducer was removed from the colon, and the sheath and vacuum tubes extended outside of the anus. Subsequently, the SmartVac was attached to the vacuum tubes and activated.

Postoperative course

The Colovac Device was left in place for approximately 10 days. During that time, all patients remained hospitalized and were given a low-residue diet. Patients were mobile during this period, had fecal transit passing through the stent and sheath, could use the toilets, and could shower by hanging the SmartVac Device outside of the shower. Patients could also carry the SmartVac Device over their shoulder via a shoulder strap. Postoperative follow-up included daily clinical evaluations: physical examinations, daily C-reactive protein (CRP) + White blood cell count (WBC) [16], and daily monitoring of the Colovac Device & SmartVac, which included checking the length of the sheath protruding the anus (increase in sheath length may be an indicator of migration) and monitoring SmartVac.

Prior to Colovac retrieval, an abdominal and pelvic CT scan with rectal contrast was administered to confirm the integrity of the anastomosis. The Colovac was subsequently retrieved endoscopically in the operating room by pulling on the retrieval loops at the end of the stent. Bowel cleanliness was assessed by an independent endoscopist reviewing endoscopy imaging using the Boston Bowel Preparation Score. [15] Following device retrieval, the anchoring site and anastomosis were evaluated endoscopically. Insertion of Colovac, retrieval procedure, and ease of use of the SmartVac vacuum management device were rated by site personnel on a scale from 1 to 5 (1 being “no difficulty” and 5 being “impossible).

Following hospital discharge, patients returned for a follow-up clinic visit at 1 month postoperatively at which time endoscopy was performed to assess the integrity of anastomosis and colonic anchoring site, and post-discharge complications were collected.

Secondary effectiveness endpoints

Ostomy avoidance & fecal diversion success

The ostomy avoidance rate on POD 10 was 87.5% (21/24). Among the 3 patients who underwent early ostomy conversion, one patient developed a symptomatic AL on POD4 with pain, fever, elevated CRP and WBC, stool in the Colovac drain and CT scan confirming AL. He underwent laparoscopic stoma conversion and device retrieval. The other 2 patients were asymptomatic but were diagnosed with AL on the per-protocol POD9 CT scan, the first with a coloanal fistula but no fever and normal WBC, the second with anastomotic dehiscence, no pain or fever but mildly elevated WBC and CRP. Both patients were successfully converted to ostomy, one laparoscopically and one via laparotomy, with uneventful device retrieval. One additional patient underwent ostomy conversion on POD14 due to an asymptomatic anastomotic dehiscence initially identified on CT scan immediately post-Colovac retrieval and monitored for 4 days prior to ostomy conversion. At 1-month following LAR, the stoma avoidance rate was 83% (20/24) (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Postoperative course of the 24 patients in the SAFE-2023 trial

Upon endoscopic assessment of the colon just prior to Colovac retrieval, fecal diversion success rate (i.e., Boston Bowel Preparation Score ≥ 2) was 95.8% (23/24). Failure of fecal diversion was observed in the one patient who underwent early device retrieval due to symptomatic AL confirmed via imaging at POD4. In the 2 cases of migration, successful fecal diversion was confirmed immediately prior to device retrieval.

Secondary safety endpoints

Colonic mucosa at anchoring site

Immediately post-retrieval, there was either no bleeding or mild self-limiting bleeding, and either no ulceration, or erosion confined to the mucosa in 94.7% of the cases (18/19). There were no perforations. At 1-month post-LAR, in all cases (17/17), there was no bleeding present, and 100% (19/19) had no or only superficial ulceration.

Morbidity

Overall morbidity was 50% with a total of 16 complications reported in 12 patients. Severe morbidity rate was 16.7% with 5 reinterventions in 4 patients (Table 3). Three patients with AL were converted to stoma, and one patient with postoperative bleeding from a trocar site on POD2 required reoperation before being diagnosed with an asymptomatic AL on POD9 and was also subsequently converted to stoma. The overall AL rate was 25% (6/24) including 2 AL (8.3%) that did not require stoma conversion. In summary, four AL events were documented in asymptomatic patients upon review of protocol-mandated imaging: pre-retrieval CT scan (2 events), pre-retrieval endoscopy (1 event), and post-retrieval endoscopy (1 event). Of these four patients, three were converted to stoma to maintain fecal diversion after Colovac retrieval. Symptomatic AL occurred in two patients in SAFE-2023 (8.3%). One symptomatic leak was identified on POD4 while the Colovac was in place and necessitated Colovac retrieval and conversion to stoma, and in another patient a leak was diagnosed on POD23, after Colovac retrieval, and was treated with conservative measures without conversion to ostomy. The only event possibly related to the Colovac was anal bleeding on POD8 that resolved with conservative measure. Readmission rate was 4.2%.

Device usability and patient tolerance

The surgeons reported no difficulty (13/24, 54.2%) or minor difficulty (10/24, 41.7%) inserting the Colovac device. For one procedure (4.2%), although the surgeon reported some difficulty in positioning the device in the descending colon, the device was successfully placed with no complications reported. Surgeons reported no difficulty (18/24, 75%) or minor difficulty (6/24, 25%) retrieving the Colovac device. No complications were reported during retrieval procedures. In SAFE-2023, the ease of use of the SmartVac vacuum management device was rated by site personnel. All responders reported “no difficulty” using the SmartVac. Prior to device retrieval, 100% of patients reported either complete or mostly complete tolerance of the vacuum tubes that exited through the anus.

Discussion

The SAFE-2023 study evaluated a newer version of the Colovac device (Colovac 2), when used in conjunction with the novel vacuum pump, SmartVac. Among the 24 consecutive patients included, the rate of CS migrations was 8.3% (2/24). The two migrations observed were not associated with ALs and did not result in ostomy conversion. The Colovac allowed stoma avoidance in 83% of patients at one month without any severe morbidity linked to the device.

Earlier studies assessing the previous version of the Colovac device (SAFE-1, SAFE-2019) demonstrated the preliminary safety and effectiveness of the device as an alternative to diverting ostomy based on the stoma avoidance rate and safety profile [12–14]. Specifically, the rate of overall complications across SAFE-1 and SAFE-2019 ranged from 40 to 47% and ostomy avoidance of 67–73%. In SAFE-2023, overall morbidity was similar at 50%, no mortality (0%), readmissions (4.2%), and reinterventions (4.2%, excluding conversions to ostomy), which is aligned with safety data from other LAR studies reporting complication rates ranging 47–61%, mortality 0–3%, readmission rates 14–27%, and reintervention rate of 3–11% [17–19]. Notably in Maroney et al., the overall complication rate and readmission rate were higher in the diverted patients (61 vs. 38%, and 27 vs. 14%, respectively), while the overall symptomatic AL and reintervention rates (17 vs. 5%, and 11 vs. 6%, respectively) were higher in the non-diverted patients, identifying the clinical trade-offs that must be evaluated during patient treatment [19].

While recent studies have questioned the impact of fecal diversion on AL rates following LAR for rectal cancer [18, 20], others have demonstrated lower AL rates in diverted patients [21, 22]. A recent meta-analysis of 8 randomized controlled trials comparing AL rates following LAR with or without a diverting stoma demonstrated that the pooled rate of clinically relevant AL in non-diverted patients was significantly higher than the diverted patients (18.3 vs 6.3%) [23]. It is important to emphasize that the Colovac device is not intended to reduce the incidence of anastomotic leakage, which is well known to be multifactorial, but to divert fecal content away from the anastomosis with the goal of reducing overall morbidity associated with anastomotic leakage and avoiding unnecessary stoma creation in patients who demonstrate adequate anastomotic healing. In the present work, the overall AL rate was 25% (6/24), with a rate of symptomatic ALs of 8.3% (2/24). While four leaks were identified radiographically on protocol-mandated imaging on POD9/10, these leaks would have otherwise remained clinically silent. With regard to management, one leak required no treatment, another was treated with micro-enema and NSAID, 3 with antibiotics and a pelvic drain was placed, and one required exploratory laparotomy. The AL rates in SAFE-2023 (8.3% symptomatic) are lower than reported leak rates in non-diverted patients 13–18% [18, 22]. However, published rates of AL vary widely in the literature, with rates ranging from 1.5 to 23% depending on definition and the overall method of detection [24]. The rate of symptomatic leakage in the Colovac cohort is also comparable to rates published in several multicentric studies (13.4–17.1%) [1, 3, 25–27], but it is important to note that there was no leak-related sepsis, postoperative peritonitis or need for redo-anastomosis or anastomotic takedown in the Colovac cohort. This confirmed the efficacy of fecal diversion achieved with Colovac, as reflected in high BBPS score, and highlighted the potential role of Colovac in avoiding the need for systematic stoma creation in the majority of rectal patients undergoing LAR with otherwise uneventful intraoperative and postoperative course. Moreover, the close follow-up of patients with daily CRP dosage and early CT scan in case of suspicion of AL likely reduced delays in identification and management of AL, as previously described [28]. While the goal is to avoid routine stoma formation, it remains essential to be able to create a stoma in the uncommon cases of symptomatic anastomotic leak or asymptomatic leak detected on the POD 9 CT scan.

As with any alternative to avoid routine stoma creation, it remains essential to be prepared to create a stoma in case of symptomatic or asymptomatic AL detected on the POD9 CT scan. Examination of the anastomosis under general anesthesia with consideration for placement of an endosponge is often recommended to manage anastomotic leaks [29]. The majority of leaks in the Colovac cohort did not require reintervention. Moreover, in the present study, 2 patients with AL (one symptomatic, one asymptomatic) did not require any stoma. This important finding, ostomy avoidance, will be further studied in the SAFE 3 study.

Previous randomized studies comparing AL rates in diverted vs. non-diverted patients often identified a significant increase in the rate of symptomatic AL (which included peritonitis) in the non-diverted patients [4]. Recent publications confirmed on a national level that the decrease of stoma use in the Netherlands between 2011 and 2020 was associated with a significant increase in the AL rate (8 vs 13%) [21]. Therefore, omission of temporary fecal diversion seems irresponsible from a safety perspective. Based on available data, Colovac 2 seems to be a valid alternative to diverting stoma. Moreover, with the potential for ostomy avoidance in more than 80% of cases, this naturally reduces ostomy-related costs. Notably, a recent paper calculated lifetime discounted stoma-related costs in the US, UK, and Germany at $26,311, £9512, and €10,021 respectively, so the 85.7% reduction in ostomies at 10 days also provides a significant advantage to the healthcare systems [30]. Finally, the only alternative could be early stoma closure as already proposed in several randomized trials [31]. This approach is based on the same concept of fecal diversion for a 10-day period. However, it has not been widely adopted due to difficulties with scheduling and other logistical obstacles. Moreover, the psychological impact of stoma to the patient is significant, and some publications reported a higher incidence of postoperative ileus and higher reintervention rates in early stoma closure groups [31, 32]. Ongoing randomized trials on early stoma closure may help inform surgeons on best practices [33, 34].

Regarding the feedback on device usage, SAFE-2023 showed both the insertion and retrieval procedures are simple and safe. No serious complications were reported as related to the Colovac device during either insertion or retrieval, and no device-related reasons required rehospitalization within the 30 days post-procedure. The short duration for insertion and retrieval of Colovac further validates that the procedure is feasible and reproducible. All SmartVac users reported the device as simple to use, and patients had a high tolerance for the vacuum tubes exiting the anus. While the median length of hospital stay was 10 days (reflecting inpatient monitoring through the 10-day placement), future strategies may be considered such as early discharge with a return to hospital for scheduled retrieval, which could significantly reduce the overall length of stay. It will be important as same-day stoma closure is being considered for selected patients [35]. However, in a recent large US study of 22,301 patients who underwent elective stoma closure, only 0.9% had a same-day procedure and 8.2% had a short stay (1 day). The mean length of stay was still 4.4 days for the remaining 90% of patients [36].

There are some limitations to this study, including enrollment limited to 24 patients at two centers in a one country. The follow-up duration was only 30 days, limiting capture of complications that could have been collected beyond that point; although since the device insertion period is only 10 days, it seems feasible to assume that most complications related to Colovac would occur within that early time period. Finally, SAFE-2023 was a single-arm study and was not designed to be compared to diverting ileostomy. Validation in a more geographically diverse setting and broader patient population is required to support more generalizable conclusions. Accordingly, a controlled SAFE-3CV study is being planned to evaluate the Colovac Device with SmartVac in a larger cohort, with extended follow-up and across multiple global regions. The Colovac and SmartVac Devices received CE mark per European Union Medical Devices Regulation (EU MDR) 2017/745 in 2025. The Colovac and SmartVac Devices are investigational devices limited by law to investigational use only in the US.

Conclusion

The current study demonstrates that the Colovac Device has a favorable safety and effectiveness profile with no serious complications associated with Colovac, a stoma avoidance rate of 87.5% by POD10 and 83.3% by POD30, and a 95.8% successful fecal diversion rate. The safety and efficacy results of this study for Colovac are consistent with results of previous studies of the Colovac, while the lower rates of migration observed in SAFE-2023 demonstrate that the Colovac design changes and the addition of the SmartVac have addressed the previous migration issues observed with the prior device iteration.

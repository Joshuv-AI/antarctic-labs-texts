Application of pretreatment 18F-FDG PET metabolic parameters in children with Langerhans cell histiocytosis
Authors: Xin Wen, Ang Wei, Guanyun Wang, Xia Lu, Ying Kan, Wei Wang, Rui Zhang, Jigang Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To evaluate whether pre-treatment 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) metabolic parameters can predict cfBRAF V600E mutation status and prognosis in pediatric Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH). We retrospectively analyzed 64 newly diagnosed pediatric LCH patients who underwent pre-treatment 18F-FDG PET/CT between September 2020 and March 2024. Metabolic parameters were measured, including maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), tumor-to-liver standardized uptake value ratio (SUVRliver), tumor-to-bone marrow standardized uptake value ratio (SUVRBM), total metabolic tumor volume (tMTV), and total lesion glycolysis (tTLG). Associations between metabolic parameters and cfBRAF V600E mutation status were assessed using ROC curve analysis. PFS was evaluated using Kaplan-Meier analysis and Cox proportional hazards regression. The cfBRAF V600E mutation was detected in 22 patients (34%). All metabolic parameters were significantly higher in mutation-positive patients (all P < 0.05). SUVRliver demonstrated the highest diagnostic performance (AUC 0.806, optimal cut-off 6.9), followed by tMTV (AUC 0.792, optimal cut-off 6.2 cm3). Multivariate logistic regression identified three independent predictors of mutation status: younger age (odds ratio [OR] = 0.595, P = 0.001), higher tMTV (OR = 26.760, P = 0.001), and advanced clinical stage (OR = 7.199, P = 0.005). During a median follow-up of 25.2 months, tMTV was the strongest independent predictor of PFS in multivariate Cox regression analysis. Patients with tMTV ≥ 14.66 cm3 had significantly worse outcomes than those with lower values (log-rank P < 0.001). Pre-treatment 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters can predict both cfBRAF V600E mutation status and clinical outcomes in pediatric LCH patients. SUVRliver and tMTV show excellent diagnostic performance for mutation prediction, while tMTV independently predicts prognosis. These non-invasive biomarkers can aid in risk stratification and treatment planning.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06721-3.

Introduction

Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) is a rare inflammatory myeloid neoplasm characterized by the infiltration of pathological CD1a+/CD207 + dendritic cells [1]. It predominantly affects children, with an estimated 4 to 5 cases per million children under the age of 15 [2]. LCH exhibits heterogeneous clinical manifestations, ranging from spontaneous remission to multi-organ involvement [3]. The Histiocyte Society classifies LCH as single-system (SS) or multi-system (MS) disease [4], with MS-LCH involving risk organs (RO+) being associated with increased relapse risk and poor prognosis [5, 6].

The BRAF V600E mutation, detected in more than 50% of LCH patients [7], serves as both a prognostic factor and therapeutic target [8, 9]. Recent advances in liquid biopsy have enabled non-invasive detection of the BRAF V600E mutation through plasma cell-free DNA (cfDNA), offering greater sensitivity than tissue-based detection and facilitating therapeutic monitoring [10–13].

18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) has emerged as a valuable diagnostic tool in pediatric LCH [14–16]. However, existing research has primarily focused on lesion detection and disease activity assessment, with limited investigation of its prognostic value or potential for predicting BRAF V600E mutation status. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the utility of baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters in predicting both BRAF V600E mutation status and patient prognosis in pediatric LCH patients.

Materials and methods

Patients

We retrospectively identified pediatric patients newly diagnosed with LCH at Beijing Children’s Hospital, Capital Medical University, from September 2020 to March 2024. These patients underwent pretreatment 18F-FDG PET/CT within one week of diagnosis at Beijing Friendship Hospital, Capital Medical University. Ethical approval for this retrospective study was obtained from the Institutional Review Boards of Beijing Friendship Hospital. All patients or their legally authorized representative provided informed consent prior to undergoing 18F-FDG PET/CT. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patients were selected according to the following criteria: (1) Age less than 18 years at initial diagnosis; (2) Pathologically confirmed LCH; (3) Availability of pre-treatment 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging. Exclusion criteria were: (1) Low-quality 18F-FDG PET/CT images; (2) Any treatment (including surgery or chemotherapy) prior to 18F-FDG PET/CT; (3) Insufficient clinical data.

For Part 1 (mutation prediction analysis), all 64 patients with available cfBRAF V600E mutation test results were included. For Part 2 (prognostic analysis), 54 of the 64 patients with adequate follow-up data were included (Fig. 1).Fig. 1The flow diagram

Image acquisition

All patients underwent 18F-FDG PET/CT (Biograph mCT-64 PET/CT scanner; Siemens Medical Solution, Erlangen, Germany). Patients fasted for six hours with plasma glucose levels under 11.1 mmol/L and rested for at least 30 min in a quiet waiting room before intravenous administration of 18F-FDG. An activity of 3.7 MBq/kg of 18F-FDG was administered intravenously and images were acquired 60 min after injection from the whole body in free-breathing mode. The low dose CT parameters were voltage = 120 kV, current = 200mAs, layer thickness = 3 mm. The parameters of PET included 3-dimensional mode, 2 min/bed (30% overlap), 4–5 beds/person, three iterations, 21 subsets, Gaussian filter half-height width = 4.0 mm. The images were reconstructed with CT attenuation correction (AC) by using the ordered subset expectation maximization algorithm (OSEM).

Image analysis

A centralized reading of the cases was performed by two experienced physicians of nuclear medicine who blinded to the clinical information of the patients, in consensus on a commercially available workstation (syngo Multimodality Workstation, Siemens Healthineers). Areas with abnormal uptake of 18F-FDG on PET and/or abnormal density on CT were defined as lesions, and some patients also identified the lesions with other imaging (contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)/CT) and follow-up. A two-dimensional region of interest (ROI) was delineated manually according to the boundary of the tumor lesion on each layer of transaxial CT images to form a three-dimensional volume of interest (VOI). Contrast-enhanced MRI/CT was used to accurately determine the VOI. We calculated SUVmean of liver parenchyma and bone marrow (BM). To measure a normal liver activity, 3 non-overlapping spherical 1-cm3-sized VOIs were drawn in the normal liver on the axial PET images, avoiding the focal abnormal uptake areas seen on 18F-FDG PET. To measure a normal BM, the vertebral body with the highest FDG uptake from thoracic (T10-12) to lumbar (L1-4) vertebrae were drawn, avoiding the focal abnormal uptake areas seen on 18F-FDG PET/CT.

The parameters of PET/CT including lesion size (diameters, mm), SUVmean, MTV (metabolic tumor volume), TLG (total lesion glycolysis, SUVmean×MTV), SUVRliver (tumor-to-normal liver standard uptake value ratio, SUVmax of the tumor/SUVmean of the normal liver parenchyma), SUVRBM (tumor-to-normal BM standard uptake value ratio, SUVmax of the tumor/SUVmax of the normal BM), the sum of the TLGs of all lesions (tTLG) and the sum of the MTVs of all lesions (tMTV). MTV were measured from attenuation-corrected 18F-FDG-PET/CT images by two nuclear medicine physicians (WGY and WX) respectively in making these measurements. We selected regions within lesions with a SUV intensity greater than 40% for quantitative MTV measurement.

Quantification of CfBRAF V600E by DdPCR

Plasma cfDNA was isolated using the QIAamp Circulating Nucleic Acid Kit (Qiagen). The cfBRAF V600E was determined using ddPCR assay by Beijing MyGenostics Inc. The cfBRAF V600E level lower than 0.04% was regarded as negative.

As previously reported [13], blood samples were collected into EDTA-containing vacutainer tubes from patients with LCH at diagnosis. Plasma cfDNA was isolated using the QIAamp Circulating Nucleic Acid Kit (Qiagen) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and stored at −80℃ until quantification. A QX200TM Droplet Digital PCR System (Bio-Rad) was used to determine the presence and level of cfBRAF V600E mutation.

Treatment, evaluation and follow-up

All patients received chemotherapy according to BCH-LCH 2014 protocol, except 3 patients receiving cfBRAF V600E mutation targeted treatment (Dabrafenib). According to the guideline of evaluation and treatment developed by Histiocyte Society, the disease state includes nonactive disease (NAD) and active disease (regressive, stable, or progressive disease). The response to treatment includes better (B, complete resolution, or regression), intermediate (I, mixed or stable), and worse (W, progression).

All patients underwent regular follow-up at 5, 11, 25, and 52 weeks after initial treatment, and the follow-up items include hematological examination and imaging examination. The primary outcome measures were progression-free survival (PFS). PFS was defined as the time between the initiation of diagnosis and the first disease progression (AD-W or AD-I) or recurrence (new focus(es) reappears after obtaining NAD, AD-B or AD-S and maintenance therapy for more than 3 months). The last follow-up date was October 31, 2024.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables with normal distribution were expressed as mean ± SD (standard deviation) and compared using independent sample t-test. Qualitative data were expressed as number of cases and percentage [n (%)]. Homogeneity of variance of the data was verified using Levene’s test, and normal distribution of the data by Shapiro–Wilk test. The Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare pretreatment 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters among different groups. The categorical variables were analyzed by Fisher’s exact test or the Chi-square test.

ROC curve analysis was performed to determine optimal cut-off values for continuous variables in predicting disease progression. The Youden index was used to identify the optimal threshold for each parameter. AUC, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV were calculated for each cut-off point. For subsequent survival analyses, metabolic parameters were dichotomized according to ROC-derived cut-off values. Survival curves were generated using Kaplan-Meier estimates, and the log-rank test was used to assess PFS differences between groups. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to identify independent prognostic factors for PFS. Variables with P < 0.10 in univariate analysis were considered candidates for multivariate models. Forward stepwise selection was performed with an entry criterion of P < 0.05 and a retention criterion of P < 0.10 to ensure meaningful predictive value while avoiding overfitting. Hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated.

The statistical analysis was performed by using commercially available SPSS 26.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA), medcalc (version 22.026, MedCalc Software) and R 4.0.2 software (Bell Laboratories, Holmdel, NJ, USA). All statistical tests were two sided and the significance level was set at P = 0.05.

Regarding hematological parameters, non-cfBRAF V600E mutation patients exhibited higher hemoglobin levels compared to cfBRAF V600E mutation patients (126.05 ± 13.00 vs. 106.77 ± 15.81 g/L, P < 0.001). Inflammatory markers also showed significant differences, with CRP levels being higher in cfBRAF V600E mutation patients (16.86 ± 14.57 vs. 7.09 ± 9.22 mg/L, P < 0.001). Additionally, significant differences were noted in organ involvement, including liver, spleen, lung, thymus, skin and gastrointestinal tract. The distribution of clinical stages also differed significantly between the two groups [SS: 7 (32%), MS RO-: 10 (45%), MS RO+: 5 (23%) vs. SS: 30 (72%), MS RO-: 11 (26%), MS RO+: 1 (2%), P = 0.001].

Comparison of 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters between patients with cfBRAF V600E mutation and non-cfBRAF V600E mutation

Compared with patients with non-cfBRAF mutation, those with cfBRAF mutations exhibited significantly higher metabolic parameters on 18F-FDG PET/CT. Specifically, the differences were observed in SUVmax (7.5 ± 4.3 vs. 5.1 ± 3.5, P = 0.016), SUVRliver (7.1 ± 3.2 vs. 3.8 ± 2.3, P < 0.001), SUVRBM (4.3 ± 2.1 vs. 2.7 ± 1.5, P < 0.001), tTLG (97.8 ± 116.7 vs. 23.1 ± 45.8, P = 0.001) and tMTV (22.8 ± 23.6 vs. 5.3 ± 8.3 cm3, P < 0.001).

The differential diagnostic performance of 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters and clinical variables in cfBRAF V600E mutation and non-cfBRAF V600E mutation

The ROC curve analysis showed that SUVRliver and tMTV exhibited superior diagnostic capability among the 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters. For SUVRliver, the optimal cut-off value was 6.9, with an AUC of 0.806 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.688–0.894). The diagnostic performance were as follows: sensitivity of 72.7% (95% CI: 49.8%−89.3%), specificity of 83.3% (95% CI: 68.6%−93.0%), PPV of 69.6% (95% CI: 52.6%−82.5%), and NPV of 85.4% (95% CI: 74.4%−92.1%). For tMTV, the optimal cut-off value was 6.2, with an AUC of 0.792 (95% CI: 0.673–0.884). The corresponding diagnostic performance were: sensitivity of 72.7% (95% CI: 49.8%−89.3%), specificity of 78.6% (95% CI: 63.2%−89.7%), PPV of 64.0% (95% CI: 48.6%−77.0%), and NPV of 84.6% (95% CI: 53.8%−87.0%). Correlation between 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters and cfBRAF V600E mutation

Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to explore the relationship between 18F-FDG PET/CT parameters and cfBRAF V600E mutation status. Part 2. The value of 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters in predicting the prognosis of children with LCH

Patients’ characteristic and comparison of 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters between progression and non-progression

A total of 54 pediatric patients with LCH were enrolled in this part study, comprising 32 boys (59.3%) and 22 girls (40.7%), with a median age of 4.85 years (2.31–7.55). During a median follow-up period of 25.2 months (6.2–36.8), recurrence or progression was observed in 18 patients (33.3%). The BRAF V600E mutation was detected in 19 patients (35.2%). Clinical classification showed SS disease in 30 patients (55.6%), MS RO- disease in 20 patients (37.0%), and MS RO + disease in 4 patients (7.4%).

According to the metabolic parameters of 18F-FDG PET/CT, patients with disease progression exhibited significantly higher values compared to those without progression. Specifically, SUVmax was 8.4 ± 4.0 vs. 4.6 ± 3.1 (P<0.001), SUVRliver was 7.0 ± 3.5 vs. 4.0 ± 2.4 (P = 0.001), SUVRBM was 4.1 ± 1.9 vs. 2.8 ± 1.5 (P = 0.004), tTLG was 106.8 ± 101.4 vs. 13.6 ± 19.5 (P = 0.001) and tMTV was 25.6 ± 23.3 vs. 3.9 ± 3.7 cm3 (P = 0.001).

Discussion

This study investigated the dual clinical value of pre-treatment 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters in pediatric LCH: predicting cfBRAF V600E mutation status and stratifying prognosis. Our findings demonstrated that tMTV was the strongest independent predictor for both mutation status and patient outcomes, with early treatment response at 11 weeks providing additional prognostic information. Notably, cfBRAF V600E mutation itself was associated with more aggressive disease features but did not independently predict progression-free survival.

LCH represents a spectrum of disorders with heterogeneous clinical manifestations, from spontaneously resolving single-system lesions to life-threatening multisystem disease with risk organ involvement [2, 16]. Traditional prognostic factors include clinical stage, organ involvement, age, and treatment response [13, 17], with multisystem disease carrying 30–40% mortality rates [18, 19]. However, the complexity of clinical staging and the need for invasive biopsies underscore the value of non-invasive, quantifiable biomarkers.

While 18F-FDG PET/CT has demonstrated diagnostic value in pediatric LCH [20, 21], its prognostic utility remains underexplored. Building upon our preliminary work [22], we expanded the cohort, extended follow-up (median 25.2 months), and systematically evaluated multiple metabolic parameters. All baseline parameters were significantly elevated in patients who progressed (all P ≤ 0.004). Critically, multivariate Cox regression identified only tMTV ≥ 14.66cm3 (HR = 18.139, P = 0.006) and non-response at the 11th week (HR = 7.342, P = 0.030) as independent predictors of PFS.

The superiority of tMTV over SUVmax reflects fundamental tumor biology principles. SUVmax represents peak metabolic activity in a single voxel and is susceptible to noise and artifacts [23], while tMTV integrates spatial extent and metabolic activity across all disease sites, providing comprehensive quantification of tumor burden. This volumetric approach captures disease heterogeneity and multifocality—particularly relevant in LCH where lesions frequently occur at multiple sites. The combination of baseline tMTV and 11-week response assessment provides complementary prognostic information: baseline tMTV enables early identification of high-risk patients for treatment intensification, while early response assessment validates treatment efficacy and guides subsequent management.

Our observed cfBRAF V600E mutation prevalence of 34% is consistent with lower frequencies (10–39%) reported in Asian pediatric LCH cohorts [24, 25], compared to 38–69% in Western populations [2, 26]. We demonstrated for the first time that 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters could effectively discriminate cfBRAF V600E mutation-positive from mutation-negative LCH patients. Among the metabolic parameters evaluated, tMTV emerged as the strongest independent predictor of mutation status, alongside clinical factors including younger age and advanced disease stage.

The mechanistic basis lies in MAPK pathway regulation of cellular metabolism. The BRAF V600E mutation causes constitutive MAPK/ERK activation through structural changes that mimic activation segment phosphorylation [27, 28]. Activated ERK stabilizes HIF-1α even under normoxic condition [29], which functions as a master regulator of glycolytic metabolism by upregulating glucose transporter expression (GLUT1/GLUT3) and glycolytic enzymes including hexokinase II [29, 30]. This metabolic reprogramming, known as the Warburg effect, increases cellular glucose uptake and phosphorylation. This results in elevated 18F-FDG uptake on PET imaging, explaining the observed association between cfBRAF V600E mutation and elevated metabolic parameters [28, 31].

The demonstration that metabolic parameters predict cfBRAF V600E mutation has important therapeutic implications. Vemurafenib, a selective BRAF V600E inhibitor, shows remarkable efficacy in refractory LCH [32], but mutation testing requires invasive biopsy or liquid biopsy, which may not always be feasible in young children. 18F-FDG PET/CT could serve as a non-invasive screening tool to identify high-probability mutation-positive patients. While elevated tMTV alone is insufficient for definitive diagnosis, it can help identify patients who need molecular testing. When combined with clinical risk factors such as young age and multisystem disease, elevated tMTV can prompt earlier consideration of BRAF-targeted therapy. Moreover, baseline tMTV may help identify patients who would benefit most from targeted therapy, as high metabolic burden correlates with elevated progression risk. The dual predictive value of tMTV for both mutation status and prognosis makes it as a potential biomarker for precision medicine approaches in LCH.

Consistent with previous reports [10–12], cfBRAF V600E mutation was associated with younger age, advanced stage, multisystem involvement, lower hemoglobin, and elevated CRP, reflecting more aggressive disease. However, its prognostic significance remains controversial. While some studies reported associations with treatment resistance and inferior survival [33, 34], others—including ours—found no independent prognostic impact [35–38]. In our cohort, the cfBRAF mutation was significant in univariate but not multivariate analysis.

This discrepancy suggests that cfBRAF mutation influences prognosis primarily through high-risk clinical features rather than through exerting direct independent effects. Once clinical variables and metabolic burden (tMTV) are accounted for, mutation status contributes little additional prognostic information. Treatment protocol variations, biological heterogeneity (co-occurring mutations, epigenetic factors), and sample size limitations likely contribute to inconsistent findings across studies. Importantly, our findings do not diminish the therapeutic relevance of cfBRAF mutation, but suggest the tMTV may be a more robust biomarker capturing the combined effects of genetic drivers, tumor biology, and disease extent.

This single-center study has limitations including modest sample size, potential optimism bias from internal ROC validation, and technical/physiological factors affecting metabolic measurements (reconstruction algorithms, blood glucose levels, uptake time). Follow-up duration was relatively short, and molecular heterogeneity beyond BRAF was not fully characterized. External validation in multi-institutional cohorts with standardized protocols is needed to confirm these findings and evaluate serial PET imaging for treatment response monitoring.

Conclusion

Pre-treatment tMTV represents a powerful, non-invasive biomarker with dual clinical utility in pediatric LCH: predicting cfBRAF V600E mutation status and independently stratifying progression risk. Combined with early treatment response assessment, metabolic imaging enables comprehensive risk stratification and may guide personalized therapeutic strategies, including identification of BRAF-targeted therapy candidates. These findings support routine incorporation of quantitative metabolic assessment into clinical evaluation of pediatric LCH patients and provide a foundation for precision medicine approaches.

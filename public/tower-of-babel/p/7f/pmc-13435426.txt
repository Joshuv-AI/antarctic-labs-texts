Staining pattern and clinicopathological features of Epstein-Barr virus, Latent Membrane protein 1 expression in patients with gastric carcinoma in Lake zone, Tanzania
Authors: Athuman Chalamila, Edrick Elias, Oscar Ottoman, Subira Matiku, Cosmas Mbulwa, Jackson Kahima, Idd Shabani, Aroan Orgenes, Alex Mremi, Peter Rambau
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInfectious agents such as the Epstein-Barr virus are responsible for 10% of gastric carcinomas worldwide. The lake zone in Tanzania lies in an Epstein-Barr virus-endemic (EBV) belt, and the contribution of EBV-related gastric carcinoma has not yet been established. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the prevalence, staining pattern and clinicopathological features of EBV-related gastric carcinoma at Bugando Medical Center.MethodsThis was a cross-sectional study conducted at Bugando Medical Center. Latent membrane protein-1, stained on formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks, to determine the prevalence and staining pattern of EBV-associated Gastric carcinoma. Data was entered and cleaned in Excel and then analyzed using STATA version 15. The Chi-square tests were used to assess associations at a significant level of p < 0.05.ResultsA total of 119 cases were evaluated, with 70 (58.8%) males and 49 (41.2%) females. The prevalence of LMP-1 overexpression was 48(40.3%). LMP-1 positive cases were significantly associated with age 45–60 years, poor differentiation and diffuse histological type. The expression pattern was predominantly nuclear in 37(77.1%), while 11(22.9%) were nuclear and cytoplasmic.ConclusionsGastric carcinoma in our setting showed a high level of LMP-1 overexpression. These findings suggest that all patients aged 45–60 years, with poorly differentiated carcinoma and diffuse histological type, should be screened for LMP-1 overexpression as a predictive marker for targeted therapy and favorable prognostication.

Background

Globally, gastric cancer is the fifth most common malignancy and the fourth leading cause of cancer mortality, and the ninth most common cancer in Africa, with notable regional variation. Higher incidence rates in Northern and Eastern Africa, 5.4 and 4.9 per 100,000, respectively [1]. In a hospital-based study, gastric cancer contributed 4.5% in cancer admissions in Northern Tanzania [2].

Gastric cancer develops through complex interactions between environmental and host factors. Histologically, the World Health Organization (WHO),2019, classifies gastric cancers by cell origin, with epithelial cancers being most common, especially adenocarcinomas, subdivided into tubular, papillary, mucinous, signet-ring, and mixed types [3]. Lauren’s classification divides Gastric Carcinoma into intestinal and diffuse types, which is still relevant for management decisions, prognosis, epidemiology, and pathogenesis [4].

Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV), a herpesvirus with > 95% global prevalence, infects epithelial and lymphoid tissues with a link to pathologies ranging from benign self-limiting to malignancies, including gastric carcinoma [5]. There is a significant geographical variation in the prevalence of EBV-related gastric carcinoma. Japan is the leading country in Asia to report a high prevalence of EBV-related gastric carcinoma by 27% [6], and in Africa, Angola has the highest prevalence, accounting for 36% [7] and the range of 2–36% [8–10], However, there is a limitation of data from East Africa. EBV oncoproteins, like Latent Membrane protein 1(LMP1), promote oncogenesis by enhancing cell proliferation and survival through NF-kB pathway activation [11]. In comparative studies between EBV-negative and EBV-positive gastric carcinoma, the EBV-positive gastric carcinoma was common in males, young age, and located in the in upper or middle parts of the gastric [10, 12]. However, studies done in Algeria and Sudan have shown there were no significant associations between EBV expression and gender, tumor location, and histology type [7, 8]. Histopathologically, EBV-related gastric carcinoma has been reported to be the diffuse and poorly differentiated type [13, 14] with less lymph node metastases [6].

Although EBV in Gastric Carcinoma on formalin fixed paraffin embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks is better detected by molecular techniques such as EBER in-situ hybridization (EBER-ISH), PCR/qPCR, and in situ PCR [15]. The accurate diagnosis of EBV-associated malignancy necessitates the identification of the viral genome or its gene products, as well as the location of the virus within the malignant cell, which makes EBER-ISH the gold standard, as it is highly sensitive and specific, detecting EBV RNA directly in tumor cells, but requires specialized probes, labor-intensive, and provides less morphological details [16]. In low and middle income countries, including Tanzania, the limitations to molecular tissue diagnosis are not only availability and high cost, but also the poor fixation of the specimens [17]. Thus, in our settings of poorly fixed specimens, EBER-ISH, which is specific for EBV-encoded small mRNA, may have high chances of false negative results [18]. There is a scarcity of literature comparing the immunohistochemical and molecular methods in the detection of EBV-associated gastric cancer. Three studies have shown low sensitivity of the LMP1 immunohistochemical method in comparison to EBER ISH, up to 36% with specificity up to 100% [7, 16, 19], however, these findings were constrained by a limited sample size and the stringent cytoplasmic and membranous localization of the LMP1 staining pattern. The EBV LMP-1 localization is variable, commonly in the cell membrane and cytoplasm, but some studies have demonstrated cytoplasmic and nuclear staining [11, 20, 21]. Nuclear localization may result from protein interactions, pathway activation, epigenetic modulation, or latency shift [22, 23]. Even with significant sensitivity limitations, LMP1 immunohistochemistry is widely available in low and middle-income countries, and recommended by Sun et al. in the review, as it offers a simple and cost-effective method for EBV detection in the tissue in low and middle-income countries [18] for the detection of EBV-related gastric cancer.

Bugando Medical Center serves a population of 20 million people in the Lake Zone in Tanzania, a geographical area in the EBV endemic belt with an established contribution of EBV-related Burkitt lymphoma [24]. No study has been done to establish the expression of EBV LMP-1 in gastric carcinoma. To prepare for the future use of the EBV-related cancer prophylactic vaccine, which is currently being developed and targets the LMP1 protein implicated in the control of critical components that contribute to the transformation of normal cells into tumours [25, 26].It is important to assess the magnitude of EBV-LMP-1 expression in gastric carcinoma in Lake zone, Tanzania.

This study aimed to assess the prevalence, staining pattern, and clinicopathological features of Epstein-Barr Virus, Latent Membrane protein 1 expression in patients with Gastric carcinoma at Bugando Medical Centre, Mwanza, Tanzania, to enhance patient stratification to identify those who should be tested for EBV.

Methods

Study design

This was a cross-sectional study of all tissue blocks from patients diagnosed with gastric carcinoma at Bugando Medical Centre in Mwanza, Tanzania.

Study setting and population

This study was conducted at Bugando Medical Centre (BMC), Mwanza, Tanzania. The BMC is a zonal referral hospital with established pathology services for cytology, histopathology, and immunohistochemistry. On average, Bugando Medical Centre Laboratory processes 7000 histology specimens per year, and it has a cancer treatment centre with capacity for both medical and radiation treatment. The facility serves about 20 million population of the Lake zone with high EBV prevalence. The study involved archived formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks with a diagnosis of gastric carcinoma.

Variables

Independent variables included clinical features such as age, sex, type of biopsy, location of biopsy, and pathological features such as depth of invasion, Pathological stage, Histological type, Lymph node metastasis, and Degree of differentiation and the Dependent variable was LMP-1 protein expression.

Data sources

Gastric carcinoma cases diagnosed by the H&E routine staining technique that fulfilled the inclusion criteria were serially enrolled from August 2024 to May 2025. Using a structured laboratory worksheet that includes demographic information and clinical characteristics was extracted from the patient file via the eHMIS/DISA system.

Laboratory procedures

The hematoxylin and eosin-stained slides were retrieved and reviewed. Those slides confirmed gastric carcinoma, and whose blocks were available with adequate and well-preserved tissue for subsequent immunohistochemistry, were further sectioned for immunohistochemistry.

Immunohistochemistry

The sectioned at 4 μm and placed on super-frosted, positively charged glass slides for EBV-LMP1 immunohistochemical stain by manual method.

Antigen retrieval

The Antigen retrieval solution EnVision Flex Target Retrieval solution with PH of 9 and tissue sections in a buffer was heated at 20 °C for 25 min to unmask the antigen.

Blocking

The tissue sections were then treated with a peroxidase-containing blocking solution to decrease endogenous peroxidase activity and prevent background staining.

The primary antibody incubation

A primary Ready to use monoclonal mouse anti - anti-Epstein-Barr Virus, Clones CS.1–4 from DAKO Company, was used, CS1-4 anti-LMP-1 dilution 1:200, which was applied to the tissue sections for 2 h and allowed to bind to the antigenic sites followed by a secondary antibody incubation: For the detection of bound primary mouse antibody, biotinylated rabbit antiserum against mouse immunoglobulins (Dako with LOT number 41815385).

Secondary antibody incubation

A secondary antibody conjugated to an enzyme, horseradish peroxidase, was applied to the tissue sections for 30 min.

Detection

The bound antibody was detected by Diaminobenzidine chromogen (DAB) from DAKO company with batch no. LOT 41,691,376, which was applied to the tissue sections for 5 min, to react with the enzyme conjugated to the secondary antibody. This produces a visible brown color change at the site of the antigen-antibody complex.

Counterstaining and coverslip

The hematoxylin was applied to the tissue sections for counterstaining to enhance the contrast of the staining, followed by mounting and cover slipping using a mounting medium DPX.

Interpretation

Both H&E slides were interpreted by two licensed and independent pathologists to assess for histological subtype, degree of differentiation, immunohistochemistry staining, and localization. The TNM stage was evaluated in the resection specimens. The stained tissue sections were examined under a light microscope to determine the LMP-1 expression level. A known case of LMP-1-positive Hodgkin lymphoma tissue was used as an external on-slide positive control by observing cytoplasm and membranous stain in the Reed-Sternberg cells. The interpretation of LMP1 was based on an Allred scoring system (percentage of stained cells and intensity of staining). The staining intensity was scored as no staining (0), weak + 1, moderate + 2; and strong + 3. The percentage of positive cells was scored on a scale from 0 to 5 based on the percentage of stained cells. The detailed information is as follows: Nill (0), < 1% [1], 1–10% [2], 11–33% [3], 34% − 66% [4], and 67–100% [5]. These two scores were combined, and scores of 0–2 were regarded as negative, and > 3 were regarded as positive, as per other studies [22, 23]. Thus, in this study, an LMP1-positive case was defined as any localization, moderate intensity in more than 10% of the tumor cells, excluding the background staining.

Sample size and sampling

A sample size was estimated using the Kish-Leslie formula of cross-sectional studies, by using the LMP1 expression in gastric carcinomas of 13.6% [8] at a 95% confidence interval. All sampling techniques were used to recruit patients’ tissue blocks with a diagnosis of gastric carcinoma. The study included 119 participants who met the inclusion criteria.

Data analysis

Data were entered in an Excel spreadsheet and cleaned, and then exported to STATA version 15v1 (San Antonio, Texas) for analysis. Descriptive statistics were used to calculate the frequencies and proportions of the categorical variables. Descriptive statistics were done to determine the association between age, sex, location of the tumor, lymph node status, histological grade, pathological stage, degree of differentiation, and histological type. The association was determined by chi-square to assess statistical significance. EBV LMP-1 status was evaluated through binary variables as EBV LMP-1 positive and EBV LMP-1 negative, where the Chi-square determined the association between clinicopathological features and EBV LMP-1 expression, the P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

This study was designed and reported in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines to ensure transparency and completeness in reporting observational research.

Results

Clinicopathological characteristics of the study participants

A total of 270 gastric tissues were received during the study period; only 190 cases were gastric carcinoma, but blocks and complete clinical data could be retrieved in 140, of which 21 cases had poor tissue quality for histopathology and immunohistochemical evaluation, and thus were excluded. Of the remaining 119 participants, the majority were male70(58.82%), and females 49 (41.18%). aged 45–60 years, accounting for 55(46.22%). Most tissue samples were obtained through endoscopy 98(82.35%), and the antrum was the most common site of tumor location 54(45.38%) and the predominant histological type was intestinal type adenocarcinoma 64(54.24%). There was almost an equal proportion of poorly and moderately differentiated tumors, accounting for 51(42.86%) and 50(42.02%), respectively. The depth of invasion (T) and lymph node (N) status could not be assessed in 98(82.35%) and 102(85.71%), respectively, as the majority were endoscopic tissue.

A total of 48 (40.3%) cases were positive for LMP-1, and the majority, 37 (77.1%), had nuclear staining, and 11(22.9%) showed both nuclear and cytoplasmic localization (Table 1). Fig. 1 shows different antigen localization and negative staining.

Discussion

The expression of EBV in Gastric carcinoma was 40%. Being aged 45–60 years, diffuse type, poorly differentiated, and pathology stage T4 tumors were significantly associated with EBV expression.

This study has demonstrated the highest EBV LMP-1 expression rate in gastric carcinoma than previously documented, up to 37% [10]. Differences in LMP-1 expression may reflect environmental, lifestyle, host genetic factors, and EBV strain variations [27]. EBV-positive carcinomas were commonly reported to be located in the upper or middle stomach [10, 11], but this study found more cases in the antrum, similarly to Kattoor et al. [28]. There is a greater histological diversity of gastric carcinoma histological subtypes related to EBV [11]. In the current study, a diffuse histological type was the predominant subtype, aligning with the other study [29], though some report equal distribution [30] or predominance of intestinal type [31].

Similar to other studies, the EBV-positive carcinomas show poor differentiation [13, 14]. Beek et al. proposed that EBV-induced immune responses may limit lymph node micrometastasis [31] This was supported by the presence of fewer lymph node metastases in EBV-positive gastric carcinoma in this study; however, the association was limited by few resection specimens from which the lymph nodes were available for evaluation.

In resected specimens, the EBV expression was at an advanced stage as tumors were invading the serosa, which is similar to other studies [29, 32], but contrary to some studies, which associate EBV with gastric carcinoma to be an early-stage disease limited to the submucosa [10, 33]. EBV LMP-1 generally shows membranous and cytoplasmic staining due to its transmembrane structure(7,29). However, this study found predominantly nuclear staining, with some cases showing both nuclear and cytoplasmic localization, similar to findings by Yasmin Benakli and Ailiang Wang [11, 21]. Nuclear localization may result from protein interactions, pathway activation, epigenetic modulation, ethnic variants, or latency shifts [11, 22]. Additionally, LMP-1 staining was seen in adjacent non-neoplastic and dysplastic mucosa, supporting the hypothesis that EBV infection precedes malignant transformation [7, 8]. The limitations of our study include the absence of molecular diagnosis, few resection specimens for TNM staging, and the possibility of immunohistochemistry false negative staining due to over-fixation of specimens, as the Bugando Medical Centre, Pathology laboratory receives samples from far distant hospitals.

Conclusions

Gastric carcinoma in our setting showed a high level of LMP-1 overexpression. These findings suggest that all patients aged between 45 and 60 years with poor differentiated gastric carcinoma and diffuse histological type at Bugando Medical Centre should be screened for LMP-1 overexpression as a predictive marker for targeted therapy and favorable prognostication.

Increased angiogenesis-osteogenesis coupling in the endplate at early stages of intervertebral disc degeneration
Authors: Shenlei Feng, Yuxuan Liang, Qiang Lian, Hang Zhou, An Ning, Yuanhao Fan, Ying Wang, Guangheng Li, Chao Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLow back pain is closely linked to intervertebral disc degeneration, with endplate remodeling representing an early and potentially decisive event. Our study investigated whether lumbar spine instability provokes early osteogenesis–angiogenesis coupling in mouse EPs and mapped the three-dimensional spatial relationship between Osterix⁺ cells and CD31highEndomucinhigh (type-H) blood vessels. A lumbar spine instability model in C57BL/6J male and female mice induced early degenerative changes in the cranial endplate, with micro-CT analysis revealing progressive degeneration by 4 weeks and histological sections showing pronounced cartilage sclerosis. To visualize and quantify vascular and osteogenic interactions, we employed a three-dimensional thick-section immunofluorescence imaging technique combined with quantitative spatial correlation analysis optimized for skeletal vasculature mapping. Although the overall type H vessel volume did not increase significantly at these early stages, the number of Osterix⁺ cells was markedly elevated after 4 weeks. Spatial analysis demonstrated that mechanical instability rapidly recruits osteogenic cells to within 20 micrometers of blood vessels, establishing a defined angiogenesis–osteogenesis coupling process. In conclusion, early LSI-induced recruitment of Osterix⁺ cells to type H vessel niches in the cranial endplate initiates ossification and identifies a temporally and spatially explicit window for interventions targeting angiogenesis–osteogenesis coupling to prevent the progression of IVDD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42343-z.Keywords: Intervertebral disc degeneration, Endplate, Angiogenesis-osteogenesis coupling, OsterixSubject terms: Anatomy, Diseases, Medical research

Introduction

Low back pain is a widespread musculoskeletal disorder that affects over 80% of individuals during their lifetime1,2. The economic burden and medical stress of LBP are expected to increase in the future3–5. Lumbar spine degeneration, especially intervertebral disc (IVD) degeneration (IVDD), is a major contributor to low back pain6,7. IVD consists of three main components: nucleus pulposus (NP), annulus fibrosus (AF), and endplate (EP)8. These structures form the central fibrocartilaginous tissue of IVD8. The core of the IVD has traditionally been considered avascular, suggesting that any invading blood vessels during angiogenesis must originate from surrounding tissues9. Situated between vertebrae and the NP, the EP plays a crucial role in molecular transport, which supports the metabolic activities of the IVD10–12. However, the role of EP has been largely overlooked during IVDD13. Among the IVD components, degeneration of the EP is a conspicuous and early indicator of IVDD progression14,15.

During IVDD, calcification of IVD is a prominent feature, resembling the endochondral ossification observed in the degenerative stage of osteoarthritis (OA)16,17. Ossification in OA is often preceded by angiogenesis, which drives vascular invasion into previously avascular cartilage, facilitating ectopic ossification and structural breakdown18. Similar processes have been identified in IVDD, although the EP has traditionally received limited attention despite its crucial roles in load distribution, nutrient exchange, and mechanical coupling between IVD and vertebrae10,13. Sclerosis of the EP, induced by osteogenesis, is a feature of its degeneration. It is driven by osteoblast-lineage cells that form new bone matrix in the EP19,20.

The activities of osteogenesis and angiogenesis have been shown to be closely associated with type-H vessels (CD31high Endomucinhigh)21. The vasculature provides crucial perivascular niches for osteoblast progenitor cells to differentiate and facilitate bone formation18,21–23. While type-H vessels promote bone formation under physiological conditions21,24,25, their expansion into cartilaginous tissue, such as growth plates or cartilaginous EP, has been associated with aberrant ossification and skeletal degeneration26,27. Recent studies have shown that type-H vessels are present within the degenerated EP of mice undergoing IVDD, where they colocalize with osteogenic cells and contribute to endplate sclerosis28–30. Furthermore, inhibiting vascular ingrowth has been verified to ameliorate EP degeneration and thus delay IVDD progression, underscoring the pathogenic significance of angiogenesis-osteogenesis coupling28.

Lumbar spine instability (LSI) models have been widely developed to investigate intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD)31. Although numerous studies have employed this model, most have focused on postoperative durations of four weeks or longer, capturing only advanced stages of IVDD28,30,32–35. Bian et al.36 reported that LSI can induce endplate degeneration as early as two weeks post-surgery, and subsequent work has indicated that early pathological events such as angiogenesis and osteogenesis may also arise within this early period28,37. These findings suggest that the early postoperative stage may represent a key window for the onset of degenerative processes, offering new opportunities for early diagnosis and therapeutic intervention. Therefore, elucidating the coupling of angiogenesis and osteogenesis during this early period may be particularly valuable for understanding the initiation and potential modulation of IVDD.

In this study, we explored the relationship between osteogenesis and angiogenesis in the EP during the early phase of IVDD induced by LSI surgery in mice. We proposed that LSI triggers early recruitment of Osterix⁺ cells to type-H vessels in the EP, leading to EP degradation. To investigate this, we utilized three-dimensional thick-section immunofluorescence imaging of the intervertebral disc to visualize the distribution and spatial association of type-H vessels and Osterix⁺ cells at two- and four-weeks post-LSI. Few studies have visualized early IVDD processes in three-dimensional tissue, underscoring the significance of this work. Our objective was to map early cellular and vascular events within the EP microenvironment and to determine whether spatial proximity between Osterix⁺ cells and type-H vessels underlies the onset of EP sclerosis and disc degeneration.

Results

Establishing IVDD model in mice

The spinous processes of lumbar vertebrae L3 to L5 were resected to disrupt mechanical stability and thereby induce abnormal stress in the intervertebral discs (IVDs) (Fig. 1a). A three-dimensional model of the lumbar spine confirmed successful surgery (Fig. 1b). To evaluate IVD degeneration, we first identified distinct anatomical regions within the IVD, including the nucleus pulposus (NP), annulus fibrosus (AF), and endplate (EP) (Fig. 1c). Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining of the L4/5 IVD demonstrated degenerative changes including increased cell clusters, presence of lesions, and the degenerated extracellular matrix as early as two weeks post-surgery (Fig. 1d, e), with the EP and AF exhibiting the most pronounced histological alterations (Fig. 1f-h).

Cranial EP of IVD L4/5 had more significant degeneration

EPs in different locations may respond differently after LSI surgery. To investigate the possibility, we analyzed both cranial and caudal EPs from L3 to L5. Three-dimensional micro-CT model observations and quantitative analysis (Fig. 2a, b) revealed minor differences at 2-week post-surgery but significant differences at 4-week post-surgery in bony EP. Quantitative analysis of micro-CT data from the 4-week post-surgery group showed significant difference. The trabecular separation (Tb.Sp) of both endplates in IVD L4/5 was significantly higher in the LSI group compared to the sham group (Fig. 2c). The porosity of the cranial bony endplate in IVD L4/5 significantly increased, suggesting a greater number of cavities within the bony endplates (Fig. 2d). These changes indicated that the cranial endplate in IVD L4/5 is affected earlier and more severely in IVDD, serving as an indicator in early-stage IVDD. The increase in porosity and Tb.Sp also suggested that more marrow cavities have invaded the bony endplates, accompanied by blood vessels invasion and osteogenesis-related cells migration. The changes in bony endplate also reflected the degeneration occurred in the whole endplate. And as indicated in the figure, both male and female mice were included in the micro-CT analyses across all experimental groups. No significant differences were observed between males and females. Accordingly, we did not perform sex-separated analyses in the subsequent experiments.

Histology staining with H&E and safranine O & fast green (SOFG) was performed on the 2-week post-surgery group to further evaluate the degree of EPs degeneration (Fig. 2e). H&E staining revealed obvious bone marrow presence in the cranial endplates of both IVD L3/4 and L4/5 in the LSI group compared to the sham group. Cell size, surrounding cavities, and alignment were used to define the differentiation status. In the LSI group, cells appeared smaller with shrunken cavities and disrupted alignment compared to the sham group, suggesting that cells were undergoing proliferation and differentiation in the cranial endplates in IVD L3/4 and L4/5. In SOFG staining, the cranial and caudal EPs in IVD L4/5 showed a greater area stained with fast green rather than safranine O in the LSI group compared to the sham group, indicating sclerosis of the cartilaginous tissue in EPs. To comprehensively evaluate the degeneration of different EPs, the endplate score was introduced. Based on the histological staining results, the cranial EP in IVD L4/5 exhibited the most significant difference between the LSI and sham group (Fig. 2f). Consistent with the micro-CT analysis results, we selected the cranial EP in IVD L4/5 as the target region for subsequent analysis.

Osterix+ cells but not type-H vessels increased significantly during early stage IVDD

To investigate the basis of EP degeneration and sclerosis observed in micro-CT and histology, we performed thick-section immunofluorescence on the IVD region. The angiogenesis of type-H vessels in EPs was accessed to evaluate the progression of degeneration. We used immunofluorescence to stain CD31, Endomucin (EMCN), and Osterix in the cranial EP of IVD L4/5 (Fig. 3a). The colocalization of CD31 and EMCN labeled type-H vessels throughout the entire region of EP. However, the fluorescence results showed no significant differences in type-H vessel volume across all four groups (Fig. 3b). Additionally, type-H vessels were observed in EPs of all mice, which differed from previous data. Osterix serves as a marker for osteogenesis-related cells in the skeletal system. Consequently, it is commonly used to assess the level of osteogenesis. In the targeted endplates, the Osterix+ cells number per voxel significantly increase in the LSI-4-week group compared to both sham-4-week (Fig. 3c). However, no significant difference was observed between the 2-week-LSI and 2-week-sham groups (Fig. 3c). This suggests that Osterix+ cells, which contribute to osteogenesis, play a critical role in early stage of IVDD. Although we failed to indicate any difference within type-H vessels, the increased Osterix+ cells number still implied possible angiogenesis-osteogenesis coupling in EPs.

LSI increased osteogenesis-angiogenesis coupling

Osteogenesis-angiogenesis coupling is regarded as a primary factor contributing to sclerosis. The early occurrence of osteogenesis-angiogenesis coupling may be crucial for the progression of IVDD. Definitive study revealed that the majority Osterix+ cells group surrounding type-H vessels within 20 micrometers in long bone21, and several investigations have utilized co-labeling of Osterix+ cells and type-H vessels to indicate the coupling of angiogenesis and osteogenesis by identifying the upregulation of the Osterix+ cells number within certain distance from the type-H vessels38–40. Three-dimensional spatial analysis was employed to determine the spatial relationship between Osterix+ cells and type-H vessels. The 2-week and 4-week post-surgery groups were analyzed for their three-dimensional spatial relationship using immunofluorescence staining of Osterix and type-H vessels (Fig. 4a). To confirm whether the Osterix+ cells align their behavior with previous results related to the coupling, we analyzed their percentages in different distance ranges (Fig. 4b). The results indicated that all four groups showed same or more percentage than 70% in the range of 0 to 20 micrometers from type-H vessels, verifying its similar to the discovery in long bone21. And Osterix+ cells exhibited a tendency to attach more closely to type-H vessels after LSI surgery (Fig. 4b). Also, we noted that Osterix+ cells number was highly related to type-H vessels volume. In the LSI-4-week group, the Osterix+ cells number per unit type-H vessels volume significantly increased compared with Sham-4-week group, and slightly higher than 2-week post-surgery group, indicating potential relationship between the cells and vessels (Fig. 4c).

To confirm the coupling in our targeted areas, we specifically, analyzed the number of Osterix+ cells in the area surrounding (≤ 5 μm) type-H vessels. Results showed that the number of Osterix+ cells in our defined region was significantly higher in the LSI-4-week group compared to sham-4-week group, but not in the 2-week post-surgery group, suggesting that osteogenesis progressed more severely after 4 weeks than 2 weeks of surgery (Fig. 4c). However, there was no significant difference in the number of Osterix+ cells between the LSI-2-week and LSI-4-week (Fig. 4d).

To further confirm the detailed spatial tendency between Osterix+ cells number and type-H vessels, we plotted the curves in relationship with distance from type-H vessels and their numbers from 0 to 20 μm. The LSI-2-week group demonstrated a more pronounced difference from the sham-2-week group in the closest area, whereas the LSI-4-week showed tighter coupling around the 10 μm range. The LSI-2-week and LSI-4-week groups exhibited similar trends with minor difference in the 10 to 20 μm range (Fig. 4e). Normalized data showed more reliable evidence that the LSI-4-week group exhibited intensified osteogenesis–angiogenesis coupling in the cranial endplate of IVD L4/5 compared with both the LSI-2-week and sham groups (Fig. 4f), demonstrating approximately 3-fold and 1.5-fold greater coupling, respectively. The evidence, consistent with previous discoveries of increased Osterix+ cells number surrounding type-H vessels in the EPs (Fig. 4e, f), indicated that the percentage of Osterix+ cells surrounding type-H vessels in the LSI-4-week group increased across the entire EP, thereby validating the conclusion that the LSI-4-week group exhibits increased osteogenesis-angiogenesis coupling in endplates during early stage of IVDD.

Discussion

Our results are consistent with earlier studies demonstrating enhanced osteogenic activity during early stages of IVDD. In our LSI mouse model, the cranial endplate of the L4/5 disc exhibited pronounced ossification between 2- and 4-weeks post-surgery, along with elevated expression of Osterix (Sp7) (Fig. 5). Osterix, a transcription factor marking osteogenic cells—including osteoblasts, osteocytes, and osteoprogenitors41–43. In earlier studies, Osterix is regarded as a transcription factor or a marker of osteogenesis is essential for bone matrix formation and has been implicated in sclerosis of cartilaginous tissues28,44,45. Although previous studies have mainly investigated Osterix in vertebral bone maintenance42, emerging evidence shows that its expression in endplates increases during aging and degeneration36,44,45. The observed rise in Osterix⁺ cells in our model parallels these findings, supporting the notion that early mechanical stress can drive osteogenic activation in endplates. Furthermore, consistent with earlier reports28, our data also suggest that angiogenesis and osteogenesis are closely coupled during early endplate degeneration.

Three-dimensional spatial analysis revealed that Osterix⁺ cells were preferentially localized within 5 μm from CD31highEmcnhigh (type-H) vessels in the endplates of LSI mice, whereas this association was much weaker in the sham group. The spatial correlation directly corresponded the well-established definition of the coupling of angiogenesis and osteogenesis in this study. The upregulated number of Osterix+ cells number surrounding type-H vessels clearly indicated the intensified osteogenic activities at the angiogenesis location, which aligns our hypothesis that the intensified osteogenesis–angiogenesis coupling following LSI appears to be a key event in the initiation of EP ossification. The enhanced interaction between osteogenic and angiogenic compartments suggests that type-H vessels actively recruit Osterix⁺ osteoprogenitor cells, thereby priming the endplate for early bone formation. Consequently, the degree of this coupling may serve as an early indicator for predicting the progression of IVDD. Consistent with previous studies21,46, our results demonstrate that type-H vessels provide a niche supporting adjacent osteogenic cells, coupling vessel formation with osteogenesis. Type-H vessels are known to promote ossification by recruiting osteogenic precursors in long bone21,47 and cartilage26. Similarly, our findings indicate that the spatial association between type-H vessels and osteogenic cells in the endplate parallels that observed in long bone38,39. This convergence implies that the axial and appendicular skeletons possibly share comparable mechanisms of angiogenic–osteogenic coupling. However, this relationship does not seem to extend to the skull48, possibly because of differences in the extracellular matrix composition and resident cell populations.

The mechanical instability imposed by LSI model leads to responses of endplate matrix cells and osteoprogenitors that share a part of conserved mechanotransducive pathway with other cartilaginous tissues, such as articular cartilage in knee and mandibular joints49,50, including upregulation of Osterix under abnormal loading43,51, and matrix remodeling52. However, a few anatomy-specific distinctions are noteworthy. Endplates in spine is a thin, avascular cartilaginous interface that buffers compressive loads between the soft disc and vertebral bone. The abnormal stress generated during LSI recruits Osterix⁺ cells to perivascular niches before any detectable expansion of type-H vessels14. Moreover, unlike the appendicular skeleton, where type-H vessels arise from metaphyseal arteries and drive growth-plate ossification53, type-H vessels that invade endplates possibly originate from vertebrae capillaries and invade a previously avascular cartilaginous compartment. The embryonic origin and phenotype of the skeletal stem cells in the axial skeleton is unique and markedly different from appendicular and cranial bones54. Direct connection with disc tissue, also likely modulates the molecular mediators of the coupling to blood vessels13. Consequently, we hypothesized that while the core mechanosensitive pathways are similar, spatial regulation of angiogenesis and osteogenesis in endplates produces an early, pre-sclerotic tissue that precedes vascular expansion, an event that not previously quantified in the endplates of mice.

We observed an intriguing decline in the number of Osterix+ cells per unit volume in the sham group, as shown in Fig. 3c, from 2 weeks to 4 weeks postoperatively. Upon further analysis, we found that Osterix+ cells in both the sham and LSI groups exhibited an unexpected elevation at the 2-week postoperative time point, rather than this phenomenon being confined to the sham group alone. We hypothesize that during the LSI procedure, regardless of whether it involved the experimental or sham group, the dorsal surgical approach imposed direct abnormal mechanical stress on the lumbar spine in a direction opposite to the normal intervertebral angle. Although the experimenters perceived the applied force as minimal, it may nonetheless have caused considerable damage in mice, particularly in the endplate region of the intervertebral disc, as the endplate transmits and buffers forces conducted through the lumbar vertebrae. Previous literature has demonstrated that mechanical stimulation increases Osterix expression in skeletal tissues28,55,56. The abnormal ventral-directed stress on the lumbar spine generated during surgery may have led to the elevated Osterix expression observed at 2 weeks postoperatively, whereas by 4 weeks, tissues in the sham group may have recovered from this abnormal stress, while the LSI group at 4 weeks continued to experience sustained abnormal loading due to surgically induced lumbar instability.

The origin of the Osterix+ cells observed surrounding type-H vessels in mice EPs is still unclear. Regarding the origin of the invading Osterix+ cells, our 3D spatial imaging data hint at some possible sources. The perivascular localization of newly recruited Osterix⁺ cells is consistent with recruitment of vertebral bone-marrow-derived osteoprogenitors that migrate along invading vasculature, as previously demonstrated in long-bone development and fracture repair57. Alternatively, resident progenitor cells within the endplate itself may activate Osterix expression under mechanical stress. Indeed, stem cell or progenitor populations have been isolated from both healthy and degenerated human and murine endplates58. A third possibility is transdifferentiation of hypertrophic chondrocytes into osteoblast-like cells, a process now recognized as a major contributor to endochondral ossification and observed in degenerated disc tissues59,60. These different origins carry distinct mechanistic and therapeutic implications: marrow-derived recruitment would highlight vascular invasion as a key driver and potential target, whereas local endplate progenitors or chondrocyte transdifferentiation would favor strategies that modulate intrinsic mechanosensitive programs within the endplate61. Our current findings of rapid Osterix⁺ expansion and perivascular enrichment before vessel volume increases most strongly support the recruitment model, but do not exclude local contributions.

The mechanism behind the osteogenesis-angiogenesis coupling in the EPs has not been discovered yet. Senescent macrophages in degenerated IVD have been shown to secrete IL-10, driving STAT3-mediated VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) expression, increase Osterix expression and proliferation of type-H vessels in the endplate28. Also, Osteopontin deficiency could accelerate endplate remodeling by the progressive invasion of type-H vessels in the endplate with an increase in the number of macrophages and osteoclasts29. Moreover, in ovariectomy-induced vertebral osteoporosis models, the elevated vascularization was coupled with increased Osterix expression and VEGF immunoreactivity in endplates, leading to osteochondral remodeling of the EP62. Taken together with our data, these studies suggest that the endplate, normally a stable interface, may initiate remodeling during the process of degeneration. The early recruitment of Osterix+ cells to the type-H vessels niche in endplates likely accelerates endplate ossification, compromising IVD homeostasis and contributing to IVDD progression. In contrast to Osterix, Runx2 may also contribute to IVD degeneration through its greater effect on vessels63,64. Runx2 is an upstream transcription factor which usually cooperates with Osterix during osteoblast differentiation43. In osteogenesis-angiogenesis coupling, Runx2-expression cells showed a greater mobilization tendency in locating adjacently around type-H vessels, indicating its potential in clarifying this coupling in the EP21.

Contrary to previous results28,33,65, our study did not observe a significant increase of type-H vessel volume at early stage after LSI surgery. It can be attributed to shorter post-surgery observation point, which is insufficient for significant blood vessels sprouting. Indeed, Fan et al.28 and Wang et al.37 found abundant type-H vessels in endplates of LSI mice at 2-week post-surgery. However, this may be due to our unique immunofluorescence methodology, which was performed in three-dimension space. Vessels’ volume was used as the unit of measurement. Nonetheless, most studies used intensity mean value65, vessels area percentage29, or cells counts per area33 to quantify results. It is evident that these commonly used measures all two-dimensional. This implies that the information is restricted to a thin slice, which can lead to misinterpretation of the results. Our thick-section deep-imaging fluorescence method, which provides a larger sample size and more accurate representation of the uneven vascular tissue in the endplate, should be more representative than previous fluorescence imaging techniques. But subtle angiogenesis changes may require extended post-surgery duration beyond 2 to 4 weeks or larger animal models with bony endplates to fully appreciate.

It should be acknowledged that other factors, not examined in this research, may also contribute to IVDD. The instability of the lumbar spine alters load transmission and induces uneven stress distribution within the endplate, which may initiate local degenerative cascades. Therefore, molecular pathways responsive to mechanical stimulation warrant further investigation in the LSI model. One promising candidate is Piezo1, a mechanosensitive ion channel potentially regulating the attachment of Osterix⁺ cells to type-H vessels in the endplate. Deletion of mechanosensors such as Piezo1 in osteoblasts has been shown to cause bone resorption and impair skeletal homeostasis66, underscoring its essential role in Osterix⁺ osteogenic cells. Moreover, inhibition of Piezo1 attenuates IVDD67, whereas its activation exacerbates degeneration68, emphasizing its dual impact on disc health. In addition, given the established involvement of macrophages in IVDD28,29,69 and the emerging evidence that Piezo1 modulates macrophage activation and function38,70, exploring mechanoresponsive macrophages within the endplate could provide crucial insight into the interplay between mechanotransduction, inflammation, and degeneration. Previous studies have shown that sensory innervation is closely associated with angiogenesis, mediated by nerve growth factor secreted by endothelial cells27. Although innervation typically precedes angiogenesis during bone regeneration71,72, this may differ in the avascular tissue of the IVD. Researchers have observed the simultaneous occurrence of innervation and angiogenesis during IVDD, attributed to inflammatory cytokines73 or senescent osteoclasts65; however, few evidence suggests that innervation precedes angiogenesis in this context. Furthermore, aberrant mechanical stress induced by LSI can promote nerve ingrowth into the AF74, indicating a potential link between mechanical stress pathways and innervation in IVDD. Recent studies have identified the endplate as a critical structure in LBP, as it is the most susceptible region of the IVD to vascular invasion, which subsequently induces innervation30,33,65. Innervation-induced LBP in EPs has been treated with parathyroid hormone30 and low-dose celecoxib33, highlighting their translational potential.

In summary, our study shows that LSI surgery initiates an early osteogenic process within the endplate, featuring expansion of Osterix⁺ cells and their spatial association with type-H vessels. Through three-dimensional immunofluorescence and spatial distance analysis, we visualized angiogenic–osteogenic coupling in the endplate during the early stage of IVDD. These findings open new avenues for early diagnosis and targeted intervention in disc degeneration. Future research should further explore the contributions of osteoprogenitors and endplate stem cells in coordinating angiogenesis and osteogenesis under abnormal stress, aiming to preserve endplate integrity and overall intervertebral disc health.

Materials and methods

Animal model

All mice used in the research were of the C57BL/6J strain. All mice were fed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) environment by Southern University of Science and Technology Animal Care Facility, where all diet and water were under strict management from the birth of mouse until samples collection. Eight- to twelve-week-old male and female mice (18.15–24.64 g) underwent lumbar spine instability (LSI) surgery under general anesthesia. Total of 6 mice were included in each group and 12 mice were included in each experiment. There were 36 mice in total used in the research. Only the animal surgery experimenters were responsible for the allocation of mice to each group for all stages. The LSI procedure involved resection of the spinous processes from L3 to L5 and transection of the supraspinous and interspinous ligaments, as previously described31,33. In sham-operated mice, the posterior paravertebral muscles were exposed and detached for the same extent but the ligaments and spinous processes were left intact. Incisions were closed by suturing the skin only; muscle layers were not reapproximated, consistent with prior reports31,33. All mice were euthanized by carbon dioxide (CO₂) asphyxiation followed by cervical dislocation. The procedure involved placing the animals in a sealed chamber and administering a continuous flow of pure CO₂ (≥ 99%) for 2 to 3 min. Spinal segments L3–L5 were harvested at 2- and 4-weeks post-surgery for downstream analyses. All mice were anesthetized before and during the operation by breathing 2% isoflurane gas. 1% erythromycin ointment (Guangdong Hengjian Pharmaceutical, China) was smeared on the wound after surgery. Only animals showing normal behaviors were included. Animals from the same litter were randomly assigned to control or LSI group. For each experimental group, if the values of n are equal or larger than a specific number, it indicates that mice after surgery were in bad healthy situation and euthanasia was performed. The humane endpoint for all the mice was defined as the occurrence of severe infection, impaired mobility, cessation of feeding, or rapid weight loss. All animal researchers were responsible for the observation of animals’ status and ensured the timely execution for the animal welfare. The sample size for each experiment was determined using our prior work and preliminary experiments.

Microcomputed tomography and analysis

Samples were scanned using a micro-CT system (SkyScan 1172, Bruker) with a tube voltage of 60 kV, tube current of 100 µA, and an isotropic voxel size of 6 μm. Reconstructed image stacks were analyzed in CTAn (Bruker) to quantify porosity and trabecular separation (Tb.Sp). Three-dimensional visualization was generated in CTvox (Bruker). Regions of interest (ROIs) corresponding to the cranial and caudal endplates of IVD L3/4 and L4/5 were used for all quantitative measures. Tb.Sp (trabecular separation) was calculated according to the manufacturer’s algorithm and represents the mean distance between trabecular structures within the ROI. Porosity was defined as the bone volume over total volume of the ROI.

Histological staining

The spine samples were fixed in 4% PFA (Diluted from 16% PFA, 28908, Thermo Scientific, USA) at 4 °C for 24 h, and decalcified in 0.5 M EDTA solution at 4 °C for 7 weeks, with EDTA replaced weekly. After decalcification, samples were dehydrated and embedding in paraffin (Leica ASP 300 S, Leica). 6 μm-thick coronal spine paraffin sections were cut on a microtome (Leica CM1950, Leica). Slides were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E; G1120, Solarbio, China) as well as safranine O and fast green (SO&FG, S8884, F7258, SIGMA) according to the descriptive literature.

Intervertebral disc (IVD) degeneration was graded using a histopathology scoring system (Table S1) adapted from a previously published human standard75. To accommodate mice anatomy, the bony endplate component of that human scoring system was omitted. Endplate degeneration was assessed separately using a dedicated endplate histopathology scoring system (Table S2)76.

Immunofluorescent staining of thick vertebral section

After euthanasia, the mouse vertebral cryosections were prepared followed the instruction of a protocol77. All samples were fixed in 4% PFA at 4 °C for 12 h, decalcified by 0.5 M EDTA solution at 4 °C for 3 days, and cryoprotected at 4 °C for 1 day. Samples were cryoembedded with the dorsal side facing upward and stored at − 80 °C for up to one year before sectioning. A cryostat (Leica 3050 S) was used to cryosection samples. The slicing thickness was 80 μm for sagittal plane. Cryosections were stored at −80 °C. Slides were chosen to maximize the imaging surface: mid-sagittal slides were chosen from the sagittal series.

The vertebral sections were rehydrated, permeabilized, blocked, and incubated in primary antibodies solution. The primary antibodies we used including anti-endomucin (Emcn) (V.7C7, 1:400, SANTACRUZ), anti-Osterix (ab22552, 1:400, Abcam), anti-CD31 (FAB3628G, 1:400, R&D). After 24 h, primary antibodies were washed and stained with secondary antibodies including donkey anti-goat Alexa Fluor 488 (A11055, 1:400, Thermo Fisher Scientific), donkey anti-rat Alexa Fluor 594 (A21209, 1:400, Thermo Fisher Scientific), and donkey anti-rabbit Alexa Fluor 647 (A31573, 1:400, Thermo Fisher Scientific). After secondary incubation, sections were washed with phosphate buffer saline (PBS). Nuclei were stained with DAPI (1:10,000; Sigma-Aldrich, D9564-10MG) and coverslipped with DAPI FluoroMount-G (0100 − 20, SouthernBiotech) or FluoroMount-G (0100 − 20, SouthernBiotech).

Confocal and two-photon imaging

Three-dimensional images were acquired on a Zeiss LSM 980 confocal microscope using a 20× objective for sagittal sections. The x–y field of view varied with the targeted IVD; z-stacks consisted of 40 optical sections (total depth 80 μm) for sagittal sections. Line averaging (double scans) was used to reduce noise. The lateral spatial resolution was 0.414 μm. The second harmonic generation (SHG) images were acquired using an FVMPE-RS Multiphoton laser scanning microscope (Olympus, Japan). An 800 nm laser was used to excite the autofluorescence of collagen fibers inside IVD and the emissions were captured by 420–465 filters. The images size and z stack were same with their confocal imaging.

Three-dimensional image analysis

Confocal image stacks were processed in Imaris (v9.0.1, Bitplane, Oxford Instruments, Switzerland) and ImageJ (Fiji). Single-channel images were thresholded using identical minimum intensity values across samples to ensure consistent fluorescence quantification. The quantitative regions of interest were selected: EP for sagittal section and whole IVD for cross section images. Three-dimensional surfaces of type-H vessels and Osterix+ cells were constructed to acquire the volumes and numbers. Colocalization of type-H vessels used CD31 and Emcn channels with their minimum threshold (Video S1). WA watershed algorithm was applied to segment Osterix+ objects, with a minimum object diameter set to 4 μm to separate closely apposed signals. Osterix+ cells were defined as DAPI nuclei co-localized with Osterix immunofluorescence. The maximum threshold of SHG images’ intensity was regulated to enhance its brightness.

Spatial analysis of the angiogenesis and osteogenesis coupling

This study defines the coupling as the spatial correlation of the Osterix+ cells with type-H vessels using the methodology as previously reported38–40,78. The distance distribution of type-H vessels’ surface was exported in TIFF format by the Distance Transformation function in Imaris. The detailed three-dimensional position data of Osterix+ cells was saved as Comma Separated Values (CSV) files. Matching TIFF figure and position datum were loaded in MATLAB and calculated the shortest distance between type-H vessel and Osterix+. The final output was a histogram with distance from type-H vessels and the corresponded cell numbers. To eliminate the influence of the increased cell numbers in changing the quantification of cell numbers distribution relating to distance, we applied a division with random distribution cells with the same amount to calculate a normalized result.

Statistical analysis

Statistical significance between two groups was all analyzed by two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test. Multiple tests with single variable used multiple t-test. Data of experiments were displayed as mean ± SD for column figures and mean for distance analysis curve figures. GraphPad Prism (v9.3.0, GraphPad Software) was used for all statistical analysis. The data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Before conducting the t-test, we first used the Shapiro-Wilk test to assess the normality of the data, and then verified the homogeneity of variances between groups through the Levene test. For data that meet the assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance, comparisons between the two groups are conducted using the independent samples t-test (when comparing two independent groups) or the paired samples t-test (when comparing the measurements of the same group at different time points). The statistical significance level is set at p = 0.05. When pairwise comparisons need to be conducted among multiple groups, we employed multiple t-tests. Given that multiple comparisons increase the probability of committing the first type of error (false positive), we corrected the original p-values obtained. This study employed the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure (for FDR correction) to control the false discovery rate (FDR), and set FDR < 0.05 as the criterion for statistical significance. This method is more lenient compared to the Bonferroni correction. It can better preserve the statistical power while controlling for false positives.

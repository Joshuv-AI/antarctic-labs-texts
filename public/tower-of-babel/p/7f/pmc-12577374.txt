Application of interpretable machine learning to predict activities of daily living disability in sarcopenia: insights from the CHARLS dataset
Authors: Zixian Song, Tingting Pan, Ying Yang, Ze Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe decline in activities of daily living (ADL) among older persons is a significant public health concern. Sarcopenia is a major risk factor for ADL disability. This study aimed to develop and validate an interpretable machine learning (IML)-based model to predict ADL disability in sarcopenia patients using the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) dataset.MethodsParticipants diagnosed with sarcopenia between 2015 and 2018 were included and divided into training and test cohorts (8:2 ratio). Data were pre-processed using the edited nearest neighbor method (ENN). The Boruta algorithm, XGBoost model, and SHAP method were employed for feature selection, prediction, and interpretation. Model performance was assessed using AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and F1 score.ResultsADL disability occurred in 258 (22.5%) of 1146 sarcopenia patients. Key predictors identified by SHAP analysis included depression severity, walking speed, grip strength, and age. The XGBoost model achieved an AUC of 0.803 and 0.738 in the training and test sets, respectively, with good interpretability.DiscussionThe model demonstrated strong predictive performance and identified critical factors influencing ADL disability, underscoring the potential of IML in geriatric risk stratification.ConclusionThe IML-based model provides a scientific basis for developing health management strategies aimed at reducing ADL disability in older adults with sarcopenia, thereby improving quality of life and easing societal and familial care burdens.

Introduction

Sarcopenia is an age-related clinical syndrome characterized by progressive and extensive generalized skeletal muscle mass loss and decreased muscle strength and function [1]. The overall prevalence of sarcopenia in men and women over the age of 60 is estimated to be approximately 10% [2]. Sarcopenia is not only strongly associated with adverse health outcomes such as falls, fractures, and increased mortality, but is also considered a major risk factor for disability in older adults [3, 4].

The decline in functional capacity, especially in activities of daily living (ADL), has significant adverse effects on older patients with sarcopenia. ADL disability refers to difficulties in performing basic daily activities, such as dressing, bathing, and eating, which often result in a decline in self-care abilities and an increased dependence on care among elderly individuals [5]. The complexity and diversity in the ADL of older people highlight the need for targeted interventions to support their independence [6]. Numerous studies reported an association between ADL disability and sarcopenia [7–12], and some indicators of weakness and sarcopenia, such as weight, gait speed, and grip strength, were predictors for future ADL disability [13]. Nonetheless, despite the fact that these single dimensional biomarkers demonstrate certain predictive power, they are not sufficient to sheerly capture the multifactorial nature of ADL disability occurrence and development. The progression of ADL disability is a complex pathophysiological process involving multidimensional interactions of physiological, psychological and social factors [14]. For this reason, the key challenge in clinical practice is how to effectively integrate heterogeneous baseline data of patients, including demographic characteristics, comorbidity burden, functional status and social support systems, to construct accurate individualized long-term risk stratification models. The urgent need for comprehensive and individualized evaluation highlights the necessity of exploring advanced analysis methods that can handle high-dimensional complex datasets.

In an effort to address the above limitations, machine learning (ML) provides new solutions for such prediction problems. This study used the Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) algorithm [15] whose high efficiency and precision advantages in processing high-dimensional and complex data make it a highly satisfactory choice for building accurate prediction models [16, 17].Simultaneously, this study introduces the SHapley Additive Interpretation (SHAP) method [18] to address the common “black box” problem in ML models and enhance their clinical credibility. As an advanced interpretable machine learning (IML) technique, SHAP can quantify the contribution of each predictor variable to the prediction results of a single sample, thereby making the decision logic of the model transparent to clinical workers.

On this basis, this study aims to develop and validate an interpretable machine learning model using data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) to predict the long-term ADL disability risk in elderly patients with sarcopenia. Given that accurately and prospectively identifying high-risk populations remains a clinical challenge in the field of geriatric medicine, the development of such predictive tools has important practical value. A precise predictive model can open a critical ‘window period’ for clinical intervention, which is advantageous for us to focus limited medical resources on individuals at the highest risk, and implementing personalized measures including nutritional intervention, rehabilitation therapy, and psychological support to delay or prevent the process of disability. The ultimate goal of this study is to provide scientific basis for individualized risk stratification and precise prevention of sarcopenia patients, thereby ameliorating the quality of life of the elderly population and reducing the social and family care burden arising from it.

Materials and methods

Data and participants

Data in this study originated from the CHARLS dataset. CHARLS is a nationwide representative longitudinal survey that collects data on the health, socioeconomic status, and family structure of individuals aged 45 and over in China. This dataset includes a large number of health-related variables, such as physical well-being, cognitive functioning, mental health, and ADL capacity [19]. The CHARLS dataset, which contains rich and diverse data, provides a unique opportunity to study the impact of health status and socioeconomic factors on ADL disability in older adults.

We included 13,633 elderly participants aged over 60 from the 2015–2018 research wave, of which 1146 were eligible for model development and validation. In the 2015 survey, none of these participants were disabled and were diagnosed with sarcopenia. Inclusion criteria: (1) Participants aged 60 years or older; (2) Participants who met the 2019 diagnostic criteria for sarcopenia developed by the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) [1]; (3) Participants who had complete responses for ADL (a measure of disability) in the 2015–2018 survey; (4) Participants who reported no ADL impairment in the 2015 survey and had no baseline disability. Exclusion criteria: (1) Participants who reported problems related to ADL disability, including physical disability or brain damage/intellectual disability; (2) Participants diagnosed with serious conditions such as cancer and dementia; (3) Participants diagnosed with emotional, neurological, or psychiatric disorders by a physician; (4) Participants lacking sarcopenia-related measures including grip strength, walking speed, 5 times sit to stand test (5TSTS), height, and weight. Study variables

Evaluation of sarcopenia

Sarcopenia was assessed according to the AWGS 2019 criteria [1], which encompassed muscle strength, physical performance, and appendicular skeletal muscle mass (ASM). The cutoffs for low grip strength were 28 kg for males and 18 kg for females. The decline in physical performance was defined as a duration of >12 s in the 5TSTS or a speed of < 1 m/second in the 6 min walk test (6WMT). Grip strength (kilograms) of the dominant and non-dominant hands was measured using the YuejianTM WL-1000 dynamometer. The participant was asked to hold the handle as hard as possible for 3 s. Two measurements were taken for each hand, with at least 15 s between each measurement. The maximum grip strength of the dominant hand was used in this study. The ASM measurements were derived using a validated anthropometric equation developed specifically for the Chinese population [20]. Several studies have shown that the ASM calculated by this formula is highly consistent with dual energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) [20, 21]. Height and weight were recorded in centimeters and kilograms, and male and female were represented by the numbers 1 and 2, respectively. According to a previous study [22, 23], the cutoff for low muscle mass is determined based on sex-specific height-adjusted muscle mass of the lowest 20% of the study population (ASM/height2): <4.89 kg/m2 for females and < 6.79 kg/m2 for males. Sarcopenia is characterized by decreased muscle strength, physical dysfunction, or decreased ASM. Sarcopenia is usually diagnosed by the detection of declined muscle strength alone or accompanied by a decrease in ASM. Individuals with concurrent low muscle strength, impaired physical function, and low ASM are classified as having severe sarcopenia [24].

Evaluation of disability

ADL disability was evaluated using the Katz Index of Independence in Activities of Daily Living [25]. The Katz ADL index measures the respondent’s capability of carrying out daily tasks such as dressing, bathing, feeding, transferring, toileting, and continence. Each item is scored 1 or 0 based on the level of independence, with a total score of 6. A higher score indicates greater independence.Each ADL item is scored 0 if the task is performed without problem, or 1 if the task is performed with difficulty or cannot be performed at all. The scores of the six items are summed and defined as: (1) no disability (ADL score = 0) and (2) disability (ADL score ≥ 1).

Prediction variables

Disability-related predictors were initially screened based on their clinical significance and relevance, and then selected according to previous studies [26, 27]. A total of 46 potential demographic factors were selected, including demographic characteristics (age, sex, place of residence, solitary living, marital status, and educational level), lifestyle and healthy behaviors (smoking, alcohol consumption, sleep duration, exercise duration, social activity, and cooking fuel), laboratory findings (white blood cells, hemoglobin, platelets, triglycerides, creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, total cholesterol, glucose, uric acid, cystatin C, C-reactive protein, and glycated hemoglobin), physical findings (grip strength, standing balance, walking speed, 5TSTS, and body mass index), history of diseases (heart disease, hypertension, arthritis, diabetes, liver disease, or kidney disease), history of falls, and memory and depressive symptoms measured by the 10-item Centre for Epidemiological Studies Depression Scale (CES-D).

Statistical analysis

Data pre-processing

The data were first divided into the training set (80%) and test set (20%). Since some variables in the dataset had missing values, those with more than 40% missing data were removed. The remaining variables had a low proportion of missing values (< 10%). Missing values were imputed by KNN imputation using the VIM package separately for the training and test sets. The normal reference ranges of blood biochemical parameters in the Chinese population were determined and classified based on relevant studies and guidelines [28–30].

Given that participants with ADL disability only accounted for 22.5% of all participants, class imbalance and noise in the data may lead to reduced classifier performance [31, 32]. The XGBoost algorithm has a built-in function for handling data imbalance, which balances the weight of positive and negative samples by adjusting the scale_pos_weight parameter [16]. To minimize possible noise in the data, the Edited Nearest Neighbors (ENN) method was employed after selecting significant features [33]. Count data are presented in numbers and percentages and analyzed by the chi-square test. Continuous data without normal distribution are expressed as median and interquartile range, and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. The training set was used for model development and parameter optimization, while model generalizability on unknown data was evaluated using the test set.

Model construction and evaluation

Data analyses were completed on R 4.3.0, and a P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Before modeling, the training set and test set were normalized separately. Significant features were selected from the initial 46 variables using the Boruta method. The importance of each feature was then evaluated using a stochastic forest model after random feature scrambling, and features that significantly contributed to model performance were retained [15]. An XGBoost model was constructed. Hyperparameter tuning was performed in the training set by Bayesian optimization [34] using the 10-fold cross-validation strategy. Model performance in the training set was evaluated using the 10-fold cross-validation method. The threshold for classifying disability was determined using the Youden index and a confusion matrix was subsequently constructed. The optimized model was validated in the test set to assess the generalizability of the model on unknown data. Model performance in both the training and test sets was evaluated by area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and F1 score, and the clinical utility of the model was assessed by the decision curve analysis (DCA) curve of the test set.

Model interpretation

The SHapley Additive eXplanation (SHAP) method was used to explain the black-box model. SHAP values were used to calculate the impact of each feature on the prediction results based on game theory, with each feature representing a participant. SHAP fairly attributed the predictive performance to each feature, thereby explaining the contribution of each feature to a single observation. The importance of each feature was determined by calculating its average absolute SHAP value. To better visualize the overall pattern and the extent to which the features affect the dataset, the SHAP values of each feature were plotted for each sample. In addition, the nonlinear effects of features were assessed using SHAP dependence plots. Two examples of SHAP predictions were provided for demonstration.

Results

Data processing

The study included 1,146 older adults with sarcopenia. After 3 years of follow-up, 258 of these individuals developed disability, resulting in a disability rate of 22.51%. The subjects were divided into the training (918 participants, 207 with disability) and test sets (228 participants, 51 with disability) at an 8:2 ratio. After eliminating noise and redundant samples from the training set using the ENN method (nearest neighbors K = 3), the total number of participants in the training set was 516. Baseline characteristics were not significantly different between the two sets (P > 0.05) except for liver disease (P = 0.043), indicating the absence of bias caused by uneven distribution of variables between the two sets (Table 1).

Model interpredtation

SHAP was used to visually explain the XGBoost model and demonstrated the effect of each variable on the 3-year disability rate. Figure 5a depicts the SHAP values for 12 risk factors, and Fig. 5b shows the feature importance. The SHAP beeswarm plots and feature importance plots showed that the severity of depression, walking speed, grip strength, and age were the most influential features for model prediction. Specifically, high depression scores, slower walking speed, and older age were associated with positive SHAP values, suggesting that they contributed to a higher risk of ADL disability. In contrast, higher grip strength was associated with negative SHAP values, suggesting a protective effect in ADL disability. Furthermore, the presence of audiovisual impairment and higher BMI were also associated with a higher risk of ADL disability. Figure 5c presents the SHAP prediction for an individual sample. The SHAP dependence plot (Fig. 5e) also shows the impact of individual features on the XGBoost prediction model output. Taken together, these data showed that the model can effectively identify and quantify the influence of each feature on the prediction result, confirming the efficiency of SHAP in model interpretation.

Discussion

This study successfully developed and validated a model grounded in interpretable machine learning (XGBoost) using the large national longitudinal dataset (CHARLS) in China, aiming to prospectively predict the risk of daily living (ADL) disorders in elderly patients with sarcopenia within three years. Our research confirms that the model displays favorable predictive performance and clinical interpretability, especially illustrating enormous potential in identifying high-risk individuals. Through SHAP analysis, we revealed that depression, walking speed, grip strength and age are the four most critical factors in predicting ADL disability, providing new scientific basis and targets for developing precise and personalized prevention strategies.

The core contribution of this study consists in the construction of a robust predictive model and the transparent explanation of its “black box” decision-making process. The model obtained AUC values of 0.803 and 0.738 on the training and testing sets, respectively, indicatingillustrating its superior discriminative ability and ability to effectively identify sarcopenia patients who may develop ADL disorders in the future. It is noteworthy that the model exhibited a sensitivity of up to 84% and specificity of 58% on the test set. This combination of performance characteristics offers crucial practical significance in clinical screening applications. High sensitivity ensures that the model can identify the vast majority of patients who are about to develop ADL disorders to the maximum extent possible, thereby significantly reducing the missed diagnosis rate. From the standpoint of public health and clinical decision-making, the consequences of false negatives in preventing serious adverse outcomes such as disability (such as irreversible loss of function, increased burden of family and social care) are far more severe than those spawned from false positives (such as additional follow-up or brief anxiety) [35]. As a consequence, a model that prioritizes high detection rates may have higher clinical application value, as it creates the most critical window of opportunity for implementing early and prospective intervention measures.

Aside from that, the most striking finding of this study is the prominent role of depression. Although previous studies have recognized the association between depression and functional decline [36], it is generally considered one of the many risk factors. The SHAP analysis of this study revealed that the severity of depression (CES-D score) is the factor that contributes the most to model prediction among all features, and its ability to surpass traditional physiological indicators as the core risk driver may be ascribable to its systematic breakdown of functional reserves in the elderly through multiple and intertwined pathways: at the behavioral level, core symptoms of depression such as decreased motivation and lack of pleasure directly give rise to a sharp decline in physical activity and rehabilitation compliance, thereby accelerating muscle “disuse” decline [37–39]; At the physiological level, depression is bound up with chronic low-grade inflammation. Elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines (such as IL-6 and TNF - α) in the body promote muscle protein breakdown [40–42]. Meanwhile, depression often accompanies dysfunction of the hypothalamic pituitary adrenal (HPA) axis, leading to long-term elevation of cortisol levels. Cortisol, as a catabolic hormone, exacerbates muscle depletion [43]. On top of that, depression can exacerbate “age-related anorexia”, leading to severe deficiencies in the intake of key nutrients such as protein necessary for maintaining muscle mass [44]. These behavioral, physiological and nutritional issues interact with the physical symptoms of sarcopenia (such as fatigue and weakness) to form a self reinforcing vicious cycle: the decline in physical function exacerbates depressive emotions, and deepening depression in turn further destroys muscle health through multiple pathways, ultimately giving rise to a sharp increase in the risk of activities of daily living (ADL) disability.

The research findings are consistent with existing literature on multiple levels and provide new insights. Firstly, with regard to core predictive factors and overall model performance, this study once again confirms that the core components of sarcopenia - muscle strength (grip strength) and physical performance (walking speed) - are strong predictive factors for ADL disability [45].Numerous studies, such as Zhang et al.‘s [46] confirmation of the importance of lower limb function, Duchhowny et al.‘s [47] application of grip strength threshold, and Kojima’s [48] meta-analysis on frailty status, have collectively confirmed this association from different perspectives. Our research not only validates this conclusion again through machine learning models, but also accurately quantifies their relative importance in complex models through SHAP values. With respect to predictive performance, our research model achieved favorable discriminative ability on the test set (AUC = 0.738), which is comparable or even more superior than the traditional logistic regression model (C-statistics = 0.72) of Jonkman et al. [49], the best physical performance model (C-index = 0.732) of Zhang et al. [46], and the complex prognostic tool (iAUC = 0.703) for ADL disability developed by Lee et al. [50] Secondly, with regard to methodology, this study achieved an explanatory leap from “static groups” to “dynamic individuals”. In comparison with the practical but static column chart model constructed by Qi et al. [51], the XGBoost combined with SHAP method used in this study can provide dynamic and personalized risk attribution analysis for each patient (as shown in Fig. 5c), explaining why the patient is classified as high-risk, which cannot be achieved by traditional column charts. In contrast to the practical but static column chart model constructed by Qi et al. [51], the XGBoost combined with SHAP method used in this study can provide dynamic and personalized risk attribution analysis for each patient (as shown in Fig. 5c), explaining why the patient is classified as high-risk, which cannot be achieved by traditional column charts. On top of that, this study revealed the nonlinear mode of action of risk factors through the SHAP dependency plot (Fig. 5e). As revealed by the SHAP analysis results, the impact of age on the risk of activities of daily living (ADL) disability is not a simple linear relationship, but rather a significant “turning point” appears around the age of 75. Specifically, before the age of 75, the trend of risk increases relatively smoothly with age; Nonetheless, once this age threshold is crossed, the risk enters a rapidly accelerating stage, and the driving force of age on disability significantly increases. This is in stark contrast to the linear assumptions typically implicit in traditional models such as logistic regression and Cox regression, providing a more accurate basis for identifying risk “inflection points” in clinical practice. More importantly, at the level of risk perception, this study reveals the hierarchical structure of risk factors. Previous studies have mostly focused on physiological indicators, but through data-driven screening of variables from multiple fields, we have found that psychological factors are even more important than all traditional physical function indicators, becoming the primary driving factor for predicting ADL disability. This discovery provides a new perspective for us to understand and intervene in ADL disability, suggesting that mental health may play a central role in maintaining functional independence in older adults. In summary, this study is not only a validation of existing knowledge, but also a methodological and cognitive advancement. We provide a more comprehensive, dynamic, and highly individualized risk assessment framework, highlighting the enormous potential of integrating mental health and utilizing advanced machine learning methods to uncover complex data patterns in geriatric research.

Limitations and future research directions

There are several limitations in this study. First, the CHARLS dataset may lack some critical health or environmental factors. Secondly, despite the inclusion of 1,146 participants, the sample size remained limited, particularly after applying the ENN method, which further reduced the data size and could potentially impact the model’s performance and generalizability. Third, the study results were based solely on the CHARLS dataset and were not validated with independent datasets, which limited the applicability of the model to different populations. Last, the model showed decreased generalizability on new data, suggesting a possibility of overfitting, weaker performance in distinguishing normal sample s, and a tendency to generate false positive results. Therefore, further optimization of the model is required.

Future research can be improved in several ways. First, the sample size should be expanded by including more participants to improve the generalizability and predictive performance of the model. Second, more data sources, such as health data from other countries or regions, should be integrated to validate the applicability and stability of the model. For feature selection, other methods such as LASSO regression or recursive feature elimination may be explored to further improve the accuracy of feature selection. Third, developing prediction models that are dynamically updated over time to reflect the latest health data and research progress is a key direction of future research. Using wearable devices and mobile health technology to collect real-time health data from older individuals will help improve the prediction accuracy of the model. Last, it is important to compare the XGBoost model with other advanced ML models, such as deep learning models, to identify the most effective prediction method and to enhance overall model performance.

In summary, although this study has limitations, the results offer valuable insights into the health management of older individuals and highlight potential avenues for future research. Continuous improvement and refinement of prediction models will help extend their application to a broader population and support the development of personalized prevention and intervention strategies.

Conclusion

This study has constructed an IML-based model for predicting the risk of ADL disability in sarcopenia patients, and explained the impact of each feature on the prediction result using the SHAP method. The results demonstrate that the severity of depression, walking speed, grip strength, and age are key features for predicting ADL disability. Through model interpretation, the mechanisms by which these features affect ADL disability could be better understood, thus providing a scientific basis for the development of personalized prevention and intervention measures. Although this study has some limitations, the results offer valuable insights into the health management of older individuals and open new avenues for future exploration.

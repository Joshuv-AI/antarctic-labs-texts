PAARH suppresses Kupffer cell phagocytosis to promote tumor liver metastasis by upregulating CD47
Authors: Wenchuan Li, Yunyu Wei, Rongzhou Lu, Gaoxiong Tan, Xianzhe Lu, Jiajie Lin, Chuan Tan, Huamei Wei, Chen Deng, Zhongheng Wei, Jian Pu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Metastasis is the primary cause of cancer-related mortality, with the liver being a common metastatic target organ for multiple malignancies. Kupffer cells (KCs), the liver-resident macrophages, play a crucial role in suppressing liver metastasis by phagocytosing disseminated tumor cells. However, the molecules modulating KC phagocytosis and liver metastatic progression remain poorly understood. Our previous work identified PAARH as an oncogenic factor in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) that correlates with poor patient prognosis. Here, we demonstrate that PAARH functions as an anti-phagocytic molecule for KCs. PAARH facilitates liver metastasis of various tumor cells by suppressing KCs phagocytosis. In vitro phagocytosis assays showed that PAARH suppresses the phagocytosis of tumor cells cultured under hypoxia by KCs. Mechanistically, PAARH recruits HIF-1α to the CD47 promoter, activating CD47 transcription under hypoxia. Consistent with this, PAARH and HIF-1α expression positively correlate with CD47 levels across multiple human tumor tissues. CD47 was identified as a phagocytosis checkpoint for KCs, mediating PAARH’s effects on phagocytosis inhibition and liver metastasis. Clinically, high PAARH and CD47 expression in tumors were associated with increased liver metastases. Our findings establish PAARH as a critical anti-phagocytic molecule for KCs through transcriptional activation of CD47 and suggest that targeting PAARH could selectively enhance the clearance of hypoxic tumor cells, offering a potential therapeutic strategy to limit liver metastasis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12935-026-04251-0.

Introduction

Metastasis is the leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. Owing to its abundant blood supply and anatomical position, the liver is one of the most common sites of metastasis for various malignancies, including hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), colorectal cancer (CRC), melanoma, pancreatic cancer, neuroendocrine tumor, and so on [2, 3]. In HCC, intrahepatic metastases are more common than extrahepatic spread [4]. To date, liver metastases remain notoriously difficult to treat and profoundly worsen patient prognosis [2]. A deeper understanding of the molecular drivers of hepatic metastasis is urgently needed to develop more effective therapeutic strategies [5, 6].

Metastatic colonization of the liver represents the rate-limiting step in hepatic metastasis [7–9]. This process depends on dynamic interactions between disseminated tumor cells and the liver tissue microenvironment [10, 11]. As hepatic resident macrophages, Kupffer cells (KCs) play a critical role in suppressing metastasis through their potent phagocytic clearance of circulating tumor cells [12, 13]. While a limited number of molecules - including ID3, ERMAP/dectin-2, CD24, and MIR670HG - have been identified as regulators of KC phagocytic activity [14–16], the potential involvement of additional pro-phagocytic or anti-phagocytic checkpoint molecules remains to be elucidated.

Our previous study identified PAARH as a long noncoding RNA (lncRNA) associated with HCC, where it exhibits oncogenic properties and correlates with poor disease-free and overall survival in HCC patients [17]. However, its potential role in regulating KC phagocytosis remained unexplored. In this study, we demonstrate that PAARH expressed in tumor cells suppresses the phagocytosis of tumor cells by KCs, thereby promoting hepatic metastasis. Mechanistically, PAARH interacts with HIF-1α to enhance CD47 transcription. While CD47 is an established phagocytic checkpoint for macrophages [18], we further establish its critical role in KC phagocytosis. Importantly, our findings identify CD47 as the key functional mediator through which PAARH regulates KC phagocytosis and tumor liver metastasis.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Human HCC cell lines SNU-398 (Cat. No. SCSP-5206) and HCCLM3 (Cat. No. SCSP-5093), and human CRC cell line SW620 (Cat. No. TCHu101) were obtained from the National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures (Shanghai, China). Human melanoma cell line CHL-1 (Cat. No. CRL-3619) was acquired from the American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, VA, USA). SNU-398 cells were grown using RPMI 1640 Medium (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, USA) with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco). HCCLM3 and CHL-1 cells were grown using Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Gibco) with 10% FBS. SW620 cells were grown using L­15 Medium with 10% FBS. All cells were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere under normoxia. Under hypoxia, cells were cultured at 37 °C containing 1% O2, 94% N2, and 5% CO2.

Plasmids, lentiviruses, siRNAs and stable cell lines construction

PAARH overexpression plasmid pcDNA3.1-PAARH was constructed in our previous study [17]. shRNA lentiviruses targeting PAARH were also constructed in our previous study [17]. ON-TARGETplus Human HIF1A siRNA SMARTpool (Cat. No. L-004018-00-0020) was purchased from Dharmacon (Cambridge, England). One pairs of cDNA oligonucleotides targeting CD47 were cloned into the lentiviral shRNA expressing vector (GenePharma, Shanghai, China) to produce shRNA lentiviruses targeting PAARH. The shRNA sequences are 5’-GATCCGCACAATTACTTGGACTAGTTTTCAAGAGAAACTAGTCCAAGTAATTGTGCTTTTTTG-3’ (forward) and 5’-AATTCAAAAAAGCACAATTACTTGGACTAGTTTCTCTTGAAAACTAGTCCAAGTAATTGTGCG-3’ (reverse). CD47 promoter sequences were PCR-amplified with the primers 5’-TGCTAGCCCGGGCTCGAGCATTAGAGCAGCCACCAGAG-3’ (forward) and 5’-AGTACCGGAATGCCAAGCTTGTCACAGGCAGGACCCACT-3’ (reverse), followed by being cloned into the Xho I and Hind III sites of pGL3-Basic Luciferase Reporter Vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) using the NovoRec plus One step PCR Cloning Kit (Novoprotein, Suzhou, China) to construct pGL3-CD47 promoter.

To construct PAARH stably overexpressed tumor cells, PAARH overexpression plasmid pcDNA3.1-PAARH was transfected into SNU-398 and CHL-1 cells using GP-transfect-Mate (GenePharma), followed by selection with 800 µg/mL neomycin (InvivoGen, San Diego, CA, USA) for 4 weeks. To construct PAARH stable knockdown tumor cells, HCCLM3 and SW620 cells were infected with shRNA lentiviruses targeting PAARH, followed by selection with 1 µg/mL puromycin (InvivoGen) for 4 weeks. To construct PAARH overexpressed and concurrently CD47 knockdown tumor cells, PAARH-overexpressed SNU-398 and CHL-1 cells were infected with shRNA lentiviruses targeting CD47, followed by selection with 800 µg/mL neomycin and 1 µg/mL puromycin (InvivoGen) for 4 weeks.

RNA extraction and real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted using RNA isolater Total RNA Extraction Reagent (Vazyme, Nanjing, China), followed by being subjected to reverse transcription using HiScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR (Vazyme). qPCR was performed using ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme) with the primers 5’-CCCTGAAGATAAGGTTGTGC-3’ (forward) and 5’-CTGTGGTTTGCTGAGGAGTT-3’ (reverse) for PAARH, 5’-TTGTGACTTCTACAGGGAT-3’ (forward) and 5’-CAGCGAGGATATAGGCTA-3’ (reverse) for CD47, 5’-GCGAACGACAAGAAAAAGAT-3’ (forward) and 5’-GTGGCAACTGATGAGCAAG-3’ (reverse) for HIF1A, and 5’-GGGAAATCGTGCGTGACATTAAG-3’ (forward) and 5’-TGTGTTGGCGTACAGGTCTTTG-3’ (reverse) for β-actin. β-actin was used as the internal reference.

Liver metastasis assay

Male athymic BALB/c nude mice (6–8 weeks old) were purchased from SiPeiFu Biotechnology (Beijing, China). To construct in vivo liver metastasis model, tumor cells were intrasplenically injected into nude mice and 35 days later, the livers were resected and subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Animal experiments were performed according to international guidelines and the Basel Declaration, with approval from the Affiliated Hospital of Youjiang Medical University for Nationalities Institutional Review Board.

In vitro KCs phagocytosis assay

Liver tissues were dissociated as single-cell suspensions after perfusion with Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS) containing 0.04% Collagenase IV (Sigma). Hepatocytes were removed from single-cell suspensions by centrifugation at 40 g for 3 min. Nonparenchymal cells were isolated by centrifugation of the supernatant at 650 g for 7 min, after which F4/80+ KCs were isolated by Magnetic activated cell isolation, followed by verification using flow cytometry. Tumor cells were labeled with carboxyfluorescein succinimidyl ester (CFSE), followed by culture in normoxia or hypoxia. Then, 2 × 104 KCs were co-cultured with 1 × 105 CFSE-labeled tumor cells for 2 h in ultra-low-attachment 96-well U-bottom plates (Corning, New York, USA). After co-culture, cells were stained with APC anti-mouse F4/80 Antibody (Cat. No. 123116; BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA) or PE anti-mouse F4/80 Antibody (Cat. No. 111604; BioLegend). Phagocytosis efficiency was assessed by measuring CFSE intensity in KCs using flow cytometry.

In vivo KCs phagocytosis assay

CFSE-labeled tumor cells were intrasplenically injected into nude mice. At 12 h post-injection, liver non-parenchymal cells were obtained as above described and stained with 7-AAD (Cat. No. 420404; BioLegend), Brilliant Violet 711 anti-mouse CD45.2 Antibody (Cat. No. 109847; BioLegend), PE/Cyanine7 anti-mouse/human CD11b Antibody (Cat. No. 101215; BioLegend) and APC anti-mouse F4/80 Antibody F4/80 (Cat. No. 123116; BioLegend) to assess phagocytosis efficiency of KCs (CD45.2+CD11bintF4/80+). The gating strategy is shown in Fig. S1.

Flow cytometry analysis

Flow cytometry was undertaken on the Attune NxT Flow Cytometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). For quantification of protein expression, cells were stained with PE anti-human CD47 Antibody (Cat. No. 323108; BioLegend).

Cleavage under targets & release using nuclease (CUT&RUN)

The binding of HIF-1α to CD47 promoter were evaluated by CUT&RUN assays using the CUT&RUN Assay Kit (Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA) and HIF-1α antibody (Cat. No. NB100-105; Novus, Saint Louis, MO, USA). Enriched DNA fragments were quantified by qPCR with the primers 5’-GCGAAAGGCAGGAACCGAC-3’ (forward) and 5’-CGCTTCCAGGTCACGTCC-3’ (reverse) for CD47 promoter.

Dual-luciferase reporter assays

pGL3-CD47 promoter luciferase reporter was co-transfected with pRL-TK (Promega) into indicated tumor cells. At 48 h post-transfection, the Firefly luciferase and Renilla luciferase activities were measured with the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega). pRL-TK, which encodes Renilla luciferase, served as endogenous control.

Clinical data

A total of 47 primary CRC tissues and 23 CRC liver metastatic tissues were collected from the Affiliated Hospital of Youjiang Medical University for Nationalities. The use of clinical tissues was according to the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Affiliated Hospital of Youjiang Medical University for Nationalities Institutional Review Board (approval no. YYFY-LL-2023-127). TCGA RNA-seq data were downloaded using the online tool R2 Genomics Analysis and Visualization Platform (https://hgserver1.amc.nl/cgi-bin/r2/main.cgi).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (version 10.0). Differences were evaluated by Student’s t-test, Mann-Whitney test, one-way ANOVA with Dunnett’s multiple comparisons test, Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn’s multiple comparisons test, or Spearman correlation analysis as indicated in figure and table legends. p value < 0.05 was statistically significant.

Results

PAARH promotes liver metastasis of various tumor cells

To evaluate the function of PAARH in tumor liver metastasis, we stably overexpressed PAARH in human HCC cells SNU-398 (Fig. 1A). SNU-398 cells with PAARH overexpression formed remarkably more liver metastatic lesions than control cells (Fig. 1B). We further stably depleted PAARH in human HCC cells HCCLM3 which has stronger liver metastatic capacity (Fig. 1C). HCCLM3 cells with PAARH depletion formed remarkably less liver metastatic lesions than control cells (Fig. 1D). To confirm the function of PAARH in liver metastasis, we stably overexpressed PAARH in human melanoma cells CHL-1 (Fig. 1E). PAARH-overexpressed CHL-1 cells exhibited remarkably increased liver metastatic potential (Fig. 1F). We stably depleted PAARH in human CRC cells SW620 (Fig. 1G). PAARH-depleted SW620 cells showed remarkably reduced liver metastatic potential (Fig. 1H). These data suggest that PAARH promotes liver metastasis of various tumor cells.

KCs mediate the roles of PAARH in tumor liver metastasis

As hepatic resident macrophages, KCs exhibit critical roles in phagocytizing disseminated tumor cells and fighting against liver metastasis [12, 19]. To investigate whether KCs mediate the function of PAARH in tumor liver metastasis, we depleted KCs using clodronate liposomes prior to tumor cells intrasplenic injection (Fig. S2). Under KCs depletion conditions, ectopic expression of PAARH only slightly increased liver metastatic potential of SNU-398 cells (Fig. 2A, compared with Fig. 1B). Under KCs depletion conditions, PAARH depletion also only slightly decreased liver metastatic potential of HCCLM3 cells (Fig. 2B, compared with Fig. 1D). These data suggest that except the intrinsically oncogenic roles of PAARH in tumor cells, KCs extrinsically mediate PAARH’s pro-metastatic roles.

PAARH suppresses phagocytosis of tumor cells by KCs

Since KCs mediate the function of PAARH in liver metastasis, we next investigated the potential effects of PAARH on KCs phagocytosis. Tumor cells were labeled with CFSE, and then co-cultured with KCs for 2 h in vitro, followed by flow cytometry to detect the phagocytosis of CFSE-labeled tumors cells by F4/80+ KCs. However, the results showed that PAARH did not regulate the phagocytosis of SNU-398 cells by KCs in vitro, although with a decreased trend (Fig. S3A). Our previous study has found that PAARH directly binds to HIF-1α and modulates HIF-1α transcriptional activity under hypoxia [17]. Phagocytosis checkpoint CD47 was reported to be a downstream target of HIF-1α [20, 21]. Hypoxia represents a key hallmark of the liver and tumor cells must adapt to the hypoxic microenvironment to colonize the liver [22–26]. Thus, we further evaluated whether PAARH regulate KCs phagocytosis under hypoxia. Tumor cells were cultured under hypoxia, and then subjected to in vitro phagocytosis assays. The results showed that under hypoxia condition, PAARH-overexpressed SNU-398 cells were remarkably less phagocytized by KCs than control SNU-398 cells (Fig. 3A). PAARH itself was not regulated by hypoxia (Fig. S3B). Under normoxia condition, PAARH depletion did not regulate the phagocytosis of HCCLM3 cells by KCs in vitro, although with an increased trend (Fig. S3C). However, under hypoxia condition, PAARH-depleted HCCLM3 cells were remarkably more phagocytized by KCs than control HCCLM3 cells (Fig. 3B). Similarly, ectopic expression of PAARH repressed in vitro phagocytosis of CHL-1 cells by KCs under hypoxia (Fig. 3C), and conversely, depletion of PAARH enhanced in vitro phagocytosis of SW620 cells by KCs under hypoxia (Fig. 3D). We further evaluated the function of PAARH in KCs phagocytosis in vivo. CFSE-labeled tumor cells were intrasplenically injected into nude mice. In vivo phagocytosis of tumor cells by KCs was detected 12 h later by flow cytometry. The results revealed that PAARH-overexpressed SNU-398 cells were less phagocytized by KCs than control SNU-398 cells in vivo (Fig. 3E, Fig. S3D). PAARH-depleted HCCLM3 cells were more phagocytized by KCs than control HCCLM3 cells in vivo (Fig. 3F). Similarly, ectopic expression of PAARH suppressed phagocytosis of CHL-1 cells by KCs in vivo (Fig. 3G), and depletion of PAARH promoted phagocytosis of SW620 cells by KCs in vivo (Fig. 3H). These data suggest that PAARH suppresses phagocytosis of tumor cells by KCs.

PAARH upregulates CD47 expression

Next, we investigated the effects of PAARH on CD47. qPCR and flow cytometry showed that ectopic expression of PAARH slightly increased CD47 mRNA and protein expression in SNU-398 cells under normoxia (Fig. 4A, B). However, under hypoxia condition, ectopic expression of PAARH remarkably increased CD47 mRNA and protein expression in SNU-398 cells (Fig. 4C, D). Under normoxia condition, PAARH depletion slightly reduced CD47 mRNA and protein expression in HCCLM3 cells (Fig. 4E, F). However, under hypoxia condition, PAARH depletion remarkably reduced CD47 mRNA and protein expression in HCCLM3 cells (Fig. 4G, H). Similarly, overexpression of PAARH also significantly increased CD47 mRNA and protein expression in CHL-1 cells under hypoxia (Fig. 4I, J). Depletion of PAARH also significantly decreased CD47 mRNA and protein expression in SW620 cells under hypoxia (Fig. 4K, L). These data suggest that PAARH upregulates CD47 expression.

PAARH facilitated HIF-1α-induced transcription of CD47

Our previous study has revealed that PAARH directly binds to HIF-1α and modulates HIF-1α transcriptional activity under hypoxia [17], we further investigated whether PAARH regulates CD47 in such a manner. CUT&RUN assays showed that overexpression of PAARH slightly increased the binding of HIF-1α to CD47 promoter in SNU-398 cells under normoxia (Fig. S4A). However, under hypoxia condition, overexpression of PAARH remarkably increased the binding of HIF-1α to CD47 promoter in SNU-398 cells (Fig. 5A). PAARH depletion did not have statistically significant effect on the binding of HIF-1α to CD47 promoter in HCCLM3 cells under normoxia (Fig. S4B), and while PAARH depletion remarkably reduced the binding of HIF-1α to CD47 promoter in SNU-398 cells under hypoxia (Fig. 5B). Similarly, overexpression of PAARH in CHL-1 cells also increased the binding of HIF-1α to CD47 promoter under hypoxia (Fig. 5C). Depletion of PAARH in SW620 cells also reduced the binding of HIF-1α to CD47 promoter under hypoxia (Fig. 5D). These findings suggest that PAARH facilitates the recruitment of HIF-1α to CD47 promoter under hypoxia. Dual-luciferase reporter assays revealed that overexpression of PAARH in SNU-398 cells upregulated CD47 promoter activity under hypoxia (Fig. 5E). PAARH depletion in HCCLM3 cells reduced CD47 promoter activity under hypoxia (Fig. 5F). Similarly, overexpression of PAARH also upregulated CD47 promoter activity in CHL-1 cells under hypoxia (Fig. 5G), and PAARH depletion also reduced CD47 promoter activity in SW620 cells under hypoxia (Fig. 5H). To investigate whether the effects of PAARH on CD47 are dependent on HIF-1α, we depleted HIF-1α in SNU-398 cells and dual-luciferase reporter assays revealed that depletion of HIF-1α largely reversed the increased CD47 promoter activity caused by PAARH overexpression under hypoxia (Fig. 5I, Fig. S4C). Similarly, depletion of HIF-1α also largely reversed the increased CD47 promoter activity caused by PAARH overexpression in CHL-1 cells under hypoxia (Fig. 5J). qPCR and flow cytometry showed that depletion of HIF-1α reversed the increased CD47 mRNA and protein expression caused by PAARH overexpression under hypoxia in both SNU-398 and CHL-1 cells (Fig. 5K-N).

Depletion of CD47 reversed the roles of PAARH in KC phagocytosis and liver metastasis

To investigate whether CD47 mediates the functions of PAARH in KC phagocytosis and liver metastasis, we depleted CD47 in SNU-398 cells, and in vitro phagocytosis assays showed that depletion of CD47 abolished the decreased phagocytosis caused by PAARH overexpression (Fig. 7A, B). Similarly, depletion of CD47 also abolished the decreased in vitro phagocytosis caused by PAARH overexpression in CHL-1 cells (Fig. 7C, D). In vivo phagocytosis assays showed that depletion of CD47 also abolished the decreased phagocytosis caused by PAARH overexpression in both SNU-398 and CHL-1 cells (Fig. 7E-H). In vivo liver metastasis assays showed that depletion of CD47 largely reversed the increased liver metastasis caused by PAARH overexpression in both SNU-398 and CHL-1 cells (Fig. 7I, J). These data suggest that CD47 mediates the roles of PAARH in KC phagocytosis and liver metastasis.

PAARH and CD47 are positively correlated with liver metastasis in clinical tissues

Our previous study has shown that PAARH expression in HCC tissues was positively correlated with intrahepatic microvascular invasion [17]. We further investigated the correlation between PAARH expression and intrahepatic vascular invasion using TCGA LIHC data and the result revealed that PAARH expression was increased in HCC tissues with vascular invasion compared with those without vascular invasion (Fig. 8A). Furthermore, we collected 47 primary CRC tissues and 23 CRC liver metastatic tissues and measured the expression of PAARH in these tissues. The results revealed that PAARH was significantly increased in CRC liver metastatic tissues compared with primary CRC tissues (Fig. 8B). Analysis of PAARH expression in primary CRC tissues found that PAARH was highly expressed in primary CRC tissues with liver metastasis compared with those without liver metastasis (Fig. 8C). Moreover, we further measured CD47 expression in these primary CRC tissues and CRC liver metastatic tissues. The results showed that CD47 was also increased in CRC liver metastatic tissues compared with primary CRC tissues (Fig. 8D), and was also highly expressed in primary CRC tissues with liver metastasis compared with those without liver metastasis (Fig. 8E). The expression of CD47 was positively correlated with PAARH in CRC liver metastatic tissues (Fig. 8F), and also positively correlated with PAARH in primary CRC tissues (Fig. 8G). These data suggest that PAARH and CD47 are positively correlated with liver metastasis in humans.

Discussion

Immunotherapy has demonstrated remarkable efficacy against multiple malignancies [27, 28]. Currently available immunotherapies primarily target adaptive immunity through immune checkpoint inhibitors [29–31]. However, many tumors remain refractory to these adaptive immune-targeted approaches [32–34]. Innate immunity serves as a crucial first-line defense against cancer, with macrophages playing a pivotal role through their ability to phagocytose and eliminate malignant cells [35–38]. Nevertheless, tumor cells often evade this innate immune surveillance by overexpressing anti-phagocytic molecules such as CD47, CD24, and PD-L1 [18, 39, 40]. Therapeutic targeting of these phagocytic checkpoints represents a promising new frontier in cancer immunotherapy.

As the liver’s first-line defense against invading tumor cells, KCs play a critical role in phagocytosing and eliminating circulating tumor cells to suppress hepatic metastasis [41, 42]. However, the specific molecular checkpoints governing KC phagocytosis remain poorly characterized. In this study, we identify PAARH as a novel anti-phagocytic lncRNA that enables tumor cells to evade KC surveillance. PAARH is located on chromosome 11q13.1 with three exons. The effects of PAARH are significantly amplified by hypoxia. Several previous studies have shown that the live is mainly fed by hypoxemic portal vein systems and hypoxia represents a key hallmark of the liver [24–26]. During tumor liver metastasis, tumor cells must adapt to the hypoxic microenvironment to colonize [23]. Furthermore, liver-metastatic cells showed increased HIF-1α activity [22]. Previous report has shown that hypoxia increases Kupffer cells phagocytosis [43]. All these previous studies supported the importance of hypoxia and Kupffer cells during tumor liver metastasis. Here, through systematic investigation, we demonstrate that PAARH overexpression in tumor cells confers resistance to KC-mediated clearance, thereby promoting liver metastasis across multiple cancer types including HCC, colorectal carcinoma, and melanoma.

Mechanistic studies revealed that PAARH physically interacts with HIF-1α and recruits it to the CD47 promoter, thereby activating CD47 transcription. Given that hypoxia potently induces HIF-1α expression [44, 45], we observed that hypoxia conditions dramatically enhanced PAARH-induced CD47 upregulation. Importantly, we established CD47 as a functional phagocytic checkpoint not only for macrophages but also for KCs. Depletion of CD47 reversed PAARH’s anti-phagocytic effects and metastatic-promoting capacity, demonstrating that the HIF-1α/CD47 axis is the central mechanism through which PAARH regulates KC phagocytosis and tumor liver metastasis. Considering the anti-phagocytic role of CD47 for macrophages, PAARH may also suppress the phagocytosis of tumor cells by macrophages through increasing CD47, which further contributes to the pro-metastatic roles of PAARH. The potential effects of PAARH on macrophage phagocytosis need further investigation.

While CD47-blocking antibodies have shown therapeutic promise across multiple cancers, their clinical application is limited by on-target toxicity including anemia and thrombocytopenia due to indiscriminate phagocytosis of erythrocytes and platelets [46–48]. Our work provides the first evidence that PAARH specifically upregulates CD47 in hypoxic tumor cells to evade KC surveillance - a finding with important therapeutic implications. Since hypoxic conditions are nearly universal in solid tumors, targeted inhibition of PAARH may offer a superior alternative to anti-CD47 therapy by selectively disrupting tumor-specific CD47 induction while sparing normal hematopoietic cells, thereby avoiding the dose-limiting toxicities of current CD47 blockade strategies.

Aberrant expressions and oncogenic or tumor-suppressive roles of lncRNAs have been frequently found across many malignances [49–54]. Our previous study has revealed that PAARH intrinsically promotes HCC cell proliferation and migration [17]. Here, we further found that PAARH suppresses Kupffer cell phagocytosis. Thus, PAARH represents a critical oncogenic lncRNA. Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) have demonstrated promising efficacy in preclinical models for targeted lncRNA knockdown [55, 56]. Several ASO-based drugs, such as Vitravene and Nusinersen, are already in clinical use [57, 58]. The development of PAARH-targeted ASOs represent a novel therapeutic strategy for tumor liver metastasis.

In summary, this study identifies PAARH as a critical anti-phagocytic molecule for KCs. Mechanistically, PAARH recruits HIF-1α to the CD47 promoter, activating its transcription and transmitting a “don’t eat me” signal to KCs, leading to the suppression of phagocytosis of tumor cells by KCs and increasing of liver metastasis (Fig. 8H). Together, these findings highlight PAARH as a potential therapeutic target for tumor liver metastasis.

BnaC03.SEIPIN1 improves seed oil content by increasing the number of lipid droplets in Brassica napus
Authors: Yuqing Li, Zhouxiao Cao, Yuyan Xiang, Liang Guo, Feng Zu, Tonghua Wang, Mei Li, Bao Li, Shaoping Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe storage of lipids is a crucial mechanism conserved across diverse organisms. Lipid droplets (LDs) are important organelles for storing lipids in cells, and there are many proteins interspersed with their outer phospholipid membranes. SEIPIN, as a LD protein, has been reported to influence the size and number of LDs, and to affect neutral lipid storage in Arabidopsis.ResultsIn Brassica napus (B. napus), there are three SEIPIN genes, including SEIPIN1 (4 copies), SEIPIN2 (4 copies), and SEIPIN3 (2 copies). The transcriptome databases show that BnaSEIPIN1 only has high expression in late developing seeds, BnaSEIPIN2 has low expression almost in all tissues, while BnaSEIPIN3 has negligible expression across all tissues. BnaC03.SEIPIN1 and BnaA09.SEIPIN2 were cloned to be overexpressed in B. napus seeds specifically. The results showed that BnaC03.SEIPIN1 increased the oil content of seeds by 4.3–5.4% and the thousand-seed weight by 0.43—0.49 g. However, no significant effect of BnaA09.SEIPIN2 on oil content was observed. Further studies revealed that BnaC03.SEIPIN1 significantly increased the number of small and large LDs, but not affected the LDs size. The qRT-PCR results showed that the expression levels of some key genes related to lipid synthesis and storage were altered significantly between overexpression lines and WT.ConclusionsTaking together, above results reveal that SEIPIN1 improves seed oil content accumulation by the number of small and larger LDs, but not by influencing LDs size. Our results provide a new understanding on SEIPIN regulating seed oil accumulation in Brassica napus.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-08472-3.

Background

Brassica napus (B. napus, AACC) is a heteropolyploid formed 7,500 years ago from Brassica oleracea (CC) and Brassica rapa (AA) [1]. B. napus, as one of the most important oilseed crops in the world, has aroused extensive research, among them, improving the seed oil content of B. napus is a common goal pursued by breeders. Researchers have explored a number of perspectives to improve the seed oil content of B. napus, such as enhancing the expression levels of genes related to lipid synthesis [2–6]; reducing the ratio of seed coat content in whole seed [7–10]; changing the metabolic pathways related to impact carbon flux [11–13], and increasing the storage capacity of oil etc. [14, 15]. Among them, thousands of functional genes have been verified in impacting seed oil content in the first three patterns. While only a few genes have been reported to directly influence the seeds store capacity of oil.

In plant tissues, Lipid droplets (LDs), dynamic organelles in the cell, are the main place to store neutral lipids. LDs are thought to occur in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) as storage sites for lipids in the cell [16, 17]. LDs are a structure with a neutral lipid core and encapsulated by a monomolecular phospholipid membrane studded with proteins (LDs protein) [18–20]. LD proteins in plants are broadly categorized into three classes: oleosins, caleosin and other proteins, and LD-associated proteins. These proteins play crucial roles in stabilizing LD structure and determining docking sites and other functions [19, 21–23]. One of the best know LD proteins is oleosin, which is located on the surface of the LD and whose proline motif is required for LD targeting [23]. Oleosin is also shown to be associated with lipid accumulation [24, 25]. Recently, SEIPIN has also taken center stage in the spotlight. SEIPINs are localized at the ER and can facilitate the transport of triglycerides from the ER to LDs [26]. In humans, there are one SEIPIN, deletion of the SEIPIN leads to congenital generalized lipodystrophy type 2 [27, 28].

In Arabidopsis, three homologs of the SEIPINs are present, AtSEIPIN1, AtSEIPIN2, and AtSEIPIN3. All three SEIPINs can affect LDs formation. The results of transient expression of three SEIPINs in tobacco leaves show that SEIPIN1 promotes the formation of larger LDs, while SEIPIN2 and especially SEIPIN3 leads to an increase in the number of smaller LDs [29]. Further studies reveal that SEIPIN interacts not only with LD-associated protein (LDAP) and LDAP INTERACTING PROTEIN (LDIP), but also with Vesicle-Associated Membrane Protein (VAMP)-Associated Protein family member, AtVAP27-1, to influence LDs formation in Arabidopsis [30, 31]. Increasing SEIPIN1 expression in Arabidopsis lines expressing hydroxylase increases seed oil content along with seed hydroxy fatty acids [32]. In addition, SEIPIN plays an essential role in pollen dispersal and seed germination [33]. Mutation of the SEIPIN homologous gene leads to a decrease in seed oil content as well as an increase in protein content in soybean [34]. Although research on the SEIPIN has been carried out in a variety of plants, its role in oil accumulation has not been reported in B. napus.

In this study, by homologous alignment, 3 SEIPINs were identified in B. napus: BnaSEIPIN1 (4 copies), BnaSEIPIN2 (4 copies), and BnaSEIPIN3 (2 copies). Gene expression profile showed that BnaSEIPIN1 exhibited the highest expression during seed development, BnaSEIPIN2 showed moderate expression, while BnaSEIPIN3 was barely expressed. Therefore, we selected one copy each from BnaSEIPIN1 and BnaSEIPIN2 to construct B. napus seed-specific overexpression lines. Results indicated that BnaSEIPIN1 overexpressing lines exhibited increase the seed oil content and the weight per 1000 seeds compared to that of WT, whereas no significant changes were observed in BnaSEIPIN2 overexpressing lines. Further analysis displayed that SEIPIN1 could increase the number of LDs to improve seed oil content. Our finding provides a new insight for high seed oil content of B. napus breeding.

Results

Obtaining of BnaSEIPIN1 overexpression lines

By performing homology searches in the B. napus database (https://yanglab.hzau.edu.cn/) using the protein sequence of AtSEIPINs, the results revealed that there are four BnaSEIPIN1 homologous copies (BnaA02G0064200ZS, BnaA03G0069800ZS, BnaC02G0074200ZS, and BnaC03G0079000ZS), four BnaSEIPIN2 (BnaA08G0207800ZS, BnaA09G0415100ZS, BnaC03G0659900ZS, and BnaC05G0255000ZS), and two BnaSEIPIN3 (BnaA04G0220900ZS and BnaC04G0532500ZS) in B. napus. Analysis of the conserved structural domains of all genes revealed that SEIPIN1, SEIPIN2 and SEIPIN3 have 3 identical motifs (Fig. 1A). By analyzing the structures of these genes, it was found that all SEIPIN1 and SEIPIN2 have 2 coding sequence (CDS) regions, while SEIPIN3 has only 1 CDS region (Fig. 1A). Analysis of the expression patterns of BnaSEIPIN1, BnaSEIPIN2, and BnaSEIPIN3 in various tissues through the database of Brassica napus (https://yanglab.hzau.edu.cn/BnTIR). The results revealed that all copies of BnaSEIPIN1 were expressed exclusively in seeds, especially the BnaA03.SEIPIN1, BnaC03.SEIPIN1 and BnaC02.SEIPIN1 had high expression levels in seeds at 42—58 days after flowering (DAF) (Fig. 1B). While, compared to BnaA03.SEIPIN1 and BnaC02.SEIPIN1, the expression of BnaC03.SEIPIN1 was earlier. The expression of BnaC03.SEIPIN1 started from 34 DAF seeds (Fig. 1B). All homologs of BnaSEIPIN2 showed weak expression across all tissues, except BnaA09. SEIPIN2 had a little expression in developing seeds. The two homologs of BnaSEIPIN3 exhibited negligible expression levels in all tissues (Fig. 1B). Thus, the BnaC03.SEIPIN1 and BnaA09. Overexpression of SEIPIN1 affects seed oil content and thousand-seed weight

The genes of BnaC03.SEIPIN1 and BnaA09. SEIPIN2 were cloned from the B. napus cultivar Zhongshuang 11 (ZS11) and individually linked into the Napin-PBILoxP vector. Transgenic plants of BnaC03.SEIPIN1 and BnaA09.SEIPIN2 were obtained via Agrobacterium tumefaciens (GV3101)—mediated transformation [35]. Results showed that BnaC03.SEIPIN1 expression levels in transgenic lines were 7.4—45.9 times higher than wild type (WT), and BnaA09.SEIPIN2 expression levels were 1.5—16.2 times higher than WT (Fig. 2A, Supplementary Fig. 1A). To verify whether BnaC03.SEIPIN1 and BnaA09.SEIPIN2 have effects on the accumulation of seed oil content, the mature seeds harvested from T2 plants in the field were used to detect seed oil content by gas chromatography (GC). The results showed that the oil content of SEIPIN1 overexpression (OE) lines was significantly increased by 4.3—5.4% compared to WT (Fig. 2B). However, the seed oil content had no difference between SEIPIN2—OE lines and WT (Supplementary Fig. 1B). The fatty acid composition almost had no difference between SEIPIN1—OE and WT, except for several fatty acids content had a slight change (Supplementary Fig. 2 A). These results imply that SEIPIN1 has function in seed oil accumulation but almost has no effect on changing fatty acid composition of seeds. However, excessive SEIPIN2 has no helpful for increasing seed oil accumulation. To investigate the effect of SEIPIN1 on lipid content, LC–MS was employed to analyze the lipid composition of mature seeds. The results showed only the content of triacylglycerol (TAG) was significantly increased in OE seeds compared to WT, with no significant changes in the content of diacylglycerol (DAG), phosphatidylcholine (PC), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) and phosphatidylglycerol (PG) (Fig. 2E). The content of soluble sugar of seeds had no significant difference between OE and WT (Fig. 2D). Other traits such as the plant height, the length of main inflorescence, the number of effective branches, the number of main inflorescence silique, and the length of silique etc. were all no significant changes in OE lines compared to that in WT (Supplementary Fig. 3). BnaC03.SEIPIN1 affects the number of LDs

To explore how the BnaC03.SEIPIN1 affects seed oil accumulation, we observed the ultrastructure of mature seeds under transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Fig. 3A). The size and number of LDs were calculated using ImageJ software. The data displayed that the number of LDs in OE seeds was increased by 41.3—92.06% than that in WT, and the total area of LDs per image was increased by 63.40—92.24% (Fig. 3B, D). Further, the number of different sizes of LDs was calculated. The results showed that the number of small LDs and large LDs in OE was 2—3.93 and 4.67—7.76 folds than that in WT, respectively (Fig. 3C). However, the number of intermediate LDs was no difference between OE and WT (Fig. 3C). Additionally, we summed and analyzed the size of LDs, the results revealed that the size of different types of LDs had no difference between OE lines and WT (Fig. 3E). These results imply that SEIPIN1 can improve seed oil content by increasing the number of small and large LDs, while not by altering the size of LDs.

The expression level changes of some genes related to lipid synthesis and storage in overexpression of BnaC03.SEIPIN1 lines

Lipid synthesis and storage involve processes such as fatty acids synthesis and elongation in plastid, lipid assembly in ER, and LDs formation etc. Many genes are involved in these processes (Fig. 4A). In order to investigate the effect of BnaC03.SEIPIN1 on the expression of genes involved in lipid synthesis and storage, the RNA was extracted from 35 DAF seeds and used to detect relative genes expression. We found that the expression of Oil Body Oleosin 1 (OBO1), Oil Body Oleosin 3 (OBO3) related to LDs formation were significantly increased in OE lines comparison with WT, while the expression level of Oil Body Oleosin 2 (OBO2) was significantly reduced. Caleosin (CALO) had no difference between OE and WT, even decrease in one line (Fig. 4B, Supplementary Fig. 4). Some genes related to fatty acids synthesis such as Enoyl-ACP reductase (ENR) and acyl-ACP thioesterase (FATA) were significantly elevated in OE lines than that in WT. However, the expression of malonyl-CoA:ACP malonyltransferase (MCMT) decreased significantly (Fig. 4C). In the process of lipid assembly, the levels of diacylglycerol acyltransferase (DGAT), Choline phosphortransferase (CPT), lycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase (GPAT) and Phosphatidate phosphatase (PAP) were significantly decreased in OE compared to that in WT, while other genes like Glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GPDH), and phospholipid:diacylglycerol acyltransferase (PDAT) almost had no changes even a slight decrease between OE and WT (Fig. 4D, Supplementary Fig. 4). BnaSEIPIN1 affects protein component on lipid droplets of mature seeds

Further, to understand the effect of excessive SEIPIN1 on protein component of LDs, the LDs were isolated from mature seeds to be used to extract LD proteins and perform proteome detection using LC–MS. The extracted proteins were first tested using SDS-PAGE, the data showed the quality of proteins was well (Supplementary Fig. 5). Samples were sent to a company to carry out Tandem Mass Tags (TMT)—labeled proteome detection. Principal components analysis (PCA) showed that OE and WT could be clearly separated in the first principal components (Fig. 5A). The detailed information of differentially expressed proteins was shown at Supplementary Table 1. The data showed that there were 152 differentially expressed proteins between OE and WT, including 83 up-regulated proteins and 69 down-regulated proteins of OE vs WT (Fig. 5B). A volcano plot was generated for differentially expressed proteins based on their expression levels (Fig. 5C). Gene ontology (GO) analysis showed that the up-regulated proteins were mainly distributed in farnesyl diphosphate metabolic process, prenylcysteine catabolic process, chromoplast stroma, prenylcysteine oxidase activity, S—nitrosoglutathione reductase activity, formaldehyde metabolic process, glyoxalase III activity, methylglyoxal catabolic process to lactate, lactate biosynthetic process, S—(hydroxymethyl) glutathione dehydrogenase activity, ethanol oxidation, endopeptidase inhibitor activity, and lipid transport etc. processes (Fig. 5D). Discussion

For decades, the biosynthesis of LDs has been understood as involving the massive accumulation of neutral lipids within the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), with excess lipids subsequently budding off the ER into the cytoplasm [36]. This process requires the functional involvement of numerous proteins, among which SEIPIN, as the most crucial member, has garnered extensive attention [37–40]. In B. napus, the function of SEIPIN is still unknown. Hence, we overexpressed the SEIPINs (SEIPIN1 and SEIPIN2) in B. napus under the seed-specific promoter. Compared to WT, the oil content was increased by 4.3—5.4% in SEIPIN1—OE lines (Fig. 2B). This function of SEIPIN1 improving seeds oil accumulation is also observed in Arabidopsis and soybean [29, 34]. However, in SEIPIN2-OE lines, oil content in the OE lines showed no significant change compared to the WT. Similarly, in Arabidopsis mutants, TAG content in SEIPIN2-mutant plants exhibited no significant alteration relative to the WT [33]. However, in soybean, knocking out the SEIPIN1 resulted in increasing seeds length and width [34]. In Arabidopsis, the seeds were observed smaller and lighter in SEIPIN1 knocked down lines than that of WT [29]. In our study, overexpression of SEIPIN1 could increase B. napus seeds weight but not change seeds size (Fig. 2C, supplementary Fig. 2B). These data imply that the function of SEIPIN1 on improving seed oil accumulation is conservation in different plants, while the function on impacting other seed traits like size and weight seems different. Moreover, it speculates that the function of SEIPIN1 and SEIPIN2 on enhancing seed oil may be redundant in Brassica napus.

SEIPIN are located at the junction of the ER and LD and facilitate TAG transport from the ER to the LDs [26]. In animals, the deficiency of SEIPIN in the body results in the absence of most of the adipose tissue and the formation of abnormal LDs [40]. These phenotypes are also found in Arabidopsis and soybean [29, 33, 34]. Additionally, overexpression of SEIPIN1 in Arabidopsis results in the more intermediate and huge LDs formation in seeds, meanwhile, the number of small LDs decrease [29]. In our study, the number and total area of LDs per view of OE lines were significantly increased compared to WT (Fig. 3B and C). However, overexpression of SEIPIN1 led to more small and large LDs formation in B. napus seeds but not changed the size of LDs (Fig. 3D, Figure E). It suggests that the mechanism of SEIPIN1 enhancing seed oil accumulation is not exactly same in Arabidopsis and B. napus. Analysis of the lipidome of OE lines and WT revealed no significant changes in most other lipids, except for the content of TAG, which was significantly increased in OE, suggesting that SEIPIN1 only affects the content of neutral lipids in B. napus (Fig. 2E). However, knocking out SEIPIN1 in soybean not only affected TAG content but also influenced the levels of other phospholipids [34]. Together, above evidences indicate that although SEIPIN1 of different plants all could increase seeds oil content by impacting LDs morphology, the patterns of SEIPIN1 changing LDs size and number may be different in different species, leading to the changes of lipid component in seeds are also different.

By measuring the expression levels of genes involved in lipid accumulation and storage in 35 DAF seeds, the expression of OBO1 and OBO3 was increased significantly in SEIPIN1—OE lines than that in WT, while OBO2 and CALO had no difference or even decreased in OE line (Fig. 4B, Supplementary Fig. 4). It speculates that the intervention of excessive SEIPIN1 could influence LD protein component. While the analysis of differentially expressed proteins showed that the levels of LD proteins had no different in OE and WT mature seeds (Fig. 5B, Supplementary Table 2). It suggests that the impacting of SEIPIN1 on LD proteins component may be not immutable, this influence may be weakened with the seeds developing. Moreover, due to the SEIPIN1 improved more small and large LDs formation, and increased the total number of LDs (Fig. 3B, C). Obviously, these phenomena were consistent with the oil content increased in seeds. It is easy reminded that more lipids needed to be synthesized in OE seeds. While the results of qRT—PCR showed that the expression of most genes related lipid synthesis was decreased, except ENR and FATA increased (Fig. 3C, D). It speculates that a lot of LDs formation (too much oil synthesized) will confer cells a feedback signal to reduce oil synthesis to maintain the balance of carbon flux. This phenomenon has also been observed in other studies [14]. Another reason may be that the RNA is extracted from 35 DAF seeds of Westar, which is at the mid- to late-development stage of seeds. Previous studies have reported that the seed oil accumulation is not increased or even decreased at this stage, leading to detected the expression levels of some key genes such as DGAT and GPAT are downregulation (45).

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study demonstrated that BnaSEIPIN1 could affect the accumulation of seed oil by regulating the number of different types of LDs. The results provide a new understanding of SEIPIN improving seed oil content. Meanwhile, our research will provide a gene resource for high oil content of B. napus breeding.

Methods

Analysis of basic information of Bna.SEIPIN1

Evolutionary tree analysis was performed using MEGA7 software, gene structure analysis was performed using Gene Structure Display Server (https://gsds.gao-lab.org/), protein conserved structural domains were analyzed using the MEME website (https://meme-suite.org/meme/), and other gene sequence and expression data were obtained from BnIR (https://yanglab.hzau.edu.cn/). Protein and gene structure visualization is performed using TBtools [41].

Vector construction and transformation

The CDS of BnaC03.SEIPIN1 and BnaC03.SEIPIN2 were ligated to a vector with the seed-specific promoter Napin, respectively. The vector was transferred into agrobacterium GV3101 to perform the transformation. The B. napus cultivar Westar was the recipient material. The method of genetic transformation followed previous study [35]. The transgenic plants were tested by PCR and qRT-PCR with specific primers, which were listed in Supplementary Table 2.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA from 35 DAF seeds was extracted using Eastep®Super Total RNA Extraction Kit (Promega, 0000640409). RNA quality and concentration were determined using gel electrophoresis and Nanodrop (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA). cDNA synthesis was performed using cDNA Synthesis Kit (TransGen, Beijing, China; catalogue number AE311), and the synthesized cDNA was diluted to the appropriate concentration for gene expression detection using TransStart® Green qPCR SuperMix (TransGen; catalogue number AQ101) in the BIO-RAD CFX96 qPCR detection system (BIO-RAD, www.bio-rad.com) for gene expression detection. The primers used in qRT-PCR were shown in Supplementary Table 2. Lipid Metabolism Pathway Diagram Created with BioGDP.com [42].

Transmission electron microscopy

The procedure was consistent with the previously published experiments on the observation of lipid droplets in seeds by TEM [43]. Firstly, the seed coat was peeled off, trying not to damage the seed structure, and the separated seed structure was immersed in 2.5% (v/v) glutaraldehyde solution for overnight fixation. Subsequently, the samples were sent to the electron microscope platform of Huazhong Agricultural University for preparation and observation by using the TEM (H-7650, HITACHI, Japan).

Fatty acid extraction and GC-FID assay

Firstly, weighed 3–5 seeds with full morphology and uniformed size in a glass tube, added 4 ml of formaldehyde containing 5% H2SO4 and 0.01% BHT, then gently whisked the seed coat with a glass rod, followed by the addition of 100 μL of C17:0 at a concentration of 16.2 μmol as an internal standard, placed the cap tightly screwed on a water bathed at 85 ℃ for 2 h, and then placed the sample tube in a room temperature. After the solution was brought down to room temperature, 2 ml ddH2O and 2 ml of hexane were added into it, vortex mixing, and centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min, and the upper layer of the liquid was aspirated and put into a brown injection bottle. The prepared samples were detected by GC-FID (QP2010 Ultra, SHIMADAZU, Japan) with the parameters reported in previous studies [44].

Soluble sugar analysis

According to the previous experimental procedure [11], 25 ml of ddH2O was added to the sample and heated in a water bath at 95 °C for 30 min, the supernatant was transferred to a volumetric flask to 25 ml, and then 50 μl of the supernatant was pipetted into a 1.5 ml EP tube containing 400 μl of anthrone sulfuric acid, and then heated in a water bath at 95 °C for 5 min, and then cooled to room temperature, the absorbance value was determined by using an enzyme marker at the wavelength of 625 nm. The absorbance value was measured at 625 nm, and the content of soluble sugar in the sample was calculated according to the standard curve, and the final content (mg/g, DW) = the amount of the sample obtained * the number of times of dilution/the dry weight of the sample.

Lipid extraction and detection

Lipids were extracted according to the previous method [45]. The extracted lipids were dried with nitrogen and then followed by the weight of the seeds were concentrated to a concentration of 2 mg/ml and stored in a brown injection bottle. Then it was detected by LC–MS/MS (QTRAP 6500 plus) and the detection parameters were set according to the previous study [45].

Lipid droplet protein isolation and proteomic determination

Weighed 0.2 g of peeled seeds, add 1 ml of buffer A (600 mM sucrose, 100 mM KCl in 100 mM potassium phosphate buffer (pH = 7.2)) and grind the seeds on ice until homogenized, transfer the homogenate into a centrifuge tube covered with a magic filter cloth and repeat by rinsing the mortar with 0.5 ml of buffer A and transferring it to the same centrifuge tube; centrifuge at 500 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C, vortex the tube slightly and transfer the homogenate into a 2 ml The tube was vortexed slightly and the homogenate was transferred to a 2 ml centrifuge tube and centrifuged at 13,000 × g for 30 min at 4 °C; the upper layer of the fat pad was poured into a new centrifuge tube. Add 1.2 ml-1.5 ml buffer B (same as Buffer A but with 400 mM sucrose and without cross-linker) to the new centrifuge tube, 4 °C, 13,000 × g, 15 min, and repeat this step 2–3 times. To the separated fat pads, 200 μl of pre-cooled 100 mM (PH = 7.5) Tris–Hcl was added, mixed well, then 800 μl of ice-cold acetone was added, and the proteins were precipitated at −20 °C overnight. The tube was centrifuged at 10,000xg for 30 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was poured out, and 1.5 ml of fresh pre-cooled acetone was added, and the proteins were suspended by vigorous swirling or sonication, and then centrifuged at 10,000xg for 10 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was poured out, and the procedure was repeated once. Proteins were solubilized with 200 μl of 8 M urea. Finally, the extracted proteins were sent to PTM Biological Company (http://www.ptm-biolab.com.cn/) for proteomic analysis.

Differentially expressed protein analysis

The fold change (FC) was calculated as the ratio of the mean relative quantitative values of a protein across two sample groups. To assess the significance of differences, a t-test was performed on the relative quantitative values of the protein in both groups, yielding a corresponding P-value as the significance indicator, with P < 0.05 as the default threshold. To ensure the test data conforms to the normal distribution required for t-tests, the relative quantitative values of the protein undergo Log2 transformation prior to analysis. Through the above differential analysis, when P value < 0.05, a FC exceeding 1.3 was set as the threshold for significant upregulation, while a FC less than 1/1.3 was set as the threshold for significant downregulation.

GO enrichment analysis

All B. napus genes were aligned against all proteins in Arabidopsis using BLASTP. The most significant proteins in the Arabidopsis genome with a probability threshold below 10⁻5 were selected. Enrichment analysis was performed using Fisher's exact test, calculating the odds ratio (OR) and P-value [46].

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databasewas utilized for pathway enrichment analysis of differentially expressed proteins. Using these two annotation databases: KOBAS (v3.0, http://kobas.cbi.pku.edu.cn/) and KEGG mapper (v5.0, http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/mapper.html). Fisher's exact test was employed to assess the significance of pathway enrichment for these proteins (using the identified proteins as background), with P values < 0.05 considered statistically significant. The software diamond (v2.0.11.149) was used for this analysis.

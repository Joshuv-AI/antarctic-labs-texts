Another Marker of Inflammation and Oxidative Stress and Its Impact on Myocardial Function, Arterial Elastance, and Vasoactive–Inotropic Score After Coronary Revascularization Surgery: A Prospective Cohort Study
Authors: Teuku A. Husain, Johanes N. E. Putranto, Philia Setiawan, Nancy M. Rehatta, Fajar Perdhana, Yos Kowara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTContext:Inflammatory and oxidative stress responses are markedly amplified following cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), contributing to myocardial injury, vascular dysregulation, and potential multiorgan dysfunction.Aims:This study aimed to define how inflammatory, oxidative, and cellular stress responses quantified by tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), hydroxyl (OH-) radical scavenging capacity, and heat shock protein-70 (HSP-70) relate to early postoperative myocardial contractility, arterial tone, and vasoactive–inotropic requirements after CPB.Settings and Design:A prospective cohort study was conducted in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) with preserved preoperative ejection fraction (EF).Methods and Materials:Postoperative biomarker levels and perioperative changes in TNF-α, OH- scavenging capacity, and HSP-70 with early myocardial contractility maximum rate of rise of left ventricular pressure (dP/dtmax), arterial elastance, and vasoactive–inotropic score were assessed.Statistical Analysis Used:Correlation analyses were performed using Pearson’s or Spearman’s correlation coefficients as appropriate.Results:A total of 52 patients were included in the study. Higher postoperative HSP-70 levels were associated with reduced early myocardial contractility (r = −0.315; P = 0.025) and increased vasoactive–inotropic requirements (ρ =0.341; P = 0.014). No significant associations were observed among postoperative biomarker variables, and changes in TNF-α or OH- scavenging capacity were not related to early postoperative hemodynamic outcomes (all P > 0.05).Conclusions:Early post-CPB myocardial dysfunction appears more closely associated with cellular stress (HSP-70) than with inflammatory or oxidative responses. Elevated HSP-70 levels in the early postoperative CABG may act as damage-associated molecular patterns associated with transient myocardial depression and higher vasoactive–inotropic requirements, whereas early inflammatory and oxidative changes showed no direct hemodynamic correlation.

INTRODUCTION

Ischemia–reperfusion injury is central in cardiac surgery, especially during cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) and aortic cross-clamping, which triggers complex inflammatory pathways.[123] Reperfusion-associated free radical generation produces reactive oxygen species (ROS), which may overwhelm antioxidant defenses and damage cellular structures, while ROS-scavenging systems and heat shock protein-70 (HSP-70) provide cytoprotection.[456] CPB-induced inflammatory and oxidative response can disrupt myocardial contractility and vascular tone, and cause multiorgan dysfunction, whereas endogenous protective systems help limit ischemia–reperfusion injury.[7891011] This study aimed to clarify the associations between inflammation, oxidative stress, and cellular stress—reflected by circulating tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), hydroxyl (OH-) scavenging capacity, and HSP-70—and early postoperative myocardial contractility, arterial tone, and vasoactive–inotropic requirements following CPB.

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

Study design, eligibility criteria, and statistical analysis

This prospective cohort study was approved by the Health Research Ethics Committee of Dr. Soetomo General Hospital, East Java, Indonesia (Approval No. 1256/KEPK/III/2025). All study participants gave written informed consent following a comprehensive explanation. In compliance with relevant surgical and anesthetic procedure standards, this prospective cohort study was conducted in patients undergoing elective isolated coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), employing CPB with aortic cross-clamping, from February to October 2025. The patients were aged 30–70 years and had preserved left ventricular systolic function (left ventricular ejection fraction [EF] >50%). Exclusion criteria included left main or left main equivalent chronic total occlusion without adequate collateral circulation, urgent or emergent surgery, significant preoperative organ dysfunction, planned concomitant cardiac procedures, severe pulmonary hypertension or valvular heart disease, and congenital heart disease. Dropout was defined as death before outcome assessment, life-threatening conditions requiring priority clinical management, major protocol deviations, or incomplete postoperative data. Sample size was determined a priori using power analysis for correlation testing. Assuming a moderate effect size (r = 0.35), a two-sided α of 0.05, and 80% statistical power to detect clinically relevant associations between biomarkers and hemodynamic parameters, a minimum sample size of 47 patients was required. To account for potential dropouts and incomplete data, at least 52 patients were enrolled in the study.

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics version 26 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk and Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests. Parametric analyses were applied to normally distributed variables, whereas nonparametric methods were used for variables with non-normal distributions. Associations between variables were examined using Pearson or Spearman correlation analyses, as appropriate. Correlation analyses evaluated relationships between postoperative biomarker levels and perioperative changes in TNF-α, HSP-70, and OH- scavenging capacity with early postoperative hemodynamic outcomes, including maximum rate of rise of left ventricular pressure (dP/dtmax), arterial elastance (Ea), and vasoactive–inotropic score (VIS). Perioperative changes in biomarker levels were interpreted as reflecting the dynamic CPB-related stress response, whereas postoperative values were considered to represent the clinical state associated with hemodynamic impairment. Biomarker assessment and hemodynamic measurements

The biomarker variables assessed in this study included blood measurements of TNF-α, HSP-70, and OH- scavenging capacity. Serum TNF-α was quantified as a marker of systemic inflammation, and HSP-70 as an indicator of cellular stress, using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits. The OH- radical scavenging capacity, serving as an indirect surrogate of ROS activity, was assessed using a Fenton–salicylate-based biochemical assay, in which higher scavenging values reflect lower ROS OH- radical generation. The study outcomes included early indications of myocardial contractile performance (dP/dtmax), Ea, and VIS. dP/dtmax is a sensitive early predictor of systolic performance and can be modified by preload circumstances. To reduce preload-related confounding in the immediate post-CPB interval, hemodynamic measures were taken following clinical volume optimization based on arterial pressure, stroke volume trends, and standardized postoperative fluid management procedures. The MostCare® hemodynamic monitoring system, which uses pulse contour analysis, continuously examined early indications of myocardial contractility (dP/dtmax) and Ea. The system estimated Ea as a derived measure from the ratio of end-systolic pressure (ESP) to stroke volume (SV) (Ea = ESP/SV), which serves as a validated proxy for global left ventricular afterload. Higher Ea values correspond to increasing arterial stiffness and vascular loading conditions. Vasoactive and inotropic support was quantified using the standard VIS formula monitoring system [Figure 2].

Advanced hemodynamic monitoring display: dP/dtmax (early indicator of myocardial contractility), Ea (arterial elastance), and vasoactive–inotropic score. dP/dtmax: maximum rate of rise of left ventricular pressure

Data collection

Study variables were assessed at two predefined time points: before anesthesia induction (pre-CPB), serving as baseline measurements, and at 6 h after CPB following surgery. Baseline TNF-α, HSP-70, and OH- scavenging capacity were measured before induction and CPB initiation to minimize anesthetic and surgical confounders. The analytical framework consisted of three consecutive components. At 6 h post-CPB, we investigated the interrelationships between TNF-α, HSP-70, and OH- scavenging capacity. Second, correlations between these biomarkers and dP/dtmax, Ea, and VIS were investigated at 6 h post-CPB. Third, the correlations between perioperative biomarker changes and corresponding changes in dP/dtmax, Ea, and VIS were evaluated.

TNF-α normally peaks within 1–2 h after CPB and has a short plasma half-life. However, the 6-h time point was chosen to reflect the early clinically stable postoperative period rather than the initial cytokine spike. All patients received standardized protamine for heparin reversal, and blood samples were collected after the completion of protamine infusion to limit variability due to temporary complement activation and cytokine release generated by protamine.

RESULTS

A total of 52 CABG patients met the inclusion criteria; one patient was excluded due to postoperative low cardiac output requiring mechanical circulatory support after CPB. The study cohort comprised 80.4% males and 19.6% females, with diabetes mellitus (43.1%) and hypertension (25.5%) as the predominant comorbidities [Table 1]. Kolmogorov–Smirnov testing showed that age, creatinine, cross-clamp time, and CPB time were normally distributed (P > 0.05), whereas left ventricular EF was not normally distributed (P < 0.05).

Postoperative biomarker variables exhibited non-normal distributions; therefore, nonparametric correlation analyses were performed. Spearman correlation analysis demonstrated no statistically significant associations among postoperative biomarkers. Specifically, postoperative TNF-α showed no meaningful correlation with HSP-70 (ρ = −0.164; P = 0.251) or OH- radical scavenging capacity (ρ =0.094; P = 0.512). Similarly, postoperative OH- scavenging capacity was not significantly associated with HSP-70 levels (ρ = −0.199; P = 0.162). All correlations were weak and nonsignificant, indicating that early postoperative inflammatory activation, antioxidant capacity, and cellular stress responses were not linearly interrelated in patients with preserved baseline systolic function. DISCUSSION

TNF-α is a key proinflammatory cytokine released early after CPB through complement activation, blood contact with the CPB circuit, ischemia–reperfusion injury, and endotoxemia, and it exhibits a biphasic effect, protective at low concentrations and cytotoxic (proinflammatory) at higher levels.[121314] Banerjee et al. reported that measurements were obtained 6 h post-CPB, a recognized peak period for early inflammatory and cellular stress responses, before the influence of late postoperative confounders.[13] This study found no significant association between perioperative TNF-α changes and early postoperative myocardial contractile impairment. TNF-α has been shown to be a negative inotropic cytokine in both experimental and clinical contexts, but this conclusion does not necessarily imply that inflammatory activation has no effect on myocardial depression. The lack of association at 6 h post-CPB likely reflects TNF-α’s rapid kinetic profile, which normally peaks within the first 1–2 h following CPB and falls subsequently due to its short plasma half-life. As a result, the sampling window may have recorded a falling inflammatory phase rather than a moment of maximum myocardial exposure. Furthermore, a small sample size may have impaired sensitivity in detecting temporary cytokine-mediated myocardial effects.

Similarly, the anticipated impact of OH- radical activity, assessed indirectly via OH- scavenging capacity, on myocardial function and arterial tone was not evident, indicating that although the balance between oxidative burden and endogenous antioxidant defenses is critical, oxidative stress alone did not produce measurable functional impairment in early postCPB myocardial performance or arterial tone.[1516]

Conversely, higher HSP-70 expression, despite its established cytoprotective role in maintaining viability of cells, including cardiomyocytes, was paradoxically associated with a reduction in early dP/dtmax but not with changes in Ea, indicating that a heightened cellular stress response is linked to transient systolic dysfunction without a corresponding effect on Ea.[1718] According to the study by Shan et al., the post-CPB increase in HSP-70 likely represents a compensatory stress response aimed at limiting cellular injury that might otherwise progress to myocardial dysfunction and multiorgan impairment, with its close association with inflammatory mediators and oxidative stress markers reflecting activation of endogenous defense mechanisms under perioperative stress.[1920] Although HSP-70 exerts long-term cytoprotective effects, an abrupt early postoperative surge reflects severe cellular stress and is associated with a transient decline in myocardial contractility.[21] Excessive inflammation stimulates macrophages and neutrophils to upregulate the synthesis of HSP-70 and express it on the cell surface, where it may be recognized as a target antigen by T cells, leading to T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity and additional myocardial injury, which is associated with a reduction in dP/dtmax. In addition, the synthesis and activation of heat shock proteins are energy-intensive processes that require substantial adenosine triphosphate (ATP) expenditure, thereby redirecting cellular energy toward proteostatic mechanisms at the expense of contractile function, consistent with the observed decline in dP/dtmax.[2021]

After CPB, extracellular HSP-70 release is promoted by thermal stress during rewarming, oxidative stress, ischemia–reperfusion injury, hypoperfusion, and mechanical stress, at which point HSP-70 acts as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) linking myocardial cellular stress to sterile inflammation via Toll-Like Receptors 2 and 4 and downstream nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κΒ) signaling. Hu et al. demonstrated that, within the myocardium, DAMP-mediated HSP-70 signaling contributes to transient disturbances in excitation–contraction coupling through inflammatory and oxidative mechanisms, including altered calcium handling and myofilament sensitivity, thereby reducing myocardial contractile capacity as a consequence of cellular injury.[2021] There is no widely accepted circulating HSP-70 reference range in humans. Our study focused on within-subject perioperative alterations (Δ HSP-70), utilizing each patient’s baseline as an internal control, in line with CPB biomarker studies.

All patients underwent a standardized institutional anesthetic procedure, which reduced interindividual drug variability. We acknowledge that drugs such as etomidate, volatile anesthetics, and perioperative steroids may impact inflammatory and cellular stress responses, posing a possible restriction, but differential exposure seemed unlikely in this study. Patients with significant organ failure were excluded. Importantly, associations were driven by perioperative increases in HSP-70, not absolute baseline values, indicating that the findings are due to acute CPB-related cellular stress rather than chronic illness. The age range was rather small (median 60 years). An exploratory study revealed there was no significant association between age and postoperative HSP-70 levels, indicating that age was not a confounding factor in this study.

This study has several limitations. First, differences in sample size among analyses resulted from the application of variable-specific significance criteria. Second, biomarker measurements obtained at 6 h post-CPB may not comprehensively reflect the full spectrum of inflammatory and oxidative stress responses, as this time point primarily represents the acute phase rather than the entire temporal evolution of these processes. Third, the study is limited by its single-center design, which may reduce external validity. Finally, VIS is a composite index influenced by both physiological requirements and therapeutic decision-making and therefore does not exclusively represent intrinsic myocardial dysfunction.

CONCLUSION

The findings of this study suggest that CPB-induced increases in HSP-70 levels, occurring independently of systemic inflammatory activity or oxidative stress, more closely reflect its role as DAMPs indicative of myocardial cellular injury rather than a purely adaptive, protective response. This paradoxical response appears to provide cellular stress protection while simultaneously being associated with impaired early myocardial contractile function. Consequently, the early reduction in myocardial contractility was accompanied by increased vasoactive–inotropic requirements during the immediate postoperative period following CPB.

Key Messages

In 52 CABG patients with preserved EF, early post-CPB myocardial dysfunction was more strongly associated with cellular stress than inflammation or oxidative stress. Higher postoperative HSP-70 correlated with reduced contractility (dP/dtmax) and vasoactive–inotropic requirements, while TNF-α and OH- scavenging capacity showed no significant hemodynamic associations

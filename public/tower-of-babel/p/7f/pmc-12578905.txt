Hierarchical multi-scale vision transformer model for accurate detection and classification of brain tumors in MRI-based medical imaging
Authors: C Sankari, V Jamuna, A R Kavitha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAutomated brain tumor detection represents a fundamental challenge in contemporary medical imaging, demanding both precision and computational feasibility for practical implementation. This research introduces a novel Vision Transformer (ViT) framework that incorporates an innovative Hierarchical Multi-Scale Attention (HMSA) methodology for automated detection and classification of brain tumors across four distinct categories: glioma, meningioma, pituitary adenoma, and healthy brain tissue. Our methodology presents several key innovations: (1) multi-resolution patch embedding strategy enabling feature extraction across different spatial scales (88, 1616, and 3232 patches), (2) computationally optimized transformer architecture achieving 35% reduction in training duration compared to conventional ViT implementations, and (3) probabilistic calibration mechanism enhancing prediction confidence for decision-making applications. Experimental validation was conducted using a comprehensive MRI dataset comprising 7023 T1-weighted contrast-enhanced images sourced from the publicly accessible Brain Tumor MRI Dataset. Our approach achieved superior classification performance with 98.7% accuracy while demonstrating significant improvements over conventional machine learning methodologies (Random Forest: 91.2%, Support Vector Machine: 89.8%, XGBoost: 92.5%), state-of-the-art CNN architectures (EfficientNet-B0: 96.5%, ResNet-50: 95.8%), standard transformers (ViT: 96.8%, Swin Transformer: 97.2%), and hybrid CNN-Transformer approaches (TransBTS: 96.9%, Swin-UNet: 96.6%). The model demonstrates excellent performance with precision of 0.986, recall of 0.988, F1-score of 0.987, and superior calibration quality (Expected Calibration Error: 0.023). The proposed framework establishes a computationally efficient approach for accurate brain tumor classification.Keywords: Automated brain tumor detection, Vision transformer architecture, Hierarchical attention mechanisms, Medical image analysis, Deep learning applications, Multi-resolution feature extractionSubject terms: Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Medical research, Oncology

Introduction

Among cancers affecting the nervous system, those originating in brain tissue stand out as particularly deadly. Their defining characteristic involves cells multiplying without regulation, creating abnormal masses inside cranial structures1. Medical professionals distinguish these growths into two categories: those that spread aggressively throughout tissue (malignant) and those that remain contained (benign). Clinical decisions about diagnosis and treatment depend heavily on variables including the growth’s position, dimensions, and speed of development2. Statistical data shows that occurrence patterns differ substantially when examining various population groups, with age, sex, ethnicity, and geographical location all contributing to these variations3. Identifying these growths at early stages proves critical for enhancing how long patients survive and achieving better clinical results4.

MRI technology has become the premier choice for identifying and analyzing brain growths because it provides exceptional visualization of soft tissues, delivers detailed spatial information, and operates without exposing patients to harmful radiation5. Medical facilities currently depend extensively on MRI for establishing precise diagnoses and developing treatment strategies6. The integration of deep convolutional neural networks powered by artificial intelligence has proven valuable in enhancing diagnostic precision while minimizing incorrect assessments7.

The emergence of Vision Transformer technology represents a groundbreaking development in medical image analysis, offering compelling alternatives to traditional convolutional network methods. When Vision Transformers first appeared in the literature8, they introduced fundamentally new approaches to processing visual information, and their application to medical imaging has yielded encouraging outcomes. Contemporary research has validated the exceptional performance of transformer-based architectures for analyzing brain growths:

One research team developed RanMerFormer in 2024, which implements a randomized approach to combining visual tokens, substantially reducing computational demands while classifying brain tumors9. Another group introduced LCDEIT in 2023, featuring linear complexity characteristics that make it particularly suitable for processing MRI data of brain growths, effectively addressing the computational burdens associated with earlier transformer designs10.

Researchers have also explored combining transformers with generative adversarial architectures for segmenting tumor boundaries, capitalizing on transformers’ ability to capture information across entire images to achieve superior segmentation precision11.

The versatility of transformer architectures extends into analyzing microscopic tissue samples as well, with researchers in 2023 presenting a weakly supervised transformer framework for examining tissue slides from primary brain cancers12. Studies have further demonstrated transformers’ proficiency in synthesizing information from multiple MRI imaging protocols alongside clinical records, leading to improved diagnostic accuracy13–15.

Related work and background

Deep learning approaches in medical imaging

The field of medical image processing has undergone substantial transformation through the adoption of deep learning technologies, especially for identifying and categorizing brain growths16. CNN architectures have dominated automated brain growth analysis, showcasing impressive capabilities in deriving multi-level spatial characteristics through successive convolutional and pooling operations17. The effectiveness of CNN-based methods stems from their capacity to autonomously discover feature patterns directly from unprocessed imaging data, removing the requirement for hand-crafted feature design18. An examination of deep learning principles and CNN frameworks has revealed their widespread utility spanning multiple application areas19.

Leveraging pre-trained CNN frameworks including VGG-16, VGG-19, ResNet-50, and InceptionV3 through transfer learning has yielded outstanding results in categorizing brain growths20. These networks, initially developed using extensive datasets, have been effectively adapted for medical imaging purposes, attaining precision levels surpassing 95% across numerous investigations21. This transfer learning strategy overcomes limitations posed by scarce medical imaging data while decreasing training duration and enhancing model robustness22. Multiple investigations have shown that frameworks such as VGG-19 reached precision rates of 99.48% when deployed for brain growth categorization applications23.

Residual network architectures have likewise exhibited encouraging outcomes in brain growth categorization. Researchers introduced Res-BRNet, an innovative residual and region-focused CNN framework designed for classifying brain growths using MRI data24. Furthermore, ResNet152-based transfer learning methods have been examined for automatically sorting brain MR scans into growth-present and growth-absent groups25. Dense CNN frameworks applied to multiple brain growth categories using brain MRI data have demonstrated substantial precision26.

Vision transformers for brain tumor classification

Vision Transformer architectures have swiftly established themselves as prominent tools in medical image processing, exhibiting outstanding capabilities for brain growth categorization applications27. An extensive investigation examining five pre-trained vision transformer variants (R50-ViT-l16, ViT-l16, ViT-l32, ViT-b16, and ViT-b32) revealed that ViT-b32 attained 98.24% precision in correctly categorizing brain growth images28.

Vision Transformer architectures handle images by dividing them into segments, which undergo linear embedding before passing through transformer encoder components featuring multi-head self-attention operations29. This methodology has exhibited remarkable effectiveness throughout diverse computer vision applications, including medical image processing30.

Numerous research initiatives have introduced refined ViT frameworks for multi-category brain growth categorization, integrating methods such as positional embedding, patch embedding, layer normalization, and multi-layer perceptron classification components31. Multi-objective optimization strategies have been utilized to refine the architectural specifications of Vision Transformer frameworks for effective brain growth categorization from 2D MRI data32. An enhanced Vision Transformer-based algorithm integrating relative position encoding and residual MLP has been created for human brain growth categorization33.

Sophisticated hybrid frameworks merging Vision Transformers with alternative deep learning methodologies have demonstrated potential in brain growth identification. Researchers developed a hybrid ViT-GRU framework to derive critical features through ViT and recognize connections between these features using GRU, tackling class imbalance while surpassing existing diagnostic approaches34. Vision transformers paired with ensemble frameworks and transfer learning have been utilized alongside explainable AI for brain growth identification and categorization35. Moreover, rotation invariant vision transformers have been created to strengthen brain growth identification in MRI by resolving rotational variance obstacles36.

Attention mechanisms in brain tumor classification

Attention-driven operations have surfaced as robust instruments for strengthening feature selection and elevating categorization precision in brain growth identification37. An ensemble attention operation incorporated into MobileNetV3 and EfficientNetB7 networks at both intermediate and terminal stages has been created to derive local and global-scale features for accurate identification of diverse patterns throughout brain growth images. The attention operation serves a critical function by selectively highlighting pertinent features, resulting in elevated categorization precision and decreased time requirements38.

Channel-focused attention operations have been investigated for dependable brain growth categorization. ResNet101 integrated with Channel-wise Attention Mode (CWAM) attained remarkable performance measurements of 99.83% precision for brain growth categorization39. Attention-directed residual multiscale CNN frameworks have been created for multiclass brain growth categorization using MR images40. Graph attention autoencoder influenced CNN methods have likewise been introduced for brain growth categorization using MRI41.

Advanced architectures and hybrid models

Multiple sophisticated frameworks merging different deep learning paradigms have been introduced for brain growth categorization. Dense EfficientNet frameworks have been created utilizing min-max normalization to sort brain MRI images into various categories with remarkable precision42. BrainNet, a high-precision brain growth categorization system employing optimized EfficientNet framework, has attained precision levels exceeding 99% throughout multiple growth categories43. Additionally, enhanced methods utilizing pretrained DenseNet with global average pooling and hyperparameter optimization have exhibited improved categorization capabilities44.

Deep feature integration methods have demonstrated encouraging outcomes in brain growth categorization. A dependable method for multi-type categorization of brain growth utilizing deep feature integration from ResNet101, DenseNet121, and EfficientNetB0 has been created45. Hybrid deep learning frameworks merging EfficientNets with transfer learning have reached test precision levels of 99.06% for brain growth categorization46. Furthermore, multi-scale attention operations incorporated with EfficientNetB4 encoder have been created for enhanced MRI brain growth processing47.

Parallel deep convolutional neural networks have been introduced for MRI brain growth identification and categorization, merging local and global features through dual-pathway frameworks48. The incorporation of sophisticated preprocessing methods with deep learning frameworks has additionally improved categorization capabilities49.

Critical analysis of CNN-to-ViT transition in medical imaging

While Vision Transformers have demonstrated remarkable success in natural image processing, their adoption in medical imaging presents a more complex landscape that warrants critical examination. The transition from CNN-based to transformer-based architectures in medical imaging is not merely a technological upgrade but involves fundamental trade-offs that must be carefully evaluated. CNNs continue to dominate medical image segmentation tasks for several compelling reasons: (1) Inductive Bias Advantage - CNNs possess inherent translation equivariance and locality bias that naturally align with medical imaging characteristics, where anatomical structures maintain consistent spatial relationships; (2) Data Efficiency - Medical datasets are typically smaller than natural image datasets (thousands vs. millions of images), and CNNs require significantly less data to achieve optimal performance compared to Vision Transformers, which traditionally demand extensive pre-training; (3) Computational Practicality - Standard ViT implementations exhibit quadratic computational complexity with respect to image resolution, making them impractical for high-resolution medical images commonly used in segmentation tasks. However, the transition to Vision Transformers becomes compelling for classification tasks when specific conditions are met. The global receptive field capability of transformers provides distinct advantages for brain tumor classification: (1) Long-range Dependencies - Brain tumors often exhibit infiltrative patterns and distant morphological relationships that CNNs struggle to capture due to their limited receptive fields; (2) Multi-scale Integration - While CNNs process features hierarchically through pooling operations, transformers can simultaneously attend to features across different spatial scales without information loss; (3) Attention Interpretability - The self-attention mechanism provides valuable visualizations of model focus areas, enhancing diagnostic confidence and enabling radiologist validation. The critical insight is that the choice between CNN and ViT architectures should be task-specific rather than universally applied. For dense prediction tasks like segmentation, CNN architectures (particularly U-Net variants) remain superior due to their spatial inductive biases and computational efficiency. However, for classification tasks requiring global context understanding and multi-scale feature integration—as is essential for brain tumor classification—Vision Transformers offer theoretical and practical advantages that justify their adoption, provided that architectural innovations address their inherent limitations.

Architectural synergy: bridging CNN and transformer frameworks

Our Novel Vision Transformer architecture directly addresses the fundamental limitations that have historically hindered ViT adoption in medical imaging. The Hierarchical Multi-Scale Attention (HMSA) mechanism resolves the data efficiency problem by processing multiple spatial scales simultaneously, reducing the dependency on extensive pre-training datasets. The computational optimization strategies address the quadratic complexity issue through grouped attention and dynamic depth mechanisms, achieving 35% training time reduction while maintaining diagnostic accuracy. The probabilistic calibration component addresses the clinical requirement for confidence quantification—a feature absent in most CNN implementations but essential for medical decision-making support. This targeted approach demonstrates that the CNN-to-ViT transition in medical imaging requires domain-specific architectural innovations rather than direct application of natural image processing techniques. Our approach preserves the global attention advantages of transformers while addressing their practical limitations for medical deployment, representing a meaningful advancement beyond both traditional CNN approaches and standard ViT implementations.

The persistence of CNN dominance in medical segmentation should not overshadow the genuine advantages that appropriately designed Vision Transformers offer for classification tasks. Recent studies have shown that hybrid approaches combining CNN feature extraction with transformer attention mechanisms can achieve superior performance, suggesting that the future lies not in complete replacement but in architectural synergy. Our approach represents this evolution by incorporating CNN-inspired inductive biases (through multi-resolution patch embedding) with transformer advantages (global attention and interpretability), creating a hybrid framework specifically optimized for brain tumor classification challenges.

Proposed novel vision transformer architecture

The Novel Vision Transformer represents a significant methodological advancement in medical image analysis by incorporating domain-specific knowledge regarding brain tumor characteristics into the transformer architecture. This innovative approach fundamentally departs from conventional Vision Transformer implementations by addressing the unique challenges inherent in medical imaging, particularly the multi-scale nature of brain tumor manifestations and the critical requirement for diagnostic confidence in clinical settings.

The proposed architecture integrates several key innovations that collectively enhance both diagnostic accuracy and computational efficiency. First, the incorporation of hierarchical multi-scale processing acknowledges that brain tumors exhibit varying morphological characteristics across different spatial scales - from fine-textural details visible at high resolution to global anatomical context discernible at broader scales. Second, the integration of probabilistic calibration mechanisms addresses the fundamental clinical requirement for trustworthy confidence estimates, enabling radiologists to make informed decisions based on model uncertainty quantification. Third, the computational optimization strategies ensure practical deployment feasibility in resource-constrained clinical environments without compromising diagnostic performance.

The architectural design philosophy centers on the principle of adaptive feature learning, where the model dynamically adjusts its attention mechanisms based on the specific characteristics of the input brain MRI scan. This adaptive behavior is particularly crucial for brain tumor classification, where different tumor types require varying levels of spatial resolution analysis. For instance, small pituitary adenomas necessitate fine-grained texture analysis, while large gliomas benefit from broader contextual understanding of their infiltrative patterns within brain parenchyma.

Furthermore, the proposed system incorporates clinical workflow considerations by providing interpretable attention visualizations that can assist radiologists in understanding the model’s decision-making process. This interpretability component is essential for clinical adoption, as it enables medical professionals to validate the model’s focus areas against their clinical expertise and domain knowledge.

In stark contrast, our probabilistic calibration model (Fig. 10b) achieves near-perfect calibration with a dramatically reduced ECE of 0.023, representing a 73.6% improvement in calibration quality. The reliability curve closely follows the ideal diagonal line, indicating that predicted confidence accurately matches actual accuracy across all confidence levels. This calibration excellence is achieved through our temperature scaling optimization that minimizes the negative log-likelihood loss combined with Expected Calibration Error, ensuring that when the model reports 90% confidence, it is indeed correct approximately 90% of the time.

Discussion and methodological significance

Superior classification performance

Our Novel Vision Transformer achieved 98.7% accuracy, representing a marked improvement in automated brain tumor detection. The 1.9% improvement over standard ViT and 2.2% over EfficientNet-B0 translates to approximately 128 additional correct classifications per 7,023 cases, which constitutes a notable improvement given the comprehensive dataset size.

The multi-resolution patch embedding proved essential for capturing multi-scale features. Analysis of learned attention weights reveals that gliomas utilize 45% fine-scale, 35% intermediate-scale, and 20% coarse-scale attention, while meningiomas employ 30% fine-scale, 40% intermediate-scale, and 30% coarse-scale attention. Pituitary tumors demonstrate 60% fine-scale, 30% intermediate-scale, and 10% coarse-scale attention, whereas normal brain tissue assessment benefits from 20% fine-scale, 30% intermediate-scale, and 50% coarse-scale attention.

This adaptive behavior aligns with tumor-specific morphological characteristics, where small pituitary tumors require fine detail analysis while normal brain assessment benefits from global context.

Dataset scale and model generalizability

The utilization of the complete 7023-image dataset represents substantial improvement over previous studies that often employed smaller subsets. This larger dataset size provides enhanced statistical significance of results, improved model generalizability across diverse cases, more robust evaluation of rare tumor variants, and stronger confidence in the reliability of the proposed framework.

The balanced class distribution with glioma comprising 23.1%, meningioma 23.4%, pituitary 25.0%, and no tumor 28.5% ensures that the model learns to distinguish between classes without bias toward majority classes.

Computational efficiency and practical deployment

Despite additional components, our model achieves 35% faster training than standard ViT through grouped attention reducing quadratic complexity, optimized FFN dimensions saving 25% computations, and dynamic depth reducing average forward passes by 20%.

These optimizations enable deployment on standard computational workstations without requiring specialized hardware, making the technology more accessible to environments with limited computational resources.

Hybrid CNN-transformer architecture analysis

The superior performance of our Novel ViT-HMSA over hybrid approaches provides several important insights into architectural design principles for medical image classification.

Architectural advantages over hybrid approaches

Our comprehensive comparison reveals key advantages of pure transformer design:

Information Flow Optimization: Pure transformer architecture eliminates CNN-transformer interface complexities that can limit information flow in hybrid architectures. The seamless attention mechanism enables direct global context modeling from input patches, avoiding potential bottlenecks at CNN-transformer boundaries present in TransBTS and Swin-UNet designs.

Multi-Scale Processing Superiority: The Hierarchical Multi-Scale Attention mechanism provides more sophisticated multi-scale processing than simple CNN feature extraction followed by transformer encoding. Unlike fixed CNN hierarchies in hybrid approaches, our scale factors (, , ) enable learnable, adaptive attention allocation that dynamically adjusts to different tumor characteristics.

Global Context Integration: End-to-end attention mechanism enables global context modeling from the input level rather than only in later processing stages, as typically occurs in hybrid approaches where CNN layers first process local features before transformer attention. This comprehensive global awareness contributes to superior diagnostic accuracy.

Performance and efficiency analysis

The performance improvements achieved by our Novel ViT-HMSA demonstrate several critical advantages:

Superior Accuracy with Reduced Complexity: Achieving 1.8% and 2.1% accuracy improvements over TransBTS and Swin-UNet respectively, while maintaining 14.6% and 7.7% parameter reduction, indicates superior architectural efficiency. This suggests that carefully designed pure transformer architectures can outperform hybrid approaches for medical image classification tasks.

Training Stability Enhancement: The smooth convergence characteristics of our approach, compared to the more volatile training behavior observed in TransBTS, indicate better optimization properties. This stability demonstrates the effectiveness of our architectural design in achieving consistent and reliable training dynamics.

Computational Efficiency: Despite superior performance, our approach achieves better computational efficiency through optimized attention mechanisms and reduced parameter count. The reduced computational requirements (38.6M vs 41.8-45.2M parameters) demonstrate the effectiveness of our HMSA mechanism in achieving parameter efficiency without sacrificing performance.

Architectural design insights

The comparison results provide valuable insights for transformer architecture design in medical imaging:

Pure Transformer Effectiveness: The superior performance over hybrid approaches validates the effectiveness of pure transformer architectures for medical image analysis when properly designed with domain-specific attention mechanisms.

Multi-Scale Attention Benefits: The HMSA mechanism proves more effective than traditional CNN-based multi-scale processing, demonstrating that learnable attention-based multi-scale analysis can outperform fixed hierarchical feature extraction.

Parameter Efficiency: Achieving better performance with fewer parameters indicates that attention-based architectures can be more parameter-efficient than hybrid designs, suggesting promising directions for future medical AI system development.

Conclusions and future directions

This investigation presents a Novel Vision Transformer with Hierarchical Multi-Scale Attention (HMSA) achieving state-of-the-art performance in brain tumor detection with 98.7% accuracy, 0.987 F1-score, and excellent calibration quality (ECE = 0.023) on a comprehensive dataset of 7023 MRI images. Key innovations include multi-resolution patch embedding for multi-scale feature extraction, scale factors derived through systematic empirical investigation (, , ), and a 35% efficiency improvement through optimized transformer blocks.

Our comprehensive evaluation using standard classification metrics demonstrates significant improvements over existing approaches: 1.9% higher accuracy than standard ViT, 2.2% improvement over EfficientNet-B0, and substantial gains over traditional machine learning methods (Random Forest: 91.2%, SVM: 89.8%) as well as hybrid CNN–Transformer approaches (TransBTS: 96.9%, Swin-UNet: 96.6%). The model achieves superior performance across all evaluation criteria including precision (0.986), recall (0.988), and AUC (0.996). The probabilistic calibration mechanism achieves excellent reliability (ECE = 0.023), addressing critical requirements for trustworthy AI systems where prediction confidence is crucial for decision-making.

Future work will extend this framework to multi-sequence and 3D volumetric MRI data, and develop more rigorous theoretical models for attention scale selection to further generalize the HMSA Vision Transformer.

The success of this Novel Vision Transformer establishes a foundation for next-generation medical imaging AI systems and demonstrates that systematic empirical investigation combined with domain expertise can achieve superior performance while maintaining scientific rigor.

Dietary berberine supplementation regulates production performance, antioxidant capacity, and immunity of Wenchang chicken (Gallus gallus domestics)
Authors: Xiaohui Zhang, Yanxia Qi, Tieshan Xu, Lihong Gu, Lehuan Wu, Yihui Peng, Shuyang Wang, Wu Liang, Xinli Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBerberine is an isoquinoline alkaloid derived from various medicinal plants, including Coptis chinensis. Berberine exhibits multiple biological activities, including immune modulation, antibacterial activity, and antioxidant properties. Wenchang chicken is a local breed from Hainan Province, China, which is still primarily raised under free-range conditions. In this study, we compared the effects of dietary berberine supplementation on body measurements, weight, meat quality, serum immunoglobulin levels, antioxidant indices, liver transcriptome, proteome, and phosphoproteome in Wenchang chickens. Berberine had no significant effect on the body measurements and weights of Wenchang chickens. However, it significantly altered the color of the breast muscles and significantly reduced the shear force of the breast and leg muscles. Berberine also significantly increased serum Immunoglobulin A(IgA) and Immunoglobulin M(IgM) concentrations and glutathione (GSH) activity in Wenchang chickens, while significantly decreasing malondialdehyde (MDA) concentration. RNA-seq identified 764 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the liver. The significantly enriched GO terms were related to immune and inflammatory processes, such as cell chemotaxis, granulocyte migration, and chemotaxis. GSEA identified 50 gene sets, many of which were associated with immune regulation and inflammatory responses, such as Hedgehog signaling, interferon alpha response, and IL-6 JAK-STAT3 signaling. Liver proteomics identified 530 differentially abundant proteins (DAPs), some of which were associated with innate immune responses, such as mitochondrial antiviral signaling protein (MAVS), TNF receptor-associated factor 3 (TRAF3), and BH3-interacting domain death agonist (BID) in the Herpes simplex virus 1 infection pathway. Joint analysis of transcriptome and proteome revealed a highly significant positive correlation between mRNA expression and protein abundance in the liver. A total of 3,482 phosphorylated proteins containing 39,142 phosphorylation sites were identified in Wenchang chicken livers. Among these, 2,086 phosphorylation sites in 714 phosphorylated proteins were significantly upregulated, whereas 2,426 phosphorylation sites in 659 phosphorylated proteins were significantly downregulated in the livers of berberine-treated Wenchang chickens. These phosphoproteins were significantly enriched in KEGG pathways associated with defense, immune signaling regulation, and apoptosis, such as the mTOR signaling pathway, Salmonella infection, and autophagy. This study provided foundational data for the application of berberine in broiler production.Keywords: Wenchang chicken, Berberine, Multi-omics, Immunity, Antioxidant

Introduction

Berberine is a bitter-tasting quaternary ammonium isoquinoline alkaloid that can be extracted from the stems and roots of various medicinal plants, including the rhizomes of barberry, phellodendron, and coptis (Luo et al., 2024). These plants have long been employed in traditional Chinese medicine for treating intestinal disorders (Song et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023). Berberine inhibits the proliferation of bacteria such as Helicobacter pylori, protects the intestinal epithelial barrier from damage, and improves liver injury (Zhang et al., 2017; Izadparast et al., 2022). Furthermore, berberine exhibits multiple biological activities, including antioxidant properties, hypoglycaemic effects, and anti-inflammatory properties (Heidarzadeh et al., 2024; Araj-Khodaei et al., 2024). Low-dose berberine (60 mg/kg diet) had no significant effect on the growth of broilers, but significantly decreased MDA levels and enhanced T-SOD activity in the liver and serum (Yang et al., 2019). High-dose berberine (2 g/kg diet) could improve intestinal morphology and barrier function in chickens and reduce the inflammatory storm (Dehau et al., 2023). These physiological functions of berberine indicate broad application prospects in the poultry industry.

Wenchang chicken is a unique local poultry breed from Hainan Province, China, highly prized for its distinctive flavor and premium meat quality (Jiang et al., 2025). The farming model for Wenchang chickens differs significantly from that of white-feathered broiler chickens. Wenchang chickens typically require about 90 days of free-range rearing in a natural environment (Xu et al., 2025). During this period, chickens frequently come into contact with soil, feces, and intermediate hosts (insects, wild birds) in open environments, placing them directly within the natural transmission chain of parasites and pathogens. This increases the risk of infection through direct contact, contaminated water sources, or food (Chowdhury et al., 2023). Consequently, the incidence of parasitic and infectious diseases rises significantly (McMullin et al., 2022).

In intensive poultry production systems, veterinary antibiotics serve as critical biological control tools. Antibiotics have four primary applications within the poultry industry: therapeutic interventions, disease control measures, disease prevention, and growth promotion (Zheng et al., 2025). Driven by the goal of profit maximization, the non-therapeutic use of antibiotics persists in the poultry industry, leading to drug residues and antimicrobial resistance (Cerqueira-Cézar et al., 2025). With the implementation of the National Action Plan for Combating Antimicrobial Resistance (2022–2025), the use of non-prescription antibiotic feed additives in China's poultry production has significantly decreased (2022). Accordingly, plant-derived non-antibiotic feed additives are gaining increasing attention in the poultry farming industry (Seidavi et al., 2021). Berberine exhibits broad-spectrum antibacterial and antiparasitic (Elizondo-Luévano et al., 2021) effects while promoting the proliferation of beneficial bacteria such as Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium (Chen et al., 2025). This optimizes intestinal microecological balance, thereby improving feed conversion rates and enhancing nutrient absorption. Berberine enhances macrophage activity and antibody production in animals, thereby reducing the incidence of disease and lowering mortality rates in poultry (Li et al., 2017). These studies indicate that berberine has broad application prospects in the poultry industry. The humid and hot climate of Hainan and the extended rearing period necessitate improvements in the antioxidant capacity and overall health of Wenchang chickens. Therefore, berberine was added to the diet of Wenchang chickens, and its effects on growth and development, meat quality, slaughter performance, as well as liver transcriptomics, proteomics, and phosphoproteomics were subsequently evaluated, with the aim of providing a scientific basis for the application of berberine in the Wenchang chicken industry.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the Guidelines for Experimental Animals established by the Ministry of Science and Technology (Beijing, China). The protocol was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at the Experimental Animal Center of Hainan Academy of Agricultural Science (HNXMSY-20240427). Additionally, protocols were in place to ensure appropriate housing conditions, adequate nutrition, and access to water.

Experimental animals

One hundred and twenty Wenchang chickens (90 days old at the fattening stage, of similar weight) were randomly divided into 2 groups with 6 replicates of 10 chickens each. The control group (NS) was fed a basal diet, and the experimental group (TS) was supplemented with 0.2 g/Kg of berberine (Shanghai Yuanye Biotechnology Co., Ltd) in the basal diet. All broilers were reared in a standardized environment where temperature was maintained at 23∼25°C and humidity ranged from 45% to 55%. The basal diet was formulated according to NY/T 3645-2020, and the duration of the experiment was 30 days, as shown in Table 1.

Phenotypic measurement indicators

At the end of the experiment, the chickens underwent a 12 h feed withdrawal period. All the chickens for each group were weighed using an electronic weighing scale, and the live body weight (g) was recorded. Subsequently, body size measurements, including body length, keel length, chest width, chest depth, pelvic width, shank length, and shank circumference, were recorded in cm using a measuring tape. Then, 6 broilers/group (1 broiler/replicate) were selected to collect blood samples from the wing vein. Blood was centrifuged, and the supernatant was carefully collected to obtain serum, which was stored in a −80°C refrigerator. After slaughter, the liver, spleen, thymus, and Bursa of Fabricius were weighed by an electronic weighing scale and recorded. Livers were cut into small pieces and placed in cryopreservation tubes, quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored in a −80°C refrigerator for further analysis. Next, a Minolta CR310 reflectance (Japan) was used to measure the muscle color on the inner surface of the left breast muscle and the inner surface of the left thigh muscle. Each muscle was measured four times, and the average values of l* (brightness), a* (red), and b* (yellow) single color parameters were calculated. The pH values of the breast and leg muscles were measured at 45 min and 24 h post-mortem respectively, using a pH meter (Model PHS-3E; Leici, Shanghai, China). The breast and leg muscle samples were cut into strips of 2 cm (length) × 1 cm (thickness) × 1 cm (width), and then the shear forces were measured using a Zhiqu shear device (Model ZQ-990 L; Zhiqu, Guangdong, China). The drip loss of the breast and leg muscles was determined by the weight loss of the meat after refrigeration at 4°C for 24 hours.

Serum antioxidant parameters and immunological assays

Serum IgA, IgG, and IgM levels and antioxidant parameters (CAT, T-SOD, MDA, GSH, and T-AOC) were measured using a spectrophotometer (BioRad, California, USA) with commercial ELISA kits (Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) following the manufacturer's instructions.

Transcriptome sequencing and analysis

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent from the liver tissues of 6 TS Wenchang Chicken and 6 NS Wenchang Chicken. (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). RNA quality and quantity were assessed using gel electrophoresis, a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, DE, USA), and an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). RNA-seq library (Pair-ended 150 bp) construction and sequencing were performed by Beijing Novogene Biotechnology (Beijing, China). The RNA-seq data of this study have been uploaded to the Sequence Read Archive (SRA), with the accession number SUB16224057.

Clean reads were obtained by removing adapters and low-quality reads using fastp v0.20.1. The clean reads were then mapped to the chicken reference genome (GCA_016699485.1) using HISAT2 v2.0.5. Feature Counts v1.5.0 was used to count the read numbers mapped to each gene. Subsequently, we assembled the mapped reads into transcripts and quantified the gene expression to fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped fragments (FPKM) using StringTie v1.3.3b. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using DESeq2 v1.20.0, with the screening criteria of P-value < 0.05 and |log2(fold change) | ≥ 1.

DIA-based proteomics mass spectrometry assay and data analysis

Liver samples from 6 TS Wenchang Chicken and 6 NS Wenchang Chicken were individually ground to frozen powder in liquid nitrogen, lysed with SDT buffer (containing 100 mM NaCl) plus 1/100 volume of DTT, and ultrasonicated on ice for 5 min. The lysates were incubated at 95°C for 12 min, cooled in an ice bath for 2 min, and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C. The supernatants were then collected and incubated with sufficient IAM in the dark for 1 h for alkylation. Each sample was mixed with 4 volumes of pre-chilled acetone, incubated at −20°C for at least 2 h, and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C to collect protein precipitates. The precipitates were washed three times with cold chloroform, fully dissolved in DB buffer, and quantified for protein content via the Bradford method. Proteins were digested overnight at 37°C using Trypsin Gold (Promega, Madison, WI, USA), and the resulting peptides were desalted and vacuum-dried.

Peptides were separated on an upgraded Vanquish Neo UHPLC system with a C18 pre-column (Cat. No. 174500; 5 mm × 300 μm, 5 μm; Thermo Fisher Scientific), then ionized via an Easy-Spray ESI source and detected in DIA mode on a Thermo Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, San Jose, CA, USA).

Raw data were searched and analyzed with DIA-NN software against the Gallus gallus protein database (27,535 entries). Search parameters were set to 10 ppm mass tolerance for precursor ions and 0.02 Da for fragmentations. Only PSMs with ≥ 99% confidence were retained for protein quantification. Independent-samples t-tests were performed to calculate P-values and assess the significance of protein abundance differences between the two groups. Proteins with a fold change > 1.5 or < 0.67 and P-value < 0.05 were identified as differentially abundant proteins (DAPs).

DIA-based phosphoproteomics mass spectrometry assay

Liver tissue preparation, protein quantification, and proteolysis procedures were consistent with the previous DIA proteomics analysis. The separated peptide samples were added to binding buffer-pretreated TiO₂ columns, incubated for 30 min, and centrifuged at 2000 × g to remove the supernatant, after which the TiO₂ columns were washed three times with washing buffer. Phosphopeptides were eluted with elution buffer, vacuum freeze-dried, and dissolved in 0.1% formic acid.

The phosphopeptides were separated on an upgraded Vanquish Neo UHPLC system, ionized by an Easy-Spray ESI ion source, and detected in DIA mode on a Thermo Orbitrap Astral mass spectrometer. The data analysis process and software were identical to those used for the proteomics analysis. Phosphoproteins with a fold change (FC) > 1.5 or < 0.67 and a P-value < 0.05 were defined as differentially phosphorylated proteins (DPPs).

GO annotation and KEGG enrichment analysis

The detected DEGs, DEPs, and DPPs were functionally annotated using Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomics (KEGG) enrichment using the R package clusterProfiler v3.8.1.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

All transcripts participated in the GSEA analysis, which was based on the chicken org.Gg.eg.db gene set collections (MSigDB v7.0, Broad Institute, Cambridge, MA, USA), and was performed via the clusterProfiler package (v4.4.2) in R software. The enrichment score of each gene set was calculated based on the full ranking, and then the normalized enriched score (NES) was determined for each gene set. The significant enrichment of gene sets was selected based on the absolute values of NES > 1, nominal P-value of NES ≤ 0.05, and false discovery rate (FDR) ≤ 0.05 (Vu et al., 2023).

Protein interactions network analysis and modules selection

Protein interactions network analysis was performed using String (v11.5) and then visualised using Cytoscape (v3.10.1) to generate protein interaction network maps and core gene screens. The application of Molecular Complex Detection (MCODE) in Cytoscape was utilized to screen the modules of the PPI network. The standard settings of MCODE rest with: degree cutoff = 2, node score cutoff = 0.2, k-core = 2, and maximum depth = 100. Additionally, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed to determine the function and pathway of proteins in the modules.

Joint analysis of multiomics data

In order to better explore the intrinsic mechanisms underlying the differences in immunity between the two populations of Wenchang chickens, Transcriptome–proteomics integrated analyses and proteomics–phosphoproteomics integrated analyses were performed.

Verification of genes expression by RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from liver tissues using TRIzol reagent (Cat. No. 15596026, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). RNA purity and concentration were measured with a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific), after which 10 μg of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into first-strand cDNA using the PrimeScript™ RT Reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (TaKaRa, Dalian, China).

Twelve DEGs were selected for qPCR analysis, with primers (Additional file 1) designed via Oligo 6.0 software. qPCR was performed on a Bio-Rad CFX-96 thermal cycler (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) with three technical replicates for each sample, and the GAPDH gene was used for data normalization. The qPCR reaction system (20 μL) consisted of 1 μL of cDNA template, 10 μL TB Green Premix DimerEraser (2 ×), 0.5 μL of each primer, and 8 μL ddH2O. The qPCR reaction conditions were as follows: pre-denaturation at 95°C for 3 min, denaturation at 95°C for 20 s, annealing for 20 s, and extension at 72°C for 20 s (40 cycles); fluorescence signals were collected at the endpoint of extension phase. The melting curve was increased by 0.5°C every 5 s from 65 to 95°C. The relative gene expression fold change was calculated using the standard 2−ΔΔCt method.

Statistical analysis

All experimental results were presented as mean ± SD, with a minimum of 6 independent replicates. We used an independent two-sample t-test to compare 2 groups and considered differences statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Phenotypic measurement indicators of TS and NS Wenchang chickens

There were no significant differences in body weight and body measurements between TS and NS Wenchang chickens, as shown in Table 2. Accordingly, no significant differences were observed in the liver, spleen, thymus, and Bursa of Fabricius indices between TS and NS Wenchang chickens, as shown in Table 2. The addition of berberine to the diet markedly altered the color of the breast muscle (Fig. 1), resulting in significantly higher a* value and b* value in the breast muscle of Wenchang chickens in the TS group compared to the NS group, as shown in Table 3. The addition of berberine to the daily diet had no discernible effect on the color of the leg muscles. Berberine had no significant effect on the pH values of either the breast or leg muscles, as shown in Table 3. The shear force of the leg muscle in the TS group Wenchang chickens was significantly lower than that in the NS group Wenchang chickens, and the shear force of the breast muscle was extremely significantly lower than that in the NS group Wenchang chickens.

Global gene expression profiles of TS and NS Wenchang chicken livers

RNA-seq of six liver tissues generated 168.75 Gb of clean data, with an average of 14.06±0.60 Gb of clean data per sample. For all samples, at least 96.06% of the reads had a quality score equal to or greater than Q30, of which 90.12%–93.17% were mapped to the chicken reference genome, and an average of 87.25 ± 0.77% of reads were uniquely mapped to the chicken reference genome (Additional file 2).

RNA-Seq analysis identified 764 DEGs, of which 494 were upregulated and 270 were downregulated in the TS group (Fig. 2A). Principal Component Analysis (PCA) showed comprehensive differences in gene expression between the two groups, and we observed a clear separation between the TS group and NS group (Fig. 2B).

Volcano and PCA maps for liver transcriptome analysis. (A) Volcano map; (B) PCA plot.

To further analyze the effects of berberine on the physiological functions of Wenchang chicken livers, we evaluated DEGs by KEGG and GO functional enrichment analyses. The significantly enriched GO terms were related to immune and inflammatory, such as defense response to other organism, defense response to bacterium, chemokine receptor binding, chemokine activity, and peroxidase activity (Additional file 3). Numerous GO-BP terms related to defense responses were significantly enriched, including defense response to other organisms, to bacteria, to viruses, and symbionts. The only significantly enriched GO—CC term was cytosolic ribosome. Significantly enriched GO-MF terms primarily involved the binding of chemokines to receptors, such as chemokine receptor binding, CCR chemokine receptor binding, chemokine activity, and peroxidase activity (Fig. 3A). Significantly enriched KEGG pathways primarily included steroid biosynthesis, thiamine metabolism, and sphingolipid metabolism (Fig. 3A).

(A)GO analysis of the liver transcriptome; (B) KEGG analysis of the liver transcriptome; (C) RT-qPCR validation of liver transcriptome; (D) Correlation of transcriptome and RT-qPCR; (E) GSEA analysis of the liver transcriptome.

We analyzed the expression levels of 10 significantly upregulated DEGs and 2 significantly downregulated DEGs in the livers of Wenchang chickens from the TS and NS groups using RT-qPCR. RT-qPCR results were consistent with the RNA-seq data (Fig. 3B), for example, the expression levels of AVBD9 and AVBD10 were significantly upregulated in the liver tissues of Wenchang chickens in the treated group. Linear regression analysis between RNA-seq and RT-qPCR revealed a significant positive correlation with a correlation coefficient (r) of 0.75, further supporting the reliability of RNA-seq (Fig. 3C).

In order to investigate the key biological pathways by which berberine modulates disease resistance and immunity in Wenchang chickens, we performed GSEA analysis on all expressed mRNAs and identified 50 gene sets (Additional file 4). Many of these gene sets are associated with innate immunity and inflammation regulation, such as inflammatory response, interferon alpha response, IL6 JAK STAT3 signaling. At the same time, oxidative phosphorylation and the reactive oxygen species pathway were negatively enriched, indicating reduced intracellular ROS production and less oxidative damage, thereby validating and supporting the accuracy of GSEA results (Fig. 3D).

Proteomic profiling of liver tissue from Wenchang chickens

A total of 8008 proteins were identified in liver tissues of TS and NS Wenchang chickens. A total of 337 upregulated DAPs and 193 downregulated DAPs were identified in the TS group compared to the NS group (Fig. 4A, Additional file 5).

Effects of Berberine Supplementation in Diet on the Proteome of Wenchang Chicken Liver. (A)Volcano map of liver proteomics; (B) GO analysis for liver proteomics; (C) KEGG analysis for liver proteomics; (D) PPI analysis of liver proteomics.

GO analysis revealed that the most enriched entries were mRNA metabolic, post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression, nucleocytoplasmic transport process (Fig. 4B, Additional file 6). DAPs were involved in various KEGG pathways, including Lysosome, mTOR signaling pathway, PPAR signaling pathway and endocytosis (Fig. 4C). Enrichment analysis of GO and KEGG pathways provided valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms by which berberine modulates disease resistance and immune responses in Wenchang chickens. We identified several DAPs associated with innate immune responses, such as mitochondrial antiviral signaling protein (MAVS), TNF receptor-associated factor 3 (TRAF3), BH3-interacting domain death agonist (BID) in the herpes simplex virus 1 infection pathway.

Protein interactions network analysis was used to identify interactions between DAPs. Three proteins were identified as hubs in the network: ubiquitin a-52 residue ribosomal protein fusion product 1 (UBA52), myb-like SWIRM and MPN domains 1 (MYSM1) and BC1 synthesis-like (BCS1L) (Fig. 4D). Both UBA52 and MYSM1 regulate the NF-κB signaling pathway through the ubiquitin modification system, profoundly and fundamentally influencing the development, activation, and inflammatory responses of immune cells. In addition, ribosomal protein s15 (RPS15), vesicle-associated membrane protein 7 (VAMP7), mitochondrial ribosomal protein s10 (MRPS10) and mitochondrial ribosomal protein l3 (MRPL3) were identified as key proteins.

Integrated analysis of transcriptomics and proteomics data

The Spearman correlation coefficient between mRNA expression and protein abundance was 0.29, and statistical analysis revealed a week but statistically significant positive correlation between them (p < 0.01) (Fig. 5). Among 7997 mRNA–protein pairs, 563 (7.04%) displayed significant positive correlations with Spearman’s coefficient > 0 and FDR < 0.05, whereas 32 (0.40%) displayed significant negative correlations with Spearman’s coefficient < 0 and FDR < 0.05. Genes whose mRNA expression was significantly correlated with protein abundance were mainly enriched in signaling pathways such as influenza A, herpes simplex virus 1 infection, and Salmonella infection. Interferons play a crucial role in avian antiviral defense mechanisms. IFIH1 and STAT1 are key molecules that trigger interferon biosynthesis, and RSAD2 is one of the most potent and critical effector molecules of interferon. In this study, the mRNA expression levels and protein abundance of IFIH1, STAT1, and RSAD2 were significantly upregulated in the livers of TS Wenchang chickens. NLRX1 inhibits interferon biosynthesis, and its mRNA expression levels and protein abundance were significantly downregulated in TS Wenchang chicken livers.

Identification of liver phosphoproteins in Wenchang chickens

95% of peptide chains ranged in length from 9 to 36 amino acids, consistent with the general characteristics of tryptic peptides. (Fig. 6A). A total of 3,482 phosphoproteins containing 39,142 phosphorylation sites were identified in TS and NS Wenchang chicken livers (Additional file 7). Among these phosphorylation sites, 78.79% were phosphorylated at serine residues, 18.18% at threonine residues, and 3.03% at tyrosine residues. Compared to NS Wenchang chickens, 2,086 phosphorylation sites among 714 phosphorylated proteins in TS Wenchang chicken livers showed significant upregulation, while 2,426 phosphorylation sites among 659 phosphorylated proteins exhibited significant downregulation (Fig. 6B). It is worth noting that 168 proteins exhibited both upregulated and downregulated phosphorylation sites simultaneously; for example, the STMN1 protein had one significantly upregulated phosphorylation site (Ser63) and one significantly downregulated phosphorylation site (Ser16), while the SRSF2 protein had three significantly upregulated phosphorylation sites (Ser208, Ser212, Ser221) and one significantly downregulated phosphorylation site (Thr29), indicating that these proteins undergo precise and complex phosphorylation regulation.

Results of protein phosphorylation analysis of liver. (A) Peptide length analysis; (B) Quantitative analysis of phosphoproteins and phosphorylation sites; (C)GO analysis of phosphoproteins; (D) KEGG analysis of phosphoproteins.

GO enrichment analysis revealed that the top two BP categories were mRNA metabolic process and positive regulation of RNA metabolic process, the top two CC categories were nucleoplasm and actin cytoskeleton, and the top two MF categories were cytoskeletal protein binding and GTPase regulator activity (Fig. 6C). Thirty-two KEGG pathways were significantly enriched, among which those related to defense, immune signaling regulation, and antioxidant were mTOR signaling pathway, salmonella infection, and MAPK pathway (Fig. 6D, Additional file 8). These pathways shared six core proteins: RAF1, MAP2K1, MAPK14, AKT1, mTOR and RAB7A. The phosphorylation levels of these proteins varied significantly; for example, the phosphorylation level of MAP2K1 was significantly downregulated, while that of MAPK14 was significantly upregulated, indicating that the regulation of phosphorylation and dephosphorylation was highly complex.

Discussion

Berberine is a natural plant extract with relatively low toxicity, demonstrating significant effects in promoting growth, exhibiting antibacterial and anti-inflammatory properties, regulating immunity, and improving gut health (Zhou et al., 2025). Berberine exhibits low residue levels in livestock and poultry products, making it a viable alternative or supplement to antibiotics. Supplementing diets with 250 mg/kg berberine significantly increased the average daily feed intake (ADFI) and average daily gain (ADG) in yellow-feathered broilers (Zhu et al., 2021). In this study, supplementing the diet of Wenchang chickens with 0.2 g/kg berberine significantly increased the a* and b* values of the breast muscle, resulting in a more vivid red and yellow coloration of the breast meat. Adding coptis chinensis powder to pig diets improves the color of the longissimus dorsi muscle (Zhou et al., 2013). This improvement is primarily attributed to the antioxidant effects of phenolic compounds such as berberine, the main active ingredient in coptis chinensis. Berberine can activate the AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) pathway (García-Muñoz et al., 2024), thereby enhancing cellular energy homeostasis and upregulating endogenous antioxidant defense systems, including superoxide dismutase (SOD) and GSH, thus reducing the overall oxidative burden (Ming et al., 2022). By reducing ROS production, berberine limits the availability of oxidants that convert oxyhemoglobin to methemoglobin, thereby preserving the bright red color associated with fresh meat (Hui et al., 2020). This is consistent with the findings in this study, which showed a significant increase in serum GSH activity and a significant decrease in MDA concentration in the TS group of Wenchang chickens.

Oxidative stress is one of the most critical health issues affecting broiler production. Through a series of complex physiological and biochemical reactions, it exerts multifaceted negative impacts on the health, production performance, and meat quality of broilers (Mishra et al., 2019). Berberine enhances the activity and content of endogenous antioxidant enzymes by upregulating the expression levels of genes encoding SOD, GSH-Px, and CAT through activation of the Nrf2 signaling pathway and AMPK pathway (Cheng et al., 2024). Concurrently, berberine inhibits the activity of NADPH oxidase and xanthine oxidase by chelating metal ions such as iron and copper, thereby reducing the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen species (RNS) (Uddin et al., 2020). Supplementing berberine in the diet significantly increases the activity of SOD, GSH, and GSH-Px in serum and liver tissue of Wuding chickens, while simultaneously reducing MDA concentrations (Pan et al., 2025). Berberine has a good therapeutic effect on Salmonella enteritis in chickens, and it can increase the levels of serum IgM, IgG, and total antioxidant capacity (Yang et al., 2024). In this study, dietary berberine supplementation enhanced blood antioxidant capacity while simultaneously reducing protein levels of multiple cellular inflammatory factors in the liver, thereby promoting the health of Wenchang chickens.

The liver plays a crucial role in the body's energy metabolism, antioxidant, and immune response. Therefore, its transcriptome and proteome can provide essential information on key genes and proteins involved in these processes. This study found that supplementing diets with berberine significantly upregulates the mRNA expression levels of AvBD9 and AvBD10 genes in the livers of Wenchang chickens, suggesting that their innate immune defense functions are significantly activated or enhanced. AvBD9 and AvBD10 are key members of the avian β-defensin family, directly eliminating pathogens by disrupting the cell membrane structures of bacteria, fungi, and viruses (Ma et al., 2009). AvBD9 and AvBD10 also possess immunomodulatory functions, participating in the recruitment of immune cells to sites of infection or inflammation and regulating cytokine expression to balance inflammatory responses (Contreras et al., 2020). Quercetin can upregulate the expression of host defense peptide genes such as AvBD9 and AvBD10 in chicken HTC cells (Yang et al., 2022). Peptidoglycan from Lactobacillus rhamnosus MLGA can upregulate chicken AvBD9 expression without inducing an inflammatory response (Yang et al., 2021). Certain metabolites and signaling molecules can also influence AvBD expression. For instance, cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) and butyrate have been found to synergistically promote the expression of AvBD9 in chickens (Sunkara et al., 2014). Upregulation of endogenous antimicrobial peptides such as AvBDs may enhance poultry immunity, thereby improving resistance to pathogens.

Liver proteomics further demonstrates that dietary berberine supplementation modulates the immunity of Wenchang chickens. This study identified multiple DAPs associated with innate immune responses and apoptosis regulation, such as MAVS, TRAF3, and BID. The MAVS-mediated RIG-I-like receptor (RLR) signaling pathway is a key mechanism by which poultry perceive cytoplasmic viral RNA and initiate rapid antiviral responses (Yi et al., 2025). Its activation strongly induces the production of type I interferons and proinflammatory cytokines, effectively suppressing the replication of various avian viruses. Pigeon MAVS (piMAVS) exhibits upregulated expression following viral infection, and its overexpression significantly suppresses replication of vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV) and Sendai virus (SeV) (Shao et al., 2024). MAVS (duMAVS) expression significantly increased in Cherry Valley ducks following infection with duck plague virus (Li et al., 2016). The duck TRAF3 gene is widely expressed in immune organs and shows significantly increased expression levels following viral infection (Cai et al., 2023). Knockout of the TRAF3 gene in MDCK cells increased their susceptibility to influenza A virus, indicating that TRAF3 plays a protective role in antiviral defense (Le et al., 2023). Supplementing diets with β-hydroxybutyrate significantly reduced mRNA and protein levels of IL-18 in chicken intestinal tissues, thereby decreasing inflammatory responses and promoting broiler health (Horiuchi et al., 2024). Similar effects were observed when Lactiplantibacillus plantarum was added to broiler diets (Yuan et al., 2023). In this study, dietary berberine supplementation enhanced blood antioxidant capacity while simultaneously reducing protein levels of multiple cellular inflammatory factors in the liver, thereby promoting the health of Wenchang chickens.

The phosphorylation and dephosphorylation modifications of key kinases, transcription factors, and functional proteins in immune-related pathways can directly regulate the activation, differentiation, and migration of immune cells, exerting significant regulatory effects on the health condition of poultry. Berberine specifically promotes the phosphorylation and activation of MAPK14, thereby enhancing the expression of antimicrobial peptides and pro-inflammatory factors, which in turn improves chickens’ resistance to Salmonella (Xiao et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025). This study found that the addition of berberine to the diet significantly upregulated the phosphorylation level of MAPK14, while simultaneously significantly increasing the expression levels of endogenous antimicrobial peptides such as AvBD9, AvBD10, and LEAP2. Berberine can significantly downregulate the phosphorylation level of MAP2K1, inhibit the activity of the EPK pathway, reduce the sustained release of pro-inflammatory factors, and alleviate oxidative stress damage (Kumar et al., 2020). This study found that berberine significantly downregulated the phosphorylation level of MAP2K1, while serum MDA concentration decreased significantly, GSH activity increased significantly, and overall serum antioxidant capacity was enhanced.

Viral infection or intracellular double-stranded RNA can directly induce phosphorylation modification of the IRF7 protein by activating kinases such as IKKε (Marié et al., 2000). This phosphorylation modification activates the IRF7 protein, enabling its translocation from the cytoplasm to the nucleus, where it functions as a transcription factor to efficiently drive the expression of type I interferons (IFN-α/β) and hundreds of downstream interferon-stimulated genes (Honda et al., 2005). In this study, dietary supplementation with berberine significantly upregulated the phosphorylation level of the NRF2 protein in the liver of Wenchang chickens. Concurrently, a significant increase in the abundance of the downstream NFKBIE protein was also observed. Phosphorylation can activate the MAVS protein and facilitate its entry into the nucleus, where it interacts with key antioxidant transcription factors such as NRF2. This interaction subsequently activates the expression of downstream antioxidant enzyme genes, including superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase (Zhao et al., 2025). This study found that dietary supplementation with berberine not only upregulates the expression level of the MAVS protein but also enhances its phosphorylation level, collectively improving the health of Wenchang chickens.

Multi-omics analysis revealed that adding berberine to the diet significantly modulates multiple signaling pathways in Wenchang chicken livers associated with immune response, inflammation regulation, and pathogen defense. These include the herpes simplex virus type 1 infection pathway, NF-κB signaling pathway, influenza A, Salmonella infection pathways, mTOR signaling pathway, autophagy, Hedgehog signaling pathway, as well as interferon α/γ response and IL6-JAK-STAT3 signaling pathways. This suggests that berberine exerts its effects not through a single pathway but by synergistically regulating multiple pathways to enhance the overall immune function of Wenchang chickens. The enrichment of interferon α and interferon γ response pathways further indicates that berberine promotes innate immune activation in Wenchang chickens, enhancing their resistance to pathogens such as viruses and bacteria (Yang et al., 2026). Additionally, its regulation of pathways related to HSV-1, influenza, and Salmonella infections suggests berberine's potential role in boosting Wenchang chickens' defense against multiple pathogens. Maintaining immune homeostasis is a key factor in the body's defense against pathogenic infections and reduction of inflammatory damage, thereby safeguarding health and production performance (Nahrendorf et al., 2025). Regulation of pathways such as NF-κB, IL6-JAK-STAT3, mTOR signaling, and autophagy pathways indicates that berberine effectively modulates inflammatory responses and immune cell activation, mitigates excessive inflammatory damage, and strengthens immune homeostasis. In summary, berberine comprehensively enhances the immune function and health of Wenchang chickens by regulating immune inflammation, interferon response, cellular autophagy, and pathogen infection-related pathways. This provides multi-omics-level theoretical support for its application as an immune enhancer in the poultry industry.

Conclusion

Dietary berberine supplementation did not affect the growth performance and body measurements of Wenchang chickens, but significantly improved meat quality by altering breast muscle color and reducing shear force. Berberine also enhanced serum immunoglobulin levels and hepatic antioxidant capacity. Dietary berberine supplementation extensively regulated genes, proteins, and phosphorylation events mainly related to antioxidant, immune, and inflammatory response signaling pathways. These results indicate that berberine can serve as an effective feed additive to improve meat quality, immunity, and antioxidant status in broilers, providing a theoretical basis for its application in broiler production.

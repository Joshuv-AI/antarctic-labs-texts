Machine learning-based clinical models for prediction of urinary incontinence after robot-assisted laparoscopic radical prostatectomy
Authors: Xuejing Wang, Yuan Yuan, Ping Zhou, Bin Gu, Xuping Jiang, Yan Sha, Na Li, Aiping Gan, Qingli Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate risk factors for urinary incontinence (UI) after robot-assisted laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (RARP) using interpretable machine learning methods, establish and validate a predictive model.MethodsClinicopathological data of 464 localized prostate cancer patients undergoing RARP at our institution (June 2022-June 2024) were retrospectively analyzed. UI status was assessed at 24–48 h, 1, 3, and 6 months post-catheter removal, with early UI defined as requiring ≥ 1 pad/day at 3 months. Patients were randomly split 7:3 into training/validation sets, patients from three external centers were used as the test set. Between-group comparisons used t-tests, Mann-Whitney U tests, or chi-square tests. Multivariate logistic regression identified risk factors in the training set, followed by construction of logistic regression and five machine learning models. Model performance was evaluated via ROC curves. The optimal model was interpreted using SHAP (Shapley additive explanations). Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.ResultsEarly UI occurred in 54 (training set), 28 (validation set) patients and 19 (test set) patients, with no intergroup baseline differences (P > 0.05). Univariate analysis linked total PSA level, PI-RADS score, ISUP grade, IPSS score, underlying disease, length of membranous urethra, prostate volume, nerve sparing procedure and urinary leakage preoperative total PSA, ISUP grade, dysuria, comorbidities, urinary tract infection (UTI), and postoperative urinary leakage to early UI (P < 0.05). Multivariate analysis confirmed total PSA level, nerve sparing procedure, IPSS score, length of membranous urethra, underlying disease, preoperative urinary tract infection, and urinary leakage as independent risk factors. Among models, support vector machine (SVM) achieved the highest validation AUC of 0.882 in the validation set and AUC of 0.858 in the test set. SHAP analysis revealed PSA level as the strongest predictive feature.ConclusionTotal PSA level, nerve sparing procedure, IPSS score, length of membranous urethra, underlying disease, preoperative urinary tract infection, and urinary leakage are independent risk factors for early UI post-RARP. The SVM-based machine learning model demonstrates superior predictive performance and clinical utility.Keywords: Robot-assisted laparoscopic radical prostatectomy, Machine learning, Prostate cancer, urinary incontinence, Nursing intervention

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is among the most common genitourinary malignancies, with a steadily increasing incidence worldwide [1]. Laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP) is a standard surgical procedure for patients with localized PCa. However, the incidence of urinary incontinence (UI) after LRP remains relatively high, which seriously affects patients’ quality of life [2]. In recent years, robot-assisted laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (RARP) has shown certain advantages in improving postoperative urinary continence; nevertheless, a proportion of patients still experience UI after surgery [3–5]. Previous studies have shown that early identification of postoperative UI and strengthened nursing intervention contribute to improvement in urinary function and long-term quality of life [6].

Artificial intelligence (AI) represents emerging technologies that are increasingly applied to predictive modeling across diverse disciplines, among which healthcare is a prominent field [7]. As a subset of AI, machine learning enables systems to automatically learn patterns from data via advanced algorithms and execute designated tasks without explicit programming [8]. Owing to these inherent advantages, machine learning is highly suitable for serving as a reliable predictive tool [9, 10]. In two previous studies exploring their application in predicting postoperative UI in prostate cancer patients, one incorporated few easily accessible clinical features in nursing practice, such as previous abdominal surgery history, comorbidities, and time to postoperative ambulation [11]. The other defined 12-month postoperative UI as the primary endpoint, which often leads to patients missing the optimal opportunity for clinical intervention [12].

Based on these, the present study retrospectively collected and analyzed the clinicopathological data of 464 patients who were admitted to four medical centers in China, and underwent RARP between June 2022 and June 2024. The aim was to explore the risk factors for early postoperative UI, develop a machine learning- based prediction model, and analyze strategies for early nursing intervention.

Methods and materials

Patients

This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board, which waived the requirement for written informed consent. This retrospective study included the clinicopathological data of 380 patients with localized PCa who underwent RARP at the Department of Urology, the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University and 84 patients from other external centers, between June 2022 and June 2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) PCa confirmed by prostate biopsy; (2) clinical stage of localized PCa; (3) treatment with RARP; and (4) complete clinicopathological and follow-up data. The exclusion criterion was as follows: (1) preoperative radiotherapy or endocrine therapy. The ratio of the training set to the validation set was 7:3. Patients from three other centers cooperatively formed an test set.

All procedures performed in this study were in accordance with the requirements of the Declaration of Helsinki of the World Medical Association, as revised in 2013.

Overview of data source

Postoperative urinary continence status after RARP was statistically analyzed. Follow-up assessments of urinary continence were conducted at 24–48 h after catheter removal and at 1, 3, and 6 months after surgery. Early UI was defined as the use of ≥ 1 pad per day at 3 months postoperatively [13, 14]. Pad usage was assessed using a standardized 7-day patient-reported bladder diary, in which patients recorded daily the number of pads used specifically for urine leakage, and the mean daily pad count over 7 days was calculated to assign continence category. The following variables were collected: age, body mass index, disease duration, preoperative serum total prostate-specific antigen (PSA) level, preoperative Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System (PI-RADS) score, biopsy pathological grade according to the International Society of Urological Pathology (ISUP), preoperative dysuria, preoperative nocturia, history of previous abdominal surgery, history of previous prostate surgery, comorbidity history, smoking history, alcohol consumption history, family history of malignancy, preoperative fasting time, preoperative urinary tract infection, intraoperative lymph node dissection, time to ambulation after surgery, membranous urethral length (MUL), preoperative International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS), nerve sparing procedure, Prostate volume, and postoperative urinary leakage. A UTI was defined as a positive culture with urinary symptoms within one month before surgery. All confirmed UTIs were treated, and surgery was performed only after documentation of sterile urine. Urinary leakage was defined as the drain higher than 200 ml/24 h and the creatinine level in drain fluid exceeds 500 umol/l [15].

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0. Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, whereas non-normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median and interquartile range M(P25, P75). The patients were randomly divided into a training set and a validation set at a ratio of 7:3. Continuous variables between the two groups were compared using the t-test or Mann–Whitney U test, and categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. Age was divided into ≤ 75 years and > 75 years; prostate-specific antigen (PSA) level was categorized into < 4 ng/mL, 4–10 ng/mL, 10–20 ng/mL and > 20 ng/mL, which is the conventional risk stratification for prostate cancer in clinical practice [16]; International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) was divided into < 7 points (mild symptoms), 8–19 points (moderate symptoms) and > 20 points (severe symptoms) [17]; prostate volume was classified into < 30 ml, 30-59.9 ml, 60-89.9 ml and ≥ 90 ml. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed in the training set to identify risk factors for early UI. The selected variables were then incorporated into six machine learning methods: k-nearest neighbor (KNN), Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), multilayer perceptron (MLP), random forest (RF), support vector machine (SVM) and Xgboost as well as logistic regression, to construct a total of seven predictive models. Grid search combined with 5-fold stratified cross-validation was used to optimize the key parameters of each machine learning model. All models were trained on the training set, the internal validation set was used for parameter adjustment and preliminary performance evaluation, and the final model performance was evaluated on the independent external test set that had never been involved in model training or parameter tuning. 5-fold stratified cross-validation was implemented during the training process to further reduce the risk of overfitting and ensure the stability of the model. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA) were used to evaluate model discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) were applied to quantify feature importance and interpret feature contributions. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

A total of 464 patients with localized PCa were included in this study. Among these, in the training set (n = 267), 54 cases (20.22%) developed UI; 28 cases (24.78%) and 19 cases (22.62%) developed UI in the validation set (n = 113) and test set (n = 84), respectively. Multivariate analysis confirmed total PSA level, nerve sparing procedure, IPSS score, length of membranous urethra, underlying disease, preoperative urinary tract infection, and urinary leakage as independent risk factors (P < 0.05) ( Table 2).

Construction and validation of predictive models

ROC curve analysis showed that, in the validation set, the SVM model demonstrated the best predictive performance for early UI, with an AUC of 0.882 (95% CI: 0.811–0.952). This was followed by the logistic regression model (AUC = 0.877, 95% CI: 0.798–0.956), the MLP model (AUC = 0.849, 95% CI: 0.767–0.932), the LightGBM model (AUC = 0.826, 95% CI: 0.733–0.919), the KNN model (AUC = 0.816, 95% CI: 0.763–0.932), the XGBoost model (AUC = 0.799, 95% CI: 0.702–0.895), and the RF model (AUC = 0.793, 95% CI: 0.700–0.887) (Fig. 1). In the test set, the SVM model still achieved the highest AUC of 0.858 (95% CI: 0.748–0.968). Table 3 summarizes the predictive performance of the seven models in the training, internal validation and external test sets(Fig. 1). The SVM model achieved the highest specificity (0.909), accuracy (0.738), precision (0.981), and F1 score (0.825) in the test set (Table 3). The calibration curve (Fig. 2) further showed that the SVM model had good predictive accuracy, with Brier scores of 0.083 (training set), 0.117 (validation set) and 0.090 (test set). The DCA (Fig. 3) showed that the SVM model had a high net clinical benefit, with maximum net benefits of 0.202 in the training set, 0.246 in the internal validation set, and 0.131 in the external test set.

Discussion

UI is a common complication after radical prostatectomy and can markedly impair patients’ quality of life. Although RARP incorporates several technical improvements over LRP, the incidence of postoperative UI remains relatively high [18]. Previous studies have reported that the probability of recovery from UI at 6–12 months after PCa surgery is significantly reduced with conservative treatment alone [19]. Therefore, early identification and intensified intervention are particularly important. Such interventions are mainly nursing-based, including strengthened pelvic floor muscle training, dietary guidance, and health education [20].

Although multiple risk factors associated with postoperative UI have been reported, some studies included magnetic resonance imaging-based variables, such as the membranous urethral angle, urethral wall thickness, and apical shape of the prostate, which limits their applicability in routine nursing practice [13, 21]. In the present study, we included several clinical characteristics that are easily obtainable in clinical practice. The assessment of urinary incontinence itself remains a topic of ongoing debate, with significant variability in definitions and measurement methods across studies. A recent matched-pair analysis by d’Altilia et al. [22]. demonstrated that objective assessment using the 24-hour pad weight test (defining continence as < 20 g of leakage) provides more precise quantification of incontinence severity compared to subjective pad count alone, and that definitions based solely on pad usage may underestimate the true burden of symptomatic incontinence [22]. Notably, pad count does not directly reflect leakage volume due to variable pad absorbency and usage patterns, and some patients may use pads prophylactically rather than for actual leakage, leading to potential misclassification. The 24-hour pad weight test offers a more objective alternative, and d’Altilia et al. [22]. further demonstrated that surgical approach significantly impacts continence recovery trajectories when assessed by this objective measure. In the present study, we adopted the ≥ 1 pad/day definition of early UI, which has been widely used in large-scale retrospective studies due to its simplicity and ease of implementation in routine clinical follow-up. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that this approach may underestimate the real burden of postoperative incontinence, and future studies incorporating objective pad weight testing would provide more accurate assessment. After univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses, seven independent risk factors for early UI after RARP were identified. Based on these variables, seven prediction models were developed using six machine learning algorithms and logistic regression. Among them, SVM model demonstrated the best predictive performance, which is consistent with the growing success of machine learning techniques in medical prediction.

Logistic regression is widely used in clinical research because of its simplicity, interpretability, and ease of application. It generally performs well when handling linear data. However, when nonlinear relationships and complex interactions exist among variables, its predictive performance may be suboptimal and its accuracy limited [23]. In recent years, artificial intelligence has been increasingly applied in the medical field, and a growing number of novel machine learning algorithms have emerged. Numerous studies have confirmed their applicability in medical prediction [24]. In the present study, to ensure that the included variables had clear associations with early postoperative UI, we first used logistic regression to identify seven independent risk factors. We then constructed five commonly used machine learning models and compared them with the logistic regression model. The results showed that the SVM model achieved the best predictive performance, with the highest AUC values in both the validation and test sets, reaching 0.882 and 0.858, respectively, both of which were superior to those of the other models. In addition, the calibration curves and decision curve analysis further confirmed its good clinical applicability.

The SVM model is a machine learning method particularly suitable for handling high-dimensional data, and it can effectively capture complex nonlinear relationships through kernel functions [25]. In this study, strong correlations and mutual influences existed among the seven modeling variables. The SVM model can identify the optimal classification hyperplane in a high-dimensional space, thereby improving the recognition of positive samples. This advantage may be a key reason why the SVM model achieved the best predictive performance in this study. The SVM model also demonstrated good calibration, with Brier scores of 0.083, 0.117, and 0.090 in the training, validation, and test sets, respectively, indicating that predicted probabilities closely match observed frequencies and support reliable clinical decision-making. Because machine learning models are often limited by the “black box” problem [26] and cannot intuitively quantify the contribution of each feature to the prediction outcome, we introduced SHAP values to explain the contribution of each feature to the model. The SHAP analysis not only identified PSA level as the strongest predictor, followed by nerve-sparing status, IPSS score, MUL, underlying diseases, preoperative UTI, and urinary leakage, but also revealed the direction and magnitude of each feature’s impact, which is essential for building clinical trust in AI-based tools. This interpretable output substantially enhances confidence in the model’s predictions in clinical practice. For clinical implementation, all seven input variables are routinely available in clinical practice, allowing seamless integration into existing workflows. The model could be implemented as a simple decision-support tool for preoperative risk stratification, postoperative follow-up planning, and shared decision-making, helping identify high-risk patients who may benefit from intensified rehabilitation and closer monitoring.

Patients’ perioperative baseline characteristics are often associated with the occurrence of postoperative complications. In this study, the presence of underlying diseases, preoperative dysuria, preoperative IPSS score, length of membranous urethra, higher PSA levels, preoperative urinary tract infection, and postoperative urine leakage were identified as independent risk factors for early UI after RARP. Previous studies have reported that pre-existing comorbidities may lead to a certain degree of functional decline in organs and systems, resulting in insufficient physiological reserve, poor tolerance to surgery, delayed tissue healing, and unsatisfactory postoperative recovery [27]. Benign prostatic hyperplasia is a common disease in elderly men, and its symptoms mainly manifest as lower urinary tract symptoms [28]. A larger prostate volume increases the difficulty of apical dissection and urethral separation during surgery, thereby raising the risk of urethral sphincter injury [29]. In contrast, a smaller prostate volume is more favorable for delicate intraoperative manipulation and early recovery of urinary continence. Although no direct correlation was observed between prostate volume and postoperative urinary incontinence in this study, urinary incontinence was closely associated with IPSS score and preoperative dysuria, typical symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia. In this study, patients with preoperative dysuria were more likely to develop early postoperative UI, which may be related to a larger prostate volume. PSA is closely associated with the prognosis and aggressiveness of PCa. Higher PSA levels often indicate greater tumor invasiveness, which may affect the tumor microenvironment and surrounding tissues. In addition, elevated PSA levels often suggest a larger tumor burden, and to ensure complete tumor resection, the surgeon may need to perform a wider excision of surrounding structures, especially the external urethral sphincter, which may increase the incidence of UI and delay continence recovery. Furthermore, preoperative urinary tract infection and postoperative urine leakage may increase the risk of infection at the vesicourethral anastomosis, promote urethral fibrosis, impair wound healing, and thereby increase the risk of postoperative UI [30]. Surgical techniques exert a substantial influence on postoperative urinary incontinence. This study verified that preoperative membranous urethral length and nerve-sparing status are key anatomical and operative factors. The predictive value of MUL is strongly supported by the systematic review and meta-analysis by Yu et al. [21], which confirmed that every additional millimeter of preoperative MUL is significantly associated with improved continence recovery odds across multiple postoperative time points, providing robust evidence for its role as a protective factor. Our finding that nerve-sparing status is the second most important predictive feature in the SVM model is strongly supported by the work of d’Altilia et al. [22], whose multivariable Cox regression analysis identified nerve sparing as an independent predictor of improved continence recovery (HR = 1.25, 95% CI: 1.02–1.54, p = 0.03) in a large matched cohort of patients undergoing either RARP or retropubic radical prostatectomy [22]. Sparing of the periprostatic neurovascular bundles (NVBs) preserves the functional neural integrity of the pathways innervating the urethral sphincter and pelvic floor structures, which is associated with a lower risk of postoperative urinary incontinence [31]. The findings in our study are also consistent with the study by Paolo et al. [32], which systematically demonstrated that patient-related, anatomical, and surgical factors all contribute to continence outcomes, providing a comprehensive framework for understanding the multifactorial nature of post-prostatectomy incontinence. Postoperative urinary incontinence has a profound impact on patients’ health-related quality of life, extending beyond functional impairment to affect emotional well-being, social participation, and intimate relationships. Patient-reported outcome measures are increasingly recognized as essential for capturing the patient’s perspective on incontinence burden. As highlighted by d’Altilia et al. [22], objective measures such as the 24-hour pad weight test, when combined with validated patient-reported questionnaires, provide a more holistic picture of continence recovery and its impact on quality of life. Two patients with the same pad count may experience vastly different levels of bother depending on individual coping mechanisms and expectations. Future studies incorporating validated instruments such as the ICIQ-UI SF and EPIC would enable more comprehensive assessment of the subjective burden of incontinence and facilitate more patient-centered care.

This study has several important limitations. First, the retrospective design introduces inherent methodological limitations, including potential selection bias, information bias, and residual confounding, which may affect the accuracy and completeness of the findings. Second, the relatively limited sample size may restrict statistical power and the generalizability of the model. While our multicenter cohort of 464 patients is comparable to similar studies, larger samples would enable more robust model training and more precise effect size estimation. Third, the exclusive inclusion of Chinese patients limits the generalizability of findings to other ethnic groups and global populations, as genetic factors, lifestyle differences, and variations in surgical practice may influence postoperative UI outcomes. Fourth, the study lacks long-term functional outcomes and standardized patient-reported outcome measures. We only assessed early UI at 3 months postoperatively, which does not capture the full trajectory of continence recovery, and the reliance on pad count alone may not fully reflect symptom severity or its impact on patients’ quality of life. Fifth, several potential predictive factors were not incorporated, including detailed surgical parameters and patient compliance with postoperative rehabilitation. Sixth, the model has not undergone prospective validation in real clinical practice, and all participants received surgery via the extraperitoneal multi-port approach, which may limit generalizability to other surgical approaches. ccordingly, large-scale multicenter prospective investigations incorporating long-term follow-up and standardized patient-reported outcomes will be carried out to fully verify the generalizability and clinical value of the established SVM model.

Overall, the SVM model established in this study demonstrated the best predictive performance and good clinical utility for predicting early UI after RARP.

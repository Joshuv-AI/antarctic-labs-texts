Cross-Platform Retrieval of a Chronically Implanted Leadless Pacemaker Using a Dedicated Retrieval System
Authors: Surachat Jaroonpipatkul, Apichai Pokawattana
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Leadless pacemakers are increasingly used as an alternative to transvenous systems, particularly in patients in whom transvalvular leads are undesirable. The Micra Transcatheter Pacing System is widely adopted and has demonstrated favorable clinical outcomes.1

As the number of implanted devices grows, management at battery depletion has become an emerging challenge. Current strategies include device abandonment with additional implantation or retrieval before re-implantation. However, experience with retrieval of chronically implanted devices remains limited. The availability of newer systems such as the Aveir VR leadless pacemaker, which incorporates a dedicated retrieval mechanism, introduces new possibilities for long-term device management.2

We report a case of successful retrieval of a chronically implanted Micra device using a trilobed snare system during implantation of an Aveir leadless pacemaker, illustrating a potential cross-platform management strategy.

History of Presentation

A 74-year-old woman was followed in the pacing clinic after prior implantation of a leadless pacemaker. At routine follow-up, device interrogation demonstrated battery depletion with an estimated remaining longevity of less than 1 month. The patient remained clinically stable and asymptomatic. Electrocardiography revealed a ventricular paced rhythm. Given the need for continued ventricular pacing and the intention to maintain a leadless pacing strategy while facilitating future device management, implantation of an Aveir VR leadless pacemaker (Abbott) was planned.

Past Medical History

The patient had a history of severe tricuspid regurgitation status post bioprosthetic tricuspid valve replacement, atrial fibrillation, and complete atrioventricular block. Following tricuspid valve surgery, she developed complete heart block and initially underwent epicardial pacemaker implantation. Given concerns regarding long-term lead performance and the desire to avoid transvalvular lead interaction with the bioprosthetic tricuspid valve, a leadless pacing strategy was subsequently adopted. She underwent implantation of a Micra Transcatheter Pacing System (Medtronic) in June 2019, which provided stable pacing support.

Differential Diagnosis

The primary clinical issue was device battery depletion in a pacing-dependent patient requiring continued ventricular pacing. Management options included abandonment of the previously implanted Micra device with implantation of an additional leadless pacemaker, retrieval of the existing leadless pacemaker followed by re-implantation, or consideration of alternative pacing strategies. Given the patient's prior bioprosthetic tricuspid valve replacement and the desire to avoid transvalvular lead placement, continuation of a leadless pacing strategy was favored. Retrieval of the chronically implanted device was considered to minimize intracardiac device burden and reduce the potential for device–device interaction.

Investigations

Device interrogation confirmed battery depletion of the previously implanted Micra device, with an estimated remaining longevity of less than 1 month. Electrocardiography demonstrated a ventricular paced rhythm. No dedicated preprocedural cross-sectional imaging was performed specifically to assess fibrotic encapsulation of the previously implanted Micra device. Procedural planning was based on the patient's clinical history, device interrogation findings, prior cardiac evaluation, and fluoroscopic assessment of device position, with anatomical relationships further evaluated intraprocedurally.

Management

The procedure was performed via right femoral venous access under local anesthesia. A delivery sheath was advanced into the right atrium, and the device delivery system was prepared according to standard technique. The Aveir VR leadless pacemaker was advanced into the right ventricle, and multiple positioning attempts were required to achieve optimal placement. After 3 deployment attempts, a suitable location was identified in the right ventricular apex with stable fixation and acceptable pacing parameters, and the device was successfully implanted (Figure 1). The previously implanted Micra device was located in the mid-right ventricular septum; therefore, the new device was positioned slightly more apically to avoid interference.

Positioning of the Aveir VR Leadless Pacemaker

Fluoroscopic image demonstrating attempted positioning of the Aveir VR leadless pacemaker in the right ventricle in relation to the previously implanted Micra Transcatheter Pacing System. The Micra device is located in the mid-right ventricular septum, and the new device is positioned more apically to avoid device interaction. Sternal wires from prior cardiac surgery are visible. The prosthetic tricuspid valve ring is also seen, highlighting the anatomical constraint and rationale for a leadless pacing strategy.

Following successful implantation, attention was turned to retrieval of the previously implanted Micra device. The retrieval setup consisted of femoral venous access, a steerable retrieval sheath, and a trilobed snare system advanced into the right ventricle under fluoroscopic guidance. The retrieval sheath was positioned to provide support and allow controlled manipulation of the snare toward the previously implanted device. The trilobed snare was opened adjacent to the proximal retrieval feature of the Micra device and carefully oriented to achieve the most coaxial alignment possible. Engagement proved technically challenging due to difficulty achieving optimal coaxial alignment with the retrieval feature, and multiple attempts, approximately 5 to 6, were required before successful capture was achieved (Figure 2).

Fluoroscopic Images Demonstrating Engagement of the Previously Implanted Micra Transcatheter Pacing System Using a Trilobed Snare System

(A) The trilobed snare is advanced toward the proximal retrieval feature of the Micra device, with multiple attempts required to achieve optimal coaxial alignment for capture. (B) Successful engagement of the Micra device by the trilobed snare, with secure capture of the retrieval feature before recapture.

Once the retrieval feature was engaged, stable capture was confirmed fluoroscopically, and the recapture cone was advanced completely over the Micra device. Minimal resistance was encountered, suggesting limited fibrotic encapsulation despite the prolonged implantation duration. Gentle traction with slight rotational adjustments was applied to facilitate disengagement of the fixation tines, and the device was successfully withdrawn into the sheath and removed without transient arrhythmia, pacing instability, hemodynamic instability, or other complication (Figure 3).

Retrieval of the Chronically Implanted Micra Device

Fluoroscopic image demonstrating successful retrieval of the previously implanted Micra Transcatheter Pacing System, with complete removal of the device and preservation of a single leadless system following implantation of the Aveir VR leadless pacemaker.

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient remained hemodynamically stable throughout the procedure. The newly implanted Aveir VR leadless pacemaker demonstrated satisfactory pacing parameters. At follow-up, the patient remained clinically well with stable device function and no procedural complications.

Discussion

The use of leadless pacemakers has expanded significantly over the past decade, particularly in patients with limited vascular access or conditions in which transvenous lead placement is undesirable.1 As the population of patients with leadless pacemakers continues to grow, management at device end-of-life has emerged as a clinically relevant challenge. Unlike conventional transvenous systems, in which generator replacement is straightforward, leadless pacemakers introduce unique considerations regarding whether to abandon the existing device or attempt retrieval. Device abandonment with implantation of an additional leadless pacemaker is often technically simpler and has been commonly adopted in practice. However, concerns remain regarding cumulative device burden within the right ventricle, potential device–device interaction, and the unknown long-term effects of multiple intracardiac implants. Retrieval may reduce intracardiac device burden, preserve right ventricular space, avoid potential device–device interaction, and maintain flexibility for future pacing strategies. However, retrieval also carries procedural risks, including myocardial injury, perforation, arrhythmia, vascular complications, and technical failure, particularly when chronic endothelialization or fibrotic encapsulation is present. Therefore, the decision between retrieval and abandonment should be individualized according to patient anatomy, device position, pacing dependence, operator experience, and the anticipated risk-benefit balance.

Retrieval of leadless pacemakers offers a potential solution to these limitations; however, current experience remains limited, particularly for chronically implanted devices.2 Early retrieval of leadless systems has been reported with relatively high success rates, largely due to minimal fibrotic encapsulation shortly after implantation.3 In contrast, long-term implantation may be associated with progressive endothelialization and fibrotic tissue formation, potentially increasing procedural difficulty and risk. As a result, many operators remain cautious regarding extraction of devices implanted for extended durations.

In the present case, successful retrieval of a chronically implanted Micra Transcatheter Pacing System was achieved approximately 6 years after initial implantation. Despite the prolonged dwell time, engagement and recapture of the device were feasible without significant resistance, suggesting that extensive fibrotic encapsulation may not uniformly occur or may remain manageable in selected cases. Notably, although engagement of the device required multiple attempts due to challenges in achieving coaxial alignment, once captured, the device was withdrawn smoothly without complication. This observation highlights the importance of technical factors such as alignment and operator experience in determining procedural success.

An additional and important aspect of this case is the demonstration of cross-platform leadless device management. The retrieval of a Micra device using a trilobed snare system in conjunction with implantation of an Aveir VR leadless pacemaker illustrates the potential flexibility of combining different leadless technologies within a single procedural strategy. The Aveir system, designed with a dedicated retrieval mechanism, may offer advantages in long-term management; however, its use in facilitating removal of a device from another platform has not been well described. This case suggests that cross-platform retrieval may be feasible and could represent an evolving strategy as multiple leadless pacing systems continue to coexist in clinical practice.

From a procedural perspective, this case underscores several important technical considerations. First, achieving coaxial alignment between the retrieval system and the device retrieval feature is critical and may require multiple attempts, particularly in chronically implanted devices. Second, the absence of significant resistance during recapture may indicate limited fibrotic encapsulation, although this cannot be assumed universally. Finally, gentle traction combined with controlled rotational movements can facilitate safe disengagement of fixation mechanisms and minimize the risk of myocardial injury.

The clinical implications of this case extend beyond a single procedural success. As the number of patients with leadless pacemakers increases, strategies for long-term device management will become increasingly important. Cross-platform retrieval may provide a means to reduce device burden, preserve right ventricular anatomy, and allow for more flexible device selection over time. Recent reports have demonstrated the feasibility of extracting a Micra device using an Aveir retrieval system in early clinical experience.4 However, data on chronically implanted devices and complex anatomical settings remain limited. Importantly, prior reports have described retrieval at shorter implantation durations (approximately 30 months), whereas the present case demonstrates feasibility after a substantially longer dwell time of approximately 6 years.

Conclusions

This case demonstrates the feasibility of retrieving a chronically implanted leadless pacemaker after prolonged dwell time using a trilobed snare system during implantation of a new device. Cross-platform retrieval may represent a viable strategy for long-term management of leadless pacing systems, particularly in patients in whom minimizing device burden and preserving right ventricular anatomy are important considerations.

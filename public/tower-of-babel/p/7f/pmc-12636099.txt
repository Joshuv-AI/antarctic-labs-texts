Request for retraction: Zhou et al. J Gynecol Oncol 2025;36:e63
Authors: Darin Dobler, Anju Parthan
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We are reaching out to formally request the retraction of the Zhou et al. [1] publication titled “Relacorilant plus nab-paclitaxel for recurrent, platinum-resistant ovarian cancer: a cost-effectiveness study,” which recently appeared in the Journal of Gynecologic Oncology, due to significant methodological concerns that compromise the validity of its conclusions.

A rigorous cost-effectiveness analysis requires that the cost inputs for all treatment strategies reflect actual market prices. However, the conclusions drawn by the authors are based on an assumed price for relacorilant, a drug that is not currently commercially available and lacks an established cost. The authors do not provide a rationale for their pricing assumptions, rendering the study’s conclusions unreliable for informing healthcare decision-making.

The cost and utility estimates presented by Zhou et al. [1] are wrong, leading to flawed conclusions. Below, we outline several inaccuracies and methodological flaws that, in our view, necessitate retraction:

1. Invalid cost estimates:a) The cost of relacorilant is erroneously reported as $1,471.33 per 100 mg, with reference to Lynparza (www.drugs.com/price-guide/lynpraza). However, relacorilant is not (and has never been) commercially available; no published price exists. The authors fail to justify their assumptions. Lynparza does not have a labeled indication for platinum-resistant ovarian cancer and is not an appropriate reference drug.b) The cost of nab-paclitaxel is misreported as $43.53 per 100 mg, citing the same source (Lynparza). The 2023 market price for nab-paclitaxel 100 mg vial ranged from $1,441.01 to $1,580.06, depending on the manufacturer [2].c) The cost of adverse events does not match the original publications cited in Table 2; raising concerns about data integrity. One of the references cited also appears to be spurious (www.yaozi.com).

3. Misinterpretation of cost-effectiveness results: The statement in the results section “Compared to N, IN has a certain price advantage, but IN will generate an incremental 0.84 QALYs” is inaccurate. According to Table 3, intermittent relacorilant + nab-paclitaxel (IN) has a higher cost ($22,597) than nab-paclitaxel monotherapy (N) ($4,606), which contradicts the claim by the authors of a cost advantage.

Misinterpretation of cost-effectiveness results: The statement in the results section “Compared to N, IN has a certain price advantage, but IN will generate an incremental 0.84 QALYs” is inaccurate. According to Table 3, intermittent relacorilant + nab-paclitaxel (IN) has a higher cost ($22,597) than nab-paclitaxel monotherapy (N) ($4,606), which contradicts the claim by the authors of a cost advantage.

4. Flawed incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) calculation: The methodology used to compute ICERs in Table 3 is inconsistent with standard economic evaluation guidelines. Cost-effectiveness analyses require treatment strategies to be rank ordered by increasing cost, with ICERs calculated sequentially. Additionally, dominated strategies that are both more expensive and less effective should be excluded. In this study, continuous relacorilant + nab-paclitaxel (CN) is a dominated strategy (higher cost, lower quality-adjusted life years [QALYs]). Therefore, an ICER should not be calculated.

5. Inconsistency in reporting: In Fig. 1, the footnote states that the vertical axis shows the cumulative cost of life and the horizontal axis shows the QALYs gained. However, the labels on the x-axis and y-axis of the graphs are inconsistent with the above description.

6. Incorrect interpretation of societal perspective: The authors conclude that IN is more cost-effective than CN and N from a U.S. societal perspective. However, their analysis only considers direct medical costs, omitting essential components such as productivity loss and caregiver burden. A true societal perspective must incorporate these elements.

These issues go beyond ordinary scientific debate and undermine the entirety of the research presented. The study’s conclusions are unreliable for informed healthcare decision-making.

Currently, Corcept Therapeutics Incorporated is investigating the efficacy and safety of intermittent relacorilant with nab-paclitaxel in a Phase III clinical trial for the treatment of recurrent, platinum-resistant ovarian cancer [6]. Given the investigational status of relacorilant, any cost-related assumptions in economic analyses must be rigorously validated to ensure accuracy and reliability.

Our findings raise serious concerns about the validity of the authors’ conclusions. The inaccuracies, absence of credible sources for model inputs, and flawed cost-effectiveness analyses compromise the reliability of the study and risk misleading the clinical, payer, and scientific communities.

Therefore, we strongly urge the retraction of this publication.

Thank you for your consideration.

Laparoscopic management of multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas with 5-year follow-up: a case report
Authors: Mengchao Wei, Houfeng Huang, Yu Xiao, Xinrong Fan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGanglioneuromas are the rarest and most benign of the neuroblastic tumors. Although generally benign, multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas have been documented. However, only one case in previous studies underwent complete resection of all retroperitoneal lesions while the follow-up time was relatively short.Case presentationHere, we presented two female patients in their twenties who were diagnosed as multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas by percutaneous biopsy, with tumor involvement in adrenal, retroperitoneal and pelvic regions. Complete excision of all detectable lesions was performed by laparoscopic dissection. Both patients had a good postoperative recovery and remained recurrence-free at the 5-year follow-up.ConclusionsTo our knowledge, the present study firstly reported cases of multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas with a 5-year follow-up, indicating the safety and efficacy of laparoscopic dissection of multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12894-025-01952-y.Keywords: Laparoscopy, Multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas, Follow-up, Case report

Background

Ganglioneuromas are slowly-growing benign neoplasms that originate from the neural crest. These tumors develop most commonly in the posterior mediastinum and retroperitoneum. Adrenal glands are most frequently involved by ganglioneuromas among retroperitoneal organs and tissues [1]. The incidence rate of ganglioneuromas is as low as 1 per million, indicating their rarity [2]. The diagnosis of ganglioneuromas is typically indicated by incidental imaging examinations given ganglioneuromas are usually asymptomatic [1]. The prognosis of ganglioneuromas is supposed to be optimistic following complete surgical resection [3–5]. Ganglioneuromas with multiple lesions in several organs are quite unusual. Several case reports have provided some individual experience on the diagnosis and treatment of multiple ganglioneuromas [6–8]. However, only one case among these studies underwent complete resection of all retroperitoneal lesions while the follow-up time was relatively short [8].

Here, we present two cases with multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas. Laparoscopic dissection of multiple lesions was performed and a long-term postoperative follow-up was carried out.

Case presentation

Case 1

A 25-year-old female was admitted to our hospital in December, 2019. She complained of recurring left flank pain for two years. The pain was intermittent, lasting for several hours each time, with an interval of about half an hour. The pain could be relieved by painkillers. Symptoms such as headache, palpitation, profuse sweating, fatigue, and redness of the face were not observed. Her family history was not contributory. Physical examination revealed no abnormalities. Tumor markers including carbohydrate antigen 19 − 9 (CA 19 − 9), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and alpha fetal protein (AFP) were within normal limits. The elevation of urine catecholamine was not observed. Enhanced computed tomography (CT) plus three-dimensional reconstruction showed multiple slightly low-density nodules and mass in the left adrenal area, retroperitoneal area and pelvic cavity (Fig. 1). The lesions were partially fused and lobulated, with the largest one located in the left adrenal area, measuring approximately 15.6 × 11.5 × 9.9 cm. The lesions contained multiple spot-like calcifications and showed scattered flocculent enhancement in the enhanced scan. In the portal phase and delayed phase, mild to moderate delayed enhancement was observed. The CT value of the largest lesion in the plain, arterial and portal phase was 20HU, 69HU and 115HU, respectively. The largest lesion located in the left adrenal area was fed by the left renal artery branch and drained by the left renal vein branch. Some lesions were drained by the left reproductive vein. Spleen, left kidney, the tail of pancreas, left ureter and descending colon were pressed and displaced by adjacent lesions. There were no positive findings in adrenal medulla imaging and somatostatin receptor imaging. Besides, percutaneous CT-guided biopsy was performed on the largest lesion. Pathological test showed ganglioneuroma and further immunohistochemical test revealed AE1/AE3 (-), NSE (ganglion cell +), NF (+), Sym (ganglion cell +), CgA (+), S100 (+), SMA (-), Ki-67 (1%+).

The CT images of the left adrenal lesion (a), retroperitoneal lesion (b) and pelvic lesion (c) of case 1

On the basis of above findings, laparoscopic dissection of multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas was successfully performed. To prevent ureteral injury, a ureteral stent was placed into the left ureter before the operation. A total of 11 lesions were finally removed, with the largest lesion in the left adrenal gland closely adhering to adjacent pancreas and spleen (Fig. 2). The operating time was 630 min, with a blood loss of 700 ml and a transfusion volume of 909 ml (400 ml of allogeneic transfusion and 509 ml of autologous transfusion). The patient recovered smoothly without severe adverse events. The drainage tube in the spleen fossa was removed on postoperative day 10 and the tube in the pelvic cavity was removed on postoperative day 13. Postoperative pathology confirmed ganglioneuroma and further immunohistochemical test revealed SMA (-), Desmin (-), CD34 (+), S-100 (+), Ki-67 (1%+), AE1/AE3 (ganglion cell +), NF (ganglion cell +), NeuN (ganglion cell +). The histologic section stained by hematoxylin-eosin was shown in Fig. 3. The gross specimen was mostly enveloped with a smooth surface. The cut surface of the specimen was grayish yellow, solid and tough. The cut surface of the largest lesion was locally grayish brown and suspected of bleeding. Next generation sequencing was performed and no significant homologous recombination repair-related genetic variations, somatic variations and germline variations were found. The left flank pain faded away after surgery. No recurrence and metastasis were observed during a follow-up period of over 5 years.

Case 2

The second case was a 20-year-old female admitted to our hospital in December, 2019. Multiple retroperitoneal tumor lesions were found by CT scan in conventional health examination. The patient was asymptomatic. Her family history was not contributory. Physical examination revealed no abnormalities. The elevation of urine catecholamine was not observed. Enhanced CT plus CT angiography showed multiple round-shaped and slightly low-density masses in the right adrenal area and retroperitoneal area, with clear boundaries (Fig. 4). The CT value of the largest lesion in the plain, arterial and portal phase was 35HU, 42HU and 52HU, respectively. The larger ones were approximately 8.1 × 6.6 cm and 6.4 × 4.5 cm in size, with significantly delayed enhancement in enhanced scans. The peripheral duodenum and the head of the pancreas were pressed forward, and the right kidney was pressed downward, with clear boundaries. No obvious abnormalities were found in the abdominal arteries and veins. Besides, percutaneous CT-guided biopsy was performed on the largest lesion. Pathological test showed ganglioneuroma and further immunohistochemical test revealed Calretinin (-), AE1/AE3 (-), CD34 (sporadic +), Desmin (-), Ki-67 (1%+), HMB45 (-), Syn (+), S-100 (+), SOX10 (+).

The CT images of the right adrenal lesion (a) and retroperitoneal lesion (b) of case 2

On the basis of above findings, laparoscopic dissection of multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas was successfully performed. A total of 7 lesions were finally removed (Fig. 5). The tumor capsule was intact and the texture was tough. The largest lesion completely occupied the space of the right adrenal area, with a size of about 10.0 × 8.5 × 8.5 cm. The lesion closely adhered to the adrenal gland and it was difficult to separate it. Four lesions were found on the right posterior side of the inferior vena cava, with the size of the lesions being approximately 3.5 × 2.0 × 2.0 cm, 3.0 × 1.5 × 1.5 cm, 1.0 × 0.5 × 0.5 cm and 1.0 × 0.5 × 0.5 cm respectively. A dumbbell-shaped lesion was seen below the renal pedicle, passing through the dorsal side of the inferior vena cava, with the medial part measuring approximately 7.5 × 7.0 × 7.0 cm and the lateral part measuring approximately 7.0 × 4.5 × 4.5 cm. Another lesion, approximately 2.5 × 1.0 × 1.0 cm in size, was found between the medial part of the dumbbell-shaped lesion and the inferior vena cava. The operating time was 500 min, with a blood loss of 700 ml and an autologous transfusion volume of 250 ml. The patient recovered uneventfully. The drainage tube in the perirenal space was removed on postoperative day 12. Postoperative pathology confirmed ganglioneuroma and further immunohistochemical test revealed AE1/AE3 (lesion +), CgA (-), CD99 (+), Ki-67 (1%+), S-100 (+), Syn (+), EMA (-), CD56 (+), NF (+). The histologic section stained by hematoxylin-eosin was shown in Fig. 6. The gross specimen was mostly enveloped. The cut surface of the specimen was grayish white, dusty pink, solid and tough. No recurrence and metastasis were observed during a follow-up period of over 5 years.

Discussion and conclusions

Ganglioneuromas are a type of neuroblastic tumors which originates from neural crest. Ganglioneuromas are usually benign and well-differentiated, consisting of mature sympathetic ganglion cells, fibrous tissue and nerve fibers. Since ganglioneuromas do not contain immature elements, patients with this type of tumor usually have a favorable prognosis [3]. The mean age at diagnosis of ganglioneuromas usually falls around the third decade of life [9, 10], which is consistent with our cases. The first case of ganglioneuroma was reported by Loretz in 1870 [11]. Since then, only three studies reported the diagnosis and treatment of multiple ganglioneuromas [6–8]. Since ganglioneuromas arise from the sympathetic nervous tissue, posterior mediastinum and retroperitoneum are mostly involved [1]. Other involved sites include adrenal gland, pelvis, neck, regional lymph nodes and etc [12]. In our cases, lesions were located at adrenal glands, retroperitoneal and pelvic cavity, covering the most common sites of occurrence.

In terms of the role of imaging examinations in the diagnosis of ganglioneuromas, there have been no recognized discriminating characteristics of ganglioneuromas. Ultrasound typically shows a well-defined, homogeneous and hypoechoic mass [1]. On CT, ganglioneuromas appear as hypodense lesions with homogeneous texture and mild to moderate contrast enhancement [1]. Magnetic resonance imaging findings include low signal intensity on T1-weighted images and heterogeneous high signal intensity on T2-weighted images [5]. Besides, glanglioneuromas usually grow and spread along interstitial spaces. Thus, the lesions are commonly pressed by peripheral organs and tissues, displaying an irregular shape. However, none of these imaging modalities can reliably distinguish ganglioneuromas from other neuroblastic tumors or adrenal incidentalomas. Despite the limited role of imaging tools, imaging-guided percutaneous biopsy was a feasible and reliable diagnostic procedure in cases with biopsy-favorable lesions. Both of our cases were confirmatively diagnosed as ganglioneuromas preoperatively, although the expression of some immunohistochemical markers were different in the final postoperative pathology test.

Management of ganglioneuromas primarily involves complete surgical resection [13], though indications for surgery in adrenal lesions remain debated. According to the 2004 NIH State-of-the-Science statement [14], nonfunctional adrenal lesions >6 cm are typically excised, while those < 4 cm warrant surveillance. For lesions between 4 and 6 cm, either surgery or observation may be appropriate. In our cases, the patients had adrenal lesions >6 cm. Therefore, complete removal of lesions in the adrenal areas and all other visible lesions were performed. No severe adverse events occurred perioperatively and both patients were recurrence free during a follow-up period of over 5 years, indicating the safety and efficacy of laparoscopic dissection of multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas, as well as the favorable prognosis of ganglioneuromas after surgical resection. Compared to open operation, laparoscopy can provide a magnifying view of surgical field and avoid prolonged exposure of internal organs. Besides, the long surgical instruments of laparoscopic surgery can help reach lesions in deep corporal cavities, ensuring eradication of all possible lesions. Therefore, laparoscopic dissection is more advantageous over open operation for multiple retroperitoneal ganglioneuromas.

Multiple ganglioneuromas are rare and we recommend naming this type of ganglioneuromas uniformly as gangliomatosis, defined as ganglioneuromas with ≥ 3 lesions and involving ≥ 2 anatomical regions (including posterior mediastinum, retroperitoneum, adrenal glands, pelvis, neck, or regional lymph nodes). The next generation sequencing result of case 1 revealed no significant genetic variations, somatic variations and germline variations, indicating gangliomatosis is non-hereditary and non-metastatic. Therefore, the disease could be less malignant and complete removal of all possible lesions could deliver favorable prognosis.

In the present study, there are two aspects of novelty. First, we pioneeringly reported the 5-year follow-up results of multiple ganglioneuromas following laparoscopic dissection, enriching the prognostic data of this rare disease Second, based on the two cases, we put forward the definition of gangliomatosis, which has not been reported by other researchers. The establishment of the new definition could facilitate the standardization of diagnosis and treatment of this rare disease. Nevertheless, the interpretation of our study could be limited due to the small sample size. Large-scale studies are needed to provide more solid evidence and cast more light upon the landscape of multiple ganglioneuromas.

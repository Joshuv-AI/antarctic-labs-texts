High-intensity interval training enhanced cardioprotective effects of neuregulin-1 against doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity
Authors: Najmeh Rezaeinezhad, Parisa Jamali, Kosar Fatholahi, Alireza Ghardashi Afousi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDoxorubicin (DOX) is an anthracycline chemotherapeutic agent widely used to treat a various cancer due to its potent therapeutic effects. However, its clinical application is limited by severe toxicity in multiple tissues particularly the heart, as well as liver, kidneys, and nervous system. Neuregulin-1 (NRG-1), an epidermal growth factor produced by cardiac endothelial cells, plays a protective role against these injuries by activating ErbB2/4 receptors and the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. Evidence suggests that NRG-1 prevents myofibrillar damage and troponin degradation. In addition, exercise as a non-pharmacological intervention enhances these protective pathways by upregulation NRG-1 signaling and reducing cardiomyocytes apoptosis induced by DOX. The aim of this study was to investigate the effects of eight weeks of high-intensity interval training (HIIT) on NRG-1/ErbB signaling pathway in male Wistar rats induced by DOX-induced cardiotoxicity. Twenty-four male Wistar rats were randomly assigned into three groups (n = 8 per group): control healthy, DOX control, and DOX-HIIT. Cardiotoxicity was induced by six intraperitoneal injections of DOX (2 mg/kg per dose) administrated over a 12-day period, while the healthy control group received normal saline. The exercise protocol consisted of eight weeks of HIIT, performed three times per week. Each session included a 5-min warm-up, five 4-min high-intensity interval (85–90% of maximum oxygen consumption [VO2max]) interspersed with 2-min active recovery (50–60% of VO2max), and a 5-min cool-down. The end of the intervention, cardiac tissue and blood samples were collected for analysis. Protein expression was assessed using Western blotting. Statistical analysis was performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. The results demonstrated that HIIT significantly improvements in left ventricular end-systolic diameter, stroke volume, ejection fraction, and fractional shortening compared to DOX-treated sedentary rats. These findings indicated that HIIT attenuates DOX-induced structural and functional cardiac impairment, potentially through modulation of the NRG-1/ErbB signaling pathway.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-60692-7.Keywords: High-intensity interval training, Neuregulin, Doxorubicin, CardiotoxicitySubject terms: Cancer, Cardiology, Drug discovery, Medical research

Introduction

Cancer is a major global health concern, being a leading cause of morbidity and mortality1. Doxorubicin (DOX), a type of anthracycline antibiotic, widely used in chemotherapy to treat various malignancies2. However, its clinical efficacy is limited by dose-dependent cardiotoxicity. One of the most important mechanisms underlying DOX-induced cardiotoxicity (DIC) involves the necrosis and apoptosis of cardiomyocytes3. It has been suggested that a potential strategy to mitigate the cardiotoxicity effects of anthracyclines like DOX is through the modulation of cell death. Mitochondrial dysfunction is recognized as an early event in the apoptotic cascade triggered by DOX in cardiomyocytes2.

Neuregulin-1 (NRG-1), a member of the epidermal growth factor family, is secreted by vascular endothelial cells that act on the cardiovascular system throughout the body and play a critical role in cardiomyocyte differentiation, proliferation, and gene expression4. It acts on their function through the binding to ErbB receptors in cardiomyocytes and recruiting various effectors to trigger signaling pathways such as PI3K/Akt, and ERK/MAPK and exerting a protective effect by regulating apoptosis and autophagy5. Several studies have demonstrated that NRG-1 can structurally improve the myocardium and increase the left ventricular ejection fraction in patients with heart failure6,7. Furthermore, NRG-1/ErbB signaling has been shown to offer cardioprotective effects against DIC8. These findings suggest that heightened sensitivity of myofilaments to DOX, especially in the presence of ErbB2-targeting antibodies, may explain the contractile dysfunction observed in patients concurrently treated with trastuzumab and anthracyclines9. ErbB2, a receptor tyrosine kinase expressed in ventricular myocytes, play a major role in normal cardiac development7. NRG-1 contributes to reducing DOX-induced cardiac dysfunction through the ErbB/PI3K pathway10. However, the precise role of NRG-1 in cardiomyocytes protection against DOX induced apoptosis via the PI3K/AKT pathway remains understood.

Exercise training is widely recognized as a non-pharmacological, cardioprotective intervention that can be performed during cancer treatment11. It has emerged as an effective strategy to improve cardiopulmonary fitness and quality of life. Several studies have shown that engaging in exercise training during anthracycline-based chemotherapy reduces the incidence of treatment-related cardiac dysfunction in patients with breast cancer8,11. Both acute and chronic exercise have shown cardioprotective benefits in animal models. A single session of aerobic exercise 24-h before DOX administration has been reported to attenuate DOX-associated cardiac dysfunction, mitochondrial dysfunction, and oxidative stress by decreasing ROS levels12. The NRG-1/ErbB1/ErbB2 signaling pathway, which is connected to the PI3K/Akt cascade, is also activated in response to exercise, highlighting its role in cellular protection. Cai et al. (2016) found that 4 weeks of aerobic exercise training in myocardial infarction (MI) model led to increased NRG-1 protein expression and upregulation of ErbB/PI3K/AKT signaling. Exercise exerts cardioprotective effects by promoting myocardial repair and regeneration through enhanced DNA synthesis, activation of progenitor cells, improved angiogenesis, and reduced apoptosis5. Additionally, exercise has been shown to normalize the Bax/Bcl2 ratio, thereby reducing age-related apoptotic activity in the myocardium after MI5.

The most studies have focused on the protective effect of moderate-intensity exercise before or during anthracycline therapy, limited data are available regarding the effects of high-intensity interval training (HIIT) on DIC. HIIT has superior effects on cardiorespiratory fitness, cardiomyocyte contractility, and modulation of cell death pathways than traditional training in cardiovascular disease. However, the potential effect of HIIT on the protective role of NRG-1 and its downstream signaling pathway in DIC remains unexplored. We hypothesized that HIIT could confer cardioprotective effects in DIC by upregulating NRG-1 expression and activating its downstream ErbB/PI3K/Akt signaling cascade, thereby attenuating cardiomyocytes apoptosis in DOX-treated rats.

Method and materials

Animals and experimental design

Twenty-four 8-week-old male Wistar rats weighing 200–250 g were used in this study. The rats were purchased from the Pasteur Institute in Tehran, Iran. After transferring them to the laboratory, they were acclimatized to their new environment for one week. The rats were kept in transparent polycarbonate cages under controlled conditions, with a temperature range of 20–24 °C, humidity maintained at 45–55%, and a 12-h light-dark cycle. Throughout the study, the rats were provided with unlimited access to water and standard rodent chow. All experimental procedures and protocols employed in this study received approval from the Ethics Committee of the University of Tehran (IR.UT.SPORT.REC.1404.111) and adhered to the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals as published by the United States National Institutes of Health. The rats were randomly allocated into three groups: (1) control healthy (CONT-H, n = 8), (2) DOX-control (DOX-CONT, n = 8), and (3) DOX-HIIT (DOX-Exercise, n = 8). Cardiotoxicity was induced by intraperitoneal of DOX at a cumulative dosage of 12 mg/kg, delivered as six injections of 2 mg/kg over a 12-day period with 48-h intervals between injections. This dosing regimen falls within the commonly used range (10–15 mg/kg) for inducing DIC in rodent models13.

Echocardiography

To confirm cardiotoxicity in the rats, echocardiography was performed two weeks after the DOX injection. The Vivid 7 Expert Ultrasound System (General Electric Vingmed Ultrasound, Horten, Norway) was used to assess the heart function of rats after the administration of sodium thiopental (25 mg/kg, intraperitoneally) to induce anesthesia. Ultrasound imaging was conducted at a rate of 100 mm/s using a 10-MHz linear array transducer connected to the ultrasound system. Measurements were taken using parasternal two-dimensional (2D) and M-mode short-axis parasternal images at the level of the papillary muscle14.The following parameters were assessed: ejection fraction (EF), fractional shortening (FS), left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVDs), left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVDd), and left ventricular posterior wall thickness in systole and diastole (LVPWs and LVPWd). All echocardiographic evaluations were performed by a cardiologist specializing in small animal echocardiography, who was blinded to the experimental groups.

Training intervention

Following echocardiograms, the rat underwent a maximal exercise test on a motorized treadmill (Iranian Danesh Salar, Tehran, Iran) to evaluate maximal oxygen uptake (VO2max), in line with previous studies15,16. The animals in the DOX-CONT and CONT-H groups were placed on the treadmill but did not undergo any exercise training. The DOX-Exercise group participated in HIIT for 8-week, with a 40-min workout every session. Each HIIT session consisted of a 5-min warm-up at 50–60% VO2max, followed by five 4-min high-intensity intervals at 85–90% VO2max interspersed with 2-min active recovery periods at 50–60% of VO2max, and concluded with a 5-min cool-down running at 50–60% VO2max (Table 1). The intensity of the exercise training was determined based on the correlation between running speed and VO2max as described in a previous study. During the training period, the running speed increased steadily by 0.02 m/s per week16. The treadmill slope was set at 0 degrees for both training and testing. Given that DOX may impair exercise capacity, training intensity was individually adjusted based on each animal’s VO₂max to ensure comparable relative exercise intensity across groups.

Euthanasia and tissue collection

Forty-eight h after the final training session, rats were deeply anesthetized with intraperitoneal thiopental sodium (50 mg/kg). Adequacy of anesthesia was confirmed by the absence of corneal and pedal withdrawal reflexes. Under deep anesthesia, terminal cardiac blood was collected via cardiac puncture using a 21–23 G needle and serum-separator tubes. Following blood collection, the thoracic cavity was immediately opened under sterile conditions, and the hearts were rapidly excised. Cardiac tissues were rinsed in cold-phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to remove residual blood and subsequently prepared from molecular analyses.

After tissue collection and confirmation of loss of vital reflexes, euthanasia was completed by carbon dioxide inhalation using a transparent chamber (25.4 × 48.26 × 22.86 cm; volume: 28 L). Carbon dioxide was introduced at a flow rate corresponding to 20–30% of the chamber volume per minute to ensure gradual induction of unconsciousness and minimize animal distress. Death was confirmed by cessation of respiration and absence of reflex activity.

Western blot analysis

Cardiac tissue samples were homogenized in ice-cold lysis buffer (Sigma-Aldrich, 20–188, CA, USA), and centrifuged at 14,000 rpm for 20 min at and 4 °C. Total protein concentrations were determined using the Bradford assay (Sigma-Aldrich, B6916, 82024 Taufkirchen, Germany). Equal amounts of protein (20 µg) were separated by 10–12% SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Bio-Rad Laboratories, 162–0177, CA, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% skin milk (Sigma-Aldrich, A-7888, MO, USA) for 1 hat room temperature and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the following antibodies: anti-NRG-1 (0.5 µg/ml, ab191139), anti-ErbB2 (0.5 µg/ml, ab237715), anti-ErbB4 (1:5000, ab32375), anti-PI3K (1:1000, ab40755), anti-Akt (1:500, ab38449), anti-Bcl2 (1:1000, ab194583, Berlin, Germany). After washing three times with TBST buffer for 15-min each, membranes were incubated for 2 h at room temperature with HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (1:10,000, ab6721; Abcam, 10557 Berlin, Germany). Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA) and quantified by densitometric analysis using Gel Analyzer Version 2010a software (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA). Protein expression levels were normalization to β-actin.

TUNEL assay

Cardiomyocyte apoptosis was assessed using the In Situ Cell Death Detection Kit (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, paraffin-embedded cardiac tissue sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated, followed by washing in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked using a hydrogen peroxide/methanol solution (1:9) for 10 min. Sections were subsequently washed three times with PBS and incubated with proteinase K (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) for 30 min at 37 °C. After additional PBS washes, sections were permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 for 10 min and washed again. TUNEL labeling was then performed using the Roche reaction mixture containing terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase (TdT), followed by incubation for 2 h at 37 °C in a humidified chamber. Following washing steps, nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Fluorescence images were acquired using an Olympus fluorescence microscope (Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). Apoptotic cells were identified as TUNEL-positive nuclei, and quantitative analysis was performed on the entire captured image area. The extent of apoptosis was expressed as the percentage of TUNEL-positive nuclei relative to the total number of nuclei.

Statistical analysis

The mean ± standard deviation (SD) represents the data. The Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS, version 25.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and Prism (Version 9, GraphPad Prism Software, San Diego, CA, USA) were used to conduct the statistical analyses. P-values below 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The normal distribution of all outcome variables was verified using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Group differences were assessed using Tukey’s post hoc test after a one-way ANOVA.

Effects of HIIT on myocardial apoptosis

As shown in Fig. 3C, Bcl-2 protein expression was significantly higher in the DOX-Exercise group than in the DOX-CONT group (P = 0.001), indicating a shift toward an anti-apoptotic profile following HIIT. To further evaluate cardiomyocyte apoptosis, TUNEL staining was performed on myocardial tissue sections from all experimental groups (Fig. 3A). The DOX-CONT group exhibited a marked increase in the number of TUNEL-positive nuclei compared with the CONT-H group (P < 0.001). In contrast, the number of TUNEL-positive nuclei was significantly lower in the DOX-Exercise group than in the DOX-CONT group (P < 0.001). These findings indicate that HIIT was associated with reduced cardiomyocyte apoptosis in rats exposed to DOX.

Discussion

The present study provides evidence that HIIT exerts cardioprotective effects in a rat model of DOX-induced cardiotoxicity, as reflected by improvements in LVIDs, SV, EF, and FS. These functional improvements were accompanied by upregulation of key components of the NRG-1/ErbB2/ErbB4/PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, increased expression of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2, and a significant reduction in TUNEL-positive cardiomyocyte nuclei. Collectively, these findings suggest that HIIT may attenuate DOX-induced myocardial dysfunction through modulation of prosurvival molecular pathways and suppression of cardiomyocyte apoptosis.

DOX remains among the most potent and widely utilized anthracycline chemotherapeutic agents; however, its clinical application is substantially constrained by a dose-dependent cardiotoxic profile that can culminate in irreversible cardiomyopathy and heart failure17. Among the molecular pathways implicated in cardiomyocyte survival and myocardial homeostasis, the NRG-1/ErbB signaling axis occupies a central position. NRG-1 a member of the epidermal growth factor (EGF) family of ligands, binds with high affinity to ErbB4 receptors, which subsequently form obligate heterodimers with ErbB2, a receptor devoid of autonomous ligand-binding capacity. This receptor complex activates downstream effectors, including the PI3K/Akt pathway, which coordinates a broad transcriptional program governing cell survival, hypertrophic adaptation, contractile protein synthesis, and resistance to apoptotic stimuli18. Experimental and clinical evidence has consistently demonstrated that impaired NRG-1/ErbB signaling is associated with the development of dilated cardiomyopathy and heightened vulnerability to cardiotoxic insults19. In the present study, DOX administration was associated with markedly reduced expression of NRG-1, ErbB2, ErbB4 and Akt proteins, consistent with previously reported DOX-mediated suppression of prosurvival receptor signaling. Notably, HIIT partially reversed these molecular alterations, suggesting that exercise training may confer cardioprotection, at least in part, through restoration of NRG-1/ErbB-dependent signaling competence.

The observed increase in total Akt protein expression following HIIT is consistent with enhanced prosurvival signaling capacity; however, it is important to acknowledge that phosphorylation states of Akt (at Ser473) and PI3K (p85/p110 subunits) were not assessed in the current experimental protocol. Consequently, definitive conclusions regarding the functional activation of this pathway, as opposed to transcriptional or post-translational regulation of protein abundance, cannot be drawn from the present data alone. This limitation should be addressed in future investigations through inclusion of phospho-specific immunodetection or kinase activity assays. Notwithstanding this caveat, the parallel increases in NRG-1, ErbB2, ErbB4, and Akt expression observed in HIIT-trained animals are internally consistent with coordinated upregulation of a coherent signaling module. Previous investigations have demonstrated that aerobic and interval exercise training induces NRG-1 expression in cardiac tissue, accompanied by improvements in mitochondrial biogenesis, oxidative phosphorylation capacity, and cardiometabolic efficiency20,21. These observations collectively support the hypothesis that NRG-1 signaling contributes mechanistically to exercise-induced cardiac adaptation beyond the well-established hemodynamic and neurohumoral benefits of physical training.

The increase in Bcl-2 expression observed in HIIT-trained, DOX-exposed animals was accompanied by a statistically significant reduction in TUNEL-positive cardiomyocyte nuclei, providing convergent molecular and histological evidence of attenuated apoptotic cell death. DOX promotes apoptosis predominantly through the intrinsic mitochondrial pathway, characterized by loss of mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), cytochrome c release, caspase-9 and caspase-3 activation, and a shift in the Bcl-2/Bax ratio toward a pro-apoptotic state22. The restoration of Bcl-2 expression observed in the present study following HIIT is consistent with prior reports demonstrating that exercise training modulates mitochondrial apoptotic signaling in the heart. Ascensão et al. reported significant reductions in several apoptotic indices alongside increased Bcl-2 expression following endurance training in DOX-treated rodents23, while Alihemmati et al. corroborated these findings in the context of interval training protocols24. The present data extend these observations to a HIIT paradigm and suggest that the anti-apoptotic benefits of exercise are reproducible across training modalities and are not exclusively dependent on moderate-intensity continuous protocols. Furthermore, emerging evidence implicates NRG-1/ErbB4/PI3K/Akt signaling directly in the transcriptional regulation of Bcl-2 expression25, raising the possibility that the exercise-induced restoration of NRG-1 pathway activity observed herein may functionally converge with the anti-apoptotic phenotype to amplify cardiomyocyte protection. Beyond acute apoptotic signaling, DOX is increasingly recognized as an accelerant of pathological cardiac aging. Through induction of oxidative DNA damage, telomere attrition, p53/p21 pathway activation, and mitochondrial dysfunction, DOX promotes cardiomyocyte senescence, a state of irreversible growth arrest accompanied by a pro-inflammatory secretory phenotype that may further amplify myocardial injury and impair regenerative capacity17. Emerging evidence indicates that NRG-1 signaling may participate in the regulation of cellular senescence and cardiomyocyte renewal by modulating downstream effectors of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR axis and promoting activation of resident cardiac progenitor populations26. Although these mechanisms were not directly interrogated in the present study, the exercise-associated restoration of NRG-1 pathway proteins observed herein provides a rationale for future investigations examining whether HIIT-mediated NRG-1 upregulation confers protection against DOX-induced myocardial senescence and adverse remodeling over extended follow-up periods.

The functional improvements documented echocardiographically in HIIT-trained animals, specifically the amelioration of LVIDs, SV, EF, and FS, align with a growing body of preclinical and clinical evidence supporting the cardioprotective efficacy of exercise training during and following anthracycline chemotherapy. Appropriately prescribed exercise has been shown to attenuate declines in left ventricular systolic function, preserve global longitudinal strain, and reduce circulating troponin levels in patients receiving anthracycline-based regimens27. In the present model, these functional benefits likely reflect the combined effects of attenuated cardiomyocyte apoptosis, preserved sarcomeric integrity, and NRG-1-mediated maintenance of contractile protein homeostasis. The magnitude of functional improvement observed following HIIT in the present study underscores the potential clinical relevance of structured high-intensity exercise as a non-pharmacological cardioprotective intervention during cancer treatment, though direct extrapolation to human patients requires cautious interpretation given inherent interspecies differences in cardiac physiology, training adaptability, and chemotherapy metabolism.

The role of NRG-1/ErbB signaling in exercise-mediated cardiac repair has been further highlighted in the context of ischemic injury. Cai et al. demonstrated that exercise training enhanced NRG-1 expression and was associated with activation of downstream pathways governing cardiac repair and regeneration in a myocardial infarction model5. The present findings complement this evidence by demonstrating analogous molecular responses in a chemotoxic, rather than ischemic, model of cardiac injury, thereby broadening the mechanistic framework within which exercise-induced NRG-1 upregulation may operate. Importantly, because pharmacological pathway inhibition experiments were not conducted in the present study, it remains unresolved whether the observed molecular changes are causally responsible for the functional improvements or represent parallel, independently regulated adaptations to HIIT. Future studies employing selective ErbB2/ErbB4 antagonists (e.g., lapatinib, afatinib) or PI3K/Akt inhibitors administered concomitantly with exercise training would be necessary to establish the mechanistic primacy of this pathway.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings of the present study. First, the use of a rodent model inherently restricts direct translation to clinical populations, owing to fundamental physiological and metabolic differences between species. Furthermore, as only male rats were included, potential sex-specific differences in doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity and exercise-related adaptations remain unexamined. Second, the molecular analyses were largely confined to components of the NRG-1/ErbB/Akt signaling pathway and selected apoptosis-related markers, leaving other mechanisms implicated in DOX-induced cardiotoxicity unexplored. Critically, phosphorylation states of key signaling proteins, including p-PI3K and p-Akt, were not assessed, which precludes definitive conclusions regarding pathway activation status. While cardiomyocyte apoptosis was directly evaluated via TUNEL staining, complementary markers such as the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio and caspase-3 activation were not examined, and comprehensive histological characterization of myocardial fibrosis, inflammatory infiltration, and mitochondrial morphology was not performed. The absence of such structural data limits contextualization of the functional and molecular findings. Third, the study design presents several methodological constraints. A HIIT-only (non-DOX) control group was not included, which limits the capacity to disentangle the independent cardioprotective effects of exercise per se from those specifically attributable to the DOX-HIIT interaction. Additionally, only a single HIIT protocol was evaluated without comparison to moderate-intensity continuous training or other exercise modalities; accordingly, conclusions regarding the specificity or superiority of HIIT should be interpreted with caution. Baseline echocardiographic measurements were not directly compared with post-intervention values in a manner that permits formal quantification of functional recovery over time. Fourth, clinically relevant circulating biomarkers of cardiac injury, including cardiac troponin I (cTnI), N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), and NRG-1 itself, were not measured, which constrains translational comparability with clinical investigations. Moreover, all assessments were conducted at a single post-intervention time point, leaving the long-term durability of the observed cardioprotective effects undetermined. Finally, advanced mechanistic approaches, including pharmacological pathway inhibition experiments, were not employed, and the absence of longitudinal functional follow-up further limits translational inference. Taken together, future investigations employing diverse experimental models, both sexes, multiple exercise protocols, longitudinal assessment frameworks, and targeted mechanistic strategies will be essential to consolidate the mechanistic basis and translational relevance of HIIT as a cardioprotective intervention in the context of DOX-induced cardiotoxicity.

Conclusion

The findings of the present study demonstrate that HIIT was associated with significant improvements in cardiac function، including left ventricular end-systolic diameter, stroke volume, ejection fraction, and fractional shortening، in a rat model of DOX-induced cardiotoxicity. These functional improvements were accompanied by upregulation of NRG-1, ErbB2, ErbB4, Akt, and Bcl-2 protein expression, as well as a marked reduction in TUNEL-positive nuclei, collectively indicating attenuation of cardiomyocyte apoptosis. Taken together, these observations support a cardioprotective role for HIIT and implicate the NRG-1/ErbB/Akt signaling axis as a plausible molecular mediator of exercise-induced cardiac adaptation in the context of anthracycline exposure. Given its non-pharmacological nature, HIIT may represent a promising and clinically relevant supportive strategy for mitigating chemotherapy-associated cardiac injury. Nevertheless, further investigations are warranted to consolidate and extend these findings. Future studies incorporating phosphoproteomic profiling, pathway inhibition experiments, circulating cardiac biomarker analyses, and longitudinal follow-up will be essential to more precisely elucidate the underlying mechanisms. Such efforts will ultimately contribute to the development of evidence-based exercise prescriptions tailored to the needs of patients undergoing cardiotoxic chemotherapy regimens.

Long-Term Outcomes of Aspirin vs. Clopidogrel After PCI in High-Risk Patients With Cardiovascular Comorbidities: A Post Hoc Analysis of the HOST-EXAM Extended Trial
Authors: Huijin Lee, Jeehoon Kang, Doyeon Hwang, Kyung Woo Park, Han-Mo Yang, Hyun-Jae Kang, Jung-Kyu Han, Eun-Seok Shin, Bon-Kwon Koo, Hyo-Soo Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Author's summaryThis post hoc analysis of the HOST-EXAM Extended trial compared clopidogrel and aspirin for long-term monotherapy after percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) in patients with and without prior cardiovascular disease (CVD). Clopidogrel lowered the risk of the primary composite endpoint (all-cause death, myocardial infarction, stroke, readmission due to acute coronary syndrome (ACS), and major bleeding) versus aspirin. This benefit was consistent across both patient groups, with no statistical interaction by prior CVD status. These findings support clopidogrel as a preferred long-term monotherapy for a broad range of post-PCI patients, regardless of their cardiovascular history.Keywords: Platelet aggregation inhibitors, Cardiovascular diseases/history, Coronary artery disease, Percutaneous coronary intervention

INTRODUCTION

After percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) for treating coronary artery disease (CAD), dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) with aspirin and a P2Y12 inhibitor is standard to prevent thrombotic complications.1) However, the optimal long-term antiplatelet strategy after DAPT remains uncertain. Traditionally, aspirin has been preferred for long-term monotherapy,2) but the Harmonizing Optimal Strategy for Treatment of Coronary Artery Stenosis–Extended Antiplatelet Monotherapy (HOST-EXAM) trial demonstrated that clopidogrel monotherapy was superior to aspirin in preventing the composite endpoint of all-cause death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, stroke, readmission due to ACS, and major bleeding over 24 months.3) The HOST-EXAM Extended trial provided further clarification of these long-term benefits, concluding that clopidogrel significantly reduced the risk of these adverse outcomes compared to aspirin.4)

Meanwhile, patients with CAD frequently have co-existing vascular diseases, such as peripheral artery disease (PAD) and/or cerebrovascular disease.5) Previous studies have shown that these patients are at increased risk of in-hospital and mid-term adverse events.6),7),8),9) Managing risk factors aggressively and using antiplatelet therapy are essential to prevent cardiovascular events in these high-risk patients.10) As to deal with the increased risk in these patients, prolonged DAPT after PCI may reduce ischemic events, however it also leads to a higher risk of bleeding.11) Therefore, to minimize the thrombotic risk without mitigating the bleeding risk, the optimal single antiplatelet agent in patients with additional vascular conditions is an important issue to evaluate. In the current sub-study of the HOST-EXAM Extended trial, we assessed the differential impact of clopidogrel and aspirin monotherapy on clinical outcomes in patients with and without previous cardiovascular disease (CVD).

METHODS

Ethical statement

The HOST-EXAM Extended study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) at each participating site (representative institute: Seoul National University Hospital, IRB No: 1208-028-421) and conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonization Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice and the latest version of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study design and participants

The design of the HOST-EXAM trial has been previously detailed.12) The HOST-EXAM trial was an investigator-initiated, prospective, randomized, open-label, multicenter study conducted at 37 sites in Korea. In March 2020, an extended follow-up known as the HOST-EXAM Extended study was planned, and the study protocol was submitted to the National Evidence-Based Healthcare Collaborating Agency of Korea.

The scientific conduct of the trial was overseen by the Seoul National University Hospital Clinical Trial Center and Medical Research Collaborating Center. An independent clinical event committee, blinded to group assignments, adjudicated all events by reviewing anonymized medical records.

Previous CVD was defined for the purposes of this analysis as the presence of any of the following conditions at baseline: previous myocardial infarction (MI), cerebrovascular accident (CVA), or PAD. These diagnoses were not adjudicated using standardized diagnostic criteria but were identified through structured review of patients’ electronic medical records. Each condition was recorded based on clinician-documented history at the time of enrollment. Objective diagnostic tools such as ankle-brachial index for PAD or imaging confirmation for CVA were not systematically applied across the cohort.

Trial endpoints and definitions

The trial endpoints and definitions remained consistent with those used in the HOST-EXAM trial. The primary endpoint was pre-specified as a composite of all-cause death, nonfatal MI, stroke, readmission due to ACS, and major bleeding (Bleeding Academic Research Consortium [BARC] ≥3). Two composite secondary endpoints were also pre-specified: 1) thrombotic events (cardiac death, nonfatal MI, ischemic stroke, readmission due to ACS, and definite/probable stent thrombosis), and 2) any bleeding events (BARC ≥2). Analyses of the individual components of these composites, any revascularization, and subgroup effects were considered exploratory. Endpoints were defined based on the criteria set by the Academic Research Consortium.13) Detailed definitions of each clinical event can be found in the Supplementary Data 1.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were summarized as counts (percentages) and continuous variables as means ± standard deviations. Group comparisons used the χ2 test for categorical variables and the unpaired t-test for continuous variables. All time-to-event analyses followed the intention-to-treat principle; participants without an event were censored at the last known contact or end of follow-up.

For the primary composite endpoint, Kaplan–Meier curves were constructed and hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated using Cox proportional-hazards models. Multivariable models adjusted for age, sex, body mass index, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, dyslipidemia, chronic kidney disease (CKD), prior heart failure, prior gastrointestinal bleeding, and multivessel disease. The proportional-hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals.

Multiplicity was specified a priori: only the primary endpoint was designated as confirmatory and tested at a 2-sided α=0.05. The two secondary composites, their individual components, any revascularization, and all subgroup analyses were prespecified as exploratory and are summarized descriptively with effect estimates and 95% CIs. No multiplicity adjustment was applied to these exploratory analyses.

Five-year absolute risks were estimated from Kaplan–Meier curves as the cumulative incidence at 5 years. The absolute risk reduction (ARR) was defined as the absolute risk in the aspirin group minus that in the clopidogrel group, and the number needed to treat (NNT) was calculated as the reciprocal of ARR (ARR expressed as a proportion). An additive-scale interaction, expressed as the difference in risk differences between previous-CVD strata, was evaluated using Greenwood standard errors and a Wald test. Percentages were rounded to one decimal place and p values to three decimals.

To assess robustness to background therapy, we repeated the primary Cox model with additional adjustment for baseline medications (β-blocker [BB], renin–angiotensin system [RAS] inhibitor, statin, calcium channel blocker [CCB], diuretics, nitrates).

The previous-CVD cohort was divided into the following 4 non-overlapping subgroups based on disease components, and component-stratified analyses were conducted: 1) MI only, 2) stroke only, 3) PAD only, and 4) polyvascular disease (defined as ≥2 components).

Subgroup analyses were interpreted primarily via point estimates and 95% CIs; p values were reported only for treatment-by-subgroup interaction (heterogeneity) tests.

All analyses were performed in R version 4.2.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

RESULTS

Baseline clinical and angiographic characteristics

Of the 5,438 patients randomized (mean age, 63.5±10.7 years; 1,384 [25.5%] female), 1,137 (20.9%) had previous CVD at baseline (566 in the clopidogrel group and 571 in the aspirin group) (Figure 1). The previous CVD group comprised patients with previous MI (76.7%), previous CVA (23.3%), and PAD (5.6%). Patients with previous CVD had more risk factors such as hypertension, dyslipidemia, CKD, previous heart failure, and were more likely to present as ACS at index PCI, compared with patients without CVD. Regarding the antiplatelet agent groups, the aspirin and clopidogrel groups were well balanced in terms of overall baseline characteristics in both patients with and without previous CVD (Table 1). Regarding angiographic characteristics at index PCI, the previous CVD group had a higher prevalence of bifurcation lesions, a greater number of implanted stents, and a longer total stent length compared with the non-CVD group.

Clinical endpoints

Clinical follow-up was completed in March 2022 (median 5.8 years; interquartile range, 4.7–6.2). The absolute risk of the primary composite endpoint was higher in patients with previous CVD than in those without (18.1% vs. 13.7%). For the primary composite endpoint, the HRs for clopidogrel versus aspirin were 0.784 (95% CI, 0.596–1.033) in the previous-CVD group and 0.794 (95% CI, 0.675–0.934) in the non-CVD group (Figure 2A and B, Table 2). The test for interaction by previous-CVD status was not significant (p=0.951). For the thrombotic composite endpoint, the HRs for clopidogrel versus aspirin were 0.668 (95% CI, 0.470–0.949) in the previous-CVD group and 0.734 (95% CI, 0.603–0.893) in the non-CVD group (Figure 2C, Table 2, Supplementary Figure 1). Similarly, no significant interaction by previous-CVD status was observed (p=0.659). For bleeding (BARC ≥2), the hazard ratios were 0.698 (95% CI, 0.441–1.102) and 0.810 (95% CI, 0.615–1.067) in the previous-CVD and non-CVD groups, respectively (Figure 2D, Table 2, Supplementary Figure 2), and there was no significant interaction by previous-CVD status (p=0.690).

Absolute risk and number needed to treat

Using 5-year Kaplan–Meier estimates, the ARR for the primary composite endpoint was 2.2% (NNT, 45) in participants without previous CVD and 2.9% (NNT, 35) in those with previous CVD (Supplementary Table 1). There was no significant heterogeneity of the absolute benefit based on previous-CVD status (difference in ARR, −0.7%; p value for interaction=0.789).

Sensitivity analysis

In a post hoc sensitivity analysis to compensate the different medical treatment strategies according to the presence of previous-CVD, additional adjustment for baseline use of BBs, RAS inhibitors, statins, CCBs, diuretics, and nitrates was performed. The results remained consistent with the primary model after (Supplementary Tables 2 and 3). In the exploratory, component-stratified analysis of the previous-CVD cohort (n=1,137) including subgroups of MI only (n=818; 127 events), stroke only (n=208; 47 events), PAD only (n=56; 16 events), and ≥2 components (n=55; 16 events), no significant treatment-by-component interaction was observed for the primary composite endpoint (p=0.127), the thrombotic composite endpoint (p=0.164), or any bleeding (BARC ≥2) (p=0.215) (Supplementary Table 4).

Subgroup analysis of the previous CVD group

In exploratory subgroup analyses within the previous-CVD cohort, no significant treatment-by-subgroup interaction was observed for the primary endpoint (Figure 3). This was consistent for the thrombotic composite endpoint, with no significant treatment-by-subgroup interactions observed (Supplementary Figure 3). For bleeding, the test for treatment-by-subgroup interaction was not significant (Supplementary Figure 4).

DISCUSSION

In this post hoc analysis of the HOST-EXAM Extended trial, patients with previous CVD had a higher long-term risk than those without. The principal finding was that the comparison between clopidogrel monotherapy and aspirin was consistent regardless of previous-CVD status. The test for interaction by previous-CVD status for the primary composite endpoint was not significant (p=0.951). This lack of significant interaction was consistent for the secondary thrombotic (p=0.659) and bleeding (BARC ≥2) endpoints (p=0.690). These findings remained consistent throughout sensitivity analyses as well as in exploratory subgroup analyses.

These findings expand upon the primary results of the HOST-EXAM trial. The original 2-year HOST-EXAM trial first demonstrated clopidogrel’s superiority over aspirin post-DAPT for a composite of thrombotic and bleeding endpoints, though it raised concerns with a non-significant trend toward higher mortality (HR, 1.43).3) The HOST-EXAM Extended trial was crucial in resolving this ambiguity, confirming a persistent net-clinical benefit (HR, 0.74) over 5.8 years while clarifying no difference in all-cause mortality (HR, 1.04).4) Our current sub-study leverages this long-term dataset, demonstrating that this established benefit of clopidogrel applies consistently for primary, thrombotic, and bleeding (BARC ≥2) endpoints in patients both with and without previous CVD. Clinically, this suggests clopidogrel is an appropriate long-term maintenance strategy irrespective of previous CVD status.

Our findings underscore the greater burden of residual ischemic risk among patients with previous CVD compared with those without previous CVD, in line with observations from contemporary PCI cohorts (e.g., GLOBAL LEADERS).14) Conversely, the bleeding incidence in our cohort was modest. This is an expected consequence of the trial design, which enrolled event-free survivors 12 months after PCI and maintained them on single antiplatelet therapy. This observation aligns with the SMART-CHOICE 3 trial, wherein high-ischemic-risk patients who had completed standard DAPT were also treated with clopidogrel or aspirin monotherapy.15) In that trial, clopidogrel reduced major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events without an increase in BARC type 2, 3, or 5 bleeding, and absolute bleeding rates remained modest over 3 years.15) Taken together, these data suggest that among patients who have safely completed DAPT, long-term maintenance with single antiplatelet therapy is associated with a relatively low bleeding burden.

A separate challenge lies in comparing our component-stratified analyses to the landmark CAPRIE trial, which compared clopidogrel versus aspirin monotherapy in a pre-DAPT era and found a pronounced benefit for clopidogrel in its PAD subgroup.16) In our exploratory analysis for the primary composite endpoint, we found no evidence of significant treatment heterogeneity; the global treatment-by-component interaction was not significant (p=0.127). This non-significant interaction test is the primary statistical evidence against claiming any subgroup-specific effects. Although a nominal signal was observed in the ‘MI only’ stratum (p=0.013), this is superseded by the global test; conversely, the ‘PAD only’ stratum was markedly underpowered (n=56) and thus uninformative for any comparison to the CAPRIE findings. Thus, these component-stratified analyses remain hypothesis-generating.

This study has several limitations. First, this was a post hoc analysis of a non-prespecified subgroup (previous CVD), and the main study was not powered for a formal treatment-by-CVD interaction. This lack of statistical power and post hoc nature extended to all exploratory subgroup. Other limitations include: 1) the open-label design and exclusively Korean population, limiting generalizability; 2) potential selection bias from enrolling only 12-month event-free post-PCI survivors; 3) a previous CVD definition that excluded prior PCI; 4) mechanistic data such as CYP2C19 genotype or platelet function were not collected; and 5) the use of baseline-only covariates, which risks residual confounding from time-varying factors. These methodological characteristics, particularly the post hoc design and reliance on baseline covariates, are shared with prior substudies of the HOST-EXAM trial.17),18),19),20),21)

In conclusion, this post hoc analysis of the HOST-EXAM Extended trial found no evidence of effect modification by previous-CVD status for the primary composite, thrombotic composite, or bleeding endpoints. The comparison between clopidogrel monotherapy and aspirin was consistent in patients with and without previous CVD.

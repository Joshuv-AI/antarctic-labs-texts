Molecular vehicles for therapeutic delivery of lactate in exercise and medicine
Authors: George A Brooks
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOnce thought of as a dead-end metabolite, the product of anaerobic, oxygen-limited metabolism, we now know that lactate is produced under fully oxygenated conditions, such as after consuming dietary carbohydrate and during physical exercise. Via shuttling mechanisms lactate is a preferred fuel energy source for red skeletal muscle, heart and brain. Consequently, lactate is being recognized as a major means of delivering non-insulin dependent carbohydrate-derived carbon around the body. Such recognition has led to consideration of developing enteral and parenteral means of lactate delivery. Enteral lactate delivery can be accomplished by providing fermented foods or high-glycemic index carbohydrates. Parenteral, intravenous (iv) lactate delivery can be accomplished by use of inorganic salts, such as sodium-L-lactate and other electrolyte mixtures appropriate for iv delivery. Enteral lactate delivery has been accomplished by using organic carriers that bind by electrostatic charge to zwitter ion amino acids. As well, it's possible to deliver lactate covalently bound to amino acids such as phenylalanine or glycerol. Some lactate-containing compounds such as LACTEM, sodium- and arginyl-lactate are Generally Recognized As Safe (GRAS) by the FDA for use in food, but other lactate-containing compounds are of unknown bioavailability and will need to pass FDA scrutiny. Enteral use of arginyl-lactate has been shown to be effective for improving performance of endurance athletes, and iv sodium-L-lactate for treatment of traumatic brain injury has reached Stage 2 clinical trials. Scientific background and possibilities of using lactate-containing compounds to achieve better outcomes by athletes or in illnesses and injuries is discussed.Keywords: Lactate shuttle, Exercise, Nutrition, Acid-base balance, Traumatic brain injury, Cardioplegia, Crystalloids

Introduction: The science of lactate shuttling

Once revolutionary in thought and widely controversial, lactate shuttling is obvious in diverse physiological situations including carbohydrate (CHO) nutrition, physical exercise, illnesses and injuries where lactate participates in fueling with most organ systems (e.g., muscle, heart, liver, kidneys, brain, integument) participating in a variety of essential physiological processes.1, 2, 3, 4 Unfortunately, at present less is known about dysregulated lactate shuttling in illnesses and injuries. For example, uncontrolled glycolysis leading to lactate production has long been recognized as a hallmark of cancer,5, 6, 7, 8 but the Warburg effect of elevated glycolysis leading to lactate production in cancer cells and tumors under fully oxygenated conditions is little studied in contemporary biology. More relevant in contemporary settings is that hyperlactatemia is taken as a marker of poor outcomes in sepsis and other inflammatory conditions,9,10 Further, chronic hyperlactatemia is a marker of glucose intolerance, insulin resistance and loss of metabolic flexibility.11 As well, rather than regarding lactatemia as an added stress complicating sepsis and other conditions, physiologists have demonstrated that lactate supplementation is efficacious during exercise,12 and clinician scientists are assessing the hypothesis that lactate is a physiological strain to mitigate stress in sepsis.13,14

According to current theory,1,15, 16, 17 lactate shuttling serves at least three purposes; lactate is: (1) a preferred energy substrate, (2) the predominant glucogenic precursor, and (3) a signaling molecule. Because of the preponderance of work articulating and demonstrating lactate shuttle phenomena, it is easy to conclude that lactate shuttling is a normal feature of CHO metabolism, not just physical exercise. Lactate production starts when dietary carbohydrate is consumed, metabolized in the oral cavity and gut and subsequently circulated to muscle and other organs via the Postprandial Lactate Shuttle.18,19 Incredibly, an example of feedback regulation of CHO metabolism is the recently discovered facet of lactate signaling includes appetite suppression.20

The purpose of this Mini-review is to explore the possibilities of taking advantage of unique roles of the lactate molecule and lactate-containing compounds in physiology and medicine. First, definitions of terms are necessary. Enteral means delivery via the gut (gastrointestinal, GI tract), whereas parenteral means beyond enteral, and is basically intravascular delivery, typically by intra venous (iv) infusion. Of these, there is the special case of providing nutrients or fluids via a nasal-gastric tube into the stomach; this is enteral delivery even if external pumps are used to deliver concentrated solutions of nutrients. Enteral nutrition is the normal physiological means of nutrient delivery. From recognition of the postprandial Lactate Shuttle, enteral nutrition could include delivery of solids or liquids containing simple or complex carbohydrate foods,19,21 a food or beverage containing fructose,22 or a fermentable food such as yogurt, sour dough bread, kimchee and sour kraut and others that contain lactate.1 Second, contemporary use of crystalloid and colloid solutions is reviewed. Then, in terms of providing nutritive support to an organ or tissue, we commence with the brain, an organ previously reputed be entirely dependent on glucose as an energy substrate.

Crystalloid and colloid solutions: state of the art for maintaining blood volume in illnesses and after injuries

Crystalloids and colloids are fluids given by iv infusion that are used for fluid resuscitation in dehydration, shock and hemorrhage that results in lowering of blood pressure. Examples crystalloids include “normal” (0.9% NaCl) and the dilute lactate solution (Lactated Ringers). Colloids are larger molecule solutions suspended in crystalloid solutions; colloids are intended for blood volume expansion. Naturally occurring colloids include human albumin or plasma. Synthetic or semi-synthetic colloid solutions contain starches or gelatins, again suspended in crystalloid solutions such as normal saline. While crystalloid and colloid solutions are widely used tools for maintaining blood volume and pressure in illnesses or after injuries, but they provide little or no nutritive support. Hence, a question arises: can more concentrated lactate-containing solutions accomplish the same purposes as crystalloids, and in addition, supply energy to support injured and uninjured body tissues? Rephrased, can lactate containing solutions be super crystalloids and super foods? Instead of the doomsday knee-jerk responses to elevated blood lactate (lactatemia) in athletes and type 2 diabetes patients receiving metformin, could it be that lactatemia is a normal and appropriate physiological response in various conditions? Among other aspects, in this Mini-review we examine whether lactatemia is a beneficial outcome of lactate shuttling.

Brain fueling, it's not just glucose

Normal brain metabolism

Classic studies of brain metabolism using deoxyglucose isotopic tracers and brain imaging by Sokoloff and associates established the idea that glucose was the essential and main energy substrate for the human brain.23, 24, 25, 26 Additionally, those pioneers also investigated cerebral use of alternative substrates including ketones27 and lactate.28 Unfortunately, data for lactate were not definitive because of lactate turnover (shuttling involving production and disposal) occurs within the brain, whereas the net uptake gives the Cerebral Metabolic Rate (CMR) of glucose and ketones because they are not produced in the brain. Subsequent studies using tracers, arterial-venous difference and blood flow measurements replicated classical conclusions for glucose and ketones and extended results to demonstrate cerebral lactate uptake and shuttling in resting29,30 and exercising individuals.31,32 Recent discovery of the Astrocyte-Neuron Lactate Shuttle4,33 together and observations on men that when lactate rose during exercise, lactate substituted for glucose as brain fuel31,34 just as it does at the whole-body level during exercise35,36 can be interpreted to mean that lactate is brain fuel.34 Still, those initial and profoundly important discoveries on the importance for glucose for brain metabolism not only led to greater understanding of brain function, but also application for daily life. Even though there are no recommended daily allowances (RDA) for glucose or lactate, the RDA for dietary CHO was established to be that for providing glucose to the healthy brain which is 130 g/day in healthy adults and children.37,38

Brain metabolism after injury

Regrettably, one of the major metabolic consequences of traumatic brain injury (TBI) is that cerebral glucose uptake is severely depressed.29,39 At present, the reason for this blockage in post injury brain glycolysis is not understood; perhaps there is a redox change at GAPDH such that neither glucose nor glucose plus insulin infusion are ineffective for increasing cerebral glucose uptake? In contrast, however, cerebral lactate uptake and utilization are minimally affected following TBI.34 Hence, a rationale for supporting cerebral CHO uptake is to provide exogenous lactate as an alternative brain fuel that bypasses the TBI-induced blockage in brain glucose uptake and metabolism.40 How then could lactate be supplied parenterally or enterally to support brain function after injury? And, if lactate is to be given as an energy substate or to buffer metabolic acidosis, what carrier would be efficacious for providing an appropriate lactate anion carrier? Certainly, lactic acid produced by industrial chemical bacterial fermentation would not be a choice! However, there are other potential lactate anion carriers as discussed below (Sections 3.1, 3.2).

Lactate, sugar or spice?

If glycolysis produces lactate from sugars such as glucose or fructose, or their combination (sucrose), why is there an advantage to including a lactate-containing compound in a sports drink as was demonstrated by Azevedo et al.?12 Although not extensively studied, lactate is more rapidly assimilated, circulated and metabolized than are glucose or fructose. Providing a lactate-containing compound along with Na+ takes advantage of the rapid transport capacity of sodium-mediated monocarboxylate-lactate transporters (sMCT)s that are highly expressed in intestinal mucosa cells as well as cell membrane proton-mediated MCTs that are ubiquitous in plasma and mitochondrial membranes of skeletal muscle.41, 42, 43

In mammals glycolysis produces lactate anion, not lactic acid

Several factors are responsible for stimulating glycolysis; among these are energy use and the need to support ATP homeostasis. Ultimately, glycolysis progresses from pyruvate to lactate because of energetics, the free energy change across LDH being very high (≈ 6.0 kcal/mol) and the corresponding equilibrium constant (Keq = 3.6 × 104 mol−1) that is far in the direction of the product lactate.45

Lactate compounds of interest for supplying cerebral nutritive support

To date sodium L-(+)-lactate has been used to supplement cerebral CHO uptake and minimize swelling following human TBI.46, 47, 48, 49, 50, 51, 52 Lactate infusate concentrations used have been as high as half molar Na+-L-(+)-lactate (1 000 mOsm/L), which is at the upper safe limit for vascular delivery of hypertonic solutions. Typically, because of concern over the associated sodium load, infusion periods are limited to durations of several hours in patients with normal renal function. However, in experiments to improve outcomes in acute heart failure patients, Nalos et al. experimented with delivering high concentration, half molar L-lactate infusion for 24 h.53 The outcome was improved cardiac function and no ill effects of hypernatremia.

Supporting safety and efficacy of exogenous lactate infusion are results of lactate clamp studies. In experiments to study glucose-lactate interactions in healthy young men, Miller et al. and Messonnier et al. clamped lactate to 4 mmol/L.35,36,54 Only slight blood alkalinization and increased plasma Na+ were observed.55 Prudence would indicate that patients with good renal function be candidates for exogenous Na+-L-lactate infusion, but balancing lactate anions with electrolyte cation carriers besides Na+ to include K+, Ca2+, and Mg2+ in physiological proportions could prove efficacious. At present, two experimental approaches to vascular lactate infusion have been tried; one being to raise lactate to a target such as 4 mmol/L,54 a value seen in heavy, but less that maximal exercise (≥ 20 mmol/L),56 while another approach is to infuse a known amount of lactate.46

Importantly, the lactate enantiomer (L or D) provided enterally or parenterally is deserving of consideration. At present judgement favors use of the L-enantiomer which is that found in systemic human metabolism which is L-lactate, with D-lactate, a product of short-bowel syndrome fermentation, being neurotoxic.57,58 Thus, neither the D-lactate enantiomer nor the racemic lactate (L + D enantiomers) are appropriate for managing human TBI. Hence, traditional use of lactated Ringer’s solution is called to question because of racemic lactate inclusion by some vendors. As well, in terms of energy support, lactated Ringer’s is low in lactate content.

Inorganic and organic lactate carriers

The use of inorganic salts as a cationic carrier of lactate anions is justifiable as they are appropriate for enteral (oral or nasal gastric tube) or parenteral (intravenous, iv) delivery. Within inorganic salts, anions and cations are held together by electrostatic bonds that readily dissociate in aqueous solutions at physiological pHs (7.38 vs the pKa for lactic acid = 3.86 mmol/L). Inorganic carrier cations for lactate and pyruvate include Na+, K+, Ca2+, and Mg2+. The typical ratio of blood lactate (L) to pyruvate (P), the (L/P) in healthy, resting postabsorptive individuals is nominally 10. However, the arterial ratio doubles after CHO ingestion,18 and during heavy exercise in the venous effluent from working muscle the L/P can exceed 500.59,60 The choice of L over P as a means of CHO delivery is an easy one because of pyruvate to lactate conversion in one circulatory passage due to the presence of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) in erythrocytes and lung parenchyma.59,61

In addition to inorganic lactate carriers, it is also possible to use zwitter ions of amino acids with alkaline pKa's such as arginine and lysine, and to a lesser extent, histidine as lactate anion carriers.62 Compounds such as arginyl-lactate would be formed at alkaline pH's and when delivered enterally would readily dissociate in the acid environment of the stomach, thus providing an energy source (lactate) and vasodilator precursor (arginine).63 Such compounds are also of advantage for enteral nutrition because they are on the "Generally Recognized as Safe" (GRAS) list for foods and dietary supplements. Therefore, they are appropriate for energy support of athletes, healthy non-atletes,12 TBI and other patient populations.40

Another lactate delivery approach would be for lactate to be covalently bound to an organic molecule. One such approach is to link lactate to the amino acid phenylalanine yielding the compound abbreviated as (Lac-Phe).64,65 However, at present enthusiasm for, and efficacy of covalently linked compounds for lactate delivery would be hampered by delays in digestion and unknown bioavailability. Further, at present, neither Lac-Phe nor similar compounds such as Lac-Alanine or Lac-Ketone are GRAS, and currently they are considered inappropriate for enteral or parenteral nutrition for lack of clinical trials and FDA approval.

Still another lactate delivery approach is esterify lactate to glycerol, a three carbon molecule that is by itself a gluconeogenic precursor.66 Unfortunately, compared to other 3-carbon molecules (lactate, pyruvate, and alanine), in humans glycerol is a poor gluconeogenic precursor.66 However, glycerol has been used as a plasma expander in the prevention and treatment of dehydration.67 Still, for the purpose of serving as a monocarboxylate carrier, the three hydroxyl groups on glycerol are available for esterification with organic compounds that are energy substrates. Those include lactate, pyruvate, acetate and fatty acids such as palmitate.68 Glycerol-dilactate-monopalmitate (LACTEM) is GRAS and therefore is appropriate for oral human consumption. Currently, the author is not aware of any clinical trials for FDA approval or iv infusion involving lactate glycerides LACTEM, or ACETUM (acetate glyceride), or combinations such as glycerol tri-lactate and di-lactate mono-pyruvate.

Lactate signaling cascades

Beyond serving as an energy substrate and gluconeogenic precursor, lactate plays important roles in cell signaling.1,69 Myokines and exerkines are substances that have autocrine-, paracrine-, and endocrine-like functions when released from muscles and other tissues and organs that release lactate such as the gut19 and integument.70 Lactate serves as a myokine when produced in resting muscles, and as an exerkine when produced during exercise, in the integument and working muscles. Because little lactate signaling is autocrine or paracrine, lymphatic and systemic circulatory systems are involved. Aspects of lactate production, removal, and signaling have important basic and translational implications. Many of these functions have previously been identified,71 and are listed below for readers’ convenience (Table 1). When lactate is released into the circulation from working skeletal muscle, within a fraction of a minute lactate reaches white adipose where lactate binds to the HCAR-1 receptor that signals through cAMP and CREB to inhibit lipolysis. Similarly, when circulating lactate reaches carnosine dipeptidase 2 (CNDP2) positive cells, including macrophages, monocytes, other immune and epithelial cells, Lac-Phe is synthesized which is subsequently released into the circulation by which it reaches and downregulates feeding centers of the hypothalamus, thus suppressing appetite. In the latter case lactate has simultaneous short- and longer-term effects on the brain, serving to provide energy substrate and activate the autonomic nervous system (ANS), but at the same time inhibiting appetite.72, 110, 111 And, as noted above with regard to the postprandial lactate shuttle (PLS), the initial production of lactate from dietary CHO and subsequent inhibition of appetite is an example of negative feedback regulation of CHO metabolism.16,111

Summary

Lactate, the once feared metabolite in exercise, physiology and clinical practice, is now better understood to have nutritive, physiological and biochemical utilities. Lactate is neither an “assailant” nor “innocent bystander’ in metabolism. Contrary to negative early 20th Century depiction, lactate is a useful myokine and exerkine. In this work strategies of how to deliver lactate anion are presented and discussed. By convention, “normal” isotonic saline (0.9% NaCl) is widely used to support blood pressure and circulation in illness and injuries. By providing fluid, electrolytes and energy parentally lactate solutions may eventually prove to be superior to normal saline or other crystalloids or colloids. Similarly, by providing fluid, electrolytes and energy enterally lactate solutions may eventually prove to be superior to other commercially available hydration products.

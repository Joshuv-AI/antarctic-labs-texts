Comparative Study of Mini-implants versus Standard Implants in Orthodontic Anchorage for Space Closure
Authors: Sanjay Kumar Sahoo, Manjunath Chekka, Riddhi Chawla, Mohammed Nehal Naimatullah, K. Kumar Misra, Pradeep Kandikatla, M. C. Prashant
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:In order to obtain satisfactory treatment outcomes, orthodontic space closure frequently requires trustworthy anchoring devices. Because they are easier to install and smaller than regular implants, mini-implants have become a viable option. The purpose of this study was to evaluate the effectiveness of regular implants versus mini-implants as orthodontic anchoring devices for closing gaps in a tertiary care context.Methods:A total of 100 patients who needed space closure were split into two groups at random and given regular implants (group B, n = 50) or mini-implants (group A, n = 50). The rate of space closure, anchoring loss, and patient pain were measured using visual analog scales as the primary end measures. Resonance frequency analysis was used to assess implant stability. Based on implant survival and the lack of peri-implant problems, success rates were calculated.Findings:Mini-implants showed much lower rates of patient pain (P < 0.001) and anchoring loss (P < 0.05) than normal implants; however, they did show comparable rates of space closure (P = 0.42). In addition, mini-implants had comparable success rates (P = 0.78) and greater implant stability (P < 0.01) when compared to normal implants.Conclusion:In conclusion, mini-implants have benefits in anchorage preservation, patient comfort, and implant stability over regular implants when used as orthodontic anchoring devices for space closure. They also offer equivalent effectiveness. These results provide credence to the use of mini-implants as practical substitutes in orthodontic practice.

INTRODUCTION

For individuals who require dental realignment, orthodontic space closure is an essential part of the treatment plan. A significant factor in achieving good closure is the effectiveness and stability of orthodontic anchoring devices. In orthodontic practice, conventional implants have long been used as dependable anchoring devices. However, a different solution with potential benefits has been made available with the introduction of mini-implants. Temporary anchoring devices (TADs) or mini-implants provide a number of advantages over other products. These include their simplicity of insertion, smaller size, and lower potential to cause damage to nearby buildings. Orthodontic professionals have shown a great deal of interest in these qualities, which has led to a change in clinical practice toward their use.

Mini-implants are becoming more and more popular, although there is still disagreement on how effective they are in orthodontic anchoring for space closure when compared to normal implants. Some research indicates that the two types of implants have similar outcomes, whereas other studies present contradicting findings. Furthermore, a variety of considerations, including treatment goals, clinician preference, and patient-specific features, frequently play a role in the clinical decision-making process when it comes to the selection of anchoring devices.

MATERIALS AND TECHNIQUES

In current tertiary care institution, 100 patients between the ages of 15 and 30 who were receiving orthodontic treatment and needed space closure were recruited in this prospective research. Two groups of patients were randomly allocated to receive orthodontic anchoring devices: Group B received regular implants (n = 50), whereas group A received mini-implants (n = 50). For space closure, all patients received regular orthodontic treatment. The rate of space closure, anchoring loss, and patient pain as measured by visual analog scales (VASs) were the primary objectives. Resonance frequency analysis (RFA) was used to assess implant stability. Based on implant survival and the lack of peri-implant problems, success rates were calculated. Appropriate tests were used in statistical analysis to compare the two groups’ results. The institutional review board granted ethical approval, and each subject gave their informed permission before they were enrolled.

RESULTS

Table 1’s findings show that there were no appreciable variations in the two groups’ rates of space closing (P = 0.42). With a P value less than 0.05, mini-implants did, however, show considerably less anchoring loss (10.2 ± 2.1%) than normal implants (15.5 ± 3.4%). Additionally, with a P value less than 0.001, patients in the mini-implant group reported much less discomfort (2.1 ± 0.5 on the VAS) than those in the regular implant group (3.5 ± 0.8).

DISCUSSION

The key finding of this study is that normal implants and mini-implants close space similarly. This reveals that both implant types produce therapeutic effects and increase tooth mobility. Other studies have shown similar gap closure rates, showing mini-implants help anchor orthodontic treatment.[1]

Mini-implants beat normal implants in anchoring preservation by a large margin. Mini-implants’ reduced size and less interference with surrounding teeth may explain their lower anchoring loss rate. Mini-implants exert less biomechanical force to surrounding structures, improving stability and reducing displacement during orthodontic therapy.[2] This discovery is crucial when anchoring reinforcement is needed for prosthesis rehabilitation or tooth movement prevention.

Another important factor supporting mini-implants is patient discomfort, as shown in this study. Mini-implant patients had less discomfort than normal implant patients. Mini-implants reduce discomfort and soft tissue irritation, according to earlier research.[3] Due to their smaller diameter and smoother surface, mini-implants reduce tissue stress during insertion and peri-implant inflammation and discomfort. Because patient comfort affects treatment satisfaction and adherence, mini-implants are beneficial in clinical practice.

Implant stability is essential for orthodontic anchoring. Current research found mini-implants more stable than conventional implants based on RFA outcomes. Mini-implants are less prone to slip or loosen during orthodontic pressure and provide better anchoring. Mini-implants exhibit superior bone-to-implant contact and biomechanical resistance, making them more stable.[4] This shows how important implant stability is for consistent treatment results and reducing treatment failure.

Mini-implants have numerous benefits, but we should know their risks before utilizing them. Mini-implant failure or fracture is a concern, especially when main stability or bone quality is inadequate. Although the present study’s mini-implant success rates were equivalent to regular implants, longer-term monitoring is needed to assess their reliability and longevity. Mini-implants must be carefully placed to avoid damage to neighboring structures and provide the optimal biomechanical support. Mini-implants require physician expertise and competence to get good results.[4]

Other studies have shown mini-implants in orthodontic anchoring for space closure work. Park et al.[1] found that mini-implants outperformed regular implants in anchoring preservation, patient comfort, and treatment efficiency. Mini-implants showed lower anchoring loss and patient discomfort than normal implants, according to Chen et al.’s meta-analysis.[56] These findings confirm previous findings and demonstrate that mini-implants can anchor orthodontic treatment.

CONCLUSION

Finally, current research shows that when it comes to orthodontic anchoring for space closure, mini-implants are superior to regular implants in several ways. Comparable space closure rates are shown by mini-implants, which also offer better anchoring preservation, patient comfort, and implant stability. The increasing use of mini-implants as trustworthy anchoring devices in orthodontic therapy is supported by these findings. Nevertheless, further investigation is required to clarify long-term effects and handle any possible drawbacks related to their application.

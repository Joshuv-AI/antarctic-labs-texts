ProGraphTrans: multimodal dynamic collaborative framework for protein representation learning
Authors: Li Zeng, Yang Liu, Guosheng Han, Zu-Guo Yu, Yuansheng Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAs the core functional carrier of life activities, the quality of protein representation directly affects the accuracy of downstream functional prediction. In recent years, multimodal deep learning methods have significantly improved the effectiveness of protein representation learning by virtue of their advantages in fusing sequence, structure, and chemical characteristics. However, current research still faces two core challenges: first, the guiding mechanism for structural information during multi-modal feature interaction has not been fully explored; second, existing fusion strategies mostly use static weight allocation mechanisms, which is difficult to adapt to sequence-structural features. The dynamic correlation between features leads to limited accuracy in identifying key functional residues.ResultsWe proposed ProGraphTrans, a multimodal dynamic collaborative framework for protein representation learning. ProGraphTrans builds a dynamic attention multimodal fusion mechanism and captures local sequential patterns through a multi-scale convolutional neural network.ConclusionsExperimental results on four protein downstream tasks show that ProGraphTrans not only outperforms other methods in various indicators but also demonstrates excellent interpretability, demonstrating its advantages and effectiveness as a protein representation method.

Background

As the main executors of life activities, proteins are involved in the regulation of biological processes through core mechanisms such as catalyzing metabolic reactions, mediating signal transduction and maintaining structural homeostasis [1]. In the field of biomedical and engineering applications, achieving precision in the downstream prediction tasks of proteins has become a central challenge in advancing drug discovery and synthetic biology, and the breakthrough of this challenge essentially relies on innovative breakthroughs in protein representation methodology [2]. The current research paradigm usually follows the path from representation method development to task application validation, in which researchers first propose novel protein representation methods, and then solve the downstream tasks through direct application or adaptation [3, 4].

With the accumulation of proteomics data, several downstream protein prediction task systems have been gradually established and formed research hotspots [5]. Typical task types cover: protein solubility prediction [6], DNA-binding protein identification [7], protein interaction prediction [8], and its action target inference [9], and so on.

For solutions to these tasks, current protein representation methods are mainly classified into machine learning [10] or deep learning techniques [11]: traditional machine learning frameworks such as plain Bayes, support vector machines [12], random forests, and XGBoost characterize proteins by artificial feature engineering and implement protein function prediction in downstream tasks, while deep learning methods including convolutional neural network [13], recurrent neural network [14], and graph neural network [15] have made significant progress in the field of protein representation learning through end-to-end learning.

In recent years, multimodal deep learning methods [8, 16] have significantly improved the characterization learning effect by fusing sequence, structural and chemical features to further enhance the characterization performance. However, existing methods still face two core challenges: First, the guidance mechanism of structural information during multi-modal feature interaction has not been fully explored. Existing methods usually treat structural data as static auxiliary inputs, such as strictly spliced sequences and structural features [8], instead of dynamically regulating the weight assignment for sequence analysis. This static fusion strategy is difficult to adapt the dynamic correlation characteristics between sequence-structural features, which fails to fully model the dynamic and complex interaction between sequences and structural modes, resulting in limited accuracy in the identification of key functional residues. Second, mainstream methods such as Transformer rely on a fixed attention mechanism, and their linear fusion approach is unable to adaptively emphasize the spatial dependencies among key residues according to protein functional scenarios.

To address the above issues, this study proposes ProGraphTrans, a dynamic collaborative framework for fusing sequence and structure information. The framework firstly designs a struct-guided dynamic attention multimodal fusion mechanism to encode 3D spatial dependencies among residues using graph convolutional network (GCN) to generate protein structural representations with edge-awareness, and dynamically injects geometrical features into the attention computation process of Transformer to enable sequence modeling to perceive local structural key patterns. Second, a multiscale sequence-structure synergy architecture is constructed, which adopts parallel dual paths to process sequence and structure information separately-capturing multi-granularity amino acid sequence features through stacked multiscale convolution, while aggregating residue contact information in graph neural networks, and finally realizing adaptive multi-modal feature through learnable relevance weight matrix Fusion. This dynamic synergistic mechanism effectively solves the modal conflict problem caused by static feature splicing in traditional methods. A series of experimental results show that ProGraphTrans outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of prediction accuracy while providing interpretability that highlights key functional residues.

Results and discussion

In order to verify the effectiveness of the proposed ProGraphTrans, we conducted a series of experiments. First, we perform a feature representation analysis by applying t-SNE dimensionality reduction to compare the discriminative power of ProGraphTransan-derived features with those in a pre-trained language model (PLM). Subsequently, we validate the practical utility of ProGraphTrans by evaluating it in downstream tasks on four representative protein-related benchmark tasks. These experiments were designed to evaluate the model’s ability to improve task-specific performance metrics compared to the baseline approach, thus confirming its effectiveness in real-world protein analysis scenarios. To further investigate the contribution of the dynamic attention fusion module to feature fusion, we compared it with several traditional static feature integration methods (e.g., cascade, summation, and gated fusion). This controlled comparison allowed us to quantify the advantages of our dynamic attention-based fusion mechanism in capturing significant protein sequence features. Finally, we utilize the attention weights generated by the AM for visual interpretation of protein sequences. By mapping the learned attention scores onto protein structures, we aim to identify key functional sites prioritized by the model during feature extraction, thus providing performance validation and biological interpretability.

To verify whether the proposed ProGraphTrans can improve the representation, we visualize the PDB2272 dataset with t-SNE. t-SNE is a nonlinear dimensionality reduction algorithm, which is very suitable for high-dimensional data dimensionality reduction to 2 or 3 dimensions for visualization. In addition, we validated that ProGraphTrans can be applied to multiple language models and improve their performance in DNA-binding proteins prediction task.

A gap appeared between the two clusters, clearly separating the DNA-binding proteins from the non-DNA-binding proteins, showing that ProGraphTrans could improve protein representation.

Evaluate on protein function prediction tasks

To further evaluate the effect of ProGraphTrans on improving protein function prediction, we evaluate ProGraphTrans on two protein function prediction tasks and compare it with other state-of-art methods.

DNA-binding protein prediction

The current research landscape of DNA-binding protein prediction methodologies primarily encompasses two categories: machine learning-based and deep learning-based approaches. In machine learning frameworks, Local-DPP [7] integrates local Pse-PSSM features with a random forest classifier, while DPP-PseAAC [17] utilizes recursive feature elimination (RFE) for optimal eigenstate extraction prior to linear SVM training. However, these conventional methods are constrained by manual feature engineering processes involving descriptors such as Pse-PSSM and EDF-PSSM-DWT, which limit their performance to accuracies generally below 83%. Although Target-DBPPred [18] achieved an accuracy of 82.06% through multi-evolutionary feature integration, its reliance on static feature fusion mechanisms impedes the comprehensive exploration of synergistic structural-sequence interactions. In contrast, ProGraphTrans overcomes these limitations by combining graphical neural networks (GNNs) and multiscale convolutional neural networks through end-to-end learning, dynamically integrating protein structure and sequence information. Compared with Target-DBPred, the accuracy rate is increased by 7.27%, MCC is increased by 20.1%, and specificity is significantly improved by 23.73%, which greatly enhances the recognition ability of non-binding proteins.

Deep learning-based approaches have better performance than machine learning-based approaches due to the automation of feature engineering and the use of more complex networks to classify features. In Table 1, PDBP-fusion [22] used CNN to extract protein evolution information for prediction, and the Acc is 77.77% and MCC is 56.65%. Transformer-BiLSTM [14] extracts protein evolutionary information features by using transformer and fuses it with sequence features extracted by BiLSTM, achieving an Acc of 80.41% and MCC of 62.16%. BiCaps-DBP uses Bi-LSTM and 1D capsule networks to predict DNA-binding proteins from protein sequences and achieved better performance, with Acc and Sen of 83.11% and 89.34%, respectively. In this paper, the proposed protein representation method ProGraphTrans has shown superior performance, overcoming the limitations of single-sequence features by integrating structural features and employing a more effective feature fusion strategy (dynamic multimodal attention), leading to significant improvements. The results obtained for Acc, Sen, Spe, and MCC are 88.33%, 89.93%, 86.73%, and 77.70%, respectively, which represented improvements of 5.22%, 0.59%, 15.94%, and 16.39% over the current state-of-the-art BiCaps-DBP model. Protein solubility prediction

Our models are compared with state-of-the-art methods that can be divided into four categories: machine learning-based methods XGBoost [24] and Support Vector Machine [12]; early deep learning-based methods DeepSol [25] and ProGAN [26]; feature embedding engineering-based methods SeqVec [27] and TAPE [28]; structural features using protein enhanced deep learning methods GraphSol [15].

In order to avoid the influence of data, all machine learning or deep learning-based models are trained and tested on the same training dataset and test dataset, respectively. The data is shown in Table 2. We observe that the machine learning-based methods possess poor performance with Acc below 76.3% and AUC below 84.3%, which may be caused by the fact that the machine learning methods rely heavily on feature engineering. Earlier deep learning-based methods, such as DeepSol and ProGAN, aimed to automate feature construction to address this issue. While they achieved better results than machine learning-based methods, their performance improvements are limited. This is primarily due to the reliance on low-level sequence features of the proteins. In order to enhance protein features, SeqVec attempted to construct a protein-based word embedding model by learning from a large number of protein sequences, which increased the AUC to 85.8%. Subsequently, the structural features of proteins began to attract increasing attention from researchers. Both GraphSol and GATSol, along with the proposed method, opted to integrate structural features with graph networks. This combination aimed to enhance the performance of protein predictors. The key difference lies in the choice of features used to construct the predictors. GraphSol relies on the physicochemical features of proteins, while both GATSol and the proposed method use amino acid node features extracted by a state-of-the-art large language protein model. As a result, both GATSol and the proposed method achieve superior performance. Experimental results show that compared with GraphSol, the ProGraphTrans method proposed in this study improves prediction accuracy by 2.3%. This performance improvement is mainly due to ProGraphTrans ’effective integration of sequence features and graph structure information, while GraphSol relies only on graph data from a single modality. This comparison results verify the effectiveness of ProGraphTrans in improving protein representation. Evaluate on protein interaction prediction

To further evaluate the effect of ProGraphTrans on improving representation, we evaluate ProGraphTrans on top of two protein-related interaction prediction tasks and compare it with other state-of-art methods.

Protein-protein interaction prediction

In order to effectively evaluate the performance of our proposed protein representation method on the protein-protein interaction prediction task [8, 29, 30], we evaluated the method based on the yeast dataset. Since the PPI task requires fusing the representation of two proteins, for simplicity, we directly contact the protein representations decoded by the method. Finally, the prediction results are outputted using a linear layer.

Our method was compared with 9 state-of-the-art sequence-based methods, including two methods based on SVM classifiers, SVM-AC and SVM-MCD, both of which achieve good performance with Acc exceeding 85% (Table 3). The RF-LPQ [35] method is based on random forests and combines with a new feature representation. It first employs a physicochemical property response matrix method to transform amino acid sequences into matrices. Then, local phrase information in the matrices is extracted using texture descriptors based on local phase quantization. On the test dataset, the method achieved a prediction accuracy of 93.92%, a sensitivity of 91.10%, and a precision of 96.45%. However, due to the limitations of feature extraction in machine learning, our proposed method achieves better performance by combining several advanced neural networks for feature extraction. Specifically, compared with the most advanced machine learning method, RF-LPQ, our proposed method is 4.28%, 4.50%, and 7.74% better than RF-LPQ in Acc, F1, and MCC, respectively. Deep learning-based methods perform better than machine learning-based methods because they can automate feature engineering. For example, DeepPPI [29], using the underlying dnn model, outperforms all other machine learning-based methods in terms of accuracy. PIPR [36] incorporates a deep residual recurrent convolutional neural network within the Siamese architecture. This network utilizes robust local features and contextual information. As a result, it effectively captures the interactions of protein sequences and achieves an accuracy exceeding 97%. TAGPPI [8] further develops the deep learning approach by utilizing structural information. It performs a one-dimensional convolutional operation on the protein sequences. Additionally, it applies AlphaFold graph learning to contact maps constructed by AlphaFold, which extracts multidimensional features. Our method is a further improvement on TAGPPI. Both combine structural and sequence features, but our method innovates in two ways: first, the two types of features are fused more efficiently through a dynamic attention multimodal fusion mechanism; second, the expressiveness of the protein features is further enhanced using a large language model. As can be seen from Table 3, our model outperforms other models in all evaluation metrics on the yeast dataset. Specifically, compared with TAGPPI, our method improves the Acc, F1, MCC, and AUC metrics by 1.93%, 1.22%, 0.39%, and 1.89%, respectively.

Ablation experiment

In order to assess whether the performance improvement of ProGrephTrans is mainly contributed by the dynamic multimodal attention mechanism, we designed systematic comparison experiments. The experiments verify the relative merits of dynamic multimodal attention by comparing it to the two most common static feature fusion methods, element-wise addition and concatenation. Furthermore, we verified its effectiveness by ablating the multi-scale CNN mechanism.Fig. 3The dynamic attention fusion methods comparing element-wise addition and concatenation and multi-scale ablation

The dynamic attention fusion methods comparing element-wise addition and concatenation and multi-scale ablation

The specific experimental setup consists of four comparison groups: (a) dynamic multimodal attention, which is the proposed ProGrephTrans method; (b) element-wise addition, which weights summation of structural and sequence features; (c) concatenation, which splies operation of the two features; and (d) the mutli-scale ablation, which removes the multi-CNN method. This hierarchical comparison design can verify the effectiveness of dynamic multimodal attention and the necessity of multi-feature fusion, respectively.

As shown in Fig. 3, the dynamic multimodal attention mechanism significantly outperforms the static feature fusion method in the three key metrics of precision, specificity and MCC. Specifically, its accuracy is improved by 3−4%, which may be attributed to the ability of the attention mechanism to dynamically capture complex interactions between features at different levels, while the static fusion method can only perform simple combinations of fixed patterns. Comparison with the baseline model shows that the introduction of structural features indeed improves model performance: the two static fusion methods bring about a 2−3% improvement in accuracy. Notably, Concatenation outperforms Element-wise Addition, possibly due to the fact that the direct splicing operation retains more complete feature information, whereas there may be feature loss during weighted summation. These results demonstrate that the introduction of structural features contributes substantially to the performance improvement, the dynamic multimodal attention mechanism significantly outperforms the traditional static fusion methods through its dynamic feature integration capability. Observing the experimental results of the ablation group for the multi-scale CNN in the task of DNA-binding protein prediction,, we note that although it demonstrates accuracy comparable to ProGraphTrans, its specificity increases while sensitivity decreases. This indicates that the model misses a significant number of positive cases that should have been detected. The 3.5% lower MCC compared to ProGraphTrans further confirms this issue. MCC serves as a balanced metric incorporating true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives. Its decline clearly reflects a degradation in the overall performance of the model, particularly in its ability to balance positive and negative case identification. This deterioration primarily stems from the constrained receptive field after ablating the multi-scale structure, which hinders the model from effectively capturing critical discriminative features across varying granularities, ultimately leading to the decline in the comprehensive performance metric, the Matthews correlation coefficient. In the task of protein solubility prediction, the ProGraphTrans model demonstrates the best overall performance. Compared to the Element-wise Addition method, its accuracy increases by 1.9 percentage points, while recall improves substantially by 17.2 percentage points. The F1 score also rises by 5.6 percentage points. When compared with the Multi-scale ablation method, ProGraphTrans maintains a superior performance in accuracy. ProGraphTrans visualization research

Using the dynamic multimodal attention module, our method effectively analyzes key residues that play a significant role in protein function. This approach not only provides a reasonable explanation for protein function prediction, but also facilitates the rapid identification of critical sites within proteins for further activity analysis. To validate this methodology, we chose four proteins from the dataset utilized in this study and illustrated their attention structures and positions, as depicted in Fig. 4.

In our analysis, sequence features serve as queries, graph features act as keys, and molecular features represent values. Subsequently, we compute the average attention weights at the molecular level across multiple attention maps generated by multi-head attention to extract relevant attention information pertaining to each protein. Each column within an attention plot reflects the score assigned to one residue relative to others; thus, we can quantify the degree of focus on each residue through calculation. The top 20% of residues deemed significant are highlighted in red. Additionally, we present a visualization of the protein molecule alongside its structure predicted using AlphaFold2.

To further elucidate the biological significance of the method, we conducted a systematic quantitative analysis of DNA binding sites for proteins within the PDB2272 dataset. Specifically, we collected functional annotation information related to DNA binding for all proteins in PDB2272 from the UniProt database and extracted experimentally verified binding site residues. Statistical results reveal that among the 1153 positive samples in this dataset, a total of 405 proteins possess reliable DNA binding site records. The proportion of DNA binding sites relative to all residues averages 4.03%.

We further utilized the trained model to predict these proteins and examined the overlap between the top 5% of residues with the highest attention weights and the experimentally confirmed binding sites. The results demonstrate that 90.86% of the proteins successfully identified functionally validated sites within their top 5% high-attention regions. When expanding the scope to the top 20% of residues, this proportion further increases to 96.92%.

For DNA-binding protein prediction, if a protein is positive, binding sites with DNA must necessarily exist. Quantitative analysis of ProGraphTrans’s dynamic attention mechanism indicates that the proposed mechanism does not assign importance randomly or uniformly. Instead, it autonomously learns and focuses on key regions within proteins that possess specific biological functions. By capturing these features of DNA-binding site, the model achieves reliable predictive performance. We visualized five proteins whose actual binding sites range between 30 and 70 residues in length in Fig. 5. Residues marked in green indicate overlaps between the top 5% attention score residues and the actual binding sites.

Evaluating the influence of protein strutct quality

To quantitatively assess the robustness of ProGraphTrans to variations in the quality of input structural information, we simulated the effect of receiving poor-quality structural predictions by randomly masking out a defined percentage of the structural features in the training data. Specifically, when 10% of the structural features were masked, the accuracy on the DNA-binding prediction task decreased by merely 1.8% at accuracy. At a more substantial masking ratio of 20%, the performance decline remained modest, with a reduction of 2.9% in DNA-binding accuracy. Even when 30% of the structural information was omitted, the model’s performance was notably preserved, with accuracy declining by only 4.2%, respectively. This robustness likely stems from the model’s training on AlphaFold-predicted structures, which inherently contain structural error to force model learns the global structural representation thereby learning to compensate for imperfect structural information.

Discussion

Conclusions

In this study, we propose a comprehensive protein representation method, named ProGraphTrans, which enhances the accuracy of protein function prediction by dynamic multimodal attention mechanism to dynamicly integrating protein sequence features and structural features. Extensive experimental results have shown that this method can effectively improve protein representation and improve its performance in protein-related tasks. However, the current method still has certain limitations, particularly in the exploration of protein three-dimensional structures. Future work will focus on incorporating more complex three-dimensional structural features and further enhancing the model’s generalization ability and its capacity to handle complex tasks through deeper structure-sequence collaborative learning.

These proteins play a key role in gene expression regulation, DNA repair, replication, and recombination [40]. Accurate prediction of DNA-binding proteins helps scientists understand intracellular gene regulation mechanisms and reveal the molecular basis of various biological phenomena.

The PDB14189 and PDB2272 datasets [22, 41] are derived from the DNA-binding protein dataset in the UniProt database. The PDB14189 dataset consists of 7129 positive samples and 7060 negative samples. Protein-protein interaction prediction

PPI prediction aims to identify potential interactions between proteins using computational methods [42, 43]. This task is essential for understanding the complex networks and revealing protein functions and their role within cells [44].

The intraspecies PPI dataset of yeast [33] is widely used as a benchmark for state-of-the-art methods. It contains 2497 proteins, with 5594 positive samples and 5594 negative samples. The high-quality yeast dataset is extracted from the database of interacting protein and contains only the most reliable physical interactions. Negative samples are generated by randomly pairing proteins without evidence of interaction. Evaluation metrics of the task include accuracy, F1 score, MCC, and AUC.

Compound-protein interaction prediction

CPI prediction aims to identify possible interactions between drug molecules and target proteins through computational methods [45]. This research contributes to drug development, understanding compound mechanisms and side effects, and designing personalized medical treatments [46].

We evaluate our method on a Human benchmark dataset. This dataset includes positive CPI pairs from DrugBank 4.1 [47] and Matador, along with high-confidence negative CPI samples obtained using a systematic screening framework [48]. Specifically, the dataset contains 3369 positive interactions between 1052 unique compounds and 852 unique proteins. The dataset is randomly divided into training, validation, and test sets [9]. This task is evaluated using the AUC, Recall, and Precision metrics.

Protein solubility prediction

Protein solubility prediction is crucial for protein research and applications, as protein solubility directly affects in vivo function, biologics production, and drug development [49].

We used the eSAL dataset from a previous work [50]. The entire solubility database of Escherichia coli proteins is downloaded from the eSAL website, where solubility is defined as the ratio of the supernatant to the total fraction in a physicochemical experiment called PURE [15, 51]. The 4132 protein matter is first mapped to the NCBI database by gene names, and 3, 144 samples are obtained. Based on previous observations, we further pruned all sequences using strict criteria with sequence identity or E-values, and the final data included a total of 2737 protein sequences. From the dataset, 75% (2052 proteins) are randomly selected as the training set, and the remaining 25% (685 proteins) are used as independent tests. Dynamic attention multimodal fusion mechanism

Relying solely on the similarity between amino acid sequences to construct attention is not enough to accurately focus on key amino acids. This approach ignores the fact that proteins are organic matter with complex structures, and these structural features are essential for the correct characterization of proteins [58]. In order to overcome this limitation, we propose a dynamic multimodal attention mechanism.

The decoder uses a multi-head attention mechanism to construct the feature space of protein residues [37]. By interacting the structural features obtained by the structural feature encoder with the sequence features obtained by the sequence feature encoder, we obtain the attention score of the residue. This attention score reveals the importance of each protein residue in the task, enhancing the predictive ability of protein function. In the attention mechanism, Q represents the query vector, K represents the key vector, and V represents the value vector, which weights the value by calculating the relevance of the query to the key, thus gaining attention for the content. Unlike previous methods, several vectors in this method are not all sequential features. Specifically, Q represents the sequence features of the protein, which comes from the sequence encoder. K represents the structural representation of the protein, which comes from the structural encoder. Vto is the feature of weighted fusion of structure encoder and sequence encoder. Value cannot be a pure sequence feature, as this would lead to an information gap between the structure-guided attention focusing and the actual semantic aggregation. Therefore, in the process of attention establishment, the residue features represented by the sequence will be queried to other residues with structural features so that they are guided by the graph.

Our decoder is a simplified transformer architecture that uses an attention mechanism to process the input. Attention score is obtained by the softmax function, which reflects the importance of the eigenvector V for each residue in the current query.

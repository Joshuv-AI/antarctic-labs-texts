Prediction of primary glaucoma: development and validation of multiple machine learning models
Authors: Huiwang Zhang, Zhenghong Luo, Huixian Xiong, Mingxuan Xie, Ping Shuai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To develop and validate a machine learning model by combining blood markers with retinal structural parameters to predict the risk of primary glaucoma and provide a risk assessment tool for early detection of primary glaucoma. A case–control study was adopted, and 268 glaucoma patients and 1072 non-glaucoma patients were included in Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital from February 2023 to November 2024. Candidate predictors include demographic data, blood markers, and retinal structural parameters. Five feature selection methods and six machine learning algorithms were used to develop the model and compare their predictive performance. Eleven potential predictors associated with primary glaucoma were screened and identified, which were subsequently used to construct a prediction model for primary glaucoma. The cross-validation results indicate that the model built using the Xtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) algorithm (AUC: 0.986, 95% CI 0.974–0.995) showed relatively better comprehensive predictive performance among the six machine learning algorithms. The SHAP diagram visualized and interpreted the risk prediction model for primary glaucoma. The XGBoost model exhibited relatively better comprehensive predictive performance, and it is of great significance to apply these models to predict and screen for primary glaucoma early.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-45340-4.

Introduction

Glaucoma, the world’s leading blinding eye disease with irreversible vision loss, is projected to affect approximately 112 million people by 2040, placing an enormous economic burden on individuals and healthcare systems1,2. This disease is essentially a characteristic optic nerve disorder, characterized by pathological manifestations that include progressive loss of retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) and their axons, as well as damage to the optic nerve. The loss of vision is often not recognised until late in the course of the disease due to the slow decline in visual acuity, the often asymmetric nature of the lesions in both eyes and the fact that the neural mechanisms compensate for some areas of visual field loss3.

Degenerative changes in the RGC usually accompany glaucoma-related vision loss. The Optic Nerve Head (ONH) is a key site of glaucomatous degeneration where axons of the RGC cross and exit the eye to transmit signals to the central nervous system. Progressive neurodegenerative lesions in glaucoma show a fan-shaped pattern that is similar to the visual loss manifested in glaucoma patients4,5. This characteristic of ONH damage and loss of RGC axons in glaucoma results in the loss of retinal neural tissue, specifically cupping of the ONH, which produces a distinct pattern of visual field loss, ultimately leading to visual impairment6. Blood reflects underlying RGC degeneration through multiple pathways, which in turn serve as biomarkers to reveal the underlying pathophysiology of glaucoma. Indirect parameters associated with blood cell counts can reflect abnormal immune system activity in glaucoma patients. Neutrophil counts, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) were found to be significantly increased in patients with glaucoma, while lymphocyte count, platelet, Lymphocyte-to-monocyte Ratio (LMR) were decreased, while NLR was also found to have potential value in reflecting disease severity7,8. NLR and PLR have been defined as inflammatory biomarkers. Although they are commonly used as predictors of prognosis in certain diseases (e.g., cancer9 and acute ischaemic stroke10, it has also been suggested that the same association exists in glaucoma patients11. A retrospective study investigated the predictive value of NLR and PLR levels in disease progression and prognosis in patients with POAG and high IOP12. Li et al.13 also investigated a significant positive correlation between NLR and the severity of PACG, whereas LMR was negatively correlated with the severity of PACG. Circulating blood cells, including erythrocytes, leukocytes and platelets, can be easily counted and measured and are the most common laboratory tests in medicine, and abnormalities in these parameters usually provide important clues for the diagnosis of different diseases14. Although the structural damage within the eye and the loss of visual function due to glaucoma are irreversible, available treatments can slow or stop its further progression.

Therefore, given the irreversibility of this damage and the insidious nature of its onset, machine learning algorithms were chosen in this study to construct a primary glaucoma prediction model and screen the optimal model, with a view to assisting medical personnel in screening primary glaucoma more conveniently and accurately at an early stage, and providing an auxiliary diagnostic tool for clinical prevention and control efforts, so that irreversible loss of vision can be prevented or delayed, and the burden of the disease can be reduced.

Methods

Study population

A case-control study design was used in this study, and 268 glaucoma patients and 1072 non-glaucoma patients from Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital from February 2023 to November 2024 were included in the study. Glaucoma patients were obtained from the Physical Examination Centre and Ophthalmology Outpatient Clinic of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital, and all patients were diagnosed by the Ophthalmology Outpatient Clinic of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital, while non-glaucoma patients were recruited from the Health Management Centre of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital. Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) of the optic disc area and blood tests were performed in all study subjects. For the case group, patients (i) aged ≥ 18 years, (ii) diagnosed with primary glaucoma in the ophthalmology clinic were included; patients (i) unable to cooperate with ophthalmological examination, such as corrected visual acuity < 0.1 or severe cataract, (ii) combined with hepatic or renal insufficiency, serious infection, major surgery or malignant tumour, (iii) with a history of intraocular diseases, such as retinopathy, uveitis, or ocular trauma, and (iv) with optic nerve lesions caused by non-glaucomatous factors, were excluded. For the control group, the subjects with (i) age ≥ 18 years old, (ii) no history of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) with two or more times of IOP ≤ 21 mmHg, (iii) no pathological changes of the fundus, and (iv) no abnormality on OCT were included; the subjects with (i) corrected visual acuity <0.1, or severe cataracts who were unable to cooperate with the completion of ophthalmological examination were excluded, and (ii) those with combined hepatic and renal insufficiencies, serious infections, major surgeries, or malignancies were excluded, as well as (iii) those with a history of eye-related diseases and intraocular surgeries. Relevant medical history and intraocular surgery history.

The aims and procedures of the study were explained to all participants, and after obtaining informed consent, the study was conducted. The study was approved by the Ethical Committee for Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital, with decision number 2023-116, and was carried out in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration.

Diagnostic criteria for primary glaucoma

Primary open-angle glaucoma open anterior chamber angle, pathologically high intraocular pressure (peak 24-hour IOP exceeding 21 mmHg) or normal intraocular pressure, the presence of characteristic glaucomatous damage to the fundus (morphologic changes in the optic disc and RNFL) and/or glaucomatous damage to the visual field, and the exclusion of other factors that cause elevated intraocular pressure or the exclusion of other optic neuropathies that have similar ocular manifestations (e.g., hereditary optic neuropathies, congenital optic disc defects, etc.)17.Primary angle-closure glaucoma for patients with acute or chronic elevation of IOP due to anterior chamber angle closure with glaucomatous optic disc changes and/or visual field damage, the diagnosis is Primary angle-closure glaucoma; for patients with only anterior chamber angle closure and IOP elevation without glaucomatous changes, the diagnosis is Primary angle-closure suspect, and these patients were excluded from our study population17.

Feature selection

Before developing the predictive models, seven feature selection methods were applied to the study population to mitigate high correlations among predictors and capture complex relationships between predictors and the outcome variable. These methods were chosen based on their established effectiveness in handling high-dimensional data and identifying the most relevant predictors in predictive modeling. The feature selection methods used were:

Stepwise regression (SR) This method can iteratively add or remove predictors based on statistical significance, making it useful for identifying the most important variables while controlling for multicollinearity. We implemented three variations of stepwise regression: forward selection (FS, starts with an empty model, and iteratively adds the predictor with the most significant statistical association with the outcome variable one by one until no additional predictor can improve the model performance), backward selection (BS, starts with a full model including all candidate predictors, and iteratively removes the predictor with the least significant statistical association with the outcome variable one by one until no removal of predictor can improve the model performance), and bidirectional elimination (BE, a combination of forward selection and backward selection, which iteratively adds significant predictors and removes insignificant predictors at each step until the optimal model is obtained).Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) By applying a penalty to the coefficients of less important predictors, LASSO helps to shrink coefficients toward zero, thus identifying a subset of relevant features while avoiding overfitting.One-way regression analysis exclusion potential predictor variables were screened by one-way analysis using a screening criterion of P < 0.05.

We summarized 26 candidate predictors screened by different filtering methods and took their intersection (adopted a feature as the final candidate predictor if it was selected by at least four of the five feature selection methods). Under this approach, we chose the predictors with more than four intersections. Finally, we consulted with clinical experts and combined them with clinical reality to determine the final predictors for developing a predictive model of primary glaucoma.

Model development and evaluation

We randomly split the entire dataset into a training set and a validation set in a 6:4 ratio. The training set is used for model development of ten machine learning algorithms, while the validation set is used for model evaluation. The machine learning algorithms include Logistic Regression (LR), Xtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), Random Forest (RF), K-nearest Neighbor (KNN), and Neural Network (NN). A grid search with five-fold cross-validation was used to obtain optimized parameters (Supplementary Table S3 shows the hyperparameter tuning details). For parameter selection (hyperparameter tuning) in each fold of the five-fold cross-validation: the hyperparameter selection was strictly performed on the inner training set (4/5 of the training dataset) with the inner validation set (1/5 of the training dataset) used for testing and evaluating the model performance with different hyperparameter combinations. Specifically, we used grid search to generate a series of hyperparameter combinations for each machine learning algorithm; for each hyperparameter combination, we trained the model on the inner training set and tested its performance on the inner validation set; the hyperparameter combination with the best performance on the inner validation set was selected as the optimal hyperparameter for that fold.

We assessed and compared the discriminatory, calibration, and clinical net benefit performance of all models. Multiple discriminatory metrics included the area under the receiver operating curve (AUC), threshold, accuracy, sensitivity (SEN, this is also called Recall), specificity, the positive predictive value (PPV, this is also called precision), the negative predictive value (NPV), and the F1-Score. Calibration was assessed by calibration plots. Clinical usefulness and net benefit were assessed using decision curve analysis (DCA). For interpreting complex optimal models, the SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) was implemented in R using the “shapviz” package. In brief, SHAP values are a unified framework for interpreting the predictions of any machine learning model, which is rooted in the game theory concept of Shapley values. Its basic connotation is to quantify the contribution of each feature to the prediction result of a single sample by calculating the average marginal contribution of a feature across all possible subsets of features. The core operating principle is as follows: (1) For a given prediction sample, generate all possible combinations of input features; (2) Calculate the prediction output of the model for each feature subset; (3) Compute the marginal contribution of the target feature when added to different feature subsets; (4) Take the weighted average of these marginal contributions (weighted by the size of the feature subsets) to obtain the SHAP value of the target feature for the sample. A positive SHAP value indicates that the feature increases the predicted risk of primary glaucoma, while a negative SHAP value means the feature reduces the predicted risk. Statistical analysis

We interpolated missing values using the Random Forest algorithm in the R package “missForest”, which estimates missing values from observed patterns in the original data without creating multiple datasets, up to a maximum of 6% for all variables. Specifically, missing values were present in partial retinal structural parameters (e.g., Rim Volume, VCDR), Live vision and IOP in our dataset. In addition, in the case of sparse data, categories were combined if necessary. In the statistical description, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was performed on the data to assess normality. For measures that conformed to a normal distribution, they were described using the mean (SD), and comparisons between groups were made using the student’s t-test; for measures that were not normally distributed, they were described using the median (interquartile range [IQR]), and comparisons between groups were made using the Mann–Whitney U test between two groups; and for counts, they were expressed as n (%), and comparisons between groups were made using the chi-square test or the Fisher exact test. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided P value < 0.05. All data were analyzed using R statistical software (version 4.3.3) and SPSS (version 27.0).

Optimal model interpretability

To explain the complex XGBoost model, SHAP values were applied to illustrate how predictors affect primary glaucoma. The feature importance and interpretation of the XGBoost model are shown in Fig. 4. The results showed that vertical cup-to-disc ratio, Superior-RNFL, Inferior-RNFL, and Inferior Retina were more important than other factors, as shown in Fig. 4A. In addition, Fig. 4B clearly illustrates the strength and direction of each predictor, with a larger vertical cup-to-disc ratio indicating a higher risk of primary glaucoma and a larger Inferior-RNFL below being associated with a lower risk of primary glaucoma. For local interpretability, Fig. 4C provides a typical sample and shows how the XGBoost model makes clinical decisions for individuals. The SHAP value for each predictor serves as the force that drives the overall SHAP value (1 = with primary glaucoma, 0 = without primary glaucoma) higher (orange) or lower (purple) and combines to predict the risk of primary glaucoma. For example, in Fig. 4C, one study subject was predicted to have a lower risk of developing primary glaucoma due to the vertical cup-to-disc ratio, Inferior-RNFL, IOP, and Inferior-Retina offsetting the risk posed by LMR and the five other characteristics.

Discussion

This study presents a relatively novel approach to identifying risk factors for predicting primary glaucoma by integrating five feature selection techniques with retinal structural parameters and blood marker data. Unlike previous models that rely on a limited number of predictors, this study combines these methods to systematically identify the most relevant risk factors, ensuring model robustness and extrapolation. Furthermore, a comprehensive evaluation of different modelling strategies by comparing six machine learning algorithms shows that the XGBoost algorithm showed relatively better predictive performance than the other five algorithms in this study. Notably, by employing SHAP values to enhance interpretability, this study bridges the gap between complex machine learning models and real-world clinical decisions, enabling healthcare providers to understand and trust the model’s predictions.

Eleven features associated with primary glaucoma were selected for this study, including Superior-RNFL, Inferior-RNFL, Superior-Retina, Inferior-Retina, Cup Volume, vertical cup-to-disc ratio, LMR, NLR, sex, IOP, and moderate or high myopia (Fig. 2). Of these, vertical cup-to-disc ratio, Inferior-RNFL, and Inferior-Retina were identified as the most significant predictors based on importance scores (Fig. 4A). The results of this study showed that an increased vertical cup-to-disc ratio was significantly associated with an increased risk of primary glaucoma. Increased cup-to-disc ratio is one of the most important indicators for glaucoma screening and diagnosis, and in glaucoma patients, increased cup-to-disc ratio reflects structural changes in the optic nerve head18. In addition, increased cup-to-disc ratio has been correlated with the extent of damage to the optic nerve fiber layer19, and studies have shown that glaucoma can trigger characteristic morphological abnormalities in the optic disc region, including focal/diffuse tissue defects at the margins, progressive enlargement of the optic cup, optic disc haemorrhage-associated RNFL damage, and peripapillary atrophy, which are typical manifestations of glaucoma20. A study by Shiga’s team showed that structural changes in the optic disc significantly preceded detectable functional impairment of the visual field21. Therefore, by accurately assessing optic disc morphological features in clinical practice, it not only helps to identify early biological markers of glaucoma in high-risk groups but also significantly improves the disease detection rate, which can provide a basis for developing effective intervention strategies, thus reducing the risk of blindness. Among the many quantitative indicators, cup-to-disc area ratio, vertical cup-to-disc ratio, disc rim area and volume, and optic cup parameters are important biomarkers for assessing disease progression. Notably, the vertical cup-to-disc ratio has high specificity and sensitivity in the diagnosis of glaucoma and is considered to be a core indicator of the extent of optic nerve damage22. In addition, we found that LMR and NLR are important predictors of primary glaucoma. Studies have shown that not only localised neuroinflammation of the retina but also systemic inflammation plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of glaucoma7. Therefore, systemic inflammation indicators have been proposed as indicators for disease diagnosis and prediction of clinical outcomes. Blood markers such as leukocytes, neutrophils, NLR, PLR, and LMR are direct indicators of systemic inflammation. These biomarkers have shown strong value as new tools for early screening and individualised identification of glaucoma23. Leukocyte and neutrophil counts, as well as NLR and PLR, have been found to be significantly increased in glaucoma patients, whereas lymphocyte counts, platelets in patients with PACG, and LMR are decreased, while NLR has also been found to be potentially valuable in reflecting disease severity7,24. Parameters combining haematocrit and serum biochemical markers (e.g., NAR) have recently emerged to indicate systemic inflammation and have been applied to inflammatory and vascular diseases and cancer.

The XGBoost model exhibited relatively better discriminatory power, calibration, and net clinical benefit among all the constructed models. Overall, the newly developed XGBoost model performed well, with an AUC value of 0.988 (95% CI 0.978–0.995) in the validation set. In addition to the AUC, we evaluated the performance of the XGBoost model using other evaluation metrics, including accuracy, sensitivity, and F1 scores. The XGBoost model showed excellent interpretability and high performance on these key metrics, and it also has strong self-learning capability, which makes it particularly suitable for capturing complex non-linear relationships in the data. The high F1 score of the XGBoost model indicates that the model strikes a reasonable balance between precision and recall, thus ensuring that false positives and false negatives are minimised, which is critical in clinical decision-making. For the calibration terms, the agreement between predictions and actuals is shown in the calibration plots. More importantly, the decision curve analysis shows that the XGBoost model can provide good clinical net benefits to support clinical decision making. In terms of model applicability, the XGBoost model has a strong self-learning capability and has an outstanding advantage in dealing with non-linear relationships. The XGBoost model exhibited relatively better discriminatory power, calibration, and net clinical benefit among all the constructed models, and the differences in predictive performance among all the models were not statistically significant. In future studies, incorporating more retinal structural parameters and blood marker data may achieve better prediction results.

Despite the growing interest in machine learning models for clinical decision making, most published predictive models never reach clinical practice. This may be because the models are difficult to understand or because the results lack representativeness and reproducibility7. In this study, we mainly included clinically applicable and easily recognisable predictors. It is worth noting that due to its black-box nature, it is difficult for XGBoost models to provide meaningful clinical interpretations. Interpretability is often defined as the ease with which humans can understand and explain the prediction process of a machine learning model25. To solve this problem, SHAP values are applied to obtain more understandable explanations. SHAP values are widely accepted and help to explain the relationship between variables and results26. It can help physicians to better understand the decision-making process of the model and provide appropriate early intervention for primary glaucoma.

For developed machine learning models, healthcare providers may need training to effectively interpret machine learning-derived risk scores and SHAP-based interpretations. While SHAP values enhance interpretability, clinicians must remain vigilant to avoid over-reliance on model outputs. Therefore, it is recommended that model predictions be integrated with clinical judgement and symptom monitoring to ensure the safety and well-being of screeners. In addition, clinicians should be aware of the potential psychological impact on patients when using these assessment tools for risk prediction, and appropriate counselling should be provided when necessary.

Limitations

Several study limitations require discussion. First, while robust internal validation through cross-validation enhances the rigor of the method, the lack of external validation on independent data remains a key constraint. The single-center nature of the dataset introduces potential selection bias and may limit the model’s generalizability to populations with different demographic characteristics, clinical practices, or regional healthcare systems. The glaucoma patients included in our study were recruited from the Ophthalmology Outpatient Clinic of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital, and the disease severity of these patients may be relatively higher than that of early glaucoma patients in the general population screening context, which may limit the direct applicability of our model to population-based early screening. To address this issue, future multi-center collaborations will prioritize validating and refining the model in different settings to ensure broader clinical applicability, and further validate and optimize the model to improve its applicability in early screening. Second, this study employed a case-control design. While case-control studies can reveal the characteristics and associated factors of study subjects at a specific point in time, they cannot establish causal relationships or observe trends over time. This limitation impairs the study’s ability to interpret causal links between variables and dynamic changes. Finally, machine learning models have inherent limitations, such as the need to balance model complexity and computational efficiency in clinical applications. While XGBoost performs exceptionally well, its “black box” nature may reduce physician trust.

Conclusion

In this study, a machine learning prediction model was developed and validated to determine the risk of primary glaucoma by combining retinal structural parameters and blood markers of primary glaucoma. The XGBoost model demonstrated excellent and reliable prediction performance and was the algorithm with relatively better performance among the six machine learning algorithms. The developed model can help physicians to make clinical decisions easily; therefore, this study can be used to individualise the prediction of primary glaucoma risk, thus assisting clinicians to make early and precise interventions to effectively reduce or delay the occurrence of primary glaucoma. However, in future studies, external validation is needed to demonstrate the accuracy of the model predictions.

Discharge Learning Needs and Associated Factors Among Patients Undergoing Cardiac Surgery: A Cross‐Sectional Study
Authors: Serdar Sever, Buse Bostan, Halime Çayli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimThis study aimed to determine the discharge learning needs of patients undergoing cardiac surgery and to identify sociodemographic and clinical factors associated with these needs.DesignA descriptive, cross‐sectional study.MethodsThis study was conducted among 130 adults who had undergone cardiac surgery in a public hospital in western Türkiye between 15 January and 30 August 2025. Data were collected at hospital discharge using a patient information form and the Patient Learning Needs Scale (PLNS), which measures learning needs across seven domains. Descriptive statistics, independent‐samples t‐tests, and multiple linear regression analysis were used to examine associations between patient characteristics and PLNS scores.ResultsOverall learning needs were moderately high (mean total PLNS score 183.65 ± 25.25). Treatment and complications (35.62 ± 5.04) and activities of daily living (32.92 ± 6.19) were the highest‐priority domains. These were followed by enhancing quality of life (31.77 ± 4.71) and medications (31.13 ± 5.09), while the feelings‐related‐to‐condition domain had the lowest importance (15.54 ± 3.99). Female patients reported significantly higher total PLNS scores than males (190.43 ± 25.36 vs. 177.65 ± 23.74, p = 0.002). In multivariable analysis, male gender (B = −15.27, p < 0.001) and lack of health insurance (B = −17.71, p = 0.004) were independently associated with lower total PLNS scores.ConclusionPatients undergoing cardiac surgery have important discharge learning needs that vary according to gender, education, employment status and insurance coverage. Systematic assessment of learning needs can help nurses design individualized, patient‐centred discharge education to support safer recovery.Implications for the Profession and/or Patient CareThis study highlights the importance of systematically assessing discharge learning needs among patients undergoing cardiac surgery. Understanding individual learning priorities enables nurses to tailor discharge education, address potential gaps in self‐care knowledge, and enhance continuity of care during the transition from hospital to home. Incorporating structured learning needs assessments into routine discharge planning may support safer recovery, improve adherence to postoperative recommendations, and reduce preventable complications.Reporting MethodThis study was reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.Patient or Public ContributionPatients were involved in the study by completing study questionnaires at the time of hospital discharge. However, patients or members of the public were not involved in the study design, analysis, interpretation of data, or preparation of the manuscript.Keywords: cardiac surgical procedures, learning needs, patient discharge, patient education

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of death worldwide, resulting in about 19.8 million deaths in 2022, around 32% of all global mortality (World Health Organization 2025). Surgeries such as coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) and valve repair are essential for extending life and improving health outcomes; however, their complexity often results in notable physical, emotional, social, and economic challenges for patients (Bueno et al. 2025; Byrne et al. 2023; Shahmoradi et al. 2022). The recovery period is long, spanning from the immediate hospital phase to the weeks following discharge, when many patients struggle with anxiety, depression and uncertainty about what lies ahead (Wu et al. 2025; Clarke et al. 2022).

The transition from hospital to home is a critical stage in recovery and often one of the most challenging parts of the surgical process (Ji et al. 2025). During this phase, patients are required to adjust their daily routines, care for postoperative wounds, and develop adequate self‐management skills to promote optimal recovery (Tobiano et al. 2025). Nonetheless, previous qualitative studies indicate that the first weeks following hospital discharge represent a particularly vulnerable transition period, during which patients frequently experience uncertainty, symptom burden, and insufficient preparedness for self‐care at home (Wu et al. 2025; Brandberg et al. 2021; Oravec et al. 2023). Limited comprehension of discharge instructions and non‐individualized education may hinder effective self‐management after cardiac surgery, potentially resulting in poor adherence to care recommendations and delayed identification of postoperative complications (Brandberg et al. 2021; Alkuwaisi et al. 2024; Becker et al. 2021).

Effective discharge education can create a clear link between hospital and home care, which plays an important role in the early detection of complications and may help reduce hospital readmissions (Becker et al. 2021; Fatani et al. 2025). Contemporary healthcare prioritizes a patient‐centered approach, wherein clinicians and patients collaborate to develop care plans that respect personal values and preferences (Tringale et al. 2022).

A previous study has shown that, although educational interventions can improve patients' preparedness for post‐discharge care, many existing programs do not adequately address essential topics required during the early recovery period from cardiac surgery (Avcı ışık and Karaöz 2022). This indicates a need for more targeted educational approaches that align with patients' immediate postoperative needs. Similarly, a recent randomized controlled trial (RCT) found that standardized education does not consistently lead to better self‐care practices, as uniform interventions may overlook individual learning requirements and therefore fail to improve patient behaviours (Alkuwaisi et al. 2024). A systematic review reported substantial variation in the content and delivery of education provided to patients undergoing cardiac surgery (Shahmoradi et al. 2022). Standardized discharge education alone may be insufficient to support recovery because it often does not reflect individual patient concerns and capacities. Tailored educational approaches have therefore been recommended to improve self‐care and adaptation following discharge (Shahmoradi et al. 2022; Alkuwaisi et al. 2024). Overall, these studies reinforce that generic education models are inadequate and highlight the importance of personalized educational strategies that reflect each patient's unique needs during the early recovery phase. Therefore, identifying patients' learning priorities is a fundamental first step in designing effective educational interventions.

Previous studies have examined discharge learning needs among cardiac surgery patients in different cultural contexts (Alkubati et al. 2013; Al‐Maskari et al. 2021). For example, studies conducted in Oman and Yemen reported high levels of learning needs, particularly in areas related to treatment complications, medication management, and lifestyle modification (Alkubati et al. 2013; Al‐Maskari et al. 2021). However, these studies were conducted in different healthcare systems and socio‐economic environments, and limited evidence exists regarding the determinants of learning needs in Türkiye, particularly in relation to health insurance status and social vulnerability. Discharge learning needs after cardiac surgery are likely to be shaped not only by the medical demands of recovery, but also by patients' sociodemographic characteristics, social support, and access to resources. Based on previous literature and clinical reasoning, factors such as gender, education, employment, health insurance, caregiver support, and comorbidities were considered particularly relevant because they may influence patients' health literacy, ability to obtain and use health information, perceived preparedness for discharge, and capacity for self‐care at home (Alkubati et al. 2013; Al‐Maskari et al. 2021; Short et al. 2025). Therefore, this study examined these factors in relation to discharge learning needs among patients undergoing cardiac surgery. Furthermore, previous studies focused primarily on descriptive comparisons rather than identifying independent determinants of learning needs using multivariable analysis (Alkubati et al. 2013; Al‐Maskari et al. 2021). Therefore, there remains a need to investigate the determinants of discharge learning needs among cardiac surgery patients. Accordingly, this study aimed to assess the discharge learning needs of patients undergoing cardiovascular surgery and identify the factors influencing these needs in order to support a more personalized and effective recovery process.

Methods

This study was reported in accordance with the STROBE reporting guidelines to ensure transparency and completeness of reporting (Elm et al. 2007).

Study Design, Setting and Sample

A descriptive, cross‐sectional design was used to evaluate discharge learning needs among patients admitted to a cardiovascular surgery ward. The study was carried out from 15 January to 30 August 2025 in a public hospital located in western Türkiye. All patients who underwent cardiac surgery during the study period constituted the target population, and those who met the eligibility criteria and provided informed consent were enrolled. Using G*Power (v3.1.9.7), the minimum required sample size for multiple linear regression was estimated assuming a medium effect size (f 2 = 0.15), an alpha level of 0.05, and a power of 95%, resulting in a minimum of 119 participants (Kang 2021). A medium effect size was selected based on Cohen's recommendations and because previous studies examining discharge learning needs in cardiac surgery populations were primarily descriptive or bivariate in nature and did not provide regression‐based effect size estimates that could be directly applied to a multiple regression power analysis (Alkubati et al. 2013; Al‐Maskari et al. 2021). To account for possible incomplete data or non‐response, the target sample size was increased, and 130 eligible patients were ultimately recruited using convenience sampling. Participants were eligible if they (a) were aged 18 years or older, (b) had no cognitive impairment, (c) were not healthcare professionals, and (d) had undergone elective CABG, heart valve, or vascular surgery and were being discharged from the cardiovascular surgery ward.

Although the study included patients undergoing CABG, valve, and vascular surgery, all participants were managed within the same cardiovascular surgery ward and followed standardized postoperative discharge protocols. Therefore, discharge education content was largely comparable across surgical subgroups. Given the sample size, stratified analyses were not performed; however, surgical procedure type will be considered in future larger‐scale studies.

Data Collection Tools

Data were gathered using a patient information form and the Patient Learning Needs Scale (PLNS).

Patient Information Form

The questionnaire was created by the researchers to capture characteristics that could affect patients' learning needs, based on the existing literature (Shahmoradi et al. 2022; Gökçen and Köçkar 2018; Erdoğan and Yılmaz 2025). It included 15 items on key sociodemographic and clinical factors, including age, gender, education status and marital status, working status, level of income, place of residence, household members and health insurance. It also assessed a history of previous surgery, availability of a postoperative support person, presence of comorbidities, whether discharge information was received, source of discharge information and patients' preferred source of discharge information.

Patient Learning Needs Scale (PLNS)

The Patient Learning Needs Scale (PLNS), developed by Bubela and colleagues, is designed to assess surgical patients' learning needs and priorities at the time of discharge (Bubela et al. 1990). In the Turkish validity and reliability study by Çatal and Dicle, the PLNS demonstrated high internal consistency, with a total Cronbach's alpha of 0.93 and subscale alpha coefficients ranging from 0.61 to 0.83 (Çatal and Dicle 2008). The PLNS includes 50 items across seven subdimensions: treatment and complications (9 items), activities of living (9 items), medications (8 items), community and follow‐up (6 items), feelings related to condition (5 items), enhancing quality of life (8 items) and skin care (5 items). Responses are recorded on a 5‐point Likert scale from 1 (not important) to 5 (extremely important). Scores can be calculated for each subdimension as well as for the overall scale; total scores range from 50 to 250. The scale has no cutoff value, and higher scores reflect greater perceived importance of learning needs (Bubela et al. 1990; Çatal and Dicle 2008). In the present study, the scale demonstrated high internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha = 0.92).

Data Collection Process

On the day of discharge, after undergoing cardiovascular surgery and before leaving the cardiovascular surgery ward, patients were provided with the questionnaires and were allowed sufficient time to complete them. The researcher presented the study information both verbally and in written form, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The questionnaires were completed either in patients' rooms or in designated areas within the cardiovascular surgery unit, and completion typically took about 15–20 min. For participants who experienced reading difficulties or low literacy, the researcher provided standardized verbal assistance by reading the items aloud in a neutral tone without interpretation or guidance. Care was taken to ensure privacy and minimize response bias.

Statistical Analysis

Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 31.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The distribution of continuous variables was checked by examining skewness–kurtosis values and histogram plots. Since PLNS scores were found to be approximately normally distributed, parametric statistical methods were applied. Descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation, frequency and percentage) were used to present the sociodemographic and clinical data. The assumptions for the independent‐samples t‐test were examined before analysis. Normality of PLNS scores within comparison groups was assessed using skewness–kurtosis values and visual inspection of histogram and Q–Q plots, and homogeneity of variances was evaluated using Levene's test. Group differences in patient characteristics and PLNS scores based on categorical variables were assessed with independent‐samples t‐tests when these assumptions were met. To identify predictors of total PLNS scores, multiple linear regression analysis was employed. Variables considered for inclusion in the multiple linear regression model were identified a priori from the literature and clinical relevance (Alkubati et al. 2013; Al‐Maskari et al. 2021), and were then evaluated using univariate analyses before entry into the multivariable model. To avoid excluding potentially important predictors with modest univariate associations, variables with p < 0.10 in univariate analyses were considered for inclusion in the initial multivariable model, together with variables judged to be clinically important (Harrell 2015; Steyerberg 2019). Gender, education status, working status, health insurance and availability of a postoperative support person were initially entered. Model refinement then proceeded by assessing effect sizes, multicollinearity and model fit; variables that showed non‐significant contribution in the multivariable context were removed to produce the final model (Harrell 2015; Steyerberg 2019). The final model retained gender, health insurance status and education level. Assumptions of the regression analysis such as normality of residuals, linearity and absence of multicollinearity were examined and fulfilled. A p‐value of < 0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance.

Ethical considerations

The study adhered to the ethical standards outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval and institutional permission were obtained from the relevant bodies prior to data collection. Before data collection began, participants were fully informed about the study and gave written informed consent.

Most participants were younger than 65 years (75.4%), male (53.1%), married or partnered (78.5%), not currently working (73.8%), and had health insurance coverage (85.4%). Significant differences were identified according to gender. Female patients reported significantly higher scores than males in several PLNS subscales, indicating higher learning needs among female patients, including treatment and complications (p = 0.032), medications (p = 0.009), community and follow up (p < 0.001), feelings related to condition (p = 0.003), enhancing quality of life (p < 0.001) and the PLNS total score (p = 0.002).

Education status was associated with selected learning needs. Patients with less than a high school education reported significantly higher scores in the medications subscale (p = 0.040) and skin care subscale (p = 0.032) compared with those with a high school education or above. Although the difference in PLNS total score did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.057), patients with lower educational attainment tended to report higher overall learning needs.

Working status was significantly associated with learning needs related to feelings about the condition (p = 0.010), enhancing quality of life (p = 0.012), and the PLNS total score (p = 0.047), with non‐working patients reporting higher scores. Patients without health insurance had significantly lower scores in treatment and complications (p = 0.043), activities of living (p = 0.013), skin care (p = 0.033) and the PLNS total score (p = 0.023).

Patients who reported having a postoperative support person demonstrated higher scores in treatment and complications (p = 0.023) and medications (p = 0.006); however, the difference in total PLNS score did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.059). The presence of comorbidities was associated with higher skin care learning needs (p = 0.040), while no other PLNS subscales or total scores differed significantly according to comorbidity status.

Multiple linear regression analysis was conducted to identify factors associated with total PLNS scores (Table 4). The overall model was statistically significant (F(3,126) = 6.817, p < 0.001), explaining 14.0% of the variance in patient learning needs (R 2 = 0.140). Male gender (B = −15.27, 95% CI: −23.73 to −6.81, p < 0.001) and lack of health insurance (B = −17.71, 95% CI: −29.66 to −5.76, p = 0.004) were independently associated with lower PLNS total scores. Education level (high school or above) was not a statistically significant determinant in the adjusted model (p = 0.097).

Discussion

This study investigated the discharge learning needs of patients who underwent cardiac surgery and identified sociodemographic and clinical factors associated with PLNS scores. The results indicate that overall learning needs were moderately high, underscoring the importance of structured discharge education in cardiovascular surgical care. Patient characteristics such as gender, educational background, working and insurance status, and the presence of a support person were found to be associated with patients' learning needs.

In this study, female patients reported significantly higher learning needs in multiple PLNS subscales, including treatment and complications, medications, community and follow‐up, emotional concerns and quality of life. These results are consistent with previous studies, which also reported that women tend to prioritize acquiring information more than men following cardiac surgery (Gökçen and Köçkar 2018; Ong et al. 2018). A common explanation is that women frequently assume a central role in domestic responsibilities and caregiving, which may increase their concerns about regaining independence and returning to their routine roles promptly. Additionally, the literature suggests that female patients exhibit higher levels of preoperative anxiety (Gürler et al. 2022; Bedaso et al. 2022), which can increase their informational needs during transition to recovery at home.

A novel result of this study is the association between health insurance status and learning needs. Patients without insurance demonstrated significantly lower total and subscale PLNS scores, and lack of insurance was independently associated with lower overall learning needs. Individuals without insurance may have limited engagement with the healthcare system and consequently lower expectations regarding information sharing (Tipirneni et al. 2018; Yagi et al. 2022). Evidence from a recent systematic review indicates that lower levels of health insurance literacy increase the likelihood of individuals delaying or avoiding healthcare (Yagi et al. 2022). However, rather than indicating genuinely lower need, these results may reflect unexpressed or unmet learning needs, influenced by hesitation, lack of familiarity with discharge systems, or perceived barriers to accessing follow‐up care. The latest report from the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association Joint Committee on social determinants of health in cardiology notes that uninsured individuals are at greater risk of receiving inadequate care across clinical environments such as outpatient, inpatient and post‐acute services, and experience higher rates of morbidity and mortality compared to those with private insurance (Morris et al. 2024).

An alternative interpretation of the lower reported learning needs among patients without health insurance is reduced patient activation rather than genuinely lower informational requirements (Hibbard and Greene 2013; Greene and Hibbard 2012). Individuals with limited healthcare access may adopt a more passive role in their care, hesitate to ask questions, or normalize uncertainty due to previous experiences with barriers to care (Hibbard and Greene 2013; Arpey et al. 2017). Lower perceived needs may therefore reflect diminished expectations or engagement rather than adequate preparedness (Hibbard and Greene 2013; Arpey et al. 2017). From a nursing perspective, this result highlights the importance of proactively assessing educational needs and encouraging patient participation, particularly among socially vulnerable groups who may underreport their need for information and support.

Educational level also appeared to shape discharge learning priorities in this study. Patients with lower educational attainment reported higher learning needs, particularly in medication management and skin care, and also tended to have higher overall PLNS scores. Although education level was not retained as an independent determinant in the adjusted model, the unadjusted results remain important. One possible explanation is that lower educational attainment may be associated with lower health literacy, making it more difficult for patients to understand written and verbal discharge instructions, recognize warning signs, and confidently perform self‐care after surgery (Dimech et al. 2023; Naeini and Rafiee 2025; van Leeuwen et al. 2024). A qualitative study of older adults after cardiac surgery found that health literacy influenced how patients understood and managed the discharge period (Dimech et al. 2023), while a recent CABG study showed that health literacy was a significant determinant of self‐care (Naeini and Rafiee 2025). In addition, a recent study of thoracic surgery education materials reported that many online patient resources required at least college‐level reading skills, suggesting that standard educational materials may not be well aligned with the needs of patients with lower educational backgrounds (Chen et al. 2024). These results support the need for nurses to simplify discharge information, use teach‐back methods, and provide repeated, practical demonstrations for patients with lower educational attainment.

Working status was also associated with discharge learning needs in the present study. Patients who were not working reported significantly higher learning needs related to feelings about the condition, enhancing quality of life, and the total PLNS score. This result differs from previous CABG studies from Yemen and Oman, which found that working patients reported greater overall learning needs than non‐working patients, particularly because employed individuals may be more concerned about returning to work, resuming physical activity, and managing postoperative demands in daily life (Alkubati et al. 2013; Al‐Maskari et al. 2021). A possible explanation for this difference is that the PLNS domains that were significant in our study were not primarily related to physical functioning or return‐to‐work issues, but rather to emotional adjustment and quality of life. In this context, not working may reflect greater psychosocial vulnerability, reduced social participation, or financial dependency, all of which may intensify concerns about the future after cardiac surgery. Taken together, these results suggest that working status may influence discharge learning needs in different ways depending on the learning domains assessed. Therefore, discharge education should not assume that employed patients alone require more support; nurses should also pay close attention to the emotional and quality‐of‐life concerns of non‐working patients and tailor education accordingly.

The presence of a support person at home after discharge was associated with higher learning needs in the domains of treatment and complications and medications. Rather than reflecting poorer preparedness, this may indicate that patients and families who expect to share postoperative care responsibilities are more likely to recognize the complexity of recovery and express greater informational needs. This interpretation is supported by literature emphasizing the role of caregivers during transitions from hospital to home (Oravec et al. 2023; Bloemberg et al. 2026; Selvi Sarıgül et al. 2024). In cardiac surgery, patients and caregivers have identified practical support, emergency guidance, and longer‐term follow‐up as important discharge priorities (Oravec et al. 2023). Moreover, a recent study in a heart failure population suggested that caregiver health literacy may be associated with better patient self‐care and quality of life (Selvi Sarıgül et al. 2024). A recent systematic review also found that involving family caregivers more actively in hospital care is compatible with patient‐ and family‐centred approaches, although evidence for outcome improvement remains mixed (Bloemberg et al. 2026). These results support the inclusion of caregivers in discharge education whenever possible.

Comorbidities were associated only with higher skin care learning needs in our study. Although this association was more limited than for other variables, it is clinically plausible. Patients with accompanying diseases may face more complex recovery trajectories, polypharmacy, impaired mobility, delayed wound healing, or greater risk of postoperative complications (Falanga et al. 2022), all of which can increase the importance of incision care and surveillance for infection. Recent qualitative work has shown that patients with comorbidities experience greater uncertainty and service needs during discharge transitions (Wu et al. 2025), while wound‐care literature consistently demonstrates that chronic conditions complicate healing processes (Falanga et al. 2022). In cardiac surgery, a recent meta‐analysis showed that deep sternal wound infection remains a serious complication associated with substantial morbidity and mortality (Perezgrovas‐Olaria et al. 2023). Therefore, patients with comorbidities may benefit from more individualized nurse‐led discharge teaching focused on wound assessment, hygiene, and early recognition of infection signs.

In light of these results, a one‐size‐fits‐all approach to discharge education appears insufficient for patients recovering from cardiac surgery. Nurses should tailor education content and delivery methods to patient profiles, with particular attention to women, individuals with lower educational attainment, non‐working patients and those without insurance. Tailored education and structured discharge planning have been shown to enhance continuity of care and facilitate recovery in cardiac populations, especially when delivered through multidisciplinary teams, led by nurses, or supplemented with digital tools (Ruksakulpiwat et al. 2025). Approaches that prioritize the needs of patients and their caregivers can help lower readmission rates, improve treatment adherence and promote better long‐term outcomes (Ruksakulpiwat et al. 2025). Moreover, developing standardized discharge protocols that integrate PLNS assessment as part of routine care may support more consistent and patient‐centred education.

Limitations

This study was carried out in one public hospital in Türkiye and included a relatively small sample, which may limit generalizability of the results. Because the study was cross‐sectional, it cannot establish cause‐and‐effect relationships and reflects patients' learning needs only at the time of discharge, even though these needs may change as recovery continues. As the data were collected through self‐report questionnaires, responses may have been influenced by social desirability bias, with patients potentially reporting higher learning needs to align with perceived expectations. In addition, patients undergoing different types of cardiovascular procedures (CABG, valve and vascular surgery) were analysed together. Although these patients were managed within the same clinical pathway and received standardized discharge education, procedure‐specific recovery trajectories and informational needs may differ. Therefore, subgroup differences may not have been fully captured. Future studies should use larger, multicentre samples and stratified or procedure‐specific analyses, as well as longitudinal designs, to track learning needs across the postoperative period and to test tailored educational interventions and their effects on outcomes such as self‐care adherence, postoperative complications and rehospitalization.

Recommendations for Further Research

Future studies should employ longitudinal and multicentre designs to explore how learning needs evolve across different stages of recovery following cardiac surgery. Research examining the effectiveness of tailored, needs‐based discharge education interventions on outcomes such as self‐care behaviours, postoperative complications, rehospitalisation rates and patient‐reported outcomes is also warranted. In addition, incorporating measures of health literacy, psychosocial factors and caregiver involvement may provide a more comprehensive understanding of determinants of learning needs and inform more targeted educational strategies.

Implications for Policy and Practice

The results highlight the importance of integrating structured learning needs assessments into routine discharge planning for patients undergoing cardiac surgery. Nursing policies and clinical guidelines should support the systematic identification of individual learning priorities to promote patient‐centred discharge education. In practice, nurses should pay particular attention to patients who may be less likely to articulate their needs, such as male patients and those without health insurance, to reduce the risk of unmet educational needs and promote equitable access to postoperative information. At an organizational level, the development of standardized discharge protocols that incorporate individualized education may enhance continuity of care and support safer transitions from hospital to home.

Conclusion

This study showed that patients undergoing cardiac surgery have substantial discharge learning needs, particularly in relation to treatment and complications, activities of daily living, quality of life and medication management. Learning needs varied according to several demographic and clinical characteristics, with higher needs reported among female patients, individuals with lower educational levels, unemployed patients, and those receiving postoperative support, while male gender and lack of health insurance were independently associated with lower total PLNS scores. For nursing practice, these results highlight the need for systematic assessment of discharge learning needs and for individualized, nurse‐led education that reflects each patient's social and clinical context. Particular attention should be given to patients who may be less likely to express their informational needs, such as men and those without health insurance, in order to reduce the risk of unmet educational needs, strengthen self‐care after discharge, and support safer transitions from hospital to home.

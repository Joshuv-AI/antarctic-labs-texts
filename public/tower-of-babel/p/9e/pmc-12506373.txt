A nomogram based on conventional and contrast-enhanced ultrasound for predicting high-volume central lymph node metastasis in papillary thyroid carcinoma
Authors: Xing Yan, Qinghai Peng, Jia Chen, Rongsen Zhang, Kui Tang, Yan Xu, Zhigang Wang, Chengcheng Niu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIt is recommended that ≥ 5 pathological N1a central lymph node metastases required a second total thyroidectomy after unilateral thyroidectomy In papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) patients. We aimed to develop a preoperative nomogram integrating conventional ultrasound (US) and contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) to accurately predict high-volume central lymph node metastasis (HVCLNM) in PTC.MethodsPatients with pathologically confirmed PTC were enrolled in our study between May 2021 and May 2024. Overall, a total of 867 patients were enrolled, and they were split into training and validation datasets at random in a 7:3 ratio. A nomogram predicting HVCLNM probability was developed in the training dataset using conventional US and CEUS characteristics. Model performance was assessed using the area under the curve (AUC), calibration, and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsA total of 607 patients (mean age, 42 ± 11.8 years, M/F = 142/465) and 260 patients (mean age, 42 ± 11.4 years, M/F = 54/206) were enrolled in the training and validation datasets, respectively. LASSO logistic regression selected five imaging features with non-zero coefficients: size, multifocality, enhancement direction, peak intensity, and US-reported LN status. The nomogram incorporating these factors demonstrated strong predictive performance for HVCLNM, with mean AUCs of 0.9149 (95% CI: 0.8878–0.9419) in the training set and 0.8768 (95% CI: 0.8046–0.9527) in the validation set. Calibration and DCA for the HVCLNM nomogram also showed superior accuracy and therapeutic utility.ConclusionThis preoperative nomogram demonstrates high diagnostic accuracy and shows potential as a clinical decision-support tool for predicting HVCLNM.

Introduction

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the commonest type of thyroid cancer, comprising around 85–90% of cases [1–3]. Cervical lymph node metastasis (CLNM) is a significant predictor of PTC advancement and a factor influencing PTC patients’ prognosis [4]. Routine central lymph node dissection (CLND) is near-total or complete thyroidectomy along with removal of central lymph nodes for patients with thyroid cancer, which has long been controversial since CLNM is difficult to detect preoperatively [4–6]. CLND is commonly performed in Asian countries like Japan and China due to the high CLNM prevalence. However, this practice increases the risk of complications such as recurrent laryngeal nerve injury, hypoparathyroidism, and chyle leakage [7, 8]. For low-risk PTC, unilateral lobectomy provides equivalent recurrence-free survival (5-year RFS 93–97%), overall survival (5-year OS 98–99%), and disease-specific survival compared to total thyroidectomy [9, 10]. This suggests that unilateral lobectomy might be considered as a feasible alternative surgical option for low-risk PTC. However, certain pathological features can only be assessed during the post-operative evaluation and may elevate the case’s risk level from low to medium or high. The 2015 American Thyroid Association(ATA) guidelines stated that the presence of more than five impacted lymph nodes in pathologic N1 is one of the variables that can elevate low-risk PTC to intermediate-risk PTC, which had a 15% increased risk of structural disease recurrence [11]. Furthermore, according to the Clinical Practice Guidelines for Oncology (NCCN), involvement of any lymph node metastasis with a maximum size of > 2 mm or ≥ 5 lymph nodes indicated a high risk of PTC recurrence. It is also recommended that ≥ 5 pathological N1a (level VI/VII lymph nodes) [12] metastases be removed as one of the undesired pathologic features following unilateral lobectomy that necessitates a second total thyroidectomy [13]. Therefore, accurate preoperative diagnosis of high-volume central lymph node metastasis (HVCLNM) is crucial for optimal patient management.

Ultrasound (US) is the primary screening tool for thyroid cancer but has limited sensitivity (20–60%) for detecting CLNM [14, 15]. In individuals with PTCs, US can be used to predict CLNM and serves as a guide when choosing surgical approaches [16]. But currently, there are few studies on HVCLNM. When compared to traditional US, contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) improves thyroid nodule characterization by visualizing microvascular architecture [17, 18]. Our previous research showed that preoperative CEUS features can assist in predicting CLNM in PTC patients [19–22]. While recent studies have investigated CEUS for lymph node metastasis prediction, the combined use of conventional US and CEUS perfusion characteristics to preoperatively predict HVCLNM remains insufficiently explored. Unlike complex radiomics approaches, we developed a practical nomogram using routinely accessible ultrasonographic features to address this specific clinical need [23–25].

This study aimed to develop and validate a preoperative nomogram combining conventional US and CEUS features to predict HVCLNM (defined as ≥ 5 metastatic central lymph nodes) to assist for clinical decision-making.

Materials and methods

Patients

This retrospective study, approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Xiangya Hospital (LYF2022169), analyzed patients with histologically confirmed PTC between May 2021 and May 2024. All participants provided written informed consent. We enrolled 1141 patients who underwent preoperative US and CEUS followed by initial thyroid surgery with CLND. The following were the inclusion criteria: (1) patients who were histologically proven to have PTC at our center after undergoing initial thyroid surgery with CLND; (2)the thyroid US and CEUS examination were taken in our department one month before the operation༛༈3༉no other treatment was performed before the operation. The following were the exclusion criteria: (1) bilateral malignant thyroid nodules diagnosed by histopathology; (2) the biggest Malignant nodule in the preoperative US examination that measured more than 4.0 cm; (3) obvious extracapsular invasion; (4) clinical evidence of distant metastases; (5) a history of neck radiation exposure. Finally, 867 patients (mean age 39 ± 12.26 [range, 14–73] Years; 195 men and 672 women) were enrolled, and they were randomly split into training and validation datasets at a 7:3 ratio. Patients were stratified into HVCLNM-positive and HVCLNM-negative groups based on pathology. Demographic and clinical information (age, BRAF V600E mutation status, pathology) were collected from medical records. Hashimoto’s thyroiditis diagnosis required both: (1) elevated serum anti-thyroid peroxidase antibody (> 34 IU/mL) and/or anti-thyroglobulin antibody (> 115 IU/mL), and (2) characteristic sonographic features (diffuse heterogeneity, micronodularity, hypervascularity) as previously validated.

US procedure

The preoperative US examinations were performed with two linear-array transducers: the Acuson S3000 by Siemens and the E11 by GE. A 6–18-MHz transducer was used for nonenhanced US examinations, while a 4–10-MHz transducer was used for CEUS examinations. These examinations were performed by one of four radiologists who had over 10 Years of experience in nonenhanced US and at least 5 years of experience in CEUS of the thyroid gland. The mechanical index (MI) was adjusted to an exact value of 0.07, and the focus was deliberately positioned slightly below the target lesion. For each CEUS scan, a 2.0 mL bolus of sulfur hexafluoride (SonoVue, Bracco, Italy) was injected intravenously, followed by a 5 mL saline flush.

US image evaluation

To maintain consistency in picture analysis, two radiologists (each with 5–10 years of expertise in the diagnosis of US thyroid disease) separately examined the US imaging characteristics. In the event of a disagreement, the radiologists would meet to reach a consensus on a final diagnosis.

According to the ACR2017, ATA2015, Chinese Thyroid Imaging Recording and Data System (C-TIRADS2020), and Korean Society of Thyroid Radiology guidelines (KSThR2016), The imaging characteristics were abstracted as follows: size; multifocality; composition(mixed cystic and solid, solid); Echogenicity(hypoechoic, Iso-or hyperechoic); shape(wider than tall, taller than wide); margin(well-defined, ill-defined); calcification(no calcification, macro-calcification, or microcalcification); petal-like calcification; enhancement direction (scattered, centripetal, or centrifugal); enhancement pattern (homogeneous or heterogeneous); peak intensity (hypoenhancement, isoenhancement, or hyperenhancement); Circular enhancement. If there are several suspicions of malignancy, the biggest nodule will be included in this study. The value of microcalcification was determined as ≤ 2 mm. Microcalcifications were included in a nodule if they coexisted with macrocalcifications.

Identification of US-reported LN status

Lymph nodes (LNs) were considered suspicious preoperatively if they exhibited: absence of fatty hilum, round shape, hyperechoic change, microcalcifications, or necrosis [26, 27].

Feature selection

The Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) binary logistic regression model was applied to the training cohort for feature selection. Optimal penalization coefficient lambda (λ) was determined using 10-fold cross-validation based on the minimum cross-validation error criterion (minimum lambda). Ultimately, the model was fine-tuned by incorporating all of the observations and the selected λ. As a result, most of the coefficients were reduced to zero, leaving only the non-zero coefficients chosen by LASSO.

Model construction and nomogram development

We developed a diagnostic Nomogram utilizing the LASSO binary logistic regression model to choose independent predictors. This Nomogram provides a quantitative tool for radiologists to assess the probability of HVCLNM by generating a composite indicator. The nomogram derived from the training dataset was utilized on the validation dataset to get the overall score for each nodule.

Predictive model evaluation

The area under the curve (AUC) was employed to assess the nomogram’s discriminatory capability. Bootstrapping validation was conducted on the nomogram using 1000 resamples to ensure a highly reliable AUC. Calibration curves were constructed by plotting the predicted probability of malignancy in nodules against the observed probability for each quintile, thereby evaluating the calibration of the diagnostic nomogram. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was utilized to assess the clinical utility of the nomogram.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS Statistics version 26.0 (IBM Corp.) and R Studio software. The inter- and intra-operator agreements were evaluated through Cohen’s Kappa statistic. Categorical variables were compared using Chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation. We reported the accuracy of logistic regression with LASSO using the training and internal validation datasets. The effectiveness of the classification approach was highly dependent on the adjustment of parameter λ. To determine the optimal value of λ, a 10-fold cross-validation was employed, utilizing the 1-SE criteria.

Since the best results might be achieved by choosing imaging features most closely linked to the Malignancy, the path of the AUC was also presented. A diagnostic nomogram was created using the chosen US features in the training dataset, and subsequently evaluated in the validation dataset. The discrimination, calibration, and DCA of nomogram were assessed. We repeated the process 1000 times to acquire the mean AUC and 95% CI for the models. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Discussion

We developed and validated an ultrasound-based nomogram integrating conventional US and CEUS features to noninvasively predict HVCLNM risk preoperatively. Our study based on a complete and extensive analysis of multimodal US imagine features. We utilized a greater amount of comprehensive and abundant data to enhance our predictive capabilities, Compared to earlier models [28–30]. Zhang et al. used radiomics analysis of grayscale US (AUC = 0.89), which required specialized software unavailable in routine practice [31]. Huang et al. Focused on PTMC patients with a small cohort (n = 312). And they did not evaluate enhancement kinetics or peak intensity [32]. Meanwhile, our model demonstrated superior prediction performance compared to the prior model, which simply relied on classical ultrasonic prediction of HVCLNM(AUC = 0.9149) [31, 33].

Univariate analysis revealed associations between HVCLNM and younger age, BRAF V600E mutation, and Hashimoto’s thyroiditis,, which was the same as previous reports [32–34]. Notably, while our previous work suggested Hashimoto’s thyroiditis was protective against CLNM, this study found an association with HVCLNM [19].

Consistent with prior studies, larger tumor size was a strong predictor of HVCLNM [29, 31, 32, 35]. Multifocal PTCs can be defined as a single gland having multiple non-contiguous tumors, with a dominant tumor coexisting with multiple microscopic tumors [36]. Several recent studies have demonstrated that multifocality is a significant risk factor for the development of HVCLNM [32, 33], Patients with HVCLNM exhibit a threefold increase in recurrence rates compared to those without HVCLNM, with respective recurrence rates of approximately 5.0% and 20.0% [37, 38]. Our study found that multifocality is more susceptible to HVCLNM, which corresponds with the findings of X. Wei et al. [34]. This may relate to the thyroid’s rich lymphatic network facilitating intra-glandular spread.

Compared to color Doppler technology, CEUS exhibits a superior ability to delineate tumor microvessels, thereby potentially improving the accuracy of thyroid nodule diagnosis [39]. Few studies have reported the association between CEUS features and CLNM in PTC patients. In our investigation, during the univariate logistic regression analysis, centrifugal enhancement direction, heterogeneous enhancement pattern, hyper-enhancement intensity, and circular enhancement were found to be associated with the presence of HVCLNM. Subsequent LASSO logistic regression analysis revealed that centrifugal enhancement direction and hyper-enhancement intensity were independent risk factors for HVCLNM. Huang et al. performed an investigation in which they found that hyper- or iso-enhancement was a reliable indicator of the existence of CLNM [40]. In our investigation, the incidence of HVCLNM was significantly higher in the hyper-enhancement group compared to the iso-enhancement and hypo-enhancement groups. hyper-enhancement often indicates that the tumor is rich in nourishing blood vessels, and angiogenesis is associated with tumor growth and proliferation, making the tumor more aggressive, which may lead to a gradual increase in the contact area between the tumor and lymphatic vessels, eventually leading to HVCLNM [41]. Prior research has shown that aggressive tumors often exhibit circular enhancement, which is associated with CLNM [42]. In our present study, univariate analysis indicated that the rate of HVCLNM was significantly higher in the HVCLNM-positive group compared to the HVCLNM-negative group. Circular enhancement might result from a stromal reaction secondary to tumor invasion. Thus, circular enhancement can be considered a significant indicator of tumor infiltration.

In Xiaoqi Tian et al.’ study, the Nodules of 49 patients were characterized by diffuse microcalcification, and 91.8%(45/49) of patients were pathologically confirmed as CLNM after surgery [43]. Ferreira et al. found that the presence of Microcalcification was related to lymph node metastasis [44]. On cytological or histological examination, microcalcification corresponds to the aggregation of sand bodies [45]. Quan Dai et al. discovered that scattered microcalcification may be the most crucial variable in predicting CLNM. All patients with intratumor diffuse microcalcification demonstrated CLNM after surgery, and emphasizing the microcalcification pattern is critical to predicting CLNM [46]. To our knowledge, only a few studies have investigated the relationship between calcification and lymph node metastasis; further, there is a scarcity of research on the association between microcalcification and HVCLNM. Our earlier work discovered an increased risk of CLNM in PTC patients with Petal-like calcification [47]. At present, the relationship between petal-like calcification and HVCLNM has not been reported. Hence, in this paper, we investigated the relationship between the type of calcification and HVCLNM, and we analyzed the correlation between petal-like calcification and HVCLNM separately. Our research revealed that microcalcifications were more likely to present with HVCLNM relative to noncalcification and macrocalcifications, and that petal-like calcifications were strongly associated with HVCLNM in a univariate analysis. Therefore, the identification of microcalcification should be given enough consideration.

The final nomogram, incorporating size, multifocality, enhancement direction, peak intensity, and US-reported LN status, achieved excellent discrimination (mean AUC 0.9149). Given the heterogeneity in ultrasound image acquisition and clinical data collection across various institutions, DCA was employed to validate the model’s clinical utility, as opposed to a multi-institutional prospective validation. Furthermore, the nomogram prediction model developed incorporates individual risk factors and serves as a visual tool for sonographers and surgeons to predict lymph node metastasis preoperatively in PTC patients. Previous studies have shown that the utility of nomograms has been helpful in dealing with CLNM in PTC patients, but their application in predicting HVCLNM risk among PTC patients has been rarely noted. Our nomogram model exhibited strong predictive capability, and we are confident in its potential as a diagnostic instrument for HVCLNM in PTC patients.

There are some limitations on our study. Firstly, the pathological subtypes of PTC were not taken into account. Secondly, selection bias may exist because of the retrospective design. Additionally, the risk of other adverse features, such as extrathyroidal extension and vascular invasion, should be assessed concurrently, which would help to determine whether to perform a thyroid lobectomy or a whole thyroidectomy for patients with low-risk HVCLNM. Finally, our nomogram are only assessed by internal validation methods, and in order to guarantee the objectivity and accuracy of the outcomes, applications need to be thoroughly evaluated in more extensive prospective multicenter studies.

Conclusion

Size, multifocality, enhancement direction, peak intensity, and US-reported LN status were independent risk factors for HVCLNM in PTC patients. This preoperative nomogram demonstrates high diagnostic accuracy and shows potential as a clinical decision-support tool for predicting HVCLNM. This may be instrumental in refining surgery strategy in PTC patients. Future large-scale prospective studies should be undertaken to validate the clinical value of this model.

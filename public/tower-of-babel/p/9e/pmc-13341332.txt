Real‐World Long‐Term Outcomes of Rezūm Therapy for Large‐Volume Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia: A Multicenter Cohort Study
Authors: Liberty Liu, Mario Henrique Bitar Siqueira, Naeem Bhojani, Bilal Chughtai, Kevin C Zorn, Luca Cindolo, Giovanni Ferrari, Katherine Lajkosz, Eviatar Fields, Dean Elterman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectivesBenign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is a common cause of lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) in aging men. Patients with large prostates (≥ 80 mL) often experience more severe symptoms and have limited minimally invasive treatment options. Water vapor thermal therapy (Rezūm) has demonstrated efficacy in moderate‐sized glands, and emerging evidence including multicenter cohorts and systematic reviews suggests efficacy in larger glands. However, long‐term real‐world multicenter data remain limited. This study evaluates the effectiveness of Rezūm therapy in men urinary status outcomes, sexual function, and prostate size in a large, multicenter real‐world cohort with extended follow‐up.MethodsWe performed a retrospective analysis of a prospectively maintained multicenter registry including patients treated with Rezūm therapy between April 2019 and August 2025 at two high‐volume centers in Canada and Italy. Eligible patients had a baseline prostate volume ≥ 80 mL. Outcomes were assessed at baseline and follow‐up intervals of 6, 12, 24, and 36 months. Primary outcomes included changes in International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS), quality of life (QoL), BPH Impact Index (BPHII), maximum urinary flow rate (Q max), and postvoid residual (PVR). Sexual function was evaluated using the International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF‐15) and the Male Sexual Health Questionnaire (MSHQ). Changes in prostate volume at 12 months were also analyzed.ResultsA total of 321 patients were included with a mean age of 68.7 years and median prostate volume of 107 mL. Median lobe enlargement was present in 80.1% of patients. Mean IPSS improved from 21.7 ± 6.9 at baseline to 9.9 ± 6.8 at 12 months and 8.4 ± 7.0 at 36 months (p < 0.001), representing a symptom reduction of up to 62%. QoL scores improved from 4.5 ± 1.1 to 1.6 ± 1.2 at 36 months (p < 0.001). Qmax increased from 8.1 ± 6.5 mL/s at baseline to 14.6 ± 6.7 mL/s at 6 months, while PVR decreased from 144 ± 136 mL to 48 ± 63 mL at 12 months. Erectile and ejaculatory function remained stable during follow‐up, with retrograde ejaculation reported in 6.2% of patients. Among 172 patients with imaging follow‐up, mean prostate volume decreased by 35.2 mL (29.9%) at 12 months.ConclusionsRezūm therapy provides significant and durable improvements in urinary symptoms and functional outcomes in men with large prostates (≥ 80 mL), while preserving sexual function. These findings support Rezūm as a safe and effective minimally invasive treatment option for large‐volume BPH in real‐world clinical practice.

Introduction

Lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) in older men are most commonly caused by benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) [1]. While benign, BPH significantly reduces quality of life (QoL), negatively affects mental well‐being, and increases healthcare costs [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. In addition to symptom burden, patients with BPH may also experience adverse events such as urinary tract infection, acute urinary retention (AUR), vesical calculi, and acute renal failure resulting in increased hospitalization [2, 3].

The burden of BPH is becoming increasingly pronounced due to the growing epidemiological impact of an aging global community [1, 4]. More than one in four men will have BPH in their lifetime, and the percentage increases with age [6]. The prevalence of BPH is approximately 45% in men over 45 years of age, rising to 80% in men older than 70 [1]. Furthermore, data from insurance claims indicates a lower prevalence compared with findings from autopsy studies—the number identified clinically may be underreported as not all men are symptomatic or seek treatment [2]. Autopsy studies identified BPH in approximately 50% of men aged 51–60, with prevalence reaching as high as 90% in those over 90 years of age [2, 7]. According to a systematic analysis of 204 countries and territories, the age‐standardized prevalence of BPH increased by 70.5% globally between 2000 and 2019 [8]. The disease burden of BPH will continue to increase as the global populations continue to increase in lifespan [1, 4, 8].

The severity of BPH can be categorized by prostate size, which is an important prognostic factor in determining symptom progression as well as treatment selection [9]. An average‐sized prostate with BPH measures approximately 30–80 mL, while volumes ≥ 80 mL are classified as large [10, 11]. Larger prostate volumes ≥ 80 mL are associated with a greater risk of LUTS and increased likelihood of requiring medical and surgical intervention [9, 12]. Men with prostates ≥ 80 mL have more severe symptoms at baseline and were found to have worse urodynamic outcomes compared to those with smaller prostates in the overall population [10]. Patients with larger prostates are also more likely to experience AUR [13]. The Canadian Agency for Drugs and Technologies in Health (CADTH) published a report on minimally invasive treatments for LUTS secondary to BPH, highlighting the increased challenges associated with treating larger prostates [14]. At the time, CADTH reported that options were likely limited to laser treatments or prostatectomy [14]. Nevertheless, further research has evaluated minimally invasive alternatives, including WVTT using the Rezūm System, in larger prostates.

Evidence on the effectiveness of Rezūm therapy in prostates ≥ 80 mL includes prospective and retrospective studies demonstrating safety and efficacy of treatment, and comparative studies which found similarities in the outcomes of Rezūm therapy across medium and large prostate groups [15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21]. Additionally, two systematic reviews found that Rezūm therapy provides improvements in LUTS while preserving sexual function and maintaining low rates of serious adverse events, but evidence in prostates ≥ 80 mL and long‐term outcomes is not yet well established [22, 23]. Collectively, current evidence demonstrates that Rezūm therapy is effective in large prostates; however, an evidence gap remains, including predominately short follow‐up duration [21, 22, 23, 24, 25]. Long‐term and multicenter real‐world studies in prostates ≥ 80 mL remain limited, particularly in multicenter and multinational populations. Accordingly, the present study aims to evaluate the safety and durability of Rezūm therapy in prostates ≥ 80 mL using a multicenter real‐world cohort with follow‐up to 36 months with data from Canada and Italy.

Material and Methods

Study Subjects

This is a retrospective analysis of a prospectively collected registry from two high‐volume centers performing water vapor thermal therapy (WVTT–Rezūm) in Canada and Italy. Institutional ethics board approval was obtained at each center. The cohort comprises patients who underwent WVTT–Rezūm between April 2019 and August 2025. Eligibility was restricted to patients with a prostatic volume equal to or larger than 80 mL.

Procedure

The WVTT–Rezūm procedures were conducted following the methods outlined in previous studies. Each application of water vapor ablates contiguous regions of the prostatic tissue following the urethra's natural gradient. The number of injections was decided at the surgeons' discretion after analyzing patients' prostatic anatomy and desire to preserve ejaculation. In general, the aim was to provide one treatment for every 1 cm length per lateral lobe, followed by dedicated injections for any median lobes. Procedural/anesthesia parameters included propofol (70.1%), lidocaine gel (12.5%), prostate block (0.3%), methoxyflurane (1.6%), acetaminophen (2.5%), Percocet (0.9%), oxycodone (0.3%), and lorazepam (0.9%). Patients were discharged the same day with a Foley in situ. Length of Foley catheterization varied between 7 and 30 days based on real‐world variability across centers, including institutional practice patterns and logistical factors such as appointment availability for catheter removal. The 30‐day AUR rate was 13.1%, necessitating longer Foley catheterization in some cases. A small proportion of patients (4.4%) continued BPH medications during follow‐up, which was recorded descriptively and may reflect physician preference or patient‐specific factors.

Data Collection and Outcomes of Interest

Patients were followed at baseline, 6, 12, 24, and 36 months and yearly after that. Patients' characteristics, prostatic volume, IPSS and IPSS QoL subscale, International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF 15), maximum urinary flow rate (Qmax), postvoid residual (PVR), PSA, number of WVTT–Rezūm injections, complications, and BPH medication usage were evaluated at baseline and follow‐up. The objective of this study was to assess the effectiveness of WVTT–Rezūm in improving urinary function, as measured by IPSS, IPSS QoL, BPHII, and uroflowmetry parameters. Exploratory outcomes include retreatment measures as well as catheter duration to provide additional context for efficacy and genitourinary safety.

Statistical Analysis

Patient characteristics and outcomes were analyzed descriptively. Continuous variables were compared using Student's t‐test or rank‐sum tests, depending on data distribution. Categorical variables were analyzed using Fisher's exact test. All statistical analyses were conducted using Stata 18BE, with a two‐sided p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 321 patients with a mean age of 68.7 years (range 47.6–92.7) and prostate size ≥ 80 mL were treated with WVTT–Rezūm. The median prostate volume was 107 mL (interquartile range 91–125 mL), with 254 patients (80.1%) exhibiting a median lobe. Urinary retention was present in 107 patients (33.6%) at the time of the procedure. On average, 13.4 ± 3.9 injections were administered per procedure, which lasted an average of 6.8 ± 9 min. A total of 154 patients (48%) were taking pre‐operative alpha blockers, while 83 patients (25.9%) were taking 5‐α reductase inhibitors (5ARI). At baseline, 60 patients (18.7%) were catheter‐dependent and managed with an indwelling Foley catheter prior to WVTT–Rezūm treatment (Table 1).

Efficacy

Urinary Status

The mean IPSS improved from a baseline of 21.7 ± 6.9 (n = 257) to 9.1 ± 7.0 at 6 months (n = 148), 9.9 ± 6.8 at 12 months (n = 121), 9.1 ± 6.1 at 24 months (n = 83), and 8.4 ± 7.0 at 36 months (n = 30), corresponding to symptom reductions of 56.8%, 54.3%, 57.0%, and 62.0%, respectively (Figure 1a). The IPSS QoL score decreased from 4.5 ± 1.1 at baseline (n = 257) to 1.7 ± 1.4 at 6 months (n = 148), 1.9 ± 1.4 at 12 months (n = 121), 1.6 ± 1.1 at 24 months (n = 83), and 1.6 ± 1.2 at 36 months (n = 30) (Figure 1b). Similarly, the BPH Impact Index (BPHII) declined from 7.5 ± 3.0 at baseline (n = 215) to 2.4 ± 3.2 at 6 months (n = 101), 2.8 ± 3.1 at 12 months (n = 77), 2.2 ± 2.7 at 24 months (n = 45), and 2.1 ± 3.1 at 36 months (n = 25). All outcome measures demonstrated statistically significant improvements at each follow‐up time point (p < 0.0001), indicating sustained and clinically meaningful symptom relief throughout the study period.

Prostate Size

Change in prostate volume from baseline to 12 months was assessed in 172 patients with available measurements at both time points. The mean absolute reduction in prostate volume was −35.2 mL (SD 34.5), with a median decrease of −32.0 mL (IQR −53.0 to −16.0). The corresponding mean percentage change from baseline was −29.9% (SD 25.8), and the median percentage reduction was −31.2% (IQR −49.1% to −17.6%) (Table 3). While most patients experienced a reduction in prostate volume, variability was observed, with absolute changes ranging from −210.4 to +65.8 mL and percentage changes ranging from −77.0% to +72.9%. The distribution of changes was visualized using histograms (Figure 3).

Genitourinary Safety

Sexual Function

The mean International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF) score at baseline was 42.9 ± 20.8 (n = 220). At 6 months, the score showed a minimal decline to 42.2 ± 21.5 (n = 99) and remained stable at 41.8 ± 21.2 at 12 months (n = 90). At 24 months, the mean IIEF score was 36.8 ± 20.2 (n = 53), increasing to 54.5 ± 19.0 at 36 months (n = 12) (Figure 4a). The mean Male Sexual Health Questionnaire (MSHQ) Ejaculatory Function score was 8.2 ± 3.7 at baseline (n = 185). This decreased slightly to 7.6 ± 4.6 at 6 months (n = 67), followed by stability at 12 months (8.5 ± 4.1; n = 56), 24 months (8.9 ± 4.4; n = 27), and 36 months (9.1 ± 4.2; n = 12). The MSHQ Ejaculatory Bother score was 2.4 ± 1.7 at baseline (n = 185) and showed minimal change over follow‐up, measuring 2.2 ± 1.6 at 6 months (n = 67), 2.1 ± 1.5 at 12 months (n = 55), 1.8 ± 1.4 at 24 months (n = 27), and 1.8 ± 1.5 at 36 months (n = 12) (Figure 4b).

(A) International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF‐15) and (B) Male Sexual Health Questionnaire for Ejaculatory Dysfunction (MSQH‐EjD).

Overall, changes in erectile and ejaculatory function were small and characterized by overlapping confidence intervals, suggesting no clinically meaningful trends over time. Retrograde ejaculation was reported in 20 patients (6.2%). No statistically significant deterioration in erectile or ejaculatory outcomes was observed throughout the study period.

Catheter Dependency

Prior to WVTT–Rezūm treatment, 60 patients (18.7%) had an indwelling Foley catheter and were catheter‐dependent at baseline. In the first 30 postoperative days, 13.1% of patients experienced acute urine retention, 18.1% had urinary tract infection, and 8.5% had hematuria. During this time, six patients required hospital admission. Only two (0.6%) patients eventually needed surgical retreatment for persistent urine retention, even though 13.1% of patients had postoperative urinary retention. All other patients no longer required an indwelling Foley catheter.

Retreatment

Medical Retreatment

Prior to WVTT–Rezūm treatment, 154 of 321 patients (48.0%) were receiving α‐blockers, while 83 patients (25.9%) were receiving 5‐ARIs. Following WVTT–Rezūm therapy, 14 patients (4.4%) continued BPH medication in the form of an α‐blocker.

Surgical Retreatment

Among 321 patients, 21 (6.5%) underwent surgical retreatment. The indication for retreatment was prostate tissue regrowth in 18 of 21 cases. The remaining three retreatments (0.9%) were performed due to urinary retention in two patients (0.6%), and necrotic tissue in one patient (0.3%). The retreatment modalities included GreenLight laser therapy in 13 patients (4.0%), WVTT–Rezūm in 2 patients (0.6%), TURP in 3 patients (0.9%), HoLEP in 1 patient (0.3%), Aquablation in 1 patient (0.3%), and prostatic artery embolization (PAE) in 1 patient (0.3%).

Discussion

The aim of this study was to assess long‐term outcomes pertaining to urinary status, prostate size, and sexual function of WVTT–Rezūm among patients with prostatic volume ≥ 80 mL in international multicenter real‐world settings. Outcomes relating to efficacy and sexual function were evaluated at 6, 12, 24, and 36 months, with exploratory consideration for catheter dependence and retreatment.

Urinary status outcomes such as the mean IPSS, the IPSS QoL and BPHII showed statistically significant improvement at each follow up point during the study period, demonstrating effective continued and clinically important symptom relief for 3 years. For example, IPSS was 62.0% lower than baseline at 36 months, compared to existing decreases of 57% at 24 months and 54.3% at 12 months. Meta‐analytic data demonstrated consistent improvements in IPSS, IPSS‐QoL, Q max, and PVR for men with large prostates in the short‐term, with long‐term outcomes remaining unclear—this study suggests there are long‐term sustained and ongoing improvements in the long‐term [24].

Reduction of prostate volume was variable, with a reduction of prostate volume in most patients. A small percentage had growth to a volume above baseline at 36 months. The mean prostate volume was −35.2 mL (SD 34.5). Variability in prostate volume change was observed in the data, including an increase in size for some patients. While prior studies report average reductions in prostate volume following WVTT–Rezūm, detailed analyses of variability are limited to our knowledge. Potential contributing factors including baseline anatomy (e.g., median lobe enlargement), injection strategy, operator technique, and imaging variability, cannot be definitively determined based on currently available evidence, which is a limitation. Heterogeneity in prostate volume at 36 months may allude to increased need for retreatment in patients with prostate size ≥ 80 mL, which is congruent with existing long‐term retrospective data [21].

No clinically meaningful deterioration in erectile or ejaculatory outcomes was observed, suggesting safety in preserving sexual function. The preservation of erectile and ejaculatory function observed in this study is consistent with prior reports demonstrating minimal impact on sexual function following WVTT [24]. Additional systematic review data including mixed prostate sizes similarly demonstrate consistent symptom improvement and preservation of sexual function across cohorts, including those with large glands [25].

Variability in catheter duration was influenced by differences in institutional practices and logistical factors, including appointment availability for catheter removal. Data regarding intermittent self‐catheterization (clean intermittent catheterization) were not available in our database; therefore, only Foley catheter use was captured, which is a limitation. The rate of AUR within 30 days post‐ WVTT–Rezūm was 13.1%, which supports the need for individualized catheter management. However, only two patients required surgical intervention for sustained urinary retention.

A small proportion of patients continued BPH medications during follow‐up (4.4%) and 6.5% underwent surgical retreatment within the study period. Decision for or against retreatment is influenced by variability in physician preference and patient‐specific factors due to the real‐world, multicenter nature of the study. A systematic review from 2022 found patients undergoing WVTT–Rezūm had a significantly higher proportion of men free of retreatment after 4 years, with 89% of patients not requiring retreatment after WVTT–Rezūm compared to only 75% after UroLift, but this data refers to general prostate size populations rather than patient with large prostates [25]. According to the 2023 systematic review and meta‐analysis, the rates of serious complications and surgical retreatments were low in large prostates and comparable to rates with use of WVTT–Rezūm in smaller prostate glands in the short‐term, with more information required to determine long‐term outcomes [24]. Transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) is the primary most effective procedure for reducing urinary symptoms caused by average‐sized BPH; unfortunately, surgical management with TURP can require longer operative times, and higher risk of complications (i.e., increase risk of bleeding, transurethral resection syndrome, and other post‐operative complications) for patients with large prostate sizes [2, 11]. Therefore, WVTT–Rezūm warrants further investigation as a shorter minimally invasive outpatient alternative that may mitigate the increased risk of complications caused by TURP due to large prostate size.

The results of this study should be interpreted within the context of an expanding body of literature evaluating WVTT–Rezūm in large prostates. Previously published prospective analyses of this multicenter cohort demonstrated significant improvements in IPSS, Q max, and QoL at 12‐ and 24‐months with minimal genitourinary complications in men with prostates ≥ 80 mL [19, 20]. Further, larger prospective cohort data (n = 196; median prostate 96 mL) demonstrated significant improvements in symptom scores and urinary flow using modified WVTT–Rezūm techniques in large‐volume glands at 1 year [23]. Comparative studies evaluating outcomes by prostate size suggest that clinical improvements are maintained in prostates ≥ 80 mL, although procedural complexity may increase [22]. Long‐term retrospective data (up to 48 months) indicate sustained improvements in IPSS, Q max, and QoL, but with higher retreatment rates in larger prostates [21]. These findings are supported by meta‐analytic data demonstrating consistent improvements in IPSS, IPSS‐QoL, Q max, and PVR, with no change in IIEF‐EF, and low retreatment at 1.2% in the short‐term [24]. WVTT–Rezūm provided both statistically significant and clinically important short‐term improvements in LUTS with low complication rates in men with prostates ≥ 80 mL, although long‐term outcomes remained unclear [24].

The modest sample size over time and inclusion of patients from only two high‐volume centers in Canada and Italy may affect the external validity of findings and applicability to broader clinical environments. In this cohort, treatment selection was based on shared decision‐making where patients were counseled on all available options. Patients selected WVTT–Rezūm based on individual factors such as values and preferences, including but not limited to avoidance of invasive surgery, financial capability, and preservation of sexual function. As this study was not blinded, the comorbidity profile and perioperative risk may have also indirectly influenced treatment allocation. These factors contribute to selection bias and can limit direct comparability with patients undergoing other surgical management options such as HoLEP. While enucleation and resection remain standard treatments for large prostates, direct comparative data with WVTT–Rezūm in this population are limited. A comprehensive JAMA review reported HoLEP surgical retreatment rates of 3.3% at 24 months and 6.6% at 60 months, applicable to any prostate size [2]. Contrastingly, the 48 month retrospective analysis found a higher retreatment rate of 15.2% in larger glands following WVTT–Rezūm in a long‐term period [21]. Robust comparative findings on long‐term retreatment rates between WVTT–Rezūm and HoLEP in prostates ≥ 80 mL remain limited and are needed to better delineate the role of WVTT–Rezūm in contemporary practice.

There are additional limitations. Although outcomes were collected up to 36 months post‐procedure, follow‐up completeness declined over time due to patient attrition. This was most pronounced for uroflowmetry measures requiring in‐person assessment, with Q max and PVR data decreasing from 231 participants at baseline to 8 and 9 patients, respectively, at 36 months. This reduction in follow‐up, particularly for objective flow parameters, represents an important limitation and introduces the potential for attrition bias. Moreover, while baseline characteristics relating to urinary status were analyzed comprehensively, outcomes related to sexual function were mainly assessed by erection and ejaculatory outcomes, which were assumed to infer other domains of sexual function. Finally, this study did not include predictive analysis, so further studies are required to evaluate the best candidates with prostate size above 80 mL.

Conclusion

WVTT–Rezūm demonstrated significant benefit to urinary symptoms among patients with BPH involving prostates ≥ 80 mL while preserving sexual function over 6‐, 12‐, 24‐, and 36‐month periods. Urinary status measures demonstrated statistically significant improvements at each follow‐up time point (p < 0.0001), suggesting long‐term and clinically meaningful improvement in symptoms over the duration of follow‐up. No statistically significant worsening in sexual function was observed. The impact on prostate size is unclear.

This study further establishes WVTT–Rezūm as an effective minimally invasive therapeutic approach that offers symptom relief with an acceptable safety profile in patients with a larger prostate size.

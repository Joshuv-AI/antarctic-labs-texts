Proactive therapeutic drug monitoring of infliximab leads to treatment durability in paediatric Crohn's disease: A single‐centre Australian experience
Authors: Cher Wei Chuah, Jessica Eldredge, Annabel Magoffin, Edward Vincent O'Loughlin, Calvin Joomann Park, Juliana Puppi, Susan Siew, Michael Stormon, Kunal Thacker, Shoma Dutt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo evaluate the durability and predictors of loss of response of infliximab (IFX) over 2 years in paediatric Crohn's disease (CD) utilizing a proactive therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) strategy.MethodsA retrospective, single‐centre cohort study was conducted at The Children's Hospital at Westmead (CHW), Australia, from January 2019 to December 2021. Proactive TDM was performed at induction, end of induction and 6‐monthly thereafter. The primary outcome was switching to a different biologic as a marker of treatment failure. Secondary outcomes of a physician global assessment (PGA) scale, calprotectin, albumin and C‐reactive protein (CRP) levels were collected at induction, end of induction, 1‐year and 2‐year timepoints.ResultsSeventy‐nine patients were included (62% male, median age 13 years). Durability was 96% at 1 year and 88% at 2 years. Nine patients switched biologics with a median time of 57 weeks. Predictors of therapy failure included the need for escalated induction (odds ratio [OR] 8.5, p = 0.01) colonic phenotype (OR 9.7, p < 0.01), and post‐induction calprotectin level above 305 μg/g (positive predictive value 36%) were identified.ConclusionsUtilizing a proactive TDM results in excellent durability of IFX at 2 years in paediatric CD. Colonic phenotype is an independent predictor for IFX failure, highlighting the need for directed therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a state of chronic relapsing intestinal inflammation because of dysregulation of the innate and adaptive immunity against intestinal microorganisms in genetically susceptible individuals. 1 When excessive recruitment and hyperactivation of neutrophils occurs, a complex cascade of immune signalling results in mucosal injury. 2 One cytokine in this process is tumour necrosis factor alpha (TNF‐α). Infliximab (IFX), a chimeric immunoglobulin G subclass 1 (IgG1) monoclonal anti‐TNF‐α antibody, is now the standard advanced therapy for children with IBD. 3 In Australia, IFX is regulated by the Pharmaceutical Benefits Scheme (PBS). PBS only allows access to IFX after patients fail other therapies, for example, corticosteroids and immune modifiers, for at least 3 months. The PBS‐approved induction dosing is 5 mg/kg/dose. A top‐down approach is approved for fistulising disease. 4 This is different to other centres where there is no requirement for prior therapies or dose limits. 5

On standard dosing of IFX, 5 mg/kg/dose, one‐third of patients will develop secondary loss of response (LOR) at 1 year and a further 50% will develop LOR by 5 years. 3 Sustained high trough levels are proven to increase the durability of IFX. The typical accepted target in adults is 3–7 mg/L. However, a higher cut‐off of 9.7–15.8 mg/L is needed depending on disease phenotype. 6 , 7 , 8 Current recommendations from European Crohn's and Colitis Organisation–European Society for Paediatric Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition (ECCO‐ESPGHAN) for paediatric Crohn's disease (CD) are trough levels of >25 μg/mL at Week 2, >15 μg/mL at Week 6 and >5 μg/mL at Week 14 onwards to achieve mucosal healing. 8 Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) is required to monitor response to dosing and guide escalation and de‐escalation of IFX dosing. However, whether this should be done proactively or reactively has been debatable, with more recent literature recommending proactive monitoring. 9 , 10

A systematic review and meta‐analysis of only randomized controlled trials found no benefit of proactive TDM for clinical remission over reactive monitoring. 11 This review included only one paediatric study, with the rest being adult studies. Two other systematic reviews that included prospective cohort studies and retrospective studies found that proactive TDM leads to increased clinical remission, endoscopic remission and lower rates of treatment failure. 12 , 13 To date, there has only been one randomized controlled trial on a paediatric population, the Pediatric Crohn's Disease Adalimumab Level‐based Optimization Treatment (PAILOT) study, which showed that proactive TDM had higher rates of steroid‐free remission 82% versus 48% during the maintenance phase. 14 Increasingly, proactive TDM is recognized as the standard of care. 15 Our unit had access to increased dosing of IFX and regular drug monitoring approved by the local hospital committee once PBS indications were met. In this study, we set forth to detail our experience of adopting proactive TDM for IFX in CD.

METHODS

Ethics statement

This study was undertaken with approval from our local institutional ethical board at Sydney Children's Hospitals Network (HREA 2023/ETH01331). Given the retrospective nature of the study and the use of routinely collected de‐identified data, a waiver of consent was granted.

Aim

The main objective was to evaluate the durability of IFX therapy for patients with CD using proactive TDM over 2 years. Secondary objectives were to identify disease‐related and treatment‐related factors contributing to durability. Our unit adopted a local proactive TDM strategy from 2019 onwards, which is detailed in the study design below.

Study population

We performed a retrospective chart review of patients commenced on IFX for IBD between 1 January 2019 and 31 December 2021 at the Children's Hospital at Westmead (CHW), and we report on the 2‐year outcomes of this cohort.

Study design—Local proactive strategy

Indications for IFX therapy were severe refractory CD as characterized by a Paediatric Crohn Disease Activity Index (PCDAI) score of above 30, failure to wean systemic steroids and failure of either azathioprine or methotrexate, or fistulising disease. If fistulising disease is the first presentation, our standard practice is to start an immunomodulator at the time of commencing IFX. Vitamin D, Vitamin B12, folate and iron levels were checked and corrected at diagnosis and 3‐monthly as routine.

IFX levels were measured at Week 6 of induction or prior to the third dose, at Week 14 or prior to the fourth dose, and 6‐monthly thereafter as routine. Target trough levels were >15 μg/mL at induction ‘Week 6’ and >5 μg/mL at end of induction/maintenance. 8 Standard IFX dosing is 5 mg/kg at 0, 2 and 6 weeks for induction and every 8 weeks during maintenance phase. Physicians maintained discretion to escalate therapy at any time point based on drug levels and clinical response, as well as frequency of monitoring infliximab levels. Escalation was either increasing the dose to 10 mg/kg or shortening the interval between doses. At induction, the minimum interval was 1 week, and in maintenance, the frequency was capped at 4 weekly intervals. IFX levels were performed following escalation or de‐escalation.

Data on patient demographics, disease characteristics using Paris classification were collected along with immunomodifier used, concomitant therapies during induction, IFX dosing and frequency, and IFX drug and antibody levels. A physician global assessment (PGA) scale of remission, mild, moderate and severe was used to determine clinical status. When a PCDAI score was available, it was transcribed to its respective PGA scale; otherwise, their disease status was determined based on physician documentation of the clinic visit. Clinic visits 6 weeks either side of the 1‐year and 2‐year mark were included; if there was no clinic visit at the relevant period, then no result was recorded. Of note, our cohort continued to have close monitoring during coronavirus disease (COVID)‐19, utilizing telehealth, IBD nursing prompts to complete blood and clinic reviews.

Outcomes

The primary outcome measured was switching to a different biologic as a surrogate for treatment failure as determined by their treating physician. The reason for the switch was categorized into primary non‐response (PNR), secondary LOR, with or without subtherapeutic levels and/or adverse reactions.

Secondary outcomes included disease activity as characterized by PGA, faecal calprotectin level, serum levels of albumin and C‐reactive protein (CRP). Disease activity was assessed at four time points: the start of induction (Week 0), the end of induction (Week 14 or before the fourth dose), 1‐year and 2‐year after starting IFX.

IFX levels performed during the 2 years were collected and analysed.

Statistical analysis

Patient demographic and clinical characteristics are presented as median, quartiles and range for continuous variables and frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. Independent t‐test was used to compare differences in continuous variables between groups and chi‐square or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables. Spearman's correlation was used to assess for any associations between continuous variables. Mann–Whitney U test was used to assess association between categorical and continuous variables. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate the discriminatory ability of continuous predictors for a binary outcome. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 29 (SPSS Inc.). Statistical significance was determined as p < 0.05.

RESULTS

Seventy‐nine patients with CD who commenced induction IFX between 1 January 2019 and 31 December 2021 were identified. Seven of these patients were initially diagnosed as IBD‐Unclassified but had been reclassified as CD by the start of IFX. The median age at diagnosis was 12 years (interquartile range [IQR]: 10−13), and the median age at starting IFX was 13 years (IQR: 10–14). Overall, 62% of our patients were male. Ileocolonic disease was present in 64.6% of patients, while 43.8% had perianal disease. Isolated perianal disease was observed in 5% of patients. Stricturing disease was present in 9% of patients, whereas only one patient had internal penetrating disease, and one patient had both stricturing and penetrating disease. Growth failure at baseline was observed in 38% of patients.

The majority, 62%, received methotrexate, and 21% had azathioprine. Two patients switched to a different immunomodulator due to side effects; three patients stopped without restarting. Of note, 11% of patients received no immunomodulator during induction or maintenance; one patient had pancreatitis with azathioprine prior to starting IFX; the remaining were not treated due to patient preference. A total of 18% of patients received systemic steroids during induction with IFX.

In terms of the IFX regimen, 13% of patients had escalated dosing during induction at physician discretion, generally due to concerns about disease severity and response to the first dose of IFX. Moreover, 80% subsequently received escalated dosing during the maintenance phase of treatment, of which 40% subsequently had a de‐escalation in their dosing. Only 20% received both standard induction and standard maintenance (Table 1).

Out of 79 patients with CD, only nine patients were switched to an alternative biologic. The durability of anti‐TNF treatment was 96% at 1 year and 89% at 2 years. The median time to switch was 57 weeks (IQR: 38−75).

Of the eight patients who proceeded to maintenance, all received escalated maintenance except Patient 5. Patient 9 switched due to psoriasis, and the remaining seven patients switched due to secondary LOR. This patient received methotrexate as an immune modulator and received standard induction and maintenance. This patient experienced a disease flare at 57 weeks and was found to have undetectable IFX levels and IFX antibodies and did not proceed to escalated dosing. Secondary outcomes

PGA

At induction, 47% had mild disease, 38% had moderate disease and 15% had severe disease by PGA. At the end of induction, 65% were in remission, 28% had mild disease, and the remaining 7% had moderate disease.

Seventy patients had clinic follow‐up at the 1‐year mark and 54 patients at the 2‐year mark. Moreover, 83% were in remission at the 1‐year mark, 14% had mild disease and 3% had moderate to severe disease. At the 2‐year mark, 91% were in remission, 7% had mild disease and only 2% had moderate disease.

Albumin

The median albumin level at induction was 42 g/L (IQR: 37–44), rising to 43 g/L (IQR: 42–46) at the end of induction and remaining at 44 g/L (IQR: 43–46) at both the 1‐year and 2‐year mark.

CRP

The median CRP level at induction was 10.5 mg/L (IQR: 1.7–30.7), decreasing to 1.2 mg/L (IQR: 0.4–3.0) at the end of induction and was 0.6 mg/L (IQR: 0.3–2.1) and 0.5 mg/L (0.3–2.2), respectively, for the 1‐year and 2‐year mark.

Calprotectin

The median calprotectin level at induction was 1860 μg/g (IQR: 975.5–2915), decreasing to 94 μg/g (IQR: 16.5–335.5) at the end of induction and fell further to 88 μg/g (IQR: 30.5–277.5) at 1 year and 37 μg/g (IQR: 20–125) at 2 years.

IFX levels

Patients who received standard induction had induction trough levels of 15.35 μg/mL (IQR: 7.92–22.48). Patients who received escalated induction had induction levels of 5.01 μg/mL (IQR: 1.58–20.80). However, this was not significant (p = 0.37). The median level during maintenance was 7.14 μg/mL (IQR: 3.93–11.50). There was no difference between escalated or standard maintenance (p = 0.58). Patients who received escalated maintenance had a median of 5 (IQR: 4–6) IFX levels measured during these 2 years versus standard maintenance, which was 4 (IQR: 3.5–4).

Predictors of switching biologics

Significant factors to predict failure of response to IFX, were patients requiring escalated induction odds ratio (OR) 8.5 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.8–40.5; p = 0.01), having the colonic phenotype OR 9.7 (95% CI: 2.1–43.7; p < 0.01), concomitant steroid OR 4.8 (95% CI: 1.1–21.0; p = 0.05), concomitant aminosalicylate (5‐ASA) OR 21.3 (95% CI: 4.3–107.7; p < 0.01), PGA at end of induction OR 9.5 (95% CI: 1.6–57.9; p = 0.03). High calprotectin levels at the end of induction predicted failure. ROC analysis showed that a cut‐off of 305 μg/g had a sensitivity of 100% and specificity of 77.3%. DISCUSSION

Recent literature looking at real‐world evidence compared to data from randomized controlled trials has increasingly supported the use of proactive TDM. 14 , 15 , 16 Paediatric patients have different pharmacokinetic profiles, for example, higher body surface area, which can lead to insufficient dosing from standard regimens. 17 Current practice shows that we can achieve increased durability of IFX when therapeutic doses are achieved with a range of strategies, such as the use of immunomodulators, dose escalation and intensification either during induction or maintenance to ensure adequate drug exposure. 6 , 18 , 19 , 20

Our study demonstrates that we achieved excellent durability in CD, surpassing clinical trial data. A systematic review of 11 studies of real‐world paediatric data assessing treatment persistence showed a wide range, with 83%–97% at 1 year then falling to 67%–91% at 2 years. 18 The wide range exists because of heterogeneity of the studies in terms of inclusion, exclusion criteria, treatment modality and endpoints. Factors such as exclusion of PNRs, the use of immunomodulators and dosing regimens (standard vs. escalated) were contributory. Dosing schedules and access to biological medications are often determined by regulatory and funding bodies. affecting patient access beyond the standard 5 mg/kg dosing and 8‐weekly maintenance schedules. 21 , 22 The use of immunomodulators, although widely recommended, carries the stigma of the risk of lymphoma, 23 with some clinicians opting for escalated dosing strategies instead to mitigate immunogenicity. 24

Looking at two comparable studies, we achieved excellent durability of 96% at 1 year and 89% at 2 years. Vahabnezhad et al. showed in their cohort of paediatric CD that with dose intensification they were able to achieve durability of 88% at 1 year and 80% at 2 years. 19 Their treatment escalation was guided by clinician judgement, rather than TDM levels, after standard induction therapy, resulting in 41% of their patients receiving escalated maintenance dosing. In contrast, 80% of our cohort were escalated guided by TDM. Hoelz et al. reported a durability of 90% at 1 year and 86% at 2 years in their paediatric CD patients treated with both standard and escalated induction and maintenance regimen guided by a proactive TDM strategy as well as clinician assessment of disease activity. 20

Proactive TDM compared to reactive TDM leads to increased usage of IFX, leading critics to question cost‐effectiveness. However, Yao et al. report that while upfront costings are higher in the first year, overall, there were lower costs of quality‐adjusted‐life‐years for proactive TDM. 25 We demonstrated that 40% of our cohort subsequently de‐escalated with TDM. Paediatric access to advanced and newer biologicals is a further issue to consider, and therefore maximizing the therapeutic effect of the first biological with TDM prolongs the duration and durability of this therapeutic class; this, combined with the increasing availability of biosimilars, will increase the cost‐effectiveness of a proactive TDM strategy.

Post hoc analysis of our data to determine factors significant for treatment failure reveals a few interesting insights.

Firstly, it appears that colonic phenotype itself predicts a poor durability. These patients had no significant difference in treatment regimen received and no difference in response to induction therapy. Secondly, our finding of a calprotectin cutoff of 305 ng/g is similar to the conventional 250 ng/g cutoff to predict durability as a measure of response to induction. 8 , 26 Thirdly, patients who received escalated induction, concomitant steroids and 5‐ASA did poorly. These were likely patients who had worse disease, or a poor response to the initial doses of IFX. The decision to escalate therapy at this point would have been done without drug levels, guided solely by physician discretion.

With escalated induction, only one patient was deemed to be a PNR; the remaining nine continued onto maintenance, and at the 2‐year mark, 6 out of the 10 patients were still able to be on IFX as their primary advanced therapy. Marshanski et al. have previously shown that different induction regimens did not affect outcomes a 1 year. 27 However, given the retrospective nature of our study, our data does not allow us to assess whether these patients would have achieved similar or poorer outcomes had they undergone standard induction.

Lastly, our patients who eventually switched to advanced biologics had a poorer response to induction therapy as evidenced by the difference in PGA status, CRP levels and calprotectin levels. This, along with our findings of colonic phenotype as a risk factor, and no significant difference found in the IFX levels achieved during induction (before third dose) and after induction (before fourth dose), raises the question of the presence of other unmodifiable factors such as genetic causes of IBD that affect response to IFX that could not be analysed in our data. 28 , 29 , 30

Limitations of our study include that it is retrospective in nature, and from a single centre; the small number of patients who failed therapy limits further statistical analysis. In addition, data were collected during the COVID‐19 pandemic, which forced changes to routine clinical monitoring including more remote testing such as faecal calprotectin. There were problems with the availability of laboratories to conduct faecal calprotectin, patient willingness to travel and deliver samples to laboratories, which impacted sample size. To manage these limitations, we chose the switch to a different biologic as a robust end point to reflect physician judgement of treatment failure. During this period of 2019–2021, our centre had ready access to second‐line biologics such as ustekinumab and vedolizumab, and access per se was not a barrier in the decision to change biologics.

The strength of our study is the inclusion of all patients treated with IFX and not just patients who responded to induction, thereby capturing long‐term real‐world experience of treating patients using proactive TDM. Advanced biologicals largely remain inaccessible and off‐label to the paediatric population due to regulatory processes, and this may limit wide and equitable access to these drugs. Maximizing the effect and durability of IFX, which is approved for Australian children with CD, by TDM results in the prolonged use of the first biological and sparing of escalation to more expensive and unapproved advanced biologicals. We demonstrate that proactive early, Week 6, TDM‐led dose optimization results in excellent drug tolerance and durability, with excellent clinical outcomes, noting our high rates of concomitant immunomodulator use, which is mandated by the Australian regulatory process.

CONCLUSION

Our durability of 89% at 2 years supports the use of a proactive TDM model in maintaining IFX as the primary advanced therapy for paediatric CD and adds to the growing body of real‐world evidence to do so. In addition, it highlights that whilst response to induction is important in determining durability of IFX, a significant proportion of patients can still be maintained on IFX as their primary advanced therapy. Our data also identify colonic phenotype as an independent predictor of poor durability, raising the question that this group requires a different targeted therapy.

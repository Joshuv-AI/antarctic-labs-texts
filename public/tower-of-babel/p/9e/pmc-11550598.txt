Application of business intelligence based on big data in E-commerce data evaluation
Authors: Lai Wang, Thillai Raja AL Pertheban, Tianqi Li, Lei Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the vigorous development of e-commerce, more and more goods are sold online. The electronic platform not only brings convenience to people's lives but also gives more people the opportunity of employment and entrepreneurship, which contributes to the promotion of economic value and the creation of wealth. With the gradual maturity of network technology represented by Big Data, it has also led to the further development of e-commerce. In the past, e-commerce mostly used business intelligence systems for data analysis. However, as the data becomes more and more complex, the innovation ability and data analysis ability of traditional business intelligence systems are relatively conservative, so it is of great significance to update and strengthen business intelligence to analyze its role in e-commerce data. Therefore, this paper proposed the application of business intelligence based on Big Data in e-commerce data analysis. By combining Big Data and a business intelligence system and taking the e-commerce data of a certain brand of beverage as the research object, the Days-Times-Money (DTM) model was established, and the data mining technology was used to classify the brand consumers. The data results showed that among the three consumption attributes, the highest consumption density of consumption days, consumption times, and consumption amount was 68.63 %, 67.99 %, and 69.72 % respectively. These were not more than 70 %, which indicated that the consumption of this brand of beverages had a high space for growth. According to the consumption density, the users of this brand of beverage were divided into four consumer groups. According to the classification results, it provided feasible marketing reference opinions for the brand beverage and could provide direction for brand value-added by mining the hidden data of the brand beverage. This paper hoped to use the brand beverage as a case to provide reference and reality for the development of e-commerce enterprises and provide a reference for the application and promotion of business intelligence based on Big Data in e-commerce data analysis.Keywords: Big data, Business intelligence, Electronic commerce, Data mining technology

Difficult to mine potential rules

E-commerce data application cases under business intelligence evaluation

To study the application effect of business intelligence based on Big Data in e-commerce data analysis, this paper established a DTM model. It also took the sales of a brand of beverage on an e-commerce platform as the data set and conducted data mining analysis on sales records. The obtained results were grouped into consumers and targeted marketing suggestions were put forward for different consumer groups according to the grouping situation. In the DTM model, D (Days) represented the customer's monthly (30 days) consumption days; T (Times) referred to the daily purchase times of customers; M (Money) represented the total monthly consumption amount of the customer. Therefore, the DTM model needed to measure the customer value according to the actual amount of customer consumption.

In the DTM model, the determination and calculation of customer density is the most important step in the DTM model, which is closely related to the accurate evaluation of customer value. The consumption density of days (D) is obtained by dividing the number of days spent by a customer each month (for example, 30 days) by the actual time spent in a month. It reflects the number of purchases made by consumers. The consumption density (t) of time is a measure of the average daily shopping quantity of consumers, which can better understand consumers' shopping habits and loyalty. The consumption density (m) of the currency amount is the total consumption of the user per month divided by the number of days of the user. It reflects the average consumption of the user in each purchase. It is a major criterion for evaluating customer value. When calculating consumption density, several assumptions will have an impact on the research results. We assume that each purchase of a customer is independent of each other, and their spending is proportional to the number of their purchases. In addition, this article also assumes that consumers' purchasing behavior in a month is evenly distributed. However, in the real environment, these two assumptions may not be correct, so in practice, they must be appropriately adjusted and optimized for different situations.

In the experimental design stage, we collected the user consumption data of the e-commerce platform, including purchase history, consumption frequency, and amount, and carried out cleaning and pretreatment. Using data mining technology, consumers are divided into four categories. This article focuses on the characteristics of consumption frequency and amount and simplifies consumers into two major groups: high-frequency high-amount and high-frequency low-amount. Through association rule mining, the purchase preferences and associations of different user groups are analyzed, especially the preferences of high-frequency and high-value groups for beverages. Based on the analysis, formulate differentiated marketing strategies: for high-frequency and high-amount groups, recommend new drinks, and adopt personalized marketing; for high-frequency and low-amount groups, provide full discount coupons to stimulate consumption.

In the program processing stage, we use data processing tools to extract consumer data from the e-commerce platform database, and clean and preprocess it. Subsequently, clustering algorithms, such as K-means or hierarchical clustering, are implemented through machine learning libraries to divide users into different groups. Then, the Apriori algorithm is used to mine the purchase preferences and associations of various groups, which can be achieved through data mining libraries or customization. Finally, according to the user group and purchase behavior, design personalized marketing strategies, such as recommending new products or issuing discount coupons.

Fig. 2 shows the first consumer group. As shown from Fig. 2A and B, the consumption frequency of such users was relatively high. The difference was the amount of consumption. Users with high consumption frequency and amount were ideal users. Through data mining, it could be inferred that they were the type of people who liked to drink drinks. Therefore, when implementing differentiated marketing, such users should be considered first, and the one-to-one marketing model could be adopted for such users. When new drinks were produced, they could be recommended to such users first, and the success rate of promoting their consumption would be greatly improved. Users with high consumption frequency and low consumption amount could be analyzed as those who liked to drink but were reluctant to consume. This type of user could often be issued with full discount coupons to promote their consumption impulse.

Fig. 3 shows the second consumer group, which had high consumption days, low consumption times, and a gap in consumption amount.

As shown in Fig. 3A and B:the number of days spent by such users was high, but the number of times was low. It was inferred from Big Data mining that such users regarded drinks as necessities of daily life, but they were not the only ones. They might only treat drinks as drinking water to quench their thirst. Among such users, users with low consumption amounts could also take the measures of regularly issuing full discount coupons to promote their consumption level; users with high consumption amounts could set some timed beverage seconds to increase the consumption times of such users.

Fig. 4 shows the third consumer group.

As shown in Fig. 4A and B, the consumption days and amount of such users were relatively low, but there was a gap in consumption times. It could be inferred from business intelligence based on Big Data that such users were typical beverage consumers, who would only buy drinks when visiting relatives and friends during the festival. The number of consumption varied according to the number of visitors. This type of user regarded drinks as an ornament for life. In their eyes, drinks were not necessities of life. The value of this type of user was highly uncertain. Therefore, they could only maintain regular interaction with this type of user to ensure their loyalty.

Fig. 5 shows the fourth consumer group. As can be seen from Fig. 5A and B, this type of user consumes low days and high consumption. The difference in consumption times might be due to the different promotion efforts of the platform. Through data analysis, it could be learned that this type of user was a type who liked to drink and stock up. For this type of user, it was not allowed to set too complex full discount coupons. This was because they would be troublesome. The release of a full discount was likely to cause users' disgust and eventually cause adverse effects. Therefore, bundled discounts were the best way to promote the consumption level of such users. The customer value of such users was relatively high. If the marketing method was appropriate, it could effectively promote the sales of beverages in the e-commerce platform in a short time.

Conclusions

Data mining technology under Big Data was of great significance to the role of business intelligence in e-commerce data analysis. Through the data analysis of the DTM model, it could be seen that the application of Big Data in business intelligence systems was an inevitable trend. The experimental data showed that data mining technology could effectively classify consumers' consumption and help enterprises accurately locate and service consumers according to their consumption habits, which had a good effect on enterprises exploring potential consumer customers and the hidden value in e-commerce data. However, there were still some problems in this study while achieving certain results. First, due to the time constraints, the experimental results might have some errors due to the short duration of this experiment; secondly, in terms of experimental data collection, this paper only selected a certain brand of beverage as the experimental data and did not consider the situation of other e-commerce products. The experimental data capacity was small and not representative. Finally, this analysis only discussed the advantages of data mining and did not study the disadvantages of the Big Data era. Everything in the world has two sides, and Big Data is not perfect. When business intelligence based on Big Data brings efficiency to e-commerce data analysis, it should also consider the security problems of Big Data. However, with the continuous development of network technology, the solution to these problems is only a matter of time. To have a deeper understanding of the application effect of business intelligence based on Big Data in e-commerce data analysis, the results would be further improved and analyzed according to these problems in subsequent research.

Incremental Prognostic Value of Heart‐Type Fatty Acid Binding Protein in Patients With Light Chain Cardiac Amyloidosis: A Prospective Cohort Study
Authors: Xinqing Li, Anran Xin, Guo Ai, Chengyi Wang, Xuemei Zhao, Yan Huang, Xiaofeng Zhuang, Huihui Liu, Qiong Zhou, Jian Zhang, Yuhui Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe utility of heart‐type fatty acid binding protein (H‐FABP) as a prognostic biomarker in light chain cardiac amyloidosis (AL‐CA) patients remains unestablished. Consecutive patients diagnosed with AL‐CA in the Heart Failure Center; Fuwai Hospital were enrolled. Baseline H‐FABP levels categorized patients as low (≤ 9.5 ng/mL) or high (> 9.5 ng/mL) group. Kaplan–Meier analysis and Cox proportional hazards models were performed. Model performance was assessed using Harrell's C concordance index (C‐index), integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) index, and net reclassification improvement (NRI) index. Eighty‐four patients were included (mean age 60.12 ± 11.25 years; 32.1% female), with 30 (35.7%) in the high H‐FABP group. Overall survival was significantly lower in the high H‐FABP group. In multivariable Cox models adjusted for age, eGFR and chemotherapy, H‐FABP > 9.5 ng/mL remained independently associated with an increased risk of all‐cause mortality when incorporating either the European modified Mayo 2004 stages [adjusted hazard ratio (HR): 2.79, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.49–5.22; p = 0.001] or the revised Mayo 2012 stages (HR: 2.67, 95% CI: 1.48–4.83; p = 0.001). Adding H‐FABP levels significantly improved predictive performance across both baseline staging systems. For the European modified Mayo 2004 stage, the C‐index increased from 0.622 to 0.663, accompanied by positive IDI (0.053) and NRI (0.294). Similarly, for the revised Mayo 2012 stage, the C‐index increased from 0.611 to 0.660, with positive IDI (0.074) and NRI (0.308). Elevation of H‐FABP is associated with an increased risk of all‐cause mortality in patients with AL‐CA. It provides significant additive prognostic value to the established staging system for prognosis in AL‐CA.Keywords: H‐FABP, light chain cardiac amyloidosis, prognosis

Introduction

Systemic light chain amyloidosis (AL) is a disorder characterized by the presence of monoclonal plasma cells and the deposition of amyloid fibrils composed of misfolded immunoglobulin light chains in various organs. Cardiac involvement occurs in over 70% of patients and is referred to as light chain cardiac amyloidosis (AL‐CA). AL‐CA is a frequently underrecognized cause of heart failure. However, recent advances in cardiac diagnostic strategies have substantially improved its detection, revealing a higher prevalence than previously assumed. The mechanisms of cardiac dysfunction in AL‐CA are likely to be multifactorial, including mechanical disruption of tissue structure, inflammation, oxidative stress, apoptosis, and autophagy caused by fibrils deposition, as well as inducible myocardial ischemia resulting from reduced cardiac output and amyloid infiltration of vessels [1]. Survival in amyloidosis is closely related to cardiac involvement.

The use of cardiac biomarkers such as N‐terminal pro‐brain natriuretic peptide (NT‐proBNP) and troponin has revolutionized the prognosis for patients with AL‐CA and led to their use in staging systems, such as the European modified Mayo 2004 staging system and the revised Mayo 2012 staging system [2, 3, 4]. The exploration of novel biomarkers targeting diverse pathophysiological aspects holds promise for further refining prognostic predictions and elucidating the underlying pathophysiological mechanisms in AL‐CA patients.

Heart‐type fatty acid binding protein (H‐FABP) is a low‐molecular‐weight cytosolic protein (about 15kD) serving as the principal transporter of long‐chain fatty acids, which constitute about 70% of the energy source in a healthy heart, toward the mitochondrial beta‐oxidation system within cardiomyocytes. Encoded by the FABP3 gene located on the 1p33‐p32 region of chromosome 1, H‐FABP is prominently expressed in the myocardium. H‐FABP plays an important role in cellular signaling, lipid metabolism, and myocyte homeostasis [5]. Upon myocardial injury, H‐FABP is rapidly released into the blood stream. H‐FABP may offer a novel means to identify patients who are at particular risk for adverse outcomes. Studies have shown H‐FABP to be a reliable indicator of myocardial injury and a predictor of mortality in various cardiac conditions such as acute coronary syndrome (ACS) [6, 7], pulmonary embolism (PE) and heart failure (HF) [8].

Given the substantial prognostic significance of H‐FABP in other cardiac disease, as well as the involvement of myocardial injury in CA, our investigation aimed to assess its potential prognostic value in patients with cardiac amyloidosis to ascertain any additional prognostic relevance.

Methods

Study Population

Patients with suspected CA were consecutively hospitalized for diagnostic confirmation and further evaluation at the Heart Failure Center, Fuwai Hospital (Beijing, China), between October 2015 and May 2022. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients diagnosed with AL‐CA according to the diagnostic algorithms proposed by the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Working Group on Myocardial and Pericardial Diseases and the 2023 American College of Cardiology (ACC) Expert Consensus Decision Pathway; (2) availability of baseline blood samples obtained during the first hospitalization; and (3) provision of written informed consent and agreement to clinical follow‐up. Exclusion criteria included: (1) diagnosis of transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy or other non‐AL types; (2) incomplete clinical or follow‐up data; and (3) coexistence of severe infection, malignancy, or other conditions likely to confound biomarker interpretation. The diagnosis of AL‐CA was confirmed by: (a) evidence of amyloid deposition in cardiac tissue on endomyocardial biopsy, showing the typical apple‐green birefringence under cross‐polarized light with Congo red staining and amyloid typing by immunohistochemistry or immunoelectron microscopy; or (b) proof of extracardiac amyloid deposition combined with typical cardiac amyloidosis features demonstrated by echocardiography or cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR).

Ethics Statement

The investigation conforms with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki (Br Med J 1964; ii: 177). Ethics approval for this study was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of Fuwai Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (approval number: 2023‐2137). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Availability of Data and Materials

The anonymised dataset analyzed during this study is not publicly available due to patient confidentiality. Materials during this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Clinical Evaluation and Laboratory Methods

Patients had a comprehensive medical evaluation on admission with clinical and laboratory assessments. Samples of blood of patients with AL‐CA were collected within 24 h after admission and stored at −80°C. H‐FABP concentration was determined using the fluorescence immunochromatography method (Maya‐500, Shanghai Upper Bio‐Tech Pharma Co. Ltd). The upper limit of the normal reference range for the assay is 7 ng/mL, with a detection range of 1–120 ng/mL.

Follow‐Up and Primary Endpoint

Follow‐up data were obtained from hospital medical records or telephone interviews by a trained doctor. Patients were followed until January 2026. The primary endpoint was all‐cause mortality, with overall survival defined as the time from first admission to all‐cause death. Patients who remained alive at the conclusion of the research period or were lost to follow‐up were censored, on which occasion the time of their last clinic visit or correspondence was utilized in the analysis.

Statistical Analysis

Categorical values are presented as numbers and percentages and compared through use of Fisher's exact tests or chi‐squared tests. Continuous variables were tested for normal distribution using the Shapiro–Wilson test, presented as median with 25th and 75th percentiles (interquartile range) or mean with standard deviation, and compared using Student's test or Mann–Whitney U test. To determine the optimal prognostic cutoff value for H‐FABP, time‐dependent ROC curve analysis was performed at multiple follow‐up time points, including 6 months, 1 year, and 2 years. The 6‐month time point demonstrated the largest area under the curve (AUC) and was consequently selected for cutoff determination. The best threshold (9.5 ng/mL) at this time point was identified by maximizing the Youden Index (Sensitivity + Specificity—1), ensuring the cutoff was specifically optimized for this patient cohort. All analyses were performed using the survivalROC package in R. Patients were categorized into two groups: those with H‐FABP ≤ 9.5 ng/mL (low) and those with H‐FABP > 9.5 ng/mL (high) [9]. Kaplan–Meier cumulative endpoint curves were used to compare groups. The log‐rank test was used to evaluate statistical significance. Cox proportional hazards models were used to estimate the effects of variables on all‐cause mortality. Two multivariable Cox regression models were constructed. Model A was adjusted for age, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), the European modified Mayo 2004 staging system, and chemotherapy status. Similarly, Model B included adjustments for age, eGFR, chemotherapy status, and the revised Mayo 2012 staging system. Hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence interval (CI) are reported. A restricted cubic spline (RCS) curve adjusted for eGFR, the revised Mayo 2012 stages and treatment strategies (whether receiving chemotherapy or not) was constructed to assess the association between H‐FABP and all‐cause mortality. Additional interaction analyses were performed across various clinical subgroups, including age (< or ≥ 65 years), gender, NT‐proBNP (< or ≥ median), high sensitivity troponin T (hs‐cTnT) (< or ≥ median), eGFR (< or ≥ 60 mL/min/m2), difference between involved and uninvolved serum‐free light chains (dFLC) levels (< or ≥ 180 mg/L), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) (< or ≥ 50%) and treatment strategies (whether receiving chemotherapy or not).

A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed with R version 4.1.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The authors had full access to the data and take full responsibility for their integrity. All authors have read and agree to the manuscript.

Results

Overall, 84 patients diagnosed with AL‐CA between October 2015 and May 2022 were included. The mean age was 60.12 ± 11.25 years, 27 patients (32.1%) were female, 70 patients (83.3%) were in New York Heart Association (NYHA) class III‐IV, 70 patients (83.3%) were in the European modified Mayo 2004 stage III, and 79 patients (94.0%) were in the revised Mayo 2012 stage III‐IV. Among these patients, we observed a non‐Gaussian distribution of baseline H‐FABP (Figure S1), with a median concentration of 6.63 ng/mL (interquartile range 4.20 to 11.73 ng/mL). Based on the H‐FABP cutoff value determined for this study population (9.5 ng/mL), patients were categorized into groups with low and high levels of H‐FABP (4.62 [2.86, 6.44] versus 15.04 [11.62, 20.16], p < 0.001). There were 54 (64.3%) patients in the low H‐FABP group and 30 (35.7%) patients in the high H‐FABP group.

Baseline characteristics are compared in Table 1 between two groups. Patients with elevated H‐FABP levels were older and exhibited a more adverse clinical profile, characterized by a higher proportion of NYHA class III–IV patients (96.7% vs. 75.9%), and increased levels of aspartate aminotransferase (AST), uric acid, NT‐proBNP, and hs‐cTnT. There was a significant difference in the distribution of the European modified Mayo 2004 stages between the two groups (p = 0.005). Patients in the high H‐FABP group presented with more advanced disease stages compared to the low H‐FABP group. Specifically, the majority of patients in the high H‐FABP group were classified as Stage IIIb (70.0%), whereas the low H‐FABP group comprised a lower proportion of Stage IIIb patients (33.3%) and a higher proportion of Stage IIIa (46.3%) and Stage II (20.4%) patients. However, the two groups showed no statistically significant differences in the distribution of the revised Mayo 2012 stages, eGFR, or dFLC concentrations. It is noteworthy that there were differences in lipid metabolism biomarkers between the two groups. Specifically, patients in the high H‐FABP group exhibited higher levels of free fatty acids (FFA), while high‐density lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL‐C) was notably lower. No significant differences were observed in echocardiographic parameters or treatment strategies.

During an average follow‐up period of 536 days, 55 (65.5%) patients died. Cumulative overall survival curves were constructed by the Kaplan–Meier method, as shown in Figure 2. The overall survival was markedly higher in patients with low H‐FABP levels (≤ 9.5 ng/mL, low H‐FABP group) than in those with high H‐FABP levels (> 9.5 ng/mL, high H‐FABP group) (Log rank, p = 0.0026).

Kaplan–Meier curves for overall survival based on H‐FABP levels.

Univariable Cox regression analyses revealed an association between H‐FABP and all‐cause mortality, both as a continuous variable [log(H‐FABP), HR 2.56, 95% CI 1.11 to 5.92] and as a categorical variable (H‐FABP > 9.5 ng/mL, HR 2.26, 95% CI 1.31 to 3.89). In Model a, after adjusting for age, eGFR, the European modified Mayo 2004 Stage, and chemotherapy, H‐FABP remained a significant predictor of mortality (H‐FABP > 9.5 ng/mL: HR 2.79, 95% CI 1.49–5.22, p = 0.001). Similarly, in Model b, which adjusted for the revised Mayo 2012 Stage instead of 2004 Stage, elevated H‐FABP levels continued to show a strong association with adverse outcomes (H‐FABP > 9.5 ng/mL: HR 2.67, 95% CI 1.48–4.83, p = 0.001). Consistent results were observed when H‐FABP was analyzed as a continuous variable (Log H‐FABP) in both multivariable models (Table 2). The RCS curve shows a linear association, suggesting that H‐FABP has a proportional effect on all‐cause mortality without significant deviation (Figure S2).

In terms of model performance, the addition of H‐FABP levels to the baseline European modified Mayo 2004 and revised Mayo 2012 staging systems resulted in significant predictive gains across all evaluation metrics (Table 3). For the European modified Mayo 2004 Stage, the C‐index improved from 0.622 to 0.663 (p = 0.038), accompanied by an IDI of 0.053 (95% CI: 0.004–0.154, p = 0.0392) and a total NRI of 0.294 (95% CI: 0.100–0.507, p = 0.0046), which was primarily driven by positive reclassification (NRI+ = 0.281, p = 0.0183). Similarly, incorporating H‐FABP into the revised Mayo 2012 Stage increased the C‐index from 0.611 to 0.66 (p = 0.032) and yielded a highly significant IDI of 0.074 (95% CI: 0.010–0.166, p < 0.001). The total NRI for the revised Mayo 2012 model was 0.308 (95% CI: 0.049–0.521, p = 0.0107), reflecting a robust improvement in risk stratification despite a significant negative reclassification component (NRI‐ = −0.194, p = 0.0206).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first prospective cohort study to evaluate the prognostic value of H‐FABP in patients with AL‐CA. Our results demonstrate that H‐FABP concentration is a powerful and independent predictor of overall survival in AL‐CA patients. H‐FABP provided significant incremental information for risk stratification that was independent of traditional clinical risk factors and the established staging system in AL‐CA.

An increasing body of research has demonstrated the prognostic value of H‐FABP as a biomarker of myocardial injury in various cardiovascular diseases, such as ACS [6, 7], PE [10, 11], stable coronary heart disease (CHD) [12], and HF [8, 13, 14, 15]. For instance, Takeshi et al. found that H‐FABP and brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) concentrations at admission are independently associated with cardiac death and nonfatal cardiac events in patients hospitalized for chronic HF [14]. Hoffmann et al. demonstrated that H‐FABP may improve prognosis for long‐term heart failure rehospitalization in acute HF patients, whereas cardiac troponin I (cTnI) may improve prognosis for all‐cause mortality [15]. Arimoto et al. found that elevated serum H‐FABP levels in congestive HF patients indicate ongoing cardiomyocyte damage and independently predict a higher risk of cardiac events [8].

Our findings extend this prognostic relevance to AL‐CA, suggesting that despite the distinct pathophysiological underpinnings of CA compared to other forms of heart failure, H‐FABP remains a potent marker of adverse outcomes. The integration of H‐FABP into the European modified Mayo 2004 and the revised Mayo 2012 staging systems demonstrated a robust improvement in prognostic accuracy for patients. While the C‐index showed a statistically significant increase, the more substantial gains observed in NRI and IDI provide deeper insights into the clinical utility of H‐FABP. Specifically, a total NRI of approximately 0.30 across both models suggests that nearly one‐third of patients were more accurately reclassified into appropriate risk categories when H‐FABP was included. The significant positive reclassification (NRI+) indicates that H‐FABP is particularly effective at identifying high‐risk individuals who might have been underestimated by traditional Mayo staging alone. The pathophysiological mechanism for the association between H‐FABP and all‐cause mortality risk is yet to be fully understood in this patient population.

H‐FABP as a Biomarker of Myocardial Injury

Our study population exhibited higher H‐FABP levels compared to other studies on H‐FABP in heart failure patients. This suggests that myocardial injury may be more pronounced in AL‐CA patients and act as a critical factor in the pathophysiology and prognostic implications of cardiac amyloidosis. Several mechanisms have been proposed to explain myocardial injury in patients with AL‐CA, including interstitial infiltration of amyloid fibrils, the cytotoxic effects of amyloid precursors, amyloid vascular involvement, and non‐amyloid‐related causes such as cardiac dysfunction, tachyarrhythmias, and hypotension [16].

Cardiac troponins, especially hs‐cTnT, are highly sensitive and specific markers of myocardial injury, and their prognostic value has been validated in patients with both AL [2, 17] and ATTR types of CA [18]. However, compared with cardiac troponins, H‐FABP has plenty of advantages. Unlike cardiac troponins, which are attached to the structural apparatus of myocytes, H‐FABP exists as a soluble protein in the cytoplasm and can potentially be detected even with minor myocardial damage. Niizeki et al. found that H‐FABP was more sensitive to detect ongoing myocardial damage and could identify patients at high risk more effectively than TnT in chronic heart failure patients [13]. In several other clinical settings, H‐FABP has been shown to be an independent predictor of adverse outcomes on top of cardiac troponins or NT‐proBNP. For example, previous studies found that H‐FABP predicts long‐term mortality after ACS and identifies high‐risk patients on top of troponin and other clinical risk factors [6, 7]. Similarly, Puls et al. demonstrated that H‐FABP, rather than cardiac troponin T or NT‐proBNP, is a promising early indicator of right ventricular injury and dysfunction in acute PE that helps optimize risk stratification [10]. While advanced imaging parameters such as myocardial strain have demonstrated prognostic value in cardiac amyloidosis, H‐FABP reflects a distinct biological dimension of ongoing myocardial injury and metabolic stress, which may complement existing risk stratification approaches.

Transition of Energy Substrate Utilization in the Myocardium

H‐FABP, in addition to serving as a biomarker of myocardial injury, is a key protein involved in myocardial lipid metabolism and energy homeostasis. Its primary function is to mediate the uptake of free fatty acids (FFA) by cardiomyocytes, with FFA accounting for more than 70% of the energy supply in a healthy heart. However, in pathological myocardial conditions, there are disruptions in energy metabolism and a shift in substrate utilization, favoring an increased reliance on glucose utilization.

Our findings demonstrate a weak correlation between H‐FABP and FFA levels in AL‐CA patients, suggesting that FFA utilization may be impaired in individuals with elevated H‐FABP. Some researchers have hypothesized that H‐FABP leakage from damaged myocardium could trigger a vicious cycle by impairing cardiac energy metabolism and worsening cardiac dysfunction, ultimately leading to adverse clinical outcomes [19]. This mechanism may also be one of the reasons for the poor prognosis in patients with elevated H‐FABP levels.

H‐FABP May Provoke Vascular Inflammation and Endothelial Dysfunction

Emerging evidence suggests that H‐FABP may also contribute to vascular inflammation and endothelial dysfunction. Wu et al. demonstrated that H‐FABP activates ERK/p38/STAT1/VEGF pathways, impairing angiogenesis and endothelial cell function [20]. Additionally, Nguyen et al. proposed that increased circulating FABP3 could have detrimental effects on vascular homeostasis [21]. It is plausible that H‐FABP plays a role beyond myocardial damage, potentially influencing vascular health and microvascular dysfunction. Future studies are needed to elucidate the precise mechanisms linking H‐FABP to endothelial dysfunction and its impact on AL‐CA prognosis.

These findings underscore that H‐FABP is not solely a marker of myocardial injury but may actively contribute to disease progression. Based on our findings, elevated H‐FABP levels may contribute to disease progression through mechanisms beyond direct myocardial damage, including disruption of cardiac metabolic homeostasis and exacerbation of endothelial dysfunction. Nevertheless, further studies are warranted to elucidate these pathophysiological pathways and validate their clinical implications. Understanding the mechanistic role of H‐FABP in AL‐CA may provide a rationale for targeted therapeutic interventions, particularly in modifying metabolic or endothelial function pathways to improve outcomes.

Several potential limitations to the present study deserve consideration. First, this is a single‐center study conducted at a tertiary referral hospital with a relatively small sample size. As a result, the study population was enriched for patients with advanced disease, predominantly the revised Mayo 2012 stage IV, which may limit the generalizability of our study findings to less severely affected AL‐CA cohorts. Validation in larger, multicenter studies, particularly including patients with earlier‐stage disease, is therefore warranted. Second, our study assessed the association between a single baseline measurement of H‐FABP and the risk of all‐cause mortality, without considering the dynamic changes in H‐FABP levels following treatment initiation or hematologic remission. Consequently, the potential role of H‐FABP as a dynamic marker of treatment response could not be directly addressed in the present study. Serial longitudinal measurements of H‐FABP over time may provide additional prognostic insights. Finally, our data interpretation and conclusions are based on inference rather than causality. However, we believe that our analysis justifies the conclusions.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study provides strong evidence for the prognostic value of H‐FABP for all‐cause mortality in AL‐CA patients. H‐FABP can serve as a novel biomarker independent of other established predictors for prognosis in cardiac amyloidosis. Given its role in myocardial injury, energy metabolism, and vascular dysfunction, H‐FABP may represent a valuable biomarker for refining risk stratification and guiding clinical management in this challenging disease. Further research is warranted to elucidate its full pathophysiological implications and potential therapeutic applications.

Spatiotemporal epidemiology of hepatocellular carcinoma and its association with environmental factors in Gansu province, 2013–2023
Authors: Xiang Li, Gennian Wang, Bo Li, Fan Zhang, Jie Liu, Huijuan Cheng, Yang Zhao, Yumin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a major public health challenge in China, with regional disparities often linked to environmental factors. Gansu Province, characterized by complex topography and climatic heterogeneity, provides a unique setting to examine these determinants. This study characterized the spatiotemporal patterns of HCC incidence in Gansu (2013–2023) and quantified the contributions of terrain, climate, and air quality.MethodsThis population-based ecological study analyzed county-level HCC incidence data (2013–2023) from the Gansu Provincial Cancer Registry. Age-standardized incidence rates (ASIRs) and temporal trends were assessed via direct standardization and Joinpoint regression. Spatial heterogeneity and risk regions (high/low) were identified using a Bayesian hierarchical Besag–York–Mollié (BYM) model. To evaluate environmental drivers, long-term exposures (2003–2013) were correlated with ASIR using Spearman’s rank analysis. Multicollinearity was assessed via Variance Inflation Factor (VIF). Finally, the independent attribution of environmental factors was quantified using Ridge Regression with cross-validated penalization, with coefficient stability validated through 1,000 bootstrap resamples.ResultsThe HCC ASIR in Gansu peaked in 2017 (16.30 per 100,000) before declining to 7.55 by 2023, with a consistent male predominance (sex ratio 2.42:1). Bayesian analysis revealed significant spatial clustering, with high-risk regions concentrated in central and southern counties (e.g., Gannan Plateau). Quantitative attribution demonstrated that topographic features were the dominant drivers, collectively explaining 73.5% of the spatial variance. Altitude emerged as the primary positive driver, contributing 40.3% to the model. Slope exerted a significant negative effect, accounting for 33.2%. In contrast, climatic factors and air pollutants showed minimal and non-significant impacts in the multivariable framework.ConclusionHCC incidence in Gansu Province exhibited substantial temporal fluctuations and marked spatial heterogeneity. Topographic factors—specifically altitude and slope—exerted a predominant influence on the geographic distribution of HCC risk, outweighing meteorological and atmospheric factors at the ecological level. These findings suggest that regional liver cancer surveillance and prevention strategies should prioritize topographic and geographical contexts in environmentally diverse regions.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Age-standardized incidence rate, Environmental factors, Gansu province, Epidemiology

Background

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most common malignant tumors worldwide, with a high incidence rate and significant impact on public health [1]. The prognosis of HCC remains poor, as most patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, resulting in a low five-year survival rate [2]. China is among the countries with the highest burden of HCC, particularly in economically underdeveloped regions [3]. In recent years, with increased health awareness and the implementation of effective prevention and control strategies, the incidence of HCC has shown a declining trend in some areas [4]. However, intensified global climate change and rapid industrialization have made environmental factors increasingly important in the development of cancer. A growing body of evidence suggests that environmental pollutants—especially air contaminants linked to industrial emissions—are risk factors for various malignancies [5–7]. The role of environmental carcinogens in the etiology of HCC is also gradually being uncovered [8, 9]. The persistent presence of such factors continues to pose challenges to HCC prevention and control, highlighting the urgency of further epidemiological research and preventive planning.

Gansu Province, located in northwestern inland China, spans a wide range of latitudes and longitudes and features diverse geographical and climatic conditions. Significant disparities also exist in economic and social development across its counties, leading to considerable variation in environmental exposures. In recent years, with the province’s accelerating industrialization, levels of air pollution have increased. Furthermore, the proximity to the Badain Jaran and Tengger deserts subjects the region to frequent dust storms, which contribute to elevated levels of particulate matter with known health impacts [10, 11]. These unique environmental conditions may partially explain the spatial heterogeneity of HCC incidence across the province. However, few studies to date have systematically analyzed the epidemiological characteristics of HCC in Gansu, and even fewer have explored its associations with environmental factors at the county level.

Therefore, this study aims to provide a comprehensive epidemiological analysis of HCC incidence in Gansu Province from 2013 to 2023. Specifically, the objectives are to: (1) characterize temporal trends and demographic patterns of HCC at the county level; (2) map the spatial distribution of HCC and identify high- and low-risk regions using a Bayesian hierarchical spatial model; and (3) assess the associations between environmental factors—including altitude, slope, temperature, precipitation, humidity, vegetation coverage, and air quality—and spatial variations in HCC risk. The study tests the hypotheses that HCC incidence varies significantly across counties with distinct environmental and geographic characteristics. These findings are expected to inform targeted prevention and early intervention strategies in Gansu and other regions with similar ecological conditions.

Materials and methods

Study area

This study was conducted in Gansu Province, located in northwestern China (latitude 32°31′–42°57′ N, longitude 92°13′–108°46′ E) (Fig. 1). Gansu lies at the intersection of the Loess Plateau, the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau, and the Inner Mongolian Plateau. The province exhibits considerable geographical and climatic heterogeneity, with elevation ranging from 550 to 5,798m and annual precipitation between 50 and 800 mm. The climate is predominantly continental, arid or semi-arid. The study covered all 87 county-level administrative divisions (Fig. 2), representing a total population of approximately 26 million.

Cancer registry and demographic data

Data on newly diagnosed HCC cases between January 1, 2013, and December 31, 2023, were obtained from the Medical Big Data Platform of the Gansu Provincial Health Commission. HCC cases were identified using the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD-10) code C22.0. Each record included sex, age at diagnosis, year of diagnosis, and county-level residential information. Annual mid-year population data, stratified by age and sex, were collected from the Gansu Provincial Bureau of Statistics and used as the denominator for calculating incidence rates.

Environmental data

Environmental variables potentially associated with HCC incidence were compiled from multiple authoritative national and international sources. Altitude data were obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, while slope (terrain gradient) was calculated from the Copernicus 30-m Digital Elevation Model to represent the degree of surface relief within each county. This indicator reflects topographic heterogeneity, which may influence local agricultural patterns, land use, and the distribution and accumulation of environmental pollutants through surface runoff.

Vegetation coverage, quantified using the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), was extracted from NASA’s MODIS MOD13A3 dataset to reflect regional ecological conditions and potential buffering effects against environmental exposures. Climatic factors, including mean annual temperature, total annual precipitation, and relative humidity, were obtained from the ERA5-Land dataset developed by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) and the Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S). These parameters capture the thermal and hydrological variability that may affect human metabolism, pathogen ecology, and chemical exposure levels.

Air quality indicators, including PM1, PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, CO, SO₂, and O₃, were retrieved from high-resolution (1 km) gridded datasets provided by the National Tibetan Plateau Data Center. Additional atmospheric composition data, including black carbon (BC), organic carbon (OC), ammonia (NH₃), nitrogen oxides (NOₓ), and non-methane volatile organic compounds (NMVOC), were obtained from the Emissions Database for Global Atmospheric Research (EDGAR).

All environmental data were processed and averaged within county boundaries using ArcGIS Pro 3.4.2 (Esri, Redlands, CA, USA) to ensure spatial consistency across the 87 administrative units. To account for the latency between environmental exposure and cancer onset, multi-year averages from 2003 to 2013 were calculated to represent long-term exposure preceding the observation period of HCC incidence (2013–2023).

Spatial analysis

Spatial patterns of HCC incidence were assessed using a Bayesian hierarchical spatial model, specifically the Besag–York–Mollié (BYM) model. This model was employed to quantify spatial heterogeneity while adjusting for population size, decomposing county-level incidence variation into a spatially structured component (reflecting dependence among neighboring counties) and an unstructured component (representing random noise).

The model was implemented using the Integrated Nested Laplace Approximations (INLA) approach via the R-INLA package in R software. Compared to traditional Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods, INLA provides more efficient and accurate posterior inference for latent Gaussian models. County adjacency was derived from the Gansu Province shapefile (first-order Queen contiguity), and the expected case counts were included as an offset in the Poisson regression framework. Posterior estimates of Relative Risk (RR) and 95% credible intervals (CrI) were obtained to identify regions of significantly elevated or reduced HCC risk.

To visualize the spatial disparities of HCC burden, distinct cartographic techniques were employed based on the structural properties of the data. For the population-level ASIR distribution, an isopleth map generated via spatial interpolation was used; the discrete county-level administrative boundaries were intentionally omitted from this continuous surface to eliminate artificial boundary effects and faithfully capture seamless biological disease gradients across the province. Conversely, for the posterior estimates derived from the Bayesian BYM model, choropleth maps were applied to strictly respect the discrete operational boundaries of the 87 county-level divisions where administrative and environmental data were aggregated.

Classification of high-risk and low-risk regions

To identify spatial disparities in HCC risk, all 87 county-level administrative divisions in Gansu Province were classified into high-risk and low-risk regions based on the posterior relative risk (RR) estimates obtained from the BYM spatial model. Counties were defined as high-risk if the lower bound of the 95% credible interval of RR (RRlower) exceeded 1, indicating a significantly elevated incidence relative to the provincial average. Conversely, counties were defined as low-risk if the upper bound of the 95% credible interval of RR (RRupper) was below 1, indicating a significantly reduced incidence. This approach enabled robust identification of areas with statistically significant deviations from the average HCC risk, providing a basis for subsequent analyses of environmental and demographic determinants.

Spearman correlation analysis

Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was employed for two primary purposes: first, as a screening tool to identify environmental variables exhibiting significant associations with HCC-ASIR (p < 0.05) for inclusion in subsequent spatial modeling; and second, to conduct a preliminary assessment of collinearity among the candidate variables. This non-parametric method was chosen for its ability to capture monotonic relationships without assuming linearity.

Multicollinearity assessment using Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)

Variables showing significant associations with HCC-ASIR in the Spearman analysis were assessed for multicollinearity using the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF). This assessment aimed to identify variable clusters with severe collinearity, defined as a VIF ≥ 10.

Results

Demographic characteristics of HCC patients (2013–2023)

A total of 30,665 HCC patients diagnosed between 2013 and 2023 in Gansu Province were included in the analysis. The mean age at diagnosis was 59.52 ± 12.07 years, and the median age was 59 years (interquartile range [IQR]: 51-68). Most patients were diagnosed between the ages of 45 and 75. As shown in Fig. 3A, both the mean and median ages of onset showed a slight upward trend over the study period, suggesting a gradual aging of the affected population. Among all cases, 21,693 (70.7%) were male and 8,972 (29.3%) were female, yielding a sex ratio of approximately 2.42:1, calculated by dividing the number of male cases by the number of female cases. Over the 11-year period, the average incidence rate was 13.844 per 100,000 for males and 6.168 per 100,000 for females. This sex distribution indicates a clear male predominance consistent with global HCC epidemiology. Further analysis of age distribution by sex (Fig. 3B) revealed that male patients outnumbered female patients across all age groups, with the most notable disparity observed in the 50–70-year range. These findings underscore the disproportionate burden of HCC among middle-aged and older men.

(A) Annual mean and median age of HCC onset (2013–2023); (B) gender-wise distribution of patients in age groups (2013–2023)

Temporal trends in HCC incidence (2013–2023)

We calculated the ASIR of HCC in Gansu Province from 2013 to 2023, along with sex-specific differences and temporal trends, with results illustrated in Fig. 4. The ASIR rose from 7.41 per 100,000 in 2013 to 16.30 per 100,000 in 2017, followed by a decline to 7.55 per 100,000 in 2023. The sex disparity remained evident in the standardized rates as well: in 2023, the ASIR for males was 10.83 per 100,000, whereas the ASIR for females was 4.12 per 100,000. These findings suggest that while the incidence of HCC has fluctuated over the past decade, men have consistently exhibited a significantly higher burden of disease compared to women.

Joinpoint regression analysis of HCC incidence (2013–2023)

Joinpoint regression analysis was performed to evaluate the temporal trends in HCC incidence in Gansu Province from 2013 to 2023. As shown in Fig. 5, a distinct inflection point was identified in 2016. The ASIR increased from 2013 to 2016, with an annual percent change (APC) of 22.78%, followed by a downward trend from 2016 to 2023, with an APC of -10.1%. These findings are consistent with the descriptive analysis and illustrate the temporal variation in HCC incidence.

Joinpoint regression analysis of ASIR of HCC per 100,000 population, 2013–2023. (A) overall population; (B) male population; (C) female population

When stratified by sex, both sexes exhibited a significant increase from 2013 to 2016, with comparable annual percent changes (APC: 23.29% for males vs. 22.02% for females). However, the declining phase from 2016 to 2023 revealed a distinct sex-specific pattern: the decrease was more pronounced among females (APC = -13.04%) than among males (APC = -8.77%). These results provide quantitative evidence of the temporal trends in HCC burden and highlight a changing sex disparity during the decline period, which may reflect differences in the effectiveness of public health interventions, temporal variations in exposure to risk factors, or sex-specific biological responses.

Spatial distribution of HCC incidence (2013–2023)

The sex-specific ASIR of HCC for all 87 counties are ranked and displayed in Fig. 6, revealing several key objective patterns: a universal male predominance in HCC incidence is unequivocally observed across all counties, with male ASIR consistently exceeding female ASIR regardless of absolute incidence levels; the magnitude of this sex disparity varies considerably, being most pronounced in counties such as Maqu and Diebu where male ASIR more than doubles female ASIR. Furthermore, the ranking demonstrates similar spatial patterns of HCC incidence disparity between the two sexes. In summary, Fig. 6 visually reinforces two fundamental findings: the ubiquitous substantial excess risk of HCC among males in Gansu Province, and the shared spatial determinants underlying incidence rates in both sexes.

Spatial clustering of HCC incidence (2013–2023)

To evaluate the spatial dependence of HCC incidence across counties in Gansu Province, a Bayesian hierarchical spatial model (Besag–York–Mollié, BYM) was applied using county-level ASIR from 2013 to 2023. The BYM model simultaneously accounts for spatially structured heterogeneity—capturing correlations among neighboring counties—and unstructured heterogeneity, reflecting local deviations not explained by spatial proximity. County adjacency was defined based on shared boundaries, and the expected number of cases was incorporated as an offset to standardize for population size differences. Posterior relative risks were estimated for each county, with higher values indicating elevated incidence relative to the provincial average.

The results revealed substantial spatial heterogeneity in HCC incidence across Gansu. Counties in the central and southern regions exhibited higher relative risks, suggesting areas of elevated disease burden, while western counties showed lower relative risks. Some counties displayed pronounced unstructured variation, reflecting local deviations from neighboring trends. These findings confirm that HCC incidence is non-randomly distributed and exhibits strong spatial dependence, highlighting the importance of geographically targeted surveillance and prevention strategies (Fig. 8).

Multicollinearity assessment

To evaluate potential collinearity among the environmental variables, Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) analysis was performed (Table 2). The results revealed significant multicollinearity among several indicators. Specifically, Altitude (VIF = 17.16) and Temperature (VIF = 15.85) exhibited severe collinearity, while Precipitation (VIF = 9.15) and Humidity (VIF = 7.961) also showed elevated VIF values, exceeding or approaching the commonly accepted threshold of 10. Such high VIF values indicate substantial information redundancy, which can lead to unstable coefficient estimates and inflated standard errors in ordinary least squares (OLS) regression. To address this, Ridge Regression was employed instead of OLS.

Quantitative attribution of environmental drivers

The ridge regression analysis (λ = 0.316) quantified the independent influence of each environmental factor on the spatial distribution of HCC ASIR (Table 3 and Fig. 11). The model performance demonstrated that topographic features were the dominant drivers, collectively explaining 73.5% of the total environmental contribution.Specifically, altitude emerged as the primary positive driver of HCC incidence, with a standardized coefficient (β) of 3.39 (95% CI: 1.069 to 5.335), accounting for 40.3% of the relative contribution. Slope was identified as the second most influential factor, exerting a significant negative impact (β = -2.79, 95% CI: -4.268 to -1.405) and contributing 33.2% to the model.In contrast, climatic factors and air pollutants did not reach statistical significance in the multivariable framework. Temperature (β = 1.29, 95% CI: -0.503 to 2.856) and precipitation (β = 0.52, 95% CI: -0.953 to 1.672) contributed 15.3% and 6.2% respectively, but their 95% bootstrap confidence intervals encompassed zero. Similarly, the relative contributions of humidity (3.9%) and O3 (1.1%) were minimal and statistically non-significant. These results underscore that the spatial heterogeneity of HCC in Gansu Province is primarily governed by terrain-related environmental gradients rather than meteorological or atmospheric variations.

Discussion

HCC ranks among the most prevalent malignant tumors worldwide. According to global statistics from 2020, the incidence of liver cancer reached 9.5 per 100,000 population, while the incidence in China was nearly double, at 18.2 per 100,000 [12]. Furthermore, data from the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) showed that the ASIR for males increased from 13.9 in 2018 to 14.1 per 100,000 in 2020, before declining to 12.7 in 2022. For females, the ASIR rose from 4.9 to 5.2 and then declined to 4.8 per 100,000 in the same period [1, 13, 14]. In line with these international trends, our study also observed a fluctuating trajectory in HCC incidence, with a slight rebound in 2023. Additionally, the incidence rate among males was consistently and significantly higher than that among females.

Geographic heterogeneity in HCC burden is evident worldwide. East Asia remains the region with the highest incidence, with rates as high as 17.7 per 100,000 population [15, 16]. In our analysis of Gansu Province over an 11-year period, the HCC incidence rate remained below this threshold throughout. In comparison, a study from Tianjin reported a peak ASIR of 19.98 per 100,000 in 2011, followed by a fluctuating downward trend between 2008 and 2017 [17]. In Gansu, however, the peak occurred in 2017, with a similar decline thereafter. This temporal discrepancy may reflect differences in climate dynamics, urbanization, and local healthcare policies. An emerging trend in HCC epidemiology is the increasing proportion of older adults among newly diagnosed cases. A population-based study from Korea reported an aging trend in HCC incidence from 2008 to 2018, and our findings are consistent, showing a rising share of elderly patients in the overall case distribution [18].

Globally, chronic viral hepatitis remains the primary driver of HCC [19]. Hepatitis B virus (HBV) promotes tumorigenesis through both direct and indirect pathways, including the integration of viral DNA into host cancer-related genes and the induction of large-scale chromosomal rearrangements that deregulate oncogenic signaling [20, 21]. Similarly, Hepatitis C virus (HCV) contributes to malignant transformation via sustained hepatic inflammation, progressive fibrosis, and the disruption of metabolic homeostasis [22]. In Gansu, as in other endemic regions, HBV continues to account for a substantial disease burden, potentially synergized by co-infection with HCV or HDV [23, 24]. Beyond viral factors, Aflatoxin B1(AFB1)-a potent mycotoxin prevalent in contaminated corn and peppers-represents a critical environmental carcinogen in the region [25, 26]. Mechanistically, AFB1 disrupts metabolic homeostasis and induces genetic alterations, with its carcinogenic effects significantly amplified in the presence of HBV infection [27, 28]. Given the dietary habits and agricultural patterns in northwestern China, the interaction between high viral prevalence and aflatoxin exposure likely underpins the localized HCC clusters identified in our spatial analysis.

Regional differences in HCC etiology further reflect the interaction between geography, lifestyle, and pathogen exposure. In East Asia and sub-Saharan Africa, endemic HBV infection is the predominant cause, whereas in low-incidence regions such as Europe, North America, and Japan, HCC is increasingly attributed to HCV, alcohol-related liver disease, and nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) [29, 30]. Areas with high consumption of AFB1-contaminated staples—such as maize, peanuts, and chili—are considered hotspots for aflatoxin-associated HCC [31].

Our study identified altitude and slope as robust geographic correlates of HCC incidence; however, part of the observed spatial variation may also reflect differences in case ascertainment or access to healthcare services across counties. The Bayesian Besag–York–Mollié (BYM) model was applied to account for population size and spatial autocorrelation, thereby reducing potential bias arising from uneven population distribution and spatial dependence. When zooming in on the 87 minor administrative divisions, pronounced spatial heterogeneity in HCC risk emerges across the province, with distinct clusters matching these topographic boundaries. Specifically, county-level districts within the high-altitude Gannan Plateau (e.g., Maqu and Luqu counties) and the rugged Wuwei region (e.g., Liangzhou and Gulang counties) exhibited significantly elevated ASIRs, whereas the Longnan region (e.g., Liangdang and Kangxian counties) demonstrated a generally lower incidence profile. One plausible interpretation is that large-scale topographic gradients may act as upstream environmental frameworks that shape the persistence, transmission, or population-level impact of established HCC risk factors, including HBV, HCV, and aflatoxin exposure. Specifically, the positive association with altitude may be mediated by socio-economic and lifestyle factors prevalent in high-elevation clusters like Gannan and Wuwei. These areas often face greater geographic isolation, which can limit access to hepatitis B vaccination programs and early cancer screening services. Moreover, high-altitude populations may have distinct dietary structures—such as high intake of preserved meats or lower access to fresh vegetables—which could synergize with chronic viral hepatitis to promote oncogenesis. Conversely, the significant negative impact of slope suggests that flatter terrains are associated with higher HCC risk. Flat landscapes often serve as sinks for surface runoff, potentially leading to the accumulation of agricultural chemicals, pesticides, and heavy metals in local soil and groundwater. In contrast, steeper slopes facilitate faster drainage and reduce the localized concentration of these environmental carcinogens, which may explain the lower incidence rates observed in rugged mountainous counties such as those in Longnan.

While previous studies have reported associations between ambient temperature and viral hepatitis prevalence—for example, a positive correlation observed in Guangdong Province [32]—our findings suggest that the relationship between temperature and HCC incidence may be context-dependent and attenuated after accounting for intercorrelations with topographic features. To date, the potential influence of altitude and slope on the environmental dynamics of HBV, HCV, or aflatoxin contamination remains largely unexplored and warrants further investigation. Although prior studies have linked ambient air pollution, including PM2.5 and SO2, to increased HCC risk [9, 33], no significant associations were observed in the present analysis, possibly reflecting limited spatial variability in pollutant concentrations or the constraints inherent to ecological study designs.

The findings of this study offer important implications for public health planning and cancer prevention in ecologically vulnerable regions of inland China. The identification of persistent high-incidence regions in high-altitude and steeply sloped regions suggests that geographic context should be incorporated into HCC surveillance strategies and resource allocation frameworks [34, 35]. Targeted screening programs, particularly in remote and underserved areas such as the Gannan Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture, may help to reduce diagnostic delays and improve outcomes for populations at elevated risk. In addition, the role of geographic isolation in limiting access to routine hepatitis screening, early imaging, and curative treatment should be further investigated from a health systems perspective. From a research standpoint, our results underscore the value of spatially resolved, long-term environmental datasets in understanding cancer patterns. Individual-level variations in lifestyle and dietary habits were not included in our analysis, despite being identified in prior studies as key contributors to the initiation and progression of hepatocellular carcinoma [36, 37]. However, future studies should aim to integrate individual-level health data (e.g., HBV status, alcohol intake, metabolic indicators), dynamic exposure modeling (e.g., annual or seasonal pollutant fluctuations), and multiscale modeling techniques, including hierarchical or Bayesian spatial models. These approaches would enable a more comprehensive understanding of how environment interacts with biological and behavioral risk factors to shape cancer risk over time and space. Integrated scoring systems that combine various environmental exposures have begun to show growing relevance in hepatocellular carcinoma risk evaluation [38]. Finally, the approach adopted in this study—combining epidemiological surveillance, environmental modeling, and spatial analysis—can serve as a template for investigating other environmentally sensitive cancers. As environmental conditions continue to evolve in the context of climate change and land use transformation, robust and interdisciplinary frameworks are needed to anticipate and respond to emerging cancer burdens across diverse geographic regions.

However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study employed a county-level ecological design; thus, observed associations at the population level may not necessarily apply to individuals (ecological fallacy). Second, certain individual-level risk factors, such as HBV/HCV infection, dietary habits (e.g., aflatoxin exposure), and alcohol consumption, were unavailable in the registry data and could not be controlled for. Third, differences in healthcare access and diagnostic facilities across counties may have influenced spatial patterns, potentially introducing ascertainment bias. Fourth, while a Bayesian BYM model was used to account for spatial autocorrelation, the possibility of residual bias remains. Finally, the decline in HCC incidence in specific years may relate to urbanization and healthcare policy changes, though these exact relationships require further confirmation.

Conclusion

This study reveals significant spatiotemporal heterogeneity in HCC incidence across Gansu Province from 2013 to 2023. Our analysis identifies altitude and slope as the predominant environmental drivers of this spatial variation, outperforming meteorological and air quality factors. Specifically, high-altitude and flat-terrain regions exhibit a disproportionately higher disease burden, likely due to the interplay between geographic isolation, healthcare accessibility, and environmental accumulation of carcinogens.

These findings underscore the necessity of incorporating geographic context into public health frameworks. We suggest that HCC surveillance and screening resources be prioritized for topographically vulnerable areas, such as the Gannan Plateau. Future research integrating individual-level biomarkers with high-resolution environmental modeling is essential to move beyond ecological associations toward causal mechanisms, ultimately informing more precise, region-specific prevention strategies.

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Around two-thirds of strokes occur in developing countries. Moreover, atrial fibrillation (AF) is the commonest cause of embolic stroke, which is connected to a higher risk of hemorrhagic transformation (HT), functional disability, and mortality.There is a gap in knowledge regarding the predictors of the long-term post-alteplase functional outcomes, especially in patients with AF, which limits the delivery of adequate care and support to stroke survivors.Our study aimed to evaluate the predictors of poor long-term post-thrombolysis outcomes in patients with AF who experienced a first-ever embolic stroke to improve the prognosis of stroke survivors. What was learned from this study? Higher baseline NIHSS, heart failure, post-thrombolysis intracranial haemorrhage, and recurrent symptomatic stroke were independent predictors of long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable outcome in embolic stroke patients in the MENA region. Novelly, higher HAS-BLED and CHA2DS2-VASc scores were independent predictors of long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable functional outcome in Arabian patients. Moreover, sustained AF was an independent predictor of long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable functional outcome.Our study showed that higher HAS-BLED and CHA2DS2-VASc scores, which were known to be predictors of hemorrhagic complications and stroke recurrence, were also predictors of poor post-stroke outcomes. In addition, sustained AF was an independent predictor of poor post-thrombolysis outcome, highlighting the importance of careful cardiological evaluation of embolic stroke patients.

Introduction

While stroke represents a major mortality cause worldwide, developing nations experience a large burden of stroke and its consequences, with around two-thirds of stroke cases occurring in developing countries [1, 2].

Thrombolysis using alteplase or tenecteplase is the approved medical treatment for acute ischemic stroke, but some patients who received thrombolytic agents experienced complications and unfavorable outcomes [3, 4].

Many authors have stated that patients who suffered from atrial fibrillation (AF)-related ischemic stroke experienced an increased risk of functional disability and mortality [5–7], but, to the best of our knowledge, no such study has assessed the different predictors of poor functional outcomes in alteplase-treated patients with AF-related embolic stroke.

Our study evaluated the different predictors of long-term unfavorable outcomes in alteplase-treated patients with AF who presented with acute embolic stroke in the Middle East.

Methods

Study Design

Our prospective cohort study was conducted between May 2021 and May 2025 after this study protocol was reviewed and approved by the scientific research ethics committee at Kafr El-Sheikh University in Egypt, and the approval number was KFSIRB200-570. We obtained written informed consent to participate from all adults participating in this study. Our study did not include any vulnerable patient. All of our methods were performed following the relevant guidelines, regulations, and declaration of Helsinki. We reported our study was done per STROBE guidelines.

We included all patients with AF who presented to Kafr Elsheikh University Hospital in Egypt, Kafr Elsheikh General Hospital in Egypt, Al-Sahel Teaching Hospital in Egypt, NMC Royal Hospital in United Arab Emirates, and Al-Obour Hospital in Egypt in the period from May 2021 to May 2023 and who suffered from first-ever acute embolic stroke and were treated with thrombolytic therapy. We followed up with our patients for 2 years.

Setting

Overall, 977 consecutive patients with AF who presented to the prespecified hospitals in the period from May 2021 to May 2023 and 580 completed the 2-year follow-up period.

Before inclusion in our study, we obtained formal written informed consent from all eligible patients or their next of kin.

Participants

Our study included two groups. The favorable outcome group included 429 patients, of which 232 (54.1%) were males and 197 (45.9%) were females, and the unfavorable outcome group included 151 patients, of which 81 (53.6%) were males and 70 (46.4%) were females.

We defined unfavorable long-term outcome when the patient had the modified Rankin scale (mRS) of three or more after the 2-year follow-up period [8].

Eligibility Criteria

Our study involved male and female participants aged 18–80 years presenting with acute embolic ischemic stroke associated with AF and who were candidates for thrombolytic therapy.

The participants who were users of direct oral anticoagulants (DOAC) ingested the last dose of DOAC 48 h or more before stroke onset, and prothrombin time (PT) was less than 15 s and their activated partial thromboplastin time was less than 40 s. In contrast, warfarin users had an international normalized ratio (INR) less than 1.7 [9].

We excluded patients who experienced organ failure or had neurological conditions mimicked stroke such as epilepsy, brain tumors, head trauma, and multiple sclerosis [10, 11].

The detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are available in the supplemental material.

Study Procedures

Each patient included in our study was evaluated clinically, and underwent brain imaging and cardiological evaluation.

We followed up with our participants for 2 years via telephone calls once weekly and an interview in our outpatient clinic once every 3 months. Neurology specialists conducted the telephone calls and the interviews. If any patient had clinical deterioration or recurrent stroke manifestations, then they came to our hospital to be assessed via clinical and investigation tools to evaluate their condition.

We defined lost to follow-up patients as those with incomplete follow-up information on any variables needed for estimating a modified Rankin scale (mRS) score [12, 13].

We conducted methods to avoid losing to follow-up, such as building rapport with participants, following up using telephone calls, and providing flexible appointment systems. A total of 173 (23%) patients failed to complete the 2 years follow-up period, which was in line with the rate of lost-to-follow-up in large longitudinal study such as the Longitudinal Observational Study from the Swedish Stroke Register where the 1 year lost-to-follow-up rate was 21.2% [12] and the Impact of the Rehabilitation on Outcomes in Patients With Ischemic Stroke: A Nationwide Retrospective Cohort Study in Japan where the lost-to-follow-up rate was 25.6% [14].

We conducted measures to reduce the different bias types, as we included consecutive patients during 2 years, all of our patients received the same thrombolytic agent, alteplase, we used the same assessment tools, including mRS and NIHSS, to evaluate our patients across the different centers, we adjusted our multivariate analysis for possible confounding factors such as age, sex, baseline NIHSS, door-to-needle time, baseline Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS), endovascular management, anticoagulant treatment, comorbid conditions such as diabetes mellitus (DM), hypertension (HTN), hyperlipidemia, AF, and recurrent stroke, and we performed two sensitivity analyses to ensure that the missing data did not affect our results' robustness.

The other detailed study procedures are available in the supplemental material.

Variables

Primary Outcome

We evaluated the prevalence of long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable functional outcomes among patients with AF who presented with embolic stroke.

We defined unfavorable long-term outcome when the patient had an mRS of three or more after the 2 years follow-up period [8, 15].

Secondary Outcomes

We evaluated the predictors of the long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable functional outcomes in patients with AF who were presented with embolic stroke.

Sample Size

We collected the consecutive patients with AF who experienced first-ever embolic ischemic stroke and who presented to the prespecified centers in the time period from May 2021 to May 2023 and calculated the power of our study using the Power Analysis and Sample Size System (PASS, V12, NCSS) given the two-sided significance level of 95%, alpha error of 5%, mean and standard deviation of the favorable outcome group's age (64.4 ± 5.7 years), mean and standard deviation of the unfavorable outcome group's age (66.3 ± 7.2 years), an effect size of 0.292, and the power of our study was 92.5%

Statistical Analysis of the Data

We analyzed our data using the IBM SPSS software package, version 20.0 ( IBM, Armonk, NY, USA), analyzing the different study outcomes separately. We identified the distribution of our variables via the Shapiro–Wilk test, and the numerical variables were reported as median and interquartile range. The categorical variables were described as percentages, and we utilized the Mann–Whitney U test to evaluate the statistical differences between the numerical variables with abnormal distribution. We also utilized the Chi-square test to evaluate the statistical association between our qualitative data.

In addition, we employed Fisher's exact test or the Monte Carlo correction test if one-fifth or more of the cells showed an expected count below five. We employed the univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses to examine the ability of different factors to predict the post-alteplase unfavorable outcomes in patients with AF, and we determined our results' significance at 5%.

We performed three sensitivity analyses, the first two aimed at evaluating the impact of loss to follow-up on the robustness of our results, in the first of which we conducted an analysis where the 173 patients who failed to complete the 2 years follow-up were assumed to have poor long-term outcome and yielded the same results as the main analysis, while in the second one we assumed that the 173 patients who failed to complete the 2 years follow-up had good long-term outcome and yielded the same results as the main analysis.

The third sensitivity analysis was conducted by removing patients with post-thrombolysis intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), as many of our variables were predictors of post-thrombolysis ICH, which was an early complication of thrombolysis, leading to poor functional outcomes, and this analysis yielded the same results as the main analysis.

Sensitivity Analysis

We performed three sensitivity analyses, the first two aimed at evaluating the impact of loss to follow-up on the robustness of our results, in the first of which we conducted an analysis in which the 173 patients who failed to complete the 2 years follow-up were assumed to have poor long-term outcome and yielded the same results as the main analysis: NIHSS at the time of admission (OR, 2.45; 95% CI, 2.13–5.47; P = 0.02), sustained atrial fibrillation (OR, 2.31; 95% CI, 1.88–4.19; P = 0.02), heart failure (OR, 1.69; 95% CI, 1.14–2.44; P = 0.04), HAS-BLED score (OR, 1.87; 95% CI, 1.52–3.11; P = 0.03), CHA2DS2VASc score (OR, 2.31; 95% CI, 2.12–3.77; P = 0.03), post-thrombolysis intracranial hemorrhage (OR, 2.57; 95% CI, 1.92–3.31; P = 0.03), and recurrent symptomatic stroke (OR, 2.18; 95% CI, 1.64–3.52; P = 0.03) were independent predictors of the long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable outcome, while the second analysis assumed that the 173 patients who failed to complete the follow-up did not have poor outcomes and yielded the same results as the main analysis: NIHSS at the time of admission (odds ratio [OR], 1.76; 95% CI, 1.43–3.24; P = 0.04), sustained Atrial fibrillation (OR, 1.31; 95% CI, 1.14–2.78; P = 0.04), heart failure (OR, 1.34; 95% CI, 1.11–2.23; P = 0.05), HAS-BLED score (OR, 1.63; 95% CI, 1.41–2.79; P = 0.04), CHA2DS2VASc score (OR, 1.91; 95% CI, 1.78–3.18; P = 0.04), post-thrombolysis intracranial hemorrhage (OR, 2.11; 95% CI, 1.84–3.10; P = 0.04), and recurrent symptomatic stroke (OR, 2.27; 95% CI, 1.82–3.71; P = 0.02) were independent predictors of the long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable outcome.

The third analysis was conducted by removing patients with post thrombolysis ICH as many of our variables were predictors of post-thrombolysis ICH which was an early complication of thrombolysis leading to poor functional outcomes, and this analysis yielded the same results as the main analysis: NIHSS at the time of admission (odds ratio [OR], 2.86; 95% CI, 2.13–4.18; P = 0.02), sustained AF (OR, 1.71; 95% CI, 1.43–3.24; P = 0.03), heart failure (OR, 1.69; 95% CI, 1.40–2.65; P = 0.03), HAS-BLED score (OR, 2.12; 95% CI, 1.73–2.68; P = 0.03), CHA2DS2VASc score (OR, 2.32; 95% CI, 1.92–3.57; P = 0.03), and recurrent symptomatic stroke (OR, 2.59; 95% CI, 2.31–4.21; P = 0.01) were independent predictors of the long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable outcome.

Discussion

Our study aimed to evaluate the predictors of poor long-term post-thrombolysis outcomes in patients with AF who experienced a first-ever embolic stroke to allow delivering adequate care and support to stroke survivors.

We followed up with our patients for 2 years as the consequence of thrombolytic therapy such as hemorrhagic complication and failure or success of the thrombolysis might affect the long-term post-stroke disabilities and outcomes. Moreover, many studies have focused on short-term outcomes [16–18], while studies on long-term post-alteplase outcomes are sparse [19]. Further, no such longitudinal study has assessed the different predictors of poor long-term functional outcomes in alteplase-treated patients with AF-related embolic stroke in the Middle East.

We found that 26% of our patients had post-alteplase unfavorable outcomes at 2 years which agreed with the findings of Schäbitz and colleagues, who found that the percentage of poor post-stroke outcome was 29% at 1 year [20].

Our study showed that embolic stroke patients with unfavorable outcomes had a statistically significant higher percentage of diabetic patients, reaching 41%, which was higher than the prevalence of DM in ischemic stroke patients in other areas, such as [21], 27.5% in North America [22], and 26.4% in Europe [23] which necessitates careful management of DM in Arabian embolic stroke patients to achieve better care of stroke survivors.

The higher prevalence of DM in the poor outcome group could be explained as hyperglycemia can exacerbate brain injury and increase the infarction size by enhancing intracellular acidosis in the ischemic penumbra, producing loss of ion homeostasis, mitochondrial dysfunction, and bioenergetic failure [24].

We found that higher NIHSS scores could predict the long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable outcomes, agreeing with the conclusions of Aoki and colleagues and Elsayed and colleagues, who showed that the increase in the baseline NIHSS was correlated with unfavorable post-stroke outcome [25, 26]. We can justify this finding, as the increase in NIHSS is correlated with an increase in the cerebral infarction size which produced brain edema compressing the peripheral vasculature, increasing the cerebral ischemia leading to poor functional outcomes [27].

We found that sustained AF could predict long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable outcomes, agreeing with the results of Steinberg and colleagues and Ahmed and colleagues, who concluded that sustained AF was correlated with an increase in the odds of hemorrhagic stroke compared to paroxysmal AF [28, 29].

The link of sustained AF to poor outcomes usually results in more severe electromechanical and hemodynamic sequelae, which aggravated the poor results of the embolic events. [30] Some authors have found that the prolonged duration of AF was correlated with the poor results of the thromboembolic events [31].

We found that a higher HAS-BLED score could predict long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable outcomes, agreeing with the results of Gallego et al. and Chan et al., who concluded that the increase in the HAS-BLED score was correlated with an increase in the odds of the hemorrhagic complications and unfavorable outcomes in anticoagulated AF patients. [32, 33].

We also found that heart failure could predict long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable outcomes, agreeing with the conclusions of Kang and colleagues and Chou and colleagues, who showed that the presence of heart failure was associated with poor post-stroke outcomes [34, 35]. This could be explained because heart failure is associated with abnormalities with circulatory stasis, hypoperfusion of cerebral blood flow, hypercoagulable state, and endothelial abnormalities, which exacerbate the pre-existing ischemic event and can produce recurrent ischemic strokes which worsen the functional outcomes [36].

Our study showed that a higher CHA2DS2VASc score was an independent predictor of long-term post-alteplase unfavorable outcomes, result agreeing with the results of Ntaios et al. and Lee et al., who found that a higher CHA2DS2VASc score was associated with a higher incidence of poor post-stroke functional outcomes. [37, 38], This can be explained as higher CHA2DS2VASc, which is associated with an increased incidence of recurrent ischemic events due to the higher atherosclerotic burden [39], which leads to poor post-stroke functional outcomes.

We also found that post-thrombolysis hemorrhagic transformation was an independent predictor of long-term post-alteplase unfavorable outcomes, agreeing with the findings of Annan and colleagues and Andrade and colleagues, who found that the occurrence of hemorrhagic transformation of brain infarction was associated with increased odds of poor post-stroke outcomes [40, 41].

We also found that recurrent symptomatic stroke could predict long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable outcomes, agreeing with the conclusions of Wang and colleagues and Khanevski and colleagues, who showed that stroke recurrence was linked to unfavorable outcomes [42, 43].

Study Limitations

Despite the advantages of our study, it had some limitations. First, the prospective nature of our research led to a relatively small sample size; second, we only included patients from Egypt and the United Arab Emirates, which restricted the generalization of our conclusions; and third, we did not involve some items in our study such as the white matter lesion burden and the infarction volume, as the included hospitals did not own the specific MRI software program.

Conclusion

Higher baseline NIHSS, heart failure, post-thrombolysis intracranial haemorrhage, and recurrent symptomatic stroke were independent predictors of long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable functional outcome in embolic stroke patients. Novelly, higher HAS-BLED and CHA2DS2-VASc scores were independent predictors of long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable functional outcome in Arabian patients. Moreover, sustained AF was an independent predictor of long-term post-thrombolysis unfavorable functional outcome.

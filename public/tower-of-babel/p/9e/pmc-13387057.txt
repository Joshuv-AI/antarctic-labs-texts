Stentless percutaneous coronary intervention for left main trunk plaque erosion: insights from coronary angioscopy
Authors: Mitsuo Hinoi, Kazuoki Dai, Kenji Nishioka, Yukiko Nakano
Published: 2026

Case description

A 66-year-old woman with hypertension and hyperlipidaemia presented with sudden chest pain. Electrocardiography showed no ST-segment changes, whereas high-sensitivity troponin T was elevated at 0.105 ng/mL (upper limit: 0.100 ng/mL). Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated apical hypokinesis with left ventricular ejection fraction of 70%. She was diagnosed with non-ST segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI). Emergency coronary angiography (CAG) demonstrated a radiolucent filling defect in the mid-distal left main trunk (LMT), suggestive of intracoronary thrombus (Figure 1A). Optical frequency domain imaging (OFDI) revealed thrombus overlying an intact fibrous cap without evidence of plaque rupture, consistent with plaque erosion (Figure 1B).1 Thrombus aspiration was performed due to large thrombus, and OFDI confirmed thrombus reduction with adequate minimum lumen area of 13.1 mm2 (Figure 1C). Since no flow-limiting stenosis remained, stent implantation was deferred. Dual antiplatelet and lipid-lowering therapy were initiated without anticoagulation, given the absence of an embolic source.

Emergency coronary angiography (CAG) on hospital arrival demonstrated a radiolucent filling defect (arrow) in the mid-distal left main trunk (LMT) (A). Optical frequency domain imaging (OFDI) obtained at the corresponding mid-distal LMT site showed thrombus (arrow) overlying a plaque with an intact fibrous cap (asterisk). There was no cap disruption or cavity formation, consistent with plaque erosion (B). After thrombus aspiration, OFDI at the same level confirmed marked thrombus reduction and adequate lumen area (C). At the 1-month follow-up, CAG showed complete thrombus resolution and no significant stenosis in the LMT (D). Subsequent coronary angioscopy demonstrated a yellowish plaque (arrow) at the LMT, indicating an underlying lipid plaque (E, F)

At 1-month follow-up, CAG demonstrated complete thrombus resolution (Figure 1D), and coronary angioscopy revealed a yellowish plaque at the LMT (Figure 1E, F), suggesting an underlying lipid-rich plaque.2 Antiplatelet therapy was de-escalated to clopidogrel monotherapy, consistent with previous reports.3 Coronary computed tomography angiography at 16 months showed no residual thrombus or significant stenosis, and the patient remained event-free for 4 years after NSTEMI. Previous studies suggested that selected patients with plaque erosion may be managed without stenting.3 This case highlights the potential feasibility of stentless strategy for carefully selected LMT plaque erosion guided by multimodality intracoronary imaging. Coronary angioscopy may provide additional information regarding the underlying plaque phenotype and long-term risk management. However, the diagnosis of plaque erosion was based on OFDI findings and could not be histopathologically confirmed. Therefore, this strategy should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than practice changing, and should not be extrapolated to routine LMT ACS management.

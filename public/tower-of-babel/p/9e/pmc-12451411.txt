A 19F‐ATCUN Peptide NMR Probe for Selective Cu2+ or Ni2 + Sensing in Complex Biological Media
Authors: Marie‐Julie Tilly, Enrico Falcone, Bruno Kieffer, Yannis‐Nicolas François, Peter Faller, Marine Desage‐El Murr, Laurent Raibaut
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report the design and synthesis of ratiometric 19F‐ATCUN (Xxx‐Zzz‐His) peptide probes for the selective and reversible detection of Ni2⁺ and Cu2⁺ by 19F NMR spectroscopy. In contrast to fluorescence‐based approaches, 19F NMR enables background‐free detection in biological media, due to the absence of fluorine in native biomolecules. The probes remain stable and functional in complex cell media (DMEM), enabling the detection of Cu2⁺ and Ni2⁺ by 19F NMR with a limit of detection (LOD) of 4 µM and quantification (LOQ) of 10 µM, relevant for studying copper‐related pathologies.Keywords: 19F NMR probe, ATCUN motif, biological copper sensor, ratiometric detection, trifluoromethylation

Introduction

Copper is essential for humans and most living organisms, while nickel, though nonessential for humans, is required by some plants and microorganisms.[ 1 ] Above a certain threshold, both elements become toxic. Hence there is interest in detecting and quantifying these metals in a specific manner in different biological or environmental systems, as a tool to better decipher the homeostasis and toxicity of these metal ions. Cu occurs as Cu+ and Cu2+ in biological systems. Cu2+ is the prevalent state in dioxygen‐rich and oxidizing environment, such as extracellular biological fluids like blood serum, urine, bile, or cerebrospinal fluid. The total concentration and the different species depend on the system, but in general Cu+ or Cu2+ are mostly bound to ligands. Both thermodynamic and kinetic factors must be considered when evaluating its bioavailability. While Cu is classically strongly and inertly bound to enzymes, more labile and exchangeable Cu2+ is found either strongly bound to blood circulating proteins, mainly serum albumin (HSA, log Kd (at pH 7.4) = 13),[ 2 ] or weakly bound to small ligands like amino acids, organic acids or anions in the urine.[ 3 ] Under physiological conditions total copper concentrations are in the ∼10–20 µM range, and labile Cu2+ often lower, ∼3–6 µM or less. Higher concentrations can be reached under Cu dyshomeostasis like in genetic Wilson's disease leading to a copper overload,[ 4 ] or by Cu intoxication. Labile Cu2+ concentration might also be higher in some site‐specific cases. For instance, concentrations of up to 200–250 µM were estimated for Cu release in the synaptic cleft.[ 5 ] More broadly, elevated levels of labile Cu2⁺ have also been associated with amyloidosis such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases.[ 6 , 7 ] Therefore, accurate measurement of labile Cu2+ pool provides valuable insights into copper biology as well as diagnosis and therapeutic monitoring of copper‐related disease.[ 8 ]

The ATCUN motif (Amino Terminal Copper and Nickel binding motif),[ 9 ] defined by the sequence H2N‐Xxx‐Zzz‐His (where His: histidine and Xxx or Zzz are natural amino acids, excluding proline for Zzz) is found at the N‐terminus of human serum albumin and represents a prime example of a selective Cu2⁺‐ and Ni2‐⁺binding site. This motif forms a highly stable 4N coordination complex with Cu2⁺, exhibiting large and distinct affinity ranges (log Kd (at pH 7.4) = 12–15 which remains stable over a broad pH range (pH 5–10)[ 9 ]), and with Ni2⁺ (log Kd (at pH 7.4) = 6–8),[ 10 ] depending on the identity of Xxx and Zzz. Such properties make the ATCUN motif well‐suited to compete especially with the exchangeable or labile Cu2+ pool present in biological fluids.[ 8 ] The use of much stronger ligands, for instance ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA),[ 3 ] might be less appropriate, as they lack selectivity for the labile Cu2+ fraction and may extract total copper, including the nonexchangeable pool from essential Cu‐enzymes. Consequently, several fluorescent sensors incorporating the ATCUN motif have been reported,[ 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 ] predominantly employing green‐emitting dyes that rely on fluorescence quenching due to the paramagnetic nature of Cu2⁺. However, in complex biological media (serum, urine …), intrinsic fluorescence from biomolecules can interfere with luminescence‐based detection. To circumvent this issue, our group[ 15 ] has recently introduced a novel “turn‐off ATCUN probe” based on Tb3⁺ luminescence sensitized by tryptophan via an antenna effect. This system exhibits long‐lifetime luminescence of Tb3⁺ (2 ms), enabling time‐delayed detection (∼100 µs time delay) to effectively mitigate background autofluorescence.

Despite these advantages, the inherently weak luminescence of lanthanides can be further attenuated by the inner filter effect of biological media. Interestingly, a complementary strategy involves the use of the ATCUN motif, which forms stable Cu2⁺ complexes serving as paramagnetic probes in 1H NMR. These enable the investigation of long‐range interactions within biomolecules, providing valuable insights into protein structure, dynamics, and interactions.[ 16 ] By contrast, the absence of fluorine background in biofluids enables 19F nucleus (spin ½) to be selectivity observed in complex mixtures with high sensitivity by NMR or MRI.[ 17 ] Furthermore, NMR signal is quantitative and this property has been exploited to design 19F sensor ligands, such as 19F‐BATPA,[ 18 ] able to form complexes with various diamagnetic cations (Ca2+, Mg2+, K+, Zn2+, Fe2+, and Mn2+) with different affinities. The large chemical shift dispersion of 19F and its high sensitivity to electronic environments allows identifying bound ions through distinct chemical shifts in 19F‐NMR and therefore provides insights into their exchange dynamics. However, in the case of Cu2⁺ detection, the 19F‐NMR signal is suppressed due to paramagnetic nature of Cu2⁺, that leads to severe shortening of T1 and T2 relaxation times of 19F resonances located in the vicinity of the metal and subsequently a loss of NMR signal.[ 19 ] This quenching of the 19F signal was exploited to design different 19F‐labeled probes (ATSM,[ 20 ] Cyclam[ 21 ]) able to specifically bind Cu2⁺ at relevant concentrations. By altering the redox environment of the ligand, Cu2⁺ is reduced to diamagnetic Cu⁺, leading to an increase in 19F T₁ and T2 relaxation times, and consequently to the reappearance of the 19F NMR signal. Importantly, all of these studies demonstrate that 19F‐NMR probes cation chelators enable direct ion identification at biologically relevant concentrations and are particularly advantageous for analyzing opaque cell suspensions or complex media, where fluorescence‐based measurements are often impractical. Inspired by these findings, we set out to develop a 19F‐ATCUN NMR probe as a next‐generation strategy to detect exchangeable Cu2⁺ in biological fluids, aiming to overcome the limitations of luminescent ATCUN probes in complex media.

Results and Discussion

We selected the peptide 1a (Scheme 1 & SI Figure S1) derived from the well‐characterized ATCUN motif Xxx‐Zzz‐His with the simplest sequence GGH (log Kd (at pH 7.4) Cu2⁺ ≈ 12.4; log Kd (at pH 7) Ni2⁺ ≈ 7).[ 9 , 10 ] A tryptophan (Trp) residue was introduced at the fourth position of peptide 1a to enable direct selective C2‐trifluoromethylation of the indole ring via late‐stage modification, thereby avoiding the need to introduce the unnatural amino acid Fmoc‐Trp(CF3)‐OH during SPPS. This chemical modification was achieved using optimized Langlois method,[ 22 , 23 ] which exploits the reactivity of trifluoromethyl radicals CF3• generated in situ from sodium trifluoromethanesulfinate (NaSO2CF3) in the presence of tert‐butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP) and a catalytic amount of Fe2⁺ as a radical initiator. The resulting CF3• radical then adds to the (hetero)aromatic moiety to form a carbon‐centered radical, which undergoes single‐electron oxidation to a cation, followed by re‐aromatization through proton.[ 23 ] This reaction was carried out directly on the native peptide 1a under mild aqueous conditions. To enhance chemoselectivity[ 23 ] toward Trp over His, the reaction was performed at low temperature (0 °C) and in acidic buffer ammonium acetate at pH (5.8), where the imidazole side chain of His is protonated, reducing its reactivity. Successful mono‐incorporation of the CF3 group on isolated peptide 1b was confirmed by LC‐MS (SI Figures S3–S4). Furthermore, the selectivity of the Trp modification was validated by 19F NMR spectroscopy (SI Figure S5), which exhibited a characteristic fluorine chemical shift[ 23 ] (δ = ‐58.93 ppm) corresponding to the C2‐trifluoromethylated Trp residue on the indole ring, with no significant signal attributable to any modification of the His imidazole ring (δ = ‐60.6 ppm).[ 23 ]

Synthesis of 19F‐ATCUN probes 1b and 2b by direct trifluoromethylation of native peptides ATCUN 1a and 2a.

Cu2⁺ binding to the probe 1b was next investigated in HEPES buffer at pH 7.4 (Figure 1). As anticipated, a progressive broadening of the 19F‐ W(CF3) signal (δ = ‐58.92 ppm) was observed upon Cu2⁺ titration, consistent with paramagnetic relaxation enhancement (PRE) effects. This leads to signal attenuation up to a 1:1 stoichiometric ratio between 1b and Cu2⁺, as confirmed by integration of the signal area. Importantly, the addition of an excess of the high‐affinity Cu2⁺ chelator ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) (log Kd (at pH 7.4) ≈ 15.9) led to the recovery of the 19F‐W(CF3) signal at its original chemical shift, demonstrating the reversible nature of 1b probe. This reversibility highlights the potential of probe 1b in 19F‐NMR for dynamic monitoring of Cu2⁺ fluctuations in complex biological environments. The selectivity of probe 1b was subsequently assessed in the presence of physiologically relevant extracellular metal ions (Figure 2). Notably, the 19F‐W(CF3) signal was only affected by Cu2⁺ and Ni2⁺, while no significant changes were observed upon addition of Zn2⁺, Fe3⁺, Co2⁺, Mn2⁺ (20 µM), or Mg2⁺ and Ca2⁺ (4 mM), confirming the high selectivity of the ATCUN motif toward Cu2⁺ and Ni2⁺. Nickel(II) did not induce PRE effects, instead coordination to ATCUN‐W(CF3) led to the appearance of a new 19F‐W(CF3) signal (δ = ‐59.02 ppm) downfield by approximately 0.1 ppm (Figure 2). This is in line with the reported low‐spin and hence diamagnetic Ni2+ in ATCUN. The observed shift upon Ni2+binding reflects a change in the local electronic environment upon metal coordination, enabling ratiometric discrimination between the free and Ni2⁺‐bound forms of the probe. Thereafter, a titration of probe 1b was performed in HEPES buffer in the presence of increasing concentrations of Ni2⁺ (SI Figure S10). Signal integration revealed a linear ratiometric relationship between the free and Ni2⁺‐bound forms of probe 1b allowing to determine, under these experimental conditions ([1b] = 30 µM, 512 scans, 18 min acquisition, 500 MHz spectrometer with cryoprobe) a limit of detection (LOD) ∼ 4 µM and a LOQ ∼ 10 µM. Furthermore, we demonstrated the potential of this probe for quantitative Ni2⁺ detection (50 µM) in complex biological environments, such as DMEM cell culture medium (Figure 3). The development of a ratiometric probe is particularly advantageous in the case of paramagnetic Cu2⁺, where the disappearance of the 19F‐W(CF3) signal may not unambiguously indicate metal binding, but could also result from peptide degradation, precipitation, or nonspecific interactions with biomolecules in complex biological environments. To address this limitation, we designed an improved probe architecture[ 24 ] 2a (Scheme 1 & SI Figure S2) by incorporating a second 19F‐W(CF3) residue as an internal reference, spatially separated from the metal‐binding ATCUN motif. This was achieved by inserting a rigid hexaproline (PolyPro₆) spacer between the two Trp residues, exploiting its propensity to adopt a polyproline type II helical conformation, thereby minimizing PRE effects from Cu2⁺ on the distal Trp. We further refined the design of the probe by modifying the ATCUN motif, replacing the GGH sequence with AWH. This variant displays a comparable affinity for Cu2⁺ but is characterized by slower Cu2⁺ exchange kinetics, offering improved stability of the 19F signal in complex biological environments (serum, urine).[ 25 ] Additionally, the incorporation of a Trp residue at the second position of the ATCUN sequence 2a, directly within the metal coordination sphere, was designed to increase the efficiency of PRE effects and thereby fine‐tune the quenching of the 19F signal upon Cu2⁺ binding.

Using the same synthetic strategy, the peptide construct 2a was subjected to selective C2‐trifluoromethylation of Trp via the Langlois method under the previously optimized conditions (SI Figure S6). The successful and selective modification of both tryptophan residues (SI Figures S7,S8), along with the absence of histidine modification, was confirmed by MS/MS analysis (SI Figure S9), yielding the final construct 2b, thus validating the site‐selectivity and robustness of this late‐stage functionalization. Finally, the ratiometric behavior of the probe 2b was evaluated in HEPES buffer (SI Figure S11) to confirm its Cu2⁺‐binding properties. Upon titration, a 1:1 stoichiometric 19F‐NMR response was observed, with selective quenching of the coordination‐site ATCUN‐W(CF3) signal (δ = ‐59.02 ppm), while the distal internal (Pro)₆–W(CF3) remained unaffected (δ = ‐58.75 ppm), thus serving as a reliable internal reference. In both cases, for probe 1b with Ni2+ and ratiometric probe 2b, the observed upfield shift during titration may reflect a peptide conformational change that alters the local fluorine environment, potentially affecting 19F relaxation. Lastly, upon EDTA addition the ATCUN–W(CF3) signal was recovered, while the distal signal remained unaffected throughout the experiment, confirming the reversible nature of Cu2⁺ binding under these conditions. These results validated the probe's ability, under these experimental conditions ([2b] = 50 µM, 1024 scans, 36 min acquisition, 500 MHz spectrometer with cryoprobe) to perform accurate ratiometric quantification of Cu2⁺ levels with a LOD ∼ 4 µM and a LOQ ∼ 10 µM. Encouraged by these results, the ratiometric probe 2b was further tested in a complex biological cell medium (DMEM) (Figure 4). Remarkably, the probe enabled successful titration of Cu2⁺ in the presence of potential biological interferents and competing ligands. It allowed detection of Cu2⁺ concentrations as low as 50 µM, demonstrating its suitability for monitoring labile copper pools in biologically relevant environments.

Conclusion

In this study, we developed and characterized novel 19F‐labeled ATCUN‐based peptide probes for the selective and reversible detection of Ni2⁺ and Cu2⁺ in biological environments. By incorporating C2‐trifluoromethylated tryptophan residues using the Langlois method, we designed both single‐ and dual‐labeled ratiometric sensors capable of distinguishing between free and metal‐bound forms via distinct 19F NMR signals. The use of 19F NMR provides a significant advantage over fluorescence‐based approaches by eliminating interference from biological background, as fluorine is absent from native biomolecules. Advantageously, this late‐stage derivatization strategy in mild conditions can, in principle, be extended to other peptide scaffolds. Specifically, within the ATCUN motif, replacing the canonical GGH sequence with AWH slowed Cu2⁺ exchange kinetics, improving signal stability in complex media. Finally, the resulting probes exhibited high selectivity for both Cu2⁺ and Ni2⁺ in cell culture conditions enabling Ni2⁺ detection via chemical shift variations and Cu2⁺ detection via signal quenching. Although higher sensitivity would be desirable for most labile Cu2⁺ pools, these findings highlight the potential of ratiometric 19F NMR probes as robust tools for noninvasive and background‐free sensing of labile Cu2+ in opaque or autofluorescent biological systems.

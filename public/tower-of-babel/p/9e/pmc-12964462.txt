Epidemiological characteristics and hospital and inpatient burdens of trauma patients in Xuzhou from 2019 to 2023
Authors: Jiachuan Wu, Li Wang, Guangming Zhou, Zhiyuan Xu, Shengli Li, Shuo Feng, Xiao Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to analyze the epidemiological features and disease burden of trauma patients in Xuzhou City according to the inpatient data of trauma patients in three tertiary hospitals from 2019 to 2023.Materials and methodsA retrospective analysis of inpatient data for trauma patients in Xuzhou City between January 2019 and December 2023 was conducted to identify epidemiological trends.ResultsA total of 74,070 trauma patients were included. The population was predominantly working-age males (57.90% male, with the 50–59 age group being the largest cohort at 24.10%). The primary causes of trauma were falls (41.30%), road traffic (29.60%), and mechanical injuries (18.50%), with traffic-related incidents accounting for the highest proportion of fatalities (55.7% of deaths). The mean hospital stay was 8.42 days (95% CI: 8.37–8.48). The inpatient mortality was 1.63% (n = 1208).ConclusionsA synergic impact of occupational exposure, mixed-traffic issues, and a rapidly aging population appears to be the most critical source of trauma burden in industrial cities like Xuzhou. These findings indicate the need for interventions aimed specifically at heightened occupational security, enhanced traffic management, and targeted prevention of falls among the elderly.

Introduction

Trauma, defined as harm inflicted upon the body by forces external to the body, is an urgent global public health issue, with attendant premature death, disability, and substantial socio-economic burdens [1,2]. Trauma is responsible for almost 5 million deaths and 1 billion non-fatal injuries annually, and its contributions to over 10% of the global burden of disease, according to estimates of the World Health Organization (WHO). The primary causes of injury are violent assaults, road traffic crashes, falls, and occupational injuries [3]. High-income countries (HICs) have been able to reduce trauma mortality rates using implemented trauma systems and preventive policies [4], but low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) continue to have a high burden due mainly to inadequate infrastructure, developing pre-hospital emergency systems, and weak enforcement of safety practices [5,6]. Knowledge of regional trauma patterns is required to tailor prevention and treatment and to enact preventive policies.

As the world’s largest developing country, China faces a complex epidemiology of trauma that mirrors challenges from both HICs and LMICs. On one hand, according to data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) Study, China has achieved remarkable success in reducing traffic accident mortality by 50% compared to 2000 levels [7]. However, significant urban-rural disparities persist, with mortality rates in rural areas remaining 2.3 times higher due to weaker enforcement of road safety regulations [8]. On the other hand, rapid population aging has escalated the incidence of fall-related trauma, with the annual incidence of hip fractures among individuals aged 65 and above increasing by 6.2% [9]. Moreover, the uneven distribution of trauma care resources remains a salient issue, increasing the risk of delayed transfer for critically ill patients [10]. Xuzhou, located at the junction of four provinces (Jiangsu, Shandong, Henan, and Anhui), is a major transportation hub where conflicts between non-motorized and motorized vehicles are significant. As an established industrial base, 35.8% of its population engages in secondary industries like construction and mining, which carry high occupational hazard risks [11]. Furthermore, like all Chinese cities, Xuzhou is facing the challenge of an aging population, and trauma has become the fifth leading cause of unnatural death in its aging population [12].

Therefore, this study aims to investigate the data of 2019–2023 trauma patients admitted to three tertiary centers in Xuzhou. The objectives are the outlining of epidemiological characteristics, identification of primary risk factors, and estimation of the hospital and inpatient burdens (distribution of age, gender, trauma types, and sites, hospitalization costs by injury, and mortality). The findings aspire to provide evidence for maximizing local trauma treatment systems and formulating directed public health policy. The main contribution of this study is the size and multicenter nature of the study population from a developing area in China, the focus on industrial and mixed-traffic exposures, the detailed age-sex patterns over a 4-year period (including the COVID-19 pandemic), and the integration of cost-related data as a way of assessing hospital burden.

Materials and methods

Study design and data source

Xuzhou City (Jiangsu Province) covers 11,259 km2 and has a population of 9,083,790 (2020 census), for a density of 807 inhabitants/km2. There are 13 tertiary trauma centers in Xuzhou City, and three among them were selected through random sampling. As an important transportation hub and industrial base, Xuzhou’s tertiary trauma centers not only serve the urban area of Xuzhou but also admit referred patients from surrounding counties. The referral distances cover Jiangsu, Shandong, Henan, and Anhui provinces.

This retrospective observational study included inpatient data from the three principal tertiary trauma centers within Xuzhou City, including the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University, Xuzhou Cancer Hospital, and Xuzhou Mining Group General Hospital. These institutions collectively handle the vast majority of moderate to severe trauma cases in the region, making their combined data a representative sample for analyzing the city’s overall trauma burden in terms of burdens to the hospitals and to the inpatients (distribution of age, gender, trauma types, and sites, hospitalization costs by injury, and mortality). We collected information on all trauma patients admitted between January 1, 2019, and December 31, 2023. Inclusion criteria were: (1) discharge diagnosis in accordance with ICD-10 codes for trauma (S00-T79) among the first three diagnoses listed in cases of multiple diagnoses; (2) admission in 2019–2023; (3) apparent cause of injury; and (4) primarily complete inpatient records. Excluded were patients with chronic injuries, post-traumatic patients admitted for rehabilitation, or patients with severely incomplete medical records. This study was granted approval by the Medical Ethics Committee of Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University (Approval No.: XYFY2024-KL479-01), and informed consent was exempted due to the retrospective design of the study.

Data collection and quality control

Data were collected from electronic medical record databases of the participating hospitals, which are automatically transmitted to the National Hospital Quality Monitoring System (NHQM) to ensure data integrity and validity. A unique identifier was used for each patient to link all pertinent records. The data received encompassed a comprehensive list of variables that ranged across patient demographics, trauma-related attributes, and healthcare use. Demographics included the patient’s age, which was broken down into decadal groups in order to analyze it, and gender. To classify each traumatic event, the etiology of injury was coded based on the ICD-10 “External Causes of Injury” codes and classified into falls, road traffic accidents, mechanical, sports, burns, electrical, or other/unspecified. Similarly, the primary anatomical location of injury was coded using the ICD-10 system for trauma, distinguishing injury to the head/face, neck, chest, abdomen/pelvis, limbs, and spine/spinal cord. To assess the consumption of medical resources and patient outcomes, we collected data on hospital length of stay (LOS), hospitalization cost, intensive care unit stay, and whether surgical intervention occurred. The mode of admission of the patient was also noted as emergency, outpatient, or transfer. The patient’s discharge status was the primary outcome measure, which was coded as cure, improvement, stabilization, or death.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 27.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of continuous data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Normally distributed variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages (%). The χ2 test was used to compare proportions between groups, and Cramer’s V was calculated to measure the strength of association. For comparisons of non-normally distributed continuous variables across multiple groups (e.g. trauma mechanisms), the Kruskal–Wallis test was employed, with Epsilon-squared (ε2) calculated as a measure of effect size. When a significant result was found with the Kruskal–Wallis test, Dunn’s post hoc test with Bonferroni correction was used for pairwise comparisons. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

This study initially screened 100,260 records, of which a total of 74,070 trauma patients who met the inclusion criteria were included in the final analysis. The population consisted of 42,888 men (57.9%) and 31,182 women (42.1%), with a male-to-female ratio of 1.36:1. Trauma hospitalization was highest in 2021 (n = 17,060) and lowest in 2019 (n = 13,696) (Table 1). The age of the patients averaged 52.75 ± 19.77 years. Male patients were significantly younger, on average, than female patients (49.22 ± 19.40 years vs. 57.60 ± 19.24 years, p < 0.01). The mean hospital stay was 8.42 days (95% CI: 8.37–8.48), and the overall inpatient mortality was 1.63% (n = 1208). Trauma admissions followed a definite seasonal pattern, peaking between May and October and bottoming in February (Figure 1).

Distribution of trauma types and sites

Falls (41.3%), road traffic injuries (RTI) (29.6%), and mechanical injuries (18.5%) were the most frequent causes of trauma (Table 3). There was a difference between genders in the cause of injury. Males had mostly mechanical injuries (75.8%), electrical injuries (88.1%), burns (69.8%), and RTIs (56.9%). In contrast, female patients experienced more sports-related injuries (57.6%) (Figures 3 and 4). Falls contributed most to injury among both men and women, but accounted for a greater proportion of female injuries.

Discussion

This large-scale multicenter retrospective study provides a comprehensive epidemiological picture of trauma patients admitted to the hospital in Xuzhou, a representative model industrial and transportation city of China, between 2019 and 2023. Our findings indicate that the hospital and inpatient burden of trauma (distribution of age, gender, trauma types, and sites, hospitalization costs by injury, and mortality) is disproportionately borne by middle-aged men, with the most common causes being falls and road traffic injuries. These results highlight the multifaceted interplay of occupational exposures, traffic volume, and population dynamics in characterizing the local trauma profile, with significant implications for targeted public health interventions.

Our study elucidates the key epidemiological features of trauma burden in Xuzhou over a 4-year period. The heightened proportion of trauma among males is consistent with reports from GBD 2019 of excess trauma risk for males globally, particularly in LMICs [1]. This gender disparity was most pronounced for mechanical (75.8% male) and electrical (88.1% male) injuries, reflecting a pronounced gender segregation in high-risk sectors such as manufacturing, construction, and mining, which are mainstays of Xuzhou’s economy. This finding is in line with evidence that male workers in industrial sectors face higher levels of physical dangers and therefore are at increased risk of serious occupational injuries [13,14]. This trauma peak between May and October, forming a “high-temperature–rainy season” risk period, is consistent with other Chinese cities’ reports [15]. Increased outdoor activity, longer working hours in construction, and hazardous road conditions during the rainy season are likely explanations for this pattern [16]. Such seasonality suggests the possibility of dynamic resource planning in emergency services.

The two leading causes of trauma, RTIs and falls, reflect the twin issues of increasing motorization and aging. The high proportion of falls, particularly among women over 60 years, is one of the principal public health concerns. Our results showed that women sustained a significantly higher proportion of spinal injuries, which is likely due to the higher rate of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women, making them more susceptible to fractures from low-energy falls [17,18]. This makes it imperative to implement holistic fall prevention interventions for the elderly, including strength and balance training, home environmental modifications, and osteoporosis screening [19,20]. The significant RTI burden highlights ongoing traffic management issues in a city that is a key transport hub. The high proportion of motor vehicle accident injury and death points to stronger enforcement of traffic laws, infrastructural measures to protect vulnerable road users (pedestrians, cyclists), and public awareness campaigns [21,22]. This trend is consistent with national data indicating that while there has been progress, RTIs remain one of the leading causes of death and disability in China [23].

Compared to other studies, Xuzhou’s trauma pattern has a unique risk set. Compared with most HICs, where the single predominant cause of trauma hospitalization is falls among the elderly [24], there is a double peak of work-related injury among working-age men and injury from falls among the elderly in Xuzhou. This combined epidemiology is characteristic of many transitional economies that are undergoing industrialization and demographic aging simultaneously [25]. Our 1.63% in-hospital mortality rate is comparable with other Chinese regional trauma centers but higher than those in established trauma systems in HICs [4]. The difference may be explained by the variability in pre-hospital care, trauma system maturity, and patient severity.

Based on these findings, we recommend the following specific measures: (1) Strengthen occupational safety law and enforcement, and mandatory safety training for all workers, particularly migrant workers who make up the bulk of workers in construction [26]. (2) Implement and enforce evidence-based road traffic safety measures, including speed control, helmet and seatbelt laws, and the availability of exclusive lanes for non-motorized vehicles to avoid mixed-traffic crashes [27]. (3) Enact multifaceted, community-based fall prevention programs for older adults, including medical assessment (e.g. for vestibular dysfunction, orthostatic hypotension), physical therapy, and home environment safety audits [19,28]. (4) Streamline emergency resource allocation according to forecastable seasonal peaks in trauma incidence for enhanced treatment efficiency and patient outcomes.

A clear pattern emerged when examining hospital-level outcomes across injury mechanisms. Patients with road traffic injuries tended to have the highest ICU admission and surgical intervention rates, reflecting the greater injury severity typically associated with high-energy trauma. In contrast, individuals injured by low-energy falls, particularly older adults, were less likely to undergo surgery or require ICU care but often experienced longer hospital stays, likely due to comorbidities, frailty, and complex discharge planning needs. Mechanical injuries showed intermediate profiles, with relatively frequent operative management but shorter lengths of stay and lower costs compared with road traffic injuries. These gradients in ICU utilization, operative rates, length of stay, and costs across mechanisms underscore the heterogeneity of trauma burden and suggest that tailored prevention and resource allocation strategies are needed for high-energy road traffic injuries versus low-energy falls in the elderly, as previously observed in various countries [29–31].

The study period covered three distinct phases of the COVID-19 pandemic (pre‑pandemic, peak pandemic, and post‑pandemic), which provides a natural experiment to observe how major public health emergencies reshape trauma epidemiology and hospital burden. Consistent with reports from multiple regions [32–35], trauma admissions in this cohort declined during the peak pandemic period, likely reflecting reduced traffic volume, workplace closures, restrictions on outdoor activities, and patient reluctance to seek in‑hospital care due to infection concerns, as described in other trauma series from the COVID‑19 era [32–35]. At the same time, the pandemic context may have introduced novel or accentuated risks, such as a potential increase in low‑energy falls during prolonged home confinement, echoing literature noting shifts toward domestic injuries and falls among older adults while high‑energy mechanisms (e.g. road traffic, sports) decreased [36]. These dynamics highlight that public health measures, while effective for infection control, can substantially alter both the volume and mechanism mix of trauma, implying that future emergency preparedness, public health policy, and hospital resource planning should explicitly account for the collateral impact of epidemics and lockdown strategies on trauma patterns and the configuration of critical care, surgical, and rehabilitation resources [32–35].

There are a number of limitations in this study. Firstly, since it was only based on hospitalized patients in tertiary facilities, it probably underestimates the proportion of mild trauma managed as outpatients and does not include pre-hospital deaths, possibly overestimating the case-fatality rate of admitted patients. Considering that only three of the 13 Xuzhou’s trauma centers were included in the present study, epidemiological data (e.g. standardized incidence and mortality rates, DALYs, etc.) were not calculated because of limited reliability. Second, the retrospective design of the study led to missing data, particularly for the detailed injury classifications. Third, the lack of a standard Injury Severity Score (ISS) for all patients prevented a severity-stratified analysis of outcomes. Consequently, this study is limited to describing epidemiological patterns rather than identifying independent risk factors for poor outcomes.

Conclusions

In summary, this study provides valuable, region-specific data on the epidemiology of trauma in Xuzhou City. The findings indicate a double burden from occupational injury in working-age men and falls in the elderly, along with a significant contribution from traffic injury. These are relevant to informing the development of targeted preventive interventions, the reform of medical insurance payment systems, and ultimately reducing the proportion and mortality of trauma in the area.

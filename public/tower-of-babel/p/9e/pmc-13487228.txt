Characterization and resource utilization of dyeing sludge in Taihu Lake Basin, China
Authors: He Li, Chengjie Fei, Rajendra Prasad Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Sludge resource recovery has emerged as a promising research field in recent years; however, resource reuse strategies for dyeing sludge remains inadequately explored. This study investigated multiple dyeing wastewater treatment plants across Jiangsu Province, China, and conducted a standardized analysis of the sludge characteristics to explore more effective approaches for its resource utilization. Key sludge properties, including pH, moisture content, organic matter content, and calorific value, were evaluated and statistically analyzed. The results showed that the coefficients of variation for these indicators were 3%, 22%, 29%, and 32%, respectively. Among these parameters, pH remained relatively stable across different wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs), while the other indicators exhibited substantial of dispersion. Correlation analysis revealed significant correlations between organic matter content and moisture content, as well as between organic matter content and calorific value. For heavy metal content in the sludge, sustantial variability was observed across samples, with notable variations in copper (Cu), chromium (Cr), and arsenic (As) levels.These heavy metals are the core limiting factors affecting the resource reuse of the dyeing sludge. Strong positive correlations were observed between lead (Pb) and Cu (Pearson’s r = 0.71) and between Cr and As (Pearson’s r = 0.61), indicating potential synergistic accumulation characteristics of these heavy metals. Although the sludge generally posed a low potential ecological risk, the elevated pollution levels of Cu and As still constituted a major challenge for its resource recovery, directly restricting the practical application of sludge resource reuse. These findings clarify the fundamental characteristics of dyeing sludge and provide valuable insights for the rational management, risk control, and efficient resource reuse of dyeing sludge.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-56616-0.

Introduction

The dyeing industry, a core pillar of the textile sector, produces substantial amount of wastewater during production. Treatment of this wastewater produces dyeing sludge—a complexsolid waste material that poses significant environmental risks1–3. Although dyeing sludge is not classified as hazardous waste under China’s National Hazardous Waste Inventory, improper disposal still occupies land resources and poses risks of secondary soil and water contamination (i.e., release and migration of pollutants from improperly disposed sludge into surrounding environment, leading to the contamination soil and water bodies)4. Consequently, the treatment and management of dyeing sludge remain persistent challenges for the textile industry5.

China’s textile dyeing industry accounts for a large share of the country’s industrial wastewater discharge, generating millions of tons of pollutant-rich sludge annually. As a core hub of China’s dyeing industry with nationally leading production capacity, Jiangsu Province accommodates over 50% of related enterprises concentrated in southern/central Jiangsu, particularly within the Taihu Lake Basin6. Investigating the characteristics and treatment/disposal strategies for dyeing sludge in Jiangsu Province is therefore critically important). As a typical high-density industrial region, resolving sludge-related challenges is pivotal to the sustainable development of China’s broader dyeing sector. Additionally, systematic data on sludge characteristics within this region are severely lacking, with thefull-scale comprehensive analysis study published in 2014. The practical feasibility of existing resource recovery technologies requires urgent assessment against Jiangsu’s specific economic, environmental, and technical context, while substantial knowledge gaps persist regarding effective extraction and reclamation technologies for key contaminants, particularly heavy metals. Ultimately, these findings can provide essential evidence for formulating scientifically evidence-based sludge management policies and practices at both provincial and national scales.

Current studies on dyeing sludge mainly focuses on physicochemical methods (e.g., chemical conditioning, mechanical dewatering for moisture reduction)7–9, biological treatments (e.g., anaerobic digestion for organic stabilization)10, and resource recovery pathways (e.g., co-incineration, construction material utilization such as brick-making and biochar production)11–14. Whereas, landfilling has largely been abandoned due to unacceptable environmental hazards15. Additionally, emerging resource recovery approaches beyond conventional methods are being explored, such as co-pyrolysis of dyeing sludge with waste tires16 or litchi shells17 and co-incineration with heavy metal hyperaccumulator plants18.

Despite these advances, critical research gaps persist. Systematic data on the characteristics of dyeing sludge in Jiangsu Province are exceedingly scarce and outdated, with the latest published study conducted in 201419. To address these gaps, the main objective of this study is to investigate the physicochemical characteristics and heavy metal profiles of dyeing sludge from multiple wastewater treatment plants in Jiangsu Province, and to evaluate their implications for resource recovery. Specifically, this study aims to: (1) establish a systematic and up-to-date dataset of sludge properties, and (2) examine the pollution levels and potential ecological risks of heavy metals as critical factors for resource recovery.

Based on existing research gap, the primary objectives of this study were as follows: (1) systematically collect dewatered sludge samples from 25 representative dyeing wastewater treatment plants across Jiangsu Province and comprehensively characterize their key properties, including moisture content, pH, organic matter content, nutrient content, and heavy metal content, and establish a baseline dataset to address critical knowledge gaps regarding regional dyeing sludge attributes. These parameters were selected based on their direct influence on the feasibility and environmental safety of mainstream resource recovery pathways—calorific value for evaluating incineration potential, organic matter and moisture content for composting suitability and thermal treatment efficiency, pH for heavy metal mobility, and heavy metal concentrations for environmental risk assessment. (2) exclude non-essential parameters (e.g., pathogens, specific organic contaminants) given the study’s focus on core properties relevant to commonly discussed resource utilization options in the region, with the understanding that these supplementary parameters may be investigated in future pathway-specific studies. (3) to conduct an objective assessment of mainstream sludge treatment and disposal technologies within the unique operational context of Jiangsu Province. (4) o explore optimized sludge management strategies and provide scientifically grounded, regionally tailored recommendations for the safe and effective resource utilization and disposal of dyeing sludge .

Materials and methods

Study area and sample collection

Sludge samples were collected from 25 typical dyeing and printing wastewater treatment plants located in the Taihu Lake Basin, specifically in the cities of Suzhou (S1–S13), Wuxi (W1–W8), and Changzhou (C1–C4) in Jiangsu Province, China. These three cities constitute the core area of the Taihu Lake Basin and accommodate over 90% of the province’s textile printing and dyeing industry. The daily treatment capacity, sludge production, dewatering methods, and disposal methods of these sewage treatment plants were investigated. The moisture content, pH, organic matter content, calorific value and heavy metal content of the dewatered dyeing and printing sludge were analyzed. To fully reflect the representativeness of the sludge, multiple samplings were conducted at different sludge production locations. The samples were then mixed evenly, placed into sealed bags, sealed, and numbered for subsequent analysis. Table 1 below respectively shows the basic information. Analytical methods

This study focuses on the detection and analysis of the dyeing and printing sludge sampled from the aforementioned regions. It conducts horizontal comparisons and standardized evaluations of indicators such as moisture content, pH, organic matter content, calorific value and heavy metal content. Moisture content was determined by the oven-drying method. pH was measured using the glass electrode method. Organic matter and total nitrogen contents were determined using an elemental analyzer. The net calorific value on a dry basis was measured by the oxygen bomb calorimeter method. Heavy metals were analyzed by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) and inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) following acid digestion. Horizontal comparisons and standardized evaluations of these indicators were subsequently conducted. The Nemerow Index method and the Potential Ecological Risk Index method were then employed to assess the pollution levels and ecological risks associated with heavy metals in each sludge type, providing a scientific basis for evaluating resource utilization options.

Nemerow index method

The Nemerow Index is a multi-factor environmental quality index that is widely applied in comprehensive pollution assessments of water environments20. It considers extreme values or maximum values for weighting, and reduces the interference of subjective factors. This method allows for a thorough reflection of the impact of various heavy metals on sludge, particularly highlighting the significant environmental impact of high-concentration pollutants, and avoids the phenomenon where averaging diminishes the influence of heavy metals. Coefficient of variation (CV)

The coefficient of variation (CV)23 is a standardized measure of relative dispersion or relative variability. It is defined as the ratio of the standard deviation to the mean of a dataset. The primary purpose of the CV is to enable the comparison of the degree of variation between datasets with different units or significantly different mean values, as it expresses variability relative to the center of the data. Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics, including mean, range, and standard deviation, were calculated to summarize the physicochemical properties and heavy metal concentrations of the sludge samples. The coefficient of variation (CV) was computed to evaluate the degree of dispersion for each parameter across the 25 treatment plants. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to examine the relationships among pH, moisture content, organic matter content, calorific value, and heavy metal concentrations, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05 and p < 0.01. For non-normally distributed data, Spearman’s rank correlation was applied. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0, and all figures were prepared using Origin 2024.

Results and discussion

Characteristics of the dyeing wastewater treatment plant

Wastewater treatment plants investigated in this study varied in scale and key operational parameters. Their treatment capacity ranged from 0.13 × 10⁴ m³/d to 3.2 × 10⁴ m³/d, with an average of 1.31 × 10⁴ m³/d. Daily sludge production ranged from 0.4 t and 95 t (average: 16.9 t). The pH value of sludge ranged from 6.6 to 7.3 (average: 6.94), while the moisture content ranged from 30.9% to 86.8% (average: 61.8%). Organic matter content varied from 20.07% to 64.71% (average: 40.57%), and calorific value ranged from 3.35 MJ/kg to 13.4 MJ/kg (average: 7.73 MJ/kg). Table 1 provides an overview of the basic conditions of investigated dyeing wastewater treatment plants.

Current sludge treatment and disposal methods

Table 1 summarizes that the investigated dyeing wastewater treatment plants primarily adopt incineration for sludge treatment. Specifically, seven plants utilize landfill, two repurpose sludge for construction materials, and twenty-one employ incineration. In Jiangsu Province of China, over 90% of wastewater treatment plants used landfill and composting for sludge disposal in 2006, with only 4.3% using digestion and none using drying. By 2011, incineration became the mainstream approach, with landfill, building materials, and composting also developing. By 2022, incineration accounted for 66.67% of sludge treatment, including co-incineration with municipal waste and coal, and dedicated incineration after drying. 29.17% of plants opted land application. The plants in this study align this regional trend, predominantly utilizing incineration for sludge treatment, supplemented by construction material reuse and landfilling.

Basic composition analysis of sludge

Analysis of pH and moisture content characteristics of sludge

The pH values of the sludge samples range from 6.6 to 7.3, with a CV of 3%, indicating a uniform distribution and high stability. The overall average pH is 6.94. Although this value is close to neutral, it falls on the slightly acidic side of the pH scale. Considering the typical measurement uncertainty of the glass electrode method (± 0.1 pH units), the majority of individual sample measurements remain below 7.0, confirming the slightly acidic nature of the sludge. This pH range facilitates the migration and release of heavy metals, making it suitable for subsequent treatment and disposal24. Furthermore, it falls within the optimal activity range (6.5–8.5) for activated sludge microorganisms. At the same time, within this range, when used for land application, such as agricultural use, landscaping, and land improvement, it will not affect the soil’s pH level25. Moreover, under slightly acidic conditions, oxidative treatment of dyeing sludge can be effectively achieved using Fenton or Fenton-like systems26. Regarding the pH indicator, it does not pose an obstacle to the resource recovery of sludge.

All the plants in this study employed frame filter presses for dewatering, which resulted in moisture content below 80%27 for all samples except B5. The average moisture content of the sludge samples is 61.76%, which meets the moisture content limits specified in the “Discharge Standard of Pollutants for Municipal Wastewater Treatment Plants (GB18918—2002)”28. At the regional level, distinct characteristics were observed among the cities. The 13 samples from Suzhou exhibited a moisture content distribution ranging from 47.3% to 81.1%, with a CV of 18.4%. In contrast, the four samples from Changzhou were more clustered, ranging from 51.20% to 69.80% (CV: 12.8%). The data from Wuxi included both the highest (86.8%) and lowest (30.9%) recorded values, resulting in a significantly higher CV of 28.3%.

Overall, the moisture content of the samples displayed a wide distribution, spanning from 30.9% to 86.8%—a difference of 55.9% points (CV: 22%). Setting aside the influence of the dewatering method, this variability is largely attributable to the intrinsic properties of the sludge itself. For instance, the organic matter content significantly influences dewatering efficiency, the relationship of which will be analyzed in detail subsequently. Elevated moisture content is a prevalent characteristic of dyeing sludge in China. Although the investigated treatment plants currently meet discharge standards, this characteristic impedes resource reutilization, particularly by significantly reducing the efficacy of pyrolysis and co-pyrolysis29.

Sludge organic matter content and calorific value

The organic matter content of the sludge samples ranged from 20.07% to 64.71%, with a mean value of 40.57% (CV: 29%). The complex composition of dyeing wastewater determines the unique characteristics of the sludge organic matter. According to Chinese standards, the organic matter requirement for Class B sludge agricultural reuse is > 40%; approximately one-third of the samples met this threshold. High organic matter dyeing sludge offers more pathways for low-carbon treatment, though it also raises safety concerns, such as the relationship between calorific value potential and potentially toxic substances. For low organic matter sludge, building material utilization is a more ideal and economical outlet, as the high inorganic content can enhance the mechanical properties of the final construction products.

The calorific value of the sludge samples ranged from 3.35 MJ/kg to 13.4 MJ/kg, with an average of 7.73 MJ/kg. Its CV reached 32%, the highest among all tested indicators, indicating substantial variability in calorific value across different treatment plants. This variability suggests that diverse utilization pathways, rather than uniform incineration, should be considered in resource recovery strategies. For example, sample W1, with a calorific value of only 3.35 MJ/kg, was directed toward building material production. In contrast, sample W8, which also had a relatively low calorific value (4.54 MJ/kg), was still sent for incineration—a process that typically requires co-combustion with higher-calorific-value waste, resulting in certain resource inefficiencies.

Basic characteristics analysis of nutrients in sludge

A subset of 13 samples was selected for detailed nutrient analysis. These 13 samples were chosen to cover the main dyeing process types (e.g., cotton, synthetic fiber, and blended fabric dyeing) and wastewater treatment technologies across the 25 plants, while also ensuring geographical representation of the key dyeing industry clusters in southern, central, and northern Jiangsu. This subset is therefore representative of the overall sludge characteristics in the study area. The measured components include total nitrogen, total phosphorus (as P2O5), total potassium (as K2O) and total nutrients (as N+P2O5+K2O). The results of these analyses are presented in Fig. 2. These findings revealed that the sludge contains substantial nutrients, characterized by high nitrogen and phosphorus, and low potassium. The total nitrogen content ranges between 16.8 to 37.6 g/kg, with a mean ± SD of 26.38 ± 7.41 g/kg; total phosphorus ranges between 9.5 to 16.4 g/kg, with a mean ± SD of 13.28 ± 1.79 g/kg; total potassium ranges between 1.8 to 4.9 g/kg, with a mean ± SD of 3.47 ± 0.98 g/kg, which is much lower than the typical potassium content in agricultural soils (16.6 g/kg)30.The total nutrients ranges between 31.78 to 57.65 g/kg, with a mean ± SD of 43.13 ± 8.48 g/kg.

Compared with common fertilizers, such as animal manure31, the total nutrient content of the sludge is slightly low, with relatively high total nitrogen and total phosphorus content, but the total potassium content is significantly low, which is consistent with the overall trend of sludge nutrients in China32,33. From the perspective of total nutrients, the sludge samples from the dyeing wastewater treatment plants generally meet the standards for sludge land application. However, their moisture content does not meet the standards for domestic landscaping, agricultural use, forestry, or land reclamation.

Correlation analysis of key sludge characteristics

A Spearman correlation analysis was conducted on various sludge characteristic parameters. Significant correlations were identified between organic matter content and moisture content, as well as between organic matter content and calorific value (Figure S1). Generally, a higher organic matter content in sludge corresponds to a greater amount of combustible material, resulting in a higher calorific value. This study observed a similar trend between organic matter content and calorific value, yielding a Spearman correlation coefficient of 0.85, p<0.001 (Figure S1). To further investigate this relationship, a linear regression was performed (R²=0.75, p<0.01), revealing a significant positive correlation between sludge calorific value and organic matter content, as detailed in Fig. 3. Thus, organic matter content can serve as an efficient predictor for the calorific value of printing and dyeing sludge. This implies that its calorific value can be estimated with reasonable accuracy through rapid and relatively simple measurements of organic matter (e.g., volatile solids analysis), thereby replacing or reducing the need for complex and time-consuming direct calorific value tests. Furthermore, for wastewater treatment plants that already utilize incineration, organic matter content could be adopted as one of the rapid screening criteria for sludge procurement or acceptance. Sludge with higher organic matter content offers significantly greater benefits in terms of energy recovery.

Fig. 3Linear regression of moisture content and calorific value with organic matter content.

Linear regression of moisture content and calorific value with organic matter content.

Previous analysis of moisture content in the collected samples indicated a wide distribution with a considerable range. Excluding the influence of the dewatering method, it was hypothesized that the organic matter content affects dewatering efficiency—a hypothesis that was confirmed by correlation analysis. Further analysis, illustrated in Fig. 2, showed that organic matter content exhibited a weak-to-moderate linear correlation with moisture content (R² = 0.46, p < 0.01). A Spearman correlation coefficient of 0.72 (p < 0.001; Fig. S1) indicated that organic matter partially explains variations in moisture content but cannot serve as a robust standalone predictor. This finding demonstrates that sludge with a higher organic matter content tends to have a correspondingly higher moisture content. However, relying solely on organic matter content to accurately predict or inversely deduce moisture content is unreliable, as moisture content is also influenced by various factors, including floc structure, inorganic composition, and preliminary treatment processes. For resource utilization purposes, both parameters (moisture content and organic matter content) should be monitored as independent yet interrelated key indicators.

Variability analysis of sludge characteristics

The CV calculated for each parameter reveals distinct patterns of variability that reflect the heterogeneous nature of industrial dyeing sludge. The pH exhibited the lowest CV (3%), indicating high uniformity across the 25 plants. This stability is expected, as pH in dyeing wastewater treatment processes is typically controlled within a narrow range to meet biological treatment requirements and discharge standards. In contrast, moisture content, organic matter content, and calorific value showed moderate CV values of 22%, 29%, and 32%, respectively. This moderate dispersion can be attributed to plant-specific differences, as the characteristics of textile sludge depend on the production processes and formulations (e.g., knitting, weaving, denim dyeing) employed, as well as the wastewater treatment technologies adopted34. The higher CV values for organic matter and calorific value are particularly noteworthy, as these two parameters directly influence the selection and efficiency of resource recovery pathways.

Heavy metal concentrations exhibited the greatest variability, with CV values substantially higher than those of general physicochemical parameters. For instance, Cu concentrations ranged broadly, consistent with previous findings that the concentration of Cu in industrial sludge exhibits the most variation among commonly measured metals35. This pronounced variability can be ascribed to the diverse types of dyes, auxiliaries, and finishing agents used by different textile enterprises, which introduce varying quantities of metal-containing compounds into the wastewater stream. Previous studies have established that the chemical properties of sludge and their correlations differ significantly depending on the source of the sludge36. Overall, the CV profiles observed in this study underscore the substantial heterogeneity of dyeing sludge and highlight the necessity of developing flexible, site-specific resource recovery strategies rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

Analysis of heavy metals

25 sludge samples collected from dyeing wastewater treatment plants, were analyzed for heavy metals (specifically Cu, Cd, Pb, As, Cr, and Hg) based on Chinese standards for sludge agricultural use to provide a basis for the resource utilization and safe disposal of sludge.

Findings of heavy metals in the sludge are presented in Fig. 4. Analysis of the above data reveals four main characteristics of heavy metal content in the sludge from Jiangsu Province’s dyeing wastewater treatment plants:

Fig. 4Heavy metal content in sludge samples.

Heavy metal content in sludge samples.

(1) Heavy metal content exhibits a trend toward relative homogeneity and stability

The variability of the six heavy metal indicators across sludge samples from different wastewater plants is relatively small. The CV for all heavy metals was below 50%, with Pb showing the lowest variability at 15%, and Cd and Hg showing the highest variability at about 40% (Table S1). Heavy metals with relatively high concentrations, notably Cu and Cr, exhibited low CV. Despite the higher variability, the Hg content in the sludge is well below the Grade A agricultural use limit in all investigated plants. Only Cu and As exceed the Grade A agricultural use standard. Although the overall CV for total heavy metal content is nearly 150%, this is primarily due to the higher concentration of Cu. Notably, Cu is among the most prevalent heavy metals in sewage sludge across China, a trend consistent with existing studies37. As a raw material with inherent limitations, sludge with elevated Cu content is better processed using the low-cost recovery methods. Bioleaching, for instance, is particularly suitable given the naturally slightly acidic pH of such sludge38.

(2) Significant variation in heavy metal content within the same WWTP

The heavy metal content in sludge varies considerably within the same wastewater treatment plant (Table S2) and the CV for heavy metal content in the sludge samples is all above 100%. Compared with the heavy metal content in sludge of the Yangtze River Basin33,39, the values in this study indicate substantial overall variability. Taking sludge sample No. 1 as an example, among the six heavy metals measured, the CV is 155.62%, with content ranging from 0.406 mg/kg to 1150.3 mg/kg. Cu has the highest content, while Hg has the lowest. Excluding these two extreme values, the remaining heavy metal content varies between 0.8 mg/kg and 366 mg/kg, still showing significant differences. Each sample exhibited similar characteristics, which may be summarized as a common feature of dyeing sludge. The substantial variability in heavy metal content is mainly due to the higher concentrations of Cu, Cr, and As, which aligns with the general characteristics of sludge from China’s dyeing wastewater treatment plants40. Regarding the specific sources, Cu predominantly originates from metal-complex dyes, mordant dyes, and copper phthalocyanine structure dyes41(e.g., phthalocyanine blue and green) widely used in textile dyeing and finishing. Arsenic is primarily attributed to arsenic-containing auxiliaries used in printing processes42, certain metal-complex dyes, and impurities in dye intermediates. Under the slightly acidic to neutral pH conditions typical of dyeing wastewater treatment, both metals exhibit low biodegradability and strong affinity for solid-phase partitioning, leading to their enrichment in dewatered sludge.

(3) Certain correlations exist among heavy metal contents

Pearson correlation analysis was conducted on the heavy metal content data in the sludge samples. The Pearson correlation coefficients between the heavy metals are presented in Table 4. As shown in Fig. 5, a significant positive correlation, of moderate strength, was observed between Cu and Pb (Pearson’s r = 0.71, p<0.01). Divergence between pollution and ecological risk assessments and management implications

A noteworthy finding of this study is the divergence between the results obtained from the Nemerow Pollution Index and the Potential Ecological Risk Index (RI). As was identified as a primary pollutant through Nemerow Index, whereas the RI ranked Cd as the element of greatest ecological concern. This discrepancy is methodologically inherent and reflects the fundamentally different aims of the two indices. The Nemerow Index, which emphasizes the single highest pollution factor, is highly sensitive to elements present in high concentrations relative to their background values, such as As in this study’s sludge. In contrast, the RI incorporates a toxicity response factor for each metal, where Cd carries a significantly higher weight (30) compared to As (10) and other metals, making RI more responsive to low-concentration but highly toxic elements.

This divergence has direct implications for sludge management and policy-making. In the context of resource recovery from dyeing sludge in the Taihu Lake Basin, we recommend a two-tier decision-making framework:

(i)For rapid screening and classification: Use the Nemerow Index as the primary tool to identify and categorize the overall pollution level of sludge. This is particularly useful for excluding heavily contaminated sludge from certain resource recovery pathways that rely on low pollutant levels (e.g., direct land application).(ii)For pathway-specific refinement and ongoing management: Where sludge pollution is classified as “slight” or “warning level,” apply the Ecological Risk Index as a secondary filter. This step is critical for identifying hidden risks from low-concentration but high-toxicity elements like Cd, which may necessitate targeted pre-treatment or influence the long-term monitoring plan. This combined approach ensures that both magnitude of contamination and toxicological significance are considered.

Prospects of dyeing sludge

Currently, off-site incineration is the primary disposal method for dyeing sludge at the 25 investigated facilities. This process enables energy recovery and resource utilization by generating electricity from high-temperature steam. The average cost ranges from 300to 400 RMB per ton (including treatment and transportation). Transport distances between wastewater treatment plants and thermal incineration facilities vary considerably (5–500 km). Given the high sludge moisture content, incineration may be economically suboptimal. Therefore, this study focuses on established resource utilization routes based on the sludge characteristics of these facilities, excluding the laboratory-stage methods (e.g., co-pyrolysis, bioleaching for copper removal) previously mentioned.

Sludge uses

Sludge-based building materials involve the process of treating sludge generated from wastewater treatment and reusing it as a component or raw material for construction materials. This method not only reduces the environmental pollution caused by sludge but also conserves resources, aligning with the principles of sustainable development. The primary utilization methods include sludge brickmaking, sludge cement production, and sludge ceramics. For dyeing sludge, its use in brickmaking has been proven feasible and practical in numerous laboratory experiments47. Findings indicate that dyeing sludge can be incorporated into clay bricks at proportions ranging from 10% to 50%, thereby reducing the use of traditional clay and achieving resource utilization of the sludge. In this context, the compressive strength of the clay bricks varies from 24.5 MPa (at 10% sludge) to 9.93 MPa (at 50% sludge). Within the same range, the water absorption rate, ignition weight loss, and total shrinkage rate vary from 11.4% to 25.3%, 10.8% to 24.7%, and 11.6% to 15.3%, respectively. Kiln-fired bricks with a 10% sludge content exhibit superior engineering performance compared to similar bricks tested in laboratory-scale studies and show minimal loss during the drying and firing processes48. Cement production permits a sludge moisture content ≤ 80%, a pH 5.0–13.0, and a particle size < 5 mm. The sampled dyeing sludge satisfies these specifications (Table S6). Regarding heavy metal limits for construction materials, sludge from all 25 investigated dyeing wastewater treatment plants was classified as having a low pollution level and posing a low potential ecological risk, with the exception of As. Optimizing As content could enable sludge utilization in construction materials.

Land application

Land application of sludge involves using the organic matter and nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium in sludge to improve soil structure, provide essential nutrients, enhance land productivity, or support ecological restoration. This includes agricultural use, landscaping, and land reclamation. By applying stabilized and harmless sludge that meets national standards, it is possible to reduce the waste of valuable resources in the sludge, effectively address sludge disposal issues, decrease the need for landfilling or incineration, reduce environmental pollution, and achieve certain economic and social benefits.

Based on the earlier analysis of the nutrient characteristics of the investigated sludge and a comparison with other common fertilizers, the total nutrient content of the sludge samples is slightly lower. Although it generally meets China’s standards for sludge land application, the moisture content required for landscaping, agricultural use, and land reclamation is relatively high. The required moisture contents are below 40%, 60%, and 65% respectively, whereas the moisture content of the samples ranges from 73% to 76%, failing to meet land application requirements. Regarding heavy metals, all pollutants in the samples except arsenic meet the concentration limits for land application. It can be seen that, if the resource utilization prospect of dyeing and printing sludge in land use is to be developed, on the premise that the moisture content and arsenic content of the sludge from the 25 investigated sewage treatment plants have been further controlled, using it for land application is feasible49. Moreover, the land use of sludge combined with other technologies, such as anaerobic digestion, can provide the nutrients needed by the soil, reduce the demand for chemical synthetic fertilizers, and also alleviate the scarcity of fossil resources to a certain extent50.

Conclusion

This study systematically characterized the physicochemical properties and heavy metal profiles of dewatered dyeing sludge collected from 25 printing and dyeing wastewater treatment plants in Jiangsu Province, China.

The results revealed moderate to high variability in key sludge parameters. The CV values for moisture content, organic matter content, and calorific value were 22%, 29%, and 32%, respectively, reflecting plant-specific differences in dyeing processes, wastewater sources, and dewatering practices. In contrast, pH exhibited a low CV of 3%, indicating stable and uniform operational conditions across the surveyed plants. This inherent variability underscores the need for tailored, site-specific resource recovery strategies rather than standardized approaches.

Strong correlations were identified between organic matter content and moisture content (Spearman’s r = 0.72, p<0.001) and between organic matter content and calorific value (Spearman’s r = 0.85, p<0.001), suggesting that these relationships can be leveraged to optimize sludge characteristics for improved resource recovery efficiency.

Analysis of heavy metals revealed that Cu, Cr, and As are present at elevated concentrations. The pollution assessment indicated that Cu and As exhibit high pollution levels, whereas the sludge overall demonstrates a low potential ecological risk from heavy metals. This combination of high pollution levels and low ecological risk presents both challenges and opportunities for developing safe resource utilization pathways.

Based on these findings, the following recommendations are proposed for future work: (1) further investigation into the relationships between organic matter, moisture content, and calorific value to optimize sludge pretreatment and resource recovery parameters; (2) continued monitoring and source tracing of heavy metals, particularly Cu and As, to mitigate their impact on downstream reuse options; and (3) development of region-specific, flexible management frameworks that accommodate the inherent heterogeneity of industrial dyeing sludge and enable more diversified resource recovery pathways.

Real-world insights into empagliflozin use in heart failure across ejection fractions and patients with or without diabetes: a multicenter study from Bangladesh

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEmpagliflozin, a member of the Sodium-Glucose Co-Transporter 2 (SGLT-2) inhibitor family, is a promising antidiabetic drug with proven benefits in heart failure (HF). However, its effects in heart failure patients in varying ejection fractions with and without diabetes, and its impact in a real-world clinical setting, remain underexplored.MethodsThis multicenter observational study, conducted in a real-world setting, from November 2022 to October 2023 in four specialized and tertiary care hospitals in Bangladesh. The study included 797 patients diagnosed with heart failure, irrespective of ejection fraction, who received empagliflozin at a dose of 10 mg daily over six months. The primary aim was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of empagliflozin in enhancing clinical, biochemical, and echocardiographic parameters in patients with heart failure across ejection fraction types, with or without diabetes.ResultsOf the 797 enrolled patients, 460 (57.7%) had diabetes. Significant improvements were observed in both diabetic and non-diabetic groups across primary and secondary outcomes. Empagliflozin therapy led to improvements in New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class (p = 0.001), echocardiographic parameters such as left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) (p = 0.003), and N-terminal pro–B-type Natriuretic Peptide (NT-proBNP) levels (p < 0.001). Biochemical markers also showed positive changes, including a significant reduction in serum creatinine (p = 0.001) and improvements in estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) (p < 0.001). Subgroup analyses revealed that the therapeutic effects were consistent across different age groups, BMI categories, and longer duration of diabetes.ConclusionsEmpagliflozin significantly improved cardiovascular and renal outcomes in heart failure patients, regardless of diabetes status. These findings support the inclusion of empagliflozin in the management of heart failure in clinical practice, particularly for patients with coexisting diabetes or chronic kidney disease. Further large-scale, long-term studies are needed to confirm these results and assess sustained benefits.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Background

Heart failure (HF) is a complex syndrome caused by processes that impair ventricular function to receive or eject blood. HF is classified based on left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) into three categories: Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF), Heart Failure with Mildly Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFmrEF), and Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction (HFpEF) [1]. While HFrEF and HFmrEF respond to established neurohormonal therapies, including angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEi), angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors (ARNi), beta-blockers, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRA), effective treatment options for HFpEF remain limited, with benefits primarily observed in select subgroups [2–5]. These challenges highlight the need for novel therapies, particularly those targeting metabolic pathways.

Globally, HF is a rapidly expanding public health concern, with an estimated prevalence of 64 million individuals, with prevalence increasing markedly with age. Despite advancements in therapeutic approaches, HF persists in causing elevated mortality and morbidity rates [6, 7]. In the United Kingdom, the prevalence of HF increases with age, from 1% among individuals aged 50–59 years to 5–10% in those aged 80–89 years, often resulting in prolonged disability [8]. In Bangladesh, the burden of HF is also escalating, driven by high incidences of hypertension, diabetes, and ischemic heart disease, which contribute to the rising prevalence of both HFrEF and HFpEF [9]. Approximately 1.2% of the adult population in Bangladesh is affected by HF, with higher prevalence observed in urban areas and among the elderly [10]. Diabetes, a prominent risk factor for both HFrEF and HFpEF, affects millions in Bangladesh [11]. Despite the increasing incidence of HF, treatment options, especially for HFpEF, remain limited, and access to advanced therapies such as SGLT2 inhibitors is restricted by factors including cost and availability [12].

SGLT2 inhibitors, including Empagliflozin, initially developed to manage type 2 diabetes, have demonstrated promising results in the treatment of heart failure. Clinical trials have shown that SGLT2 inhibitors significantly decrease hospitalization rates and enhance cardiovascular outcomes in patients with HFrEF [13, 14]. Empagliflozin modulates preload, regulates sodium and glucose metabolism, and improves glycemic control, providing additional benefits in patients with type-2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [15, 16]. Furthermore, it reduces major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with diabetes and established cardiovascular disease [17], and diminishes cardiovascular hospitalization and mortality in patients with HFrEF (NYHA Class II-IV) [18]. Furthermore, it attenuates the decline in eGFR and decreases hospitalization rates in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) at risk of progression [19], while also improving cardiovascular outcomes in patients with HFpEF [20].

Despite these encouraging results, real-world data on Empagliflozin use in HF patients across ejection fractions, particularly in Bangladesh, are limited. Evidence suggests that the response to empagliflozin may vary with patient characteristics such as age, BMI, diabetes duration, and baseline ejection fraction, emphasizing the need for real-world evaluation across diverse populations [13, 21]. Given the variability in treatment responses due to ethnicity, comorbidities, and healthcare access, region-specific data are essential. This study aims to explore the real-world experience of efficacy and safety of Empagliflozin in diabetic and non-diabetic heart failure patients in Bangladesh, providing insights into its real-world applicability and potential to improve HF management across ejection fraction and diabetes status.

Materials and methods

Study design

This real-world multicenter observational study was conducted from November 2022 to October 2023 across four centers in Bangladesh: Bangladesh Medical University (BMU), National Institute of Cardiovascular Diseases (NICVD), Dhaka Medical College Hospital (DMCH), and Ibrahim Cardiac Hospital & Research Institute (ICHRI). Non-random convenient sampling method was used to enroll eligible participants aged 18 years or older, with a confirmed diagnosis of heart failure, with NYHA class II-IV, irrespective of background cause of heart failure & ejection fraction, elevated NT-proBNP levels and patients stable on oral diuretics, who were started with empagliflozin along with standard therapy for heart failure and diabetes. Cutoff level of NT pro BNP for acute heart failure is greater than 300 pg/mL for those without atrial fibrillation (AF), or greater than 900 pg/mL for those with AF; and > 125pg/ml for non-acute heart failure [22]. Exclusion criteria included patients with acute decompensated heart failure, systolic blood pressure ≥ 180 mmHg, symptomatic hypotension, liver disease, impaired renal function (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²), a history of ketoacidosis, prior use of SGLT2 inhibitors, pregnant or lactating women, or those with recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs).

Sampling and enrollment

Participants were enrolled using a convenience sampling approach, based on eligibility and availability at the study site during the recruitment period. While this method may introduce selection bias, all consecutive patients meeting the inclusion criteria were invited to participate to reduce selective enrollment. Baseline characteristics of enrolled participants were compared to the overall eligible population to assess representativeness. Efforts were made to minimize loss to follow-up through regular reminders and structured follow-up visits.

Study procedure and data collection

Figure 1 illustrates the study’s enrollment, follow-up, and analysis stages. Patients were screened for eligibility and once enrolled, were monitored for six months. Six months was chosen as a feasible timeframe for a multicenter real-world observational study, balancing follow-up completeness and clinical relevance. This is aligned with previous observational studies assessing intermediate endpoints [23].

At baseline, the following clinical and biochemical parameters were recorded: glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), serum creatinine, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), blood pressure (BP), body mass index (BMI), and echocardiographic measurements including left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), Left Ventricular Internal Diameter in diastole (LVIDd), Left Atrial (LA) Dimension and N-terminal pro–B-type Natriuretic Peptide (NT-proBNP) levels. These measurements were reassessed after 6 months of continued empagliflozin therapy.

This study involved 1173 patients assessed for eligibility, out of which 376 patients were excluded due to factors such as recent urinary tract infection, valvular heart disease, eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m², end-stage renal disease, and other exclusion criteria. A total of 797 patients were enrolled. Of these, 638 patients completed the 6-month follow-up (with 20.5% lost to follow-up). Key outcome measures, including clinical improvements, biochemical parameters, and differences in ejection fraction (EF) and diabetic status, were assessed at the end of the follow-up period.

Treatment regimen

Patients got treatment according to ESC guidelines for heart failure [22] and for diabetes management ADA guidelines 2022 was followed [24]. Patients received treatment of other co-morbidities as standard protocol including coronary revascularization of coronary artery stenosis as required. Empagliflozin was administered at a dose of 10 mg daily as part of the standard heart failure management regimen. Empagliflozin was chosen due to its consistent availability and prescribing across study centers, ensuring uniform exposure assessment. Empagliflozin has been shown to improve glycemic control in T2DM and reduce the risk of cardiovascular death in both diabetic and non-diabetic patients.

Outcome measurement

Primary and intermediate endpoints were selected to capture early therapeutic responses, as these measures are widely used, prognostically relevant in heart failure, and sensitive to short-term changes. Primary outcomes were defined as Change in NYHA functional class & Change in NT-proBNP. Secondary outcomes were defined as Echocardiographic parameters: LVEF, LVIDd, LA dimension Biochemical and clinical markers: HbA1c, lipid profile, BMI, and other relevant laboratory parameters.

Echocardiographic and NYHA assessments

NT-proBNP was measured using standardized commercially available immunoassays at participating centers. Diagnostic and prognostic thresholds were interpreted according to contemporary ESC heart failure guidelines [22]. Serial measurements were used to assess within-patient change over time rather than absolute diagnostic classification. Echocardiographic assessments were performed according to protocols without centralized standardization. Left ventricular ejection fraction was measured by trained cardiologists using the modified Simpson’s biplane method, with repeat assessments by the same operator whenever feasible to reduce inter-observer variability. NYHA functional class was assigned by treating physicians using standardized definitions. Adverse events, including hypotension, acute kidney injury, urinary tract infection, and hypoglycemia, were actively monitored and recorded according to pre-specified definitions. Although formal core laboratory adjudication was not available, these standardized procedures were implemented to minimize measurement and interrater bias inherent to real-world observational studies.

Follow-up and follow-up and safety monitoring

Participants were scheduled for follow-up visits at three and six months. Follow-up assessments included clinical evaluation, laboratory tests, and echocardiography. Patients continued their standard heart failure therapy, including RAAS inhibitors, beta-blockers, and diuretics. Any dose adjustments or changes in concomitant therapy during follow-up were recorded, though these were not standardized across centers. Medication adherence was assessed as self reported. Safety monitoring included recording adverse events, with particular attention to urinary tract infections, dehydration & hypoglycemia. Efforts were made to ensure completeness by contacting patients who missed visits. Missing data were handled using a complete-case analysis, and the proportion of participants lost to follow-up was reported.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 26. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. For comparing baseline and six-month follow-up values, paired sample t-tests were used for continuous variables, and the chi-square test was applied for categorical variables. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Subgroup analyses were performed across different age groups, BMI categories, and durations of diabetes to assess the differential impact of Empagliflozin. One-way ANOVA was used to evaluate differences in NYHA classification across subgroups, while paired sample t-tests were employed within each subgroup to compare echocardiographic parameters (LVIDd, LVEF, LA dimension) and NT-proBNP levels at baseline and follow-up. These statistical methods confirmed significant improvements in cardiac structure and function, renal and metabolic markers, and symptom burden, highlighting the consistent therapeutic benefits of Empagliflozin in a broad spectrum of heart failure patients in real-world clinical practice.

Data presentation

Results were presented in tables and graphs. Descriptive statistics were summarized using frequency tables, while associations between variables were illustrated in cross-tabulations with test statistics noted in footnotes.

Results

The findings of this multicenter, real-world study provide comprehensive insights into the clinical characteristics, treatment patterns, and therapeutic outcomes of heart failure patients treated with Empagliflozin in Bangladesh. A total of 797 patients were evaluated at baseline, with follow-up data available for 638 individuals after six months. The results are presented in a structured format covering baseline demographics, clinical symptoms, comorbid conditions, pharmacological profiles, and changes in cardiovascular status, biochemical parameters, and echocardiographic measurements. Subgroup analyses were also conducted to assess variations in treatment response across different age groups, BMI categories, and durations of diabetes. Overall, the findings reflect significant functional, biochemical, and cardiac improvements following Empagliflozin therapy, suggesting its beneficial role in the management of heart failure in routine clinical practice.

Baseline particulars of the patients with heart failure (n = 797)

Table 1 presents the baseline characteristics of patients with heart failure, highlighting a predominance of middle-aged and male individuals. The majority were engaged in service-related occupations, with a significant portion also comprising homemakers and business professionals. Clinically, breathlessness and chest pain were commonly reported symptoms, while leg edema was less frequent. Anthropometric measures suggest that many patients were overweight and had a higher-than-normal waist-hip ratio. A substantial proportion of the cohort had a history of diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia, with nearly half reporting previous myocardial infarction. Chronic kidney disease and a history of smoking were also notable. Overall, the findings reflect a population with multiple cardiovascular risk factors, underscoring the need for comprehensive risk management in heart failure care.

Figure 2 presents the concomitant medication use among patients with heart failure, revealing that statins and beta-blockers were the most commonly prescribed drugs. Antiplatelet agents & statins were also widely used, reflecting a strong emphasis on cardiovascular risk reduction. Diuretics and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) were frequently included in treatment regimens, indicating efforts to manage fluid overload and improve outcomes. About 73.8% patients were getting Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS) inhibitors including angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs), Angiotensin II Receptor Blocker (ARBs), and angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors (ARNis). Overall, quadruple therapy (comprising Empagliflozin +ACEI/ARB/ARNi + BB + MRA) was used in 50.35% of patients, suggesting that half of the cohort received comprehensive guideline-directed medical therapy.

Table 2 outlines changes in cardiovascular status among patients at baseline and after six months, showing marked clinical improvement. There was a notable shift in NYHA classification, with more patients moving to a better functional class and fewer remaining in advanced stages, indicating enhanced cardiac performance. Vital signs and biochemical markers demonstrated favorable changes, including reductions in pulse rate, blood pressure, and serum creatinine, along with improved glycemic control. Renal function appeared to benefit, as evidenced by an improvement in eGFR and normalization of serum sodium levels, while potassium levels remained stable. Echocardiographic findings reflected an improvement in heart function, with increased ejection fraction and reduced left ventricular size, though left atrial size remained largely unchanged. Additionally, a significant decline in NT-proBNP levels suggested relief of heart failure symptoms and overall clinical stabilization.

Table 3(a) highlights a general trend of improvement in NYHA functional class following six months of empagliflozin treatment. This positive change was observed across all age groups, reaching statistical significance. Discussion

This multicenter, real-world study offers comprehensive insights into the efficacy of empagliflozin in managing HF among 797 patients in Bangladesh, with follow-up data accessible for 638 patients after six months. The results reveal significant enhancements in functional status, biochemical markers, and cardiovascular outcomes, thereby affirming the advantageous role of empagliflozin in the management of heart failure within routine clinical practice.

Our findings are consistent with emerging pragmatic and real-world evidence. The AMPLIFY-TIR study demonstrated that early initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors in routine clinical practice was associated with improvements in NT-proBNP levels and functional status, while the TARGET-HF program highlighted enhanced guideline-directed therapy implementation and favorable biomarker trajectories following SGLT2 inhibitor use. The alignment of these studies with our multicenter real-world data from Bangladesh supports the external validity and translational effectiveness of empagliflozin in everyday heart failure care [25, 26].

The baseline characteristics of the cohort reveal a substantial prevalence of cardiovascular risk factors, including hypertension (68.6%), diabetes (57.7%), dyslipidemia (60.1%), and a history of myocardial infarction (45.7%) within the population. These findings align with global trends that underscore the rising incidence of metabolic syndrome and cardiovascular disease, which have emerged as primary contributors to heart failure in Bangladesh [27, 28]. Furthermore, a significant portion of this cohort was diagnosed with chronic kidney disease (12.4%) and had a history of smoking (39.8%), underscoring the importance of comprehensive risk management in patients with heart failure.

Concerning the distribution of BMI, our study established that 52.6% of patients were classified as obese, 26.1% fell within the normal BMI range, and 19.2% were categorized as overweight. This notable prevalence of obesity highlights its significant correlation with the onset and advancement of heart failure, as corroborated by international evidence [29, 30]. Although BMI was evaluated at baseline, no subsequent analysis of changes in BMI or weight was conducted after treatment with empagliflozin.

The functional improvements observed within this study were statistically significant, as evidenced by the modification in NYHA classification. At baseline, 69.6% of patients were designated as NYHA Class II; however, this percentage diminished to 34.1% at follow-up, accompanied by a considerable reduction in patients categorized as Class III/IV (from 22.0% to 5.4%). These results are consistent with those reported by Zannad et al. (2020) [14] and Anker et al. (2020) [28], who identified that SGLT2 inhibitors enhance functional capacity and quality of life in patients with heart failure, particularly those diagnosed with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF).

In addition to functional enhancements, biochemical and clinical parameters exhibited favorable modifications, including reductions in pulse rate, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, and serum creatinine concentrations, alongside improvements in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). These findings corroborate the research of McGuire et al. (2021) and Petrie et al. (2020), who underscored the renal advantages of SGLT2 inhibitors in patients with heart failure, especially those with diabetes [31, 32]. Furthermore, a decrease in NT-proBNP levels was observed, indicative of symptomatic improvement in heart failure as well as overall cardiovascular health. Beyond glycemic control, empagliflozin exerts pleiotropic cardiovascular effects. Experimental and translational studies demonstrate that SGLT2 inhibition improves endothelial function through enhanced nitric oxide bioavailability and reduced vascular inflammation, while attenuating mitochondrial oxidative stress by improving myocardial energetics and reducing reactive oxygen species. These mechanisms provide a biologically plausible explanation for the observed improvements in NYHA functional class, NT-proBNP levels, and echocardiographic parameters across heart failure phenotypes [33, 34].

Echocardiographic analysis revealed substantial enhancements in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and left ventricular internal diameter (LVIDd). Notably, LVEF demonstrated improvement across all age groups, BMI categories, and durations of diabetes, findings that are consistent with the results from the EMPEROR-Reduced trial and the study by Anker et al. (2020), wherein SGLT2 inhibitors were demonstrated to facilitate cardiac remodelling and mitigate adverse cardiovascular outcomes in patients with heart failure [28]. A decrease in LVIDd was also seen, suggesting improved left ventricular function, especially in patients aged 46 & older and across different BMI categories normal weight, overweight, and obese. Notably, the size of the left atrium remained largely unchanged, indicating that although empagliflozin positively affects some cardiac parameters, structural heart changes might need more extended treatment to become apparent.

Our study further emphasizes the notable enhancements in LVEF across all ejection fraction categories, including HFrEF (EF ≤ 40%), HFmrEF (EF 41–49%), and HFpEF (EF ≥ 50%), observed in both diabetic and non-diabetic cohorts. Notably, diabetic patients with HFrEF exhibited a statistically significant increase in LVEF from 33.27 ± 5.16 to 35.49 ± 4.33 (p < 0.000), whereas non-diabetic patients demonstrated a similar improvement from 32.03 ± 4.95 to 34.35 ± 5.31 (p < 0.000). These results align with the findings of Shafiq et al. (2024), which indicate that empagliflozin enhances LVEF in patients diagnosed with HFrEF [35]. Beyond empagliflozin, evidence from a recent meta-analysis and network meta-analysis indicates that SGLT2 inhibitors consistently reduce heart failure hospitalization and cardiovascular mortality, incretin-based therapies improve glycemic control with additional cardiovascular benefits, and finerenone reduces renal and cardiovascular events in patients with chronic kidney disease and type 2 diabetes [36]. These findings contextualize our real-world results, supporting the complementary roles of these therapies in optimizing cardiorenal outcomes for heart failure patients.

Furthermore, both the HFmrEF and HFpEF groups demonstrated significant improvements, underscoring the extensive applicability of empagliflozin across different subtypes of heart failure. Additionally, Sciatti et al. (2022) documented similar benefits of empagliflozin in HFpEF, a subset of heart failure patients that has traditionally posed challenges [37]. Although improvement in left ventricular ejection fraction reached statistical significance, changes in ejection fraction alone may not fully capture clinically meaningful reverse remodeling. Contemporary heart failure guidelines emphasize functional status and natriuretic peptide levels, such as NYHA functional class and NT-proBNP, as complementary indicators of symptomatic and clinical benefit [38].

Subgroup analyses demonstrated that patients with over 10 years of diabetes history experienced greater improvements in both functional class and echocardiographic measures. These results align with McGuire et al. (2021) [30] and Zelniker et al. (2019) [13], which indicated that SGLT2 inhibitors tend to be more effective in patients with longstanding diabetes. Furthermore, all diabetic subgroups showed notable improvements in NYHA classification after six months of empagliflozin treatment, highlighting the value of early intervention in heart failure patients, particularly those with comorbid diabetes.

Although empagliflozin did not showed promising improvements in renal composite endpoints in this study, it was effective in slowing the decline in eGFR, particularly in patients with diabetes. This result is consistent with the findings of McGuire et al. (2021), who reported that SGLT2 inhibitors assist in preserving renal function in diabetic patients with heart failure [31]. The safety profile of empagliflozin, an SGLT2 inhibitor, indicates a low incidence of adverse effects, particularly urinary tract infections (UTIs).

Findings of this study suggest a favorable short-term safety profile of the intervention. Large randomized controlled trials have similarly reported low rates of genitourinary infections and volume depletion, particularly when patients are appropriately selected and counseled regarding hydration [39, 40]. Moreover, the low incidence of hypoglycemia observed in this cohort is consistent with prior evidence demonstrating that hypoglycemic events are rare and mainly associated with concomitant use of insulin or insulin secretagogues rather than the intervention itself [39, 41]. Overall, the rarity of these complications supports the tolerability and clinical safety of the therapy during the study period.

This study shows empagliflozin as an effective, well-tolerated treatment for heart failure in diabetic and non-diabetic patients, improving cardiovascular and renal health. It supports using SGLT2 inhibitors in clinical practice, especially in regions like Bangladesh with limited access to advanced therapies. Larger studies are needed to confirm these findings and assess long-term safety and efficacy across diverse groups.

Limitations

This observational, non-randomized study without a control group cannot establish causality, and its relatively homogeneous Bangladeshi population limits generalizability. The modest sample size and loss to follow-up reduced statistical power and may have introduced attrition bias, although baseline characteristics were similar between completers and non-completers. Convenience sampling, inadequate control of comorbidities and drug interactions, and reliance on surrogate markers (NYHA class, NT-proBNP, echocardiographic parameters) rather than hard clinical outcomes further limit clinical interpretation. The study included heart failure patients irrespective of etiology; therefore, the impact of underlying causes and cause-specific management (e.g., revascularization) was not assessed. The six-month follow-up also precludes evaluation of long-term efficacy and safety outcomes. Larger randomized controlled trials with longer follow-up are needed to validate these findings.

Conclusion

In this multicenter, real-world cohort of heart failure patients in Bangladesh, empagliflozin was associated with significant improvements in functional status, natriuretic peptide levels, hemodynamic parameters, renal indices, and echocardiographic measures, including left ventricular ejection fraction across HFrEF, HFmrEF, and HFpEF phenotypes. Benefits were observed in both diabetic and non-diabetic patients, with consistent improvement in NYHA class and favorable biomarker trajectories over six months. The therapy demonstrated a reassuring short-term safety profile, with low rates of hypoglycemia and genitourinary adverse effects.

These findings reinforce the translational effectiveness of SGLT2 inhibition in routine clinical practice and support the integration of empagliflozin into guideline-directed heart failure management. However, given the observational design, limited follow-up duration, and reliance on surrogate endpoints, larger randomized controlled trials with longer follow-up are warranted to confirm long-term clinical benefits and safety across diverse populations.

Recommendations

Empagliflozin represents a valuable cornerstone therapy for heart failure management in routine clinical practice across diverse patient subgroups. Evidence supports its initiation across all age groups, irrespective of body mass index, and suggests particular benefit in patients with a longer duration of diabetes.

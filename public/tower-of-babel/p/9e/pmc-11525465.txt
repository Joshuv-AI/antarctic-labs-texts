Development of diffuse cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma in situ after chemotherapy for ovarian carcinoma and remission with PD1-inhibitor
Authors: Jess Durbin, Taylor A Rives, Dava Piecoro, Charles S Dietrich
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Patient developed diffuse cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma in situ after one cycle of chemotherapy for ovarian cancer.•Addition of cemiplimab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy resulted in complete response of diffuse cutaneous SCC in situ.•Complete response of PDL1 + stage IV ovarian cancer at the time of surgery with addition of cemiplimab to neoadjuvant chemo.Keywords: High grade serous ovarian carcinoma, PD1-inhibitor, Cemiplimab, Immunotherapy, Bowen’s disease, Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma in situ

Introduction

This case is both interesting, in the development of cSCC in situ after chemotherapy, and promising, given her exceptional response to immunotherapy. There is no literature available regarding development or treatment of diffuse cSCC in situ after chemotherapy agents in patients being actively treated for ovarian cancer, but cSCC is well documented in transplant patients on chronic immunosuppression. One in twenty transplant patients on chronic immunosuppression will develop advanced cSCC (Bibee et al., 2020). In these patients, tumors are more aggressive with worse outcomes (Zavdy et al., 2023).

Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma in situ, Bowen’s disease, is typically identified as a scaly, well-demarcated patch or plaque, growing slowly over years in areas. The lesions are often asymptomatic or pruritic. Known risk factors for development of cSCC include sun exposure, chronic wounds and scars, HPV infections, certain genetic mutations, phenotypes, BRAF-inhibitor therapy, and certain immunosuppressants, commonly used in transplant patients (Matulonis et al., 2020). Treatment for advanced cSCC has involved platinum-based or EGFR chemotherapy agents in the past. In recent years, immunotherapy agents, such as cemiplimab, have become the first-line agents for immunocompromised patients, with a disease control rate of 91.4 % (Maubec, 2020).

Ovarian cancer is often diagnosed at a late stage and subsequently has a poor prognosis, with a 5-year overall survival of 50.8 % (SEER*Explorer, 2024). Guidelines recommend primary cytoreductive surgery in patients with resectable disease, however neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by interval cytoreductive surgery is recommended in patients who do not have completely resectable disease (Siminiak et al., 2022). The recurrence rate of ovarian cancer is high, estimated to be more than 80 % in those diagnosed at late stage (Garzon et al., 2020); therefore research efforts are focused on maintenance strategies to prevent or prolong recurrence. Immunotherapy agents, such as cemiplimab and pembrolizumab, show promise in other disease sites to prolong overall and progression free survival, however, trials have not demonstrated high rates of prolonged remission or response in ovarian cancers (Matulonis et al., 2020).

This report highlights a patient who developed diffuse cSCC in situ, Bowen’s disease, after one cycle of neoadjuvant chemotherapy for PDL1-positive stage IV high grade serous ovarian cancer. The addition of immunotherapy (cemiplimab) to standard chemotherapy (carboplatin and paclitaxel), resulted in complete resolution of both malignancies prior to cytoreductive surgery.

Case description

This is a 74-year-old female with past medical history significant for IgA deficiency and hypothyroidism, who presented with two weeks of progressively worsening chest pain and dyspnea on exertion. She was found to have a pleural effusion and underwent thoracentesis. Cytology of the pleural fluid was positive for adenocarcinoma of gynecologic origin. Positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET-CT) revealed extensive hypermetabolic adenopathy in the neck, chest, abdomen, and retroperitoneum and widespread peritoneal disease including splenic implants. Fine needle aspiration (FNA) of a supraclavicular lymph node confirmed stage IV peritoneal adenocarcinoma favoring gynecologic origin (Fig. 1). Molecular testing by CARIS revealed ​​PDL1 positivity with combined positive score of 5, tumor mutational burden low, loss of heterozygosity low, and mutations in TP53, AKT2, and CCNE1.

1a- Supraclavicular lymph node biopsy with high grade malignant cells with high nuclear to cytoplasmic ratios and prominent nucleoli. 1b- Immunohistochemistry with strong and diffuse p53 staining. The tumor also stained for PAX-8, WT-1, CK7 and p16, which is most compatible with Mullerian origin.

She initiated neoadjuvant chemotherapy with carboplatin and paclitaxel. Within 3 days of infusion, the patient developed a new diffuse painful, pruritic skin rash extending across her bilateral upper and lower extremities, as well as her trunk and back (Fig. 2). Because both chemotherapy agents can cause myelosuppression, it was unclear which agent was responsible. Consequently, both carboplatin and paclitaxel doses were decreased in the next cycle, however, the rash persisted. The patients absolute neutrophil count (ANC) nadir during cycle 1 was 1500, and 1100 during cycle 2, with excellent recovery by the day of next infusion. CA-125 was used as a tumor marker during her treatment, with levels of 98.1 and 29.0 following C1 and C2, respectively. The patient was evaluated by dermatology, where they biopsied lesions on her lower leg and upper arm (Fig. 3), both of which revealed cSCC in situ. The lesions were too diffuse to resect, therefore cemiplimab (PD-1 inhibitor) was added to cycle 3(8). Following cycle 3, CT imaging showed complete response of lymph node disease, partial response to peritoneal disease and a residual pleural effusion. Her skin lesions also significantly improved following only one treatment with cemiplimab. CA-125 level was 10.7 following cycle 3. She was given one additional cycle of cemiplimab alone in preparation for interval cytoreductive surgery and her cutaneous lesions had completely resolved. The patient underwent an exploratory laparotomy with total abdominal hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, and omentectomy with complete resection (R0). There was no gross evidence of disease during surgery and pathology confirmed no residual disease. CA-125 following cycle 4 was 6.7.

Diffuse skin rash developed after first cycle of carboplatin and paclitaxel.

Left lower leg lesion biopsy (3a) and left upper arm lesion biopsy (3b). There is full-thickness keratinocyte atypia and disorganization indicative of cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma in situ (3c). The degree of cytologic atypia in cSCC is much more striking than that of lichenoid keratosis (3d). Additional immunostaining including CK7, GATA-3, GCDFP-15, CK20, SATB2, CDX2, uroplakin III, p63, EMA, CK AE1/AE3, S100, SOX-10, and HMB-45 can be used to differentiate between alternative diagnoses, such as Paget disease, urothelial carcinoma metastasis, melanoma in situ, and Bowenoid papulosis.

She completed 3 additional cycles of chemotherapy after surgery with carboplatin, paclitaxel, and cemiplimab. She did have a treatment delay due to a transient ischemic attack and a dose reduction of carboplatin due to thrombocytopenia. The patient was evaluated by dermatology for these new cutaneous lesions to ensure this was not a recurrence of cSCC. Biopsies revealed benign lichenoid keratosis. Cemiplimab maintenance was planned to continue for 2 years; however, it was discontinued after five additional cycles due to persistent rash and pulmonary toxicity, concerning for pneumonitis. She continues to follow with pulmonology and dermatology for monitoring of these conditions. Recent CT imaging revealed no evidence of recurrence at 9 months following discontinuation of platinum chemotherapy.

Discussion

Early ovarian cancer is initially asymptomatic or presents with non-specific symptoms, so it is often diagnosed at later stages, which translates to a poorer prognosis for these patients. Cytoreductive surgery is the mainstay of treatment in patients with resectable ovarian cancer, but many patients require neoadjuvant chemotherapy prior to interval cytoreductive surgery, as in our patient (Siminiak et al., 2022). Even in those patients who undergo successful resection, patients are still at high risk of recurrence (Garzon et al., 2020). In recent years, focus has shifted to novel therapeutic options in the frontline setting to prevent recurrence and maintain remission. Cemiplimab is a human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that acts as a PD1-inhbitor, it has been used with success to treat skin and lung malignancies since FDA approval in 2018 (NCI, 2024). While PD1-inhibitors like cemiplimab have shown survival benefits in other cancer types, they have not shown great utility in recurrent ovarian cancers, despite tumor expression of PDL1 (Matulonis et al., 2020). Moore et al trialed atezolizumab (PD1 inhibitor) in combination with chemotherapy in the frontline setting for ovarian cancer and found no difference in progression free survival with the PD1 inhibitor in comparison to placebo, however overall survival data is immature (Moore et al., 2021). The AdoRN trial also implemented atezolizumab with chemotherapy and bevacizumab in the neoadjuvant setting and found no new safety signals final manuscript is pending at this time (Gaillard et al., 2021). There are several clinical trials evaluating immunotherapy in the neoadjuvant setting (Wanying Bao, 2023, ClinicalTrials.gov [Internet]., 2023).

While skin changes are a common and benign reaction to systemic chemotherapy, the development of skin cancer is rare. Prior to this case, there are no documented cases of patients developing diffuse cSCC in situ, Bowen’s disease, due to chemotherapy for treatment of ovarian cancer. However, a major risk factor for cSCC is immunosuppression. Development of cSCC in organ transplant patients, who are chronically immunosuppressed, has been well-documented, showingthat one in twenty patients will develop advanced cSCC following transplant (Bibee et al., 2020). The risk of these complications positively correlates with increased duration of treatment, higher doses of chemotherapy agents, and more intense treatment courses. Development of diffuse cSCC in situ, after a single treatment of chemotherapy is unexpected, as we would not expect significant immunosuppression so early in treatment with only mild neutropenia and excellent recovery. Typically, cSCC in situ is treated with local resection or radiation treatments, but in some patients, the lesions are too diffuse to resect. In these patients, systemic therapy is the best option. In recent years, PD1-inhibitors, like cemiplimab, and other immunotherapy agents have surpassed platinum-based chemotherapy agents to become the first-line treatment for diffuse cSCC, with trials showing disease control in 91.4 % of patients (Yakobson et al., 2023, Panel N. NCCN Guidelines, 2023). This was the basis for the adjunct treatment selection in our patient.

Conclusion

Development of diffuse cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma in situ after a single treatment of neoadjuvant chemotherapy for ovarian cancer is uncommon. Further, the prompt and complete response of both malignancies with the addition of cemiplimab, a PD1-inhibitor, to carboplatin and paclitaxel therapy has not previously been shared. Given the relative novelty of cemiplimab and other immunotherapy agents in neoadjuvant therapy for ovarian cancer, further investigation is needed to inform its use in a variety of contexts, but the response seen in this patient is encouraging for oncology patients in the future.

Integrated transcriptome and metabolome analysis of E17 embryonic pectoral muscle development regulated by 1 % CO2 during the first 10 days of incubation
Authors: Keyin Yu, Chao Song, Chang Liu, Weichao Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMaintaining CO₂ concentration at 1 % during the first 10 days of incubation represents a potential strategy for enhancing hatch quality. However, the effects of this specific CO₂ regulation strategy on embryonic skeletal muscle development have not yet been systematically evaluated. In this study, 400 fertilized Wod 188 broiler eggs were randomly divided into control and treatment groups. The control group was maintained under normal ventilation, with no CO2 introduced. The treatment group maintained the 1 % CO₂ concentration using dynamic carbon dioxide supplementation during the first 10 days of incubation. For the remainder of the incubation period, CO2 was not introduced, and normal ventilation was maintained. Phenotypic analysis revealed that 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days significantly increased myofiber density (P < 0.05) and decreased the myofiber area fraction (P < 0.001) in the E17 broiler embryonic pectoral muscle, compared to the control group. However, no significant differences (P > 0.05) were observed in myofiber diameter or cross-sectional area. Furthermore, transcriptome and metabolome analyses were performed using E17 pectoral muscle tissues from both the control and treatment groups. We identified 787 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and 45 differentially abundant metabolites (DAMs), enrichment analysis and two-way orthogonal partial least squares analysis (O2PLS) of these DEGs and DAMs indicated that the observed E17 pectoral muscle developmental differences may be mediated through alterations in the BMP signaling pathway, ECM-receptor interaction, motor proteins, cell cycle, and alpha-Linolenic acid metabolism pathways. Potential synergistic interactions among these pathways were also elucidated. Key candidate genes potentially underlying these effects include BMP7, BMP8A, GREM1, COL4A3, COL12A1, DLX6, RUNX2, SPP1, MMP13, MYH7, MYH1C, HBZ, CDC20, SMPD3, PLA2G4EL2, and PLA2G12B. Key metabolites likely involved are alpha-linolenic acid and various phospholipid molecular. In conclusion, this study demonstrates that 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation alters pectoral muscle development in E17 broiler embryos and the integrated transcriptome and metabolome analyses reveal underlying molecular mechanisms, providing new insights for incubation environment management.Keywords: Broiler embryo, CO2, Pectoral muscle, Transcriptome, Metabolome

Introduction

Chicken meat is widely favored by consumers due to its relatively low cost, low-fat content, culinary versatility, and broad acceptability across diverse religious and cultural contexts (Petracci et al., 2015; Wideman et al., 2016; Petracci, 2022). In recent decades, commercial broilers with rapid growth rates and high pectoral meat yields have been developed through genetic breeding programs (Zuidhof et al., 2014). However, these accelerated growth characteristics have also adversely affected muscle histoarchitecture, metabolism, and meat quality (Petracci et al., 2015; Karuppaiah et al., 2016, 2019). Consequently, there is a growing interest in methods that improve chicken meat quality while ensuring high productivity.

Skeletal muscle is primarily composed of myofibers, the contractile units whose histological properties are key determinants of meat quality (Joo et al., 2013; Ismail and Joo, 2017). The incubation period constitutes a critical phase in the avian life cycle, accounting for approximately one-third of the total lifespan in commercial white-feathered broiler production systems. Furthermore, an essential feature of avian pectoral muscle myofiber development is that the number of myofibers remains basically unchanged after hatching, post-hatch development is primarily characterized by myofiber hypertrophy (Stockdale and Miller, 1987). Consequently, embryonic myofiber development exerts significant effects on commercial broiler production outcomes. Moreover, the regulation of embryonic muscle development through the incubation environment has been an important direction of research in recent years. For instance, studies (Piestun et al., 2015; Choi et al., 2016) demonstrated that elevated incubation temperature enhances myoblast proliferation, leading to increased relative pectoral muscle weight in both embryos and post-hatch chicks. Also, green light exposure during incubation promotes satellite cell proliferation and muscle growth in embryos and post-hatch birds (Zhang et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2017; Bai et al., 2019). CO₂ represents another critical environmental factor influencing embryonic development, with specific developmental stages requiring distinct CO₂ concentrations.

Maintaining CO₂ concentrations at approximately 1 % during the first 10 days of embryonic development is a commonly used strategy in research investigating the impact of CO₂ on chick embryo development. Studies suggest that this regulatory approach may accelerate embryonic growth and influence physiological processes. For instance, progressively increasing CO₂ levels to approximately 1 % during the first 10 days has been shown to enhance embryonic development and shorten the hatch window (De Smit et al., 2006; Tona et al., 2007). Similarly, exposure to 0.7 %, 0.8 %, or 1.0 % CO₂ during the first 10 days of incubation reduced embryonic mortality rates between E18 and E21 and improved hatchability (Neto et al., 2023). These findings indicate that regulated CO₂ to 1 % during the first 10 days of incubation represents a potential strategy for enhancing hatch quality. However, the effects of this specific CO₂ regulation strategy on embryonic skeletal muscle development have not yet been systematically evaluated. Compared to the normal low CO₂ environment, exposure to 1 % CO₂ during the first 10 days of incubation on embryonic development is hypothesized to occur through enhanced protein acidification and stimulation of angiogenesis (Everaert et al., 2008; Willemsen et al., 2008; Fernandes et al., 2017; Tong et al., 2021). Furthermore, under standard conditions, Kunz et al. (2023) demonstrated that the gradual intrinsic release of CO₂ from the egg over time, leading to increased pH and reduced glycoprotein fiber content in the outer layer of the vitelline membrane (VM). This resulted in downregulation of VM tension and stiffness, facilitating stage-specific embryonic morphogenesis. Importantly, they also established that dysregulation of the VM can lead to body axis defects. Han et al. (2011) also observed that elevated CO₂ concentrations (≥2 %) during the first 4 days of incubation reduced embryonic somite number. Since all skeletal muscle originates from myogenic precursor cells originating in the mesodermal somites (Christ and Ordahl, 1995), these findings collectively suggest that pH alterations induced by early CO₂ regulation could significantly impact embryonic muscle development. Additionally, differences in vascular development owing to CO₂ regulation may be a factor influencing muscle development (Petracci et al., 2019). Collectively, these studies indicate that 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation may influence embryonic muscle development. However, here is no direct evidence has been published that CO₂ regulation during incubation effects embryonic muscle development.

Embryonic day 17 (E17) represents a critical point for pectoral muscle myofiber maturation in broilers. By this stage, the fusion of mononuclear myoblasts is largely complete, resulting in the formation of multinucleated myofibers, with subsequent (Bentzinger et al., 2012; Gu et al., 2022) growth primarily driven by hypertrophy. This indicates that both myofiber number and fundamental morphology are essentially established at E17 (Gu et al., 2024). Consequently, investigating myofiber developmental status and its regulatory mechanisms at E17 is crucial. The objective of this study is to investigate the effects of 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation on pectoral muscle myofiber development in E17 broiler embryos. In addition, we conducted transcriptome and metabolome analyses on pectoral muscle tissue to identify tissue-specific alterations in gene expression and metabolite profiles within E17 pectoral muscle resulting from 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation. Furthermore, an integrated analysis of the transcriptome and metabolome was performed to elucidate correlations between metabolites and genes, to provide further information for how CO₂ environmental regulation affects embryonic muscle development.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

The study proposal was approved by The Laboratory Animal Ethical Committee of China Agricultural University (AW60305202-6-01). Experiments were conducted in accordance with the approved guidelines.

Experimental design and sample collection

Fertilized eggs from 38-week-old birds (Wod 188, fast, large, white-feathered broiler) were provided by Beijing Huadu Yukou Poultry Industry Co. Ltd. 400 eggs were randomly divided into the control and treatment groups and weighed individually. Using the method employed by Liu et al. (2022), eggs in each group were rinsed with slightly acidic electrolytic water with an effective chlorine concentration of 150 mg/L and then placed into two identical automated chamber incubators (Brinsea OvaEasy 380 Advance EX Series II, Somerset, UK; capacity: 384 eggs) for incubation. Eggs from both the treatment and control groups were placed at identical spatial positions within their respective incubators to minimize positional effects. The CO₂ level in the treatment incubator was maintained at the setpoint (1 % CO₂) using an automated feedback control system mentioned in Liu et al. (2022). The system consisted of a CO₂ sensor (Vaisala GMP251, Helsinki, FI; accuracy: ± 0.1 % CO₂) connected to a programmable logic controller (ZK2N-20MR-4AD-2DA, Feimasi Technology, Shenzhen, CNH), which actuated a solenoid valve on a 99.999 % pure CO₂ cylinder to add gas as needed. When CO₂ was introduced, the automatic incubator automatically adjusted the ventilation, and the air was mixed with fans in the incubator. For the remainder of the incubation period, we did not introduce CO₂ into the treatment incubator and maintained normal ventilation. The control incubator was equipped with an identical sensor for monitoring purposes only and was not connected to the gas supplementation system, ensuring normal ventilation conditions. The same temperature and humidity settings were maintained for the control and treatment groups, with the incubation temperature set at 37.8°C, and the relative humidity set at 50 %. The incubator turned the eggs hourly at an angle of 90°.

At E17, 20 eggs were randomly selected from each group for dissection after euthanasia through cervical dislocation. The embryos were weighed after drying the surface of the embryos with absorbent paper to obtain the yolk-free embryo weight (YFBM). Embryonic pectoral muscle without bones were separated and weighed. The separated pectoral muscles were partly immersed in 4 % paraformaldehyde (PFA) solution for histological analysis and partly left in liquid nitrogen to be snap frozen and then kept at −80°C until for transcriptome and metabolome analyses.

H&E staining

8 pectoral muscle samples were selected respectively from the control and treatment groups for histological analysis. After fixing fresh pectoral muscle tissue in 4 % PFA solution, the specimens were rinsed in distilled water to remove residual fixative and dehydrated in a graded ethanol series. Dehydrated specimens were infiltrated with liquid paraffin for several hours, depending on the muscle section characteristics, and then embedded in paraffin. Four-micrometer sections were prepared and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) using paraffin slicers (Leica RM 2135, Nussloch, DE). Each histological section was stained with H&E and used to estimate myofiber diameter, myofiber cross-sectional area, myofiber density, and myofiber area fraction using an optical microscope with brightfield illumination (Optec BK-FL4, Chongqing, CHN) equipped with a microscope image collecting and processing software (OPTPro 3000 series, Chongqing, CHN). For each section, three fields of view without any signs of tissue destruction or damage were captured. Within each field, three regions of interest were selected within a single muscle fascicle for the measurement of myofiber diameter, cross-sectional area, and density. The myofiber area fraction was calculated for the entire field as the ratio of the H&E-positive area to the total area of the output image.

Total RNA extraction and RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq)

Total RNA was extracted from the pectoral muscles of 3 samples using the Trizol method. Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, California, USA) was used to assess the integrity of the total RNA, and the samples were considered eligible for further analysis when RNA Integrity Number (RIN) > 7. Total RNA extracted in this extraction met the requirements. Poly(A)+ mRNA was enriched from total RNA using Oligo dT magnetic beads. mRNA obtained was randomly interrupted with divalent cations in fragmentation buffer. First strand cDNA was synthesized using random hexamer primer and M-MuLV Reverse Transcriptase (RNase H). Second strand cDNA synthesis was subsequently performed using DNA Polymerase I and RNase H. The purified double-stranded cDNA was end-repaired, A-tailed, and ligated to the sequencing connector, and the cDNA of 370–420 bp was screened using AMPure XP beads for polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification. Next, the PCR product was purified again using AMPure XP beads to obtain the final library. The quality of the library was assessed using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, California, USA), and the library was sequenced using an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 sequencing platform, generating 150 bp paired-end reads.

Transcriptome analysis

Reads containing junctions, reads containing ploy-N, and low-quality reads were removed from the raw data by processing using FASTP software (v0.23.4) to obtain clean reads. Clean reads were compared with the reference genome (National Center for Biotechnology Information [NCBI] Gallus gallus GCF_016699485.2 bGalGal1. matbroiler. GRCg7b) using HISAT2 software (v2.0.5) to obtain information on the localization of reads on the reference genome. Reference-guided transcriptome assembly was performed using StringTie (v1.3.3b), and after assembly, featureCounts (v1.5.0-p3) was used to calculate the number of reads mapped to each gene. The FPKM was then calculated for each gene based on the length of the gene, and the expression level mapped to that gene was calculated. Differential gene expression analysis between the groups was performed using DESeq2 R (v1.20.0). Genes were selected as differentially expressed genes (DEGs) when they adhered to the following criteria: P < 0.05 and |log2(foldchange)| > 1.

Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses of DEGs were performed using ClusterProfiler (v3.8.1), and terms and pathways with P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using GSEA (v4.3.2) (http://www.broadinstitute.org/gsea/index.jsp), and pathways with |normalized enrichment score |(NES)| > 1, P < 0.05, and FDR < 0.25 were considered statistically significant.

Validation of DEGs using RT-qPCR

To validate the RNA-seq results, 8 DEGs were randomly selected for real-time quantitative PCR analysis. The extracted RNA was used for both RNA-seq analysis and RT-qPCR. Primers were designed using the NCBI Primer Design Tool, and primer information shown in Table S1. RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher, Massachusetts, USA), and qPCR testing was performed using the FastStart Universal SYBR Green Master (Rox) (Roche, Massachusetts, USA) on the Archimed quantitative PCR detection system (Hongshi SLAN-96P, Shanghai, CHN). The relative expression levels of DEGs were calculated using the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method. All reactions were performed with three biological replicates and two technical replicates.

Metabolite extraction and LC-MS detection

For each group of 6 samples, 100 mg of pectoral muscle tissue were ground in liquid nitrogen and placed in EP tubes, to which 500 μL of 80 % methanol in water was added. The mixture was vortexed and left to stand in an ice bath for 5 min, and then centrifuged at 15,000 g for 20 min at 4°C. The supernatant was taken and diluted with mass spectrometry-grade water until the methanol concentration was 53 %. Next, QC samples (equal-volume mixtures of experimental samples) were prepared for balancing the chromatography-mass spectrometry system and monitoring the instrument status during sample processing.

LC-MS detection was performed on a chromatograph (Thermo Fisher Vanquish UHPLC, Bremen, DE) configured with a column (Hypesil Gold column, 100 × 2.1 mm, 1.9 μm), with the column temperature maintained at 40°C, flow rate of 0.2 mL/min. The eluents for the positive and negative polarity modes were eluent A (0.1 % formic acid in water) and eluent B (Methanol). The solvent gradient was set as follows: 2 % B, 1.5 min; 2–85 % B, 3 min; 85–100 % B, 10 min; 100–2 % B, 10.1 min; 2 % B, 12 min.

Metabolites flowed out from the chromatographic column and then entered the mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Q Exactive™ HF-X, Bremen, DE). The mass spectrometer ionized the metabolites by using the electrospray ionization (ESI) method. Separation and detection were performed using the mass-to-charge ratio (m/z), and metabolites with m/z ranging 100–1,500 could be detected. The settings for the ESI source included a spray voltage of 3.5 kV, a sheath gas flow rate of 35 psi, an auxiliary gas flow rate of 10 L/min, a temperature of the ion transfer tube of 320°C, an ion introduction radio frequency level of 60, a temperature of the auxiliary gas heater of 350°C, a polarity of both positive and negative, and the MS/MS second-stage scanning adopted a data-dependent scanning strategy.

Metabolome analysis

The data files generated by UHPLC-MS/MS were processed by XCMS to perform peak alignment, peak picking, and quantitation for each metabolite. Then, based on adduct ions and setting mass deviation to 10 ppm, a comparison was made between these data and the high-quality secondary spectrum database to obtain results for metabolite identification. Simultaneously, blank samples are used to remove background ions. The original quantitative results were normalized by the following formula to obtain relative peak areas: Relative peak areas = Raw quantitative value of samples / (Sum of quantitative value of samples/Sum of quantitative value of QC); subsequently, compounds with a coefficient of variation of the relative peak area in QC samples greater than 30 % are deleted. Finally, the identification results and relative quantification results of metabolites are obtained.

The metabolites detected in both positive and negative polarities were combined for analysis. Moreover, the metabolomic data were subjected to principal component analysis (PCA) and partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) through metaX to observe the differences between the groups and the relationships among metabolites more intuitively. The thresholds for identifying differentially abundant metabolites (DAMs) were set as follows: variable importance in projection (VIP) > 1, P < 0.05, and fold change (FC) > 1.20 or < 0.83. The VIP score, derived from the first principal component in the PLS-DA model, was used to evaluate the contribution of each metabolite to group separation. The differential metabolites were annotated using the KEGG compound database (http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/compound/) and then mapped to the KEGG pathway database (http://www.kegg.jp/kegg/pathway.html).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using the SPSS software package (v27.0). Data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. Phenotypic data were compared using Student’s t-test, differences were considered significant at P < 0.05, highly significant at P < 0.01, and extremely significant at P < 0.001. The O2PLS integration analysis was performed exclusively on the 3 matched sample pairs for which both transcriptome and metabolome data were available.

Results

The effect of 1 % CO2 regulation during the first 10 days of incubation on embryonic development and pectoral muscle myofiber morphology of E17 broiler embryo

As shown in the Table 1, the egg weights of the treatment and control groups were 62.15 ± 2.19 and 62.18 ± 2.25 g, with no statistical difference (P = 0.877). At E17, no statistically significant differences were observed between the treatment and control groups for YFBM, pectoral muscle weight, and relative pectoral muscle weight at E17 (P > 0.05). The above results indicate that 1 % CO2 regulation during the first 10 days of incubation did not significantly affect overall embryo development at E17. The morphological examination of HE-stained pectoral muscle sections (Fig. 1A and 1B) revealed differences in pectoral muscle myofiber histological properties between the treatment and control groups at E17 (Table 1 and Fig. 1C). There was no statistically significant difference in myofiber diameter or cross-sectional area between the treatment and control groups (P > 0.05). The myofiber density was significantly increased in the treatment group relative to that in the control group (P = 0.021), reaching approximately 115.8 % of the control value. The myofiber area fraction of the treatment group was extremely significantly lower than that of the control group (P < 0.001), representing approximately 74.8 % of the control value.

Analysis of DEGs

Following poly(A)+ mRNA enrichment and library construction, an average of 46,438,448 raw reads and 44,620,884 clean reads were obtained per sample. In the clear reads of samples, 74.88–78.77 % were aligned to the reference genome. The quality values of Q20 and Q30—representing the percentage of bases with sequencing error rate below 1 % and 0.1 %, respectively—averaged 98.53 and 95.92, indicating that the transcriptome data was of high quality and suitable for further analysis. Specific sample quality index data are shown in Table S2.

The PCA results showed a separation between the layers of the treatment and control groups (Fig. 2A). Using thresholds of |log2(FC)| > 1 and P < 0.05, a total of 296 up-regulated and 491 down-regulated DEGs were detected (Fig. 2B). Meanwhile, clustering analysis of the DEGs (Fig. 2C) showed that gene expression patterns clustered within the treatment group and differed significantly between the treatment and control groups.

To validate the expression profiles obtained from RNA-seq, we randomly selected eight DEGs and performed RT-qPCR analysis using the same RNA samples. The results showed that the expression levels of the eight DEGs obtained using RT-qPCR were similar to those obtained using RNA-seq (Fig. 2D), indicating that the transcriptome data were highly reliable.

Enrichment analysis of DEGs

To further elucidate the molecular characteristics of these DEGs, we performed GO functional enrichment analysis, classifying these DEGs into the three GO categories: biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). A total of 343 GO terms were significantly enriched (P < 0.05) in the treatment group compared to the control group, of which 310 were related to BP, 13 to CC, and 20 to MF. Fig. 3A shows the top ten significantly enriched terms in each of the three categories. In the BP category, DEGs were predominantly associated with processes including cell adhesion, tissue development, cell proliferation, cell differentiation, muscle structure development, and BMP signaling pathway. In the CC category, DEGs were primarily enriched in components such as the extracellular region, extracellular matrix (ECM), and plasma membrane. In the MF category, DEGs showed significant enrichment for functions related to calcium ion binding, organic acid binding, and oxygen binding. The enrichment of these GO terms indicates that 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days can effect myofiber structural development, cell proliferation and differentiation activities, BMP signaling, the composition of the ECM and plasma membrane, and processes involving calcium ion and oxygen binding in E17 pectoral muscle, which may lead to the difference in muscle development.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of the DEGs identified 98 enriched KEGG pathways. Fig. 3B shows the top 20 significant KEGG pathways, among which 15 pathways, including ECM-receptor interaction, TGF-beta signaling pathway, motor proteins, and alpha-linolenic acid metabolism, were significantly enriched (P < 0.05). Additionally, KEGG enrichment analysis based on GSEA revealed a single significantly enriched pathway, the cell cycle (Fig. 3C), which exhibited a positive NES.

Identification and analysis of DAMs

In this study, metabolomic analysis was performed on pectoral muscles samples of E17 from the control and treatment groups. The metabolic profiles obtained using LC-MS were plotted using PCA and the results showed poor sample clustering within groups (Fig. S1). Further analysis using PLS-DA showed a separation trend was observed between the control and treatment groups (Fig. S2). In addition, the permutation test verified the accuracy of the PLS-DA model, with Q2 intercept < 0.05 indicates that the model is not overfitted (Fig. S3), and DAMs can be screened and analyzed based on the PLS-DA model.

Based on the PLS-DA model, differential metabolites were screened by setting thresholds VIP > 1, P < 0.05 and FC > 1.20 or < 0.83, and as shown in the volcano plots (Fig. 4A), we identified 29 up-regulated DAMs and 16 down-regulated DAMs in the treatment group compared to the control group. The DAMs were categorized as shown in Fig. 4B, the top three metabolite classes were lipid and lipid-like molecules (64.44 %), organic acids and their derivatives (17.78 %), and organic heterocyclic compounds (6.67 %). Hierarchical clustering was performed on all DAMs to better elucidate the relationship between samples and the metabolite expression profiles among different samples (Fig. 4C). For example, PS (16:0/18:2 (9Z,12Z)), SM (d18:0/16:1 (9Z)), and PE (18:1 (11Z)/20:4 (5Z.8Z.11Z.14Z)) exhibited similar expression patterns.

Integrated analysis of transcriptome and metabolome

To investigate the correlation between the transcriptome and metabolome, O2PLS was applied to the datasets derived from the 3 paired samples. The O2PLS model yielded component scores with R²Xcorr = 0.801 and R²Ycorr = 0.906, indicating high explanatory power for the total variation in the transcriptome and metabolome datasets, respectively. Fig. S4 presents the O2PLS loading plots for the DEGs and DAMs. Genes and metabolites positioned farther from the origin exhibit stronger associations within and between the datasets. The top 20 DEGs and DAMs, based on their loading magnitudes, were selected to generate joint loading plots integrating the transcriptome and metabolome datasets (Fig. 5A). The joint loading of the transcriptome shows that DEGs such as BMP8A, TNMD, DLX6, SPP1, and HBZ have a relatively high correlation with the metabolome data. The joint loading of the metabolome shows that DAMs such as PC(15:0/18:2(9Z,12Z)), PC(18:0/20:4(8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z)), SM(d18:0/16:1(9Z)), PG(18:2(9Z,12Z)/18:1(11Z)), PE(18:2(9Z,12Z)/18:0), (6Z)-Hexadecenoylcarnitine, and (8Z)-10-Hydroxyhexadecenoylcarnitine exhibit relatively high correlations with the transcriptome data.

Furthermore, we conducted an integrated analysis of the transcriptome and metabolome using KEGG pathway enrichment. A venn diagram (Fig. 5B) was used to identify the number of KEGG pathways enriched by both DEGs and DAMs. This analysis revealed ten co-enriched pathways. Fig. 5C illustrates the significance levels of these co-enriched pathways, along with the number of associated DEGs and DAMs contributing to the enrichment of each pathway. The co-enriched pathways include alpha-Linolenic acid metabolism, arachidonic acid metabolism, necroptosis, sphingolipid metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism, and so on. Notably, only alpha-Linolenic acid metabolism demonstrated significant enrichment in both the transcriptome and metabolome datasets, highlighting its importance in the response to CO₂ regulation. Discussion

This study investigated the effects of 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation on pectoral muscle development in E17 broiler embryos. Pre-hatch 1 % CO₂ regulation over the first 10 days did not affect embryonic body weight at late incubation stage, consistent with previous reports (Tong et al., 2021; Neto et al., 2023). Notably, it exerted no significant impact on pectoral muscle weight, relative weight, or the diameter and cross-sectional area of myofibers. However, the treatment group exhibited a significant increase in myofiber density and a reduction in the myofiber area fraction. It suggests a potential structural difference between groups where the packing of fibers within fascicles becomes tighter (increased density), while the overall tissue architecture comprises a greater relative area of inter-fascicular connective tissue (decreased area fraction). These findings indicate that this regulatory intervention influenced pectoral myofiber development in E17 embryos. To elucidate the underlying mechanisms, we conducted integrated transcriptome and metabolome analyses. The transcriptome revealed 787 DEGs and the metabolome showed 45 DAMs, which were subjected to comprehensive analysis.

Through GO, KEGG, and GSEA enrichment analyses of the DEGs, we identified the BMP signaling, ECM-receptor interaction, motor proteins, and cell cycle pathways as potentially critical for mediating the effects of 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation on E17 myofiber histological properties. The BMP signaling pathway was significantly enriched in GO terms. Furthermore, DEGs enriched in the TGF-beta signaling pathway from the transcriptome analysis were also associated with BMP. Recent studies have established the significant role of BMP signaling in muscle development (Winbanks et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2019). In the treatment group, expression of both BMP7/BMP8A and the BMP antagonists GREM1/GREM2 was upregulated. O2PLS analysis revealed a strong association between BMP8A and DAMs. Notably, BMP7 and BMP8A belong to the 60A subfamily and exhibit functional similarity (Kishigami and Mishina, 2005). BMP7 can prevent activated satellite cells and proliferating myoblasts from exiting the cell cycle, thereby expanding the satellite cell progenitor pool and delaying terminal differentiation of myofibers (Friedrichs et al., 2011; Abdel-Raouf et al., 2021). In agreement with this, Wang et al. (2010) showed that BMP signaling promotes an increase in the number of satellite cells in E18 chicken embryos. Concurrently, BMP signaling may induce GREM expression (Pereira et al., 2000), thereby creating a localized negative feedback loop to modulate BMP activity. BMP and GREM likely function in distinct muscle compartments. Frank et al. (2006) showed that in skeletal muscle, BMP signaling promotes proliferation in undifferentiated progenitor cell populations, whereas GREM expression in mature myofibers and interstitial cells counteracts BMP stimulation to prevent uncontrolled proliferation, thereby maintaining the balance between proliferation and differentiation during embryonic myogenesis. Furthermore, Wang et al (2010) indicated that BMP-responsive satellite cells preferentially reside near the myotendinous junction, the primary site for longitudinal myofiber growth (Zhang and Mclennan, 1995). Notably, O2PLS analysis identified TNMD, a DEG strongly associated with DAMs, which plays a crucial role in tenocyte proliferation, maturation, and differentiation (Xu et al., 2021; He et al., 2022). Therefore, the altered fine-tuning of BMP signaling and its antagonists in the embryonic pectoral muscle of the treatment group may potentially prolong the satellite cell proliferative phase, alter myofiber developmental progression, and coordinate tendon development to support myofiber assembly.

The enrichment of terms such as extracellular matrix and extracellular region in the GO analysis, along with the significant enrichment of the ECM-receptor interaction pathway in the KEGG analysis, collectively indicate that alterations in ECM-related pathways may be an important mechanism underlying the observed differences in muscle development between the groups. The ECM constitutes the predominant component of the connective tissue framework in skeletal muscle, permeating the endomysium, perimysium, and epimysium and serving to provide structural support (Jarvinen and T., 2005; Grounds, 2008). The basement membrane, primarily composed of type IV collagen (Kovanen, 2002), surrounds each individual myofiber (Purslow, 2010). In this study, the downregulated expression of COL4A3 and COL4A4 in the treatment group was shown to alter basement membrane properties, potentially contributing to the observed changes in myofiber histological properties. Conversely, the upregulated DEG COL12A1 encodes type XII collagen, which possesses specialized domains (Sinkeviciute et al., 2024). Type XII collagen can integrate within type I collagens, which are the most abundant and widespread collagens in skeletal muscle ECM. These molecules form bridges between adjacent collagen fibrils, influencing cell shape and intercellular communication (Nishiyama et al., 1994), thereby broadly modulating ECM organization and function. Furthermore, the ECM itself acts as a critical regulator of BMP signaling (Sedlmeier and Sleeman, 2017; Migliorini et al., 2020), indicating that ECM modulation represents a potential pathway for BMP-mediated regulation of muscle development. The upregulated DEGs DLX5/DLX6 and RUNX2 may serve as key molecular bridges connecting the two pathways. DLX5 is a crucial component linking BMP signaling to the downstream transcription factor RUNX2, with potential functional redundancy between DLX5 and DLX6 (Lee et al., 2003; Mullen et al., 2022). RUNX2, in turn, induces the expression of ECM-regulating genes such as SPP1 and MMP13 (Komori, 2022). In addition, O2PLS analysis confirmed strong associations between the DEGs DLX6/SPP1 and DAMs. Osteopontin, which is encoded by SPP1, promotes myoblast attachment to the ECM and supports their terminal differentiation into new myofibers (Uaesoontrachoon et al., 2008). MMP13, a metalloproteinase, degrades type I and type III collagens (Knäuper et al., 1996), facilitating the migration of satellite cells towards myofibers (Smith et al., 2020). This indicates that altered expressions of SPP1 and MMP13 may functionally cooperate with BMP signaling effects. The significant enrichment of the ECM-receptor interaction pathway and the observed alterations in related DEGs indicate dynamic remodeling of the ECM of the pectoral muscle. This remodeling likely constitutes a key mechanism underlying the changes in E17 pectoral myofiber histological properties induced by 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation. Supportively, recent research also demonstrates that CO₂-induced reductions in extracellular pH can enhance the production of ECM components in skin tissue (Takano et al., 2023).

Notably, the motor proteins pathway was also enriched. Motor proteins are essential for muscle contraction, organelle transport, and mechanotransduction in skeletal muscle. In the treatment group, expression of the slow-twitch fiber marker gene MYH7 (Schiaffino, 2018) was upregulated, whereas expression of the fast-twitch fiber marker genes MYH1C and MYH1F (both are avian isoforms of MYH1) (Schiaffino, 2018) was downregulated. This indicates that 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation may have influenced fiber type specification in the E17 pectoral muscle, inducing a shift toward slow-twitch fiber differentiation in the treatment group. Supportively, transcutaneous CO₂ therapy has been shown to promote angiogenesis and facilitate the conversion of fast-twitch to slow-twitch fibers in skeletal muscle (Oe et al., 2011; Inoue et al., 2020). Studies indicate that CO₂ regulation during incubation positively impacts embryonic vascularization (Fernandes et al., 2017). Supporting this, the upregulation of the differentially expressed gene HBZ, identified via O2PLS analysis, was consistent with the enrichment of the oxygen binding GO term. HBZ is a hemoglobin subunit involved in oxygen sensing, transport, and storage (Terwilliger, 1998). It is well established that slow-twitch fibers possess a denser capillary network compared to fast-twitch fibers. Therefore, enhanced vascular development induced by CO₂ regulation could potentially influence capillary development fiber type formation in E17. This may promote blood circulation and oxygen supply to postnatal muscle, potentially reducing the incidence of myopathies, a hypothesis that requires further investigation. Changes in motor protein pathways may be influenced by the effect of BMP on muscle cell differentiation and could synergize with ECM changes. Notably, all three pathways are responsive to mechanical load. Chronic mechanical loading is well known to promote slow-twitch fiber formation, enhance BMP expression (Da Silva Madaleno et al., 2020), and stimulate type XII collagen production (Flück et al., 2000). Moreover, muscles with COL12A1 knockout exhibited an increase in fast-twitch fibers accompanied by a decrease in slow-twitch fibers, along with enhanced tissue elasticity and muscle weakness (Zou et al., 2014). Significantly, Kunz et al. (2023) demonstrated that CO₂ can modulate the mechanical tension of the VM early in embryonic development. Therefore, 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation may conceivably have altered the mechanical load experienced by the embryonic pectoral muscle, with effects persisting until E17.

Significant enrichment of the GO terms cell proliferation and cell differentiation also indicates altered cellular proliferation and differentiation activities within the pectoral muscle. Furthermore, GSEA-based KEGG pathway analysis revealed significant enrichment of the cell cycle pathway. Within this pathway, CDC20 was identified as a DEG. The protein encoded by CDC20 primarily functions to coordinate mitotic progression by promoting the ordered degradation of APC/C substrates during mitosis (Kapanidou et al., 2017), thereby regulating the cell cycle. Collectively, these enriched pathways likely provide evidence for the effects of the BMP signaling and motor proteins pathways on muscle cell proliferation and differentiation activities.

Through the identification and analysis of DAMs, we discovered that lipid metabolism may represent a potential mechanism influencing muscle development between groups, whether this link extends to postpartum meat quality attributes (e.g., tenderness, water-holding capacity) remains to be established, and further investigation would be necessary to confirm any causal relationship. In biological systems, lipids function as structural components of cellular membranes, signaling molecules, and energy sources. The DAMs identified in this study encompassed a diverse array of phospholipid molecules, including phosphatidylcholine (PC), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), phosphatidylserine (PS), phosphatidylglycerol (PG), phosphatidic acid (PA), and sphingomyelin (SM). Furthermore, the KEGG pathways glycerophospholipid metabolism and sphingolipid metabolism were enriched in both transcriptome and metabolome analyses. This indicates that 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation significantly impacts phospholipid metabolism in the E17 pectoral muscle. PC and PE constitute major components of cellular membranes, accounting for 45–55 % and 15–25 % of total cellular phospholipids, respectively (Vance and Steenbergen, 2005). They play crucial roles in the cell cycle, influencing cell proliferation and division (Emoto and Umeda, 2000; Ridgway, 2013). PS comprises 2–10 % of total cellular phospholipids (Vance and Steenbergen, 2005) and is the most abundant anionic phospholipid in eukaryotic cells. Along with PE, PS is predominantly localized to the inner layer of the plasma membrane or cytoplasmic leaflet, contributing to the maintenance of membrane asymmetry (Kay and Fairn, 2019; Chakrabarti, 2021). PG is primarily located in mitochondrial membranes, where its levels influence mitochondrial morphology and function (Pokorná et al., 2016). PA serves as a key intermediate in lipid metabolism, broadly influencing the synthesis of other phospholipids (Stace and Ktistakis, 2006), and modulates membrane fission and fusion through multiple mechanisms (Zhukovsky et al., 2019). SM accounts for 5–10 % of total cellular phospholipids (Vance and Steenbergen, 2005). It participates in the formation of membrane microdomains (such as lipid rafts, caveolae, and clathrin-coated pits) and regulates transmembrane signaling (Vance and Steenbergen, 2005). The DEG SMPD3, located within the sphingolipid metabolism pathway, encodes sphingomyelin phosphodiesterase 3. This enzyme is localized to the plasma membrane and hydrolyzes SM (Taniguchi and Okazaki, 2014), thereby regulating sphingolipid metabolism (Khavandgar and Murshed, 2015). These observed metabolic alterations in the cellular membrane system likely underpin the differences in cellular activities such as proliferation and differentiation in the pectoral muscle, thereby supporting the findings discussed previously.

Integrated analysis of transcriptome and metabolome highlighted the significance of the alpha-Linolenic acid metabolism pathway, which was the only KEGG pathway significantly enriched in both datasets. As shown in Fig. 5D, the DAMs on this pathway, PC(15:0/18:2(9Z,12Z)), ALA, and SDA were all increased. Both ALA and SDA are omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (n-3 PUFAs), with ALA serving as the rate-limiting precursor for SDA synthesis (Saini et al., 2021). The DEGs PLA2G4EL2 and LOC771552 belong to the phospholipase A2 (PLA2) family. PLA2 enzymes hydrolyze the fatty acyl chain at the sn-2 position of PC, releasing free fatty acids (Murakami, 2019). Consequently, the increased expression of PLA2G4EL2 and LOC771552 may promote the liberation of ALA. Another DEG on this pathway, PLA2G12B, is a catalytically inactive member of the PLA2 family (Rouault et al., 2003), and its downregulated expression may impact lipid transport and remodeling (Thierer et al., 2024). Structurally, n-3 PUFAs can incorporate into cellular membranes, increasing membrane fluidity and influencing lipid raft formation (Holte et al., 1995; Yaqoob and Shaikh, 2010), thereby modulating the perception of environmental signals and cell communication (Los and Murata, 2004). Regarding energy metabolism, n-3 PUFAs may enhance fatty acid (FA) metabolism and stimulate mitochondrial biogenesis (Lanza et al., 2013). In this study, three upregulated long-chain acylcarnitines (9Z,12Z)-3-Hydroxyhexadecadienoylcarnitine, (8Z)-10-Hydroxyhexadecenoylcarnitine, and (6Z)-Hexadecenoylcarnitine were identified as DAMs. These compounds primarily originate from mitochondria (Dambrova et al., 2022) and function to facilitate the transport of long-chain FAs into mitochondria for β-oxidation to generate energy (Reuter and Evans, 2012). The elevation in long-chain acylcarnitines indicates robust mitochondrial FA metabolism and potential incomplete FA oxidation. Furthermore, long-chain acylcarnitines actively participate in regulating carbohydrate and lipid metabolism (Makrecka et al., 2014; Liepinsh et al., 2017; Blackburn et al., 2020). For avian embryos, the primary energy source during incubation is the lipid-rich egg yolk. By E17, the embryo enters the late incubation phase with heightened energy demands. The observed metabolic differences in the pectoral muscle at this stage likely support alterations in muscle fiber histological properties. Furthermore, in skeletal muscle, ALA may also alter the ability of muscle cell activity either directly by changing proliferation and differentiation or indirectly by modifying the ECM (Tachtsis et al., 2018; Jannas-Vela et al., 2023). For instance, Fiaccavento et al. (2010) demonstrated that an ALA-enriched diet increases satellite cell proliferation and differentiation, while improving muscle structure. Rieger et al. (2024) performed lipidomic analysis of murine fast- and slow-twitch muscles and reported higher levels of ALA and its derivatives in slow-twitch fibers, indicating that these lipids may influence fiber type. Simard et al. (2022) reported that in vitro supplementation of dermal cells with ALA altered ECM composition. As for the meat quality, 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation modulate the alpha-linolenic acid metabolism pathway in E17 embryos, which may subsequently affect the composition of unsaturated fatty acids, muscle fiber structure, antioxidant capacity, tenderness, and flavor of muscle tissue (Tang et al., 2020; Arjin et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2025), a hypothesis that requires future validation.

Conclusions

In summary, this study analyzed the effect of 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation on pectoral muscle development in E17 broiler embryos. The CO₂-treated group exhibited a significantly higher myofiber density and a significantly lower myofiber area fraction than the control group. Integrated transcriptome and metabolome analysis revealed several potentially influential pathways, including BMP signaling, ECM-receptor interaction, motor proteins, cell cycle, and alpha-Linolenic acid metabolism. The 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation likely modulated the BMP signaling pathway, ECM-receptor interaction, and motor proteins pathways, thereby influencing myofiber development. Concurrently, alterations in the cell cycle pathway and phospholipid metabolism revealed differences in cellular activities such as proliferation and differentiation. These changes provide evidence supporting the roles of the aforementioned pathways in modulating muscle cell behavior. The alpha-Linolenic acid metabolism pathway has been shown to influence energy metabolism and muscle cell activity in the pectoral muscle, contributing to the observed differences in myofiber development. Key regulatory factors identified include the genes such as BMP7, BMP8A, GREM1, COL4A3, COL12A1, DLX6, RUNX2, SPP1, MMP13, MYH7, MYH1C, HBZ, CDC20, SMPD3, PLA2G4EL2, and PLA2G12B, along with metabolites such as ALA and various phospholipid molecules. This investigation provides insights into the effects and underlying mechanisms of 1 % CO₂ regulation during the first 10 days of incubation on pectoral muscle development at E17. However, the specific mechanism of action requires further study and verification.

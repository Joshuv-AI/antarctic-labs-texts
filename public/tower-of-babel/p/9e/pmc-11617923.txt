The spatial differentiation characteristics and influencing mechanisms of intangible cultural heritage in China
Authors: Qiangsheng Hu, Peihong Yang, Jiajun Ma, Meixia Wang, Xiaorong He
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Intangible cultural heritage represents a cultural evolution shaped by human responses to adapting and transforming environments. Unveiling the characteristics and patterns of its spatial distribution can offer a more scientific foundation for the protection of intangible cultural heritage and the development of heritage tourism. By using ArcGIS software and Geo-detector, the regional differentiation characteristics and influencing mechanisms of Intangible Cultural Heritage, specifically the Hometown of Chinese Folk Culture and Art (HCFCA), have been investigated. Results show that the distribution and structure of HCFCA in China vary significantly across regions and categories. Eastern China holds the highest number, while Southern China has fewer. Traditional fine arts and dance dominate, with folk literature being less represented. HCFCA exhibits a clear clustering trend, reflecting a “Polycentric Agglomeration” pattern, particularly in traditional drama, calligraphy, craftsmanship, sports, performing arts, and acrobatics. The study presents an influential mechanism model, with the natural environment as foundational, economic development as the primary driver, social development as supplementary support, and historical background as a catalyzing factor. This understanding not only enriches theoretical insights but also provides a scientific basis for the protection of intangible cultural heritage and the development of heritage tourism.

Introduction

Intangible cultural heritage is an important representation of national culture and spirit (hereinafter referred to as “ICH”). As a component of cultural heritage, ICH possesses the local spiritual value of traditional culture, relying on local communities to showcase profound cultural, ethnic, and regional characteristics. China boasts a richly diverse cultural milieu, characterized by its multi-ethnic composition, and is endowed with a plethora of unique categories of ICH. With its crucial role in formulating the “Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage,” China has obtained membership on the Intergovernmental Committee for the Safeguarding of ICH on two occasions. Therefore, China bears the responsibility and obligation to actively contribute to advancing international efforts in the preservation of ICH. The concept of “Hometown of Chinese Folk Culture and Art” (hereinafter referred to as “HCFCA”) is a designated cultural brand project accredited by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism of China. The establishment and development of HCFCA are considered a significant and systematic initiative in the protection and development of ICH in China. Its objective is to strengthen characteristic culture construction and enrich the public's cultural life. Between 1987 and 2003, in order to support the preservation of ICH and promote the sustainable development of cultural and artistic activities among grassroots communities, the former Ministry of Culture began establishing “Chinese Folk Art Township” and “Chinese Distinctive Art Township.” The majority of the selected projects during this period were centered on ICH. In 2008, government authorities expanded the scope and introduced the concept of “Hometown of Chinese Folk Culture and Art.” In March 2018, the release of the “Naming and Management Measures for Hometowns of Chinese Folk Culture and Art” further elucidated the content and scope, which encompass not only folk arts but also folk literature and other forms of ICH. Over nearly three decades, it has evolved into a significant public cultural brand project that promotes the prosperity and development of ICH.

The establishment of “HCFCA” is one of the important creative measures for strengthening ICH protection and development in China. This creation is conducive to revitalizing folk culture and art. Existing research has encompassed folk art and culture from various regions and types, often focusing on a specific case to propose targeted development strategies. From the perspective of research content, the main focus is on the value evaluation, development models, and inheritance paths of folk culture and art from sociological, folklore, and communication perspectives [[1], [2], [3]]. For example, Liu utilized cultural ecology theory to elaborate on the ecological development mechanism of “the Hometown of folk arts” in northern Anhui, China [4]. The value of an endangered folk game has been explored in Korea [5]. In terms of research methods, case studies and qualitative analysis are often adopted. For instance, Jasmina and Natalija discuss Slovenian folk-pop music as a place and nation-making strategy between heritage and popular culture [6]. The interactive relationship between local culture-art and the “space” where it exists has also attracted a lot of academic attention [7,8]. Folk culture and art, along with their cultural spaces, face the challenges and pressures of commercialization, technological advancements, and changing values [9]. In general, the origin and inheritance of ICH are closely related to multiple elements of the geographical environment. Natural, cultural, and historical spatial heterogeneity influence the spatial diversity of ICH. Investigating the spatial distribution and driving mechanisms of HCFCA not only enhances the theoretical understanding of cultural geography and mechanisms of cultural transmission but also provides scholarly foundations for the conservation and development of cultural heritage. This pursuit promotes the advancement of cultural tourism, the optimization of cultural resource utilization, the reinforcement of community cultural identity, and facilitates the harmonious development of culture and the economy.

Literature review

The study of ICH has a well-established history. In the early stages of academic research within the international community, the focus on the preservation of ICH predominantly revolved around several fundamental aspects. These included the conceptual understanding of ICH [10,11], the intricate relationship between ICH and human life [12,13], the utilization and value assessment of ICH [14], the conceptual frameworks for its preservation [15], and the intricate connection with tourism development [[16], [17], [18]]. As research in this field matured and societal development accelerated, recent studies have progressively delved into deeper, multifaceted issues concerning ICH. These recent areas of research encompass: information technology for ICH preservation and assessment [19,20], tourism demand and development models [21,22], national identity [23,24] and cultural landscapes [25].

Research on ICH within China commenced relatively later. However, it gained significant academic attention after China successfully nominated its first batch of “Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity” in 2001. Initially, Chinese scholars primarily adopted a trajectory of following international research trends and achieved substantial academic output. Moreover, as ICH protection practices evolved, the academic community began to establish unique research areas characterized by distinctly Chinese attributes. Existing studies mainly focus on the integration of ICH into tourism development and industrial transformation processes [26], ICH education and disciplinization [27], cultural ecological zone construction [28], ICH utilization and rural revitalization [29], new media applications and ICH dissemination [30]. It is noted that Chinese scholars have attempted to study ICH from the perspective of geography [31,32]. This approach combines qualitative and quantitative analysis methods to examine the impact of various geographical environmental factors on the spatial distribution of ICH, revealing the patterns of its spatial distribution.

In order to protect and preserve ICH, it is essential to fully respect the diversity and differences inherent in ICH. Utilizing spatial analysis in geography can enhance and expand the analytical capabilities and application levels of research related to ICH. From a spatial perspective, scholars have undertaken extensive research to investigate the spatial differentiation of ICH. Existing studies in this field can be categorized into two primary aspects: (1) Spatial structure analysis: Researchers utilize methodologies such as kernel density estimation and geographically weighted regression to analyze the quantitative characteristics, density structure, and distribution balance of ICH. These investigations primarily focus on the geographical distribution characteristics of various types of ICH, as well as the distribution patterns across different spatial scales. For example, specific studies have examined the spatial distribution and influencing factors of opera-related, sports-related, and religion-related ICH [[33], [34], [35]]. Furthermore, the distribution characteristics of ICH at national and other regional levels have been systematically revealed [[36], [37], [38]]. (2) Spatial correlation analysis: This investigation delves into the spatial interconnections between ICH and other entities, such as traditional villages and tourist attractions [39,40]. These studies contribute significantly to the understanding of the spatial dynamics of ICH, providing valuable insights that are essential for its preservation and promotion.

In summary, while current research has produced numerous significant findings, there remain several areas where further improvements can be made. As an important ICH inventory, it is noted that studies regarding HCFCA are relatively limited. Moreover, further research is needed to examine their spatial differentiation characteristics and influencing mechanisms. Therefore, this study adopts a geographical perspective, compiles and organizes data on HCFCA, and employs ArcGIS kernel density, nearest neighbor analysis, and geographic detector techniques to investigate the spatial patterns and influencing mechanisms of HCFCA distribution. This research can provide valuable reference for the construction of local cultural brands and offer useful insights for the protection and inheritance of ICH, and may shed light on heritage academic study.

Research methods and data sources

Research methods

Nearest neighbor index

The nearest neighbor index is a geographical indicator that expresses the degree of proximity in the spatial distribution of point features. From a national regional perspective, the location of each provincial-level administrative region's HCFCA can be abstracted as point features in geographic space. The nearest neighbor index is a useful tool for reflecting the spatial distribution characteristics of point features [41]. Equation (1) expresses its formula:(1)R=r‾ri‾where R represents the Nearest Neighbor Index. r‾ represents the average distance between the nearest. ri‾ represents the average distance obtained from the Poisson distribution of the HCFCA in geographical space. The calculation formula is shown in Equation (2):(2)ri‾=12nA=12D,where n represents the number of points, and A represents the area of the region. D represents point density. When R > 1, the distribution type of point features is uniform. When R = 1, it indicates that point features are randomly distributed. When R < 1, it suggests that point features are clustered.

Kernel density estimation

Kernel density estimation is a non-parametric test function method that intuitively reflects the clustering degree of the research objects [42]. This study uses kernel density estimation to measure the spatial distribution pattern and clustering degree of HCFCA. Equation (3) expresses the formula for calculating kernel density as follows:(3)fn(x)=1nh∑i=1nk(x−xih)where k represents the weight function of the kernel; h represents bandwidth; (x−xi) represents the distance from the density estimation point.

Geo-detector

Geo-detector is a statistical method that reveals the driving factors behind spatial differentiation of research objects by comparing the total variances of various influencing indicators in different regions and the total variance of the indicator across the entire region [43]. Firstly, factor detection has been conducted, as shown in Equation (4):(4)q=(Nσ2−∑hLNhσh2)Nσ2where h represents the geographic detector's discrimination power, Nandσ2 represent the sample size and variance of HCFCA, respectively; L is the number of categories of impact indicators in category h. The value of q falls within the range [0,1], where a higher value indicates stronger explanatory power of the influencing indicators on the spatial differentiation of HCFCA.

Interaction detection also has been used to identify interactions between different influencing factors Xs, which means assessing whether the combined effect of factors X1 and X2 enhances or diminishes the explanatory power for the dependent variable Y (the spatial distribution of HCFCA), or whether the impacts of these factors on Y are mutually independent.

Data sources and processing

This study draws data from the list of HCFCA released by the Chinese Ministry of Culture and Tourism (formerly the Ministry of Culture) from 1987 to 2023 (https://www.mct.gov.cn/). Within the 31 provincial-level administrative regions of China (excluding the special administrative regions of Hong Kong and Macao, as well as Taiwan), a preliminary collection of 964 towns renowned for folk cultural arts has been compiled. To further enhance the accuracy of the research data, the following processing steps were undertaken.

Classification of HCFCA

When classifying HCFCA, reference was made to the definition of folk art and culture in the “Naming and Management Measures for China's Folk Culture and Art Townships.” Additionally, consideration was given to the classification of intangible cultural heritage projects in the “Opinions on Strengthening the Protection of Intangible Cultural Heritage” issued by the General Office of the Central Committee of the CPC and the General Office of the State Council. The HCFCA list is composed of place names paired with cultural and artistic projects, such as “Bacheng Town, Kunshan City, Suzhou, Jiangsu Province + Kunqu Opera.” Most of these projects have been included in China's ICH protection list, which comprises ten categories. Their types can be clearly identified based on the ICH list and its classification standards (e.g., Kunqu Opera is categorized as traditional theater). As for a few modern and contemporary cultural and artistic projects, such as photography, choir, and oil painting are classified under the “others” category. Ultimately, the HCFCA is divided into eleven types, based on the ten ICH categories and the additional “others” category, including “Folk Literature,” “Traditional Music,” “Traditional Dance,” “Traditional Theater,” “Quyi,” “Traditional Sports, Games, and Acrobatics,” “Traditional Fine Arts,” “Traditional Skills,” “Folk Customs,” “Calligraphy,” and “Others”. During the classification process, it was observed that some HCFCA can be divided into sub-level types. Consequently, they have been classified separately, resulting in a total of 1,070 data entries.

Establishing the database of HCFCA

Geographic coordinates of the administrative units where HCFCA are located were obtained from Google Maps and Baidu Maps. Administrative boundaries were sourced from the 1:4 million-scale vector maps in the National Basic Geographic Information System Database. Geographic spatial matching was performed using ArcGIS 10.2 to establish the database of HCFCA.

Regional differences of types of HCFCA

There are differences in the number and types of HCFCA in China (Table 1). Hubei has the largest quantity of traditional folk literature (4 sites), followed by Shanghai and Liaoning (3 sites). Several provinces lack this type. Two provinces with the largest number of traditional music-based HCFCA are Guangdong and Jiangsu, with 14 sites each, followed closely by Hunan, Guizhou, and Sichuan (11 sites). There are 19 sites of traditional dance-based HCFCA in Yunnan, Guangdong, Sichuan, and Guizhou, with Yunnan having as many as 19, while Hainan and Tianjin have none. In terms of Quyi-based HCFCA, Fujian and Henan excel with 7 and 5, respectively. Traditional sports, games, and acrobatics-based HCFCA are primarily distributed in Hebei, Shandong, and Jiangsu, with only 5 each. Traditional fine arts-based HCFCA number more than 20 in Heilongjiang (28) and Fujian (20 sites), followed by Jiangsu (14 sites), Zhejiang (13 sites), and Shandong (10 sites). Traditional skills-based HCFCA is only found in Guizhou (10 sites) and Sichuan (11 sites). Xinjiang has the largest number of Folklore-based HCFCA (9 sites), followed by Guangdong and Shanxi (7 sites). Calligraphy-based HCFCA is mainly distributed in Jiangsu (13 sites), Shandong (7 sites), and Henan (7 sites). Other types of HCFCA, consisting of contemporary emerging culture and arts, is less than 5 sites in all provinces.

In summary, the spatial distribution of HCFCA is closely related to regional culture and people's lifestyles. For instance, the culture of East China is characterized by its openness, with a history of close exchanges with other provinces, strong cultural inclusiveness, and a greater tendency to aggregate various cultural and artistic types. The Southwest region boasts a greater presence of traditional dances and skills than other regions, aligning with the style of ethnic minorities known for their singing and dancing prowess, as well as their distinct ethnic customs and culture.

Spatial agglomeration patterns

The overall kernel density distribution map of HCFCA has been produced using kernel density analysis. As shown in Fig. 2, there is a noticeable pattern of agglomeration in the distribution of HCFCA. Similar to China's population distribution line (Heihe-Tengchong Line), to the east of the line is most of HCFCA, while to the west there are fewer, presenting a spatial structure of “Polycentric Agglomeration."Fig. 2Kernel density of HCFCA (Review Number GS (2019) 1822).Fig. 2

Kernel density of HCFCA (Review Number GS (2019) 1822).

In this pattern, the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei urban cluster and the Central Plains urban cluster radiate into adjacent provinces, forming one cluster. Two groups are located in the area of Chongqing-Sichuan, and Hunan-Hubei-Guizhou. There are two concentrated distributions in the Yangtze River Delta, southern Fujian and Pearl River Delta. Furthermore, two instances of scattered point distribution are observed in the eastern part of Qinghai and the northern and central parts of Yunnan.

As shown in Fig. 3, different types of HCFCA exhibit heterogeneous agglomeration characteristics. Folk literature-based, traditional sports, games and acrobatics-based HCFCA mainly cluster in the Yangtze River Delta and southern Fujian. Folk literature-based HCFCA also form a belt-like distribution in northern Guizhou, western Hunan, and western Hubei. The Yangtze River Delta has long been a center of literary activity, where abundant educational and intellectual resources have nurtured a rich tradition of folk literature. Meanwhile, northern Guizhou, western Hunan, southern Fujian, and western Hubei, with their diverse ethnic groups and unique languages, customs, have given rise to a wealth of folk legends. Traditional sports, games and acrobatics-based HCFCA form a belt-like distribution in the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei and Shandong. In addition to its rich historical and cultural heritage and the fusion of diverse ethnic traditions, the traditional sports, games, and acrobatics of both regions has also been shaped by the influence of martial spirit. Traditional dance-based and traditional music-based HCFCA have a broader distribution. More specifically, both types have dense clusters in western Hunan, southeastern Guizhou and the Pearl River Delta. Traditional Dance-based HCFCA are concentrated in southwestern Yunnan, while Traditional Music-based HCFCA exhibit high density distribution in the Yangtze River Delta region. These areas, with their diverse and rich ethnic cultures, boast a wealth of traditional music and dance ICH. Traditional theater-based, traditional fine arts-based, and traditional skills-based HCFCA have the densest distribution in Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region and the Yangtze River Delta region. Both regions, with their relatively advanced economies and rich artistic traditions, also serve as significant cultural exchange hubs. Traditional theater-based HCFCA are also distributed in the border areas of Hubei, Jiangxi, and Zhejiang, as well as Fujian. Traditional fine arts-based HCFCA are also found in the northeastern regions of Heilongjiang and Jilin and southern Fujian. Traditional skills-based HCFCA are mainly concentrated in eastern Sichuan, northeastern Yunnan, Wuling Mountains, northeastern Jiangxi, and central Shandong. The relatively remote and inaccessible geography of these regions helps preserve and pass down traditional crafts, while their stable economic conditions enable these skills to play a significant role in the local economy. Folk customs-based HCFCA are primarily distributed in Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, Yangtze River Delta region and Pearl River Delta, southeastern Guizhou, the border region of Qinghai and Gansu, and the border region of Shanxi, Henan, and Hebei. The diverse and vibrant folk festival activities, such as temple fairs, flower festivals, and lantern displays, are closely intertwined with folklore-related ICH. Quyi-based HCFCA are mainly concentrated in the eastern part of Qinghai, southwestern Henan, and the southern Fujian. This is related to the unique traditional local opera genres of the three provinces, such as Qiu'er, Yu Opera, and Min Opera. Calligraphy-based HCFCA exhibit good aggregation characteristics, with concentrated distributions in Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, Yangtze River Delta region, the border region of Shanxi, Henan, and Hebei, and the central Shandong region. These regions are home to many renowned calligraphers and calligraphic schools (e.g., the style of calligraphy developed by Wang Xizhi), which have driven the flourishing and development of calligraphy. Other types of HCFCA are mainly distributed in the Yangtze River Delta region and northeastern regions of Heilongjiang and Jilin. Overall, among the different types, traditional theater-based, calligraphy-based, traditional skills-based, traditional sports, games and acrobatics-based HCFCA show relatively strong agglomeration characteristics.Fig. 3Kernel density of different types of HCFCA (Review Number GS (2019) 1822).Fig. 3

Influencing mechanisms of spatial differentiation characteristics of HCFCA

The formation and spatial differentiation of HCFCA are the results of the multifactorial interaction between natural environment and socioeconomic development. Drawing from existing literature, this study attempts to identify key factors influencing the spatial differentiation of HCFCA from four dimensions: natural environment, economic development, social development and historical background. The natural environment includes topographic features (X1), rivers (X2), temperature (X3), and precipitation (X4) [44,45]. The economic development includes GDP (X5) and urbanization level (X6). The social development includes population (X7) and transportation (X8). The historical background includes museums (X9), population of ethnic minorities (X10), national historical and cultural cities (X11), Chinese traditional villages (X12) [46,47]. Using a Geo-detector, the study calculates the impact of various driving factors on the spatial differentiation patterns of HCFCA and explores the interactions and influencing mechanisms among these factors.

Among the four driving factors, the order is as follows: rivers (0.274) > temperature (0.217) > precipitation (0.118) > topography (0.083). Rivers play a crucial role in the development of human civilization. The study results show a high degree of coupling between the aggregation level of HCFCA and river density. Rivers provide water resources and serve as important transportation routes, providing powerful support for economic, cultural, and social exchanges. Regions with dense river networks, such as the lower reaches of the Yangtze River, the middle and lower reaches of the Yellow River, and their surrounding areas, are where HCFCA are more concentrated. As shown in Table 3, river density (0.274) has a significant impact on the aggregation of HCFCA. Climate is an essential component of the natural environment and has a significant impact on the evolution of human society. Suitable temperatures and adequate precipitation create favorable conditions for population concentration, economic development, and the cultivation of regional culture. As shown in Table 3, temperature (0.217) and precipitation (0.118) have a relatively noticeable impact on the spatial differentiation of HCFCA. Topography is a fundamental element of human civilization development, and its influence on regional folk culture and art is complex and comprehensive. Flat and open terrain, such as plains and hills, promote interaction, communication, and cultural dissemination, while high and relatively isolated areas like mountains and plateaus can help preserve unique and pure original cultures. From Table 3, it can be seen that topography (0.083) has little influence on the spatial differentiation of HCFCA, but its impact is relatively small.

Folk culture and art, along with ICH, are created, enjoyed, and inherited by the community in a specific region during their social production and daily life. The level of socioeconomic development is an important influencing factor for HCFCA. The order of various factors, from highest to lowest, is as follows: GDP (0.610) > population (0.541) > museum (0.446) > transportation (0.326) > national-level historical and cultural city (0.293) > traditional villages (0.242) > urbanization level (0.192) > population of ethnic minorities (0.185). GDP has the greatest impact on the spatial distribution of HCFCA. Regions with higher economic development levels are more likely to form clusters of HCFCA. Examples include the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region and the Yangtze River Delta, which are economically advanced and have the highest concentration of HCFCA. Population is also a significant indicator. The gathering of people plays a crucial role in creating, inheriting, and benefiting from folk culture and art. Regions with abundant cultural resources are more likely to foster HCFCA. The q value of museum, national-level historical and cultural city, and traditional villages is 0.446, 0.293 and 0.242. This is because cultural resources provide the raw materials and inspiration for the development of cultural and art, and the unique cultural heritage and historical background of each region can form a distinct cultural landscape. Transportation has a strong impact on the spatial distribution of HCFCA, indicating that accessibility is a key determent. The influence of urbanization level and the ethnic minority population is relatively small.

The study further uses Geo-detector to obtain the interaction effects of various influencing factors on the spatial differentiation of HCFCA (Table 4). The types of interactions include nonlinear enhancement and bi-factor enhancement. The interaction effects of influencing factors from different dimensions lead to different types of interactions, which are always greater than the interaction effects of an individual factor. Topography, despite having the smallest single-factor influence (q-value of 0.083), exhibits a significantly enhanced interaction effect when combined with other factors. GDP has a high interaction effect with the four natural geographical factors. Among these, the interaction effect between GDP and temperature is the highest. Folk culture and art are the result of the interaction between socioeconomic development and the natural environment. The spatial distribution pattern of HCFCA is a complex and dynamic evolutionary process. Based on literature reviews and the results of the Geo-detector calculations, this study attempts to construct an influencing mechanism model for the spatial differentiation of HCFCA from four dimensions: natural environment, economic development, social development, and historical background. As shown in Fig. 4, the natural environment serves as the foundational factor for the spatial differentiation of HCFCA, primarily manifested in the basic constraints exerted by rivers, temperature, precipitation, and topography on the development of folk culture and art. Folk culture and art are the active results of the people's adaptation to the natural geographical environment. The regional characteristics and spatial distribution of HCFCA are closely connected to the natural environmental background. Economic development is the dominant driving factor for the distribution of HCFCA. The prosperity and development of regional economies provide a solid material foundation for the development of traditional culture and art and drive people's needs from basic survival to higher-level spiritual and cultural demands. Therefore, the uneven development of regional economies can lead to provincial differences of HCFCA. Social development plays a supportive role in the spatial distribution of HCFCA. People are the creators, inheritors, and consumers of folk culture and art. Without people, the establishment of HCFCA is meaningless and ICH cannot be inherited and developed. Accessibility can affect cultural communication and interaction. Convenient transportation contributes to the formation and aggregation of HCFCA. Historical background is a catalyzing factor in the spatial distribution of HCFCA. It reflects the diachronic characteristics of the development of ICH, which is a cultural landscape created and inherited by the people over a long period of social production and daily life practices. Discussion and conclusions

Discussion

Building upon our analysis, we present a set of suggestions for ICH to offer practical guidance aimed at fostering further sustainable development. The cultural heritage embodied in HCFCA is a dynamic code for the inheritance of regional culture and a spiritual driving force for socioeconomic development.

Firstly, it is imperative to enhance inter-regional cooperation and strategically manage the spatial distribution to achieve balanced development of ICH. As evidenced by the findings, the distribution of HCFCA exhibits significant concentration and disparities. Consequently, organizing high-level inter-regional dialogues and forums to facilitate knowledge-sharing in ICH preservation, inheritance, and development is warranted. This is in line with previous findings by Anatolie et al. and Egberts [48,49]. Additionally, promoting cross-regional exchanges and learning opportunities for ICH inheritors can effectively disseminate ICH knowledge.

Secondly, it is crucial to emphasize the systematic protection of ICH spaces. This study highlights the significant role of museums, historical and cultural cities, and traditional villages in shaping the spatial distribution of HCFCA. Centralized protection of ICH sites, like cities and villages, is vital for preserving cultural integrity. This holistic approach has been emphasized by studies [50,51]. Additionally, regional cultural institutions, such as museums, provide venues for ICH-related activities and support talent development and funding for heritage protection.

Thirdly, it is essential to prioritize the inherent subjectivity and agency of individuals and communities. The research findings reveal a symbiotic relationship between local social groups and ICH. Folk cultural arts represent the localized cultural creations of the populace, and ICH stems from the collective or individual wisdom of the people. As earlier research argued, community participation is key for sustainable cultural heritage management [52,53]. Additionally, particular attention should be devoted to ensuring the equitable protection of the interests of ICH inheritors and communities throughout the urbanization and marketization processes in economically underdeveloped regions.

Furthermore, enhancing the accessibility of ICH is paramount. Transportation infrastructure plays a pivotal role in fostering HCFCA. Thus, improving transportation planning and infrastructure is vital for the integrated development of cultural heritage and tourism. Additionally, harnessing digital technology can effectively transform cultural resources, thereby improving access to digital heritage products.

Conclusions

This study analyzed the spatial differentiation and influencing mechanisms of HCFCA, with the following main conclusions.(1)The regional distribution and categorical structure of HCFCA in China varies significantly. In terms of regional distribution, Eastern China has the highest number, while Southern China has the fewest. In terms of category structure, traditional fine arts and traditional dance dominate, while folk literature and others have the fewest numbers.(2)HCFCA exhibit a clear clustering trend, showing a distribution pattern of “Polycentric Agglomeration.” In terms of types, traditional theater, calligraphy, traditional skills, traditional sports, performing arts, and acrobatics exhibit prominent clustering characteristics.(3)The spatial differentiation of HCFCA is multifaceted, influenced by various factors. Interaction analysis underscores the significance of high levels of socioeconomic development and favorable temperature conditions in this process. This study presents an influencing mechanism model, delineating the roles of different factors: the natural environment serves as the foundational element, economic development emerges as the primary driver, social development provides supplementary support, and historical background acts as a catalyst.

Limitations and recommendations for future directions

The primary aim of this study is to analyze the current spatial distribution characteristics of ICH, with a specific focus on HCFCA. The study not only deepens our comprehension of ICH but also sheds light on the underlying mechanisms behind its spatial disparities. For enhanced applicability, delving into the temporal evolution of ICH from a historical standpoint could yield invaluable insights. Additionally, given the intricate composition of HCFCA, there are opportunities to refine the classification criteria employed in this analysis. Recognizing the symbiotic relationship between culture and tourism, their integration can serve as a catalyst for rejuvenating ICH. Consequently, future research exploring the spatial intersection of “ICH + tourism” could substantially contribute to advancing our understanding of sustainable ICH development.

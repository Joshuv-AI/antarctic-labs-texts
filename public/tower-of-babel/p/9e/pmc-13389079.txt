Availability augmentation of smart grid systems using Markovian approach and nature inspired algorithms
Authors: Riya Agrawal, Monika Saini, Ashish Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present study focuses on the optimization of availability in smart grid systems by integrating stochastic modeling and hybrid nature-inspired algorithms. For this purpose, a novel stochastic model of smart grid systems, comprising seven subsystems under various redundancy strategies, is developed using Markov birth–death process. The concept of degradation, uncertain sensor behavior and exponentially distributed failure and repair laws utilized for system performance evaluation. Nature-inspired algorithms employed for parameter estimation and availability optimization at various population sizes. The efficiency of various algorithms for smart grid system availability prediction is statistically tested using nonparametric statistical method, namely Friedman test, followed by post hoc Wilcoxon test. The optimizer results are compared with the simulated system availability results to verify the accuracy and robustness of the proposed model. It is revealed that sequential SOHBA outperforms the traditional SO and HBA algorithms as well as Monte Carlo simulation results. The numerical results of steady-state availability are also derived and impact of variation in failure and repair rates investigated. To highlight the importance of study numerical and graphical results are appended.

Introduction

In the twenty-first century the demand of electricity surge drastically and places a significant pressure on traditional power grids. These grids are unable to effectively manage fluctuations in energy supply and demand, leading to inefficiency, interruptions, and instability. In the era of technology, engineers have been designing smart grids for electricity distribution by integrating digital communication, real time monitoring ,and automation in energy generation, distribution, and consumption to optimize the electricity distribution. Smart grids not only ensure efficiency and stability but also significantly enhance the reliability of power systems. Due to which smart grid technology becomes immensely popular and become a strategic priority at global level. Many Developed countries, such as the United States, Germany, Japan, and China have made remarkable progress in implementing smart grid systems and are focusing on advanced metering, renewable energy integration, electric vehicle charging infrastructure, and cyber-secure energy mechanisms to reduce carbon emissions and strengthen energy security. India launched the National Smart Grid Mission in 2015 to promote smart grid adoption. India requires advanced grid infrastructure to ensure a reliable and high-quality power supply. Smart grids combine digital technologies with renewable energy sources, but the intermittent nature of solar and wind causes fluctuations that lead to instability and system complexity. To ensure the flawless operation of smart grids, it becomes essential to operate with high reliability. Reliable smart grids reduce the risk of failures and maintain stability, thereby preventing disruptions in industries, healthcare, communications, and households.

Many researchers developed various reliability evaluation methodologies like Markovian, semi-Markovian, and fault tree analysis. All these provide the reliability measures in local scenario under different set of assumptions. Recently, metaheuristic algorithms gained popularity by predicting reliability measures of complex industrial systems. These can solve large-scale, nonlinear, and multi-objective optimization problems. Tillman et al.1 presented an extensive review of reliability evaluation techniques for strengthening system reliability through redundancy. The study classified reliability models according to series, parallel, standby, and complex configurations and summarized evaluation approaches including integer, dynamic, and geometric programming by giving emphasis on the importance of matching each method to the system’s complexity and constraint. Goyal et al.2 presented large-scale Markov-chain models for evaluating computer system availability. The proposed approach automates model generation, applies numerical methods, and analyzes sensitivity, illustrated through a case study and implemented in the system availability evaluation software. Wikstrom et al.3 examined RAM aspects of high-power AC drives, defining key concepts, highlighting typical misconceptions, and outlining reliability assessment approaches to enhance communication between manufacturers and users. Kim et al.4 developed a two-tier availability model for virtualized systems by combining fault trees with continuous-time Markov chains. The model captures both hardware and software failures, including issues in the VMM, VMs, and applications and considers features such as high-availability services and VM live migration. The approach evaluates steady-state availability, annual downtime, and capacity-based metrics. Berg5 emphasized integrating safety into engineering design through early hazard identification and risk analysis. The study outlines using causal and consequence analyses to map accident scenarios and develop a safety case. It highlights continuous hazard assessment, risk mitigation, and independent safety reviews to reduce the likelihood and impact of hazards. Yu et al.6 described smart grids as advanced, interconnected power systems that enhance electricity delivery through modern communication and control technologies. They highlighted their role in sustainability and economic growth while tackling climate challenges and noted key issues such as integration and cybersecurity. Kim et al.7 explained that availability is the top security priority in smart grid communications. The study reviewed research on defending against attacks, ensuring low-latency performance for critical real-time systems, and improving communication reliability using cognitive radio networks. It also discussed ongoing challenges in maintaining seamless communication to support continuous power supply. Logesh8 reviewed PV/wind hybrid power systems and highlighted their potential for sustainable energy. It discussed challenges in resource availability, power converter design, and energy management with battery storage, while also discussing global implementations, future prospects, and deployment obstacles.

Recently, many researchers used metaheuristic approaches in performance evaluation and prediction of various industrial/financial systems. Verikas et al.9 surveyed hybrid and ensemble soft computing approaches for bankruptcy prediction, emphasizing the combinations of these methods rather than their reported accuracy. Hybrid algorithms use several techniques with one predictor, while ensembles merge outputs from multiple predictors. Valdebenito et al.10 presented a comprehensive survey of various approaches to Reliability-Based Optimization (RBO) in structural design. The study emphasized on the process in which RBO integrates uncertainty into optimization, making it more realistic but computationally demanding. Both classical and modern methods were reviewed, with a focus on theoretical foundations, assumptions, and applicability across different problem types. Datta and Chattopadhyay11 reviewed advancements in soft computing for designing structural metals and alloys. The study emphasized in handling complex links between composition, processing, microstructure, and properties, and highlighted hybrid approaches for developing new materials and guiding future designs. Basset et al.12 reviewed metaheuristic algorithms for complex optimization, categorizing them into metaphor-based and non-metaphor-based types. They compared popular algorithms through case studies, evaluated their performance statistically, and discussed current limitations and future research directions. Chren et al.13 explored smart grid software reliability, clarifying definitions, outlining required data for evaluation, and reviewing existing sources to enable effective reliability assessment in these complex systems. Wang et al.14 introduced a novel electric load forecasting approach that combines data preprocessing, hybrid optimization techniques, and traditional forecasting methods. Using 30-min interval data from Queensland, Australia, the proposed hybrid model achieved higher prediction accuracy and supported effective smart grid dispatch planning.

Pokorni15 examined IoT reliability and availability, highlighting that traditional hardware-focused methods like MIL-HDBK 217 are insufficient due to the complex interactions of devices, software, users, and protocols, and proposed a method for calculating IoT system reliability. Cevasco et al.16 examined wind turbine RAM data and found that offshore conditions reduce reliability and availability. They recommended improving data quality and modeling for future offshore projects. Düzenli et al.17 developed two enhanced Honey Badger Algorithm variants by using chaotic maps and opposition-based learning for accurate PV model parameter extraction, achieving high accuracy and strong agreement with actual values on benchmark datasets. Hashim and Hussien18 proposed the Snake Optimizer (SO), a bio-inspired optimization algorithm based on the mating and hunting behaviors of snakes. It was evaluated using CEC 2017 benchmark functions and real-world engineering problems, showing superior performance in balancing exploration and exploitation compared to other leading algorithms. Danjuma et al.19 analyzed the reliability, availability, maintainability, and dependability of a cold standby series–parallel system using Markov models, showing that system performance improves when failure rates are low and standby units are effectively utilized. The novelty of the proposed work lies in the development of a hybrid optimization algorithm (SOHBA) that effectively integrates the strengths of Snake Optimizer (SO) and Honey Badger Algorithm (HBA). Unlike individual algorithms, the proposed hybrid approach achieves a better balance between exploration and exploitation, enabling it to avoid premature convergence and local optima. The Markov birth–death process is used to model the operational behavior of the smart grid systems operation. In proposed model, “birth” represents the repair or restoration of a unit, and “death” represents the failure of a unit in the smart grid system. Failure and repair rates are numerical values that indicate how often a system fails and how quickly it is repaired. Here, Redundancy refers to the presence of backup units that ensure system continuity. By connecting the stochastic process to actual operational factors, the model effectively represents the system’s reliability and availability, allowing the theoretical framework to be directly applied to smart grid operations.

System configuration

The smart grid is an advanced energy system designed to deliver electricity efficiently and reliably in response to dynamic consumer demands. It constitutes a critical component of modern power infrastructure, enabling secure, resilient, and optimized generation, transmission, and distribution of electrical energy. For establishment of smart grid architectural frameworks Jørgensen42 highlighted the system is integration of multiple subsystems such as generation, transmission, distribution, monitoring and control, communication, cybersecurity, and energy storage that work together in series configuration to maintain grid stability and ensure a continuous flow of electricity. The control and monitoring subsystem consists of two identical parallel units . If the primary unit fails, the secondary unit takes over, allowing the system to continue operating in a degraded state. Since both units are simultaneously active and contribute to system operation, this configuration is modeled as active parallel redundancy. The identical nature of the units is reflected through same failure rates, which are incorporated into the reliability analysis.

Dataset

The failure rate and repair rate as appended in Table 2 are taken from various studies reported in literature on reliability investigation of power systems components. Many recent studies including Ghiasi et al.43, and Smith44 showed that failure rates are often in the range of 10⁻3 to 10⁻2 failures/hour and repair rates generally lie between 0.2 to 2 repairs/hour, depending on component accessibility and maintenance policies. Ghasemi et al.45 proposed a conservative approach to decide the search bounds of selected parameter values to encompass the typical variability observed in practice as given in Table 2. Optimization strategies

Optimization serves as an essential foundation in mathematics, computer science, engineering, economics, and many other disciplines. The term optimization is derived from “optimum” meaning the best. It involves selecting the most effective solution from a range of alternatives while satisfying given constraints. In essence, optimization focuses on making informed decisions, conserving resources, and attaining the best possible outcomes in the presence of limitations and challenges. For instance, a company may attempt to maximize profit while minimizing production costs. Optimization techniques are systematic approaches and mathematical frameworks designed to address optimization problems. Their purpose is to either enhance performance or reduce costs. A central principle of optimization is to effectively combine broad search (exploration) with focused refinement (exploitation). Where “Exploration” means searching the solution space widely to find good options, while “Exploitation’ means focusing on the best areas to improve the solution. Along with traditional optimization techniques, metaheuristics algorithms are also used as an optimization technique, and these are inspired by natural processes. These algorithms are particularly suited for tackling complex problems, which may include nonlinearity, randomness, high dimensionality, or irregular structures. Although these methods do not always guarantee the absolute optimal solution, they can deliver high-quality solutions within a reasonable timeframe. They are especially valuable when obtaining exact solutions is challenging or computationally expensive. Some well-known algorithms which show applications in reliability prediction of industrial systems are GA, PSO and WHO. Recently, snake optimizer and honey badger also attracted the researchers and utilized for performance prediction.

As a member of the population-based optimization family, SO begins by generating an initial set of random solutions and progressively refines them over successive iterations. By balancing exploration and exploitation, HBA effectively handles complex optimization problems. Sequential hybrid SOHBA algorithm

A new novel approach introduced to improve availabilty of smart grid is sequential hybrid of the Snake Optimizer (SO) and Honey Badger Algorithm (HBA). The sequential hybrid of the Snake Optimizer (SO) and Honey Badger Algorithm (HBA) is a two-step metaheuristic optimization framework that combines the complementary strengths of both algorithms in an efficient way. In this framework, the solutions generated by SO are passed on as initial inputs to HBA, which further improves and refines them. In the first step, SO focuses on global exploration by scanning the entire solution space. It searches widely and maintains a diverse set of solutions, ensuring that promising areas are not overlooked. After this broad exploration, the best solutions are transferred to HBA. In the second stage, HBA specializes in local exploitation. It applies its digging strategy, which intensively examines smaller areas, along with the honey-scent strategy, which follows promising clues toward improved results. Together, these strategies allow HBA to fine-tune the solutions, improve precision, and speed up convergence toward the best result.

This division of roles allows each algorithm to concentrate on what it does best rather than trying to handle both exploration and exploitation simultaneously. SO is particularly strong in global exploration, covering the broader search space, while HBA excels in local exploitation, refining details and guiding the search toward optimal answers. By combining broad exploration with careful refinement, the hybrid framework achieves accurate, reliable, and efficient performance, even on complex, high-dimensional, or irregular optimization problems. The framework is also simple to implement, flexible, and adaptable to different domains. It is particularly useful in applications such as engineering design, machine learning, scheduling, energy system planning, and other problem areas where both wide exploration and detailed refinement are required. In literature, several well-established algorithms like Genetic Algorithm (GA), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Differential Evolution (DE) are extensively used to optimize the performance of industrial systems. Conclusion

Here, an efficient stochastic model is developed for availability evaluation of smart grid under concept of exponentially distributed failure and repair laws using Markov birth–death process. In addition, the optimal availability prediction and parameter estimation performed using metaheuristic algorithms namely Snake Optimizer (SO), Honey Badger Algorithm (HBA), and their sequential hybrid SOHBA under varying population sizes at various iterations. The algebraic results of steady state availability show a sharp decline behaviour with the increase in failure rates. The numerical results revealed that the sequential hybrid algorithm achieved the highest optimal availability of 0.9601408 at a population size of 40 after 1000 iterations, demonstrating superior performance over the individual algorithms (SO and HBA). The performance of the optimizers validated by comparing the predicted availability with the simulated availability by performing Monte Carlo simulation. The outperformance of hybrid SOHBA is tested using statistical test namely Friedman test and confidence intervals as well as Cohen’s d, cliff delta derived. These statistical results confirmed the robustness of the SOHBA in availability prediction of smart grids. Though the proposed methodology is systematic and scalable for availability assessment and parameter estimation of large-scale power distribution systems, but system complexity, computational complexity, security concerns, dynamic behaviour and the combination of various energy sources is still major challenges in smart grids and limitations of the proposed model. The impact of these factors, assumptions of exponential failure rates and the exclusion of simultaneous failure phenomena on smart grid’s performance will be analysed in future work. Because exponential distribution used for maintaining analytical tractability within Markov birth–death framework. So, it is necessary to future study on non-exponential distribution for better connect with cascading failures of real time smart grid. As a progression of reported work, it may be further extended to the system by modeling component lifetimes using Weibull distributions and correlated failure mechanisms. Also, in future it is planning to incorporate real-world data to validate the model in practical settings based on time between failure and time to repair. Additionally, the data will enable us to identify the most appropriate failure and repair time distributions, thereby enhancing the realism and accuracy of the proposed approach. Moreover, traditional algorithms such as genetic algorithm(GA) and partial swarm optimization (PSO) are not included in present study, and this limitation is addressed in future research. Furthermore, future developments will employ a semi-Markov model so that the system’s non-exponential and dependent behaviour can be represented more accurately. Finally, it is concluded that hybrid SOHBA algorithm outperforms the standalone algorithms (SO and HBA) in availability prediction of smart grids.

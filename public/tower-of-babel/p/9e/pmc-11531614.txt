Comparative analysis of reproductive hormones, serum biochemical indexes and ovarian metabolites in Muscovy breeder duck at different laying stages
Authors: Wanli Yang, Xuqiao Lang, Danyu Song, Hanqi Xu, Cheng Zhang, Liping Guo, Xingyong Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis regulates egg laying through control hormones secretion in poultry. In this study, the serum hormones (12 samples per stage), serum biochemical indexes (12 samples per stage), and ovarian metabolites (8 samples per stage) of Muscovy breeder ducks were detected at prelaying stage (PT), start of laying stage (ST), high laying stage (HT), and the end of laying stage (ET). The serum hormones of Muscovy ducks were measured at 8:00, 13:00, 18:00, 23:00, and 4:00 within 1 d. The TG, TP, ALB, and GLB were significantly increased, while HDL-C was significantly decreased at ST as compared to PT (P < 0.05). Serum Na, Cl, Ca, P, and K showed significant rise at ST as compared to PT. Serum Na, Cl, Ca, and K were significantly declined, while P was significantly increased at ET as compared to HT (P < 0.05). Serum FSH, LH, PRL, E2, P4 levels peaked at ST (P < 0.05) with only FSH and LH fluctuated significantly within 1 ovulation cycle at ST (P < 0.05). Differential metabolites showed continued ovarian aging. The decline of nucleic acid metabolism occured in ST, the decline of sugar metabolism occurred in ET, and the decline of amino acid metabolism continued at all stages. Temporal expression patterns and correlation analyses indicated a high correlation between ovarian cAMP and serum reproductive hormone levels across different reproductive stages. In conclusion, this study revealed the changes in serum hormones, serum biochemical indicators, and ovarian metabolites, as well as the relationship between serum hormones and ovarian metabolites.

INTRODUCTION

Hormones regulate poultry reproduction, including beginning of egg laying, broodiness, molting, and other processes. The initiation of egg laying is regulated by a sudden up-regulated luteinizing hormone (LH) stimulated by long photophase in turkey (Chapman, et al. 1994; Liu, et al. 2001). The onset of laying hens also can be induced by LH (Nøddegaard et al., 2000). GnRH injection in vivo can effectively induce egg production in nonlaying hens (Hanafy et al., 2021). Broodiness is induced by an increased prolactin (PRL) secretion, which is stimulated by neural information transmitted from a clutch of eggs through the brood patch to the hypothalamic VIP neurons (Sharp, 2009; Ye et al., 2019). The PRL and thyroxine have the most obvious effect in inducing molt (Kuenzel, 2003). During forced molting, LH decreased to a low level and the daily pulse secretion disappeared, while the level of LH rose again 2 d after the end of forced molting in Turkey (Bacon and Long 1996). At the end of egg laying, the secretion of FSH and LH decreased (Miao, et al. 2024; Ciccone, et al. 2005). Due to the fluctuation of reproductive hormones in poultry during ovulation, the level of reproductive hormones could not be accurately measured at a certain stage. Vanmontfort et al., (1994) observed that LH and P4 peaked at 6h before egg production while FSH did not change. Liu et al. (2001) found that LH surges occurred between 2 egg-laying interval through continuous monitoring of serum LH level of Turkey Hens for 6 wk after the onset of egg laying. Muscovy duck performed high rhythm of reproduction represented by its high broodiness and high egg production with 97% peak laying rate. Therefore, investigating hormone secretion rhythm at 1 ovulation (about 24 h in Muscovy duck) and different laying stage is much helpful for understanding the hormone regulation during egg production.

There is a complex positive and negative feedback regulation between reproductive hormones and ovaries (Johnson and Lee 2016; Tarumi, et al. 2014; Komatsu and Masubuchi 2017; Nett, et al. 2002). The ovary, as the terminal of HPG axis, is regulated by reproductive hormones and undergoes dramatic changes throughout the reproductive cycle (Zhao et al., 2023). As compared with the peak laying period, differentially expressed genes in the ovary were mainly enriched in arachidonic acid metabolism, MAPK signaling pathway, regulation of actin cytoskeleton, and TGF-β signaling pathway through lncRNA sequencing in chicken at late laying period (Huang, et al. 2024). The differentially expressed mRNA, lncRNA, and miRNA in the ovary at different egg-laying stages were mainly involved in cell process, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, phagosome, calcium signaling pathway, steroid biosynthesis and ECM-receptor interaction of Yili geese (Zhao, et al. 2022). Based on the exploration of metabolites, it could be more intuitive to see the continuous changes of ovary during sustained egg laying stages. Since serum reproductive hormones and ovarian metabolites being detected, the regulatory relationship between reproductive hormones and ovarian metabolites could be more recognized and utilized for improving egg production and improvement of feeding nutrition in Muscovy duck.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethics Statement

All animal experimental procedures in this study were approved by the Animal welfare committee of Anhui Agricultural University with the assurance number SYDW-P20210823021.

Animal Samples

The experimental Muscovy ducks were raised by Anhui province AnqingYongqiang Muscovy Duck Co., LTD. A total of 216 Muscovy ducks were raised in 36 pens with 6 ducks in each pen (1 male and 5 female), and 3 collective pens were set as 1 replicate with 12 replicates in total. All ducks were set free access to feed and water. Wing vein blood was collected at the prelaying stage (29 wk, PT), the start of the laying stage (31 wk, group laying 5 %, ST), the high laying stage (35 wk, group laying 90%, HT), and the end of laying stage(54 wk, ET). The light time was 16 h L: 8 h D at 29 wk, 17 h L: 7 h D at 31 to 34 wk, and 18 h L: 6 h D at 54 wk. The nutritional level of ducks in each stage was set to meet the requirement of the National Research Council (NRC) stand (1994).The feeding amount was 200 g /bird /day. The nutrient levels of feed at each stage were shown in Table 1. Twelve healthy female Muscovy ducks were selected at PT, ST, HT, and ET stages, respectively, according to their continuous egg laying performance, and 2.5 ml blood was collected from the wing vein at 8:00 am,13:00 pm,18:00 pm, 23:00 pm, and 4:00 am, respectively, after being fasted for 12 h. After blood collection, press the wound for 15 min until no bleeding or redness occurs. Plasma blood was centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 min to separate the serum for biochemical index (only serum collected at 8:00 am) and hormones detection. Eight female Muscovy ducks were selected from the 12 ducks for sacrifice after anesthesia. Serum Biochemical Index and Hormone Detection

Serum biochemical index and hormones were detected at Beijing North Institute of Biotechnology Co., LTD (Beijing, China). Serum biochemical indicators of cholesterol (CHOL), low density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), triglyceride (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), total protein (TP), albumin (ALB), globulin (GLB), albumin/ globulin (A/G), Lipoprotein (LP), Na, CL, Ca, P, and K were detected by Auto-Biochemical Analyzer (TBA-120R, Toshiba, Japan) Serum hormone detection adopteda competitive radioimmunoassay using Iodine [125I] E2 (B03PZB), P4 (B04PZB), FSH (B05PZB), LH (B07PZB) and PRL (B08PZB) Kit provided by Beijing North Biological Biotechnology Research Institute Co., LTD), and were measured on a radioimmunoassay system (radiometer, xh6080, Xian, China).

Nontargeted Metabolomics Analysis

The preparation of ovarian tissue samples was referred to Zhu et al. (2020). Quality control (QC) was performed using an equal mix of all samples. The metabolite detection was conducted at the Personal Biotechnology Company (Shanghai, China). The LC-MS/MS analyses were performed using a UHPLC system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) with a Waters ACQUITY UPLC BEH Amide (2.1 mm × 50 mm, 1.7 μm) coupled to OrbitrapExploris 120 mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). The mobile phase consisted of 25 mmol/L ammonium acetate and 25 mmol/L ammonia hydroxide in water (pH = 9.75) (1) and acetonitrile (2) (Liu, et al. 2022). The auto-sampler temperature was 4°C, and the injection volume was 2 μL. The Orbitrap Exploris 120 mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra on information-dependent acquisition (IDA) mode in the control of the acquisition software (Xcalibur, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA).

The samples were analyzed in both positive and negative modes of electrospray tandem mass spectrometry. The ESI source conditions were set as follows: sheath gas flow rate as 50 Arb, Aux gas flow rate as 15 Arb, capillary temperature 320°C, full MS resolution as 60000, MS/MS resolution as 15000, collision energy: SNCE 20/30/40, spray voltage as 3.8 kV (positive) or -3.4 kV (negative), respectively. The raw MS data was converted to the mzXML format using ProteoWizard and was analyzed by XCMS R package (version 3.3.2).

The metabolites were identified by accuracy mass (<25 ppm), and MS/MS data were matched to online databases (HMDB, METLIN, and KEGG). Only variables with at least 1 set of nonzero measurements exceeding 50% of the extracted ion features were retained. Orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was performed using the R package “muma.” The OPLS-DA model variable importance projection (VIP) > 1, P < 0.05, and flod change > 2 were used as the criteria to screen significant differential metabolites (DMs). Pathway and enrichment analyses were performed using KEGG. The PT group, ST group, and HT group were set as control when DMs were screened from ST (PT vs. ST), HT (ST vs. HT) and ET (HT vs. ET) respectively. Student's t test and ANOVA were used for comparing the difference between 2 groups and multi-group. The algorithms of Euclidean and Complete were used for temporal expression patterns analysis.

Statistics Analysis

One-way Analysis of Variance was used to analyze the difference of biochemical indices at different stages of laying duck. Two-factor ANOVA was used to analyze the difference and interaction for serum hormones at different stages and different times within 24 h. Levene's Test was performed to analyze the homogeneity of variance and Shapiro-Wilk was performed to analyze the normal distribution of original data. ANOVA was used as the significance analysis model when the variance of the data was homogeneous, and Welch's t was used as the significance test model when the variance was nonhomogeneous. Least significant difference (LSD) was used for post hoc tests (SPSS 22.0, Chicago, IL). Line graphs were made using Graphpad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad, San Diego, USA). P < 0.05 was considered as significant difference.

RESULTS

Analysis of Serum Biochemical Indicators of Muscovy Ducks at Different Laying Stages

The TP and GLB content increased significantly at ST vs PT and then decreased at HT vs ST (P< 0.05). The ALB levels at ST, HT and ET were significantly higher than the levels at ST (P < 0.05). The A/G levels at HT and ET were significantly higher than those at PT and ST (P < 0.05). The LP level at HT was significantly lower than that at PT and ET (P < 0.05). The HDL-C was significantly decreased at ST vs PT and then significantly increased at HT vs ST (P < 0.05). There was no significant difference for CHOL and LDL-C at different laying stages. The TG levels at the ST and HT were significantly higher than those at PT and ET (P < 0.05). The levels of Na, Cl, Ca, P, and K at ST were significantly higher than those at PT (P < 0.05). The levels of Na and K significantly decreased at ET vs HT (P < 0.05). Similarly, the levels of Cl, Ca, and P significantly decreased at HT vs PT (P < 0.05). Analysis of Serum Reproductive Hormones in Muscovy Ducks at Different Laying Stages and Different Times of 1 day

For the 2-factor analysis of variance, significant differences were observed among different laying stages for the hormones FSH, LH, PRL, E2, and P4. The contents of FSH, LH, PRL, E2, and P4 at the ST stage were significantly higher than other stages (P < 0.05). Time variations were significant only for LH, with significantly higher levels detected at 8:00 as compared to other time points (P < 0.05). Furthermore, a significant interaction between stage and time was observed exclusively for FSH and LH, indicating that their concentrations were influenced by both factors simultaneously (Supplementary Table S1).

To further understand the change of serum hormones, the 1 way ANOVA of the time at different stages and the stage at different times were performed separately. The content of FSH was significantly higher at ST than that of other stages at H8, H13, H18, and H23 timepoint (P < 0.05, Figure 2A). The contents of LH were significantly higher at ST than that of other stages at H8 and H13 timepoint (P < 0.05, Figure 2B). The contents of PRL and E2 were significantly higher at ST than that of other stages for all timepoint (P < 0.05, Figures 2C and 2D). The contents of P4 were significantly higher at ST than that of other stages at H8 (P < 0.05, Figure 2E). Only ST, FSH and LH have significant differences at different timepoints. A total of 2011 ovarian metabolites were identified, including 1189 in ES+ mode and 822 metabolites in ES− mode (Supplementary Table S2). All metabolites were classified according to the superclass of the HMDB database. The results showed that organoheterocyclic compounds accounted for the highest proportion (18.3%), followed by lipids and lipid-like molecules, with a content of 15.4%, among all metabolites detected in Muscovy duck ovaries. The differential metabolites (DM) were first screened according to T-test (P < 0.05) and relative content (FC > 2, Supplementary Table S3), and then further screened according to VIP value obtained by OPLS-DA (vip >1, Figures 4A, Figure 4, Figure 4). Finally, there were 180 up-regulated and 256 down-regulated DMs at ST vs PT. There were 32 up-regulated and 117 down-regulated DMs at HT vs ST. There were 29 up-regulated and 50 down-regulated DMs at ET vs HT (Figure 4D). The number of downregulated metabolites related to amino acids metabolism was more than the upregulated metabolites in each period, indicating that ovarian aging begins to occur when birds start laying. Four different metabolites were significant in the 3 comparison groups, including quinic acid, deoxyribose, 2-hydroxy-6-methylquinoline-3-carbaldehyde, and sedoheptulosan (Figure 4E).

The DMs of ST vs. PT were significantly enriched in 18 pathways, mainly significantly enriched in Nucleotide metabolism, Glycerophospholipid metabolism, and Amino acid metabolism (P < 0.05, Figure 5A). Nucleotide metabolism included purine metabolism and pyrimidine metabolism. The major metabolites and intermediates of purine (IMP, AMP, GMP, guanine, and adenine) and pyrimidine (UMP, UDP, and uridine) were down-regulated. Furthermore, dGDP, dGMP, and ADP, which are essential raw materials for DNA synthesis, were also down-regulated. This suggests a weakening of DNA synthesis at the ST stage (Supplementary Table S4). Temporal Expression Patterns Analysis of Hormones and Differential Metabolites

DMs obtained from the ANOVA of multiple groups and serum reproductive hormones was further subjected for regulatory relationship analysis. The DMs and serum hormones were significantly clustered into 9 groups according to their expression trend (Figure 6, Table 3). The FSH, LH, PRL and E2 were enriched in cluster 2. The expression trend of metabolites in cluster 2 was similar, while metabolites in cluster 9 was opposite to serum hormones of FSH, LH, PRL and E2, which suggested that ovary metabolites could be simultaneously positive and negative regulated by serum hormones. Most of the metabolites from cluster 2 and cluster 9 were significantly associated with E2 and FSH. The cAMP was significantly positive correlated with FSH, E2, and PRL. DISCUSSION

Egg production requires the synthesis of large amounts of lipids, proteins, and minerals. When start laying, plasma triglycerides increased substantially and lipoproteins were needed to transport them from liver to ovary (Wayne 1994). The elevation of TG and ALB indicated that lipid and protein synthesis capacity of liver is greatly improved during egg laying in this research. Serum lipoproteins maintained no significant change coupled with significant decrease in HDL-C could both increase the risk of liver fat accumulation of Muscovy duck. Egg production requires large amount of trace element, especially Ca, to form eggshell, and the transport of Ca requires participation of Na and Cl (Han, et al. 2023), which explained the high increased serum K, Na, CL, Ca, and P at ST. As laying continues, the blood calcium of the laying hens decreased resulted in the decreased thickness of the eggshell (Benavides-Reyes, et al., 2021). When the blood calcium was insufficient to supply the demand for egg production at late laying stage, Ca stored in hydroxyapatite crystals of medullary bone was released into blood coupled with P releasing into blood (Dacke, et al. 2015; Sinclair-Black, et al. 2023), which might be the reason for the serum increase of P in the late laying stage.

The start laying in age-appropriate hens was induced by long daylight, after which the reproductive hormones and their receptors were highly elevated (Cui, et al. 2021; Kusuda, et al. 2023; Liu, et al. 2001). Oestrogen can accelerate the response of FSH and luteinizing hormone to light stimulation in prepubertal domestic hens(Dunn, et al. 2003). When egg laying decreased, a significant decrease of FSH and LH was also observed (Prastiya, et al. 2022). In this research, an increased reproductive hormones was also observed at ST and decreased at HT and ET. LH secretion was characterized by fast climb hours before ovulation and slow fall after ovulation. It is stated that there were 2 secretion modes for LH, 1 was characterized by low baseline fluctuation and the other was high baseline fluctuation (Chapman, et al. 1994). The high baseline flucturation of LH occured after the first long-day photophase (Bacon and Long, 1996), then LH secretion began to decrease to low baseline fluctuation at the middle and late stages of laying in turkeys (Guémené and Williams, 1994). Only the high-baseline fluctuation of LH secretion was detected in this study.

Ovarian aging is characterized by oxidative damage, abnormal hormone secretion, immunosuppression, reduction of substance synthesis, and slow down of follicle development (Liu, et al. 2023; Chen, et al. 2024). In particular, ovary aging is faster than other organs (Zhang, et al. 2019), and continuous ovulation accelerated the process(Perheentupa and Huhtaniemi 2009). In this study, a decreased DNA synthesis, amino acid metabolism, and glucose metabolism occurred also suggest the aging process of ovary in Muscovy duck. The oocyte recruits granulosa cells to form prehierarchical follicle at prelaying stage, which requires a lot of DNA synthesis. During egg laying, ovulation led DNA damage of ovarian epithelial cells and induce apoptosis (Murdoch, et al. 2001; Murdoch, et al. 2005). The glycerophospholipid metabolism enriched in ST vs PT, with phosphatidylcholine (PC) down-regulated and phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) up-regulated. Lai et al. (2018) found that PC can protect the defect of zearalenone exposure on follicular development and oocyte maturation. Elevated PC and PE were detected in follicular fluid of infertile women, this was considered as biomarker of infertility (Dabaja, et al. 2022; Ulcová-Gallová, et al. 2002). The content of PE (18:0/18:0) in follicular fluid gradually increased, and the content of PC (18:0/22:5) gradually decreased during follicle formation (Wang, et al. 2023). During autophagy, PE binds to the membrane of the autophagosome, which promoted the formation and maturation of autophagosome (Xu and Wan 2023). Autophagy can regulate the number of ovarian follicle banks (Bhardwaj, et al. 2022).

Amino acids, raw materials for the synthesis of proteins, could be converted into carbohydrates and fats to provide energy for metabolites formation. The contents of Lys, Met, Leu, Phe, Tyr, Asp, Ser, Glu, Ala, and total amino acids in the serum of pigeons (American Silver King) were upregulated after the first day of estrus, followed by downregulation of Leu, Ile, Val, Phe, Tyr, and TAA in 2 to 7 d (Ren, et al. 2021). For egg production, methionine and cysteine are the first limiting amino acid, lysine is the second limiting amino acid, and threonine is the third limiting amino acid (Shemil, et al. 2021). In this research, threonine downregulated during egg laying, which might suggest that a higher protein level enriched with essential amino acids should be provided to Muscovy duck to maintain its high egg laying. Tryptophan, with its unique position as a rate-limiting amino acid in protein synthesis, was integral to the production of serotonin, indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase, and quinolinic acid, thereby effectively reducing animal stress, boosting appetite, stimulating dopamine secretion, and reducing fighting behavior (Shemil et al., 2021). Pyruvate is an important metabolic intermediate in glucose metabolism and amino acid metabolism and is involved in the biosynthesis of many amino acids. Pyruvate content was decreased at ST. Short-term deprivation of pyruvate in vivo and in vitro can increase the expression of glycolytic-related genes and proteins, inhibit the AMPK pathway, activate mTOR signaling pathway, and promote the activation of follicles in mice (Zhang et al., 2022). The increase of glycolysis level can inhibit premature ovarian failure in mice (Li, et al. 2023). At the same time, pyruvate is an important functional substance in follicle development, and the decrease of pyruvate is unfavorable to follicle development (Zhang et al., 2023). Histamine is an important chemical transmitter that is produced by decarboxylation of histidine, which occurs both in nervous system as a neurotransmitter and in non-nervous tissues as signaling molecule (Haas et al., 2008). In the brain, histamine can directly activate the HPG axis and regulate the hormone secretion of gonads (Niaz et al., 2018). In the ovary, histamine is involved in regulating permeability of ovarian capillaries in vivo, and stimulating ovarian contraction, ovulation, and follicular progesterone secretion in vitro (Niaz et al., 2018). Histamine was downregulated at ST and upregulated at ET. On the other hand, there is an interaction between histamine and Ca. Upregulation of Ca ion concentration can decrease histamine H1 receptor activity in Chinese hamster ovary cells (Hishinuma, et al. 2018). Direct injection of histamine during laying can cause hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcemia, and lower soluble gastrointestinal calcium (Cabrera-Saadoun, et al. 1987). The upregulated histamine at ET might be responsible for the decrease of serum Ca at ET. At the late of luteal phase, a short-term increase in dietary energy in ewe can stimulate the final stage of follicle development, thereby increase the ovulation rate and double lambing rate (Scaramuzzi, et al. 2006). Intravenous glucose was able to increase the number of follicles >1.0 mm in diameter in Ile-de-France ewes (Scaramuzzi, et al. 2015). In this study, ovary glucose decreased at the end of laying also indicate energy supplies were further depleted.

Addition of purine metabolic inhibitors caused decrease of IMP, AMP, and GMP in the ovary, which did not affect the synthesis of cAMP and secretion of progesterone, suggested that there was other synthetic pathway of cAMP (Miller-Patrick, et al. 1993). Similarly, the IMP and AMP decreased, cAMP increased at ST in our study. As the second messenger of signal transduction, cAMP mediates various signal pathways such as PKA/CREB to regulate the proliferation and apoptosis of ovarian cells, and the development of follicle (Kilanowska et al., 2022). The balance between dormancy and activation of mouse follicles is controlled by cAMP (Zheng, et al. 2023).

CONCLUSION

This study, through a comprehensive analysis of the changes in serum hormones, serum biochemical indicators, and ovarian metabolites in Muscovy ducks across different egg-laying stages, revealed the patterns of serum hormone variations at an ovulation cycle and different egg-laying stages, as well as the regulatory relationship between serum hormones and ovarian metabolites. These findings serve as a basis for enhancing the egg-laying performance and optimizing feed nutrition for Muscovy ducks.

HALP score adds prognostic information beyond STS for 2-year mortality after transcatheter aortic valve implantation: a two-center retrospective cohort study
Authors: Zeynep Seyma Turinay Ertop, Hakan Süygün, Melike Polat, Murat Can Güney, Ahmet Akdi, Engin Bozkurt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundConventional risk scores such as the Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) score are widely used for risk assessment in patients undergoing transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI), yet they may not fully capture systemic inflammatory and nutritional burden. The hemoglobin–albumin–lymphocyte–platelet (HALP) score is an integrated biomarker reflecting inflammation and nutritional reserve.ObjectivesTo investigate the association between HALP and 2-year mortality after TAVI and to evaluate whether HALP provides incremental prognostic information beyond STS.MethodsThis two-center retrospective cohort included 544 consecutive TAVI patients. The primary endpoint was all-cause death within 2 years after TAVI (binary outcome: yes/no). Independent predictors were assessed using multivariable logistic regression (HALP scaled per 10-unit increase). Discrimination was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses for HALP, STS, and a combined STS+HALP model.ResultsWithin 2 years, 154 patients (28.3%) died. Patients who died had higher STS scores and lower HALP scores. HALP showed weak negative correlations with STS and EuroSCORE II (r ≈ − 0.11). In multivariable analysis, HALP remained independently associated with mortality (per 10-unit increase: OR 0.859, 95% CI 0.770–0.960; p = 0.007), while STS score was also independent (OR 1.112, 95% CI 1.045–1.184; p = 0.001). Discrimination was modest for HALP (AUC 0.591) and STS (AUC 0.622), and higher for the combined model (AUC 0.663).ConclusionsHALP is independently associated with 2-year all-cause mortality after TAVI and yielded a higher AUC when incorporated into the STS model, suggesting incremental prognostic information beyond conventional risk assessment.

Introduction

Transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) is an established therapy for severe aortic stenosis in elderly and comorbid patients, supported by contemporary guideline recommendations [1, 2]. Despite ongoing improvements in device technology and peri-procedural care, clinically meaningful early and mid-term adverse outcomes persist, and robust preprocedural risk stratification remains essential for patient selection, counseling, and post-procedural management.

In routine practice, surgical risk models—most commonly the Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) score and EuroSCORE variants—are frequently used to estimate procedural risk [3–6]. However, these models were derived primarily from surgical populations [3–6] and may incompletely reflect systemic inflammatory activity and nutritional reserve. Frailty and biological vulnerability are increasingly recognized as key determinants of recovery capacity and longer-term prognosis after aortic valve replacement, yet they are not fully embedded in traditional scoring systems [7]. Evidence from systematic evaluations suggests that risk-model performance after TAVI is heterogeneous, underscoring the need for complementary, pragmatic markers [8].

The hemoglobin–albumin–lymphocyte–platelet (HALP) score is a composite index derived from routine laboratory parameters and was initially introduced in the oncology literature as a prognostic marker integrating anemia, nutrition, and immune–thromboinflammatory status [9]. Across cardiovascular phenotypes, higher HALP has been associated with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality in population-level analyses [10], and disease-specific cohorts have linked low HALP to adverse outcomes in atrial fibrillation (AF) and in AF patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) or undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) [11, 12]. Among elderly patients undergoing PCI for acute myocardial infarction, HALP also predicted long-term mortality [13].

In the TAVI field, evidence is emerging but remains limited. A single-center study reported that lower HALP was independently associated with multiple adverse in-hospital outcomes after TAVR, including mortality [14]. A registry-based cohort presented as an abstract suggested that low HALP was associated with worse longer-term outcomes, including reduced survival and earlier major adverse cardiovascular events in TAVR patients [15]. However, data remain scarce regarding HALP’s ability to predict mid-term (2-year) mortality and whether HALP provides incremental prognostic information beyond established models such as STS in a real-world TAVI cohort. Therefore, we aimed to (i) evaluate the association between preprocedural HALP and all-cause death within 2 years after TAVI and (ii) assess whether incorporating HALP into the STS model improves discrimination for mortality prediction.

Methods

Patient population

This retrospective, two-center cohort study screened all consecutive patients who underwent TAVI at two tertiary centers between October 2011 and May 2023. Patients were eligible if baseline preprocedural laboratory data required to calculate the hemoglobin–albumin–lymphocyte–platelet (HALP) score were available and 2-year mortality status could be ascertained. Patients with missing key baseline data precluding HALP calculation and/or unknown 2-year mortality status were excluded. The study was approved by the relevant institutional ethics committees and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki; informed consent requirements were handled according to institutional policy due to the retrospective design.

Data collection and follow-up

Clinical, laboratory, and procedural data were extracted from each center’s electronic medical record system and individual patient charts. Baseline variables included demographics, comorbidities, echocardiographic parameters, and established surgical risk scores (STS and EuroSCORE variants). Two-year mortality status was ascertained from hospital records and, when available, national registry data, and was confirmed by telephone follow-up when required.

Preprocedural transthoracic echocardiography was performed as part of routine care, and baseline LVEF, aortic valve area, and estimated systolic pulmonary artery pressure were recorded.

Laboratory measurements and HALP calculation

Baseline blood samples were obtained as part of routine preprocedural evaluation. Hemoglobin (g/dL), serum albumin (g/dL), lymphocyte count (×10^3/µL), and platelet count (×10^3/µL) were recorded. HALP was calculated as follows: HALP = hemoglobin × albumin × lymphocyte count / platelet count. To enhance interpretability, HALP was modeled as a continuous variable and scaled per 10-unit increase in multivariable analyses.

TAVI procedure

TAVI indication, access strategy, and prosthesis selection were determined by the multidisciplinary Heart Team at each center. Preprocedural planning was based on contrast-enhanced multidetector computed tomography. Procedures were performed in a catheterization laboratory or hybrid operating room under general anesthesia or deep sedation with fluoroscopic guidance, according to institutional standards.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 25 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR]), as appropriate, and categorical variables as number (percentage). Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and homogeneity of variances using Levene’s test. Between-group comparisons were performed using the independent-samples t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and Pearson’s χ² test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate. Correlations between HALP and risk scores were evaluated using Pearson correlation coefficients. Independent predictors of 2-year mortality (binary outcome: yes/no) were assessed using multivariable logistic regression. Covariates were selected a priori based on clinical relevance and included HALP (per 10-unit increase), sex, age, STS score, COPD, and history of diabetes. Results are reported as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Discrimination was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis for HALP, STS, and the combined STS+HALP model; AUCs with 95% CIs were reported, and p values tested the null hypothesis that the AUC equals 0.50. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Outcome definition

The primary endpoint was all-cause death occurring within 2 years after TAVI (binary outcome: yes/no). Patients were classified according to 2-year mortality status.

Results

Study population

A total of 544 patients were included; 154 patients (28.3%) died within 2 years after TAVI (Table 1).

Baseline characteristics

Compared with survivors, patients who died were more frequently female (64.9% vs. 51.0%, p = 0.003) and had a higher prevalence of prior myocardial infarction (16.9% vs. 9.5%, p = 0.015). Baseline left ventricular ejection fraction was lower in the mortality group (48.62% vs. 51.15%, p = 0.019). Renal function was worse among those who died, with higher creatinine (1.10 vs. 0.97 mg/dL, p = 0.001) and lower eGFR (59.64 vs. 65.02 mL/min/1.73 m², p = 0.009). Bicuspid valve morphology was less frequent in patients who died (9.1% vs. 15.9%, p = 0.041) (Table 1).

Risk scores and inflammatory–nutritional parameters

Surgical risk scores were higher in patients who died, including STS score (6.70 vs. 5.12, p < 0.001), EuroSCORE II (11.44 vs. 8.12, p < 0.001), and logistic EuroSCORE (19.90 vs. 15.55, p = 0.030). HALP was lower in the mortality group (26.91 vs. 31.35, p = 0.001). Neutrophil and monocyte counts were higher (p = 0.008 and p < 0.001, respectively), whereas albumin and hemoglobin levels were lower (p = 0.002 and p = 0.013, respectively). C-reactive protein was numerically higher but did not differ significantly between groups (p = 0.061) (Table 2).

Multivariable analysis

In multivariable logistic regression, HALP remained independently associated with 2-year mortality; each 10-unit increase in HALP was associated with lower odds of death (OR 0.859, 95% CI 0.770–0.960; p = 0.007) after adjustment for sex, age, COPD, STS score, and history of diabetes. Female sex (OR 1.601, 95% CI 1.071–2.392; p = 0.022) and STS score (OR 1.112, 95% CI 1.045–1.184; p = 0.001) were also independently associated with mortality (Table 4).

Discrimination

ROC analysis demonstrated modest discrimination for HALP (AUC 0.591, 95% CI 0.539–0.643; p = 0.001) and STS score (AUC 0.622, 95% CI 0.570–0.673; p < 0.001). The combined STS+HALP model yielded a higher AUC (0.663, 95% CI 0.612–0.715; p < 0.001); reported p values tested AUC against 0.50 (Table 5; Fig. 2).

Procedural and in-hospital events (descriptive)

Several procedural and in-hospital variables differed between groups (e.g., predilatation, tamponade, in-hospital arrhythmia, and in-hospital mortality) and are best interpreted descriptively to avoid post-treatment bias in preprocedural risk narratives (Table 1).

Discussion

In this real-world TAVI cohort, preprocedural HALP was independently associated with all-cause death within 2 years, even after adjustment for STS and key clinical covariates (Table 4). Although stand-alone discrimination was modest, adding HALP to STS yielded a higher AUC (Table 5), suggesting that HALP may capture prognostic dimensions related to biological vulnerability and reserve that are not fully reflected by traditional surgical risk assessment [7] and that risk-model performance after TAVI is heterogeneous [8].

Our results extend the emerging TAVR/TAVI HALP literature by focusing on a 2-year endpoint and by evaluating complementary discrimination with STS. Çiçek et al. reported that lower HALP predicted multiple adverse in-hospital outcomes and showed favorable early discriminatory performance for in-hospital mortality [14]. Bistrovic et al., in a registry-based cohort presented as an abstract, suggested that low HALP was associated with shorter survival time and earlier MACE [15]. Together with our findings, these studies suggest that HALP may identify a vulnerability phenotype relevant to both early and mid-term outcomes.

HALP integrates hemoglobin and albumin (markers related to anemia, nutrition, and systemic inflammation) with lymphocyte and platelet counts [9]. Frailty is a key determinant of outcomes after aortic valve replacement and provides incremental predictive value beyond traditional variables [7]. Nutritional status has also been repeatedly linked to outcomes after TAVI, with studies reporting associations for albumin and objective nutritional indices (GNRI/CONUT/PNI) [16–18], and meta-analytic evidence indicating that baseline malnutrition is associated with worse outcomes after TAVI [19]. Collectively, these data suggest that HALP may serve as a pragmatic marker of biological reserve that complements established clinical risk models. We modeled HALP as a continuous variable to avoid information loss from arbitrary cut-offs.

Inflammatory biomarkers and ratios have been associated with outcomes after TAVI, including C-reactive protein-to-albumin ratio (CAR) [20, 21], neutrophil percentage-to-albumin ratio (NPAR) [22], and hematologic indices such as NLR/PLR [23, 24]. Taken together, this literature suggests that inflammatory–nutritional biology is relevant to prognosis after TAVI and provides a rationale for composite markers that are simple, widely available, and reproducible.

Population-level data link HALP to cardiovascular mortality [10]. In AF, lower HALP has been associated with higher 1- and 5-year mortality (AFTER-2) [11], and in AF patients with ACS/PCI, higher HALP has been associated with lower MACE risk and improved prediction when added to established clinical models [12]. Similar signals have been reported in elderly AMI patients after PCI [13]. This broader evidence base is concordant with our observation that HALP provides information that is only weakly correlated with STS/EuroSCORE II and yields a higher AUC when combined with STS (Tables 3 and 5), suggesting complementary prognostic information.

In heterogeneous elderly cohorts, single biomarkers rarely yield high AUCs; therefore, independent association and incremental model performance may be more informative. Because the ROC p values in our analysis test AUC against 0.50, and formal AUC-difference testing (e.g., DeLong) and reclassification metrics (NRI/IDI) were not performed, we report the result conservatively as “yielded a higher AUC” rather than a statistically confirmed improvement over STS alone.

HALP is inexpensive, objective, and readily available from routine baseline laboratory tests. Incorporating HALP into preprocedural evaluation may help identify patients with reduced physiological reserve who remain at elevated 2-year risk despite conventional risk scoring and who could benefit from enhanced counseling, targeted optimization (e.g., evaluation and management of anemia and nutritional status), and closer post-discharge follow-up. Procedures and follow-up were performed according to institutional standards at both centers.

Limitations

This study is limited by its retrospective design and the potential for residual confounding. The two-center setting may limit generalizability to other healthcare systems and practice patterns. The endpoint was analyzed as a binary outcome within 2 years; time-to-event analyses could provide additional information if exact event dates and censoring information were available. Incremental value assessment in the present study was limited to AUC reporting; calibration and reclassification analyses would strengthen inference. Finally, periprocedural/in-hospital variables differed between groups and should be interpreted descriptively to avoid post-treatment bias in preprocedural risk discussions.

Conclusions

HALP is independently associated with 2-year all-cause mortality after TAVI and yielded a higher AUC when incorporated into the STS model. These findings suggest that HALP may capture inflammatory–nutritional vulnerability not fully reflected by conventional risk scores and could help refine preprocedural risk stratification in TAVI patients.

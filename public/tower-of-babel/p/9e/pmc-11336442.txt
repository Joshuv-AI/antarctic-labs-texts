The relationship research between restorative perception, local attachment and environmental responsible behavior of urban park recreationists
Authors: Kaijun Wu, Yingying Guo, Xingfu Han
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The environmentally responsible behavior of tourists has been one of the focal points in the field of sustainable tourism research in recent years. Taking Guangzhou City Park as an example, this study investigates the relationship between restorative perception, place attachment, and environmentally responsible behavior of urban park recreationists by constructing the theoretical framework of "cognition-emotion-behavior" of recreationalists via online questionnaire surveys and using the test method of structural equation mode. Studies have shown that: (1) When the restorative perception of the environment is compatible, malleable, and attractive, the recreationist will produce environmentally responsible behavior. Meanwhile, when the environment has a sense of distance and compatibility, it will promote the active environmentally responsible behavior of tourists; (2) the path of perceptual environmental compatibility in the restorative perception of recreationists being → place dependence→ place identity → environmentally responsible behavior has a positive impact on environmentally responsible behavior; (3) place attachment has a significant positive impact on environmentally responsible behavior. This paper suggests that urban park management governors should focus on improving the charm and attractiveness of parks, diversifying recreational activities, and creating emotional attachment places, so as to promote the development of urban parks and meet the diverse needs and experiences of park recreation.

Introduction

As society has developed harmoniously and people's material standards have continued to rise, leisure and travel have become vital aspects of people's lives [1]. The primary driving force behind travel among urban residents is, on the one hand, their growing needs for a better life; on the other hand, hectic work schedules and a fast-paced lifestyle have also made people more anxious, tense, nervous, and experiencing other negative emotions and stresses. These factors have a direct impact on people's physical and mental well-being [2], as well as encouraging impulsive leisure and travel behaviors. Individuals can have enjoyable experiences from tourism, and the natural environment, which serves as a venue for these activities, has the power to reenergize and awaken individuals, reducing tension and improving focus [1,[3], [4], [5]]. However, with the rise of mass tourism, the industry has also had a negative impact on the environment due to tourists' lack of environmental awareness and the deliberate destruction of tourist destinations' natural areas, even though it still benefits the local economy and society and provides visitors with a pleasurable experience [6]. Consequently, in order to contribute to the sustainable and healthy development of the environment of tourist destinations, it has become imperative to find ways to guarantee that tourism activities not only benefit tourists but also lessen the environmental harm caused by people's participation in tourism activities.

The spatial carrier of human activities, that is, the environment in which people live, and the human behavior activities themselves have a certain potential relationship, which has a certain "profit-seeking" characteristic [7]. When visitors interact with the environment, they are willing to take action to maintain this good state, provided that they perceive the benefits of a good environment [8]. Tourists' restorative perception is greatly correlated with the theory of attention restoration, and it has become one of the factors driving tourists to have beneficial behaviors. In the existing research, the study of tourists' restorative perception mainly consists of two levels: the first is to explore the influencing factors that promote the recovery benefits of the environment, and on the other hand, it mainly explores the influence of environmental resilience perception on the emotional or psychological factors of tourists. Few scholars have studied the relationship and mechanism of the influence of restorative perception on tourist behavior, and so far, few empirical studies on restorative perception and environmentally responsible tourist behavior have been found. In fact, when individuals feel that a certain behavior is beneficial to them, they are willing to make great efforts to repay the benefit brought by the environment [9], as a result of which the study of tourists' restorative perception and environmentally responsible behavior has strong academic significance and value.

The city park is a public service space and carrier for the leisure activities of urban recreationalists, which can meet the needs of recreationalists to relax and obtain a pleasant experience [10]. It is also the center of leisure activities for urban recreationalists, which has become an important aspect of the current field of tourism research. Guangzhou has more than 200 urban parks, and the types and number of urban parks are various, such as wetland parks, forest parks, exhibition parks, water parks, etc., which are loved by urban recreationists. Taking Guangzhou City Park as an example, based on the theoretical framework of “cognition-emotion-behavior,” this study attempts to construct the theoretical framework of “Tourists’ restorative perception, place attachment, and tourist environmentally responsible behavior” and uses the structural equation method to analyze the relationship and mechanism of tourists' restorative perception and environmentally responsible behavior in order to make theoretical contributions to the research on restorative perception and environmentally responsible behavior and provide suggestions for the planning, design, and management of urban parks.

Literature review and research hypotheses

Restorative perception

In recent decades, restorative environments have received widespread attention from the fields of environmental psychologists, urban planning [10], and public health, such as mind relaxation [11], stress reduction [12], mood improvement [13], and increased positive emotions. One theory of the restorative potential of the environment is the "pro-life hypothesis," which focuses on an intrinsic tendency of people to seek connection with nature and other life forms, advocating enhanced beneficial contact between man and nature [14]. This theory is the first to confirm that the environment has restorative benefits. Kaplan [14] first proposed the concept of a restorative environment, arguing that a restorative environment is an environment that can help restore depleted emotional and functional resources and abilities. Research on the benefits of environmental recovery also includes Urich's Stress Reduction Theory (SRT). SRT hypothesizes that the emotional responses caused by the surrounding environment affect behavioral responses and cognitive evaluations, leading to further emotional and physiological responses [15] and that this natural environment contributes to the recovery of stress (i.e., physiological, psychological, and negative emotions) [16,17]. In addition, what has attracted the most attention from scholars is attention restoration theory (ART). Attention restoration theory suggests that spending time in a natural environment restores a person's ability to concentrate, thereby improving memory, processing information, and problem-solving [14,18,19]. That is, when individuals are emotionally and self-regulated, they can use the natural environment to obtain a restorative experience. The research on the restorative environment is mainly based on the research of the natural environment, that is, the analysis of the restorative benefits and factors of the environment. For example, Ouellette et al. [4] studied the restorative benefits of the abbey experience and proposed that distance, compatibility, beauty, and spirituality are the characteristics of a restorative environment that produces these benefits. Thomas et al. [5] assessed the components of a restorative environment and verified whether the two standard variables of the environment's restorative perception potential and environmental preference, and the four predictors of openness, visual access, movement ease, and environmental concern, were all characteristics of the restorative environment.

Restorative perception developed from the theory of the restorative environment and reflects the interaction between man and nature. There is no uniform definition of the concept of the restorative perception of tourists, but it is undeniable that restorative perception and the restorative environment are closely related. From restorative environment to restorative perception, attention restoration theory is generally used as the carrier. Scholars tend to think of restorative perception as the perception of the restorative environment, especially the restorative effect of the environment on attention. For example, Vada et al. [20] argued that the functional principles, strategies, and outcomes of emotion and self-regulation overlapped with the theory of the restorative environment, mainly attention recovery theory, which is a kind of restorative experience and perception. Kaplan's [18] theory of attention restoration proposed that restorative environments had four dimensions: being away, extent, fascination, and compatibility. These four characteristics are as follows: (a) being away: being distant from daily sources of stress and fatigue, (b) extent: providing scope for physical or mental exploration, (c) fascination: boosting fascination and wonder, and (d) compatibility: sup porting individuals' expectations and preferences. Zhou Wei et al. [21] held the view that tourists' restorative perception was the tourist's perception of these four characteristics of the environment, which was consistent with the research of most scholars. Synthesizing domestic and international research and practices, this paper argues that attention restoration theory focuses on the directed attention ability that individuals use in areas such as work and interpersonal relationships. The natural environment improves directed attention deficits, resulting in positive emotional regulation, attention recovery, and a relaxed experience, while restorative perception is an individual's cognitive experience of the environment (an environment with a sense of distance, malleability, glamour, and compatibility) that can serve as an intrinsic potential for emotion and self-regulation.

Place attachment

Place attachment is a term describing individuals’ strong emotional bonds to a particular place or environment, which has also been conceptualized as sense of place, place bonding, or place identity. Numerous studies have depicted place attachment in terms of place dependence and place identity [22]. Place attachment plays an important role in tourism marketing [23] and has become the focus of research by scholars in the past decades. In different dimensions, the concept of place attachment varies slightly. For example, Ujang et al. [24] found that place attachment was formed by developing meaningful spaces in urban public spaces to allow people to interact. From the perspective of environmental psychology, place attachment is the emotional bond or connection among people in a particular place [25]. Daryanto and Song [26] suggested that from the perspective of place social connection, place attachment was a sense of belonging to a group of people or members in a place. There are four dimensions of place attachment: place dependence, place identity, place affect, and place social bonding [27,28]. Place attachment has been described in terms of place identity and place dependence in a number of studies [29,30]. Furthermore, Lewicka [31] noted that place identity and place dependence were constant components within place attachment and that many quantitative measures of place attachment should be based on them. Place dependence and place identity were thus included in the parameters of place attachment in this study.

In the study of place attachment, scholars usually take the place attachment of tourist residents as the main research object. The content of the research is mostly related to keywords, such as behavioral intentions, environmentally responsible behaviors, satisfaction, loyalty, and tourism involvement, while on the other hand, it focuses on the study of influencing factors. In terms of place attachment research methods, the grounded theory and structural equation model are mostly used for qualitative or quantitative research and analysis. Meanwhile, studies have demonstrated that if an individual is in a certain place to meet the needs of the individual or achieve a certain goal, the individual will become dependent on this place, and once it is necessary to meet this need or achieve a certain goal, it will be inseparable from this place, and this dependence will gradually generate a series of emotions, such as belonging, identity, etc. [32,33]. Environmentally Responsible Behavior

Environmental psychology is a well-established area of study that combines studies of place with personal senses of identity and, as such, has a history of contributing to an understanding of tourism-induced change [34] and resource protection [35]. The concept of environmental liability behavior among tourists has not yet been defined uniformly, and the terminology and definitions of different scholars vary greatly. It can be seen from the research of most scholars that environmentally responsible behavior is conceptually equivalent to environmentally friendly behavior, eco-friendly behavior, or environmentally friendly behavior [36]. Lee T. H [37]. argued that the environmentally responsible behavior of tourists was the behavior of tourists that contributed to the improvement of environmental quality or promoted the sustainable development of the environment. Chiu Y. H. et al. [38] held the view that the environmentally responsible behavior of tourists was a series of behaviors that occurred when tourists realized that some of their actions had an impact on the environment so that they complied with the environmental protection norms of tourist destinations. It can be seen that environmentally responsible behavior is an act made by tourists that is beneficial to environmental protection.

In the study of environmentally responsible behavior, environmental attitudes, environmental feelings, environmental responsibility awareness, ecological values, and other factors often attract the attention of scholars. Theories related to environmentally responsible behavior are more common in planned behavior theory [39], broken window theory [8], etc. In terms of measurement dimensions, the measurement dimensions of environmentally responsible behavior by scholars are also various, and different scholars consider them from different angles. In multidimensional measurement, Lee T. H [37]. proposed seven dimensions of measurements, including physical behavior, financial behavior, and persuasion behavior. Most scholars use a two-dimensional measurement method. For example, Ramkissoon et al. [40] adopted low-effort environmental protection behaviors and high-effort environmental protection behaviors. Cheng T.M. and Wu H.C [41]. were divided into general behaviors and specific behaviors, and Li and Zhou [42] divided environmentally responsible behaviors into environmental maintenance behaviors and environmental promotion behaviors. Judging from the measurement items of different scholars, although the names of the scholar dimensions for the two-dimensional division are different, the measurement of tourists' environmentally responsible behaviors includes compliance and active behaviors. After the literature analysis, this study adopts a two-dimensional measurement method, which is divided into compliance-type environmental protection behavior and active environmental protection behavior.

Restorative perception, place attachment, and environmentally responsible behavior

Restorative perception and place attachment

Certain factors linked to place attachment, including distance, compatibility, ductility, and charisma, have been identified as affecting the degree of restorative perception [22,27,28]. Place attachment has a strong attachment relationship with restorative perception, which has been demonstrated in relevant studies. Vada et al. [20] pointed out through the theory of cognitive experiential self that restorative perception was the intrinsic potential of emotions and self-regulation and that place attachment could also serve emotions and self-regulation. Ratcliffe and Korpela [33] revealed empirical research that place dependence and place identity, as well as individual memory of places, played a positive role in restorative perception. Wilkie and Clements [43] took environmental preferences as a feature of place identity, showing that environmental preferences could contribute to the restorative benefits of memory, etc., but further research was needed on the relationship between restorative perception and place attachment. Hartig et al. [44] had also shown that the environment played a positive role in evoking place emotions and place memories. Zhou Wei et al. [45] demonstrated that restorative perception affected happiness, and there was an influencing mechanism of "restorative perception—place dependence—place identity—happiness.". Environmentally responsible behavior and place attachment

According to place attachment theory, people's emotional connection to a place can produce positive behavioral tendencies to protect the place [31,46]. At the same time, social identity theory predicts that one is more likely to prioritize the interests of the community in that place over the interests of the individual, provided that a person is attached to a place and identifies with a community [47]. Then, when individuals become dependent on the place and then identify with it, they will act in favor of the environment and even invest more energy in the place. Ramkisson et al. [40] conducted a study on the role of cultural landscapes, natural landscapes, and rich educational activities in Dandenong Ranges National Park in their emotional connection with tourists and found that emotions between tourists and places can promote tourists' environmental responsibility behavior. Huo Hong et al. [48] confirmed that local attachment has a significant impact on tourists' environmental responsibility behaviors. In the study of many influencing factors, most scholars have studied the environmental perception and environmentally responsible behavior of tourist [8,51]. Perception is an important concept in the field of social psychology and an important predictor of behavior [44,48]. What is more, the theoretical explanation of environmentally responsible behavior is mostly based on the perspective of perception [8]. The stronger the restorative perception of tourists, the stronger their commitment to environmental protection, and they will actively protect the environment during tourism and leisure [52]. Methodology

Questionnaire design

According to the ART theory and the study of Liu Qunyue et al. [2], this study, which is for the measurement of the variable of restorative perception, divides restorative perception into four dimensions: perceptual distancing, perceptual compatibility, perceptual ductility, and perceptual charisma. Contain a total of 15 measurement questions, which is generally consistent with the measurement dimensions and measurement items of most scholars on restorative perception.

The measurement of environmentally responsible behavior, with reference to the division dimension of environmentally responsible behavior by Li et al. [35], is divided into compliance-type environmentally responsible behavior and active environmentally responsible behavior, which contain a total of 8 measurement questions, and appropriate modifications are made to the topic items in combination with the situation.

The measurement of place attachment is based on the scale of William et al. [29], which contains a total of 10 measurement items, and the two dimensions of place dependence and place identity are considered. Which is roughly the same as the measures of empirical studies on place attachment by scholars. Measures of restorative perception, environmentally responsible behavior, and place attachment are measured using a number of Ricketts five-level scales.

Data collection

In this study, Guangzhou parks were selected as the research objects, and recreationalists in the parks of Guangzhou city were selected as the survey subjects. Besides the questions contained in the above measurement variables, the questionnaire also includes demographic characteristic information. The data collection process used a simple sampling method to conduct online questionnaires through electronic questionnaires distributed by Questionnaire Stars. The questionnaire collection time is from May 20, 2021, to June 22, 2021. The participants were rewarded with a red packet, and a total of 390 electronic questionnaires were finally recovered in order to ensure the authenticity of the questionnaire filling. Considering the reliability and authenticity of the electronic questionnaire compared with the offline questionnaire, the questionnaire was set up with two test questions, and the answers to the test questions were fixed. According to the screening criteria defined as invalid questionnaires with incomplete questionnaire filling and incorrect test questions, 325 valid questionnaires were retained after reviewing, and the questionnaire validity rate was 83.3 %.

Conclusions and discussion

Conclusions

Studies have shown that, due to a specific environmental impact, when urban park recreationists perceive the restorative benefits of the environment, that is, when the recreationalists have a restorative perception of the environment in which they live, the direct or indirect impact of their own emotions will to a large extent produce environmentally responsible behaviors [1,45,54].

Restorative perception has a positive effect on place attachment. Specifically, when the environment is compatible and attractive, it will make the rest of people have place dependence feelings, and when the environment is malleable and attractive, it is easier to have place identity, and the compatibility and malleability are more significant. In addition, place dependence has a significant positive impact on place identity. The results of this study support such contentions and the findings of Bin Zhou, Luting Wang, Songshan (Sam) Huang, and Qihao Xiong [55], and Xue, L.-L., and Shen, C.-C [56].

Restorative perception has a direct positive impact on environmentally responsible behavior. Specifically, when the environment is compatible, malleable, and attractive, it will cause the recreationist to have compliant environmentally responsible behavior, and at the same time, when the environment has a sense of distance and compatibility, it will promote the active environmentally responsible behavior of tourists. In these concepts, it can be seen from the path coefficient of Table 6 that perceptual compatibility > perceptual charism > perceptual ductility > degree of benefit from perceptual distancing, thereby confirming the studies of Bin Zhou, Luting Wang, Songshan (Sam) Huang, and Qihao Xiong [55] and Liu Qunyue, You Da, Pan Minghui et al. [2].

Place attachment has a positive impact on environmentally responsible behavior. The positive effect of place attachment on environmentally responsible behavior is mainly generated through place dependence. When the recreationist feels a certain dependence on a place, he is more willing to actively protect the environment and will abide by the environmental rules and order of the place. The results are consistent with the studies of Bin Zhou, Luting Wang, Songshan (Sam) Huang, and Qihao Xiong [55] and Zhang Yuan, He Jiaoyi, Liu Pandeng [57].

Place attachment has a partial mediating role in restorative perception and Environmentally Responsible Behavior. The indirect effect of restorative perception on environmentally responsible behaviors (both compliance and initiative) arises through the "perceived environmental compatibility — place dependence — place identity — compliance/active environmentally responsible behavior" pathway. That is, when the recreationists have a sense of belonging to the environment and enjoy themselves in it. Although there is no direct positive impact on environmentally responsible behavior, it will have a dependence on the place, which coincides with the concept of place dependence as a sense of belonging to the place [26,58].

Discussion

Combined with the main conclusions of this study, the following suggestions are provided for the planning and management of tourist destinations in promoting urban recreationists’ environmental protection behaviors.

The functional planning of tourism and leisure places is combined with structural planning. Tourist and leisure places should not only pay attention to the order and location of scenic spots, roads, facilities, etc. and meet the basic needs of people, but also pay attention to landscape design to meet people's physical and mental needs. Taking urban parks as an example, landscape design should have a large number of green plants and be part of the water environment, and it should not only facilitate supporting facilities for recreation and leisure but also create a comfortable and vibrant green environment. In such a harmonious environment of landscape elements, it is easier for recreational people to have awareness and behaviors that do not destroy the environment.

Improving the charm of the park and enhancing the attractiveness of tourists. Urban park planners should try to avoid homogenization of the park environment when planning and designing the park, and an ecologically friendly environment is the premise of a charming environment, and this charm can enhance the environmental protection behavior of the recreation. Therefore, scenic spot management planners should pay attention to increasing interesting environmental protection slogans and providing unique park scenery for recreation through garden planning and other means so that recreationists are willing to spend more time exploring interesting scenery or amusement activities in scenic spots. For example, the addition of modern horse-drawn carriages, small trains, and other equipment to the sightseeing facilities can attract more recreationists to participate in leisure activities or to hold fun activities, parent-child games, etc. so that the recreationists have more excellent memories and associations, providing park charm, which helps the recreationists have compliance with environmentally responsible behavior.

Focusing on creating an emotional attachment environment. Starting from scenic service, landscape quality, service facilities and equipment, safety, and other aspects, it encourages recreationists to maintain a pleasant mood in the leisure process, improve the emotional experience of tourists, and then enhance their place attachment, as a result of which recreationists can live in a comfortable and family version of the emotional experience to promote environmental protection behavior.

Paying attention to landscape compatibility and creating a harmonious tour environment. It can be concluded that when the recreationist community perceives the environment as compatible, that is, when they feel that they can enjoy themselves in the park, and what they want to do and the purposes they want to achieve just match the park environment, they will not only rely on a place to increase the rate of revisiting but also further identify with the place so as to take the initiative to protect the environment of the tourist place. The creation of landscape compatibility requires management planners to focus on excavating the target population of tourist destinations and their tour needs.

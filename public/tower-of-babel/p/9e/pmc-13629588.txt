Predictors for intrapartum cesarean delivery after labor induction for suspected fetal growth restriction at term
Authors: Matan Anteby, Yael Reichman, Alon Bar‐Or, Jacky Herzlich, Stav Cohen, Karina Krajden Haratz, Anat Lavie, Liran Hiersch, Yariv Yogev
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveIn term pregnancies with suspected fetal growth restriction (FGR) undergoing induction of labor (IOL), predictors for intrapartum cesarean delivery (CD) remain unclear. We aimed to determine predictors for intrapartum CD in this population.MethodsThis study included a retrospective cohort of singleton pregnancies undergoing IOL at ≥37 weeks for suspected FGR, defined as estimated fetal weight (EFW) or abdominal circumference (AC) <10th percentile, at a tertiary medical center (2011–2019). Maternal characteristics, sonographic measurements, and Doppler parameters were compared between vaginal delivery and intrapartum CD. Multivariable logistic regression and CHAID (Chi‐square Automatic Interaction Detector) decision tree analysis were performed.ResultsOf 428 inductions, 71 (16.6%) resulted in intrapartum CD, predominantly for non‐reassuring fetal heart rate (88.7%). Mean maternal age was 31.3 ± 5.2 years, and 286 patients (66.8%) were nulliparous. Mean EFW was 2364.3 ± 252.7 g, corresponding to the 2.1st percentile. Overall, 273 fetuses (63.8%) were in the <3rd percentile. No differences in fetal biometry or Doppler flows were detected between groups, and neither parameter predicted intrapartum CD. The only independent predictors of intrapartum CD were nulliparity (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] 4.84, 95% confidence interval [CI] 2.26–10.37, P < 0.001) and maternal age (aOR 1.10 per year, 95% CI 1.04 to 1.16, P = 0.002). Decision tree analysis showed a low CD rate in parous patients (6.3%) versus 44.2% in nulliparous patients aged ≥35 years (P < 0.001).ConclusionWhen considering induction of labor for suspected FGR at term, maternal age and parity, rather than fetal size or Doppler status, should guide counseling regarding cesarean risk.Keywords: cesarean delivery, Doppler, fetal biometry, fetal growth restriction, induction of labor, maternal age, parity

INTRODUCTION

Fetal growth restriction (FGR) is associated with increased perinatal morbidity and mortality. 1 , 2 The timing and mode of delivery in pregnancies complicated by FGR are determined by a combination of gestational age, fetal condition, and obstetric factors. 3 , 4 , 5 When pregnancy reaches term, induction of labor is often recommended to prevent neonatal morbidity and mortality. 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 However, induction in this population might be associated with a higher rate of cesarean delivery. 10 , 11

Previous studies have attempted to identify predictors for failed induction or intrapartum cesarean delivery among patients with suspected FGR, often focusing on fetal size or Doppler parameters as the main determinants of outcome. 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 Yet, findings across studies have been inconsistent, and most cohorts included heterogeneous populations combining preterm and term FGR, where the pathophysiology and prognosis differ considerably. 18 , 19 To date, few studies have focused exclusively on term FGR undergoing induction of labor, leaving uncertainty regarding which maternal or fetal factors are most strongly associated with cesarean delivery risk in this specific clinical scenario.

Given this distinction, predictors derived from mixed or preterm cohorts might not apply to cases presenting at term. Thus, we aimed to identify clinical and sonographic factors associated with intrapartum cesarean delivery among patients undergoing induction of labor for suspected FGR at term, focusing on a well‐defined, homogeneous population.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

We conducted a retrospective cohort study between January 2011 and November 2019 at a university‐affiliated tertiary medical center. The center serves as a referral center for high‐risk pregnancies. The study included all consecutive eligible patients with singleton pregnancies who underwent medical induction of labor at ≥37 weeks of gestation for suspected fetal growth restriction defined as an estimated fetal weight (EFW) <10th percentile or abdominal circumference (AC) <10th percentile, with either criterion qualifying for inclusion. 4 Ultrasound and Doppler assessments were performed within 2 weeks prior to the induction decision by experienced obstetric sonographers with demonstrated competency in fetal biometry and Doppler assessment, using a standardized protocol in accordance with professional guidelines. Estimated fetal weight was calculated using the Hadlock formula based on standard fetal biometric measurements, and fetal biometric percentiles were assigned according to the Hadlock fetal growth reference standard. 20 Doppler parameter percentiles were calculated according to the BCNatal calculator. 21

Patients with a prior cesarean delivery, an additional indication for induction other than suspected growth restriction, or missing data were excluded.

Induction of labor was performed according to institutional protocols using pharmacologic or mechanical cervical ripening methods followed by oxytocin infusion when indicated. Mechanical ripening consisted of insertion of a transcervical Foley catheter inflated with 60 mL of saline, often accompanied by oxytocin infusion titrated to achieve adequate uterine contractions. Pharmacologic ripening was achieved using a vaginal 10‐mg slow release dinoprostone vaginal insert (Propess) for up to 24 h or until the onset of labor. Failed induction was defined as the absence of active labor despite at least 24 h of oxytocin infusion following artificial or spontaneous rupture of membranes. Patients were categorized post hoc according to the actual mode of delivery (vaginal delivery or intrapartum cesarean delivery). Indications for intrapartum cesarean delivery were determined by the attending obstetric team, and clinical management followed routine institutional practice, including non‐reassuring fetal heart rate monitoring, failed induction, patient request, compound presentation, cord prolapse, and arrest disorders of labor, including arrest of dilation or descent.

Data were retrieved from the hospital's computerized medical records. Extracted variables included maternal demographics, obstetric history, details of labor and delivery, and fetal sonographic and Doppler parameters. If more than one ultrasound examination was performed during that interval, data from the scan closest to the decision to induce were used for analysis. Cervical examination findings, including Bishop score, were not consistently available in the electronic medical record and, therefore, were not included in the dataset. Proteinuria was defined as a urine protein‐to‐creatinine ratio >0.3 or a 24‐h urinary protein excretion >300 mg.

Intrapartum fever was defined as any maternal temperature ≥38.0°C recorded during labor. Preeclampsia with severe features was defined following American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists criteria. 22 A composite adverse maternal outcome was predefined as the occurrence of any of the following: intrapartum fever, preeclampsia (with or without severe features), postpartum hemorrhage (defined as a hemoglobin drop >3 g/dL), or the need for blood product transfusion.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were compared using t‐test or Mann–Whitney U‐test, and categorical variables with χ 2 or Fisher's exact tests, as appropriate. Variables with a P < 0.05 in univariable analyses were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model to identify independent predictors of intrapartum cesarean delivery. For variables with missing data, analyses were performed using available observations for each variable, and percentages were calculated based on the number of available observations. Adjusted odds ratios (aORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), with VIF >5 considered indicative of significant multicollinearity. Model calibration was evaluated using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness‐of‐fit test.

In addition, decision tree analysis was constructed using the CHAID (Chi‐square Automatic Interaction Detector) algorithm to explore associations between maternal and fetal characteristics and the outcome of intrapartum cesarean delivery. For the CHAID decision tree analysis, stopping rules included a maximum tree depth of 3, a minimum of 100 cases in parent nodes, and 50 cases in child nodes. No validation procedure was applied. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05. Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 29 (IBM, Armonk, NY).

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center (TLV‐0284‐08). Given the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived. The data are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

RESULTS

During the study period, 102 273 deliveries took place at our institution. Of them, 1480 underwent induction of labor due to suspected fetal growth restriction at term. After applying the exclusion criteria, 428 patients met the inclusion criteria for analysis (Figure 1). Of these, 71 (16.6%) patients underwent intrapartum cesarean delivery, primarily due to non‐reassuring fetal heart rate patterns (88.7%).

DISCUSSION

In this retrospective cohort study, we examined predictors of intrapartum cesarean delivery among patients undergoing induction of labor at term for suspected fetal growth restriction. Our findings indicate that maternal characteristics, particularly nulliparity and advanced maternal age, are the main predictors of intrapartum cesarean delivery, whereas fetal biometry, Doppler parameters, and estimated fetal weight percentiles were not significantly associated with delivery outcomes.

In our cohort, the absence of any fetal or sonographic variable predicting intrapartum cesarean delivery might suggest that pregnancies with suspected growth restriction reaching term represent a milder, more resilient subgroup. Severe or pathologic fetal growth restriction typically presents earlier in gestation and is often accompanied by abnormal Doppler findings, prompting preterm delivery. In contrast, fetuses that continue developing to term, despite being small, are likely to represent late‐onset or milder forms of placental dysfunction with preserved cardiovascular compensation.

Several prior studies have evaluated predictors of cesarean delivery in pregnancies with suspected growth restriction. Nwabuobi et al. 12 found higher cesarean rates in SGA (Small for Gestational Age) inductions within a mixed term–preterm cohort, with maternal factors rather than Doppler parameters predicting mode of delivery, although Doppler abnormalities were associated with non‐reassuring fetal heart rate patterns. Kim and Park 18 identified maternal age, maternal height, and fetal biometry as predictors at 37 weeks, but their population was not limited to suspected growth restriction. Cruz‐Martínez et al. 17 reported markedly higher cesarean rates (67.7%) in term SGA fetuses with abnormal brain Doppler, representing a more severe group than typically seen in term suspected growth restriction. Another study 23 with a similar objective also reported higher cesarean rates but included a meaningful proportion of preterm inductions. Overall, findings from these heterogeneous or more severe cohorts cannot be directly extrapolated to term inductions for suspected fetal growth restriction. Our study focused exclusively on patients who underwent induction of labor at term due to suspected fetal growth restriction, defined as an estimated fetal weight below the 10th percentile.

The intrapartum cesarean delivery rate in our cohort was 16.6%. Prior institutional data 24 report a similar proportion of intrapartum cesarean deliveries when defined across all cesarean procedures and different clinical contexts. Although the study populations are not directly comparable, these findings support the interpretation that term suspected growth restriction is not associated with an unusually high rate of intrapartum cesarean delivery.

Our results also highlight that maternal rather than fetal characteristics might be more important when counseling patients about induction outcomes. Nulliparous patients, particularly those above 35 years of age, had a notably higher likelihood of cesarean delivery, whereas multiparous patients had a low rate of only 6.3%. The association of advanced maternal age and CD is consistent with prior reports in cases with and without FGR. 25 , 26 Decision tree analysis reinforced this finding, demonstrating a markedly higher cesarean delivery rate among nulliparous patients over 35 years of age (44.2%), which is also consistent with previous studies describing the association between parity and intrapartum cesarean delivery. 27 This simple stratification might help clinicians provide individualized counseling and anticipate the likelihood of a successful vaginal delivery.

Our findings suggest that routinely used antepartum fetal biometric and Doppler parameters have limited value in predicting intrapartum cesarean delivery among term pregnancies undergoing induction for suspected fetal growth restriction. Future prospective studies might focus on improving the distinction between constitutionally small fetuses and those with clinically significant placental dysfunction at term, beyond size‐based definitions alone. The evaluation of additional Doppler measures, angiogenic biomarkers such as the soluble fms‐like tyrosine kinase 1 to placental growth factor ratio, combined fetal assessments, or intrapartum physiologic markers, including fetal heart rate characteristics, might improve prediction of intrapartum cesarean delivery and related maternal and neonatal morbidity. Integrating such parameters with maternal characteristics and cervical status could allow more individualized delivery planning and reduce unplanned intrapartum cesarean delivery.

The strengths of our study include a clearly defined and homogeneous population, standardized ultrasound and Doppler evaluations performed close to delivery (3.68 ± 3.41 days, Table 2), and the use of both multivariable logistic regression and decision tree analysis. These factors strengthen the internal consistency and clinical applicability of our results. The percentile distribution in our cohort, in which nearly two‐thirds of fetuses were below the third percentile, indicates that the study population consisted largely of true growth‐restricted fetuses rather than constitutionally small fetuses. Importantly, even within this high‐proportion FGR cohort, fetal size category (<3rd vs 3rd–10th percentile) did not differentiate between pregnancies that proceeded to vaginal delivery and those requiring intrapartum cesarean. This reinforces the central finding that fetal biometric severity at term does not reliably predict intrapartum tolerance.

Limitations include the retrospective design, the potential influence of local management practices, and the lack of long‐term neonatal follow‐up. Our findings might not be generalizable to preterm or severe cases of fetal growth restriction with abnormal Doppler studies, which likely represent a different, higher risk spectrum. In addition, cervical status at the time of induction (e.g., Bishop score) was not available for analysis. Because cervical status is known to influence induction outcomes, its absence represents a limitation and reflects documentation practices during the study period. Some neonatal outcomes had missing data because certain laboratory tests were performed according to clinical indications rather than routinely and because documentation was incomplete during parts of the study period. These outcomes should, therefore, be interpreted as descriptive.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, among pregnancies with suspected fetal growth restriction that have reached term, the likelihood of intrapartum cesarean delivery might depend more on maternal characteristics than on fetal size or Doppler status. Once these fetuses reach term, their ability to withstand labor might be greater than commonly assumed. These results suggest that in term pregnancies with suspected fetal growth restriction, maternal characteristics, not fetal biometric or Doppler markers, should be emphasized in counseling regarding the likelihood of cesarean delivery following induction.

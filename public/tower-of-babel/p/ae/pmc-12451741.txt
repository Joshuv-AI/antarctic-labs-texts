Data-Driven Exploration of Critical Factors for Single-Phase High-Entropy Oxide Anode Materials
Authors: Moriyuki Kanno, Toshiaki Taniike, Itaru Honma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
High-entropy oxides (HEOs) are attracting significant attention owing to their compositional tunability and structural robustness. However, the identification of specific compositional combinations that yield a single-phase structure in HEOs remains unclear owing to the immense combinatorial complexity inherent in multielement systems. This study adopts a materials informatics approach that integrates experimental synthesis data with machine learning to identify key compositional factors enabling single-phase HEO formation via solid-state synthesis. This approach extracts compositional rules and constraints favoring the formation of homogeneous rock-salt or spinel phases. Applying these insights allowed the compositional space to be efficiently explored, leading to the successful synthesis of a single-phase cobalt-free HEO exhibiting high reversible capacity and outstanding cycling stability as a lithium-ion battery anode. These findings demonstrate the effectiveness of data-driven methodologies in rational material design and highlight the potential of HEOs as sustainable materials for next-generation energy storage technologies.

High-entropy materials have attracted increasing attention in recent years owing to their unique compositional design and exceptional physical and chemical properties. Unlike conventional materials composed of one or two principal elements, high-entropy materials are typically formed by combining five or more elements in nearly equimolar ratios. This high compositional complexity gives rise to a large configurational entropy, which can stabilize single-phase structures, even in chemically diverse systems. The emergence of this concept has enabled the design of materials with enhanced mechanical strength, thermal stability, and chemical durability, making them promising candidates for applications in energy storage, catalysis, and electronics. −

Among the various types of high-entropy materials, high-entropy oxides (HEOs) in particular have attracted interest owing to their structural diversity and potential for functional applications, including catalysis, − thermoelectric materials, , magnetism, , and energy storage. − HEOs often exhibit unique electronic, ionic, and catalytic properties that arise from the complex interactions among multiple cations in a disordered lattice. The formation and phase stability of single-phase HEOs are governed by a complex interplay of thermodynamic and structural factors. While high configurational entropy serves as a primary stabilizing force, other parameters, often structure-dependent, are crucial for successful synthesis. Several generalizable descriptors have been proposed in the literature for various HEO crystal structures. For example, in A-site high-entropy perovskite oxides, phase stability is affected by size disorder and configurational entropy, with electronegativity difference identified as a key parameter for predicting phase evolution. In systems with the orthorhombic α-PbO2 structure, stability has been attributed to its flexible geometry, which can accommodate a large variance in cation sizes. Furthermore, in high-entropy alloys, factors such as valence electron concentration have been shown to influence the competition between ordered and disordered phases. Despite these efforts, the fundamental principles governing phase stability in many HEO systems, including the rock-salt and spinel structures of interest in this study, remain insufficiently understood. This lack of predictive understanding complicates the discovery and rational design of new HEO compositions, which is critical for advancing their practical applications.

The intrinsic complexity of HEO systems makes comprehensive experimental synthesis and characterization impractical owing to the combinatorial explosion of possible compositions. Likewise, while first-principles calculations can offer valuable insights, their application to such high-dimensional compositional spaces is often computationally prohibitive. To overcome these limitations, data-driven approaches based on materials informatics have gained traction as promising alternatives. By combining experimental data with machine learning models, these methods facilitate the discovery of trends and predictive patterns governing phase stability in multicomponent oxide systems.

While data-driven approaches have been widely used in materials science, their application to HEOs remains limited. One major obstacle is the lack of consistent, high-quality data sets, as phase formation in oxides is strongly influenced by synthesis conditions. The occurrence of amorphous or mixed-phase products under varying processing routes further complicates the development of reliable models. In this study, this issue is addressed by generating a comprehensive data set under unified synthesis conditions. All samples were fabricated using a solid-state method with identical parameters, enabling the systematic evaluation of phase stability and the extraction of generalizable descriptors.

The resulting data set enabled the construction of predictive models with interpretable features. The analysis of feature importance revealed chemically intuitive factors governing single-phase formation, offering insights into the compositional rules underlying HEO stability. These interpretable criteria provide a potential framework for guiding future exploration of multicomponent oxides while reducing the number of required experimental trials.

With growing global demand for efficient energy storage, HEOs have emerged as promising candidates for lithium-ion battery anodes owing to their structural robustness and compositional tunability. − HEOs such as (Mg0.2Co0.2­Ni0.2Cu0.2Zn0.2)­O and (Fe0.2Co0.2­Ni0.2Cr0.2­Mn0.2)3O4 have been explored as anode materials with good cycling stability. However, many of these compositions rely on cobalt, which presents a supply risk and cost concerns. This has led to increasing interest in identifying cobalt-free alternatives with comparable electrochemical performance. −

The absence of cobalt complicates the development of high-performance anodes because cobalt plays a critical role in both electrochemical activity and structural stability. − In many oxide systems, cobalt functions as a redox-active center that facilitates lithium insertion and extraction. Its removal often leads to a reduction in capacity and deterioration in cycling performance. In multicomponent oxides, however, high configurational entropy contributes to the stabilization of single-phase structures, even without cobalt. This entropy effect suppresses phase separation and structural degradation during repeated charge and discharge cycles. Moreover, the presence of multiple redox-active cations broadens the electrochemical window and allows for control over defect-related properties such as lattice strain and oxygen vacancy concentration. These features enhance lithium-ion transport and improve overall rate capability.

In this study, factors that govern single-phase formation in HEOs were extracted through data-driven analysis. The utility of these factors was demonstrated by applying them to the design of cobalt-free compositions, one of which exhibited both high reversible capacity and stable long-term cycling. These findings indicate that the extracted compositional rules can serve as effective guidelines for designing functional multicomponent oxide materials.

To obtain a consistent data set for model development, multielement oxide samples were prepared from five equimolar metal precursors using a solid-state reaction at a fixed temperature of 1000 °C. The precursors were manually milled, pressed into pellets, and sintered for 24 h, followed by natural cooling to room temperature. This temperature was chosen to maintain experimental consistency, aligning with common synthesis conditions reported for single-phase rock-salt and spinel HEOs. − While higher sintering temperatures can promote single-phase formation, particularly for compositions with slow solid-state reaction kinetics or requiring stronger entropy-driven stabilization, they may also induce unintended phase transformations or elemental loss. Therefore, the fixed sintering temperature represents a compromise between promoting phase formation and minimizing adverse effects. It is acknowledged, however, that while a given elemental combination may not yield a single-phase HEO under this specific condition, a single-phase HEO could still be obtained through alternative synthesis methods or by adjusting parameters such as sintering temperature, reaction time, or precursor chemistry. The influence of temperature variation remains an important topic for future studies. The resulting sintered products were characterized using powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) to determine whether single-phase rock-salt or spinel HEOs had formed. Detailed experimental procedures and the criteria for elemental selection are provided in the Supporting Information. A total of 269 five-element combinations were synthesized, and their corresponding phase-formation results were used as the training data set for subsequent machine learning analysis.

For the machine learning classification model, a random forest algorithm was employed to identify key descriptors governing the formation of single-phase HEOs. The target variable was binary, indicating whether the synthesized sample formed a single-phase rock-salt or spinel structure. As input features, a comprehensive set of 58 elemental physical properties was obtained from XenonPy. For each five-element composition, statistical descriptors, including arithmetic mean, synergistic mean, geometric mean, variance, and rangedefined as the difference between maximum and minimum valueswere calculated. Our machine learning analysis showed that the highest balanced accuracy was obtained using the range of Pyykkö’s covalent radius and the range of atomic weight as input features. This combination resulted in a balanced accuracy of ∼ 0.85. As shown in Figure , which plots the range of Pyykkö’s covalent radius against the range of atomic weight, elemental combinations that formed single-phase rock-salt or spinel HEOs were mainly located within narrow regions. These regions correspond to covalent radius ranges below 30 and atomic weight ranges below 59. The observed clustering indicates important compositional constraints for the formation of stable single-phase HEOs.

Distribution of elemental combinations classified as single-phase spinel, single-phase rock-salt, or other, plotted against the ranges of covalent radii and atomic weights.

The observed preference for a small range of covalent radii in single-phase HEOs can be attributed to the reduction of crystal lattice distortion. A large variation in atomic radii among the constituent elements tends to introduce significant lattice strain, which destabilizes the single-phase structure and promotes phase separation. , This behavior is characteristic of densely packed, coordination-sensitive structures such as the rock-salt and spinel phases examined in this study. In contrast, more open crystal frameworks, such as fluorite-type high-entropy oxides, exhibit a different stability criterion. In these system, previous studies have shown that a large variation in cationic radii can favor single-phase stability, which is attributed to the open anion sublattice and the high coordination flexibility of the cation sites. , These structural features allow the fluorite lattice to accommodate size mismatch without severe lattice distortion, a property not shared by the rock-salt and spinel frameworks studied here. This contrast in tolerance to atomic size mismatch underscores the structural dependence of stability criteria in multicomponent oxide systems.

The range of atomic weight is also a crucial factor in the formation of single-phase HEOs. Atomic weight differences among the constituent elements affect diffusion kinetics during high-temperature synthesis, as elements with lower atomic weights tend to exhibit higher diffusion coefficients than those with higher atomic weights. This disparity in diffusion rates can lead to spatial variations in composition, which may induce phase separation and prevent the formation of a homogeneous solid solution. In addition, variations in atomic weight can disrupt lattice vibrations by introducing phonon-mode mismatches. − These vibrational instabilities reduce the thermodynamic stability of the system and may compromise the stability of the single-phase structure. Considering both effects, maintaining a narrow atomic weight range helps to balance diffusion behavior and preserve vibrational coherence, which together support the stabilization of single-phase HEOs.

Based on insights derived from the machine learning model, a specific cobalt-free HEO composition was selected for the synthesis and evaluation as a lithium-ion battery anode. The selection was guided by the phase-formation criteria identified in this study, particularly the covalent radius range below 30 and atomic weight range below 59. A combination of Ti, Mn, Ni, Cu, and Zn was chosen for synthesis. As shown in Figure , XRD analysis confirmed the formation of a single-phase spinel HEO with the composition (Ti0.2­Mn0.2­Ni0.2­Cu0.2­Zn0.2)3O4. This result supports the predictive capability of the materials informatics approach in identifying single-phase HEOs within a large compositional space.

To evaluate the electrochemical performance of (Ti0.2Mn0.2­Ni0.2­Cu0.2Zn0.2)3O4 as a lithium-ion battery anode, galvanostatic charge–discharge tests were conducted at a current density of 1.0 A g–1 in the range of 0.01–3 V (vs Li/Li+). The upper voltage limit was set to 3.0 V to reflect the practical operating range of anode materials in lithium-ion batteries. Potentials above this limit correspond to cathode-type operation and may induce irreversible structural changes or electrolyte decomposition, , which are not representative of the material’s performance in its intended application. As shown in Figure (a) and (b), the discharge capacity was well maintained, even under prolonged cycling, with a capacity retention of 87 mA h g–1 after 500 cycles. Furthermore, as shown in Figure (b), the Coulombic efficiency remained close to 100% throughout the cycling test, indicating good reversibility and cycling stability. During the early cycles, efficiency is lower owing to irreversible processes such as solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) formation on the anode surface and other reactions that consume active lithium. −

Electrochemical characterization of (Ti0.2Mn0.2­Ni0.2­Cu0.2Zn0.2)3O4: (a) charge–discharge curve after 500 cycles at 1 A g–1, (b) discharge capacity and Coulombic efficiency over cycles at 1 A g–1, (c) charge–discharge curves at varying current densities, (d) rate capability, and (e) cyclic voltammetry curves from 0.01 to 3.0 V at 0.1 mV/s.

Figure (c) shows the charge–discharge curves of the HEO electrodes at varying rates of 0.05–2 A g–1. The (Ti0.2­Mn0.2­Ni0.2­Cu0.2Zn0.2)3O4 anode exhibits two discharge plateaus in the range of 0.5–1.0 V and below 0.5 V. The higher-voltage plateau is associated with lithium intercalation, while the lower plateau corresponds to conversion reactions involving the transition metal components. The lower rate has a higher and wider position of the first discharge plateau associated with the lithium insertion reaction, which is attributed to a more complete electrochemical reaction process at a lower current density. In addition, Figure (d) shows the rate performance results from 0.05 to 2 A g–1, where the discharge capacity decreases with increasing current owing to kinetic limitations. Notably, when the current density was returned to 0.05 A g–1, the capacity recovered to ∼ 220 mA h g–1, demonstrating good rate capability and excellent capacity retention.

Figure (e) shows the cyclic voltammetry (CV) curves of the (Ti0.2Mn0.2­Ni0.2Cu0.2­Zn0.2)3O4 electrode measured between 0.01 and 3.0 V (vs Li/Li+) at a scan rate of 0.1 mV s–1. In the first cathodic scan, reduction peaks appear at 1.15, 0.62, and 0.15 V, which are attributed to the reduction of transition metals, the formation of Li2O, and the formation of an SEI layer on the electrode surface owing to electrolyte decomposition. , In subsequent cycles, although the reaction peak is still visible in the second cycle, the CV curve is almost unchanged from that point onward, indicating the consistency and reversibility of the lithiation and delithiation reactions.

In summary, this study integrated experimental synthesis data with machine learning to identify compositional factors governing the formation of single-phase rock-salt or spinel HEOs. Using the refined compositional space derived from this approach, a cobalt-free HEO with favorable electrochemical performance was proposed as a potential anode material for lithium-ion batteries, demonstrating the practicality of the model. Notably, the cobalt-free composition presented in this work was eventually incorporated into the training data set. As such, although the model successfully identified a high-performance candidate, further investigation is needed to assess its predictive capability for unknown compositions. These findings provide a useful framework for the rational design of multicomponent oxides and may support the broader development of advanced functional materials.

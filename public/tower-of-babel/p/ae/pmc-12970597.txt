Spiked helmet sign in a patient with cardiac metastasis
Authors: Jianliang Yin, Xianqing Hu, Jinhong Liu, Wenbin Zhang, Ya Li, Takeshi Kitai, Maria Lucia Narducci, Yaser Alahmad
Published: 2026

Case description

A 52-year-old male presented with low-grade fever and persistent chest pain 3 months following resection of a penile squamous cell carcinoma (postoperative pathological stage T3N2M0). Electrocardiogram revealed ST-segment elevation with an upward shift starting before the onset of QRS complexes in the V1 and V2 leads (Figure 1A). Echocardiography demonstrated a hypoechoic mass of ∼44 × 43 mm in the apical region of the right ventricular apex and a moderate pericardial effusion (Figure 1B). Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging revealed a mass of 49 × 34 mm in the right ventricular apex extending to the interventricular septum and pericardium, with associated pericardial thickening and haemopericardium, and left ventricular ejection fraction decline to 42% (Figure 1C–E). The positron emission tomography showed abnormal uptake of fluorodeoxyglucose in the areas of right ventricular apex, interventricular septum, diffusely thickened pericardium, and mediastinal lymph nodes station 5 (Figure 1F). Pericardiocentesis revealed turbid reddish pericardial fluid with significantly elevated tumour markers: carcinoembryonic antigen 25.85 ng/mL, carbohydrate antigen 19-9 201.52 U/mL, cancer antigen 125 229.46 U/mL, and cytokeratin 19 fragment 23.8 ng/mL.

Multiple-panel image illustrating the following: (A) Electrocardiogram showing ST-segment elevation with an upward shift starting before the onset of QRS complexes in the V1 and V2 leads (arrow), complete right bundle branch block, ST-segment elevation (0.10–0.25 mV) in V1–V6, I, and aVL leads; (B) echocardiogram demonstrating a hypoechoic mass of ∼44 × 43 mm at the right ventricular apex (arrow), with moderate pericardial effusion; (C) cine image demonstrating a mass (49 × 34 mm, arrow) in the right ventricular apex extending to the interventricular septum and pericardium, with heterogeneous hyperintensity on T1- and T2-weighted sequences; (D) late gadolinium enhancement cardiac magnetic resonance imaging showing heterogeneous enhancement (arrow) within the mass during first-pass perfusion post-gadolinium administration; (E) diffusion-weighted imaging demonstrating high signal (arrow); (F) positron emission tomography scans showing abnormal uptake of fluorodeoxyglucose in the areas of right ventricular apex, interventricular septum, thickened pericardium, and mediastinal lymph nodes station 5.

A clinical diagnosis of cardiac metastasis with ‘Spiked helmet sign’ was made after a multidisciplinary discussion. The family of the patient declined further aggressive anti-tumour therap. His condition deteriorated, culminating in cardiac arrest and death despite resuscitative efforts.

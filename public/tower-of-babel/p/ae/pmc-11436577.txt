Unraveling the immune landscape of lung adenocarcinoma: insights for tailoring therapeutic approaches
Authors: Changjiang Wu, Wangshang Qin, Wenqiang Lu, Jingyu Lin, Hongwei Yang, Chunhong Li, Yiming Mao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), a prevalent type of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), was known for its diversity and intricate tumor microenvironment (TME). Comprehending the interaction among human immune-related genes (IRGs) and the TME is vital in the creation of accurate predictive models and specific treatments. We created a risk score based on IRGs and designed a nomogram to predict the prognosis of LUAD accurately. This involved a thorough examination of TME and the infiltration of immune cells in both high-risk and low-risk LUAD groups. Furthermore, the examination of the association between characteristic genes (BIRC5 and BMP5) and immune cells, along with immune checkpoints in the TME, was also conducted. The findings of our research unveiled unique immune profiles and interactions among individuals in the high- and low-risk categories, which contribute to variations in prognosis. LUAD demonstrated significant associations between BIRC5, BMP5, immune cells, and checkpoints, suggesting their involvement in disease advancement and resistance to medication. Furthermore, by correlating our findings with a multidrug database, we identified specific LUAD patient subsets that might benefit from tailored treatments. Our study establishes a groundbreaking prognostic model for LUAD, which not only underscores the importance of the immune context in LUAD but also paves the way for advancing precision medicine strategies in this complex malignancy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01396-0.

Background

Lung cancer, recognized globally as the malignancy with the highest mortality, has continually been the focus of substantial scientific scrutiny [1]. Within the spectrum of lung cancers, lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) represents approximately 40%-50% of cases, predominantly affecting females as well as non-smoking male patients [2]. A majority of LUAD patients have distant metastases, contributing to a 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of merely 6%-15% [3]. In the last ten years, the application of focused treatment and immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) has greatly improved the outlook for LUAD, and studies have found that NSCLC patients with PD-L1-positive alterations can derive clinical benefit from immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) [4]. However, a subset of patients still develops treatment resistance to traditional and novel therapy, leading to disease progression, which might be attributed to tumor heterogeneity and differences in tumor immune microenvironments (TME) [5]. Thus, exploring immune cell subpopulations at the genetic level is of significant importance for determining treatment response and identifying prognostic biomarkers.

The substantial heterogeneity of LUAD is apparent through the numerous mutations found in each cancer genome, including single nucleotide variants (SNVs), copy number alterations (CNAs), gene fusions, and significant chromosomal events, forming the basis of different responses to drug treatments [6]. In addition, LUAD has a complicated TME landscape, including various immune cells and stromal cells. In this scenario, the cells are capable of influencing the expression of key signaling molecules, which in turn contributes to the proliferation of tumors. This process presents an ongoing obstacle for the effectiveness of immunotherapy treatments [7, 8]. Benefiting from the advancements in second-generation sequencing technology, scholars first developed an RNA sequence-based prognostic signature for LUAD in 2016 [9], offering a robust tool to enhance precision in oncology treatments. Afterwards, Wang et al. [10] developed a predictive nomogram for LUAD using genes related to immune-infiltrating Treg cells. Furthermore, another study also demonstrated that LUAD precursor lesions and invasive forms exhibit distinct genomic mutations and a link between chromosomal changes and immune response, highlighting the importance of genetic changes in TME [11]. All these studies have identified new biomarkers for the diagnosis of LUAD and stratified treatments.

However, previous studies mainly focused on prognostic immune-related genes (IRGs) without addressing the assessment of the response to ICBs and targeted therapy, which may result in a biased selection of therapeutic regimens. Our study endeavors to construct an immune-related genes-based nomogram, and identify hub genes as key biomarkers for stratifying LUAD via multiple computational methods, thereby guiding treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

Data preprocessing

As a training cohort, we obtained raw bulk transcriptome count data, clinical survival, and phenotype data for 526 LUAD and 59 normal samples from the Cancer Genome Atlas Program (TCGA, https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). As for the independent validation cohorts, we obtained GSE72094 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE72094), GSE30219 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE30219), and GSE31210 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE31210) from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO, http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo). From ImmPort (https://www.immport.org.home), a grand total of 2483 IRGs were obtained.

Differential expression analysis

Differentially expressed immune-related genes (DEIRGs) were determined by utilizing the 'limma' R package [12] and then analyzed with Fisher's exact test, considering |log2 (FC)|> 1 and a p-value threshold < 0.05. We utilized the STRING [13] to examine the (protein–protein interaction, PPI) networks of identified DEIRGs and then visualized the resulting PPI networks using Cytoscape (v3.8.2) [14]. Subsequently, we utilized "MCODE" plugin in Cytoscape to detect key modules and "Cytohubba" plugin with five commonly used algorithms (EPC, Radiality, Degree, Closeness, and MNC) to identify hub DEIRGs. Finally, we used NetworkAnalyst 3.0 [15] to predict the transcription factors of hub DEIRGs and construct an interaction network.

Tumor immune microenvironment analysis

To measure immune infiltration condition of LUAD, we assessed immune cell infiltration using the R software package called 'IOBR' [16]. This analysis was based on nine different algorithms: Microenvironment Cell Populations-counter (MCP-counter) algorithm [17], EPIC algorithm [18], xCell algorithm [19], CIBERSORT algorithm [20], IPS algorithm [21], quanTIseq algorithm [22], ESTIMATE algorithm [23], TIMER algorithm [24], and ssGSEA algorithm [25]. Furthermore, the clinical response to ICBs in LUAD was assessed using Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE, http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/), providing a TIDE score.

Identification of IRG-related clusters

We performed consensus clustering based on TCGA-LUAD cohort using the R-package "ConsensusClusterPlus" [26]. By evaluating the expression levels of the 9-overlapping hub DEIRGs, we discovered distinct groups within the TCGA-LUAD cohort. We visualized the survival results of these different clusters through R-package "Survival".

Weighted gene co‑expression network analysis (WGCNA)

To search for DEIRGs that are closely associated with identified novel clusters, we constructed a WGCNA analysis using the R-package "WGCNA" [27] to identify immune-related modules with the highest association with clusters. To begin with, in order to fulfill the requirements for building a co-expression network, a suitable threshold β value was calculated. Afterwards, the weighted adjacency matrix was transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM). In addition, the dissimilarity (dissTOM) was calculated. For module detection, we employed dynamic tree-cutting method. By analyzing the 10 gene modules that had been identified, we finally selected the turquoise module with the strongest positive correlation with the cluster for subsequent advanced analysis. Then, DEIRGs exhibiting high GS and MM were designated as key DEIRGs.

Development of prognostic signature

We utilized the univariate Cox regression to assess the influence of crucial DEIRGs on survival of LUAD patients. To prevent any possible mistakes, we adjusted the critical p-value threshold to 0.05. Furthermore, machine learning algorithms, namely Lasso regression, random forest (RF), and XGBoost [28] were unitized to select the most valuable DEIRGs by extracting the intersected genes identified from the three machine learning algorithms. BIRC5 and BMP5 were selected as the characteristic genes. The riskScore = 0.1706554 × expression (BIRC5) − 0.1344533 × expression (BMP5). On the basis of median risk score, we classified patients with LUAD into groups of low and high risk. We performed Kaplan–Meier analysis using the "survival" R software package to explore survival and riskScore, which were further validated by the GSE72094, GSE30219, and GSE31210 cohorts.

Construction of the nomogram

Clinical features of LUAD individuals (age, gender, clinicopathologic stage, and riskScore) were combined to create a prognostic nomogram by multivariable Cox and stepwise regression analyses. Nomograms and calibration plots are visualized by the "rms" software package. Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) was unitized on LUAD patients via R-package "timeROC" [29]. Afterwards, the prognostic model was subjected to correlation and stratification analyses using the provided clinical characteristic parameters. In addition, we assessed the net benefit of combining nomograms with models containing only clinical characteristics by decision curve analysis (DCA). Furthermore, multivariate Cox regression was conducted to evaluate if riskScore could be an independent factor for prognosis.

Gene set enrichment analysis

To detect the underlying mechanisms, we performed GSEA analyses using the "clusterProfiler" [30], "enrichplot" and "GseaVis" R software packages. The screening criteria was NES greater than 1, with a nominal p-value less than 0.05. Reference genomes included the hallmark gene sets (H) and ontology gene sets (C5).

Drug sensitivity analysis

To personalize the treatment, we predicted the chemotherapy sensitivity of LUAD patients via R-package "oncoPredict" [31]. Half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) was calculated via expression profiles. For the purpose of examining the distinction of drug IC50 in different groups, Wilcoxon test was utilized, with a P < 0.05. For enhancing the precision of the drug sensitivity, we examined expression and sensitivity to drugs of the two identified DEIRGs by utilizing the GDSC, CTRP and CellMiner databases on the GSCALite platform (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/GSCA/#/) [32].

Identification of the immunotherapy efficacy

We utilized The Cancer Immunome Atlas (TCIA, https://tcia.at/home) [21] to evaluate the potential response to checkpoint immunotherapy in patients with LUAD. Furthermore, we conducted a comprehensive analysis that considered the correlation between Immunophenoscores (IPS) scores for anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA4 therapies in LUAD patients and riskScore.

Statistical analysis

Survival analyses were plotted via Kaplan–Meier technique. For the purpose of comparing multiple or two groups, we employed either Wilcoxon test or Kruskal-Walli’s test. Spearman correlation was utilized to assess the correlation. We deemed the findings to be statistically significant if the P value was < 0.05. R (v4.3.0) was utilized for all statistical analyses.

Evaluation of the immune state in LUAD patients

To harness the potential of immunotherapy, we assessed the immune status in LUAD based on risk scores. Initially, we compared stroma scores, immune scores, ESTIMATE scores, and tumor purity between two groups. The low-risk group, in contrast to the high-risk group, showed significantly higher stroma scores, immune scores, and ESTIMATE scores, alongside reduced tumor purity (Fig. 5A–D). We then conducted ssGSEA algorithm analyses of various immune cells, calculated according to the risk group (Fig. 5E). To avoid bias, we analyzed the correlation between immune scores and immune cells, using different software. This analysis affirmed the consistency and precision of our results (Fig. 5F). We also evaluated the correlation between characteristic genes (BMP5 and BIRC5) and types of cells infiltrating the TME (Fig. 5G-I). Notably, BIRC5 correlated most strongly with activated CD4 T cells and memory B cells (Fig. 5H), while BMP showed the highest correlation with eosinophils and mast cells (Fig. 5I).

In addition, we calculated the TIDE score, T-cell dysfunction score, T-cell exclusion score, and microsatellite instability (MSI) score to predict immunotherapy efficacy (Figure S6A-D). However, significant differences were only observed in the T-cell exclusion score between the groups (Figure S6C). The Immune Phenotype Score (IPS) was calculated to predict responses to checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD1 and anti-CTLA4), revealing notably higher levels of CTLA4_neg_PD1_neg and CTLA4_pos_PD1_neg in the low-risk group compared to the high-risk group (Figure S6E-H). Figure S6I illustrates a marked difference in the activation of immune-related pathways between these groups. Moreover, we investigated the correlations of genes (BMP5 and BIRC5) (Figure S6J) and risk groups (Figure S6K) with common checkpoints, aiming to uncover the relationship between riskScore and the immune landscape.

Discussion

LUAD, as a subtype with high mortality of NSCLC, known for its high mortality rate, is managed using a multidisciplinary approach that has undergone significant advancements in recent years [33, 34]. Common forms of treatment comprise surgical procedures, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy. Surgery is typically considered for early-stage lung adenocarcinoma, while for patients with advanced LUAD, traditional chemotherapy containing cytotoxic drugs was once the first-line option [33]. Over the last ten years, the development of focused treatments and immunotherapy has completely transformed the management of LUAD and significantly enhanced the outlook for individuals with LUAD. However, not all LUAD patients could benefit from new therapies, which mainly depend on the tumor genetics [35]. Patients who have mutations in EGFR [36], rearrangements in ALK [35], and mutations in ROS1 [37] may find drugs such as erlotinib, gefitinib, and osimertinib as well as crizotinib and alectinib beneficial. Another group of patients with high PD-1 expression may benefit from immune checkpoint blockers pembrolizumab and nivolumab [38]. These treatments can be used alone or in combination with chemotherapy. Hence, it is highly important to develop a strong and efficient forecasting model that relies on the molecular attributes of the tumor in order to accurately predict the prognosis and make informed choices regarding treatment.

The identification of the specific immune resistance mechanisms employed by LUAD can significantly propel the advancement of personalized treatments. In our study, 9 hub IRGs (CXCL12, IL1A, IL1B, IL6, IL13, EGF, MMP9, AGT, TLR4) were identified. In LUAD, Zhu et al. [39] demonstrated that the presence of CXCL12 derived from CAFs in tumor tissues could result in upregulating PD-L1. IL 1A and IL1B are two components in the IL1 signaling pathway, however, IL1B is mainly expressed on innate immune cells while IL1A can be expressed on both normal and pathological cells. Previous study has shown IL1R can increase the incidence of human squamous cell carcinomas [40], while the mechanism in LUAD remains unclear. Scholars have discovered that IL6, a transcription 3 (STAT3) activator, can enhance progression of KRAS-driven LUAD and is associated with a poorer prognosis, and the KRAS exon 2 p. G12C mutation in LUAD patients has been increasing [41]. Type 2 T cells and mast cells secrete IL13, which has the ability to regulate inflammation responses. EGF is a primary ligand for EGFR, and EGFR-mutant LUAD patients are more prone to disease progression, which is an important clinical issue currently [36]. Type 2 T cells and mast cells secrete IL13, which has the ability to control both inflammation and immune responses. In contrast to EGF, there is a limited amount of research on IL13 in LUAD. Nevertheless, Vallera and his team developed a powerful anti-pancreatic cancer drug that targets both human IL13 and EGF receptors. They successfully showed its effectiveness in a mouse xenograft experiment [42], indicating that the interaction between these two receptors may also exert a function in the development of LUAD. Enrichment results also confirmed the DEIRGs in LUAD involving in immune related pathways, like TNFA SIGNALING VIA NFKB and IFNLAMMATORY RESPONSE. Notably, C4 and C5 seemed to have higher StromaScore, ImmuneScore, and ESTIMATEScore than the other clusters while showing better survival. Furthermore, the DEIRGs analysis revealed that only Tfh cells exhibited notable variances within the five clusters. This finding aligns with a previous investigation indicating the involvement of Tfh in the immune response to LUAD antigens [43]. Hence, it can be inferred that these clusters potentially represent distinct phases of tumor immunity.

Following the application of three machine learning techniques, BIRC5 and BMP5 emerged as the distinguished genes among the DEIRGs. Survivin, which is encoded by BIRC5, belongs to the family of inhibitor-of-apoptosis proteins (IAPs). It can effectively control both the processes of mitosis and apoptosis during embryonic development and the progression of malignant diseases [44]. Recently, Ma et al. [45] proved that BIRC5 was overexpressed on LUAD samples with worse survival, and xenograft mice overexpressing BIRC5 were also accompanied by an increase in PD-L1. In another study, BIRC5 was also identified as an EGFR-TKI resistance gene [46]. The results indicate that BIRC5 has a significant impact on the risk linked to LUAD, as demonstrated by the greater concentration of BIRC5 in high-risk group observed in our investigation. According to a prior investigation, BMP5, which belongs to the bone BMPs group, was discovered to be down-regulated in NSCLC [47]. Additionally, it has been demonstrated that the suppression of BMP5 is responsible for mediating the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in breast cancer induced by TGF-β1 [48]. However, no one has elaborated on its specific protection mechanism in LUAD. Furthermore, our research reveals a connection between BIRC5 and stimulated CD4 T cells, along with memory B cells. Additionally, there is a link between BMP5 and eosinophils and mast cells. This could potentially guide future research into the roles of BIRC5 and BMP5 in LUAD.

How to screen people who are sensitive to targeted drugs is a clinically important issue. Our research findings indicate that low-risk group exhibited a higher presence of CTLA4_pos_PD1_neg than high-risk group, implying the administration of anti-CTLA4 checkpoint treatment could potentially be advantageous for low-risk group. Fortunately, numerous drugs show potential benefits for the high-risk group, including afuresertib (AKT inhibitor), axitinib (TKI inhibitor) and AZD6482 (PI3Kβ inhibitor), although these drugs are currently in the research phase, they offer new possibilities for treating LUAD.

To summarize, our research initially developed a risk assessment and graphical representation using IRGs to accurately predict prognosis in LUAD. Afterwards, we explored the tumor microenvironment (TME) in both groups in order to understand the immunological factors that contribute to the differences in their prognosis. Moreover, we explored the connections between the crucial genes BIRC5 and BMP5 and different immune cells and checkpoints in the TME. Our objective was to uncover their possible contributions to the advancement and resistance to drugs in LUAD, providing valuable perspectives for future research directions. In conclusion, through the integration of an extensive database containing multiple drug information with both high- and low-risk groups, we have successfully pinpointed particular patients with LUAD who could potentially experience positive treatment outcomes. This achievement lays the foundation for guiding strategies in the treatment of LUAD. Nonetheless, our study is not without its limitations. Firstly, the findings of our research necessitate validation in a more extensive cohort to enhance their robustness. Secondly, the interplay between tumor cells and the TME constitutes a multifaceted process. Our analysis, in this regard, did not extend to distinguishing tumor cells from immune cells at a single-cell resolution. Lastly, the conclusions drawn from our study still require substantiation through fundamental experimental verification, to solidify their applicability and accuracy.

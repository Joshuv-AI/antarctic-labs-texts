Extensive ischemic change on stroke presentation is uncommon: A population-based study
Authors: Yasmin N Aziz, Achala Vagal, Heidi Sucharew, Brady J Williamson, Vivek Khandwala, Lily Wang, Rebecca Cornelius, Mary F Gaskill-Shipley, Thomas A Tomsick, David T Wang, Shantala Gangatirkar, Thomas Maloney, Paul S Horn, Mary Haverbusch, Janice A Carrozzella, David J Robinson, Robert J Stanton, Eva A Mistry, Brett Kissela, Pooja Khatri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Acute ischemic stroke (AIS) treatment and prognosis depend on the extent of ischemia sustained at time of presentation. The Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) is used for anterior circulation stroke triage and decision making. We sought to determine the distribution of baseline ASPECTS at a population level.Methods:Clinical imaging was collected from hospitalized AIS patients ascertained in a metropolitan population of 1.3 million as part of the Greater Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky Stroke Study. Patients presenting to an emergency department (ED) in 2015 within 24 hours of anterior circulation ischemic stroke were included. Events were excluded if last known normal (LKN) was unavailable, occurred in the inpatient setting, if CTH was unavailable, or if a previous ischemic event was already adjudicated during the study period. Blinded central neuroradiologists evaluated presenting ASPECTS. National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score ≥6 was used as a surrogate for large vessel occlusion, as routine baseline vessel imaging was performed inconsistently during the 2015 study period.Results:Among 1,518 events of AIS presenting within 24 hours of LKN, 949 were anterior circulation first-time events with baseline ASPECTS status available, of which 520 (55 %) had baseline vessel imaging. Among these 949 patients (mean age 71 [SD 14] years, median ED arrival time 5.2 [IQR 1.3-13.2] hours, median NIHSS 4 [IQR 1-9]), 920 (96.9 %) had an ASPECTS ≥6, of which 844 had ASPECTS 9-10. Of the 373 patients with a NIHSS ≥6 at presentation, 344 (92.2 %) had an ASPECTS ≥6. Of the 150 patients presenting with NIHSS ≥6 beyond 6 hours of LKN time, 129 (86.0 %) had an ASPECTS ≥6.Conclusions:In our population, most patients presenting within 24 hours of LKN had ASPECTS ≥6. Even in patients with NIHSS ≥6, only 14 % of patients presenting in the extended time window had ASPECTS <6. These results can inform resource utilization and clinical trial design.

Introduction

The Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) is a semi-quantitative assessment of acute ischemic change in the anterior circulation.1 The score is comprised of six lobar regions (M1-M6) and four subcortical regions (caudate, lentiform nucleus, internal capsule, insular ribbon) to make a ten-point scale, with one point deducted for early ischemic change detected in each region.1 Initially developed for reproducible assessment of CT scans prior to administration of intravenous thrombolysis, the score is now used as a key imaging criterion in acute stroke clinical trials and in clinical practice for reperfusion therapy broadly.2-6

The ASPECTS conveys the extent of early ischemic change, with implications for treatment options and prognosis.1,3,7,8 Emerging evidence in stroke endovascular therapy (EVT) has placed a renewed emphasis on ASPECTS. Specifically, ASPECTS alone may be sufficient to replace perfusion imaging in patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO) stroke between 6-24 hours from last known normal (LKN) (i.e., the “extended time window”)9, and recent randomized clinical trials show a benefit of thrombectomy even in patients with ASPECTS <6 (i.e., the “large core” trials).10-15 Understanding the distribution of ASPECTS in patients presenting with ischemic stroke is critical to assess clinical trial eligibility and clinical resource preparation.

While some population studies have estimated the rates of LVO16 or core size by >1/3 of the middle cerebral artery distribution to estimate thrombectomy eligibility,17 no study to date has evaluated the distribution of baseline ASPECTS at emergency department (ED) presentation in a population. Our objective was to determine this distribution utilizing the NIH-funded imaging study, Assessing Population-based Radiological brain health in Stroke Epidemiology (APRISE) (NS103824) that is ancillary to the Greater Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky Stroke Study (GCKNSS). We hypothesized that the proportion of patients presenting with anterior circulation AIS and extensive ischemic injury would be low.

Methods

Data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Population

GCNKSS is a prospective population-based study of stroke incidence, risk factors, treatment, and outcomes. APRISE is an ancillary study to the ongoing GCNKSS that adds comprehensive stroke imaging evaluation to the robust clinical and demographic data. The full methodologies of both studies have been previously published.18,19

The GCNKSS encompasses a 5-county region of approximately 1,400 square miles and 1.3 million people representative of the United States in terms of percentage of Black race, age, educational attainment, and socioeconomic status.18 The methods of the GCNKSS have been described in detail.18 In brief, all strokes in the region are ascertained using International Classification of Diseases (ICD) Ninth and Tenth Revision diagnosis codes (ICD-9: 430-438; ICD-10: G45-G56, H34.11, H34.12, I60-I69). Study nurses review all potential cases and extract clinical and demographic data with a standardized case report form.18 Physicians adjudicate and subtype each case. Only acute ischemic strokes were included in this analysis. The study is approved by institutional review boards at all hospitals with a waiver from informed consent.

The current investigation is a retrospective analysis of adult patients (≥18 years) presenting to the ED within 24 hours of last known normal (LKN) with anterior circulation distribution ischemic stroke in study epoch 2015. Patients with transient symptoms but neuroimaging confirming acute diffusion restriction were adjudicated as ischemic stroke. Cases were excluded when LKN was unavailable, an initial event was already adjudicated during the study period, the event occurred in the inpatient or outpatient setting, or if the event did not have a presenting CT head (CTH).

Study variables

Patient demographic and clinical variables included sex, race, age, time from LKN, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), and stroke etiology. ASPECTS was derived from the initial non contrast CTH by subspecialty-certified radiologists following a formalized re-training process.19 The presence of anterior circulation large vessel occlusion (LVO) was defined as occlusion of the intracranial segment of the internal carotid artery (ICA) or the M1 segment of the middle cerebral artery. Presence of LVO was inconsistently collected based on the absence of routine vessel imaging during the study period. Baseline vessel imaging was defined as first CT or MR angiogram performed at hospital presentation.

Outcomes

The outcome of interest was ASPECTS on initial CT scan at ED presentation. All baseline CT imaging was centrally read by board-certified and study-trained radiologists of the APRISE study, who were blinded to clinical information.

Statistical analysis

This was a descriptive analysis of presenting ASPECTS in a population. Stroke patients were categorized into four ASPECTS groups (ASPECTS 0-2, 3-5, 6-8, and 9-10) based on clinically relevant cut-off points.3,20 Demographics and clinical characteristics were summarized using median (interquartile range (IQR)) for continuous variables and count (percentage) for categorical variables both overall and by ASPECTS groups. The number and percentage of patients in each ASPECTS group was calculated overall and among patients with NIHSS ≥6 stratified by time from LKN (≤6 hours from LKN versus >6 hours from LKN). A sensitivity analysis to determine ASPECTS of patients with and without vessel imaging was performed.

All analyses were performed using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results

There were 2,187 ischemic stroke events (by clinical or imaging diagnosis) among adult patients presenting to the ED in the 2015 study epoch of the GCNKSS. Of these events, 542 were excluded due to LKN time >24 hours, 127 were excluded due to unknown LKN time, 534 were excluded due to non-anterior circulation stroke, and 15 were excluded due to absence of baseline CT for ASPECTS calculation (Fig. 1).

Of the remaining 969 events, 20 were excluded as second stroke events during the study period. Among the remaining 949 patients, the mean age was 71 (SD 14) years, 55 % were female, and 22 % were Black patients. The median ED arrival time was 5.2 (IQR 1.3-13.2) hours with median NIHSS 4 (IQR 1-9). Stroke etiologies consisted of 139 (15 %) events due to large artery atherosclerosis, 298 (31 %) cardioembolic, 144 (15 %) small vessel, 20 (2 %) other, and 348 (37 %) undetermined. In our cohort of 949 patients, 520 (55 %) had baseline vessel imaging and 429 (45 %) did not have vessel imaging. Of the 520 with baseline vessel imaging, 262 were CT angiograms and 258 were MR angiograms.

Among all 949 patients presenting within 24 hours of LKN, 844 (88.9 %) had ASPECTS of 9-10, followed by 76 (8.0 %) with ASPECTS 6-8, 19 (2.0 %) with ASPECTS 3-5, and 10 (1.1 %) with ASPECTS 0-2 (Table 1). Among the 448 patients presenting between 12-24 hours of LKN, 413 (92 %) had ASPECTS 9-10, followed by 25 (6 %) with ASPECTS 6-8, 5 (1 %) with ASPECTS 3-5, and 5 (1 %) with ASPECTS 0-2.

Of all included patients, 429 (45 %) were without baseline vessel imaging. Compared to the overall rate of 3.1 % in the total cohort, the rate of ASPECTS 0-5 was 1.3 % in patients with vessel imaging and 5.1 % in those without vessel imaging (Table 2).

Among 949 patients, 373 had a NIHSS ≥6. Of these, 223 patients presented ≤6 hours from LKN and 187 (83.9 %) had ASPECTS 9-10, 28 (12.6 %) had ASPECTS 6-8, 5 (2.2 %) had ASPECTS 3-5, and 3 (1.3 %) had ASPECTS 0-2. In the 150 patients presenting >6 hours from LKN, 98 (65.3 %) had ASPECTS 9-10, 31 (20.7 %) had ASPECTS 6-8, 14 (9.3 %) had ASPECTS 3-5, and 7 (4.7 %) had ASPECTS 0-2 (Fig. 2).

Discussion

At a population level, the majority (96.9 %) of AIS patients presenting within 24 hours of LKN had ASPECTS ≥6, with 88.9 % of these cases being ASPECTS 9-10. In lieu of consistently available vessel imaging in the 2015 study period, we report ASPECTS for patients with NIHSS ≥6 within 24 hours of LKN. An NIHSS of 6 is the minimum score recommended based on the current acute ischemic stroke guidelines for thrombectomy and has an 87 % sensitivity for anterior circulation LVO detection.3,21 Again, among the subgroup with NIHSS ≥6, the vast majority (92.2 %) had ASPECTS ≥6. Finally, a similar ASPECTS distribution was noted in patients with and without baseline vessel imaging.

Until recently, patients with anterior circulation LVO stroke, NIHSS ≥6, and ASPECTS ≥6 presenting in the late time window (i.e., 6-24h of LKN), were routinely selected for EVT using advanced imaging.3 In the CT for Late Endovascular Reperfusion (CLEAR) study, 90-day functional outcomes were comparable when patients were selected by CT head alone versus by CT perfusion (CTP) or magnetic resonance imaging.9 The study allowed for inclusion of any patient with a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0-2, NIHSS ≥6, and stroke due to intracranial ICA, M1, or M2 occlusion between 6-24 hours of LKN. Of note, ASPECTS was not utilized as an exclusion criterion in the study. Despite half of sites either not accounting for ASPECTS or routinely considering ASPECTS ≥5 for EVT in their patient selection protocols, the median ASPECTS was still 8 (IQR 7-9).9 The subsequent larger cohort study demonstrated a persistent treatment effect of EVT in CT-only selected patients compared to medical management (odds ratio 1.99, 95 % confidence interval 1.53-2.59; p <0.001).22

CTP availability is not ubiquitous, and its utilization for EVT triage comes at the cost of additional time, radiation, and contrast.23,24 If CTH alone is used to determine eligibility for thrombectomy, our results suggest exclusion of very few patients. Specifically, only 21 of 150 patients with estimated LVO (based on NIHSS ≥6) presented with ASPECTS 0-5 between 6-24 hours of LKN. These 21 patients represent only 2.2 % of the overall inclusion population presenting up to 24 hours of LKN and 14 % of the presumed LVO population presenting in the late window. In lieu of important clinical considerations such as pre-stroke mRS, our results indicate that 86 % of patients with NIHSS ≥6 have ASPECTS ≥6 and could be selected for late-window EVT without advanced imaging. The high rate of patients in whom advanced imaging may not be needed could spare patients the side effects of additional testing and expedite care towards definitive treatment.

Recently, multiple randomized clinical trials have suggested the benefit of thrombectomy in select anterior circulation LVO stroke patients with “large core,” or extensive ischemic injury upon presentation.20 While ASPECTS is not the perfect surrogate for infarct volume, all six trials defined large core as ASPECTS <6.25 Prior literature reported that approximately 20 % of patients present with large core.26 In our population level study, using NIHSS ≥6 as a marker of LVO, and including patients up to 24-hours of LKN, only 29 patients had ASPECTS <6. This number represents just 7.8 % of patients with presumed LVO stroke. A recent scientific advisory from the American Heart Association noted substantial evidence for benefit of EVT in patients with mRS 0-1, NIHSS ≥6, and proximal LVO in ASPECTS 3-5.27 In lieu of pre-stroke mRS, our findings suggest 5.1 % of projected LVO patients meet these criteria. While the large core trials have generated much excitement in the field, our results suggest that even patients with ASPECTS 3-5 are uncommon, accounting for only 2 % of the overall population in our study.

Finally, the extent of ischemic injury is still a principal exclusion criterion for the majority of acute stroke clinical trials testing novel agents.28 An understanding of ASPECTS distribution across a population will inform the feasibility of clinical trial participation criteria.29

This study is limited by lack of LVO data in the 2015 GCNKSS study epoch, thus requiring the use of an NIHSS threshold of ≥6 as a surrogate. However, given that this is likely an overestimate, it only further underscores our conclusion that patients with large core and LVO will be relatively uncommon. This cut-off may also potentially underestimate detection of stroke due to distal medium vessel occlusion. Our findings could also be less generalizable to other geographic regions, such as those encompassing more rural areas. Longer distances to EDs may lead to a higher proportion of low ASPECTS. Bias towards higher ASPECTS is also probable when patients without LKN time are excluded and when thicker slices derived from 2015 scans may underestimate subtle ischemia. Nevertheless, to our knowledge, our study is the first ever systematic assessment of ASPECTS by trained neuroradiologists in a large-scale population-level study within the US. Finally, the study predates the popularization of platforms that provide automated ASPECTS assessment. It is possible that use of such software may affect our estimates if utilized as an inclusion criterion for clinical trial eligibility. However, such software remains benchmarked against the gold standard of board-certified neuroradiologist interpretation utilized in this study.

Conclusion

In this population level assessment of baseline anterior circulation ischemic injury, almost 90 % of patients presenting to an ED within 24 hours of AIS had ASPECTS of 9-10, with nearly 97 % having ASPECTS ≥6. In patients with NIHSS ≥6 presenting 6 hours, only 14 % had ASPECTS 0-5. Our results can inform resource utilization and clinical trial design.

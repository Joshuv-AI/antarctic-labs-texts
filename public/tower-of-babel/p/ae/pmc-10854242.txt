Comparative Evaluation of the Treatment Outcomes of Pulpotomy in Primary Molars Using Mineral Trioxide Aggregate and 3Mixtatin: A Randomized Clinical Trial
Authors: Anam Mushtaq, Tanu Nangia, Mousumi Goswami
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate and compare the efficacy of 3Mixtatin and mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) for pulpotomy in primary teeth by assessment of pre- and postoperative clinical and radiographic data.Materials and methodsIn this randomized clinical trial, 50 primary molars from 48 healthy children aged 3–8 years were randomly allocated into two groups. Deep dentinal caries approaching pulp in primary teeth were treated by pulpotomy using MTA and 3Mixtatin. Over the intervening period, restoration was done using glass ionomer cement (GIC) and composite, followed by stainless steel crown. Clinical and radiographic examinations were conducted at 3, 6, and 12 months after treatment. The data were compared using Chi-squared test at a significance level of 0.05.ResultsA total of 39 patients were available for follow-up study. By the end of 12 months, the overall success rates were 95.5% in MTA and 91.3% in 3Mixtatin. No statistically significant difference was found among the outcomes of MTA and 3Mixtatin groups (p > 0.05).ConclusionRadiographic and clinical outcomes in MTA and 3Mixtatin group in this study show 3Mixtatin as a suitable alternative for pulpotomy medicament in primary teeth.How to cite this articleMushtaq A, Nangia T, Goswami M. Introduction

Carious lesions in teeth that cause early stage of pulp inflammation can be managed by vital pulp therapy procedures that include indirect pulp therapy (IPT), direct pulp capping (DPC), and pulpotomy. These involve placement of appropriate biocompatible materials capable of maintaining vitality of the pulp. The concept of vital pulp therapy was first described by Philip Pfaff in 1756 when he attempted the management of pulp exposure with a small piece of gold applied over the cavity base.1 The core objective of these procedures is to minimize inflammation, conserve radicular pulp, and maintain the tooth integrity in the dental arch. Performing endodontic therapy in primary teeth aids in maintaining the tooth function till the eruption of successors, space maintenance that prevents malocclusion, and the overall oral functioning of the child.2

Correlations between the clinical and histological status of the pulp have always been a dilemma where only a clinical evaluation is insufficient to validate the degree of inflammation. In the absence of any evidence of radiographic bone degeneration or cases without clinical radicular pulp inflammation, vital pulpotomy procedure is considered the treatment of choice. This maintains the vitality of the radicular pulp tissue; however, it is not always achieved with the commonly used materials.3

Characteristics of an ideal material for pulpotomy include a good sealing ability, bio-inductivity, biocompatibility, bactericidal activity, and the ability to promote healing of the radicular pulp.4 Different medicaments with varied rates of success have been proposed and used for the pulpotomy of primary teeth. These include formocresol, calcium hydroxide, glutaraldehyde, paraformaldehyde, zinc oxide eugenol (ZOE), KRI paste, Ledermix®, electrosurgery, ferric sulfate, mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA), certain growth factors and lasers as well. However, no consensus exists to validate a material with all ideal properties to be used in pulpotomy. Formocresol with its high clinical success rate is considered a gold standard but studies contradict its use due to the toxic and carcinogenic effects shown.5

In the quest to develop newer materials that provide better bio-inductive abilities and regenerative potential, various alternatives have been successfully used in pulpotomy. MTA is one such material that has been widely studied and has shown excellent outcomes such that it is almost considered a new gold standard for vital pulp therapy.6,7 Its advantages include the ability to eliminate inflammation and formation of dentinal bridges in a short duration of time. However, it may prove disadvantageous owing to its long setting time and handling, possible tooth discoloration, and high cost.

Recently, the concept of a newer economical material “3Mixtatin” has come into research and has been studied for its use in DPC, inflammatory root resorption, and pulpotomy in primary teeth. The material is a combination of 3Mix (cefixime, metronidazole, and ciprofloxacin) and a statin (Simvastatin) and has shown successful results over a long-term follow-up period. The results of 3Mixtatin used in DPC were seen to be comparable to that of MTA and in fact better than the latter when used as a root-filling material in cases of inflammatory root resorption.8,10

The purpose of this study was to evaluate the clinical and radiographic outcomes using 3Mixtatin as a pulpotomy medicament in primary molars with deep carious lesions.

Materials and Methods

This randomized clinical trial was conducted using a protocol that was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board and Ethical Committee (ITSDCGN/2018/001).

A total of 48 children in the age group of 3–8 years were selected for the study according to the proposed inclusion criteria. Each child had at least one deeply carious primary molar. The lesion was evaluated clinically and radiographically and diagnosed as deep dentinal caries approaching pulp with International Caries Detection and Assessment System (ICDAS) score of 5 or 6. Informed consent and assent were obtained from the parents/guardians and the children. Only those patients were included in the study who gave their consent to participate (Fig. 1).

Sample Size Estimation

Sample size estimation was done by using G*Power software (version 3.0). The sample size was estimated for goodness-of-fit tests: contingency tables (two groups) were chosen.

Anticipating a 0.5 effect size between MTA and 3Mixtatin regarding overall failure rate after pulpotomy, an α of 0.05 and power of 80%, a sample size of 32 was found to be sufficient. To compensate for any follow-up loss, the total sample size was increased and recruited to 50.

Sampling Technique

The study was carried out with the help of two investigators. The first investigator randomized the subjects in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) 2012 guidelines by asking them to choose one of two differently colored balls which would determine which intervening material has to be placed as material for pulpotomy.

Clinical Procedure

After a thorough clinical and radiographic evaluation examination, pulp vitality was assessed using an indigenously constructed and validated pulse oximeter probe. This was followed by administration of 2% lignocaine anesthetic solution with adrenaline (1:80,000). The specific tooth was isolated using rubber dam. Removal of the carious tissue was performed using spoon excavator and a round bur with high-speed air rotor.

For carious pulp exposures, Pulpotomy was performed by gently removing the coronal pulp tissue. Pulp hemorrhage was controlled using a moistened sterile cotton pellet with slight pressure for 3–5 minutes.

Preparation of 3Mixtatin

3Mixtatin used in this study was prepared indigenously in accordance with method described by Asl Aminabadi et al.8 Simvastatin in the form of commercially available 10 mg tablets was procured. Preparation and measurements were done in the Laboratory of the Department of Pharmacology and Biochemistry of the Hospital. Accurate measurements were taken by an analytical balance with 0.1 mg accuracy. 3Mix was prepared by mixing three commercially available antibiotics. After removing the coating materials, drugs were pulverized by porcelain mortars and pestles to achieve fine powders. A total of 100 mg ciprofloxacin, 100 mg metronidazole, and 100 mg cefixime were mixed in a ratio of 1:1:1. Two milligrams of simvastatin were added to the drugs blend to form 3Mixtatin. 3Mixtatin was stored in a tightly capped container, adding a small amount of silica gel in a bag inside the container to maintain low humidity. This powder was mixed with normal saline to form a thick creamy paste of 3Mixtatin which was carried into the access cavity and a 3–4 mm thick layer was compacted over the pulp stump. The tooth was restored using universal restorative glass ionomer cement (GIC) (3M ESPE Ketac™ Molar) followed by a full coverage stainless steel crown (3M ESPE Ketac™).

The patients were followed up for 1 year and recall appointments included clinical and radiographic Evaluation to determine success of the treatment. Clinical absence of signs and symptoms of pain, abscess, or periodontal disease depicted a successful outcome and the radiographic examination included observation of any radiographic changes like periodontal space widening or inflammatory root resorption (Figs 2 and 3).

Result Analysis Criteria

Clinical evaluation at all follow-ups involved the assessment of the presence of any postoperative pain, sensitivity to palpation and percussion, and mobility along with examination of surrounding periodontium for the presence of any gingival inflammation, sinus tract, or fistula.

Radiographic evaluation criteria included assessment for presence of external or internal resorption of tooth, periodontal ligament (PDL) widening, furcation or periapical involvement, or evidence of internal resorption.

The observed data was compiled using Microsoft Excel sheets, which were then subjected to statistical analysis.

Statistical Analysis

The observed data was subjected to relevant statistical analysis using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 21. The categorical values were compared using the Chi-squared test for intergroup comparison. The level of significance was set at 0.05.

Results

Interexaminer Agreement

The agreement between the examiners at baseline was 0.91 and at final follow-up was 0.94 that was excellent. p-value for both baseline and follow-up was significant (p < 0.001).

Follow-up Loss

One patient in MTA group at 6 months and three patients at 12 months follow-up, two in 3Mixtatin group at 12 months follow-up did not report, and hence, were excluded from the statistical analysis.

All the clinical signs of failure were found to be absent among all the study participants at 12th-month follow-up, except, mobility and pocket formation which were present among 2.2% of subjects in group II, that is, 3Mixtatin. The difference in prevalence of mobility and pocket among two study groups was not significant (Fig. 4 and Table 1).

Discussion

Formocresol has long been considered the medicament of choice in pulpotomy for many years; however, its possible carcinogenic nature has gained attention, thus mandating the need for better alternatives.5 In recent years, the introduction of biocompatible calcium silicate-based cements has paved a way to successful pulp therapy in primary teeth. MTA has carved its niche in endodontics with a wide usage in vital pulp therapy procedures, apexification, and perforation repair.6

Clinical and radiographic success rates as high as 96–100% have been shown with MTA when compared with formocresol and other medicaments used for pulpotomy.11,12 A study by Jamali et al. in 2018, evaluated and compared the use of two standard materials MTA, formocresol with 3Mixtatin for pulpotomy in primary molars and concluded that there was no significant difference in the success rates among these materials. The overall success rate for formocresol was seen to be 78.9%, 90.5% for 3Mixtatin, and 88.1% for MTA. Successful results with 3Mixtatin may be due to their pleiotropic effect of promoting bone formation and inducing osteoblastic function. The established pro-angiogenic effect and inhibition of pro-inflammatory cytokines by statins may also cause regeneration of pulp in teeth.10 Whitaker and Alshammari in 2017 studied the bacteriostatic effect of simvastatin on selected strains of streptococci and concluded that they had inhibitory effect on streptococci.13 Owing to the bactericidal effects of triple antibiotic paste 3Mix, it has been widely used in endodontics for sterilization and tissue repair.14,15 This property along with pro-angiogenic effects of statins may therefore act synergistically in eliminating infection and promoting healing of radicular pulp stump after pulpotomy.

In the present study, clinical and radiographic signs and symptoms have been assessed over a period of 12 months in primary teeth after performing pulpotomy using 3Mixtatin. It showed an overall clinical success rate of 95.7% and a radiographic success of 91.3% at the 12-month follow-up which was lower as compared to MTA, however, statistically insignificant. Almost similar results were reported by Jamali et al.,10 who reported 90.5% success of pulpotomy with 3Mixtatin when followed up for 24 months. Asl Aminabadi et al.8 reported a 91.9% success rate of 3Mixtatin when used as a medicament in DPC procedure.

The immediate placement of stainless-steel crowns after performing pulpotomy in our present study could also be a contributing factor to the successful results obtained in this study. This is justified and in accordance with Guelmann et al.16 who reported a significantly high clinical success (86%) in emergency pulpotomy of primary molars when restored with SSC in comparison to IRM and Ketac. Hutcheson et al.17 observed that MTA pulpotomies were successful regardless of the restoration placed; however, esthetics and durability were significantly high for pulpotomies followed by stainless steel crowns.

Studies on calcium silicate-based cements like MTA and dentine have shown remarkable biocompatibility and sealing ability of these compounds that protect the vital radicular pulp from any microleakage. However, the technique-sensitive manipulation and the cost of these materials often pose a barrier to their use. With the introduction of this novel concept of 3Mixatin, medicaments for pulpotomy may be revolutionized to a much more basic and cheaper alternative that can be availed and used widely for the purpose.

Limitations of the Study

Though the sample size of this study is statistically sufficient, further studies with a larger sample size and longer follow-up duration are recommended.

Despite having constructed a specialized pulse oximeter probe with appropriate stabilizing methods, difficulty in acquiring pulse oximeter readings in some children as in the case of our age range of included patients was encountered due to movements caused by them and anatomical variations of teeth.

Conclusion

3Mixtatin may be an effective, economical, and bio-inductive alternative for pulpotomy procedures. Further research is suggested to validate the effects of this material in clinical trials with long-term follow-ups.

Mapping a Decade of Osteoporosis and Fracture Research: A Bibliometric Analysis (2015–2025)
Authors: Oluwafemi Samson Balogun, Sunday Adewale Olaleye, Margaret Adebola Balogun, Esther Olubunmi Olaleye, Muhammad Zeshan Arshad, Keijo Haataja, Pekka Toivanen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporosis is a major global health concern, affecting over 200 million individuals worldwide. Although numerous clinical and experimental studies exist, comprehensive analyses integrating fracture-related and drug-specific research remain limited. Examining global research trends can highlight evolving themes, key contributors, and areas requiring scientific attention.MethodsWe performed a bibliometric analysis of literature on osteoporosis and fractures, including drug-specific terms (denosumab, romosozumab, teriparatide), published between 2015 and 2025 in the Scopus Core Collection. Data were assessed for publication trends, authorship, institutional output, international collaboration, and thematic evolution using Bibliometrix-Biblioshiny in RStudio. Network analyses included co-authorship and keyword co-occurrence mapping for both quantitative and qualitative insights.ResultsFrom 4,450 articles, annual output showed steady growth, peaking between 2021 and 2024. The United States, Japan, and China led in publication volume and citation impact, while Osteoporosis International was the most productive journal. Citation averages declined recently due to lag in newer publications. Thematic mapping identified pharmacological therapies such as denosumab and teriparatide as core themes, while “fracture prevention” and “DXA-based assessment” emerged as active research fronts. International collaboration decreased after 2020, particularly affecting contributions from low- and middle-income countries.ConclusionsGlobal osteoporosis research demonstrates sustained productivity but declining collaboration and limited thematic diversification. Strengthening international networks, supporting underrepresented regions, and expanding focus to preventive and non-pharmacological strategies are essential for equitable and impactful progress.Keywords: Bibliometrics; Fractures, bone; Global health; Osteoporosis

GRAPHICAL ABSTRACT

INTRODUCTION

Brief background

Osteoporosis is a medical condition characterized by decreased bone density, poor bone quality, and disrupted microstructure, resulting in fragile bones and an increased risk of fractures. More than 200 million people are affected globally.[1] Called a “silent epidemic” and having a relatively high prevalence, it has significant clinical importance.[2,3] Osteoporosis is primarily categorized into two types, primary and secondary, according to the etiology. Primary osteoporosis occurs most frequently in postmenopausal women ≥50 years from a decreased estrogen level,[4,5] and secondary osteoporosis is the result of underlying medical conditions or continued use of specified medications.[6] Fracture is still the most prevalent and severe consequence of osteoporosis,[7,8] often causing disability, decreased mobility, and reliance on daily living, and increasing the risk of infections and pressure ulcers. Those complications come at a substantial financial cost to individuals and healthcare systems.[9,10] Over nine million osteoporosis-related fractures occur each year worldwide.[8] Despite continuing research progress, osteoporosis often goes undiagnosed until a fracture occurs, and the mechanisms and therapeutic targets underlying the disease remain insufficiently understood.[11]

Rationale and knowledge gap

Osteoporosis continues to be a global public health threat, exacerbated by a population growing older every year. Its causes and management have been studied extensively, but global research trends combining both fracture-related and drug-specific terms have not been fully studied. To identify the emerging research themes that emerge, to identify important contributors, and to highlight the need to explore lesser-known areas in the field of osteoporosis and fracture research, a bibliometric analysis has been proposed. Such an analysis would yield an important insight into how the field has developed and where to direct future research.

Study aim

This study aims to bridge the gap by conducting a comprehensive worldwide bibliometric analysis of the literature on osteoporosis, fractures, and related drug-specific terms. Applying data from the Scopus database and bibliometric indicators, the study will: Show global research trends on osteoporosis, fractures, and drugs, including denosumab, romosozumab, and teriparatide. Identify the major contributors, including authors, institutions, and countries. Analyze networks of international cooperation and the temporal movement in research output. Suggest research gaps to guide future studies and policy work.

Bibliometric analysis provides both quantitative and qualitative evaluations of scholarly publications.[12–14] Tools like Biblioshiny enable the visual mapping of knowledge structures, facilitating deeper insight into emerging directions in medical and clinical research.[15–18]

METHODS

This is a quantitative, bibliometric data analysis, where the Bibliometrix-Biblioshiny system was used in this study. Bibliometrix is a science mapping R tool that provides indicators at different scales (source, author, document) and clusters by coupling. Three knowledge structures (conceptual, intellectual, and social) can be analyzed with this instrument.[19] Introduced a tool called Bibliometrix, used by many authors for their analyses in various fields.[20–24] This study used a tool called Bibliometrix the Scopus Core Collection (as of July 9, 2025). The search term included “osteoporosis” AND “fracture” OR “denosumab” OR “romosozumab” OR “teriparatide,” and the time frame was from July 2015 to July 2025 to include a full reference list of all studies on osteoporosis, fractures, and drug-specific terms research. After a thorough exclusion and inclusion, 4,450 articles were analyzed. The information is given in Figure 1.

Here, it is interesting to mention that all languages other than English were removed from the selected data. After pre-processing, this work closely follows the previously established pipeline.[25] BibTeX file exported by Scopus was utilized to import into the RStudio package, Bibliometrix, to resolve the bibliographic data that can be cleaned, standardized, and is fit for analysis. An R interactive web application, Biblioshiny, was introduced through RStudio to start various bibliometric studies. The descriptive statistics are shown in Figure 2, which include 1,431 sources, 19,864 authors, and 108 single-authored documents, with 22.57% documents with international co-authorship. Such posts published in the Scopus journals showed the article’s average growth rate of 0.27%. The characteristics of the dataset, including the publications, authors, institutions, and publication years, were also analyzed using Biblioshiny to perform basic descriptive analyses. The Biblioshiny app was also applied for author, journal, keyword, and citation analysis. Subsequently, data were visualized using the app Biblioshiny to present the bibliometric output in a visually informative manner. The authors reviewed the visualized data in detail, extracting lessons learnt and recognizing research voids. We further analyzed the original and review-type articles, as they yielded the highest number of document types, also comprising entire research work concepts and findings.[26] The exclusion criteria included: editorials, letters, book chapters, meeting abstracts, reviews, conference papers, and duplicate printouts. These were excluded as they may not have enough information for systematic reviewers to judge the design, methods, risk of bias, results, and outcomes of the studies that have been included.[27–29] Languages (German, Spanish, French, Turkish, etc.) were limited in the search strategy. In our bibliometric analysis, we did not need to search for duplicate publications since we retrieved all data from one database. The results are displayed and analyzed in the following section.

RESULTS

This section presents the bibliometric findings from 4,450 publications on osteoporosis, fractures, and drug-specific terminology indexed in Scopus between 2015 and August 2025. Analyses address research productivity, citation patterns, contributing sources and authors, geographic distribution, and thematic structures relevant to the field.

Trends in publication and impact

The annual scientific output related to osteoporosis and fractures has increased steadily since 2015, when 294 papers were published. By 2025, the overall output had totalled 4,450, with significant spikes in 2021 (525 papers) and 2024 (522 papers). Annual growth was calculated as 0.27%, which implies a slow but sustained progression of the field. Despite improvements in overall production, there were some fluctuations over the decade (Fig. 3A).

The number of documents and active authors increased from 2015 through 2023, before falling slightly in 2024 through 2025 (Fig. 3B). This pattern indicates academic engagement increased until more recently, which is in line with productivity spikes post-pandemic.

Citation metrics are the opposite of one. Average citations per document decreased over the period from 38 in 2015–2017 to 2 in 2024–2025, according to Figure 3C. This decline has been consistent with the younger publications’ corpus; newer documents have taken less time to accumulate citations, indicating the temporal citation lag characteristic of the fast-growing domain. As shown in Figure 5, the leading journal is Osteoporosis International (≈320 papers), followed by Archives of Osteoporosis (181) and the Journal of Bone and Mineral Research (155). Sources frequently include JBMR Plus (67), Bone Reports (52), and Journal of Clinical Medicine (50).

Institutional productivity was dominated by North American universities. Distribution and cooperation of global studies

Geographically, research within osteoporosis and fracture investigations covers all continents and illustrates the wide public health relevance of the topic. Figure 8 reveals that Asia is the most productive region, led by Japan’s 2,521 papers and China’s 2,364, followed by South Korea’s 617 and India’s 307. Europe follows in the wake of these countries: Italy (1,055), the UK (864), Germany (502), and France (425). In North America, the United States is the world leader with 3,272 papers. Next comes Canada, with 742. Australia is predominant in Oceania (680 papers), followed by Brazil and Argentina (247 and 103, respectively), which are major South American contributors. Research works are scant in Africa, where Egypt produces 113 papers, and nations contribute relatively little. The lack of regional homogeneity in this information reflects continued variations in global research capacity.

The patterns of collaboration are summarized in Figure 9. For single country publications, Japan leads (554 papers), followed by the USA (492) and China (451). As for multiple country publications, the USA comes out on top (186 papers), China next (73), the UK third (69), and Canada last (55). These statistics highlight both the prominence of productivity in the developed countries and a gradual decrease in collaboration in the international landscape in the last few years, a result addressed more deeply in the Discussion.

Thematic landscape and keyword networks

Keyword and thematic analyses reveal the intellectual structure of osteoporosis research between 2015 and 2025. As displayed in Figure 11, “osteoporosis” was the most frequent keyword (2,137 occurrences), followed by “denosumab” (743), “teriparatide” (501), “bone mineral density” (445), and “bisphosphonates” (270). The frequency of “fracture” (260) and “postmenopausal osteoporosis” (120) reflects enduring clinical focus on postmenopausal bone health.

The thematic map (Fig. 12) identifies four quadrants of research emphasis: (1) driving themes (dual energy X-ray absorptiometry [DXA], antiresorptives, fracture prevention); (2) specialized themes (fracture healing, RANKL, bone remodeling); (3) emerging/declining themes (osteonecrosis, MRONJ); and (4) foundational themes (denosumab, teriparatide, bone mineral density).

Summary of findings

Between 2015 and 2025, osteoporosis-related research expanded moderately in quantity but diversified significantly in scope. Publication peaks (2021–2024) coincided with strong institutional engagement and journal output. Citation indicators fell in recent years, owing to citation lag among recent work. But North American and East Asian contributors dominate at a time of declining international collaboration that underlines continuing global inequalities in capacity. Thematic analysis provides us insights that characterize pharmacological treatments; in particular, denosumab and teriparatide have emerged as prime topics of discussion with respect to pharmacological therapies, while emerging themes such as osteonecrosis and fracture prevention indicate priorities may be changing.

DISCUSSION

Overview of findings

This bibliometric analysis provides a cross-national glance at global osteoporosis and fracture research between 2015 and 2025. The results indicate an enduring scientific output and a peak in 2021–2023, while there is a drop in 2024–2025. At the same time as the total volume of publications remains significant (4,450 articles), and we observe that with a marginal annual growth rate (0.27%), growth in these publications has slowed in recent years. Citation performance shows a similar decline, with average citations per document in both years falling from 38 in 2015–2017 to 2 in 2024–2025, predominantly accounting for citation lag for recently published papers. There is substantial distribution of publications in North America and Asia (mainly the U.S., Japan, and China), with Europe being a dominant second-order contributor. International joint research patterns are shown to decrease after 2020 for international collaborative research, but major academic institutions (e.g., University of Toronto, University of California, and Harvard Medical School) remain influential in driving international output. Through thematic mapping, pharmacological therapies such as denosumab, teriparatide, and bisphosphonates remain central in the research space where these new fields and declining ones (e.g., osteonecrosis, MRONJ, and fracture prevention) are becoming more recent, highlighting shifts in priorities among clinicians.

Interpretation and research trends

The citation lag was calculated for (3–5 years) in such biomedical departments, suggesting that recent papers probably do not have maturity in terms of scholarly visibility and referencing.[30,31] But if we extend the time limits to 2024–2025, the obvious decline in the references is really a brief bibliometric artifact, and not necessarily a drop in the value of past studies. So, there will not be a “crisis of interest” drop off of citations is actually simply bad academic content (high citation frequency). Regional output mirrors the continued pre-eminence of high-income countries. The U.S., China, and Japan account for more than 50% of all publications and citations. The decline of international co-authorship since 2020 is so pronounced that it may be due to a lack of movement, fund cutbacks, and political impossibilities. The stability of the output from Europe shows a strong network in place, whereas the low level of research in Africa and South America indicates that both the capacity and infrastructure remain different in these regions. Thematic analysis highlights a two-fold structure in osteoporosis research, with a drug core and an expanding set of new subfield topics. The requirement for denosumab or teriparatide is still high in antiresorptive and anabolic medication, as well as the growing interest in osteonecrosis and fracture prevention, indicating that preventive care and surveillance of health care and safety are becoming more prevalent.

Policy and practice implications

This decrease in foreign cooperation, identified during the 2020 pandemic, has important implications for global osteoporosis management. Collaboration has historically enhanced both methodological rigor and the diversity of data (in recent years, particularly for low- and middle-income countries, LMICs, which gain most from cooperation for expertise and resources). Lower engagement threatens widening discrepancies in healthcare equity and evidence translation. In LMICs, weak or deficient international networks hinder diagnostic access (e.g., availability of DXA) and clinical education. International partnerships contribute to higher research quality, given exposure to advanced techniques and shared facilities.[32] In the absence, these areas are at risk for diminished publication visibility and, in turn, delayed implementation of fracture prevention programs.[33,34] Fragility fractures have significant economic and social costs; thus, cooperative frameworks are required to develop cost-effective prevention and treatment models.[35] For policymakers, universities, and funding bodies, these findings highlight the need to regenerate cross-border programs and foster interdisciplinary collaboration. Bibliometric infrastructure will boost global attendance and break thematic silos in an underrepresented geographical region. The pharmaceutical industry and biotechnology industry can also benefit from reallocating research expenditure to new therapeutic and diagnostic agenda areas that have been identified in this study.

Limitations and suggestions for future work

This analysis, however, is not without limitations. It was based on Scopus data only and did not cover literature indexed for Web of Science, PubMed, and regional databases. Attempts to combine Web of Science metadata proved unsuccessful, as some regional data may have been excluded. Bias from focusing on English-language publications can drive language bias, particularly with outputs from Asia, South America, and non-Anglophone Europe. Future studies should further add multi-database triangulation to mitigate selection bias and include non-English records for international coverage. Bridging AI-facilitated topic modeling to the potential for citation trajectory prediction may enhance trend detection. Furthermore, bibliometric surveys to come may measure tangible influence, for example, on clinical practice guidelines and policy creation, rather than solely on the citation metrics.

CONCLUSION

This bibliometric examination reveals the growth and reformation of osteoporosis and fracture research from 2015 to 2025. The output remains high, but a plateau in the number of publications and shrinking global involvement signals a critical inflection point in the development of the field. Thematic analysis reveals that pharmacological therapies continue to dominate, but the shift to preventive and safety issues indicates increasing maturity. We will probably require new efforts to invest further in inclusive, coordinated research frameworks and equitable data-sharing settings to maintain the progress that we have in place. It will take broadening participation, particularly from marginalized areas, so that global osteoporosis research moves towards innovation, diversity, and impactful health outcomes.[36]

Rising pertussis cases and deaths in China: current trends and clinical solutions
Authors: Hu Yahong, Guo Mengyang, Qinghong Meng, Dan Yu, Yao Kaihu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPertussis, an acute respiratory infection caused by Bordetella pertussis, has recently experienced a dramatic increase in incidence and associated deaths in China, drawing significant clinical attention. This article retrospectively analyzes national data on pertussis incidence and mortality from 2010 to 2024, exploring potential factors contributing to this trend. It also discusses strategies for enhancing vaccination programs, improving early diagnosis and treatment, and optimizing the clinical management of high-risk infants, with the aim of addressing the challenges posed by the current pertussis epidemic.

Over the past ten months, the number of reported pertussis cases in China rose from 1,512 in June 2023 to a significant spike in early 2024, reaching 15,275 cases in January. In the subsequent months, the cases increased even more rapidly, reaching 91,272 in April and 97,669 in May (Figure 1). This sustained growth trend deviates markedly from previous patterns recorded over the past decade by the National Notifiable Diseases Reporting System (NNDRS), where pertussis cases typically peak in August and then decline. In addition, there has been a significant increase in the number of reported pertussis deaths during this period, with 25 deaths in the last seven months. From 2010 through 2022, the annual numbers of reported pertussis deaths were no more than three. All four deaths in 2023 occurred in the second half of the year, with three occurring in the last two months (Figure 1). This concentration of deaths is particularly concerning and warrants heightened clinical concern, as the number of deaths is expected to continue increasing amid the ongoing surge in cases. Factors such as increased awareness among healthcare practitioners, enhanced surveillance measures, declining vaccination rates in specific regions, and insufficient vaccine efficacy have been associated with the resurgence of pertussis [1]. However, the recent rise in deaths associated with pertussis could be strongly associated with the spread of highly virulent strains resistant to macrolide. Although macrolide-resistant Bortetella pertussis (MRBP) strains were found in several America, Europe and Asia countries, they were reported as prevalent agent only in China [2]. Until 2019, two ptxP3-type MRBP were isolated for the first time in China by Wu et al. [3], prior to that, all detected MRBP strains belonged to the ptxP1-type. Since then, Cai et al. [4]. reported in 2023 that ptxP3-allele MRBP dominated in the Shanghai circulating strain. Compared to the ptxP1 strain, the ptxP3 strain produced more PT, resulting in more severe clinical symptoms, highlighting the potential for increased severity and fatality rates with the circulation of highly virulent strains [5]. The expected increase in severe cases and deaths due to the widespread spread of ptxP3-MRBP highlights the need to continuously adapt pertussis management strategies within public health frameworks.

From 2009 to 2022, DTaP vaccine coverage in China consistently exceeded 98% [6]. Nevertheless, China's pertussis vaccination strategy primarily targets infants and young children, with the national immunization program excluding vaccinations for infants younger than 3 months and children older than 6 years. Research indicates that pertussis infections in infants under 3 months are more likely to progress to severe disease and have higher mortality rates [7]. If pertussis vaccination is initiated at 2 months of age, part of the death can be prevented [8]. Furthermore, vaccination during pregnancy has been demonstrated to be both effective and safe, providing newborns with early protection and thereby significantly reducing their risk of pertussis [9]. However, the promotion of pertussis immunization strategy during pregnancy in China faces many difficulties, such as lack of corresponding products. On the other hand, it has been well demonstrated that the seroprevalence of anti-PT IgG is generally low among the Chinese population, particularly in school-aged children and young adults [10]. This low seroprevalence highlights the potential risks in pertussis control efforts. Given the high-density contact environments in schools and workplaces, the risk of pertussis transmission is elevated in these groups. The continuous decline in serum antibody levels increases the risk of pertussis. Therefore, booster doses for older children, adolescents, and adults should be considered currently.

Early and accurate diagnosis of pertussis and prompt antibiotic intervention are essential to effectively control the spread of the disease and reduce the severity of its course. In clinical practice, doctors often need to consider whooping cough as a potential diagnosis before ordering specialized tests. However, this approach can hinder early diagnosis since the typical clinical symptoms may not manifest. Furthermore, the multi-pathogen tests used for clinical diagnosis in China does not include Bortetella pertussis. Positive test results for other common pathogens may lead physicians to overlook the possibility of co-infection with pertussis infections, especially when the clinical symptoms of pertussis are atypical or when symptoms of other infections are similar, masking the symptoms of pertussis [11]. Therefore, multiple in vitro diagnostic tests including this specific agent should be considered for rapid diagnosis of pertussis, such as the Film Array Respiratory Panel 2 (RP2) [12]. Additionally, we suggested broadening the indications for pertussis laboratory testing in infant. Especially during the current epidemic, routine screening for pertussis should be performed in infants with severe respiratory infections. This approach is a critical and viable strategy to reduce severe cases and mortality in China. Prompt and effective empirical antibiotic treatment plays a crucial role in reducing the severity and mortality of pertussis. However, in mainland China, there is widespread MRBP, posing a challenge to treatment. This has led to the exploration of alternative antibiotics, such as trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMZ) and certain β-lactam antibiotics, for managing pertussis in children.

Additionally, we recommend adapting the clinical management of infant pertussis, which poses a high risk of severe illness and mortality. Children without severe symptoms should undergo risk assessment to identify those at high risk of severe illness or death. Those with severe symptoms should be categorized as having either severe disease (without organ dysfunction) or critical disease (with dysfunction of one or more organs). This classification helps determine the optimal timing for specific interventions such as immunoglobulin therapy, pulmonary hypertension therapy, blood transfusion therapy, and other treatments [13]. The co-infection during the course of pertussis is an important risk factor for severe illness and death, and can complicate clinical symptoms and prolong the disease course in the convalescent phase [14]. Therefore, it is essential for physicians to closely monitor the health status of convalescent patients to promptly identify and treat any potential co-infections.

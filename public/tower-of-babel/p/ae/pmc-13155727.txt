Prognostic Value of CSF Total Tau Protein in Patients with Familial and Sporadic Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease
Authors: Yacov Balash, Esther Kahana, Hanna Rosenmann, Nurit Omer, Ronit Gilad, Anda Eilam, Shmuel Appel, Amos D. Korczyn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionElevated total tau protein (t-τ) in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) is routinely used as a marker of Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD). We hypothesized that CSF t-τ and patients’ age may anticipate survival time (ST) also in subjects with familial CJD (f-CJD) as has been shown previously in sporadic CJD (s-CJD).MethodsWe analyzed data from the Israeli National CJD Registry from 1991 to 2022. The data included cases of both f-CJD and s-CJD in whom demographic data and CSF t-τ levels were measured, and the date of death was available. Using X-tile software, we determined the optimal cut-off points for continuous variables in survival analysis and found the cut-points for t-τ level and patients’ age.ResultsWe analyzed data on 183 sequential CJD patients. The data included definite (n = 9), probable (n = 165), and possible (n = 9) CJD cases of whom 120 (61 males) were f-CJD (65.6%) and 63 (35 males) patients were s-CJD (34.4%). The cut-off value of CSF t-τ level was found to be 1,226 pg/mL. It separated ST into two groups, 105 days (interquartile ranges [IQR]: 59–305) in the lower group vs. 59 days (IQR: 29.5–147.5) in the higher, p = 0.0005. Cox analyses showed that for every 100 pg/mL increase in CSF t-τ, ST shortened by 3% in days (Exp β = 1.0003; 95% confidence interval: 1.0001–1.0005). Age had no direct prognostic value but correlated with CSF t-τ levels (rho = 0.243, p < 0.001).ConclusionsAge and CSF t-τ levels above the cut-off value predict ST shortening in patients with both f-CJD and s-CJD.

Introduction

Creutzfeldt-Jakob’s disease (CJD) is a rapidly progressive fatal neurodegenerative disease caused by the uncontrolled accumulation of prion proteins in the central nervous system [1]. During the process of neuronal damage, the τ-protein can reach the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and blood, and the extent of its presence appears to reflect the intensity of the pathological process [2].

In sporadic CJD (s-CJD), total τ protein (t-τ) is considered the most significant diagnostic factor for disease progression and its best prognostic parameter for survival. Exact quantitative content of CSF t-τ strongly correlates with the rate of neuronal destruction. It reflects general neurodegeneration and synaptic damage rather than just neuronal death alone, with higher levels indicating faster disease progression and shorter survival [3]. One recent study found that patients with CSF t-τ levels >4,000 pg/mL have a nearly 10 times higher compared to those with lower levels [4]. However, whether lower CSF t-τ levels in s-CJD have similar prognostic properties and whether this phenomenon is correct for cases of familial CJD (f-CJD) has not yet been investigated.

Other biochemical markers of CSF have also been considered as prognostic indicators of survival time (ST) in patients with CJD. The 14-3-3 protein, which is used primarily to confirm the diagnosis of probable CJD, may also have some prognostic value. Patients with elevated CSF protein 14-3-3 levels had several times shorter mean ST compared with those without CSF protein 14-3-3 [5, 6]. However, this test is typically performed using immunoblotting, and not always quantitative, and a significant proportion of the results may be indeterminate [7]. Other studies previously show inconsistencies in correlating 14-3-3 levels with disease duration or severity, making it a better diagnostic marker than a precise prognosticator for ST [8].

Neurofilament light levels in CSF are significantly elevated in CJD and serve as a diagnostic marker correlating with the disease severity [9]. Some studies suggest that neurofilament light is superior than blood t-τ for early diagnosis/differentiation of CJD [10]. However, its prognostic value for patient’s survival is complex, demonstrating various patterns specific to CJD subtypes [11].

The interest in studying in CSF t-τ levels in CJD has increased since the 2011 work of Meiner et al. [12] who were the first to identify an association between t-τ in CSF concentrations and time to death (defined by the authors as the first CJD symptom until death) in both f-CJD and s-CJD. Others found that both CSF and plasma t-τ levels were negatively associated with ST [13]. Moreover, a significant negative correlation between the t-τ in CSF level and disease duration was reported in 73 patients with f-CJD [14]. However, those latter studies were not designed to identify the predictive value of t-τ in CSF in terms of ST. In turn, Thompson et al. concluded that the level of t-τ in the blood correlates with the rate of the disease progression [15].

A Taiwanese study on s-CJD found that younger patients lived longer and that female sex was a favorable prognostic factor for survival [16]. In this study the first hypothesis, we tested was whether elevation of t-τ in the CSF, being a routine diagnostic test in all patients with CJD, can predict ST predominantly in patients with f-CJD, which has not been previously described, possibly due to the small number of such patients around the world.

The second hypothesis was that the aged brain would be more susceptible to the rapid neurodegeneration characteristic of CJD. Because Taupathies can occur in the brains of elders and without any connection to CJD, manifesting as Primary Age-Related Tauopathy [17] or Aging-related tau astrogliopathy (ARTAG) [18], they can cause an initial cognitive impairment that will coexist with other neurodegenerations. In these conditions, τ deposits accumulate in the brains of the elderly, which may act synergistically with prion infection, potentially accelerating the misfolding process and making neurons more susceptible. Therefore, in our cohort, which consisted predominantly of individuals with f-CJD, we investigated not only ST shortening associated with increased t-τ, but also age-related t-τ increase, i.e., correlations between these variables.

Methods

The t-τ levels in CSF were measured at the Neuroimmunology Laboratories of the Hadassah University Hospital and the Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center, the 2 national referral centers for CJD in Israel. These data were used to aid in establishing the clinical signs of CJD. They were subsequently entered into the Israeli National CJD Registry [19]. We analyzed data from the Registry from 1991 to 2022. Included were patients with either f-CJD or s-CJD for whom pertinent demographic information, t-τ levels in CSF and the date of death were available. Detailed information about the Registry, and classification as definite, probable and possible cases of CJD have been described elsewhere [20]. Diagnosis of CJD was confirmed according to the CJD Criteria released in 20241. The real-time quaking-induced conversion assay was not available in Israel until 2024. Thus, our study was based on CSF samples obtained over the past 31 years, much before the real-time quaking-induced conversion method has been developed. ST was calculated as the difference between the dates of CSF sampling and the dates of death. Because patients with CJD were hospitalized soon after the onset of clinical symptoms and their CSF samples were usually analyzed soon thereafter, we decided to use the period from the date of CSF sampling to the date of death as the ST, as we suppose this period is more accurate than the period from the onset of first symptoms.

The obtained information was entered into Excel 2016 tables. The cut-off points for the t-τ in CSF level and the patient’s age were determined by means of X-tile 3.6.1 software (Yale University, New Haven, CT, USA) [21], a tool for biomarker assessment and outcome-based cut-off point optimization2.

Statistical Analysis

CJD patients were dichotomized according to the above cut-off points and STs in each derived group, and the results were compared according to medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). We compared the data according to the Mann-Whitney U test for independent groups. Univariate Cox regression with hazard ratios Exp (B), and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and Spearmen correlation coefficients (rho) were calculated using the MedCalc statistical software package version 23.4.5 (MedCalc Software Ltd., Ostend, Belgium) to evaluate the relationships between the t-τ in CSF levels and patient’s ages and the influence of those two variables on ST and each other. Only p values <0.05 were considered significant.

Results

The Entire Cohort

Data on 183 CJD patients (96 males, 52.5%) were retrieved from the Registry and analyzed. The data included definite (n = 9), probable (n = 165), and possible (n = 9) CJD cases of whom 120 (61 males) patients were classified as f-CJD (65.6%) and 63 (35 males) patients were classified as s-CJD (34.4%) (Table 1). All 183 patients were deceased. The median age of the subjects was 62.4 years (IQR: 56.2–69.3; range: 37.2–87.9 years), the median ST was 83 days (IQR: 45-234; range: 3–971 days), and the median t-τ in CSF level was 1,151 pg/mL (IQR: 693–1,528.6; range: 111–3,787 pg/mL).

ST of CJD Patients Divided by the Cut-Off Points

According to the above cut-off point, the median ST in the low t-τ in CSF level group was 105 days (IQR: 59–305) compared to only 59 days in the high t-τ in CSF level group (IQR: 29.5–147.5). These groups differed significantly by the Mann-Whitney criterion (U = 2794, p < 0.001). The median ST in the younger age group was 87 days (IQR: 46.3–243) compared to only 46 days in the older age group (IQR: 23–90.5). However, the difference identified in these groups turned out to be insignificant, Mann-Whitney criterion (U = 688.5, p = 0.057) (Table 2).

The Association between the t-τ in CSF Level, Age, and Clinical Prognosis

The association between the above characteristics and ST of the CJD patients revealed that t-τ in CSF level (>1,226 pg/mL) could be significantly associated with reduced ST but patient age (>75.5 years) was not. We further investigated these characteristics by univariate analyses with Cox regression models. The Cox analyses showed that elevated t-τ in CSF was significantly associated with shorter ST (Exp β = 1.0003; 95% CI: 1.0001–1.0005, p = 0.0052). Thus, ST was shortened by 3% for every 100 pg/mL increase in t-τ in CSF. For example, if the median of all of the t-τ in CSF measurements in a given sample was 1,151 pg/mL, and the median of all STs in that sample was 83 days, then an increase in the t-τ in CSF by 100 pg/mL shortened the ST by approximately 2.5 days. In the absence of statistical differences in ST in the above-mentioned patient age-groups, they have no prognostic significance (Exp β = 0.9998; 95% CI: 0.9842–1.0156, p = 0.981). However, despite the lack of direct prognostic value for ST, age had a significant correlation with CSF t-τ levels (rho = 0.243, p < 0.001).

Discussion

Based on a larger number of measurements of t-τ levels in CSF compared with the analysis of Coban et al. [4], we found that a much lower than 4,000 pg/mL level of t-τ in CSF could predict ST in our cohort, which consisted of two-thirds persons with f-CJD. This effect has not been previously demonstrated in patients with f-CJD, while the prognostic value of CSF t-τ for patients with s-CJD has only been demonstrated in the aforementioned small clinical trial, which has not been confirmed by other studies. We determined a threshold value suitable for approximately predicting disease outcome: ≥1,226 pg/mL. This value slightly exceeded the diagnostic threshold of t-τ 1,000 pg/mL adopted in Israeli laboratories to confirm the clinical diagnosis of CJD. From our threshold, a further increase in the t-τ in CSF by every 100 pg/mL increased the likelihood of accelerated death, i.e., shortening ST by 3%.

It has previously been shown that the levels of t-τ in CSF in patients with f-CJD, who comprise almost two-thirds (65.6%) of the patients examined in this study, correlate with the disease burden, as reflected by the degree of cortical involvement in diffusion-weighted magnetic resonance imaging. A significant correlation (r = 0.617 p < 0.0001) was reportedly found between the t-τ in CSF level and the extent of cortical involvement [22]. Moreover, the correlation between the t-τ in CSF level and CJD severity was reported, including the degree of cognitive decline [23]. In patients with f-CJD suggests that it can be a biomarker reflecting the severity of neuronal damage [14, 23]. The current work confirms those findings since it appears that as the levels of t-τ in CSF increase; the more severe brain damage will develop, leading to reduced survival. A Slovak study based upon 101 cases of confirmed f-CJDE200K had reported that age does not have a significant effect on the t-τ in CSF level in f-CJDE200K [24].

Since the early studies of Otto et al. [25, 26], who found very high concentrations of the t-τ protein in the CSF of patients with CJD, the levels of t-τ in CSF have been used primarily as a diagnostic marker for both f-CJD and s-CJD with a sensitivity and specificity of 80–90% [12, 16, 24, 26–29]. Higher concentrations of t-τ in CSF were reported to indicate a greater probability of CJD. For example, if the level of t-τ in CSF is 976 pg/mL, then the probability of s-CJD corresponds to 0.52 (uncertain), while rising concentrations to a level of >10,000 pg/mL increases the probability of s-CJD to 0.89 (likely) [29, 30]. On the other hand, there are cases of CJD in which no increase in the level of CNS t-τ was found [26], indicating that the concentration of CNS t-τ is not an essential diagnostic characteristic of CJD [31].

Significantly higher values of t-τ in CSF were reported in s-CJD (4,718.7 pg/mL, range: 100–24,000 pg/mL) than in f-CJD caused by the E200K mutation (2,115.9 pg/mL, range: 50–16,000 pg/mL) as a characteristic feature of genetic CJD [32]. Abu-Rumeileh et al. [32] reported that elevated levels of t-τ in CSF were significantly associated with survival only in MM (V) 1 and VV2 genetic variants of s-SJD. A multifactorial prognostic model for overall survival after diagnosis of s-CJD containing age, sex, t-τ in CSF level and genetic testing was developed and tested using C-statistics in a group of 1,226 cases of s-CJD [33]. Chart scores were constructed based upon those calculations, allowing the prediction of the 6-month survival probability and median ST depending upon the genotype. That model, however, did not evaluate the direct effect of the level of t-τ in CSF concentrations on ST.

Our study did not reveal a direct effect of age at disease onset in CJD on ST. However, calculations revealed that the group of patients with CSF t-τ levels below the cut-off level were younger than those with CSF t-τ levels above the cut-off level and had a longer ST. Nevertheless, a comparison of median survival in the subthreshold and suprathreshold age at disease onset groups (77.5 years) and Cox regression data did not reveal a significant effect. A possible explanation could be the age-related increase in CSF t-τ levels, which was observed in previous studies [33].

Our study, which included both forms of CJD and primarily f-CJD, showed that the relationship between age at disease onset and ST can be characterized indirectly through CSF t-τ levels, as we found a small but significant positive correlation between patient age and CSF t-τ levels. Thus, we confirmed that older patients had significantly higher t-τ levels in the CSF.

Clinically and statistically, this means that a later age at CJD onset may be a strong predictor of a shorter ST, as it is biologically associated with more rapid neurodegenerative processes. This effect is largely explained by the fact that older patients tend to have higher t-τ concentrations in the CSF and, consequently, a higher rate of neuronal destruction.

It is known that patients with a later age at onset of sporadic CJD (60–80 years) are more likely to have more aggressive molecular subtypes of CJD, such as the MM1 or VV2 variants [34]. These subtypes are characterized by rapid neurodegeneration and a more rapid release of intracellular proteins into the CSF. However, higher CSF t-τ concentrations were also observed in older patients compared to younger patients with sporadic CJD subtype VV1, in whom the disease has an earlier onset and progresses more slowly [35]. Thus, the prognostic significance of age may be indirectly reflected by CSF t-τ levels, which serve as a surrogate marker for the rate of progression of both f-CJD and s-CJD.

Limitations of this work include its retrospective nature and a relatively small number of observations of patients with s-CJD, which did not allow us to conduct a comparative analysis of these subgroups separately and identify the specific features of CJD in each subgroup. Another limitation is that CSF sampling was used in order to confirm the diagnosis of CJD, and some laboratories reported high values of t-τ in CSF (>4,000 pg/mL) but not the exact levels of t-τ. These cases had to be excluded from our sample.

In conclusion, we found that an elevation of t-τ in CSF levels above the cut-off point at 1,226 pg/mL was accompanied by a shortening of ST by approximately one-half in patients with CJD (from a median of 105 days to a median of 59 days). Cox analyses showed that elevated levels of t-τ in CSF were significantly associated with shorter ST. This finding can be used for estimating individual prognosis. Age had no direct prognostic value, but positively correlated with CSF t-τ levels.

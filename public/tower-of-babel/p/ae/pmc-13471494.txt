MSTM-Net: a two-stage prostate cancer segmentation network based on swin-transformer-mamba architecture
Authors: Jiatao Chen, Xiang Liu, Shuohong Wang, Wenbin Ji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMagnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has become a core imaging modality for prostate cancer screening and diagnosis. Accurate and automatic segmentation of lesion regions is critical for subsequent staging assessment and treatment planning.MethodsTo this end, this research proposes a two-stage segmentation framework for multimodal MRI. In the first stage, the prostate gland is segmented to extract the region of interest (ROI), thereby removing complex pelvic background structures. In the second stage, fine-grained prostate cancer lesion segmentation is performed within the ROI, enabling the model to focus on anatomically plausible lesion regions.A segmentation network, termed MSTM-Net, is developed based on this framework. The network adopts a Swin Transformer–based decoder architecture. At the input stage, T2-weighted images and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps are spatially aligned and concatenated along the channel dimension. During decoding, a Mamba module based on state-space modeling is introduced to jointly capture local structural information and long-range dependencies. Multi-head attention fusion and multi-scale feature fusion are further integrated into the skip connections to enhance the consistency between shallow spatial details and deep semantic representations.ResultsExperiments conducted on the cleaned PROSTATEx dataset demonstrate that the proposed method achieves a Dice score of 95.38% for prostate gland segmentation and 63.89% for lesion segmentation, outperforming the best comparative network by approximately 4% points, with an mIoU of 61.32%. Furthermore, cross-dataset validation on the PI-CAI dataset yields a Dice score of 63.14%, indicating good generalization ability and clinical feasibility for automated prostate cancer segmentation.ConclusionThe proposed MSTM-Net demonstrates effective performance for prostate cancer segmentation in multimodal MRI, achieving improved accuracy and feature representation compared with existing methods. The results indicate that the two-stage framework combined with multi-modal fusion and state-space modeling is a promising approach, although further validation on larger and more diverse datasets is required to enhance robustness and generalization.Keywords: Prostate cancer, Deep learning, Segmentation, MSTM-Net

Introduction

According to GLOBOCAN 2020 data from the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), prostate cancer is one of the most common malignancies among men, accounting for approximately 7.3% of newly diagnosed cancer cases worldwide, with over 1.4 million new cases annually [1, 2]. Although prostate cancer is often asymptomatic at an early stage, timely detection is associated with high curability. Conventional screening based on prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing and systematic biopsy has contributed to reduced advanced disease and mortality, but it also carries a risk of overdiagnosis [3]. Consequently, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has become increasingly important in prostate cancer screening and clinical assessment.

Accurate lesion segmentation is essential in clinical diagnosis, yet prostate cancer segmentation is still largely performed manually in current practice, which is time-consuming and prone to inter-observer variability. Although deep learning-based prostate segmentation has shown promising performance, studies focusing specifically on prostate cancer lesion segmentation remain limited and require further improvement.

To address this challenge, we propose a multimodal two-stage segmentation network termed MSTM-Net (Multimodal Swin-Transformer-Mamba Network). The network employs a dual-modality Swin Transformer encoder to hierarchically extract features from T2-weighted images and ADC maps. A multimodal feature fusion module enables effective cross-modal interaction and semantic alignment. In the decoding stage, a state-space modeling-based Mamba decoder, combined with multi-scale feature fusion and skip connections, integrates deep semantic representations with shallow structural features. This design enhances lesion sensitivity while suppressing redundant background information, leading to more accurate and robust prostate cancer segmentation. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

We propose MSTM-Net, a multimodal two-stage segmentation framework that first delineates the prostate region and subsequently performs lesion segmentation within the region of interest. By jointly exploiting T2-weighted images and ADC maps through multimodal feature fusion, the network achieves effective cross-modal semantic alignment and complementary representation learning, thereby improving progressive prostate cancer segmentation performance.

Related work

Medical image segmentation

With the rapid advancement of deep learning, medical image segmentation has made substantial progress and has been widely applied in tasks such as disease diagnosis, image denoising, and anatomical structure segmentation. Among these tasks, accurate organ and lesion segmentation remains a fundamental challenge in medical image analysis.

In 2015, Long et al. introduced the Fully Convolutional Network (FCN), the first end-to-end deep learning framework for image segmentation [4]. By incorporating upsampling and shallow feature fusion, FCN laid the foundation for semantic segmentation and became a representative early model in medical image analysis.To address fine-grained segmentation with limited medical data, Ronneberger et al. proposed the U-Net architecture [5], which has become a cornerstone model in medical image segmentation. U-Net adopts a symmetric encoder-decoder structure with skip connections to fuse shallow and deep features, effectively preserving contextual information and boundary details.Based on U-Net, Zhou et al. introduced U-Net++ in 2018 [6], which enhances skip connections through dense pathways and deep supervision, enabling more effective multi-scale feature utilization and improved robustness. In 2020, Huang et al. proposed U-Net3+ [7], which employs full-scale feature aggregation in the decoder to unify hierarchical features, demonstrating strong performance in complex tasks such as multi-organ segmentation.In 2021, Liang et al. proposed MA-Net [8], which integrates multiple attention mechanisms into the U-Net framework, including position attention and multi-scale channel attention, enabling the network to better focus on salient regions in medical images with complex backgrounds.With the rise of Transformer-based vision models, Cao et al. introduced Swin-Unet in 2021 [9], incorporating the Swin Transformer into medical image segmentation. Through hierarchical window-based self-attention, Swin-Unet effectively models long-range dependencies and global contextual information, outperforming conventional CNN-based methods on multiple medical segmentation benchmarks.

Prostate segmentation studies

With the growing application of deep learning in medical image analysis, extensive studies have focused on prostate organ segmentation. Vacacela et al. proposed a dual encoder-decoder framework combining a global U-Net and a local U-Net [10], where the global network segments the whole prostate and the local network targets the central gland, with the peripheral zone obtained by region subtraction.Wang et al. introduced MDDU-Net, a hybrid dilated convolution U-Net with multi-scale dense connections [11], integrating hybrid dilated convolution modules, dense connections, and multi-scale feature fusion blocks, and demonstrated strong performance in prostate ultrasound segmentation. Fu et al. proposed a three-dimensional bidirectional multi-scale feature aggregation network (BMA-Net) [12], which combines global frequency-based feature filtering and Gaussian-guided spatial attention, achieving competitive results on public prostate segmentation datasets.

Prostate cancer segmentation studies

Although substantial progress has been made in prostate organ segmentation, prostate cancer lesion segmentation remains considerably more challenging. Clinically, lesions exhibit high variability in size, shape, and location, and often present subtle and ambiguous appearances on MRI, making accurate feature extraction and delineation difficult.Lin et al. evaluated a biparametric MRI-based method for intraprostatic lesion detection and segmentation [13], achieving reasonable detection performance but a low lesion Dice of 0.29. Mehralivand et al. trained a cascaded deep learning model using PI-RADS annotated biparametric MRI data [14], reporting a median lesion Dice of 0.359 and a classification accuracy of 30.8%. Chen et al. proposed MSR-Net for prostate cancer segmentation [15], which integrates multi-scale residual feature extraction and improved CGA-based fusion, achieving a Dice score of 60.5% in lesion segmentation experiments.

Summary

In recent years, deep learning has substantially advanced medical image segmentation, evolving from early models such as FCN and U-Net to improved variants (e.g., U-Net++), attention-based architectures such as MA-Net, and Transformer-based methods including Swin-Unet. These developments have enhanced multi-scale feature fusion, boundary delineation, and global dependency modeling, forming a solid technical foundation for medical image segmentation.In prostate imaging, existing approaches have effectively improved organ segmentation by exploiting global-local context, multi-scale features, and three-dimensional modeling. However, prostate cancer lesion segmentation remains more challenging due to high morphological variability, indistinct boundaries, and low image contrast. Despite recent progress, current methods still fall short of clinical requirements, highlighting the need for segmentation models capable of jointly capturing global contextual information and precise lesion representations to improve accuracy and robustness.

Method

Architecture of MSTM-Net

In prostate cancer lesion segmentation, large variability in lesion size and location, together with indistinct boundaries, poses significant challenges for accurate delineation. To address these issues, MSTM-Net is designed as a two-stage multimodal feature fusion network for prostate MRI segmentation. In the first stage, the network performs prostate gland segmentation to obtain a coarse localization of the prostate region. Based on the predicted mask, a region of interest (ROI) is extracted and spatially normalized, which is then used as the input to the second stage for fine-grained prostate cancer lesion segmentation. Notably, only spatial prior information is transferred between the two stages, without reusing intermediate feature representations, thereby maintaining structural decoupling and enhancing model flexibility. The framework consists of a multimodal pre-fusion strategy, a hierarchical Swin Transformer encoder, a Mamba-based decoder, multi-head attention fusion (MHAF), and a multi-scale feature fusion module (MSFF) embedded within skip connections.

Recent studies have demonstrated that hybrid architectures combining transformer-based representation learning with attention-guided feature modeling can effectively capture both global contextual information and fine-grained spatial details. In particular, hybrid attention-based multi-scale frameworks have shown strong capability in enhancing feature interaction and representation across different resolutions [16]. Inspired by these designs, the proposed framework integrates Swin Transformer, Mamba-based sequence modeling, and multi-scale feature fusion to achieve comprehensive feature representation for prostate cancer segmentation.

During multimodal fusion, T2-weighted images and ADC maps are preprocessed to ensure spatial and numerical consistency, and then concatenated along the channel dimension to form a unified six-channel input. This early fusion strategy enables joint modeling of cross-modal spatial and semantic correlations and facilitates effective exploitation of complementary information. The fused features are subsequently processed by lightweight convolution and normalization layers before being embedded into patches for hierarchical feature extraction.

The encoder adopts a multi-stage Swin Transformer architecture to model local-to-global contextual information through shifted window self-attention. Features extracted at different scales are delivered to the skip connections, where the MSFF module integrates multi-resolution representations via FPN, DFF, and PFF based branches, enhancing contextual consistency and feature completeness. In the decoding stage, a state-space-structured Mamba decoder is employed to reconstruct segmentation features. By combining dynamic state-space modeling with depthwise separable convolutions, the decoder effectively captures long-range dependencies while preserving local structural continuity, resulting in improved boundary delineation and structural integrity.

To further enhance cross-scale and cross-modality interactions, MHAF modules are incorporated into both the encoder and decoder, enabling adaptive attention weighting across spatial and channel dimensions. Such hybrid attention mechanisms, which combine multiple forms of feature interaction, have been shown to improve representation capacity and cross-scale information exchange in complex imaging tasks [16]. Finally, the refined features are transformed through normalization and convolution layers to generate the final segmentation outputs. The overall architecture of MSTM-Net is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Multimodal feature fusion

To facilitate effective cross-modal feature learning, semantic and scale alignment is performed before multimodal MRI inputs are fed into the backbone network. Accordingly, a multimodal feature fusion module is designed, consisting of modality preprocessing, channel-level fusion, and feature integration.In the preprocessing stage, T2-weighted and ADC images are spatially resampled and normalized to ensure consistent resolution and intensity distributions across modalities. Channel-level fusion is then performed by concatenating the two modalities along the channel dimension to form a six-channel input, which preserves modality-specific spatial details while enabling inter-modal interaction with low computational overhead. Subsequently, the fused features are processed by a shallow convolutional integration block with normalization and activation to achieve channel compression and semantic alignment. This operation mitigates modality-induced distribution gaps and extracts low-level cross-modal correlations, producing stable representations compatible with the backbone network. This design not only reduces computational cost but also enhances feature alignment and the effectiveness of cross-modal information interaction, providing a stable and efficient feature fusion scheme for multimodal prostate segmentation tasks.

Mamba block

During the decoding stage, to better handle the challenges of prostate cancer lesion segmentation, the original upsampling module is replaced with a Mamba-based decoder block. Leveraging state-space modeling, the Mamba block preserves local details while capturing long-range dependencies during feature upsampling, achieving a favorable balance between segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency.Specifically, the input features are first projected into a higher-dimensional latent space and then split into a main branch and a gating branch. The main branch applies depthwise separable convolution to enhance local contextual representation, followed by a lightweight state-space modeling (SSM) module to capture long-range dependencies. The gating branch dynamically modulates information flow, enabling effective feature propagation and global context modeling across the decoding process.

To further regulate inter-channel interactions, a gating mechanism is introduced to adaptively modulate the main-branch features via element-wise nonlinear weighting, enabling selective enhancement of informative channels. The gated features are then projected back to the original feature dimension through a linear layer. A residual connection is subsequently applied to preserve feature consistency and stabilize gradient propagation, followed by layer normalization to regularize feature distributions and improve training stability and generalization. Multi-head attention fusion

To enhance multimodal semantic alignment and multi-scale feature fusion, a Multi-Head Attention Fusion (MHAF) mechanism is incorporated into both the encoder and decoder. MHAF employs multiple parallel attention branches to jointly model global context, channel-wise selectivity, and spatial adaptivity, enabling dynamic feature re-weighting.The MHAF module consists of three complementary branches: Pyramid Attention Network (PAN) for multi-scale global semantics, Channel-Spatial Attention Module (CSAM) for highlighting salient regions, and Deformable Attention (DA) for adaptive receptive field modeling. The outputs of these branches are fused through a learnable weighting strategy to generate enhanced feature representations, as illustrated in Fig. 4.

Architecture of the MHAF module

In the PAN branch, multi-scale contextual information is captured using a pyramid structure with convolutional kernels of different receptive fields. Features from all pyramid levels are upsampled to a unified resolution and fused via concatenation and channel compression, producing semantically consistent multi-scale representations that enhance global context modeling and structural coherence.The CSAM branch enhances feature saliency through sequential channel and spatial attention. Channel attention assigns adaptive weights based on global response statistics, while spatial attention highlights critical lesion regions and boundaries, suppressing redundant information and improving localization accuracy.The DA branch introduces deformable attention to improve spatial adaptability. By dynamically predicting sampling offsets and weights, it adjusts the receptive field to accommodate irregular lesion shapes and complex anatomical structures, enabling precise spatial and semantic representation.

By integrating three complementary attention mechanisms, MHAF enables joint global dependency modeling, salient region enhancement, and adaptive spatial perception. This design improves semantic representation and spatial discriminability while maintaining computational efficiency, thereby enhancing lesion focus and boundary delineation in multimodal prostate cancer segmentation.

Multi-scale feature fusion

To improve multi-level feature representation, a Multi-Scale Feature Fusion (MSFF) module is introduced at the intermediate connections of MSTM-Net. MSFF integrates features from different encoder layers to enable cross-scale interaction and semantic alignment, balancing global context and local detail representation. As shown in Fig. 5, MSFF comprises three complementary branches: Feature Pyramid Network (FPN), Dense Feature Fusion (DFF), and Progressive Feature Fusion (PFF), each employing distinct fusion strategies.At the input stage, feature maps from multiple encoder depths are first projected into a unified feature space through channel alignment, ensuring consistency across different resolutions and channel dimensions. The aligned features are then fed into three parallel branches FPN, DFF, and PFF which respectively perform top-down semantic propagation, dense multi-level feature reuse, and progressive semantic refinement.

Architecture of the MSFF module

The MSFF module integrates three complementary fusion strategies. The FPN branch propagates high-level semantic information from deep to shallow layers via top-down pathways and lateral connections, enhancing semantic consistency while preserving fine-grained spatial details. The DFF branch introduces dense inter-layer connections to enable fully connected multi-scale information flow, improving feature completeness and representation compactness through channel compression. The PFF branch performs progressive, stage-wise feature fusion with adaptive weighting, dynamically balancing deep semantic consistency and shallow structural details to refine hierarchical feature representations under complex anatomical conditions.

After the three branches complete their respective fusion operations, the module concatenates their outputs and applies a convolutional layer for unified feature projection, as formulated in Eq. 12. After the projection and activation operations, the resulting fused feature not only preserves low-level texture details and edge information, but also integrates high-level semantic abstractions and global dependencies, thereby providing the subsequent decoder with structurally rich and information-complete input features.

Prostate organ segmentation

Dataset configuration

The prostate cancer dataset used in this research was derived from the publicly available PROSTATEx Challenge, which provides multimodal prostate MRI data, including T2-weighted (T2W) and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) images. The dataset inherently contains data from 203 patients with corresponding annotations, where the labels include two foreground categories, namely the prostate gland and lesion regions, in addition to the background. After preprocessing and slice-level filtering to remove non-lesion images, 1,090 T2W and 1,090 ADC images were retained for this study. The dataset was divided at the patient level with a ratio of 9:1, where data from 20 patients were selected as the independent test set, and the remaining data were used for training.

To ensure data consistency and improve model generalization, a standardized preprocessing pipeline was applied. All images were resized to a fixed spatial resolution of 320 × 320. Subsequently, intensity normalization was performed using channel-wise mean and standard deviation values of (0.485, 0.456, 0.406) and (0.229, 0.224, 0.225), respectively.To mitigate overfitting and enhance robustness, various data augmentation techniques were applied during training. Specifically, geometric transformations including random horizontal flipping (p = 0.5), vertical flipping (p = 0.3), and 90-degree rotation (p = 0.5) were employed. In addition, affine transformations such as random shifting, scaling (±10%), and rotation () were introduced. To simulate anatomical variability, elastic deformation was applied to mimic realistic tissue distortions. Furthermore, intensity-based augmentations including Gaussian noise, Gaussian blur, random brightness and contrast adjustment, and contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) were incorporated to enhance local contrast and feature diversity. All augmentation strategies were applied only to the training set, while validation and test sets underwent only resizing and normalization to ensure fair evaluation.

Evaluation metrics

To evaluate prostate segmentation performance, six quantitative metrics were employed: Dice similarity coefficient (Dice), global accuracy (G_Acc), F1-score (F1), mean Intersection over Union (mIoU), specificity, and Hausdorff distance (HD). Dice measures the overlap between predicted and ground truth regions, while G_Acc reflects overall pixel-wise classification accuracy. The F1-score balances precision and recall, and mIoU quantifies the average region overlap. In addition, specificity evaluates the model’s ability to correctly identify background pixels, which is particularly important for medical image segmentation with highly imbalanced class distributions. The Hausdorff distance measures the maximum boundary deviation between the predicted segmentation and the ground truth, providing a strict assessment of boundary accuracy. Together, these metrics provide a comprehensive evaluation of both region-based overlap and boundary-level segmentation performance.

Such incremental performance improvements are consistent with prior works emphasizing disciplined evaluation and careful interpretation of modular contributions in medical imaging systems [17].

Discussion

In this research, we proposed MSTM-Net, a multimodal feature fusion network with state-space modeling for high-precision prostate cancer lesion segmentation. Extensive experiments on MRI datasets demonstrate that MSTM-Net consistently outperforms competing methods across multiple metrics. Compared with the second-best model, MSTM-Net improves the Dice score by approximately 4.03% and the mIoU by 3.10%, indicating superior accuracy in boundary delineation and fine-grained lesion representation. In addition, for prostate organ segmentation, MSTM-Net achieves a Dice score of 95.4%, exceeding traditional convolution-based models by about 2.2%, which further confirms its strong feature modeling capability in complex anatomical structures.

Despite these promising results, several limitations remain. The current research is limited to dual-modality MRI data (T2W and ADC) with a relatively small dataset, and the model is trained on two-dimensional slices without fully leveraging three-dimensional volumetric information. Moreover, residual spatial misalignment and intensity inconsistencies between modalities may affect fusion effectiveness. In addition, the proposed model may be sensitive to variations in MRI acquisition protocols and differences among scanning devices, which are common in multi-center clinical scenarios. Such variations may lead to distribution shifts in the input data and consequently affect the generalization performance of the model. Furthermore, the imbalance in tumor size and morphological distribution within the training data may introduce bias, potentially resulting in less stable performance when handling lesions with significantly different characteristics. Finally, as the experimental dataset has undergone preprocessing and filtering, the model may exhibit a certain degree of dependency on relatively well-curated data, which could limit its robustness in real-world clinical applications.

Conclusion

In this work, we proposed a two-stage multi-modal segmentation network, MSTM-Net, for prostate cancer segmentation. The network employs a Swin-Transformer as the backbone encoder and introduces a multi-modal pre-fusion mechanism to align and semantically integrate T2-weighted (T2w) and ADC diffusion images at the input stage. In the decoder, a Mamba module based on state-space modeling is embedded to enhance long-range dependency modeling and structural reconstruction during upsampling. Additionally, multi-scale feature fusion (MSFF) and multi-head attention fusion (MHAF) modules are incorporated in the skip connections to achieve synergistic enhancement of shallow detailed features and deep semantic representations. Leveraging these designs, MSTM-Net achieved a lesion segmentation Dice score of 63.89% and mIoU of 61.32% on the prostate dataset constructed in this research. These results show clear improvements over classical segmentation networks and their variants, indicating that the proposed two-stage, multi-modal, and attention-enhanced framework is effective.

Future research will proceed in two directions. First, under the premise of maintaining a manageable parameter count, the current multi-modal pre-fusion strategy will be extended to three-dimensional or spatiotemporally consistent multi-sequence MRI data, thereby further optimizing the network architecture. In addition, to improve cross-center generalization, future work will incorporate multi-center and multi-scanner datasets, along with domain adaptation and data normalization strategies, to enhance robustness against variations in MRI acquisition protocols and scanner differences.Second, future work will further refine the existing network design to improve the representation of subtle lesion features, particularly in regions with low contrast and weak texture variations. This includes enhancing feature consistency and stability within the Mamba-based decoding process, thereby improving the overall segmentation accuracy and robustness for prostate cancer.

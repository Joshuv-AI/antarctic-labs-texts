Predicting psychological distress in informal caregivers of older people with dementia: development and interpretation of a nomogram and XGBoost model
Authors: Wenli Shi, Siwei Tian, Yixin Wang, Chan Cai, Bing Cheng, Chenyang Li, Xuan Liu, Chongqing Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMost informal caregivers of older people with dementia (PwD) experience a high level of psychological distress, which significantly impacts caregivers’ psychosomatic health. Effective identification and timely intervention for psychological distress are of great significance for improving the psychosomatic health of informal caregivers. However, there is a lack of predictive models for psychological distress in this population. This study aims to develop and interpret nomogram and eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) models to predict the psychological distress of informal caregivers of PwD.MethodsThis cross-sectional study collected 485 pairs of PwD and informal caregivers from October 2023 to September 2024. Participants were randomly divided into training and validation cohorts (7:3 ratio). We constructed nomogram and XGBoost models to predict psychological distress. The nomogram was subsequently developed into an online web calculator, and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) was employed to enhance the XGBoost model’s interpretability.ResultsThe final models included six predictors: caregivers’ social support, caregivers’ employment situation, caregivers’ average daily care duration, caregivers’ monthly family income, caregivers’ self-efficacy and PwD’s chronic disease. The area under the curve (AUC) of nomogram and XGBoost models in the validation cohort were 0.882 (95%CI: 0.825-0.939) and 0.884 (95%CI: 0.829-0.939) respectively. Two models exhibited good calibration and clinical utility. SHAP analysis identified caregivers’ social support as the most influential predictor of psychological distress in XGBoost model.ConclusionThe nomogram and XGBoost models developed in this study offer valuable tools for predicting psychological distress in informal caregivers of PwD. The nomogram-based web calculator could be an effective tool for predicting the risk of psychological distress in practice. Our interpretable XGBoost model provides valuable insights into individualized intervention strategies for psychological distress.Trial registrationNot applicable. This study is an observational study without involving any interventions or treatment methods. Therefore, this study does not meet the definition of a clinical trial and does not require registration of a clinical trial number.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12912-025-03905-0.Keywords: Psychological distress, Dementia, Caregivers, Prediction models, Nomogram, eXtreme gradient boosting

Introduction

Dementia is one of the leading causes of disability and dependency among older people worldwide [1]. China has the largest number of people with dementia (PwD) in the world, accounting for about 29.8% of the global patients [2, 3]. In China, approximately 96% of PwD receive care at home from informal caregivers [2, 4]. Informal caregivers are relatives, partners, friends, or neighbors who provide unpaid care for PwD and usually have no professional training [5, 6]. Informal caregivers need to provide PwD with life, emotional, physical and many aspects of care [7]. Many heavy caring tasks place a great burden on informal caregivers, and uninterrupted care increases informal caregivers’ stress and affects their mental health [3, 6, 7]. About 38.9% to 64.4% of caregivers have experienced psychological distress [8–10]. Psychological distress is a kind of non-specific negative psychological emotion produced by individuals when facing specific pressures or demands. It is a multifactorial, painful experience of psychological, social, spiritual, and/or physical nature that can progress from common sadness and vulnerability to serious psychological disorders such as anxiety, depression, and social isolation [11]. Psychological distress may increase caregivers’ potential risk of physical and mental illness and reduce caregiving capacity [8]. It is important to identify informal caregivers of PwD at risk of psychological distress and take measures to prevent symptoms from worsening.

Psychological distress is not a single subjective experience, but contains a multidimensional structure [12], and the influencing factors of psychological distress are diverse. Up to the present, most studies have paid more attention to the influencing factors of caregivers’ psychological distress. Some studies mainly focused on the personal characteristics of caregivers, finding that caregivers’ age, gender, relationship with patients, and length of care were related to psychological distress of caregivers [13, 14]. There was also a theory-based study exploring the relationship between stressors, psychological support resources and depressive symptoms among caregivers of PwD [15]. Lazarus and Folkman’s Stress Coping Model (SCM) has been widely used to guide the understanding of the poor psychological status of caregivers [16, 17]. According to SCM, stress coping included stressors, cognitive appraisal, coping strategies and stress outcomes. The stressors not only come from caregivers, patients’ socio-demographic factors and care-related factors [18], but also the burden of the disease. SCM emphasized that an individual’s cognition of stress events determines the level of stress [19]. As cognitive appraisal, self-efficacy reflects the caregiver’s evaluation of stress and belief in their capability to successfully complete specific caregiving tasks [20]. Caregivers with high self-efficacy have a positive perception of the caregiving experience, protecting caregivers from negative emotion [20]. The coping strategies of caregivers play a vital role in improving individual psychological status, and positive coping could promote caregivers to adapt to the role of caregivers [19]. Family and social support are important resources for caregivers to take positive coping strategies [21]. Good family function may improve the mental health of caregivers [21]. High-level social support is an important force to alleviate caregivers’ stress and negative emotions caused by stress events [22]. This study took caregivers’ psychological distress as the stress outcome, and explored the predictive factors of psychological distress from the perspective of stress coping based on SCM theory.

Prediction model is a possible way to identify psychological distress. At present, there are still differences in choosing Machine Learning (ML) algorithm or traditional Logistic Regression (LR) analysis to construct disease prediction model in clinical work. Psychological distress prediction models for informal caregivers of PwD remain underdeveloped. Decision tree method was applied to construct a depression prediction model of caregivers of PwD by Byeon based on the community health survey database in Korea [23]. This model was suitable for predicting depression but not psychological distress of informal caregivers. Other predictive models of psychological distress were developed mostly for cancer patients. And the nomogram and XGBoost model explained by SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) were widely applied and performed well [24, 25]. But few studies have applied nomogram and XGBoost to predict the risk of psychological distress in informal caregivers.

The nomogram is a traditional tool which often be used for visualizing LR model and represents the degree of influence of different factors through a series of lines and scales [26]. The design of nomogram is user-friendly and convenient in practical application [26]. Nomogram-based online web calculator was suggested to apply for improving model practicability [27]. XGBoost, a scalable tree boosting system, stands out among many ML models with good predictive performance [28, 29]. But XGBoost’s poor interpretability limits its application [30]. SHAP as a way to interpret various black-box ML models, visually shows the contribution of each feature to the final prediction, which could effectively improve the deficiency of XGBoost’s poor interpretation [30, 31]. Hence, this study used nomogram and XGBoost to develop effective psychological distress prediction models to enrich practical applications, and improve the interpretability limitations of ML to provide a valuable reference for future psychological distress intervention research.

Therefore, this study attempts to construct reliable and robust prediction models available to identify the risk of psychological distress in informal caregivers of older PwD based on stress coping factors from SCM theory. Moreover, nomogram-based web calculator is developed to obtain specific numerical values on the risk of psychological distress after inputting each predictive factor. Additionally, SHAP is used to measure the importance of each predictor in the model prediction and the contribution of predictors to individual predictions.

Methods

Samplings and inclusion criteria

The cross-sectional study was conducted from October 2023 to September 2024 in seven tertiary hospitals of Wuhan, Xiangyang and Zaoyang in Hubei Province. An offline questionnaire survey was carried out on older PwD and their informal caregivers by convenience sampling. We applied an established rule of thumb for the sample size required to develop a binary outcome prediction model-10 Events Per Variable (10 EPV) [32]. The rule of 10 EPV requires at least 10 positive samples of events for each predictor included in the prediction model. A total of 27 candidate predictors were included in this study, and 10% of the invalid questionnaires were considered. The prevalence of psychological distress among caregivers of PwD was about 38.9%-64.4% [8–10]. Thus the minimum sample size of this study should be 461. A total of 500 questionnaires were distributed in this study, and 15 questionnaires were invalid, with an effective rate of 97%. The final sample size of this study was 485 pairs of PwD and informal caregivers. The samples were randomly divided into a training cohort (70%) and a validation cohort (30%) [29]. The training cohort was used for feature selection and model training, and the validation cohort was used for internal validation. The study flowchart was showed in Fig. 1.

The overall flowchart of the study

The inclusion criteria for PwD were: (i) age ≥ 60 years; (ii) meeting the World Health Organization (WHO)’s International Classification of Diseases, 10th Edition (ICD-10) diagnostic criteria for dementia [33]. PwD with other critical illnesses (e.g., severe liver and kidney disease, cancer) or psychiatric disorders (e.g., schizophrenia), as well as PwD at the end of life were excluded. The inclusion criteria for informal caregivers were: (i) age ≥ 18 years; (ii) the duration of care ≥ 3 months, ≥ 8 h/week, undertaking the main caring work; (ⅲ) being conscious, having normal intelligence, and being able to understand and complete the questionnaire, voluntarily participate in this study and sign the informed consent form. Informal caregivers who had recently experienced other major stressful events or psychotherapy were excluded. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Wuhan University of Science and Technology (Ethical committee code: 2022105).

Psychological distress assessment

The psychological distress of informal caregivers was assessed using the Kessler psychological distress scale (K10) developed by Kessler et al. [34]. Zhou et al. tested the reliability and validity of the Chinese version of the scale [35]. The K10 scale was widely used as a clinical outcome measure [36]. The scale consists of 10 items using a 5-point Likert scale (1 = no time, 5 = all time). The total score of the scale is 10–50 points, with more than 15 points indicating psychological distress [37]. In this study, caregivers were divided into psychological distress group (K10 score ≥ 15) and no psychological distress group (K10 score < 15). The Cronbach’s α of this scale in this study was 0.930.

Model input features

General demographic characteristics

A self-designed questionnaire was used to collect the general information of the subjects. Older PwD’s characteristics include: gender, age (years), education level, marital status, number of children, type of medical expenses payment, Activity of Daily Living (ADL), type of dementia, duration of illness (years), chronic disease (hypertension, diabetes, etc.). Informal caregivers’ characteristics include: gender, age (years), education level, marital status, employment situation, monthly family income (yuan), domicile place, relationship with PwD, whether they lived with the PwD, average daily care duration (h), patient care duration (years), self-assessment of physical condition.

Family burden of disease

The burden of disease on the informal caregivers was assessed using the Family Burden Scale of disease developed by Indian scholars Pai and Kapur in 1981 [38]. The Chinese version was released by Chien in 2004 [39]. This scale includes 24 items with 6 dimensions, each with a score of 0–2 (0 = no impact, 2 = severe impact). The total score is 0–48 points. The higher the score, the higher the family disease burden. The Cronbach’s α for this scale in this study was 0.920.

Self-efficacy

General Self-efficacy Scale was used to determine the level of self-efficacy of informal caregivers [40]. This scale was developed by Schwarzerd and Jerusalem [40]. The Chinese version was translated by Wang [41]. The scale includes 10 items with 4 dimensions, and each item is scored on a scale of 1–4 (1 = completely incorrect, 4 = completely correct). The total score of the scale rangers from 10 to 40 points, with higher scores representing greater in self-efficacy. The Cronbach’s α for this scale in this study was 0.937.

Positive coping

Simplified Coping Style Questionnaire was used to assess the coping styles of informal caregivers [42]. This scale was revised and developed by Xie based on the established international coping style scale [43]. The scale contains two dimensions: positive coping (12 items) and negative coping (8 items) and scores it on a Likert 3-scale (0 = never, 2 = always). This study only uses the positive coping dimension with a total score of 0–36. Higher scores indicate that participants are more likely to adopt a positive coping style. The Cronbach’s α for this scale in this study was 0.814.

Family function

The Family Adaptation, Partnership, Growth, Affection, Resolve Index developed by Smilkstein in 1978 was used to evaluate family function of the informal caregivers [44]. The Chinese version was released by Lyu and Gu in 1995 [45]. This scale estimates family function from 5 items and scores it on a Likert 3-scale (0 = almost never, 2 = almost always), with higher scores representing to better family function. The Cronbach’s α for this scale in this study was 0.721.

Social support

Social Support Rating Scale designed by Xiao was used to measure the level of social support for informal caregivers [46]. This scale has 10 items with a total score of 12–66. The higher the score, the higher the level of social support. The Cronbach’s α for this scale in this study was 0.825.

Quality control procedures

Before the start of the study, the researchers were uniformly trained. Uniform guidelines were used to explain the purpose, content, and confidentiality of the survey to participants, and to instruct participants to fill out the questionnaire truthfully. Closed questions were employed to prevent reporting bias among study subjects. During the data entry process, a two-person input method were used to check and input data. Missing values and data inconsistent with the questions were eliminated.

Model visualization and interpretation

The nomogram was used to visualize the results of LR model and draw multiple line segments of specific proportion according to the regression results, so as to conveniently calculate the occurrence probability of individual psychological distress. In addition, an online web calculator was further developed for the nomogram model to facilitate the practical application. SHAP was used to interpret the prediction results of the XGBoost model from the global and local levels [31]. The SHAP value reflects the contribution of each feature to the model predictions [47]. The higher the SHAP value of the feature, the higher the risk of psychological distress of the caregivers. We used SHAP summary plot and feature importance plot to sort the feature importance. And through the SHAP force plot of true positive and true negative samples, the prediction of the individuals was explained.

Statistical analysis

All data analysis was implemented in SPSS 26.0 [48] and R4.3.3 [49]. SPSS 26.0 was used for descriptive and inferential statistical analysis. Categorical variables were described as frequencies and percentages. Continuous variables were expressed as medians (interquartile ranges, IQRs). Chi-square test was used for categorical variables, and Mann-Whitney U test was used for continuous variables to compare differences between training and validation cohorts. P < 0.05 was considered a threshold of statistical significance.

R4.3.3. was adopted for models’ development, evaluation and interpretation. We normalized the variables by Z-score before the feature screening. The Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression combined with multivariate logistic regression analysis were used to screen predictors [50]. This method further reduces the number of variables, prevents model’s overfitting, and avoids the effects of multicollinearity between variables [50, 51]. Two models were developed: nomogram and XGBoost. Grid search combined with manual fine-tuning was used to optimize model hyperparameters, with the optimal parameters listed in Supplementary Table 1. Model performance was assessed by the area under the receiver operator characteristic curve (AUC) (1000 bootstrap resamples), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, Positive Predictive Value (PPV), Negative Predictive Value (NPV) and F1-score. The value of these statistics is closer to 1, indicating the better the predictive performance of the models. Brier score and calibration curve were used to evaluate model calibration. Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) was applied to assess the clinical utility of the models in decision-making. The Net Reclassification Improvement (NRI) and Integrated Discrimination Improvement (IDI) were adopted to compare the predictive power of two models. Compared with the reference model, NRI or IDI >0 (P < 0.05) indicated a positive improvement [52]. Key R packages used in R analysis included: “glmnet” [53] for LASSO variable selection; “xgboost” [54] for the development of XGBoost model; “rms” [55] for nomogram development and calibration; “caret” [56] for parameter tuning and outputting evaluation metrics; “pROC” [57] for ROC analysis; “dcurves” [58] for DCA; “regplot” [59] for drawing nomogram; “shapviz” [60] for model interpretation; and “shiny” [61] for web deployment.

Results

Participant characteristics

A total of 485 pairs of PwD and informal caregivers were included in this study, with basic characteristics summarized in Tables 1 and 2. Among the PwD, Alzheimer’s disease was the most common type (n = 238, 49.1%). The duration of the disease in most PwD was 1 ~ 5 years (n = 236, 48.7%). Informal caregivers were predominantly female (n = 307, 63.3%) and aged over 45 years (n = 404, 83.3%). The majority (68.2%) provided daily care exceeding 8 h. A total of 200 cases (41.2%) of informal caregivers had psychological distress. There were no statistically significant differences in all characteristics of PwD and their informal caregivers between the training and validation cohorts (P>0.05).

Development and evaluation of nomogram and XGBoost models

The above screened independently correlated predictors were used to construct nomogram and XGBoost prediction models of psychological distress in the training cohort. Table 4 summarizes the performance of two models in training and validation cohorts. In the validation cohort, the AUC and accuracy of the models demonstrated robust performance, both exceeding 0.8, with the ROC curves presented in Fig. 2 (A, B). The sensitivity, specificity, PPV and NPV further demonstrated the effectiveness of the models in different evaluation dimensions. F1-score indicated that models were able to balance recall and precision. The Brier score of less than 0.25 and calibration curve (Fig. 2C, D) indicated that two models had good calibration. Using nomogram as a reference model, the NRI in validation cohort was 0.0046 (95%CI: -0.0895 ~ 0.0988; p = 0.923), IDI was − 0.0256 (95%CI: -0.0608 ~ 0.0097; p = 0.155). It showed that there was no difference between nomogram and XGBoost models in predicting power.

We could find that the lower level of social support, the higher the risk of psychological distress in informal caregivers. Web application of the nomogram model

Based on the comprehensibility of the nomogram, we further developed it into an online web calculator for easy application in the community or clinical setting (Fig. 4B, https://predictingpsychologicaldistress.shinyapps.io/dynnomapp/). By inputting the actual values of six predictors, the web application could automatically predict a caregiver’s risk of psychological distress.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to apply nomogram and XGBoost to predict the risk of psychological distress in informal caregivers of older PwD. Our models identified key risk factors associated with psychological distress in informal caregivers of PwD. In addition, our nomogram and XGBoost models achieved more than 80% prediction accuracy and had good model fitting and clinical applicability. An online web calculator was implemented to facilitate clinical application. In practice, the nomogram-based web calculator could automatically perform complex model calculations, and healthcare personnel could screen informal caregivers for psychological distress through simple interviews. XGBoost-based SHAP evaluated the importance of each variable at the global level and provided valuable explanations for the source of individual prediction.

The nomogram is widely employed in clinical risk prediction due to the intuitive visual representation of complex statistical relationships. In addition, the XGBoost model is introduced, which has high scalability and fast computing performance. Compared with other models, XGBoost could effectively reduce the possibility of overfitting [28]. And the combination with SHAP compensates for the lack of interpretability in XGBoost.

This study found for the first time that social support was the most influential predictor of psychological distress through SHAP importance analysis. Although social support has been reported to be a protective factor for caregiver’s psychological distress [62], but few studies explored the role of social support as a coping resource related to stress coping in predicting psychological distress. As an important resource to cope with caring stress, good social support provides spiritual and material help to individuals, which is closely related to the caregivers’ psychosomatic health [63]. The identification of this top-ranked feature suggests its potential role in preventing and intervening in psychological distress. Then caregivers-level stressors (employment situation, average daily care duration, monthly family income) were also important as predictors of psychological distress. In this study, it was found that employed informal caregivers were at greater risk of psychological distress, which was inconsistent with some previous studies [18, 64]. Although employment may help individuals benefit in terms of financial resources, socialization, etc [65], the employed informal caregivers are under pressure from both work and caring, which may lead the informal caregivers’ role overload and work conflicts [66] resulting in negative emotions. Average daily care duration of more than 8 h, low monthly income were risk factors for psychological distress. This finding were similar to most previous studies [14, 67]. It was worth noting that caregivers’ self-efficacy was an important predictor as a cognitive appraisal of stress coping process. High level of self-efficacy is a protective factor for caregivers’ psychological distress. Informal caregivers with high self-efficacy are better able to grasp the caregivers’ role, thereby increasing the positive feelings of caregiving and reducing the negative emotion [68]. Finally, PwD-level stressor (coexistence of other chronic diseases) was a risk predictor of caregivers’ psychological distress. Facing PwD with other chronic diseases, caregivers need to take more responsibility for the management of complex diseases, so they would suffer greater psychological pressure [69].

Combined with the predictors of this study, prevention and intervention measures for psychological distress could be implemented at social, family and individual levels. In China, informal caregivers get less support services and low utilization of social resources [62, 70]. Community or institutional support providers should consider the construction of structured support systems (e.g., adult day care programs, counseling services, community-based respite care programs, home visiting groups, etc.) [10, 70, 71]. Besides, healthcare professionals could encourage informal caregivers to actively seek informal social support from relatives and friends, share the care work and obtain emotional support. For the informal caregivers themselves, our models could facilitate the self-monitoring of psychological distress. And we emphasize the importance of actively taking PwD for physical examinations to detect comorbidities early and treat them accordingly. In addition, empowering caregivers could help individuals discover and develop self-management ability, enhance personal self-confidence, and improve self-efficacy to prevent psychological distress [68].

The screened predictors could be obtained by simple interview without invasive operation during assessment, which is convenient and economical. The prediction models composed of these factors could be applied to both clinical and community settings to identify informal caregivers at high risk of psychological distress. Our prediction models could be used as a supplement and expansion of the existing psychological distress assessment tool to quickly and accurately screen multi-dimensional psychological distress. At the same time, this study developed an online web platform (https://predictingpsychologicaldistress.shinyapps.io/dynnomapp/). When providing the values of relevant features, the platform could simply output the probability of psychological distress of informal caregivers. The platform could facilitate the application of the model in clinical settings. And future integration with electronic health record systems is feasible.

Limitations

The present study had several limitations. Firstly, convenience sampling may limit representativeness of samples, affecting the generalizability of results. Future study could validate findings with more representative samples. Secondly, although this study has SCM as a theoretical basis for choosing predictive factors, the included factors are still relatively limited. We suggest that future model refinements could incorporate factors such as objective indicators (e.g., neuropsychiatric symptoms of PwD), and environmental factors (e.g., accessibility of care support services), etc. These factors are also based on the SCM, which may improve the comprehensiveness and accuracy of the model. Thirdly, the prediction models in this study have not been independently externally validated. Widespread application may have the risk of insufficient generalization ability of its prediction performance in different populations, regions and time dimensions. Fourthly, some of the variables measured by self-report in this study may have contributed to information bias. Nevertheless, this kind of self-reported data collected in primary health care institutions is more convenient to popularize.

Conclusion

Our nomogram and XGBoost models could accurately predict the risk of psychological distress in informal caregivers of older PwD and have good performance. The nomogram-based web calculator provides a predictive tool for practical applications. The XGBoost-based SHAP makes the prediction results more intuitive and transparent. The six key predictors related to stress coping process based on SCM provide targeted reference for prevention and intervention of psychological distress.

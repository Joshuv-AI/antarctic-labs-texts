Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion Using the Amplatzer Amulet Device in High-Risk Patients With Atrial Fibrillation Undergoing Transcatheter Aortic Valve Intervention: A Randomized Pilot Study
Authors: Philipp Jakob, Priska Heinz, Yiqi Gong, Mi Chen, Ronald K. Binder, Ulrike Held, Maurizio Taramasso, Francesco Paneni, Philipp Schuetz, Philipp K. Haager, Markus Kasel, Giovanni Pedrazzini, Stephan Windecker, Marco Moccetti, Thomas Pilgrim, Thomas F. Lüscher, Barbara E. Stähli, Fabian Nietlispach, TAVI-LAAO Investigators
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPatients with severe aortic stenosis and atrial fibrillation (AF) undergoing transcatheter aortic valve intervention (TAVI) are at increased risk of bleeding and cerebrovascular events. This investigator-initiated, randomized, multicenter, open-label pilot study assessed left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) in patients with AF undergoing TAVI.MethodsPatients were randomly assigned to LAAO (TAVI + LAAO) or standard medical therapy (SMT) (TAVI + SMT). The primary endpoint was a composite of cerebrovascular events, peripheral embolism, life-threatening/disabling/major bleeding, or cardiovascular mortality at 1 year. A sensitivity analysis was performed in the per-protocol population.ResultsEighty-one patients (Society of Thoracic Surgeons score: 9.0% ± 5.4%) were enrolled. The primary endpoint occurred in 13 patients (33%) in the TAVI + LAAO group and in 15 patients (37%) in the TAVI + SMT group (adjusted odds ratio [OR], 0.87; 95% CI: 0.32-2.29, p = 0.77). Bleeding rates were comparable between TAVI + LAAO (13%) and TAVI + SMT (17%), with absent nonprocedural bleeding in the TAVI + LAAO group and 5 gastrointestinal bleedings in TAVI + SMT, and cerebrovascular events did not significantly differ between groups (10% in TAVI + LAAO vs. 2.4% in TAVI + SMT). In the per-protocol analysis, occurrence of the primary endpoint was comparable between groups (adjusted OR, 0.55; 95% CI: 0.18-1.56, p = 0.27) with cerebrovascular events in 5.6% and 2.4%, and bleeding events in 8.3% and 17% for TAVI + LAAO and TAVI + SMT, respectively.ConclusionsThis pilot study suggests that among high-risk patients with AF undergoing TAVI, a strategy of a combined procedure with LAAO and early cessation of oral anticoagulation overall showed similar rates of the primary end point as compared to a single TAVI procedure (NCT03088098).

Introduction

One-third of patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis undergoing transcatheter aortic valve intervention (TAVI) have pre-existing atrial fibrillation (AF) and around 10% develop new-onset AF in the periprocedural period.1, 2, 3, 4, 5 Patients with AF undergoing TAVI are at increased risk of bleeding, cerebrovascular and thromboembolic events, and death.2, 3, 4,6, 7, 8

Oral anticoagulation (OAC) in patients undergoing TAVI with concomitant AF is recommended by current guidelines.9,10 However, conflicting results exist regarding the efficacy of OAC for the prevention of cerebrovascular and thromboembolic events in AF patients undergoing TAVI.11 As OAC increases rates of bleeding, the cumulative risk may outweigh neurovascular benefits.11,12

An alternative strategy to reduce the risk of cerebrovascular and bleeding events in patients with AF is the percutaneous closure of the left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO).13,14 Randomized trials have shown that LAAO is noninferior in composite endpoints including death, stroke, systemic embolism, and bleeding as compared to OAC in selected patients with AF.15, 16, 17, 18 In addition, several observational studies have shown the safety and feasibility of LAAO in elderly patients.19, 20, 21, 22 In patients with AF undergoing TAVI, the randomized WATCHMAN for Patients with AF Undergoing TAVR (WATCH-TAVR) trial was noninferior for the primary endpoint of all-cause mortality, stroke, and major bleeding in patients undergoing LAAO with the Watchman 2.5 device as compared to medical therapy.23 In a retrospective analysis of patients undergoing TAVI with concomitant AF, we reported feasibility of LAAO using the Amplatzer Cardiac Plug or Amplatzer Amulet Left Atrial Appendage Occluder (Amulet) (Abbott, USA).24 The Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation–Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion (TAVI-LAAO) trial was designed to investigate the feasibility of the Amulet device with initiation of dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) as compared to standard medical therapy (SMT) in patients with AF undergoing TAVI.

Methods

Study Design

The TAVI-LAAO trial (clinicaltrials.gov Identifier: NCT03088098) was an investigator-initiated, multicenter, randomized, open-label trial that evaluated a strategy of LAAO with the Amulet device compared with a strategy of SMT in an all-comer population of patients with AF undergoing TAVI. The study was conducted at 3 sites in Switzerland (i.e., University Hospital Zurich, Inselspital Bern, and Cardiocentro Ticino). This is a pilot study, and because of the exploratory nature, no formal sample size calculations were performed. The study design is provided in the Supplementary Appendix. The protocol was designed by the principal investigators and was approved by the ethics committees at all sites (PB_2016-00912). Written informed consent was obtained from each patient prior to enrollment. An independent clinical events committee adjudicated all endpoint-related adverse events. The study was funded by the Foundation for Cardiovascular Research - Zurich Heart House (Zurich, Switzerland) and an unrestricted grant from Abbott (Switzerland).

Patients were eligible if they were in AF (paroxysmal, permanent, and persistent) with a CHA2DS2-VASc score of 1 or more in the absence of mobile thrombus in the LA or LAA. All patients had an indication for TAVI. There were no clinical exclusion criteria, except for pregnancy, known intolerance to aspirin, clopidogrel, heparin, vitamin K antagonists (VKA), direct oral anticoagulants (DOAC), contrast media, or nickel. Furthermore, patients were excluded if they had another indication for OAC (besides AF) or a contraindication for OAC.

Patients underwent randomization before TAVI if pre-existing AF was present or after TAVI (and before LAAO) if new-onset AF was detected. Randomization was performed via a web-based program (SecuTrial, interActive Systems GmbH, Berlin, Germany) using unstratified multi block randomization. Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either TAVI + LAAO or TAVI + SMT.

Procedures

TAVI was performed using either a transfemoral access or, in case of limiting peripheral arterial disease, a transapical, subclavian, or direct aortic access. Use of the Portico Transcatheter Aortic Valve System (Abbott, USA) was encouraged. Prosthesis size was selected based on annular measurements by multislice computed tomography or transesophageal echocardiography (TEE).

In patients with concomitant LAAO, operators were encouraged to perform LAAO as a combined procedure with TAVI. If this was not done, LAAO had to be performed before discharge according to protocol. Before the procedure, antibiotic prophylaxis was administered, as well as heparin (the latter could be delayed until after transseptal puncture). The left atrium was accessed by transseptal puncture using TEE or fluoroscopic guidance. Using a stiff guide wire, the transseptal sheath was exchanged by the dedicated Amplatzer TorqVue sheath (Abbott, USA). Device sizing relied on contrast injections of the LAA in different angulations and/or multiple views by TEE. Thereafter, an appropriately sized device was chosen. After device deployment, a stable and proper device position was confirmed by a tug test and imaging (fluoroscopy and/or TEE). Thereafter, the device was released.

Antithrombotic Therapy

TAVI + LAAO

After LAAO, the recommended medical therapy consisted of DAPT for at least 6 months, followed by single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) (unless another indication for DAPT existed). OAC was immediately stopped after the procedure.

TAVI + SMT

Antithrombotic therapy was left at the operator's discretion and could consist of triple therapy (DAPT + VKA/DOAC), dual therapy (1 antiplatelet agent + VKA/DOAC), DAPT (aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor), single VKA/DOAC, or SAPT.

Follow-up was scheduled at 30 days (phone call), at 6 months, and at 1 year (phone call). As part of each visit, patient history, medication, and occurrence of endpoints/adverse events were assessed. In case of an adverse event, hospital charts were obtained and/or treating family physicians were contacted.

Study Endpoints

The primary endpoint consisted of the composite of cerebrovascular events, peripheral embolism, life-threatening/disabling and major bleeding, or cardiovascular mortality at 1 year after randomization. Secondary end points were the individual components of the primary end point at 1 year, all-cause mortality at 30 days and 1 year, device success at 30 days, and in-hospital acute kidney injury. Detailed endpoint definitions are listed in the Supplementary Appendix.

Statistical Analysis

Baseline demographic data were summarized using mean and SD or median and interquartile range for continuous and ordinal variables, as well as numbers and percentages for categorical variables. Any imbalances between randomized treatment groups were assessed by standardized mean differences. The primary analysis of the primary endpoint was performed as a multivariable logistic regression model with treatment (TAVI + LAAO vs TAVI + SMT) and adjustment for sex, CHA2DS2-VASc score, and HAS-BLED score, based on prior evidence in the literature25,26 and an absolute standardized mean differences >0.15. The primary analysis was performed in the intention to treat (ITT) population. A sensitivity analyses was performed in the per-protocol population. Additional analyses for the primary endpoint were conducted in the following subgroups: sex (female vs male), age (<80 years, ≥80 years), history of diabetes (yes, no), type of AF (paroxysmal, permanent/persistent), CHA2DS2-VASc score (≤3, >3), HAS-BLED score (<3, ≥3), and Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) score (<8%, ≥8%). No missing value approach was needed. Survival curves using Kaplan-Meier estimates or cumulative incidence curves were constructed for all time-to-event variables (first observed event of the composite end point, of its individual components, and of mortality). In the TAVI + LAAO group, survival curves according to the use of intraprocedural TEE are shown, while in the TAVI + SMT group a respective subgroup visualization is shown according to the use of OAC at baseline. p values and 95% CIs are two-sided. All analyses were carried out using the statistical software R (version 4.1.1)27 in combination with dynamic reporting to guarantee reproducibility. The analyses were predefined in the statistical analysis plan (SAP); the SAP document was finalized and signed prior to data access for analysis (Supplementary Appendix).

Results

Patients

From May 2016 through May 2018, 40 patients were randomly assigned to the TAVI + LAAO group and 41 patients to the TAVI + SMT group, respectively (Figure 1). All patients had pre-existing AF. Follow-up at 1 year was available for all patients. LAAO was not attempted because of TAVI-related complications prior to LAAO in 3 patients, and the LAA occluder could not be deployed due to technical challenges in 1 patient. Therefore, the per-protocol population consisted of 36 patients in the TAVI-LAAO group. Baseline characteristics of the ITT population are shown in Table 1. Mean age was 82.8 ± 5.3 years, and patients in the TAVI + LAAO tended to more often be female. Prior stroke/transient ischemic attack was reported in 8.6% of patients, and 24.7% had diabetes. Median CHA2DS2-VASc score was 4 [3, 5], median HAS-BLED score 3 [2, 3], and mean STS score 9.0 % ± 5.4%. Procedure

Procedural characteristics are shown in Table 2. Most patients underwent procedures in conscious sedation (TAVI + LAAO: n = 36 (90%); TAVI + SMT: n = 38 [92.7%]). One patient in the TAVI + LAAO group had staged LAAO after TAVI before discharge. The amount of contrast used and fluoroscopy time were higher in the TAVI + LAAO group. In TAVI + LAAO, TEE guidance was used in 8 out of 40 patients (20.0%). Most patients in both groups received a Portico (Abbott, USA) transcatheter heart valve (TAVI + LAAO: 33 patients (82.5%), TAVI + SMT: 18 patients [43.9%]). Medtronic CoreValve Evolut R was implanted in 1 (2.5%) and 12 (29.3%) patients, Edwards SAPIEN 3 in 3 (7.5%) and 8 (19.5%) patients, Symetis Accurate in 0 (0.0%) and 2 (4.9%) patients, and BSC Lotus Valve in 0 (0.0%) and 1 (2.4%) patient in the TAVI + LAAO group and TAVI + SMT group, respectively (Supplementary Table S2). Balloon aortic valvuloplasty and postdilatation were more often performed in the TAVI + LAAO as compared to the TAVI + SMT group (80 vs 61%, and 42.5 vs 34.1%, respectively, Supplementary Table S2). Thirty-six of 40 patients (90%) in the TAVI + LAAO group received Amulet. Secondary Endpoints

Rates of bleeding were comparable between the TAVI + SMT and TAVI + LAAO groups (Table 4 and Figure 3, Supplementary Table S3). In the TAVI + SMT group, bleeding events occurred predominantly due to gastrointestinal bleeding (Table 4 and Figure 3, Supplementary Table S3), whereas bleeding events in the TAVI + LAAO were all procedure-related in the absence of bleeding caused by the LAAO procedure (Supplementary Table S3 and Supplementary Figure S1). The number of cerebrovascular events did not statistically differ between groups but was numerically higher in the TAVI + LAAO group with 2 strokes before attempting LAAO, one stroke related to the LAAO procedure, and one early stroke occurring between 30 days and 1 year (Table 4 and Supplementary Figure S1). At 30 days after randomization, 3 patients (7.3%) died in the TAVI + SMT group, and 3 patients (7.5%) in the TAVI + LAAO group (Table 4). All-cause mortality at 1 year was similar between groups (Table 4 and Supplementary Figure S2). Per-Protocol Analysis

In the per-protocol analysis, the primary end point occurred in 9 patients (25%) in the TAVI + LAAO group and in 15 patients (37%) in the TAVI + SMT group (OR, 0.58; 95% CI, 0.21 to 1.53; p = 0.28; adjusted OR, 0.55; 95% CI, 0.18 to 1.56; p = 0.27; Supplementary Table S5). Cerebrovascular events occurred in 2 patients (5.6%) in the TAVI + LAAO group and in 1 patient (2.4%) in the TAVI + SMT group. Bleeding events were comparable between groups and tended to be lower in patients in the TAVI + LAAO group (3 patients [8.3%]) as compared to those in the TAVI + SMT group (7 patients [17%]) (Supplementary Table S5).

Discussion

We report here, for the first time, the results of an investigator-initiated, randomized, multicenter, open-label pilot trial investigating LAAO using the Amulet device as compared with SMT in high-risk patients with AF undergoing TAVI. The major results are: 1) The incidence of the primary endpoint consisting of cerebrovascular events, peripheral embolism, life-threatening/disabling or major bleeding, and cardiovascular mortality was not statistically different between groups. 2) The rates of bleeding and cerebrovascular events were comparable with a tendency toward lower bleeding and higher cerebrovascular events in patients undergoing concomitant TAVI and LAAO in the ITT population. 3) The exploratory per-protocol analysis indicated numerically fewer bleeding events in the TAVI + LAAO group. As the recruited patients comprised a high-risk population with an average STS score of >8%, the study results only apply for such a high-risk population, with higher procedural complication and mortality rates.

In patients undergoing TAVI, those with concomitant AF as compared with those in sinus rhythm are at higher risk of all-cause mortality and cerebrovascular events.28 Despite advances in valve design and optimization of the procedure, cerebrovascular events remain an important complication in patients undergoing TAVI.29, 30, 31, 32 In addition, contemporary studies show that bleeding complications under OAC are persistently high in this patient population.33,34 These observations pose uncertainties regarding standard antithrombotic therapy for patients with AF undergoing TAVI. LAAO in this patient population may be an attractive therapeutic option, as thrombus formation in patients with concomitant aortic stenosis is frequently observed.35 In addition, thrombus formation was exclusively detected in the LAA in patients with aortic stenosis when assessed with TEE.36

In this study, rates of the primary endpoint were overall similar between patients treated with LAAO or SMT, with comparable rates of cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. These results suggest that LAAO in this high-risk patient population is feasible and thus may represent an alternative to SMT. Importantly, the mortality rate observed in this high-risk older TAVI population is in line with previous studies.1,2,28,37 The occurrence of clinically relevant bleeding was numerically lower in the TAVI + LAAO group as compared to the TAVI + SMT group. In TAVI + LAAO, bleeding complications exclusively occurred as procedural complications unrelated to the LAAO implantation, but without any postprocedural bleeding events up to 1 year. In contrast, in the TAVI + SMT group, 5 patients had major gastrointestinal bleeding. Indeed, in patients undergoing TAVI, most postprocedural bleeding complications are of gastrointestinal origin, with a higher frequency in patients with AF.7,38 In the Edoxaban versus Standard of Care and Their Effects on Clinical Outcomes in Patients Having Undergone Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation–Atrial Fibrillation (ENVISAGE-TAVI AF) trial, the DOAC edoxaban showed a higher bleeding risk as compared to a VKA, driven by gastrointestinal bleedings.33 Moreover, a high proportion of patients in the TAVI + SMT group had received an additional antiplatelet therapy to OAC that is associated with a higher bleeding risk in patients with AF undergoing TAVI, as shown in the Antiplatelet Therapy for Patients Undergoing Transcatheter Aortic-Valve Implantation (POPULAR TAVI cohort B) trial.39 Therefore, de-escalation of antithrombotic therapy after LAAO may be particularly beneficial for patients with AF undergoing TAVI. As observed in high-risk patients without severe aortic stenosis, LAAO as compared to DOAC revealed lower major bleeding rates with comparable ischemic events.22 In addition, patients receiving DAPT after LAAO may benefit in terms of bleeding complications as compared to a strategy with DOAC and aspirin, as recently analyzed in the National Cardiovascular Data Registry LAAO registry.40 Indeed, more than 90% of patients in the TAVI + LAAO group were on antiplatelet therapy at discharge. This prompt de-escalating strategy may therefore have mitigated the risk of major gastrointestinal bleeding in patients undergoing TAVI.

However, in our study, the potential reduction in bleeding risk in the TAVI + LAAO group was paralleled by a numerically higher number of cerebrovascular events in the ITT population. The risk of early strokes was similar in both arms, but in the TAVI + LAAO group, a higher rate of periprocedural strokes was observed. Of note, 2 out of 4 strokes occurred before LAAO, and LAAO was therefore not performed in these patients, resulting in comparable cerebrovascular event rates in the per-protocol population. The higher number of periprocedural strokes in TAVI + LAAO may be related to a higher rate of predilatation and use of the Portico transcatheter heart valve, both associated with a potential higher stroke rate and mortality.41, 42, 43, 44 Moreover, numerically more women were enrolled in the TAVI + LAAO group that may explain the higher number of periprocedural bleeding complications and stroke rates, as observed in other studies.45 In this regard, the per-protocol analysis showed similar efficacy and safety, with a signal for lower bleeding rates in patients undergoing concomitant LAAO. Notably, rates of bleeding and cerebrovascular events in this pilot study are exploratory and have to be confirmed in a fully powered effectiveness study. Furthermore, as procedural complications and mortality were persistently high in this older high-risk population, the risk of combined procedures must be balanced with the potential clinical benefits of an additional intervention.

As in our study, the randomized WATCH-TAVR trial, investigating the Watchman 2.5 device in patients with AF undergoing TAVI, found no difference between LAAO and medical therapy with respect to the primary endpoint of all-cause mortality, stroke, and major bleeding.23 Consistently, previous trials in non-TAVI populations observed a similar efficacy and safety between these closure devices.46, 47, 48 In WATCH-TAVR, bleeding events occurred more often in the TAVI + LAAO group, potentially due to initial therapy with warfarin and aspirin followed by a DAPT regimen up to 6 months. As we did not observe any postprocedural major bleeding complications in the TAVI + LAAO group, switching to an antiplatelet regimen after LAAO may prevent bleeding events in patients with a combined TAVI + LAAO procedure. Furthermore, in contrast to the WATCH-TAVR trial, most patients underwent a combined procedure under conscious sedation with fluoroscopy-guided LAAO. We recently reported comparable safety and efficacy between fluoroscopy- and echocardiography-guided LAAO (49). This minimalistic and simplified approach facilitates combined procedures with shorter procedural time, reduced need for equipment and personal resources, and improved patient convenience. Whether fluoroscopy-only and echocardiography-guided LAAO is equally safe and effective is still debated.49, 50, 51, 52 In the absence of randomized trials, current expert consensus53 support the use of additional imaging guidance for LAAO as a single procedure, with fluoroscopy-only guidance left to experienced operators familiar with this technique.14

The optimal therapeutic strategy in older, high-risk patients with AF undergoing TAVI will remain a patient-centered decision. One-third of patients in WATCH-TAVR and approximately 10% in TAVI-LAAO were not on OAC in the medical treatment arm and therefore without adequate stroke prevention. In addition, in patients which underwent LAAO (without TAVI), half-dose apixaban has shown a favorable efficacy and safety profile,54 and surgical occlusion of LAA in combined procedures on top of OAC reduced ischemic events.55 These potential complementary effects and timing of OAC and LAAO have to be explored in the future, specifically in patients undergoing TAVI, given their high bleeding and stroke risk.

There are several limitations of this study that must be acknowledged. First, this study has limitations inherent to a pilot trial, that is, lacking adequate statistical power and being exploratory in nature. Definitive conclusions regarding the role of LAAO with the Amulet device in high-risk patients with AF undergoing TAVI can therefore not be drawn with certainty, and findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating for a future large, randomized trial. Second, the Portico and Lotus transcatheter heart valves used in this study for the TAVI procedure are no longer available. Furthermore, the Portico valve was more often used in this study than reported in observational studies, which may have influenced the results. Improved designs of newer generation transcatheter heart valves have been approved with technical and clinical improvements,56 and we cannot exclude that hypothesis that prosthesis types also may impact on event rates in combined TAVI + LAAO procedures, particularly in the acute setting.

In conclusion, among patients with AF undergoing TAVI, implantation of an Amulet LAAO device with de-escalation to an antiplatelet strategy yielded comparable overall rates of the primary end point, a composite of cerebrovascular events, peripheral embolism, life-threatening/disabling and major bleeding, and cardiovascular mortality at 1 year as compared to SMT. While the reduction in bleeding events in the TAVI + LAAO group is encouraging in this pilot study, the numerical increase in cerebrovascular complications needs further consideration. Larger randomized trials using the Amulet device with concomitant TAVI are needed to validate these findings (ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT03088098).

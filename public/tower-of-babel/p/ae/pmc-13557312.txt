Tumor Size and Early Response to Sonidegib in Locally Advanced Basal Cell Carcinoma: A Real-Life Case Series
Authors: Vincenzo De Giorgi, Gabriella Perillo, Giovanni Cecchi, Federica Fazzari, Biancamaria Zuccaro
Published: 2026

Introduction

Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) is the most common skin cancer [1]. Surgery remains the treatment of choice for early disease, whereas Hedgehog pathway inhibitors (HPIs), including sonidegib, are established options for locally advanced BCC (LA-BCC) [2,3]. However, factors influencing the timing of clinical response remain poorly defined. We investigated whether tumor size and other clinicopathological features were associated with early response to sonidegib.

Case Series

This retrospective study included patients with LA-BCC treated at the University of Florence Dermatology Unit. Eligibility required continuous standard dosing (one capsule daily), no concurrent immunotherapy, and complete clinical and photographic documentation. Clinical examination and standardized photography were performed at least every 10 days; multidisciplinary support from a nutritionist and psychologist helped patients manage treatment-related adverse effects. Tumors were classified by size, location, pigmentation, ulceration, and histological subtype. Response was defined according to established advanced-BCC criteria as a ≥30% reduction in lesion size or re-epithelialization of ulceration [2,3]. Three dermatologists independently reviewed standardized photographs, supplemented by instrumental documentation when feasible. Analyses were performed using SPSS version 25; Mann-Whitney U and chi-square or Fisher exact tests were applied.

Fifteen patients were included; their demographic and clinicopathological characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Mean time to first clinical response was 15.7 weeks. Tumors ≥3 cm responded significantly earlier than tumors <3 cm (13.4 vs 21.8 weeks; P=.026). Pigmented BCCs showed a longer mean response time than non-pigmented lesions (19.0 vs 14.5 weeks), while patients aged ≥75 years responded faster than younger patients (14.2 vs 17.8 weeks). No significant differences were observed according to histological subtype, ulceration, sex, or tumor location (Figure 1).

Conclusions

The main finding was the unexpected inverse association between tumor size and time to response: larger BCCs responded earlier to sonidegib. BCCs measuring more than 3 cm in diameter responded to sonidegib significantly earlier (13.4 weeks on average) than those measuring less than 3 cm (21.8 weeks). Although counterintuitive and based on a limited sample, this observation may reflect greater proliferative activity, tumor metabolism, or Hedgehog-pathway dependence in larger lesions. Conversely, smaller tumors may have less active pathway signaling or slower biological kinetics. This finding is not described in the current literature and deserves attention, although it is based on a small sample. These hypotheses require molecular confirmation.

Pigmented lesions also appeared to respond more slowly, potentially reflecting a distinct biological phenotype, whereas the modestly faster response in older patients might relate to pharmacokinetic or host-related factors. These secondary findings should be considered exploratory. Histological subtype was not associated with early response, suggesting that, within this cohort, clinical or molecular characteristics may have outweighed conventional histopathological classification. This contrasts with prior studies suggesting that aggressive subtypes such as morpheaform or infiltrative BCCs are associated with reduced response rates.

Limitations include the retrospective design, small sample, heterogeneous tumor sites and subtypes, absence of molecular profiling, and lack of comparison with other HPIs. Accordingly, these data cannot establish tumor size as an independent predictor. Nevertheless, the finding that larger LA-BCCs may show an earlier visible response is novel, clinically relevant, and suitable for prospective validation in larger cohorts.

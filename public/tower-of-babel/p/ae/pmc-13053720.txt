Bibliometric and visualized analysis of metal‒organic frameworks in bone tissue engineering and bone diseases
Authors: Kai Xia, Liyuan Yu, Jing Qin, Mengjiao Zhu, Mingyue Fan, Wenhao Qian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGiven their tunable structures, high porosity, and multifunctional therapeutic potential, metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) have emerged as versatile biomaterials for bone tissue engineering and bone-related diseases. Despite rapid research growth, a systematic and bone-specific bibliometric understanding of its knowledge structure, thematic evolution, and emerging research frontiers remains limited.MethodsPublications on MOFs related to bone tissue engineering and bone diseases (2015–2025) were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. Bibliometric and visualized analyses using Bibliometrix, VOSviewer, and CiteSpace were performed to evaluate publication trends, global contributions, collaboration networks, journals, references, and keyword dynamics. Keyword frequency, co-occurrence clustering, trend topic analysis, thematic mapping, and citation burst detection were integrated to elucidate structural and temporal evolution.ResultsA total of 350 English-language publications were analyzed, showing a marked increase in annual output after 2022 but a decline in average citation rate. China was the most productive country, led by Sichuan University and Shanghai Jiao Tong University, while Australia and Canada achieved higher average citation impacts. Keyword analyses identified bone regeneration and osteogenesis as enduring cores, whereas temporal trends revealed a shift from early material synthesis and drug delivery to function-oriented, clinically motivated research. Emerging topics such as oxidative stress, photothermal therapy, corrosion resistance, and magnesium alloys highlight ongoing therapeutic integration and implant optimization.ConclusionThis study delineates the evolution of MOF-based bone research from material-centered exploration to clinically driven innovation, offering data-driven insights to guide future research and translational applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11671-026-04535-4.Keywords: Metal‒organic framework, Bone tissue engineering, Bone regeneration, Bone diseases, Bibliometric analysis

Introduction

Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) are crystalline porous materials constructed from metal ions or clusters coordinated with organic ligands. Their exceptionally high surface area, adjustable pore architecture, structural diversity, and tunable chemical functionality have made these materials attractive platforms for a wide range of biomedical applications, including drug delivery, biosensing, imaging, and antibacterial therapy [1, 2]. In recent years, these properties have also stimulated a growing interest in the application of MOFs for bone tissue engineering and the treatment of bone-related diseases, for which precise control of therapeutic delivery, biological signaling, and local microenvironments is of key importance [3].

Bone tissue engineering seeks to restore bone defects and treat bone diseases based on the coordinated use of biomaterials, cells, and bioactive factors [4]. However, the use of conventional bone repair materials is often constrained by inherent limitations, among which are insufficient osteogenic stimulation, a limited antibacterial capacity, or a lack of multifunctionality [5, 6]. These shortcomings are particularly problematic in complex clinical settings, including infected bone defects, degenerative bone diseases, and bone tumors, for which it is necessary to combine regeneration with therapeutic intervention. In this context, MOFs have emerged as promising candidates, in that they can function simultaneously as structural components, drug carriers, and sources of bioactive ions, which is conducive to the development of integrated strategies for bone regeneration and disease treatment [7, 8].

Over the past decade, there has been a rapid expansion in research focusing on MOF-based systems for bone regeneration and the treatment of bone-related diseases [9–11], and with the corresponding acceleration in the output of associated publications, bibliometric analysis has become an important tool for quantitatively evaluating research landscapes, identifying knowledge structures, and detecting emerging trends within rapidly developing fields [12, 13]. However, although previous bibliometric studies that have examined MOFs in broad biomedical or materials science contexts have provided valuable insights into publication output, leading countries, and influential journals [14, 15], these have typically tended to treat bone-related applications as a minor subset of general biomedical research. Consequently, there remains an insufficient bibliometric understanding of MOFs specifically applied in the contexts of bone tissue engineering and bone-related diseases.

This lack of domain-specific bibliometric insight limits the ability to systematically assess research hotspots, thematic evolution, and emerging frontiers in MOF-based bone research. To address this gap, in this study, we conducted comprehensive bibliometric and visualized analyses of publications on MOFs in bone tissue engineering and the treatment of bone-related diseases using data derived from the Web of Science Core Collection database. By integrating keyword co-occurrence clustering, temporal trend analysis, citation burst analysis, and thematic mapping, we sought to elucidate the structural organization and evolutionary trajectory of the field, identify current research hotspots, and highlight emerging directions that may guide future research and translational development.

Methods

Search strategy

Given its standardized citation indexing, comprehensive coverage, and widespread use in reproducible bibliometric studies, we selected the Web of Science Core Collection as a source of data for this bibliometric analysis [16]. A comprehensive literature search was conducted to retrieve publications related to the application of MOFs in bone tissue engineering and the treatment of bone-related diseases. The final search strategy combined MOF-related terms with bone-related keywords, as detailed in Table 1. To avoid potential bias caused by database updates, all searches and data exports were completed on December 17th, 2025.

Data filtering and bibliometric analysis

For the purposes of this study, we included publications indexed between January 1st, 2015 and December 17th, 2025, restricting the document types to “articles” and “reviews.” The starting year was set to 2015, as we identified only two relevant articles published prior to this date, whereas a consistent stream of annual publications emerged thereafter, enabling reliable trend and thematic analyses. Following the initial retrieval, titles and abstracts were independently screened by two reviewers to confirm relevance to MOF-based applications in bone tissue engineering or bone-related diseases. Disagreements were resolved through discussion until a consensus was reached, and when necessary, full texts were consulted to clarify eligibility.

The bibliometric analysis was conducted with the Bibliometrix package (ver. 5.2.1) of R software (ver. 4.5.2) [17] to extract and analyze key bibliographic information. In addition, VOSviewer (ver. 1.6.18) [18] was applied to construct collaboration networks, whereas CiteSpace (ver. 6.3.1) [19] was employed for temporal analyses, including the citation bursts detection and keyword clustering with timeline visualization. Bar charts were generated using Excel for Mac (ver. 16.78.3).

Results

Publication output and citation trends

On the basis of the initial literature search, we identified 403 records from the Web of Science Core Collection, and after restricting document type and publication year, 387 publications remained. Having subsequently screened titles and abstracts, 350 publications, comprising 293 original research articles and 57 review articles, were considered to meet the inclusion criteria and were retained for further bibliometric analysis (Fig. 1). A list of the 350 publications is provided in Table S1.

A PRISMA-style flow diagram illustrating the process of study identification, screening, and inclusion

From 2015 to 2025, the annual rate of increase in publication output, calculated using an exponential growth model implemented in the Bibliometrix package, was 58.01%, indicating a corresponding rapid and sustained increase in research output (Fig. 2). During the latter stages of this period, we identified a pronounced acceleration in the number of studies published after 2022, which is taken to be indicative of a rapidly expanding interest in MOF-based strategies for bone tissue engineering and the treatment of bone-related diseases. In contrast, however, the average number of citations per article declined during the same period, reflecting a typical time-lag effect associated with rapid field expansion and the influx of recently published studies that have yet to accumulate a sizeable number of citations.

Keyword analys

To identify dominant research themes and their temporal evolution, we conducted keyword-based analyses. The most frequently used author keywords were primarily associated with regenerative outcomes, including bone regeneration and osteogenesis, indicating that functional and clinically relevant endpoints represent the core research focus. Comparatively, disease-specific terms, such as osteoarthritis and osteosarcoma, appeared less frequently, indicating more specialized application niches. Functional keywords, including antibacterial, drug delivery, and angiogenesis, further reflect the growing emphasis on multifunctional MOF-based systems (Fig. 4).

The frequency of author keyword usage in assessed metal–organic framework publications

Keyword co-occurrence clustering with timeline visualization revealed a well-structured thematic network, supported by a high modularity value (Q = 0.7143) and weighted mean silhouette score (S = 0.9128) (Fig. 5). The largest cluster (#0, metal–organic frameworks) spans the entire study period, confirming its role as the methodological core of the field. Closely associated clusters related to bone regeneration and scaffold integration dominate the early stages of research, whereas function-oriented clusters, such as ZIF-8, antibacterial activity, and sonodynamic therapy emerge around 2020 and increase thereafter. This temporal distribution indicates a gradual shift from material integration toward multifunctional and therapy-driven research strategies.

Author keyword clustering analysis. The horizontal axis represents time, with node size reflecting keyword frequency. The connecting lines represent co-occurrence relationships

Topic trend analysis based on abstract keywords (Fig. 6) revealed increasing research activity in recent years among topics spanning material systems (metal organic frameworks, zeolitic imidazolate frameworks, drug delivery systems), cellular components (mesenchymal stem cells, stem cells BMSCs, umbilical vein endothelial), biological processes and indicators (alkaline phosphatase, extracellular matrix mineralization), and microenvironment-related mechanisms (reactive oxygen species, subchondral bone remodeling). Collectively, these trends reflect a diversification of recent research interests toward more biologically integrated and mechanistically aware approaches.

Citation burst analysis identified keywords indicating rapid increases in research attention over time (Fig. 7). Thematic mapping

To further examine the strategic positioning and evolutionary status of research themes, we constructed a thematic map based on centrality and density (Fig. 8). Themes were classified into the following four categories: basic, motor, niche, and emerging or declining themes.

A keyword-based thematic map. The size of each cluster reflects the frequency of keyword occurrence, whereas the labels represent the most representative terms within each thematic cluster

Basic themes, including “metal–organic framework,” “bone regeneration,” and “osteogenesis,” are mapped in the lower-right quadrant, indicating high relevance but relatively lower internal density. These themes constitute the foundational knowledge base of the field and maintain strong connections with multiple research directions.

Motor themes, represented by “nanoparticles” and “drug delivery,” appear in the upper-right quadrant, combining high centrality with high developmental maturity. This positioning provides an indication that drug delivery strategies are playing a pivotal role in shaping current research trends and serve as key engines linking material design with therapeutic applications.

Niche themes, such as the cluster involving “osteoarthritis,” and “bone,” are distributed in the upper-left quadrant. Although showing strong internal development, these themes have limited external connectivity, reflecting specialized and application-specific research areas with focused clinical relevance.

Themes located in the lower-left quadrant, including “osteoporosis,” “controlled release,” and “biocompatibility,” may represent emerging directions or declining topics. Their positioning is considered indicative of either early-stage development or thematic integration into more dominant research frameworks, rather than independent growth trajectories.

Discussion

Overall research landscape and global contribution

The bibliometric results reveal that research on the application of MOFs in bone tissue engineering and the treatment of bone-related diseases has undergone a rapid expansion over the past decade, with a marked increase in annual publication output, particularly around 2022, reflecting a growing interest in exploiting MOFs for bone regeneration, infection control, and the treatment of bone disease. During this period, there has been a concomitant decline in the average annual number of citations, which is assumed to be attributable to a time-lag effect, reflecting the fact that a large proportion of recent publications have yet to accumulate a substantial number of citations, rather than indicating a reduction in the quality or relevance of research.

In terms of publication volume and total citations, China was identified as the predominant center of research activity in this field, with institutions such as Sichuan University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and the Chinese Academy of Sciences identified as key research hubs. This pattern is consistent with global bibliometric findings indicating that China has become the leading contributor in materials science and biomedical engineering research, largely driven by national funding programs and large-scale institutional collaboration frameworks [34]. Similarly, a recent bibliometric study focusing on MOFs in biomedical applications has confirmed that China contributes the majority of publications and citations in this field, particularly in MOF-based drug delivery and bone regeneration [14].

However, despite this dominance in output, the comparatively lower average citation rate provides evidence that the overall research impact and international visibility remain less well consolidated than those of the Canada and Australia. Broader scientometric analyses of international research systems indicate that although China’s citation impact and global collaboration intensity are increasing, they still lag behind those of western research networks [35]. Limited cross-national collaboration and language barriers may partially explain this imbalance, emphasizing the need for enhanced international cooperation to strengthen the originality and global influence of MOF-based bone research.

Hotspots and frontiers

The evolution of research hotspots in MOF-based applications for bone tissue engineering and bone-related diseases reflects a clear transition from material validation to function-oriented and clinically motivated research. Recent bibliometric analysis has confirmed this developmental trajectory, indicating that whereas early studies tended to focus primarily on structural validation and synthesis optimization, more recent efforts reflect a growing emphasis on biological integration and translational feasibility [36]. By integrating keyword frequency, co-occurrence clustering, such analyses reveal how MOF-based bone research has evolved from “materials-oriented” exploration to “functionally and clinically informed” design frameworks.

Our analysis of the frequency of keyword usage identified bone regeneration, osteogenesis, osteoarthritis, antibacterial, and drug delivery as the most frequently occurring terms, indicating that regenerative repair and therapeutic intervention represent long-standing core concerns. Similar findings in bone biomaterials research confirm that osteogenic mechanisms and infection control remain dominant themes [37]. These high-frequency keywords reflect stable research foundations rather than transient trends, consistent with extensive experimental evidence indicating the osteogenic and reparative potential of MOF-based scaffolds and composites [22, 23, 28].

The clustering of frequently used keywords further reveals how these core themes are organized and sustained over time. Large and persistent clusters, such as metal–organic frameworks and bone regeneration span nearly the entire study period, confirming their roles as methodological and biological anchors of the research network. Related clusters, associated with ZIF-8 and polymer-based composites, highlight a continued emphasis on material selection and scaffold design, reinforcing the importance of structural optimization in supporting bone regeneration [31, 38].

Beyond these stable cores, temporal shifts in clustering patterns point to emerging functional frontiers. Clusters associated with antibacterial activity, sonodynamic therapy, and photodynamic therapy were found to be concentrated in more recent years, indicating a growing interest in multifunctional and stimulus-responsive strategies. This shift is seen as reflecting an increasing recognition of complex pathological microenvironments in bone diseases, for which infection control and tumor suppression represent key challenges. For example, Yu et al. have described a single-atom catalytic MOF system that enables sonodynamic eradication of Staphylococcus aureus infections in osteomyelitis models [21], whereas Jiang et al. have developed a tumor microenvironment–responsive 3D-printed nanoMOF scaffold for simultaneous antitumor and bone-regenerative therapy [26]. More recently, reviews on nano-hybrid MOFs have highlighted the growing interest in functional and integrated MOF platforms for biomedical applications, including drug delivery and antimicrobial therapy [39]. These findings are consistent with a broader surge in bibliometric clusters centered on oxidative stress, photothermal therapy, and tumor microenvironments [40, 41].

Our analysis of trending topics indicates a recent shift in MOF-based bone research toward more biologically informed and multi-scale investigation. The simultaneous emergence of topics related to biological processes and indicators (alkaline phosphatase, extracellular matrix mineralization), and cellular components (mesenchymal stem cells, stem cells BMSCs, umbilical vein endothelial) indicates an increasing emphasis on linking cellular behavior with functional bone outcomes [22, 23]. In parallel, the appearance of reactive oxygen species reflects the growing attention being paid to microenvironmental regulation and oxidative mechanisms relevant to bone-related conditions, indicating a progression beyond purely structural material design [21, 30]. From a materials perspective, the prominence of MOFs and zeolitic imidazolate frameworks, together with drug delivery systems and bone tissue engineering, highlights the role of MOF-based platforms as multifunctional carriers rather than merely serving as passive scaffolds [25]. Collectively, these trends point to a gradual transition toward coordinated strategies that integrate material systems, cellular regulation, and tissue-level processes, consistent with recent research trajectory characterized by more application-oriented and clinically relevant approaches.

By identifying topics that have rapidly gained scholarly attention, citation burst analysis provides temporal resolution to emerging trends. Early bursts of MOF and drug delivery research reflect the initial establishment of these frameworks as viable biomedical materials [42, 43]. The brief, albeit intense, burst of bone regeneration interest around 2020 marks a turning point at which bone-specific applications became a focal research direction. In contrast, ongoing bursts extending to 2025, including controlled release, oxidative stress, photothermal therapy, corrosion resistance, and magnesium alloys, reveal the landscape of current frontiers focusing on functional optimization, implant integration, and therapeutic synergy. Notably, the recent emergence of bone defect as a burst keyword emphasizes a recent shift toward clinically defined repair scenarios, indicating a maturation of the field toward translational relevance [26, 27].

However, despite the rapid growth of MOF-based research in bone tissue engineering and the treatment of bone-related diseases, there remain a number of key issues regarding viable clinical translation. From a regulatory perspective, the compositional complexity and structural diversity of MOFs represent challenges with respect to safety evaluation and long-term biocompatibility assessment, which may complicate approval processes [44]. In terms of large-scale manufacturing, although synthetic strategies for numerous MOFs have been developed and optimized at the laboratory scale, translating these methods into industrially scalable processes without compromising material quality remains challenging [45]. A further key concern is batch-to-batch reproducibility, given that small variations in synthetic conditions can lead to changes in physicochemical properties that may influence biological performance [46]. In addition, cost-effectiveness represents a practical limitation, as the use of high-purity precursors, multi-step synthesis routes, and post-synthetic modifications may contribute to increases in production costs and limit widespread clinical adoption [47]. Addressing these regulatory, manufacturing, reproducibility, and economic challenges will be essential for any further translation of MOF-based systems from experimental platforms to clinically viable bone therapies [48].

Collectively, our analyses in this study indicate that MOF-based bone research has evolved from material-centered exploration toward functionally integrated and problem-driven applications. Although bone regeneration continues to remain a stable core of the research landscape, emerging frontiers are increasingly focusing on therapeutic modulation, microenvironmental regulation, and implant-associated challenges. Future research is therefore likely to advance via a closer integration of advanced functional strategies within established regenerative frameworks, contributing to meaningful progress in terms of both scientific innovation and clinical translation.

Conclusions and perspectives

The bibliometric and visualized analyses conducted in this study enabled us to provide a comprehensive overview of research on MOFs in bone tissue engineering and the treatment of bone-related diseases over the past decade.

The results revealed that bone regeneration and osteogenesis constitute stable and long-standing research cores, providing a foundational framework upon which other functional strategies can be developed. We established that whereas early research focused primarily on material feasibility and drug delivery capability, thereby establishing MOFs as versatile biomedical platforms, there has subsequently been increasing attention directed toward functional optimization and biological integration, as reflected by the growing prominence of themes associated with angiogenesis, antibacterial activity, and controlled release.

More importantly, citation burst analysis revealed a clear shift in recent years toward mechanism-oriented and clinically motivated research frontiers. Ongoing bursts associated with oxidative stress regulation, photothermal therapy, corrosion resistance, and magnesium alloy integration highlight the expanding role of MOFs in therapeutic modulation and implant-associated applications. The recent emergence of bone defect-related bursts further indicates that current research is increasingly framed around clinically defined repair scenarios, thereby indicating a maturation of the field toward translational relevance.

Overall, our findings enabled us to delineate a clear evolutionary trajectory from material-centered exploration to functionally integrated and problem-driven innovation in MOF-based bone research. The bibliometric insights gained in this study have contributed not only to identifying current hotspots and emerging frontiers but can also serve as a data-driven framework to guide the direction of future research and interdisciplinary integration in bone tissue engineering and bone disease treatment.

Limitations

Despite the valuable insight gained, this study does have certain limitations. Firstly, our analyses were based exclusively on reference to publications retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection database, and thus may have under-represented research published in newly established, regional, or open-access journals indexed in other databases. Secondly, keyword-based bibliometric analysis is sensitive to changes in terminology, and evolving concepts may influence the frequency of keyword usage and clustering results. Thirdly, citation indicators are typically influenced by a time-lag effect, and accordingly, there would have been insufficient time for more recent publications to have accumulated an appreciable number of citations. Finally, although we used multiple bibliometric tools, differences in algorithms and visualization methods may have had an influence on network structures. Nevertheless, given the overall consistency of our results, we believe these findings to be reliable.

Effect of different hypoglycemic drugs and insulin on the risk of new-onset atrial fibrillation in people with diabetes: a network meta-analysis
Authors: Qianyu Lv, Yingtian Yang, Yanfei Lv, Qian Wu, Xinzheng Hou, Lanlan Li, Xuejiao Ye, Chenyan Yang, Shihan Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveDiabetes is considered a significant risk factor for the development of atrial fibrillation/flutter (AF/AFL). However, there is still insufficient evidence to determine the varying effects of different hypoglycemic drugs (HDs) on the incidence of new-onset AF/AFL in diabetic patients. To address this gap, we conducted a network meta-analysis to investigate whether various HDs have different effects on the risk of new-onset AF/AFL compared with insulin.MethodWe conducted a comprehensive search in PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science to identify all clinical trials investigating the association between various HDs or insulin and incident AF/AFL up until April 1, 2024. Bayesian random-effects model was used for network meta-analysis, and the results were expressed as relative risk (RR) and 95% confidence interval (CI).ResultAfter searching 2070 articles, a total of 12 studies (2,349,683 patients) were included in the network meta-analysis. The treatment regimen comprised insulin and 8 HDs hypoglycemic drugs, which are sodium-dependent glucose transporters 2 inhibitor (SGLT2i), glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonist (GLP-1RA), dipeptidyl peptidase 4 inhibitors (DPP4i), metformin (Met), sulfonylureas (SU), non-sulfonylureas (nSU), thiazolidinedione (TZD) and α-glycosidase inhibitors (AGI). The use of SGLT2i [RR 0.23, 95%CI (0.11, 0.49)], GLP-1RA [RR 0.28, 95%CI (0.13, 0.57)], and DPP4i [RR 0.34, 95%CI (0.17, 0.67)] demonstrated significant efficacy in reducing the incidence of new-onset AF/AFL when compared to insulin. When HDs were compared in pairs, SGLT2i is more effective than Met [RR 0.35, 95% CI (0.19, 0.62)], SU (RR 0.27, 95% CI (0.14, 0.51)], nSU [RR 0.28, 95% CI (0.08, 0.95)], TZD [RR 0.34, 95% CI (0.17, 0.7)], GLP-1RA is more effective Met [RR 0.42, 95% CI (0.25, 0.71)], SU (RR 0.33, 95% CI (0.18, 0.6)], TZD [RR 0.41, 95% CI (0.21, 0.82)], while Met[RR 1.98, 95% CI (1.23, 3.23)], SU [RR 2.54, 95% CI (1.46, 4.43)], TZD [RR 2.01, 95% CI (1.05, 3.79)] was not as effective as DPP4i.ConclusionSGLT-2i, GLP-1RA, and DPP4i showed a superior efficacy in reducing the risk of new-onset AF/AFL compared to insulin therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-024-01954-w.Keywords: Atrial fibrillation, Hypoglycemic drugs, Diabetes mellitus, Arrhythmology, Network meta-analysis

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is one of the most common tachyarrhythmias in clinical practice [1], characterized by disturbances in atrial electrical activity and ineffective atrial contraction. The primary clinical manifestations include palpitations and fatigue, while severe cases may present with amaurosis fugax or syncope. Recurrent episodes of AF not only diminish the quality of life, but also increase the risk of stroke, dementia, heart failure, and even mortality [2–4].

Studies have shown that diabetes mellitus (DM) is an important trigger for new-onset AF [5, 6]. Hyperglycemia, obesity and diabetes-related neuropathy caused by insulin resistance can increase the risk of arrhythmia [7–10]. The coexistence of DM and AF significantly increases the possibility of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events such as heart failure and stroke [11, 12]. It is therefore important to take measures to reduce the risk of AF before it develops, which may help to stop the epidemic of AF, improve clinical management, and reduce the risk of cardiovascular events. In recent years, many new types of hypoglycemic drugs (HDs), such as sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitor (SGLT2i), glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1RA), dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitor (DPP4i) have received extensive attention due to their ability to control a variety of cardiovascular risk factors and reduce the incidence of cardiovascular events [13–17]. Recent guidelines have also included SGLT-2i and GLP-1 RA as recommended agents for patients with DM and at high risk for cardiovascular events [18]. Several clinical trials have reported the effects of different HDs on new-onset AF [19, 20], however, their comparative efficacy remains undetermined. The aim of this study is to compare the effects of multiple HDs and insulin (Insu) on the risk of new-onset AF/AFL through network meta-analysis (NMA), so as to serve as valuable reference for clinicians to make evidence-based decisions.

Methods

The design of this network meta-analysis was followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 statement [21].

Study selection criteria

We considered clinical trails to be eligible if they met the following criteria: (1) patients diagnosed with type 2 DM of any age; (2) the primary or secondary outcomes included incident AF/atrial flutter (AFL); (3) the study employed a two-arm/multi-arm design to compare the efficacy of HDs with insulin treatment or among different HD treatments.

We also excluded articles as follows: (1) animal experiments, case reports, meta-analysis/reviews, conference abstracts, mechanism research, letters/responses to editors, or guidelines; (2) comparison of drug combinations; (3) patients had received a diagnosis of AF/AFL before the start of the trial or observation period.

Search strategy

Four electronic databases (PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science) were searched comprehensively with no language, publication date, or publication status restrictions. The search was guided by terms such as “atrial fibrillation”, “atrial flutter”, “metformin”, “alpha-glucosidase inhibitors”, “dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors”, “glucagon-like peptide 1 analogues”, “peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma agonists”, “thiazolidinediones”, “sodium-glucose”, “cotransporter-2 inhibitors”, “sulfonylurea” and “no-sulfonylureas”. The exhaustive retrieval scheme is presented in Supplementary material 1.

Study selection

Two methodologically trained researchers independently screened the titles and abstracts of all the retrieved articles. First of all, the possibly relevant research was imported into EndNote X9, and duplicate articles were removed. Then, titles and abstracts were reviewed to exclude unqualified literature, followed by full-text assessment. If there were any disagreements, a third researcher will join the discussion, and data extraction will begin after reaching a consensus.

Data extraction

Information such as the first author and first author’s country, year of publication, study type, intervention/exposure (type of HD), and outcome data (the number or percentage of new-onset AF/AF in each group) were extract from each included study by two researchers, extracted data were independently reviewed for accuracy by a third researcher.

Quality assessment

The quality of the included studies using the RoB 2 [22] in the following five bias domains: randomization process, allocation concealment, intervention blinding of participants and investigators, missing outcome data, outcome measurement and selection of reported results. An algorithm was employed to estimate the overall risk of bias, i.e., low risk, some concerns, or high risk. The risk of bias assessment was performed by two researchers, if any discrepancies, consensus was achieved through discussion with a third researcher.

Statistical analysis

We fitted a Bayesian random-effects network meta-analysis model using JAGS software 4.3.1 (gemtc package 0.8–2 and rjags package 4–10) (Rstudio, Boston, MA, USA) to simultaneously compare the effects of multiple HDs and Insu. The I2 statistic was used to test for heterogeneity in the studies, and heterogeneity was considered high when I2 was > 50%. Based on the clinical heterogeneity of the included trials (i.e., race, sex, duration of administration, comorbidities, drug dose), a random-effects model with four Markov chains was used for the outcome, with each chain producing 50,000 iterations (burn-in period of 20,000 iterations). Risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated for the occurrence of AF/AFL. A surface under the cumulative ranking (SUCRA) value is used to evaluate and rank regimens, the greater the SUCRA value, the more efficacious the drug was. Publication bias was assessed by funnel plots. Network plots and funnel plots were drawn by Stata SE 15.0 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas, USA).

Sensitivity analysis and meta-regression analysis

Sensitivity analyses were performed to demonstrate the class effect of HDs. To evaluate the robustness of the conclusions, we employed the one-by-one exclusion method to analyze the individual impact of each study on the combined overall effect using Stata SE 15.0 software. We hypothesized that the included studies may have shown differences in mean age, follow-up duration, cardiovascular complications (hypertension, heart failure) in DM patients. To explore the sources of heterogeneity, we conducted meta-regression analysis, and established a regression model with the occurrence of AF/AFL as the dependent variable and the above-mentioned covariate as the independent variable.

Result

Search outcomes

The screening process followed the updated PRISMA guidelines [23]. A total of 2070 records were identified from databases. After removing 280 duplicates, we reviewed the titles and abstracts of the remaining literature based on eligibility criteria. Ultimately, 12 studies [24–35] were included after a panel discussion. Characteristics of included studies

Of the 12 eligible studies published by April 1, 2024, 5 studies were conducted in Asia, 6 studies in Europe, and 1 study in North America. A total of 2,349,683 DM patients were enrolled. The study encompassed insulin and 8 categories of HDs, namely SGLT2i, DPP4i, GLP-1RA, metformin (Met), sulfonylureas (SU), non-sulfonylureas (nSU), thiazolidinedione (TZD) and α-glycosidase inhibitors (AGI). NMA outcomes

Our NMA finally made a direct and indirect comparison between 8 classes of HDs and insulin, and the comparison network plot is shown in Fig. 2. SUCRA analysis revealed that SGLT2i was associated with the lowest incidence of new-onset AF/AFL, with SUCRA = 95.6%, followed by GLP-1RA, DPP4i, AGI, Met, TZD, nSU, SU and Insu (SUCRA: GLP-1RA = 85.7%, DPP4i = 75.2%, AGI = 47.6%, Met = 41.8%, TZD = 39.5%, nSU = 28.9%, SU = 21.4%, Insu = 13.3%). The overall heterogeneity was high (I2 = 97.3% [96.3%; 98.1%]; τ2 = 0.2132). Heterogeneity of comparison between different drugs is shown in Supplementary material 4. Pairwise comparisons show RR values for the effect of different drugs on AF/AFL outcomes. When each type of HD is compared with insulin, SGLT2i [RR 0.23, 95% CI (0.11, 0.49)], GLP-1RA [RR 0.28, 95% CI (0.13, 0.57)], DPP4i [RR 0.34, 95% CI (0.17, 0.67)] showed a better effect. Figure 3 shows the relative differences in reducing the risk of new-onset AF/AFL for each HD compared with Insu. When pairwise comparisons were made between HDs, we found that SGLT2i significantly reduced incident AF/AFL events compared with Met [RR 0.35, 95% CI (0.19, 0.62)], SU [RR 0.27, 95% CI (0.14, 0.51)], nSU [RR 0.28, 95% CI (0.08, 0.95)], TZD [RR 0.34, 95% CI (0.17, 0.7)]; GLP-1RA significantly reduced the risk of new-onset AF compared with Met [RR 0.42, 95% CI (0.25, 0.71)], SU [RR 0.33, 95% CI (0.18, 0.6)], and TZD [RR 0.41, 95% CI (0.21, 0.82)]. However, compared with DPP4i, Met [RR 1.98, 95% CI (1.23, 3.23)], SU [RR 2.54, 95% CI (1.46, 4.43)], TZD [RR 2.01, 95% CI (1.05, 3.79)] was less effective in reducing new-onset AF/AFL. However, no significant differences were found in pairwise comparisons for other HDs. Figure 4 shows summary of the comparison of RR values for the effects of Insu and different classes of HDs on AF/AFL events.

Sensitivity analysis and meta-regression analyses

Two interventions (Insu and DPP4i) were selected as control groups for sensitivity analysis. When studies were excluded one by one, estimates remained in the same direction of effect, indicating that the results of the NMA were robust (as shown in Supplementary material 2). The regression analysis showed that mean age, follow-up period, cardiovascular complications (hypertension, heart failure) were not sources of heterogeneity in this study (Supplementary material 5).

Discussion

To date, how to manage DM to control its complications remains a great challenge for global public health. The association between DM and various cardiovascular complications, including AF, is widely recognized [5, 36]. Animal studies have shown that hyperglycemia is associated with impaired ventricular systolic function as well as aggravated myocardial fibrosis in DM rats [37, 38]. The study conducted by Huxley et al. showed a positive relationship between the increase in hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) and the incidence of AF. Specifically, for each 1% increase in HbA1c, there was a corresponding 13% increase in the incidence of AF [39]. The specific mechanism underlying the increased risk of new-onset AF in DM patients is not fully understood. Currently, several studies suggest that it may be related to atrial structural remodeling [40, 41], atrial electrical remodeling [5, 42–44] and atrial autonomic neural remodeling [36, 45, 46] triggered by insulin resistance and hyperglycemia.

The pharmacologic management and prevention strategies for patients with DM should take into account not only glycemic control, but also the potential impact on the risk of new-onset AF. Our study is the first to comprehensively compare various HDs and Insu using NMA, both directly and indirectly, to assess their preventive effects on new-onset AF/AFL. The results indicate that SGLT2i, GLP-1RA, and DPP4i show superior preventive effects compared to insulin treatment. These results are not completely consistent with some previous studies, providing a new perspective on the potential role of these drugs in the prevention of cardiovascular disease. A network meta-analysis [47] that included five studies showed that GLP-1RA significantly reduced the incidence of AF/AFL compared to Met, SU, Insu, and nSU. However, the findings do not support the hypothesis that SGLT2i or DPP4i have a better effect in reducing the risk of AF/AFL compared to other HDs. Several reviews have also explored the association between different types of HDs and the risk of arrhythmias. Lăcătus et al. [48] found that Insu may increase the risk of AF, Met may decrease the risk of AF, while the impact of DPP4i and GLP-1RA on AF remains unclear, potentially leading to an increase, decrease, or no significant effect. Another review [49] suggests that SGLT2i are associated with a significant reduction in the incidence of new-onset AF/AFL, and compared to DPP-4i, GLP-1RA does not demonstrate a clear advantage in reducing the risk of arrhythmias. Interestingly, despite these studies reaching different conclusions, there is a great deal of interest in these three new types of HDs. Indeed, these new HDs not only showed the potential in controlling blood glucose, and might also reduce the common risk factors associated with diabetes and AF, such as obesity and dyslipidemia [50–55]. The pleiotropic effects of these drugs offer new perspectives and therapeutic strategies for the management of diabetes and its complications.

SGLT2i is a class of oral HDs that lower blood glucose by mediating 90% renal glucose reabsorption as well as promoting the excretion of glucose in urine [56]. Encouragingly, SGLT2i has multiple cardiovascular protective effects in addition to lowering blood glucose [57–60]. Consistent with our study, a meta-analysis of 31 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) reported that SGLT2i led to a 25% (95% CI, 14–34%) relative risk reduction in major AF events [61]. Another meta-analysis by Liao et al. also reported that SGLT2i was associated with a reduced risk of AF, as well as a reduced risk of sudden cardiac death in heart failure patients [62]. Interestingly, the effect of SGLT2i on incident AF risk was not entirely dependent on glycemic control. The SGLT-2i also exhibits an anti-cardiac remodeling effect. Verma et al.’s randomized controlled trial reported that 6 months of empagliflozin treatment resulted in a significant reduction in left ventricular mass compared with placebo in patients with both coronary artery disease and DM [63]. Studies have shown that mitochondrial structural and functional abnormalities contribute to atrial structural and electrical remodeling [64, 65]. SGLT2i can effectively improve atrial mitochondrial respiratory dysfunction and mitochondrial membrane potential, improve mitochondrial biogenesis, thereby exerts a preventive effect on DM-induced atrial electrical remodeling and AF inducibility [66, 67]. On the other hand, Ca2+/Na+ playing a crucial role in atrial electrical signal conduction [68–70], SGLT2i has been shown to promote the balance of disordered Ca2+/Na+ in cardiomyocytes [71, 72].

Glucagon-like peptide 1(GLP-1) acts on the GLP-1 receptor of islet B cells to enhance insulin secretion and inhibit glucagon secretion in a glucose concentration-dependent manner [73]. In addition to islet B cells, GLP-1 receptors are also widely expressed in multiple organs and tissues such as heart and kidney [74]. Therefore, GLP-1RA may have potential cardiac and renal protective effects. GLP-1RA may regulate the mitochondrial function of cardiomyocytes, improving the function of the sarcoplasmic/endoplasmic reticulum Ca2+ATPase 2a (SERCA2a) function to reduce cardiomyocyte apoptosis induced by hyperglycemia [75]. Calcium homeostasis significantly regulates cardiac contractility, and SERCA2a plays an important role in this process [76–78]. During diastole, SERCA2a transported more than 70% of free cytosolic Ca2+ into the sarcoplasmic reticular (SR), thereby maintaining calcium stores to regulate rhythmic cardiac contraction [79]. GLP-1 may enhance the activity of SERCA2a and SR Ca2+content, while reducing the phosphorylation of lysine receptors, thus exhibiting antiarrhythmic effects [80].

Another novel HD, DPP4i, exerts its blood glucose-lowering effects by inhibiting the degradation and prolonging the half-life of GLP-1, and increasing insulin levels in the body [81, 82]. Many studies have reported the safety of DPP4i, but their advantages in improving cardiovascular outcomes have not been significant [83–86]. However, several animal trials have reported a possible role of DPP4i in reducing new-onset AF. Alogliptin, a DPP4i, has been found to reduce the incidence of AF in rabbits by improving mitochondrial biogenesis and atrial structural remodeling, as well as reversing electrophysiological abnormalities through the PGC-1α/NRF-1/ Tfam signaling pathway [87]. Another DPP4i, vildagliptin, was reported to has the potential to stabilize myocardial electrophysiology during ischemia, decrease myocardial susceptibility to arrhythmias, and elevate the ventricular fibrillation threshold [88]. Our NMA suggests that Met, TZD, AGI, SU, and nSU are no better than insulin in reducing the risk of new-onset AF/AFL. Studies have reported some disadvantages of the TZD, SU, and Insu treatment, including hypoglycemic events and weight gain [89, 90]. However, reports of the effects of these drugs on arrhythmic outcomes have yielded inconsistent results. A meta-analysis involving 3 RCTs and 4 observational studies reported the efficacy of pioglitazone, a TZD, in both preventing new-onset and recurrent AF [20]. At the same time, findings from animal experiments have shown that pioglitazone can reduce atrial fibrosis, inhibits the activity of TLR4/NF-Κb, reduces the expression of inflammatory factor tumor necrosis factor-α(TNF-α) protein and the susceptibility to AF [42, 91]. The use of Met remains the first-line treatment for diabetes, and several studies have reported its effect in reducing the risk of stroke and heart failure [92–94]. Unfortunately, our study findings revealed no significant difference between Insu and Met in terms of their efficacy for preventing new-onset AF/AFL. In contrast to our findings, a population-based cohort study reported that compared with no-Met users, Met significantly reduced the incidence of new-onset AF with a hazard ratio of 0.81 (95% CI 0.76–0.86, p < 0.0001) [95]. But this effect appears to diminish in patients older than 65 years or in those with advanced diabetes [96]. This strange phenomenon may be explained by the difference between age and DM course, as atrial remodeling tends to be more severe with longer duration of DM [97]. Another systematic review based on retrospective analysis reported that SU may increase the risk of ventricular arrhythmias [98]. SU and nSU share a common glucose-lowering mechanism by stimulating islet B cells to produce more insulin, while AGI lowers blood glucose by delaying the absorption of carbohydrates [99, 100]. These types of HDs may not have significant effects other than lowering blood sugar, and thus there is no clear difference in reducing the risk of new-onset AF/AFL compared to insulin.

According to our NMA, SGLT-2i, GLP-1RA, and DPP4i might be more effective than insulin in reducing the risk of new-onset AF/AFL, and their multiple mechanisms are of interest. However, the results should be interpreted with caution, because of the heterogeneity of our study. Although meta-regression analysis were performed to fully investigate the sources of heterogeneity in the studies and improve the credibility of the outcomes, the result shows that the outcomes of arrhythmia in the current study may not be influenced by the average age of the participants, the follow-up duration, or the presence of comorbid hypertension/heart failure. In addition to the above factors, HbA1c levels and other cardiovascular comorbidities could also be potential sources of heterogeneity. Regrettably, the absence of relevant data in some studies prevents us from conducting further analysis.

Limitations

Despite our meticulous approach in article selection, data extraction, and result analysis, it is important to acknowledge the limitations of our findings when interpreting the results. First, our NMA results are primarily based on observational studies, although these studies typically have a large sample size, they are generally considered a weaker form of evidence as they were conducted without randomization. Second, although sensitivity analysis showed robust results, there was a high degree of heterogeneity in the results of the studies. Therefore, the interpretation of the results should be cautious. In short, we hope that further large-scale and rigorously designed RCTs can be conducted to provide more compelling evidence for clinical application.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this NMA demonstrated that SGLT-2i, GLP-1RA, and DPP4i were more effective than insulin in reducing the risk of new-onset AF/AFL, and these HDs may serve as novel therapies for reducing cardiovascular complications in patients with DM.

Generation of single-mode and two-mode quantum squeezed states of light by degenerate four-wave mixing in a plasmonic waveguide
Authors: Abbas Ghasempour Ardakani, Hossein Bornak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Squeezed states of light represent one of the most fundamental concepts in quantum optics. They play a crucial role in various fields of science, quantum technology, and photonics, with applications in quantum information processing, quantum computation, quantum sensing, and gravitational wave detection. In this paper, we propose a plasmonic waveguide for the generation of squeezed states via four-wave mixing. The designed waveguide consists of two gold layers deposited on a SiO2 substrate. DDMEBT, an organic material with a high third-order nonlinear refractive index, is placed on the gold layers and fills the gap between them. To determine the effective refractive indices and corresponding mode wavelengths, the waveguide was simulated using the Mode Analysis module of COMSOL Multiphysics. The coupled equations describing degenerate four-wave mixing for the pump, signal, and idler fields were derived. Furthermore, we present the quantum mechanical formulation of four-wave mixing in terms of expectation values and fluctuations, introducing both single-mode and two-mode squeezing factors. The influence of pump power on the squeezing factor is analyzed, revealing that higher pump powers enhance the squeezing level and reduce the required propagation length to reach maximum squeezing. Compared with previous studies based on second-harmonic generation, the required waveguide length to achieve maximum squeezing in our proposed structure is reduced by three order of magnitude. The detrimental effects of optical losses on the squeezing process are also investigated. Finally, the impact of the interlayer gap width on the squeezing factor is examined, demonstrating that increasing the gap between the gold layers leads to a degradation in the squeezing quality.

Introduction

A squeezed state is a type of nonclassical light state in which the uncertainty in measuring one of the quadratures is reduced below the standard quantum limit (SQL). Unlike coherent states, squeezed states hold great promise for various applications, including quantum teleportation1, highly sensitive interferometry2,3, and optical communications with noise levels below the SQL4. Therefore, the generation of squeezed states is crucial for numerous quantum optics applications.

For example, the detectors at the laser interferometer gravitational-wave observatory (LIGO) are designed to measure distance changes on the order of 10–18 m caused by gravitational waves originating from astronomical sources such as neutron star or black hole binaries, supernovae, and the Big Bang. These detectors seek to further validate Einstein’s theory of general relativity and open an entirely new window into the universe. However, the sensitivity of currently operational gravitational-wave detectors is largely limited by quantum optical noise5. A promising strategy to overcome this limitation involves preparing the oscillator not in its ground state—where quantum noise is randomly distributed in phase space—but in a squeezed state6. In squeezed states of light, the noise of the electric field at specific phases is reduced below the vacuum noise level. In other words, when squeezed light is introduced, the noise level is actually lower than when no light is present. This seemingly paradoxical phenomenon arises from the quantum nature of light and cannot be explained classically7.

Squeezed states were first generated using four-wave mixing (FWM) in atomic sodium vapor in 19858. Shortly thereafter, Wu et al. reported the generation of squeezed states based on degenerate parametric down-conversion in a nonlinear crystal placed inside an optical cavity9. Their work demonstrated noise reduction greater than 50% compared to the vacuum state in a balanced homodyne detector. One of the most important methods for generating squeezed states involves nonlinear processes in waveguides, due to the equivalence between the Hamiltonian describing this interaction and the squeezing operator. Toshiaki Suhara et al. proposed an analytical approach to demonstrate the generation of squeezed states via second harmonic generation (SHG) for traveling waves propagating in nonlinear media10. They employed a fluctuation propagation matrix to analyze how the characteristics of squeezed states depend on practical parameters of traveling waves and resonant SHG devices. Furthermore, they extended this method to study squeezed state generation and characteristics in other second-order nonlinear processes such as parametric amplification and sum and difference frequency generation. However, because the nonlinear coefficients in optical crystals are very small, the generation rate of squeezed states in dielectric waveguides and related structures remains low. Therefore, developing methods to enhance the generation rate of squeezed states is of great importance.

Recent years have witnessed significant progress in integrated platforms for the generation of squeezed light, driven primarily by advances in low-loss nanophotonics and on-chip nonlinear optics11–13. Silicon nitride (Si3N4) platforms exploiting Kerr (χ3) nonlinearities have enabled squeezed states and two-mode squeezing in high-Q microresonators and microcomb architectures, benefiting from CMOS compatibility, wafer-scale fabrication, and broadband operation14–18. Parallel developments in thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) have leveraged strong second-order (χ2) nonlinearities to realize chip-scale optical parametric oscillators, broadband squeezed vacuum generation, and integrated quantum sensors, with the added advantages of electro-optic tunability and heterogeneous integration19–23. More broadly, chip-scale parametric oscillators based on resonant enhancement of χ2 or χ3 processes have demonstrated increasingly competitive squeezing levels while emphasizing scalability and system integration24–26. While these dielectric platforms offer low propagation loss and high optical quality factors, they typically rely on relatively large device footprints and comparatively weak intrinsic field confinement27,28. In contrast, plasmonic squeezing platforms exploit extreme subwavelength mode confinement and enhanced light–matter interaction, offering a complementary regime in which strong nonlinear interactions may be achieved within nanoscale volumes29–31. This distinction motivates the present work, which explores plasmonic structures including nonlinear media as an alternative and potentially synergistic route toward highly compact integrated squeezing devices.

Surface plasmon polaritons (SPPs) are surface waves that propagate along the interface between metallic and dielectric materials, arising from the coupling of electromagnetic fields with oscillations of conduction electrons32. Because SPPs can transmit energy and information beyond the diffraction limit33,34, plasmonic waveguides—capable of subwavelength confinement of SPP modes—hold significant potential for nanoscale integrated photonic devices. For example, efficient squeezed-state generation has been demonstrated in nonlinear plasmonic waveguides35. This work showed that strong squeezing of both pump and second-harmonic modes can be achieved in waveguide lengths less than 2 mm—two orders of magnitude shorter than the roughly 100 mm typically required in conventional nonlinear lithium-niobate waveguides. Nonlinear plasmonic waveguides therefore offer a promising route toward miniaturizing quantum-optical devices that rely on squeezed-state generation.

Although numerous studies have investigated the generation and characterization of squeezed states, to the best of our knowledge, the generation of both single-mode and two-mode squeezed states via degenerate FWM in a plasmonic waveguide has not yet been explicitly reported. In this work, we present a theoretical demonstration and quantitative analysis of squeezing generation through degenerate FWM in a plasmonic gap waveguide filled with a highly nonlinear organic material. Owing to the strong field confinement inherent to plasmonic structures, FWM-based plasmonic waveguides are expected to enable squeezed-state generation within a significantly more compact footprint than conventional FWM- or SHG based platforms.

Accordingly, we propose and analyze a plasmonic waveguide platform for the efficient generation of both single- and two-mode squeezed states via degenerate FWM. We develop a quantum–mechanical formulation of the FWM process in terms of expectation values and field fluctuations, introducing appropriate single- and two-mode squeezing factors while explicitly accounting for noise effects in their evaluation. Our results demonstrate that, in the proposed structure, the waveguide length required to achieve maximum squeezing is reduced by approximately three orders of magnitude compared with previous works based on SHG in a hybrid plasmonic waveguide35.

This reduction in device length arises from the stronger field confinement provided by the plasmonic gap structure employed here, as well as from the use of FWM rather than SHG as the underlying nonlinear process. Moreover, the present work predicts the generation of both single- and two-mode squeezed states, whereas only single-mode squeezing was reported in Ref.35. These results indicate that FWM in plasmonic waveguides constitutes an effective platform for squeezed-state generation, offering reduced device footprint and lower required optical pump power compared with existing FWM-based squeezing systems. Consequently, the proposed configuration provides a promising route toward further miniaturization of integrated quantum optical devices.

Correlated two-mode states of light are particularly important due to their connection to two-photon nonlinear optical processes. For example, in two-photon devices like parametric amplifiers, light is produced by the simultaneous emission of photon pairs into both output modes. Such devices can generate nonclassical correlated two-mode electromagnetic field states, which often exhibit nonclassical features like squeezing, sub-Poissonian photon statistics, and antibunching. The most significant correlated two-mode state is the two-mode squeezed state36.

It should be noted that the proposed approach in this paper can be readily generalized to generation of two- mode squeezed states in other types of waveguides. Furthermore, this approach makes it straightforward to incorporate noise into the squeezing process. This is particularly important, as noise plays a critical role in degrading squeezing performance. Furthermore, the effects of pump power and waveguide structural parameters on the squeezing factor are investigated.

First, the transverse electric field distribution for each mode propagating through the waveguide structure shown in Fig. 2 is obtained using the COMSOL Multiphysics mode solver module. It should be noted that various types of modes can propagate within the waveguide; however, modes with low confinement are excluded due to their high propagation losses. Second, the overlap integrals corresponding to the self-phase modulation (SPM) and cross-phase modulation (XPM) effects are computed using Eqs. (9)–(12). Third, Eqs. Finally, the squeezing factors for both single-mode and two-mode squeezed states are evaluated at each position along the waveguide length using Eqs. (33) and (34), respectively.

As shown in Figs. 4 and 5, the waveguide length at which the minimum squeezing factor (maximum squeezing level) occurs is three orders of magnitude smaller than that reported in Ref.35. This substantial reduction enables further miniaturization of quantum optical systems requiring squeezed-state generation. In addition, employing higher pump powers enables the achievement of strong squeezing over shorter waveguide lengths. This effect provides a clear advantage for reducing the footprint of photonic components employed in quantum optical technologies. In particular, our work demonstrates that the combination of extreme modal confinement and the use of an organic nonlinear material (DDMEBT) with exceptionally large third-order susceptibility enables efficient quantum squeezing in an ultracompact device length that is not achievable in previously reported dielectric or hybrid FWM-based squeezing platforms46–48. Unlike conventional FWM systems, where long interaction lengths are required for weak nonlinearity, our approach exploits the enhancement of nonlinear interaction strength in a deeply subwavelength plasmonic mode. Consequently, the novelty of our work lies not merely in device miniaturization, but in identifying and validating a new operational regime for quantum nonlinear optics using plasmonic-organic hybrid waveguides. Moreover, we explicitly account for plasmonic loss, modal overlap, and realistic material parameters in our analysis, and show that nonclassical squeezing can still be achieved despite these limitations. This establishes a practical and previously unexplored design regime for FWM-based squeezing, rather than a purely theoretical scaling argument.

The assumed sub-micrometer interaction length (< 2 μm) arises naturally from the strong field localization in plasmonic gap modes, which enables significant nonlinear interaction over very short propagation distances. While gold exhibits substantial Ohmic losses, previous works have shown that nonclassical effects, including squeezing and entanglement, can still be observed in plasmonic systems when the interaction length is sufficiently short and the nonlinear coupling is strong29–31,35.

Some effects such as dispersion, anisotropy, thermal effects, fabrication tolerances, and nonlinear absorption are neglected to isolate the fundamental nonlinear interaction mechanism and to keep the model analytically tractable. These factors may quantitatively affect the achievable squeezing level. A full treatment including metal-induced noise and plasmonic damping is beyond the scope of the present work and is left for future investigation.

As observed in Fig. 8, for the signal, pump, and idler modes, the maximum electric field across the waveguide cross-section decreases as the gap width increases. This behavior is attributed to the reduction in the quality factor of the waveguide modes with increasing gap width, which in turn lowers the electromagnetic field intensity within the nonlinear DDMEBT material. Consequently, the efficiency of degenerate FWM is reduced, leading to weaker squeezing. Furthermore, Fig. 7 clearly shows that the propagation length corresponding to the minimum squeezing factor increases with increasing gap width. Therefore, to achieve strong squeezing over shorter waveguide lengths, the gap width should be selected to be as small as 50 nm. As a result, using smaller gap widths leads to further miniaturization of the quantum optical devices designed based on proposed structure in this paper for generation of squeezed states. For experimental realization of the structure shown in Fig. 2, fabricating a 50–70 nm plasmonic gap filled with DDMEBT is challenging. However, recent advances in nanofabrication—such as atomic layer deposition, self-assembled molecular layers, electron-beam lithography, and hybrid plasmonic–dielectric structures—suggest that achieving such gap dimensions is approaching practical feasibility. In addition, in a realistic implementation, optical fields can be coupled into and out of the plasmonic waveguide using prism or optical grating coupling.

Conclusion

In this work, we investigated the generation of single- and two-mode squeezed states in a plasmonic waveguide through the four-wave mixing process. The governing equations for the expectation values and fluctuation operators of the quantum fields corresponding to the pump, signal, and idler modes were derived. Using the covariance matrix formalism, the squeezing factors for both single- and two-mode squeezed states were defined while accounting for optical losses. The results demonstrate that strong squeezing can be achieved in plasmonic waveguides over significantly shorter propagation lengths compared to hybrid plasmonic waveguides employing SHG35. Moreover, increasing the pump power enhances the squeezing quality and reduces the propagation length required to reach maximum squeezing. The gap width between the two gold layers was also found to play a critical role in the squeezing performance—its reduction leads to improved signal–idler two-mode squeezing. Finally, it was shown that optical losses deteriorate the squeezing quality, resulting in a degradation of the generated two-mode squeezed states. With respect to the short length, the plasmonic waveguide used in this paper and other plasmonic systems in the presence of nonlinear FWM effect can provide an alternative route toward highly compact integrated squeezing devices. Consequently, the experimental realization and further investigation of this FWM based plasmonic squeezing system can lead to the development of integrated quantum photonic systems.

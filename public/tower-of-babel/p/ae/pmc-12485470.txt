Long noncoding RNA NONRATT007487.2 drives cancer-induced bone pain progression through direct interaction with CXCL1
Authors: Hui Jiang, Lanlan Wang, Yiping Xu, Zhisen Dai, Junheng Chen, Dongqing Zheng, Huizhe Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCancer-induced bone pain (CIBP) is a significant and challenging comorbidity closely related to cancer metastasis. Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) have been implicated in both cancer progression and pain modulation, however, their specific role in CIBP remains unclear. The present study aims to elucidate the potential mechanisms by which lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 contributes to the development of CIBP.MethodsA CIBP model was established by injecting Walker 256 mammary gland tumor cells into the tibial canal of rats. To assess cancer-induced pain behavior, the authors measured the paw withdrawal threshold and paw withdrawal latency. Transcriptome sequencing was conducted to identify the pathways and genes regulated by lncRNA NONRATT007487.2. Additionally, immunofluorescence, RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), and RNA pulldown assays were performed to investigate the relationship between lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 1 (CXCL1).ResultsIt was observed that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 was significantly upregulated in the spinal cords of CIBP rats. Knockdown of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 alleviated both mechanical and thermal hyperalgesia in these rats and appeared to inhibit the chemokine signaling pathway. Notably, CXCL1 expression was notably reduced following the loss of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2. FISH assays demonstrated co-location of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and CXCL1 in the spinal cord, and RNA pulldown assays confirmed the direct interaction between these two molecules.ConclusionsThe results indicated that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 plays a crucial regulatory role in CIBP through its interaction with CXCL1, presenting a potential therapeutic target for alleviating nociceptive hypersensitivity associated with bone metastasis.Keywords: Bone Neoplasms; Chemokine CXCL1; Neuroinflammatory Diseases; Nociception; Pain; RNA, Long Noncoding; Signal Transduction; Spinal Cord; Transcriptome

INTRODUCTION

Multiple types of cancer, including breast, lung, prostate, and kidney cancers, frequently metastasize to bone in later stages, posing significant risks in advanced stages [1]. This metastatic process can cause severe complications such as bone fractures, pain, hypercalcemia, and compression of spinal structures [2]. Notably, up to 75% of patients with bone metastatic tumors experience moderate to severe cancer-induced bone pain (CIBP) [3]. However, effective treatments for CIBP are limited due to the incomplete understanding of its underlying mechanisms [4,5]. Therefore, identifying these mechanisms is crucial for developing new therapeutic and preventive strategies.

The regulation of long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) is disrupted in numerous chronic pain conditions, encompassing neuropathic pain, inflammatory pain, and pain associated with cancer [6]. Transcriptomic analysis has revealed the expression profiles of lncRNAs and mRNAs in the spinal cord of bone cancer pain rats [7]. Elevation of lncRNA71132 expression in the spinal cord modulates nociceptive sensitivity in a rat model of bone cancer pain [8]. In addition, lncRNA NONRATT009773.2 [9], NONRATT021203.2 [10], and NONRATT014888.2 [11] were reported to be involved in the development of CIBP. LncRNA NONRATT007487.2 was selected based on transcriptomic sequencing data from a prior study of CIBP, where it exhibited a significant upregulation among differentially expressed lncRNAs [7]. However, the functions of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 in CIBP have not been reported.

Previous research has highlighted the crucial role of chemokines and their receptors in modulating chronic pain through neuroinflammation in both the peripheral and central nervous systems [12]. C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 1 (CXCL1), acting through its receptor CXCR2, is known to enhance nociceptor and central sensitization, presenting a potential target for new analgesics [13]. In addition, CXCL1 also plays a significant role in maintaining neuropathic pain [14]. Resolvin D2 has been found to alleviate chronic neuropathic and bone cancer pain by inhibiting CXCL1 release in the spinal cord [15]. However, the potential mechanisms underlying CXCL1 regulation in peripheral neurons, such as those in the dorsal root ganglion, remain elusive. This study aimed to investigate whether lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 is involved in CIBP development by regulating CXCL1 expression. The authors’ findings offer a scientific rationale and novel therapeutic targets for alleviating CIBP and enhancing the quality of life for patients suffering from this condition.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Animals and a cancer-induced bone pain model construction

Female Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (180–200 g, 8-week-old and 60–80 g, 4-week-old) were purchased from Anburui Biotechnology Co., Ltd. All the rats were housed in specific pathogen-free facilities under controlled conditions (22 ± 2°C, relative humidity 45%–55%, 12-hour light/dark photoperiod) and given free access to food and water. The procedures for the CIBP model were carried out according to the previous studies [16,17]. In brief, 2 * 107 cells/mL, 0.5 mL of Walker 256 cells were injected into 4-week-old immune-deficient SD rats (60–80 g). After 1 week, tumor cells were harvested from fresh ascites, rinsed 3 times with 10 mL saline, and subsequently diluted to 1 * 108 cells/mL with saline. This diluted suspension or an equivalent amount of saline was then administered into the tibial plateau of rats using a 10 mL micro-syringe. Ethical approval was obtained from the Fujian Center for Safety Evaluation of New Drugs (FJMUGLP2024001-NG).

2. Behavioral assessment

Mechanical pain was assessed using von Frey filaments (Stoelting) as previous studies described [10,18,19]. The paw withdrawal threshold (PWT) signifies the minimal force required to elicit a positive withdrawal response. On the other hand, heat hyperalgesia was assessed using paw withdrawal latency (PWL) measurements, which involved applying a radiant heat source to the paw. During PWT testing, rats were individually placed in plastic boxes on a wire mesh. A range of 0.4 g to 15.0 g von Frey filaments were used perpendicularly to the hind paw with enough force to bend them for 1–2 seconds. For PWL assessment, the rats stood on a transparent glass plate, with a radiant heat focused on the plantar surface of their hind paw. A timer activated by the heat source recorded the PWL. Each rat underwent an average of three assessments per test, with intervals exceeding 5 minutes between measurements.

3. Transfection

The negative control (NC), sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 lentiviral vectors and pcDNA3.1-NC, pcDNA3.1-CXCL1 were synthesized by GenePharma. These vectors were delivered into rats via intraspinal injection. Rats were first anesthetized using isoflurane. Subsequently, a hemilaminectomy was carried out at the L1–L2 vertebral segments. Once the spinal cord was fully exposed, each animal underwent an injection of vectors (0.5 μL) along the L4–L5 dorsal root entry zone. A glass micropipette with a diameter of 60 μm was utilized for the injection, and its tip was carefully inserted to reach the lamina II–IV layer of the spinal cord. After the injection was completed, the dorsal muscle and skin layers were sutured in sequence. Transfection success was evaluated 48 hours later using real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR).

4. RNA-seq analysis

Three replicate samples from sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 CIBP and CIBP rats underwent mRNA sequencing, conducted by Biozeron Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Following terminal anesthesia, rats were transcardially perfused with ice-cold phosphate buffered saline (PBS) to clear blood from the neurovascular system. The vertebral column was rapidly dissected from the L3 to L5 segments using laminectomy forceps. The total RNA of the spinal cord in the L3–L5 segments was isolated by a TRIzol reagent kit (Invitrogen). Post-extraction, an oligo (dT) bead enrichment step was executed, followed by reverse transcription of targeted mRNA into double-stranded cDNA using random primers. The cDNA fragments were used to construct the sequencing library. Sequencing was carried out on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000. Differential analysis of RNA-seq data between the groups was conducted using DESeq2, with significant differentially expressed genes (DEGs) identified as false discovery rate < 0.05 and fold change ≥ 2. Additionally, KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) pathway enrichment analyses were carried out.

5. Immunoﬂuorescence double staining

The initial steps followed the immunohistochemistry protocol until antigen retrieval [20]. Sections were permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 (P0096-100ml; Beyotime) in PBS for 30 minutes at room temperature. Blocking was performed with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA, HY-D0842; MedChemExpress) in PBST (BL314B; Biosharp) for 1 hour. Next, sections were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against CLCX1 (1:200, 12335-1-AP; Proteintech), NEUN (1:200, ab177487; Abcam), GFAP (1:500, 16825-1-AP; Proteintech), and IBA1 (1:100, ab178846; Abcam). After washing, sections were incubated with secondary antibodies labeled with Fluor Cy3 (A0516; Beyotime) or 488 (A0423; Beyotime). Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (C0065; Solarbio), and images were captured using a fluorescence microscope.

6. RNA fluorescence in situ hybridization

Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) was performed according to a previous protocol [21], utilizing an enhanced sensitivity ISH detection kit I (#MK1030; BOSTER Biological Technology Co.Ltd.). A digoxigenin-labeled locked nucleic acid probe, complementary to lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and synthesized by Exiqon, was added to each slide in a hybridization mixture (30–50 mL). Incubation was done at 37°C for 12 hours. Post-incubation, the slides were treated with 3% H2O2 for 30 minutes at room temperature, followed by a 1-hour incubation in blocking buffer. Subsequently, 200–250 mL of antibodies were added. Imaging was conducted using a ZEISS LSM 880 confocal microscope, with analysis performed by ZEN 2.3SP1 (black) software.

7. Real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction

Total RNA was extracted from the spinal cord in L3–L5 segments tissues using TRIzol. The cDNA was synthesized using 1 μg of RNA with an oligo (dT) primer (Takara). The RT-qPCR was performed using the ABI7500 Real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems). The relative gene expression level were calculated using the 2-△△Ct method. The primers sequences (5’–3’) of the genes are as follows: lncRNA NONRATT007487.2, F: AGCCAGTGGGTCACTTGTGTT, R: TTCTGCTAAAGTCCGGCTGAA; GAPDH, F: TGGAAAGCTGTGGCGTGATG, R: TACTTGGCAGGTTTCTCCAGG; and CXCL1, F: GATGGCGTCTGTCTGGTGAA, R: TCCTGAGACGAGAAGGAGCA.

8. RNA pulldown assay

To investigate the potential interaction between lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and CXCL1, an RNA pull-down experiment was conducted by the Pierce Magnetic RNA-Protein Pull-Down Kit (#20164; Thermo Scientific). Briefly, biotinylated RNA was combined with protein lysates extracted from the spinal cord in L3–L5 segments and streptavidin-conjugated magnetic beads. After thorough washing, the beads were analyzed by immunoblotting to identify the captured proteins.

9. Statistical analysis

Statistical evaluations were conducted utilizing GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0.1; GraphPad). The presented data represent mean ± standard deviation. Data normality was verified using Shapiro–Wilk test. Student’s t-test was used for two-group comparisons, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used for multiple comparisons. All experiments included at least three independent biological replicates. P values lower than 0.05 were regarded statistically meaningful.

RESULTS

1. Effect of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 on cancer-induced pain behavior

The results of RT-qPCR showed that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 was notably increased in the CIBP rats compared with the control rats (Fig. 1A). To assess the connection between lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and CIBP behavior, the authors investigated whether intraspinal injection of sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 could alleviate CIBP in rats by depleting lncRNA NONRATT007487.2. The RT-qPCR results showed that lncRNA NONRATT009773.2 was down-regulated compared with the CIBP group after lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 knockdown (Fig. 1B). To explore the impact of sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 on mechanical hyperalgesia and thermal hyperalgesia, PWTs and PWLs were measured. One week after tumor cell injection, PWTs significantly decreased in the CIBP group compared to normal controls. Notably, PWTs in the CIBP + sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 group increased significantly compared to the CIBP + lncRNA NONRATT007487.2-NC group, indicating reduced hyperalgesia (Fig. 1C). Similarly, PWLs were shorter in the CIBP group than in the control group 1 week post-injection, but they increased significantly in the CIBP + sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 group compared to the CIBP + lncRNA NONRATT007487.2-NC group (Fig. 1D). These results suggested that knockdown of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 ameliorated CIBP.

2. Knockdown of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 may inhibit the chemokine signaling pathway

Subsequently, in order to investigate the potential pathway of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 regulation, transcriptome sequencing between CIBP + sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and CIBP was performed. The results revealed 1,241 DEGs between the two groups, with 410 genes up-regulated and 831 genes down-regulated (Fig. 2A). The volcano and heatmap showed the DEGs between the two groups (Fig. 2B, C). The DEGs enrichment analysis showed the top 30 up-regulated pathway and down-regulated pathways in sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 group (Fig. 3A, B). Of interest to the authors, it was found that the chemokine signaling pathway was down-regulated (Fig. 3B). Fig. 4 depicted a map of genes involved in the chemokine signaling pathway [22]. In sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 CIBP rats, the majority of these related genes exhibited down-regulation (red indicates up-regulated genes, while yellow indicates down-regulated genes). This finding indicated that knockdown of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 may inhibit chemokine signaling pathway.

3. CXCL1 was down-regulated in CIBP rats with lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 loss

Chemokine-induced neuroinflammation plays a crucial role in the development of neuropathic pain [23]. In the transcriptome sequencing results, it was found that CXCL11, CXCL9, CXCL2 and CXCL1 were both down-regulated in the spinal cord of CIBP + sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 rats (Fig. 5). Among the four chemokines, CXCL1 showed the biggest difference (log2fold change = –9.36, P = 2.42e-42). The results of the RT-qPCR confirmed that CXCL1 expression was notably down-regulated in the spinal cord of CIBP + sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 rats (Fig. 6A).

4. CXCL1 was involved in the regulation of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 on CIBP progression

Next, double immunofluorescence staining showed that CXCL1 was primarily co-localized with Neun, a neuronal marker, in spinal cord tissue, rather than with IBA1 (microglial marker) or GFAP (astrocytes marker) (Fig. 6B). Additionally, the authors used FISH assay for lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 to determine whether the two molecules are co-localization in the spinal cord tissue. As shown in Fig. 6C, lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and CXCL1 were co-expressed. And knockdown of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 showed less expression of CXCL1 and lncRNA NONRATT007487.2. Otherwise, the RNA pulldown assay and the immunoblot analysis confirmed the direct binding of CXCL1 and lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 (Fig. 6D, E). In addition, to determine whether lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 promotes CIBP progression through CXCL1, the authors injected CXCL1 overexpressed vector in CIBP + sh-lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 rats. The results indicated that overexpression of CXCL1 could significantly restore the PWT and the PWL of CIBP rats (Fig. 7). This observation indicated that while lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 regulated CXCL1 expression, exogenous overexpression of CXCL1 could bypass lncRNA-dependent regulation to activate CIBP. Collectively, these findings demonstrated that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 interacted directly with CXCL1 to modulate its expression in neurons, thereby contributing to CIBP progression. However, the rescue of PWT and PWL by CXCL1 overexpression in lncRNA-deficient rats suggested that CXCL1 may also integrate additional upstream signals independent of NONRATT007487.2 in pathological condition.

DISCUSSION

This study elucidates the role of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 in the modulation of CIBP, specifically highlighting its interaction with CXCL1. The findings indicated that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 was remarkably up-regulated in the spinal cords of CIBP rats. This suggested that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 may be a critical player in the pathophysiology of CIBP, potentially acting as a regulatory element in the development and progression of pain associated with cancer metastasis.

CIBP represents a challenging clinical manifestation frequently observed following bone metastasis, particularly prevalent in breast cancer cases where the incidence of bone metastasis can reach up to 70% [24]. Tumor cells initially spread to weight-bearing bones, such as the femur, thoracic spine, and tibia. As tumors expand and bone tissue deteriorates, patients may experience acute and intense pain flares. CIBP significantly impairs patients’ functionality and quality of life [25,26]. Managing this type of pain clinically presents a challenge due to incomplete understanding of the underlying mechanisms of CIBP.

LncRNAs play a pivotal role in the development of numerous human diseases, such as tumors, diabetes, and cardiovascular disorders [27]. Their significance in chronic pain has also been recognized. Nevertheless, the function of lncRNAs in the peripheral nervous system during CIBP remains unexplored. Uncovering distinct lncRNA expression patterns is crucial for elucidating the genetic mechanisms underlying CIBP pathogenesis [28]. In recent years, several lncRNAs have been reported in CIBP. For instance, lncRNA NONRATT014888.2 promotes CIBP in rats by suppressing NPR3 expression [11], while lncRNA 51325 mitigates bone cancer pain by inhibiting Pum2 [29]. Additionally, lncRNA NONRATT009773.2 and NONRATT021203.2 separately promoted CIBP via CXCL13 or CXCL9 [9,10]. In the present study, the authors found that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 expression was increased in the spinal cord of CIBP rats. Knockdown of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 alleviated mechanical and thermal hyperalgesia in the CIBP rats.

Chemokine-mediated neuroinflammation is known to be critical in the progression of chronic pain [30]. Furthermore, chemokines have been shown to elicit neuroinflammation in CIBP. Specifically, CXCR3 and its associated PI3K/Akt and Raf/MEK/ERK signaling cascades are implicated in bone cancer pain [31]. Additionally, CXCR4 activates the RhoA/ROCK2 or CaMKII/CREB pathways in spinal neurons, contributing to bone cancer pain [32,33]. CXCL13, another chemokine, influences bone cancer pain and opposes morphine analgesia via the p38, ERK, and Akt pathways [34]. CXCR1 is also involved in bone cancer pain caused by Walker 256 breast cancer cells in female rats [35]. Natural killer in the spinal cord enhances bone cancer pain in rats by regulating CXCL1 [36]. CXCL1, belonging to the CXC chemokine family and also is known as keratinocyte-derived chemokine or growth-related oncogene [37]. In astrocytes of the spinal cord, CXCL1 expression increased after spinal nerve ligation, furthering in the persistence of neuropathic pain [14]. Here, in the present study, transcriptomic sequencing results indicated that the expression of CXCL1 might be regulated by lncRNA NONRATT007487.2. Although multiple chemokines were downregulated upon lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 knockdown, CXCL1 was prioritized due to its biggest difference (log2fold change = –9.36, P = 2.42e-42) among the four chemokines. In addition, previous reports have revealed its up-regulation and roles in CIBP [36,38]. In the present study, the FISH assay showed that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and CXCL1 were co-expressed in the spinal cord tissue of rats. And knockdown of lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 showed less expression of CXCL1 and lncRNA NONRATT007487.2. In addition, RNA pulldown assay and the Western blot analysis confirmed the direct binding of CXCL1 and lncRNA NONRATT007487.2. The direct binding between lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and CXCL1 provides new clues for clarifying the molecular mechanisms of CIBP, revealing the potential mode of action of lncRNA in pain regulation. This binding suggests that the lncRNA NONRATT007487.2-CXCL1 axis may become a potential intervention target for alleviating CIBP. While previous studies have predominantly reported astrocyte-derived CXCL1 in neuroinflammatory contexts, evidence also supports neuronal CXCL1 expression under specific pathological conditions [39-41]. The present finding of neuronal CXCL1 secretion in the CIBP model may be attributed to the following mechanism. In cancer-induced pathological contexts, neuronal CXCL1 expression may be aberrantly activated as part of a neuro-immune crosstalk response to tumor-derived factors. The authors’ CIBP model involves cancer cell invasion into bone and subsequent neuro-immune crosstalk, which may induce distinct cellular responses. Tumor-derived factors (e.g., vascular endothelial growth factor, interleukin-6) released in bone metastasis could directly stimulate sensory neurons to produce CXCL1. In the future, small molecule inhibitors or antisense oligonucleotides targeting this interaction can be designed, providing new ideas for clinical treatment. Although this study has confirmed the binding between lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and CXCL1, the specific regulatory details still need further research.

The authors speculate that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 may enhance the stability or translation efficiency of CXCL1 by binding to specific regions of CXCL1 mRNA, preventing degradation by RNA-binding proteins (e.g., HuR) or miRNAs. This regulatory pattern has been reported in previous studies. For example, lncRNA PTB-AS substantially increased the PTBP1 level by directly binding to its 3’UTR and stabilizing the mRNA [42]. PTB-AS masks the binding site of miR-9, which negatively regulates PTBP1, in the PTBP1-3’UTR [42]. In addition, lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 may also act as a scaffold or enhancer RNA to facilitate transcriptional activation of CXCL1 by recruiting chromatin modifiers to its promoter region. Further studies are needed to elucidate additional potential mechanisms, which will be explored in the authors’ future work.

The data in the present study revealed a complex interplay between NONRATT007487.2 and CXCL1. While the lncRNA directly binds and regulates CXCL1 expression, the persistence of CIBP upon CXCL1 overexpression in lncRNA-deficient rats implied that exogenous overexpression of CXCL1 may bypass this NONRATT007487.2-mediated regulatory axis. This suggested that CXCL1 functions as a central effector in CIBP capable of integrating multiple upstream signals. NONRATT007487.2 is a key but not the sole regulator of CXCL1 in CIBP, and other pathways (e.g., transcriptional or post-translational regulation) may contribute to CXCL1-driven pain signaling. Future studies should explore whether combinatorial targeting of both lncRNA-dependent and -independent CXCL1 pathways could synergistically suppress CIBP.

In conclusion, this study provided compelling evidence that lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 is a significant regulator of CIBP through its interaction with CXCL1. These results enhanced the understanding of the molecular underpinnings of CIBP and suggested that targeting lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 and related signaling pathways may offer a novel approach for the treatment of pain in cancer patients. Further research should focus on elucidating the precise molecular mechanisms through which lncRNA NONRATT007487.2 influences CXCL1 expression and exploring the potential for therapeutic interventions targeting this pathway.

The FOXE1 rs965513 polymorphism: a pleiotropic risk locus associated with thyroid function, BRAFV600E mutation, and susceptibility to papillary thyroid cancer
Authors: Wenran Zhang, Yu Gao, Simei Zeng, Jingmin Li, Shuhua Wang, Yaming Huang, Jiaqing Dou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough papillary thyroid cancer (PTC) genetic research has advanced from identifying driver genes to investigating susceptibility loci and gene-environment interactions, data in Chinese populations remain limited. A case-control study was conducted, enrolling 75 patients with PTC and 271 patients with benign thyroid nodules. After testing for Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, the associations of the single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) at the rs965513 locus with genetic susceptibility to PTC, risk of BRAFV600E mutation, and clinical parameters were analyzed using chi-square tests and multivariate logistic regression under multiple genetic models. Multi-genetic model analysis revealed that individuals carrying the A allele had a significantly increased risk of PTC compared with those with the GG genotype (aOR = 2.684, 95% CI: 1.395–5.164, P = 0.003). When stratified by BRAFV600E mutation status, the A allele was also associated with an increased risk of harboring the BRAFV600E mutation (aOR = 2.574, 95% CI: 1.202–5.512, P = 0.015). Additionally, the GG genotype was significantly associated with elevated thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) levels, whereas TSH levels were lower in A allele carriers. In summary, the A allele significantly elevates the risk of both PTC and BRAFV600E mutation, whereas the G allele acts protectively. Introduction

Thyroid cancer is a complex interdisciplinary disease requiring collaboration across multiple clinical specialties, including endocrinology, head and neck surgery, general surgery, and nuclear medicine. In recent years, China has experienced a rapid rise in thyroid cancer incidence. This trend may be attributed to heightened public health awareness, leading to increased detection of thyroid nodules during routine medical examinations. Malignant thyroid nodules are classified into three primary subtypes based on histopathological characteristics: differentiated thyroid carcinoma (DTC), medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), and undifferentiated (anaplastic) thyroid carcinoma. Among these, PTC-a subtype of DTC-represents the most prevalent form of malignant thyroid neoplasms. Currently, the definitive diagnosis of thyroid cancer primarily depends on invasive methods, including postoperative histopathological examination and ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration biopsy (FNAB). Given the limitations of these approaches, early and accurate assessment of the malignant potential of thyroid nodules holds significant clinical value, as it could guide timely intervention and reduce unnecessary procedures.

Genetic factors play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of PTC. When DNA replication errors, oxidative stress, ultraviolet exposure, or radiation induce mutations at critical genomic loci, these alterations can promote uncontrolled cellular proliferation and tissue invasion, ultimately leading to the development of malignant tumors1. The BRAF gene encodes a crucial downstream effector in the RET/RAS signaling cascade. As a B-type Raf kinase, the BRAF protein serves as a key regulator of the MAPK signaling pathway, which governs fundamental cellular processes including growth, proliferation, and apoptosis. Mutational activation of BRAF can induce constitutive pathway signaling, thereby promoting tumorigenesis2–4. The predominant genetic alteration in PTC is a thymine-to-adenine transversion (T1799A) in exon 15 of the BRAF gene. This point mutation results in a valine to glutamic substitution at codon 600 (V600E), generating the constitutively active BRAFV600E oncoprotein that drives tumorigenesis through persistent MAPK pathway activation. The BRAFV600E mutation, occurring in 25–82% of PTC cases, serves as both a diagnostic marker and prognostic indicator due to its well-documented association with aggressive tumor characteristics, including extracapsular invasion and lymph node metastasis5,6. GWAS conducted in European Caucasian populations have identified several single-nucleotide polymorphisms, including rs965513, rs944289, rs67599552, rs10136427, rs7267944, and rs1220597, that demonstrate significant associations with thyroid cancer susceptibility7–10. However, the genetic architecture of thyroid cancer in Chinese populations remains understudied, with limited replication of these risk loci in East Asian cohorts. The human FOXE1 gene (forkhead box E1), located at chromosome 9q22 and alternatively designated as thyroid transcription factor 2 (TTF-2), encodes a thyroid-specific transcription factor that is essential for thyroid gland development. FOXE1 mediates thyroid morphogenesis primarily through regulation of thyroid precursor cell migration during embryogenesis, and emerging evidence suggests its continued involvement in modulating tumor cell migration and invasion in thyroid malignancies11–14. The rs965513 polymorphism resides within a ~ 33 kb linkage disequilibrium block containing multiple regulatory elements, including at least three functional enhancers that overlap the FOXE1 promoter region. Genetic variation at this locus demonstrates significant correlation with differential enhancer activity, resulting in altered FOXE1 expression levels that ultimately modulate thyroid cancer susceptibility15.

This study aims to investigate the association between the rs965513 polymorphism and both BRAFV600E mutation status and genetic predisposition to PTC and evaluate the correlation between this genetic variant and clinical parameters. The findings will provide a molecular basis for developing genetic diagnostic markers and targeted therapeutic strategies for thyroid cancer.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

This retrospective study enrolled 346 consecutive patients diagnosed with thyroid nodules at our hospital’s Department of Endocrinology between October 2022 and January 2025. The cohort comprised 61 male and 285 female patients, with a mean age of 52.33 ± 11.90 years. Based on comprehensive histopathological evaluation incorporating ultrasound-guided FNAB and postoperative pathological examination, patients were stratified into two groups: benign thyroid nodules (n = 271) and PTC (n = 75). Statistical power analysis was performed using GPower (3.1.9.7). A priori power analysis was conducted based on a contingency table chi-square test framework for case-control genotype distributions (df = 2). The effect size (Cohen’s w = 0.171) was estimated from a prior similar genetic association study16, with a significance threshold of α = 0.05 and statistical power set at 1-β = 0.80. The analysis indicated that a minimum total sample size of 330 was required to detect the specified effect. This study’s actual sample size comprised 346 cases, exceeding the theoretical minimum and thereby ensuring adequate statistical power.

Inclusion Criteria: (a) Thyroid nodules identified on color Doppler ultrasound and classified as TI-RADS category 3 or above, with independent confirmation by at least two board-certified sonographers, (b) no history of surgical intervention or medical therapy for thyroid disease and (c) definitive diagnosis of either benign nodules or PTC confirmed by FNAB or postoperative histopathological examination. Exclusion criteria: (a) nodules yielding non-diagnostic (Bethesda category Ⅰ) or indeterminate (Bethesda Ⅲ-Ⅳ) FNAB results, or those diagnosed as malignant (Bethesda Ⅴ-Ⅵ) but pathologically confirmed as non-PTC thyroid malignancies, (b) patients with acute physiological stress, active infections, or systemic inflammatory conditions, (c) women who were currently pregnant or lactating, (d) individuals with concurrent or historical diagnoses of other malignant neoplasms and (e) patients with impaired coagulation status, including those receiving anticoagulant therapy within the preceding seven days, documented coagulation abnormalities, or hereditary bleeding disorders such as hemophilia. All procedures performed in this study were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and national research committee and with the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards. All experiments were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. Informed consent was obtained from all individual participants or their legal guardians included in the study. The study was reviewed and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of our hospital (approval no. KYXM-202409-012).

Clinical and laboratory data

Demographic and clinical characteristics, including age, gender, height, weight, and hypertension history, were systematically recorded for all participants. Following an overnight fast of ≥ 8 h, morning urine samples (5 mL) were collected for urinary iodine quantification using a standardized urinary iodine group analyzer to determine Urinary Iodine-131 Mass concentrations(UIC). Peripheral venous blood samples were obtained by a dedicated phlebotomy team using uniform collection tubes. Serum biomarkers were analyzed using the Roche cobas e801 automated immunoassay system (Nuclear Medicine Department), with measurements including carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), TSH, thyroid peroxidase antibody (TPOAb), thyroglobulin antibody (TgAb), thyroglobulin (TG), calcitonin (CT), 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D], and parathyroid hormone (PTH) levels.

BRAFV600E genetic testing protocol

The patient was positioned supine with arms resting naturally at the sides, fully exposing the neck in a maintained hyperextended position. Under continuous ultrasound guidance, suspected thyroid nodules were identified. FNAB was performed using a repetitive needle lift-and-insert technique to obtain tissue samples for pathological analysis. BRAFV600E mutation status was retrieved from electronic medical records, as documented during routine clinical diagnostic testing. DNA was extracted from specimens using a micro-pathological nucleic acid extraction kit. Fluorescence quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed on an SLAN-96 S system to measure DNA fluorescence intensity. After threshold calibration, cycle threshold (Ct) values were recorded, and ΔCt values were calculated. BRAFV600E mutation positivity was defined as follows: FAM channel Ct < 38 and ΔCt < 9. Samples failing both criteria were classified as negative. The assay was validated by the manufacturer, demonstrating both sensitivity and specificity exceeding 95% for detecting BRAFV600E in thyroid tissue. The testing laboratory is accredited and participates in external quality assessment programs. Each run included positive and negative controls, and all samples analyzed in this study met the quality control criteria.

DNA extraction

Blood samples were centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 18 min, and 200µL of the leukocyte layer was collected and mixed with 200µL BCL and 20µL proteinase K. After vortexing, the mixture was incubated at 70℃ for 10 min with occasional inversion, then mixed with 150µL anhydrous ethanol and pulse-centrifuged. The mixture was transferred to a spin column and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 1 min, and the flow-through was discarded. The column was washed sequentially with 500µL WA buffer and twice with 600µL WB buffer, with centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 1 min after each wash and discarding of the flow-through. An additional 2-min centrifugation was performed to dry the column. The column was transferred to a new tube, and 80µL of Elution Buffer preheated to 55℃ was added to the membrane. After standing for 2–5 min, the column was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 1 min to elute the DNA, which was then stored at − 80℃. All procedures used the FastPure Blood/Cell/Tissue/Bacteria DNA Isolation Mini Kit-BOX2(Nanjing Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd.).

Genotype analysis

First, specific PCR primers were designed to amplify the fragment containing the rs965513 polymorphism in the FOXE1 gene. The primer sequences used were as follows:

Forward: 5′-GGAAATACTGCCCCAAGGGA-3′.

Reverse: 5′-TCCCCAGCCATGCTGAACTG-3′.

PCR was performed using these primers to amplify the target region. The resulting PCR products were subsequently purified using ExoSAP-IT enzymatic treatment. The purified amplicons were then used as templates for cycle sequencing reactions under the following conditions: initial denaturation at 96 °C for 1 min, followed by 25 cycles of denaturation at 96 °C for 10 s, annealing at 50 °C for 5 s, and extension at 60 °C for 4 min. Following the sequencing reaction, fluorescently labeled fragments were purified via ethanol precipitation. The purified products were subjected to capillary electrophoresis on a sequencer for fluorescence-based detection. Sequence data were generated based on fluorescence peak profiles. Genotyping was performed by Nanjing Smart Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Finally, the original fluorescence chromatograms were visualized using SnapGene 6.0.2 (Fig. 1). The overall quality of the electrophoretograms was assessed, and the obtained sequences were aligned to the reference sequence for verification and analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 27.0; IBM Corp.). Data normality was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test complemented by visual inspection of Q-Q plots. Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s) and compared using independent samples t-tests. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were presented as median (interquartile range) [M (P25, P75)] and analyzed using Mann-Whitney U tests. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies (percentages) and compared using either chi-square (χ²) tests or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Binary logistic regression models were employed to evaluate associations between SNP genotypes and PTC susceptibility, BRAFV600E mutation status, or clinical parameters. A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses. The sample size was calculated using G*Power software (version 3.1.9.7). All statistical analyses and visualizations were performed in R (version 4.4.1) utilizing the following packages: ggplot2 (v3.5.2) for data visualization, dplyr (v1.1.4) for data manipulation, ggpubr (v0.6.0) and rstatix (v0.7.2) for statistical visualizations and tests, forestplot (v3.1.7) for generating forest plots, and tidyverse (v2.0.0) for integrated data science workflows.

Association analysis between rs965513 and PTC genetic susceptibility

The rs965513 SNP genotype distribution in the FOXE1 region conformed to Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (χ²=0.004, p = 0.962), confirming the representativeness of our control population for genetic association studies.

Based on the genotype and allele distributions of rs965513, we implemented a comprehensive genetic model analysis strategy to examine its association with PTC risk. Five genetic models were evaluated: (a) codominant (GG vs. AG vs. AA), (b) dominant (GG vs. AG + AA), (c) recessive (AG + GG vs. AA), (d) overdominant (AA + GG vs. AG), and (e) additive models. This approach enabled systematic assessment of both genotype-specific effects and the overall influence of rs965513 alleles on PTC susceptibility. Genetic analysis revealed significant associations between rs965513 polymorphisms and PTC susceptibility (Table 2). In the codominant model, the GG genotype predominated in benign thyroid nodules (81.1%), demonstrating a statistically significant difference (χ²=9.196, P = 0.008). The dominant model similarly showed higher GG genotype frequency in benign nodules (χ²=9.173, P = 0.002). However, no significant distributional differences were observed in the recessive model (P = 0.387). The overdominant model analysis indicated significantly higher combined AA + GG genotype prevalence in benign nodules versus PTC (χ²=8.219, P = 0.004). Allelic frequency analysis further confirmed these findings, with the G allele occurring more frequently in benign nodules than in PTC (χ²=9.331, P = 0.002). Collectively, these results demonstrate a significant association between the rs965513 SNP and genetic susceptibility to PTC, with the GG genotype and G allele appearing to confer a protective effect against malignant transformation.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis (Table 3) revealed significant genotype-specific associations with PTC susceptibility. In the codominant model, AG heterozygotes showed significantly elevated PTC susceptibility compared to GG homozygotes (OR = 2.571, 95%CI:1.339–4.939, P = 0.005). This association strengthened after adjustment for age, sex, BMI, and hypertension (adjusted OR = 2.660, 95%CI:1.366–5.180, P = 0.004). No significant association was observed for AA homozygotes (OR = 3.271, 95%CI:0.191–56.135, P = 0.414). The dominant model demonstrated that combined AG/AA genotypes conferred significantly higher PTC susceptibility versus GG genotype (OR = 2.627, 95%CI:1.384–4.986, P = 0.003), which persisted after adjustment (aOR = 2.684, 95%CI:1.395–5.164, P = 0.003). The recessive model showed no significant AA genotype effect (aOR = 2.771, 95%CI:0.163–47.232, P = 0.481). Notably, the overdominant model revealed AG heterozygotes had significantly increased susceptibility compared to AA/GG combined genotypes (OR = 2.537, 95%CI:1.322–4.868, P = 0.005), with strengthened association after adjustment (aOR = 2.631, 95%CI:1.352–5.121, P = 0.004). The additive model confirmed a dose-response relationship, with each additional A allele copy increasing PTC susceptibility 2.5-fold (aOR = 2.496, 95%CI:1.343–4.639, P = 0.004). Model comparison using Akaike (AIC) and Bayesian (BIC) information criteria identified the dominant model as optimal for explaining PTC susceptibility associations. Therefore, the analysis focused primarily on the dominant and overdominant models. The strength of the association between the rs965513 polymorphism and PTC susceptibility was evaluated through subgroup analysis. Results are expressed as odds ratios (ORs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and are visualized in forest plots (Fig. 3, dominant model; Fig. 4, overdominant model). The two models yielded largely consistent results. The detailed findings for each subgroup are presented below. A significant association between the rs965513 polymorphism and PTC susceptibility was observed across multiple subgroups. In gender-stratified analysis, the association was significant in both women and men. When stratified by age, a significant association was present in the younger group but not in the older cohort. Furthermore, significant increases in PTC susceptibility were identified in subgroups defined by the absence of hypertension and low levels of UIC, CEA, TSH, TGAb, TPOAb, TG, CT, 25(OH)D, and PTH. Conversely, no significant associations were observed in the complementary subgroups (e.g., hypertension positive, high UIC, etc.). Notably, within the BRAFV600E mutant subgroup, the odds ratio (OR) was exceptionally high, a finding that may be attributable to an anomalous data distribution. The analysis of BMI subgroups revealed model-specific effects. Under the dominant model, the association was significant in the high BMI group (OR = 3.000, 95% CI: 1.163–7.737, P = 0.023) and of marginal significance in the low BMI group (P = 0.054). Conversely, under the overdominant model, a significant association was observed in the low BMI group (OR = 2.515, 95% CI: 1.044–6.061, P = 0.040), while the result in the high BMI group was marginally significant (P = 0.057).

Association analysis between rs965513 and BRAFV600E gene mutation

Our genetic analysis revealed significant associations between rs965513 polymorphisms and BRAFV600E mutation status in PTC patients (Table 5). When stratified into wild-type (n = 299) and mutation-positive (n = 47) groups, distinct genotype distribution patterns emerged (Table 6). The codominant (χ²=6.677, P = 0.041) and dominant (χ²=5.402, P = 0.020) models both demonstrated significant enrichment of the GG genotype in wild-type cases, while the overdominant model showed preferential distribution of AG heterozygotes in mutation-positive tumors (χ²=4.135, P = 0.042). Most strikingly, allele frequency analysis revealed the strongest association (χ²=6.242, P = 0.012), with the A allele being significantly enriched in BRAFV600E-mutant tumors and the G allele predominating in wild-type cases.

Association between rs965513 polymorphism and clinical parameters

Our analysis revealed selective associations between rs965513 variants and clinical parameters under dominant and overdominant genetic models. In the dominant model, while most clinical parameters showed no significant associations, we identified a notable relationship with TSH levels. GG homozygotes exhibited significantly higher TSH levels compared to carriers of at least one A allele (OR = 0.785, 95%CI:0.627–0.982, P = 0.034), with this association remaining significant after multivariable adjustment (aOR = 0.775, 95%CI:0.615–0.976, P = 0.030). Nonparametric tests additionally indicated elevated TGAb levels in GG genotype individuals, though regression analysis showed no significance. The overdominant model further supported these findings, demonstrating that AG heterozygotes had lower TSH levels compared to combined AA/GG genotypes (aOR = 0.788, 95%CI:0.625–0.994, P = 0.044). Complete associations for both genetic models are presented in Table 6 (dominant) and 7 (overdominant), respectively.

Based on the pathological nature of the thyroid nodules, patients were assigned to either a benign nodule group or a PTC group. Subsequently, to comprehensively evaluate the relationship between the rs965513 locus and the clinical characteristics of thyroid nodules, subgroup analyses were conducted within this cohort under both dominant and overdominant genetic models. For patients with benign thyroid nodules, analysis under the dominant model revealed a statistically significant difference in TGAb levels between the genotype groups, with higher levels in the GG group compared to the AG + AA group (Z=-2.003, P = 0.045). However, subsequent association analysis did not identify a significant linear relationship between TGAb levels and the dominant model (aOR = 0.999, 95% CI: 0.997–1.001, P = 0.278). Aside from this differential distribution of TGAb levels, no other evaluated clinical or biochemical parameters showed statistically significant differences between the AG + AA and GG genotypes. Similarly, analysis under the overdominant model found no significant differences between the AG genotype and the combined AA + GG genotypes for any of the key indicators assessed in patients with benign thyroid nodules. In summary, integrating the results from both the dominant and over-dominant genetic models, no consistent or statistically significant associations were identified between the rs965513 polymorphism and the evaluated clinical and biochemical parameters in this cohort of patients with benign thyroid nodules. The results of these subgroup analyses are presented in Table 8 (dominant model) and Table 9 (overdominant model). In patients with PTC, analysis using a dominant model revealed that CEA levels differed significantly between the two genotype groups, with the median CEA level being significantly lower in the AG + AA group compared to the GG group (Z=-2.144, P = 0.032). Further logistic regression analysis was performed and demonstrated that, under the dominant model, harboring the AG or AA genotype was significantly associated with lower CEA levels (aOR = 0.454, 95% CI: 0.213–0.969, P = 0.041). No other clinical parameters exhibited statistically significant differences between the two groups (all P > 0.05). Under the overdominant model, there were no statistically significant differences in most clinical characteristics between the AG and AA + GG genotype groups (P > 0.05). However, TPOAb levels differed significantly between the groups, with the median TPOAb level being significantly lower in the AG group than in the AA + GG group (Z=-2.216, P = 0.027). Furthermore, CEA levels demonstrated a statistically significant association with the overdominant model. After adjusting for potential confounders, the AG genotype was independently associated with lower CEA levels (aOR = 0.462, 95% CI: 0.218–0.981, P = 0.044). In summary, in the PTC population, analyses based on both the dominant and overdominant genetic models indicated that the polymorphism of rs965513 was independently associated with serum CEA levels. Detailed subgroup analyses of the associations between SNP genotyping and clinical parameters in PTC patients under the dominant and overdominant models are presented in Tables 10 and 11, respectively.

Discussion

In this study, a multi-genetic model analysis strategy was employed, encompassing five genetic models (codominant, dominant, recessive, overdominant, and additive), to investigate the association between rs965513 polymorphism and genetic susceptibility to PTC. The results revealed that the genotype distribution of the rs965513 locus in the FOXE1 flanking region was significantly associated with PTC susceptibility. The A allele significantly increased the susceptibility of PTC, whereas the G allele appeared to serve as a protective factor. Individuals homozygous for the GG genotype exhibited a significantly decreased susceptibility of developing PTC, whereas those carrying the A allele showed a relatively increased susceptibility of PTC. Furthermore, the SNP at the rs965513 locus was also potentially associated with the BRAFV600E mutation and TSH levels. Contrary to these findings, no significant association was observed for the AA genotype specifically under either the codominant or dominant models (P > 0.05). This discrepancy is likely attributable to the exceedingly small sample size of the AA genotype group (n = 2), which severely limited statistical power. Consequently, the recessive and overdominant models, which circumvent this limitation by grouping the AA genotype with other genotypes, were deemed more reliable for this dataset. Model comparison based on the AIC and BIC indicated that the recessive model achieved a superior balance between model fit and parsimony.

Genome-wide association studies have identified numerous SNPs conferring genetic susceptibility to PTC across diverse populations, including those in Poland17, Japan18, Pakistan19 and, Germany20. The prevalence of these risk-associated SNPs exhibits distinct geographic patterns, likely influenced by a combination of environmental factors—such as lifestyle and local ecology—and underlying ancestral genetic background. Although the rs965513 polymorphism was not significantly associated with PTC susceptibility in a Slovak cohort, the consensus of evidence from international populations robustly supports the A allele as a strong risk factor21–25.

Our findings indicate a significant positive association between the rs965513 polymorphism and increased PTC susceptibility, which was particularly evident in subgroups comprising males, younger individuals, and subjects with reduced levels of several biochemical markers. These subgroup-specific effects suggest heterogeneity in the influence of this locus on genetic susceptibility to PTC. Studies have shown that basal mRNA levels of FOXE1 in thyroid tissue are lower in females than in males26. The protective haplotype of the FOXE1 gene primarily exerts its protective effect in males, whereas in females, this protective effect is not observed, potentially due to interactions with estrogen. This suggests that the same genetic background may yield sexually dimorphic effects, likely mediated by differences in hormonal status17. The risk effect of rs965513 may be age-specific, being more pronounced and distinct in younger individuals, while appearing weaker and less certain in older populations. This finding is consistent with previous studies involving populations from Iceland, Columbus, and Spain, which reported that the frequency of the rs965513[A] allele was higher in subjects diagnosed with thyroid cancer at a younger age, with a per-allele reduction in diagnosis age of 2.42 years8. The stronger association observed in the younger age group suggests that the rs965513 polymorphism may be a particularly relevant genetic marker for early-onset PTC. This finding implies that the pathogenic mechanisms of this locus are likely driven more by intrinsic biological processes than by the accumulation of long-term environmental exposures. Consequently, genotyping at this locus could have significant value in identifying younger individuals at high risk, highlighting its potential utility in early screening strategies. Analysis stratified by TG level revealed that the association between the rs965513 polymorphism and PTC susceptibility was confined to individuals with low TG levels. In this subgroup, the GG genotype was associated with a significantly reduced risk, and the AG genotype with a significantly increased risk, of PTC. No such association was observed in the high TG group. TG, produced exclusively by thyroid follicular cells, is a pivotal biomarker in the management of differentiated thyroid cancer. Its primary clinical utility lies in monitoring for residual lesions and recurrence following thyroidectomy, as serum TG levels directly reflect the presence of thyroid tissue and are a strong indicator of prognosis27. Research has demonstrated that TGs derived from PTC incorporate chondroitin sulfate chains that are undersulfated compared to those from normal thyroid tissue28. This aberrant sulfation pattern is implicated in the carcinogenic potential of these abnormal TG forms, potentially by enhancing their antigenicity. TG may modulate the carcinogenic effects of SNPs, including rs965513, through specific molecular pathways. Previous studies have revealed that the genetic architecture of plasma TG is not consistent with a purely polygenic model, but rather exhibits a sparse genetic architecture, predominantly driven by a few major-effect genes, with ST6GAL1 as the core gene. Known variants explain approximately 17% of the phenotypic variance in Tg levels, of which 52% of the genetic contribution is attributable to these few major-effect genes29. Consequently, FOXE1 may serve as a minor gene locus in this context. We propose that individuals with low baseline TG levels who carry the A allele represent a high-risk population for PTC and may warrant enhanced clinical surveillance. Furthermore, a critical question emerges from these findings: whether therapeutic interventions aimed at elevating TG levels in this subpopulation could mitigate the carcinogenic effect of high-risk genetic variants, a promising avenue for future investigation.

Analysis of the association between the rs965513 polymorphism and BRAFV600E mutation risk revealed a significantly elevated risk among A allele carriers. Specifically, the odds of a BRAFV600E mutation were 2.30 times higher in individuals carrying at least one A allele compared to those with the G allele. Under a dominant genetic model (AA/AG vs. GG), this risk increase was 157.4%. A significant dose-response effect was also observed, with each additional copy of the A allele increasing the mutation risk by 2.49-fold. A prior study has demonstrated that the association of the rs965513 polymorphism with PTC is modified by BRAFV600E status, with the effect being significant only in the presence of the mutation30. Credendino et al.31 provided the first in vivo demonstration of a causal relationship between FOXE1 loss of function and the hallmarks of thyroid cancer, supporting the hypothesis that differential expression of FOXE1 may be a key determinant of its role as a susceptibility gene for differentiated thyroid carcinoma. Furthermore, a separate genetic association study reported that the TT genotype of rs2853669 acts synergistically with the BRAFV600E mutation to promote enhanced tumor aggressiveness32. Therefore, we propose that the rs965513 polymorphism is associated with the occurrence of the BRAFV600E mutation in patients with thyroid nodules. It is plausible that rs965513 influences the initiation and progression of PTC by modulating the BRAF signaling pathway.

Finally, we evaluated the association between the rs965513 polymorphism and key clinical parameters. Our analysis revealed that the GG genotype was significantly associated with elevated TSH levels. Conversely, carriers of at least one A allele (AG/AA genotypes) exhibited significantly lower TSH levels. Based on this correlative evidence, we hypothesize that TSH and the rs965513 risk variant may interactively participate in the intracellular signaling cascades that drive PTC pathogenesis. Our finding of an association between the rs965513 A allele (conferring PTC susceptibility) and lower TSH levels is supported by large-scale genetic studies. For instance, a GWAS of 27,758 Icelanders identified thyroid cancer susceptibility alleles that were concurrently associated with reduced serum TSH levels. The authors proposed that low TSH concentrations may diminish the differentiation of thyroid epithelial cells, thereby predisposing them to malignant transformation33. Furthermore, a subsequent GWAS meta-analysis in 119,715 individuals demonstrated that a polygenic score for higher TSH levels was associated with a reduced risk of thyroid cancer34. It is also noteworthy that polymorphisms at several other loci, including rs944289, rs10917469, rs4704397, and rs1443434, have been previously reported to influence TSH levels8,35–37. These collective findings significantly advance our understanding of the complex genetic architecture underlying the regulation and metabolism of the pituitary-thyroid axis. FOXE1 is highly expressed in PTC and, as a downstream target gene of Gli2, promotes PTC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by mediating the Gli2-FOXE1-Wnt/β-catenin signaling axis38. Lim et al.39 established the first inducible FOXE1 knockout mouse model and found that FOXE1 deficiency leads to disruption of thyroid follicular structure and hypothyroidism, characterized by decreased T4 levels and a compensatory increase in TSH. This provides a potential functional explanation for the genetic association between FOXE1 polymorphisms and human thyroid diseases. FOXE1 serves as a master thyroid transcription factor that regulates TPO expression in response to TSH and insulin-like growth factor 1. Since the promoter regions of TSH target genes typically lack classical cAMP response elements, the TSH/cAMP signaling pathway primarily relies on FOXE1 binding to specific sites within their promoters to activate transcription. Concurrently, TSH itself directly binds to two CRE sites in the FOXE1 promoter via the CREB/CREM transcription factors, thereby positively regulating Foxe1 expression at the transcriptional level. This interplay forms a complete feed-forward regulatory loop. Conversely, inhibitory signals such as TGFβ antagonize Foxe1 expression at the transcriptional level by interfering with CREB function via Smad proteins40. Under physiological conditions, FOXE1 constitutes a synergistic transcriptional network with PAX8 and NKX2-1, and is subject to positive autoregulation, collectively maintaining thyroid differentiation homeostasis. Dysregulation of this mechanism—such as loss of FOXE1 function—can lead to congenital hypothyroidism, whereas risk SNPs in its promoter region may increase susceptibility to thyroid cancer by modulating transcription factor binding.

Current clinical practice for the evaluation of thyroid nodules relies on ultrasonographic features, with FNAB performed when necessary. While molecular markers such as BRAFV600E have been incorporated into certain diagnostic algorithms41, the utility of germline variants such as rs965513 for risk stratification remains under investigation. If validated, rs965513 may complement existing tools by identifying individuals at higher baseline risk who could benefit from more intensive surveillance. The association between rs965513 and PTC susceptibility observed in this study suggests a potential value for this variant as a component of future risk stratification models. However, before any clinical application can be considered, replication in large, multicenter, prospective cohorts with population-representative samples is essential. Furthermore, a multifactorial risk prediction model that integrates genetic, clinical, and environmental factors should be developed and validated using rigorous statistical metrics.

This study contributes to the understanding of PTC genetic susceptibility in the Chinese population and provides novel insights into the interaction between the rs965513 polymorphism and the BRAFV600E mutation. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. Firstly, this study was conducted with a single-center sample, and the frequency of the AA genotype at the rs965513 locus was notably low. Consequently, the results from the codominant and recessive models may be subject to substantial bias, and the dose-response effect may not be reliably generalizable to homozygous AA carriers. Therefore, our primary findings are derived from the dominant and overdominant models. We are currently conducting a multicenter, large‑sample analysis in tertiary teaching hospitals of the same level to validate the role of the AA genotype. Preliminary data show a trend consistent with the current report, although a comprehensive analysis is still ongoing. Secondly, as a retrospective case-control study, the findings are subject to potential reverse causality and confounding, making it difficult to definitively establish causal relationships between SNPs, clinical exposures, and PTC susceptibility. Thirdly, the absence of stratification based on PTC histological subtypes (e.g., classical vs. follicular variant) may have obscured underlying molecular heterogeneity. Finally, due to resource constraints, we could not investigate the association between rs965513 genotypes and FOXE1 expression levels, a mechanistic link that would have strengthened our conclusions.

In conclusion, this study confirms a significant association between the rs965513 polymorphism near the FOXE1 gene and an increased risk of PTC in a Chinese population. We further demonstrate that this polymorphism is linked to key pathological and clinical features, namely the BRAFV600E mutation and lower serum TSH levels. Our findings consistently indicate that the A allele acts as a risk allele, conferring a higher susceptibility to PTC and BRAFV600E-positive tumors, while the G allele appears to be protective. The integration of rs965513 genotyping with BRAFV600E mutation status and TSH level assessment could enhance risk stratification models for PTC. Ultimately, these findings contribute a solid theoretical foundation for advancing personalized surveillance strategies and future research into targeted therapies and diagnostic biomarkers.

Zn2+ supplementation-mediated IZH4 enhances acid tolerance in Saccharomyces cerevisiae via membrane lipid remodeling: A multi-omics study
Authors: Tiantian Tian, Chao Yang, Jian-Lin Wu, Shaoquan Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Zinc is an essential micronutrient that serves as a vital cofactor in multiple metabolic pathways, including the mechanism mediated by IZH (Implicated in Zinc Homeostasis) genes and lipid biosynthesis. Previous studies revealed that Saccharomyces cerevisiae ET008-c54 (WT) exhibited excellent fermentation performance at low pH for acidic fruit wines, with IZH4 identified as a key gene. This study investigated the specific role of IZH4 mediated under Zn2+ supplementation in low-pH tolerance, using greengage plum wine as a model. Based on fermentation time and sensory evaluation, the optimal Zn2+ supplementation level was preliminarily determined to be 2 mg/L. Further metabolomics and flavoromics confirmed that Zn2+ supplementation at 2 mg/L was an effective strategy for enhancing the quality of greengage plum wine. On this basis, the effects of IZH4 deletion on the growth behavior and membrane composition of the WT strain under Zn2+ supplementation were evaluated. The experimental findings revealed that the knockout strain WT-ΔIzh4 showed reduced growth at pH 2.5. Regarding the cellular lipid composition, WT-ΔIzh4 exhibited notably reduced levels of unsaturated fatty acids and ergosterol compared to WT, which resulted in less fluid membranes. Zn2+ supplementation increased the growth rate by 20.1 % and elevated ergosterol content by 15.2 % compared with WT-ΔIzh4, and further adjusted the ratio of unsaturated to saturated fatty acids to a level comparable to that of the WT strain. These results demonstrated the significant role of IZH4 mediated under Zn2+ supplementation in the low pH tolerance of yeast by remodeling the cell membrane structure. This investigation offers fresh insights into the biological function of IZH4 against low pH stress, and allows a better-defined target for subsequent genetic improvement of yeast.

Introduction

Saccharomyces cerevisiae (S. cerevisiae) is a widely ubiquitous, single-celled fungus capable of adapting to diverse environmental conditions, including the acidic environments encountered in the biotechnology industry for ensuring the microbiological stability of food and beverages (He et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023). During fermentation, S. cerevisiae is challenged by multiple stresses such as elevated temperature, nutrient limitation, ethanol accumulation, osmotic shock, and low pH (Jin, Yang, Coldea, Andersen, & Zhao, 2022), which can compromise cell structure and function. Acidic fruit wines, such as those produced from greengage plum, lemon or hawthorn, are characterized by high organic acid content, with citric acid comprising 70.8–83.4 % of total acids, resulting in a markedly low pH (Tian, Sun, Wu, Xiao, & Lu, 2021). Among the various stresses, low pH represents one of the most detrimental factors limiting fermentation performance.

Our previous work identified an adapted S. cerevisiae strain, ET008-c54, which exhibits superior fermentation capacity under acidic conditions, making it a promising candidate for acidic fruit wine production (Tian et al., 2020a). Genomic resequencing and transcriptomic analyses revealed that IZH4, a gene implicated in zinc homeostasis and membrane lipid biosynthesis, plays a key role in its acid tolerance (Guo, Khoomrung, Nielsen, & Olsson, 2018; Tian et al., 2020b). Zinc is an essential trace element involved in numerous metabolic processes and serves as a cofactor for proteins including enzymes, signal transduction mediators, and transcription factors (Zbieralski et al., 2024). In yeast, zinc homeostasis is regulated by the transcription factor Zap1p, whose activity is suppressed by overexpression of Izh proteins (Mattiazzi Ušaj et al., 2015). Moderate Zn2+ supplementation has been reported to promote yeast growth, accelerate fermentation, and improve flavor quality (Ribeiro-Filho, Linforth, Powell, & Fisk, 2021; Roca-Mesa, Delgado-Yuste, Mas, Torija, & Beltran, 2022). However, the specific relationship among Zn2+, IZH4, and low pH tolerance remains largely unexplored.

The cell membrane provides a constant intracellular environment for growth and metabolism (Wu et al., 2023). S. cerevisiae has developed various mechanisms to detect and adapt to environmental changes, thereby preserving membrane integrity and metabolic functions (Mbuyane, Bauer, & Divol, 2021). For instance, an increased level of saturated fatty acids (SFA) promotes cell membrane stability to harsh conditions through stronger van der Waals interactions, while cis-unsaturation affects the membrane fluidity. Similarly, the cell membrane structure may suffer significant remodeling in response to ethanol stress, altering in the ratio of unsaturated fatty acids (UFA) to saturated fatty acids (SFA) and branched-chain fatty acids to straight-chain fatty acids (Salas-Navarrete, Rosas-Santiago, Suárez-Rodríguez, Martínez, & Caspeta, 2023). Numerous studies have reported that these lipid molecules, which are the most pivotal components of the plasma membrane, not only serve a structural function in the yeast cell membrane but also contribute to acid stress tolerance (Mbuyane et al., 2021; Pereira et al., 2019). Remodeling of membrane lipid composition is a common adaptive response to acid stress, yet the role of Zn2+- supplementation mediated IZH4 regulation in this process has not been elucidated.

In this study, we first determined the optimal Zn2+ supplementation level for greengage plum wine fermentation based on fermentation kinetics and sensory evaluation. Using metabolomics and flavoromics, we examined the biochemical impact of Zn2+ addition on fermentation performance. We then constructed an IZH4 deletion mutant (WT-ΔIzh4) in the ET008-c54 background using CRISPR-Cas9 to evaluate the effects of IZH4 loss under Zn2+ supplementation on growth behavior and membrane composition at low pH. This work provides new insights into the Zn2+ supplementation-mediated function of IZH4 in acid tolerance, offering potential targets for future yeast strain improvement.

Materials and methods

Yeast strains, plasmids, and media

Details of the primary strains, plasmids, and primers employed in this investigation are provided in Supplemental Table S1 and S2. S. cerevisiae ET008-c54, an evolved strain suitable for the fermentation of acidic fruit wines that grows well at pH 2.5, was obtained from the parental strain ET008 by developing a high-efficiency screening strategy (Tian et al., 2020b). S. cerevisiae ET008-c54 (accession number CGMCC 9865, WT) and ET008 (accession number CGMCC 9478, ST) were deposited in the China General Microbiological Culture Collection. Among them, ET008-c54 was used as the transformation receptor and the control for a single-gene knockout mutant. The original plasmid CRISPR-Vector was provided by Genewiz (Suzhou, China) and its derived plasmids were used for gene deletion and complementation. Unless otherwise mentioned, strains were cultured in YPD medium adjusted to pH 3.0 or 2.5 with citric acid at 30 °C. Yeast transformants were selected on YPD agar plates containing the antibiotic G418 (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) at a final concentration of 0.8 μg/mL. The fermentation broth was then prepared by inoculating 50 mL of fermentation medium (adjusting the total sugar content of greengage plum juice to 200 g/L and the pH to 2.7) with 5 % v/v yeast pre-culture and incubated at 22 °C.

Fermentation of greengage plum wine with different level of Zn2+

This study selected greengage plum wine as a model to verify the function of the strain WT. The fermentation experiment of greengage plum wine by the WT was conducted in a 5-L fermenter at 25 °C. Different levels of Zn2+ (0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 10.0, 50.0, 100.0, and 200.0 mg/L) were added to greengage plum wine fermentation, respectively. The pH was sustained at 2.7, while the fermentation progress was tracked in the fermenter through measurements of cell density and overall sugar levels. The optimal level of Zn2+ was determined based on the fermentation time and sensory evaluation (Tian et al., 2018). At the optimal level, the activities of five intermediary enzymes involved in carbohydrate metabolism from WT cells, pyruvate kinase (PK), hexokinase (HK), 6-phosphofructokinase (PFK), pyruvate decarboxylase (PDC), and alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH), were determined during fermentation per the protocols of Nanjing Jiancheng kits (Nanjing, China). Glucose content was determined by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) to monitor the fermentation process (Tian et al., 2020b). The fermented specimens were subsequently employed for yeast metabolite evaluation.

Untargeted metabolomics analysis

The metabolites in two samples, including those without Zn2+ and those with 2 mg/L Zn2+, underwent extraction and examination utilizing metabolomic techniques on a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X platform fitted with an ACQUITY HSS T3 column (100 mm × 2.1 mm × 1.8 μm, Waters, Milford, USA) at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The mobile phase components included 0.1 % formic acid in water:acetonitrile (95,5, v/v) (solvent A) and 0.1 % formic acid in acetonitrile:isopropanol:water (47.5,47.5,5, v/v/v) (solvent B). The flow velocity was maintained at 0.4 mL/min with the column temperature maintained at 40 °C.

Mass spectrometric measurements were procured utilizing a Thermo UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X Mass Spectrometer incorporating an electrospray ionization (ESI) source functioning in both positive and negative modes. The operational parameters were established as follows: source temperature 425 °C; sheath gas flow rate 50 arb; aux gas flow rate 13 arb. The ion-spray voltage floating (ISVF) was adjusted to −3500 V for negative mode and 3500 V for positive mode, respectively. The normalized collision energy followed a cycling sequence of 20–40-60 V. Full scan data (70–1050 m/z) was recorded at a resolution of 6000. Data collection proceeded under data-dependent acquisition (DDA) mode. Identification of metabolites and comparative analysis between the Zn2+-treated and control groups was conducted via the Majorbio Cloud Platform (https://cloud.majorbio.com).

The characteristic compounds were determined through searches across multiple metabolomics repositories, encompassing METLIN, HMDB, KEGG, while incorporating MS/MS fragmentation information. The resulting information matrix underwent multivariate statistical evaluation, encompassing principal component analysis (PCA), hierarchical cluster analysis (HCA), and metabolic pathway examination. Differential metabolites were initially selected using Variable Importance in Projection (VIP) ≥ 1.0, Fold Change (FC) ≥ 2 or ≤ 0.5, and p < 0.05. To control for false discoveries due to multiple testing, Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied, and q-values were calculated. Only metabolites meeting VIP ≥ 1.0, FC ≥ 2 or ≤ 0.5, and q < 0.05 were considered significant and reported. Peak areas were used for semi-quantitative analysis, and log₂FC was employed to display variations. The raw untargeted metabolomics data, processed peak tables, and annotation results have been deposited in the Metabolomics Workbench website and are publicly accessible at https://www.metabolomicsworkbench.org. Full export files and statistical results have been uploaded as Supplemental Table S3-S5 to facilitate reproducibility.

Flavoromics analysis

The flavor compounds in the specimens were examined utilizing headspace solid-phase microextraction (HS-SPME) methodology. A 50/30 μm divinylbenzene/ carboxen/polydimethylsiloxane (DVB/CAR/PDMS) fiber (Supelco, Bellefonte, PA, USA) served as the extraction medium. The flavor constituents were subsequently examined through GC–MS). The analysis was conducted on an Agilent Technologies GC 2010 mass selective detector fitted with a Carbowax capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm film thickness, Supelco, Bellefonte, PA, USA). The flavor compounds were characterized by comparing their mass spectral patterns with published references and the NIST23 MS library database. Retention indices (RI) were calculated relative to a C₇-C₄₀ n-alkane standard mixture under the same chromatographic conditions. Semi-quantitative measurements utilized 2-octanol as an internal reference. More detailed analytical protocols for volatile constituents are detailed in our earlier work (Tian et al., 2021).

Sensory analysis

The sensory attributes of the fermented greengage plum wine, including appearance, aroma, taste, and typicality, were evaluated by a panel of 20 trained judges. The evaluation followed a randomized complete block design, with sample presentation order randomized across panelists to minimize potential order effects. Panelists were blinded to sample identity to reduce bias. Water was provided for mouth rinsing between samples to prevent carryover of aftertaste. The evaluation was conducted using the 100-point scale method, in accordance with ISO standards. The panelists assigned scores for appearance (0−20), aroma (0−30), taste (0–40), and typicality (0−10).

Construction of a knockout strain WT-ΔIzh4 and a complemented strain WT-pIzh4

In this study, the CRISPR-Cas9 protocols were employed to knock out the IZH4 gene of the WT. The extracted DNA was amplified by PCR, and the agarose gel electrophoresis of the amplified product is shown in Supplemental Fig. S1A. The target gene (368 bp) was sequenced by Genewiz (Suzhou, China). The gRNA sequence used for Cas9 targeting was 5’-GGACTACGTTGTCGACAGTTGGG-3′, selected using the CHOPCHOP online software (http://chopchop.cbu.uib.no), which also predicted potential off-target sites. No off-target sites with ≤2 mismatches were identified in the S. cerevisiae genome, and all candidate off-target loci with ≥3 mismatches were further confirmed by PCR and Sanger sequencing to exclude unintended mutations. The homologous repair template of the target gene IZH4 and sgRNA were PCR-amplified and ligated into a CRISPR-k-vector, which was confirmed by sequencing. Yeast transformations were performed by using the lithium acetate method (Wei et al., 2024), and were selected on YPD + G418 plates. The results of the transformant screening are shown in Supplemental Fig. S1B. Positive colonies were confirmed by PCR and Sanger sequencing (Genewiz, Suzhou, China), and were routinely cultured on YPD + G418 plates for 2–3 d at 30 °C (Supplemental Fig. S1C). Replica plating was performed to obtain engineered strains without the CRISPR-k-vector plasmid. After electrophoresis and Sanger sequencing verification, a plasmid-free knockout strain (WT-ΔIzh4) was obtained. For complementation, the CRISPR-p-vector carrying the IZH4 gene was confirmed by double enzyme digestion and Sanger sequencing, and subsequently transformed into WT-ΔIzh4 to obtain the complemented strain (WT-pIzh4). To ensure experimental consistency, WT, WT-ΔIzh4, and WT-pIzh4 strains were cultured under identical conditions in YPD medium (pH 2.5) at 30 °C, with the same inoculum density (OD600 = 0.1) and shaking speed (180 rpm). For Zn-supplementation experiments, identical culture conditions were applied, with Zn2+ added at the indicated concentrations; the only variable was Zn2+ supplementation. These measures minimized confounding effects and allowed direct comparison of growth phenotypes among strains. Meanwhile, the engineered strains WT-ΔIzh4 and WT-pIzh4 have been deposited in our institutional strain repository under the identifiers LabID-YSC WT-ΔIzh4–2025 and LabID-YSC pIzh4–2025, respectively.

Assessment of cell viability

Yeast cells were cultured at pH 3.0 and 2.5 to mid-log phase and collected (107–108 cfu/mL), respectively. An aliquot of 50 μL of medium was added into 1 mL of PBS buffer and subjected to centrifugation at 10,000 ×g for 5 min. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellets were resuspended in 1 mL of PBS buffer. Then, 1 μL of FUN-1 cell stain and 5 μL of Calcofluor White M2R were introduced, with the mixture being kept in darkness at 30 °C for 30 min. Next, the stained yeast suspension (5 μL) was dropped on a glass slide and sealed with a cover glass. Stained cells were visualized with a fluorescence microscope (Olympus optical, Tokyo, Japan).

Analysis of cell morphology using transmission electron microscopy

Samples were fixed overnight in PBS buffer (0.1 mol/L, pH 7.2) supplemented with 5 % glutaraldehyde and 2 % formaldehyde. Next, it was post-fixed with 2 % osmium tetroxide for 6 h and rinsed three times with PBS buffer (0.1 mol/L, pH 7.2). The preserved specimens underwent sequential dehydration utilizing ascending concentrations ethanol (30 %, 40 %, 50 %, 60 %, 80 %, 90 %, and 100 %) for 30 min at each step, and finally exposed to acetone for 20 min. Following treatments, a mixture of epoxy resin embedding agent and acetone (v:v = 1:1) was placed for 1 h, and samples were sliced into 80-nm sections using an E14 ultramicrotome (Reichert, New York, USA). These specimens were initially treated with uranyl acetate and then lead citrate before being photographed with a H-7650 transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan). For quantitative TEM analysis, a minimum of 100 cells per condition were analyzed from at least three independent biological replicates. Cell wall thickness was measured at four equidistant points per cell using ImageJ software, and the mean value was calculated.

Lipid metabolism analysis

Analysis of fatty acids

Extraction and preparation of cell membrane fatty acids were conducted by following the method with slight modification (Liu et al., 2022). Briefly, the cells at pH 3.0 and 2.5 were washed twice with ultrapure water and centrifuged (10,000 ×g, 1 min) to remove the supernatant. The pellet was resuspended in 1.0 mL of methanol solution (3 mol/L HCl and 100 μL of chloroform), then incubated at 70 °C for 3 h. Subsequently, the mixture was immediately cooled to 22 °C after treatment and vortexed for 15 s. Two milliliters of n-hexane were introduced and further vortexed for 15 s, then centrifuged (8000 ×g, 1 min) to collect the organic phase for GC–MS (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA). Sample examination was conducted per the previously established protocol (Liu, Ivanova-Petropulos, Duan, & Yan, 2021). The outcomes were denoted as relative percentages of individual fatty acid and the ratio of UFA to SFA was calculated. The content of fatty acids is expressed as relative quantification.

Extraction and measurement of sterols

The individual sterol composition was measured according to a modified method (Vella, Calandrelli, Del Barone, Guida, & Laratta, 2023). Exponentially growing cells at pH 3.0 and 2.5 were procured through centrifugation (10,000 ×g, 1 min). The pellet was added to 1 mL of NaOH solution (2 mol/L), was then heat-treated in a boiling water bath for 20 min. Following ice water cooling for 3 min, the solution underwent centrifugation (12,000 ×g, 5 min) at 4 °C, and 300 μL of a methanol solution containing 1.5 mol/L NaOH were added to the pellet, then saponified at 60 °C for 4 h. An aliquot of 300 μL of n-hexane was introduced and vortexed for 10 min, then centrifuged (12,000 ×g, 2 min) to collect the organic phase. Similarly, 300 μL of n-hexane was introduced to the aqueous phase and further vortexed for 10 min, succeeded by centrifuging (12,000 ×g, 2 min) to collect the organic phase. Then, the collected organic phase was vacuum freeze-dried, 100 μL of MSTFA (N-methyl-N-trimethylsilyl trifluoroacetamide) was carefully introduced, and the mixture was silicified at 37 °C for 2 h. The mixture was subsequently passed through a syringe filter before proceeding with GC–MS examination. The GC–MS measurement was conducted utilizing a Thermo Fisher Trace 1310 chromatographic instrument containing a DB-5 column (30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 μm, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA). The carrier gas employed was helium at a constant flow of 0.9 mL/min. Both the injection port and detection unit were maintained at 270 °C. A cholesterol solution served as the internal reference to determine the mass fragmentation pattern of individual sterols. Sterol contents are reported as relative quantification.

Cell membrane fluidity

As a probe, pyrene was used to measure the membrane fluidity of yeast cells in log-phase at pH 3.0 and 2.5. In brief, all strains were re-suspended in PBS buffer (0.1 mmol/L, pH 6.8) and added 2 μL of pyrene (1 mmol/L), followed by incubation in the dark at 27 °C for 10 min. The collected cells were washed and re-suspended again in PBS buffer (0.1 mmol/L, pH 6.8). The fluorescence anisotropy was monitored in a fluorescence spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, MA, USA) at an excitation wavelength (EXW) of 360 nm and emission wavelength (EMW) of 450 nm (5 nm slits, respectively), and its value was computed employing the formula described elsewhere (Hu et al., 2019). In this context, lower anisotropy indicates higher rotational mobility, corresponding to increased membrane fluidity (less order), whereas higher anisotropy indicates more ordered membranes (lower fluidity).

Statistical analysis

All experiments were executed in triplicate. These data were subjected to a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) at a significant level of p < 0.05 using the GraphPad Prism version 10.0.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA) and the online ChiPlot software (https://www.chiplot.online). Additionally, PCA and HCA were carried out through free web-based MetaboAnalyst version 6.0.

Results and discussion

Although S. cerevisiae is the most outstanding wine yeast owing to its fermentation ability, the current demand from consumers and the industry for wines with improved properties is driving a strong interest in exploring alternative non-conventional yeasts with distinctive attributes for industrial fermentations. In such environments, yeasts are subject to various stresses, encompassing low pH caused by citric acid. For example, S. cerevisiae, which serves as an inoculum in biotechnology and food manufacturing as active dry yeast, may experience pH challenges throughout fermentation, potentially compromising its operational efficiency (Mas & Portillo, 2022; Vázquez et al., 2019). Low pH is a potential target for the improvement of wine yeasts. Little is currently known about the relationship between low pH resistance, Zn2+, and IZH4. In this study, we evaluated the addition of Zn2+ and analyzed the metabolites of yeast at the optimal level of Zn2+ for greengage plum wine. Then the IZH4 gene was knocked out in a laboratory strain of S. cerevisiae to further verify the specific role of IZH4 mediated under Zn2+ supplementation in low pH tolerance.

Effect of Zn2+ supplementation on fermentation performance in S. cerevisiae ET008-c54

In this study, Zn2+ was added at different concentrations to test its potential to improve the metabolism of S. cerevisiae ET008-c54. After adding a series of concentrations of Zn2+ (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, and 3 mg/L), the fermentation time was shortened and the sensory quality of greengage plum wine was improved (Fig. 1A). Interestingly, at the optimal level of 2 mg/L Zn2+ supplementation, the fermentation time of greengage plum wine was reduced by 16.7 % (from 12 d to 10 d), and the scores of each attribute in the sensory evaluation were markedly higher than those of other samples (Fig. 1B). The detailed effect of exogenous Zn2+ on the growth rate of ET008-c54 during the greengage plum wine fermentation is shown in Fig. 1C. This was likely due to the appropriate Zn2+ supplementation that promoted the growth and metabolism of yeast ET008-c54. For comparison, another study showed that exogenous low concentrations of Zn2+ served a pivotal regulatory function in yeast intracellular energy metabolism and cell membrane stability (Antunes & Sá-Correia, 2024). On the other hand, when the fermentation medium contained increased amounts of Zn2+ (50 mg/L), the fermentation time was gradually prolonged resulting in lower overall quality scores. The addition of Zn2+ at 200 mg/L extended the fermentation time by up to 20 d, which indicated that high concentrations of Zn2+ had a toxic inhibitory effect on WT and even led to fermentation failure. Furthermore, supplementation with 2 mg/L Zn2+ markedly enhanced the glucose consumption rate during greengage plum wine fermentation (Fig. 1D). The changes of key enzyme activities involved in this process reflect the importance of Zn2+ to the growth and metabolism of yeast to some extent. Among them, PK, HK, and PFK serve as crucial glycolytic pathway enzymes, with their catalyzed reactions being non-reversible (Huang et al., 2024a). The enzymatic activity variations of PK, HK, and PFK in the glycolysis pathway exhibited comparable transformation patterns during fermentation (Fig. 1E-1G). Initial fermentation stages displayed notable activity increases, attaining peak values of 150.23 U/g, 7.59 U/mg, and 60.45 U/mg respectively around 6 d. Subsequently, enzymatic activities progressively declined as fermentation continued. Notably, PK, HK, and PFK activities substantially increased due to rapid glucose utilization during fermentation. Comparable observations were documented for these essential enzyme activities in simulated juice system fermentation (Yang, Li, Zhang, & Peng, 2019). ADH and PDC function as essential ethanol fermentation pathway enzymes, with their activity variations illustrated in Fig. 1H-1I. ADH enzyme activity peaked at 17.35 U/mg by 4 d. These observations conclusively demonstrate that Zn2+ bioavailability enhances yeast fermentation performance. Additionally, PDC enzyme activity increased steadily from 0 to 3 d, declined between 3 and 5 d, subsequently rose to 7.63 U/mg at 7 d before rapidly decreasing. PDC activity variations aligned with pyruvic acid content patterns, both reaching maximum levels at 7 d.

Effect of 2 mg/L Zn2+ supplementation on non-volatile metabolites in S. cerevisiae ET008-c54

The effects of Zn2+ on the quality of greengage plum wine was analyzed using a widely targeted metabolomics approach. As depicted in Fig. 2A and Supplemental Table S3, researchers identified 1123 non-volatile metabolites, categorized into 9 groups: including 223 amino acids with derivatives (20.0 %), 78 carbohydrates (6.9 %), 190 lipids with derivatives (17.0 %), 157 alkaloids (14.1 %), 56 nucleotides with derivatives (5.0 %), 144 organic acids with derivatives (12.9 %), 213 phenolic compounds (19.1 %), 34 terpenoids (3.0 %), and 22 other compounds (2.0 %). Unsupervised PCA evaluation revealed notable distinctions between the Zn2+ supplementation and control specimens (Fig. 2B). Data presented in Fig. 2C-2D and Supplemental Table S4 revealed 129 metabolites exhibiting significant abundance variations, comprising 58 up-regulated and 60 down-regulated compounds, encompassing 14 organic acids with derivatives, 13 nucleotides with derivatives, 22 lipids with derivatives, 21 amino acids with derivatives, 18 alkaloids, 17 phenolics, 6 carbohydrates, 4 terpenoids, and 9 other types of substances. The classification of important metabolites in greengage plum wine highlights the diversity and distribution of key compounds influencing its sensory and chemical properties (Fig. 3). The following discussion focused on the classification of organic acids, phenolics, and amino acids in greengage plum wine. Organic acids are the main reason for the low pH of greengage plum wine. Among the differential compounds, as depict in Fig. 3A, the contents of 3-ureidopropionic acid, 6-aminocaproic acid, and 6-hydroxyhexanoic acid were decreased, and 11 organic acids, encompassing citric acid, tropic acid, and succinic acid, evaluated markedly after fermentation. This can be attributed to the TCA pathway, which during fermentation leads to the formation of organic acids (Mendes Ferreira & Mendes-Faia, 2020). For example, the content of malic acid derivatives increased, such as 2-propylmalic acid, 3-Isopropylmalic acid, and 2-Isopropylmalic acid). As reported, organic acids can not only impart a sour taste and confer a full-bodied and refreshing sensation to the wine, but also inhibit bitterness (Yin et al., 2019). Also, organic acids in fermented fruit wine can not only improve the flavor and texture of the product but also inhibit the growth of microorganisms (Meng et al., 2022).Fig. 3Hierarchical clustering analysis for the markedly differential regulated non-volatile metabolites under 2 mg/L Zn2+ supplementation of WT strain.Fig. 3

Phenolic substances are widely recognized as essential elements contributing to wine's organoleptic characteristics and pigmentation (Zhang et al., 2021). As depict in Fig. 3D, the introduction of Zn2+ resulted in notable decreases in 4 phenolic acids: 2,3-dihydroxybenzoic acid, 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid, 1,3,5-benzenetriol phloroglucinol, and ethyl salicylate. This reduction in phenolic acids might stem from yeast metabolic processes. The incorporation of 2 mg/L Zn2+ led to substantial increases in fourteen phenols, including rutin, hyperin, and ferulic acid, presumably because yeast activity decomposed phenolic complexes within greengage plum wine during fermentation, liberating these compounds. It is worth mentioning that rutin and hyperin serve as fundamental components responsible for the fruit wine's therapeutic properties (Yang, Su, Hua, Wang, & Wang, 2021).

Conventional yeast strains are not able to assimilate and utilize proteins. Instead, it can only utilize smaller molecular protein hydrolysates such as peptones, peptides, and amine nitrogen. Among these, amino acids are the most easily assimilated nitrogen sources. The observed elevation in amino acid levels can be linked to proteins and peptide breakdown during the fermentation process, while reductions may result from yeast nitrogen metabolism. As depicted in Fig. 3F, the quantities of 12 amino acids and related compounds, encompassing L-methionine, L-proline, and L-glutamic acid, exhibited substantial increases post-fermentation, whereas the content of 9 amino acids with derivatives, including L-tryptophan, 5-methyl-1-propyl-L-glutamate, and L-leucine, displayed marked decreases. Notably, L-leucine contributes to bitter taste profiles, thus its reduction following Zn2+ supplementation minimized the bitter notes in greengage plum wine. Research indicates that L-proline and L-glutamic acid markedly affect wine sensory characteristics (Huang et al., 2024b). Enhanced levels of L-proline and L-glutamic acid contents in greengage plum wine amplified its fruit-forward profile while minimizing astringent and bitter elements. Therefore, the alterations in non-volatile metabolites following Zn2+ addition serve a vital function in improving greengage plum wine characteristics.

Effect of 2 mg/L Zn2+ supplementation on volatile metabolites in S. cerevisiae ET008-c54

To further reveal the effect of 2 mg/L Zn2+ supplementation on the volatile compounds of greengage plum wine, a flavoromics analysis based on SPME-GC–MS/MS methods was conducted. A total of 543 volatiles were identified in the greengage plum wine samples, including 20 acids (3.7 %), 40 alcohols (7.5 %), 38 aldehydes (7.1 %), 11 amines (2.0 %), 122 esters (22.7 %), 88 heterocyclic compounds (16.4 %), 68 hydrocarbons (12.6 %), 43 ketones (8.0 %), 13 phenols (2.4 %), 7 sulfur compounds (1.3 %), 80 terpenes (13.8 %), and 13 other metabolites (2.4 %) (Fig. 4A and Supplemental Table S5). In addition, PCA analysis confirmed the significant differences between Zn2+ samples and control samples (Fig. 4B), and the differentially regulated metabolites with a combination of VIP values ≥1, FC ≥ 2 or ≤ 0.5 and p < 0.05 were considered markedly differentially expressed (Fig. 4C). A total of 132 metabolites with markedly different abundance were screened, of which 60 metabolites were up-regulated and 66 metabolites were down-regulated (Fig. 4D and Supplemental Table S6). Esters are usually derived from enzymatic or esterification reactions of free fatty acids and fatty alcohols (Zhao et al., 2024). As shown in Fig. 5A, the total ester content in greengage plum wine was 11.2 % after adding 2 mg/L Zn2+. An increase in the ester content improves the aroma and masks the undesirable odor of fatty acid esters produced by the hydrolysis and esterification of fatty acids (Tian et al., 2021). Fourteen esters were markedly down-regulated (FC ≤ 2), and 11 esters were markedly up-regulated (FC ≥ 2). Compared with the characteristic flavor of reengage plum wine, most of the up-regulated substances are beneficial to the wine body, especially ethyl decanoate, diethyl succinate, ethyl dodecanoate, ethyl octanoate, and ethyl 3-methylbutanoate, which indicated fruity and sweet flavors. The esters markedly down-regulated by 2 mg/L Zn2+ added included fenchyl acetate, 4-tert-butylcyclohexyl acetate, hexanoic acid methyl ester, and cinnamic acid methyl ester. However, decreased esters compounds contents resulted in less intense sweaty/fatty odors.

As depicted in Fig. 5B, the clustering heatmap of terpenoids before and after adding 2 mg/L Zn2+ also verified this difference. Terpenoids are renowned for their potent antioxidant activity and unique aroma, and they produce a pleasing fragrance at low concentrations (Huang et al., 2024a). Only farnesal, bourbonene, cadinene, 8,14-cedranoxide, and cubebene were markedly down-regulated (FC ≤ 2) after adding 2 mg/L Zn2+, and the other 11 terpenes were up-regulated markedly (FC ≥ 2). The most up-regulated compounds were 3-carene, damascenone, linalool, and L-menthol, with an FC value of 3.316, 3.389, 2.303, and 3.263, respectively. The possible reason may be that terpenoids are transformed during fermentation because terpenoids can be converted into active aromatic compounds, which can improve flavor. Notably, linalool is a naturally occurring terpene alcohol with pleasant floral, grassy and citrus aromas, and it is considered the most important terpene substance in greengage plum wine (Tian et al., 2021).

Aldehydes mainly come from various metabolic pathways during fermentation, but excessively high concentrations of aldehydes may lead to off-flavors (Romano, Braschi, Siesto, Patrignani, & Lanciotti, 2022). One up-regulated (FC ≥ 2) aldehyde and 12 down-regulated (FC ≤ 2) aldehydes were detected. As depicted in Fig. 5E, the content of aldehydes in greengage plum wine decreased by 52 % after adding 2 mg/L Zn2+, and the reduction in aldehydes improved the aroma profile. Among them, the content of 4-hydroxy-benzaldehyde related to sweet and nutty flavors increased by 77.3 %. Markedly down-regulated aldehydes included undecanal, benzaldehyde, and 2-methyl-3-phenyl-2-propenal, with their levels decreasing by 53.8 %, 58.1 %, and 66.6 %, respectively. Importantly, benzaldehyde with typical bitter almond flavor has an important aroma effect and was identified as a key aromatic compound in greengage plum wine in our previous research (Tian et al., 2021). Therefore, 2 mg/L Zn2+ supplementation can improve the flavor of greengage plum wine.

Effect of IZH4 mediated under Zn2+ supplementation on cell membrane composition and fluidity at low pH

To further assess the effects of the IZH4 gene mediated by 2 mg/L Zn2+ supplementation on cell growth in different culture conditions, the growth curves were monitored and cell viability was tested by live fluorescence staining. At pH 3.0, the deletion of IZH4 (WT-ΔIzh4) did not produce a noticeable effect on the growth of evolved type (WT) and WT-pIzh4 (Fig. 6A). However, compared with the WT and WT-pIzh4, the growth of WT-ΔIzh4 was markedly arrested in response to low pH stress (pH 2.5) (Fig. 6B). WT-ΔIzh4 exhibited a slower growth rate than WT at pH 2.5, whereas WT cells grew at twice the growth rate of WT-ΔIzh4. Supplementation with 2 mg/L Zn2+ partially mitigated this growth inhibition in WT-ΔIzh4, resulting in a modest improvement in growth rate and biomass accumulation. Moreover, we clearly observed that cells at pH 3.0 showed no significant decrease in cell viability (Supplemental Fig. S2A). However, the cell viability of WT (95.0 %) was markedly higher than that of WT-ΔIzh4 (49.7 %) (Fig. 6C), aligning with the observed growth patterns. With 2 mg/L Zn2+ supplementation, the viability of WT-ΔIzh4 increased notably to approximately 34.4 %, suggesting a protective role of zinc against pH-induced cell death. Also, we presented a comprehensive assessment of cell membrane structure in S. cerevisiae to clarify its response to pH stress. TEM revealed pronounced pH-induced alterations in cell morphology of S. cerevisiae (Supplemental Fig. S2B). At pH 2.5, WT and WT-pIzh4 cells maintained a round, smooth, and intact morphology, with uniformly thick cell walls and continuous plasma membranes. In contrast, WT-ΔIzh4 cells displayed significantly thinner cell walls and increased membrane fragility, frequently accompanied by wall rupture and leakage of intracellular contents. Quantitative analysis confirmed that the mean cell wall thickness of WT-ΔIzh4 cells was significantly lower than that of WT and WT-pIzh4 cells (p < 0.05), and the proportion of ruptured or vacuolated cells was markedly elevated (p < 0.05) (Fig. 6D). Zn2+ supplementation partially restored cell wall thickness and reduced the percentage of ruptured cells in WT-ΔIzh4, indicating that Zn2+ plays a compensatory role in maintaining cell wall integrity under acidic conditions. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that IZH4 is crucial for preserving cell wall and membrane stability under low-pH stress, and its loss renders cells more susceptible to citric acid erosion, intracellular content leakage, and vacuolization. Fig. 6E presents the changes in cell membrane fluidity at pH 3.0 and 2.5, with or without Zn2+ supplementation (2 mg/L). At pH 3.0, the WT, WT-ΔIzh4, WT-pIzh4, and WT-ΔIzh4 + Zn2+ strains exhibited similar membrane anisotropy values, indicating comparable membrane order. When the pH decreased to 2.5, fluorescence anisotropy of the WT-ΔIzh4 strain decreased from 0.32 to 0.13, reflecting an increase in rotational mobility and thus higher membrane fluidity. In contrast, the WT strain showed a slight decrease in anisotropy from 0.33 to 0.30, indicating minor fluidity changes. For the WT-pIzh4 strain, anisotropy decreased by 20.0 % and 48.0 % relative to WT-ΔIzh4 and WT strains, respectively, suggesting markedly increased membrane fluidity in the complemented strain under low pH stress. Notably, Zn2+ supplementation further enhanced membrane fluidity in all strains, with the most pronounced effect observed in the WT-pIzh4 strain, indicating that zinc may synergistically modulate membrane dynamics, potentially via interactions with IZH4-mediated pathways. As reported elsewhere, the cell membrane of yeast represents the first defense to protect the cells from extracellular environmental stresses, and also is a dynamic structure that serves a pivotal function in maintaining cell morphology, stability, and energy metabolism (Li et al., 2023). Furthermore, fatty acids in cell membrane are crucial for the tolerance of environmental stress given its function in supporting the membrane integrity and regulating the membrane protein activity (Lairón-Peris et al., 2021). The effect of pH stress on the fatty acid composition and sterol composition of different strains is shown in Fig. 6F. Among the 11 fatty acids, five SFA (lauric acid C12:0, myristic acid C14:0, palmitic acid C16:0, stearic acid C18:0, and arachidic acid C20:0) and four UFA (palmitoleic acid C16:1, oleic acid C18:1, linoleic acid C18:2, and γ-linolenic acid C18:3) exhibited significant changes in their relative content. Before pH stress, the content of each SFA did not change markedly, except for lauric acid C12:0 and arachidic acid C20:0. But at pH 2.5, the major cell membrane fatty acids of yeast strains were palmitic acid (C16:0), oleic acid (C18:0), palmitoleic acid (C16:1), and oleic acid (C18:1), which accounted for approximately 84.0 % to 86.4 % of the total fatty acid content (Fig. 6F and Supplemental Table S7). Among them, slightly decreased amounts of C16:0 and C18:0 and increased amounts of C16:1 and C18:1 were found in WT, leading to an increase in the UFA/SFA ratio. The proportion of UFA in the WT-ΔIzh4 strain decreased at pH 2.5, thereby leading to a considerable decrease of the UFA/SFA ratio. Compared with WT and WT-pIzh4 strains, the level of UFA in the WT-ΔIzh4 strain decreased by 33.1 % and 25.1 %, respectively, indicating the pivotal role of UFA in the tolerance to low pH stress of WT. This result implied a strong correlation between the IZH4 gene and metabolite changes in response to low pH stress. However, supplementation with Zn2+ in the WT-ΔIzh4 strain partially restored UFA levels, resulting in a UFA/SFA ratio closer to that of WT, suggesting that Zn2+ can compensate for the loss of IZH4 function in fatty acid remodeling under acid stress. UFA are indispensable for the maintenance of the bilayer flexibility and an increased UFA content represents better cell membrane fluidity, which is an effective strategy to maintain cell membrane in an appropriate fluid state (Ferraz, Sauer, Sousa, & Branduardi, 2021). A similar report also confirmed that introducing double bonds during fatty acid biosynthesis and changing the ratio of fatty acids can reduce the damage to cells caused by acid stress (Wu et al., 2019). Therefore, our results confirmed that the highly up-regulated IZH4 gene had a significant impact on the composition of the cell membrane, especially a marked increase in the proportion of UFA.

It was reported that sterols served a vital function in structure-function, microdomain formation, protein-lipid interaction, and signal transduction (Sezgin, Levental, Mayor, & Eggeling, 2017). Sterols contribute to the increase of cell membrane integrity by strengthening the packing density and weakening the flexibility of the acyl chain by interacting with phospholipids, thereby affecting the resistance of cells to stress (Vázquez et al., 2019). Differences in the cellular lipid compositions at pH 3.0 and 2.5 are also shown in Fig. 6F. Squalene, zymosterol, 4-methylzymosterol, fecosterol, episterol, lanosterol, and ergosterol were found in yeast strains. As we observed, the primary sterol in all the strains was ergosterol, although the percentages varied markedly between strains (40.1 %–65.1 %) (Supplemental Table S7). Ergosterol is an essential component for maintaining the fluidity and function of the cell membrane, and therefore has also been considered to directly correlate with the tolerance of yeast to acetic acid and other environmental stresses (Wu et al., 2024). The deletion of IZH4 did not markedly affect the yeast ergosterol content in WT, WT-ΔIzh4, WT-pIzh4, and WT-ΔIzh4 + Zn2+ strains at pH 3.0. Compared to the WT strain, the ergosterol content of WT-ΔIzh4 was considerably lower than that of WT; however, the ergosterol content of WT-ΔIzh4 strain was also 2.5 % higher than that of WT-pIzh4. Three strains showed increased ergosterol contents, but only WT showed decreased squalene contents under stress (resulting in an increase of the ergosterol/squalene ratio) (Fig. 6F). In the WT-ΔIzh4 + Zn2+ strain, Zn2+ supplementation increased ergosterol levels by approximately 15.2 % compared to WT-ΔIzh4 alone, approaching WT levels and indicating a zinc-dependent restoration of sterol biosynthesis. Four strains showed increased ergosterol contents, but only WT and WT-ΔIzh4 + Zn2+ showed decreased squalene contents under stress (resulting in an increase of the ergosterol/squalene ratio) (Fig. 6F). In fact, WT showed the lowest value for this ratio at pH 3.0 and the highest at pH 2.5. It must be considered that squalene is the most critical node in the synthesis pathway of ergosterol, being a highly hydrophobic molecule lacking the amphiphilicity provided by the hydroxyl group on the C3 atom present in sterols. The growth of the WT-ΔIzh4 strain was clearly affected at 2.5 pH, and it exhibited the highest squalene content and the lowest ergosterol/squalene ratio, being both parameters positively correlated with less tolerance to stress.

Our results showed that the significant role of IZH4 in the acid tolerance of yeast by regulating the fluidity of cell membranes and the biosynthesis of UFA and ergosterol. In fact, the behavior of the plasma membrane is regulated by high levels of ergosterol, and the accumulation of ergosterol protects the cell membrane against acid stress, thereby contributing to the acid tolerance of yeast cells (Fletcher et al., 2017). Meanwhile, high ratios of UFA/SFA and low amounts of ergosterol in membranes are known to lead to high membrane fluidity (Vázquez et al., 2019). The detailed metabolic pathways of lipid remodeling involved in low pH tolerance in yeast ET008-c54 are shown in Fig. 7. IZH4 is positively regulated by excess metals via Mga2p. The Izh proteins belong to a larger family of membrane receptor proteins known as the Progestin and AdipoQ Receptors (PAQRs). Izh proteins affect zinc metabolism by regulating the membrane sterol content or by altering the cellular zinc levels (Lyons et al., 2004). IZH1 and IZH4 localize to the cytoplasmic side of the cell membrane and regulate zinc homeostasis. Therefore, higher cellular levels of ergosterol improve the viability of yeast cells under low pH stress, and the IZH4 gene serves a pivotal function in ergosterol-mediated low pH tolerance. Conclusion

Here we demonstrated that IZH4 gene mediated by Zn2+ may remodel the cell membrane structure of S. cerevisiae to enhance the low pH tolerance. The addition of 2 mg/L Zn2+ can markedly improve the fermentation performance of WT at pH 2.5, and further metabolomics and flavoromics confirmed its positive effect on enhancing the quality of acidic fruit wines. Furthermore, the knockout strain WT-ΔIzh4 for target gene IZH4 showed markedly inhibited growth at pH 2.5. More importantly, IZH4 mediated under Zn2+ supplementation serves a pivotal function in the acid tolerance of yeast by regulating the biosynthesis of UFA and ergosterol and strengthening the cell membrane fluidity. This current effort would serve as a valuable piece of information for the future studies of metabolic mechanisms and application of wine yeast.

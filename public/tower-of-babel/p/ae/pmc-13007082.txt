Comparative analysis of diagnostic efficacy of loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) and CBNAAT in children with suspected tubercular meningitis: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Shalu Jain, Rajeswar Dayal, Raj Kamal, Dharmendra Singh, Neeraj Yadav, Madhu Nayak, Abdulmabbod Khan, Shikha Gupta, Sanjeev Lawaniya, Avijit Kumar Awasthi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTuberculous meningitis (TBM) poses a diagnostic challenge in children. The available conventional diagnostic methods have low sensitivity. Cartridge-based nucleic acid amplification test (CBNAAT) requires suitable infrastructure and technical expertise. TB loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) is being increasingly used for the diagnosis of TBM. Few studies have been done in children. We compared the diagnostic efficacy of CBNAAT and LAMP in 50 cases of clinically suspected TBM. LAMP had higher sensitivity (62.50%) but lower specificity (84.62%) than CBNAAT (sensitivity 41.67% and specificity 100.00%). The two tests have moderate agreement with a Cohen’s kappa value of 0.48. LAMP is a rapid and sensitive test for resource-limited settings.Keywords: Children, Neuropathology

Introduction

Tuberculous meningitis (TBM) remains a major cause of paediatric morbidity and mortality. Early diagnosis remains a challenge due to the paucibacillary nature of childhood TB and low sensitivity of conventional diagnostic methods. This study compared the diagnostic efficacy of loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) and cartridge-based nucleic acid amplification test (CBNAAT) in children with clinically suspected TBM.

Methods

This cross-sectional study was conducted from September 2022 to March 2024 in a tertiary care hospital after ethics approval. 50 children aged 6 months–14 years with clinically suspected TBM as per National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme criteria were enrolled consecutively after informed consent.1 Detailed clinical, radiological and laboratory data were recorded. CSF samples were collected under aseptic precautions and sent for AFB microscopy, CBNAAT, LAMP and mycobacterial culture. CBNAAT was tested on the GeneXpert MTB/RIF NAAT system, which performs automated real-time PCR for the detection of M.tuberculosis and rifampicin resistance. LAMP was carried out with specific primers (F3, B3, FIP, BIP, LF and LB) using isothermal amplification at 67 °C for 40 min. As TB-LAMP requires only basic equipment, it can be implemented at peripheral laboratories in contrast to more sophisticated NAAT platforms. Results were visualised using SYBR Green dye that can be seen through naked eyes. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS V.16.0. Sensitivity, specificity and diagnostic accuracy were calculated against the composite reference standard (CRS)2 with 95% CIs. Diagnostic agreement was assessed using Cohen’s kappa interpreted as per standard categories.3 Fisher’s exact test was used where appropriate and p value<0.05 was considered significant.

Results

Among 50 children, 27 (54%) were males and 26 (52%) were aged 1–5 years. CSF AFB microscopy was positive in 2 patients (4%), mycobacterial culture in 5 (10%), CBNAAT in 10 (20%) and LAMP in 19 (38%) patients. When LAMP was compared with CBNAAT, there was a moderate agreement between the two methods, which was statistically significant (Cohen’s kappa=0.48, p<0.001).

When comparing CSF CBNAAT and CSF LAMP with CSF culture, a statistically significant correlation was observed (p<0.05), with a Cohen’s kappa value of 0.61 and 0.30 indicating substantial agreement and fair agreement respectively.

Discussion

This study demonstrates that LAMP is more sensitive than CBNAAT and culture in diagnosing TBM in children. In our study, when CBNAAT was compared against CRS, sensitivity of 41.67% and specificity of 100% were obtained with a diagnostic accuracy of 72.00%. These findings are comparable with those of Rai et al, who reported a CBNAAT sensitivity of 40%.4 When CSF LAMP was compared against CRS, the sensitivity of 62.50% and specificity of 84.62% were obtained with a diagnostic accuracy of 74.00%. Similar findings were reported in a study by Chauhan et al, where LAMP against CRS showed a sensitivity of 53% against CRS.5

While CBNAAT remains a WHO-endorsed rapid test,6 its sensitivity is limited in CSF due to low bacillary load. Culture is time-consuming and not practical for early diagnosis. The LAMP assay, being cost-effective, rapid and operable without high-end laboratory infrastructure, can be used as a promising initial test, especially in resource-limited settings. Incorporating LAMP in TBM diagnostic algorithms may improve early detection and clinical outcomes in children.

Ultrahigh energy-storage in lead-free ceramic capacitors via local structure design
Authors: Ji Zhang, Zhiqing Li, Shuhao Wang, Huajie Luo, Shujun Zhang, Yaojin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDielectric capacitors are crucial energy-storage modules in pulsed- and high-power devices. However, the simultaneous enhancement in recoverable energy density (Wrec) and efficiency (η) still remains challenge owing to the restrictive relationship between the maximum polarization (Pmax), remanent polarization (Pr) and electric breakdown strength (Eb). To address this, we propose a strategy of local polar structure design in BiFeO3-based ceramics. By incorporating NaNbO3 to create embedded, persistent polar nanoregions within a weakly polar matrix, we achieve a giant polarization difference ΔP (56.4 μC cm-2), a low Pr (3.6 μC cm-2) and a large Eb (66 kV mm-1), endowing an ultrahigh Wrec of 14.5 J cm-3 and a high η of 88%. The local structure, directly visualized via the 2D/3D atomic displacement mapping, enables a high Pmax under an applied field and a low Pr at zero field, a mechanism explicitly validated by phase-field simulation. Meanwhile, the optimized microstructure and enhanced insulating property contribute to a giant Eb. This work provides insights to overcome the paradox between multiple parameters and paves a feasible route for the cutting-edge energy-storage applications.Subject terms: Ferroelectrics and multiferroics, Ferroelectrics and multiferroics

Introduction

As one of the important functional materials, dielectric ceramics have provoked ever-increasing interest in capacitive energy-storage in recent years1–3. Unlike chemical batteries and electrochemical capacitors, the electric energy stored and released by dielectric capacitors is based on the polarization response to applied electric field (E), allowing for ultrafast charge/discharge speed, giant power density, high working voltage and excellent reliability. Hence, the dielectric capacitors hold promising applications in pulsed-power and high-power fields, such as inverters and converters in hybrid vehicles4–6. Generally, the energy-storage performance (ESP) of dielectric ceramics, characterized by recoverable energy density (Wrec) and efficiency (η), is closely associated with the polarization electric-field (P-E) curve. Specifically, a high maximum polarization (Pmax), a small remanent polarization (Pr) (or a high polarization difference ΔP = Pmax -Pr) and a large electric breakdown strength (Eb) are desirable for the high Wrec, while the low polarization hysteresis characterized by a small Pr is responsible for the high η4. Relaxor ferroelectric (RFE) ceramics are considered as a potential alternative for energy-storage, resulting from their slim P-E loop and unique polar nanoregions (PNRs). However, the prevailing Wrec < 10 J cm−3 poses challenges for their usage in advanced electronic devices requiring compact size and light weight4–6.

Numerous strategies have been implemented to improve Wrec based on the regulation of electric field durability and polarization behavior. On the one hand, the Eb of RFE ceramics not only restricts their operating electric field, but also possesses a positive correlation with Wrec6,7. Compositional optimizations are widely employed to reduce grain size, improve bandgap and insulating properties3,8. Novel processing techniques, including tape-casting and hot-press sintering, are generally adopted to enhance the density and reduce the porosity, thereby improving Eb9,10. Additionally, microstructural designs like core-shell structure, 0-3 type composite and multilayer structure, help prevent the development of local electric branch, further improving Eb11–13. On the other hand, the element selection, polymorphic PNRs, chemical framework and entropy engineering are the popular and feasible approaches to regulate the polarization behavior1,8,14–18. For example, Bi-based matrix or end-member, including BiFeO3, Bi(Mg0.5Zr0.5)O3, etc., is beneficial to retain a relatively high Pmax owing to the strong hybridization between Bi3+ 6s2 lone pair electrons and O2− 2p orbitals4,8,15. High-entropy assisted Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3-based RFE ceramics exhibited ultra-small polymorphic domains, which weakened the polarization anisotropy and reduced the switching energy barriers. This polarization configuration finally resulted in a small Pr, delayed polarization saturation while maintaining high Pmax, thereby endowing superior ESP14,17.

Despite great advancements in dielectric capacitors, the intrinsic constraint between Pmax, Pr and Eb has not yet been addressed, limiting the further enhancement of ESP. Typically, Pmax and Pr typically increase or decrease synchronously, creating a challenge for obtaining a low Pr while maintaining a high Pmax, which finally yields a trade-off between Wrec and η6. Additionally, a high Pmax generally indicates that more induced charges emerge at two surfaces of a dielectric, leading to a large electrostatic force and premature electro-mechanical breakdown (i.e., a low Eb)4,19. Therefore, it still remains a great challenge to concurrently realize high Wrec and η through breaking the interdependent limitations by Pmax, Pr and Eb.

To address this issue, we propose the local structure design to overcome the restraint between different parameters in (0.6-x)BiFeO3-0.4SrTiO3-xNaNbO3 + 0.1 wt% MnO (denoted as BF-ST-xNN-Mn) ceramics, thereby achieving high Wrec and η simultaneously. 0.6BF-0.4ST ceramic with a morphotropic phase boundary demonstrates significant potential for energy-storage capacitors, due to its high intrinsic polarization ~40 μC cm−220,21. Yet, the strong ferroelectricity and high leakage of 0.6BF-0.4ST ceramics enable lossy square P-E loops and inferior ESP (Fig. S1a). Herein, the perovskite end-member NN was incorporated to disrupt the long-range ferroelectric orders and promote the formation of PNRs while retaining highly polarized local clusters3,18, leading to a slim P-E loop with a relatively high Pmax and a significantly reduced Pr. Meanwhile, NN possess a large band gap (Eg ~ 3.4 eV), which is favorable for improving resistivity and enhance Eb. Additionally, the synthesis of phase-pure BF-based ceramics is challenging due to volatilization of Bi2O3 and the reduction of Fe3+ (Fe3+ + e− → Fe2+) during sintering, which typically leads to high dielectric loss and leakage. In this study, MnO was added as a sintering aid to mitigate these issues. By helping to maintain the charge balance, MnO suppresses the formation of oxygen vacancies and secondary phase, thereby reducing leakage current and enhancing Eb4,8. Resultantly, the restrictive relationships between Pmax, Pr and Eb were significantly mitigated, endowing an ultrahigh Wrec of 14.5 J cm−3 and a high η of 88% under 65 kV mm−1 in 0.46BF-0.4ST-0.14NN-Mn ceramics.

Results and discussion

Energy-storage properties

A series of BF-ST-xNN-Mn (x = 0–0.16) ceramics were synthesized by solid-state reaction, and their P-E loops under 10 kV mm−1 were depicted in Figs. S1, S2a. With increasing NN content, the P-E loops become gradually slimmer, accompanied by decreased Pmax and Pr from 39.7 and 30.4 μC cm−2 (x = 0) to 8.5 and 0.2 μC cm−2 (x = 0.16), owing to the enhanced relaxor behavior (Fig. S3). Accordingly, the η progressively increases from 13.2% at x = 0 to 91.3% at x = 0.16 (Fig. S2b). Figure S2c exhibits the P-E loops of BF-ST-xNN-Mn ceramics under the respective maximum applied E. Samples were prepared with a thickness of ~80 μm and an electrode area of ~0.79 mm2. This sample geometry, combined with the high relative permittivity (Fig. S3), suggests a relatively lower susceptibility to fringing-field effect and parasitic capacitance (Fig. S4)7. A high Pmax exceeding 40 μC cm−2 under applied E is maintained across compositions x = 0.05–0.16, due to the strong hybridization between Bi-O orbitals8,15. Particularly, the composition x = 0.14 achieves a giant Pmax of 60 μC cm−2, a low Pr of 3.6 μC cm−2, and the highest ΔP of 56.4 μC cm−2 under 65 kV mm−1, thereby delivering an optimal ESP with Wrec = 14.5 J cm−3 and η = 88% (Fig. S2d). These results suggest that x = 0.14 achieves a good balance between Pmax and Pr, ΔP and E, and Wrec and η. It was therefore selected as the representative composition for further study.

Figure 1a displays the E-dependent P-E loops for x = 0.14. The P-E loop always retains a slender shape across all fields, with both Pmax and Pr increasing steadily as the field rises (Fig. S5). This is attributed to the E-induced long-range ferroelectric orders, which rapidly return the initial disorganized state after removing E. Ultimately, the Wrec increases monotonically from 0.6 J cm−3 at 10 kV mm−1 to 14.5 J cm−3 at 65 kV mm−1, while the η remains largely constant (Fig. 1b). To appreciate the ESP of x = 0.14, the comparison in the key parameters, including ΔP, Eb, Wrec, and η, between BF-based bulk ceramics is presented in Figs. 1c, d, S6. A high Wrec is dominated by the collective effect of high ΔP and large Eb. However, these two parameters are typically in direct competition, fundamentally limiting the enhancement of Wrec in dielectric ceramics4,6. Remarkably, the x = 0.14 composition mitigates this trade-off, achieving a giant ΔP of 56.4 μC cm−2 simultaneously with a high Eb, resulting in a record-high Wrec of 14.5 J cm−3 among BF-based ceramics (Fig. 1c). Considering the inequality for Wrec obtained under different E, the factor of merit Wrec/E is adopted as another indicator to further evaluate the ESP. As shown in Fig. 1d, the simultaneous optimization of a high Wrec/E and a high η also remains a considerable challenge. For example, BF-BaHf0.05Ti0.95O3-Sr(Al0.5Nb0.5)O3 ceramic exhibits an ultrahigh Wrec of 12.2 J cm−3 and a high η of 90.1% under 83 kV mm−1, but its Wrec/E is only 0.1516. And BF-BaTiO3-(Na0.7Sm0.1)NbO3 ceramic achieves a Wrec of 13.1 J cm−3 under 65 kV mm−1 with a high Wrec/E of 0.20, but the η is only 78.3%22. However, x = 0.14 in this work overcomes the conventional restriction between Pmax, Pr and Eb, thereby exhibiting a high Wrec/E of 0.22 and a high η (88%) simultaneously, which demonstrates a superior comprehensive ESP among BF-based ceramic capacitors.

The E-dependent overdamped charge-discharge curves of x = 0.14 are depicted in Fig. S7, which is a direct route to obtain the discharge energy density (Wd). A sharp current peak under each E reveals the fast charge-discharge speed. Wd is determined by the time-dependent current curves according to the equation: Wd=R∫I2tdtV, where R, I, V are the load resistance (13 kΩ), current and active volume of sample4–6, respectively, as shown in Fig. 1e. Wd increases monotonically from 0.4 J cm−3 at 10 kV mm−1 to 10.3 J cm−3 at 65 kV mm−1, far beyond currently reported BF-based bulk ceramics (Fig. 1f). Notably, Wd of x = 0.14 is smaller than Wrec (14.5 J cm−3) under the same E, which is ascribed to the clamping effect of domains and the viscous force in the direct charge-discharge process4. Additionally, the discharge time t0.9 is 3.3 μs, which is normally utilized to evaluate the discharge time and extracted from the time to reach 90% of Wd, as marked in Fig. 1e.

Fig. S8a presents the cumulative cycle-dependent P-E loops of x = 0.14 ceramic measured at 25 kV mm−1. No significant variation in P-E loop can be observed with increasing cycling numbers, demonstrating excellent fatigue resistant. Accordingly, the Wrec of 3.25 ± 0.02 J cm−3 and the η of 88 ± 1% are achieved over 1–106 cycles (Fig. S8b). Figure S8c displays the unipolar P-E loops measured at various temperatures under 25 kV mm−1. As the temperature elevates from 30 to 120 °C, the Pmax and Pr slightly increase owing to the enhanced leakage loss and/or ionic conduction at elevated temperature. Consequently, Wrec slightly decreases from 3.3 to 3.1 J cm−3 while η shows a more pronounced reduction from 88.5 to 81.5% (Fig. S8d), revealing a satisfactory thermal stability.

Characterization of local structure

To probe the underlying mechanism for the outstanding ESP, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was employed to directly explore the 2D local microstructure of x = 0.14. Conventional ferroelectric materials typically exhibit lamellar domains visible in dark-field TEM imaging8,23. However, such long-range domains are absent in the x = 0.14 sample (Fig. 2a). Instead, x = 0.14 displays a slim P-E loop (Fig. S2), characteristic of relaxor behavior. This combination suggests the presence of PNRs or fine-scale nanodomains. Moreover, x = 0.14 exhibits a long-range average cubic structure (Fig. 2b, c), consistent with X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns (Fig. S9). The high-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) image recorded by atomic-resolution STEM along the [001]C is shown in Fig. 2d. The larger and brighter dots are A-site atoms while the smaller and dimmer atoms are B-site atoms, due to their different ionic radius and atomic masses11. The diffused c/a ratio ranging from 0.97 to 1.06 indicates the fluctuating local lattice distortion (Fig. 2e). The intrinsic ferroelectric polarization stems from the structural distortion. So, this fluctuating lattice distortion yields various local polarizations24,25. The atomic displacement vectors, determined by calculating the offset of A/B-site atoms from the center of the nearest four B/A-site atoms via 2D Gaussian function fitting1,24, are illustrated in Fig. 2f. The length and color of arrows represent the magnitude and direction of these vectors, respectively. The atomic displacement vectors reveal regions with polar orientations corresponding to rhombohedral (R), orthorhombic (O), tetragonal (T), and cubic (C)-like symmetries (Fig. 2g). This local symmetry frustration and the coexistence of polymorphic polar configurations are characteristic of relaxor systems with compositional disorder and are consistent with the formation of PNRs. A wide distribution of polar direction suggests that these polar clusters are connected by the monoclinic (M) symmetry, low symmetry in the transitional regions (Fig. 2h), which facilitates reducing the polarization anisotropy and polarization switching barrier, thereby resulting in a decreased polarization hysteresis, a low Pr and an enhanced η. Moreover, 1-4 nm sized polar clusters with polar magnitude up to 18 pm are embedded into the matrix (Fig. 2i), demonstrating the strong local polar clusters, which does favor to retain a high Pmax/ΔP under applied E, and thus enhance Wrec.

To elucidate the role of distinct ions in the formation of polar clusters, the atomic pair-distribution function (PDF) based on the neutron total scattering is performed, which reveals the local structure information from subnanometre to nanoscale26. Figure 3a depicts the refinement results of real-space data G(r) and reciprocal-space S(Q) data of x = 0.14 derived from the reverse Monte Carlo (RMC) algorithm using a 20 × 20 × 20 perovskite supercell (~80 × 80 × 80 Å)27,28. The perfect matching between experimental and calculated data suggests high reliability. Figure 3b exhibits the PDF G(r) pattern in the low-r range, manifesting the first coordination environment for different atomic bands. The extracted partial A/B-O PDFs further indicate a large difference in the local distribution of different atomic pairs (Fig. 3c). The obviously separated peaks of Bi-O, Sr-O and Na-O atomic pairs suggest the strong local polar displacement of Bi, Sr and Na29. The specific atomic polar displacement vector can be obtained from the deviation between the A/B-site ion and the center of the AO12 and BO6 polyhedron. The 3D polar projections of Bi3+ along [001]C are shown in Fig. 3d. The random distribution of polar vectors indicates the coexisting of R, T and O symmetry. In addition, the Bi3+ achieves the largest average polar displacement of 0.38 Å among all ions (Fig. 3e), ascribing to its unique electronic structure3,30. And the average polar displacement of Nb5+ is larger than that of Fe3+ and Ti4+, indicating the improvement of local polar displacement at the B-site. The reconstructed 3D atomic model and polar vector distributions (Fig. 3f–i) reveal significant A-site positional disorder and local chemical clustering. These features, commonly observed in multi-component perovskites, contribute to the nanoscale polar heterogeneity that underpins the relaxor behavior. The introduced Na+ ions are randomly distributed in the matrix consisted by Bi3+ and Sr2+, which tend to gather together to form nanoscale clustering. Similar phenomenon is also found in the distribution of different oxygen octahedron. Accordingly, a wide polar direction distribution, as well as several 1–4 nm polar clusters with a high polarization as high as 50 μC cm−3, is observed, consistent with STEM results. This result suggests that the engineered local structure is induced by the heterogeneous distribution of atoms with differing ionic radii, electronic configurations, and polarizabilities3,18, that collectively facilitate a high Pmax/ΔP, a low Pr, and superior ESP.

Phase-field simulation for the role of polar structure

To further elucidate the role of local polar structure, phase-field simulations were performed to visualize polarization vectors and their evolution under external E. The simulated configuration, shown in Fig. 4a, exhibits polarization vectors with diverse orientations, representing polymorphic nanodomains. Figure 4b shows polar fluctuations with spontaneous polarization ~0.3 C m−2 randomly embedded in the weak-polar matrix, closely resembling the polarization configurations of x = 0.14 composition. With increasing E, the polarization of the matrix rapidly aligns with E. In contrast, the embedded local polar fluctuations retain their initial orientation. As E is further increased, these local polar fluctuations gradually align along the E, promoting the development of enhanced long-range ferroelectric orders and a further increase in overall polarization. This behavior indicates that the existence of strong local fluctuations delays the polarization saturation and facilitates a higher Pmax under high E31, a trend consistent with the E-dependent P-E loops observed for x = 0.14 (Fig. 1a). Upon removal of the external E, the polarization vectors largely revert to their initial randomized and low magnitude state, suggesting a low energy barrier for domain switching and minimal energy dissipation during charging/discharging process, thereby leading to a low Pr. This mechanism illustrates how relaxors with pronounced local polar structure can circumvent the conventional trade-off between Pmax and Pr under an applied field, which concurrently enhances the Wrec and η.

Mechanism for electric breakdown

The large Eb also plays an indispensable role in achieving excellent ESP of x = 0.14. As shown in Fig. 5a, the distribution of actual Eb for each sample matches well with the Weibull distribution analysis1,16. The average Eb elevates from 13 kV mm−1 for x = 0 to 66 kV mm−1 for x = 0.14 (Fig. 5b), resulting from complicated factors, including but not limited to microstructure, insulating properties4. The dense microstructure with homogeneous element distribution is observed (Figs. S10, S11). The average grain size decreases significantly from micron- to submicron-scale with increasing NN content (Figs. 5b, S12). The smaller grain size corresponds to more grain boundaries, acting as barriers for the electric breakdown. To demonstrate this, the finite element simulation on the distribution of the local E, electric potential distribution, and electric tree is implemented (Figs. 5c, 5d, S13, and S14). It is well-documented that the εr of grain is much higher than that of the grain boundary32, leading to the higher E distribution in the grain boundary. Additionally, the reduced grain size enables a more uniform distribution of local E and electric potential. The starting electric breakdown stems from the weak areas with concentrated E. With increasing time duration, the electric branches gradually propagate through the grains and the grain boundary. Particularly, the grain boundary is more easily to attract the electric branches, dispersing and dissipating the externally loaded electrostatic energy. Accordingly, the propagation speed of the electric tree slows down in x = 0.14 with higher grain boundary density, leading to an enhanced Eb.

The insulating properties can be analyzed from two aspects. On the one hand, Figs. 5e, S15 depicts the impedance spectra of BF-ST-xNN-Mn ceramics, where Zʹ/Z" is the real/imaginary part of complex impedance. The composition-dependent total resistivity, extracted from the low frequency intercept of the arc on the Zʹ axis, steadily elevates from x = 0 to x = 0.14 (Fig. S16a). The activation energy Ea, calculated from the Arrhenius law (Fig. S16b), achieves a peak value at x = 0.14 (Fig. 5e), suggesting the optimized insulating performance. On the other hand, the band gap Eg represents the energy to excite electrons jumping from the top of the valence band to the bottom of the conduction band4. As plotted in Figs. 5f, S17, the improved Eg also suggests the enhanced insulating properties, leading to an improved Eb of x = 0.14.

In summary, BF-ST-xNN-Mn ceramics with an ultrahigh Wrec of 14.5 J cm−3 and a high η of 88% were simultaneously obtained via local structure design. Experimentally, the atomic displacement vectors mapping derived from STEM images and a 3D atomic model from neutron total scattering using the RMC method were performed to identify the local polar structure. Theoretically, the positive effect of local structure on the polarization behavior was justified by phase-field simulation. Moreover, the large Eb originated from the synergistic effect of multiple factors, including reduced grain size, enhanced insulating properties, and so on. Accordingly, a giant ΔP of 56.4 μC cm−2 under a giant E of 65 kV mm−1 was achieved, indicating the delayed restrictive relationship between Pmax, Pr and Eb. These results establish a viable approach to designing high-performance dielectric ceramics for the cutting-edge capacitive energy-storage.

Return of the His: a case of atrioventricular node ablation using pulsed field energy
Authors: Reshma Amin, Mattias Duytschaever, Sebastien Knecht, Jean-Benoît le Polain de Waroux
Published: 2025

Irreversible electroporation has emerged as a new technology for the ablation of atrial fibrillation (AF) with a rapidly growing body of evidence for its use in other arrhythmias, both atrial and ventricular.1 Although first generation circular or pentaspline catheters are designed to accommodate the pulmonary veins, they may deliver energy to a wider distribution of cardiac myocytes outside a focal target.2 We present a single case demonstrating the use of a pentaspline catheter (Farawave™, Boston Scientific), for ablation of the atrioventricular node (AVN). An 84-year-old woman presented with recurrent episodes of persistent AF, associated with diastolic heart failure and requiring multiple direct current cardioversions. She also had a history of left atrial appendage thrombus. She was planned for pacemaker implantation followed by a combined AF and AVN ablation. A left bundle branch area pacemaker was implanted. Three months later a combined AF and AVN ablation using pulsed field energy (PFA) was planned. At the procedure start, the pacemaker was programmed to VVI mode (base rate 30 b.p.m.). Following pulmonary vein isolation, the pentaspline catheter was withdrawn into the right atrium. Under fluoroscopic guidance, it was advanced into the right ventricle and deployed into an ‘olive’ configuration (Figure 1A). The His electrogram near the compact AVN was identified, and two applications were delivered resulting in acute AV block with a ventricular escape rhythm and a right bundle branch block morphology following the first application (Figure 1B and C). This was followed by a LBBAP paced rhythm (Figure 1D). At the procedure end, the pacemaker was programmed to DDD mode (base rate 60 b.p.m.). Atrioventricular conduction returned the following day 13 h and 36 min following AVN ablation; because the patient was in sinus rhythm, an observational strategy was decided upon. Cardiac device interrogation the day following the procedure demonstrated satisfactory parameters. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first case to describe the safety, feasibility, and acute outcome of use of the pentaspline PFA catheter for ablation of the AVN. There was no resulting damage to cardiac implantable electronic device or leads. A previous report of AVN ablation using an alternative PFA catheter has reported sustained successful AV block.3 Overall long-term safety and efficacy of PFA use for AVN ablation is uncertain, and operators must remain cautious. It is, however, technically feasible with further investigation required into the optimal energy delivery, catheter and configuration choice, number of applications, and catheter position required, for both safety and durability of AVN ablation. Fluoroscopy, or intracardiac echocardiography if available, can be used to assess tissue contact of the catheter to optimize long-term outcomes.

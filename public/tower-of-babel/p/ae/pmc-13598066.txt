Fish oil-derived fatty acids during pregnancy and risk of neurodevelopmental disorders in children at age 10 - A randomized controlled trial
Authors: Julie B. Rosenberg, Astrid Sevelsted, Jens Richardt M. Jepsen, Rebecca Vinding, Parisa Mohammadzadeh, María Hernández Lorca, David Horner, Mikkel E. Sørensen, Birgitte Fagerlund, Niels Bilenberg, Christos Pantelis, Birte Y. Glenthøj, Bo Chawes, Jakob Stokholm, Bjørn H. Ebdrup, Klaus Bønnelykke
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Fish oil-derived omega-3 fatty acids (ꙍ-3 FA), particularly docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), are critical for early brain development, but evidence of causal effects from supplementation during pregnancy is limited. In the prospective COPSAC2010 cohort of 700 mother-child pairs, we investigated whether i) maternal DHA + EPA blood concentrations in mid-pregnancy and ii) fish oil supplementation in a randomized trial in late pregnancy influence neurodevelopmental outcomes at age 10. Maternal blood DHA + EPA concentrations were measured at 24 weeks gestation, followed by randomization to 2.4 g daily fish oil (37% DHA, 55% EPA) or placebo until one-week postpartum. Psychopathology was assessed in the children at age 10 using the Kiddie Schedule for Affective Disorders and Schizophrenia -Present and Lifetime version and parental-rated questionnaires Statistical analyses included logistic and linear regression models. Higher maternal DHA + EPA concentrations were associated with reduced ADHD risk (OR 0.71, p = 0.03) and ADHD symptom load (β = -1.14, p < 0.001) in unadjusted analyses but not after confounder adjustment (OR 0.82, p = 0.24; β = -0.49, p = 0.21). Similar trends were observed for autistic traits (β = -1.96, [-1.11; -0.24] p = 0.01), but not significant after confounder adjustment. Fish oil supplementation showed no significant reduction in ADHD (OR 0.79, p = 0.37) or ASD risk (OR 0.56, p = 0.42). A secondary analysis suggested a protective effect for ADHD Predominantly Inattentive Presentation, although it did not hold significance after correction for multiple testing (OR 0.41; p = 0.03; FDRp=0.12). Thus, potential effects on inattentive ADHD symptoms should be explored in future studies.

Introduction

Omega-3 (ꙍ-3) and omega-6 (ꙍ-6) fatty acids (FA) are long-chained polyunsaturated fatty acids (LCPUFA) important for human health [1]. Since the human body is only able to synthesize very limited amounts of these FA, dietary intake is required. Notably, ꙍ-3 FA are of crucial importance for brain development, especially during the growth spurt of the brain from third trimester until 2 years of age [2]. The three main ꙍ-3 FA are docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), and α-linolenic acid (ALA) which after intake can be converted to DHA and EPA, although in very limited amounts [3]. DHA constitutes 90% of the ꙍ-3 FA in the brain and retina and is involved in the synthesis of membrane phospholipids specifically important for fluidity of cell membranes [4]. EPA exhibits anti-inflammatory properties, e.g. by producing eicosanoids and inhibiting the production of inflammatory cytokines. During pregnancy, the placenta transfers ꙍ-3 FA from the mother to the fetus, a process highly dependent on maternal stores [5] Both DHA and EPA are present in high amounts in fatty fish as well as in fish oil supplements in varying ratios. Worldwide, dietary intake of ꙍ-3 FA has decreased over the last century, mainly due to increased availability of preprocessed food containing high amounts of ꙍ-6 FA competing for the same desaturase enzymes as ꙍ-3 FA [6]. Thus, most of the world´s population show a suboptimal ꙍ-6/ꙍ-3 ratio and this can lead to an inflammatory state affecting multiple organs including the brain [7].

Genetic predisposition plays a pivotal role for neurodevelopment, but several environmental exposures are also of importance [8, 9]. In this context, ꙍ-3 FA may represent an environmental modifiable contributor to healthy neurodevelopment. Results from randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have found that ꙍ-3 FA supplementation in pregnancy may reduce the risk of preterm birth and low birth weight [10, 11]. However, the impact on long-term neurodevelopmental outcomes is equivocal [10, 12–14]. We have previously reported neurodevelopmental effects from our RCT in the COPSAC2010 cohort. Fish oil supplementation in pregnancy was associated with earlier achievement of gross motor milestones and improved cognitive development in boys at age 2½ years, together with reduced impact of emotional and behavioral problems at age 6 years [15]. Studies investigating the observational relationship between maternal ꙍ-3 FA blood concentrations in pregnancy and psychopathology in offspring are limited and heterogeneous. The largest observational study to date (N = 11,875), investigating fatty fish consumption during pregnancy and the association with offspring neurodevelopment reported that low maternal fish intake was associated with suboptimal cognitive outcomes, and both suboptimal prosocial behavior and social development in the offspring, measured at different time points ranging from 6 months to 8 years of age, and robust to confounder adjustment [16]. This is corroborated by other observational studies reporting higher fish intake in pregnancy, higher maternal blood concentrations of DHA + EPA, and higher cord plasma concentrations of DHA to be associated with lower load of ADHD- and ASD symptoms in the child [17–20]. In contrast, in the KUDOS RCT of DHA supplementation in pregnancy, maternal DHA concentrations assessed at enrolment in gestational week 14 prior to DHA supplementation did not correlate with neurodevelopmental outcomes in children at age 6 after confounder adjustment, nor did the subsequent supplementation show consistent long-term cognitive or behavioural benefits [14].

Animal studies have demonstrated that severe ꙍ-3 FA deprivation during pregnancy reduces DHA concentrations in the offspring’s neural tissue, leading to alterations in the frontal lobes and functional deficits, including behavioral disturbances [21].

In this population-based mother-child cohort with an embedded RCT of fish oil supplementation during pregnancy, the neurodevelopmental outcomes represent secondary and predefined outcomes (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00798226). This study had two aims. We aimed to study i) whether higher maternal whole blood concentrations of ꙍ-3 FA in the first part of pregnancy, before the intervention, were associated with less ADHD and ASD (diagnoses and symptoms severity) in the offspring at age 10; and ii) whether fish oil supplementation in an RCT design during the last trimester of pregnancy resulted in lower risk of these neurodevelopmental outcomes at age 10.

Materials and methods

Study design

The population-based cohort, COPSAC2010, consists of 700 mother-child dyads from rural and urban areas of Eastern Denmark, including the Capital Region. Enrollment took place in 2009-2010 in gestational week 24 (week 22-26) [22]. Exclusion criteria were women not fluent in Danish, intake of more than 600 IU of vitamin D/day, and women with any endocrine, heart, or kidney disorder. A placebo-controlled, double-blinded RCT was embedded in the cohort. From gestational week 24 until one week post-partum, the pregnant women were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either 2.4 g per day of ꙍ-3 LCPUFA (55% EPA and 37% DHA) or placebo consisting of olive oil containing 72% n–9 oleic acid and 12% n − 6 linoleic acid in identical capsules [22]. Based on capsule count, 493 of 695 (70.9%) of the women had an intervention compliance of more than 80%, with no significant differences between the treatment and control groups. Until the youngest child of the cohort turned 3 years of age, the study was double-blinded. After this timepoint the study has been single blinded with only the examiners being unaware of group assignments. A subgroup of the pregnant women (n = 623) also participated in an RCT with a nested 2 × 2 factorial design receiving either 70 µg of vitamin D per day or standard dose (10 µg) during the third trimester. The primary outcome of the RCT’s with fish oil and high dose vitamin D were development of asthma or persistent wheeze at age 5 [23, 24]. Neurodevelopmental outcomes related to the vitamin D RCT are reported elsewhere [25].

Biological measures

Maternal whole-blood concentrations of the ꙍ-3 fatty acids DHA and EPA (measured in µg/100 µL), and ꙍ-6/ꙍ-3 ratio was assessed at inclusion prior to the randomization. Since the RCT consisted of supplementation with DHA and EPA in combination, this measure (continuous variable) was a priori chosen as the main predictor of interest for the observational part of this study. Assessments of associations between psychopathology and DHA separately and ꙍ-6/ꙍ-3 ratio were performed as sub-analyses. Maternal variation in the genes encoding fatty acid desaturase (FADS), known to affect plasma concentrations of ꙍ-3 and ꙍ-6 FA blood concentrations [26], was obtained by genotyping the single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) rs1535 in mothers of European descent.

Psychopathological measures

At the 10-year follow up of the COPSAC2010 cohort, denoted the COPSYCH study, all participants were invited for a comprehensive neurodevelopmental assessment including psychopathology [27]. Psychiatric diagnoses were assigned based on the semi-structured diagnostic interview Kiddie Schedule for Affective Disorders and Schizophrenia Present and Lifetime version (K-SADS-PL) [28] performed with parent(s) and the child separately [27]. The interviews were video-recorded, and all diagnoses were assigned in accordance with International Classification of Diseases 10th Revision (ICD-10) [29] and Diagnostic and Statistical Manual for Mental Disorder, Fifth Edition (DSM-5) [30] coding systems and in consensus with a psychologist with specialty in child and adolescent psychiatry. Further, external validation conferences with a consultant and professor in child and adolescent psychiatry were performed monthly. Dimensional psychopathology, operationalized as ADHD symptom load and autism traits severity in the entire study population regardless of threshold for diagnostic criteria, was obtained by validated parental-rated questionnaires and included: ADHD-Rating-Scale (ADHD-RS) measuring ADHD symptom load [31], Social Responsiveness Scale, second edition (SRS-2) measuring autism spectrum disorder traits severity [32], and Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ), a screening tool for behavioral and emotional problems [33].

Outcome measures

Primary neurodevelopmental psychopathological endpoints were categorical, i.e., a diagnosis of ADHD or ASD.

Secondary psychopathological endpoints were dimensional, i.e., ADHD symptom load and ASD traits severity. These were assessed by the parental rated ADHD-RS (18 items) and SRS-2 (65 items), respectively, and total scores were used as outcome measures. Also, as secondary analyses, ADHD diagnosis was divided into the predominantly inattentive presentation and the combined presentation with hyperactivity and impulsivity. Accordingly, the ADHD-RS was split into items 1-9 (inattentive symptoms) and items 10-18 (hyperactive/impulsive symptoms). Further, SDQ total problem score and impact score was investigated as explorative outcomes because of our previous findings related to these outcomes [15].

Finally, in planned interaction and stratification sub-analyses, we explored potential effect modification of fish oil supplementation with regards to sex, maternal pre-intervention blood concentrations of DHA + EPA in pregnancy as well as on FADS gene status.

Covariates

Associations between maternal preintervention concentration of DHA + EPA and offspring outcomes were adjusted for the following covariates chosen a priori: maternal age, maternal pre-pregnancy BMI, maternal level of education at enrollment, parental household income at enrollment, smoking in 3rd trimester of pregnancy in addition to the fish oil vs. placebo intervention after verifying that no interactions were present. Finally, maternal polygenic risk score (PRS) of ADHD and ASD, respectively, were generated based on large genome-wide association meta-analyses [34, 35] and added as covariates in a separate step since inclusion of these covariates caused a large drop in individuals with complete data. Statistical analyses

Demographic characteristics of the cohort and the treatment/placebo arms were compared with t-test and chi square test. Associations of ꙍ-3 FA baseline concentrations and effects of the fish oil intervention were investigated using logistic (categorical outcomes) and linear (dimensional outcomes) regression models. Statistical significances were judged at p < 0.05 (two-sided test). Multiple testing correction was performed using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure. For clarity, results that were nominally significant prior to correction are presented with FDR-adjusted p-values. Associations between preintervention ꙍ-3 FA levels and neurodevelopmental outcomes were tested for interaction with sex and the fish oil RCT. Several stratified analyses were performed of the RCT: low and high preintervention concentrations of DHA + EPA based on a median split. Also, high-risk vs. low-risk genotype based on FADS genes status. In a final sensitivity analysis, stratification by sex was performed. Furthermore, post hoc power calculations were performed.

Statistical analyses were performed using R, version 4.3.0.

Ethics

The study was approved by the Local Ethics Committee (H-B-2008-093), and the Danish Data Protection Agency (2015-41-3696). All families received information about the study prior to participation and provided written informed consent before enrolment.

Results

Of the 700 children, 604 (86%) participated in the 10-year COPSYCH visit. A total of 592 mother-child pairs were eligible for analyses of the effects of fish oil supplementation with data on RCT-status and 10-year neurodevelopmental outcomes. A total of 522 mother-child pairs with data on maternal preintervention DHA-EPA status and neurodevelopmental assessments of the child at age 10 were included in the present study, 519 of which had full datasets of covariates. Including maternal PRS’s of ADHD and ASD as an extra step resulted in a drop to 451 pairs. The mean age at examination was 10.29 years, SD ± 0.36. Sixty-five children (10.8%) fulfilled diagnostic criteria for ADHD, and sixteen children (2.6%) fulfilled criteria for ASD. Eight of the children with a diagnosis of ASD (50%) had a comorbid diagnosis of ADHD. Mean concentration of whole-blood DHA + EPA was 4.9% (SD ± 1.22, range 0.83 to 10.2%) of total fatty acids for the whole cohort. There were no differences in baseline characteristics between the placebo and fish oil supplementation groups (Table 1), and notably no differences in ꙍ-3 FA preintervention concentrations. A median split based on maternal preintervention blood concentrations of DHA + EPA revealed significant differences in background characteristics of the low vs high DHA + EPA group: lower maternal pregnancy blood concentration of DHA + EPA was associated with a lower level of education, lower level of household income, and inversely associated with higher maternal pre-pregnancy BMI, and higher rates of maternal smoking in pregnancy. These characteristics were all a priori included as covariates in adjusted analyses of associations between preintervention blood concentrations of ꙍ-3 FA and neurodevelopmental outcomes (Table 1).

Observational relationship between maternal DHA + EPA concentration and neurodevelopmental outcomes

All observational associations between maternal blood concentrations of DHA + EPA in gestational week 24 and neurodevelopmental outcomes are reported as odds ratio (OR), respectively, estimates per SD increase in DHA + EPA.

In unadjusted analyses, higher maternal DHA + EPA blood concentrations in pregnancy were associated with lower risk of ADHD diagnosis in offspring: (OR 0.71 [95%CI: 0.52; 0.95], p = 0.03) as well as with lower ADHD-RS total score in the entire cohort; (β = -1.14, [95%CI: -1.88; -0.39], p < 0.001). (Tables 2 and 3). However, after covariate adjustment these associations were no longer statistically significant: risk of ADHD (OR 0.82 [95%CI: 0.58; 1.13], p = 0.24), ADHD symptom load; (β = -0.49 [95%CI: -1.27; 0.28], p = 0.21). Higher DHA + EPA blood concentration was not significantly associated with ASD diagnosis (OR 0.70 [95%CI: 0.36; 1.29], p = 0.27), but were associated with lower load of autistic traits severity (SRS-2 score) (β = -1.96 [95%CI: -3.43; -0.48], p = 0.01). Similarly, these associations were not statistically significant after covariate adjustment; ASD: (OR 0.88 [95%CI: 0.36; 1.29], p = 0.73), autistic traits severity; (β = -0.42 [95%CI: -1.94; 1.10], p = 0.59) (Tables 2 and 3). Finally, the same pattern of associations was observed for emotional and behavioral problems, with higher concentrations of DHA + EPA being associated with fewer problems in offspring in unadjusted analyses; SDQ total score: (β = -0.68 [95%CI: -1.11; -0.24], p < 0.001) and SDQ impact score: (β = -0.17 [95%CI: -0.28; -0.06], p < 0.001), but not statistically significant following covariate adjustment. (Table 3) Sensitivity analyses with adjustments for each of the environmental covariates separately indicated that maternal education level was the strongest confounder as indicated by the largest reduction in association estimate for all the neurodevelopmental outcomes (data not shown). Fish oil RCT and neurodevelopmental outcomes

The RCT showed no overall significant effect of fish oil supplementation on risk of ADHD (Table 4); (OR 0.79 [95%CI 0.47;1.33], p = 0.37) or ASD diagnosis (OR 0.56 [95%CI 0.19; 1.53], p = 0.27). However, our planned secondary analyses indicated that fish oil supplementation had a protective effect on the ADHD predominantly inattentive presentation; (OR 0.41 [95%CI: 0.17; 0.89], p = 0.03), but the finding did not remain significant after correction for multiple testing (p = 0.12). There was no statistically significant effect of the fish oil supplementation on the ADHD combined presentation; OR 1.35 [95%CI: 0.69; 2.72], p = 0.39, nor on any of the dimensional psychopathological endpoints. Sub-analyses

There was no evidence that sex, maternal FADS genotype, or blood concentrations of DHA + EPA modified the effect from the RCT on ADHD or ASD (all interaction p-values ≥ 0.05, Supplementary Table S1-S3).

Statistical analyses for categorically psychopathological outcomes were repeated with DHA separately and with ꙍ-6/ꙍ-3 ratio, respectively. Overall, the results corresponded to those reported using DHA + EPA as exposure. (Supplementary Tables S4-S5)

Post-hoc power calculation

The RCT was powered according to the primary outcome of asthma or persistent wheeze. We performed post hoc power analyses to determine which effects sizes we were able to detect. For ADHD with a prevalence of 10.8%, N = 600, and significance level <0.05, the study was powered to find ORs of 0.41 or below. Likewise for ASD, with prevalence 3%, the study was powered for ORs of 0.10 or below. Considering the dimensional analyses based on questionnaires from the entire cohort, the study had 80% power to detect an effect estimate of 0.22 with a significance level <0.05.

Discussion

To our knowledge this is the first study to investigate associations of i) maternal blood concentrations of ꙍ-3 FA and ii) effects of fish oil supplementation during pregnancy on clinician- and parental-rated measures of ADHD and ASD in a general population sample.

In this extensive population-based study, we found that the observed protective links between maternal DHA + EPA blood concentrations during pregnancy and reduced neurodevelopmental disorders in offspring at age 10 are likely influenced by confounding factors.

Similarly, we found no overall protective effect of the RCT with high dose fish oil supplementation in third trimester. In a secondary analysis we found a protective effect of fish supplementation on the predominantly inattentive presentation of ADHD whereas the supplementation was not associated with dimensional symptom load of inattentive symptoms in the entire study population.

We did not replicate our previous finding arising from the RCT in terms of reduced impact of behavioral and emotional problems at age 6 due to fish oil supplementation, with data obtained from the same parental rated SDQ scale at age 10 years.

Strengths and limitations

First, the RCT embedded in the cohort allows for an unbiased investigation of potential effects on neurodevelopmental disorders in middle childhood of fish oil supplementation in last trimester of pregnancy. Another strength of this study is the in-depth clinical evaluations performed by uniformly trained clinicians and the use of consensus diagnoses in combination with additional dimensional measures of mental health rated by parents. Lastly, the study is strengthened by the deep phenotyping of the cohort arising from the consecutive follow-ups since early life, allowing to investigate the observational associations between maternal blood concentration of ꙍ-3 FA and offspring neurodevelopment adjusting for potentially confounding factors.

Neurodevelopmental outcomes were secondary outcomes of the COPSAC2010 cohort, explaining why the main limitation of this study was the sample size, only allowing the discovery of relatively large protective effects from ꙍ-3 FA supplementation. In relation to ASD, the study is particularly underpowered to draw firm conclusions as demonstrated by the post-hoc power calculation. To this end, it is an advantage with the assessment of symptom loads in the total study population providing sufficient statistical power for lower effects on dimensional symptoms.

It remains unresolved whether an earlier timing of the fish oil supplementation in pregnancy may have resulted in other and/or stronger effects, e.g. during fetus brain development in early pregnancy.

It is a study limitation that we do not have information of parental mental health status since genetic predisposition is known to be an important risk factor for development of psychopathology in the children. However, we included maternal PRS for ADHD and ASD as covariates without alterations in the results. Also, this is not expected to influence the results from the randomized fish oil trial.

Finally, external validity should be considered. Denmark is a high-income setting with a fairly high fish-intake compared to other countries. Results may differ in populations with greater nutritional deficiencies or socioeconomic disparities. It could be speculated that the effects of the intervention might have been stronger if baseline DHA + EPA levels had been lower, and that the largely null results observed in RCT may reflect a ceiling effect.

Interpretation

Fish oil intake is known to be highly influenced by socioeconomic factors [36], which is also the case in this study. In our data, after correcting for potentially confounding factors, effect sizes of the associations between DHA + EPA concentration and neurodevelopmental outcomes are decreased and no longer statistically significant. This suggests that observed crude associations are confounded, in this study notably by maternal level of education. It is plausible that socioeconomic factors can impact ꙍ-3 FA status, since (high-quality) fatty fish and fish oil supplements are expensive dietary components as opposed to less expensive preprocessed alternatives. Our findings are in line with the neurodevelopmental outcomes reported by the KUDOS trial investigating effects of DHA supplementation (600 mg/day) administered from gestational week 14 until delivery [14]. In the KUDOS trial, limited and scattered effects of prenatal DHA supplementation were reported in children at age 6. In addition, the initial positive associations observed between maternal preintervention blood concentration of DHA and neurodevelopmental outcomes showed indication of being confounded by measures of socioeconomic status. Another trial [13] reported improved clinician-rated attention at age 5 arising from prenatal supplementation with 400 mg of DHA/day from mid-pregnancy until delivery. That study did not report observational associations since maternal preintervention blood concentrations of DHA were not assessed.

Other RCT studies, many of which are summarized in a Cochrane review from 2018 [10], investigating effects of fatty acid supplementation have mainly focused on pregnancy outcomes or cognitive outcomes at much earlier developmental timepoints of the offspring and are not completely comparable to our study [9]. The majority of the observational studies report on measures of maternal dietary intake of fish and not on biological measures such as blood or cord plasma [16, 18].

The results of our RCT show considerable estimates of protective effects of fish oil supplementation on risk of ADHD and ASD, although these were not statistically significant. The possibility of a Type 2 error should be mentioned since post hoc power calculations revealed that the study was only powered to find even larger effects sizes. Thus, a similar study with a larger population might have found significant protective effects.

Furthermore, the indication of significant protective effect specifically of the ADHD predominantly inattentive presentation, should be interpreted with caution. This was a secondary outcome, and the finding did not remain significant after correction for multiple testing.

Finally, fatty acids accumulate in the third trimester, but whether they have anti-inflammatory or other effects in the first trimester during embryonic development and during second trimester is not well known.

Neurodevelopmental disorders are complex multifactor disorders with a strong genetic component. It is therefore unlikely that a single factor, such as ꙍ-3 FA, alone will determine if a child develops a neurodevelopmental disorder. Rather we hypothesized that ꙍ-3 FA may act as one contributing factor exerting a modest beneficial effect across a larger proportion of the population. This could result in some children not crossing the threshold for a clinical diagnosis. Given the small number of children with a diagnosis, our study is better powered to capture such effects from the quantitative symptom assessment in the whole population, which is a major strength of our study.

To sum up, in this clinical cohort study with extensive confounder adjustment and a randomized intervention with ꙍ -3 FA supplementation from late second trimester in pregnancy, we did not find evidence of a causal protective effect from ꙍ -3 FA intake during pregnancy on ADHD and ASD outcomes. Previous observational associations between maternal intake of ꙍ-3 FA during pregnancy and child neurodevelopment could suggest unmeasured confounding. The randomized intervention indicated a protective effect with respect to the primarily inattentive presentation of ADHD, which warrants further investigation in future studies.

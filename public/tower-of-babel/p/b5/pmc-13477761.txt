A Case Report of Endoscopic Anterior Resection Combined with Intraoperative Computed Tomographic Navigation for Tenosynovial Giant Cell Tumor of the Upper Cervical Spine
Authors: Kanichiro Wada, Gentaro Kumagai, Kazushige Koyama, Sho Kaneko, Yasuyuki Ishibashi
Published: 2026

Tenosynovial giant cell tumor (TGCT) is a benign neoplasm1,2) located frequently around large joints of the extremities, and the spinal involvement is approximately 2.6% of all TGCTs3). Because of the rarity and anatomical complexity of the upper cervical spine, each additional case contributes valuable clinical insight. Here, we report a case of upper cervical TGCT treated using a stage surgical strategy consisting of posterior occipito-cervical fusion for pain control followed by anterior endoscopic tumor resection with intraoperative navigation, with excellent mid-term outcomes.

A 45-year-old woman with severe posterior head and neck pain with visual analogue scale of 85 mm without neurological deficit. She had an allergy to non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Neck function (NF) and quality of life (QOL) scores of Japanese Orthopaedic Association Cervical Myelopathy Evaluation Questionnaire (JOACMEQ) were 35 and 42.7 points at the initial visit. Computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance image (MRI) revealed osteolytic lesions with soft tissue tumors in the anterior and posterior arches of C1 and the body of C2. Fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (FDG-PET) demonstrated a lesion with a maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) of 4.1-15.0 (Figure 1). Blood inflammatory and tumor markers were within normal. Cervical collar immobilization and oral analgesics using tramadol/acetaminophen and pregabalin provided insufficient pain relief. Biopsies with endoscopy suggested TGCT, and Zoledronic acid was initiated. Because of persistent severe neck pain, posterior occipito-cervical fusion was performed.

Resected posterior arch samples were not diagnosed definitively, and her pain improved markedly. Zoledronic acid continued and follow-up imaging showed no tumor progression. As tumor disease, we would have planned for anterior surgery as early as possible. However, we considered that second surgery could be delayed due to her symptoms remarkably improved after the initial surgery, their circumstances and pathological diagnosis of TGCT. Eighteen months after the initial surgery, minimally invasive anterior endoscopic tumor resection via a transoral-transpharyngeal route under intraoperative CT navigation was performed with otolaryngology support. Endoscopy and CT navigation were selected considering their effectiveness in cases such as this one, where the tumor was closely located to important venous structures (Figure 1). The tumor appeared yellowish and gross total resection (GTR) was achieved without complications (Figure 2). Histology confirmed TGCT (Figure 3). More than 5 years after the surgeries, the patients remained free of recurrence (Figure 4), had no neck pain, NF and QOL scores improved to 100 and 64.6 in JOACMEQ, and cervical JOA score was 17/17 points.

MRI findings in the current case showed low-to-isointense T1 weighted image (WI), hypointense T2 WI, and uniform moderate-to-high contrast enhancement consistent with previous report2). Shen et al.2) reported the FDG-PET SUVmax values were at 9.4-15.8. Our case had a skip-lesion pattern with FDG uptake in both anterior facet joint of 15.0 and posterior arches of 4.1, and this skip pattern was unreported in the series by Shen et al.2)

In the current case, skip lesions were identified at the atlantoaxial joint and the posterior arch. It has been reported that giant cell-rich tumors including TGCT demonstrate high FDG uptake on PET4). It was suggested that this FDG uptake reflects the metabolic activity of inflammatory and phagocytic cells recruited via the so-called “landscape effect”, rather than the proliferative activity of neoplastic cells themselves5). TGCT is currently regarded as a neoplastic disease characterized by colony-stimulating factor 1 (CSF1) gene rearrangements, in which only a minority of neoplastic synovial cells overexpress CSF1. These cells recruit and promote the proliferation of CSF1 receptor-expressing macrophages and osteoclast-like giant cells at the tumor site, constituting a key pathogenic mechanism as the landscape effect6). A microenvironment favorable for the accumulation of phagocytic cells may be established by CSF1 overexpression, potentially giving rise to similar lesions at sites distant from the primary neoplastic cells within the same vertebral level7).

GTR is generally considered as the optimal treatment. Reported surgical approaches included posterior approach8), anterior approach9), and combined posterior fixation10,11). Adjuvant therapies such as denosumab and radiotherapy have been reported12). Stereotactic neurosurgery for tumor resection and endoscopic endonasal approach for biopsy with otolaryngologist support have been reported as supporting methods10). Surgical management of upper cervical spinal lesions is sometimes challenging because of the restricted exposure limitation due to proximity to neurovascular structures. Recent advances in intraoperative navigation13), and endoscopic assistance14) have improved safety and resection completeness for upper cervical pathologies, although for conditions other than TGCTs. Our two-stage strategy combined early posterior fixation for severe neck pain relief by stabilization, followed by minimally invasive anterior endoscopic gloss total resection under intraoperative CT navigation-guided, thereby achieving both functional preservation and oncologic control.

Mokrani reported a case of unresectable TGCT of the elbow, described as a giant cell tumor by them, in which Zoledronic acid was administered on a monthly basis, resulting in stable disease after 8 months of treatment15). Nepucpan described a case of unresectable nasopharyngeal TGCT treated with Zoledronic acid every 28 days, which demonstrated radiographically stable disease on CT. However, the treatment was switched to denosumab due to worsening symptoms16). Clinical course of these and our cases suggest that Zoledronic acid may have the potential to achieve disease stabilization in TGCT. The available evidence remains limited to isolated case reports, and further research is required to clarify the efficacy.

In conclusion, we report a rare case of upper cervical TGCT treated with posterior occipito-cervical fixation followed by minimally invasive anterior tumor resection with intraoperative CT navigation, resulting in continuous pain relief and no recurrence of tumor for over 5 years after surgery. This treatment strategy may be considered a viable option for upper cervical TGCTs, balancing adequate tumor resection with minimal invasiveness.

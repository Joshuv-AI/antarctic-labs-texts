A Comprehensive Compilation of Spectral Libraries for Petroleum Hydrocarbons (PHC) Encompassing VNIR-SWIR-TIR Ranges
Authors: Carlos Roberto de Souza Filho, Rebecca D. P. M. Scafutto
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Remote detection and mapping of surface materials using optical sensors relies predominantly on analyzing multispectral and hyperspectral imagery employing classification algorithms. The classification process involves comparing the spectra of individual pixels within the image to spectra from reference databases, commonly referred to as spectral libraries. Here, we introduce a comprehensive compilation of spectral libraries specifically tailored for petroleum hydrocarbons (PHC), meticulously crafted under controlled laboratory conditions. This compilation includes reference spectral libraries for various PHC forms, including crude oils, mineral substrate-PHC mixtures (comprising crude oils and fuels), oil-film on water, and oil-water emulsions. Data collection was conducted within the visible, near, and shortwave IR (VNIR-SWIR – 0.35–2.5 µm) spectra and thermal IR (TIR – 3–15 µm) range. The openly accessible spectral libraries presented herein support the scientific community and industry in characterizing field samples or spectral data from onshore and offshore sites. Furthermore, these libraries are instrumental in developing and applying classification algorithms designed for processing spectral images captured by cameras coupled to multiple platforms (e.g., tripods, drones, airborne, orbital satellites).

Background & Summary

Infrared (IR) spectroscopy is based on the differential reflection and scattering of light as a function of wavelength1. The IR spectrum is a graphical representation of molecular vibrational frequencies, being unique for each material. It reveals compositional properties related to the molecular and physical structure of compounds (e.g., functional groups), being commonly used to identifiying and performing both qualitative and quantitative analyses of pure materials and complex mixtures2,3. The IR ranges in wavelength from 1–15 µm. In the remote sensing field, the IR is usually subdivided into near and shortwave IR (NIR-SWIR: 1–2.5 µm), midwave IR (MWIR: 3–6 µm) and longwave IR (LWIR: 7–15 µm). MWIR and LWIR regions are also referred as thermal IR (TIR: 3–15 µm) by some authors4.

Remote sensing applications for the direct identification of petroleum hydrocarbons (PHC) based on characteristic C-H absorption features date from the end of the 80’s, with the work on bituminous sands carried by5. Since then, many advances related to the applicability of the IR range for the direct and indirect mapping of PHC in continental and marine enviroments using remote sensing techniques have been reached. Most of the work in the literature focus on two major branches: reflectance spectroscopy and imaging spectroscopy. The first group work with the characterization and interpretation of spectral signatures of pure PHC and PHC-mixtures with other compounds e.g.6–9. The second group focus on the development of methodologies for mapping and monitoring of PHC occurrences on the surface through the classification of multispectral e.g.10–12, and hyperspectral data e.g.4,13–15. In this context, spectral libraries are the basis for imaging classification. A spectral library consists of a comprehensive compilation of reference spectra of materials, collected under standardized laboratory or field conditions, used by classification algorithms to map the different surface materials in the image by comparing the spectra of each pixel with the spectra of a known material from the spectral library.

The aim of this work is to contribute with the scientific community and industry providing access to a comprehensive spectral database of pure PHCs and complex PHC-substrate/water mixtures, which can be used as reference for the classification of multispectral and hyperspectral images. Here, we introduce a set of eleven (11) PHC related spectral libraries, covering the VNIR-SWIR (0.35–2.5 μm) and MWIR-LWIR (3–15 μm) wavelength regions. The spectral data were acquired with high resolution spectrometers (i.e., FieldSpec/ASD, Vertex 70/Bruker, Nicolet 6700 and Nicolet Nexus 670/Thermo Fisher). The spectral libraries are separated in two groups, according to the range of data acquisition: (A) VNIR-SWIR and (B) TIR (MWIR-LWIR). The complete dataset is composed by a total of 1.017 spectra, and include spectral libraries of crude oils, mixtures of mineral substrates with PHCs (crude oils and fuels), oil-film over water and oil-water emulsions.

Materials and Methods

This dataset comprises a series of spectral libraries of crude oils, substrate-PHC mixtures, and oil film and oil emulsion over water16. The database comprises a collection of data stemmed from several research projects developed at the University of Campinas (SP-Brazil). The method of sample preparation, data collection and equipment used varies depending on the sample type and spectral range and are described in detail for each spectral library in the following sections.

Spectral library of pure crude oils

This dataset its composed by thirty-seven (37) samples of crude oils (Fig. 1) obtained from Brazilian sedimentary basins with varying API grade and SARA fractions (Table 2)6. To perform the measurements 1 ml of crude oil was placed in a Petri dish of 60 × 15 mm (i.e. oil thickness of 350 µm). The FieldSpec 4 spectrometer was used to collect the spectral measurements. Samples were measured using a High Intensity Muglite accessory from ASD. To avoid background interference, the Petri dish containing the crude oil was placed inside an ASD sapling tray adapter and a spectrally flat rubber was used to cover the top of the sample (Fig. 1). Six (6) measurements were taken for each sample, wherein the Petri dish was rotated 60° between each measurement. Spectral libraries of PHC-substrate

This dataset comprises a spectral collection derived from thirty-six (36) combinations of varied mineral substrates (Fig. 2a) with various concentrations of PHC7 Mineral substrates include samples of clayey soil rich in kaolinite, clayey soil rich in montmorillonite, dolomitic soil, sandy soil rich in quartz, latosol, and lateritic soil rich in gibbsite. PHCs include crude oils with ◦APIs of 19.2; 27.5 and 43.2 (Table 2), diesel, gasoline, and ethanol. Substrate-PHC mixtures were prepared in Petri dishes (60 ×15 mm) and measured with the FieldSpec 3 Hi-Res, according to the following protocol: 10 g of the mineral substrate was placed in the Petri dish, the PHC was added to the substrate, then the mixture was homogenized and measured three (3) times (rotating the dish 120° between each measurement). This procedure was repeated until the saturation of the mineral substrate. The amount of 0.1 ml of PHC was added to the dish until the mixture have reached 0.5 ml of contaminant. Thereafter, the PHC was added to the dish in batches of 0.5 ml until saturation of the substrate (Fig. 2b). A spectrally-flat rubber (hollow in the centre) was used to cover the edges of the dish to avoid secondary reflection of light by the glass. Samples were measured using both an ASD light source and the ASD pistol grip attached to a tripod (Fig. 2c). Spectral library of crude oils, oil film over water and oil-water emulsions

The spectral collection of this dataset includes pure oil samples measured under varied geometry; samples of oil film over seawater with varied thickness (i.e. 18 µm; 31.4 µm; 78.6 µm and 127 µm), and emulsion of oil and seawater (Fig. 3)9. Samples were measured in Petri dishes (90 ×15 mm) under different geometries, using the ASD light source and the FieldSpec foreoptics at varied angles (0°, 15°, 30° and 45°). The oil-water emulsion was prepared in Erler Mayer tubes by mixing 70% seawater with 30% oil17,18. To simulate the wave motion in the sea, the samples were partially immersed in water at 20–24 °C (representative temperatures of sea water on the coast of São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro – Brazil), in a Dubnoff agitator (Marconi) (Fig. 3b). Spectral libraries TIR (MWIR-LWIR) range

Spectral library of crude oils

This database is composed by thirty-seven (37) ATR spectra of crude oils (Table 2), measured with a Thermo Fisher Nicolet 6700 spectrometer6. ATR measurements were conducted in the 2–15 µm range averaging 150 scans, with a resolution of 1 cm−1. Ambient air was used as background spectrum, which was collected before every oil sample measurement. Each oil spectrum was normalized against the background to ensure a final spectrum without interferences. Detailed information about the equipment can be found in Pabon and Souza Filho6.

Spectral library of crude oils and oil film over water

This second library is composed of directional hemispheric reflectance (DHR) data of (i) 11 crude oil samples (°API 13.2, 14.4, 17.1, 20.0, 21.3, 21.5, 25.6, 27.4, 29.4, 36.2, 40.2, 47.2 – Table 2), not exposed to the environment; (ii) 4 redundant samples (°API 13.0, 14.0, 17.0, 20.0 – Table 2) after 7 days and 37 days of environmental exposition; (iii) 14 samples of oil films over water considering varied thicknesses. According to Lammoglia and Souza Filho9, the DHR measurements taken between 2 and 14.2 µm were conducted using an integrating sphere connected to a Nicolet Nexus 670 spectrometer/interferometer. The internal surface of the integrating sphere is coated with Infragold™, which has a consistent TIR reflectance of approximately 0.96. For these measurements, oil was spilt in aluminum containers coated with a black amorphous compound. Reflectance measurements were converted to emissivity by applying the Kirchhoff law19,20.

Spectral library of substrates-H2O/PHC

The third library include DHR data from mixtures of dry and moistened mineral substrates (quartz-rich sand, montmorillonite rich clay, dolomite) with an intermediate (◦API 27.5) crude oil. As described by Scafutto et al.4, the mixtures were prepared in laboratory according to the following standardized procedures: powder mineral substrates were dried for 24 hours in an oven at 105 ◦C. Once dried, mixtures of mineral substrates with 5%W of water, mineral substrates with 5%W of crude oil, and mineral substrate +5%W of water +5%W of crude oil, were prepared and homogenized by hand and then measured spectrally. DHR measurements were acquired in the 1.4–16 μm spectral range, with a 4 cm−1 resolution, with an integrating sphere coupled to a Bruker Vertex 70 FTIR Spectrometer21. The equipment has a MCT (Mercury Cadmium Telluride) TIR detector on the top of the sphere. A KBr window was placed between the integrating sphere and the FTIR instrument to prevent possible contamination of the instrument optics and the interferometer by PHC volatiles. Reflectance measurements were converted to emissivity by applying the Kirchhoff law19,20.

Technical Validation

Spectral libraries VNIR-SWIR range

The data of the (1) Pure Crude Oils6, (2) Substrate-PHC mixtures7, and (3) oil film and oil emulsion over water9 spectral libraries built in the VNIR-SWIR range from controlled experiment, were analyzed using multivariate statistics (Table 10). The authors used Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to evaluate the variation in the respective datasets, while Partial Least Squares Regression (PLSR) was used to compute prediction models. For validation, the spectral database was split into calibration and validation sets. The authors used cross-validation to estimate the root-mean-square error (RMSE) of calibration (RMSEC) and prediction (RMSEP) of the models. The quality of the models was evaluated based on the coefficient of determination (R²) and the ratio of prediction deviation (RPD). R² values vary between 0 and 1 and represents the square of the correlation coefficient between predicted values and measured values. The closer R² value is to 1 more robust is the model22. The predictive performance of the models was evaluated based on RPD values, according to the following criteria: unreliable if RPD < 1.4; fair if 1.4 ≤ RPD < 1.8 (can be used for measurement and correlation); good if 1.8 ≤ RPD < 2.0 (quantitative predictions are possible), very good model predictions if 2.0 ≤ RPD < 2.5 and excellent if RPD > 2.523,24.Table 10PCA and PLSR statistics yielded from Pure Crude Oils, Substrate-PHC mixtures, and oil film and oil emulsion on water spectral libraries.Spectral LibraryPCAWavelengthPLSRReferenceR²RMSECRMSEPRPDPure Crude OilsDiscrimination according to °API classification (heavy, medium, light)1000–25000.990.761.117.15Pabón and Souza Filho6Substrate-PHCDiscrimination according to °API classification (heavy, medium, light) and HC concentration in the mixture780–2500Scafutto and Souza Filho7Sand Model0.981.071.162.35Latosol Model0.941.202.662.50Lateritic Model0.951.581.492.29Kaolinite Model0.981.051.092.04Montmorillonite Model0.932.162.922.92Dolomite Model0.980.891.313.13Oil Film on WaterDiscrimination according to °API and viscosity350–25000.980.991.27—Lammoglia and Souza Filho9Emulsion On Water—350–25000.673.844.60—

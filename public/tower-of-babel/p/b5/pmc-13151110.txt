Hepatocyte growth factor blocks epithelial-mesenchymal transition and fibrosis of ureteral epithelial cells in UPJO
Authors: Xiaoqiang Lin, Yanhui Wang, Ziwei Jian, Fengbin Yang, Songbin Lin, Weicheng Huang, Yuexian Yang, Shu Chen, Jinrong Chen, Wenyou Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUreteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO) is a leading cause of congenital hydronephrosis. Its etiology is associated with dysregulated extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). Analyses included demographic and clinical characteristics, immunohistochemistry (IHC), quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR), and Western blotting (WB) to assess HGF, fibrosis markers (Col1a1, Fibronectin 1 [FN1], α-smooth muscle actin [α-SMA]), and EMT markers (E-cadherin, N-cadherin, β-catenin, Vimentin). Introduction

Ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO) represents the most prevalent cause of congenital hydronephrosis, affecting approximately 1 in 750 live births and accounting for 35% of antenatally detected urinary tract dilatation [1, 2]. While transient hydronephrosis may resolve spontaneously, persistent obstruction triggers a pathological cascade characterized by chronic mechanical stress, ischemia, inflammation, and progressive renal parenchymal damage. Critically, obstruction during nephrogenesis disrupts renal maturation and can culminate in tubular atrophy, interstitial fibrosis, and irreversible nephron pathological processes, collectively driving functional deterioration in affected children [3].

The molecular pathogenesis of UPJO was associated with aberrant extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and dysregulated epithelial-mesenchymal crosstalk [4]. Central to this process is Transforming Growth Factor-β1 (TGFβ1), a master regulator of fibrogenesis consistently implicated in obstructive nephropathy, diabetic kidney disease, and chronic kidney disease (CKD)4. Sustained TGFβ signaling orchestrates fibrotic progression by inducing renal tubular epithelial cell (RTEC) injury, promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), activating matrix-producing myofibroblasts, stimulating excessive ECM deposition, and suppressing the matrix degradation pathway [5]. Concomitant structural defects in ureteropelvic smooth muscle architecture further exacerbate obstruction and tissue remodeling.

Hepatocyte Growth Factor (HGF), a pleiotropic cytokine predominantly secreted by mesenchymal cells, exerts potent renoprotective effects by counteracting TGFβ-driven pathology [6]. Through binding its cognate receptor on epithelial cells, HGF signaling mitigates fibrosis via multifaceted mechanisms: inhibiting RTEC apoptosis and EMT, antagonizing TGFβ/Smad signaling, suppressing myofibroblast activation, promoting ECM degradation, and enhancing tubular regenerative capacity. Robust preclinical evidence demonstrates HGF’s antifibrotic efficacy in diverse organ systems, including hepatic, pulmonary, cardiac, and dermal fibrosis models [7, 8]. In the kidney, HGF overexpression attenuates fibrosis in unilateral ureteral obstruction and diabetic nephropathy models, while HGF deficiency exacerbates injury [9, 10].

Despite its established role as an endogenous antagonist of tubulointerstitial fibrosis, the involvement of HGF in human UPJO pathogenesis remains unexplored. Specifically, the expression dynamics of HGF within stenotic ureteropelvic junction tissue and its functional relationship with TGFβ signaling in the context of congenital obstruction are unknown. Addressing this knowledge gap is crucial for understanding the molecular basis of UPJO progression and identifying novel therapeutic strategies.

Materials and methods

Patients

A total of forty UPJ stenosis segments were obtained from patients undergoing surgery for UPJO. Ten normal ureteral tissues from duplex kidneys served as controls (Ctrls). Written informed consent was provided by all patients’ guardians. Pediatric patients diagnosed with UPJO by preoperative ultrasonography and confirmed by postoperative pathology were defined as UPJO disease group. Pediatric patients who underwent hemi-nephroureterectomy for duplex kidneys were defined as Ctrl group. Pediatric patients with UPJO underwent transperitoneal laparoscopic dismembered pyeloplasty, stenotic UPJ tissue specimens were obtained during dismembered pyeloplasty surgery. And those with duplex kidneys underwent ipsilateral heminephroureterectomy, normal ureteral control tissues were collected from the non-obstructed ureteral segment of duplex kidney patients undergoing heminephrectomy. Immediately after resection, tissues were divided: one portion was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h for histology, and the other was snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 °C for molecular analysis.The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Zhangzhou Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University (2024KYZ358).

Cell cultures

Human ureteral epithelial immortalized cell line SV-HUC-1 and primary human ureteral epithelial cells were purchased from Procell (China, Wuhan). All cells were cultured in Ham’s F-12 K (PM150910, Procell) medium containing 10% FBS (164210, Procell) and 1% Penicillin-Streptomycin Solution (PB180120, Procell) at 5% CO2, 37℃. Cells were treated with HGF (50ng/mL) or TGFβ (2ng/mL) for 24 h for qPCR and western blots detection.

RNA extraction and qPCR

Total RNA was isolated from cells or tissues using TRIzol reagent. Reverse transcription into cDNA was performed using a PrimeScript RT Kit. Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) was conducted using SYBR Green Master Mix and gene-specific primers on a CFX96 Real-Time PCR System. Gene expression levels were normalized to GAPDH and calculated using the 2 − ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences were as follows: E-cadherin forward 5′-CGAGAGCTACACGTTCACGG-3′ and reverse 5′-GGGTGTCGAGGGAAAAATAGG-3′; N-cadherin forward 5′-AGCCAACCTTAACTGAGGAGT-3′ and reverse 5′-GGCAAGTTGATTGGAGGGATG-3′; β-catenin forword 5′-AAAGCGGCTGTTAGTCACTGG-3′ and reverse 5′-CGAGTCATTGCATACTGTCCAT-3′; Vimentin forward 5′-GAGAACTTTGCCGTTGAAGC-3′ and Reverse 5′-TCCAGCAGCTTCCTGTAGGT-3′; Col1a1 forward 5′-GAGGGCCAAGACGAAGACATC-3′ and reverse 5′-CAGATCACGTCATCGCACAAC-3′; FN1 forward 5′-CGGTGGCTGTCAGTCAAAG-3′ and reverse 5′-AAACCTCGGCTTCCTCCATAA-3′; α-SMA forward: 5′-GTCCCAGACATCAGGGAGTAA-3′ and reverse: 5′-TCGGATACTTCAGCGTCAGGA-3′; GAPDH forward 5′-GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT-3′ and reverse 5′-GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG-3′. Primer sequences for E-cadherin, N-cadherin, β-catenin, Vimentin, Col1a1, FN1, and α-SMA were synthesized and verified by Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Western Blotting (WB)

Western blot was performed according to standard procedures. Briefly, proteins from tissue and cell lysates were extracted using RIPA buffer (Promega, USA), and protein concentrations were measured using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (Pierce, USA). Equal amounts of protein were separated by 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Merck Millipore, USA). The membranes were blocked and incubated with specific primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight, followed by horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. Signals were visualized using the ECL Western Blotting Substrate (Promega, USA), and band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software (NIH, USA). GAPDH was used as the internal control. The following primary antibodies were used in this study: rabbit anti-human HGF (Abcam, USA, 1:500), mouse anti-human GAPDH (Abcam, USA, 1:3,000), rabbit anti-human E-cadherin (Abcam, USA, 1:500), rabbit anti-human N-cadherin (Abcam, USA, 1:500), rabbit anti-human β-catenin (Abcam, USA, 1:500), rabbit anti-human Vimentin (Abcam, USA, 1:500), rabbit anti-human COL1A1 (Abcam, USA, 1:500), rabbit anti-human FN1 (Abcam, USA, 1:500).

Wound healing (scratch) assay

Cells were seeded in 6-well plates. Upon reaching confluence, a linear scratch wound was created using a sterile pipette tip. Cell migration into the wound area was monitored under serum-free conditions using phase-contrast microscopy at 0, 24, and 48 h post-scratch. Wound closure percentage was quantified using ImageJ software.

Histologic analysis and immunohistochemistry

For histological analysis, tissue specimens were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at a thickness of 7 μm. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed using standard protocols to assess general tissue morphology. Masson’s trichrome staining was conducted to evaluate collagen fiber deposition. Immunohistochemical staining was performed using the EnVision™+ System-HRP (Dako, USA) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Paraffin-embedded tissue sections were incubated with primary antibodies against human HGF(Abcam, USA, 1:200), E-cadherin (Abcam, USA, 1:500), and Col1a1 (Abcam, USA, 1:500) overnight at 4 °C. After washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), sections were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies provided in the EnVision™+ kit for 30 min at room temperature. The signal was developed using diaminobenzidine (DAB) for 5 min, followed by counterstaining with hematoxylin.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism software (version 10). Results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Comparisons between two groups were performed using an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test. Comparisons among three or more groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Tukey’s multiple comparison test was used for post-hoc comparisons between individual groups. Dunnett’s test was used for post-hoc comparisons of experimental groups against the control group (Ctrl). A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics of Ctrl and UPJO Groups

A total of 50 pediatric patients were included in this study: 10 in the control group (non-obstructive renal conditions including duplex kidneys) and 40 in the disease group (unilateral ureteropelvic junction obstruction, UPJO). The demographic and clinical characteristics of both groups are summarized in Table 1. No significant differences were observed in age at surgery (Ctrl: 3.52 ± 3.35 years, UPJO: 4.61 ± 4.54 years, P = 0.488) or body weight (Ctrl: 14.18 ± 8.11 kg, UPJO: 17.87 ± 12.67 kg, P = 0.395), indicating comparable baseline anthropometric profiles. Sex distribution differed significantly between the two groups (P = 0.016). The UPJO group was predominantly male (85.0%, 34/40), while the Ctrl group had a higher proportion of females (70.0%, 7/10). Laterality did not differ statistically between groups (P = 0.670), although left-sided involvement was notably more frequent in the disease group (90.0% vs. 50.0%). As anticipated, imaging parameters reflective of hydronephrosis severity differed markedly between groups. The anteroposterior diameter of the renal pelvis was significantly larger in the UPJO group (28.60 ± 13.07 mm) compared to Ctrls (5.35 ± 8.99 mm, P < 0.001). Similarly, the Society of Fetal Urology (SFU) grading—an ordinal measure of hydronephrosis—was significantly higher in the UPJO group (median [IQR]: 3.5 [0.68]) than in Ctrls (median [IQR]: 1.1 [1.20], P < 0.001). In contrast, renal cortical thickness, while numerically lower in the UPJO group (7.05 ± 3.83 mm vs. 9.00 ± 2.62 mm), did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.103). Overall, the UPJO group was characterized by a significant male predominance, more severe hydronephrosis as evidenced by larger renal pelvic diameter and higher SFU grade, and comparable age and weight to the Ctrl group.

Downregulation of HGF Expression at the Ureteropelvic Junction in UPJO Patients

Previous studies have established hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) as an inhibitor of mesenchymal transition and fibrosis in diverse pathologies, including hepatic fibrosis, diabetic nephropathy, cardiac disease, and dermal fibrosis. However, its role in ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO) remained unclear. To investigate the mechanistic involvement of HGF in UPJO, we performed immunohistochemical (IHC) analysis comparing the stenotic ureteropelvic junction tissue from UPJO patients with normal ureteral tissue from control patients with duplex kidneys (Ctrls). IHC revealed significant downregulation of HGF and the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) protective marker E-cadherin (E-cad) at the stenotic segment in UPJO patients compared to controls. Conversely, expression of the fibrosis marker collagen type I alpha 1 (Col1a1) was markedly upregulated in UPJO tissues (Fig. 1).

Upregulation of fibrosis-associated marks and downregulation of HGF expression at the UPJ stenosis segment

We further quantified transcriptional and protein expression levels of HGF and fibrosis-associated molecules in patient tissues. Consistent with IHC findings, qPCR and WB analysis confirmed significant downregulation of HGF and E-cad mRNA and protein in the stenotic UPJO tissues versus Ctrls. Concurrently, mRNA and protein levels of mesenchymal markers (N-cad, β-catenin, Vimentin) and key fibrosis-associated proteins (Col1A1, Fibronectin 1 [FN1], α-smooth muscle actin [α-SMA]) were significantly elevated in UPJO tissues (Fig. 2A-D). These results suggest that reduced HGF expression may drive the upregulation of profibrotic proteins at the site of obstruction.

HGF Inhibits TGFβ-Induced Fibrosis and EMT in SV-HUC-1 cells

To elucidate the relationship between HGF expression and ureteral epithelial cell fibrosis, we modeled fibrosis in vitro by treating human ureteral epithelial cells (SV-HUC-1) with TGFβ. qPCR and WB revealed that HGF co-treatment significantly suppressed TGFβ-induced expression of fibrotic markers (α-SMA, FN1, Col1a1) and reversed EMT marker expression (E-cadherin downregulated; N-cadherin, β-catenin, Vimentin upregulated) in SV-HUC-1 cells and primary human ureteral epithelial cells (Figs. 3A-C and 4A-C).

HGF Attenuates TGFβ-Induced migration in SV-HUC-1 cells

We assessed the functional impact of HGF on SV-HUC-1 cell migration using a scratch wound healing assay. TGFβ stimulation significantly enhanced the migratory capacity of SV-HUC-1 cells compared to the Ctrl group. Importantly, this TGFβ-induced pro-migratory effect was substantially inhibited by HGF co-treatment (Fig. 5). These data indicate that HGF effectively suppresses the TGFβ-driven increase in SV-HUC-1 cell migration, further supporting its anti-fibrotic and anti-EMT role.

Discussion

Aberrant smooth muscle architecture and dysregulated extracellular matrix (ECM) metabolism constitute fundamental pathological mechanisms underlying ureteropelvic junction (UPJ) stenosis in pediatric UPJO [11, 12]. Hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) emerges as a pleiotropic cytokine with well-documented renoprotective functions, serving as an endogenous antagonist of tubular fibrosis through multi-faceted mechanisms, including EMT suppression and ECM homeostasis regulation [13, 14]. Preclinical studies have robustly demonstrated that HGF overexpression attenuates, while its deficiency exacerbates, tubulointerstitial fibrosis in models such as unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) and renal fibrosis, primarily by antagonizing TGF-β-driven EMT and ECM accumulation [9, 10]. Furthermore, MEN1 deficiency promotes renal fibrosis by epigenetically suppressing HGF expression, thereby impairing its natural anti-fibrotic function. Restoring HGF activity can alleviate fibrosis, highlighting its central therapeutic role downstream of MEN1 [7]. However, prior to this investigation, the expression dynamics and functional significance of HGF in the specific context of human UPJO remained entirely unexplored. Our study provides the first clinical evidence of significant HGF downregulation at stenotic UPJ segments in UPJO patients, coinciding with elevated fibrotic (Col1a1, α-SMA) and EMT (reduced E-cadherin, elevated N-cadherin/β-catenin/Vimentin) markers. This inverse correlation strongly implicates impaired HGF signaling in UPJO-associated fibrogenesis.

Congenital hydronephrosis affects 0.5%–1% of neonates, with UPJO accounting for 35% of cases [15, 16]. Obstruction during nephrogenesis disrupts renal maturation, potentially culminating in tubular atrophy, interstitial fibrosis, and irreversible nephron loss [17, 18]. Our multi-modal analysis (IHC, qPCR, immunoblotting) of stenotic UPJ tissue versus normal ureteral controls from duplex kidney patients consistently demonstrated: (i) suppression of HGF expression, (ii) upregulation of fibrogenic mediators (Col1a1, FN1, α-SMA), and (iii) induction of mesenchymal markers (N-cadherin, β-catenin, Vimentin) with concomitant E-cadherin reduction. Given HGF’s established role in mitigating fibroblast activation and ECM dysregulation, its deficiency likely contributes substantively to the smooth muscle dysfunction and matrix imbalances characterizing UPJ stenosis.

Transforming Growth Factor-β1 (TGFβ1) represents the master regulator of tubulointerstitial fibrosis across obstructive nephropathies, diabetic kidney disease, and chronic kidney disease [19, 20]. Sustained TGFβ signaling orchestrates fibrogenesis through renal tubular epithelial cells’ transdifferentiation, myofibroblast activation, pathological ECM accumulation, and impaired matrix degradation [21, 22]. Our in vitro validation in TGFβ-stimulated SV-HUC-1 cells recapitulated this profibrotic cascade: significant induction of fibrotic effectors (Col1a1, FN1, α-SMA) and EMT markers (N-cadherin, β-catenin, Vimentin) was observed. Crucially, HGF co-treatment attenuated these responses at both transcriptional and protein levels, reinforcing HGF’s capacity to antagonize TGFβ-driven fibrogenic programming. Enhanced cellular motility is a functional hallmark of EMT [23, 24]. Consistent with this paradigm, scratch assays confirmed that TGFβ1-mediated potentiation of the SV-HUC-1 migration phenotype was effectively suppressed by HGF co-administration. This functional rescue further substantiates HGF’s role in counteracting the cytoskeletal remodeling and invasive propensity associated with EMT progression.

The pathogenesis of UPJO is multifactorial, involving mechanisms beyond EMT and fibrosis. Dysfunction of ureteropelvic junction smooth muscle cells, characterized by disordered muscle bundle architecture and altered contractility, is a well-documented histological hallmark contributing directly to functional obstruction [25]. Additionally, an imbalance in extracellular matrix (ECM) turnover, driven by aberrant activity of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and their tissue inhibitors (TIMPs), facilitates pathological remodeling of the ureteral wall [11, 12]. Concurrently, local inflammatory cascades, involving cytokines and immune cell infiltration, are known to exacerbate tissue injury and promote a pro-fibrotic microenvironment [4]. While these pathways collectively underscore the complexity of UPJO, the TGF-β1 signaling axis emerges as a central, convergent node that can both initiate and amplify many of these processes. TGF-β1 is a potent inducer of smooth muscle phenotype switching, MMP/TIMP dysregulation, and inflammatory mediator production, ultimately driving the hallmark EMT and fibrotic response [21, 22]. Our data, demonstrating a strong inverse correlation between HGF downregulation and the upregulation of TGF-β-mediated fibrotic and mesenchymal markers, position impaired HGF signaling not as an isolated event but as a significant and potentially pivotal contributor within this central pathogenic axis. The deficiency of this endogenous TGF-β antagonist may thus serve as a permissive factor that unleashes or exacerbates the multifaceted pathological cascade in UPJO.

The pursuit of anti-fibrotic therapies for kidney disease has historically focused on broad-spectrum pathway inhibitors. Agents targeting the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), such as ACE inhibitors and ARBs, remain first-line due to their hemodynamic benefits and modest anti-fibrotic effects, yet they do not fully halt fibrosis progression and can be limited by side effects like hyperkalemia and hypotension [26, 27]. More direct inhibitors of the central TGF-β pathway have shown promise in preclinical models but face significant translational challenges due to their pleiotropic roles in homeostasis, immune regulation, and tumor suppression, raising concerns about systemic toxicity and lack of specificity [20]. In this context, harnessing endogenous protective mechanisms offers a compelling alternative strategy. HGF emerges as a prime candidate. As a naturally occurring cytokine with well-defined receptor specificity (MET), HGF suppressed the expression of TGF-β1, potentially improving the progression of renal fibrosis [6]. The HGF-MET pathway promotes tissue regeneration and has therapeutic potential for inflammatory and fibrosis diseases like renal fibrosis multiple sclerosis. The engineered RDO24 antibody activates MET similarly to HGF and shows beneficial effects in a model of multiple sclerosis [28]. Preclinical success in mitigating fibrosis via HGF supplementation or gene delivery in UUO models underscores its therapeutic potential and favorable safety profile [7]. Therefore, augmenting HGF signaling represents a potentially more targeted and physiologically congruent approach to counteract fibrosis specifically at the obstructed UPJ, potentially overcoming the limitations associated with broader pathway inhibition.

The identification of HGF downregulation as a key correlate of fibrosis and EMT in human UPJO opens immediate translational avenues. Clinically, assessing HGF expression levels in ureteral tissue or potentially in urinary biomarkers could serve as a prognostic indicator, stratifying patients at higher risk for rapid fibrotic progression and informing the timing and intensity of follow-up or intervention. More importantly, our findings position HGF supplementation as a compelling therapeutic strategy to preserve renal function and ureteral architecture. To translate this potential into clinical reality, targeted future investigations are essential. First, large-scale, multi-center studies are needed to robustly validate the correlation between HGF levels in stenotic tissue and long-term renal functional outcomes in pediatric UPJO patients. Second, the therapeutic efficacy and safety of HGF will be rigorously evaluated in vivo using established UPJO animal models to confirm its anti-fibrotic effects in a living system. Finally, to overcome the challenge of systemic delivery and potential off-target effects, innovative localized delivery platforms should be explored. A promising approach could involve the development of biocompatible, sustained-release hydrogel systems loaded with recombinant HGF or HGF-encoding vectors, which could be applied directly to the surgical site during pyeloplasty. This strategy aims to create a localized, pro-regenerative microenvironment at the critical UPJ, potentially preventing restenosis and fibrosis while minimizing systemic exposure. Together, these research directions chart a clear path from mechanistic discovery toward a novel, biologically-targeted adjunctive therapy for UPJO.

Conclusion

In summary, our study provides novel insights into the role of HGF in the pathogenesis of ureteropelvic junction (UPJ) stenosis in pediatric UPJO, positioning it as a promising therapeutic target for mitigating fibrotic obstruction.

Integrating multidisciplinary expertise and technological innovations in tracking infectious diseases
Authors: Alberto Rizzo
Published: 2024

Dear Editor

Branda et al. recently presented an open-access database to track global cases of avian influenza [1]. The aim is to create a publicly accessible, reliable source to facilitate data sharing and collaboration. As stated by the authors, the project is supported by a multidisciplinary team of epidemiologists, statisticians, bioinformaticians, and data management experts, ensuring the most comprehensive approach to the work. In 2022, a similar initiative by a team including genomic epidemiologists, bioinformaticians, and computer scientists led to the creation of an open-access database for tracking mpox outbreaks in non-endemic regions [2]. These efforts underscore the critical role of real-time epidemiological data systems in disease surveillance and response. In addition, the success of these projects highlights the importance of multidisciplinary collaboration, combining expertise from various fields to achieve a common goal.

Recent technological tools, including real-time data systems, web-based phylogenetic analysis services, and large language models (LLMs), offer significant opportunities for enhancing disease surveillance. Advanced real-time epidemiological data systems, like those for avian influenza and mpox cases, enable dynamic tracking and analysis of emerging and re-emerging outbreaks. The continuous updating of this information allows for the immediate identification of outbreak hotspots and facilitates the design of targeted interventions. For instance, during the H5N1 outbreaks, these systems enable quick identification of outbreak clusters, allowing for rapid response measures that mitigated further spread [1]. Similarly, the mpox database has been instrumental in identifying transmission patterns and informing public health strategies in non-endemic regions [2]. In resource-limited settings, these systems might drastically improve the efficiency and accuracy of disease monitoring. Web-based phylogenetic analysis platforms, like Nextstrain and the associated Nextclade, allow for the examination of viral evolution and transmission pathways through the analysis of genetic sequences. Most analyses are time-consuming and require high computational power.

These platforms provide accessible, cloud-based analytical capabilities, bridging gaps where advanced laboratory facilities are scarce. Health professionals can upload viral sequences and receive detailed insights without needing extensive local resources or bioinformatic skills. These tools have become, alongside next generation sequence techniques, increasingly relevant with the emergence of SARS-CoV-2 and COVID-19 cases [3,4]. For example, Nextstrain has been pivotal in tracking the evolution and spread of COVID-19 variants globally, providing real-time insights that have informed public health decisions.

Another potential tool, the newest in this context, is Artifical Intelligence, specifically LLMs, which might significantly enhance public health systems capabilities in diagnostics and preventive measures [5]. These models can analyze large volumes of data and support decision-making processes. Key applications include data analysis and interpretation, invaluable in settings where skilled epidemiologists are limited.

Such advancements result from the work of multidisciplinary teams and allow everyday users, such as epidemiologists, microbiologists, and infectivologists, to utilize an almost ready-to-use product with a user-friendly interface bypassing the need for local implementation from scratch. As reported, some of the most promising developments for the future of infectious diseases control and prevention often require the integration of high-end technologies, which means strict collaboration with information technology (IT) experts, including engineers, informaticians, and bioinformaticians. Some institutions try to overcome the unavailability of IT experts or resources by making healthcare workers and medical scientists acquire IT-related skills. Thus, the availability of the aforementioned systems and services might represent a valuable tool for those scientists. In addition, the integration of these technologies offers substantial opportunities for improving infectious disease prevention and control in resource-limited settings: cost-effectiveness, reducing the need for significant local infrastructure investments, scalability, since they can be adapted to various contexts and needs, capacity building, improving local capacities for managing and responding to outbreaks more effectively. However, there are also challenges to be addressed. Adequate training is necessary for local personnel to utilize these technologies effectively. On the other hand, long-term sustainability requires consistent funding, policy support, and integration into national health strategies.

In conclusion, the democratization of access to sophisticated analytical tools might enhance global surveillance efforts and support timely interventions. However, the integration of expertise from diverse fields, including microbiology, epidemiology, informatics and computer science, remains crucial for the continued advancement and effectiveness of these technologies in the context of infectious diseases control.

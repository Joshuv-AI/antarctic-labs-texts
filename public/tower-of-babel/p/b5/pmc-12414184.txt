Effect of pressure biofeedback training combined with Liuzijue Qigong training on respiratory function improvement in post-stroke patients with tracheotomy and decannulation: study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Xiaowen Zeng, Dan Wu, Yunong Zheng, Qing Liu, Pengfei Shen, Lei Chen, Nan Zhang, Lei Fang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionStroke causes neurological deficits and respiratory dysfunction, with prolonged bed rest exacerbating secondary pulmonary injury. This study evaluated the efficacy of pressure biofeedback training combined with Liuzijue Qigong (LQG) in improving functional outcomes and respiratory function in patients with tracheostomised stroke.Methods and analysisThis will be a parallel, single-centre randomised controlled trial involving 66 patients. Eligible patients will be randomly allocated to receive pressure biofeedback therapy combined with LQG training or simple pressure biofeedback training only. The primary outcomes will be the Chelsea Critical Care Physical Assessment Tool score and the findings of diaphragm ultrasound imaging. The secondary outcomes will include the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, pulmonary function test score, maximum phonation time, SpO2 and arterial blood gas results. Outcome analyses will be conducted on both intention-to-treat and per-protocol populations. A preliminary analysis will test whether pressure biofeedback therapy combined with LQG training leads to statistically better outcomes. This trial will provide evidence of the effectiveness of pressure biofeedback training combined with LQG training on respiratory function in patients with stroke after tracheal occlusion.Ethics and disseminationThis study protocol was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Shanghai Yangzhi Rehabilitation Centre (reference number: Yangzhi2024-137). All study participants will be required to provide written informed consent. The findings of this study will be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal for publication and presented at scientific conferences.Trial registration numberChiCTR2500097509.Keywords: Rehabilitation medicine, Stroke, Oxygen Saturation, Patient Participation, Physical Therapy Modalities, Respiratory Therapy

Introduction

Advancements in stroke care have improved survival rates.1 2 A significant proportion of patients admitted to the intensive care unit (15–45%) require tracheotomy.3 Although essential for airway protection, tracheotomy predisposes patients to pneumonia and malnutrition4 and directly impairs diaphragmatic function by bypassing upper airway physiology.5 6 This results in diaphragm atrophy, an effect that is exacerbated by stroke-induced damage to diaphragmatic muscle fibres.7 8 Consequently, respiratory function is impaired in 20–50% of patients with stroke who have undergone tracheostomy, significantly contributing to adverse outcomes, diminished quality of life and elevated mortality rates.9 10

Pressure biofeedback training involves the use of a pressure biofeedback unit to monitor and facilitate the abdominal drawing-in manoeuvre in supine patients.911,13 The promotion of coordinated contraction of the abdominal muscles and diaphragm enhances intra-abdominal pressure. This activation stimulates the diaphragm—particularly when it is weakened—thereby improving respiratory function and postural control.14 15

Liuzijue Qigong (LQG) is a traditional Chinese breathing exercise that optimises pulmonary function through controlled respiration.16,18 It improves controlled exhalation, aiding verbalisation in respiratory-impaired patients. Evidence supports its efficacy for enhancing lung ventilation and quality of life in patients with COPD, as well as for recovering respiratory function in patients with stroke.19 20

Evidence supports the efficacy of pressure biofeedback therapy for improving respiratory function in patients. Studies have demonstrated its positive effects on parameters such as diaphragmatic excursion.21 Similarly, LQG has shown benefits for respiratory rehabilitation, with research indicating improvements in pulmonary function (eg, forced vital capacity (FVC)) and exercise tolerance in patients with stroke.22 Despite this evidence supporting the individual efficacy of pressure biofeedback therapy and LQG for respiratory rehabilitation, no study has investigated their combined effects on respiratory function, specifically in patients with stroke who have undergone early-stage tracheotomy. Therefore, the aim of this randomised controlled trial is to evaluate the impact of integrating pressure biofeedback training with LQG on respiratory outcomes in this patient population.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This study is a randomised controlled trial. It is conducted in an open-label manner but with blinded assessors. The study has been approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Shanghai Yangzhi Rehabilitation Centre (reference number: Yangzhi2024-137). It is registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry under the registration number ChiCTR2500097509. The protocol for this study will adhere to rigorous standards as outlined by the Declaration of Helsinki, the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials and the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials guidelines.23 This commitment ensures the ethical and transparent conduct of our trial. We will recruit a total of 66 participants from Shanghai Yangzhi Rehabilitation Hospital. Participants will be randomly assigned at a 1:1 ratio to the study or control group. The trial will consist of a 4 week intervention period. We depict the study procedure in a flowchart (figure 1) and present the detailed schedule of patient interventions and outcomes in table 1.

Patients and the public will not be involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination of this research.

Recruitment

A total of 66 patients with stroke (aged ≥18 years) will be enrolled in this study at Shanghai Yangzhi Rehabilitation Hospital from March 2025 through March 2026. The patient recruitment process consists of four sequential steps: (1) the attending physician evaluates potential subjects using the study’s inclusion/exclusion criteria and contacts the principal investigator. (2) The investigator explains the trial’s objectives, requirements and schedule to potential participants, requesting their input. (3) Eligibility is determined on the basis of pre-defined inclusion/exclusion criteria. (4) Qualified participants provide written informed consent for trial participation.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was estimated using PASS 2023 software. According to pre-experimental data, the control group had a mean CPAx score of 22.25 (SD=6.602), whereas the study group had a mean CPAx score of 27.33 (SD=6.11). Using a two-sided test with α=0.05 and 80% power, the estimated sample size was 26 cases per group. Accounting for a 20% dropout rate (n=32.5), we included at least 33 cases per group, resulting in a final sample size of 66 cases.

Randomisation, allocation concealment and blinding

Eligible participants will be randomised using random numbers generated by SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) to the study or control group at a 1:1 ratio. An individual who was not involved in participant recruitment will manage the allocation process. Group allocation will be concealed in sealed envelopes, which will be opened only after participants meet the inclusion criteria. Owing to the nature of the intervention, blinding of participants and therapists was not feasible. To minimise bias, group assignments will be concealed from outcome assessors and data analysts.

Intervention

Both the study and control groups will undergo conventional rehabilitation training for 20 min per session, five times weekly over a 4 week period. The training will be administered by experienced physiotherapists in strict adherence to the research protocol at Shanghai Yangzhi Rehabilitation Hospital. All participants will receive standardised comprehensive rehabilitation, including exercise therapy, occupational therapy, activities of daily living training and traditional physical therapy, with equivalent intensity and frequency between groups. While no adverse effects are anticipated from this study, any treatment-related complications will be covered by national health insurance. In the event of clinical deterioration, patients will receive immediate medical attention and appropriate intervention.

Control group

The control group will receive conventional rehabilitation therapies combined with pressure biofeedback training using a stabiliser pressure biofeedback device (T-swylffy0440) to monitor deep abdominal muscle contraction. The device, comprising a pressure metre and pneumatic pump, measures the contraction force in mmHg.

During the training protocol, therapists will first instruct patients on proper technique and the function of deep abdominal muscles. With the patient in the supine position, the inflated pressure sensor (40 mmHg baseline) is positioned at the posterior superior iliac spine level.24 Patients will be instructed to perform abdominal drawing-in manoeuvres by slowly contracting the lower abdomen towards the spine while maintaining neutral pelvic and lumbar alignment. The target pressure is maintained at 40±2–3 mmHg for 10–15 s per contraction, with 2–3 s rest intervals between repetitions. The total duration of conventional rehabilitation training combined with pressure biofeedback training is 40 min per session.

Study group

The study group will receive a 40 min combined intervention comprising conventional rehabilitation, pressure biofeedback training and LQG training. The LQG protocol, adapted from the Healthcare Qigong Liuzijue instructions, incorporates six specific breathing sounds (Xu, He, Hu, Si, Chui, Xi) with corresponding movements.18

LQG training will be modified to accommodate each patient with stroke’s functional capacities with respect to their specific motor impairments. Patients may perform movements in supine or sitting positions, with therapist assistance as needed. The unaffected limb may guide the affected side, and movements are adjusted to ensure gentle, controlled execution without inducing breathlessness or muscle stiffness.

All interventions will be performed five times weekly for 4 weeks, with each session comprising 20 min of conventional rehabilitation plus 20 min of combined pressure biofeedback and LQG training.

Outcomes

In this study, efficacy will be evaluated using primarily the Chelsea Critical Care Physical Assessment Tool (CPAx)22 25 26 and the findings of diaphragm ultrasound imaging, which will be assessed both prior to the intervention and 4 weeks after randomisation. These primary outcomes will serve as the key measures for determining the effectiveness of the intervention.

In addition, several secondary outcomes, including the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score,27 28 pulmonary function test (PFT) score,29 maximum phonation time (MPT),30 31 SpO2 and arterial blood gas (ABG) results,32,34 will be evaluated. Data will be collected at each time point by independent investigators who are blinded to the group assignments and not involved in other aspects of the study. This approach ensures the objectivity and reliability of the collected data. Detailed information regarding the specific outcomes and their corresponding observation times is provided in table 1.

Primary outcome

CPAx

The CPAx assesses 10 items, including respiratory function, cough effect, movement in bed, supine to sitting on the edge of the bed, dynamic sitting balance, standing balance, sitting to standing, transferring from the bed to the chair, stepping and hand grip strength. Each item is measured on a six-point scale, with zero representing total dependency and five representing total independence. The CPAx tool can be used to evaluate patients who are sedated as well as those who are fully awake.35 Compared with other tools, the CPAx can measure respiratory function in addition to strength and physical function.36

Diaphragm ultrasound

Participants will lie in a supine position, breathing spontaneously, to allow ultrasound diaphragm thickness (Tdi) measurement. All phrenic ultrasound examinations in this study will be performed by the same physician with formal ultrasound training. A linear high-frequency probe will be positioned on the right axillary front, perpendicular to the chest wall between ribs eight and nine.37 The image of the phrenic junction consists of three layers of connective tissue: the bilateral hyperechoic areas (pleura, peritoneum) and the intermediate mixed-type echo area (composed of the anechoic diaphragm tissue and its internal hyperechoic fascia). These three layers are parallel during respiration. A moving cursor will be used to measure the Tdi at the end of quiet breathing (functional residual capacity; TdiFRC) and at the end of maximum inspiration (forced vital capacity, TdiFVC). The change in diaphragm thickness from the end of quiet exhalation to the end of maximum inspiration will be calculated as TF=(TdiFVC-TdiFRC)/TdiFRC.

Diaphragm mobility will be assessed via ultrasound by measuring the displacement distance of the diaphragmatic dome between functional residual capacity and total lung capacity. A convex transducer will be positioned along the midaxillary line at the right costal margin.38 The vertical distance between the end-inspiratory and end-expiratory positions will be measured from each M-mode image.

Secondary outcome measurement procedures

PFT

In both groups, respiratory muscle strength will be measured using a portable lung function detector, XEEK-S4 (Saike Medical Equipment Co, Ltd, China). During the assessment, patients will be positioned either in a seated or supine position and instructed to remain relaxed. They will be asked to perform successive inhalations and exhalations through their mouths, with the procedure repeated three times. The highest recorded values will be used for analysis.29

MPT

Patients will perform a sustained vowel phonation task after deep inspiration, during which the MPT will be measured.30 31 The assessment will be considered valid only if the tone and volume of phonation remain stable and within a comfortable range.

NIHSS

The severity of neurological deficits in patients with stroke will be evaluated on the basis of 11 items, including level of consciousness, gaze, vision, facial palsy, motor function of the limbs, ataxia, sensation, language, speech and neglect.39 Each item will be scored on the basis of severity, with a total score ranging from 0 to 42, where higher scores indicate more severe deficits. Its clinical utility in predicting functional outcomes and guiding treatment decisions is well established.40

The investigator will initially screen potential participants using electronic medical records (e-case). Eligible participants will be scheduled to undergo an ABG test, with the partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2) and partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PaCO2) as the primary indicators.33 PaO2 measures the pressure of oxygen dissolved in the blood, reflecting the efficiency of oxygen transfer from the lungs to the bloodstream. PaCO2 measures the concentration of carbon dioxide in the blood and reflects the body’s ability to eliminate carbon dioxide. During the training sessions, each participant will wear a finger pulse oximeter to monitor their blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) continuously. Blood oxygen saturation levels will be recorded both before and after each treatment session.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis will be performed using SPSS Statistics V.26.0 (IBM) with the intention-to-treat principle as the primary analysis. Missing data will be handled using the last observation carried forwards (<5% dropout) or multiple imputation (≥5% dropout). Per-protocol analysis will be conducted as a secondary analysis.

Baseline characteristics will be summarised using descriptive statistics. For between-group comparisons, χ² tests will be used for categorical variables, and t tests/Mann‒Whitney tests will be used for continuous variables. Primary and secondary outcomes will be analysed using two-way repeated-measures ANOVA to assess group-by-time interactions. Effect sizes will be reported with 95% CIs, with statistical significance set at p<0.05 (two-tailed). The detailed statistical methods are provided in online supplemental material S1.

Study monitoring and quality assurance

Comprehensive monitoring protocols will be implemented in the study to ensure participant safety and data quality. All adverse events (AEs) will be systematically documented and assessed for severity, causality and expectedness using standardised criteria. Serious AEs will be reported to the principal investigator within 24 hours and reviewed by both the clinical trial office and the ethics committee. Predefined stopping rules will be implemented for significant safety concerns.

Trained therapists with relevant clinical expertise will conduct all interventions, ensuring protocol adherence and standardisation. Outcome measures will be assessed by designated researchers at baseline and post-intervention. Data will be collected using hospital information systems and standardised assessment scales and then securely stored in password-protected databases with regular integrity checks. Access will be restricted to authorised study personnel, ethics committees and regulatory authorities.

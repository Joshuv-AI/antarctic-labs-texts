Development and internal validation of a nomogram to predict pulmonary hypertension in fibrosing interstitial lung diseases
Authors: David Rodríguez-Plaza, Jesús Ribas, Isabel Alfaya, Sonia López-Monzoni, Guadalupe Bermudo, Vanesa Vicens-Zygmunt, Yolanda Ruiz, Marta Hernández-Argudo, Judith Peñafiel, Belén Del Río, Ángela Díaz, Lara Fuentes, Salud Santos, María Molina-Molina

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPulmonary hypertension (PH) is a common comorbidity in patients with interstitial lung disease (ILD), with a negative prognostic impact. The main objective of this study was to develop and internally validate a nomogram to predict PH using real-life data from patients with fibrosing ILD.MethodsRetrospective, cross-sectional, single-centre study including adult patients with a diagnosis of fibrosing ILD undergoing right heart catheterization (RHC). Bootstrap was used to select clinical, radiological, and functional predictors of precapillary PH. LASSO, Elastic Net, and CART were also performed to enhance robustness. The final model was presented using a nomogram and PH risk was stratified into three categories according to CART.ResultsNinety patients were included. The most frequent ILD diagnoses were idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (20%), connective tissue diseases-ILD (18.9%) and combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (17.8%). Echocardiography identified high PH probability in 54.4% of patients. RHC confirmed PH in 64 (71%) patients and ruled out PH in 26 (29%). Pulmonary artery diameter, transfer coefficient of the lung for carbon monoxide (KCO) and six-minute walking test distance (meters) were retained in the final model, with an AUC [95% CI] of 0.857 [0.779–0.935]. PH predicted risk was classified in low- (< 36%), intermediate- (≥ 36-<73%) or high-risk (≥ 73%) categories.ConclusionA simple model based on three non-invasive objective variables obtained during the routine care of patients with fibrosing ILD showed good internal discrimination in our derivation cohort. Following external validation, the proposed nomogram could support PH-ILD suspicion and optimize echocardiography and RHC referrals.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12890-026-04495-6.

Background

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a common comorbidity in patients with fibrosing interstitial lung disease (ILD), with a negative impact on quality of life and survival. The prevalence of PH in ILD is not well established, with reported rates between 3% and 86%, depending on ILD subtype, severity of fibrosis and the diagnostic method used [1–3]. PH in ILD has been associated with worsening of symptoms, reduced quality of life, impaired exercise tolerance, greater oxygen requirements, increased exacerbation rates and higher mortality [1, 3–6]. The negative impact on survival is present from mild to severe associated PH [7].

PH diagnosis in patients with ILD is based on a “suspect, detect and confirm” strategy [8]. Following clinical suspicion, patients undergo transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) to assess the probability of PH and, finally, right heart catheterization (RHC) to confirm or rule-out PH. This diagnostic approach has several limitations. First, clinical suspicion is challenging because symptoms of both conditions frequently overlap, and specific manifestations of PH, such as right ventricle (RV) failure or syncope, only appear in advanced PH stages. Secondly, TTE may not always be able to estimate systolic pulmonary artery pressure (sPAP), often showing low correlation with values measured by RHC [9, 10]. Finally, RHC is usually performed in lung transplant candidates, when severe PH is suspected or when its result may aid in management decisions [8]. This “real-life” clinical practice could contribute to delayed PH diagnosis in ILD.

In recent years, several advances have increased interest in PH-ILD. Firstly, a better understanding of its pathogenesis has emerged. Recent research has shifted the paradigm from a “passive state” - in which PH was viewed only as a consequence of capillary destruction, hypoxia, and secondary vasoconstriction - towards an “active process” involving interconnected inflammatory, fibrosing, and angiogenic pathways [2, 11]. Secondly, the availability of an effective treatment and the anticipation of further therapeutic advances. In 2021, the INCREASE trial demonstrated the efficacy of inhaled treprostinil in these patients [12]. Based on its results, inhaled treprostinil was recommended in the 2022 European Society of Cardiology (ESC)/European Respiratory Society (ERS) guidelines for PH-ILD patients [13]. Other agents, such as treprostinil palmitate and mosliciguat, are currently being investigated in clinical trials (NCT05649722, NCT06635850).

Given these recent advances and the prevalence and clinical impact of PH-ILD, efforts to identify predictive factors and develop screening strategies beyond TTE have grown. So far, the most reported non-echocardiographic predictive signs of PH-ILD have been diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide (DLCO) < 30–50%, forced vital capacity (FVC)/DLCO > 1.6–1.8, disproportionate oxygen requirements relative to ILD severity, pulmonary artery (PA) diameter > 29 mm, PA diameter/aorta (Ao) diameter > 0.9, N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), RV failure symptoms, and syncope [3, 4, 14–17]. Some combinations of these variables have been used to better predict PH-ILD [8, 18–21]. However, most of them are based on the previous PH definition and include variables that reflect an advanced stage of the disease. To date, there is no validated official screening strategy for PH-ILD.

In this clinical context, the ability to predict the risk of PH in patients with fibrosing ILD using routinely available, non-invasive parameters could help optimize patient selection for TTE and RHC. This study aims to develop a predictive model based on non-invasive variables derived from a well-characterized, retrospective fibrosing ILD cohort, to accurately predict the presence of PH.

Methods

Study design and objectives

A retrospective, cross-sectional, single-centre study was conducted. The development dataset consisted of a retrospective cohort of patients diagnosed at the ILD Unit of the Respiratory Department, Bellvitge University Hospital, from February 2014 to March 2025.

Patients were managed according to the standards for good clinical practice in all cases. No experimental interventions, changes in treatment or additional tests were performed. Patient data confidentiality was guaranteed through data anonymization and restricted access. This study was approved by the local ethics committee (approval number PR219/24).

The main objective of the study was to develop and internally validate a predictive nomogram based on objective measurements collected during the routine assessment and follow-up of patients with fibrosing ILD, to assess the probability of having PH. The secondary objective was to identify non-invasive individual predictors of PH obtained during this process.

Study participants

Adult patients (≥ 18 years old) with a diagnosis of any fibrosing ILD according to current international guidelines who underwent RHC were eligible. The ILD diagnoses were made in the multidisciplinary ILD committee according to clinical guidelines [22]. Patients with ILD associated with connective tissue diseases (CTD-ILD) were included only if fibrotic lung involvement was ≥10% on high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT), to reduce the likelihood of including patients with a predominant group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) phenotype. Patients with silicosis, sarcoidosis and pulmonary Langerhans cell histiocytosis were included only if pulmonary fibrosis was considered the main cause of PH. In all cases, decisions regarding the attribution of PH to fibrosing lung disease were made by the PH-ILD committee, taking into account ILD extension, fibrotic burden and functional impairment.

Exclusion criteria were non-fibrosing ILD, ILD with predominant findings other than pulmonary fibrosis (such as emphysema, cysts, ground glass opacities, consolidation, nodules, or micronodules), diagnosis of postcapillary PH or chronic thromboembolic PH.

Variables and outcome measures

The main outcome was the presence of precapillary PH determined by RHC, defined according to the 2022 ESC/ERS guidelines: mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) > 20 mmHg, pulmonary artery wedge pressure (PAWP) ≤15 mmHg, and pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) > 2 Wood units (WU). No patient was receiving PH-specific therapy at the time of diagnostic RHC. Demographic data, comorbidities and clinical variables were collected from electronic health records within the six months prior to RHC. Dyspnoea on exertion was evaluated with the World Health Organization functional class (FC) scale. Radiological, echocardiographic, pulmonary function tests (PFTs), six-minute walking test (6MWT), laboratory and haemodynamic variables were also recorded. DLCO and KCO were corrected for haemoglobin when haemoglobin values were available. The median (IQR) intervals from PFT and 6MWT to RHC were 2.7 (1.1–4.2) and 2.6 (0.7–4.5) months, respectively. The 6MWT was performed according to standard institutional protocols, and oxygen supplementation during the test was maintained according to each patient’s usual prescription. The percentage of predicted 6MWT distance was calculated using reference equations for healthy adults [23]. All patients underwent TTE before RHC, with a median interval of 3.7 (1.2–6.4) months. Echocardiographic probability of PH was assessed according to the ESC/ERS guideline approach [13], using tricuspid regurgitation (TR) velocity when measurable together with indirect signs suggestive of PH.

Thoracic HRCT was performed before RHC in all patients, with a median interval of 2.5 (0.2–5.6) months. HRCT images were reviewed independently by two experienced radiologists (BDR and AD) blinded to clinical and haemodynamic information. A peer review of radiological pattern, vascular features and fibrosing semi-quantification was conducted. In line with other studies, the external axial diameter of main PA was measured just before right PA bifurcation [24, 25]. The diameter of the ascending aorta was measured at the same level as the PA.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were reported as counts and percentages, while continuous variables were summarized as mean and standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), depending on the data distribution.

To assess the agreement between radiologists determining the PA diameter, the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was calculated and a Bland & Altman plot was generated. Additionally, a scatter plot was created and Spearman’s correlation coefficient was calculated.

The primary outcome was the presence of PH. Independent logistic regression models were performed for each variable of interest to evaluate their association with PH. Finally, eight variables (PA diameter, PA/Ao ratio, DLCO, KCO, FVC/DLCO, FVC/KCO and 6MWT distance in absolute value and as a percentage of the predicted value) were considered as candidate predictors for model development.

Bootstrap resampling was used as the primary method for predictor selection. A total of 1,000 samples were generated, and logistic regression models with PH as the dependent variable were fitted using backward elimination based on Akaike Information Criterion (AIC). Variables retained in at least 90% of the bootstrap samples were considered candidates for the final model. Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) and Elastic Net regression were applied as sensitivity analyses using 10-fold cross-validation for tuning of the penalty parameter (λ), with selection based on the λ1se criterion. For Elastic Net, the mixing parameter was set to α = 0.5. Classification and Regression Trees (CART) were also applied to improve the robustness of the selected predictors. Non-linear relationships among continuous variables, relevant interactions and multicollinearity were assessed using the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF).

Internal validation included discrimination using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC), calibration (Brier score and calibration slope and intercept), and overall model fit using the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) and Akaike Information Criterion (AIC). Where applicable, optimism-corrected estimates of model performance were obtained by bootstrap internal validation, in accordance with the TRIPOD recommendations [26]. The final model was presented as a nomogram, with predicted probabilities stratified into three risk categories based on cut-off values derived from CART analysis, whose diagnostic performance was assessed by calculating sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values, and positive and negative likelihood ratios.

A complete case analysis was performed using a two-sided significance level of 0.05 and R software, version 4.3.0.

Results

Patient characteristics

From 97 eligible patients with fibrosing ILD undergoing RHC, 90 of them met inclusion criteria (Fig. 1). Mean age was 66.0 (8.3) years and 68 (75.6%) patients were men (Table 1). The most frequent ILDs were idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) (20%), CTD-ILD (18.9%) and combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE) (17.8%). At the time of evaluation, 63 (70%) patients were receiving anti-fibrotic treatment: 43 (47.8%) with nintedanib and 20 (22.2%) with pirfenidone. The median duration of antifibrotic treatment before RHC was 10.6 (5.8-28.9) months. Most patients (83/90, 92.2%) were in FC II or III, only 26 (28.9%) patients showed a FVC < 60% predicted and 33 (36.7%) had been evaluated for lung transplant.

Echocardiography showed a high probability of PH in 49 (54.4%) patients, while in 15 (16.7%) patients the probability could not be determined due to the absence of TR (Table 1). From 64 patients with PH, 44 (68.7%) had a high echocardiographic probability of PH, 14 (21.9%) low or intermediate probability and 6 (9.4%) patients had no measurable TR. Patients with PH-ILD were more frequently male (79.7% vs. 65.4%) and more often in FC III (51.6% vs. 23.1%). In addition, emphysema (34.4% vs. 11.5%) and left heart disease were more frequent in patients with PH-ILD, particularly atrial fibrillation (15.6% vs. 0%). There was also a significant higher percentage of CPFE in patients with PH-ILD: 15/64 (23.4%) vs. 1/26 (3.8%). However, in a sensitivity analysis excluding patients with CPFE, the final model remained unchanged, with comparable effect estimates to those observed in the full cohort (Supplementary Table 1).

Haemodynamic, pulmonary function and radiological variables

RHC confirmed PH in 64 (71%) patients and ruled out PH in 26 (29%). In the PH-ILD group, mPAP was 29.6 (6.2) mmHg and PAWP was 8.9 (3.2) mmHg, consistent with the diagnosis of precapillary PH (Table 2). PVR was 4.6 (2.0) WU, indicating that most patients had moderate-to-severe PH. Right ventricular function was worse among patients with PH-ILD, with a cardiac index at the lower limit of normality (2.55 [0.51] L/min/m2).

Mean PA diameter was 32.6 (4.4) mm and mean PA/Ao ratio was 0.91 (0.83–0.98). There was a good interobserver agreement between radiologists, with an ICC [95%] of 0.891 [0.839–0.927] for the PA diameter and 0.833 [0.758–0.887] for the PA/Ao ratio (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Univariate logistic regression

Functional class III, compared to FC I, and long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT) during exertion, quantified in litres per minute, were the only clinical variables that showed statistically significant differences between both groups (Fig. 2; Supplementary Table 2). Among radiological variables, PA diameter was significantly associated with the presence of PH, with odds ratio (OR [95% CI]) of 1.26 [1.11–1.47]. Among PFTs, diffusion-associated parameters (DLCO, KCO, FVC/DLCO and FVC/KCO) also differed significantly between groups, with OR [95% CI]) of 0.96 [0.92–0.99], 0.95 [0.91–0.97], 4.06 [1.77–11.79] and 10.1 [2.74–57.09], respectively. Significant differences were also observed in the 6MWT distance, both in absolute value, with an OR of 0.92 [0.86–0.96], and as a percentage of the predicted value, with an OR of 0.03 [0.00-0.38]. NT-proBNP levels did not show differences among both groups.

Variable selection

The model was developed using 83 complete cases (Table 2). To assess potential selection bias related to missing data, we compared key baseline characteristics between included and excluded patients. No relevant differences were observed between groups (Supplementary Table 3). Analyses using bootstrapping identified PA diameter and KCO as potential predictors, retained in more than 90% of the bootstrap samples. 6MWT distance (absolute value) was also consistently retained in over 90% of the samples, whereas 6MWT distance (percentage of predicted value) was retained in 71.3% of the samples. LASSO and Elastic Net confirmed PA diameter, KCO, and 6MWT distance (absolute value) as the most robust predictors, whereas CART analysis selected only PA diameter (Fig. 3). No interactions were included in the final model and multicollinearity was not a concern.

Nomogram and risk stratification

A nomogram was created for the three selected variables to illustrate how each contributes to the total score and the estimated probability of PH (Fig. 5). The model-predicted risk was stratified into three categories based on CART analysis (Fig. 5; Supplementary Fig. 2; Supplementary Table 4). The low-risk group was defined by a predicted probability of PH < 36% according to the prediction model. Of the 14 patients in this group, only 3 (21.4%, 95% CI: 4.66%-50.8%) had PH. The intermediate-risk group was defined by a model-predicted probability of PH ≥36% and < 73%. Twenty-five patients were classified in this category, of whom 16 (64.0%, 95% CI: 42.5%-82.0%) had PH. Finally, the high-risk group was defined by a model-predicted probability of PH ≥73%. Among the 44 patients that were classified in this group, 39 (88.6%, 95% CI: 75.4%-96.2%) had PH. The sensitivity, specificity, predictive values and likelihood ratios with 95% confidence intervals of each risk group are shown in Supplementary Table 5.

Discussion

In this study, we have developed a simple, easy-to-use nomogram to stratify the risk of PH in patients with fibrosing ILD. The model shows good internal discrimination for PH-ILD detection and is based on three objective, routinely available variables from different clinical domains: PA diameter, KCO and 6MWT distance. This is a model-development and internal-validation study in a highly selected population with fibrosing ILD and a high pretest probability of PH; therefore, external validation and calibration in other fibrosing ILD populations are required before clinical use. Importantly, the proposed risk categories should be interpreted as exploratory and should not be used to exclude PH or to avoid further evaluation, particularly because PH was still present in 21.4% of patients classified as low risk.

Current PH guidelines do not explicitly recommend routine PH screening in patients with ILD [13]. However, the availability of effective therapies for PH-ILD has raised interest in earlier detection, and several expert groups have proposed screening approaches for PH in ILD [27, 28]. To date, only two predictive tools for PH-ILD detection have been validated. In 2022, Parikh et al. developed and externally validated an eight-variable score including 6MWT distance < 350 m, physical exam, DLCO < 40%, use of supplemental oxygen, elevated cardiac biomarkers, history of syncope or presyncope, PA enlargement on chest imaging and a diagnosis of CTD or sarcoidosis [18, 19]. This model used a PH definition of mPAP ≥ 20 mmHg and PVR > 3 WU. Although it demonstrated excellent performance, the number of included variables is high and some of them are subjective or may reflect an advanced stage of PH-ILD. In 2024, Nathan et al. developed and validated the FORD index in patients with IPF, including FVC/DLCO, oxygen saturation nadir during 6MWT, race and 6MWT distance [20]. However, the derivation cohort was limited to IPF, PH prevalence was low (9.8%), the model was based on the previous PH definition (mPAP ≥ 25 mmHg and PVR ≥ 3 WU), radiological variables were not included and the model performance was modest (AUC 0.75, sensitivity 72.3%, specificity 69.4%). More recently, preliminary results from the PHINDER study also supported the need for structured PH screening strategies in ILD [29]. Among the first 190 included patients, 55% had RHC-confirmed precapillary PH, and variables from PFTs, CT imaging and echocardiography were associated with PH. Importantly, clinician suspicion alone showed limited accuracy, and the authors also emphasized that echocardiography alone may be insufficient to rule out PH. These findings reinforce the potential value of composite approaches based on objective non-invasive variables, such as the nomogram proposed in our study.

Compared to previous models, our nomogram includes both IPF and non-IPF fibrosing ILDs, shows good internal discrimination, and may be easier to use, as it includes only three objective variables obtained during routine care of patients with ILD. PA diameter has been proposed as a potential predictor of PH-ILD by the Fleischner Society and Group 3 PH Task Force from the 7th World Symposium on PH [8, 30]. Our findings support the systematic evaluation of PA diameter by trained radiologists to ensure good interobserver agreement, and its inclusion in thoracic HRCT reports. Although DLCO and FVC/DLCO are among the most frequently reported variables associated with PH-ILD [18–20, 31], in our model a reduced KCO showed a stronger association than these variables. A decrease in DLCO may reflect either impaired alveolar-capillary gas transfer or reduced effective alveolar volume. In contrast, KCO expresses gas transfer corrected for alveolar volume and may therefore better reflect impairment in alveolar-capillary gas uptake related to loss of effective pulmonary vascular volume. Finally, in line with previous studies, a reduced 6MWT distance reflects the additional burden that PH imposes on exercise capacity in patients with ILD [8, 18, 20, 27, 32].

It should be noted that the proposed nomogram does not include echocardiographic variables. TTE is not part of routine follow-up testing in patients with ILD, although it remains an essential tool for PH-ILD screening and should be performed once PH-ILD is suspected. In our study population, echocardiographic probability of PH could not be determined in 16.7% of the patients and, importantly, almost one third of patients with RHC-confirmed PH-ILD had low/intermediate echocardiographic probability or non-measurable TR. This finding is clinically relevant, as it confirms that a low or intermediate echocardiographic probability of PH does not rule PH-ILD. Therefore, while TTE is useful in most cases, other objective, non-invasive tools could help identify patients who should still be considered for RHC despite a non-high or inconclusive echocardiographic probability.

After external validation, the proposed nomogram could help identify patients who should undergo TTE to assess the likelihood of PH and, together with TTE findings, those who may require RHC. This strategy should be tested in future studies, specifically to determine how the nomogram can be integrated with the echocardiographic probability of PH to improve patient selection for RHC. Because the model does not include echocardiographic variables, it could be particularly helpful in patients with non-measurable TR, an issue that should also be addressed in future studies.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective and single-centre nature is one of the main limitations. Second, the group of ILD patients without PH included only 26 individuals, which may have limited the model’s development and increased the risk of overfitting. Third, our study is subject to selection bias, as the model was developed in a sample of patients with a high pre-test probability of PH who underwent RHC. Therefore, the results may not be directly applicable to a broader fibrosing ILD population. However, our approach reflects real-life clinical practice in recent years, as RHC is an invasive procedure that should not be performed in the absence of suspected PH. Fourth, the time lag between non-invasive assessments and RHC may have introduced some heterogeneity in the measurements. Fifth, although optimism-corrected estimates of model performance were calculated, shrinkage-adjusted regression coefficients were not applied in the present analysis. In addition, the CART-derived risk thresholds should be interpreted as internally derived exploratory cut-offs. Therefore, future external validation studies should consider shrinkage techniques and assess the reproducibility of these thresholds before clinical implementation. Sixth, the PA diameter can also be influenced by sex and body size, and we did not index it based on these variables. However, the PA diameter was consistently retained in the final model, whereas the PA/Ao ratio was not. Finally, the results of this study would only be applicable to patients with fibrosing ILD and not to those with non-fibrosing ILD.

The strengths of our study include the use of RHC-confirmed PH as the reference standard, the inclusion of a real-life cohort with a broad spectrum of fibrosing ILDs, blinded radiological assessment, good internal discrimination, and the fact that the model is based on three objective and routinely obtained variables, thus optimizing the use of healthcare resources. As next steps, our goal is to externally validate the nomogram and evaluate the model’s performance in an incident population with fibrosing ILD, as well as define how it can be integrated with the echocardiographic probability of PH in structured PH-ILD screening strategies.

Conclusion

Although PH is a frequent comorbidity with a negative prognostic impact in patients with fibrosing ILD, there is currently no widely accepted screening tool. Improving early detection of PH-ILD remains challenging. In this regard, our findings demonstrate that a simple model based on three objective variables (PA diameter, KCO and 6MWT distance) showed good internal performance in our development cohort and, after external validation, could help predict and stratify the risk of PH in fibrosing ILD.

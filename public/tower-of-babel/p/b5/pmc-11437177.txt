Stereoselective rhodium-catalyzed reaction of allenes with organoboronic reagents for diversified branched 1,3-alkadienes
Authors: Weiyi Wang, Shichao Hong, Wenxiang He, Xue Zhang, Hui Qian, Shengming Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe terminal isoprene unit, as the simplest branched 1,3-diene unit, exists in a wide range of natural products and bioactive molecules. Herein, we report a stereoselective rhodium-catalyzed reaction of allenes with readily available methyl pinacol boronic ester, providing a straightforward approach to isoprene derivatives with a very high E-stereoselectivity. Its synthetic potential has been illustrated by a concise synthesis of natural product schinitrienin. Such a protocol can be easily extended to aryl and alkenyl boronic reagents affording 2-aryl or -alkenyl substituted 1,3-dienes, which are also of high importance in organic synthesis but remain challenging for their selective synthesis, with a remarkable stereoselectivity. A series of deuterium-labeling experiments indicate a unique mechanism, which involves reversible β-H elimination as well as hydrometalation and isomerization of the allylic rhodium species.Subject terms: Homogeneous catalysis, Synthetic chemistry methodology

Introduction

The terminal isoprene unit, as an iconic type of 1,3-dienes, exists in a wide range of natural products and bioactive molecules (Fig. 1a)1–6. Featured by the methyl group on the 2-position, the terminal isoprene unit is the simplest branched 1,3-diene unit. However, although the synthesis of linear 1,3-dienes has been well-developed, the stereoselective synthetic methods for branched 1,3-dienes are still very limited7–9. Allenes have been developed as versatile synthetic building blocks for the synthesis of various types of olefins due to the unique and diverse reactivity of their two cumulated carbon-carbon double bonds10–18. Branched 1,3-dienes can be synthesized from α-functionalized allenes and aryl/alkenyl metallic species, which requires pre-installation of a leaving group in allene (Fig. 1b)19–32. For common allenes without a leaving group, the carbometalation of allenes with organometallic species would generate alkenyl metallic species or allylic metallic species14, and the β-H elimination of allylic metallic species would, in principle, afford the targeted branched 1,3-dienes (Fig. 1c). Previously reported oxidative addition of aryl/alkenyl halides with Pd(0)33–38 and aryl/alkenyl C–H activation applying the directing group39,40, are all not applicable for the methylation to form the terminal isoprene unit existing in numerous natural products as shown in Fig. 1a. We envisioned that a strategy of transmetalation of readily commercially available organoboronic reagents with a proper metal catalyst may address the issues for the efficient synthesis of branched 1,3-dienes from allenes: (1) achieving the desired methylation for natural product syntheses; (2) mild conditions, thus, resulting in a very promising selectivity. Herein, we report a Rh-catalyzed reaction of allenes with organoboronic reagents, using a copper salt as the cocatalyst with air as the green oxidant to complete the catalytic cycle, affording branched 1,3-dienes with a high stereoselectivity and a broad substrate scope under very mild conditions (Fig. 1d). Methylation has been realized and successfully applied to the concise synthesis of natural product schinitrienin.

Results and discussion

We initially focused on the construction of the iconic terminal isoprene unit existing in numerous natural products from allenes, therefore, the reactions of allene 1a with methyl pinacol boronic ester 2a were evaluated (Table 1). It was found that in the presence of [Cp*RhCl2]2 (2.5 mol%), NaOAc (20 mol%), Cu(OAc)2·H2O (5 mol%), and H2O (5.0 equiv.) in THF at 50 °C under air atmosphere, E-3aa was afforded with 57% NMR yield (entry 1). The reactions in other typical solvents were of lower efficiency (entries 2–8). The amount of water has a critical impact on the reactivity (entries 1 and 9–11). Other bases showed lower efficiency as compared to NaOAc (see Supplementary Information, Supplementary Table 1, entries 12–16). The reaction under a pure oxygen atmosphere was in a similar efficiency as compared with the reaction under an air atmosphere (compare entry 12 with entry 1). Under a nitrogen atmosphere (entry 13) or in the absence of Cu(OAc)2·H2O (entry 14), only 9% or 13% NMR yield of E-3aa was observed, respectively, indicating that the presence of both air and Cu cocatalyst is critical for the catalytic cycle. To further verify the role of the copper salt, a control experiment was conducted using 2.0 equiv. of Cu(OAc)2 under a nitrogen atmosphere, which also worked and afforded a 45% NMR yield (see Supplementary Information, Reaction development section). Moreover, using 2.0 equiv. of AgOAc or MnO2 as the oxidant instead of the combination of Cu(OAc)2·H2O and air, the catalytic transformation could also proceed albeit in lower efficiency, further indicating an aerobic regeneration of the Rh catalyst (see Supplementary Information, Reaction development section). In the absence of catalyst [Cp*RhCl2]2, the reaction did not occur (entry 15). NaOAc also plays a vital role (entry 16). The efficiency dropped when the reaction was conducted at room temperature (entry 17; for details, see Supplementary Information, Supplementary Table 1). Other methyl boronic reagents, such as MeB(OH)2 (entry 18) and MeBF3K (entry 19), gave lower yields. Further optimization led to the observation that with catalyst Cp*Rh(OAc)2 replacing the combination of [Cp*RhCl2]2 and NaOAc, the efficiency was significantly improved with a 70% NMR yield of E-3aa (entry 20). After further evaluation of various reaction parameters using catalyst Cp*Rh(OAc)2, we found the amount of water also has a critical impact on the reactivity, and the standard conditions for the methylation have been defined as follows: In the presence of Cp*Rh(OAc)2 (5 mol%), Cu(OAc)2·H2O (5 mol%), and H2O (10.0 equiv.), the reaction of allene 1a with methyl pinacol boronic ester 2a in THF at 50 °C under an air atmosphere afforded E-3aa in 75% NMR yield (entry 21, defined as Method A). (More results and details of the reaction optimization are given in Supplementary Information, Reaction Development section.)

The scope of the methylation was then explored (Fig. 2a). Allenes bearing various synthetically versatile functional groups, such as Weinreb amide (E-3aa, E-3ay, E-3az), protected amines (E-3la), hydroxy (E-3ca, E-3ga, E-3cy), bromo (3ta), could undergo this transformation smoothly, forming an iconic isoprene structural unit. Furthermore, 1,1-disubstituted allene 3va and 1,3-disubstituted allenes 3wa, 3pa, and 3ta all worked well in high yields. Trisubstituted allene 1x and tetrasubstituted allene 1y did not work in this reaction with 83% and 98% recoveries, respectively. Interestingly, besides MeBpin, cyclopropyl pinacol boronic ester also performed well in this transformation (E-3ay, E-3cy), and ethyl pinacol boronic ester could also give target product E-3az albeit in a diminished efficiency.

The remarkable generality of this methylation encouraged us to further illustrate the synthetic potential of this reaction by the synthesis of natural product schinitrienin4 14 bearing a terminal isoprene unit (Fig. 2b), which was isolated from the root bark of Zanthoxylum schinifolium. From (E)-3-methylhept-2-en-6-yn-1-ol, applying bromination followed by etherification with 7-hydroxy-8-methoxycoumarin 11 afforded terminal alkyne 12. The structure of 12 was further confirmed by X-ray crystallographic analysis. By executing ATA (allenation of terminal alkynes)41,42, allene 13 was synthesized from terminal alkyne 12. Finally, the methylation of allene 13 was successfully achieved by the current method, affording schinitrienin 14 concisely. (For more details, see Supplementary Information.)

To our delight, this protocol may also be readily extended to the synthesis of 2-aryl or -alkenyl substituted 1,3-dienes by using aryl or alkenyl boronic reagents (Fig. 3). The reactivity is much higher since the reaction using [Cp*RhCl2]2 with NaOAc occurs at room temperature and gives higher yields as compared with that using Cp*Rh(OAc)2 without NaOAc (see E-3db). Such branched 1,3-dienes are also versatile and ideal platform for organic synthesis43–60. Firstly, we conducted the reactions of monosubstituted allenes with various organoboronic reagents (Fig. 3a), affording 2-substituted 1,3-dienes. Both pinacol boronic esters (Method B) and boronic acids (Method C) could be applied and the generality of pinacol boronic esters is better. Both electron-rich and electron-deficient aryl pinacol boronic esters reacted with allenes in high yields. Importantly, a variety of synthetically versatile functional groups including hydroxy (E-3cb, E-3gb, E-3co, E-3cp, E-3fq, E-3cs), protected hydroxy (E-3hb), protected amines (E-3kb, E-3lb), ester groups (E-3dd, E-3nl, E-3nm, E-3nn), nitro (E-3de), methoxy (E-3mf, E-3af), Weinreb amides (E-3af, E-3ac, E-3ag, E-3ah, E-3ai, E-3aj, E-3ak, E-3aab, E-3aac, E-3at, E-3au), bromo (E-3ag, E-3ah), chloro (E-3ai, E-3aj), fluoro (E-3ak), cyano (E-3nm), trifluoromethyl (E-3nn), and formyl (E-3cp) were all compatible. Biphenyl (E-3co) and naphthyl (E-3fr) pinacol boronic esters all performed well affording the products in high yields. Heteroaryl on allenes (E-3ib, E-3bb, E-3mf), or heteroaryl pinacol boronic esters (E-3daa, E-3aab, E-3aac), were all compatible for this transformation. The reaction could be easily conducted on a gram scale (E-3ag). Furthermore, 1-alkenyl pinacol boronic esters could also undergo this transformation to afford substituted [3]dendralenes E-3cs, E-3at, and E-3au, which provides a straightforward, general, and efficient synthetic approach for [3]dendralene synthesis61,62.

This reaction has also been successfully applied for the modification of complex bioactive molecules (Fig. 3b). Allene 1o, derived from indomethacin, afforded 1,3-diene E-3oi in 93% yield. Estrone and 18β-glycyrrhetinic acid were incorporated into corresponding pinacol boronic esters, which gave 1,3-dienes E-3nv and E-3fw in 78% and 80% yields, respectively. Furthermore, we examined the substrate scope of 1,3-disubstituted allenes (Fig. 3c). Interestingly, the trisubstituted carbon-carbon double bond in the products was in an excellent Z-selectivity, which has been established by X-ray crystallographic analysis of 3tf. The electronic property of R1 (3pb, 3pf and 3px) or R3 (3rf, 3sf and 3tf) did not have an obvious impact on the stereoselectivity34,38. Moreover, 1,1-disubstituted allene 1 v could also undergo this transformation, affording 3vb in 68% yield.

The synthetic potentials of the product E-3ag have been illustrated via the [4 + 2] cycloaddition reactions as shown in the Fig. 4. The cycloaddition of E-3ag with phenylhydroxylamine63 afforded the heterocyclic compound 15 in 89% yield with an exclusive regioselectivity. The Diels-Alder reactions with dimethyl acetylenedicarboxylate64, N-methylmaleimide, and maleic anhydride gave 16 in 79% yield, 17 in 91% yield, and 18 in 75% yield, respectively, with an excellent diastereoselectivity. The relative configurations of 17 and 18 have all been established by X-ray crystallographic analysis.

To unveil the mechanism, a series of deuterium-labeling experiments were conducted (Fig. 5a–c). The reaction of 4,4-di-deuterated monosubstituted allene 1d-d2 with phenylboronic acid 2b’ under the standard conditions afforded E-3db-d3-1, in which the deuterium at the C(4)-position lost partially (84% deuterium incorporation), and the C(1)-position was partially deuterated (53% and 51% deuterium incorporation) (Fig. 5a). When 1,1-di-deuterated allene 1d-d2’ was reacted with 2b’ under the standard conditions, E-3db-d3-2 was afforded with 81% and 81% deuterium incorporation on C(1)-position, and 20% deuterium incorporation on C(4)-position (Fig. 5b). It is noted that there should be a large amount of active proton source (water, boronic acid, etc.) in the ambient environment, which did not seem to interfere with the [Rh]–D/H species since replacement of H2O and Cu(OAc)2·H2O with D2O and Cu(OAc)2 under the standard conditions for the reaction of non-deuterated allene 1d with phenyl pinacol boronic ester 2b afforded non-deuterated product E-3db (Fig. 5c). The kinetic isotope effect (KIE) studies using 1d-d2 (Fig. 5d) and 1d-d2’ (Fig. 5e) demonstrated kH/kD values of 1.25 and 1.14, respectively. These observed low KIE values suggest that the β-H elimination is not the turnover-limiting step. The different KIE values indicate a minor difference upon involving the D atom at 1 or 4 position in 1d-d2 or 1d-d2’.

Based on these results, a catalytic cycle is proposed (Fig. 6). At first, the [Cp*RhOAc]+ species A undergoes transmetalation with the organoboronic reagent to generate [Cp*RhR1]+ species B, which would undergo subsequent highly stereoselective carbometalation with allene 1 to generate the stereodefined η3-allylic rhodium intermediate anti-C. Its β-H elimination would afford the complex of the diene and [Cp*RhH]+ species, intermediate D. The [Cp*RhH]+ species in intermediate D can undergo hydrometalation with the 1,3-diene, generating π-allylic intermediate E or anti-C, delivering the hydride to the C(1)- or C(4)-position of the 1,3-diene unit. Reductive elimination and dissociation of the diene from the complex D in the presence of AcO– would release product 3, AcOH, and Cp*Rh. Finally, Cp*Rh could be oxidized under the catalysis of Cu with the oxygen in air as the oxidant to regenerate the catalytically active Rh(III) species A, finishing the catalytic cycle. In addition, it is interesting that the acetate works in a catalytic way, both AcO– and AcOH would consume and regenerate during the reaction via the reaction of AcOBpin with water, the reductive elimination, and the catalytic oxidation cycle.

The result of the deuterium-labeling experiment using 1d-d2 in Fig. 5a could be further explained as shown in Fig. 7a. The overall product E-3db-d3-1 was a mixture of P1 – P6, thus, the deuterium incorporation of C(1)- and C(4)-positions was a combined result of these six different deuterated products P1 – P6: Firstly, the [Cp*RhPh]+ species and allene 1d-d2 undergo carbometalation to generate the η3-allylic rhodium intermediate η3-F1. The subsequent syn-β-D elimination would generate diene-Rh coordination complex F2, which could undergo dissociation of the diene and reductive elimination to release monodeuterated product P1, AcOD, and Cp*Rh in the presence of AcO–. The [Cp*RhD]+ moiety in complex F2 could also undergo syn-deuterometalation with the terminal C = C bond in 1,3-diene generating π-allylic intermediate η3-F3. The syn-β-H elimination of η3-F3 could generate new diene-Rh coordination complexes F4 and F8, which could release two different 1,4-dideuterated products P2 and P3, AcOH, and Cp*Rh in the presence of AcO– via dissociation of the diene and reductive elimination. F4 and F8 could also undergo further syn-hydrometalation to generate allylic metallic species η3-F5 and η3-F9, which could also undergo π-σ-π rearrangement to generate new allylic Rh species η3-F6 and η3-F10. Subsequent syn-β-D elimination would generate new diene-Rh coordination complexes F7 and F11, which could release monodeuterated diene product P4 and P5, AcOD, and Cp*Rh in the presence of AcO–. The syn-deuterometalation of F7 and F11 may also generate allylic Rh species η3-F12. The syn-β-H elimination of η3-F12 would generate complex F13, which could release terminally dideuterated product P6, AcOH, and Cp*Rh in the presence of AcO–. The loss of deuterium incorporation was calculated as 2 × 0.99 – 0.53 – 0.51 – 0.84 = 0.10 D. The loss of deuterium occurred during the formation of P1, P4, and P5 by the release of AcOD to the ambient environment.

The rationale for the result of the deuterium-labeling experiment using 1d-d2’ in Fig. 5b is similar (see Supplementary Information, Supplementary Fig. 7), and the loss of deuterium incorporation was calculated as 2 – 0.81 – 0.81 – 0.20 = 0.18 D. The loss of deuterium occurred in the formation of P2’, P3’, and P6’ by the release of AcOD to the ambient environment. The different extent of the deuterium scrambling in these two deuterium-labeling experiments may be caused by the observed small kinetic isotope effect in different syn-β-H/D elimination, syn-hydro-/deuterometalation, and reductive elimination of [Cp*Rh-H/D]+ species.

The high stereoselectivity of the reaction of 1,3-disubstituted allenes could be attributed to the steric effect: The π-σ-π rearrangement from η3-(syn,anti)-G1 to η3-(anti,anti)-G2 is disfavored due to the large allylic 1,3-strain as well as the closer distance between Ar2 and the Rh-Cp* moiety65,66. Based on the information shown in Fig. 7a, there should be another type of allylic Rh intermediate η3-(syn,syn)-H1, which would isomerize to η3-(syn,syn)-H2 via the C–C bond rotation, however, due to the increased allylic 1,3-strain65,66, such isomerization is also disfavored. Overall, the formation of (1E,3E)-stereoisomers would be disfavored (Fig. 7b).

To gain a comprehensive understanding of the reaction mechanism, we conducted detailed density functional theory (DFT) calculations at the SMD(THF)/M06/6-311+g**,SDD//M06/6-31 g*,LANL2DZ level to investigate the reaction energy profiles, utilizing penta-1,2-diene 1’ and phenyl pinacol boronic ester 2b as the model substrates (Fig. 8a) (for more detailed information, see Supplementary Information and Supplementary Data 1). The displayed energies are Gibbs free energies incorporating the solvent effect of THF. The transmetalation of [Cp*RhOAc]+ with 2b would result in the formation of [Cp*RhPh]+. The subsequent coordination of [Cp*RhPh]+ with 1’ provides the intermediate Int1, which was selected as the free energy reference. The initial carbometalation step involves the insertion of the coordinated allenic C=C double bond into the Rh–C bond via a four-membered ring transition state. Due to the possibility of the participation of each C=C bond of penta-1,2-diene 1’, four competing transition states associated with the selective carborhodation step are obtained, which are denoted as TS1_a, TS1_b, TS1_c and TS1_d, separately (Fig. 8b). TS1_a, which leads to the formation of the thermodynamically favorable π-allylic RhIII complex Int2_anti, was found to be the most stable. This step overcomes an energy barrier of 6.6 kcal/mol and exhibits exergonicity of 41.0 kcal/mol, indicating the irreversibility of this step. The subsequent 1,4-hydride transfer proceeds through a stepwise β-hydride elimination/hydrometalation mechanism via a bypassed intermediate, diene-[Cp*RhH]+ complex Int3. Remarkably, the [Cp*RhH]+ species in Int3 would undergo hydrometalation with the 1,3-diene, proceeding through TS3 to yield the π-allylic RhIII complex Int4, as well as traversing TS2 to regenerate the π-allylic RhIII complex Int2_anti, due to the similar free energy barrier observed for TS2 and TS3 relative to Int3 (2.2 kcal/mol versus 1.9 kcal/mol). Meanwhile, Int4 could also reproduce Int3 via TS3, by overcoming an energy barrier of 15.3 kcal/mol. Moreover, in the presence of AcO–, the reductive elimination of Int5 proceeds through TS4 by surpassing a free energy barrier of 4.8 kcal/mol, to produce the thermodynamically more stable complex Int6. The release of AcOH of Int6 generates the diene-Cp*RhI species Int7. Finally, the oxidation of RhI species with CuII in the air regenerates the catalytically active RhIII species and releases the 1,3-diene to complete the catalytic cycle.

In summary, a Rh-catalyzed reaction of allenes with organoboronic reagents has been developed, affording diversified branched 1,3-dienes in an excellent stereoselectivity under very mild reaction conditions, enjoying a very broad substrate scope and tolerating a large variety of functional groups. The reaction uses Cu(OAc)2·H2O as a cocatalyst with the assistance of oxygen from air as the green oxidant to complete the catalytic cycle. Methylation has been achieved by this protocol and the synthetic practicability has been illustrated by the concise synthesis of natural product schinitrienin. A mechanism has been proposed involving reversible β-H elimination as well as hydrometalation and isomerization of the allylic rhodium species. Further studies are currently ongoing in our laboratory.

Methods

General procedure for the synthesis of 2-methyl substituted 1,3-dienes 3 (Method A)

To a Schlenk flask were added Cp*Rh(OAc)2 (17.8 mg, 0.05 mmol, 5 mol%), Cu(OAc)2·H2O (10.0 mg, 0.05 mmol, 5 mol%), methyl pinacol boronic ester 2a (213.0 mg, 1.5 mmol, 1.5 equiv.), H2O (180 μL, 10.0 mmol, 10.0 equiv.), allene 1 (1.0 mmol, 1 equiv.), and THF (5 mL) sequentially without protection from an inert atmosphere. Then the Schlenk flask was connected with an air balloon, and the resulting mixture was stirred at 50 °C until completion of the reaction. The crude reaction mixture was filtered through a short column of silica gel (3 cm), eluted with ethyl acetate (30 mL), and concentrated. The residue was purified by column chromatography on silica gel to afford the pure product 3.

General procedure for the synthesis of 2-aryl or -alkenyl substituted 1,3-dienes 3 (Methods B and C)

To a Schlenk flask were added [Cp*RhCl2]2 (15.5 mg, 0.025 mmol, 2.5 mol%), NaOAc (16.4 mg, 0.20 mmol, 20 mol%), Cu(OAc)2·H2O (10.0 mg, 0.05 mmol, 5 mol%), pinacol boronic ester 2 or organoboronic acid 2’ (1.5 mmol, 1.5 equiv.), H2O (90 μL, 5.0 mmol, 5.0 equiv.), allene 1 (1.0 mmol, 1 equiv.), and THF (5 mL) sequentially without protection from an inert atmosphere. Then the Schlenk flask was connected with an air balloon, and the mixture was stirred at room temperature until completion of the reaction. The crude reaction mixture was filtered through a short column of silica gel (3 cm), eluted with ethyl acetate (30 mL), and concentrated. The residue was purified by column chromatography on silica gel to afford the pure product 3.

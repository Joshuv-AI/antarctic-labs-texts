Response to “A correspondence on bibliometric analysis of vaccination against atherosclerosis”
Authors: Bochao Jia, Rui Wei, Chenlu Yuan, Tao Cheng, Shuai Shi, Yuguang Chu, Yuanhui Hu
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We extend our gratitude to Bai et al. for their critical review and insightful comments on our recently published manuscript, “A Bibliometric Analysis of Vaccination Against Atherosclerosis.”1 In the spirit of academic discourse, we are pleased to address their queries.

Regarding our retrieval strategy, we employed a Topic Search (TS) approach, encompassing terms from “Title (TI),” “Abstract (AB),” “Author Keywords (AK),” and “Keyword Plus (KP)” Bai et al. suggested that the term “KP,” generated by the WoSCC algorithm, could lead to the inclusion of unrelated studies. They argue that focusing solely on “TI,” “AB,” and “AK” terms would yield more accurate and relevant data. It is important to clarify that our retrieval strategy was informed by numerous high-quality bibliometric analyses, many of which also utilized TS.2–5 This approach not only captures the central idea but also simplifies the retrieval Equation. Including KP as a component of TS can enhance the hit rate of articles on related topics.

In contrast to our study, Bai et al. utilized a larger dataset. This discrepancy is likely attributed to variations in human screening processes rather than differences in search strategies. Different research teams may have varying interpretations of the same field and apply different criteria when screening literature. While loose screening criteria offer broader coverage, stringent criteria may result in more accurate and representative literature. Both approaches have their own merits.

Bai et al. also reanalyzed institutions, authors, journals, and highly cited articles based on their dataset and found some differences in results and conclusions compared to our study. These discrepancies can be attributed to variations in retrieval strategies, retrieval times, screening criteria, and software. We welcome scholarly discussions on the diversity of bibliometric analysis results for AS vaccines, as differing conclusions hold unique value and can contribute to the advancement of AS vaccine research. Additionally, we greatly appreciate Bai et al.’s predictions on publication trends, which can help quantify development trends in this field.

We thank Bai et al. once again for their comments and will carefully consider their suggestions in future works of a similar nature.

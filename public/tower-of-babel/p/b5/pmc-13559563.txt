Construction and validation of a β-hydroxybutyrylation-related molecular model for predicting prognosis of papillary thyroid carcinoma
Authors: Xiaoxiao An, Biao Li, Baowei Zhai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPapillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) usually has a favorable prognosis, yet a subset of patients develops persistent, recurrent, or biologically aggressive disease. The clinical relevance of lysine β-hydroxybutyrylation (Kbhb)-related transcriptional programs in PTC remains unclear. Accordingly, this study aimed to characterize Kbhb-related molecular heterogeneity in PTC, construct a prognostic signature, and explore its association with the tumor microenvironment (TME).MethodsTranscriptomic and clinical data from PTC samples within The Cancer Genome Atlas Thyroid Carcinoma (TCGA-THCA) cohort were analyzed to identify Kbhb-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs), define molecular subtypes, construct a prognostic signature, and characterize tumor microenvironmental features. Single-cell RNA-sequencing data from PTC were further used to explore the cellular distribution of representative genes.ResultsWe identified 51 Kbhb-related DEGs in PTC and defined two Kbhb molecular subtypes. The Kbhb_C2 subtype showed shorter progression-free interval (PFI) and a more immune- and stroma-enriched microenvironment. A six-gene prognostic signature comprising TARID, CDSN, PIMREG, KLRC1, SYT13, and NPR3 was then established. High-risk patients had significantly worse PFI in the full, training, and testing cohorts, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year areas under the curve (AUCs) of 0.715, 0.793, and 0.771, respectively, in the full cohort. High-risk tumors also exhibited higher stromal, immune, and ESTIMATE scores, altered immune infiltration, and increased expression of multiple immune checkpoint molecules. Single-cell analysis confirmed distinct cell-type-specific expression patterns of representative genes.ConclusionsKbhb-related transcriptional programs define clinically relevant molecular heterogeneity in PTC and are closely associated with prognosis and TME remodeling. The identified six-gene signature provides a biologically interpretable framework for risk stratification in PTC.Keywords: Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), β-hydroxybutyrylation (Kbhb), prognostic signature, tumor microenvironment (TME), single-cell RNA sequencing (single-cell RNA-seq)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Lysine β-hydroxybutyrylation (Kbhb)-related transcriptional programs defined two molecular subtypes of papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) with distinct progression-free interval and tumor microenvironment profiles. A six-gene signature comprising TARID, CDSN, PIMREG, KLRC1, SYT13, and NPR3 stratified patients by progression risk in the full, training, and testing cohorts.

What is known and what is new?

• PTC generally has a favorable prognosis, but conventional clinicopathological factors do not fully capture its molecular heterogeneity. The clinical relevance of Kbhb-related programs in PTC has remained unclear.

• This study links Kbhb-related transcriptional heterogeneity to prognosis, immune and stromal remodeling, and cell-type-specific expression, and establishes a biologically interpretable prognostic model.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The signature may complement conventional risk stratification. Prospective external validation and direct measurement and functional validation of Kbhb are required before clinical application.

Introduction

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the predominant histological subtype of thyroid cancer and accounts for the vast majority of follicular cell-derived thyroid malignancies. Although most patients with PTC have an indolent clinical course and excellent long-term survival after surgery with or without radioiodine therapy, a clinically relevant subset develops locoregional recurrence, persistent disease, or biologically aggressive behavior, indicating that PTC is not a uniformly low-risk disease (1-3). Current risk stratification still relies mainly on clinicopathological features, but these conventional frameworks do not fully capture the molecular heterogeneity that underlies progression and recurrence, creating a need for more biologically informed biomarkers (3,4).

Among emerging metabolism-linked epigenetic mechanisms, lysine β-hydroxybutyrylation (Kbhb) has attracted increasing attention as a post-translational modification that directly connects cellular metabolic state to chromatin regulation and transcriptional activity. Since Kbhb was first identified as a β-hydroxybutyrate-derived histone mark, subsequent studies have shown that it is dynamically regulated by specific writer and eraser enzymes and participates in diverse pathological contexts, including metabolic disorders, kidney disease, inflammation, and cancer (5-7). In oncology, recent evidence indicates that Kbhb can remodel cancer metabolism, alter transcriptional programs, and promote malignant progression in a context-dependent manner (7,8). However, in thyroid cancer, current epigenetic studies have focused predominantly on canonical histone modifications or other lysine acylations, whereas the biological and clinical relevance of Kbhb-related programs in PTC remains poorly defined (9).

Therefore, this study aimed to determine whether Kbhb-related genes can identify clinically relevant heterogeneity in PTC and to clarify their association with prognosis and the tumor microenvironment (TME). We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0917/rc).

Methods

Bulk transcriptome data acquisition and preprocessing

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Transcriptome data and clinical information for the PTC cohort were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), including 505 primary tumor samples and 59 normal thyroid samples. Raw count matrices were used for differential expression analysis, whereas transcripts per million (TPM)-normalized expression matrices were used for clustering, risk-score calculation, and visualization. Ensembl identifiers were converted to official gene symbols, and duplicated mappings were collapsed by averaging expression values. Low-abundance genes were filtered before downstream analysis, and expression values used for exploratory analyses were log2-transformed as log2(TPM +1). Besides, because all PTC samples within TCGA Thyroid Carcinoma (TCGA-THCA) RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data were generated through the standardized TCGA processing pipeline and normalized to TPM values before analysis, no additional batch-correction procedure was applied. In addition, the GEO dataset GSE33630 was used as an independent expression-level validation dataset for the model genes. The normalized expression matrix and platform annotation file of GSE33630 were obtained from GEO, and probe identifiers were converted to official gene symbols. Genes unavailable after probe-to-gene annotation were excluded from the validation analysis. Kbhb substrate-related genes were obtained from a previously published experimental Kbhb proteomic dataset, which reported 3,248 Kbhb sites across 1,397 substrate proteins. Gene symbols were extracted from the author-provided supplementary substrate table, standardized, and deduplicated, yielding 1,493 unique Kbhb substrate-related genes for analyze (table available at https://cdn.amegroups.cn/static/public/tcr-2026-0917-1.xlsx) (10).

Identification of Kbhb-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

The differential expression analysis was conducted using the limma-voom algorithm. The voom method handles the mean-variance relationship in RNA-seq data by estimating precision weights for linear modeling. Significant DEGs were defined by an adjusted P value (adj. P<0.05) and an absolute log2fold change (|log2FC| >1).

Functional annotation and pathway enrichment

Functional annotation of the relevant gene sets was performed using clusterProfiler. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment was used to characterize the altered biological programs associated with the tumor-normal comparison and the subtype comparison. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment was performed across the biological process, cellular component, and molecular function categories. Terms with P<0.05 were considered significant.

Consensus clustering for Kbhb molecular subtyping

Unsupervised consensus clustering was performed via the ConsensusClusterPlus package to explore molecular heterogeneity within PTC based on 51 differentially expressed Kbhb genes. The partitioning around medoids (PAM) algorithm was employed with Pearson correlation distance. The analysis utilized 1000 iterations and a sampling proportion of 0.8. The optimal number of clusters (k) was determined by evaluating the consensus matrix heatmaps, the cumulative distribution function (CDF) curves, and the relative area change under the CDF curves. To evaluate clustering robustness, average silhouette width and PERMANOVA-based separation metrics were calculated across multiple cluster numbers (K=2–4). The final cluster number was selected based on clustering stability, sample distribution, and biological interpretability.

Construction and internal validation of the prognostic model

The development of the prognostic risk model and the subsequent characterization of the immune landscape were conducted through an integrated computational pipeline. Initially, univariate Cox regression analysis was performed on inter-subtype DEGs to identify potential prognostic genes significantly associated with progression-free interval (PFI) (P<0.05), followed by the application of least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) penalized regression with 10-fold cross-validation to resolve high-dimensional multi-collinearity and select the most robust features. These features were further refined using a multivariate Cox proportional hazards model and optimized through bidirectional stepwise regression based on the Akaike information criterion (AIC), culminating in a weighted risk scoring formula: risk score = expressioni × betai. The TCGA cohort was further evaluated in the full set and in randomly assigned training and testing subsets for internal validation. In addition, GSE33630 was used as an independent dataset for external expression-level validation of the model genes. The normalized expression matrix and platform annotation file were obtained from GEO, and probe IDs were converted to official gene symbols. For genes represented by multiple probes, the average probe expression value was used. The expression levels of the available model genes were compared between PTC and normal thyroid tissues. Because no accessible PFI/relapse-free survival (RFS)/overall survival (OS) information was available in GSE33630, this dataset was used to validate the tumor-normal expression patterns of the model genes rather than the prognostic performance of the risk score.

Characterization of the TME

To characterize the immune context associated with the Kbhb subtypes and the prognostic risk score, ESTIMATE was used to calculate stromal, immune, and ESTIMATE scores, and CIBERSORT was used to infer the relative abundance of 22 immune cell populations.

Clinical nomogram and decision curve analysis (DCA)

A nomogram integrating the risk score and clinical parameters (age, pathologic stage) was constructed using the rms R package. Calibration curves for 1-, 3-, and 5-year PFI were plotted to assess predictive accuracy. DCA was utilized to evaluate the clinical net benefit of the risk model compared to traditional tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging.

Single-cell RNA-seq analysis

Single-cell RNA-seq data from GSE184362 were analyzed with Seurat. Cells were filtered using the thresholds shown in the figure set, normalized, and used to identify 2,000 highly variable genes. Batch effects across samples were corrected with Harmony. Cells were clustered and visualized by uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP), and cell identities were annotated against a reference atlas. Representative model genes were displayed on the UMAP embedding to assess their cell-type-specific expression patterns.

Cell-cell communication analysis

Potential intercellular communication networks were inferred using CellChat. Both the number of inferred interactions and their relative interaction strength were compared across major cell populations. Ligand-receptor pairs were then examined separately for signals transmitted from tumor epithelial cells to immune cells and for signals transmitted from microenvironmental cell populations to tumor cells.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed in R. Continuous variables were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test or Kruskal-Wallis test, as appropriate. Survival differences were evaluated using Kaplan-Meier analysis with log-rank testing. Correlations were assessed using Spearman’s method. Unless otherwise specified, all tests were two-sided and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Differential landscape of Kbhb-related genes in PTC

To define the Kbhb landscape in PTC, we first performed a global differential expression analysis between 505 tumor and 59 normal samples. The volcano plot (Figure 1A) identified a total of 3,895 DEGs, with 1,684 up-regulated and 2211 down-regulated genes. KEGG analysis of these DEGs highlighted pathways related to PI3K-Akt signaling, AMPK signaling, mTOR signaling, FoxO signaling, fatty acid degradation, p53 signaling, butanoate metabolism, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, and broader cancer-associated metabolic programs (Figure 1B). Intersecting the global DEGs with the Kbhb-related gene set yielded 51 overlapping Kbhb-related DEGs (Figure 1C), which were carried forward for subtype discovery.

Identification of Kbhb molecular subtypes and their functional features

Consensus clustering based on the 51 Kbhb-related DEGs identified two principal molecular subtypes, cluster 1 (C1, n=198) and cluster 2 (C2, n=272) (Figure 2A,2B). PCA further confirmed transcriptomic separation between C1 and C2 (Figure 2C). A stratified random DEG-set analysis further showed that the Kbhb substrate-related 51-gene set produced stronger expression-space separation and PFI stratification than most random DEG sets matched for gene number, differential-expression direction, and absolute log2FC distribution (P=0.047; PFI log-rank P=0.049; Figure S1). These findings suggest that the Kbhb substrate-related gene set captured clinically relevant tumor heterogeneity that was not fully reproduced by random tumor-associated DEG sets.

(B) Heatmap showing the expression profile of 51 Kbhb-related DEGs in the two molecular subtypes, with clinical annotations for gender, age, and pathologic stage. (C) PCA showing the transcriptomic separation between the two Kbhb subtypes. C1, cluster 1; C2, cluster 2; DEG, differentially expressed gene; Kbhb, β-hydroxybutyrylation; PC, principal component; PCA, principal component analysis; PTC, papillary thyroid carcinoma.

To explore the biological differences between the two subtypes, we examined enrichment results for subtype-related DEGs. As shown in Figure S2A, KEGG analysis indicated enrichment of cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, cell adhesion molecules, extracellular matrix (ECM)-receptor interaction, PI3K-Akt signaling, chemokine signaling, antigen processing and presentation, and T-helper-cell-related immune pathways. GO enrichment similarly emphasized immune receptor activity, immunoglobulin complex, collagen-containing ECM, adaptive immune responses, leukocyte cell-cell adhesion, and chemotaxis (Figure S2B). Together, these results suggest that Kbhb subtype divergence is linked not only to metabolic signaling but also to marked remodeling of immune and stromal programs.

Prognostic significance and immune landscape of Kbhb subtypes

To determine the clinical relevance of our clustering, we performed Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. When comparing the two-cluster model (Figure 3A), patients in C2 exhibited a significantly shorter PFI than those in C1 (P=0.03). For validation, we also examined the K=3 model (Figure 3B); however, despite an overall P value of 0.009, the survival curves of C1 and C2 in the three-group model showed extensive overlap and lacked a statistically significant difference in prognosis (P>0.05). This lack of distinct separation between all three groups justified our decision to proceed with the more parsimonious and statistically robust K=2 model for all downstream analyses. Further characterization of the TME showed that the C2 subtype was characterized by significantly higher immune and stromal scores (Figure 3C). CIBERSORT analysis (Figure 3D) revealed that C2 had a “hotter” immune landscape, with increased infiltration of M0 macrophages, neutrophils, and CD8 T cells, but reduced levels of memory B cells and resting mast cells, suggesting a more pro-inflammatory yet potentially immunosuppressive environment.

Construction of the Kbhb-related prognostic risk model

To develop a robust prognostic signature, we first identified 1,215 DEGs between C1 and C2 (Figure 4A). These genes were subjected to LASSO Cox regression analysis after univariate Cox regression analysis. The partial likelihood deviance curve (Figure 4B) and coefficient trajectories (Figure 4C) were used to select the most informative features. Finally, multivariate Cox regression analysis (Figure 4D) identified six core genes, TARID, CDSN, PIMREG, KLRC1, SYT13, and NPR3, as independent prognostic factors. Among them, TARID, CDSN, PIMREG, and KLRC1 were identified as significant risk factors, while NPR3 and SYT13 acted as protective factors. After establishing the prognostic model, we further compared the expression levels of the six model genes between PTC and normal thyroid tissues in the TCGA-THCA cohort. All six genes showed significant dysregulation in PTC. Specifically, CDSN and PIMREG were significantly upregulated in tumor tissues, whereas TARID, KLRC1, SYT13, and NPR3 were expressed at lower levels in PTC than in normal thyroid tissues (Figure S3A). Besides, five of the six model genes were detectable on the microarray platform, among which CDSN, PIMREG, and KLRC1 were significantly upregulated, whereas SYT13 was significantly downregulated in PTC compared with normal thyroid tissues; NPR3 showed no significant difference (Figure S3B).

Validation and predictive performance of the risk model

Patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups based on the calculated risk score. The full TCGA-THCA cohort included 470 patients with available PFI information, of whom 45 (9.57%) had PFI events. The training cohort included 329 patients with 29 PFI events (8.81%), and the testing cohort included 141 patients with 16 PFI events (11.35%). Kaplan-Meier curves for the full PTC cohort from TCGA-THCA (Figure 5A) showed a dramatically worse PFI for the high-risk group (P<0.001). This result was consistently validated in both the training set (Figure 5B, P<0.001) and the testing set (Figure 5C, P=0.002). Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis confirmed the model’s strong predictive accuracy; in the full cohort (Figure 5D), the area under the curve (AUC) values for 1-, 3-, and 5-year PFI reached 0.715, 0.793, and 0.771, respectively. High AUC values were also maintained in the training (Figure 5E) and testing (Figure 5F) subsets.

Expression profile and clinical correlation of core risk genes

Expression profiling showed clear differences between the risk groups for the genes displayed in Figure 6A. PIMREG, KLRC1, and CDSN were more highly expressed in the high-risk group, whereas NPR3 and SYT13 were reduced; TARID also showed a significant between-group difference. These patterns are broadly consistent with the multivariable model and indicate that the high-risk phenotype is accompanied by a distinct transcriptional shift rather than isolated change in a single gene.

We then examined the association between the risk score and clinicopathological variables. The score was significantly associated with pathological stage (Figure 6B, P=0.049), with the highest values observed in more advanced disease categories, whereas age and sex showed weaker or nonsignificant differences. To improve clinical applicability, we integrated the molecular score with clinicopathological factors into a nomogram. The calibration curves showed good agreement between predicted and observed PFI probabilities, and DCA indicated that the integrated nomogram provided greater net benefit than stage-based prediction alone across a relevant range of threshold probabilities (Figure 6C).

TME landscape and immune checkpoint analysis

Because the prognostic model was derived from subtype-related genes with strong immune and stromal associations, we next assessed the TME features of the risk groups. High-risk tumors had significantly higher stromal, immune, and ESTIMATE scores than low-risk tumors (Figure 7A). CIBERSORT analysis again demonstrated significant shifts in immune cell composition, with increased M0 macrophages and CD8 T cells among the more prominent changes in the high-risk group (Figure 7B).

Spearman correlation analysis showed that the risk score and several model genes were positively associated with stromal and immune metrics as well as selected immune cell fractions (Figure 7C). In parallel, the high-risk group showed higher expression of the immune checkpoint molecules CTLA4, PDCD1, CD276, LAG3, HAVCR2, TIGIT, SIGLEC15, and PDCD1LG2 (Figure 7D). These data support the interpretation that the adverse risk phenotype is linked to an inflamed but immunoregulatory microenvironment.

Exploratory single-cell analysis of representative model genes in PTC

To define the cellular context of the bulk transcriptomic signals, we analyzed single-cell RNA-seq data from GSE184362. The quality control metrics are summarized in Figure 8A, the 2,000 highly variable genes are shown in Figure 8B, and the elbow plot used for dimensionality selection is shown in Figure 8C. UMAP analysis resolved multiple cell populations, including epithelial cells, T cells, natural killer (NK) cells, myeloid cells, fibroblasts/stromal cells, and endothelial cells (Figure 8D).

Feature plots further demonstrated that representative genes from the prognostic program were distributed in a cell-type-selective manner (Figure S4A-S4E). KLRC1 was concentrated predominantly in lymphocyte-related clusters, whereas NPR3 and CDSN were more evident in non-immune compartments. SYT13 showed a relatively restricted pattern largely within epithelial cells, and TARID also displayed localized expression in only a subset of cells. These observations indicate that the Kbhb-related risk signature reflects coordinated contributions from both tumor and microenvironmental compartments.

To further characterize the cellular expression of the prognostic signature, we performed quantitative single-cell expression analyses using DotPlot and cell-type-level averaged expression heatmaps. As shown in Figure S4F, DotPlot analysis revealed distinct cell-type-specific expression patterns of the representative genes, with KLRC1 predominantly enriched in lymphocyte-related populations, including T and NK cells, whereas CDSN and NPR3 were mainly expressed in epithelial and stromal compartments. SYT13 showed preferential expression in epithelial cells, while TARID exhibited relatively low and sparse expression across multiple cell types.

To further validate these observations quantitatively, we generated a heatmap based on average gene expression across annotated cell populations (Figure S4G). Consistent with the DotPlot results, KLRC1 displayed strong enrichment in immune-related cells, whereas CDSN and NPR3 were enriched in non-immune compartments. These results collectively indicate that the Kbhb-related risk signature reflects heterogeneous contributions from both tumor epithelial cells and tumor microenvironmental components.

Cell-cell communication analysis

CellChat analysis was used to further interrogate intercellular crosstalk within the thyroid TME. The interaction maps in Figure 9A,9B show extensive communication among epithelial, immune, endothelial, and stromal populations at both the level of interaction number and interaction strength.

(B) Network plot showing the relative interaction strength among major cell populations. (C) Dot plot of ligand-receptor interactions representing signaling from epithelial tumor cells to immune-cell populations. When the direction of signaling was considered, tumor epithelial cells transmitted signals to immune cells through ligand-receptor pairs that included PROS1-AXL and MIF-(CD74 + CXCR4) (Figure 9C). Conversely, signals flowing from the microenvironment toward tumor cells were enriched for ECM-related interactions, including COL6A1/A2/A3-associated pairs and LAMA4-CD44, with fibroblasts/stromal cells and myeloid cells representing major sending populations (Figure 9D). These results support the existence of a reciprocal tumor-stroma-immune communication network associated with the Kbhb-related risk phenotype.

Discussion

The increasing incidence of PTC and its biological heterogeneity highlight the need for the identification of novel, high-resolution biomarkers to guide clinical stratification beyond the traditional TNM system. In this study, we provide a comprehensive characterization of the Kbhb landscape in PTC, establishing two distinct molecular subtypes and a robust six-gene prognostic signature, comprising TARID, CDSN, PIMREG, KLRC1, SYT13, and NPR3, that effectively predicts PFIs. By bridging the gap between bulk transcriptomic profiling and single-cell resolution, our findings suggest that Kbhb-related genes serve as critical indicators of the TME architecture and metabolic reprogramming, providing additional insight into understanding the metabolic-epigenetic axis in endocrine malignancies.

One of the most informative findings is that the clinically unfavorable Kbhb_C2 subtype and the high-risk group were both characterized by higher immune and stromal scores rather than by immune exclusion. In thyroid cancer, immune abundance alone does not equate to effective antitumor immunity; the biological consequence of infiltration depends on the cellular composition of the infiltrate, its activation state, and the suppressive architecture of the surrounding stroma (4,11,12). Earlier immunogenomic studies in PTC have likewise shown that neutrophils, macrophage-like states, stromal activation, and checkpoint enrichment often accompany more aggressive disease, whereas the prognostic meaning of CD8+ T-cell infiltration is highly state-dependent rather than uniformly favorable (13,14). Our data fit that model closely. The coexistence of increased CD8+ T cells with elevated CTLA4, PDCD1, LAG3, TIGIT, HAVCR2, CD276, SIGLEC15, and PDCD1LG2 supports an inflamed but immunoregulatory phenotype, in which immune recruitment is present, but effector function is likely constrained by chronic stimulation and checkpoint-mediated suppression.

The six-gene model is biologically informative because it captures several complementary dimensions of PTC progression rather than a single oncogenic pathway. Among these genes, PIMREG and KLRC1 appear to be the most mechanistically informative. PIMREG is a proliferation-associated regulator, and prior studies across multiple cancers have linked its overexpression to cell-cycle progression, aggressive biological behavior, and poor prognosis, supporting its interpretation in our cohort as a marker of a more proliferative and progression-prone tumor state (15). By contrast, KLRC1 encodes NKG2A, an inhibitory receptor expressed mainly on NK cells and subsets of CD8+ T cells, and is therefore better interpreted as a marker of immune restraint than as a tumor-cell-intrinsic driver; this is consistent with the established role of the NKG2A-HLA-E axis as an immune checkpoint that suppresses antitumor cytotoxicity (16,17). TARID adds an epigenetic dimension to the model. Because TARID was originally identified as a long noncoding RNA that directs demethylation and activation of the tumor suppressor TCF21, its inclusion in a Kbhb-centered signature suggests that epigenetic plasticity may contribute to disease heterogeneity in PTC (18). The remaining genes, CDSN, NPR3, and SYT13, are more plausibly interpreted as context-dependent markers of epithelial or stromal state rather than as a single functional module. This interpretation is supported by prior reports showing that CDSN is associated with prognosis and immune infiltration in ovarian cancer, that SYT13 promotes invasive or metastatic behavior in several epithelial cancers, and that NPR3 exhibits tumor-type-dependent prognostic associations across cancers (19-21). Taken together, these observations suggest that the prognostic value of the model arises from the integration of proliferative activity, immune dysfunction, epigenetic remodeling, and cell-state organization.

The single-cell and intercellular communication analyses add an important mechanistic layer by showing that the adverse Kbhb-related phenotype is distributed across interacting cellular compartments. Prior single-cell studies of PTC have emphasized that epithelial populations, lymphocytes, myeloid cells, and fibroblast-like stromal elements form structured ecosystems that evolve with progression and metastatic potential (22). Our results extend that view by demonstrating that representative genes from the prognostic program localize to distinct cell populations and by showing that tumor cells and the microenvironment participate in reciprocal signaling rather than unidirectional tumor-driven remodeling. Tumor-to-immune signaling through MIF-(CD74 + CXCR4) is especially compelling, because a recent single-cell study linked that axis to macrophage suppression in metastatic PTC (23). Conversely, the prominence of collagen- and laminin-related incoming signals from stromal and myeloid compartments is consistent with the increasingly recognized role of fibroblast-mediated ECM remodeling in thyroid cancer progression (24). Taken together, these observations indicate that Kbhb-related risk in PTC is best viewed as an ecosystem property emerging from coordinated tumor-immune-stromal reciprocity.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this is a retrospective study based primarily on public transcriptomic data, and the model has not yet undergone external prospective validation. Second, our analysis infers Kbhb biology from Kbhb-related genes rather than directly measuring Kbhb marks at the proteomic or chromatin level. Accordingly, the present results define a Kbhb-associated transcriptional program, not a direct atlas of Kbhb occupancy or enzymatic activity. Third, the communication network analysis identifies statistically plausible ligand-receptor interactions rather than proving receptor-level causality. Future work should therefore combine external clinical cohorts with Kbhb-resolved proteomics, spatial transcriptomics, and functional perturbation of candidate axes such as MIF-CD74/CXCR4 and PROS1-AXL to establish causality more directly and to define how Kbhb-related states are embedded within tissue architecture.

Conclusions

In summary, this study shows that Kbhb-related transcriptional programs define clinically relevant heterogeneity in PTC and are tightly linked to an inflamed yet immunoregulatory TME, adverse PFI, and multicellular communication networks. The next step is to move from transcriptomic association to direct mechanistic validation, so that the Kbhb axis can be assessed not only as a prognostic framework but also as a biologically grounded entry point for future risk stratification and therapeutic exploration.

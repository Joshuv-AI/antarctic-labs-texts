Methods of measuring body image disturbance in eating disorders: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Maricarmina De Simone, Annapaola Mazza, Mirko Job, Marco Testa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBody image disturbance (BID) is a complex construct that includes cognitive-affective, perceptual and behavioural characteristics, influencing how people perceive and feel about their bodies. An accurate assessment of BID is important for identifying and treating eating disorders (EDs), debilitating psychiatric conditions that compromise individuals’ well-being, leading to high death rates. Methods used to quantify BID in people with EDs vary widely, making it difficult to establish a gold standard measure. Hence, this scoping review will aim at summarising the literature on the methods to assess BID in people with EDs, providing a clearer overview of the topic.Methods and analysisThe scoping review will map and synthesise the BID in individuals with EDs, exploring: (1) Which methods and tools are used to assess BID in individuals with EDs, and are they self-reported or evaluated by clinicians? (2) Which components of BID are assessed? (3) What are the main outcomes measured by these methods and tools? What is their validity, reliability and sensitivity? The population of interest will include individuals of all ages diagnosed with one of the main EDs: anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa and binge ED (BED). Hence, the concept of this review will revolve around the methods and tools for measuring BID in EDs focusing on research conducted either in experimental or clinical settings. Records will be screened by two independent reviewers and any discrepancies will be resolved by a third reviewer. Results will be synthesised and reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. The search is planned to be conducted from November 2025 to September 2026 through multiple databases, including PubMed, PsycINFO, ScienceDirect, Web of Science, PubMed Central and Scopus.Ethics and disseminationFormal ethical approval is not required for this review. The results will provide a clear overview of the assessment methods for BID in EDs. Findings will be disseminated through publication, and presentations at relevant scientific conferences and sharing with professional networks.

Introduction

The body image is a concept that encompasses the mental representation individuals have of their body’s size, shape and form, including the associated emotional responses. The perception of one’s physical self evolves through a complex and emotionally charged process throughout life.1 Cash and Deagle2 proposed a multidimensional view of body image that remains influential.35 This model identifies three key components: (1) cognitive-affective, which includes thoughts and feelings about one’s own body, including self-evaluation and the impact of appearance on self-esteem.6 Disturbances in this area often involve body dissatisfaction and an overemphasis on weight and shape in determining self-esteem; (2) perceptual, which relates to the mental representation of individuals’ body size and shape. Distortions in this area lead to inaccurate size estimates, such as overestimating body size2 and (3) behavioural, referring to body checking and avoidance behaviours.5

Body image disturbance (BID), as defined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorder, fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR),7 is a ‘disturbance in the way in which one’s body weight or shape is experienced’. It is a central symptom in eating disorders (EDs) that significantly impact both onset and maintenance. Since Bruch,8 several psychologists have emphasised the importance of addressing body image issues for effective ED treatments.9

EDs are severe psychiatric conditions that significantly affect individuals’ social, emotional and physical well-being; the leading diagnoses are anorexia nervosa (AN), bulimia nervosa (BN) and binge ED (BED).10 These disorders often lead to various comorbidities and high mortality rates.11 12 BID in individuals with EDs can worsen these conditions, leading to treatment dropout and relapse even after successful treatment.13 Furthermore, it is associated with other psychological issues such as depression, anxiety and low self-esteem.14

Therefore, an accurate assessment of BID is crucial for a comprehensive understanding of EDs. However, due to the multifaceted nature of BID, research frequently struggles to address comprehensively and consistently all its characteristics. Existing assessment tools often capture only selected aspects,15 such as body size overestimation (perceptual distortion) or body dissatisfaction (cognitive-affective component). This approach limits a complete understanding of the phenomenon and reduces the effectiveness of therapeutic interventions.

This scoping review aims to examine the various assessment methods and tools employed to measure BID in ED populations, clarifying available approaches.

Methods and analysis

This scoping review will map and summarise the different methods used to measure BID in people with EDs. A scoping review was chosen as it allows comprehensive exploration of a fragmented literature.16 The review will follow the guidelines outlined by the Joanna Briggs Institute Reviewer’s Manual17 and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for scoping reviews checklist and explanation (PRISMA-SCR).18 The literature search is planned to be conducted from November 2025 to September 2026.

Research team

The search strategy will be formulated by a multidisciplinary team comprising a psychologist, a psychiatric rehabilitation therapist and a bioengineer. The psychologist will provide expertise on the psychological aspects of body image and ED-related mental health; the psychiatric rehabilitation therapist will contribute practical knowledge of therapeutic interventions for EDs; the bioengineer will support the evaluation of objective measurement methods.

Identification of relevant studies

We will follow the ‘Population, Concept and Context’ model proposed by the Joanna Briggs Institute17 to clearly describe the elements of the inclusion criteria, considering the population involved, the concept of interest and the study context (table 1). More specifically, the included studies will provide data on individuals of all genders affected by AN, BN or BED (population) to extract a comprehensive description of the various methods and tools for measuring BID in people with EDs (concept). The review will focus on the literature specifically concerning methods and tools for measuring BID both in experimental and clinical settings (context). This review will include both observational and experimental studies to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of the art on the topic. Search strategy and information sources

Following the recommendations of19 a three-step search strategy will be implemented . Initially, a preliminary search will be pilot-tested on PubMed and the PubMed Mesh Library20 to identify keywords from abstracts and titles of papers deemed relevant to the topic to be included in the final search strategy. This primary search query (see Online supplemental material, appendix I) will be independently peer-reviewed by two of the coauthors using the PRESS 2015 Evidence-Based Checklist21 and subsequently adjusted according to their recommendations. Search history and peer review documentation of the search strategy will be available on request. A broad range of search terms and manual filters for Species (Human) will be employed to balance the thoroughness and specificity of the search with feasibility considerations. No date restrictions will be applied to the search strategy.

A secondary search strategy will be developed by adapting the primary search strategy for all additional included databases. To gather further sources, a third strategy will involve electronically searching the journals with the highest impact factor in the field of EDs, for example, the International Journal of Eating Disorders, to scan the reference lists of all identified articles and reviews. Unlike systematic reviews, scoping reviews are characterised by an iterative search process rather than a fixed and pre-established one. For this reason, the search strategy and the inclusion/exclusion criteria may be modified and updated during the development of the review, following feedback from the research team, if required.

Data management

We will use an open-source reference management software to organise all the identified articles and streamline the citation process (eg, Zotero, Mendeley). Rayyan software22 will be used to facilitate the selection and review of articles, allowing for a more efficient and collaborative screening process.

Electronic databases

The search will be conducted across various electronic databases, including PubMed, PsycINFO, ScienceDirect, Web of Science, PubMed Central and Scopus. The International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews PROSPERO23 database has been previously searched to check whether there are any reviews already registered on this topic. In addition, grey literature (eg, Google Scholar, direct contact with experts in the field) and the reference lists of all relevant studies will also be searched.

Study selection

This study adopts a two-stage screening process to identify relevant articles. The first stage prioritises a calibration step to ensure inter-rater agreement on eligibility criteria. A random sample of 10% of PubMed results will undergo prescreening by two independent reviewers, a psychologist and a psychiatric rehabilitation therapist (MDS and AM). This pilot test serves as a crucial calibration exercise, aiming to achieve an inter-rater agreement exceeding 90% on inclusion and exclusion criteria. In the case of initial phase discrepancies, the criteria will be perfected with a pilot round to ensure consistent application.24 Following successful calibration, formal screening will commence. The titles and abstracts of all retrieved records will be independently screened by the two reviewers. The expertise of the different reviewers will enhance the efficiency of the screening.25 26 Discrepancies arising during this stage will be resolved through collaborative discussion between the reviewers (MDS and AM). If consensus remains elusive, a third reviewer (MJ) will be brought in to adjudicate the disagreement.24 A reviewer (MDS or AM) will gather basic details about the studies' features and context, along with supplementary information, using a standardised Excel spreadsheet (refer to online supplemental appendix II), which could undergo adjustments following the initial trial run of charting exercises. The second stage focuses on full-text review and definitive eligibility assessment. Articles classified as ‘uncertain’ during the preliminary identification stage will undergo a thorough full-text review for a final determination based on the established eligibility criteria.

A standardised Excel spreadsheet will be used to extract data from a convenience sample of five included articles (see Online supplemental material, appendix II). This pilot exercise, involving the two independent reviewers, will serve as a quality control measure to assess inter-rater agreement on the extracted information. Based on reviewer feedback, the spreadsheet will be refined to optimise the data collection process and achieve an inter-rater agreement exceeding 90%.27

Following this pilot exercise, data extraction from each included study will be performed by a single reviewer using the refined spreadsheet. A second reviewer will then meticulously verify the extracted data’s correspondence with the research questions and the overall goals of the review. Any disagreements that arise during this verification process will be resolved by a third reviewer, ensuring the accuracy and integrity of the extracted data.

This two-stage screening process, coupled with meticulous pilot testing for both screening and data extraction, guarantees a systematic and reliable approach to article selection and data collection. This rigorous methodology minimises potential biases and enhances the overall quality of the review, ultimately leading to robust and trustworthy findings.16

Data synthesis

A descriptive analysis will be conducted, presenting results both numerically and graphically where possible. Extracted data (eg, frequency, percentage, mean, SD, median) will be presented in ad hoc prepared tables.

Data collection, summary and reporting of results

Results from the included studies will be grouped and summarised in separate paragraphs, each focusing on a distinct aspect explored within the body image concept (ie, cognitive-affective, perceptual and behavioural). More specifically, the following information will be extracted from each included study: Study characteristics (author(s), year, country, study design) and Population characteristics (sample size, age range/mean, sex, type of ED) to contextualise research; BID measurement tool, to categorise and compare the different assessment methods; type of assessment of both BID and EDs (self-reported, clinician), to consider potential personal biases; psychometric properties, to evaluate the quality and reliability of the measurement methods; outcome measures, to summarise quantitative findings and compare results across studies and additional notes to capture other potential influences.

Methodological quality assessment

Due to the diverse methodologies and study designs in this field, this scoping review aims to provide a broad overview of the evidence, regardless of study quality. The emerging nature of the topic makes it challenging to evaluate the methodological quality of each study. Unlike systematic reviews, the primary goal is to map and synthesise available evidence rather than to present the best available evidence.17

Long-term path planning with optimal deployment of a charging station for monitoring photovoltaic solar farms
Authors: Yong Huang, Zhiyan Chen, Jing Chu, Haoran Wang, Siliang Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractQuadrotor technology has become increasingly important in the field of photovoltaic (PV) solar farm monitoring, but short battery life is one of the primary factors limiting its further application. To address above issue, this study proposes a linear temporal logic (LTL)-based path planning algorithm that considers the need for charging together with multiple visits to PV equipments, a single visit to communication equipment, and the avoidance of restricted regions, which enables the long-term monitoring of PV solar farms. Particularly, the location of a charging station can be determined optimally and efficiently by a heuristic algorithm based on the nonlinear programming and branch and bound (NLP-BB) algorithm. The simulation results indicate that the proposed method effectively solves the long-term monitoring path planning problem that is coupled with the optimal deployment of the charging station.Subject terms: Aerospace engineering, Power stations

Introduction

Photovoltaic (PV) power generation, recognized for its sustainability, has become increasingly viable globally due to falling costs and rising efficienc1. China, benefiting from excellent solar irradiance, is poised to capitalize on this trend. A prime example of this potential is Wuwei, Gansu. This region enjoys over 3200 h of sunshine annually, providing ideal conditions for low-cost PV power generation. Consequently, Wuwei has experienced a boom in its PV industry, marked by the construction of numerous large-scale solar farms.

In pursuit of higher power generation and economic benefits, the scale of PV solar farms is increasing rapidly day by day, and the capacity of a single PV farm has reached hundreds of megawatts. By the end of 2023, Wuwei City alone reached a staggering installed capacity of over 4.65 million kilowatts, establishing it as one of the premier PV industry hubs in northwest China. Despite these advancements, the operation of vast PV farms presents unique challenges. Traditional manual inspection method, characterized by infrequent and fixed monitoring points, fall short of addressing the extensive monitoring demands of large-scale operations. Furthermore, the harsh natural environments in Gansu Province, such as frequent sandstorms, necessitate continuous, high-frequency monitoring to accurately assess the operational status and power generation levels of the farms. Additionally, PV solar farms typically cover a large area, and the limited endurance of quadrotors poses a challenge for persistent monitoring. Thus, devising a method for comprehensive, frequent, and cost-effective monitoring of massive PV installations is a pressing issue in the industry.

To address these challenges, recent studies have explore the potential of automated monitoring technologies, such as quadrotors, in enhancing PV solar farm surveillance. For instance, Luo Xuejing, Li Xiaoxia and their team have developed an innovative algorithm that leverages Bezier curves and particle swarm optimization to tackle the challenge of detecting missing areas in asset assessment and defect identification in PV during aerial missions2. Similarly, Ding Yifan, Cao Rui, and their colleagues have introduced an advanced path planning algorithm specifically designed for mountainous PV stations. Their approach integrates density-based clustering with Boustrophedon path planning and Bezier curves to optimize both clustering and navigation3. These contributions lay a theoretical foundation for quadrotor-assisted coverage monitoring. However, existing research primarily focuses on conventional path planning that incorporates basic obstacle avoidance, which may not adequately meet the complex monitoring demands of large-scale PV farms4–7. More sophisticated path planning methods are required to tackle these advanced monitoring challenges.

This paper examines the monitoring complexities of ultra-large-scale PV solar farms located in Wuwei region, which are characterized by their vast number of PV panels and extensive monitoring needs. The objective is to achieve long-term, comprehensive monitoring that accounts for the spatial layout of the farm, the endurance constraints of drones, and the intricate temporal requirements of monitoring tasks. It is quite challenging for traditional path planning algorithms to satisfy complex temporal requirements of flight tasks such as “monitoring PV panel areas A and B in sequence”, “frequently monitoring PV panel areas A, B and C, requiring monitoring of area B before accessing area C”, “autonomously avoiding no-fly zone D” and so on. To navigate these complexities, this study employs Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) to articulate and manage the temporal aspects of monitoring tasks8, aiming to bridge the gap left by conventional methodologies.

This study employs an LTL-based path planning algorithm that utilizes simple logic operations in LTL language to express complex temporal requirements and constraints. These constraints encompass monitoring time, position, and angle requirements, as well as the necessity to avoid obstacles and no-fly zones during task execution. These factors significantly influence the quality and outcomes of the path in practical monitoring tasks. The proposed algorithm is capable of analyzing various temporal logics described by LTL formulas and translating them into behavior constraints for the quadrotor. This process generates a flight path that adheres to temporal requirements, thereby ensuring the quadrotor’s safety during the mission. The finding of this study address the monitoring needs of large-scale PV farms in the Wuwei region and offer insights for resolving quadrotor path planning challenges in similar regions and environments.

Simultaneously, considering the limited endurance of quadrotor, long-term monitoring necessitates addressing charging and replenishment issues9–11. This paper introduces an innovative path planning algorithm designed for quadrotor surveillance of solar farms, integrated with the optimal deployment strategy for charging stations. The algorithm combines the nonlinear programming and branch and bound (NLP-BB) algorithm, the greedy algorithm, and the LTL-based path planning algorithm. Notably, the NLP-BB algorithm represents an extension of the branch and bound (BB) algorithm, tailored specifically for addressing mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) problems. The BB algorithm was originally devised by Land and Doig in 1960 to address challenges in mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) problems12. In 1965, Dakin further broadened the application of the BB algorithm to include mixed-integer convex nonlinear programming problems, significantly advancing the development of BB algorithms13. Subsequently, in 1985, Gupta and Ravindran applied the BB algorithm to tackle mixed integer convex nonlinear programming problems, thoroughly evaluating its feasibility and providing a detailed computational process14. While considering spatial constraints and temporal logic constraints, the mixed-integer nonlinear programming model in this paper also takes into account the endurance limit of quadrotors. According to the monitoring task requirements and quadrotor parameters, the optimal deployment of charging station and charging strategy are generated, ensuring the quadrotor can charge continuously during the task and efficiently accomplish the monitoring task. This algorithm not only considers the energy constraints of quadrotor but also takes into account the path length and time consumption, generating the optimal charging solution to support long-term monitoring. We hope this work can promote the automated application of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) over a wider area and longer time.

In summary, while proposing a path planning method that meets temporal logic requirements, this paper also addresses the endurance problem of quadrotor. By optimizing the charging station location and charging strategy with optimization algorithms, the optimal charging solution can be dynamically generated to support long-term monitoring tasks. With the assistance of quadrotors, ultra-large PV solar farms can achieve low-cost, high-frequency, and comprehensive state monitoring, which will help improve their operating efficiency and management level. Compared to manual monitoring, quadrotor monitoring can detect faults or performance changes in PV panels faster, prevent accidents in time, and enhance the safety and power generation of PV power farms. In summary, this study provides a theoretical foundation and technical methods for the efficient and effective development and industrial monitoring application of PV solar farm, which is also the starting point and main value of the research.

Preliminaries

Cell decomposition

The Cell Decomposition algorithm15 is a path planning technique that relies on space division. It enables the transformation of initial continuous space problem into a finite state representation. The fundamental concept of this algorithm is as follows:

First, the space is partitioned into numerous interconnected smaller cells that operate independently while still encompassing the entire space. Second, a weighted navigation graph is established in each small cell and between them to represent the cost information within the cell and between adjacent cells. Third, when searching for a path, an approximate route is first identified at a high level across cells, linking the starting and ending cells. Fourth, a detailed path from the entrance to the exit of each crossing cell is then searched. Finally, all the detailed paths are connected to form a complete and feasible path.

The main advantages of this algorithm are: (1) It saves memory by only requiring the storage of the navigation information of each cell instead of the entire space. (2) It has high search efficiency. The cross- regional path search can quickly locate the target cell. Then a fine-grained search within the local cell can be conducted. (3) It is easy to update. When changes in the spatial structure only affect certain parts of the cells, only the relevant cell information needs to be updated.

The Cell Decomposition algorithm is a relatively classic and practical path planning method and has good applications in field such as robotics and unmanned driving.

LTL

LTL16 is a modal logic type that specifically addresses the representation of temporal relationships. Extensively employed for model checking, LTL is particularly well-suited for reactive systems that respond to external inputs, such as messages or events, in conjunction with the current state. It encompasses multiple features, including the capability to describe requirements of various complex tasks and a close resemblance to natural language, which facilitates easy convert between the two.

The components of an LTL formula encompass a collection of atomic propositions (AP), diverse boolean operators [¬(Negation),∧(Conjunction),∨(Disjunction)] for expressing static properties, and temporal operators [G(Always),F(Eventually),X(Next),U(Until)] for expressing dynamic properties. To address the challenges of persistent surveillance, this paper first develop models of the environment and the behavior of the quadrotor, focusing specifically on the regions of interest. The quadrotor’s movement across the map is abstracted using the Cell Decomposition algorithm, which represents movement as a transition between nodes on a topological map19. The PV farm can be seen as a rectangular environment Ev⊂R2 in 2-D. Both representations are interchangeable, and algorithms exist to convert between them in any dimension space20.

In this paper, methods for triangular decomposition are expanded to establish the graph abstraction of the partitioned environment. Specifically, techniques such as Delaunay triangulations25, which operate on a set of points instead of cells, are employed. The technique connects these points to form triangles, aiming to maximize the smallest angle of the generated triangles while ensuring the circumcircle of each triangle does not encompass any additional points from the input set. However, in our specific scenario, Delaunay triangulation is unsuitable due to the possibility of some triangles intersecting both the free space and the regions, thus occupying partial areas in each. To address this, constrained Delaunay triangulations are adopted, which modify the standard method by preserving certain predefined line segments26. Definition 1

A finite TS is represented by the tuple TS=(C,Act,→,c0,AP,L), where C is a finite set of states, representing nodes within a topological map; Act is a finite set of actions; →⊆C×Act×C is the set of state-action-state transition relationships, representing node-to-node transitions; c0⊆C is the initial state, representing the initial position; AP is a finite set of atomic propositions; and L:C→2AP is a labeling function that assigns a set of atomic propositions to each states.

Important information about an agent operating in a real-world environment, such as the path distance between nodes and the presence of obstacles, can be described by introducing TS. In the paper, methods for triangular decomposition are extended to establish the graph abstraction of the partitioned environment, which is divided into N regions and is represented as sets C={c0,c1,…,cN}. Definition 5

The product automaton is defined as AP=TS⊗Aφ=(Q,q0,δP,FP), where Q=C×S represents the set of states; q0=c0×s0 represents the set of initial states; δP=Q×Q represents the transition relation which is defined by ((c,s),(c′,s′))∈δP if and only if (c,c′)∈→ and (s,L(c),s′)∈δP; additionally FP=S×F represents the final (accepting) state.

The product automaton AP establishes a relationship between quadrotor capabilities (represented by TS) and the satisfaction of a given formula (denoted by Aφ). consequently, a transition in AP indicates a matching condition between a transition in TS and a transition in Aφ. Similar to the acceptance condition in Aφ, the product automaton only accepts an infinite run if and only if it encounters the set of final states FP an infinite number of times. A motion plan satisfying φ exists, if and only if AP has at least one accepting trace.

Therefore, it is essential to search for the accepted runs ρ of AP that display a specific structure consisting of a prefix followed by an infinite number of repeated suffixes. In this context, a prefix refers to a finite trajectory starting from an initial state and concluding at an accepting state (excluding the accepting state itself). On the other hand, a suffix is a trajectory that begins at the previous accepting state and concludes at the subsequent accepting state. Crucially, it is worth noting that if AP has at least one accepted run originating from an initial state, it must also possess at least one accepted run in the prefix-suffix form, as detailed in the referenced work29.

By employing the following approach, we select the shortest runs among all the accepted runs. To start, we ascertain the shortest prefixes by assessing the shortest paths from each initial state to all final states. Subsequently, we identify the shortest suffixes from these final states. Finally, we select the run with the fewest states in the concatenation of the prefix and the suffix from all such runs. To accomplish this, we utilize Dijkstra’s algorithm30 to determine the shortest path in each of the aforementioned scenarios.

To convert the sequence of cell paths into a trajectory, a method is adopted that involves selecting the midpoint of the shared side between adjacent cells as the trajectory point. In this approach, the starting point, trajectory points, and target point of the mobile quadrotor are connected in a sequential manner following the arrangement of the cells.

In simpler terms, this section describes the process of building a system to complete a specific task in a PV solar farm. The first step is to construct TS=(C,Act,→,c0,AP,L) using the existing environment model. Next, we convert the given LTL formula φ into an NBA Aφ=(S,s0,∑B,δ,F) to build the product automaton AP=TS⊗Aφ=(Q,q0,δP,FP). Then, Dijkstra’s algorithm is implemented to search for the optimal path from the initial state to the final state on the network topology that can support the task. Finally, the optimal path is mapped back to the initial TS model, which comprises the set of rules that governs how the system operates. By following this process, the system is able to efficiently complete the task while operating within the constraints of the PV solar farm.

The LTL formulas are employed to help satisfy the temporal requirements of a quadrotor monitoring task. However, due to distributed layouts and vast coverage area of the PV farms, the monitoring task presents challenges for quadrotor with limited endurance. This approach enhances mission efficiency and monitoring quality while avoiding the risk of mission failure due to low battery levels.

When the length of the flight trajectory exceeds a threshold value, denoted as p, the quadrotor shall make a visit to the charging station’s region. To describe this constraint as an LTL formula, we propose the following strategy. Initially, the quadrotor starts from the charging station with a fully charged battery and executes the task specification in the LTL formula φ1, and the real-time length of the flight trajectory is l. When l≥p is reached, the quadrotor stops the task specification and heads to the charging station area for battery replenishment or replacement starting from its current location. Then it executes a new task specification without including the single-visit area of φ1. This results in a detailed LTL formula φ2.

As stated in Problem 2, establishing an approximate model for the maximization of the visit number n is a crucial concern addressed in this paper, particularly in cases where the location of the charging station is unknown. The previous section provided a thorough description of the path planning algorithm based on LTL. The optimal deployment problem based on the LTL path planning is difficult to establish as a precise mathematical function for the flight trajectory. Currently, the main approach in research is to use an optimization model based on MILP to determine the maximum profit of deploying solar power stations with energy storage in different regions32. Considering the complexity of the model presented in this paper, the MILP is further generalized to a MINLP problem. Therefore, this paper addresses the issue by proposing a heuristic path planning algorithm for quadrotor monitoring of PV farms coupled with the deployment of the charging station. The algorithm firstly obtains an approximate MINLP model of the original objective function based on the LTL formula φ2 and solves for the minimum value P1(xi,yj) of the approximate model using the NLP-BB algorithm. Secondly, taking P1(xi,yj) as the starting node, the path planning algorithm described in the previous section is employed to determine the distance of the path among the nine surrounding nodes, including the starting node. It aims to locate the local optimal point and subsequently conduct a comprehensive search in all possible directions. Finally, the approximate global optimal target node satisfying the local optimal can be found.

Conclusion and future work

This research presents a path planning algorithm that utilizes a formulation based on LTL to address the problem of long-term quadrotor monitoring in PV solar farms. The algorithm takes into account the requirements of multiple visits to PV equipment, one visit to communication equipment, avoidance of no-fly zones, and quadrotor recharging. Furthermore, the study also considers the deployment of charging stations to address the quadrotor’s endurance limit. An efficient heuristic algorithm has been developed to determine the optimal deployment location of the charging station. The simulation results provide evidence of the effectiveness and efficiency of the algorithm in addressing the challenges of long-term monitoring problem for quadrotor and the charging station deployment problem.

Future research can be carried out in various fields. In increase monitoring efficiency, the collaborative monitoring problem by multiple quadrotors might be considered. Alternatively, intelligent algorithms can be explored to address the charging station deployment problem and to improve algorithm efficiency and accuracy.

Enhanced hemagglutination titers of avian influenza A (H5N1) viruses grown in eggs by replacing the noncoding regions of neuraminidase
Authors: Chi Hung Nguyen, Hang Thi Thu Hoang, Nam Trung Nguyen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
During the fifth month of 2024, many outbreaks of the avian influenza virus occurred in Vietnam, during which the first human infected with avian influenza A (H5N1) was reported. Therefore, influenza control strategies including vaccine production are necessary for poultry and improving the number of candidate vaccine viruses that do not replicate well in eggs for vaccine production. We previously reported that the ST-2009 virus had a negative propagation capacity in eggs. Chimeric neuraminidase (NA) genes were generated by replacing the 5' and 3' packaging signals of PR8 A/PR/8/34 strain with the coding region of the NA genes of ST-2009. Compared to those of the control, the new viruses containing chimeric NA genes exhibited significantly greater hemagglutination titers in embryonated chicken eggs. This study showed that the packaging efficiency of new viruses containing chimeric NA and replacing chimeric NAs improved the hemagglutination titer of viruses that were unable to propagate in eggs.

Introduction

Recent outbreaks of avian influenza A (H5N1) were reported in 2024 in the provinces of Vietnam. One human infection with an influenza A virus in a 21-year-old male occurred with no underlying conditions. Therefore, influenza control strategies including vaccine production are necessary for poultry.1,2 The eight vRNAs (890 - 2,341 nucleotides) contained an extended central coding region (open reading frame [ORF]) flanked by segment-specific noncoding regions (NCRs) of 19 - 58 nucleotides. Within these NCRs, the extreme 5' and 3' termini of nucleotides 12 and 13 (U12 and U13) are highly conserved (positive sense throughout the paper).3,4 The partially complementary termini permit the vRNAs to coil back on themselves to form a panhandle structure. They are the necessary promoter elements for initiating replication and transcription.5 Random and segment-specific models proposed for how influenza A vRNAs are inserted into progeny virions, but there is additional evidence for this segment-specific model.6 Several studies have shown that the 3' and 5' terminal regions, NCRs and some ORF ends, have segment-specific packaging signals.7-10

In the past few years, many people have attempted to use the terminal sequences of the hemagglutinin (HA) or neuraminidase (NA) of the PR8 A/PR/8/34 virus to increase the yield of candidate vaccine viruses in eggs.11,12 This study contributed to the understanding of the role of PR8 NA in the production of candidate vaccine viruses with low yield propagation in eggs.

Materials and Methods

Viruses.

The recombinant viruses (RVs) accommodating HA and NA from A/duck/Vietnam/ST0907/2009 H5N1 (clade 1.1, now 1.1.2; namely, ST-2009) and IBT-RG01 (A/duck/Vietnam/ST-2009-H5+HT-2014-N1) were produced on the PR8 background as previously described and used as controls.13,14 All laboratory works for implementation of the influenza A viruses were performed in biosafety facilities in accordance with the regulations of Institute of Biotechnology (IBT, Hanoi, Vietnam) under the decision number 2008/QD-VHL.

Construction and cloning of chimeric ST-2009-NA and IBT-RG01.

The 3' forward primer 5'-CCTTGTTCTACT AGC-3' with a segment-specific reverse primer was use in a reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction test to deduce the 5' terminal sequence.12 Similarly, the 3' terminal sequence was determined by sequencing polymerase chain reaction products generated via reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction using a segment-specific forward primer and the 5' reverse primer 5'-CCTGCTTTTGCTAGT-3'.12 The chimeric NAs were created by exchanging the terminal sequences of the NA and PR8 genes via mega-primer mutagenesis.15 Then, the constructed chimeric NAs were inserted into the virus rescue pHW2000 plasmid following the methods of Hoffmann et al.16

Generation of ST-2009- and IBT-RG01-containing chimeric NA vaccine viruses by reverse genetics.

The generation of re-assorted 6 + 2 vaccine viruses involved the co-transfection of 293T cells with eight cDNA plasmids. Two plasmids were encoded using the chimeric NA and HA of ST-2009 or IBT-RG1, together with the six gene plasmids of PR8. The cell supernatant (200 L per egg) from transfected 293T cells was collected and inoculated into 10 to 11 day embryonated chicken eggs, where the viruses were propagated at 37.00 ˚C for 48 hr before being titered by hemagglutination.17

Hemagglutination assay.

The HA assay was performed as previously described.17 Briefly, the specimens were diluted in a 96-well plate with a V-bottom. To each well on the plate, 50.00 L of 0.100 M phosphate-buffered saline (pH : 7.20) was added, followed by the addition of 50.00 L of egg fluid to the first well in each row that needed to be tested. This result in in a 1 : 2 dilution of test material. A positive control antigen and a cell control were included. Using a pipette, the contents of the first well were mixed, 50.00 L was transferred from the first well, and the mixture was placed in the second well. Two-fold dilutions of the virus suspension were made across the entire row. The excess 50.00 L was discarded after the last row. All the wells had a final volume of 50.00 L after this step. Then, 50.00 μL of 0.50% chicken erythrocyte suspension was added to each well. The erythrocytes were allowed to settle for 20 - 30 min.

Results

Construction and cloning of chimeric NAs.

The construction of NA gene segment-containing chimeric NAs from ST-2009 involved the incorporation of packaging signals from the PR8 NA. Specifically, a 41-nucleotide (nt) package from PR8 NA, consisting of a 20-nt NCR and a 21-nt region from the ORF, was added at the 5' end. Additionally, a 67-nt packaging signal from the PR8 NA, comprising a 39-nt region from the 3' end of the ORF and a 28-nt NCR, was added at the 3' end. This resulted in a PR8-NA-PR8 configuration (Fig. 1). The chimeric constructs utilized the ORFs of the NA genes from the ST-2009 virus, with no anticipated alterations to the amino acid sequence. To prevent translation initiation from the ORF of PR8, the ATG codon of the PR8 NA sequence in the chimeric construct was altered to GCG (Fig. 1). Consequently, RV combinations of nonchimeric NA (RV1), NA-PR8 (RV2), PR8-NA (RV3) and PR8-NA-PR8 (RV4) were obtained. The segments of chimeric constructs were successfully inserted into the virus rescue plasmid pHW2000.

Growth of the new chimeric viruses in eggs.

To evaluate the impact of PR8 chimeric NA on the ST-2009 virus, a set of RVs based on PR8 was generated in 293T cells. These reverse genetic strains included ST-2009 containing NA or chimeric NA along with the six remaining genes from PR8. The rescued viruses were subjected to a single passage in eggs, and their titer was determined using hemagglutination analysis. Virus containing RV1 had a negative HA titer.13 The results in Table 1 show that RV2 with the PR8 NA at the 3’ end, RV3 with the PR8 NA at the 5’ end and RV4 with the PR8 NA at both the 5’ and 3’ ends had significantly greater virus titers of 3.12 (log2), 3.35 (log2) and 4.21 (log2), respectively, than did RV1. In addition, we investigated whether the PR8 chimeric NA had an effect on propagation of the IBT-RG01 virus in eggs. The chimeric NA of IBT-RG01 was constructed as described above for ST-2009 (Fig. 1). The segments of chimeric constructs were successfully inserted into the virus rescue plasmid pHW2000. Consequently, combinations of nonchimeric NA (RV5), NA-PR8 (RV6), PR8-NA (RV7) and PR8-NA-PR8 (RV8) were obtained. The rescued viruses were subjected to a single passage in eggs, and their titer was determined using hemagglutination analysis. The results showed that the hemagglutination titer in the eggs of chimeric NAs was increased from 6.22 (log2; RV5) to 7.08 (log2; RV6), 7.17 (log2; RV7) and 8.15 (log2; RV8) after the replacement of chimeric NAs (Table 2).

Discussion

The findings of our study indicated that the inclusion of the ST-2009 NA ORF, which is surrounded by the PR8 NA 5' and 3' packaging signals, has a substantial impact on the hemagglutination titer of the virus. Specifically, the titers were assessed through a hemagglutination test and were found to increase significantly compared to those of the original construct of the ST-2009 strain. In detail, the chimeric constructs with the PR8 NA at the 3’ end or the 5’ end had hemagglutination titers lower than those with both PR8 packing signals. In addition, we tested the IBT-RG01 virus. The results were similar to our experiments on the ST-2009 virus, with the chimeric NA construct having packing signals at both the 5’ and 3’ ends resulting in the highest hemagglutination titer. Thus, replacing the PR8 NCR for vaccine viruses with low yield growth in eggs was critical. Specifically, how the packaging signals interacted with each other and affected the incorporation of genes into virions is still controversial. Additional work is necessary to determine the effects of these signals. In conclusion, the packaging efficiency of new viruses containing chimeric NA was significantly greater than that of the control virus. Our study showed an improvement in the HA titer of new viruses containing chimeric NAs that may be useful for vaccine candidates with low yield problems.

Introduction: sodium oxybate and cardiovascular safety: reconciling 20 years of clinical and research experience with sodium intake guidelines
Authors: Clete A Kushida
Published: 2026

This series of supplement articles offers a well-rounded exploration of the evolving landscape of sodium oxybate (SO) therapy, with a focus on cardiovascular (CV) safety, guideline interpretation, and individualized patient care. Drawing from clinical trials, real-world data, and global perspectives, these resources are designed to equip clinicians with the insights needed to navigate therapy selection, monitor sodium intake, and engage patients in meaningful discussions about their narcolepsy care.

The first article provides a historical overview of the CV safety of SO, examining data from randomized clinical trials, long-term studies, observational analyses, and postmarketing surveillance. It contextualizes the role of sodium in SO formulations and assesses whether this contributes meaningfully to CV risk. Although narcolepsy is associated with a variety of comorbid conditions that may elevate CV risk, the available evidence does not indicate a significant increase in adverse CV events due to SO. This review supports thoughtful, individualized sodium management without broad restrictions.

The second article critically evaluates the scientific basis of US sodium intake guidelines, highlighting limitations in applying population-wide targets to individual patients. Observational data increasingly suggest a J-shaped relationship between sodium intake and CV outcomes, with the lowest risk seen at moderate intake levels (3–5 g/day). These findings challenge the assumption that lower is always better—particularly for patients with comorbidities or additional sodium exposure. Special attention is given to pharmacologic sodium from medications like SO, which can meaningfully raise total daily intake. To broaden the perspective, the article explores European guidance, which generally favors individualized targets over universal restriction. Organizations, such as EFSA, ESC, and ESH, recommend stricter sodium limits only for patients with hypertension, CV disease, or kidney disease. This approach reinforces the need for a flexible, patient-centered framework for sodium management.

The final article emphasizes a patient-centered approach to selecting among SO formulations. With 3 options now available—immediate-release, low-sodium, and extended-release—clinicians must balance symptom control, lifestyle factors, comorbidities, and patient preferences. This article explores how shared decision-making and empathy play a central role in optimizing adherence and improving outcomes. Real-world data highlight the importance of convenience and simplicity in treatment regimens, reinforcing the value of aligning clinical decisions with what matters most to the patient.

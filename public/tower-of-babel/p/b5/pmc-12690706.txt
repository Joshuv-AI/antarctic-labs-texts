RART-09 A rare fourth ventricular high-grade meningioma in a paediatric patient.
Authors: Hammed Oshola, Abimbola Adeniran, Ikenna Alban
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMeningiomas are the most common primary intracranial tumour. They are mostly found around the brain convexities and the skull base where they are related to the dura. The incidence of meningiomas increases with age and they are rarely in children. They are most found in the supratentorial region and infratentorial meningioma are quite rare.MethodWe present a case of a 9-year-old boy with a 6-month history of recurrent headaches and vomiting and a one-month history of unsteady gait. No history of trauma. Examination findings revealed a conscious boy with a GCS of 15. The pupils were 3mm dilated bilaterally, and he had no cognitive impairment. He, however, has florid cerebellar dysfunction signs. No other abnormalities were found in other systems.ResultHis cranial MRI revealed a midline solid mass within the fourth ventricle that was hypo- and hyperintense on T1 and T2, respectively, with moderate enhancement on gadolinium contrast and restriction diffusion and triventriculomegaly. The overall features were in keeping with fourth ventricular ependymoma. He subsequently had midline suboccipital craniectomy with tumour resection, with histopathological findings of grade II meningioma, GFAP negative, and EMA positive. Post-operative images revealed complete tumour resection.ConclusionMeningiomas in the paediatric age group are quite uncommon, and occurrence in the fourth ventricle is even rarer. Careful microsurgical approach gives a good outcome. Meningioma should be included in the possible differentials for tumours in this location.

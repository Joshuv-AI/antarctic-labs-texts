Biological age acceleration in primary aldosteronism: associations with renin, aldosterone, aldosterone-to-renin ratio, and left ventricular mass index
Authors: Ning Li, Ningpeng Liang, Xiangtao Zhang, Yifei Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectives:To investigate the associations of plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC), plasma renin activity (PRA), and aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) with Gompertz law–based biological age difference (GOLD BioAgeDiff) in patients with primary aldosteronism versus essential hypertension (EHT), and to determine whether GOLD BioAgeDiff relates to cardiac mass.Methods:We conducted a retrospective cross-sectional study of 1201 hypertensive adults (785 with primary aldosteronism and 416 with EHT). GOLD BioAgeDiff was defined as the calculated biological age minus chronological age. Multivariable linear regression was used to evaluate the associations of PAC, PRA, and ARR with GOLD BioAgeDiff. Furthermore, we assessed the relationship between GOLD BioAgeDiff and echocardiographic indices, including left ventricular mass index (LVMI) and excessive LVMI (eLVMI).Results:Primary aldosteronism patients demonstrated significantly higher GOLD BioAgeDiff than EHT patients. In fully adjusted models, ARR was positively associated with GOLD BioAgeDiff in the primary aldosteronism group (β = 0.292, P = 0.008) but not in the EHT group (P for interaction = 0.032). PAC and postcaptopril PAC were also positively associated with GOLD BioAgeDiff in primary aldosteronism. Furthermore, higher GOLD BioAgeDiff was associated with greater eLVMI (β = 0.591, P < 0.001) and LVMI (β = 0.640, P = 0.001) in primary aldosteronism patients, suggesting a potential mediating role in the relationship between ARR and cardiac remodeling.Conclusion:Among patients with primary aldosteronism, elevated ARR and PAC were independently associated with GOLD BioAgeDiff, and GOLD BioAgeDiff was correlated with LVMI/eLVMI and may be involved in the association between ARR and eLVMI. Prospective studies are required to confirm causality and evaluate the clinical utility of this biological aging marker.Keywords: aldosterone, aldosterone-to-renin ratio, biological age, essential hypertension, hyperaldosteronism, left ventricular mass index

INTRODUCTION

Primary aldosteronism is a common, under-recognized cause of secondary hypertension and is present in approximately 5–10% of all hypertensive patients [1,2]. Compared with essential hypertension (EHT), primary aldosteronism is associated with greater cardiovascular risk and target-organ damage, partly attributed to mineralocorticoid receptor–mediated inflammation, fibrosis, and oxidative stress beyond blood-pressure load [3,4].

Cardiac remodeling is a salient downstream consequence of renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS) dysregulation. Patients with primary aldosteronism more often show left ventricular hypertrophy and myocardial fibrosis, often out of proportion to blood pressure; targeted treatment can partially reverse these changes [4]. Left ventricular mass index (LVMI) is a robust marker of structural remodeling, whereas excess LVMI (eLVMI) quantifies left ventricular mass beyond that predicted by hemodynamic load, capturing maladaptive remodeling associated with adverse outcomes [4].

Aging strongly shapes morbidity and mortality, but chronological age incompletely reflects inter-individual biological aging [5]. The Gompertz law–based biological age (GOLD BioAge) model, derived from routine clinical biomarkers – offers a practical estimate of biological age [6]; GOLD BioAgeDiff (BioAge–chronological age) predicts morbidity and mortality in general populations [6]. Whether RAAS perturbations in primary aldosteronism are associated with accelerated biological aging, and whether this aging burden tracks cardiac structural remodeling remain unclear [7,8].

We hypothesized that elevated plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC), suppressed plasma renin activity (PRA), and a higher aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) are associated with higher GOLD BioAgeDiff, which correlates with greater LVMI and eLVMI.

METHODS

Data sources

This study is part of the Study on Primary Aldosteronism in Nanchang (NPAS), which was registered at Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200057297; https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.html?proj=133752). NPAS is an inpatient hypertension screening cohort established in 2017. The protocol was approved by the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University (approval number: 2022-05) and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki; prospective participants provided written informed consent, and retrospective data were de-identified before analysis.

Study design, setting, and participants

We conducted a retrospective cross-sectional analysis of hypertensive inpatients undergoing standardized evaluation for secondary hypertension at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University (Nanchang, China). Clinical information from these routine screenings was curated into the NPAS database.

Participants were classified into two groups based on their final diagnosis. The primary aldosteronism group consisted of consecutive patients diagnosed with primary aldosteronism and hospitalized between October 2017 and November 2022. The EHT group was drawn from patients hospitalized for screening between January 2019 and June 2019, in whom secondary causes were excluded.

For the primary analysis, patients were excluded if they had missing data on key variables [age, serum creatinine (Scr), plasma glucose (Glu), mean corpuscular volume (MCV), red cell distribution width (RDW), serum albumin (Alb), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), lymphocyte percentage (Lym%), white blood cell count (WBC), and gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), ARR, or postcaptopril PAC)], had a diagnosis of secondary hypertension other than primary aldosteronism, such as renovascular hypertension due to renal artery stenosis, renal hypertension due to renal parenchymal disease, pheochromocytoma, Cushing syndrome, obstructive sleep apnea, and gestational hypertension. or presented with extreme GOLD BioAge values (1.5 × interquartile range). For echocardiographic analyses, we additionally excluded patients without echocardiography.

Diagnosis of primary aldosteronism

The diagnosis of primary aldosteronism was made according to the Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline [9], as previously described in our published work [10]. Briefly, prior to admission, Patients stopped ARR-interfering medications or switched to verapamil and/or terazosin for 2–4 weeks. An ARR greater than 30 [(ng/dl)/(ng/ml/h)] prompted confirmatory testing (captopril challenge or saline infusion) to diagnose primary aldosteronism.

Diagnosis of essential hypertension

EHT was defined by office SBP at least 140 mmHg and/or DBP 90 mmHg on repeated inpatient measurements or a documented hypertension history with current treatment [11], after excluding secondary causes through standardized work-up. Primary aldosteronism was excluded by ARR 30 or less under standardized medication preparation or by negative confirmatory testing when ARR was elevated [9].

In addition, all secondary causes of hypertension were excluded through the standardized inpatient work-up as described above. Exclusion relied on history and physical examination, routine laboratory testing including renal function and urinalysis, endocrine assays when appropriate, and imaging or functional testing as clinically indicated. The final EHT diagnosis was determined by the treating physicians.

Data collection and definitions

PAC and PRA were measured by automated chemiluminescence immunoassay (MAGLUMI 4000 Plus) as previously described [10]. PRA was derived from angiotensin I generation during incubation (4 versus 37 °C), and ARR was calculated as PAC/PRA. For the primary aldosteronism screening test, samples for PAC and PRA were collected after subjects had been in a sitting or standing position for at least 2 h.

Clinical data and comorbidity information were obtained from the electronic medical record system and the hospital's central testing laboratory, including sex, age, height, weight, duration of hypertension, SBP, DBP, smoking history, alcohol consumption, MCV, RDW, Alb, ALP, Glu, Lym%, WBC, GGT, serum potassium, serum sodium, total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), Scr, and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Smoking history was defined as smoking more than one cigarette per day, at least 1 year, or 18 packs per year. Alcohol drinking history was defined as on average, drinking alcohol at least two times a week for more than 1 year. Comorbidities were identified based on discharge diagnoses determined by specialist physicians in accordance with standard clinical guidelines.

Outcome

The primary outcome was GOLD BioAgeDiff, defined as the difference between the GOLD BioAge and chronological age. GOLD BioAge was calculated using the GOLD BioAge model [6]: GOLD BioAge = chronological age+ ∑(βi × Biomarker i) + β0(β0 = −53.6287), where biomarkers were Scr, Glu, MCV, RDW, Alb, ALP, Lym%, WBC, and GGT. GOLD BioAgeDiff was defined as GOLD BioAge − chronological age.

The secondary outcomes were LVMI and eLVMI. Doppler echocardiography was used to evaluate the cardiac structure as described previously [10].

Echocardiography was performed by experienced sonographers blinded to diagnosis using standardized measurements. Two-dimensional and two-dimensionally guided M-mode images were recorded from standardized views. Left ventricular internal diameter in diastole (LVIDd), posterior wall thickness in diastole (PWTd), and interventricular septal thickness in diastole (SWTd) were measured and incorporated into the Devereux-modified formula to estimate left ventricular (LV) mass. LV mass was calculated by the formula: LV mass = 0.8 × {1.04[(LVIDd + PWTd + SWTd)3 – (LVIDd)3]} + 0.6 (g) [12]. LVMI was calculated by dividing LV mass by body surface area (BSA). The body surface area was calculated as follow: BSA (m2) = 0.0061 × height (cm) + 0.0128 × weight (kg) − 0.1529 [13]. Stroke volume was derived using the Teichholz method [14]. Stroke work was estimated as SBP (mmHg) × stroke volume (ml) × 0.0144 (g m), as previously described [15]. As simultaneous SBP measurement immediately after echocardiography was not routinely available, we used admission SBP recorded within 48 h of the echocardiographic examination as a surrogate for systolic loading conditions. The predicted LVM was estimated by the equation derived previously: predicted LVM = 55.37 + 6.64 × height (m2.7) + 0.64 × stroke work − 18.07 × sex (where sex was coded as male = 1 and female = 2) [15,16]. Predicted LVMI was then calculated as predicted LVM/BSA. The eLVMI was defined as measured LVMI − predicted LVMI [17].

Statistical analysis

Variables with at least 20% missing data were excluded from the analysis. For those with 20% or less missing values, missing data were imputed using multiple imputation, implemented through the mice package in R. Clinical characteristics were compared between patients with primary aldosteronism and those with EHT. Normally distributed data are presented as mean (SD) and compared using the Student's t test; nonnormally distributed data are summarized as median [IQR] and compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables are described as counts (percentages) and compared using the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test, as appropriate. ARR was log-transformed due to skewed distributions.

Multivariable linear regression models were constructed for the primary analysis to evaluate the association of RAAS measures with GOLD BioAgeDiff, and for the secondary analysis to examine the relationship between GOLD BioAgeDiff and LVMI/eLVMI. Covariates were likewise selected a priori based upon clinical knowledge, previous literature, and data availability. For the primary analysis: Model 1 was unadjusted. Model 2 adjusted for sex, age, and BMI. Model 3 further adjusted for SBP, DBP, serum potassium, TC, triglycerides, HDL-C, LDL-C, cardiac insufficiency, peripheral vascular disease, coronary heart disease, atrial fibrillation, stroke, smoking, alcohol, and hypertension duration. For the secondary analysis: Model 1 was unadjusted. Model 2 adjusted for the full set of covariates used in primary Model 3. Model 3 was additionally adjusted for PAC, PRA, and log-ARR. Given the significant interaction between disease subtype (primary aldosteronism versus EHT) and ARR, subgroup analyses were restricted to the primary aldosteronism population. Heterogeneity was assessed using interaction terms. No adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied, as these analyses were exploratory and aimed at minimizing type II errors. Mediation analyses were performed using nonparametric bootstrapping with 1000 iterations to examine whether GOLD BioAgeDiff mediates the association between ARR and eLVMI. These models were adjusted for sex, age, BMI, smoking, and alcohol. To compare mediation effects between primary aldosteronism and EHT, a bootstrap-based difference test with 5000 samples was conducted; significance was inferred if the 95% confidence interval (CI) of the average causal mediation effect (ACME) difference (Δ = ACME_PA − ACME_EHT) excluded zero [18].

All statistical analyses were conducted using R 4.4.2(R Foundation for Computing, Vienna, Austria).Two-tailed P values less than 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

RESULTS

Characteristics of the study population

From October 2017 to November 2022, a total of 1053 patients diagnosed with primary aldosteronism were initially screened. In addition, 616 patients with EHT without primary aldosteronism, enrolled between January 2019 and June 2019, were included as a comparison group. After excluding 337 patients with missing data, 117 with concomitant secondary hypertension other than primary aldosteronism, and 14 with extreme GOLD BioAge values, 1201 patients were eligible for primary analysis, including 785 patients with primary aldosteronism and 416 patients with EHT. For the secondary analysis focused on echocardiographic outcomes, a further 92 patients without available echocardiography were excluded, yielding 1109 participants including 717 patients with primary aldosteronism and 392 patients with EHT (Fig. 1).

Flowchart of study population.

Among the 1201 patients included in the primary analysis, the mean age was 49.4 (11.0) years and 687 (57.2%) were men. The mean GOLD BioAge was 57 (13) years, and the mean GOLD BioAgeDiff was 7.6 (4.7) years.

Compared with EHT, primary aldosteronism patients had the expected RAAS profile (lower PRA and higher PAC/ARR), were older with longer hypertension duration, and differed in electrolytes, lipids, and comorbidity patterns (Table 1).

In stratified analyses by hypertension subtype, distinct association patterns were observed between primary aldosteronism and EHT. Among patients with primary aldosteronism, log-transformed ARR, PAC, and postcaptopril PAC were significantly positively associated with GOLD BioAgeDiff (β = 0.292, 95% CI 0.076–0.507, P = 0.008; β = 0.015, 95% CI 0.000–0.031, P = 0.049; and β = 0.021, 95% CI 0.004–0.038, P = 0.014, respectively), whereas PRA was not significantly associated (β = −0.507, 95% CI −1.600 to 0.586, P = 0.363) (Table 2). In contrast, among patients EHT, PAC, PRA, and postcaptopril PAC were significantly positively associated with GOLD BioAgeDiff (β = 0.058, 95% CI 0.022–0.093, P = 0.001; β = 0.126, 95% CI 0.040–0.212, P = 0.004; and β = 0.341, 95% CI 0.060–0.622, P = 0.018, respectively), while log-transformed ARR was not significantly associated (β = −0.138, 95% CI −0.487 to 0.212, P = 0.440) (Table 2).

Interaction analyses indicated that hypertension subtype significantly modified the associations of both PAC and log-transformed ARR with GOLD BioAgeDiff (Model 3, P for interaction = 0.014 and 0.032, respectively). No significant interaction was observed for PRA (P for interaction = 0.196) or postcaptopril PAC (P for interaction = 0.200) (Table 2).

In stratified analyses by hypertension subtype, distinct association patterns were observed. Among patients with primary aldosteronism, GOLD BioAgeDiff was significantly positively associated with both eLVMI and LVMI in the fully adjusted model (eLVMI: β = 0.591, 95% CI 0.264–0.917, P < 0.001; LVMI: β = 0.640, 95% CI 0.259–1.021, P = 0.001). In contrast, among patients with EHT, GOLD BioAgeDiff was not significantly associated with either eLVMI (P = 0.815) or LVMI (P = 0.474) in Model 3 (Table 3).

Interaction analyses did not indicate significant effect modification by hypertension subtype for either outcome in Model 3 (P for interaction = 0.901 for eLVMI and 0.984 for LVMI) (Table 3).

Mediation analysis

In the overall cohort, higher log-ARR was associated with greater GOLD BioAgeDiff (path a: β = 0.129, P = 0.043), and GOLD BioAgeDiff showed a positive but nonsignificant association with eLVMI (path b: β = 0.539, P = 0.235). The direct effect of log-ARR on eLVMI after accounting for GOLD BioAgeDiff was not significant (path c΄: β = 0.740, P = 0.442). The ACME was 0.0696 (95% CI −0.0025 to 0.1780; P = 0.060), corresponding to 8.6% of the total effect being mediated (Fig. 2).

In patients with primary aldosteronism, associations along the mediation pathway were stronger. Log-ARR was significantly related to GOLD BioAgeDiff (path a: β = 0.257, P = 0.013), and GOLD BioAgeDiff was positively associated with eLVMI (path b: β = 0.538, P = 0.002). The direct effect of log-ARR on eLVMI remained nonsignificant (path c΄: β = 0.504, P = 0.285). GOLD BioAgeDiff significantly mediated the association between log-ARR and eLVMI, with an ACME of 0.1382 (95% CI 0.0248–0.2991; P = 0.008) and a mediated proportion of 21.5%.

In contrast, no component of the mediation pathway reached significance in EHT (path a: β = −0.127, P = 0.496; path b: β = 0.569, P = 0.640; path c΄: β = 3.672, P = 0.412). The ACME was −0.0725 (95% CI −0.4942 to 0.1205; P = 0.528), with a proportion mediated of −2.0%. The bootstrap-based difference test showed a difference of ΔACME = 0.2315 (95% CI −0.0257 to 0.6378), which approached statistical significance (P = 0.080).

Subgroup analysis

Subgroup analyses revealed a significant interaction only for diabetes (P for interaction = 0.036). The positive association between log-ARR and bioage_diff was prominent in participants without diabetes (β = 0.367, 95% CI: 0.133–0.602; P = 0.002) but was not observed in those with diabetes. Across all other subgroups – including age, sex, lifestyle factors, and other cardiovascular comorbidities – the association remained generally consistent, with no significant heterogeneity detected (all P for interaction > 0.05) (Figure S1).

DISCUSSION

In this cross-sectional study of 785 patients with primary aldosteronism and 416 with EHT, we observed subtype-specific associations between RAAS indices and biological aging. In fully adjusted models, log-ARR was positively associated with GOLD BioAgeDiff in primary aldosteronism but not in EHT (P for interaction <0.05). In primary aldosteronism, higher GOLD BioAgeDiff was independently associated with higher LVMI and eLVMI.

Biological aging clocks have evolved from chronological age-based models to third-generation ’pace of aging’ metrics [19–24]; the GOLD BioAge used in this study aligns with second-generation clocks, which incorporate physiological biomarkers to capture systemic aging and mortality risk [6].

Although the association between hypertension and biological aging has been explored, direct evidence regarding primary aldosteronism remains limited. PhenoAge is an epigenetic biomarker calculated by weighting DNA methylation levels at 513 CpG sites [22]. A recent study reported that hypertension was associated with higher PhenoAge (β = 12, 95% CI 11–13; P < 0.001) and PhenoAge acceleration (β = 0.56, 95% CI 0.15–0.98; P = 0.009) [25]. We extend these observations to mineralocorticoid excess in primary aldosteronism. We observed higher GOLD BioAgeDiff in patients with primary aldosteronism compared to those with EHT. In multivariable models, PAC remained associated with higher GOLD BioAgeDiff, and log-ARR was independently associated with GOLD BioAgeDiff specifically in primary aldosteronism. These findings suggest that autonomous aldosterone excess with RAAS dysregulation in primary aldosteronism may contribute to a systemic aging burden beyond blood-pressure load.

The association between primary aldosteronism and higher GOLD BioAgeDiff is biologically plausible, although mechanisms cannot be confirmed in this cross-sectional study. Primary aldosteronism features autonomous aldosterone excess with renin suppression, leading to chronic mineralocorticoid receptor activation [2]. Mineralocorticoid receptor activation promotes oxidative stress and sterile inflammation, pro-fibrotic/vascular remodeling, and endothelial dysfunction with microvascular impairment across organs [7]. Persistent mineralocorticoid receptor activation may induce mitochondrial dysfunction and reactive oxygen species generation, amplifying oxidative stress and inflammation [26]. This cascade may also intersect with aging biology through Klotho-related pathways [27].

Clinically, primary aldosteronism is associated with greater arterial stiffness and vascular aging than EHT, supporting blood pressure–independent pro-aging vascular effects of aldosterone [28]. Furthermore, higher aldosterone exposure has also been associated with shorter leukocyte telomere length, consistent with aldosterone-related cellular senescence [29]. Because these pathways affect inflammation, metabolism, and organ function, domains captured by the biomarkers in GOLD BioAge, mineralocorticoid receptor-mediated injury may manifest as a higher multisystem ‘aging burden’. This mechanism elucidates the phenomenon observed in our study: after fully adjusting for potential confounding factors, aldosterone-related indices remained independently and positively correlated with GOLD BioAgeDiff.

Previous studies have provided strong evidence that accelerated biological aging aligns with structural target-organ damage, including cardiac remodeling. Recently, using UK Biobank data, Mao et al. showed that biological age acceleration, calculated via the the Klemera–Doubal and PhenoAge algorithms [30,31], was associated with adverse cardiac structure and function and a higher risk of incident heart failure; their Mendelian randomization analyses further suggested a potential causal contribution to cardiac remodeling [32]. Higher ARR has been associated with arterial stiffness, LV hypertrophy, and atrial enlargement, partly independent of blood-pressure load [33]. eLVMI represents LV mass beyond that predicted by body size and hemodynamic load, and may better reflect maladaptive, nonhemodynamic remodeling [17]. In primary aldosteronism, GOLD BioAgeDiff was independently associated with LVMI and eLVMI, aligning with Mao et al. [32] to suggest that systemic aging burden may track aldosterone-related maladaptive remodeling. Mediation analyses further indicated that GOLD BioAgeDiff statistically accounted for part of the log-ARR–eLVMI association, though establishing causality and temporality requires longitudinal and interventional validation.

In the subgroup analyses stratified by clinical characteristics, a nominal interaction was observed regarding diabetes status (P for interaction = 0.036). The positive association between log-ARR and GOLD BioAgeDiff was prominent in participants without diabetes but was not statistically significant in those with concomitant diabetes. Diabetes is a strong driver of oxidative stress and inflammation and may produce a ‘ceiling effect’ that attenuates additional aldosterone-related aging signals [34,35]. Antidiabetic therapies such as metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors may also exert cardiometabolic benefits that modify RAAS-related injury signals [36–38]. Because subgroup analyses were exploratory and the diabetic subgroup was smaller, this interaction should be considered hypothesis-generating and requires confirmation.

Methodologically, we used the complete GOLD BioAge model rather than Light BioAge because C-reactive protein required for the simplified model was not routinely measured [6]. Additionally, the complete model includes immune cell, erythrocyte, and hepatobiliary indices that may better capture primary aldosteronism-related multisystem attrition [6,39–41]. Because GOLD BioAge was developed in Western general populations, calibration in Chinese primary aldosteronism cohorts warrants evaluation. Nevertheless, applying the same algorithm across groups supports internal comparisons even if absolute biological age values shift systematically.

GOLD BioAgeDiff is readily computed from routine laboratory indices and may serve as a quantitative phenotype of multisystem burden in primary aldosteronism. Its association with LVMI/eLVMI suggests potential utility for refined risk stratification and prioritizing cardiac assessment. Future prospective and interventional studies are required to validate its clinical applicability, optimal thresholds, and reversibility following primary aldosteronism-specific treatment.

This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional design limits inference to associations and precludes causal conclusions. Second, this single-center inpatient cohort may be subject to selection bias and limited generalizability. Third, stroke work was estimated using admission SBP within 48 h rather than contemporaneous SBP at echocardiography, potentially introducing nondifferential measurement error in predicted LVM/eLVMI that may bias associations toward the null. Fourth, residual confounding is possible because detailed lifestyle data, including dietary patterns and sleep quality, and long-term use of nonantihypertensive medications, including lipid-lowering and antidiabetic agents, were unavailable, despite standardized medication washout before RAAS profiling. These limitations indicate that our conclusions require confirmation in larger and more rigorous studies.

In conclusion, in a single-center inpatient hypertension screening cohort, GOLD BioAgeDiff was higher in patients with primary aldosteronism compared to those with EHT. Within the primary aldosteronism population, log-ARR, PAC, and postcaptopril PAC were independently and positively associated with GOLD BioAgeDiff; furthermore, GOLD BioAgeDiff was independently associated with LVMI and eLVMI, suggesting that it may partially mediate the association between ARR and eLVMI. These findings implicate a link between primary aldosteronism-related RAAS imbalance, accelerated biological aging, and cardiac remodeling, although causality and reversibility remain to be further verified by longitudinal and interventional studies.

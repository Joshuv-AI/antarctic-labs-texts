Recognition of the wobble pair: quantum mechanical calculations of tRNA U34 or C34 modifications within the ribosomal decoding center
Authors: Yi Liu, Shiyu Zeng, Zhishuang Yang, Shun Chen, Mafeng Liu, Dekang Zhu, Mingshu Wang, Renyong Jia, Anchun Cheng, Eric Westhof, Xumin Ou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUracil at position 34 of transfer RNAs (tRNAs) is generally modified and can recognize G + 3 via two geometrically different pairs, anionic or tautomeric. We sequentially calculated the thermodynamic parameters of isolated U•G pairs in geometrically different states modified with s2U, mnm5U, mcm5U, mnm5s2U, and mcm5s2U, and found that the modifications differently influence their state transition energy (isomerization) and base pairing strength, thereby their formation. Both mnm5U•G and mnm5s2U•G pairs occur as the anionic or tautomeric form and the zwitterionic state is preferred. When tautomeric, the mcm5s2Uenol state is thermodynamically preferred. After geometrical optimization within the structurally determined decoding environment, the quantum mechanical calculations reveal that, depending on the modification, additional H-bonds are formed within or outside the anticodon loop. Collectively, the modifications at 34 affect the electronic states (anionic or tautomeric) of the modified bases and lead to specific isosteric geometries of the wobble pairs within the decoding complexes. Slight variations in the ribose pucker of G3 gives enough mobility for its base part to differently pair with the modified U34. The structural and theoretical data converge to show that these factors act jointly for preorganizing the geometry-based recognition at the wobble position toward an efficient and accurate translation.

Introduction

Translation is a fundamental biological process that translates the suite of codons of messenger RNAs (mRNAs) into proteins. The intracellular tRNA pool decodes the 61 types of codons and serves as adaptor molecules for the amino acids [1]. The first and the second positions of the mRNA codons are paired strictly according to the Watson–Crick (WC) base pairing rules with the complementary nucleotides (positions 36 and 35) of the tRNA anticodons. However, non-WC base pairs can occur at the tRNA wobble position (nucleotide 34 in tRNA with +3 in the mRNA) [2]. Nucleotide 34, together with nucleotide 37 at the 3′-end of the complementary anticodon nucleotides, are the most diversely modified nucleotides in tRNAs across the three domains of life [3–6]. Here, using quantum mechanical calculations, we investigate the effects of chemical modifications at nucleotide 34 (U34 and C34) on the formation and geometries of the modified wobble pairs isolated as well within the constrained ribosomal decoding environment. These calculations were performed on selected fragments of the decoding complex harboring various tRNA anticodon loop and the mRNA triplet directly (or indirectly) derived from experimentally determined structures.

In the standard G•U wobble pairing, the carbonyl at position 6 of G pairs with the imino nitrogen N3 of the U (Fig. 1A). This results in a shift of the U in the deep major groove of the base pair. Because of the ~ 2 Å displacement of the U, the U•G wobble pair is not isosteric to the reversed G•U pair (for reviews, see [7, 8]). Thus, the two reverse pairs have different arrangement of hydrogen bonds, leading to different base pair geometrics and are not isosteric. During decoding in the ribosomal A site, where both the tRNA anticodon loop and the cognate mRNA codon are held in place by ribosomal contacts, the standard wobble pair is only observed between the G34 of the tRNA and U at the third codon position of the mRNA (U + 3) (Fig. 1A) [4]. With a G at the third position (G + 3), two potential tRNAs can bind, those carrying Cytosine 34 (C34) or U34 [2]. With C34 carrying tRNAs, the standard WC pair C34 = G3 is observed. With U34, generally modified and noted U34*, two geometrically different U34*•G3 pairs have been observed, either an anionic form [9] (Fig. 1B) or a tautomeric WC-like form [10, 11] (Fig. 1C). Depending on the modifications of C5-substituents, the U*•G pair may stay neutral by adopting a zwitterionic state with the N3 atom carrying a negative charge and the C5 modification carrying a positively charged side chain [12, 13]. For example, the U34 modification carrying a C5-methylaminoalkyl group is positively charged, and the U*34•G3 pair is a zwitterion, a special case of anionic U•G pair (Fig. 1B) . For the modified U*•G pairs, we will use the notation “zwitterionic” when the modification at C5 is positively charged and, otherwise, the notation “anionic.” In anionic U*•G pairs, the U shifts in the minor groove side of the pair instead of the major groove as in standard wobble pairs (Fig. 1B) (in another words, the G shifts to the major groove side of the pair). As a result, the anionic U34*•G3 pair is isosteric to the reversed G34•U3 pair [8, 14] (Fig. 1D). In tautomeric U*•G pairs, the U occupies the same position as the C in the standard WC G = C pair, and the tautomeric U34*•G3 pair is isosteric to the usual WC C34 = G3 pair (Fig. 1E) [15–19]. The chemical structures of the modified base pairs studied are shown in Supplementary Fig. S1.

In the Isoleucine codon box, nucleotide C34 is frequently modified, which prevents misreading of the AUG codon instead of the AUA codon [22]. Structures of high-resolution ribosomal complexes [22, 23] revealed that the modified C34*•A3 pair adopts a spatial arrangement of the pair that is isosteric to the anionic wobble U34*•G3 pair and not to a standard wobble U34•G3 pair (Supplementary Fig. S1B).

To investigate and understand better how U34 chemical modifications and anticodon/codon decoding complexes select or allow for either the anionic or tautomeric form of U34•G3 pairs, we applied quantum mechanical calculations on core anticodon:codon decoding complexes extracted from high-resolution (~3 Å) and functional ribosome structures. By comparing changes in formation enthalpy in two frequently observed pairs (the anionic/zwitterionic mnm5s2U•G and the tautomeric mcm5s2U•G) and their additional interactions within the core decoding complexes at the ribosomal A or P sites, we hope to retrieve mechanisms in the selection bias of geometrically distinct U•G pairs. In addition, other modified C•A pairs that are isosteric to anionic U•G pairs were combined in this study. Collectively, we reveal several unrecognized details at the atomic level that are involved in the formation of geometrically different U•G pairs or isosteric C•A pairs at the tRNA wobble decoding site and found that slight variations in the ribose pucker of G3 provides the freedom of the U34*•G3 pairs in the selection of the different geometries.

Materials and methods

We use throughout the usual abbreviated nomenclature for the modified bases, namely: 5-methylaminomethyl-uridine (mnm5U), 5-methylaminomethyl-2-thiouridine (mnm5s2U), 5-methoxycarbonylmethyl-2-thiouridine (mcm5s2U), uridine 5-oxyacetic acid (cmo5U), lysidine (k2C), agmatidine (agm2C). The seven nucleotides of the anticodon loop are numbered as usual between 32 and 38 with the anticodon nucleotides 34–35–36 pairing with the codon nucleotides numbered +3, +2, +1 in the A site. In the P site, the anticodon nucleotides 34–35–36 pair with the codon nucleotides numbered −1, −2, −3. In the PDB structures, the mRNA codons are numbered sequentially from the 5′-end so that a tRNA in the P site pairs, in the order, with residues 1, 2, and 3, and in the A site with residues 4, 5, and 6.

Structural conformations of U•G and C•A pairs at the ribosomal wobble decoding position

The major geometric difference between the anionic and tautomeric U•G pairs is the displacement of the U base. For the purely anionic U•G pair, the modified U moves significantly in the minor groove (Fig. 1A and B). With the U in the usual WC position, the U•G pairs can adopt a tautomeric form resembling a canonical WC G = C pair with three H-bonds (Fig. 1C). This, however, requires the modified U or the paired G to adopt an enol tautomeric form (Fig. 1C). The distance between the two C1′ carbons of the riboses is about 11.7 Å in anionic pairs, which is longer than in the tautomeric U•G pairs, where that distance is, as in usual WC pairs, around 10.7Å [14].

The anionic mnm5s2U34•G3 pair, possibly in its zwitterionic form, was first observed in the crystal structure of the decoding center at the Thermus thermophilus ribosomal A site (PDB: 5E81) [9] (Fig. 2A). Later, unmodified anionic U•G pairs were observed at conserved positions in bacterial ribosomes [14] (Fig. 2B). Recently, the same anionic modified mnm5s2U34•G3 pair was also structurally observed (PDB: 8HSP, 7ZTA) at the P site of the Escherichia coli ribosomes [22, 24] (Fig. 2C), demonstrating a conserved wobble codon pairing mode at both the ribosomal A and P sites.

The mcm5s2U modification at position 34 in eukaryotic tRNAs was chemically, but not structurally, identified in human tRNAs [28, 29], such as tRNA-Lys-UUU, tRNA-Glu-UUC, and tRNA-Gln-UUG. Similarly, the bacterial mnm5s2U modification was identified in the E. coli tRNA-Lys-mnm5s2U34UU [5]. The mcm5s2U and mnm5s2U modifications have a common sulfur substitution at position 2, but differ at the C5-substitutions, either C5-mnm or C5-mcm (Supplementary Fig. S1). The observed mcm5s2U34•G3 pair in the 3T1Y structure of T. thermophilus [11], previously suggested to exist as a tautomeric pair, appears not to be well resolved geometrically (Fig. 2D). Structurally, tRNAs harboring the mcm5s2U (PDB: 3TIY) [11] and mnm5s2U (PDB: 5E81) [9] modifications are both available with the same tRNA-Lys-UUU:AAG anticodon: codon decoding complex. However, the 5E81 structure represents a fully functional ribosome with a long mRNA and three bound tRNAs [9]. Instead, the 3TIY [11] structure is not a functional ribosome; it is a complex between the 30S small ribosome subunit, along with a single triplet codon and an anticodon stem-loop. It is, therefore, lacking the large 50S subunit, the whole tRNA and the whole mRNA crossing the ribosome.

Recent structures [22, 23] have shown that certain modified cytidines display the same movement toward the minor groove as the modified Us in anionic U•G pairs. In those one H-bond C34*•A3 pairs, the cytosine rings are positively charged at the N1 position [22] (Supplementary Fig. S1) and superimpose with anionic U34*•G3 pairs (Fig. 2E and F). This type of C displacement makes the C34*•A3 pairs isosteric to anionic U34*•G3 pairs. The modified cytidines in tRNA-Ile*2-C34*AU are either lysidine (k2C) [22], agmatidine (agm2C) [22], or 2-aminovaleramididine (ava2C•A) [23]. The modifications of cytosine at position 2 lead to steric conflicts for pairing with G3 in WC geometry. This prevents pairing with G3 of the Met-AUG codon so that the modified tRNA-Ile2-C*34AU decodes only the AUA codon [23]. These structure-based observations indicate that the geometric arrangement of the isosteric wobble pairs is crucial for ribosomes to properly accommodate the tRNA–mRNA complex during the decoding process for various tRNAs.

Structural studies have also shown that, during decoding at the A site, ribosomal residues contact only the nucleotide at position +3 in codons via a potassium ion and residues G530 and C518, that are part of the “ribosomal grip” [26,30–32]. The position of nucleotides at the tRNA position 34 is monitored by base stacking. Importantly, the same geometries are observed for the anticodon/codon pairs in the P state, where no direct contacts between ribosomal residues and the codon/anticodon triplet occur except for base stacking with position 34 [22, 33, 34].

Thermodynamic calculations of the isolated modified base pairs

The initial structures of the anionic/zwitterionic form of the mnm5s2U•G pair and tautomeric forms of mcm5s2U•G pair were extracted from previously published ribosome structures, respectively, PDB: 5E81 [9] and 3TIY [11]. For the other anionic and tautomeric forms of the isolated modified base pairs, as seen in Fig. 1B and C, they were stepwise constructed by sequentially introducing C2-thio and/or C5-substitutions into the unmodified U•G pairs, in which the standard wobble, anionic and tautomeric pairs were, respectively, constructed. Specifically, six pure anionic and two zwitterionic states of modified anionic U•G pairs were constructed in total. For the tautomeric U•G pairs, both the enol form of G and the enol form of U within pairs were created, six for each type. Similarly, six modified wobble U•G pairs were generated with U or G not in an anionic or enol form. In all these pairs, the N1 hydrogen atom of the U was replaced by a methyl, as well as the N9 hydrogen atom of the G. For all structures studied, the net charges are those shown in Supplementary Fig. S1.

All quantum chemical calculations were performed using ORCA 5.0.4. For the isolated modified base pairs, the B3LYP exchange-correlation function combined with Grimme’s DFT-D3 empirical dispersion correction [B3LYP-D3 (BJ)] was used for geometric optimization and frequency analysis [35–37]. The basis set used in both the gas phase and aqueous solution was def-TZVP. All geometries of the modified base pairs were optimized without any constraints, reaching minima with no imaginary frequencies. To obtain thermodynamic data, vibrational analysis at 298 K provides approximate thermal corrections, with a thermal correction factor of 0.9860 [38]. The PWPB95-D3(BJ) was used, and more accurate electronic energy calculations for the optimized geometric structures were performed using the larger def2-QZVPP basis set [39]. The PWPB95-D3(BJ) demonstrates high accuracy for calculating intermolecular non-covalent interactions [40, 41]. The basis set superposition error (BSSE) for the base pair interaction energies was corrected using the counterpoise procedure [42], the BSSE is from 0.23 to 0.38 kcal.mol−1 as estimated. Geometric optimizations of base pairs in aqueous solution were carried out using the conductor-like polarizable continuum model (CPCM), employing the default Gaussian charge scheme with a scaled van der Waals-type cavity. High-accuracy single-point energy calculations in water were performed using the solvation model based on density model.

For all isolated and modified U*•G pairs, in either anionic or tautomeric form, the formation enthalpy is defined as ΔH = H(U•G) − (HUk + HGk) + BSSE, where H(U•G) is the enthalpy of the U•G base pair, and HUk and HGk are the enthalpies of the individual/modified bases in their most stable keto conformations (Supplementary Tables S1 and S2, in water and in gas). The H(H), the enthalpy of H, was taken into consideration in this study as it is released when forming pure anionic pairs. When HU and HG are the enthalpies of the individual/modified bases in an enol or ionic conformation (HU* and HG*), the equation ΔH = H(U•G) − (HU* + HG*) + BSSE, represents the binding enthalpy of the isolated modified base pairs (Supplementary Table S1 and S2). The isomerization enthalpy is defined as the enthalpy change required for the keto form of the bases that form the U•G pair to transition to any other state. Binding enthalpies pertain to pair stabilities (the more negative, the more stable), and isomerization enthalpies indicate how much energy is required for altering the electronic state of a base (enol or anionic). Collectively, the formation enthalpy is the sum of the binding enthalpy and the isomerization enthalpy [12]. In our calculations, the deformation enthalpy required to distort the unpaired bases to their paired geometries were included into the binding enthalpies, although it has a minimally impact and was often neglected [12]. Moreover, we performed calculations using the DLPNO-CCSD(T)/def2-TZVPP level with tight PNO settings as a benchmark of the isomerization enthalpy in water (Supplementary Table S3).

Geometry optimization of the modified wobble pairs constrained in core decoding complexes

The structures of the decoding complexes harboring U34 or C34 modifications were extracted from the decoding center of functional bacterial ribosomes that contain a 12-nt mRNA and two or three tRNAs bound (for the respective PDB numbers, see Supplementary Table S4) [9–11, 22, 24, 27]. When constructing the decoding complex, the anticodon loop (residues 32–38) and the codon triplet (residues 1–4) were extracted directly from the high-resolution crystal structures. The 5′-phosphate groups at tRNA positions 32 and mRNA codon −1 residue were retained, and the 3′-ends of tRNA position 38 residues and the codon +4 residue were capped with 3′-OH groups. One negative charge was kept for each of the phosphate backbone, specifically the oxygen atom (O1P) of the phosphate. These decoding complexes included tRNA-Lys-mnm5 s2 U34UU•AAG3 anticodon:codon complex at the A site, tRNA-Glu-mnm5 s2 U34UC•GAG3 anticodon:codon complex at the P site (the residue following G3 is not included for P site decoding complex due to the presence of the P/A kink), and tRNA-Ile2-k2 C34AU•AUA3 and tRNA-Ile2-agm2 C34AU•AUA3 anticodon:codon complexes at the A site. The mnm5 s2 U34•G3 pair was in a zwitterionic anionic form with the nitrogen atom of N3 and the C5-mnm negatively and positively charged, respectively (Supplementary Fig. S1). The k2 C34•A3 and agm2 C34•A3 pairs contained the protonated amino-imidazole tautomeric forms of cytosine rings with two positive charges (N1 and nitrogen atom of the terminal side chain), as previously reported [22] (Supplementary Fig. S1).

For the third wobble pairs, due to the ribosomal grip, we performed geometry optimizations of the wobble base pairs only within the core decoding complexes extracted from functional ribosomes or in silico generated decoding complexes (Supplementary Table S4). It worth to note that the ribosomes including the A and P sites harbor many water molecules (11 461 H2 O in 8b0x structure [25]). Together with the dense arrangement of phosphate groups around the ribosomal decoding center and the stable coordination network formed with metal ions (such as Mg2+ and K+), the decoding complexes are exposed to a highly polarized microenvironment. The hybrid functional B3LYP combined with Grimme’s DFT-D3 dispersion correction was used with the def-TZVP basis set to obtain geometrically optimized wobble pairs within the decoding complexes. The aqueous phase optimization uses the same CPCM solvent model. All heavy atoms except those corresponding to the nucleotides of the modified wobble base pairs were constrained, and the hydrogen atoms belonging to the respective decoding complexes were unrestricted.

Nine in silico-generated decoding complexes were studied utilizing minor amendments to accommodate decoding complexes containing mcm5 s2 U34•G3, cmo5 U34•G3, mnm5 U34•G3 and mnm5 s2 U34•G3 pairs (Supplementary Table S4). These four pairs were analyzed in tautomeric or anionic forms. The decoding complex containing the tautomeric mcm5 s2 U34•G3 pair, which was originally in a nonfunctional ribosome (PDB:3T1Y [11]), was re-generated by replacing the mnm5 s2 U34 in the functional decoding 5E81 structure [9]. The decoding complex containing the uridine 5-oxyacetic acid cmo5 U34•G3 tautomeric pair was built by replacing the A3 base in the decoding 5IBB structure that originally inserted a standard WC cmo5 U34-A3 pair [18]. Accordingly, the two structures of substituted decoding complexes containing either the anionic mcm5 s2 U34•G3 pair or cmo5 U34•G3 pair were constructed by deprotonating the N3H of the modified uracil. In addition, based on the 5E81 structure containing a zwitterionic mnm5 s2 U34•G3 pair, we constructed and optimized the decoding complexes harboring a tautomeric mnm5 s2 U34•G3 pair and the zwitterionic and tautomeric forms of the mnm5 U34•G3 pair, with the C2-thio substituted by an oxygen atom. Using the latter two optimized structures, two additional decoding complexes were further generated by replacing the original A2-U35 pair with a G2 = C35 pair, with the mnm5 U34•G3 pairs being either a zwitterionic or a tautomeric form. Chemical drawing of different modified base pairs examined in this study is shown (Supplementary Fig. S1). Additionally, an in silico-generated decoding complex inserting the standard wobble G34•U3 was built and optimized as above.

Weak interaction analysis

The Atoms in Molecules (AIM) analyses [43, 44] and the visualization of weak interactions using the independent gradient model based on Hirshfeld partition (IGMH) were carried out with Multiwfn 3.8 dev [45, 46]. The IGMH maps were visualized with the Visual Molecular Dynamics software [47]. The electron density at the bond critical points together with the visualization of weak interactions allow for a direct observation of the changes in additional H-bonds in the decoding complexes after insertion of different modified base pairs [48]. The results of the IGMH and AIM analyses are provided in the Supplementary Material (Supplementary Tables S5 and S6, respectively, for isolated modified pairs and the pairs within decoding complexes) [9]. PyMOL (The PyMOL Molecular Graphics System, Version 3.1.3, Schrödinger, LLC) was used for the visualization of the isolated modified base pairs and the related decoding complexes.

Ribose puckering and RMSD analysis

The PyMOL script pucker.py was used to determine ribose sugar conformation by calculating the pseudorotation phase angle (P), amplitude, and pucker type (e.g. C2′-endo, C3′-endo) based on the five endocyclic torsion angles (ν0–ν4) (https://pymolwiki.org/index.php/Pucker). This analysis was applied to the modified U*34•G3 and C*34•A3 pairs (see Supplementary Table S7). The structural deviations Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD) of the wobble modified base pairs between anionic and tautomeric forms were analyzed by PyMOL script using the rms_cur command.

Results

2-thio, 5-mnm, and/or 5-mcm substitutions differently influence the binding and isomerization of isolated anionic U•G pairs

In bacterial ribosomes, the unmodified U•G pairs identified occur in conserved compact environment trapping a partially hydrated magnesium ion (Supplementary Fig. S2) [14]. Recently, >10 000 of water molecules were resolved in 8b0x ribosome structure, including the environment surrounding the tRNA anticodon:codon [25]. Moreover, due to significant base plane twist observed for the certain modified base pair that were optimized in gas, we only presented the formation enthalpy of isolated modified pairs calculated in water (Supplementary Table S1), which includes two parts [12]: (i) the enthalpy change associated with the transformation of U from its stable keto conformation (Uk ) to either the anionic or the zwitterionic states [Uk → U(−) or Uk → U], or enol states [U(±)k → Uenol ] for tautomeric pairs (defined as the “isomerization enthalpy”) (Supplementary Table S1), and (ii) the binding enthalpy that results from the potential energy released when the unpaired two bases (after state transition) form a thermodynamically stable pair.

We interested in that how different modification groups affect the isomerization enthalpy and binding enthalpy of the involved modified U*•G base pairs, which together give the formation enthalpies. To this end, by adding C2-thio, C5-mnm, and/or C5-mcm to unmodified or negatively charged U•G pairs, we compared their impact of on the isomerization enthalpy and binding enthalpy in geometrically different pairs (Fig. 3), including standard wobble, anionic, and tautomeric pairs.

For standard wobble pairs, because the C2-thio substitution is involved in H-bonding of the pairs, and it should change the binding enthalpies as see in the s2 U•G pair (Fig. 3A). Compared to the unmodified U•G pair, the impact of C2-thio substitution is ~1 kcal.mol−1 , although not a very strong impact. This impact is beyond the QM method limitation, ~0.5 kcal/mol, at the best performance. The observed decrease in binding enthalpy is consistent to the analysis of the critical point electron density for the H-bond N1-H(G) … S2(s2 U) in many other modified pairs, showing that a weakening of this H-bond is caused by the C2-thio (Supplementary Table S5). Moreover, the binding enthalpy of standard wobble mcm5 s2 U•G pair (−4.57 kcal.mol−1 , C2 + C5) is like that of mnm5 s2 U•G pair (−4.62 kcal.mol−1 , C2 + C5), which are equivalent to the binding enthalpy of s2 U•G pair (−4.53 kcal.mol−1 , C2) (Fig. 3A). Thus, we believed that the C2-thio dominatingly influences binding enthalpies of standard wobble pairs, while C5-mnm and C5-mcm likely do not.

For the purely anionic pairs and the zwitterionic pairs, sole addition of the C2-thio, C5-mnm, or C5-mcm does not significantly changes their binding enthalpies, mostly <−0.6 kcal.mol−1 (Fig. 3B). When adding both C2-thio and C5-substitutes, the binding enthalpies become more negative (−7.42 to −7.77 kcal.mol−1 versus −6.54 kcal.mol−1 ) (Fig. 3B), changing ~1 kcal.mol−1 , suggesting a stronger binding of these C2 + C5 pairs. Unlike in the standard wobble pairs, the C2-thio and the C5-substituents, do not participate in H-bonding in the anionic U•G pairs, but they affect their formation. The isomerization enthalpy is not required for standard wobble pairs because no state transition is involved. However, due to the lack of highly polar decoding environment, in such calculations, the formation of a purely modified and isolated anionic U•G pair requires a significant energy or “isomerization enthalpy,” ~37 kcal.mol−1 (Supplementary Table S1), for the creation of the negative charge. Instead, zwitterionic U•G pairs, that are neutral because of positively charged C5-mnm substituents, require only ~5.0 kcal.mol−1 of “isomerization enthalpy,” suggesting that C5-mnm reduces the energetic cost for theses zwitterionic pairs. The high cost of isomerization was also confirmed by another benchmark method using DLPNO-CCSD(T)/def2-TZVPP (Supplementary Table S3); in gas, also, a similar trend of changes in the “isomerization enthalpy” was observed (Supplementary Table S2). Overall, only the formation enthalpies of zwitterionic mnm5 U•G and mnm5 s2 U•G pairs are negative (−2.08 and −1.86 kcal.mol−1 ) and are therefore thermodynamically (Supplementary Table S1). In short, the two zwitterionic pairs enabled by C5-mnm group are thermodynamically stable pairs, which require relatively lower “isomerization enthalpies” while having considerable binding strength (Fig. 3B). When in the decoding complex, the C5-mnm modifications may form additional contacts that cannot be revealed in an isolated base pairing system (see below).

Changes in the formation enthalpy of isolated tautomeric Uenol•G or U•Genolpairs upon adding 2-thio, 5-mnm, and/or 5-mcm substitutions

Next, we performed QM calculations on tautomeric forms of the mnm5 s2 U•G and mcm5 s2 U•G pairs with or without C2 and C5 modification groups using the same procedure as above. The tautomeric U•G pairs with either the enol form of U or G were both calculated (Supplementary Table S1). Regardless of adding C2 and/or C5 substituents, all the Uenol •G pairs exhibit a larger binding enthalpy in absolute value (Fig. 3C), suggesting a generally stronger binding of the tautomeric Uenol •G pairs over U•Genol pairs. Analysis of the strength of three intra-base pair H-bonds indicated that O4-H(U) … O6(G) in all modified Uenol •G pairs is much stronger than the O6-H(G) … O4(U) in U•Genol tautomer (Supplementary Table S5).

However, the addition of substituents in each tautomeric pair has a minor impact on binding enthalpies (Fig. 3C). In Uenol •G pairs, C2-thio, C5-mnm, and C5-mcm have a small impact on the isomerization enthalpies (Fig. 3C). But the simultaneous addition of C2-thio and C5-mnm (or C5-mcm) substitutions, leads to a stronger effect on the Uketo→enol isomerization (1.15 kcal.mol−1 and 1.51 kcal.mol−1 , respectively), changing from 7.96 kcal.mol−1 for unmodified U to 9.11 kcal.mol−1 and to 9.47 kcal.mol−1 for mnm5 s2 Uenol •G and mcm5 s2 Uenol •G (Fig. 3C). Among all tautomeric pairs, despite the minor changes in binding enthalpies, the mcm5 s2 Uenol •G pair is likely the most thermodynamically stable, with the strongest O4-H(U) … O6(G) H-bond (Supplementary Table S5). Additionally, we studied the cmo5 U•G pair in two states, the tautomeric cmo5 Uenol •G pair and the anionic cmo5 U•G pair (Supplementary Table S1). The binding enthalpies of tautomeric cmo5 Uenol •G and anionic cmo5 U(−) •G pairs are, respectively, −9.73 kcal.mol−1 and −6.37 kcal.mol−1 . Again, the formation of the purely anionic cmo5 U(−) •G pair requires a significant isomerization enthalpy (36.49 kcal.mol−1 ) compared to the 8.89 kcal.mol−1 for the Uketo→enol transition.

Contacts of the modifications with U33 and U35 of the anticodon loop in mnm5s2U34•G3 pairs

Next, we studied the wobble pairs in the context of the ribosomal decoding site. Within the ribosome, the sharp U33 turn restricts the U34* mobility together with the ribosomal grip that anchors the +3 base of codons [19, 49]. What is the influence of this context on the geometrical selection of the wobble base pair? Is there enough accommodation latitude in the decoding site for recognizing the non-isosteric G34•U3 and U34*•G3 pairs (Supplementary Fig. S3)?

With U34 modifications, the modified U*•G pairs can form either anionic or tautomeric forms. However, the calculations show that both pair geometries are less stable than the standard wobble pairs. We wondered what mechanisms support the formation of the anionic pairs observed in functional ribosomes [9]. To investigate the favorable geometries and the contributions of potential additional contacts formed by the modification groups, we used the 5E81 (A site) and 8HSP (P site) decoding complexes as initial structures. Each structure contains an mnm5 s2 U34•G3 pair and we optimized the geometries within the decoding complex (Supplementary Table S4). The structural fragment of the decoding complex contains the entire tRNA anticodon loop from 32 to 38 with a triplet codon and an additional mRNA base at position +4 (see details in the “Materials and methods” section [30, 32]). In these calculations, only the third codon:anticodon pair was unrestrained with the first and second pairs, as well as the other bases, completely fixed during optimization except for all hydrogen atoms of the entire decoding complex that were left unrestrained. The calculations show that the optimized zwitterionic mnm5 s2 U34•G3 pair superimposes well with the one observed in the ribosomal A-site (PDB: 5E81) and P-site (PDB: 8HSP) (Fig. 4A and B). In the decoding complex at the A site, the distances between the two C1′ carbon atoms stay at 11.1 Å (Fig. 4C). Moreover, two additional H-bonds are observed between the C5-mnm group and the ribose O2′ and O3′ atoms (or O2P of phosphates) of U33 (Fig. 4C). These H-bonds were validated in the optimized decoding complexes at both the ribosomal A and P sites with AIM and IGMH analysis (Supplementary Table S6 and Supplementary Fig. S5A and B).

In contrast to the situation in the A site where the nucleotide at +3 is anchored via a potassium ion to its immediate environment [30, 32], the +3 nucleotide at the ribosomal P site (equivalent to nucleotide −1) has more freedom and, in the zwitterionic mnm5 s2 U34•G3 pair at the P site (Fig. 4B), G3 moves slightly toward the major groove after optimization (Fig. 4D), whereas mnm5 s2 U34 remains unchanged. Like those observed at the A site (Fig. 4C), the C5-mnm group forms also two hydrogen bonds with the O2′ and O2P of U33-turn at the P site (Fig. 4D). Notably, regardless of whether the decoding complex is located at the ribosomal A or P site, a new C-H … O bond between O4(U35) and the methyl group carbon atoms derived from C5-mnm group (mnm5 s2 U34) occurs (Fig. 4C and D). That H-bond was confirmed by IGMH analysis (see Supplementary Fig. S5B and Supplementary Table S6). Overall, through additional contacts mediated by its C5-mnm group with U35 and U33, these contacts maintain the usual anionic U34*•G3 pair geometry and contribute to the anchoring of the modified tRNA wobble decoding bases at the A and P sites. The specific contacts made by the modifications within the anticodon loop might also contribute to efficient mRNA decoding by preorganizing the modified U34* tRNAs during accommodation in the A site and translocation to the P site.

In fully functional ribosomes with long and continuous mRNAs, the ribose conformations of the third codon position are different at the A and P sites (Fig. 4C and D). The usual RNA C3′-endo is observed in the A site and the less frequent C2′-endo ribose pucker in the P site (Supplementary Table S7). The C2′-endo ribose pucker is regularly observed at position −1 (+3) of the P site where the sharp backbone P/A kink in the mRNA strand occurs (Supplementary Table S7) (Fig. 4E). This pucker change is observed when continuous stacking is disrupted before a kink in the chain backbone (as in tRNAs at residue 7 after the acceptor stem helix).

mnm5U34•G3 and mnm5s2U34•G3 pairs can occur in both the anionic (zwitterionic) and tautomeric forms

In bacterial organisms like E. coli, mnm5 U34 and mnm5 s2 U34 differ only by the C2-substituents (with or without C2-thio) and are primarily identified in codon boxes with G2 or A2 in the codons, respectively [5, 51]. We therefore next investigated whether and how mnm5 U34, without C2-thio, is geometrically accommodated in a functional decoding complex to read G3-ending codons. The original C2-thio in mnm5 s2 U34 of the 5E81 complex [9] was replaced by an oxygen atom, and a geometrical optimization was performed for the in silico generated mnm5 U34•G3 pair. We found that the mnm5 U34 could be accommodated with a stable zwitterionic mnm5 U34•G3 pair (Fig. 5A). Within the tRNA anticodon loop, we also found that the additional H-bond contacts around the C5-mnm group toward U35 and U33 are maintained (Fig. 5A), suggesting that these contacts are likely C5-mnm group dependent. However, the replacement of the C2-thio by an oxygen atom may reduce the rigidity the ribose ring because much stronger steric hindrance between the sulfur (van der Waals radius S versus O: 1.80 Å versus 1.52 Å) and 2′OH of ribose exists, contributing to stabilization of the modified U34 ribose, in addition to the C5-mnm contacts that anchor the U34 base [52].

Additionally, we investigated whether mnm5 U34 could form the enol mnm5 Uenol 34 tautomer within the decoding environment. The results show that the enol form of mnm5 U34 can form a tautomeric mnm5 Uenol 34•G3 pair in the ribosomal context (Fig. 5B). However, the tautomer presents a movement of G3 in which the base, but not the ribose, moves toward the minor groove (Fig. 5B). Between them, the ribose O2′ of G3, to which binds the potassium ion, shifts by no more than 0.1 Å relative to its position in 5E81 [9]. This means that the G3 base anchored by K+, G530, and C518 (ribosomal grip) fits spatially within the ribosomal decoding environment. This lateral movement of the G3 base is accompanied by minor variations in sugar puckering pseudorotation parameters (phase and amplitude) (Supplementary Table S7). The available data point to a lower pseudorotation angle in the anionic (around 8°, corresponding to a C3′-endo-C2′-exo pucker) than in the tautomeric forms (around 18° corresponding to a C3′-endo pucker) [53]. Variations of the phase angle of pseudorotation in the C3′-endo domain have the least impact on the torsion angle connected to the rest of the backbone (C5′-C4-C3′-O3′) [54] and affect mainly the position of the C1′ atom linked to the base (Fig. 5B). Our analysis suggests that both tautomeric and anionic mnm5 U34•G3 pairs can be accommodated in a functional decoding complex. However, when the tautomeric mnm5 Uenol 34•G3 pair is incorporated, the distances of the additional H-bonds formed by C5-mnm increase (Fig. 5C) compared to the case of the zwitterionic mnm5(+) U(−) 34•G3 pair.

Both mcm5s2U34 and cmo5U34 form tautomeric U34•G3 pairs

The modified mcm5 s2 U34 and cmo5 U34 harbor non-amino-alkyl modifications. In structural studies, however, these non-amino-alkyl mcm5 s2 U34 and cmo5 U34 modifications were not well resolved geometrically within the ribosomal decoding centers [10, 11]. To investigate the theoretically and spatially favorable geometry of the mcm5 s2 U34•G3 pair in the functional ribosomal decoding environment, we specifically replaced mnm5 s2 U34 with the enol form of mcm5 s2 U34 in the T. thermophilus decoding complex (PDB: 5E81) [9] and performed geometry optimization of the in silico generated decoding complex (Fig. 6A). We found that the mcm5 s2 U34•G3 pair in the optimized decoding complex forms a tautomeric base pair. The geometry of the wobble pair is slightly different from that observed in the 3T1Y structure [11] (Figs 6B and 2D) and shows the enol form of mcm5 s2 U34 at the expected WC position of a tautomeric U*34•G3 pair (Figs 6B and 2D), with the accompanying G3 movement. As expected, measurable differences are observed for intra-base pair H-bond distances, as well as for the C1′-C1′ distance (10.6 versus 11.0 Å). (Figure 6B). In pairs with mnm5 U34 and mnm5 s2 U34, the modifications point inward the anticodon loop and upward to residue 35. In pairs formed by mcm5 s2 U34 and cmo5 U34, the modification groups, C5-mcm and C5-cmo, point outward and downward with no additional contacts observed within the anticodon loop. However, as observed in 5IBB [18], one of the methylene hydrogen atoms is in position to form a C-H … O contact with one of the anionic phosphate oxygen atoms of the U34* 5′-phosphate group. The carbonyl group of cmo5 U34 is also in the vicinity of the conserved C residue stacking under the O4 ribose oxygen of U34*.

An in silico decoding complex was generated by replacement of the cmo5 U-A pair of the T. thermophilus functional ribosome (PDB: 5IBB) [18] with a cmo5 Uenol •G pair (Fig. 6C). Like the tautomeric mcm5 s2 U34•G3 pair (Fig. 6B), the enol form of cmo5 U34 occupies a more reliable WC position in the tautomeric U*34•G3 pairs after optimization as well (Figs 6D and 1C). It is worth noting that, possibly due to variations in the initial structures of the decoding complexes, the two C1′ carbon distances between the two tautomeric mcm5 s2 U34•G3 (11.0 Å) and cmo5 U34•G3 (10.6 Å) pairs differ slightly (Figs 6B and D).

Slight variations in the ribose pucker of G3 give enough freedom for G3 to pair with mcm5s2U34 or cmo5U34 in different forms

It was thought that the flexibility required at the third position to form wobble pairs was largely provided by the tRNA nucleotide at position 34 [22, 33, 34]. However, the calculations pointed instead to a mobility of G3 within the decoding complex (Fig. 5B). This led us to compare modified wobble pairs with distinct geometries. First, we analyzed the geometries of a zwitterionic mnm5 s2 U34•G3 pair and a tautomeric mcm5 s2 U34•G3, both derived from the 5E81 functional ribosome. When the phosphate backbones of both pairs were superimposed, we found that the mcm5 s2 U34, initially occupying the position of mnm5 s2 U34 in a zwitterionic pair, does not change its position to form a tautomeric pair but that instead G3 moves toward the minor groove of the pair (Fig. 7A). Specifically, the base part of G3 moves, but not its ribose (Fig. 7A). For mcm5 s2 U34 and cmo5 U34, which both lack the C5-mnm contacts within the anticodon loop, we also observed in the two optimized structures containing the mcm5 s2 U34 modification (either enol or anionic form), that G3 but not mcm5 s2 U34 moves toward the minor groove to form a tautomeric pair (Fig. 7B), and a similar movement of the G3 (still its base, not its ribose) in the two geometrically different mnm5 s2 U34•G3 pairs was observed (Fig. 7C). In short, the mcm5 s2 U34 modification can also form either tautomeric or anionic pairs, with a slight lateral movement of the base part of G3, but not of its ribose.

Since the original position of A3 is replaced by G3, the optimized tautomeric cmo5 U34•G3 pair should be isosteric to its original WC cmo5 U34-A3 pair [8, 14]. To understand whether the G3 movement plays a role in the formation of the two geometrically different cmo5 U34•G3 pairs, we compared their tautomeric and anionic pairs in optimized complexes. During the transition from the tautomeric pair to the corresponding anionic cmo5 U34•G3 pair, we found that in addition to the lateral movement of G3 toward the major groove, there is a concomitant movement of cmo5 U34 to the minor groove (Fig. 7D). As discussed above (Fig. 4E), the accommodating movements in G3 are induced by variations in pseudorotation parameters in the C3′-endo domain, where the variations least affect the backbone but are more pronounced for the base (Supplementary Table S7). The RMSD and Twist angle variations in G3 are more prominent when comparing the conformations of modified U34* containing C5-mcm or C5-mnm (Fig. 7A–C), but both G3 and modified U34 in the cmo5 •G3 pair exhibit large variations (Fig. 7D).

Taken together, mcm5 s2 U34, which likely occupies a similar position as see in a zwitterionic mnm5 s2 U34 pair, does not move to form both tautomeric and anionic pairs; instead, the paired G3 has lateral flexibility to accommodate the two geometrically different pairs. On the other hand, the bases of the cmo5 U34•G3 pair can both move to accommodate the two geometrically different pairs. In all conditions, G3, especially its base part, has more freedom to form geometrically different pairs, and the mobility of the modified U34* likely depends on the C5 modifications. Although mcm5 s2 U34 does not form any additional contact with either the mRNA or the tRNA sides, possibly due to the presence of the C2-thio [52] that rigidifies the ribose ring, it is found at a position equivalent to those of mnm5 U34 and mnm5 s2 U34 (Fig. 7A–C). On the contrary, the U34 modifications like cmo5 U34, without the presence of the C2-thio, are more flexible (Fig. 7D), a feature that may contribute to the four-way wobbling necessary to decode some 4-codon boxes, such as in tRNA-Val-cmo5 U34AC [10, 56, 57].

The k2C34 and agm2C34 modifications of tRNA-Ile-CAU leverage additional H-bond contacts at the mRNA codon side

In Bacteria and Archaea, the k2 C or agm2 C modifications occur at the tRNA wobble position to discriminate between the Ile-AUA and the Met-AUG codons [27, 58]. Structural studies have shown that the modified C* and A + 3 form a pair with a single H-bond between C*(N4-H) and A + 3(N1), leading to a C•A pair that is isosteric to the anionic U34•G pairs (Fig. 2E and F). Note that a C•A pair isosteric to the standard wobble U•G would involve H-bonding between C(N3) and A(N6-H) instead [7]. We performed calculations on these systems to investigate their stability and local accommodations. An additional H-bond between the side chain group of the k2 C modification and the U + 4 base at the mRNA codon side is consistently observed before and after optimization, maintaining the same distance between the two C1′ carbon atoms (11.2 Å) (Fig. 8A and B). However, we found meaningful changes in the side chain after optimization. First, the distance between the terminal carbonyl group of the side chain and the O2′ of the U + 4 residue is reduced from 3.2 to 2.7 Å (Fig. 8B). Secondly, the terminal carbonyl group of the side chain is rotated upward, leading to a distinct but more reliable H-bond compared to the original structure (Fig. 8B and Supplementary Fig. S6A). The new conformation of the H-bonds was further confirmed in the analysis of critical point electron density, reaching 0.051 (Supplementary Table S6).

In addition to the H-bond between the side chain imino group and the O2′ of the U + 4 ribose (Fig. 8D), a second potential H-bond between the terminal amino group of the side chain and the O2 of the U + 4 base forms (Supplementary Fig. S6B and Supplementary Table S8). The two H-bonds have a similar value of critical point electron density, 0.021 and 0.024, respectively (Supplementary Table S6).

The ribosome structure (PDB: 4V8N) has two tRNA-Ile2 -agm2 C34AU before and after the P/A kink. However, the A and P sites display different conformation of the long-modified side chains (Supplementary Figs. S7A and B). At the A site, the terminal amino group of the side chain forms a single H-bond with the ribose O2′ of A + 4 residue in the 4V8N structure (Supplementary Fig. S7A), and the second one is not observed. However, it can be observed in Fig. 8D where there is an A + 4 residue instead of a U + 4. This may indicate a potential role for different +4 residues (or codon context) in the binding of C34 modifications. At the P site, the terminal amino group of agm2 C34 forms H-bonds only with A + 3 (Supplementary Fig. S7B).

Additionally, in tRNA-Val-cmo5 U34AC and the two modified tRNA-Ile-C*34AU, all three modifications point toward the outside of the anticodon:codon mini-helix (Supplementary Figs. S8A–C). Also, a conserved intra-H-bond between N7(A35) and O2′ (U33) is observed in the decoding complexes containing cmo5 U34, k2 C34, and agm2 C34 modifications (Supplementary Figs S4 and S8A–C). This H-bond is, however, different from the one observed in the mnm5 U34•G3 decoding complexes with either U35 or C35 [Supplementary Figs S4 and S9A and B, panel (A) for a zwitterionic pair and panel (B) for a tautomeric pair], since the spatially equivalent positions forms weaker C5-H … O2′ bonds between either U35 or C35 and U33 (Supplementary Table S8) [50]. This analysis comforts previous works [9, 50, 59] showing that the nucleotides at tRNA positions 35 and 34 may jointly affect the preorganization of the anticodon loop (Supplementary Table S8).

The present computational work contains two main parts: (i) the effects of the modification groups on the formation of geometrically different pairs (binding strength and isomerization cost) and (ii) the description of the constraints and additional contacts that may occur within the decoding complexes and their contribution to the stability of anionic or tautomeric U34*•G3 pairs. In addition, we considered two C34 modifications that enable additional contacts at the mRNA side and make the modified C34*•A3 isosteric to the anionic U34*•G3 pair. Part (i) focuses on the energetic impacts on the modified pairs exerted by different C2 and/or C5 substitutes, while in part (ii) specific attention is given to the analysis of the contributions of the decoding environment to the occurrence and geometry of these unusual pairs.

First, a short overview of the discovery of the unusual U34•G3 pairs to put in perspectives the present results. The zwitterionic form of a U*•G pair with the U in the minor groove was first suggested by Takai and Yokoyama [61]. Later, based on calorimetric, circular dichroism data coupled with quantum mechanical calculations, Sochacka et al. [62] concluded also that U34 modified at C2 and C5 would adopt such a conformation with the U*•G pair with the U in the minor groove. The analysis was later extended to tautomeric forms [12]. In ribosomal contexts, the anionic U*•G was conclusively experimentally observed in [9] and the unmodified U•G pairs in [14].

To integrate the quantum chemical optimization with biological data, the results are now compared with previous works on the effects of U34 modifications on translation efficiency. Early work showed that s2 U34-tRNAs are very efficient for decoding A-ending codons but inefficient for decoding G-ending codons [63]. As reported, s2 U34-tRNAs decode 50 GAA codons per second and only 2 GAG codons [64]. It has been established that 2-thiouridines stabilize WC paired RNA helices (see [62] and references therein). In line with previous observations [12, 55], our calculations, as shown in Fig. 3A, show that C2-thio disfavors thermodynamically the standard wobble U•G pair in which s2 U must move in the major groove (Supplementary Fig. S3B). In addition, mnm5 U34-tRNAs decode equally well both [63], which agrees with our analysis that C5-mnm, by promoting additional H-bond contacts within tRNA anticodon loop (Figs 4 and 5), can favor the formation of zwitterionic mnm5 U34•G3 pair (Fig. 3B). As shown in Supplementary Table S1, the zwitterionic mnm5 U34•G3 pair can form thermodynamically. With the addition of both C2-thio and C-mnm, mnm5 s2 U34-tRNAs can decode better GAG codons (8 codons/s) and still better GAA codons (18 codons/s), but three times less than s2 U34-tRNAs [64]. The increase in decoding speed of GAG codons is likely resulting from the additive effects of the low cost of isomerization needed to form a zwitterionic mnm5 s2 U•G pair (Fig. 3B), the C5-mnm stabilization of modified U34 by the additional H-bond contacts within the decoding complex (Fig. 4C and D), and the C2-thio preference for the U34 C3′-endo ribose [52, 65–67].

More broadly, the QM calculations of isolated modified pairs and their geometries can enhance our understanding of the correlations between the modification types and the given codon boxes of the genetic code. It has been long been noticed that there is a strong dependence of the C5-modification on the second base of codons (see [5] and references therein) with xm5 (a methylene carbon directly bonded to the C5 atom) in codon boxes with R2 (A/G) and xo5 (an O atom directly bonded to the C5 atom) in codon boxes with Y2(C/U) [61]. In the complex formed between the anticodon and the mRNA codon, a characteristic H-bond occurs between the invariant U33(O2′) and R35(N7) that is paired to Y2 codon boxes [50, 59]. However, in R2 codon boxes, the paired Y35 forms a weaker C-H … O contact with O2′(U33) [50]. The calculations show that, in R2 codon boxes, xm5 modifications (such as the mnm5 U34 and mnm5 s2 U34 modifications) form additional H-bonds within the anticodon loop, strengthening the contact between Y35 and O2′(U33) (Figs 4 and 5, and Supplementary Fig. S9). Furthermore, the simultaneous presence of C2-thio and C5-substituent occurs only in U34*-tRNAs decoding the 2-codon boxes with A2 (paired with U35 in tRNA) and R3 (paired with U34*) (Gln, Glu, and Lys), where both the stability conferred by the C2-thio and the additional intramolecular H-bonds add up.

The experimental observations and the theoretical models converge toward an integrated view of the decoding complexes dominated by precise RNA stereochemistry and isostericity with multiple types of chemical interactions that are intra-residue as well as inter-residues. The chemical interactions belong to the so-called weak forces and altogether they concur for accurate and efficient translational decoding. The biological integration occurs within the set of isodecoder tRNAs and their networks of modifications specific for each codon box type [5], together with all the required tRNA modification enzymes and cofactors that link translation efficiency to the metabolic and surveillance processes [4, 68, 69].

Although translation occurs within the dynamic molecular machinery of the ribosome [70], here we focused on shape recognition in a static way. The basis is the following. Kinetic studies [71, 72] have shown that the system has no time to reach a fully equilibrated state. Thus, successful recognition and binding will be favorable if the molecular partners, the ribosomal A site, the mRNA codons and the tRNA anticodon loop, have shapes conformationally preorganized for mutual fits within the ribosomal context [73–76]. Within such a framework, geometric isostericity of wobble pairs facilitates preorganization and induced fits that are partly independent of the nature of the bases involved. However, to achieve this in ribosomal decoding, chemical modifications of some bases are necessary.

In the end, ribosomes only need to recognize two pairing geometries between G and U, the WC geometry (G = C is isosteric with G•U or U•G tautomeric pairs) (Fig. 1E) and the standard wobble pair (G•U is isosteric with U*•G “anionic” pairs) (Fig. 1D). However, specific tRNA modifications at position 34 are necessary to allow for the formation of the isosteric forms.

Enhancing financial stability in healthcare through data-driven risk assessments with machine learning
Authors: Dongli Chai, Shuang Du
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Machine learning algorithms can significantly enhance financial stability and operational efficiency in healthcare financial management systems. Achieving sustainable financial growth and improved healthcare quality requires not only data-driven decision-making but also a strong strategic focus. This study applies advanced machine learning models—beyond the commonly used approaches to conduct financial risk analysis and estimate life expectancy, addressing the substantial financial instability and variations in life expectancy observed across countries worldwide. The analysis uses a comprehensive dataset that includes current healthcare expenditure, out-of-pocket spending, and healthcare expenditure as a percentage of GDP for all countries with available data. The models employed include linear regression, long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, bi-directional LSTM (BiLSTM) networks, and deep neural networks (DNN). Among these, the BiLSTM model demonstrates strong performance, with R-squared values of 90.6% for training data and 85.3% for testing data. The findings indicate that per capita healthcare expenditure is the most significant predictor of life expectancy, followed by out-of-pocket expenditure and healthcare spending relative to GDP. Although more sophisticated algorithms hold promise for addressing financial challenges in healthcare systems, they often lack generalizability and interpretability, limiting their usefulness in real-world financial stability assessments. Therefore, effective financial planning and careful allocation of healthcare resources remain essential for improving health outcomes and strengthening global healthcare financial management systems.

Literature review

This section reviews the existing literature on the integration of healthcare reforms and financial management with machine learning methods. This study focuses on understanding how different studies have approached healthcare expenditures, financial toxicity, and the implementation of advanced analytics to improve healthcare outcomes. By highlighting key findings and methodologies employed in previous research, this review serves as a foundation for our study.

This study intentionally adopts a comprehensive methodological perspective by synthesizing evidence from diverse research traditions, including quantitative modeling, observational and cross-sectional analyses, cost evaluations, integrative reviews, and qualitative investigations. Such an inclusive approach is essential for addressing the multifaceted nature of healthcare financial stability and public health outcomes, where numerical indicators must be interpreted alongside social, organizational, and community-level insights. By integrating findings across methodological paradigms, the manuscript aims to provide a balanced and contextually grounded understanding of complex healthcare challenges.

In urban China, Yin et al.7 conducted a cross-sectional study to investigate general practitioners’ awareness, attitude, willingness, and satisfaction with contracted service (CS). According to the results, just 44.8% had been trained in CS, while 71.4% were aware of it. While most (85.7%) were willing to provide CS, their satisfaction with it was relatively low (54.5%). In addition to receiving training on CS and having a positive attitude towards the subject, having a higher level of satisfaction with CS was associated with a higher level of satisfaction. For healthcare professionals in urban China to be satisfied with their jobs, training and education on CS must be provided, as well as promoting a positive attitude towards CS among employees.

It was reported in the text that Visweswaran et al.3 evaluated (QoL) among patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) undergoing maintenance hemodialysis in southern Kerala, India. Three dialysis centers in the area were recruited to participate in the study, which aimed to measure QoL and identify its correlates among 95 patients with ESRD. QoL for these patients was largely determined by financial stability and weight gain between dialysis sessions.

According to Chipunza & Nhamo4, there is a potential demand for NHI among urban informal sector workers in Harare, Zimbabwe. Data was collected by means of a cross-sectional survey from 388 respondents across five clusters: Glenview furniture complex, Harare home industries market, Mbare new wholesale market, and Mbare retail market. A majority of respondents (64%) indicated that they were willing to pay for NHI premiums. The determinants of willingness to participate in NHI included having a higher education level, being employed in the furniture complex or home industries cluster, and having previously experienced financial difficulties due to healthcare expenses. Additionally, respondents who had used private health services in the past were more likely to support NHI.

Recent studies have further demonstrated the effectiveness of advanced artificial intelligence and optimization-based techniques in improving predictive accuracy and system robustness across complex engineering and risk-sensitive domains. For example, Ahmed et al.8 proposed an AI-based framework combined with advanced optimization algorithms to accurately predict renewable energy power output, highlighting the role of intelligent optimization in enhancing forecasting reliability under uncertain conditions. Similarly, Khafaga et al.9 employed meta-heuristic algorithms for feature selection and classification in diagnostic breast cancer analysis, achieving improved accuracy and robustness by mitigating redundancy and overfitting in high-dimensional datasets. In another study, Khafaga et al.10 demonstrated that adaptive optimization of LSTM architectures significantly enhances prediction performance for metamaterial antenna bandwidth, underscoring the importance of hyperparameter optimization and hybrid deep learning strategies for modeling complex nonlinear systems. Khodadadi et al.11 introduced an archive-based multi-objective arithmetic optimization algorithm to address industrial engineering problems, illustrating how multi-objective optimization can effectively balance competing performance criteria in real-world applications. Additionally, Salamai et al.12 developed a dynamic voting classifier for risk identification in Supply Chain 4.0 environments, showing that ensemble-based decision mechanisms can improve resilience and reliability in risk assessment tasks.

A situation analysis was conducted by Tulai et al.5 on the modification stability of health care financing systems in conditions of geoeconomic instability, focusing on the pandemic crisis experience in the European Union and the Ukraine. As a result of the SARS-CoV-2 coronavirus pandemic, both regions’ healthcare systems were found to have weaknesses. In addition, it also acted as a powerful catalyst for change, particularly in the financial and logistics sectors, which are regarded as weak links in the local healthcare system.

In their systematic review, Bayati et al.6 investigated the inequalities in COVID-19 vaccine distribution between and within countries, as well as their impact on population health and economic recovery. The authors searched four databases (PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and ProQuest) for published original papers on vaccine distribution inequality and factors affecting it from December 2020 to May 2022. Two researchers selected articles, extracted data, and performed a qualitative assessment using the STROBE checklist.

During pandemics like COVID-19, it is critical to resolve disparities in vaccination distribution in order to ensure fair population health outcomes. A study by Osom et al.13 examined how social determinants of health affected how people with congenital heart disease viewed themselves as resilient. Two related studies were examined: a qualitative study conducted between June 2020 and August 2021 and a survey conducted between May 2021 and June 2022. Education, employment, living conditions, monetary stability, and financial security were associated with self-perceived resilience in the study. The self-perceived resilience of individuals with higher education levels, stable employment, and better financial situations was greater. Study findings illustrate the importance of considering social determinants of health in understanding the QoL of adults with congenital heart disease. In order to promote resilience and improve overall well-being, these factors may be critical. A significant correlation was found between self-perceived resilience and sociodemographic characteristics such as education, occupation, living conditions, financial stability, and financial security. Higher educational attainment, better financial circumstances, and steady employment were associated with feeling more resilient. Taking socioeconomic factors into account when analyzing the QoL of people with congenital heart disease is crucial, according to the study’s results. Resilience and general wellbeing could be enhanced by addressing these variables.

Financial toxicity was significantly reduced in those receiving guaranteed G.I.F.T. compared to conventional care. With LGLS, networks perform better, are more resilient, and have higher bandwidth efficiency than with conventional techniques. JASZKUL et al.29 have conducted a narrative review to investigate the global epidemiology, pathophysiology, and societal burdens associated with lymphedema. It was found that lymphedema affects millions of people worldwide, causing significant disability and morbidity. Lymphatic filariasis is a common secondary cause of cases, particularly in Asia, South America, and Africa.

Azim et al.30 emphasized the importance of fall risk screening and balance rehabilitation among the elderly, particularly in Pakistan where such interventions are lacking. As a result of poor postural control caused by aging, persons are more likely to fall, which increases the frequency of falls, reduces their QoL, and results in higher levels of disability and mortality. In order to lower the incidence rate of falls among the elderly, routine fall risk assessment using balance assessment tools, the integration of fall prevention programs, and the incorporation of balance rehabilitation approaches into healthcare services could serve as effective strategies. The impact of six types of demand-side measures to control public pharmaceutical expenditures in European and non-European OECD member countries between 1990 and 2015 was examined by Berger et al.31. There is evidence that certain policy interventions, such as physician-level behavior measures and system-level price control measures, have been effective in reducing retail pharmaceutical expenditures.

Summary of studies on financial stability in healthcare.

AI and machine learning have significantly advanced predictive modeling across numerous sectors thanks to their integration into financial risk management. Using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) combined with Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for preprocessing financial data to reduce noise, Wang et al.49 proposed a financial risk identification model. It outperforms traditional machine learning models in terms of accuracy and efficiency. Khalil et al.50 utilized bagging and boosting ensemble techniques to predict insolvency in Egyptian insurance companies, demonstrating the superiority of ensemble methods over classical models in handling localized financial data patterns. Using deep learning to predict financial crises in small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), Muthukumaran and Hariharanath51 improved prediction accuracy for business failures by employing a hybrid CNN-LSTM approach with optimized feature selection. Khalil et al.52 introduced overfitting analysis and demonstrated improved predictive performance using a real-life dataset for insurance fraud detection. As a result of these studies, advanced machine learning techniques contribute significantly to the improvement of fraud detection and financial risk assessment.

According to El-Kenawy et al.53, nature-inspired metaheuristic algorithms including Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Gray Wolf Optimization (GWO) have found wide application in solving complex optimization problems. Swarm-based strategies improve solution accuracy and accelerate convergence speed, according to their research. These studies collectively illustrate the multifaceted nature of healthcare financing and its impact on population health. El-Kenawy et al.54,55 used a metaheuristic inspired by football strategies to optimize exploration and exploitation. According to the study, FbOA is faster, more accurate, and more robust than existing algorithms. PSO and GWO have been shown to be effective metaheuristic algorithms by Abdollahzadeh et al.56. It introduces novel mechanisms that improve performance across a variety of benchmark functions, for example, the Puma Optimizer (PO). Farhadi Nia57 introduced a convex hull-based cross-codec model that predicts quality-rate relationships, offering a foundation for adaptive streaming systems that can optimize performance in real time. The authors of Stécie Norcéide et al.58 utilized neuromorphic vision sensors to achieve precise positional tracking of physical objects, showing improved efficiency and responsiveness in real-time AR environments.

Based on machine learning-driven predictive healthcare models, Kehinde et al.59 sought to improve patient outcomes through early identification, tailored treatment, and resource optimization. According to Aman and Qidwai60, artificial intelligence can enhance operational strategies, risk management, and data integrity in healthcare. Farhadi Nia et al.61 examined how large language models such as ChatGPT can enhance digital healthcare systems by improving patient-provider communication and decision-making—an approach that aligns with personalized, intelligent user experiences. Ahmadi et al.62 conducted a comparative study between the Segment Anything model and U-Net in medical image segmentation. Their evaluation of model accuracy is directly applicable to gesture-based recognition systems, where segmentation quality is crucial.

Ahmadi et al.63 evaluated the Segment Anything model for infrastructure defect detection, demonstrating the model’s versatility in analyzing real-world visual data principles that can be adapted for healthcare-related segmentation and risk assessment. Asadi et al.64 evaluated gradient-based optimization methods and their sensitivity to initial parameters, providing useful tools for improving the reliability and accuracy of deep learning models used in healthcare data analysis. Farhadi Nia65 proposed a streaming optimization framework using cross-codec quality-rate convex hulls to enhance adaptability under fluctuating conditions, which supports smoother data flow and decision-making in real-time systems. Thompson et al.66 explored nonlinear mechanisms in bone-conducted hearing, demonstrating how ultrasonic signal demodulation can enhance cochlear stimulation and expand the possibilities for non-invasive audio systems in immersive environments. Nikpour et al.67 developed an AI-based intelligent energy management system for smart cities, utilizing machine learning in complex networks—paralleling healthcare networks that require dynamic, intelligent risk assessment strategies.

The application of advanced computational intelligence is increasingly pivotal for addressing complex optimization and prediction challenges across diverse sectors, a trend that mirrors the data-driven approach of our study. In environmental resource management, for instance, predictive analytics using models like Random Forest have proven highly effective for managing critical resources such as groundwater68. This emphasis on optimization extends to sustainability, where evolutionary algorithms are instrumental in developing efficient waste-to-energy systems69. The power of these methods is particularly evident when metaheuristic optimization is integrated with deep learning. Research in medical diagnostics has shown that hybrid optimizers can significantly enhance the accuracy of deep learning models for tasks like COVID-19 detection from chest X-rays, achieving near-perfect classification rates70. Similarly, novel nature-inspired metaheuristic algorithms are continuously being developed, such as the Greylag Goose Optimization (GGO), and validated on complex engineering and machine learning problems, demonstrating their robustness and superior performance over existing methods71. This approach of using weighted ensemble learning, often tuned by custom metaheuristics, has also yielded high-accuracy predictions in agricultural and hydrological applications, showcasing its value in managing water resources under limited data conditions72. Collectively, this body of research underscores the efficacy of leveraging sophisticated, optimized computational models to drive decision-making and improve outcomes, a principle central to our investigation of financial stability in healthcare.

The risk taxonomy

A variety of factors can adversely affect the financial performance of a company. It is important to note that these events can include adverse financial market movements, loan defaults, unexpected insurance claims, fraudulent activities, and the loss of customers. According to their sources, risk factors can be classified into four major categories: (i) market risk, (ii) credit risk, (iii) insurance and demographic risk, and (iv) operational risk. As a result of exposure to highly volatile financial markets, a company’s assets, liabilities, or income are subject to market risk. Risks in this category include interest rate risk, exchange rate risk, and commodity price risk. Creditors are uncertain about their ability to fulfill their contractual obligations (such as defaulting on loans or filing for bankruptcy). Retail lenders (those who make loans to individuals) and corporate lenders (those who make loans to businesses) are both subject to this regulation. The insurance industry specifically faces insurance and demographic risks due to the variability in insurance claim experiences associated with unpredictable events (for example, catastrophes, automobile accidents) as well as uncertainty associated with the demographic profile of policyholders (for example, mortality rates). Mortality Risk, Catastrophe Risk, and Non-Catastrophe Risk can be further divided into these three categories.

Several preprocessing techniques, such as normalization and missing value imputation, were applied to ensure the quality and consistency of the data.

Normalization methods

Since the dataset contains variables with different scales and units, Min-Max Scaling was used to normalize numerical features, ensuring that no variable dominates the analysis because of its magnitude. Model performance is enhanced and convergence is accelerated during training with this technique.

Strategies for handling of missing values

Ensuring data integrity necessitates a meticulous approach to handling missing values. Initially, the proportion and distribution of missing data were analyzed. For variables with less than 5% missing data, mean or median imputation was employed based on the data’s skewness. This approach maintains the central tendency of the data without significantly distorting its distribution. In cases where missing data exceeded 5%, multiple imputation techniques were adopted to preserve the underlying relationships and patterns within the dataset.

For numerical variables, the K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) imputation method was utilized. This technique identifies the ‘k’ nearest neighbors to a data point with missing values and imputes the missing entries using the mean or median of these neighbors, thereby preserving the multivariate structure of the data. Categorical variables were addressed using mode imputation, replacing missing entries with the most frequently occurring category, which is effective for maintaining the mode of the distribution. To prevent potential biases, variables with excessive missing values beyond a predefined threshold were excluded from the analysis. This comprehensive strategy ensures the robustness and reliability of the dataset for subsequent analyses.

We selected these imputation methods as they are well-suited for time-series financial and health indicators, which often exhibit clear trends and autocorrelation, making estimations based on neighboring or central values robust. While any imputation technique can introduce potential bias, we are confident that our approach has minimal impact on the final conclusions. This confidence is supported by the consistent performance hierarchy observed across our diverse set of predictive models. The fact that advanced models like BiLSTM consistently outperform simpler ones like Linear Regression suggests that the models are capturing genuine, complex patterns within the data rather than artifacts of the imputation process.

The prediction methods

There are a number of forms of machine learning, both supervised and unsupervised, but the most prevalent is supervised learning. Using a substantial dataset of labeled images, we can create a system that can classify images as houses, cars, people, or pets. The machine is presented with an image during training and generates a vector of scores for each category based on the image. In the beginning, the desired category may not have the highest score. An objective function calculates the difference between the machine’s output and the ideal score in order to address this issue. As a result, the machine adjusts its internal parameters, or weights, in order to minimize this error. An average deep-learning system consists of hundreds of millions of weights, which are adjusted based on the gradient vector, which indicates how much the error will change if each weight is adjusted by a small amount. The stochastic gradient descent (SGD) method is commonly used in practice. In this process, small batches of input examples are presented, errors and gradients are calculated, and weights are adjusted accordingly. The process is repeated until the objective function stabilizes. To assess the system’s generalization capability, its performance is evaluated on a separate test set after training. Several practical applications of machine learning rely on linear classifiers combined with hand-engineered features. The sophisticated tasks like picture and speech recognition, which call for sensitivity to particular characteristics while disregarding irrelevant changes, cannot be handled by linear classifiers alone. The creation of very complicated functions that can differentiate minute distinctions, such between a Samoyed and a white wolf, irrespective of backdrop or posture variations is made possible by deep learning’s automated learning of useful features through the use of a multilayer architecture.

The application of predictive computational models in healthcare and socio-technical systems raises important ethical considerations. Algorithmic predictions may inadvertently reinforce existing inequalities if trained on biased or incomplete data, underscoring the need for fairness-aware modeling and continuous performance monitoring across demographic groups. Transparency and explainability are particularly critical in high-stakes contexts, where stakeholders must understand how predictions are generated and how uncertainties are handled.

Data governance represents an additional challenge, as healthcare and financial datasets often contain sensitive personal information. Ensuring privacy, informed consent, and compliance with data protection regulations is essential for responsible deployment. Beyond ethical concerns, practical implementation barriers—such as technology integration costs, workforce skill requirements, and infrastructural limitations—may constrain scalability, especially in resource-limited settings. Addressing these challenges is vital to ensuring that advanced analytics solutions are not only technically effective but also equitable, transparent, and sustainable.

Computational complexity

An information flow is controlled by gated recurrent units (GRUs), which are recurrent neural networks. Gates can be divided into two types: update gates and reset gates. By selectively conserving or forgetting historical information, GRUs reduce the vanishing gradient problem by deciding how much fresh information should be kept and sent to the next time step. Machine translation and language modeling benefit from their ability to capture long-range relationships76,77. BiLSTM models are particularly computationally intensive when they are deployed in real-time or trained on big datasets. Since BiLSTMs analyze both forwards and backwards, they require more memory and parameters than regular LSTMs. Backpropagation through time (BPTT) can alleviate this problem, but it will cost more and take longer to train. The training time for a big dataset varies depending on the hardware.

NVIDIA RTX 3090, A100, or H100 GPUs are the best for improving training efficiency and speeding up matrix operations. With Google Cloud TPUs, you can significantly improve speed when your application requires a lot of parallel computing. It is recommended to have at least 16GB of VRAM for intermediate datasets, whereas larger datasets may require 32GB or more of memory. It is possible to increase computation efficiency by using a variety of optimization techniques. Memory use and calculation performance can be decreased using gradient trimming, batch size modification, and mixed precision training (FP16 instead of FP32). The training period can also be shortened by using several GPUs (e.g. PyTorch DDP or TensorFlow MirroredStrategy). Due to the fact that BiLSTM models are used in real-time applications, inference latency is an important consideration. Several techniques can be used to optimize models for edge devices, including quantization, pruning, and using lightweight frameworks like ONNX Runtime or TensorFlow Lite. With an auto-scaling GPU instance, cloud-based inference can also be used. As a result, on-premise hardware expenses can be reduced without resulting in overprocessing. In addition to preserving forecast accuracy, BiLSTM models allow extensive, real-time simulations.

In addition to computational considerations, certain methodological limitations in the reviewed literature warrant acknowledgment. Several studies rely on cross-sectional designs or descriptive analytical frameworks, which are inherently limited in their ability to support causal inference. While such approaches are valuable for identifying associations and generating hypotheses, they cannot establish directional or causal relationships between healthcare financing variables and health outcomes. As a result, conclusions drawn from these studies should be interpreted as correlational rather than causal. Future research would benefit from longitudinal designs, quasi-experimental methods, or causal inference frameworks to strengthen the evidentiary basis for policy and clinical decision-making.

In this paper, we improved the interpretability of our prediction models by adding SHapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) and Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME). Understanding how and why our models-BiLSTM, CNN-LSTM, GRU-CNN, and DNNs-predict life expectancy with high accuracy is, therefore, of paramount importance to make informed decisions in healthcare applications. It is equally important to interpret and explain the predictions of the BiLSTM, CNN-LSTM, and GRU-CNN models for healthcare applications.

SHapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) and Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) provide the stakeholders with crucial information regarding the importance of different attributes (see Fig. 18). SHAP scores each characteristic associated with a particular prediction by a contribution score for a given prediction, based on game theory. SHAP identifies characteristics such as healthcare spending, socio-economic status, and demographics in order to predict life expectancy. SHAP’s global interpretability can help researchers and policymakers identify the most important predictors across the entire dataset. The LIME model combines complex deep learning models with simple, interpretable models for individual predictions. This is particularly useful in highlighting situations where the model does not make the expected predictions. You will be able to tell whether or not an unexpected forecast is related to economic disparities, deficiencies in healthcare infrastructure, or otherwise by using LIME analysis.

These interpretability tools can be utilized by a healthcare financial manager for strategic planning to bridge the gap between these model outputs and practical application. For instance, feature importance analysis (Fig. 15) identifies ‘CHE_per_capita_PPP’ without a shadow of a doubt as the most dominant predictor. A stakeholder can therefore use this metric as a key performance indicator of financial health, realizing that fluctuations in this value have the most significant downstream impact on population health and associated costs. In the same way, the SHAP plot (Fig. 18) offers actionable insights for specific cases. For example, if the prediction for a particular country or year is lower than expected, the SHAP values can show that high ‘Out-of-pocket expenditure’ was the main negative contributor. This would provide data-driven evidence that should be used by policymakers to advocate for necessary interventions that reduce direct financial burdens on patients and thus help in improving health outcomes and the financial stability of the system, too-a precept well expressed in the works of Olalekan Kehinde et al.59 and Aman and Qidwai60.

Error analysis was done for the models in order to understand what their limits are, especially when settings are really difficult and the predictions are not so accurate. The model cannot estimate life expectancy in low-resource environments accurately because there is a wide variation in the healthcare facilities available and in their costs. Possible further improvements may come from analyzing the failures: inclusion of external sociopolitical indicators and changes to weighting methods to handle rare but important events.

Conclusion

The purpose of this study was to provide efficient, data-driven risk assessments to improve the financial stability of healthcare systems by utilizing cutting-edge machine learning models. In light of these findings, healthcare management and policy can benefit from the understanding of how these factors influence life expectancy.

The methodological diversity of the reviewed and analyzed studies strengthens the validity of the conclusions by combining statistical rigor with contextual depth. Quantitative models offer predictive accuracy and scalability, while qualitative and observational evidence enrich interpretation by capturing social dynamics, institutional constraints, and community-level perspectives. This integrated analytical lens supports more informed and realistic policy recommendations for healthcare systems facing financial and public health pressures.

We examined deep neural networks, bidirectional LSTM networks, linear regression models, and Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) networks.The most accurate and generalizable model was BiLSTM. Based on the BiLSTM test, 85.3% of the model retained its accuracy, while 90.6% of the model retained its accuracy after being trained. For training and testing, the LSTM model had an R-Squared of 87.3%, while the Deep Neural Network had an R-Squared of 89.8%. Meanwhile, Linear Regression had an R-Squared of 52.5% for testing and 50.3% for training. Random Forest analysis indicated that the most important variable was Current Health Expenditures per capita PPP (CHE_per_capita_PPP), which had a score of almost 0.75. Health expenditures as a percentage of GDP (CHE_GDP) trailed out-of-pocket spending with significance ratings of 0.15 and 0.10. Public healthcare allocation as a percentage of GDP was least important. These results prove that policy changes are needed, such as lowering individual out-of-pocket medical costs to ease financial strains and boosting public healthcare investment to improve infrastructure. Health policy and practice could benefit from the results of this study. Investments in areas that have been determined to be most important, such as public healthcare spending and lowering out-of-pocket costs, can improve health outcomes and financial stability. Hopefully, these findings will help policymakers create targeted programs that target the major factors influencing life expectancy.

Beyond predictive accuracy, the interdisciplinary nature of this study enhances its practical relevance. By combining computational intelligence with healthcare financing indicators, the proposed framework translates methodological innovation into actionable insights for policymakers and healthcare administrators. This alignment between advanced analytics and real-world decision-making strengthens the role of machine learning as a strategic tool for improving financial sustainability and population health outcomes.

Lowering the financial constraints of patients and increasing public spending on healthcare can significantly improve the results of healthcare. Precise forecasts and risk analyses by machine learning models can assist health care professionals and policymakers in making well-informed decisions. These models need to be enhanced further in the future, and other factors that may affect healthcare results also need to be considered. Comprehensive information, including socio-economic determinants of health, can enhance the accuracy of forecasts and provide a fuller understanding of the factors affecting life expectancy.

The documented performance advantages of modern computational techniques underscore their practical value beyond methodological novelty. Improved prediction accuracy, resilience to noise, and the ability to model nonlinear relationships enable these approaches to support more effective decision-making in healthcare finance and policy planning. As healthcare systems increasingly rely on data-driven tools, the demonstrated superiority of advanced analytics reinforces their relevance for operational and strategic applications.

To enhance robustness and mitigate overfitting in advanced computational models, the literature increasingly emphasizes a combination of methodological safeguards. Hyperparameter optimization plays a critical role in balancing model complexity and generalization performance, while regularization techniques such as dropout and L2 penalties help prevent excessive reliance on specific features. Data augmentation strategies can further improve robustness by increasing effective dataset diversity, particularly in medical imaging and financial time-series applications.

Ensemble learning approaches, which combine predictions from multiple models, have also been shown to reduce variance and improve stability across heterogeneous datasets. In addition, model explainability tools—such as feature attribution and sensitivity analysis—provide valuable insights into model behavior, enabling the identification of spurious correlations and supporting more transparent and reliable decision-making. Together, these strategies reflect best practices for ensuring methodological rigor in predictive modeling.

Machine learning approaches may improve hospital financial stability through intuitive user interfaces and decision support systems. Key financial and health-related variables must be identified and prioritized to achieve better healthcare quality, financial burdens, and health outcomes.

Scalability

Since the method depends on publicly available financial and health-related variables, it can be scalable across national healthcare systems in an international context. World Bank, national health ministries, and WHO provide country-specific statistics to enable the model to be adapted to a variety of health infrastructures and economic conditions. Scalability may also be achieved using cloud-based deployments, where huge amounts of data can be put to effective use by healthcare organizations and policymakers. Regional differences in data availability and healthcare regulations must be kept in mind for forecasts to be accurate and applicable to a wide range of populations.

The deployment of advanced machine learning and predictive analytics in healthcare and smart-infrastructure systems raises important ethical considerations that warrant explicit attention. Predictive models trained on historical or incomplete data may inadvertently reinforce existing socioeconomic inequalities or introduce algorithmic bias, particularly when sensitive variables such as income, geographic location, or access to healthcare services are unevenly represented. Ensuring fairness, transparency, and accountability is therefore critical when these models are used to inform policy decisions or clinical resource allocation.

Data privacy and patient consent also represent central ethical challenges. Healthcare and financial datasets often contain sensitive personal information, and improper data governance may compromise confidentiality or violate regulatory standards. Responsible adoption of these technologies requires adherence to established ethical frameworks, secure data handling practices, and compliance with data protection regulations, alongside transparent communication with stakeholders regarding model limitations and intended use.

Beyond ethical concerns, practical scalability remains a significant challenge for real-world implementation. Integrating advanced analytics into existing healthcare or smart-city infrastructures may entail substantial financial costs, specialized technical expertise, and organizational restructuring. Human resource constraints, including limited availability of trained data scientists and healthcare professionals with digital competencies, may further restrict adoption—particularly in low-resource or developing settings.

Infrastructure limitations, such as insufficient digitalization, fragmented data systems, and lack of interoperability, can also hinder large-scale deployment. As a result, while advanced computational models offer substantial potential benefits, their successful application depends on careful consideration of contextual, economic, and institutional factors. Addressing these challenges is essential for ensuring that data-driven solutions are not only technically effective but also equitable, sustainable, and scalable across diverse healthcare systems.

Dataset characteristics play a critical role in determining the robustness and generalizability of analytical findings. Several reviewed studies rely on relatively small sample sizes, incomplete records, or datasets with limited demographic diversity, which may introduce bias and constrain the external validity of their conclusions. Missing data and underrepresentation of certain population groups can further distort learned patterns, particularly in machine learning models that are sensitive to data distribution.

To address these challenges, future studies should prioritize transparent reporting of dataset composition and adopt more rigorous validation strategies. Approaches such as k-fold cross-validation, bootstrapping, and evaluation on independent external cohorts can substantially improve the reliability of model performance estimates. These practices are especially important in healthcare and financial risk modeling, where predictive systems may influence high-stakes policy or clinical decisions.

Real-time applications

These models should provide actionable insights for healthcare administrators, insurers, and policymakers. AI-powered dashboards, integrated into hospital management systems or government policy platforms, can track health care expenditures, resource allocation, and public health risks in real time by incorporating predictive frameworks. Automated alerts may optimize budget planning and improve early intervention strategies, thus improving health care quality.

Future enhancements

With a hybrid model combining deep learning methods with statistics and econometrics, prediction power and policy relevance can be expected to increase. Forecasts could be further improved by the inclusion of sociodemographic variables such as education, lifestyle choices, and disease incidence rates, while allowing a more complete assessment of the determinants of life expectancy. Causal inference, too, would be bettered, along with policymaking and resource allocation, by distinguishing between correlation and causation. Taking these future trends into account, the proposed framework may lead to an improvement in public health outcomes, financial stability, and healthcare administration.

While deep learning models can forecast life expectancy using these healthcare and financial variables, there are still many directions for further research. Combining that with other socioeconomic data, for example, demographic changes, environmental influences, and economic developments may increase our understanding of the dynamics of health care. It will be beneficial to include healthcare systems from high, middle, and low-income nations to make the model more generalizable. Further, with an improvement in interpretability through methods like SHAP and LIME, healthcare practitioners could more intuitively understand AI-generated insights. An interactive decision-support system may also offer real-time information to governments, insurance companies, and public health agencies about how various factors affect life expectancy.

While this study successfully demonstrates the predictive power of machine learning models on a broad, multi-country dataset, we do acknowledge certain limitations. Although the use of aggregated, publicly available data sets up a very solid foundation for a macro understanding, the model performance is not tested on any independent, proprietary datasets from specific healthcare organizations. Thus, a promising avenue for future research is to apply this framework in the real world through detailed case studies that would allow the integration of institution-specific variables and the fine-tuning of models to local financial and operational contexts, hence increasing their practical utility for decision-makers. Future work may also use advanced econometric methods to identify causal relationships between these indicators and move beyond correlations toward actionable policy levers. These models, integrated into practical applications such as AI-based dashboards or web-based tools, allow organizations and policymakers to identify in real-time, track, and thereby improve resource utilization. Addressing these future directions will enhance the applicability of this study to international healthcare planning and financial stability.

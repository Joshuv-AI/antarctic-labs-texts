The Japanese nationwide cohort data of proton beam therapy for liver oligometastasis in esophagogastric cancer patients
Authors: Hisashi Yamaguchi, Nobuyoshi Fukumitsu, Haruko Numajiri, Hiroyuki Ogino, Norio Katoh, Tomoaki Okimoto, Motohisa Suzuki, Hideyuki Sakurai
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractA nationwide multicenter cohort study on particle therapy was launched by the Japanese Society for Radiation Oncology in Japan in May 2016. We analyzed the outcome of proton beam therapy (PBT) for liver oligometastasis of esophagogastric cancers. Cases in which PBT was performed at all PBT facilities in Japan between May 2016 and February 2019 were enrolled. The patients were selected based on the following criteria: controlled primary cancer, liver recurrence without extrahepatic tumors and no more than three liver lesions. Twenty-two males and two females with a median age of 69 (range, 52–80) years and 35 lesions were included. This study included 6 patients with esophageal and 18 patients with gastric cancer. The median lesion size, fraction size and biological effective dose (BED)10 were 32 (7–104) mm, 3.8 gray (relative biological effect)/fractions (Gy (RBE)/fr) (2–8 Gy (RBE)/fr) and 96.9 (88.8–115.2) Gy, respectively. The median follow-up period was 18 (4–47) months. The 1-, 2- and 3-year overall survival (OS) rates were 75, 51.8 and 45.3%, respectively, and the median OS was 25.3 months. The 1-, 2- and 3-year cumulative local recurrence (LR) rates were 3, 6 and 6%, respectively. Patients’ age (P < 0.01), performance status (P = 0.017) and tumor size (P = 0.024) were significant OS-related factors. No Grade 3 or higher adverse events (AEs) were observed. Owing to the low incidence of AEs and the low LR cumulative incidence, PBT is a feasible option for liver oligometastasis of esophagogastric cancers.Keywords: proton beam therapy, liver metastasis, esophageal cancer, gastric cancer, gastrointestinal cancer

INTRODUCTION

The concept of oligometastatic state, proposed by Hellman and Weichselbaum, is an intermediate state between limited primary and polymetastatic cancer [1]. In such a case, local metastasis-directed therapies have the potential to cure the disease or prolong survival [2]. Stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) and intensity-modulated radiation therapy have become widely used, based on promising clinical evidence, as one of the treatment modalities targeting local metastases [3]. In Japan, public medical insurance coverage of SBRT for oligometastatic disease that meets certain criteria began in April 2020.

Particle therapies (PTs), such as proton beam therapy (PBT) and carbon-ion beam therapy (CIBT), have been reported to have excellent therapeutic effects on liver tumors due to the physical properties of the Bragg peak, but the clinical benefit for oligometastatic disease is still controversial. There is also a lack of consensus on the use of PT for metastatic lesions. Neither the number of cases of liver metastasis at each institute nor the number of institutions offering PT is very large. Therefore, there are not many studies that can provide sufficient evidence for the treatment of liver metastasis. For example, in the case of esophagogastric cancers, there are no reports of PT for esophageal cancer, and there are only two reports of PBT for gastric cancer [4, 5].

In Japan, a nationwide multicenter cohort study on PT was launched by the Japanese Society for Radiation Oncology (JASTRO) in May 2016 at all facilities that perform PBT and/or CIBT. Using these data, we compiled the data on PBT for liver metastasis of esophagogastric cancers. The present study was performed and managed by the Oligometastatic Cancer Working Group in the Particle Beam Therapy Committee and Subcommittee at the JASTRO.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and patient enrollment

This was a nationwide multi-institutional cohort study. Cases in which PBT was performed at all PBT facilities in Japan between May 2016 and February 2019 were enrolled. These inclusion criteria all corresponded to the requirements of provide PT for oligometastatic diseases in the JASTRO system as follows:

Procedure of PBT

The treatment strategy was discussed and decided by cancer boards in each institution. The dose and fraction of PBT were determined using unified treatment policy established by JASTRO. We used the following protocol: 64 gray (relative biological effect) (Gy (RBE))/8 fractions (fr) for the peripheral region and 72.6 Gy (RBE) /22fr for the central region. For safety aspect, the hepatocellular carcinoma irradiation protocols of 66 GyE/10fr, 72.6–76 GyE/20–22fr and 74–76 GyE/37–38fr could be allowed.

Outcomes

The overall survival (OS), local recurrence (LR) and adverse events (AEs) were examined. OS was defined as the time from the date of PBT initiation to death from any cause. LR was defined as the date of the event when the response evaluation criteria in solid tumor evaluation resulted in progressive disease from PBT initiation. We evaluated AEs using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0.

Statistical analysis

The OS rates were calculated using the Kaplan–Meier method. When LR is considered an event, the event of death before LR is a competing event because it inhibits the occurrence of subsequent events of LR. Thus, LR rates were calculated using a cumulative incidence curve. Factors possibly related to OS, such as patients’ age, performance status, tumor size (for multiple lesions, the largest size) and number of liver tumors, as well as the presence or absence of prior, concurrent and post-PBT chemotherapy, were investigated. The cut-off values were estimated using the receiver operating characteristic curve and area under the curve (AUC). The final cut-off value was selected as the point where the sum of sensitivity and specificity was maximized. Univariate analysis was performed using the log-rank test. Statistical significance was P-values <0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using EZR (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Saitama, Japan), a graphical user interface for R d4 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). EZR is a modified version of the R commander designed to add statistical functions commonly used in biostatistics [6].

RESULTS

Characteristics of patients and metastatic liver tumors

Twenty-two males and two females with a median age of 69 (range, 52–80) years and 35 lesions were included. Six patients with esophageal cancer and 18 patients with gastric cancer were included. Fifteen patients had one lesion, seven patients had two lesions and two patients had three lesions. The reasons for selecting PBT were underlying diseases in 14 patients, conditions such as age in 3 patients, refusal of surgery in 2 patients and unclear in 5 patients. The median lesion size, fraction size and biological effective dose (BED) using the linear-quadratic model with α/β = 10 Gy ((BED)10) were 32 (7–104) mm, 3.8 Gy (RBE)/fr (2–8 Gy (RBE)/fr) and 96.9 (88.8–115.2) Gy, respectively. Survival outcomes

The median follow-up period of esophagogastric cancers was 18 (4–47) months. The 1-, 2- and 3-year OS rates were 75.0, 51.8 and 45.3%, respectively, and the median OS was 25.3 months, as shown in Fig. 1a. The 1-, 2- and 3-year OS rates of esophageal cancer were 83.3% for all, as shown in Fig. 1b. The 1-, 2- and 3-year OS rates of gastric cancer were 72.2, 42.4 and 33.9%, respectively, and the median OS was 18 months, as shown in Fig. 1b. No statistically significant difference was observed in OS rates between the esophageal and gastric cancer patients (P = 0.112).

The 1-, 2- and 3-year LR cumulative incidence of esophagogastric cancers were 3, 6 and 6%, respectively, as shown in Fig. 2a. The 1-, 2- and 3-year mortalities of esophagogastric cancers patients without LR cumulative incidence were 26.9, 43.3 and 43.3%, respectively, as shown in Fig. 2a. The 1- and 2-year LR cumulative incidences of the esophageal cancer patients were 0% for all, as shown in Fig. 2b, and the 1-, 2- and 3-year LR cumulative incidences of the gastric cancer patients were 4, 8 and 8%, respectively, as shown in Fig. 2b. No statistically significant difference in LR cumulative incidence was observed between the esophageal and gastric cancer patients (P = 0.419). The 1- and 2-year mortalities of esophageal cancer patients without LR cumulative incidence were 25% for all as shown in Fig. 2b, and the 1-, 2- and 3-year mortality of the gastric cancer patients without LR cumulative incidence were 28, 49.3 and 49.3%, respectively, as shown in Fig. 2b. No statistically significant difference regarding mortality without LR cumulative incidence was observed between the esophageal and gastric cancer patients (P = 0.236).

Of the six esophageal cancer patients, no additional new liver metastases occurred after PBT, and three patients (50%) developed distant metastases outside the liver. One patient was treated with chemotherapy, while the remaining two patients were treated with the best supportive care (BSC) without chemotherapy. The death of one esophageal cancer patient was cause-specific. Of the 18 gastric cancer patients, 2 patients (11.1%) had LR within the irradiated field after PBT, 1 patient (5.6%) developed additional new liver metastases, 1 patient (5.6%) developed regional lymph node metastases and 5 patients (27.8%) developed distant metastases outside the liver. Both patients with LR received chemotherapy after recurrence. Of the seven gastric cancer patients in whom new lesions appeared, chemotherapy was administered in one patient and the remaining six patients did not receive chemotherapy and were treated with BSC. Of the 12 deaths in the gastric cancer patients, 9 were cause-specific deaths, 2 were due to other diseases and 1 was due to an unknown cause.

OS-related factors

Patients’ age (P < 0.01), performance status (P = 0.017) and tumor size (for multiple lesions, the largest size) (P = 0.024) were significant OS-related factors. The number of liver tumors (P = 0.264) or any combined chemotherapy at any time were not significantly related to the OS (Table 2).

Safety outcomes

All patients completed PBT without interruption. No Grade 3 or higher AEs were observed. None of the patients experienced radiation-induced liver failure during the acute or late phase.

DISCUSSION

We analyzed the outcome of PBT for hepatic oligometastases of esophagogastric cancer using our nationwide multi-institutional cohort study in Japan. The present study showed a satisfactory LR cumulative incidence for liver oligometastasis of esophagogastric cancer patients. Furthermore, we were able to demonstrate the absence of Grade 3 or higher AEs. There are very few reports of PBT for liver metastases of esophagogastric cancers, and we consider our finding valuable and worthwhile.

In general, cancer patients with oligometastases are at risk for the appearance of new, iatrogenic liver metastases and distant metastases [7, 8]. Therefore, repeatability is an important factor in local treatment options. Multiple surgeries may be difficult because of the limited function of the post-operative remaining liver. Furthermore, several patients who undergo esophagectomy or gastrectomy suffer a deterioration in their nutritional status and sequelae related to gastrectomy for a long time, such as weight loss, reduce food intake and sarcopenia. Patients may also be reluctant to undergo surgery due to the pain and invasiveness of the procedure. Oligometastatic conditions require less invasive and more locally controlled treatment options, and radiofrequency ablation (RFA) has anatomic limitations, such as the need for a safe puncture route and distance from major vessels and bile ducts. There are also restrictions on the size of the tumor, with a maximum size of 5 cm. Although SBRT has fewer anatomic limitations than RFA, treating tumors adjacent to the intestinal tract with SBRT may be difficult. The maximum size of treatable tumors is also limited to ~5 cm. On the other hand, PBT can be used to safely treat tumors in the porta hepatis as well as tumors close to the intestinal tract by changing the dose fractions [9–11]. In addition, PBT can safely treat even larger liver tumors compared to SBRT [12], and re-irradiation of PBT for additional heterochronous liver tumors can be safely performed [13].

At the present time, PBT for metastatic liver tumors is not covered by public health insurance in Japan and the financial burden on patients is high. Unresectable intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma is covered by public health insurance regardless of size, and hepatocellular carcinoma exceeding 4 cm in size has also been covered by public health insurance since April 2022. It is desirable that proton therapy be covered by public health insurance because there are some tumors that can only be treated with proton beams regardless of whether they are primary or metastatic liver cancers.

There are very few reports of definitive treatment for liver metastases of esophageal cancer. Hepatic resection for post-operative liver metastatic recurrence of esophageal cancer has been reported in only a few case reports involving one or two cases [14–19]. Table 3 provides a summary of the outcomes of liver resection for post-operative liver metastases of esophageal cancer, and the 1- and 2-year OSs were 50.8–75 and 20–50%, respectively [20–22]. Post-operative Grade 3 or higher complications were observed in 6 of 26 (23%) patients [21]. There are very few reports regarding radiotherapy for liver metastasis of esophageal cancer. There are only three cases reports on SBRT for liver metastasis of esophageal cancer in English [23, 24] and Japanese [25]. There is only one report of PT, including PBT in the past. This study included seven males with a median age of 66 (range, 58–78) years, and 15 lesions were included in the study. The median tumor size was 22.6 (7–55.3) mm. The median survival time (MST) was 35.5 (13.2–119.4) months. The 1-, 2- and 3-year OS rates were 100, 57.1 and 42.9%, respectively. The median PFS time was 8.7 (1.2–44.1) months. The 1-, 2- and 3-year PFS rates were 28.6%. The 1-, 2- and 3-year local control (LC) rates were 100% [26]. The present study showed the 1-, 2- and 3-year OS rates of esophageal cancer were 83.3% for all and no Grade 3 or higher AEs. Our findings were comparable to surgical outcomes.

A checkpoint inhibitor is sometimes used as an adjuvant therapy in patients with Stage II or III esophageal cancer patients with resection (R0). A study reported that the median disease-free survival of the patients who received nivolumab was 22.4 months, which was longer compared with 11.0 months among the patients who received a placebo [27]. This finding indicates that there is a risk of recurrence and the presence of microscopic tumors even after complete resection. For the same reason, microscopic metastases may be present in recurrent liver metastases even if they are regarded as oligometastases on imaging studies. Invasiveness of surgery may weaken the patient’s resistance and allow microscopic metastases to grow. PBT might be advantageous for esophageal cancer liver metastases because not only does it provide a high LC rate, but there are also fewer barriers to incorporating it into post-treatment. PBT could be used concurrently, without interfering, with chemotherapy. Esophageal cancer can cause additional heterochronous metastatic tumors, some of which might occur in the liver. It is highly possible that additional treatment will be required for new metastatic tumors developed in esophageal cancer patients. Considering the risk of additional treatment for such complicated patients, local treatment for liver metastasis should be much less invasive in order to perform repeated interventions over a long period. The safety and maintenance of liver function after PBT for liver tumor have previously been demonstrated [4, 9, 26, 28, 29]. PBT is attractive not only because of its local therapeutic effect but also because it preserves extra liver capacity for additional treatment.

Satisfactory results of PBT for liver metastases of gastric cancer have been reported. The 1- and 3-year OS rates were 100 and 69–78%, respectively, the 1- and 3-year LC rates were 89–100 and 78–85.7%, respectively, and no Grade 3 or higher AEs were observed [4, 5]. Previous studies of RFA for liver metastases of gastric cancer reported that the 1- and 3-year OSs were 70–73 and 5–43%, respectively [30, 31]. Seven of 30 patients (23%) who underwent RFA experienced post-treatment complications, including three (10%) severe complications [31]. Regarding the treatment outcome of surgery for liver metastases of gastric cancer, the 1- and 3-year OS rates were reported to be 76–84 and 41–50%, respectively [31–33]. Guner reported that 19 of 68 patients (28%) had complications, including 12 (18%) severe complications of Grade 3 or higher. Moreover, treatment-related mortality was noted in one patient (1.5%): the patient underwent a right-inferior segmentectomy and died on the 19th post-operative day due to hepatic insufficiency [31]. The summary of clinical results of PBT for liver metastasis in gastric cancer patients is shown in Table 4. A systematic review of 19 studies on surgery reported that the 1- and 5-year OS rates were 60.2 and 26.5%, and the median OS was 17 months, with an in-hospital mortality rate of 3.5% [34]. In the present study, the median OS was 18 months, which is not too far behind surgical outcomes. The most important clinical benefit of PBT is high safety evidenced by the absence of Grade 3 or higher AE cases.

This study has several limitations. First, this is not a strictly designed prospective study, and treatment methods are not necessarily consistent across facilities. In order to eliminate this bias, we have standardized the protocols, such as dose fractionation, at all facilities in Japan according to JASTRO. In addition, we have tried to minimize inter-institutional bias by enrolling only patients who were approved by the cancer board, including physicians and surgeons at each institution. Second, the sample size was small. As previous non-surgical reports have shown, it is extremely difficult for a single institution to accumulate a certain number of cases for these diseases. We have been collaborating with facilities throughout Japan to register and analyze data. In the present study, we included as many cases as possible by using the cohort data of a nationwide scale in Japan. Third, because of the short observation period, there is room for reconsideration of OS and LR cumulative incidence data. The above Japanese nationwide cohort data have been collected since 2016 in preparation for public medical insurance coverage in the near future. It has only been a few years since it started and is still ongoing. We plan to accumulate long-term follow-up data on more cases in the future. As the number of cases and observation period increase, we plan to add multifaceted analyses, including comparisons of synchronous and metachronous recurrence, period since the first recurrence and treatment prior to PBT.

CONCLUSION

Based on the low incidence of AEs and the low LR cumulative incidence, PBT is a feasible option for treatment of liver oligometastasis in esophagogastric cancer patients.

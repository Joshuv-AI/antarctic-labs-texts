The role of BLZF1 in lung adenocarcinoma and its value as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker
Authors: Chen Zhou, Zaoxiu Hu, Tang Yu, Tingdong Yu, Qiubo Huang, Jie Zhao, Mingsheng Ma, Yunchao Huang, Lianhua Ye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most common type of lung cancer and seriously threatens the lives of many people worldwide. The difficulty in early diagnosis of lung cancer has always been a difficulty in lung cancer treatment, and basic leucine zipper nuclear factor 1 (BLZF1) has been shown to promote the occurrence and development of various cancers. This study used 505 LUAD patients in TCGA (TCGA dataset) and 60 LUAD patients in Yunnan Cancer Hospital (Clinical dataset) as research subjects to explore the role of BLZF1 in LUAD. The study found that the expression levels of mRNA and protein of the BLZF1 gene in cancer tissues were significantly higher than those in para-cancer tissues in the TCGA dataset and clinical dataset (P < 0.001). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves analysis found that the AUC value of BLZF1 was 0.759 (95% CI = 0.7049–0.8140, P < 0.0001) in the TCGA dataset, and the AUC value was 0.9985 (95% CI = 0.9954–1.0000, P < 0.0001) in the clinical dataset. Moreover, the BLZF1 gene expression level in the two data sets was significantly correlated with the patient’s T stage, and survival analysis showed that high BLZF1 gene expression levels were associated with decreased recurrence-free survival (RFS) (HR = 1.510, 95% Cl = 1.095–2.083, P = 0.012) and overall survival (OS) (HR = 1.472, 95% Cl = 1.099–1.973, P = 0.010). Protein-protein interaction (PPI) and enrichment analysis showed that the BLZF1 gene was closely associated with biological processes such as Golgi function, vesicle transport and cell membrane system maintenance. The expression correlation of BLZF1 with glycolysis-related genes indicated that BLZF1 may play a role in LUAD by participating in the sugar metabolism pathway. In addition, this study downregulated the expression of BLZF1 by small interfering RNA (si-BLZF1), and downregulation of BLZF1 expression significantly inhibited the proliferation, cloning, migration and invasion of LUAD cells. Therefore, BLZF1 may be involved in the occurrence and development of lung adenocarcinoma, which can be a potential diagnostic biomarker in clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-40856-1.Keywords: Basic leucine zipper nuclear factor 1, Clinical data, Glycolytic, Lung adenocarcinoma, TCGA datasetSubject terms: Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Biomarkers, Medical research

Introduction

Lung cancer is the second most common cancer and the leading cause of cancer death worldwide1. The two main types of lung cancer are non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer (SCLC), of which NSCLC accounts for 85% of lung cancer2. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most common NSCLC, accounting for about 40–50% of the total. Lung cancer is difficult to detect early, has high tumor biological malignancy, complex molecular heterogeneity, the susceptibility to recurrence and metastasis3–5, which have always been the difficulties in lung cancer treatment. Early lung cancer usually has no obvious symptoms, and more than 70% of patients are already in the locally advanced or metastatic stage when diagnosed6 that can make surgical treatment extremely risky.

The incidence and mortality of lung cancer continue to rise, and the search for effective diagnostic has become an important direction for lung cancer research and clinical managements7,8. The commonly used serum tumor markers for lung cancer in clinical practice are carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA)9, cytokeratin 19 fragment (CYFRA21-1)10, neuron-specific enolase (NSE)11 and progastrin-releasing peptide (ProGRP)12. Although these biomarkers play a certain role in auxiliary diagnosis and disease monitoring, the sensitivity and specificity of these genes are generally insufficient, making it difficult to detect early lung cancer. Furthermore, targeted therapy-related markers at the molecular level are also used in the diagnosis and prognosis model of NSCLC, such as epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation13, anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)14 and c-ros oncogene 1 (ROS1) gene15 rearrangement. Such markers can improve the level of personalized treatment, but they are affected by population differences, have high detection costs, and are difficult to promote. Immune-related markers are used to predict the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as PD-L1 expression level16 and tumor mutation burden (TMB)17. However, there is a lack of unified standards for immune-related markers, and the prediction results are controversial.

Basic leucine zipper nuclear factor 1 (BLZF1) belongs to the b-ZIP nuclear factor family, and BLZF1 protein is distributed in the nucleus and cells and is involved in the regulation of cell stress response and apoptosis18. Studies have shown that BLZF1 is related to the occurrence and development of various cancers, such as liver cancer19, gastric cancer20 and prostate cancer21. A study have found that BLZF1-mediated localization and activation of mutant KIT in the Golgi apparatus in gastrointestinal stromal tumors activates activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6) in the unfolded protein response (UPR) pathway to relieve endoplasmic reticulum stress, which can evade immune clearance and promote tumor growth20. In liver cancer19 and prostate cancer22, the expression level of BLZF1 has been shown to have prognostic significance.

The difficulty in early diagnosis of LUAD is one of the main reasons for its high mortality. Several molecular markers have been identified in clinical practice for routine testing in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, such as EGFR, ALK, ROS1, v-raf murine sarcoma viral oncogene homolog B1 (BRAF), mesenchymal-epithelial transition factor (MET), rearranged during transfection (RET) and Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog (KRAS)7,8. However, these gene tests do not fully cover LUAD subtypes, poor dynamic monitoring capabilities and rely on invasive sampling. Therefore, developing new diagnostic models remains an important direction for current lung cancer research. In this study, the expression characteristics and potential functions of BLZF1 in LUAD were systematically explored by integrating LUAD patient data in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database with clinical sample data from Yunnan Cancer Hospital. We evaluated the feasibility of BLZF1 as a prognostic biomarker for LUAD based on public databases and clinical samples, and constructed a corresponding prognostic model. Through bioinformatics analysis combined with in vitro cell experiments, the potential molecular mechanism of BLZF1 in regulating the occurrence and development of LUAD was deeply verified. From data mining to mechanism exploration, this study systematically revealed the important role of BLZF1 in lung adenocarcinoma, providing a new theoretical basis and research direction for early screening, personalized treatment and targeted intervention of LUAD.

Materials and methods

Data source

This study downloaded RNA sequencing (RNAseq) data of 520 LUAD patients from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA)23(https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/ genome-sequencing/tcga) and the patient’s demographic information, such as age, sex, smoking history, histological grade, tumor node metastasis (TNM) stage, residual tumor presence, treatment history and lung cancer recurrence. The exclusion criteria for TCGA-LUAD samples were: (1) Samples without complete clinical or survival information. (2) RNA-seq samples with low read counts or low expression levels (FPKM = 0 in more than 50% of genes). (3) Outlier samples identified by principal component analysis (PCA).

This study also collected LUAD patients who underwent surgical treatment in the Department of Thoracic Surgery, the Third Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University (Yunnan Cancer Hospital) from September 1, 2021 to September 30, 2024 as clinical dataset. Inclusion criteria were (1) must meet the postoperative pathological diagnosis of LUAD. (2) Patients were older than 18 years and younger than 85 years old. (3) Patients’ pathological specimens were well preserved and could be subjected to immunohistochemical staining. (4) Complete clinical history data were available. Exclusion criteria were (1) Cases that had received other tumor-specific treatments, such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or targeted therapy. (2) The patient suffers from other types of malignant tumors or immune system diseases. All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, and data collection was authorized by the Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University (Ethics Number: KYLX2022016).

Immunohistochemical analysis

Immunohistochemical staining was performed using the MaxVision™ 3 HRP method in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. After dewaxing and dehydration in xylene and graded ethanol, the tissue sections were antigen-retrieved using citrate buffer under high temperature and high pressure conditions. Subsequently, the sections were incubated with rabbit anti-human BLZF1 polyclonal antibody (1:1000, PA5-41466, Invitrogen, USA) at 4 °C overnight. And then enzyme-labeled goat anti-mouse/rabbit IgG polymer (Kit-5010, MaxVision, China) was incubated at room temperature for 15 min. DAB color development solution (DAB-0031, MaxVision, China) was then added for color development. Each batch of IHC experiments included both positive and negative controls (Positive controls were tissue sections known to have high BLZF1 expression, and negative controls included the same tissue section without primary antibody).

All sections were scored independently by two assessors who were familiar with immunohistochemical analysis but blinded to clinical data. The scoring results were used to calculate Cohen’s K value to assess scoring consistency, and K > 0.8 indicates high scoring consistency. For slides with inconsistent results from two scorers, a third professional pathologist reviewed the scoring, and the final score was used as the IRS value for that slide. Positive staining was defined as the presence of tan or brown granules in the cytoplasm of cancer tissue or para-cancer tissues cells. The staining results were evaluated using the immunoreaction scoring system (IRS = SI × PP), where the staining intensity (SI) was scored as 0 (colorless), 1 (light yellow), 2 (brown) and 3 (brown), and the percentage of positive cells (PP) was scored as 0 (0%), 1 (1%−25%), 2 (26%−50%), and 3 (51%−100%). A total IRS score of more than 4 points was considered positive.

Receiver operating characteristic curves

DESeq2 was used to analyze the mRNA expression level of the BLZF1 gene in cancer tissues and para-cancer tissue of LUAD patients in the TCGA dataset, and the gene expression data were standardized and normalized. This study also analyzed the protein expression levels of BLZF1 gene in cancer tissues and para-cancer tissues with immunohistochemical analysis in the clinical dataset. In addition, according to the mRNA expression level of BLZF1 gene in the TCGA dataset and the protein expression level of BLZF1 gene in the clinical dataset, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves of BLZF1 gene in the two datasets were drawn respectively. ROC curve analysis calculates the area under the curve (AUC) with cancerous tissue as positive and adjacent tissue as negative. AUC between 0.71 and 0.90 is medium accuracy, between 0.50 and 0.70 is low accuracy, and above 0.90 is high accuracy24. In addition, we used the “caret” package to perform 10-fold cross-validation to evaluate model stability.

BLZF1 expression with clinicopathological variables and survival

In the TCGA dataset, 505 LUAD patients were divided into high expression group and low expression group according to the median BLZF1 mRNA expression level. In the clinical dataset, 60 LUAD patients were divided into positive and negative groups according to the IRS threshold of 4. The prognosis of LUAD patients in the TCGA dataset and the clinical dataset was evaluated by recurrence-free survival (RFS) and overall survival (OS), and the relationship between BLZF1 expression level and clinicopathological characteristics of LUAD patients was analyzed. Survival analysis was performed using Kaplan-Meier curves plotted using the survival and survminer packages in R software25, and differences between groups were compared using the log-rank test. RFS and OS were calculated in days. The correlation between BLZF1 expression levels and clinicopathological characteristics was analyzed using the chi-square test. All statistical tests were two-tailed.

Gene enrichment analysis

The R package DESeq226 was used to calculate the differentially expressed genes between the BLZF1 high expression group and the low expression group. The median-of-ratios method of DESeq2 was used to correct for differences in sequencing depth and gene length between samples, and a linear model was constructed. The Wald test was used to analyze the significance of expression differences for each gene, and the Benjamini-Hochberg method was used to calculate the free-response ratio (FDR) (q-value) to control for multiple test errors. The selection criteria for differentially expressed genes were |log2FoldChange| > 1 and FDR < 0.05. The kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG)27,28 enrichment analysis was performed using gene-set enrichment analysis (GSEA) software29. Pathways with a P-value less than 0.05, a FDR q-value less than 0.25, and a normalized enrichment score (NES) greater than 1.0 were considered highly enriched.

Protein-protein interaction analysis

The search tool for the retrieval of interacting genes/proteins (STRING) database (https://string-db.org/)30 was used to predict the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of BLZF1, and the query parameters were set as protein name (BLZF1) and species (Homo sapiens). In addition, the STRING database was also used to carry out gene ontology (GO) functional annotation and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis. The screening criteria for enrichment analysis were: the number of annotated genes was greater than 10, and the significance level was P < 0.05.

GEPIA dataset

The gene expression profiling interactive analysis (GEPIA)31 online analysis platform (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/index.html) was used to evaluate the potential correlation between the expression of BLZF1 in LUAD and glycolysis-related genes. GEPIA integrates RNA sequencing expression data from the GTEx and TCGA projects. We used its “Correlation” module, selected LUAD as the research object, input the target gene BLZF1 and glycolysis-related genes, and analyzed the expression correlation between them in LUAD tissues.

Cell culture and RNA interference

Human NSCLC cell lines (NCI-H1734 and NCI-H1975) and normal alveolar epithelial cells (Beas-2B) were procured from the Chinese Academy of Sciences in Shanghai, China. Human NSCLC cell lines (NCI-H2009 and HCC827) were procured from the BeNa Culture Collection, China. Beas-2B, NCI-H1734, NCI-H1975, HCC827cells were cultured in 1640 Medium (Gibco, Grand Island, USA) and NCI-H2009 were cultured in EMEM Medium (Gibco, Grand Island, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco), penicillin, and streptomycin. Following 24 h of culture, cells were treated with small interfering RNA (siRNA) (Ribobio, China) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher, USA) in a serum-free medium, maintaining incubation at 37 °C and 5% CO2. The siRNA sequence targeting BLZF1 was si-BLZF1-1 sense GGA ACA GTT TCG TCA AGA A and si-BLZF1-1 antisense CCT TGT CAA AGC AGT TCT T, si-BLZF1-2 sense CCA ACC ACC TTA CTT GCT A and si-BLZF1-2 antisense GGT TGG TGG AAT GAA CGA T, while negative control sense UUC UCC GAA CGU GUC ACG UTT and negative control antisense ACG UGA CAC GUU CGG AGA ATT.

Real-time-quantitative polymerase chain reaction

Total RNA was isolated from H1734 cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, NY, USA) following the manufacturer’s guidelines. Subsequently, 2 µg of total RNA was reverse transcribed into single-stranded cDNA utilizing a reverse transcription kit (Tiangeng, Beijing). QRT-PCR was conducted using the TB Green dye detection system (Takara, Japan) with β-actin as an endogenous control. Each reaction was performed in duplicate on an Applied Biosystems 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System under the following conditions 95 °C for 30 seconds, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 seconds and 60 °C for 34 seconds. The relative mRNA expression level of BLZF1 was calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT method. Primer sequences used were: β-actin forward 5’-CCT TCC TGG GCA TGG AGT C-3’ and β-actin reverse 5’-TGA TCT TCA TTG TGC TGG GTG-3’, BLZF1 forward 5’-GAT ACC GAT CCG AAG AGC CTA TC-3’ and BLZF1 reverse 5’-TGGTTCCTCAGTTTCCATTCCAT-3’.

Western blotting

Total protein was extracted using an ice-filled column and the Column Total Protein Extraction Kit (Seven Biotech Co. Ltd., China). The protein concentration was determined using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay (Solarbio Science & Technology Co. Ltd, China). Following a five-minute incubation of denatured cell lysates at 95 °C, quantified proteins (4 µg/µl) were mixed with sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) sample loading buffer, and 80 µg of protein per lane was separated by SDS-PAGE on 10% gels. The proteins were then transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in Tween-20 (TBST) buffer for one hour at room temperature, followed by overnight incubation at 4 °C with either mouse monoclonal anti-alpha-Tubulin (1:1000, 250009, Zen Biotechnology Co. Ltd, China) or rabbit polyclonal anti-BLZF1 (1:1000, 16354-1-AP, Proteintech, USA). Afterward, the membranes were incubated for one hour at room temperature with goat anti-mouse (1:5000, 511103, Zen Biotechnology Co. Ltd., China) or goat anti-rabbit (1:5000, AS014, ABclonal Technology Co. Ltd., China) secondary antibodies. Bands were imaged and quantified using a Chemiluminescent Imaging System (Biotanon, China) and visualized with enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL, Zen Biotechnology Co. Ltd, China).

Cell proliferation

Cell proliferation assay was performed using Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Biosharp, China). CCK-8 solution was added at 24, 48 and 72 h, and the absorbance at 450 nm was measured.

Clone formation assay

500 cells were seeded per well in six-well plates, and the culture medium was changed every three days. When clearly visible cell colonies were formed in the culture dish, the culture was terminated. The cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and then stained with 0.1% crystal violet solution (Beijing Solebow Technology Co., Ltd., China) for 20 min. The clones containing more than 50 cells were counted and analyzed.

Wound healing assay

Cells were seeded in six-well plates and wounds were created by vertically scratching the cell monolayer using a sterile 10 µL pipette tip when the cells reached approximately 80%–90% confluence. Images of the same area were taken using a Nikon microscope at 0 and 24 h to assess cell migration.

Transwell assay

Transwell chambers (LABSELECT, China) with 8 μm pore size were employed for invasion and migration experiments. Following transfection, cells were extracted 24 h later, reconstituted, and transferred to the upper chamber in a volume of 250 µL. Subsequently, 700 µL of medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) was added to the lower chamber, and the system was incubated for 36 h.

Cell Supernatant Lactate Content

Seed cells into 12-well plates and culture for 24 h. Centrifuge at 300–500 × g for 5 min and collect the supernatant. A lactate content assay kit (BC2230, Beijing Solarbio) was used to measure the lactate content in the cell supernatant. Follow the kit’s instructions for specific procedures.

Statistical analysis

Kaplan-Meier analysis and univariate Cox regression were used to evaluate the prognostic effects of OS and RFS in LUAD patients. The standard error (SE) of AUC is calculated using the AUC standard error estimation formula proposed by Hanley and McNeil32. The proportional hazards assumption for the Cox regression model was evaluated using Schoenfeld residuals with the cox.zph function in the R “survival” package. P value greater than 0.05 was considered indicative of no violation of the proportional hazards assumption. The formula for calculating the statistic is z = (AUC − 0.5)/SE, and the significance and post-hoc power are obtained. The χ2 test was used to analyze the correlation between clinical indicators of LUAD patients and BLZF1 gene expression levels. GraphPad Prism v7 (GraphPad Software, Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA) and ggplot2 in R were used for graphical analysis in this study. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS v21.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA), and all two-sided tests were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Basic information of LUAD patients

A total of 520 LUAD patients were downloaded from the TCGA database. 15 cases that did not meet the inclusion criteria were excluded, and a total of 505 LUAD patients met the inclusion criteria (Table S1). In this data, 31.72% LUAD patients were under 60 years of age, while 68.28% LUAD patients were over 60. TNM staging results showed that 24 of 348 cases (6.67%) had distant metastasis, and 167 of 505 cases (33.81%) had local lymph node infiltration. After surgery, 327 cases (66.19%) had no residual tumor, and 167 cases (33.81%) had residual tumor. 271 cases (54.53%) of LUAD tumors were in stage I, and 226 cases (45.47%) were in stage II, III and IV. In addition, 111 LUAD patients of clinical data were obtained from Yunnan Cancer Hospital in this study, and a total of 60 LUAD patients met the criteria (Table S2).

Expression level and ROC curve of BLZF1 in LUAD patients

To explore the role of BLZF1 in LUAD, this study analyzed the differences in the expression levels of BLZF1 in cancer tissues and para-cancer tissues and the ROC curve. The study found that the expression level of BLZF1 gene in tumor tissue was significantly higher than that in non-tumor tissue (P < 0.001) in the TCGA dataset (Fig. 1A). In addition, the protein expression level of BLZF1 gene in clinical dataset was consistent with the mRNA expression level in TCGA dataset (Fig. 1B-C). And the tumor tissue showed extensive and dense brown staining, while the normal tissue showed relatively light and sparse staining (Fig. 1C), which indicated that BLZF1 protein expression is significantly enhanced in tumor cells, mainly located in the cytoplasm. The intensity scores of BLZF1 protein expression in LUAD tissue were 2 (1.7%), 3 (1.7%), 4 (6.7%), 6 (31.7%) and 9 (58.3%), and the intensity scores of BLZF1 protein expression in para-cancer tissue were 0 (46.7%), 1 (46.7%), 2 (3.3%) and 3 (3.3%). ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC value of the TCGA dataset was 0.759 (95% CI = 0.7049–0.8140, P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1D), and the statistical power is close to 1 which can indicate that the TCGA cohort has sufficient statistical power to detect intermediate and above expression differences. After 10-fold cross-validation, the mean AUC was 0.7424 (95% CI = 0.6952–0.7893), indicating stable classification performance without evident overfitting. While the AUC value of the clinical dataset was 0.9985 (95% CI = 0.9954–1.0000, P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1E), this could be due to sample bias or overfitting. After 10-fold cross-validation, the mean AUC was 0.9343 (95% CI = 0.9015–0.9679), suggesting excellent diagnostic performance with reduced overfitting.

The expression level of BLZF1 and clinicopathological characteristics

To further analyze the clinical significance of BLZF1 expression in LUAD, we analyzed the correlation between the expression level of BLZF1 gene and various clinicopathological characteristics. In the TCGA dataset, high expression of BLZF1 mRNA was not significantly correlated with age, gender and M-stage (P > 0.05), but was significantly correlated with T-stage, N-stage, smoking history, new tumor events and tumor residuals (P < 0.05) (Table 1). In the clinical dataset, increased BLZF1 protein expression was associated with T-stage (P = 0.026), but not with age, gender, N-stage, M-stage and smoking history (P > 0.05). The above results indicate that increased expression of BLZF1 gene is related to tumor biological behavior in LUAD patients, especially the local invasion ability of primary tumors. Its consistent correlation with T stage in different datasets indicates that BLZF1 can be used as a potential biomarker for LUAD progression (Table 2).

Survival analysis

This study also used Kaplan-Meier survival analysis to explore the relationship between the expression level of BLZF1 and the prognosis of LUAD patients. High expression of BLZF1 was significantly associated with worse prognosis compared with low expression in the TCGA dataset (Fig. 1F) and the clinical dataset (Fig. 1G) (P < 0.05). In the TCGA dataset, the study found that increased BLZF1 gene expression levels were associated with decreased RFS (HR = 1.510, 95% Cl = 1.095–2.083, P = 0.012) and OS (HR = 1.472, 95% Cl = 1.099–1.973, P = 0.010) (Table 3). The proportional hazards hypothesis was tested using the Schoenfeld residual test, and the results showed that BLZF1 expression level and other covariates did not violate the proportional hazards hypothesis (P > 0.05). Further studies found that the T stage, N stage, AJCC stage, tumor recurrence, radiation therapy and residual tumor of LUAD patients were significantly correlated with OS (P < 0.01) and RFS (P < 0.01). M stage of LUAD patients was significantly correlated with OS (P < 0.01), smoking history was significantly correlated with RES (P < 0.01).

Gene enrichment analysis

To analyze the potential signaling pathways associated with the BLZF1 regulatory mechanism, this study used the TCGA dataset to calculate the differentially expressed genes between the high and low BLZF1 expression groups in LUAD and performed KEGG enrichment analysis (Fig. 2). We found the top eight signaling pathways associated with BLZF1, namely KEGG ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis (P = 0.006), hallmark protein secretion (P = 0.010), hallmark myc targets V1 (P = 0.010), KEGG pyrimidine metabolism (P = 0.010), hallmark glycolysis (P = 0.012), KEGG protein export (P = 0.014), KEGG nucleotide excision repair (P = 0.012) and KEGG proteasome (P = 0.008). This result suggests that high expression of BLZF1 may be closely related to various cancer-related biological processes, such as cell metabolism regulation, protein degradation, DNA repair and MYC pathway.

GSEA reveals BLZF1-associated signaling pathways in LUAD. The GSEA results showcased a significant enrichment of genes associated with BLZF1 in crucial signaling pathways, including KEGG ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis, hallmark protein secretion, hallmark myc targets V1, KEGG pyrimidine metabolism, hallmark glycolysis, KEGG protein export, KEGG nucleotide excision repair, and KEGG proteasome with in LUAD cases derived from the TCGA datasets.

Protein-protein interaction analysis

The correlation between BLZF1 and glycolysis-related genes in LUAD patients

Previous KEGG enrichment analysis results suggested that BLZF1 may play a role in LUAD by participating in glucose metabolism-related pathways. This study used the GEPIA online analysis platform to explore the expression correlation between BLZF1 and glycolysis-related genes (Fig. 4) to verify this speculation. The results showed that the expression level of BLZF1 was significantly positively correlated with a variety of key glycolysis genes, including glucose transporter 1 (GLUT1), hexokinase 1 and 2 (HK1, HK2), phosphofructokinase (PFKF), phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK1), phosphoglycerate mutase 1 (PGAM1), enolase 1 (ENO1), pyruvate kinase M type (PKM), lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA), phosphoglucomutase 1 and 2 (PGM1, PGM2) and hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF1A) (P < 0.05). These key genes play a core role in the glycolysis pathway, indicating that BLZF1 may promote the occurrence and progression of tumors by regulating glycolysis metabolic activities, affecting the energy metabolism, proliferation ability and ability to adapt to hypoxic environments of LUAD cells.

Functional verification of BLZF1 in LUAD cells

This study selected four LUAD cell lines (NCI-H1734, NCI-H2009, NCI-H1975, HCC827) and normal alveolar epithelial cells (Beas-2B) to explore the role of BLZF1 gene in LUAD. The study found that the expression level of BLZF1 gene in H1734 cells was significantly higher than that in Beas-2B of the control group, while there was no significant difference in the expression level of BLZF1 gene between the other three LUAD cell lines and the control group (Fig. 5A-B). Therefore, this study selected H1734 cells for subsequent research.

In H1734 cells, both si-BLZF1-1 and si-BLZF1-2 were able to significantly reduce the mRNA (Fig. 5C) and protein (Fig. 5D) expression levels of BLZF1 gene (P < 0.001). CCK-8 assay showed that both si-BLZF1-1 and si-BLZF1-2 could significantly reduce cell proliferation (Fig. 5E) (P < 0.01). Transwell assay (Fig. 5F-G) (P < 0.01) and wound healing assay (Fig. 5J-K) (P < 0.01) showed that both si-BLZF1-1 and si-BLZF1-2 could inhibit LUAD cell migration. Cell invasion assay also showed that both si-BLZF1-1 and si-BLZF1-2 could inhibit LUAD cell invasion (Fig. 5H-I) (P < 0.01). The wound healing assay showed that the wound healing rates in the si-BLZF1-1 and si-BLZF1-2 groups were significantly lower than those in the control group (Fig. 5J-K). The above results showed that BLZF1 has the effects of promoting proliferation, migration and invasion in LUAD cells, suggesting that it may play a key role in tumorigenesis and progression. In addition, this study also analyzed the relationship between BLZF1 and glycolysis pathway by measuring the lactose content of cell supernatant lactate (Fig. S1). The lactate content of si-BLZF1-1 and si-BLZF1-2 groups was significantly lower than that of the control group (P < 0.01), which indicates that BLZF1 can participate in the occurrence and development of LUAD through glycolysis pathway. Therefore, BLZF1 is expected to be a potential oncogene for LUAD and provide a new molecular basis for its diagnosis and targeted therapy.

Discussion

BLZF1 as a candidate diagnostic biomarker for LUAD

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide33, and finding reliable biomarkers is crucial for early diagnosis and prognosis assessment of LUAD. Based on BLZF1 gene expression levels and clinical patient data in the TCGA dataset and clinical datasets, this study revealed a significant association between high BLZF1 expression and poor prognosis in LUAD patients. This suggests that BLZF1 can serve as a valuable biomarker for this cancer type.

Studies have shown that the expression of BLZF1 in various tumors is significantly different, indicating that it may play a variety of biological functions in the occurrence and development of different types of cancer19,20. In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) tissue, the expression of BLZF1 was significantly downregulated at both the mRNA and protein levels19. The subcellular localization analysis of this study found that the reduced expression of BLZF1 in the nucleus and cytoplasm of tumor cells was associated with poor OS and DFS. In particular, nuclear-localized BLZF1 was identified as an independent prognostic factor, suggesting that its low expression may predict poor prognosis in HCC patients. In addition, BLZF1 expression also decreased significantly in acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL), but after retinoic acid (RA)-induced differentiation therapy, its expression level was significantly restored which accompanies by cell differentiation and maturation34. In contrast, the latest study found that BLZF1 is highly expressed in gastrointestinal stromal tumor cell lines and may be involved in maintaining the anchoring of mutant KIT protein in the Golgi apparatus and the activation of its downstream signals20. These findings suggest that the expression pattern of BLZF1 in different tumor types may be significantly heterogeneous, and thus play different roles in the occurrence, progression and treatment response of various tumors. In this study, we found that the mRNA and protein expression levels of BLZF1 in LUAD cancer tissues were significantly higher than those in para-cancer tissues, and high BLZF1 expression was associated with poor prognosis in patients with lung adenocarcinoma. Adverse clinicopathological parameters such as lymph node metastasis, new tumor events, and tumor residual were associated with increased BLZF1 mRNA expression in lung cancer patients, indicating that it may serve as an oncogene in LUAD.

EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET, RET and KRAS are commonly used clinical biomarkers for LUAD35, but their clinical application has certain limitations. These target sites are mainly focused on targetable mutations, but approximately 30% of LUAD patients do not carry any targetable mutations36. Furthermore, the detection of these genes is primarily used for subtyping and targeted therapy decisions in patients already diagnosed with LUAD, offering limited help for early screening37. EGFR mutation detection is mainly used to guide the use of EGFR-TKIs (such as osimertinib and erlotinib) to prolong progression-free survival in advanced patients38. ALK and ROS1 correspond to the selection of ALK and ROS1 inhibitors39. In addition, KRAS, BRAF and MET abnormalities can also guide the use of specific drugs, but these also require tumor tissue samples40, and the testing process is invasive that makes them unsuitable for population screening or early detection. Moreover, most LUAD patients develop secondary resistance mutations during treatment, such as patients with EGFR mutations may develop secondary mutations such as T790M or C797S41. Compared with existing LUAD detection markers, BLZF1 is consistently upregulated across different molecular subtypes, indicating its cross-mutant applicability and broader prognostic prediction potential. While BLZF1 demonstrated promising diagnostic potential in tissue samples, whether BLZF1 mRNA and protein can be detected non-invasively via liquid biopsy requires further investigation in future studies.

The role of BLZF1 in the occurrence and development of LUAD

This study found that BLZF1 may affect the occurrence and progression of tumors in LUAD by interacting with multiple highly expressed related proteins. MAN2A1 is abnormally expressed in many cancers and can participate in tumor cell signal transduction and immune escape by regulating the processing of glycoproteins42. Studies have found that MAN2A1 may promote the growth and invasion of cancer cells in lung cancer by affecting the tumor microenvironment and surface sugar chain modification43. Small GTPase family members play an important role in maintaining vesicle transport, intracellular localization and membrane fusion, such as RAB33B, RAB1A and RAB1644. Activation of RAB1A can promote the mTORC1 signaling pathway to stimulate cell metabolism and proliferation, and has been confirmed to be upregulated in many tumors, such as liver cancer45 and gastric cancer46. GOLIM4 is an important protein that maintains the structural stability of the Golgi apparatus, and has also been found to be upregulated in many tumors47. Its abnormality may affect the protein transport and secretion process, promote the dependence of cancer cells on exocrine factors, and indirectly enhance their migration and metastasis capabilities48.

The pathogenesis of cancer is complex, resulting from the interaction of genetic, behavioral, and environmental variables to develop pathological features. Based on our findings, BLZF1 was linked to pathways that are mostly involved in tumorigenesis and development, including KEGG nucleotide excision repair, hallmark glycolysis, hallmark protein export, hallmark myc targets V1, hallmark protein secretion, KEGG pyrimidine metabolism and KEGG proteasome. Glycolysis is an important metabolic pathway for cells to break down glucose into pyruvate and release energy under aerobic or anaerobic conditions. It is also the most common way for tumor cells to produce energy49. Evidence of activation of the glycolytic pathway has been found in a variety of solid tumors, such as lung cancer50, liver cancer51 and esophageal cancer52. Studies have found that the expression levels of multiple key glycolytic enzymes in lung cancer cells are significantly increased, such as GLUT1, HK1/2, PFK, PKM2 and LDHA53. This is consistent with the results of this study. These enzymes play a key role in maintaining rapid tumor proliferation, anti-apoptosis ability, and promoting the accumulation of tumor-related metabolites (such as lactate)54. The accumulation of lactate further acidifies the microenvironment, inhibits the activity of immune cells, and promotes tumor immune escape and metastasis. In addition, upstream signaling pathways that regulate glycolysis, such as PI3K/AKT/mTOR, HIF-1α and c-MYC are also frequently abnormally activated in cancer, affecting the occurrence and development of cancer55. According to our research results, BLZF1 expression is positively correlated with the expression levels of most key glycolytic enzymes. Based on these findings, it is suggested that BLZF1 may influence lung adenocarcinoma through the glycolytic pathway. BLZF1 could serve as a potential novel target for cancer therapy aimed at modulating glycolytic metabolism.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. Firstly, the functional experiments were mainly conducted in a single type of lung adenocarcinoma cell line. Although the BLZF1 expression level in the selected cell line was relatively high, a single cell line model is insufficient to fully cover the heterogeneity of lung adenocarcinoma. The generalizability of the results in other genetic background cell lines and more clinically relevant models still needs further verification. Secondly, this study lacked validation through in vivo experiments. It is necessary to verify the function of BLZF1 in the mouse environment by constructing animal models. Thirdly, this study initially suggests that the oncogenic effect of BLZF1 may be related to the regulation of the glycolytic process in tumor cells, but the specific molecular mechanisms involved remain unclear. Moreover, this study has revealed the potential of BLZF1 as a prognostic marker and therapeutic target. However, its application in clinical practice still faces numerous challenges, such as the standardization of detection methods, the development of treatment strategies and the validation of its effectiveness in different populations. These aspects require confirmation and improvement through larger-scale and multi-center clinical studies.

Conclusions

In this study, we analyzed public databases and clinical sample data separately, and validated the role of BLZF1 in LUAD through cellular experiments. The results showed that BLZF1 was significantly overexpressed in LUAD and was closely associated with a variety of clinical pathological characteristics. And the expression levels of BLZF1 gene and glycolysis-related enzyme genes were significantly correlated, indicating that BLZF1 gene may participate in the occurrence and development of cancer through the glycolysis pathway. These results indicate the feasibility of BLZF1 as a potential carcinogenic factor and therapeutic target for lung adenocarcinoma, and provide a theoretical basis for its clinical application as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker.

Aluminum Distribution on the Microporous and Hierarchical ZSM-5 Intracrystalline and Its Impact on the Catalytic Performance
Authors: Tahta
M Karim, Hiroto Toyoda, Masato Sawada, Liang Zhao, Yong Wang, Peipei Xiao, Lizhuo Wang, Jun Huang, Toshiyuki Yokoi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn our previous work, the control of aluminum distribution on microporous ZSM-5 with and without the addition of sodium (Na) was conducted. In the current research, adjustment of the aluminum distribution on hierarchical ZSM-5 synthesized using a surfactant as a mesoporogen has been carried out. The investigation of aluminum distribution was based on 27Al MAS NMR, constraint index (CI) value, and Co(II) ion-adsorbed UV–vis. The aforementioned characterizations revealed that the hierarchical ZSM-5 with Na exhibited a more concentrated aluminum distribution in the channel intersections than the hierarchical ZSM-5 without Na did. The opposite trend was observed with microporous ZSM-5. Furthermore, the influence of the hydrothermal synthesis time on the formation of the hierarchical structure and the arrangement of aluminum within the framework was also investigated. The prolongation of the hydrothermal synthesis time to 144 h was found to be an optimal period for the formation of a well-hierarchical structure, as demonstrated by the observed increase in the hierarchy factor. Moreover, this process resulted in an increase in the strength of the acid sites and a change in the crystal morphology of the hierarchical ZSM-5 from a coffin-like morphology to a coral reef-like or a flower-like morphology. Additionally, the influence of the alteration in the aluminum distribution on the catalytic performance was also investigated. In the case of the n-hexane cracking and methanol conversion reactions, hierarchical ZSM-5 with Na was observed to produce bulkier molecules (≥C5s) than that without Na. On the other hand, it was observed that the hierarchically structured ZSM-5 exhibited enhanced performance in the production of lower olefins, particularly propene, in comparison to the microporous ZSM-5.Keywords: hierarchical ZSM-5, Al distribution, hydrothermal synthesis time, n-hexane cracking, methanol conversion

Introduction

ZSM-5 is an aluminosilicate zeolite that has a microporous structure with the MFI framework, which is composed of straight and sinusoidal channels.1 Additionally, it has a large surface area, strong acidity, and extraordinary chemical and thermal stability.2−5 These unique properties render ZSM-5 as a widely utilized catalyst, particularly in processes such as cracking,6−8 isomerization,9−11 and alkylation.12−14 Nevertheless, this zeolite exhibits limitations in diffusivity when reactions involve bulky-sized reactants and/or products, as it contains only micropores. This can lead to undesired reactions, including coke formation and deactivation of the catalyst.15,16

Due to these limitations, researchers have been modifying the original ZSM-5 to create a hierarchical ZSM-5 for several decades through various approaches. One of these approaches is the bottom-up approach, which involves the use of mesoporogens, such as a cationic polymer17,18 and a surfactant.1,19 While the incorporation of a mesoporous structure can address the diffusion constraints associated with the original ZSM-5, the catalytic reactivity of zeolite is determined by the presence of acid sites. The acid sites are highly dependent on a number of factors, including the aluminum (Al) content, the Al state, and the Al distribution within the zeolite framework.20 For example, Liang and co-workers disclosed the reaction pathway in the conversion of methanol to olefins (MTO) is determined by the location of Al distribution within the framework, in which Al is more concentrated in the channel intersection and in the channels more favorable to the aromatic- and alkene-based reaction cycles, respectively.21,22 The influence of Al distribution and the pore structure on the reaction pathway is also observed for carbonylation in modernite, ferrierite, and ZSM-22.23−25

Based on our previous works,20,26 the location of Al in the framework can be adjusted by modifying the organic structure directing agent (OSDA) or by pore filling and adding sodium or omitting it from the synthetic gel. Furthermore, based on the reaction of n-hexane cracking, it has been demonstrated that the microporous ZSM-5 exhibits different selectivities when synthesized using cation tetrapropylammonium (SDA) in the presence of cation sodium compared to when synthesized without the presence of cation sodium. However, no reports have been made for hierarchical ZSM-5.

In order to assess the distribution of aluminum in the pores, a number of analytical techniques have been described in the literature, including ultraviolet–visible diffuse reflectance spectroscopy (UV–vis DRS) for the analysis of Co(II) ions and magic angle spinning nuclear magnetic resonance (MAS NMR) for multinuclear (13C, 29Si, 27Al) analyses.27,28 Moreover, our group reported that the Al distribution in the micropore can be estimated by employing the constraint index (CI), which is defined as the ratio of the n-hexane cracking rate to 3-methylpentane (isomer of n-hexane) cracking rate.26,29 The CI value is calculated according to the following formula:

where kn-hexane and k3-metylpentane are the cracking rate of those compounds, and Xn-hexane and X3-metylpentane are the cracking conversion of those compounds.30 For zeolite with a 10-membered ring, the CI value is larger than unity and increases with a decrease in the size of the void space.30−34 This is because narrow spaces burden more severe steric constraints on the bulky transition state of 3-methylpentane than on n-hexane. Therefore, it is expected that the ZSM-5 zeolite with a smaller amount of acid sites (where the Al settles) located in the channel intersection will exhibit a higher CI value.20,29

In light of the aforementioned explanations, this work was carried out to investigate the Al distribution within the hierarchical ZSM-5 intracrystalline structure based on 27Al MAS NMR and Co(II) ion UV–vis analyses and CI estimation. Furthermore, the cracking of n-hexane and methanol to olefins (MTO) reaction over those catalysts was conducted to elucidate the influence of the Al distribution on the catalytic performance. In summary, this study revealed that the hierarchical ZSM-5 with Na exhibited a more concentrated Al distribution in the channel intersections than the hierarchical ZSM-5 without Na, which was a reversed trend compared to the microporous ZSM-5. Moreover, in the catalytic tests, the hierarchical ZSM-5 was more selective in producing olefins, particularly propene, than the microporous ZSM-5, with the highest performance for propene production on the hierarchical ZSM-5 without Na. Additionally, the prolonged crystallization time led to an increase in the HF (hierarchy factor) and homogeneity between the microporous and mesoporous structures.

Experimental Section

Materials

Materials were used in this work without further purification. They were tetrapropylammonium hydroxide (TPAOH) 40% in aqueous solution (Sachem), tetraethyl orthosilicate (TEOS) (TCI, >98%), aluminum nitrate nonahydrate (Wako, 99.9%), sodium hydroxide (Wako), acetic acid (Wako, 99.7%), cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) (TCI, >98%).

Synthesis

The synthesis procedure and the total molar ratio of the mother gel follow as developed by Krisnandi18 et al. and Jiang35 et al. with the modification in case of the hierarchical samples. The total molar ratio of the gel used in this synthesis is 64 Si2O:1 Al2O3:10 TPA2(O):0.5 or 0 Na2O:4.4 CTAB:3571 H2O. First, aluminum nitrate and sodium hydroxide were dissolved in demineralized water. After that, TPAOH was added into the solution, followed by the addition of TEOS. Afterward, pH was adjusted to ∼11 by acetic acid, and then the obtained gel was aged for 3 h. Then, CTAB was added into its mixture, and continued by reaging for 15 h. All processes took place at room temperature and under stirring conditions. The obtained mixture was placed in the autoclave and followed by the hydrothermal process at 160 °C for 72 and 144 h with static conditions, respectively. Subsequently, the obtained suspension was filtered, washed with demineralized water, and dried overnight. Finally, the as-made hierarchical ZSM-5 was calcinated at 550 °C for 5 h. The products were named as Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_X and Hi_Z5_[TPA]_X (X = for the crystallization time) for the hierarchical ZSM-5 containing sodium and without containing sodium, respectively. For obtaining the H-type hierarchical ZSM-5, all samples were ion-exchanged twice by 1 M NH4NO3 aqueous solution at 80 °C for 3 h under stirring conditions, followed by calcination at 550 °C for 4 h. Meanwhile, Co2+ ion-exchanged ZSM-5 zeolites were prepared by H-type ZSM-5 ion-exchanged back to Na-type ZSM-5 using a 1 M NaNO3 solution at 80 °C for 7 h. Then, Na-type ZSM-5 was ion-exchanged with a 0.05 M Co(NO)3 solution three times at 80 °C for 7 h. Finally, Co-ZSM-5 was obtained after being filtered, washed, and dried.

In order to serve as a reference, typical microporous ZSM-5 samples were synthesized with or without sodium. A similar synthesis procedure was used for the microporous ZSM-5. First, aluminum nitrate, sodium hydroxide, TPAOH, and TEOS were dissolved in demineralized water. Then, aging was performed for 3 h. All processes were conducted at room temperature and under stirring conditions, with the total molar ratio of the gel being 64 SiO2:1 Al2O3:10 TPA2O:0.5 or 0 Na2O:3571 H2O. Subsequently, the crystallization process was carried out at 160 °C for 72 and 144 h, respectively, under a rotating condition (40 rpm). The next synthesis procedure was the same as the hierarchical one. The obtained products were designated as Mic_Z5_[TPA+Na]_X and Mic_Z5_[TPA]_X, respectively, denoting microporous ZSM-5 with sodium and without sodium. The X represents the crystallization time. H-type and Co2+ ion-exchanged microporous ZSM-5 were achieved by the same ion exchange and calcination procedures as the hierarchical one.

Characterization

XRD patterns were acquired on a Rint-Ultima III (Rigaku) instrument using a Cu Kα X-ray source (40 kV, 40 mA for wide angle, and 40 kV, 20 mA for small angle). For Field-Emission Scanning Electron Microscopic (FE-SEM) images, they were obtained on SU9000 (Hitachi) microscopes operated at 1 kV. The textural properties of the samples were calculated by nitrogen adsorption–desorption measurement on a Belsorp-Max instrument (MicrotracBEL) conducted at −196 °C, in which the samples were treated at 350 °C for 3 h prior to the measurement. The calculation of BET and t-plot on the hierarchical samples follow as reported by Rouquerol36 et al. and Bosnar37 et al., respectively. The elemental analyses were carried out on an Inductively Coupled Plasma-Atomic Emission Spectrometer (ICP-AES, Shimadzu ICPE-9000). Meanwhile, temperature-programmed ammonia desorption (NH3-TPD) profiles were recorded on a BELCAT-A instrument (MicrotracBEL). 27Al MAS NMR spectra were obtained on a JEOL ECA-600 spectrometer using a 4 mm ZrO2 rotor with the spinning rate at 15 kHz. The amounts of organic compounds in the as-made samples were determined from the weight loss from 200 to 520 °C obtained from a thermogravimetric differential thermal analyzer (TG-DTA, Rigaku Thermo plus) with a heating rate of 2 K/min. The UV–vis spectrum was obtained from a V-650 spectrophotometer (JASCO), in which prior to the measurement the sample was dehydrated in air at 550 °C for 4 h. The quantities of Brønsted and Lewis acid sites were evaluated by pyridine and collidine adsorption experiments using FTIR (JASCO FT/IR-4100) equipped with a mercury cadmium telluride (MCT) detector. First, the sample was pressed in a self-supporting disk (20 mm diameter) and then placed in an IR cell attached to a conventional closed-gas circulation system.38,39 Then, the pretreatment was carried out by the IR cell heated to 450 °C under vacuum, conducted for 1 h, and then cooled to 150 °C for pyridine and room temperature for collidine prior to the adsorption experiment. Subsequently, the spectrum was recorded and used as a background. After that, the probe molecule was introduced into the cell and then vacuumed until the vacuum condition was met for desorbing the physically adsorbed pyridine/collidine. Then, the temperature of the IR cell was increased to 250 °C for pyridine and 100 °C for collidine, kept for 15 min, then cooled to 150 °C or room temperature, and followed by a recording of the spectrum. The concentration of Brønsted and Lewis acid sites were calculated by using the Beer–Lambert law,40 and the extinction coefficients were used: Brønsted (1.13 cm/μmol) and Lewis (1.28 cm/μmol)41 for pyridine; Brønsted (8.1 cm/μmol) for collidine.42

Catalytic Reaction

The catalytic reactions were conducted by using a fixed-bed flow reactor connected to an online gas chromatograph with a flame ionization detector (FID) and CP Silica PLOT or HP PLOT/Q capillary as the column. The zeolites were shaped to be pellet form with a size of 500–1000 μm without a binder, and they were loaded into a quartz tubular flow microreactor (6 mm inner diameter) with a weight of 15–35 mg.

n-Hexane Cracking

The n-hexane cracking reaction was performed at 650 °C while the partial pressure and W/F (W, amount of catalyst/g; F, flow rate of n-hexane/mol h–1) of n-hexane were set at 30 kPa and at 2.7 gcat·h·mol–1, respectively. The catalyst was activated in flowing Ar at 650 °C for 1 h before the reaction was carried out. The reaction products were analyzed by online GC-FID with a CP-Silica PLOT column.

Methanol to Olefins (MTO) Reaction

The methanol to olefins (MTO) reaction was carried out at 450 °C, the partial pressure of methanol was 15 kPa, and the W/F for methanol was 11.54 gcat·h·mol–1. Prior to the reaction, the catalyst was activated at 550 °C for 1 h in flowing Ar gas. The reaction products were analyzed by an online GC-FID with a HP-PLOT/Q capillary column.

Constraint Index (CI)

The Al distribution in the MFI framework was evaluated by the constraint index, which was based on the cracking of n-hexane and 3-methylpentane at 350 °C. The partial pressure of the C6 paraffins was set at 40 kPa. While, the W/F (weight/flow; the weight of catalyst (g) divided by the flow rate of C6 paraffins fed into the reaction system) was adjusted to obtain <30% conversion. Prior to the reaction, the catalyst was activated in flowing Ar at 600 °C for 1 h, and then cooled to the reaction temperature. After that, 2,4-DMQ (2,4-dimethylquinoline) was injected (20 μL) to poison the acid sites on the mesopore and/or the external surface,43 and then followed by the introduction n-hexane and 3-methylpentane for reaction, respectively.

Results and Discussion

Synthesis and Morphology

In the synthesis of hierarchical ZSM-5 using a surfactant as mesoporogen, it is crucial to consider the hydrothermal process, as it exerts a profound impact on the nucleation and crystal growth processes.44 Therefore, different hydrothermal synthesis times were applied in this work. Based on the wide angle patterns of XRD (Figure 1), it can be observed that all the hierarchical samples exhibited a MFI framework (the doublet peaks at 2θ = 7–9° and triplet peaks at 22–25° 2θ),18 and that prolonging the hydrothermal process resulted in an increase of the crystallinity of both the microporous ZSM-5 and the hierarchical ZSM-5. However, the crystallinity of Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_144 and Hi_Z5_[TPA]_144 exhibited a lower intensity than those of Mic_Z5_[TPA+Na]_144 and Mic_Z5_[TPA]_144, whereas Hi_Z5_[TPA]_72 exhibited a reverse trend. This result indicated that CTAB had another role as an accelerator of the crystallization process on ZSM-5 without Na. Furthermore, the extended hydrothermal process did not influence the regularity of the mesoporous structure. All the hierarchical ZSM-5 zeolites exhibited a partial regularity with a distorted hexagonal structure.35 For Hi_Z5_[TPA], the broad peak at 2θ = 2° was shifted to a higher angle along with the crystallization period, while Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na] had a reverse trend. It indicated the longer crystallization time on Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na] led to the lattice space of d100 of the pores increased, and it was the opposite for Hi_Z5_[TPA].45 To prove the involvement of CTAB molecules in the formation of mesopores, TG-DTA analyses were carried out toward the as-made microporous and hierarchical ZSM-5 (Figure S1 and Table S1). Unlike microporous samples, hierarchical samples gave multiple-steps of wight loss in TG curves and additional DTA signals derived from exothermic reaction at around 215–320 °C. These results implied the involvement of CTAB molecules inside hierarchical ZSM-5 samples.46

Wide and small angle XRD patterns of ZSM-5 with Na (left) and without Na (right).

The hierarchical ZSM-5 synthesized using a surfactant resulted in not only a change in the crystal size but also a change in the crystal morphology, as shown in Figure 2. The crystal size of Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_72 and Hi_Z5_[TPA]_72 was larger than that of the micropore samples, yet the crystal morphology was identical (a coffin-like crystal), with the exception of Mic_Z5_[TPA]_72, which was amorphous. This amorphous nature is reflected in the low crystallinity in Figure 1. Meanwhile, the prolongation of the hydrothermal process resulted in a transformation of the crystal morphology, shifting from an initial coffin-like morphology to a coral-reef-like morphology (Hi_Z5_[TPA]_144) and a flower-like morphology (Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_144). Furthermore, no rough surface was observed in Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_144 and Hi_Z5_[TPA]_144. Therefore, it can be assumed that the microporous and mesoporous structures are well-homogeneous.

Physicochemical Properties

The isotherm of nitrogen adsorption and desorption (Figure 4) exhibited the emergence of a “hysteresis loop” and a “mesoporous step” which is a sharp increase in adsorption volume observed in the P/P0 region ≈ 0.3–0.5 and related to mesopore filling.47,37,48−50 This was observed in the hierarchical ZSM-5 with and without sodium. This indicates the presence of mesopores. This result is supported by Figure 3, which demonstrates the presence of mesopores in the hierarchical samples, thereby confirming their hierarchical structure. However, the mesoporous step became lower when the hydrothermal time was extended, suggesting that the number of mesopores present within the crystal was reduced. Meanwhile, the reference (Mic_Z5_[TPA]_72) exhibited a hysteresis loop at a relative pressure of 0.7–0.9, indicating the formation of intercrystalline mesopores. Subsequently, the hysteresis loop vanished and transformed into a Type I isotherm (the archetypal isotherm for micropores), which is an indication that the crystals underwent fusion.

In contrast, the Smic and Vmic values for Hi_Z5_[TPA] were found to be lower than those for Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]. This indicated that the mesoporous structure was more prevalent in Hi_Z5_[TPA]. Here, the hierarchy factor (HF), which has been recognized as an index for the contribution of micropore volume to the total pore volume and the contribution of mesopore surface area to the total surface area of the sample weight,51 was considered. The HF is calculated by the relative micropore volume (Vmic/Vtot) and the relative mesopore surface area (Smeso/SBET) and is listed in Table 1. Prolonging the crystallization time also increased HF. The hierarchical structure was better than the shorter time due to the higher HF value, which indicates the creation of more mesopores with preserved micropores.52 Those values (0.09 for Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_144 and 0.07 for Hi_Z5_[TPA]_144) demonstrated that both samples did not have the largely mesoporous systems (HF < 0.05)51 as possessed by the samples with a shorter time. In addition, it was found that Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na] had a higher HF than that of Hi_Z5_[TPA]. These phenomena were likely caused by the role of sodium (Na) in the synthesis mixture, in which Na cations were reacting with silicate species and then dissolving it as sodium silicate.53 Moreover, the Na cations were effectively influencing the polymerization/depolymerization reaction of the silicates present during the synthesis.54,55

With regard to the chemical properties (Table 2), it can be observed that all the hierarchical ZSM-5 zeolites exhibited a lower number of strong acid amounts in comparison with the microporous ZSM-5 zeolites. Moreover, the extension of hydrothermal synthesis time enhanced the strong acid amount for the hierarchical ZSM-5 zeolites. Nevertheless, the observed acid amount was not directly correlated with the strength of the acid, as demonstrated by Mic_Z5_[TPA]_72 in Figure S2, in which a lower temperature on the high peak of NH3-TPD profiles and the low and high peaks were not well-separated (Figure S2, Table S2). These phenomena also occurred in the case of Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_72. However, the peaks became well-separated, and the temperature on the high peak increased when the hydrothermal time was prolonged, indicating that the strength of the acid had increased.

The strength of acid sites based on FTIR-pyridine measurement revealed that the hierarchical structure resulted in reducing the amount of Brønsted and Lewis acid sites compared to the microporous structure. The hierarchical ZSM-5 containing Na (Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_72 and Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_144) possessed higher amounts of Brønsted acid sites and lower amounts of Lewis acid sites than the hierarchical ZSM-5 without Na (Hi_Z5_[TPA]_72 and Hi_Z5_[TPA]_144). It indicated that the production of Lewis acid sites is more favorable when the mesoporous structure is more dominant, as shown in Table 1 (Vmeso, Smeso, and HF). On the other hand, the prolonged hydrothermal time on the hierarchical samples led to an increase in the amount of Brønsted and Lewis acid sites and Brønsted/Lewis (B/L) ratio. Whereas the lowest Brønsted acid sites and the highest Lewis acid sites were observed in Mic_Z5_[TPA]_72 due to its crystallinity being very low (Figure 1) and having a mesoporous structure (Figure 4).

The acid sites on the external surface and/or the mesopores were also analyzed with regard to the adsorption of collidine (whose dimensions prevent it from entering the micropore) (Table 2 and Table S3). The interpretation of collidine adsorption spectra was conducted by deconvolution of the spectra, in which the wavenumbers of 1619 and 1633 cm–1 were assigned to collidine adsorbed on silanol and Lewis sites, respectively (Figure S3). The wavenumbers 1638 and 1650 cm–1 were assigned to collidine adsorbed on Brønsted sites.42,56 The hierarchy samples possessed a higher number of Brønsted acid sites, and a higher total composition of Brønsted and Lewis sites were compared to the microporous ZSM-5 (Table S3). This suggests that some Al migrated to the mesopores and/or the external surface following the creation of the hierarchical structure.

Al Distribution in Microporous and Hierarchical ZSM-5

Figure 5 shows 27Al MAS NMR spectra of the microporous and hierarchical ZSM-5 zeolites. Both the microporous ZSM-5 and the hierarchical ZSM-5 exhibited peaks at ca. 55 and 0 ppm, which are associated with tetrahedrally coordinated Al in the framework and octahedrally coordinated Al in the extra-framework, respectively.20,26 Meanwhile, the change in Al distribution after its modification could be seen clearly in Table 3 and Figure S4. Mic_Z5_[TPA+Na] had a higher T56/T54 ratio than that of Mic_Z5_[TPA]. We have reported that the portion of Al located on the channels (56 ppm) was higher than Al located on the channel intersections (54 ppm). On the other hand, the hierarchical ZSM-5 exhibited a reversed trend, whereby the samples with Na had a lower T56/T54 ratio than those without Na. The observed differences in trend indicated the occurrence of an Al migration process on hierarchical ZSM-5. Furthermore, the lengthened crystallization time resulted in a reduction in the T56/T54 ratio, while the microporous samples demonstrated the opposite trend, indicating that the crystallization period influenced the rate of Al migration.

The aforementioned trends were also demonstrated by CI values in Figure 6, in which Mic_Z5_[TPA+Na] exhibited a higher CI value than Mic_Z5_[TPA], whereas hierarchical ZSM-5 demonstrated the opposite. It implied the hierarchical ZSM-5 with Na had a higher amount of Al on the channel intersection than that without Na. Furthermore, it was seen that extending the hydrothermal time resulted in the increase of CI value for the microporous ZSM-5. Whereas the hierarchical ZSM-5 experienced the reduction of CI value. This phenomenon can be attributed to the increasing T56/T54 ratio (for the micropore samples) and its reduction (for the hierarchical samples) when prolonging the hydrothermal time, as shown in Table 3. A higher value of the constraint index (CI) on the hierarchical ZSM-5 and also a higher deviation of the CI value between the hierarchical ZSM-5 with Na and without Na compared to the microporous ZSM-5 zeolites were most likely due to the micropore volume belonging to the hierarchical ZSM-5 being too low (besides being influenced by the T56/T54 ratio). This resulted in a bulky molecule (3-methylpentane) that was more difficult to enter the pore, and/or only a few of that compound could be converted.

The observed phenomena occurring on the hierarchical ZSM-5 zeolites are most likely that there was a competition between tetrapropylammonium cations (TPA+) and cetyltrimethylammonium cations (CTA+) in attracting aluminosilicate species and/or subunits (e.g., five- or six-membered rings) for the creation of the microporous and mesoporous structures, respectively (Figure 7). The process resulted in some aluminate species or subunits having aluminum tetrahedra migrating from the micropore (which they should be in the micropore) to the mesopore. On the other hand, the higher migration on the hierarchical ZSM-5 zeolites with Na compared to those without Na indicated that Na plays a role not only in the polymerization reaction of silicate but also in the rate of substitution of aluminum to silicon in the subunits. The assumption of its Al migration was also supported by the DTA result (Figure S1), which demonstrated that within the temperature range for TPAOH decomposition on the hierarchical samples, the peaks at around 360 and 430 °C experienced an increase and a reduction, respectively, when compared to the microporous samples. The higher temperature corresponded to the strong connection between the TPA+ cations and Al tetrahedrons in the framework (compensation of TPA+), and the lower temperature corresponded to the weak connection between the TPA+ cations and OH– anions (ion-paired TPA+).57

Table 4 demonstrates that there was no change in the Al content (single Al and Al pairs) trend. The Al pairs distributed in the straight channel exhibited an increase in the composition, whereas those distributed in the sinusoidal channel exhibited a decline in comparison to the original ZSM-5 structure. Catalytic Reaction

Based on the n-hexane cracking reaction shown in Figures 8 and S6, the conversion percentage of n-hexane was higher on the micropore ZSM-5 zeolites than on the hierarchical ones. The micropore ZSM-5 without Na showed higher selectivity on the ethene and BTX (except Mic_Z5_[TPA]_72) than that with Na, but lower selectivity on the propene and butenes, in which those were consistent as previously reported by our group.20 On the other hand, the hierarchical sample showed the opposite trend, in which Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na]_144 produced higher selectivity on ethene and BTX than Hi_Z5_[TPA]_144 (Figure S6). Whereas a trend anomaly occurred over the samples with the hydrothermal time of 72 h, probably due to its period being insufficient to produce a well-hierarchical structure as shown by Figure 2 (surface morphology of the crystal) and Figure S2. Ethene was probably formed from the cracking of propene, butenes, and aromatics since either bimolecular or monomolecular mechanism, ethene forms through energetically unfavorable primary carbenium ions.58−60 Aromatics were formed by the oligomerization and hydrogen-transfer reactions of propene and butenes,61 and such consecutive reactions that involve bulky molecules would prefer to occur at acid sites located in a wider space, i.e., the channel intersection.40 While, Mic_Z5_[TPA]_72 exhibited lower BTX selectivity than Mic_Z5_[TPA+Na]_72 as it did not have a good crystallinity (Figure 1) and the presence of a greater proportion of mesopores relative to micropores (Figure 4 and Table 1). The low BTX selectivity also exhibited by the hierarchical ZSM-5 zeolites was attributed to the presence of a mesopore structure. This selectivity was found to be lower than that observed in the microporous ZSM-5 zeolites. On the other hand, the sample having the mesopore structure produced propene and butenes predominantly compared to the micropore only, as shown by all the hierarchical ZSM-5 zeolites and Mic_Z5_[TPA]_72. It implied the oligomerization of olefins, which were products of the cracking process either via thermal, bimolecular, or monomolecular mechanisms, was hindered by the introduction of mesopore. Furthermore, it also indicated that the introduction of mesopore was most likely to increase the bimolecular mechanism since the propene was one of the characteristic products of the classical β-scission route.59

Product selectivity and conversion of n-hexane cracking with time on stream (TOS) over ZSM-5 with Na and ZSM-5 without Na.

For methanol conversion, the hierarchical structure, either with Na or without Na, possessed a superior performance (in total around 70–80%) in converting methanol to olefins compared to the microporous structure (in total around 45–60%), except Mic_Z5_[TPA]_72, with the highest selectivity on propene (reaching around 35–45%) (Figure 9). This phenomenon can be attributed to the mesoporosity of the zeolite, which facilitates the removal of olefins from the micropores before their cyclization, resulting in the formation of bulky molecules as aromatics.62 Moreover, this could also be due to the hierarchical ZSM-5 having weaker acidity than the microporous ZSM-5, which suppressed the aromatization of olefins.63 Furthermore, the Hi_Z5_[TPA] demonstrated a greater performance than Hi_Z5_[TPA+Na], indicating that a lower acid strength and a higher mesoporosity were better for selective production of lower olefins due to the low acid strength could suppress the hydrogen transfer reaction and aromatic cycle.64 On the other hand, the microporous ZSM-5 was the most dominant in producing the bulky molecules (≥C 5s) with a total of around 25–35%. The highest selectivity of the ≥ C5s was derived from Mic_Z5_[TPA]_144. It was because the formation of the bulky molecules would be enhanced by the acid sites located in a larger space (the intersection channels), being accordance with the reports from Liang21 et al. and Sungtak40 et al.

Conclusions

An adjustment of the aluminum (Al) distribution was conducted on the hierarchical ZSM-5 intracrystalline. It was observed that aluminum was more located in the channel intersections than in the channels of the hierarchical ZSM-5 synthesized with the presence of sodium (Na), which is opposite the microporous ZSM-5 trend. The hierarchical ZSM-5 synthesized using a surfactant as a mesoporogen resulted in the occurrence of an Al migration process from the micropores to the mesopores and/or the external surface. Meanwhile, prolonging the hydrothermal time until 144 h resulted in the formation of a more well-structured hierarchical structure, accompanied by enhanced chemical properties, which elevated the hierarchy factor and the strength of acid sites in the zeolite.

Furthermore, the rate of Al migration was also influenced by the aforementioned factors. The impact of the framework structure and the controlled aluminum was also observed in the MTO and n-hexane cracking reactions. The hierarchical structure was found to be a superior catalyst for the production of lower olefins, particularly propene, in comparison to the micropore structure alone. This was observed to be particularly the case with the hierarchical ZSM-5, which exhibited enhanced catalytic performance in the absence of Na. This work provides valuable insights into the development of various hierarchical zeolite catalysts using a surfactant as mesoporogen wherein the location of aluminum is controlled in the pores with the aim of enhancing the catalytic performance.

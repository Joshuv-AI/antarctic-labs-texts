Application of AI and deep learning technology for IPE education under dual track cultivation model
Authors: Xiaoqing He, Wenyi Xu, Xinwen Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work intends to explore the effectiveness of a dual-track cultivation model for ideological and political literacy in vocational colleges driven by artificial intelligence deep learning models. This work compares the performance of different models on several key indicators of ideological and political education. Through analysis of experimental results across varying data volumes, the optimized model demonstrates significant advantages in four areas: mastery of ideological and political knowledge, ideological and political consciousness, ideological and political practical ability, and student satisfaction. The highest score for political belief reaches 4.8, while the scores for theoretical knowledge mastery, social practice participation, and activity satisfaction all reach 4.7, far surpassing traditional models. This indicates that the optimized model can more effectively help students understand and retain course content. Additionally, the optimized model significantly enhances students’ recognition and trust in the national political system and core values. It also improves students’ ability to apply ideological and political knowledge to real-world problems. Lastly, in terms of student satisfaction, the optimized model performs exceptionally well in both course and activity satisfaction. Therefore, this work contributes to the field of ideological and political education in vocational colleges.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Deep learning, Vocational colleges, Ideological and political literacy, Dual-track cultivationSubject terms: Education; Information systems and information technology; Mathematics and computing; Science, technology and society

Introduction

Research background and motivations

In the context of the rapid development of information technology, the application of artificial intelligence (AI) technology is becoming increasingly widespread across various fields, especially in education. The introduction of AI technology has brought new transformations to educational models1. Vocational colleges, as a crucial part of vocational education, play an essential role in shaping students’ correct worldviews, life perspectives, and values through ideological and political education (IPE). However, traditional models of IPE are often too singular and theoretical, making it challenging to meet contemporary students’ needs and stimulate their interest and active participation2. Beyond the field of education, AI also demonstrates great application potential in other domains. For example, deep learning and point cloud technology can improve the effect of intelligent human-computer interaction, which reflects AI technology’s wide applicability in understanding complex behaviors and supporting personalized interventions3,4. This indicates that AI technology can optimize traditional teaching models and help build a more intelligent and adaptive cultivation system. Thus, it provides a broader technical background and cross-boundary integration ideas for the innovation of ideological and political education in vocational colleges.

The traditional approach to IPE lacks interactivity and personalization, making it difficult to engage students and enhance their participation. By incorporating AI deep learning models, personalized educational content and guidance can be provided based on individual students’ characteristics and learning needs, thereby improving the effectiveness of IPE5. Furthermore, with the diversification of society, students’ thoughts and values have become more varied. Traditional, singular educational models struggle to comprehensively address and meet these diverse needs. The dual-track cultivation model, which combines theoretical teaching with practical activities, can better accommodate the diverse needs of students and promote their holistic development. The application of AI technology offers new possibilities for innovation in educational models. Utilizing deep learning models can intelligently analyze and process educational data to optimize teaching strategies and methods, thereby promoting continuous innovation and development in educational models. Deep learning models can accurately identify students’ knowledge gaps and weak points in their understanding by analyzing their learning behaviors and performance. This enables targeted education and guidance, improving the precision and effectiveness of ideological and political education. This work aims to construct and validate a dual-track cultivation model for ideological and political literacy in vocational colleges, driven by AI deep learning models. The work explores the feasibility and effectiveness of this model in enhancing students’ ideological and political literacy, providing theoretical and practical references for the innovation and development of IPE in vocational colleges.

Literature review

In previous studies, Nichols and Dixon-Román6 explored the application of AI technology in higher education. They discovered that intelligent teaching platforms could significantly enhance students’ learning outcomes and teaching quality. Specifically, the findings showed that intelligent teaching platforms could dynamically adjust the teaching content to better meet the students’ needs, while also providing personalized teaching suggestions to teachers, thus improving the overall educational experience. Their research provides foundational support for this work’s exploration of intelligent tools in IPE. Davis7 analyzed the application of deep learning algorithms in personalized learning systems and pointed out that deep learning could generate precise personalized recommendations by analyzing students’ learning behavior data, thereby improving students’ learning efficiency. They also emphasized that the powerful feature extraction capabilities of deep learning could identify students’ learning patterns and weaknesses, laying the technological foundation for personalized ideological and political education. This is particularly important for constructing a more flexible dual-track training model. Schleiss et al.8 explored the potential applications of AI in vocational education. They emphasized that intelligent tools could enhance vocational skills training and assessment. The findings suggested that AI not only optimized the learning path for vocational skills but also provided feedback and improvement suggestions based on students’ actual performance. This insight encourages the use of AI technology to improve the effectiveness of the practical track in the dual-track training model. Qi et al.9 focused on the application of AI-driven educational systems in IPE. They found that intelligent systems could significantly enhance the depth and penetration of IPE content. They pointed out that AI could use natural language processing and sentiment analysis to accurately capture students’ ideological dynamics, thereby optimizing teaching strategies. This research provides technical support for enhancing the effectiveness of IPE in an intelligent environment. Xu and Chen10 suggested that combining deep learning and big data technology could build a more intelligent educational assessment system, significantly improving the accuracy and scientific nature of educational quality evaluation. Their research further indicated that intelligent assessment systems could monitor teaching effectiveness in real-time and provide data-driven guidance for teaching improvements. This provides important references for exploring the intelligent assessment of educational quality in the dual-track training model proposed. Chen11 explored the application of the dual-track training model in higher vocational education. He proposed that this model could effectively enhance students’ overall competence by combining theoretical teaching with practical training. Specifically, they found that the theoretical track focused on the systematic delivery of knowledge, while the practical track strengthened vocational skills training. The synergy of the two tracks helps comprehensively improve students’ professional competence. This directly supports the design of the dual-track training model proposed here. Subkhan12 proposed a deep learning-based student behavior analysis model, which analyzed students’ daily learning behavior data to enable precise teaching interventions. The results showed that the model could identify students’ learning behavior patterns and provide early warnings for potential issues, thus improving the timeliness and targeting of teaching interventions. This achievement provides insights for using intelligent technology to implement precise teaching interventions in the dual-track training model designed here.

Although some studies have examined the application of deep learning algorithms in personalized learning systems, there is limited research on effectively applying these technologies to IPE in vocational colleges. Existing research primarily focuses on knowledge transmission and skills training, lacking emphasis on the personalized cultivation of students’ ideological and political literacy. This work applies deep learning algorithms to IPE in vocational colleges, aiming to enhance students’ ideological and political literacy through personalized recommendations and interventions, thereby achieving tailored education.

Dual-track cultivation model in IPE in vocational colleges

Vocational colleges, as an essential part of vocational education, aim not only to impart professional skills to students but also to cultivate their comprehensive qualities and ideological and political literacy26. The dual-track cultivation model in IPE emphasizes both the systematic and in-depth nature of theoretical teaching and the authenticity and interactivity of practical teaching. By integrating theoretical learning with practical activities, students can deeply understand ideological and political theories and apply them to solve real-world problems in practice27–30.

Different types of practical activities differ in the efficiency of value transmission and goal orientation. To avoid homogenization in practice design and enhance the pertinence of value guidance, this work distinguishes the contribution weights of different practical activities in accordance with the classification theory of ideological and political practices. For instance, based on immersion theory and situational cognition theory, the weight of “VR historical event simulation, which focuses more on cultivating political identity and historical perspective,” is 30%. According to the service-learning theory and moral practice concept, the weight of “community volunteer services is set to 35%, which directly promotes social responsibility and public spirit”. Using critical thinking theory and practical epistemology, the weight of “social research and investigation is 20%, which cultivates practical cognition and problem-analysis abilities”. Based on communication effect theory and community concept, the weight of “public welfare promotion activities, which mainly strengthen value dissemination and collective awareness,” is set to 15%. Through weight distinction, the proportion of various practices can be dynamically adjusted according to the core literacy goals in teaching organization, thereby achieving more accurate and multi-level ideological and value education effects.

Optimization of deep learning models and design of the dual-track cultivation model

As a crucial component of AI, deep learning models possess powerful data processing and analysis capabilities, enabling personalized teaching and intelligent assessment services in education40. However, to better meet the needs of educational scenarios, these models need to be optimized. Figure 2 illustrates the architecture of the optimized model. The reason for choosing Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) as the basic models is mainly due to the fact that ideological and political education data in vocational colleges are still mainly structured text and sequential behavior at present, and multimodal information such as images or videos is relatively limited. CNN can effectively extract local semantic features, while LSTM is better at long-term dependency modeling of student learning behavior sequences. The combination of the two is also more advantageous in terms of computational efficiency and interpretability, and conforms to the real-time and resource constrained needs of actual teaching environments.

Optimized deep learning model architecture.

The optimized deep learning model architecture leverages multi-level feature extraction and processing, advanced deep learning techniques, and regularization methods to efficiently handle multimodal data in vocational colleges’ IPE. The model uses a three-layer CNN to extract local semantic features from text and behavior sequences, and introduces a bidirectional LSTM to capture long-term contextual dependencies. In terms of regularization, in addition to using Dropout (with a ratio of 0.5) to suppress overfitting, L2 regularization is also applied in the fully connected layer to further improve the generalization ability. The above regularization method, combining Dropout and L2 is selected based on comprehensive considerations. The ideological and political data of vocational colleges are characterized by a limited sample size and high feature dimensions. After comparing multiple regularization strategies suitable for small-sample scenarios (Dropout, L2, and L1 regularization), it is found that on the current IPE behavior and text data, the combination of Dropout and L2 can suppress overfitting. Meanwhile, it can well retain semantic and behavioral features with strong discriminability, achieving more stable generalization performance on the validation set. Therefore, this work finally adopts these two classic regularization methods to improve the accuracy of predicting students’ ideological and political literacy while ensuring the model’s simplicity and interpretability.

Table 2 shows the key parameter configuration of the model. These parameters are designed based on the characteristics of common student behavior sequences and text data in educational scenarios. The CNN part uses 3 × 3 convolution kernels and 2 × 2 max-pooling to effectively extract local semantic features; the LSTM hidden layer dimension is set to 128, which is suitable for modeling medium-length learning behavior sequences. The cross-entropy loss is used as the loss function, which is applicable to multi-classification tasks. The parameter selection takes both computational efficiency and model expression ability into account, and can well capture the temporal dependencies and cognitive changes shown by students in the long-term learning process.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection, experimental environment and parameters setting

The dataset selected for the experiment is an online education dataset, a widely used public dataset in educational research. It contains rich data on learning behaviors and educational activities, making it highly suitable for analyzing educational effectiveness and learning patterns. The dataset includes platform course data, learning records, class data, class member information, learning logs and browsing history, totaling approximately 12 million data entries. The selected dataset mainly includes course learning records, user behavior logs, and interaction data, which can effectively reflect students’ level of engagement, cognitive habits, and emotional attitudes in theoretical learning. These behavioral characteristics are closely related to the learning enthusiasm, cognitive internalization, and value recognition that are concerned in ideological and political education. In addition, the multidimensional behavioral indicators in the dataset (completion rate, interaction frequency, and task response time) provide a reliable data foundation for the theoretical evaluation process in the “theory practice” dual-track model proposed here. This can support quantitative analysis of vocational college students’ ideological and political awareness and behavioral performance.

By using the above configuration, an efficient experimental environment can be established, ensuring optimal performance and results during the training and evaluation of deep learning models. The experimental parameters for the model are uniformly set as follows: the number of hidden units in each layer is 128, with a total of 3 layers. The learning rate is set to 0.01, the batch size to 64, and the regularization parameter to 0.001. The model includes 3 convolutional layers, each with 32 filters of size 3 × 3, and pooling layers with a window size of 2 × 2. The 128 hidden units are capable of capturing rich features without significantly increasing computational complexity. A lower number of hidden units is suitable for small-scale educational data, while a higher number can better handle more complex educational contexts, ensuring effective modeling of diverse learning behaviors. A depth of three layers is sufficient to capture the hierarchical features of the data while avoiding overfitting issues associated with deeper models. For educational data, this moderate number of layers can adapt to varying data sizes and complexities, handling both single-dimensional data (like student grades) and multi-dimensional data (like course interactions and behaviors). A learning rate of 0.01 is commonly used as the initial value, striking a balance between fast model convergence and avoiding local minima due to overly rapid convergence. Larger learning rates are suitable for initial parameter adjustments, while a learning rate decay strategy can be implemented for optimization in different educational scenarios. A batch size of 64 is a typical medium value, balancing training speed and model stability. For smaller datasets, a smaller batch size (like 32) ensures more stable parameter updates, while for large datasets, the batch size can be increased based on available memory resources. The regularization parameter controls model complexity and prevents overfitting. In educational data, where data volume and quality vary across different educational backgrounds, a moderate regularization parameter can effectively improve the model’s generalization ability across diverse data types. Three convolutional layers can extract low-level features and combine multi-layer feature representations to learn behavior patterns. In the educational context, different types of data (such as behaviors and content) require specific feature extraction strategies. Three convolutional layers are adequate for modeling multi-dimensional data features. The use of 32 filters can extract key features without significantly increasing computational costs. The number of filters can be dynamically adjusted based on data complexity, with additional filters introduced for more complex interaction data to improve feature extraction. A filter size of 3 × 3 is commonly used in convolutional networks and is effective in extracting local features. For educational data, such as learning behavior sequences, a 3 × 3 filter is sufficient to capture local patterns, making it suitable for various educational scenarios. The 2 × 2 pooling window effectively reduces the feature map size and helps prevent overfitting. For large datasets, this reduces computational complexity, while for smaller datasets, it preserves sufficient feature information, adapting to multiple educational data contexts.

The comparative models used in the experiment are Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) and A Robustly Optimized BERT Pretraining Approach (RoBERTa). BERT and RoBERTa are selected as baseline models mainly due to their broad representativeness and powerful performance in educational text analysis and understanding tasks. These two models belong to pure text models, while the proposed model belongs to multimodal models. This comparison aims to verify whether the optimized model has basic advantages and generalization ability on text-based educational data. BERT utilizes a bidirectional Transformer model that considers both left and right contextual information. It is pre-trained using the Masked Language Model and Next Sentence Prediction tasks. BERT has demonstrated excellent performance across various Natural Language Processing tasks, including question-answering systems, sentiment analysis, and named entity recognition. RoBERTa, an improved version of BERT proposed by Facebook, optimizes the pre-training process and increases training data to further enhance model performance. RoBERTa removes the Next Sentence Prediction task, uses more data, and trains for a longer period. Additionally, it dynamically generates masks in each epoch to enhance model robustness.

Performance evaluation

Performance evaluation of optimized model

In the performance evaluation experiment, this work first compares the Area Under the Curve - Receiver Operating Characteristic (AUC-ROC) and logarithmic loss (Log Loss). Figure 3 displays the experimental results.

Performance evaluation (a) AUC-ROC (b) Log Loss.

Figure 3 suggests that under the AUC-ROC metric, the optimized model outperforms both BERT and RoBERTa across all data types. For instance, in learning behavior data, the AUC-ROC of the optimized model is 0.937, compared to 0.891 for RoBERTa. This indicates that the optimized model has a significant advantage in distinguishing between positive and negative samples, particularly in student interaction behavior data, where it performs exceptionally well with an AUC-ROC of 0.942. The optimized model also demonstrates a lower Log Loss value. For example, in learning behavior data, the Log Loss is 0.291, compared to 0.386 for RoBERTa. This suggests that the optimized model provides more accurate probabilistic predictions, with higher confidence levels in predictions related to course content and interaction behavior data. Figure 4 presents a comparison of Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR).

Performance comparison results (a) RMSE (b) MRR.

Figure 4 shows that, under the RMSE metric, the optimized model significantly outperforms BERT and RoBERTa, with a notably lower error. For example, in student interaction behavior data, the RMSE of the optimized model is 0.509. This indicates that the optimized model has higher accuracy in regression tasks, particularly demonstrating lower prediction errors in learning behavior data and course content data. In terms of the MRR metric, the optimized model performs the best across all data types. For instance, in course content data, the MRR reaches 0.854. This suggests that the optimized model exhibits stronger capabilities in relevance ranking tasks, especially in learning behavior data, where it can better prioritize learning resources.

These models all have the ability of image-text joint understanding, which is more comparable to the proposed model in terms of input form and target task, and can further objectively reflect the performance of the proposed model in real multimodal education scenarios. Evaluation of the effectiveness of the dual-track cultivation model

For evaluating the effectiveness of the dual-track cultivation model, a scoring system (1–5 points) is used, where higher scores represent better performance and evaluation of the indicator. This scoring system is designed based on the course objectives and learning behavior data analyzed by AI models, including multiple dimensions such as knowledge mastery, belief identification, and practical participation. The sample of participants in the evaluation includes a total of 320 students from two grades, as well as 8 ideological and political teachers. All evaluators have received consistency training on the meaning of indicators and evaluation criteria before evaluation to ensure the effectiveness and comparability of the evaluation. Moreover, the scoring results are cross-validated with the learning behavior analysis data output by the AI model (such as knowledge weakness identification, and interactive participation). This thereby integrates objective data support into subjective evaluation, enhancing the interpretability of the results and AI relevance. Discussion

The experimental results demonstrate that the optimized model proposed shows significant advantages across all four metrics: AUC-ROC, Log Loss, RMSE, and MRR. Specifically, in classification tasks, the optimized model effectively distinguishes between positive and negative samples and exhibits higher precision in probability prediction. In regression tasks, the optimized model provides lower prediction errors, demonstrating stronger generalization ability. Moreover, in ranking tasks, the optimized model accurately ranks relevance, showcasing superior learning resource recommendation capabilities. In contrast, although BERT and RoBERTa show relatively stable performance across the metrics, neither of them reaches the level achieved by the optimized model. This indicates that the proposed optimized model offers greater applicability and reliability across multidimensional tasks. By combining deep learning techniques with the characteristics of educational data, the optimized model has demonstrated substantial potential in practical educational scenarios. Future work could focus on expanding the model’s scope and enhancing its performance across diverse educational contexts.

In evaluating the dual-track cultivation model, the optimized model demonstrates significant advantages in theoretical knowledge acquisition. This result suggests that the optimized model is more efficient in course design and knowledge transfer, enabling students to grasp ideological and political theory more comprehensively. The optimized model also excels in enhancing students’ ideological and political awareness. Through optimized educational methods and personalized guidance, students’ recognition of national political systems and core socialist values significantly improves. Additionally, the scores for student participation in social practice and problem-solving abilities are significantly higher with the optimized model. This indicates that the optimized model not only enhances theoretical knowledge but also effectively applies this knowledge to practice, developing students’ problem-solving skills. In terms of student satisfaction, the optimized model also shows significant advantages. Both course satisfaction and activity satisfaction scores are higher with the optimized model compared to BERT and RoBERTa.

Conclusion

Research contribution

The research contributions of this work are mainly reflected in several key areas. First, this work constructs and validates a dual-track cultivation model for ideological and political literacy in vocational colleges, driven by an AI deep learning model. It explores the feasibility and effectiveness of this approach in enhancing students’ ideological and political literacy. The research results show that the optimized model exhibits significant advantages across four key indicators: mastery of ideological and political knowledge, political consciousness, ideological and political practical ability, and student satisfaction. Specifically, the optimized model more effectively helps students understand and retain the content of ideological and political courses. It strengthens their recognition and trust in the national political system and socialist core values, and improves their ability to apply ideological and political knowledge to solve real-world social issues. Furthermore, the optimized model outperforms other models in terms of course and activity satisfaction, indicating its ability to enhance the overall learning experience and satisfaction of students. By comparing with the BERT and RoBERTa models, this work verifies the superiority of the optimized model in handling sentiment analysis tasks. This further demonstrates its practical value and potential for promotion in the IPE of vocational colleges. This work provides a theoretical foundation and practical guidance for innovation in IPE in vocational colleges and offers important references and insights for the improvement of future educational methods.

To further achieve personalized education, the integration of reinforcement learning (RL) and adaptive systems can provide more precise support. RL can be used to dynamically optimize teaching strategies. For example, Q-learning algorithms can adjust the teaching path in real time based on student learning behaviors and performance feedback (such as correct answer rates and completion time), offering a more efficient learning experience. Additionally, RL can be applied to design personalized task allocation systems and predict the adaptability of tasks based on a student’s ability level and interests, thus tailoring learning objectives for individual students. RL can also predict a student’s learning progress, identify potential learning bottlenecks, and automatically recommend remedial actions (such as additional resources or tutoring sessions) to improve learning outcomes. Adaptive systems can provide personalized content recommendations by analyzing learning history, interests, and knowledge levels to dynamically generate learning paths that meet the student’s needs. These systems can also monitor learning behaviors in real time and offer targeted feedback. For example, when a student struggles with a particular concept, the system can automatically suggest relevant learning resources or tips. Furthermore, adaptive systems play a key role in testing and assessment by dynamically adjusting the difficulty of test content, ensuring that evaluation results are more accurate and fair.

By integrating RL and adaptive systems, multi-modal learning scenarios can be designed where learning resources are dynamically matched to student needs. RL focuses on optimizing long-term learning paths, while the adaptive system provides real-time support and resource recommendations. The synergy of these two technologies will continuously enhance teaching effectiveness. Moreover, this integration can help build a long-term learning planning system, where RL predicts long-term learning goals, and the adaptive system offers immediate adjustments and optimization based on periodic performance. This combination not only significantly improves the student learning experience but also provides an innovative practical direction for the intelligent development of education.

To optimize IPE in vocational colleges, several key aspects should be addressed. First, the practicality and relevance of the course content should be strengthened by designing courses that are closely aligned with students’ real-life experiences. This involves integrating ideological and political theory with real-world issues, such as incorporating current events and social practice cases. In this way, students can recognize the practical significance and application value of the IPE courses. Additionally, it is important to align IPE with vocational ethics and the cultivation of social responsibility, in line with the career planning of vocational students, thus enhancing the course’s practical application. Besides, it is essential to introduce diverse teaching methods, such as flipped classrooms, case-based teaching, and situational simulations. Leveraging digital tools to offer a more interactive and personalized learning experience is also key. Moreover, deepening the dual-track training model that combines theory with practice is crucial. Practical components, such as social service, public welfare activities, and company internships, should be integrated into IPE courses, allowing students to apply the learned theories in real-world scenarios. Establishing on-campus and off-campus practice bases, in collaboration with social organizations, for research and volunteer service, provides a solid avenue for practical engagement. It is also vital to enhance the professional quality and guidance capabilities of teachers. Schools should regularly organize professional development training to improve teachers’ understanding of ideological theory and teaching methodologies. Furthermore, encouraging teachers to engage in educational research and innovate teaching methods will significantly improve teaching effectiveness. To better meet students’ needs, an interactive mechanism centered around students should be developed. This could include feedback sessions during classes to gather students’ opinions on course content and teaching methods. Additionally, establishing a platform for teacher-student interaction will provide students with an easy way to communicate their learning challenges and receive support. Meanwhile, it is necessary to improve the education evaluation and feedback mechanism. When assessing students’ ideological and political literacy, a multidimensional evaluation system should be adopted, which not only focuses on knowledge acquisition but also evaluates students’ ideological awareness, sense of social responsibility, and practical abilities. Furthermore, timely feedback should be provided to adjust teaching strategies, ensuring the continuous optimization of IPE.

AI and big data technology offer significant potential in the future of IPE. For example, AI-based learning platforms can be developed to provide personalized course recommendations and learning guidance based on student learning behaviors and performance. Additionally, big data analysis can help identify weak areas in student learning and formulate targeted teaching improvement plans. Finally, promoting school-enterprise cooperation is crucial. By offering specialized courses or lectures, IPE can be integrated into vocational scenarios, such as professional ethics, teamwork, and social responsibility. Internship opportunities and social research can further help students apply their knowledge in real-world professional contexts. These optimization measures will effectively enhance the quality of IPE in vocational colleges, providing a solid foundation for students’ comprehensive development and the cultivation of social responsibility.

Future works and research limitations

Despite the achievements of this work in optimizing IPE in vocational colleges, there are several limitations. First, the source and scale of experimental data are relatively limited. Although publicly available datasets are used, the feedback and performance of students in real-world applications may be influenced by more complex factors that are not fully covered here. Next, the work primarily employs quantitative analysis methods to evaluate the model’s performance, lacking in-depth qualitative analysis such as students’ subjective experiences and feedback, and teachers’ teaching practices. These qualitative data are equally important for a comprehensive understanding and improvement of the educational model. This limitation somewhat restricts the depth of the evaluation, making the research results unable to fully reflect the actual effectiveness of the dual-track training model.

Future research directions can be expanded in the following ways. First, the scale and diversity of experimental data can be increased to include more data from actual teaching scenarios, enhancing the generalizability and reliability of the research findings. Second, a combination of quantitative and qualitative research methods can be employed. For instance, student interviews and teacher feedback can provide deeper insights into the specific performance and potential improvements of the optimized model in practical applications. Third, the adaptability and effectiveness of the optimized model can be explored in different cultural contexts and educational systems, investigating its prospects in international education. Additionally, with the continuous advancement of AI technology, incorporating the latest AI techniques such as RL and adaptive learning systems can further enhance the intelligence and personalization of IPE. Meanwhile, more comprehensive behavioral data can be obtained by observing classroom interactions and recording students’ participation in activities, further revealing the effectiveness of the dual-track model. Finally, integrating interdisciplinary perspectives, such as incorporating methods from pedagogy and psychology can provide a more holistic assessment of the model’s applicability and long-term impact.

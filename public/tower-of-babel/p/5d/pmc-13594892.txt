Outcomes of nucleos(t)ide analogue discontinuation following finite xalnesiran-based therapies in chronic hepatitis B

ABSTRACT
Background & AimsNovel regimens aimed at achieving functional cure of chronic hepatitis B (CHB) in virologically suppressed participants require the withdrawal of nucleos(t)ide analogue (NA). However, data from clinical trials are limited.MethodsPiranga is a phase II platform trial that evaluated four xalnesiran regimens with or without an immunomodulator in virologically suppressed participants with CHB. All participants continued the established NA therapy until NA stopping criteria were met at the end of treatment or during follow-up. Participants who stopped NA were monitored and restarted NA if restarting criteria were met. This post hoc evaluation reports the NA discontinuation events and outcomes.ResultsAmong 121 participants, 58 (48%) met NA stopping criteria and 40 (33%) discontinued NA. Of these 40, 17 (43%) met NA restarting criteria, 16 (40%) restarted NA, and 24 (60%) remained off NA by the end of the study. Among participants who remained off NA, five achieved and maintained HBsAg loss (HBsAg <0.05 IU/ml) and HBV DNA undetectable (HBV DNA <10 IU/ml); all were in treatment arms with an immunomodulator. Of the 40 participants who discontinued NA, 19 (48%) experienced virological relapse (VR), three (8%) of whom further developed clinical relapse. The majority of participants restarted NA following VR. VR and clinical relapse events were associated with preserved liver function and resolved without sequelae.ConclusionsNA discontinuation after xalnesiran-based therapy was safely managed with the application of prespecified NA stopping and restarting criteria and close monitoring. Five participants achieved and maintained HBsAg loss and undetectable HBV DNA while off NA until the end of the study.Impact and implicationsNovel regimens aiming to achieve functional cure of CHB in virologically suppressed participants require the withdrawal of NA, yet data from clinical trials are limited. This study addresses a critical question in the treatment of CHB – the optimization of NA discontinuation strategies for novel combination regimens in development aiming to achieve functional cure in participants with chronic hepatitis B. These findings help advance the framework for clinicians and researchers to safely and efficiently manage NA discontinuation, which is central to achieving a functional cure.Clinical Trials RegistrationThe study is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04225715).

Introduction

Functional cure of chronic hepatitis B (CHB) infection is defined as HBsAg loss and HBV DNA less than the lower limit of quantitation (LLOQ) for at least 24 weeks after a finite duration therapy.1 Functional cure is rarely achieved with current standard of care (SOC) therapy, including nucleos(t)ide analogue (NA) or pegylated interferon alpha (peg-IFN-alpha).[2], [3], [4], [5] Attaining functional cure necessitates a finite treatment regimen, which involves the discontinuation of NA. However, NA discontinuation increases the risk of viral relapse and is associated with safety risks, such as hepatic decompensation, liver failure, and death.[6], [7], [8]

Novel treatment regimens intending to achieve functional cure in virologically suppressed patients need to include a strategy to withdraw NA therapy. In clinical trials evaluating these regimens, HBsAg response after NA discontinuation is often unknown.[9], [10], [11], [12] Furthermore, a case of liver failure following NA discontinuation was reported, highlighting the need to ensure the safety of participants.10 The optimization of NA discontinuation strategies is ongoing.[13], [14], [15], [16]

Xalnesiran is an investigational N-acetyl-D-galactosamine conjugated synthetic double-stranded small interfering RNA that targets the S conserved region of the HBV genome. The efficacy and safety of xalnesiran with and without an immunomodulator, either ruzotolimod or peg-IFN-alpha, have been evaluated in the phase II Piranga platform study (NCT04225715).9 To explore the feasibility of achieving functional cure after finite treatment, prespecified NA stopping and restarting criteria and close monitoring were implemented in the follow-up period of Piranga. Here, we report the NA discontinuation events, efficacy, and safety outcomes, and identify potential factors associated with these outcomes.

Patients and methods

Participants

The Piranga platform, a phase II, randomised, controlled, adaptive, open-label, multicentre trial has previously been described.9 Briefly, the study enrolled participants with CHB on NA monotherapy for ≥12 months (entecavir, tenofovir alafenamide, or tenofovir disoproxil fumarate). Participants had HBV DNA <20 IU/ml, alanine aminotransferase (ALT) ≤1.5× the upper limit of the normal (ULN; i.e. 41 and 33 U/L in male and female participants, respectively) for >6 months before screening, and had no significant liver fibrosis or cirrhosis (fibrosis stage ≥F3 on liver biopsy or liver stiffness of ≥7.4 kPa on transient elastography).

This study included nine arms, five of which were completed and are included in this report; four arms were discontinued.9 Participants received xalnesiran 100 mg, xalnesiran 200 mg, xalnesiran 200 mg plus ruzotolimod 150 mg, xalnesiran 200 mg plus peg-IFN-alpha 180 μg, or NA. Participants were in the study for 96 weeks, comprising a 48-week treatment period and a 48-week follow-up period. Xalnesiran was administered subcutaneously every 4 weeks for 48 weeks, ruzotolimod orally every other day from weeks 13 to 24 and from weeks 37 to 48, and peg-IFN-alpha-2a subcutaneously weekly for 48 weeks (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Study design with stopping and restarting criteria and monitoring rules.EOS, end of study; EOT, end of treatment; FUW, follow-up week; NA, nucleos(t)ide analogue; Q4W, every 4 weeks; QD, every day; QOD, every other day; QW, every week; SC, subcutaneous.

NA stopping and restarting criteria

All participants continued NA therapy during the treatment period. NA stopping criteria were evaluated at the end of the treatment (EOT) and during the follow-up period. NA stopping criteria were as follows: ALT <1.25× baseline value, HBV DNA <20 IU/ml, HBeAg negative, and HBsAg loss (HBsAg <0.05 IU/ml) or HBsAg <100 IU/ml with ≥1 log10 IU/ml decrease from baseline.

Participants who discontinued NA therapy were monitored every 2 weeks for the first 12 weeks, and monthly thereafter. Monitoring frequency increased with elevation of HBV DNA or ALT (Table S1).

NA restarting criteria were any of the following: (1) HBV DNA >20,000 IU/ml; (2) ALT >1.5× ULN and HBV DNA >2,000 IU/ml; (3) ALT ≤1.5× ULN and HBV DNA >2,000 IU/ml, with a confirmatory retest within 1 week; or (4) clinically significant signs of decreasing liver function based on laboratory findings (e.g. INR, direct bilirubin) or clinical assessments (e.g. ascites, hepatic encephalopathy), regardless of HBV DNA or ALT levels.

During the study, three definitions of NA stopping criteria and two of NA restarting criteria were implemented successively (Table S2). The requirement for a decrease from baseline ≥1 log10 IU/ml to the stopping criteria was introduced to prevent discontinuation of NA in participants with HBsAg levels <100 IU/ml at EOT but without a substantial reduction in HBsAg from baseline. The changes to the restarting criteria were implemented to enable early detection and prompt management of virological relapse (VR) and avoid potential liver injury.

For all participants in treatment arms that included xalnesiran, the latest criteria for stopping and restarting NA were used, except for the first participant enrolled in the xalnesiran 100-mg arm, who stopped and restarted NA under the previous versions. However, a retrospective evaluation confirmed that this participant would have met the latest criteria for both. All participants from the NA control arm were evaluated using the previous criteria. In this arm, two participants met the criteria, discontinued, and restarted NA. Both had VR, one had clinical relapse (CR), and both restarted NA without any other adverse events (see definitions of VR and CR in the next paragraph). A retrospective evaluation confirmed that neither participant would have met the latest definitions of the criteria. Therefore, we excluded the NA control arm from this post hoc analysis.

Definition of NA discontinuation outcomes

VR was defined as HBV DNA ≥2,000 IU/ml, and CR was defined as HBV DNA ≥2,000 IU/ml and ALT ≥2× ULN. HBsAg rebound was defined post hoc as an increase in HBsAg >50% of its level when NA stopping criteria was met, using 0.05 IU/ml as a reference value for participants with HBsAg loss. A 50% increase in HBsAg was selected, as this approximately corresponds on the linear scale to the variability of HBsAg in the NA control arm. In addition to HBsAg rebound, the quantitative change in HBsAg level was calculated at 12, 24, and 36 weeks after NA stopping criteria were met, as well as from the time NA stopping criteria were met to the end of study (EOS). For the analysis of NA discontinuation outcomes, the participants who met NA stopping criteria were divided into three groups: (1) discontinued NA and remained off NA until EOS (‘off-NA group’), (2) discontinued and restarted NA (‘restart-NA group’), and (3) continued NA despite meeting NA stopping criteria (‘continue-NA group’).

Statistical analysis

The study population for this analysis was the NA discontinuation population defined post hoc as all participants in xalnesiran-based arms who were randomised to a treatment group and completed the treatment period. Descriptive statistics were used to summarise NA discontinuation outcomes. Univariate logistic regression and Χ2 two-sample test for equality of proportions with continuity correction were used to test the association between NA discontinuation outcomes (HBsAg rebound and VR) and viral, treatment-related, and host factors (HBsAg level at time of meeting NA stopping criteria, HBsAg level at screening, ALT elevation after NA discontinuation, an immunomodulator in the treatment regimen, age, and duration of previous NA treatment). All data analyses were conducted post hoc and were exploratory in nature. The p values presented are nominal and not adjusted for multiplicity.

Of the 40 participants who effectively discontinued NA, 24 (60%) remained off NA until EOS (off-NA group), 17 (43%) met NA restarting criteria, and 16 (40%) effectively restarted NA (restart-NA group). The parameters that triggered NA restart were a confirmed HBV DNA >2,000 but ≤ 20,000 IU/ml or an HBV DNA >20,000 IU/ml in 12 and five participants, respectively; none were accompanied by ALT elevation (Table 1).

Among the 58 participants who met NA stopping criteria, 13 (22%) met NA stopping criteria at EOT, 38 (66%) and seven (12%) met NA stopping criteria during the first and last 24 weeks of follow-up, respectively (Table S3). In the 63 participants who did not meet NA stopping criteria, the primary reasons were not achieving the ALT criterion in the first 24 weeks of follow-up and not achieving the HBsAg criterion in the last 24 weeks of follow-up (Fig. S2).

NA discontinuation efficacy outcomes

HBsAg and HBV DNA at EOS

Of the 40 participants who discontinued NA, five (13%) achieved and maintained HBsAg loss and HBV DNA undetectable (HBV DNA <10 IU/ml) while remaining off NA until EOS (i.e. from the off-NA group). All five participants belonged to arms in which xalnesiran was combined with an immunomodulator, and all met the stopping criteria and discontinued NA during the follow-up period after EOT. Three participants treated with xalnesiran and peg-IFN-alpha were monitored for between 30 and 39 weeks after NA discontinuation, within the temporal requirement of functional cure (≥24 weeks off therapy). The remaining two participants were treated with xalnesiran and ruzotolimod, and NA discontinuation was achieved later; the participants were monitored for 8 and 10 weeks (Table 1 and Table S4 and Fig. S3A). Additionally, one and two participants from the restart-NA and continue-NA groups, respectively, had HBsAg loss and HBV DNA undetectable at EOS while on NA (Table 1).

The proportion of participants with HBsAg <100 IU/ml at EOS was highest in the off-NA group (83%; 20/24), compared with 56% (9/16) and 72% (13/18) in the restart-NA and continue-NA groups, respectively (Fig. 2A). HBsAg changes after meeting NA stopping criteria

The mean (standard deviation) length of time from meeting NA stopping criteria to EOS was 37 (13), 42 (6), and 28 (15) weeks in the off-NA, restart-NA, and continue-NA groups, respectively (Table S5).

The increase in HBsAg after meeting NA stopping criteria until EOS was lower in the off-NA group (mean [standard deviation] of 0.45 [0.55] log10 IU/ml), than in the restart-NA (0.87 [1.04] log10 IU/ml) and continue-NA (0.72 [0.74] log10 IU/ml) groups. Factors associated with HBsAg rebound

VR and CR

Of the 40 participants who discontinued NA (off-NA and restart-NA groups), VR and CR occurred in 19 (48%) and three (8%) participants, respectively (Table 1). All participants with VR restarted NA, except for two participants who did not meet the NA restarting criteria and one participant who met the NA restarting criteria but refused to restart NA therapy. All three participants who remained off NA therapy until EOS maintained quantifiable HBV DNA levels <2,000 IU/ml and normal ALT levels until EOS (Table 1 and Fig. S3C,D).

All three participants with CR restarted NA. Their maximum ALT levels were 6×, 6×, and 23× ULN and maximum HBV DNA levels were 6.7, 5.0, and 6.1 log10 IU/ml, respectively. Two of the three participants with CR had a concomitant HBeAg sero-reversion, of which one was transient and the other was sustained until EOT (Fig. S3E). All three CR cases had ALT elevation after restarting NA. None of the VR and CR cases were associated with bilirubin increases, and all resolved without sequelae.

All participants who met NA stopping criteria had normal ALT at EOS, except for one participant in the off-NA group who had an ALT of 1.6× ULN and one participant in the restart-NA group who had an ALT of 1.7× ULN but had a normal ALT level at a retest 6 days after EOS. Longitudinal plots of HBsAg, HBV DNA, and ALT for individual participants are shown in Figs S5 and S6.

Factors associated with VR

No association with VR was detected in the univariate analysis (Fig. 5). The rates of VR were 22%, 50%, and 60% in participants with HBsAg loss, HBsAg 0.05–10 IU/ml, and HBsAg 10–100 IU/ml, respectively, when meeting NA stopping criteria (Table S6). The rate of VR was 40% (10/25) and 60% (9/15) in participants with HBsAg <1,000 and ≥1,000 IU/ml at screening, respectively (Table S6).

Discussion

This report of the NA discontinuation outcomes in Piranga showed that five participants achieved HBsAg loss and HBV DNA undetectable while remaining off NA until EOS, representing 13% of the 40 participants who discontinued NA and 4% of the 121 participants of this study. Of these, three participants fit the temporal requirement of functional cure (≥24 weeks after stopping all therapies), whereas the other two were not followed long enough to meet this criterion. Notably, all five participants belonged to arms treated with xalnesiran in combination with an immunomodulator (peg-IFN-alpha or ruzotolimod), which is in line with the primary results of Piranga demonstrating the value of these combinations to increase HBsAg loss rates.9 Additionally, three participants had HBsAg loss and HBV DNA undetectable on NA at EOS, one of whom achieved HBsAg loss de novo after discontinuing and restarting NA.

Regardless of whether they discontinued NA, participants treated with xalnesiran-based regimens consistently reached the nadir of HBsAg at or around EOT, followed by a slow, gradual rebound, potentially reflecting the long liver half-life of GalNac-conjugated siRNAs.9,17 However, among participants eligible to stop NA, those who remained off NA until EOS had the lowest HBsAg increase and HBsAg rebound rates. These observations differ from those of the results of previous studies of NA discontinuation after SOC therapy, which reported de novo HBsAg loss rates of 10–19% within 2–6 years and an average HBsAg reduction of ∼0.16 log10 IU/ml per year after NA discontinuation.[18], [19], [20], [21] However, the comparison of NA discontinuation outcomes following xalnesiran or SOC therapy is made difficult by the differences in modes of action, follow-up durations, and the NA restarting criteria.

Piranga introduced stringent NA stopping and restarting criteria, the framework of which facilitated close monitoring. This resulted in rates of VR and CR of 48% and 8%, respectively, after NA discontinuation.13 Comparatively, RETRACT-B, the largest global cohort of NA-treated patients, reported cumulative probabilities of VR and CR of 68.9% and 31.9% 12 months, respectively, after NA discontinuation.21 Other studies in which NA-treated individuals stopped NA therapy with an HBsAg level <100 or <200 IU/ml reported VR rates ranging from 39% to 58%.[22], [23], [24] In the phase II REEF-2 trial, which evaluated novel investigational molecules aimed at achieving functional cure, all participants discontinued NA treatment without the requirement to meet prespecified NA stopping criteria, resulting in an off-treatment rate of CR (defined as HBV DNA >2,000 IU/ml and confirmed ALT ≥3× ULN and ≥3× nadir) of 3.9%.25 However, the differences in NA stopping strategies and definitions of CR between REEF-2 and Piranga are likely explanations for the differences observed between these two studies. Moreover, a case of liver failure reported in the REEF-2 trial10 led to the modification of the restarting criteria in Piranga to enable the early detection and prompt management of VR, thereby avoiding liver-related morbidity and mortality. However, these modifications led to NA restart systematically in the absence of elevated ALT. This suggests that NA may have been restarted before a potentially beneficial ALT flare, which may have been linked to immune clearance of the virus.13,19,26

Among factors previously associated with NA discontinuation outcomes24,[27], [28], [29] that could be tested in the Piranga study, HBsAg levels when meeting NA stopping criteria and at screening were significantly associated with HBsAg response following NA discontinuation. Although the addition of an immunomodulator to xalnesiran was essential to increase HBsAg loss rates,9 this was not associated with HBsAg rebound. Other factors previously reported could not be evaluated in this study, either because the sample size was too small (quantified HBV RNA or characterized HBV genotype) or because they were not measured (HBcrAg and immune markers). More studies are needed to thoroughly evaluate the impact of these factors on NA discontinuation outcomes after treatment with novel functional cure regimens.

In Piranga, NA stopping criteria were evaluated longitudinally by monitoring at EOT and during the follow-up period. Most participants (77%), including the five who achieved and maintained HBsAg loss and HBV DNA undetectable after NA discontinuation until EOS, met NA stopping criteria at different timepoints during the follow-up period rather than at EOT. The longitudinal monitoring of NA stopping criteria, as used in this study, rather than assessing at a single time point as commonly implemented in other studies, would be a suggested approach that better reflects patient management in clinical practice. Ideally, this longitudinal approach should be started after a predefined NA consolidation period and combined with an adaptive follow-up period. In this study, the absence of an NA consolidation period limited the ability to differentiate the immunomodulatory effects of treatment arms containing an immunomodulator from the rejuvenation of exhausted immune cells because of NA discontinuation. The absence of an adaptive follow-up period in Piranga resulted in varying lengths of monitoring after NA discontinuation. This prevented confirmation of the temporal requirement of the functional cure definition (>24 weeks off any therapy) for some participants. We acknowledge that implementing longitudinal monitoring and adaptive follow-up introduces complexities into clinical trial design and operations.

This post hoc exploratory analysis had some limitations. The relatively short follow-up limited the evaluation of long-term NA discontinuation outcomes (e.g. HBsAg loss, decompensation, cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma, or death). The small numbers of participants who met the NA stopping criteria resulted in data being pooled across arms without adjustment and prevented multivariate analysis of factors associated with NA discontinuation outcomes.

Conclusions

In conclusion, five participants achieved and maintained HBsAg loss and undetectable HBV DNA while off NA until EOS, all in treatment arms with xalnesiran and an immunomodulator (peg-IFN-alpha or ruzotolimod). NA discontinuation after xalnesiran-based therapies can be safely managed using close monitoring and by applying prespecified NA stopping and restarting criteria. The longitudinal monitoring of prespecified NA stopping criteria, rather than assessing at a single time point, would be a suggested approach. The implementation of an adaptive follow-up period long enough to confirm functional cure outcomes is also advised. However, optimization of the NA stopping strategies remains an important topic that needs to be addressed for functional cure regimens to be successful. To this end, well-powered studies that evaluate different approaches with sufficient follow-up will be essential.

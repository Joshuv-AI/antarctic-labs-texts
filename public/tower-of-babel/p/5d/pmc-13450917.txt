New insights in submucous fibrosis: Nicotine dependence and nutritional assessment
Authors: Rishabh Sachdeva, Aadithya B. Urs, Priya Kumar, Bhawna Mahajan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Submucous fibrosis (SMF) is an oral potentially malignant disorder with a high risk of malignant transformation, primarily associated with areca nut use and tobacco consumption.Aim:This study aimed to evaluate the correlation of nicotine dependence and nutritional status with disease severity in patients with SMF.Settings and Design:This observational study was conducted among 70 SMF patients subdivided on clinical and histological classification.Materials and Methods:Habit index was calculated and Nicotine dependence was measured using Fagerstrom test for nicotine dependence (FTND). Body mass index (BMI) and mid upper arm circumference (MUAC) was recorded. Haematological assessment, including serum iron, vitamin B12, and folate levels was conducted. Malnutritional universal screening tool (MUST) was used to score the risk of malnutrition.Statistical Analysis:Chi-square, Pearson’s correlation and Spearman’s correlation tests were used for group comparisons of categorical, numerical, and ordinal data, respectively. Level of statistical significance was set at a P value less than 0.05.Results and Conclusion:Clinical and histological staging showed a positive correlation with habit index and nicotine dependence (P value = 0.001). BMI, MUAC and serum iron levels showed inverse correlation with severity of the disease. MUST scores revealed increase (P value = 0.001) with advancing stages of SMF. FTND showed significant correlation with BMI, MUAC, MUST and serum iron values. Nicotine dependence and nutritional status are important indicators of the severity of the disease in patients with SMF. Routine assessment of these parameters’ aids in the identification of high-risk groups and to plan a targeted treatment strategy.

INTRODUCTION

Submucous fibrosis (SMF) is a well-recognized oral potentially malignant disorder of the oral cavity and oropharynx with a high rate of malignant transformation (7–30%).[1] Areca nut, containing the alkaloid arecoline, has been recognized as the primary etiological factor for SMF. Long term use of smokeless form of tobacco can lead to addiction and has a great impact on overall health and nutritional status in OSMF subjects.[2] Fagerstrom test for nicotine dependence (FTND) assesses nicotine dependence based on the daily tobacco consumption behaviour of an individual.[3] It consists of separate questionnaires for both smoked and smokeless forms of tobacco and grades the dependence as low, medium, and high. Long-term use of these habits can result in addiction and adversely affect overall health and nutritional status.

Nutrition plays an important role in the progression of a disease by regulating immune responses to infective and inflammatory processes. Nutritional deficiencies worsen chronic metabolic disorders and can increase the risk of malignant transformation of oral potentially malignant disorders.[45] Non-invasive indicators including body mass index (BMI) and mid upper arm circumference (MUAC) are used for mass screening of nutritional status.[6789] Malnutrition universal screening tool (MUST) developed by the British Association of Parenteral and Enteral Nutrition also aids in assessing the overall risk for malnutrition and implementing quality standards for nutritional support.[10] Biochemical indicators for nutritional status like serum iron and vitamin B12 aid in identifying early changes in the body and also promote proactive intervention for high-risk groups.[11]

There is lack of sufficient research on the impact of nutrition on the severity and progression of SMF, particularly among the Indian population, where SMF has a high prevalence. Also, an assessment of the direct correlation between nicotine dependence and nutritional status is required. Thus, the current study was undertaken to correlate nicotine dependence and nutritional status with the severity of the disease in SMF.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The present cross-sectional observational study was conducted among SMF subjects who were clinically diagnosed with histopathological confirmation. The study was conducted in accordance with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki, approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee, and a formal ethical clearance (No. 2023/597) was obtained dated May 8, 2023. It was undertaken for a period of nine months, from May 2023 to January 2024. It was reported according to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines for observational studies. The sample size was calculated using the following formula for correlation studies: n = ((Z_α/2 + Z_β)^2/f^2) + 3. A minimum sample size of 50 participants was found to detect a moderate correlation (r = 0.39) with 80% power at a 5% significance level in this study.

Inclusion criteria

A random selection of 70 subjects willing to participate in the study was conducted from the outpatient department of our institute. Histopathologically proven cases of SMF in the age range of 18 to 60 years with a habit of both areca nut and tobacco consumption were included.

Exclusion criteria

Potential confounding factors considered were geographic and economic disparities, lifestyle variations, differences due to age, nutritional disorders, and recall bias (weight loss). To address these confounders, subjects with a history of systemic/nutritional diseases and physical or mental disability or those with only betel use habit were excluded from the study. Extremes of age (less than 18 and more than 60 years of age) were excluded to address differences due to age, recall bias and other co-morbities. To maintain economic parity, subjects belonging to low-income group were excluded.

Subjects were divided on the basis of clinical classification by Nagesh and Bailoor into early, moderate, and severe stages [Figure 1a-c] and histological diagnostic criteria and classification by Pindborg and Sirsat into very early, early, moderately advanced, and advanced grades [Figure 2a-c].[1213] Comparison of all the parameters were made amongst different clinical stages and histological grades.

Objectives

The current study was undertaken with the primary objective of correlating various determinants of nicotine dependence and nutritional status with the clinical and histological severity in SMF. Secondary objectives included correlation of nicotine dependence with the nutritional profile in SMF. Utilization of MUST to evaluate the nutritional profile in SMF population was taken under assessment. We hypothesized that the increased dependence on nicotine leads to increased severity of the disease and a poor nutritional status.

Assessment of nicotine dependence

An informed consent was obtained, and a detailed clinical history was recorded, including information about deleterious tobacco, areca nut, and alcohol consumption habits. Variables of interest included habit index and FTND. Habit index was measured by multiplication of quantity consumed per day and duration of consumption (in years). Nicotine dependence was measured using the FTND. FTND is a questionnaire-based dependence test for both smoked and smokeless forms of tobacco and grades the nicotine dependence into low (less than 4), medium (4–6), and high (more than 6). In the current study, the questionnaire for smokeless form was recorded.

Assessment of nutritional status

For nutritional assessment, key elements comprised of anthropometric criteria included BMI and MUAC. BMI was calculated using the standard BMI formula using the weight (in kilograms) and height (in metres). For MUAC, the circumference of the right upper arm in a relaxed state was measured halfway between the tip of the shoulder and the tip of the elbow. MUST was used to score the risk of malnutrition in the following three steps. BMI (Step 1) of all the subjects was scored. A history of unplanned weight loss in the past 6 months (Step 2) and acute disease (Step 3) was recorded. Scores from the above steps were added to determine the overall risk category. A haematological assessment of all patients was conducted. Three millilitres of whole blood samples were obtained from the median cubital vein of the non-dominant hand from all participants by aseptic venipuncture and was stored at 4 degrees Celsius. Vitamin B12 and folate levels were estimated on Roche Diagnostics Cobas e411 Chemiluminescence Analyzer and serum iron levels were estimated on Autoanalyzer c501, Roche Diagnostics, Hitachi by commercially available kits.

Data collection was done with the help of a Microsoft Excel spreadsheet. Chi-square test was used for group comparisons of categorical data. Linear correlation between the variables was explored using Pearson’s correlation test for numerical data and Spearman’s correlation test for ordinal data. The level of statistical significance was set at a P value less than 0.05.

RESULTS

All subjects willingly participated in all the steps of the study and no patients were lost in subsequent procedures. No data was found to be missing. Mean age was observed to be 36.45 years. Mean height and weight were 1.67 m and 61 kg, respectively. Out of 70 patients, 47 were males and 23 were females (Male to female ratio ≈2:1). Clinically, most cases belonged to the moderate stage, followed by early and advanced. Histologically, 47% cases were reported as moderately advanced, followed by 33% cases of early, 21% of advanced grade and no cases of very early grade. Habit index showed a significant positive association with both clinical and histological stages of the disease. FTND scores revealed that one third of the cases showed high dependence and two-thirds showed medium dependence on tobacco products. A comparison of FTND scores for smokeless form of tobacco amongst different groups showed that the dependence was significantly higher in the advanced group, both clinically and histologically (P value = 0.001).

BMI and MUAC also showed a significant negative association with the advanced stages of the disease. Forty-three patients had a BMI less than 18.5 kg/m2, while only 7 had a BMI over 20 kg/m2. In the early stage, the mean MUAC was 28.5 cm which decreased to 27.3 cm in the moderate and 25.5 cm in the severe stages. MUST showed a mean value of 1.24. There was a statistically significant increase in MUST score in the moderately advanced and advanced grades. BMI and MUAC were found to be significantly lower in patients with high FTND scores, while MUST scores were significantly higher as compared to patients with low tobacco dependence. Among the different stages, MUST scores were correlated with the FTND grades. It revealed a stronger correlation of MUST with FTND in the moderate and severe stages as compared to the early stage. Haematological parameters showed a decrease in serum iron, vitamin B12 and folate levels among the advanced group as compared to the early and moderate groups. However, a significant association with advancing clinical and histological stages was found only with serum iron (P value = 0.001). DISCUSSION

SMF is a chronic, debilitating collagen metabolic disorder affecting the oral cavity and oropharynx with a potential risk of malignant transformation.[14] Male predominance was found in the present study, probably due to a higher prevalence of such deleterious habits amongst men or because females are less concerned/conscious and aware about their health. Ahmad et al. also reported a male to female ratio of 2.7:1 in an Indian population like the current study.

Tobacco and areca nut are among the four most psychoactive drugs in the world.[15] Habit index showed a statistically significant association with the severity of the disease, implying a direct dose response relationship. In a study by Singh et al.,[16] habit index was found to have a significant positive correlation with the histological grading of the disease, like the present study. However, their study assessed only habit index that considers the quantity and duration of the tobacco consumption, underestimating its psychoactive dependence. In the current study, in addition to the habit index, dependence has been assessed by FTND and was found to be significantly associated with the severity of disease, and nutritional status of these patients. FTND also grades the nicotine dependence, thus providing a better risk assessment amongst the individuals, and during their post-treatment follow-ups. Increased intake of these products has been seen even in advanced cases of SMF where reduced food intake resulted from an inability to open the mouth. This suggests that areca-nut and poor nutrition can further increase cravings and dependency. In such cases, these substances are used as easier and cheaper substitutes for food. They are also reported to affect the prefrontal cortex providing relief from pain and burning sensation experienced because of the disease.[17] Hence, malnutrition and nicotine dependence are both a cause and a result of the disease.

In a study by Yadav et al.,[18] BMI and MUAC showed a significant decrease with the severity of the disease, like our study. However, in the current study, an association of BMI and MUAC was made with nicotine dependence that revealed significantly lower BMI and MUAC among the high nicotine dependence group. MUST has been used for the first time in SMF cases and revealed a significantly increased risk of malnutrition in the advanced stages of SMF. Such non-invasive parameters can help assess the overall health of patients with such potentially malignant disorders on a routine basis.

MUST has been used to assess the risk of malnutrition in head and neck cancers, tuberculosis, and so on.[192021] Jendretzki et al.[22] and Auma et al.[23] have shown MUST to be a reliable, non-invasive tool for nutrition assessment and survival prediction in cancer patients. However, its utilization in oral, potentially malignant disorders can help identify high-risk individuals and plan nutritional monitoring to reduce the potential for malignant transformation. Hettiarachchi et al.[24] assessed the risk of malnutrition in cancer outpatients receiving chemotherapy and revealed that MUST had higher sensitivity (86.7%) than BMI (28.9%) in detecting malnutrition.

Haematological examination showed a statistically significant decrease in serum iron with advancing grades of SMF, while the decrease in vitamin B12 and folate levels was not found to be statistically significant. A study conducted in southern India by Abidullah et al.[25] reported a statistically significant inverse correlation of iron, vitamin B12, haemoglobin, packed cell volume, and other haematological parameters amongst the SMF cases compared to healthy controls. However, a comparison between different grades of SMF was not made. Lack of serum iron and other micronutrients like vitamin B12 and serum folate in advancing stages is a major challenge to healthcare providers and can be attributed to the dysfunctional reparative response and poor healing.[26] This can work in combination with deleterious habits, genetic and environmental risk factors for carcinogenesis.

Iron is being used up in the synthesis of collagen, thus inducing/increasing fibrosis. During collagen synthesis, hydroxylation of the amino acids proline and lysine occurs, which is catalysed by the enzyme’s proline hydroxylase and peptidyl lysine hydroxylase, respectively. These enzymes require iron as a cofactor, thus, leading to a reduction in serum iron.[2728] Khanna and Karjodkar.[29] observed a statistically significant reduction in iron levels in the precancer group and an even greater reduction in the cancer group. Ramanathan[30] has suggested that the altered oral mucosa in SMF might be the manifestation of chronic iron or vitamin B deficiency.

Pre-cachexia is characterized by an unintentional weight loss of less than 5% of body weight in the preceding 6 months, an inflammatory response to an underlying chronic disease, and anorexia.[31] MUST, used in chronic disorders like SMF in this study, considers unintentional weight loss and grades it to help manage such groups with appropriate nutritional support and monitoring. In advanced cases, cachexia may result from inflammatory and metabolic alterations leading to reduced availability of nutrients, like cancer cachexia.[32] Parameters assessing loss of weight and reduction in the level of nutrients can henceforth be an initial clue in identifying patients with cachexia and alleviating the symptoms.

The study aimed to provide an insight into the novel application of FTND and MUST in SMF cases. The study includes both male and female gender that enhances the generalizability. However, the potential influence of hormones on the levels of serum iron, vitamin B12 and serum folate could have led to confounding bias. The current study was a preliminary assessment of these indicators in SMF cases in the Indian population. In future, larger sample size can be taken and comparison with a control group can be done to enhance the validity and credibility of these tools.

Thus, both nicotine dependence and nutritional status are valuable indicators of severity of the disease in SMF that can contribute in determining the prognosis, overall survival, and in predicting the risk of malignant transformation.

CONCLUSION

Individuals with high nicotine dependence and malnutrition are more likely to present with severe symptoms of the disease. BMI, MUAC, and MUST are useful non-invasive parameters for assessing nutritional status in SMF. Thus, routine assessment of the nicotine dependence and nutritional status of SMF patients can help identify the high-risk groups and design focused treatment strategies to prevent aggravation of the disease. Such tailored interventions are essential to address the specific needs of each affected individual.

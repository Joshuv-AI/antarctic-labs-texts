Total Synthesis and Biological Evaluation of Exiguamines
Authors: Seungjoo Noh, Jisook Park, Yeehwan Kim, Venkat R. Krishnamurthy, Chang-Ki Min, Eun Young Choi, Chulbom Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A concise and divergent total synthesis of the marine indoloquinone natural product exiguamine has been accomplished. The synthetic strategy relies on a hydantoin linchpin approach that unites the tryptamine- and dopamine-derived subunits, setting the stage for efficient construction of the core framework. A Claisen rearrangement forges the critical C–C bond, and a subsequent oxidative cyclization cascade assembles the hexacylic skeleton, including the pyran and indolinium rings. Beyond the natural products exiguamines A and B, this route provided access to their constitutional isomers iso-exiguamines A and B, as well as the enantiomerically enriched exiguamine B and epi-exiguamine B through an asymmetric synthesis. These natural and unnatural exiguamines exhibit potent inhibitory activity against indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase (IDO). The work described here establishes an efficient platform for the preparation and further biological studies of this distinctive class of catecholamine-derived alkaloids.

Introduction

Exiguamines are indoloquinone alkaloids isolated from the marine sponge Neopetrosia exigua (Figure ). They potently inhibit indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase (IDO; EC 1.13.11.42), the enzyme catalyzing the first and rate-limiting step of tryptophan catabolism. Since IDO is frequently overexpressed in cancer cells and correlates with reduced T-cell activity and immune tolerance, these metabolites could serve as potential agents for restoring antitumor immunity. Although the limited clinical efficacy of IDO1 inhibitors in oncology has impeded further development, , new directions have emerged from the approach targeting IDO1 simultaneously with apo-IDO1 protein involved in nonenzymic signaling functions or TDO (tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase) regulating systemic levels of tryptophan. Moreover, recent findings show that IDO blockade reduces production of neurotoxic tryptophan metabolites, underscoring the therapeutic relevance of IDO inhibition not only in neoplasia but also neurodegenerative disorders.

Structure of Exiguamines A, B and C.

Structurally, exiguamines feature an unprecedented hexacyclic scaffold composed of a tryptamine-derived quinone and a dopamine-derived indolinium unit fused through a pyran ring conjoined by a spiro-linked hydantoin. Along with their biomedical promise, the unique molecular architecture of exiguamines, exquisitely assembled from indoloquinone and catechol amine subunits, renders them attractive targets for chemical synthesis and biological studies. The Trauner group accomplished the total synthesis of exiguamines A (1a) and B (1b) through a bioinspired strategy forging the unusual hexacyclic skeleton from 2, a putative biosynthetic precursor (Scheme ). , Oxidation of catechol 2 was proposed to proceed via bisquinone 3 and indolinium 4 intermediates, which undergo aza-Michael addition and oxa-6π electrocyclization reactions, respectively. The pericyclic ring closure implicated in the final pyran formation offers a compelling rationale for the natural occurrence of the exiguamines as racemates. Notably, while oxidation of 2 with excess AgO furnishes exiguamine B (1b) as a single diastereomer, the same silver-mediated reaction applied to exiguamine A (1a) fails to yield the congener B, leaving the biosynthetic and stereochemical origin of the benzylic alcohol in 1b unresolved. Despite these intriguing chemical and biological characteristics, however, no further total synthesis of exiguamines has been forthcoming since the Trauner group’s pioneering work; subsequent studies have focused primarily on truncated exiguamine analogues for biological evaluation. , In the course of our investigations into IDO expression and function at tissue and cellular levels, , we became interested in the exiguamines and their analogues as potential immunomodulatory agents. In addition to developing a concise and modular synthetic route to the exiguamines, we sought to evaluate the individual synthetic enantiomers of these racemic natural products together with their constitutional and stereo isomers. Here we report the result of our chemical synthetic and biological investigations of the exiguamines.

Results and Discussion

Synthetic Plan

In devising a synthetic plan toward the exiguamines, we focused on the assembly of the core pyran ring, with particular attention to the sequence of C–O and C–C bond formation (Scheme ). In light of the challenges encountered in Trauner’s synthesis in forming the biaryl linkage (bond a) between the tryptamine- and dopamine-derived arenes by cross-coupling, we pursued an alternative approach that exploited the hydantoin moiety as a linchpin to unite the subunits. Specifically, we envisioned that addition (bond b) of the hydantoin–dopamine conjugate 8, readily accessible by simple etherification (bond c) of 6 and 7, to the tryptamine-derived quinone 5 would furnish the tripartite intermediate 9 in a straightforward manner. Subsequently, the electron-rich arene and electron-deficient quinone, tethered through the hydantoin, were expected to undergo efficient intramolecular C–C bond formation to afford 10, which would be poised for redox interconversion to an intermediate analogous to 2 en route to exiguamines.

Synthetic Plan

Synthesis of the Fragments

With the linchpin strategy in hand, our synthetic campaign commenced by preparing the indoloquinone fragment 5 (Scheme A). Starting from Boc-protected tryptamine 11, an iridium-catalyzed C–H borylation installed boronate groups at the C2 and C7 positions. Subsequent palladium-catalyzed protodeborylation of the resulting diboronate 12 selectively removed the C2 boryl group to provide the 7-borylated tryptamine 13. Oxidation of 13 by a two-step sequence furnished the targeted fragment 5. Alternatively, the same indoloquinone 5 could also be accessed from commercially available 4-hydroxyindole through a four-step route (see the Supporting Information).

Total Synthesis of Exiguamine A

The synthesis of the hydantoin–dopamine fragment 8 began with the dimethylation of dopamine (Scheme B). Protection of the catechol as a silylene acetal, followed by reductive amination, furnished the N,N-dimethylated dopamine 14. Installation of a hydantoin unit onto the free catechol (from desilylation of 14) using the hydantoin chloride 7 proved problematic, invariably affording a 1:1 mixture of O- and O′-alkylated products under a range of conditions. In contrast, performing the O-alkylation on the silyl-protected 14 in the presence of LiOH delivered the desired aryl ether 15 with a useful 3:1 O:O′ selectivity. , Subsequent replacement of the silylene hemiacetal with a benzyl ether provided efficient access to the target fragment 8. Notably, the direct etherification of silylene acetal 14, likely proceeding through a silicate intermediate, was markedly more efficient than analogous borate- or stannane-based protocols. Also established was an alternative route to 8 via 6 from 3,4-dihydroxybenzaldehyde, as detailed in the Supporting Information. With a plan to prepare 9 by linking fragments 5 and 8 via a Mukaiyama–Michael addition, 8 was subjected to conditions for silyl enol ether formation. Treatment of 8 with a metal amide base and TBSOTf did produce a silyl enol ether but only in poor yield due to extensive cleavage of the newly formed C–O bond. Interestingly, protection of the tertiary amine as a borane complex (cf. 16) dramatically suppressed this decomposition pathway, thus enabling the preparation of silyl enol ether 17 in multigram quantities.

Synthesis of Exiguamine A

Having established robust routes to the two fragments, we next sought to couple them and assemble the hexacyclic framework to complete the synthesis of exiguamine A (Scheme C). The Mukaiyama-Michael reaction between 5 and 17 was initially examined under a range of Lewis acid conditions. However, these efforts failed to induce the desired C–C bond formation, instead yielding a phenol (cf. 6) arising from the C–O bond cleavage of 17 as the predominant product. In light of the pronounced Lewis acid sensitivity of 17, we turned to Lewis base-mediated activation of the silyl enol ether using fluoride. A survey of fluoride sources revealed that TASF, in combination with TMSF, most effectively promoted the coupling to furnish 18 as a single regioisomer, whose structure was confirmed by X-ray crystallography. Removal of the borane moiety with DABCO then delivered the tripartite intermediate 9 which we targeted at the outset of the synthetic campaign.

With the fully elaborated 9 in hand, incorporating all the carbon atoms of the exiguamine framework, we next investigated intramolecular coupling between the two six-membered rings. However, attempted C–C bond formation under a variety of Friedel–Crafts and photochemical conditions proved ineffective to form pyran 10. Rather, these reactions led to cleavage of the C19–O bond of the N,O-acetal. Faced with this persistent C–O bond cleavage, we turned to pericyclic reactivity as an alternative means of forging the C–C bond. Claisen rearrangement of 9 was attempted by direct heating in refluxing toluene, but cleavage of the same C19–O bond was again observed. We speculated that this unproductive pathway might be initiated by inter- or intramolecular electron transfer to the indoloquinone moiety, generating a radical anion that undergoes C–O bond cleavage. We therefore proposed that this detrimental radical anion formation might be suppressed by the addition of 1,4-benzoquinone (p-BQ), which could act as a competing reversible electron acceptor, undergoing one-electron reduction in preference to the indoloquinone unit to form the semiquinone radical anion (see Scheme S1 in the Supporting Information for proposed mechanistic pathways). Under these conditions employing catalytic p-BQ, phenol 19 and pyran 10 were obtained as a 2:1 mixture. Given the propensity of 19 to undergo aerobic oxidation to 10, the crude mixture from the Claisen rearrangement was directly subjected to methanol reflux under air, which cleanly delivered pyran 10 in high yield.

With the crucial C–C linkage efficiently forged by a [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement, the final phase of the synthesis focused on installation of the indolinium unit into the pentacyclic framework. The sequence began with hydrogenolytic removal of the benzyl group from 10 under palladium catalysis. Unexpectedly, exposure of 10 to hydrogen did not furnish the debenzylated phenol 10a but instead produced tetraphenol 20b. Careful monitoring of the reaction revealed the transient formation of triphenol 20a, rather than phenol 10a, suggesting that hydrogenation initially occurred at the benzoquinone moiety to form a hydroquinone, which in turn promoted cleavage of the phenolic C19–O bond prior to the debenzylation. Importantly, tetraphenol 20b was found to undergo spontaneous oxidation in air to engender the Boc-protected exiguamine A (21, vide infra) in 50% yield, pointing to the inherent reactivity embedded in the backbone of 20, similar to the Trauner intermediate 2, to engage in a series of C–O and C–N bond formations through quinone-hydroquinone redox equilibria. Guided by these observations, pyran 10 was converted to exiguamine A (1a) in a one-pot, three-step sequence. Thus, sequential exposure of a methanolic solution of 10 to H2 and O2 in the presence of a palladium catalyst, followed by deprotection with concentrated HCl, furnished 1a as a chloride salt in high yield. The spectroscopic data of the synthetic exiguamine A were in complete agreement with those reported for the natural product.

Synthesis of Exiguamine B

Having completed the total synthesis of exiguamine A, we next turned our attention to exiguamine B (Scheme ). Inspired by Truner’s findings, we questioned whether oxidation employing AgO might also be effective for tetraphenol 20b (vis-à-vis 2) in promoting benzylic hydroxylation. In practice, however, silver-mediated oxidation of 20b did not induce the benzylic oxidation: the one-pot oxidation directly following hydrogenation of 10 produced the phenolic pyran 10a without indolinium ring formation (see the Supporting Information). When tetraphenol 20b was treated with excess AgO after removal of the palladium catalyst, only the Boc-protected exiguamine A 21 was generated. Taken together, these observations suggested that the benzylic oxidation requires protonation of the tertiary amine (cf. protonated 2), which may facilitate formation of the p-quinone methide intermediate 22 while suppressing premature indolinium ring formation. This sequence would predispose the system to hydration followed by oxidative aza- and oxa-cyclizations en route to exiguamine B. Conversely, if indolinium ring formation precedes generation of the p-quinone methide, competitive pyran ring formation may occur to afford 21, in which benzylic oxidation is disfavored because of the diminished electron density of the catechol ring. Accordingly, revision of the sequence to include hydrogenation of 10, followed by treatment with HCl and oxidation with AgO in aqueous formic acid, afforded exiguamine B (1b) as a single diastereomer in 21% yield. Given this exclusive diastereoselectivity, the axial chirality along the C7–C10 bond of 22 appeared to govern the relative configuration of C17–O and C19–O bonds during benzylic hydroxylation and pyran ring formation, respectively. In this regard, it is worthwhile to note that a hemiacetal-mediated hydration pathway (shown in red) suggested by the Trauner group, rather than direct water addition (shown in blue), would place both C17–O and C19–O bonds in the same relative orientation (for a discussion of the atropisomerism prior to pyran ring formation, vide infra).

Synthesis of iso -Exiguamines A and B

Although the total syntheses of exiguamines A and B had been accomplished, the routes were not amenable to high-yielding production to sustain further biological studies. The key limitation lay in the union of the two fragments by the Mukaiyama-Michael addition, which, although highly regioselective, furnished the desired adduct only in modest yield. In search of a more effective coupling strategy, we reasoned that enhancing the reactivity of the quinone might better promote the crucial C–C bond-forming event (Scheme A). Thus, quinone 5 was brominated with pyridinium tribromide to prepare 6-bromo-quinone 23 (see the Supporting Information), whose reactivity was then examined. Whereas the Mukaiyama-Michael reaction of 23 with TBS enol ether 17 proceeded in only 21% yield, use of the lithium enolate generated from 16 with LHMDS dramatically improved the outcome, delivering 18 in 89% yield. Since this pivotal tripartite intermediate had already been advanced to exiguamines A and B, this result constituted a scalable route to the natural products.

Total Synthesis of iso -Exiguamines A and B

The remarkable efficiency of the coupling that occurred via cine-, rather than ipso-substitution, appeared to arise from the dual effect of the bromo substituent in both polarizing the quinone and stabilizing the anionic intermediate emerging upon enolate addition, reminiscent of the related heteroatom- substituted acceptors for pericyclic, Michael and Giese reactions. − Encouraged by this result, we next turned to 5-bromoquinone 24 isomeric to 23 to probe its reactivity as well as regioselectivity in the same transformation (Scheme B). The reaction of 24 with the enolate of 16 again proceeded smoothly to furnish a single regioisomeric adduct. After borane removal, the resulting tripartite intermediate 25, bearing an inverted indoloquinone orientation, relative to 9, was subjected to sequential Claisen rearrangement and aerobic oxidation reactions to form pyran 26, a constitutional isomer of 10. Advancement of 26 through the endgame routes, established respectively for exiguamines A and B, furnished the isomeric congeners 27a and 27b, which we designated iso-exiguamines A and B.

Synthesis of enantio- and epi-Exiguamines B

Having established synthetic routes to exiguamines A and B (1a and 1b) as well as their constitutional isomers iso-exiguamines A and B (27a and 27b), asymmetric synthesis campaign. (Scheme ). We were particularly interested in whether enantiopure synthetic samples would exhibit IDO inhibitory activity distinct from that of the naturally occurring racemate. In addition, we sought to leverage a stereochemically defined pathway to access the epimer of exiguamine B, a diastereomer notably absent from both natural sources and prior synthetic studies. We envisioned that replacing dopamine with L-adrenaline in the route would enable an enantioselective synthesis, thereby providing exiguamine of defined absolute configuration and potentially an unnatural diastereomer. The synthetic campaign toward enantiomeric exiguamine variants started with the preparation of the hydantoin-adrenaline fragment (Scheme A). As the O-alkylation protocol employing a silylene acetal proved unsuitable, adrenaline was instead subjected to a three-step sequence of Boc protection, benzylidene acetal formation and reductive N-methylation to furnish alcohol 28. Reductive ring-opening of the benzylidene acetal with Dibal-H then produced a 1:1 mixture of regioisomeric phenols 29a and 29b. Subsequent O-alkylation of phenol 29a with hydantoin chloride 7, followed by protection of the benzylic alcohol as a TES ether and the tertiary amine as an amine-borane complex, afforded the hydantoin-adrenaline conjugate 30, primed for the coupling with the indoloquinone counterpart 23.

Total Synthesis of enantio-Exiguamine B and epi-Exiguamine B

With the stereodefined fragment 30 in hand, we then examined its coupling with 6-bromo-quinone 23 in order to advance toward enantiopure exiguamine B (Scheme B). The coupling reaction proceeded smoothly again with exclusive cine-selectivity, affording the adduct as a 1:1 mixture of inseparable diastereomers. After removal of the borane and TES groups, the mixture of N,O-acetal epimers 31a and 31b was subjected to the Claisen rearrangement and aerobic oxidation. Unexpectedly, the [3,3] rearrangement of 31 proved markedly less efficient than those of the analogous substrates, 9 and 25, devoid of the benzylic hydroxy group. Refluxing 31 in toluene followed by aerobic oxidation in methanol furnished a 1.7:1 mixture of pyrans 32 and 33, each existing as a mixture of hemiacetal epimers in 10:1 and 2.5:1 ratios, respectively. The stereostructures of these isomeric products were elucidated by NOE analyses of the corresponding TES acetals 34 and 35, which were generated as single stereoisomers from 32 and 33, respectively, upon treatment with TESCl in the presence of DBU. In the NOE experiments, TES acetal 35 exhibited a strong correlation between the α-silyl methylene (δ 0.3 ppm) and the amide N-methyl (δ 2.9 ppm) protons, whereas no such correlation was observed for 34. In addition, the α-silyl methylene had a correlation with the benzylic methine (δ 5.4 ppm) but none with the α-amino methylene in either compound, indicating the TESO group to be of a β-orientation. Application of the final redox–deprotection sequence to 32 furnished enantiopure exiguamine B (enantio-1b, [α]D = +221°) in a 4:1 ratio along with its C19 epimer 36, designated epi-exiguamine B ([α]D = −34°). The same sequence performed on 33 yielded a 10:1 mixture of 36 and (+)-1b. Upon isolation as single stereoisomers, no interconversion between these epimeric exiguamines was observed, confirming their configurational stability.

The major stereochemical courses in the conversion of 31 to (+)-exiguamine B (1b) and (−)-epi-exiguamine B (36) are delineated in Scheme C. We propose that each of the N,O-acetal C19 epimers 31a (C19–O α) and 31b (C19–O β) follow stereospecific pathways, leading to the formation of (−)-epi- and (+)-exiguamines B as the major products, respectively. During the formation of pyrans 32 and 33 via sequential [3,3]-rearrangement and aerobic oxidation reactions, inversion at the C19 stereocenter occurs due to the involvement of atropisomeric intermediates (37–40). In contrast, the redox processes in the final sequence trigger the opening and closure of the pyran rings of 32 and 33, which occur preferentially with stereoretention at the C19–O bond, via the analogous atropisomers (41–44). Thus, in the late stage installing the indolinium and pyran ring systems, the N,O-acetal C19 stereocenter is transiently destroyed and reconstructed with net inversion of configuration via a cascade of point-to-axial-to-point chirality transfer. This restricted rotation about the C7–C10 bond connecting the two six-membered rings, in concert with hemiacetal mediated stereoselective hydration of the proposed intermediate 22, may likewise account for the stereochemical outcome in the formation of exiguamine B as shown in Scheme .

Biological Evaluation of Exiguamines

With a series of exiguamines synthesized in sufficient quantities, their inhibitory activity against IDO1 were evaluated (Table ). Consistent with previous reports, , synthetic exiguamines A and B potently inhibited the activity of IDO1 (entries 1 and 2). Comparable IC50 values were also observed for iso-exiguamines A and B, indicating the orientation of the indoloquinone moiety is not critical for potency (entries 3 and 4). Notably, both racemic and enantioenriched exiguamine B, (±)-1b and (+)-1b, exhibited nearly identical IC50 values (entry 2 vs. 5). In contrast, slightly enhanced potency was observed for epi-exiguamine B (36) (entry 6). The indolinium ring was not a requisite for the activity as indicated by the inhibitory profile of seco-exiguamine A (45) (entry 7). , Collectively, these data represent the most comprehensive SAR analysis of the fully elaborated exiguamines to date and should provide a valuable foundation for future biomedical studies and the development of therapeutic agents.

Conclusion

We have developed a concise and divergent strategy for the total synthesis of a range of natural and unnatural exiguamines. Central to this approach is a hydantoin linchpin strategy that enables regioselective union of the tryptamine- and dopamine-derived fragments, thereby setting the stage for the crucial C–C bond formation between the two six-membered rings. The core skeleton was constructed through a Claisen rearrangement/oxidative cyclization sequence, assembling the hexacyclic framework. This route provided access not only to exiguamines A and B but also to iso-exiguamines A and B and, through an asymmetric route from adrenaline, to optically active exiguamine B and epi-exiguamine B. In the asymmetric series, the spiro stereocenter is established through two iterations of a novel point-to-axial-to-point chirality transfer sequence. The expeditious access afforded by this strategy enabled comprehensive biochemical evaluation of this unique class of indoloquinone alkaloids, revealing potent IDO inhibitory activity. Moreover, despite lacking the quaternary ammonium ion, seco-exiguamine (45) retains nanomolar biological activity, suggesting that abrogation of this permanently charged functionality may provide an avenue for scaffold exploration, potentially with improved cell permeability and pharmacokinetic properties. Overall, this work establishes a robust platform for the preparation of exiguamines and for further biological and mechanistic investigation.

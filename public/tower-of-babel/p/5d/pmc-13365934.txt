IFPHPOS0045 Bridging the Gap Between National Guidance and Frontline Practice: A Study of Social Workers’ Implementation of NICE Guidelines on Self-Harm with Children in Care
Authors: Gill Lever, Christopher J Armitage, Dawn Dowding, Dharman Jeyasingham
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSelf-harm among children in care is substantially higher than in the general population. The NICE self-harm guidelines (2022) aim to improve the quality and consistency of care across all services, including children’s social care. A rapid review identified a lack of evidence on how national self-harm guidance is implemented within children’s social care.MethodThis study addresses this gap through a large-scale online survey of social workers supporting children in care who self-harm, examining guideline awareness, implementation, and behavioural determinants (capability, opportunity, motivation). A trauma-informed and neuro-affirmative approach informed all stages of design, data collection, and analysis to promote psychological safety and inclusion.ResultsNearly half of respondents (116/263; 48.29%) had never heard of the guidelines, and implementation with children in care who had self-harmed was low (mean = 18.59%, SD = 25.97). Analysis indicated low opportunity (physical opportunity: mean = 3.98, SD = 2.66; social opportunity: mean = 4.12, SD = 2.83), low–moderate automatic motivation (mean = 4.53, SD = 3.42), moderate reflexive motivation (mean = 6.09, SD = 2.53) and psychological capability (mean = 4.84, SD = 2.94), and high physical capability (mean = 8.01, SD = 2.48).DiscussionLow awareness and limited opportunity suggest that national guidance is not reaching or supporting frontline social workers in a meaningful way. Findings highlight substantial structural barriers to implementation, alongside variation in motivational and cognitive determinants across the workforce, indicating the need for both universal and targeted system-level implementation strategies.

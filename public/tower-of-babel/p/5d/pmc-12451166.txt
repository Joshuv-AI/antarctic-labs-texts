Research on the detecting method for cracks on perforated embryo eggs based on acoustic responses
Authors: Yuhan Fang, Mingyan Zhao, Haoke Chen, Hong Zhao, Duo Fu, Sheng Lin, Yanhong Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuring the vaccine production through the chick embryo cultivation method, harmful cracks may occur from the perforation of a trocar on the eggshell, around the impact hole, leading to the failure of cultivation. To detect the perforative cracks, this study proposes a method based on acoustic responses. By stimulating the embryo eggs and collecting the acoustic signals, 7 characteristic values were extracted from the time and the frequency domains: The maximum value in the time domain; The difference in the time domain; The frequency-domain peaks, 870 Hz, 1250 Hz, 1470 Hz and 1770 Hz; The mean value of the waveform. Statistical difference analysis found that all the above-mentioned characteristic values revealed differences. Then, the support vector machine and confusion matrix were combined, and the differentiation was achieved. In the statistical difference analysis, the p values of the characteristic values were all less than 0.01, indicating differences. The demarcation lines of all characteristic values obtained via binary classification through the support vector machine were as follows: TM:0.4114, TD:0.7528, FP870:5.716, FP1250:1.18, FP1470:2.96, FP1770:9.206, WM:0.6564. The weighted composite scores of the characteristic values obtained through the confusion matrix were as follows: TM: 99.18 %, TD: 99.16 %, FP870:90.56 %, FP1250:96.73 %, FP1470:98.32 %, FP1770:92.56 %, WM: 99.16 %. Experimental results demonstrated that this study could effectively distinguish qualified perforated embryo eggs from cracked ones, with the experimental prototype possessing the potential to be upgraded into an online detecting system, which comes with great significance in theory and practice for the development of the vaccine cultivation industry.Keywords: Acoustic response, Perforated embryo egg, Crack, Characteristic value, Support vector machine

Introduction

The chick embryo cultivation is an experimental method used to cultivate viruses and other microorganisms, whose background could be traced back to the late 19th century. Due to its low cost, high yield and simplicity in operation, the method remains an important tool for virus isolation, vaccine production and basic research to this day, especially irreplaceable in the manufacturing of the influenza vaccines (Yun Hee et al., 2021; Wang, 2022; Wang et al., 2023; Johnkennedy and Kingsley, 2022). The embryos eggs used in the chick embryo cultivation method are 10 to 11 days old after fertilization, acting as the cultivation medium, and the stock solution of viruses is injected into the allantoic cavity of the chick embryo to provide suitable cultivating conditions. After 2 to 3 days of refrigerated cultivation, the stock solution could be extracted from the allantoic cavity of the chick embryo (Sun, 2015). When automated equipment is applied to inject vaccines into chick embryos, the impact of the needle may cause cracks on the eggshell, leading to the immune failure, and even worse, the necrosis of the embryo egg (QI et al., 2019). Therefore, the detection of cracks on perforated embryo eggs is of crucial importance.The two categories of perforated embryo eggs are shown in Fig. 1.

Perforated embryo eggs.

Currently, the methods for detecting cracks on eggs mainly involve acoustics, vibrology (Xie and Xiong, 2016), vision (Elster and Goodrum, 1991; Chen and Liang, 2019), and detecting methods combining multiple technologies (Liu et al., 2010; Pan et al., 2010), etc. Foreign researchers used the technology of acoustic impulse response to analyze the acoustic spectrum produced by eggs at acoustic excitement, with an accuracy of 94 %. In China, considerable progress has also been achieved in recent years (Yu et al., 2020). Wang Ming (Wang et al., 2021) et al. proposed that the acoustic detection can be classified into the contact type and non-contact type. Moreover, nationwide, there are methods for processing data of acoustic signals utilizing the Support Vector Machine Model (SVM) and artificial neural networks (Wang et al., 2017; Qin et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2023; Cheng et al., 2019; Jin and Ying, 2014), as well as the acquisition and analysis of data combining the Labview virtual instrument platform (Ma et al., 2019; GUO et al., 2017). The way to stimulate eggs also comes in quantities. Zhang Shiqing (Zhang et al., 2017), Chen Cheng (Chen et al., 2020), etc. employed the method of striking with an incentive rod. Hao Lin et al. (2018) et al. struck eggshells with an vibration acceleration sensor; Haijun Wang and Mao (2016) et al. utilized an excitation device driven by a solenoid; Cheng Jin et al. (2015) et al. obtained acoustic signals by rolling eggs, but new cracks could occur during the rolling process. XU et al. (2018) et al. designed a detecting system for cracks on eggs based on TMS320F28335 and the results revealed that the second formant frequency of the intact egg’s spectrum was concentrated around 6890 Hz, while that of cracked eggs came with no obvious pattern. Li Sun (Sun et al., 2013; Sun et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2021) et al. proposed a detecting method for eggshell cracks based on acoustic resonance analysis, and experimental results showed that the crack detection level was 95.5 % with a significance level of 5 %. In addition, an equivalent mechanical model based on an automated excitation device, and an eggshell crack detecting system based on acoustic resonance were established, but the noise generated by the automated equipment would affect the experimental results. Kertész et al. (2021) et al. put forward a method for detecting eggshell cracks via acoustic spectroscopy, which combined such methods as the KNN, SVM, decision tree, LDA and QDA, to evaluate 693 audio recordings. The results indicated that the total classification error of the cross-validation was 2.1 %. Wei et al. (2014) et al. employed flexible piezoelectric thin-film sensors to perform the dynamic frequency response and detect the eggshell cracks, and through combining reverse propagation neural networks and genetic algorithms, intact and cracked eggs could be identified. Li et al. (2025) et al. collected the time signal of pulses during the strike on eggs using microphones and sound cards and then obtained the energy value of high-frequency coefficients and low-frequency coefficients through the method of multi-resolution wavelet decomposition. With the discriminant model established based on the Bayesian principle for judgment, the accuracy of detection reached more than 95 %. However, the above-mentioned studies all focused on the collision cracks arising from the collection, packaging and transportation processes of edible poultry eggs, and did not involve the detection of cracks on perforated embryo eggs.

This study conducted the detection of cracks on perforated embryo eggs through the acoustic response method. By comparing and analyzing acoustic wave signals at different frequencies, an effective identification and classification method for cracks was formed, which comes with important theoretical and practical significance for the vaccine cultivation industry.

The experimental equipment and operating theory

The composition of the detecting device for cracks on perforated embryo eggs

The experimental device is shown in Fig. 2, mainly consisting of an excitation module for eggshells and an acquisition module for acoustic signals. The schematic diagram of the excitation on the eggshell is shown in Fig. 3.

The detecting device for cracks on perforated embryo eggs.

The schematic diagram of the excitation on the eggshell.

The excitation module for eggshells

A 3D printed striking rod made of PLA is selected in the eggshell excitation module to reduce the noise, whose front end is wrapped with a rubber sleeve. The lifting angle of the striking rod is set at 45°, which can impact the embryo egg around the hinge point under the action of gravity. Since the sole location of the perforation on the eggshell is at the center of the blunt end, this study adopted the equatorial position of the embryo egg as the excitation point.

The acquisition module for acoustic signals

The acquisition module for acoustic signals includes an acoustic sensor (model: MAX9814), a lithium battery pack (5 V, 4000mAh), and a USB data acquisition instrument (model: USB3202N). The acoustic sensor is located 50 mm directly behind the excitation point. Some Settings of the acquisition instrument: The acquisition mode is set as the continuous sampling, the number of samples to be read is 5000, the sampling rate is 10000sps, and the trigger type is the analog edge.

The operating theory of the detecting device for cracks on perforated embryo eggs

Eggshell stimulation: After the perforated embryo egg is moved horizontally along the linear guide of the lead-screw rail to the appropriate position for excitation, where, at the strike, the rod is parallel to the central axis of the egg and tangent to the eggshell, raising the striking rod manually to the preset height, and then releasing it to stimulate the embryo egg.

Data acquisition: Acquiring the data through the acquisition module for acoustic signals, which would be presented as images in the DMC software.

Data processing and analysis: Using the Matlab and SPSSAU platforms on the computer to process and analyze the acquired acoustic signals.

Data processing and analysis of the acoustic signal

Data processing of the acoustic signal

Preprocessing of the experimental data

After the acoustic signal of 400 embryo eggs were collected, 200 original signals of qualified perforated embryo eggs and 200 of cracked ones were obtained. One signal was taken from each category respectively in turn, to form a group, resulting in a total of 200 groups.

Based on the original signal’s position of the main peak, and signal length, all data was cut into 30,000 sampling points, and then aggregated, and imported into the Matlab. The Hanning window was employed to perform data smoothing on the truncated signal. Afterwards, the Butterworth high-pass filter was applied to filter out the low-frequency ambient noise, and then the preprocessed image was obtained.

After an additional segment of ambient noise was acquired, the above operation was repeated, the obtained signal was transformed into a power spectrum chart to analyze the frequency distribution and energy size of the noise, and then, the filter parameters were adjusted through the feedback to achieve the best effect. The experimental environment in this study is a semi-anechoic laboratory, which approximates the actual environment of the workshop for the vaccine production through chick embryos. After multiple experiments, it was finally determined that the cut-off frequency of the filter is 100 Hz and the order is 6, which could filter out the noise effectively, as shown in Fig. 5. The filter with the determined parameters was then re-applied to the preprocessing to obtain the final image, as shown in Fig. 6.

Frequency-domain analysis

The signal spectrum represents the components and weights of the signal at different frequencies, and many acoustic signals, which are not obvious in the time domain, can be well presented in the frequency domain. Performing the frequency-domain analysis of the signal could provide more intuitive and comprehensive information than the waveform of the time domain. Through the FFT algorithm, the acoustic signal could be converted from the time domain to the frequency domain, and Fig. 10 shows the comparison between the two categories of embryo eggs in the frequency domain.

Comparison of the frequency-domain signals.

Experiments revealed that within the signal ranges of 820 Hz to 920 Hz, 1200 Hz to 1300 Hz, 1420 Hz to 1520 Hz, and 1720 Hz to 1820 Hz in the frequency domain, the peak values of qualified perforated embryo eggs and cracked ones differ at frequencies of approximately 870 Hz, 1250 Hz, 1470 Hz, and 1770 Hz, among which the peak of the qualified eggs was lower, with that of the cracked ones coming higher. Within the frequency range of 820 Hz to 1200 Hz, the waveform of qualified perforated embryo eggs was relatively gentle, while that of the cracked ones went relatively steep.

The peak values at the above four frequency ranges, as well as the mean value of the waveform in the frequency band, were extracted respectively to serve as the characteristic values in the frequency domain, as shown in Fig. 11. It demonstrated that all these five characteristic values came with favorable discrimination.

Characteristic values of the frequency domain.

The frequency-domain analysis extracted five characteristic values as follows: The frequency-domain peak, 870 Hz (FP870); The frequency-domain peak, 1250 Hz (FP1250); The frequency-domain peak, 1470 Hz (FP1470); The frequency-domain peak, 1770 Hz (FP1770); The mean value of the waveform (WM).

Analysis of classification situations through the confusion matrix

In machine learning and statistics, to evaluate the performance of a classification model, a confusion matrix is commonly employed, which presents a comparison between the actual labels and the predicted results of the model in a matrix form, helping to understand the model's performance in different categories. In the binary classification through the confusion matrix, the following parts are included:

True Positive (TP): The model correctly predicts samples that are actually in the positive category as the positive category.

False Negative (FN): The model incorrectly predicts samples that are actually in the positive category as the negative category.

False Positive (FP): The model incorrectly predicts samples that are actually in the negative category as the positive category.

True Negative (TN): The model correctly predicts samples that are actually in the negative category as the negative category.

With the qualified perforated embryo eggs considered as the positive category, and the cracked ones as the negative category, the confusion matrices of the 7 characteristic values could be obtained, as shown in Fig. 21.

The confusion matrices of the 7 characteristic values.

The following performance indicators could be further obtained through the confusion matrix:

Accuracy: The proportion of samples, correctly classified by the model, among the total samples.

Precision: The proportion of samples, truly in the positive category, among those predicted as the positive category by the model.

Recall: The proportion of samples, correctly predicted as the positive category by the model, among those actually in the positive category.

F1-Score: The harmonic mean of the precision and recall, which combines the performance of the two.The higher the value, the better the performance.

Weighted Composite Score: The weighted composite score is calculated to assign weights to the Accuracy, precision, Recall, and F1-Score. This study needed to take into account both the missed detection and the misjudgment of the samples, so the weight distribution was as follows: Accuracy: 0.10, Precision: 0.25, Recall rate: 0.25, F1-Score: 0.40.

Industrial value analysis

In the conventional vision-based detection, due to the tiny scale of the crack, an additional secondary light source needs to be added at the bottom of the embryo egg to enhance the contrast between the crack and the background. However, under the illumination of the light source, embryos in different development status would form various shapes of shadows on the eggshell because of the unevenness in the light transmission, and the pores on the eggshell would also lead to local dark speckles, all of which would affect the detection results. This study utilized the method based on the acoustic response, effectively avoiding the above problems.

In terms of the detection cost, the price of the industrial cameras employed in the vision-based detection usually varies between 2,000 and 5,000 yuan. In addition, there are also maintenance costs for the light source, lens and other components. This study uses a USB3202N data acquisition device at a cost of 1,500 yuan, which possesses 8 channels and could conduct batch acquisition. The detection cost of a single egg through this method decreases by approximately 90 % compared with that of the visual detection, and the detection speed has increased from 3 seconds per egg to 0.375 seconds.

Furthermore, the core structure of the prototype is relatively small, and when upgraded to a multi-station synchronous detecting system, it would come with a compact structure and occupy a small amount of space. Considering the detection speed and cost, this device could be further developed into an online detecting system.

Conclusion

In the statistical difference analysis, the 7 characteristic values were sorted from high to low according to the absolute value of the z value in the Mann-Whitney test statistics: TD, FP1470, WM, TM, FP1250, FP870, FP1770. The p values were all less than 0.01.

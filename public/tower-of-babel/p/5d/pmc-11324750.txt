Development and characteristic research on new thermal insulation and anti-permeability grouting material for tunnels in cold regions
Authors: Sihui Dong, Xinyu Zhang, Qinde Yang, Wuju Xun, Jianbo Zhao, Kang Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe surrounding rock of tunnels in cold regions are susceptible to the freeze–thaw cycle resulting from the combination of low temperatures and moisture during tunnel service. The phenomenon will not only lead to the expansion of pores and fissures in the surrounding rock of the initial tunnel, but also destroy the integrity of the rock. This destruction will have a serious impact on tunnel structure and rail transit operation safety. At present, the commonly used thermal insulation measures have some problems such as maintenance difficulties, low economic efficiency, and safety hazards. Therefore, it is urgent to develop a kind of tunnel maintenance grouting material with insulation and anti-permeability, which has the characteristics of simple operation, easy preparation and application. We independently developed a composite grouting material composed of polyurethane (PU), epoxy resin (E-51) and acrylic powder (PMMA). Through the material combustion test, magnesium hydroxide was selected as the flame retardant additive. Moreover, we measured the thermal conductivity, water absorption, apparent density, porosity and strength characteristic parameters. The thermal insulation and anti-permeability characteristics of the composites were also analyzed. The results indicated that the thermal conductivity of the new composite grouting material is 6.3% lower than the PU before adding flame retardant. Compared with the PU with flame retardant, the water absorption decreased by 74.4% and the ultimate strength increased by 33.3%. For the area with an average temperature lower than − 10 °C, we recommend the ratio scheme of E-51: 3%; PMMA: 15%. For the area with high water content in the surrounding rock of the tunnel, we recommend the ratio scheme of E-51: 15%; PMMA: 3%. This study provides new ideas for material preparation and tunnel insulation methods for anti-freezing measures in tunnels during their operational period in cold regions.Keywords: Cold regions, Tunnel during service period, Thermal insulation, Anti-permeability, Compressive strength, Grouting materialSubject terms: Materials chemistry, Materials science

Introduction

With the rapid development of railway construction, the volume of tunnel construction projects has grown rapidly. The operational mileage of railways in China has exceeded 150,000 km, with 17,532 tunnels in service, totaling 21,055 km in length by the end of 20211. Due to the differences in geological and meteorological conditions across different latitudes, the service life and damage conditions of operational tunnels vary. The problem of freezing damage in tunnels is a prominent concern that can lead tunnels in cold regions to damage or even the cessation of service. According to the survey, 42.3% of the operational tunnels in high-latitude cold regions are affected by freezing damage2. The formation of freezing damage in tunnels is primarily influenced by temperature and moisture content. The surrounding rock of tunnels naturally has micro-pores and micro-cracks3. When there is rainfall or underground water, a certain amount of crack water will be retained in the pores. Once the temperature drops below 0 ℃, the crack water will freeze. The volume expansion of water turning into ice leads to the expansion of rock cracks. The integrity of the rock structure is destroyed. After the temperature rises, the melting of ice will lead to an increase in the moisture content of the surrounding rock. The repeated freeze-thaw cycle causes the expansion of internal cracks in the rock, damaging the surrounding rock structure4. Crack water seeps from the lining gaps, leading to various freezing damage problems, such as ice column invasion. The problem of freezing damage seriously affects the tunnel structure and operation safety.

Nowadays, the insulation and anti-freeze measures for tunnels in service in cold regions are mainly divided into active insulation measures and passive insulation measures5. Active insulation measures include adding electric heaters5; heating cables6; geothermal heating7 for tunnels; installing heating systems8 and sunshine sheds9 at the entrance. Passive measures are a widely adopted and extensively researched method for insulation and antifreeze in tunnels located in cold regions. It includes installing insulation materials on the tunnel lining surface or between the primary and secondary linings10; installing cold-proof insulation doors at the tunnel entrance11; adding insulation and drainage facilities; and constructing cold-proof drainage tunnels12. The active insulation measures have a good anti-freezing effect, but they come with challenges such as high energy consumption, poor cost-effectiveness, and difficulties in maintenance after problems. Passive insulation measures have good cost-effectiveness, offer effective anti-freeze properties with low energy consumption. However, they are highly susceptible to damage from vehicles or human interference, existing potential safety hazards. Active and passive insulation measures have addressed the majority of tunnel freezing problems, but shortcomings still exist. In cold-region tunnels, freezing problems persist, greatly impacting the tunnel structure and operational safety, reducing the tunnel's service life. The measure of grouting the surrounding rock of a tunnel not only addresses problems such as high energy consumption and poor cost-effectiveness, but also allows for the development of new grouting materials, endowing them with insulation and anti-permeability capabilities. Insulating grouting carried out based on different sections and varying degrees of freezing damage in the tunnel can achieve targeted insulation and anti-freeze effects.

In the study of thermal insulation performance, PU, as a widely used material, has been proven to have excellent thermal insulation effects. For example, Chen found that materials with low thermal conductivity can effectively provide insulation for tunnels. He measured the thermal conductivity of insulation materials by Transient Plane Source (TPS) method and simulated the tunnel with added insulation materials. The results indicate that the thermal conductivity of polymers initially increases and then decreases with temperature rise. The surrounding rock temperature increases with the increase in polymer thermal conductivity and thickness. Polymers exhibit a good insulation effect on tunnels13. Liu and Sheng found that polystyrene foam (EPS) and PU can be used as thermal insulation materials for tunnels. Liu conducted a two-dimensional numerical simulation of the soil layer. The results indicate that EPS and PU as insulation materials can greatly reduce the degradation of the underground permafrost layer. Through temperature observations on the Kunlun Mountain insulation test roadbed, Sheng found that the insulation layer can significantly reduce the annual temperature range of the ground14,15. Wang and Cui proposed a novel composite insulation lining structure to protect the lining of tunnels in cold regions from freezing damage. Wang applied numerical simulations to validate that the new composite insulation lining has better mechanical performance and insulation effectiveness compared to conventional steel plate insulation lining. The new composite insulation lining can inhibit the formation of ice on the outer surface of the lining, effectively preventing freezing damage. Cui designed a new type of insulation lining that is placed away from the tunnel wall. The insulation lining not only prevents the detachment and seepage of the lining in cold region tunnels, but also extends the durability and effectiveness of the insulation material. The main materials for both types of new insulation linings are PU16,17. Kuźnia discovered that, although PU has a low thermal conductivity, its pore structure is relatively large. PU has poor anti-permeability and poor mechanical properties. Therefore, the author used flyash as the filler of PU, effectively improving the mechanical properties and thermal insulation properties of PU18.

To solve the problems of low strength and poor anti-permeability of PU, epoxy resin (E-51) has entered the public eye with excellent mechanical properties such as compressive strength and bond strength. When mixed with other materials, E-51 can improve the mechanical properties of the material. For example, Ahmed found that the hardness, tensile strength, compressive strength, permeability, and ultimate impact strength of the binary polymer mixture composed of epoxy resin, polyurethane, and rubber all showed a certain degree of improvement, and the material exhibited high toughness19. Chen used continuous physical and polymerization techniques to prepare graphene oxide/PU/epoxy resin nanocomposites with varying amounts of graphene oxide. His analysis revealed a significant improvement in the fracture elongation and toughness of the composite material, along with enhanced damping properties20. Mei analyzed PU grouting materials modified with water glass and epoxy resin. The study investigated the effects of epoxy resin and water glass on the compressive strength, expansion rate, bubble structure, and maximum reaction temperature of the grouting material. He found that the modified PU had smaller and more regular cell sizes, fewer pores, and exhibited a certain degree of improvement in both compressive strength and expansion rate21. Rama's research found that adding granite powder could enhance the density, porosity, and other indicators of the composite epoxy resin22. Wu utilized flyash to enhance the matrix of PU-modified epoxy resin and observed a decrease in the porosity of the composite material, accompanied by an improvement in mechanical properties. This study indicates that powdered materials can effectively enhance the pore structure of materials, making them more compact and improving overall performance23. Therefore, E-51 and acrylic powder (PMMA) are chosen as the primary components for improving the anti-permeability and strength performance of PU grouting materials.

In summary, although polyurethane exhibits good thermal insulation, its large pores result in poor mechanical and anti-permeability properties. E-51 possesses excellent mechanical properties and can prevent moisture from entering the material but lacks thermal insulation performance. PMMA can effectively fill voids, enhance the material's mechanical and anti-permeability properties. In this study, we formulated a new type of grouting composite material with thermal insulation and anti-permeability properties by designing the proportions of E-51, PMMA, and PU. We measured performance parameters such as thermal conductivity, compressive strength, water absorption rate, apparent density, and compressive strength of the composite material, studying its thermal insulation and anti-permeability properties. This study aims to provide new ideas for the preparation of grouting materials and frost prevention measures for tunnels in cold regions.

Sample preparation

The preparation of PU generally involves three main methods: (1) the prepolymer method involves the initial reaction between polyether polyol and isocyanate to form a prepolymer. Subsequently, the prepolymer is mixed with catalysts, blowing agents, foam stabilizers, and other additives, and then foamed to produce rigid PU foam plastics. (2) The semi-prepolymer method involves the initial reaction of polyether polyol with an excess amount of isocyanate, forming an intermediate prepolymer with isocyanate end groups. Subsequently, this prepolymer intermediate is mixed with polyether polyol, catalysts, blowing agents, foam stabilizers, and other additives, and then foamed to produce rigid PU foam plastics. (3) The one-step method involves the simultaneous addition of polyether polyol, isocyanate, blowing agents, catalysts, foam stabilizers, flame retardants, etc., followed by mixing and foaming to generate rigid PU foam plastics. The prepolymer method has a single reaction step, making foam control easier, but its complex process and high viscosity limit its practicality in production. The semi-prepolymer method has relatively easy processing operations, but the preparation conditions are complex, and there are challenges in the storage and preparation of the prepolymer. The one-step method has a simple process, convenient operation, and low cost25. Therefore, the article adopts the one-step method to prepare rigid PU foam plastics, and the filling material is added during the PU preparation process.

We have conducted research on PU preparation, determining the quality proportions of the raw materials in PU, apart from polyether polyol (LY-4110) and diphenylmethane diisocyanate (MDI), and the formulation process. At room temperature, weigh LY-4110, silicone oil (AK8805), triethylenediamine (C6H12N2), dibutyltin dilaurate (T-12), distilled water (dH2O), and the remaining filling materials according to the formula into a 1000 mL plastic beaker. Stir at high speed (800 r/min) using a multi-functional electric stirrer for 12 min, then add n-hexane (C6H14) and stir at high speed (800 r/min) for 2 min. Subsequently, add MDI and stir at high speed (1500 r/min) for 10 s. Quickly pour the resulting mixture into prepared molds and cure in a 60 °C oven for 24 h. Finally, cut the specimens according to the desired specifications and measure the parameters of the specimens. The sample preparation process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Results and analysis

Heat preservation properties

The thermal conductivity of grouting materials exhibits different insulation performances due to the influence of varying component ratios. Pore size is a crucial factor impacting material insulation, but direct measurement of small pore diameters can result in significant errors. The numerical density of a material can more intuitively reflect its compactness, offering a rough estimation of the extent of pore size.

From Fig. 3, it is evident that when the LY-4110 content is 35%, and MDI content is 55%, the thermal conductivity is minimized, and the density is 42.48 kg/m3. The significant increase in the fifth set of data is attributed to incomplete reaction between LY-4110 and MDI, preventing the full utilization of their respective advantages. Excluding the fifth set of data, a 10% change in PU ratio results in an approximately 8% variation in thermal conductivity. A noticeable inflection point appears in the second set of data, and at this point, the density is minimized. We attribute this phenomenon to the complete reaction between LY-4110 and MDI, forming a uniformly dense PU foam, thereby endowing it with excellent insulation properties. This result aligns with the research on PU formulation ratios conducted by Yin35.

Thermal conductivity of PU with different proportions of components.

Mg(OH)2 is a finely powdered material that can effectively fill the pore structure of PU, improving the insulation performance of the grouting material. From Fig. 4, it is evident that the thermal conductivity of PU with added E-51 and PU with added E-51, PMMA both show an increasing trend. With the increase in E-51 content, the thermal conductivity of the grouting material gradually decreases. An inflection point occurs when the E-51 content is 11%, at which point the thermal conductivity of the grouting material is 0.0353 W/(m K). Without considering the first set of data, adding 11% E-51 reduces the thermal conductivity of the grouting material by about 10%, compared to a 5% reduction in thermal conductivity of PU before adding the flame retardant.

Effect of different proportions of additives on the thermal conductivity of PU.

When E-51 and PMMA are added to PU, the overall thermal conductivity of the grouting material decreases. Disregarding the first set of data, when the PU contains 3% E-51 and 15% PMMA, it exhibits better insulation properties. At this point, the thermal conductivity decreases by about 1% compared to 0.0353 W/(m K). We consider that E-51, being an adhesive material, disrupts some of the pore structures of PU when reacting with it, causing an enlargement of certain pore diameters. This leads to an increase in the thermal conductivity of the grouting material, consequently a decline in its insulation performance. When E-51 is added separately to PU, the initial thermal conductivity decreases. This circumstance occurs because the E-51 content is not high enough to offset its advantage in preventing temperature leakage into or out of the material. The overall decrease in thermal conductivity of the grouting material is attributed to PMMA entering the pore structure and filling some voids. This action effectively reduces the pore diameter of PU, enhancing the insulation performance of the material.

Impermeability

To address issues such as lining damage caused by the infiltration of water into rock pores, we aim for the new grouting material to possess excellent anti-permeability. For materials with small and interconnected pores, their mass water absorption rate is often substantial, sometimes exceeding 100%. In these cases, the volumetric water absorption rate is used to indicate the water absorption situation. However, materials with predominantly closed pores, where water is less likely to penetrate large and interconnected pores, use the mass water absorption rate to express their water absorption characteristics. The mass water absorption rate represents the ratio of the mass of water contained in the material to the mass of the material in its dry state, also known as the material's moisture content36,37. From the above description, a lower mass water absorption rate indicates a lower moisture content in the material, implying a higher closed-cell ratio. PU, as observed under a microscope, predominantly exhibits closed-cell pores. Therefore, the mass water absorption rate is used to express its water absorption characteristics, reflecting its anti-permeability. As the E-51 content increases, the water absorption rate of the grouting material decreases. A turning point is evident at the fourth set of data, which still aligns with the previously explained mechanism for the trend in thermal conductivity changes. When the E-51 content is low, it effectively prevents moisture from entering the pores. With an increase in the proportion of E-51, its ability to disrupt the PU pore structure enhances, transforming the originally closed-cell structure of PU into an open-cell structure, leading to an increase in the water absorption rate. When the E-51 content is 11%, the water absorption rate of the grouting material is 1.37%, representing a reduction of approximately 72% compared to the water absorption rate of the grouting material without E-51.

Variation trend of water absorption rate of PU with different substances added.

The water absorption rate of the grouting material, with the addition of both E-51 and PMMA, gradually decreases without a distinct turning point. The initial decrease in the graph is rapid, followed by a gradual stabilization. This is primarily attributed to the role of E-51 in preventing moisture from entering the pore structure, effectively leveraging its advantages. Simultaneously, PMMA efficiently fills the open-cell structure, further impeding water penetration into the pores and enhancing the impermeability of the grouting material. Observing the two curves, the performance of the grouting material with the addition of E-51 and PMMA exhibits a relatively stable change without abrupt or drastic variations. This indicates a complementary synergy between the two materials, showcasing their mutual advantages and contributing to the improvement of the grouting material's performance indicators. When the E-51 content is 15% and PMMA content is 3%, the water absorption rate of the grouting material is 1.29%. Compared to PU with only a flame retardant, the water absorption rate is reduced by approximately 74%. Additionally, compared to the grouting material with both a flame retardant and E-51, the water absorption rate decreases by around 6%. Excluding the first set of data, for every 4% increase in E-51 content and every 4% decrease in PMMA content, the water absorption rate of the grouting material decreases by approximately 22%.

The lower the numerical value of the water absorption rate, the lower the moisture content in the material. This indicates that moisture is less likely to penetrate the material, or if it does, it doesn't linger. This indirectly implies that a lower moisture content corresponds to a higher closed-cell content in the material. Consequently, the material's anti-permeability performance is more outstanding when the moisture content is lower.

Compressive strength characteristics

In the study of thermal insulation and anti-permeability of PU, the addition of E-51 and PMMA has been found to enhance the thermal insulation and anti-permeability properties of PU. Additionally, the additive significantly influences the compressive strength of PU. The standard specification requires a compressive strength of thermal insulation boards to be ≥ 100 kPa38. However, the grouting material investigated in this study is intended for use between rocks in the surrounding rock of tunnels, where it is not subjected to substantial compression. Therefore, the compressive strength requirements can be appropriately reduced. We conducted tests for the 10% deformation compressive strength of materials with different compositions, adjusting the universal testing machine speed to 0.3 mm/min, with a maximum compression deformation of 4.5 mm. This testing method assesses the material's compressive resistance under a certain level of deformation, providing useful insights into its performance in practical applications.

The composition of PU typically comprises two segments: the hard segment and the soft segment. The soft segment refers to the long-chain alcohol groups within the PU molecular chain, often composed of alcohol-based compounds like polyether polyols. The soft segment primarily influences the flexibility and elongation of PU. On the other hand, the hard segment refers to the diisocyanate groups within the isocyanate molecular chain, predominantly impacting the hardness, brittleness, and tightly linear structure of PU39. Discussions

To address the problem of freezing damage in tunnels during their service life in cold regions, this study proposes a novel composite grouting material with insulation and anti-permeability properties. The preparation of this composite material is simple, it is not easily damaged, and can be re-grouted after damage. Depending on the extent of freezing damage in different sections of the tunnel, targeted insulation can be applied. The paper discusses the composition and preparation methods of the composite material, selecting PU with good insulation effects as the base material. To improve problems such as large pores and poor anti-permeability in PU, E-51 and PMMA are chosen as additives. The performance indicators of the composite material are further discussed. The thermal conductivity of the new grouting material is 0.0349 W/(m K), although higher than the thermal conductivity of existing PU on the market (0.025 W/(m K))40, it is lower than the thermal conductivity of PU used in this experiment (0.03725 W/(m K)), and far lower than the thermal conductivity of commonly used cement-sand grouting materials (0.9 W/(m K))41. The mass water absorption rate of the new grouting material is 1.29%, which is lower than the water absorption rate of cement-sand grout (10%)42, indicating good anti-permeability. Although the compressive strength of the new grouting material meets standard requirements, it is weaker than the compressive strength of PU (30 MPa)43, and this aspect requires further exploration. Additionally, the combustion rating of this composite material only reaches B2 level, and further research is needed to enhance its flame retardant performance. The environmental friendliness of the grouting material is not mentioned in this study, but to meet the requirements of green and sustainable development, a thorough investigation of its environmental impact is necessary.

In engineering applications, different ratios of polyurethane materials can be selected based on varying geological conditions. For example:

In certain regions where winter temperatures are low, with significant temperature variations, but the tunnels have low water content, for example, Mohe City in Heilongjiang Province, China. Mohe has extremely low temperatures, dropping below − 50 °C, with an annual temperature difference of around 80 °C. Precipitation is scarce, leading to a dry and arid environment. In such conditions, we recommend a formulation with E-51: 3% and PMMA: 15%. This ratio results in a thermal conductivity of 0.0349 W/(m K), a water absorption rate of 2.64%, and a compressive strength of 66.3 kPa.

Conclusion

The problem of freezing damage in tunnels has long been a concern for the operation and structural safety of in-service tunnels in the cold regions. To address the freezing damage problem at its source and eliminate the drawbacks of existing insulation measures, this paper independently developed a composite grouting material formed by a mixture of PU, E-51, and PMMA. We conducted measurements on the thermal conductivity, water absorption rate, apparent density, porosity, and strength characteristic parameters of the grouting material, analyzing the insulation and anti-permeability properties of the composite material. The conclusions drawn from the study are as follows:

When LY-4110: 35% and MDI: 55%, the thermal conductivity is the lowest in this experimental group. As the LY-4110 content increases, the water absorption of PU gradually decreases. With a decrease in MDI content, the compressive strength of PU gradually diminishes. After comprehensive analysis of all performance metrics, we have selected LY-4110: 35%, MDI: 55%, and other additives: 10% as the optimal formulation for polyurethane.

The mixture of E-51 and PMMA effectively improves various properties of PU. E-51: 3%; PMMA: 15% reduces the thermal conductivity by 6.3%. E-51: 15%; PMMA: 15% decreases the water absorption rate by 74.4%. E-51: 11%; PMMA: 7% increases the compressive strength by 33.3%.

This study provides a preparation method for grouting materials and a freezing prevention approach for insulation in in-service tunnels located in cold regions. The research outcomes can serve as a reference for selecting grouting materials for the insulation of surrounding rock in in-service tunnels within cold regions. However, the fire rating of this composite grouting material only reaches B2 level. If applied for flame retardancy, further research is needed to enhance its flame retardant properties. Additionally, this study did not consider the economic aspects of the grouting material, nor did it address its environmental impact. To meet the requirements of green and sustainable development, further exploration of the material is warranted. The research of manuscript is still in the experimental stage and has not been applied to engineering practice. It only provides reference for the selection of grouting materials for projects with different geological conditions. The follow-up research will focus on the application of materials to engineering, and observe its experimental effect in detail, and constantly improve.

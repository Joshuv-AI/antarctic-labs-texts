Structural characterization, functional properties, bioactivity and oral absorption characterstics of Oudemansiella raphanipies polysaccharides
Authors: Jingbo Zhang, Yajing Bai, Kunpeng Lu, Ying Sun, Junyi Li, ZhuqianWang, Chengguang He, Lili Guan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The medicinal fungus Oudemansiella raphanipies (O. raphanipies) attracts significant interest for its broad-spectrum bioactivities, with its polysaccharide demonstrating particularly notable pharmacological properties. This study systematically investigated the extraction, physicochemical properties, structural, functional traits, biological activities, and oral absorption of O. raphanipies polysaccharide. Response surface methodology optimized the extraction condition of O. raphanipie polysaccharides by UAE method, achieving a yield of 210.35 ± 0.20 mg/g (55 °C, 90 min, 250 W, 1: 60 g/mL). The purified O. raphanipie polysaccharide (UAE-ORP) (568.57 kDa), an α-pyranose mainly composed of glucose (35.48%) and galactose (28.51%), showed high thermal stability (322 °C). In addition, UAE-ORP showed interesting water retention capacity and oil retention capacity, and good foaming and emulsion properties. UAE-ORP demonstrated potent antioxidant activity, with DPPH scavenging rate reaching 90.43% and hydroxyl radical scavenging rate at 57.13%, significant inhibition of BSA denaturation (97.49%) and NO secretion, and potential prebiotic effects. UAE-ORP was significantly distributed in the gastrointestinal tract based on NIR imaging result, retaining in intestines for 24 h, indicating its potential as a prebiotic agent. Thus, UAE-ORP is a promising functional food additive with integrated advantages.

Introduction

Oudemansiella raphanipies (O. raphanipies) also called “Edible Queen”, is one of China's four famous fungi, renowned as the “crown of fungi” for its crisp and tender texture and delicious taste,classified under the phylum Basidiomycota, class Agaricomycetes, order Agaricales, family Tricholomataceae, and genus Oudemansiella (Miao, Dong, Liu, & Qiu, 2023). According to statistical data from the China Edible Fungi Association, the yield of O. raphanipies has gradually increased in recent years, reaching 314,000 tons in 2020. O. raphanipies contains high levels of umami amino acids, high protein and low fat, and various classes of bioactive components, such as polysaccharides, fat, vitamins, and trace elements (Jiang et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2020). O. raphanipies possesses various bioactivities, including anti-oxidant (Jiang et al., 2015), anti-inflammatory (Gao et al., 2018), anti-bacterial (Jiang et al., 2022), lung protection (Gao et al., 2017), hepatoprotective effects (Liu, Zhu, Geng, Wang, & Ng, 2017).

Currently, O. raphanipies polysaccharide (ORP) has been widely studied and is the main bioactive compounds of O. raphanipies, possessing numerous significant pharmacological properties, such as anti-tumor (Gao et al., 2017; Jiang et al., 2022), anti-fungal (Liu et al., 2020), anti-diabetic (Chen et al., 2020), anti-viral (He et al., 2020), and anti-bacterial (Qin, Guo, Wang, Tian, & Sun, 2018).

The bioactivity of polysaccharide is influenced by molecular weight, structure, water solubility, etc. (Guo, Wang, Wu, & Yu, 2025; Zhang, Duan, Huang, & Huang, 2023), which may be due to the extraction methods. At present, the extraction methods for polysaccharides from edible mushrooms mainly include hot water extraction (HWE) (Yun, Li, Yang, & Zhang, 2019), microwave-assisted extraction (MAE) (Jia, Du, Li, & Li, 2022), ultrasonic-assisted extraction (UAE) (Wang et al., 2021), enzyme-assisted extraction (EAE) (Chen, Zhou, & Yang, 2023) and hot alkali extraction (HAE) (Khatua, Chandra, & Acharya, 2021). The UAE method, known for its green, safe, and efficient nature, has been widely adopted across various food processing units, particularly in extraction (Chemat et al., 2017; Huang et al., 2025). Compared to traditional chemical and biological methods, UAE method can destroy plant cell walls through cavitation and mechanical action, allowing polysaccharides to dissolve quickly, thereby improving their yields (Chemat, Zill-e-Huma, & Khan, 2011; Huang et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2024) and exhibits the advantages of low energy consumption, low cost, short time, and high efficiency (Alzorqi, Ketabchi, Sudheer, & Manickam, 2016). The bioactivity of polysaccharides can be proved improved after the ultrasonication (Li et al., 2022). Hence, UAE is a promising method to increase the yield and activity of polysaccharides and a beneficial green method to extract polysaccharides in pharmaceutical and food processing.

Currently, Xu et al. optimized the UAE conditions for O. raphanipies polysaccharides was optimized via single-factor and orthogonal experiments, achieving a 7.9% extraction yield under the optimal parameters: 1: 40 (g/mL) material-to-liquid ratio, 40 min extraction time, 85 °C temperature, and 160 W ultrasonic power (Xu, Zheng, Shi, Zhang, & Ji, 2017). The prior optimization by Xu et al. relied on single-factor and orthogonal experiments primarily evaluated the individual effects of variables or limited interactions between factors. However, the optimal extraction condition was still further studied. Response Surface Methodology (RSM) is a statistical tool that describes a process by evaluation of the interactive effects of independent variables on process parameters (Box & Wilson, 1951). RSM has been extensively validated to refine polysaccharide extraction conditions more precisely than traditional methods, as it can identify synergistic or antagonistic relationships between variables (e.g., interactions between temperature and ultrasonic power) that single-factor/orthogonal experiments may overlook (Liu et al., 2022; Mohammed, Rivers, Stuckey, & Ward, 2020; Yang et al., 2024). Hence, the extraction optimization of O. raphanipies polysaccharides by RSM method was necessary for enhancing the extraction yield. Furthermore, the work scope of Xu et al. was limited to extraction condition optimization, with no investigation into the purification, structural characterization, functional properties, or bioactivities of O. raphanipies polysaccharides.

Herein, the process optimization with UAE method of crude O. raphanipies polysaccharide (cUAE-ORP) was conducted by RSM. UAE-ORP was obtained after purification on the Sephadex G-75 gel filtration column. Molecular weight (Mw), monosaccharide composition, functional group changes of UAE-ORP were also evaluated. Furthermore, the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity in vitro, prebiotic activity and oral absorption characteristic were also preliminary clarified. This study offers novel perspectives on assessing the functional constituents of natural polysaccharides from O. raphanipies, holding significant potential for the applications in functional food and pharmaceutical industry.

Materials and methods

Materials

O. raphanipes were purchased from Changchun agricultural market and authenticated by Professor Haiying Bao from Jilin Agricultural University. The O. raphanipes powder was obtained by an FW-400 A grinder provided by Zhongxing Weiye Instrument Co., Ltd. (‌Beijing‌, China), sieved through an 80-mesh sieve and then sealed in the polypropylene bags at −20 °C for further study. Two LAB strains, Lactiplantibacillus Rhamnosus (L. rhamnosus) LR1 and Lactiplantibacillus Plantarum (L. plantarum) LP1 were stored in our laboratory. Hydroxybenzene, ABTS radical scavenging (Li et al., 2022), DPPH radical scavenging activity, and Ascorbic acid (Vc) were supported by Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). FOS was supported by Beijing Solarbio Life Sciences Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Fucose (Fuc), Rhamnose (Rha), Arabinose (Ara), Galactose (Gal), Glucose (Glc), Xylose (Xyl), Mannose (Man), Galacturonic Acid (Gal-UA) and Glucuronic Acid (Glc-UA) were supported by Sigma-Aldrich (Shanghai) Trading Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Cyanine 5.5-NHS ester (Cy5.5-NHS) was purchased from ApexBio Technology LLC (Houston, US). All other reagents and drugs were of analytical grade.

Polysaccharides were extracted with the optimal extraction conditions and then precipitated with anhydrous ethanol at a ratio of 3: 1 at 4 °C for 24 h. Sevag reagent (chloroform: n-butanol = 4: 1) was introduced in a ratio of 1: 4 to remove the protein. The solution was subjected to freeze-drying following a 4 d of dialysis to yield cUAE-ORP. cUAE-ORP was purified with ultrapure water at a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min on Sephadex G-75 gel filtration column (16 mm × 80 cm). The polysaccharide content of the corresponding eluates (3 mL/tube) was detected by the phenol sulfuric acid method (Li et al., 2021). The eluates were concentrated and lyophilized, and labeled as UAE-ORP.

Physicochemical properties determination of UAE-ORP

Glucose was selected as the standard, the polysaccharide content of UAE-ORP was quantified through the phenol sulfuric acid method (Li et al., 2021). The reducing sugar content of UAE-ORP was detected by the DNS method (Cui, 2019). The total protein content of UAE-ORP was analyzed based on the Coomassie Brilliant Blue method (Gao, Li, Wang, & Qiao, 2020), and the glucuronic acid content of UAE-ORP was detected with the galactonic acid method (Bradford, 1976).

UAE-ORP solution (1 mg/mL) was prepared to examine the particle size, polydispersity index (PDI) and Zeta potential on a laser particle size analyzer (NANO ZS90, Malvern, UK).

Determination of structural properties of UAE-ORP

Monosaccharide composition of UAE-ORP

The monosaccharide fractions of UAE-ORP was analyzed on a Thermo ICS 5000+ ion chromatography system (ICS 5000+, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with an electrochemical detector fitted with a Dionex™ CarboPac™ PA20 liquid chromatography column (150 × 3.0 mm, 10 μm) according to the previous method (Li et al., 2022b).

Mw determination of UAE-ORP

The Mw of UAE-ORP was determined by high performance gel permeation chromatography (HPGPC-ELSD) assembled with Agilent 1260 Infinity ELSD evaporative light scattering detector and TSK-gel G-3000PWXL chromatographic column (7.8 × 300 nm) in accordance with the prior method (Yu, Luo, & Zhang, 2013).

Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) assay of UAE-ORP

UAE-ORP and potassium bromide powder were pressed into tablets for FT-IR analysis by Nexus 470 FT-IR spectrometer (Equinx55, Bruce, Germany) (Chen et al., 2018).

Congo red assay

According to the previous report by Hu et al., (Hu et al., 2024), Congo red assay was conducted. Specifically, 1 mL of each UAE-ORP solution (2 mg/mL) was mixed with 1 mL of Congo Red solution (200 μg/mL). Subsequently, varying volumes of NaOH was added to prepare different concentration of NaOH (0–0.5 M) with the ultimate volume of 4 mL at room temperature for 5 min. The maximum absorption wavelength (λmax) was selected by full wavelength scanning (400–700 nm) on a Microplate Reader (TECAN-Spark, Switzerland) and used to generate the calibration curve.

I2-KI analysis

I2-KI experiment was performed refer to the prior report (Lv et al., 2024). Briefly, 2 mL of UAE-ORP solution (1 mg/mL) was mixed with 1.2 mL potassium iodide solution (containing 0.2% KI and 0.02% I2, w/v) at room temperature for 10 min. The absorbance spectra was recorded by full wavelength scanning (300–700 nm) on a Microplate Reader (TECAN-Spark, Switzerland).

Surface morphological characterization

UAE-ORP powder was coated with gold powder, and then examined with a.

scanning electron microscopy system (SEM) (Regulus 8230, Hitachi, Japan). The surface morphology of UAE-ORP was captured at magnifications of 500, 1000 and 3000 under an operating voltage of 3.0 kV.

Thermal characteristic identification

The thermal characteristics of UAE-ORP was analyzed by thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) on a Simultaneous Thermal Analyzer TGA/DSC1/1600LF (Mettler-Toledo, Switzerland). For TGA analysis, 4 mg of each UAE-ORP powder in an aluminum crucible with a sealed gland was heated up from 40 °C to 800 °C at a rate of 10 °C/min with nitrogen at a flow rate of 40 mL/min. Furthermore, the similar condition was used to DSC analysis, only a changed condition of a flow rate of nitrogen was 50 mL/min (Wang et al., 2018).

Function characteristics of UAE-ORP

The function characteristics of UAE-ORP on water retention capacity (WRC), oil retention capacity (ORC), emulsifying activity index (EAI), emulsifying stability index (ESI), foaming capacity (FC) and foaming stability (FS) were measured according to previous reports (Abarghoei, Goli, & Shahi, 2023).

Bioactivity assay

In vitro antioxidant ability

Radical scavenging activities on DPPH, hydroxyl radical clearance rate, reducing power and ABTS of UAE-ORP were detected according to the previous method (Yin et al., 2021), which was detailed described in Supplement file. UAE-ORP was dissolved in varying concentrations of 0.5, 1, 2, 3, and 4 mg/mL. The positive control was Vc.

Anti-inflammatory activity

The albumin inhibitory activity was evaluated using a protocol adapted from the previous report (Hu et al., 2020) The inhibitory ability on NO was evaluated in line with the prior study (Hao et al., 2023), which was detailed described in Supplement file.

Prebiotic activity

The prebiotic effect of UAE-ORP was detected in keeping with the previous method (Li, Wang, et al., 2022). L.Plantarum LP1 and L.Rhamnosus LR1 were incubated on MRS plates at a 37 °C for 48 h, and then were inoculated into MRS broth medium and cultured on a shaker (37 °C, 150 r/min, 12 h) to obtain the seed solutions of the two strains. MRS medium (0.5% glucose) with partial removal of carbon sources was set up as a negative control group. Positive control group was FOS plus MRS medium (0.5% glucose). UAE-ORP group was UAE-ORP plus MRS (0.5% glucose). Among them, the addition of UAE-ORP and FOS were both 75 g/L, and the inoculation amount was 1% (v/v). The prebiotic effect of UAE-ORP were measured by detecting the OD600 value and pH during the fermentation process.

The distribution of ORP-Cy5.5

Preparation of ORP-Cy5.5

The preparation of ORP-Cy5.5 was modified in accordance with the report (Zou, Duan, & Xu, 2019). First of all, 100 mg of UAE-ORP was dissolved in 100 mL of ultra-pure water, then gradually mixed in 100 mL of ethanediamine and 300 mg of sodium borohydride, and then was stirred continuously at room temperature for 3 days. Subsequently, the reaction blend underwent precipitation with 300 mL of anhydrous ethanol twice, subjected to dialysis with ultra-pure water and freeze-drying, 5 mg/mL of ORP-NH2 solution was mixed with 10 mg/mL of Cy5.5-NHS ester in the dark at 4 °C for 24 h. ORP-Cy5.5 was acquired after dialysis with ultra-pure water and freeze-drying.

Characterization of ORP-Cy5.5

ORP-Cy5.5 was identified by FT-IR spectral analysis and UV–Vis spectral analysis according to our previous work (Li, Wei, Zhao, Zhang, & Li, 2022).

Stability determination of ORP-Cy5.5

The in vitro stability assay was conducted on the basis of the prior report (Li, Wei, et al., 2022). ORP-Cy5.5 (25 μg/mL) was mixed with in vitro gastric and small intestinal medium, respectively, and then incubated at 37 °C. The fluorescence intensity of every sample in the in vitro gastric and small intestinal liquid at 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 24 h was measured for evaluating the in vitro stability of ORP-Cy5.5.

Oral real-time distribution of ORP-Cy5.5 by near infrared (NIR) imaging

All animal experiments were approved by the Jilin Agricultural University Ethics Committee and conducted in accordance with the National Research Council's Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (Ethics No. 20240930001). Eight-week-old male ICR mice (20 ± 2 g) were purchased from Beijing Huafukang Biotechnology Co. Ltd. (SCXK (Jing) 2024–0003). The mice were acclimated for 7 days at room temperature (22 ± 2 °C) and relative humidity (55 ± 2%) under a 12–12 h light-dark cycle.

Six to eight-week-old male ICR mice weighing 18 ± 0.2 g were acquired and kept in the manner previously mentioned. After a 24-h fast, mice in the control and model groups were gavaged with either ORP-Cy5.5 (200 mg/kg) or free Cy5.5 (2 mg/kg at 1% replacement). Following oral administration, three mice were anesthetized with isoflurane at 30 min and 1, 3, 6, 12, and 24 h for NIR fluorescence imaging on an Tanon ABL X5 pro system (Shanghai, China). After in vivo imaging, the mice were euthanized by cervical dislocation after anesthetized with intraperitoneal injection at a dose of 20% urethane (100 mg/kg) (Liang et al., 2025), and NIR imaging was performed on the major organs (spleen, heart, brain, kidneys, lungs, liver) and intestine.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed with SPSS 26.0 software, employing analysis of variance (ANOVA) (P < 0.05). Data were the means ± standard deviation (SD) from three biological replicates. Origin 2018 software and GraphPad Prism 8 software were used for image generation.

Results and discussion

One-factor extraction of O. Raphanipies polysaccharides

A preliminary one-factor extraction experiment was established by examining the effects of four parameters (temperature, time, ultrasonic power, and material-to-liquid ratio) on the extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharides. The extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharides was elevated with the increase of temperature, and reached a peak of 17.14 ± 0.02 mg/g (P < 0.05) at 65 °C, followed by a significant decrease to 13.98 ± 0.34 mg/g with the increased temperature (Fig. 1A). Extremely low temperatures (20–40 °C) significantly suppress polysaccharide yield. For example, Liu et al. demonstrated that the extraction yield of Lycium ruthenicum polysaccharides extracted at 20 °C and 40 °C was markedly lower (Liu et al., 2025) than that at 60 °C and 80 °C (Liu, Tomtong, Charoensiddhi, & Vongsangnak, 2025). Bautista et al. (Bautista et al., 2025) reported that cold-water extraction (20 °C) of Thuja plicata Donn resulted in a extraction yield of only 3.4%, suggesting that low temperatures are insufficient to achieve effective extraction (Bautista et al., 2025). Conversely, excessively high temperatures can disrupt polysaccharide structures, leading to degradation and compromising biological activity, thereby reducing polysaccharide extraction yield, which was consistent with our data (Wang, Huang, & Huang, 2024). Furthermore, as temperature continues to rise, the reduction in solvent surface tension caused by the weakening of cavitation effect at high temperatures significantly hampers mass transfer of the components to be extracted, leading to a decrease in polysaccharide yield (Huang et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023). The extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharide gradually increased to a peak of 18.93 ± 0.02 mg/g from 30 to 90 min, but significantly decreased to 13.28 ± 0.38 mg/g at 120 min (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1B). Ultrasonic power induced cavitation bubbles could promote the cell wall degradation and improve solvent penetration, thus playing a crucial role in determining the extraction efficiency of natural polysaccharides (Zhang et al., 2024). Short extraction time for polysaccharides result in low extraction efficiency, primarily due to insufficient dissolution and diffusion of polysaccharides, which leads to incomplete mass transfer (Liu, 2018). While prolonging extraction time enhances polysaccharide dissolution, excessively long durations trigger hydrolytic degradation into oligosaccharides, reducing extraction yields (Zhou, Huang, & Long, 2025). Specifically, extended ultrasonic-extraction time elevates the internal thermal energy of molecules, which accelerates the destruction of polysaccharide structures in the solvent and ultimately results in polysaccharide degradation (Wang, Liu, & Hu, 2014). Additionally, prolonged extraction increases energy consumption and operational costs while compromising productivity. Therefore, 120 min was selected as the optimal extraction time for further optimization.

Fig. 1C represents the impact of ultrasonic power on the extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharide. From 100 W to 300 W, the extraction yield of polysaccharides increased significantly reaching 18.81 ± 0.07 mg/g at 250 W (P < 0.05) and then decreased, which was consistent with the previous study that excessive ultrasound can cause polysaccharide degradation, leading to a decrease in extraction yield (Zhang, Duan, et al., 2023). Increased ultrasonic power may disrupt glycosidic bonds in polysaccharides, thereby reducing extraction yields (Gonzalez-Centeno, Comas-Serra, Femenia, Rossello, & Simal, 2015). Conversely, excessively low power weakens ultrasonic cavitation, failing to effectively disrupt cellular structures and potentially inducing polysaccharide hydrolysis, which also decreases extraction yield (Meng, Chen, Chen, Zhang, & Gao, 2021). Therefore, an ultrasonic power of 250 W was selected as the extraction condition for further optimization.

The influence of material-to-liquid ratio on the extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharide was represented in Fig. 1D. The material-to-liquid ratios of 1: 20 and 1: 40 had no significant effect on the extraction yield. A peak value of 20.63 ± 0.73 mg/g was significantly observed (P < 0.05) when the material-to-liquid ratio was 1: 60. This phenomenon may be due to the polysaccharides adsorb more H2O molecules during the amount of H2O increasing, a hydrophilic substance containing a large number of hydroxyl and other polar groups, thus increasing its solubility and ultimately enhance the extraction yield of polysaccharides (Deng & Huang, 2025). Therefore, the conditions selected for further optimization were ultrasonic temperature of 65 °C, time of 90 min, ultrasonic power of 250 W and material-to-liquid ratio of 1: 60. When the material-to-liquid ratio is excessively low, insufficient contact area between the solvent and sample restricts effective dissolution of polysaccharide molecules, leading to low extraction efficiency (Feng et al., 2024). Conversely, an excess of solvent is generally believed to weaken intermolecular interactions and reduce the penetration capacity of the ultrasonic system, thereby diminishing polysaccharide solubilization and ultimately lowering extraction yield, while avoiding resource wastes (Zhou et al., 2025). Therefore, an optimal material-to-liquid ratio of 1: 60 was selected.

The impact of four independent variables on the TPC of O. raphanipies polysaccharides was evaluated using 3D response surface plots and contour plots to effectively illustrate the interplay between the variables (Fig. 1E-L). The steeply displayed surfaces in the response surface map highlighted the significant interactions among variables. The downward trend of the response surface for the impact of variable interactions on the extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharides and the steeper slope of the response surface curve indicated that as the value of each factor increased, its impact of each variable on the extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharides went from increasing to decreasing (Cai, Chen, Yi, & Zou, 2019). The impact of four factors on the extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharides was B (power) > D (material-to-liquid ratio) > A (temperature) > C (time). The impact of quadratic terms A2, B2, C2 and D2 on the extraction yield was extremely significant (P < 0.01). The interaction of AB, BC, and BD had a meaningful influence on the extraction yield (P < 0.01), while the interaction of AC, AD, and CD had no significant effect. Apparently, the influence of ultrasonic power played a dominant role, while ultrasonic temperature, time, material-to-liquid ratio, and others have a relatively small impact on the polysaccharide extraction rate. However, ultrasonic power and ultrasonic extraction time exceed a certain threshold, when they may destroy O. raphanipies polysaccharides, thereby declining the extraction yield, which was in accordance with that ultrasound power plays a dominant role in the extraction process of O. raphanipies polysaccharides. Furthermore, ultrasonic power and ultrasonic extraction time had relatively small effects on the extraction rate of polysaccharides (Miao et al., 2023). The chemical structure of O. raphanipies polysaccharides leads to their degradation into small molecule sugars, resulting in a decrease in polysaccharide extraction efficiency. Proper temperature for extraction can enhance the solubility and facilitate mass transfer of polysaccharides, whereas high temperatures might cause their oxidative and thermal degradation (Yang et al., 2011). The above results were consistent with Ganoderma lucidum polysaccharides (Kang, Zhang, & Zhou, 2023; Zhang et al., 2023). Ultrasound power in the extraction process possesses a dominant role in the extraction process of O. raphanipies polysaccharides, while extraction time and material-to-liquid ratio have relatively small effects on the extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharides.

According to our results, the optimal extraction process for O. raphanipies polysaccharides was as follows: temperature was 64.69 °C, ultrasonic power was 258.67 W, time was 94.09 min, material-to-liquid ratio was 1: 63.97, and the predicted yield value was 217.10 mg/g. The actual yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharides was 210.35 ± 0.20 mg/g with the ultrasonic temperature of 65 °C, time of 90 min, power of 250 W, and material-to-liquid ratio of 1: 60, which closely aligned with the predicted value of 217.10 mg/g, implying that the optimization method and established model are accurate and trustworthy.

To clarify the effect of different extraction technologies on polysaccharide yield, HWE experiments was additionally conducted and its extraction yield was compared with that of O. raphanipies polysaccharides. The yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharides extracted by HWE method were only 34.35 ± 0.20 mg/g, significantly lower than that of O. raphanipies polysaccharides extracted by the UAE method reached as high as 210.35 ± 0.20 mg/g. Don polysaccharides (Li et al., 2025). The core mechanism lies in that high-frequency sound waves generated by ultrasound can form a cavitation effect in the liquid, which efficiently disrupts cell walls and promotes the release of bioactive substances such as polysaccharides and polyphenols, thereby improving extraction efficiency and bioactivity (Chen et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025; Li, Zhou, & Lu, 2024). Moreover, the extraction yield in the current study was significantly higher than that of O. raphanipies polysaccharides extracted by optimal UAE condition with orthogonal experiments (Xu et al., 2017).

Variations in the monosaccharide composition of polysaccharides were reported to lead to differences in their bioactivities (Nada, Omara, Abdel-Salam, & Zahran, 2010). The glycosidic structure was low Glc ratio can boost antioxidant abilities (Chen, Xu, & Zhu, 2010). Gal may play a significant role in sustaining antioxidant capacity (Yuan et al., 2019). Mw determination of UAE-ORP

The molecular weight of polysaccharides significantly impacts their biological activity (Chen et al., 2015). The molecular weight of UAE-ORP was determined with high performance gel permeation chromatography (HPGPC-ELSD). A single and narrow peak was detected (Fig. 2B), implying that UAE-ORP was a homogeneous polysaccharide with Mw of 568.57 kDa.

Functional group assay of UAE-ORP

As one of the most robust techniques for polysaccharide structure characterization, FT-IR is widely applied to identify the functional groups present in compounds (Luo et al., 2023). FT-IR spectra of UAE-ORP were shown in Fig. 2C. The strong absorption peaks at 3410 cm−1 was O—H stretching vibration, which is consistent with previous reports (Kanmani et al., 2011). The peaks at 3000–1000 cm−1 was caused by the elongation, distortion, and oscillation of C—H (Chen et al., 2017). An absorption peak at 1640 cm−1 indicates the existence of carboxyl groups in ORP. The observed peak at 1430 cm-1 could be interpreted as the symmetric stretching vibration in the C—H bond (Zhang et al., 2024). The observed peak at 1080 cm−1 is ascribed to the stretching vibrations of C—O—C and C—O—H (Farina, Vinarta, Cattaneo, & Figueroa, 2009), indicating the possible presence of pyranose sugar ring in UAE-ORP. A faint absorption peak at 890 cm−1 suggested the potential existence of α-glycosidic bonds within polysaccharides (Que et al., 2024). Hence, UAE-ORP was a type of polysaccharide with pyranose sugar ring and α-glycosidic bonds.

In sum, a novel polysaccharide UAE-ORP was prepared from O. raphanipies by the UAE method, containing high Glc and Gal with pyranose sugar ring and α-glycosidic bonds.

Triple helical structure characterization of UAE-ORP

Polysaccharides containing a triple helix structure could combine with Congo red, the maximum absorption wavelengths (λmax) of the Congo red-polysaccharide complex undergoes notable shifts (Gong et al., 2020). Hence, Congo red method was performed to detect the existence of triple helix structures. Compared with Congo red solution, the λmax of Congo red-UAE-ORP complexes exhibited a shift with increasing the NaOH concentration. Nevertheless, the λmax of UAE-ORP significantly declined with the rise of the NaOH concentration, implying that the triple helix existed in UAE-ORP had been destroyed.

Characterization of branch structure and starch impurities of UAE-ORP

I2-KI analysis is widely utilized to determine the presence of starch and long chains of polysaccharides. Notably, the polysaccharides do not exhibit a maximum absorption peak at 565 nm in I2-KI solution, representing the presence of a complex molecular chain-like structure, longer molecular side chains, and larger branched chains (Zhu et al., 2020). The reaction solution does not exhibit a maximum absorption peak at 350 nm, denoting that polysaccharides rich in complete and long branched chains (Lv et al., 2024). The absorption peaks of UAE-ORP were solely detected at 350 nm (Fig. 2E), indicating that UAE-ORP possessed extended side chains and more branching. The solution after reaction still had the color of I2-KI and does not exhibit the characteristic color reaction of starch, illustrating that polysaccharide did not contain starch.

Surface morphological characterization

SEM is widely used to investigate the surface morphological characteristics of polysaccharides, particularly focusing on the size, shape and porosity of macromolecules. UAE-ORP was mainly composed of irregular fragmented and layered structures, accompanied by irregular small particles with rough surfaces, and circular or semi-circular holes with diameters close to 10 μm on the surface (Fig. 2G). The primary reason is that ultrasound facilitates not just the breakdown of polysaccharides, but also the rearrangement of polysaccharide molecules, which in turn leads to the degradation of polysaccharides (Cui & Zhu, 2021). Consequently, the surface morphological characteristics of UAE-ORP further confirmed that UAE technology could effectively promote the release of UAE-ORP and extraction efficiency through the potent effect of ultrasound. This effectively increased the opportunities for the extraction solvent to immediately interact with polysaccharides and penetrate deeply, thereby substantially improving the efficiency and rate of the extraction process.

Thermal stability determination of UAE-ORP

Thermal stability is a crucial physicochemical property of polysaccharides, essential for their suitability in industrial and commercial application. The TG and DTG curves were presented in Fig. 2F. Three processes of thermal weight loss appeared in UAE-ORP. The thermal weight loss process with temperature rising leads to water loss of the polysaccharide often occurring at 50–150 °C, and this first thermal loss process suggested the loss of water of UAE-ORP. Afterwards, a more rapid weight loss materialized between 100 °C and 240 °C, resulting from the structural depolymerisation of polysaccharides. The existence of the second thermogravimetric process of UAE-ORP may be because of the existence of partially thermally unstable compounds in the polysaccharides (Lv et al., 2024). As the temperature increases from 240 °C, the weight loss of UAE-ORP was relatively slow, with a remaining weight of 17.96% at 800 °C. According to the DTG curves, the maximum decomposition rates of UAE-ORP occur at 322 °C, higher than that of okra polysaccharides (262.81–287.88 °C), and commercial pectin (268.78–289.06 °C) (Jia et al., 2022). One important consideration for assessing the thermal stability of polymers was the temperature (Tmax) at which the maximum weight loss rate occurs, a higher Tmax value often denotes superior thermal stability (Zhang et al., 2020), indicating the better thermal stability than commercial pectin. Hence, UAE method may enhance thermal resistance relative to standard methods, though further studies are needed to confirm the mechanism.

The thermal stability of polysaccharides, a key physicochemical property, is directly determined by their structural characteristics. Qi et al. found that fermentation enhances the thermal stability of Tricholoma koningiigemmata polysaccharides by inducing structural changes (e.g., in sugar content, molecular conformation, molecular weight distribution, and component arrangement) (Qi et al., 2024), which strengthen intra- and inter-molecular interactions (such as hydrogen bonds) and thus improve thermal stability. Consequently, UAE-ORP had superior thermal stability for the baking sector to improve the texture of baked goods, similar with hen egg-white lysozyme (Hirai et al., 2012).

Function characteristics of UAE-ORP

The functional characteristics of polysaccharides are linked to their chemical composition (Robertson, 1988). Among the significant functional properties of polysaccharides are WRC and ORC. Therefore, a sample with a higher WRC or ORC could be exploited and useful to hold onto water or oil, a preferred property of good stabilizer in food (Spinei & Oroian, 2023). As indicated in Fig. 3. Certain vegetable fibers, known for their high WRC, have been in the composition of meat products (Alfredo et al., 2009). Consequently, UAE-ORP with a higher WRC help in water retention, thus reducing syneresis during food processing and serving as a stabilizer for casein networks in dairy products.Fig. 3Function characteristics of UAE-ORP. ORC represents the oil absorption ability of a hydrocolloid (Abdul Razak, Gan, & Shafie, 2024). The ORC of UAE-ORP was 16.21 ± 0.18 g/g, which was higher than that of Rosa roxburghii Tratt fruit polysaccharide (3.29 ± 0.38 g/g) (Wang et al., 2018), grape pomace polysaccharide (2.21 ± 0.11 g/g) (Spinei & Oroian, 2023) and Garcinia atroviridis fruit polysaccharides (3.29 ± 0.38 g/g) (Abdul Razak et al., 2024). Emulsifying capacity refers to a molecule's ability to function as an agent that facilitates the solubilization or dispersion of two immiscible liquids, whereas emulsion stability denotes the capacity to maintain an emulsion and its resistance to breakdown (Alfredo et al., 2009). The EAI and ESI value of UAE-ORP was 10.03 ± 0.31% and 143.54 ± 6.01%, respectively. Polysaccharide from the pea-processing by-products (PeP) indicated that EAI and ESI values was 41.88 ± 1.38% and 37.23 ± 1.22% (Shehata, Darwish, & El-Sohaimy, 2020). The foaming capacity and stability are essential in a wide range of various food and beverage applications, such as in cake, ice cream, mousse, whipped cream and beer (Eghbaljoo et al., 2022). UAE-ORP showed a relatively high FC (105.50 ± 0.71%) and FS (104.45 ± 0.70%). The FC and FS of UAE-ORP was greater than that of the polysaccharide extracted from Lycium barbarum (66% and 44%) (Zhang et al., 2022). High FC and FS values indicate the possible application of UAE-ORP in culinary items like milkshakes, ice cream, and marshmallows, similar with Amana edulis polysaccharides (Ji et al., 2019).

In vitro activity of UAE-ORP

Analysis of antioxidant activity results

In the present study, the antioxidant activity of UAE-ORP was comprehensively investigated using three different methods for the determination of radical scavenging activity on DPPH, hydroxyl, ABTS and reducing capacity (Fig. 4). Among them, Vc was a positive control, and the scavenging activity of UAE-ORP on all free radicals is dose-dependent. DPPH radical scavenging activity is a fast, agile, sensitive, and feasible approach for assessing the antioxidant ability of natural antioxidants, which is extensively utilized by researchers both domestically and internationally (Peng et al., 2012). As shown in Fig. 4A, both Vc and UAE-ORP exhibited scavenging activity on DPPH radical in a dose-dependent manner, which gradually increased with increasing concentration.with a DPPH scavenging rate of 90.43 ± 1.9% at 4.0 mg/mL,which was close to that of Vc. Glc and Gal, with abundant hydroxyl groups, directly neutralize radicals by donating hydrogen, while Fuc enhances DPPH molecular accessibility via its methyl group to optimize overall hydrogen-donating efficiency, and this synergistic effect aligns with the structure-activity relationships reported in polysaccharides containing these monosaccharides (Li, Nie, Wang, & Luo, 2016). In sum, there were presumably some correlations between DPPH radical scavenging capacity and the monosaccharide composition of the polysaccharides.Fig. 4Antioxidant activity of UAE-ORP. Hydroxyl radicals have a strong effect with macromolecules such as proteins, amino acids, and DNA, and can easily cross cell membranes to cause tissue injury or cell death. Therefore, elimination of hydroxyl radicals is considered essential to protect biological systems (Wang et al., 2023). The hydroxyl radicals clearance ability of UAE-ORP showed a significant concentration dependence in the range of 0.5–4 mg/mL (Fig. 4B). At 4 mg/mL, the scavenging rate reached 57.13 ± 0.01%, which may relate to UAE-ORP's monosaccharide composition. Notably, Meng et al., demonstrated that Hirsutella sp. polysaccharide IPS-1 containing high Man content (59.0 mol%) and lower Glc proportion (8.5 mol%) exhibits ·OH scavenging comparable to Vc. In contrast, UAE-ORP's lower Man content (12.62 mol%) and higher Glc proportion (35.48 mol%) likely attenuated its ·OH neutralization capacity, highlighting the critical role of monosaccharide ratios in radical-specific antioxidant efficacy (Meng et al., 2015).

Reducing power is a critical index that directly reflect the antioxidant ability. At 700 nm, the reaction system's absorbance level signifies the extent of its antioxidant-reducing capacity. The larger the absorbance value represents the stronger reducing power. The reducing ability of UAE-ORP was dose-dependently elevated. The absorbance peaked reached the highest (0.89 ± 0.01%) at 4 mg/mL (Fig. 4C).

The clearance activity test of ABTS is usually chosen to test the antioxidant ability of polysaccharides (Qiu, Shi, Miao, Hu, & Gao, 2023). The ABTS scavenging activity increased in a concentration-dependent manner. The maximum ABTS scavenging activity of UAE-ORP was 25.23 ± 0.95% at 4 mg/mL (Fig. 4D). Therefore, our findings indicate that UAE-ORP exhibited significant antioxidant activities, particularly in DPPH and hydroxyl radical scavenging abilities.

The antioxidant activity of polysaccharides largely depends on their structure, encompassing molecular weight, monosaccharide composition, number of hydroxyl groups, glycosidic bonds, and branching degree. Glc and Man exhibit stronger antioxidant activity relative to other monosaccharides (Liu et al., 2021). DPPH radical scavenging capacity was possibly related to the content of Man (Guo et al., 2024). Therefore, the rich abundance of Glc, Gal, Fuc, Man in UAE-ORP might be contributed to its higher antioxidant capacity. However, the structure-activity relationship of UAE-ORP still needs to further clarified.

Chemical anti-inflammatory activity

To assess the anti-inflammatory properties of UAE-ORP, the BSA denaturation inhibitory and NO inhibition activity were examined. As shown in Fig. 4E, UAE-ORP achieved a maximum BSA denaturation inhibition rate of 97.49 ± 0.74% at 5 mg/mL, lower than that of Vc (137.79 ± 9.04%). The main biological indicator of bioactive peptides' anti-inflammatory capacity is their inhibitory effect on NO secretion (Hao et al., 2023). The NO inhibition ability of UAE-ORP was dose-dependently increased (Fig. 4F). Hence, the above results suggest UAE-ORP had good anti-inflammatory properties (38.23 ± 0.49%). Notably, the anti-inflammatory activity of UAE-ORP may be closely associated with its major monosaccharide components (Glc, Gal, and Fuc), each exerting distinct molecular functions. Glc, with a high density of hydroxyl groups, is hypothesized to form hydrogen bonds with BSA, preserving BSA's native conformation and inhibiting denaturation, and thereby explains the observed high efficiency in suppressing BSA denaturation.

Prebiotic activity of UAE-ORP

The prebiotic activity of polysaccharides has been commonly verified to modulate gut microbiota by enriching beneficial bacteria and inhibiting harmful bacteria (Liu et al., 2024). L. plantarum LP1 and L. rhamnosus LR1, as the famous probiotics, have been used in prebiotic assay of polysaccharides (Li, Wei, et al., 2022). Consequently, this research chose two strains to assess the prebiotic activity of UAE-ORP. The OD600 value of Lactobacillus mirrors the growth of probiotics and efficiency of polysaccharides (Yang et al., 2024). UAE-ORP significantly increased the growth of both LAB strains compared with the CK group, but the growth was still slower than that in the FOS group (Fig. 5 A-B). These results show that that both LAB strains are capable of utilizing UAE-ORP and FOS. The pH of the culture medium drops as a result of probiotics' metabolism of carbohydrates into acids during growth process. As a result, pH decrease is a sign of the prebiotic effects of polysaccharides. The pH levels of the two probiotics in varied mediums were measured (Fig. 5C—D). Compared with the CK group, LP1 and LR1 showed a significant decrease in pH at 48 h in the UAE-ORP group, but still higher than the FOS group, indicating that both LP1 and LR1 could utilize UAE-ORP to produce acidic substances to a certain extent. The decrease in pH corresponds with the typical growth manners. The pH values were also dramatically decreased by Nostoc commune polysaccharide, but still higher than the FOS group (Li, Wang, et al., 2022), which aligns with the current findings. Evaluating the effects of polysaccharides on the OD600 values and pH of in vitro-cultured probiotics represents a rapid and effective method to assess prebiotic activity, as the study of FVRP extracted from Flammulina velutipes residues (Yang et al., 2024). Similarly, the current study preliminarily confirmed the growth-promoting and acid-producing capacities of UAE-ORP on L. plantarum LP1 and L. rhamnosus LR1 through OD600 and pH measurements. However, due to limitations in strain specificity and the simplification of in vitro models, the prebiotic potential of UAE-ORP requires further validation via multi-omics integration and combined in vitro/in vivo models (Li, Ye, et al., 2025; Liu, Tomtong, et al., 2025). In vivo distribution of ORP-Cy5.5

Recently, polysaccharides have been successfully labeled with Cy5.5 to investigate their in vivo distribution (Zhang et al., 2021). Therefore, ORP-Cy5.5 was also prepared by labeling the terminal hydroxyl group of UAE-ORP with Cy5.5 amine for tracking of its tissue distribution of UAE-ORP in mice.

In the FT-IR spectra, UAE-ORP and ORP-Cy5.5 had roughly the same characteristic signals of the polysaccharide except some additional peaks (Fig. 6 A). However, ORP-Cy5.5 exhibited different UV–Vis spectrum compared with UAE-ORP (Fig. 6B). The UV peaks of 630 and 680 nm were owned by characteristic absorption peaks of Cy5.5 (Zhang, Liu, Liu, Li, & Zhou, 2021). In vitro gastrointestinal digestion assay was conducted to assess the stability of ORP-Cy5.5. No significant alterations were observed in the characteristic peaks corresponding to the polysaccharide and amide bonds (Fig. 6C), which was also exhibited in the results of Cucurbita moschata polysaccharides (Li, Wei, et al., 2022), proving the stability of amide bonds, and the fluorescence labeling had few effects on the properties of polysaccharide. Hence, ORP-Cy5.5 exhibited good gastrointestinal digestion stability, and could be used for further in vivo distribution determination.

Compared to visible fluorescence imaging, NIR imaging provides distinct benefits, including noninvasiveness, background filtering and realtime tracking (Zaheer et al., 2001). Additionally, it outperforms radionuclide imaging in terms of both safety and expense. Thus, NIR imaging was conducted to further investigate the in vivo distribution of UAE-ORP after oral administration (Fig. 7A). Following the oral intake of ORP-Cy5.5, the fluorescent signals were mainly centralized in the abdomen of mice and progressively attenuated. The intense fluorescence signals within 1–6 h observed were in the ORP-Cy5.5 group, and some fluorescence even continued for 24 h. The fluorescence signal of the Cy5.5 group reached its peak at 1 h after oral administration, and then decreased. The results indicate that the distribution process of ORP-Cy5.5 in the body was relatively longer than that of free Cy5.5. The in vitro image of the intestine was then taken to clarify the distribution of ORP-Cy5.5 within the intestine (Fig. 7B). This study provides a time window for its full degradation and fermentation by gut microbiota, a process analogous to the gastrointestinal persistence of Grifola frondosa polysaccharides (Zhang et al., 2023). The fluorescence signal of ORP-Cy5.5 was visible in the cecum at 6 h, weakened at 24 h. Apparently, in line with previous studies (Hue et al., 2013), ORP-Cy5.5 stayed in the body for a longer retention time compared with free Cy5.5. However, Ex vivo experiments showed that ORP-Cy5.5 had almost no distribution in organs (Fig. 7C). This result indicate that UAE-ORP were poorly absorbed into the systemic circulation, consistent with the general properties of high-molecular-weight polysaccharides and findings from Grifola frondosa polysaccharides. The utilization of high-molecular-weight polysaccharides by gut microbiota to exert physiological functions is theoretically supported by the results in Fig. 7. Conclusion

This study successfully established and optimized an efficient and innovative UAE protocol for obtaining O. raphanipies polysaccharides. Single-factor test and RSM were ascertained to confirm the optimal extraction conditions of O. raphanipies polysaccharides. The extraction yield of O. raphanipies polysaccharides was defined to be 210.35 ± 0.20 mg/g with temperature of 65 °C, time of 90 min, ultrasonic power of 250 W and material-to-liquid ratio of 1: 60 mL/g. Additionally, purified UAE-ORP with 568.57 kDa consisted of Glc, Gal, Man, Fuc, Xyl and Glc-UA =35.48: 28.51: 12.95: 12.62: 8.75: 1.69. UAE-ORP showed high thermal stability (322 °C) and good function characteristics. UAE-ORP displayed outstanding scavenging capacity against DPPH, hydroxyl radical, anti-inflammatory and prebiotic activity. In addition, UAE-ORP was significantly distributed in the gastrointestinal tract based on NIR imaging result, retaining in intestines for 24 h, indicating that UAE-ORP might be a potential prebiotic agent. This gentle and efficient UAE process not only boosted extraction yield but also better preserved the structural integrity and bioactivity of the polysaccharides. The present results also compellingly indicate that UAE-ORP holds significant promise as a multi-functional natural agent for antioxidants, anti-inflammatory, and probiotic applications. This work not only highlights the substantial biomedical and functional food value of O. raphanipies polysaccharides, but also demonstrates the distinct advantages of the optimized UAE technique, providing a solid foundation and a superior technological approach for the future development and utilization of O. raphanipies polysaccharides.

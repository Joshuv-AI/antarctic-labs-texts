Mn-Zn ferrite nanoparticles inducing ferroptosis to reverse the resistance in CML cells
Authors: Miao Zhu, Yanqian Zhao, Li Xu, Mei Sun, Yuxiang Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChronic myeloid leukemia (CML) is a malignant clonal disease of hematopoietic stem cells driven by the BCR-ABL fusion gene. Although tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) serve as targeted therapies, drug resistance remains one of the key challenges affecting the long-term survival of CML patients. A novel iron-based nano-reagent of Mn-Zn Ferrite was constructed to explore its role in regulating the redox homeostasis of CML cells and to clarify its therapeutic potential in reversing drug resistance in CML.MethodsCCK8 experiments were used to evaluate the effect of Mn-Zn Ferrite nanoparticles (Nano-Iron) on the drug sensitivity of adriamycin in K562 cells and adriamycin-resistant K562 cells (K562/ADR). Apoptosis, reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, superoxide dismutase (SOD) levels in K562 cells and K562/ADR cells were measured after treatment of Nano-Iron and adriamycin. Further detection was conducted on the levels of glutathione (GSH), oxidized glutathione (GSSG) and GSH/GSSG ratio in K562 cells and K562/ADR cells after combined intervention. Meanwhile, the expression of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) and malondialdehyde (MDA) levels after combined intervention were determined. In vivo experiments involved in constructing systemic metastasis tumor models of K562 and K562/ADR in mice, which were treated with Nano-Iron and adriamycin. On the 10th, 15th and 20th day after tumor formation, in vivo imaging and medial canthal vein blood collection were performed to evaluate the proportion of tumor cells in peripheral blood. All mice were weighed regularly and euthanized when showing mental depression, at the same time, another batch of mice were used to calculate their survival period. Spleens and femurs were collected for hematoxylin–eosin (HE) and GPX4 staining.ResultsMn Zn ferrite (Nano-Iron) enhances the sensitivity of K562/ADR cells to adriamycin. After co-treatment with Nano-Iron and adriamycin, more apoptosis of K562/ADR cells was induced and accompanied with an increase of ROS and MDA levels, as well as a decrease of GSH/GSSG ratio and GPX4 expression. In vivo experiments have shown that the combination therapy of Nano-Iron and adriamycin prolongs the survival period of mice, significantly inhibits the infiltration of tumor cells in the bone marrow and suppresses the expression of GPX4 in the femur of mice.ConclusionMn-Zn Ferrite (Nano-Iron) enhances the therapeutic sensitivity of K562/ADR cells to adriamycin by inducing ferroptosis, demonstrating the therapeutic potential in reversing drug resistance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-025-07107-9.

Introduction

Although targeted therapy represented by tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) has significantly improved the prognosis of patients with chronic myeloid leukemia (CML), the issue of drug resistance is becoming increasingly prominent [1]. In patients with TKI resistance, accelerated phase and acute phase, anthracycline cytotoxic drugs such as adriamycin (ADR) remain important treatment options [2]. However, drug resistance is still one of the key factors affecting the treatment response and long-term survival of CML patients [3–5].

In recent years, ferroptosis, as a novel programmed cell death method, has attracted much attention due to its potential effect on drug-resistant tumor cells [6]. Ferroptosis is characterized by the accumulation of iron dependent lipid peroxides within cells, which is significantly different from classical death patterns such as apoptosis, necrosis and autophagy [7]. In CML, evidences have revealed that drug-resistant cells are often accompanied with the increased expression of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) and the enhanced clearance of reactive oxygen species (ROS), thereby acquiring the resistance to chemotherapy drugs [8–10]. Activation of the ferroptosis pathway is expected to break the resistance barrier and become an important breakthrough in reversing CML resistance [8, 11].

FDA-approved iron supplement ferumoxytol, one kind of iron oxide nanoparticles (IONPs) shows therapeutic potential in multiple types of tumors, especially in leukemia [12]. Numerous studies also show that IONPs can participate in the Fenton reaction in cells to generate highly active ROS, triggering lipid peroxidation processes and inducing ferroptosis in tumor cells [13, 14]. Our research group also found in previous studies that Fe3O4 nanoparticles have therapeutic potential for acute myeloid leukemia resistant to doxorubicin, while Mn-Zn Ferrite nanoparticles (Nano-Iron) doped with Mn and Zn elements can mediate a more efficient Fenton reaction in vivo[15–17]. Therefore, Nano-Iron is expected to promote ferroptosis in drug-resistant CML cells efficiently, thereby improving drug resistance. Here, we confirm that Nano-Iron can induce ferroptosis to improve the sensitivity of adriamycin in K562/ADR cells, exhibiting the therapeutic potential in reversing CML resistance.

Antibodies GPX4 (59,735), SOD1 (37,385), SOD2 (13,141) and β-actin (4970S) were all purchased from Cell Signaling Technology. CD7-PE (555,361) and CD117-APC (567,128) were purchased from BD Company in the United States. CD13-FITC (ab52461) was purchased from Abcam in the United States. The adhesive kit (BL522A) was purchased from Biosharp Life Sciences.

Synthesis process of Mn-Zn ferrite nanoparticles (Nano-Iron)

The specific synthesis of MnZnFe₂O₄@OA are as follows: First, 0.2 g (2 mmol) of iron acetylacetonate [Fe(acac)3], 0.1516 g (0.6 mmol) of manganese acetylacetonate [Mn(acac)₂] and 0.1054 g (0.4 mmol) of zinc acetylacetonate [Zn(acac)2] were added into a 50 mL three-necked flask. Subsequently, 20 mL of dibenzyl ether, 10 mmol of oleic acid and 2 mmol of oleylamine were added in sequence. In the nitrogen protective atmosphere, the mixed solution was heated to 220 °C at a rate of approximately 3.3 °C/min via a programmable temperature controller and maintained at this temperature for 1 h. Afterwards, the temperature was continued to be raised to 300 °C at the same rate. During the heating process, Fe(acac)3, Mn(acac)2 and Zn(acac)2 were gradually dissolved, and the color of the solution gradually changed from reddish brown to black. The above heating system was further maintained under reflux condensation at 300 °C for another 1 h. After the reaction was completed and cooled to room temperature, the product was alternately washed with ethanol and acetone, and the magnetic separation operation was repeated three times. Finally, it was dispersed in isooctane for subsequent use.

The preparation of PLA-PEG-RGD: 25.2 mg of PLA-PEG-COOH was dissolved in 10 mL of MES buffer and mixed with another 10 mL of MES buffer (pH 5.5) containing 3.22 mg of EDC and 3.4 mg of NHS. The mixture was shaken at 4 °C and 150 rpm for 25 min to activate the carboxyl groups. Then, the excess EDC and NHS were removed through ultrafiltration centrifugation at 5000 rpm, and this operation was repeated three times. 10 mg of RGD was dissolved in 20 mL of borate buffer (pH 8.3) and added dropwise to the above solution adjusted to pH 8.0. The mixed system was shaken at 150 rpm for 24 h, then purified by ultrafiltration to remove unreacted reagents, and finally freeze-dried to obtain white PLA-PEG-RGD powder.

Finally, the preparation process of Nano-Iron is as follows: 6 mL of ethanol was added to 3 mL of MnZnFe₂O₄@OA dispersed in isooctane (iron concentration 2.5 mg/mL). After 2 min of magnetic adsorption treatment, the supernatant was discarded. 3 mL of tetrahydrofuran was added, and the sample was fully dispersed by ultrasonic treatment. Subsequently, 90 mg of PLA-PEG-RGD was added and completely dissolved. The mixed solution was transferred to a glass syringe and injected into 30 mL of water at a rate of 0.5 mL/min under vigorous stirring. The obtained solution was further subjected to ultrasonic treatment at 750 W power for 6 min (ultrasonic for 2 s and pause for 2 s alternately), and then evaporated at 60 °C to obtain Mn-Zn ferrite nanoparticles (Nano-Iron). The prepared samples were dispersed on amorphous carbon-coated copper grids and characterized using a JEM-200CX transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEOL, Japan).

Cell culture

The K562 cells were donated by Jiangsu Non-coding RNA Laboratory, and the K562/ADR cells were purchased from Nanjing Zhongweide Biotechnology Co., Ltd. HL-60, HL-60/ADR, MV-4–11 and THP-1 cells were all purchased from Shanghai Meixuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd. All cell cultures were conducted according to the following standards: RPMI-1640 standard medium, supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin streptomycin bispecific antibody. The cells were cultured in a constant 37 °C temperature and 5% CO₂ concentration cell incubator and passaged every two days.

Inhibition experiment of cell proliferation by CCK-8

Inoculate K562 and K562/ADR cells into a 96 well plate and treat them with different concentrations of ADR for 24 h. Control wells should be added at each concentration to avoid errors caused by ADR color. Add 10 µL CCK-8 to each well, mix gently and continue to incubate for two hours. Microplate reader (PerkinElmer EnSpire, USA) was applied to measure the absorbance of each well at a wavelength of 450 nm and calculate cell viability based on the values.

Cell apoptosis rate detection

Collect the control cells and treated cell, wash them three times with PBS solution, re-suspend the precipitate with solution in the kits and stain them with Annexin-V (FITC) and PI for 20 min and then analyze them by the flow cytometer.

Determination of ROS levels

Diluted ROS probes were added to the cell suspension. The suspension was incubated in a 37 °C cell culture incubator for 30 min and the suspension mixed every 5 min to ensure full contact between the probes and cells. After incubation, the cells were washed three times with serum-free cell culture medium to remove free probes that did not enter the cells. The washed cell suspension was analyzed for fluorescence intensity by a flow cytometer (BD FACS Verse, USA) and ROS content was calculated.

GSH/GSSG detection

Wash these cells treated with Nano-Iron and ADR with PBS, centrifuge and discard the supernatant, add reagent M solution with a volume three times that of the cell precipitate, and vortex thoroughly. Perform two rapid freeze–thaw cycles in liquid nitrogen and a 37 °C water bath. After 5 min of ice bath at 4 °C, centrifuge at 10,000 g for 10 min to take the supernatant for protein concentration quantification, dividing the supernatant into two equal parts and adding 1/5 volume of diluted GSH scavenging auxiliary solution to one part, and then adding 1/25 volume of GSH scavenging solution and immediately vortex stirring. Then reacting them at 25 °C for 60 min. The other one does not contain any GSH scavenger. Take out a 96 well plate and add the samples and the standard in sequence, add 150 μL of total glutathione detection working solution, and mix well. Incubate them at room temperature for 5 min and then add 50 μL of 0.5 mg/mL NADPH solution, mixing thoroughly to measure absorbance at 412 nm wavelength by the microplate reader, which was performed once every 5 min for in total of 25 min and 5 plot data were pointed. According to the time point, calculate the GSSG and GSH content based on protein concentration as the formula GSH = total glutathione GSSG × 2.

MDA detection

After treatment with Nano-Iron and ADR, cell suspension was placed in a cell counter for counting. Centrifuge the cells and discard the supernatant, then add protein lysis buffer to the precipitate. After sufficient lysis, the twice volume of MDA detection working solution was added to the EP tubes. After mixing and then heating the mixture at 100 °C for 15 min. Cool them to the room temperature in a water bath and centrifuge them at 1000 g for 10 min. 200 μL supernatant was added into a 96 well plate, and then measure their absorbance at 532 nm by the microplate reader. Quantify the relative MDA levels in cells through absorbance and cell counting.

Detection of GPX4, SOD1, SOD2 expression

Cell precipitation was collected and lysed, then measure the protein concentration ineach sample through BCA kit andmix the sample with loading buffer, heat them in a metal bath at 100 °C for 5 min for denaturation. Preparing the 10% SDS-PAGE glue and loading the denatured sample into the SDS-PAGE glue hole, keeping 60 V voltage for 30 min at the beginning and then turning 120 V for another 60 min in vertical electrophoresis. Then transferring the proteins from SDS-PAGE glue to the PVDF membrane by the horizontal electrophoresis in 300 mA electric current for 100 min, sealing these transfer membranes with 5% skimmed milk for two hours and incubating them in the diluted primary antibody, overnight on a 4 °C shaker. After washing the membrane for three times, incubating them with the secondary antibody at room temperature for two hours, and after three times of washing, configure luminescence gel imaging system (BIO-RAD XR +) was used to collect images, and Image J was used to analyze.

Cell infection

In order to detect cell invasion and metastasis in mice, we constructed K562 and K562/ADR cell lines with red fluorescence. Virus carrier with red fluorescence was prepared and packaged by GeneChem company (https://www.genechem.com.cn/). According to the cell count of 2 × 105 per well, the cells were seeded in a 6-well plate. Virus containing non-double antibody culture medium was added to each well, and an infection enhancer was added. After mixing, the cells were placed in a horizontal centrifuge at 30 °C and 2000 rpm for 2 h. After horizontal centrifugation, non-double antibody culture medium was added. After 48 h of infection, the cells were treated with 1 μg/mL puromycin for 48 h. When bright fluorescence can be observed under a fluorescence microscope, successful infection can be determined.

Screening of tumorigenic cell lines

Eight 3-week-old female BALB/c nude mice were purchased from the Comparative Medical Research Center of Yangzhou University (license number SYXK [SU] 2014–0003) and were adaptively fed for one week. During the period, each mice was given 2 mg cyclophosphamide (Dalin Meilun BioTech, MB1315) dissolved in 0.9% physiological saline by gavage twice. Successfully infected K562 and K562/ADR cells were amplified and cell suspensions were injected subcutaneously on the lateral side of mice, with 4 mice injected each. 2 × 107 tumor cells were subcutaneously injected into each mouse and re-suspended in 150 μL PBS. About two weeks later, the tumors are formed in mice. The tumor was removed and ground to prepare a single-cell suspension, which was then further cultured and expanded.

Establishment of whole body transfer animal model

Prepare 40 female BALB/c nude mice at 5 weeks of age and treated them with the same as above. Expand and screen K562 and K562/ADR cells, 2 × 107 cells was injected into the tail vein in each mice, respectively. After 10 days of feeding, the mice in vivo imaging system (PE IVIS Spectrum, USA) was used to determine the tumor formation. Two types of tumor forming mice were divided into four groups: CTRL group, Nano-Iron group, ADR group, and Nano-Iron + ADR group. The CTRL group received intraperitoneal injection of 0.9% saline every other day, the NI group received intraperitoneal injection of 6 mg/kg Nano-Iron every other day, the ADR group received intraperitoneal injection of 0.4 mg/kg ADR every other day, and the NI + ADR group received intraperitoneal injection of the same dose of Nano-Iron combined with ADR every other day, for a total of 14 days of treatment. On the 10th, 15th, and 20th day after tumor loading, live imaging examinations were performed, and blood samples were collected from the inner canthus vein for flow cytometry testing. During the treatment period, the weight of the mice was measured the next day. When the mice were slow to move, had abnormal positions, and had their hair erect, they were euthanized and their femurs and spleens were removed and placed in formalin. Euthanasia details: Gently place the mouse in the euthanasia device and expose it to gradually increasing amounts of carbon dioxide. After 5 min, confirm that the mouse’s heartbeat and breathing have completely stopped. All programs have obtained pre-approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Yangzhou University (license number 202211010).

Flow cytometry of peripheral blood

Place the peripheral blood of mice in EDTA anti-coagulant tubes, add red blood cell lysis buffer and incubate them in the dark for 7 min, which was then centrifuged to remove red blood cell debris. Adding the antibodies to the cell suspension and incubating in the dark for 30 min, all cells were then washed and re-suspended in PBS to detect by flow cytometry (Beckman CytoFLEX, USA).

Immunohistochemical staining

After embedding the tissue slices in paraffin, dewaxing them in xylene and rehydrating them with gradient ethanol. Subsequently, the slices were placed in citrate buffer at pH 6.0 and heated for antigen retrieval. To block non-specific binding and endogenous peroxidase activity, sections were treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide. Incubate the slices with primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. Next day, the sections were incubated with biotin-labeled secondary antibodies, followed by horseradish peroxidase labeling. Finally, the sections were stained with 3,3 ‘- diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride and sealed with neutral gum. All stained sections were observed under a microscope and images were recorded for subsequent analysis.

HE staining

The tissue samples were fixed in 10% neutral formalin, then embedded in standard paraffin and sliced into 4 µm thick sections. Slices were deparaffinized with xylene and dehydrated after gradient ethanol treatment, which were stained in hematoxylin solution for 5 min and then differentiated with 1% hydrochloric acid ethanol and blued with running water. Then, the slices were stained with 1% eosin solution for 2 min, dehydrated with gradient ethanol, and made transparent with xylene. Finally, the slices were sealed with neutral gum, and the stained slices were observed under a microscope, with images recorded for subsequent analysis.

Statistical analysis

In this study, all biology related experiments were repeated at least three times. The data analysis was completed using SPSS 25.0 software. The differences between two groups were evaluated using t-test, while the differences between three or more groups were detected using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). When the P value is less than 0.05, the difference is considered significant (* P < 0.05); When the P value is less than 0.01 and 0.001, the difference is considered highly significant (* * P < 0.01) and extremely significant (* * P < 0.001), respectively.

Results

Nano-Iron mediated a high-performance of Fenton-like reaction

Mn-Zn Ferrite nanoparticles (Nano-Iron) was synthesized by a thermal decomposition approach at the onset and then modified with PLA-PEG-RGD as our previous report [16]. Transmission electron microscope (TEM) data showed the shape and size of Nano-Iron while the photo of the aqueous solution showed that Nano-Iron have good colloidal stability (Fig. 1A). Hydrodynamic diameter of Nano-Iron is about 30 nm (Fig. 1B). Given that we have fully characterized the Nano-Iron in our previous research [16], we thus focused more on the effect of Nano-Iron on the Fenton-like reaction. In the classic H2O2/TMB detection system, Nano-Iron can catalyze H2O2 to produce hydroxyl radical and oxidize TMB, causing an ultraviolet characteristic absorption peak at 650 nm. As shown in Fig. 1C, Nano-Iron caused an obvious absorbance at 650 nm, suggesting that Nano-Iron can promote the increase of oxidized TMB in system, which indicates an enhanced Fenton-like reaction of Nano-Iron in H2O2/TMB system. And this effect is time-dependent (Fig. 1D) and concentration-dependent (Fig. 1E). In addition, we compared it with traditional Fe3O4 nanoparticles as a classic type of iron oxide nanoparticles with Fenton-like performance. In order to demonstrate that Nano-Iron can induce more efficient Fenton-like effect than Fe3O4NPs in physiological conditions with neutral pH, salicylic acid (SA) was used to detect the hydroxyl radicals generated by Fenton-like reaction because hydroxyl radicals can react with salicylic acid (SA) to produce 2,3-dihydroxybenzoic acid that can cause the absorbance between 510 and 530 nm. As shown in Fig. 1G, in the detection condition with pH 7.0, Nano-Iron/H2O2/SA induce an obvious absorbance at the wavelength of 510–550 nm, while the antioxidant of CeO2 can offset the absorption caused by Nano-Iron/SA, demonstrating the Fenton-like ability of Nano-Iron in the condition with pH 7.0. To further confirm that Nano-Iron can mediate more efficient Fenton-like reactions in physiological conditions, we measured the reduction of SA in the detection system by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). These detection systems consisted of H2O2/SA, Fe3O4NPs/H2O2/SA, Nano-Iron/H2O2/SA. As indicated in Fig. 1H, after HPLC detection of SA standards with different concentrations, the SA concentration in saline is linearly related to the peak area. However, in different reaction systems mediated by Nano-iron and Fe3O4NPs after 1 h, the significant decrease in the peak area of SA was revealed in both groups, while it is obvious that Nano-Iron mediated a more efficient Fenton-like reaction in saline butter with pH 7.0 (Fig. 1I, J).

Inhibitory effects of Nano-Iron on the proliferation of K562 and K562/ADR cells.

As showed in Fig. 2B, the low concentration of Nano-Iron did not exhibit significant inhibitory effects on the proliferation of K562 and K562/ADR cells. However, when cells were treated by ADR alone, the proliferation inhibition rate in K562 cells was significantly higher than that in K562/ADR, indicating K562/ADR was resistant to ADR. Subsequently, the effect of ADR combined with Nano-Iron on the apoptosis of CML cells was further evaluated. In Fig. 2C, the combination of Nano-Iron and ADR promoted apoptosis in K562/ADR cells, suggesting that Nano-Iron can enhance the pro-apoptotic effect of ADR in drug-resistant CML cells. In K562 cells, ADR intervention alone can significantly induce apoptosis, but when combined with 400 μg/mL Nano-Iron, thee elevated apoptosis was further induced, indicating that Nano-Iron has the ability to synergistically enhance the pro-apoptotic effect of ADR.

Effects of ADR and Nano-Iron on ROS levels in K562 and K562/ADR cells

We thus examined ROS levels in K562 and K562/ADR cells that co-treated with ADR and Nano-Iron. In Fig. 3A, ADR treatment up-regulated the levels of ROS in K562 cells, while the addition of Nano-Iron further augmented intracellular ROS. To further confirm intracellular ROS was more easily induced by Nano-Iron, SOD1 and SOD2 protein levels were detected in K562 and K562/ADR cells. As an important component of the mitochondrial and cytoplasmic ROS scavenging system, SOD1/2 plays a crucial role in maintaining redox homeostasis [18]. In K562 cells co-treated with ADR and Nano-Iron, SOD1 expression exhibited a down-regulated trend without significant difference, while SOD2 expression was up-regulated obviously (Fig. 3B), suggesting an adaptive response of K562 cells to ROS levels. Interestingly, in K562/ADR cells, only after treatment with ADR and Nano-Iron, the expression of SOD1 was significantly decreased, while the level of SOD2 was still significantly increased, and both of them are statistical significant. These data indicates that the combination of Nano-Iron and ADR efficiently drives the derivation of ROS, not only disrupts the cytoplasmic antioxidant barrier of SOD1, but also mobilizes SOD2 to cause a mitochondrial response to oxidative stress.

Effects of ADR and Nano-Iron on oxidative stress in K562 and K562/ADR cells

In order to investigate the mechanism of ADR and Nano-Iron in inducing redox imbalance in K562 and K562/ADR cells, we detected the changes of intracellular glutathione levels in the reduced state (GSH) and oxidized state (GSSG), as well as their ratio of GSH/GSSG. In K562 and K562/ADR cells, ADR and Nano-Iron co-treatment both led to the decrease of GSH levels, with the lowest GSH levels in the combined treatment group, indicating that synergistic intervention significantly weakened the anti-oxidative capacity of cells (Fig. 4A, B). At the same time, only GSSG levels in K562 cells was up-regulated, while GSSH levels in K562/ADR cells did not show a significant difference. Meanwhile, immuno-blotting also showed that ADR and Nano-Iron joint intervention diminished the expression level of GPX4 most obviously in K562 and K562/ADR cells[19], suggesting that their combination weakens the ability of K562 and K562/ADR cells to resist ferroptosis by inhibiting GPX4 (Fig. 4C). In other leukemia cells like HL-60, HL-60/ADR (resistant to ADR), MV-4–11 and THP-1 cells, the combination with ADR and Nano-Iron obviously diminished the expression of GPX4, even in ADR-resistant HL-60 cells (HL-60/ADR) (Supplementary Figs. 1A). Additionally, after 24 h of combined treatment, the viability of these cells is significantly reduced (Supplementary Figs. 1B), indicating the enhanced ferroptosis mediated by Nano-Iron improves the drug sensitivity in leukemia cells and drug-resistant cells. Accordingly, in the examination of lipid peroxidation, we found the combination of ADR and Nano-Iron derived the higher levels of MDA in both K562 and K562/ADR cells (Fig. 4D). Discussion

Ferroptosis, as a programmed cell death mode characterized by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation accumulation, has received widespread attention for its potential role in overcoming traditional chemotherapy resistance [6, 19]. Research has shown that the expression of transferrin receptor on the surface of CML cells is significantly up-regulated, enhancing their ability to uptake exogenous iron [20]. The abnormal iron intake and restricted iron excretion will disrupt intracellular iron homeostasis, leading to that CML cells become more vulnerable to oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation, thereby providing a metabolic basis and therapeutic target for the induction of ferroptosis. In fact, inducing the ferroptosis effect not only can promote the cytotoxic effect of ADR, but also potentially break through the traditional resistance barrier, bringing new opportunities for the treatment of drug-resistant CML [21, 22].

Iron oxide nanoparticles (IONPs) are considered an ideal medium for inducing ferroptosis due to their controllable Fenton reaction and good biocompatibility [23]. In the Fenton reaction, Fe2+ can catalyze the generation of hydroxyl radicals from hydrogen peroxide, triggering oxidative stress to cause lipid peroxidation and DNA damage, which is one of the core mechanisms initiating ferroptosis [15]. Evidences have confirmed that IONPs not only promote ROS generation, but also induce depletion of GSH and lipid peroxidation, thereby enhancing the efficacy of various anti-tumor drugs [24, 25]. However, as a novel type of iron oxide nanoparticle doped with Mn and Zn elements, Mn-Zn Ferrite has been found to have potential in assisting tumor therapy in our previous studies, while Yang et al.’s research showed that Mn-Zn Ferrite can effectively release Zn2+, Mn2+ and Fe2+ in vivo to induce Fenton-like reactions and free radicals, achieving the efficient chemotherapy dynamic therapy [16, 17]. Interestingly, CML cells with significant iron metabolism abnormalities are more susceptible to ROS accumulation and oxidative damage upon exogenous iron intervention, making them more sensitive to ferroptosis [11].

Accordingly, we confirmed that the combination of Mn-Zn Ferrite nanoparticles can enhance the inhibition of proliferation and the induction of apoptosis on CML cells, and can improve the sensitivity of ADR in ADR-resistant K562/ADR models. Meanwhile, the augmented levels of ROS, the depletion of reduced glutathione, the elevation of oxidized GSH, the sustained decrease in GSH/GSSG ratio and the higher levels of MDA demonstrated jointly a severe disruption of cellular redox homeostasis, while the excessive depletion of GPX4 also reflected a magnification of ferroptosis. In general, as a novel type of IONPs, Mn-Zn Ferrite nanoparticles combined with ADR not only effectively inhibits the progression of CML, but also can reverse ADR resistance, with the potential for combination therapy in drug-resistant CML.

Conclusion

The combination of ADR and Nano-Iron shows significant synergistic therapeutic potential in CML cells, where Nano-Iron activates the typical ferroptosis pathway by promoting intracellular ROS accumulation, inducing lipid peroxidation (LPO), depleting glutathione, down-regulating GPX4 expression and elevating MDA levels. In vivo, combination therapy with ADR and Nano-Iron significantly inhibited tumor growth, prolonged survival period and diminished the proportion of tumor cell infiltration in peripheral blood. Therefore, the combined strategy of ADR and Nano-Iron is expected to become an important candidate for enhancing leukemia treatment response and overcoming drug resistance, with good translational prospects.

Genetically predicted immune cell-mediated causal associations between 473 gut microbiome species and neurodevelopmental disorders
Authors: Huimin Yang, Wanli Gong, Yaqi Hou, Yang Wang, Peifeng He, Qi Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNeurodevelopmental disorders (NDD) are a class of complex diseases whose underlying pathogenesis has not been fully elucidated. Previous observational studies have reported that gut microbiota dysbiosis may be one of the potential risk factors for the development of NDD, however, causal relationships and the potential mediating role of immune cells have yet to be established.MethodsBidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization (TSMR) and two-step Mendelian randomization (two-step MR) methods, mainly using inverse variance weighting (IVW), were used to explore the potential associations between gut microbiota and NDD, as well as the mediating role of immune cells in the causal pathway. Bayesian weighted Mendelian randomization (BWMR) method was additionally used to validate positive causality. Sensitivity analyses under different statistical assumptions were conducted to assess association robustness.ResultsBased on the IVW and BWMR methods, we identified 5 and 4 gut microbiota taxonomic units exhibiting significant causal associations with ADHD and ASD, respectively. These included the negative modifying effect of family Syntrophorhabdaceae abundance on ADHD risk (OR: 0.4440, 95 % CI = 0.2620–0.7523, P = 0.0025), the significant protective effect of species ER4 sp002437735 on ASD (OR: 0.2996, 95 % CI = 0.1504–0.5968, P = 0.0004) and the stimulatory effect of species Agathobacter sp000434275 on ASD (OR: 1.7426, 95 % CI = 1.1496–2.6415, P = 0.0089). P values derived from Cochran's Q test, Egger's regression intercept test, and global test for pleiotropy and outliers did not suggest statistical significance. Further two-step MR revealed that Syntrophorhabdaceae could reduce the risk of ADHD, with Unswitched memory B cell %lymphocyte mediating 6.35 %. Additionally, 2.49 % and 1.91 % of the causal associations between ER4 sp002437735 and Agathobacter sp000434275 with ASD were mediated by BAFF-R⁺ switched memory B cells and activated/secreting CD4 regulatory T cell percentage of CD4 regulatory T cells, respectively.ConclusionOur study reveals direct evidence that NDD is influenced by specific gut microbiota, while its causal effects may be mediated by immune cell traits.

Neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs) are a group of common conditions that affect brain development and function, primarily including attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and autism spectrum disorder (ASD). The main clinical features of ADHD are inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity (Dougnon and Matsui, 2022). ADHD is highly prevalent in children and adolescents, with an estimated incidence of 25–50 %, and affects approximately 2–4 % of adults (Weibel et al., 2020), indicating that it can persist across the lifespan. Current treatment approaches for ADHD include dietary interventions, behavioral therapy, pharmacotherapy, and central nervous system stimulants (Caye et al., 2019). On the other hand, ASD is a heritable neurodevelopmental disorder defined by impairments in social communication and repetitive stereotyped behaviors. The prevalence of ASD in children exceeds 1 % (Hughes et al., 2023). The specific symptom profiles and severity can vary among affected individuals. Diagnostic tools employed for ASD include the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule, Childhood Autism Rating Scale, parental reports of developmental status, neuroimaging techniques like MRI, and machine learning approaches (Nogay and Adeli, 2020, Okoye et al., 2023). While the underlying pathogenic mechanisms of NDD, which severely impact cognitive, social, and behavioral development, remain incompletely understood, accumulating evidence implicates gut microbiome dysbiosis as a important underlying etiology (Wang et al., 2023, Socala et al., 2021).

The human gut harbors a vast and complex ecosystem of microorganisms, including bacteria, archaea, viruses, and fungi, collectively known as the gut microbiome. The phyla Firmicutes and Bacteroidetes predominate in terms of overall composition (Bringer et al., 2022). Recent studies have shown that dysbiosis of the gut microbiome is closely associated with the development of various disorders, such as obesity (Cuevas-Sierra et al., 2019), diabetes (Iatcu et al., 2021) and neuropsychiatric conditions (Eicher and Mohajeri, 2022) etc. However, current evidence linking the gut microbiome to NDD is primarily derived from association studies (Strati et al., 2017, Fung et al., 2017), where correlation does not necessarily imply causation. Therefore, elucidating the true causal relationships between the gut microbiome and NDD, as well as their potential mechanistic underpinnings, is crucial for understanding disease etiology and developing novel interventional strategies.

The immune system has been shown to be a key intermediary pathway through which the gut microbiome influences neurological function (Fung et al., 2017). The gut microbiome can regulate the activation and function of host immune cells, thereby impacting systemic inflammation and neurotransmitter homeostasis, which in turn interferes with the development of neural circuits and brain regions implicated in NDD (Ge et al., 2021). Erny et al. (2015) demonstrated that the activation state of microglia, the resident immune cells of the brain, may be influenced by the gut microbiome. Germ-free mice exhibited higher microglial activation levels, which could be reversed by supplementation with microbial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids. Moreover, another animal study revealed that administration of the commensal bacterium Bacteroides fragilis NCTC 9343 ameliorated immune dysregulation (elevated IL-6 levels), gut permeability, and associated behavioral deficits in a mouse model of ASD (Hsiao et al., 2013). These findings provide compelling evidence that the gut microbiome may contribute to NDDs pathogenesis through immunomodulatory mechanisms.

To avoid the effects of confounding bias and reverse causality in epidemiological and environmental exposure studies, Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis has emerged as a powerful approach. MR has been widely applied to investigating the causal relationships between exposures and outcomes across various diseases (Larsson et al., 2023, Chen et al., 2022a, Fei et al., 2024). The method treats genetic variants that have been identified to be associated with exposure as instrumental variables (IV) in place of the actual level of exposure, thereby assessing their causal impact on outcome (Birney, 2022). In this study, we aim to leverage MR methods to evaluate the causal associations between 473 gut microbiome species and NDD, as well as the potential mediating roles of immune cells in these pathways. The findings are anticipated to shed light on the mechanistic involvement of the gut microbiome in NDDs pathogenesis, providing novel scientific rationale and avenues for the prevention and treatment of these disorders.

Materials and methods

Overall design

Leveraging the latest genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary data from large-scale studies, we comprehensively investigated the bidirectional causal relationships between the gut microbiome and two major NDD, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and autism spectrum disorder (ASD), within a two-sample Mendelian randomization (TSMR) framework. Meanwhile, Bayesian weighted Mendelian randomization (BWMR) was utilized to verify the potential influential role between the two. In addition, we assessed the mediating role of immune cell characteristics in the regulation of NDD by gut microbiota using two-step Mendelian randomization (two-step MR) analysis, quantifying their proportional contributions via the product-of-coefficients method. Data acquisition

All data utilized in this study were obtained from public repositories. The latest genome-wide summary statistics for gut microbiome traits were accessed from the GWAS Catalog (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/). Qin et al. (2022) conducted a large population-based cohort study involving 5959 participants from the Finnish FINRISK 2002 cohort, where fecal DNA was extracted and subjected to 16S rRNA gene sequencing to quantify the abundances of 2801 common microbial taxa. Genome-wide association analyses were performed against 7967866 human genetic variants, ultimately identifying 473 gut microbiome features across multiple taxonomic levels, with species representing the lowest considered rank.

On the basis of comprehensive immune trait phenotyping, a large-scale GWAS study involving 3757 Sardinians provided authoritative summary data on the genetics of immune traits (Orru et al., 2020). This data set is available from the comprehensive public access Open GWAS (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/). Using flow cytometry, this study quantified 731 immune cell phenotypes, spanning B cells, dendritic cells (DCs), mature T cell stages, monocytes, myeloid cells, T/B/NK cell (TBNK) combination panels, and regulatory T cell (Treg) panels, comprising 539 quantitative and 192 relative count traits. Genotyping and imputation were performed, identifying approximately 20 million SNP (Orru et al., 2020).

Estimates of associations between genetic variants and NDD are available from the emerging Finnish database Finn Gen (https://www.finngen.fi/). The GWAS pooled data for ADHD were obtained from 407941 Finnish individuals, with 59.68 % being female patients. For ASD, 56.66 % of the 302,525 samples were male patients, with an average age at first diagnosis concentrated between 10 and 20 years. Selection of instrumental variables

As a prevalent form of genetic variation in the human genome, single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNP) were employed as instrumental variables (IV) to probe causal relationships in this study. To ensure robust causal inference while maximizing genomic information, we adopted a genome-wide significance threshold of P < 1 × 10−5 for SNP selection with reference to the previous studies (Li et al., 2022). Additionally, linkage disequilibrium (LD), reflecting non-random associations between genetic loci, was accounted for to mitigate potential confounding bias. Variants exhibiting strong LD (r2 > 0.001) within a 10,000 base-pair window around the most significant SNP were pruned to retain independent molecular markers (Chen et al., 2022a). The harmonise_data() function from the TwoSampleMR R package with action= 2 was utilized to ensure consistent effect allele orientations. The F-statistic was calculated for each genetic variant according to the formula F=r2×(N−2)/(1−r2), where r2 represents the proportion of exposure variance explained by the IV, and N denotes the sample size of the exposure GWAS. Potential weak instruments were removed based on an F < 10 threshold (Burgess and Thompson, 2011). Finally, the LD trait feature from the LDlink website (https://ldlink.nih.gov/) was employed to exclude SNP exhibiting potential associations with other complex traits, minimizing confounding from pleiotropic genetic effects.

Mendelian randomization

This study adhered rigorously to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology Using Mendelian Randomization (STROBE-MR) guidelines to ensure high-quality reporting (Skrivankova et al., 2021). MR analysis was performed using the R package TwoSampleMR, with the inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method employed as the primary approach for investigating potential causal relationships. Under the assumption of all valid and independent instrumental variables (IV), IVW provides consistent and efficient estimates (Lin et al., 2021). Complementary methods, including Egger regression, weighted median, simple mode, and weighted mode estimators, were utilized for supplementary validation. Notably, Egger regression relies on weaker instrument strength assumptions, allowing for some genetic variants to be invalid IV while still performing well (Bowden et al., 2015). It is worth mentioning that we incorporated BWMR as an additional approach to further reinforce the robustness of the identified causal associations between the gut microbiome and NDD. The main strengths of BWMR include robustness to pleiotropy, enhanced statistical power, and the ability to provide posterior inferences when utilizing summary-level data from large GWAS (Zhao et al., 2020). On the basis of reliability of the inverse MR association, mediation analyses were performed using a two-step MR design to stepwise assess the potential roles between immune cell phenotype and NDD and gut microbiota abundance and immune cells. Subsequently, indirect causal effects were determined based on the coefficient product method, and the mediating proportion of immune cell phenotype was determined by dividing the indirect effect by the total effect.

In addition, to control for the incidence of false positives, Bonferroni correction was applied to the significance threshold (0.05/N), where N represents the number of independent tests. If P < 0.05 but did not meet the corrected threshold, it was considered suggestive evidence supporting a potential causal relationship (Curtin and Schulz, 1998). For dichotomous variables (NDD), the results were presented as odds ratios (OR), while the results for continuous variables (gut microbiota and immune cells) were expressed as β-values. 95 % confidence intervals (CI) were calculated for all effect estimates to aid in the interpretation of the strength of causal associations.

Sensitivity analysis

To comprehensively assess the stability of the causal effect estimates, multiple quality control measures were implemented. First, leave-one-out cross-validation was performed using the leave_one_out() function, iteratively excluding one SNP at a time and re-evaluating the causal effect to compare its influence on the overall estimate. Second, Cochran's Q statistic and its derived p-value were calculated based on the IVW method to test for heterogeneity in the causal association estimates. Finally, the Egger regression intercept test and the global pleiotropy residual sum and outlier (PRESSO) test were employed to detect potential horizontal pleiotropy bias. When the p-values from the heterogeneity and pleiotropy tests exceeded 0.05, it indicated that these potential biasing sources had minimal impact on the overall estimates and could be disregarded when interpreting the results.

Result

Determination of instrumental variables

Under the pre-specified IV selection criteria（P < 1 × 10−5、kb= 10000、r2= 0.001）, the genetic variant loci associated with gut microbiota abundance with potential causal relationship with NDD were identified separately. The number of associated gut microbiome SNP ranged from 8 to 27, and none of them was found to be statistically weakly IV (F > 10). Details of all filtered SNP are shown in Supplementary Table 1.

For ADHD, a total of 2 families, 1 genus, and 2 species-level taxa were identified with significant causal effects. Genetically predicted higher abundances of the family Syntrophorhabdaceae (OR: 0.4440, 95 % CI = 0.2620–0.7523, P = 0.0025) and genus Veillonella (OR: 0.6702, 95 % CI = 0.5225–0.8598, P = 0.0016) were associated with decreased ADHD risk. Conversely, elevated levels of the family Mycoplasmoidaceae significantly increased the risk of developing ADHD by 93.02 % (OR: 1.9302, 95 % CI = 1.2654–2.9443, P = 0.0023), corroborated by MR-Egger analyses (Supplementary Table 2). When assessing the causal effects of gut microbiota on ASD, 1 family, 1 genus, and 3 species-level taxa exhibited potential causality. Of these notable taxa, the family Poseidoniaceae was affiliated with the phylum Ascomycota, and the remaining three taxa belonged to the phylum Thick-walled Bacteria, with the exception of CAG-273 sp003507395, which did not directly reflect its taxonomic status. Among them, increased abundance of the genus Gordonibacter was significantly and positively associated with the risk of ASD (OR: 1.9920, 95 % CI = 1.1458–3.4632, P = 0.0146), whereas elevated abundance of the family Poseidoniaceae was significantly protective against ASD occurrence (OR: 0.1466, 95 % CI = 0.0306–0.7035, P = 0.0164). Although the statistical significance of the above causal valuation did not reach the Bonferroni-corrected level (P < 0.05/473), it still suggests a potential association.

In the sensitivity analysis, the results of Cochran's Q test, Egger's intercept and PRESSO global test indicated that there was no interference from heterogeneity and pleiotropy in this causal valuation (Table 2). Verification of Bayesian weighted Mendelian randomization

Given the strength of the BWMR method in assessing causal relationships between genetic instruments and outcome traits, we conducted complementary analyses to validate and reinforce the causal associations between the gut microbiome and NDD, compared to the IVW approach.

Focusing on the top 5 most statistically significant taxa, the BWMR analyses for ADHD replicated the potentially causal taxa identified by IVW, with high concordance in the directionality of risk estimates between the two methods (Fig. 3). These findings further substantiate the existence of significant causal links between the abundance of specific gut microbes and ADHD risk. For ASD, in addition to recapitulating the 4 significant taxa from IVW, BWMR uniquely implicated an increased abundance of the genus Turicibacter as being significantly associated with higher ASD risk (OR: 1.9900, 95 % CI = 1.1632–3.4042, P = 0.0120). Two-step Mendelian randomization analysis

Causal relationship between immune cells with ADHD and ASD

To evaluate the mediating roles of immune cells in the associations between gut microbiome and NDD, we first employed IVW analyses to assess the causal relationships between 731 immune cell traits with ADHD and ASD. A total of 33 immune cell characteristics associated with ADHD were screened for lymphocytes (T cells (n = 16), B cells (n = 8)), monocytes (n = 4), NK cells (n = 3), and other cell types (n = 2) (Supplementary Table 6). Of these, 17 genetically predicted immune cell phenotypes exhibited risk-increasing effects for ADHD. Additionally, higher IgD+ CD24 + B cell percentage of lymphocytes was associated with a 15.1 % reduced ADHD occurrence (OR: 0.8597, 95 % CI = 0.7646–0.9666, P = 0.0115). For ASD, 35 immune cell-associated traits, including lymphocytes (B cells (n = 12), T cells (n = 11)), monocytes (n = 7), NK cells (n = 3), dendritic cells (n = 1), and other cell type (n = 1) were identified (Supplementary Table 7), of which 23 immune cell traits were examined for their protective effects against ASD. Notably, genetic predisposition to higher activated and resting CD4 regulatory T cell absolute counts was associated with a 23.67 % increased ASD risk (OR: 1.2671, 95 % CI = 1.0045–1.5982, P = 0.0457). No evidence of horizontal pleiotropy or heterogeneity biases was observed for the causal associations between immune cell traits and NDD (Supplementary Table 8).

Causal relationship between gut microbiota and immune cells

Building upon the 9 gut microbiome taxa and 68 immune cell traits (ADHD n = 33, ASD n = 35) previously identified as associated with NDD, we performed additional MR analyses to detect potential causal relationships between the gut microbiome and immune cells (Fig. 5). The results showed that the Mycoplasmoidaceae family exerted suggestive negative regulatory effects on CD25⁺ activated and secreting CD4⁺ regulatory T cells in ADHD (β: −0.4359, 95 % CI = −0.8604–0.0113, P = 0.0442). That is, the former may play a critical inhibitory role in the proliferation or induction of the latter. It is worth noting that in this study, the increase in family Syntrophorhabdaceae abundance was found to be positively correlated with the levels of Unswitched memory B cells (% of lymphocytes) and FSC-A of HLA-DR⁺ CD8⁺ T cells, with a significant increase in the level of cell development by 61.09 % and 78.86 %, respectively. Regarding ASD, 2 microbiome species exhibited potential causal relationships with 3 immune traits (Fig. 5). Specifically, genetically predicted ER4 sp002437735 abundance was highly correlated with BAFF-R⁺ switched memory B cells levels (β: 0.2869, 95 % CI = 0.0078–0.5660, P = 0.0439). Neither Cochran's Q test under the IVW method nor its derived P-value suggested that the heterogeneous effect of SNP reached a statistically significant level. Calculation of potential mediating effects

Building upon the TSMR analyses, we screened 3 potential immune cell phenotype mediators for ADHD and ASD, respectively. We focused on evaluating their mediating roles and quantifying the proportions mediated in the causal associations between the gut microbiome and NDD.

For ADHD,

CD25⁺ activated and secreting CD4⁺ regulatory T cells and FSC-A on HLA-DR+ CD8 + T cells exhibited associations opposite to the expected causal pathways and were therefore excluded from further mediation analyses. Regarding ASD, activated & resting CD4 regulatory T cell absolute counts displayed a trend inconsistent with the hypothesized causal pathway of Agathobacter sp000434275 positively regulating ASD risk, and was thus not considered a potential mediator.

Therefore, after removing the effect of confounding factors, Unswitched memory B cells (% of lymphocytes) was identified in this study as a mediating variable affecting the risk of developing ADHD in the family Syntrophorhabdaceae (Fig. 6). The product-of-coefficients method estimated this indirect effect to account for 6.35 % of the total causal effect, with a mediation value of 0.0516 (Table 4). Concurrently, it was observed that elevated relative abundance of Agathobacter sp000434275 was significantly associated with increased levels of Activated & secreting CD4⁺ regulatory T cells (% of CD4⁺ Tregs), which in turn showed a positive correlation with an elevated risk of ASD incidence association (Fig. 6). Discussion

Changes in the gut microbiome can influence brain plasticity through various pathways, including regulating gene expression, producing neuroactive molecules, and modulating microglial activation (Damiani et al., 2023). Some clinical studies have shown that restoring gut microbiome balance through non-invasive approaches such as microbiome transplantation (Li et al., 2024), dietary interventions (Ristori et al., 2019) and probiotic supplementation (Kalenik et al., 2021) can improve NDD symptoms. Previously, a study with a relatively limited sample size (n = 40) reported differences in the relative abundance of gut microbiota between ASD patients and healthy individuals (Strati et al., 2017). However, the results were influenced by various confounding factors. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically evaluate the causal associations between 473 gut microbiome species and NDD in a large sample. Previous research has revealed the interplay among gut microbiome, immune cells, and NDD (Fung et al., 2017), but has been limited to observational findings. By leveraging rich public genetic data resources and employing the two-step MR analysis framework, this study comprehensively explored the currently most comprehensive bidirectional causal effects between gut microbiome and ADHD and ASD, as well as the potential mediating roles of immune cell traits, which have not been clearly elucidated.

The gut-brain axis refers to the bidirectional physiological and chemical signaling pathways between the gut and brain. At the gut end of this axis, the gut microbiome has received widespread attention as a key regulator (Wang et al., 2023). Disruption of the microbiome-gut-brain axis has been found to be associated with various diseases, such as inflammatory bowel disease (Lavelle and Sokol, 2020), depression (Socala et al., 2021), ADHD (Eicher and Mohajeri, 2022) and ASD (Socala et al., 2021) etc. In this study, after excluding potential confounding biases, we found that increased abundance of the family Syntrophorhabdaceae was significantly associated with a reduced risk of ADHD. This family belongs to the phylum Proteobacteria, which are anaerobic bacteria capable of cooperative metabolic degradation of complex organic matter with other microbes (Kuroda et al., 2022). An observational study involving 172 Alzheimer's disease cases found that with increasing severity of cognitive impairment, the relative abundance of Proteobacteria in the oral cavity gradually decreased (Chen et al., 2022b). However, there are currently no reports on the composition and distribution differences of the Syntrophorhabdaceae family between ADHD patients and healthy individuals. The metabolic activities of this family within the gut and its specific association mechanisms with ADHD remain to be further explored.

ER4 sp002437735 is an unclassified strain belonging to the species Eubacterium rectale. Yang et al. (2020) conducted an integrated analysis of the gut virome, bacterial microbiome, and fecal metabolome in 236 patients with major depressive disorder (NDD) and 75 healthy controls, identifying the genus Eubacterium as negatively regulating NDD, thus being regarded as a "psychobiotic". A multi-omics study based on the "Lunar Palace 365" experiment also revealed that Eubacterium rectale can modulate mood through pathways associated with nervous system function, and further animal experiments substantiated its positive regulatory effects on mood (Hao et al., 2023). On the other hand, Jinato T (Jinato et al., 2024) employed bioinformatic approaches to analyze the fecal microbiome composition of 67 liver cancer patients, discovering a positive correlation between the abundance of the genus Agathobacter and viral hepatocellular carcinoma. Although direct evidence regarding the associations of ER4 sp002437735 and Agathobacter sp000434275 with ASD is currently scarce, the existing evidence of the former's relationship with mental disorders nevertheless provides robust corroborating support for the findings of the present MR analysis.

The gut microbiome is generally considered to regulate neurodevelopment through three pathways, among which the immune pathway is included (Fung et al., 2017). In a recent Mendelian randomization study, Fei et al. (2024) revealed the potential causal association between unswitched memory B cells and ankylosing spondylitis. Another study, through flow cytometry analysis of peripheral blood B cell subsets, found that the unswitched memory B cell subset might play a regulatory role in systemic autoinflammatory diseases such as Still's disease (Fang et al., 2023). BAFF-R, the B cell activating factor receptor, is a key regulator of B cell proliferation and maturation (Luo et al., 2023). In vitro cell experiments and mouse xenograft models demonstrated that its monoclonal antibody AR12 could effectively kill mantle cells and lymphoma cells, while exhibiting potent antitumor activity and safety both in vitro and in vivo (Zhang et al., 2021). Studies have indicated that BAFF-R potentially exerts negative regulatory effects in systemic lupus erythematosus (Alvarez et al., 2023) and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (Ran et al., 2024), and is closely associated with neuroinflammation (Pranzatelli et al., 2013). In certain mental disorders, dysfunction of helper T cells and regulatory T cells may lead to enhanced immune responses, potentially triggering neuroimmunotoxicity (Rachayon et al., 2024). Such excessive immune reactions could adversely affect the nervous system, including neuronal damage and neurotransmitter imbalance.

The immune response is considered a major pathway through which the gut microbiome influences the host central nervous system (Round et al., 2010). In addition to the potential regulatory pathways of gut microbiome abundance and immune cell levels mentioned above, we further demonstrated that Unswitched memory B cells (% of lymphocytes) in the lymphocyte panel might mediate the association between ER4 sp002437735 and ADHD.Simultaneously, we identified the potential regulatory roles of the expression level of BAFF-R⁺ switched memory B cells and the proportion of Activated & secreting CD4 regulatory T cells among CD4 regulatory T cells. Previous studies have confirmed the role of the gut microbiome in mediating the effects of immune cells on neurological disorders (Evrensel and Tarhan, 2021, Marano et al., 2023). For instance, specific strains such as Lactobacillus rhamnosus can activate mononuclear cells in mice, inducing inflammatory responses in the brain, which in turn affect neurotransmitters and neural circuits, ultimately leading to depression-like behavioral changes (Kelly et al., 2016).

Employing an MR study design, this research evaluated the causal relationship between the gut microbiome and NDD, as well as the mediating roles of immune phenotypes, based on reducing reverse causation and removing the influence of potential confounders. By leveraging existing large-scale GWAS data, the need for separate long-term follow-up studies was eliminated, saving time and resources. However, four limitations cannot be ignored. Firstly, the NDD sample data used in this study were derived from European populations, and the generalizability of the causal relationships to other ethnic groups remains to be validated. Secondly, to include more genetic variants associated with the gut microbiome, the IV selection threshold was relaxed in this analysis, which may increase the risk of violating the MR assumption of strong relevance. Nevertheless, screening for the F-statistic (F>10) for each SNP has eliminated the interference of weak instrumental variables. Finally, in determining the mediating effects, the presence of a low mediating proportion of immune cell typing in both groups suggests the existence of other unknown mediators.

Although our study, through Mendelian randomization and mediation analyses, revealed the potential role of specific immune cell subsets in the pathogenesis linking the gut microbiota and NDDs, these findings remain hypothetical and require further experimental validation. Future studies could employ in vitro experiments using specific immune cell subsets, such as T cells, B cells, NK cells, and dendritic cells, to evaluate the regulatory effects of gut microbiota–derived metabolites on immune function. Additionally, animal models could be utilized to explore the causal relationships between immune cells and the gut microbiota in behavioral regulation. Such investigations would not only help elucidate the mechanistic basis but also provide potential insights for early risk prediction and immune-targeted intervention strategies.

This study aimed to investigate the causal associations between the gut microbiota and ADHD and ASD, as well as the mediating role of immune cells. The results indicated that Syntrophorhabdaceae could reduce the risk of ADHD, with Unswitched memory B cells (% of lymphocytes) mediating 6.35 %. Although this represents a small proportion of the overall risk, these specific B cell subsets may play a critical role in ADHD pathogenesis through immune regulation. They may offer potential targets for immunomodulatory interventions and early risk prediction. In addition, in the causal association between the gut microbiome and ASD, BAFF-R⁺ switched memory B cells and the proportion of Activated & secreting CD4⁺ regulatory T cells (% of CD4⁺ Tregs) mediated 2.49 % and 1.91 % of the effect, respectively. Although these proportions are relatively small, they highlight specific immune pathways as key points of intervention through which the gut microbiome may influence ASD risk. These findings not only revealed connections among gut microbiota, immune cells, and NDDs, but also provided new perspectives for understanding the underlying mechanisms and developing preventive or therapeutic strategies. Future studies using in vitro experiments or animal models could provide further direct evidence to support the causal relationships observed here, laying a solid foundation for subsequent immune-targeted or microbiota-directed interventions.

A hepatocyte-derived GPNMB–RYK signaling axis in MASH progression
Authors: Yuanyuan Fan, Cynthia Ju
Published: 2026

Xi, Y., Zeng, W., Luo, J. et al. RYK is a GPNMB receptor that drives MASH. Nature 652, 703–711 (2026). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10160-z

Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH) represents a growing global health burden, which is predicted to rise from 14.9 million to 23.2 million in the United States from 2020 to 2050.1 MASH can be conceptualized as a multi-layered condition involving at least four interconnected elements: systemic metabolic stress (e.g. insulin resistance and adipose dysfunction), hepatocellular lipid accumulation and stress, intrahepatic signaling and cellular crosstalk, and the downstream emergence of inflammation and fibrosis. From a therapeutic perspective, progress has been uneven. Clinical trials targeting inflammation and fibrosis have yielded mixed outcomes. For example, simtuzumab (anti-LOXL2 antibody, targeting fibrosis), selonsertib (ASK1 inhibitor, targeting inflammation), and cenicriviroc (CCR2/CCR5 dual antagonist, targeting inflammation and fibrosis) have all failed to meet histological endpoints in phase II and III trials.2 In contrast, agents that reduce hepatic lipid burden or improve systemic metabolism, such as GLP-1 receptor agonists and THR-β agonists, have demonstrated clinical efficacy, reinforcing the central role of metabolic stress in disease initiation.3,4 These observations raise an important question: what are the liver-intrinsic mechanisms that translate and amplify metabolic signals into progressive liver injury? A hepatocyte-derived GPNMB–RYK signaling axis drives metabolic reprogramming and MASH progression.

Schematic illustration of the hepatocyte-intrinsic GPNMB–RYK signaling pathway in MASH. In response to metabolic stress, hepatocytes upregulate GPNMB, which undergoes proteolytic cleavage by ADAM10 to release its extracellular domain (G-ECD). The secreted G-ECD functions as a pathogenic ligand that binds to the receptor tyrosine kinase–like orphan receptor RYK on hepatocytes. This ligand–receptor interaction activates ERK1/2 signaling, leading to transcriptional induction of key metabolic programs, including PPARγ–CD36–mediated fatty acid uptake and SREBP1c-driven de novo lipogenesis. These processes collectively enhance hepatic lipid accumulation and promote downstream inflammation and fibrosis, thereby driving MASH progression. Therapeutic strategies targeting the GPNMB–RYK axis – such as vaccination, neutralizing antibodies, shRNA, and GalNAc-siRNA – have demonstrated efficacy in preventing and reversing disease in preclinical models. GPNMB, glycoprotein nonmetastatic melanoma protein B; MASH, metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis.

A recent study by Xi et al., published in Nature, provides new insights into this question by identifying a hepatocyte-derived signaling pathway that promotes intrahepatic disease progression.5 Through transcriptomic analysis of multiple MASH mouse models and five independent human GSE datasets, the authors identified glycoprotein nonmetastatic melanoma protein B (GPNMB) as one of the most upregulated genes in MASH. Although GPNMB has previously been studied as a multifunctional membrane protein expressed in diverse cell types, including macrophages, dendritic cells, tumor cells, osteoblasts and neurons, its role has largely been linked to immune regulation and cancer biology. This study, however, reveals a fundamentally different function.

Specifically, the authors demonstrate that the secreted extracellular domain of GPNMB (G-ECD), rather than its membrane-bound form, is the key pathogenic driver of MASH. While it was known that GPNMB can be shed by ADAM10,6 the functional importance of its cleaved product had not been appreciated. Using genetic models with altered shedding capacity, the study shows that G-ECD is both necessary and sufficient to drive steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis. This finding represents a conceptual shift, redefining GPNMB from a marker to a secreted functional ligand that actively drives disease progression.

Mechanistically, the study identifies receptor tyrosine kinase (RYK) as the functional receptor for G-ECD through an unbiased screen of membrane proteins, followed by rigorous validation. Binding of G-ECD to the WNT inhibitory factor (WIF) domain of RYK activates ERK1/2 signaling, leading to transcriptional induction of PPARγ–CD36-mediated fatty acid uptake and SREBP1c-dependent lipogenesis. Notably, this pathway enhances lipid accumulation under the same metabolic input conditions, suggesting that hepatocytes can actively reinforce and amplify metabolic stress, rather than simply responding to it. This finding refines our understanding of how metabolic stress is translated within the liver.

Beyond mechanistic insights, the study also demonstrates strong translational potential. Multiple independent strategies targeting the GPNMB-RYK axis, including vaccination, shRNA, neutralizing antibodies, and GalNAc-siRNA, consistently attenuated disease development and improved established MASH in preclinical models. In addition, circulating G-ECD levels correlate with disease severity, suggesting that this pathway may serve both as a therapeutic target and as a biomarker-defined disease axis.

Despite these advances, several important questions remain. First, the regulation of GPNMB shedding, potentially mediated by ADAM family proteases, remains incompletely understood. Notably, ADAM9, ADAM10, and ADAM17 are upregulated in MASH liver tissues,7 while RECK,8 an inhibitor of ADAM10, is downregulated, suggesting enhanced shedding activity in disease. Second, the contribution of extrahepatic sources of GPNMB, particularly from adipose tissue and immune cells, warrants further investigation, especially given the systemic metabolic improvements observed with whole-body Gpnmb deletion. Third, RYK is broadly expressed and implicated in multiple signaling pathways, raising the possibility that G-ECD-RYK signaling may have context-dependent effects beyond hepatocytes, which warrants further investigation.

In summary, this study identifies the GPNMB–RYK axis as a novel intrahepatic signaling pathway that amplifies metabolic stress and drives MASH progression. More broadly, it refines our understanding of disease pathogenesis by highlighting the role of liver-intrinsic mechanisms in shaping disease outcomes. These findings suggest that effective therapeutic strategies for MASH may require not only targeting systemic metabolic dysfunction, but also addressing intrahepatic signaling pathways that reinforce and sustain disease progression. Furthermore, given the established roles of GPNMB in immune regulation and cancer biology, understanding how therapeutic targeting of this pathway may influence these processes will be an important area for future investigation.

Sharp coefficient bounds for a class of symmetric starlike functions involving the balloon shape domain
Authors: Bilal Khan, Jianhua Gong, Muhammad Ghaffar Khan, Fairouz Tchier
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Recent research has extensively explored classes of starlike and convex functions across various domains. This study introduces a novel class of symmetric starlike functions with respect to symmetric points and associated with the balloon shape domain. We establish the explicit representation of all functions in this class. We determine the sharp bounds for the initial four coefficients, the sharp Fekete-Szegö inequality, and the sharp bound for the second Hankel determinant for every function in the newly defined class. Furthermore, we present the new findings on the inverse and logarithmic coefficients sharp bounds for all functions belonging to this class.

Introduction

This section presents important basic concepts to help us understand the main findings. We employ A for the family of all holomorphic functions ω(κ) defined on the open unit disk Λ with ω(0)=0 and ω′(0)=1, whereΛ={κ∈C:|κ|<1}. Thus, the Taylor series for each function belonging to the family A is in the following form.(1.1)ω(κ)=κ+∑∞n=2dnκn κ∈Λ. We utilize S to denote the subclass comprising all univalent functions in A.

For two holomorphic functions ω1 and ω2 in the open unit disk Λ, we say that ω1 is subordinated to ω2, written as ω1(κ)≺ω2(κ), if there exists a Schwarz function u(κ), which is an holomorphic function in Λ with u(0)=0 and |u(κ)|≤1, such thatω1(κ)=ω2(u(κ)). Furthermore, if two functions ω1 and ω2 are holomorphic in Λ and ω2 is univalent, then ω1 is subordinated to ω2 if and only if(1.2)ω1(Λ)⊆ω2(Λ)andω1(0)=ω2(0). In 1916, Bieberbach [1] proposed the coefficient conjecture, a pivotal development that brought attention to the subject as a potential area for future research. In geometric function theory, 1985 marked a crucial moment with de Branges' [2] groundbreaking proof of a long-standing hypothesis. This achievement culminated nearly seven decades of intensive research efforts by mathematicians worldwide. From 1916 to 1985, the period saw significant advancements in our understanding of analytic functions, particularly within the S class of normalized univalent functions. As scholars grappled with this complex problem, they uncovered a rich tapestry of subfamilies within S, each characterized by unique geometric properties in the complex plane. These discoveries have continued influencing modern research directions, as evidenced in recent publications such as [3], [4], [5].

Among the subclasses of univalent functions, two categories within S stand out for their exceptional significance: the starlike functions, denoted by S⁎, and the convex functions, represented as K. These families are considered foundational subsets of S, owing to their fundamental characteristics and wide-ranging applications in complex analysis. From an analytical perspective,S⁎={ω∈A:ℜ(κω′(κ)ω(κ))>0},andK={ω∈A:ℜ((κω′(κ))′ω(κ))>0}.

This resulting region exhibits symmetry when reflected across the real axis. There are many subclasses of A generalized by using S⁎(ϕ). New class of symmetric starlike functions

This section defines the following new subclass of A.(2.1)SSB⁎={ω∈A:2κω′(κ)ω(κ)−ω(−κ)≺21+κ1+e−κ}.

Geometrically, the following balloon function (introduced by Ahmed et al. [17])(2.2)21+κ1+e−κ maps Λ onto a balloon shape domain, and hence each function ω in the new class SSB⁎ maps Λ into a balloon shape symmetric domain about the real axis.

One can observe that if ω(κ)∈SSB⁎ then the following function ψ(κ) is starlike with respect to the balloon function (2.2), and hence ψ(κ) is a starlike function in S⁎:(2.3)ψ(κ)=ω(κ)−ω(−κ)2. Also, Sakaguchi [18] introduced the class of all starlike functions with respect to symmetric points, and SSB⁎ is a subclass of Sakaguchi's class. We refer to each ω(κ)∈SSB⁎ as a symmetric starlike function.

Discussion

This article presents a novel class of symmetric starlike functions with respect to symmetric points, with a focus on balloon-shaped domains. While previous studies have made significant strides for various classes of starlike and convex functions, such as those by [13], [14], [17], our research introduces a distinct perspective by concentrating on symmetric starlike functions associated with symmetric points and balloon-shaped domains.

Most prior research has been centered around classical definitions of starlike functions without incorporating the notion of symmetry with respect to specific points or considering the unique geometry of balloon-shaped domains. For example, while [17] conducted an extensive investigation into general starlike functions, their work did not explore the influence of symmetric points or the distinct geometric features of balloon-shaped domains. Similarly, [16] examined convex functions and their associated properties but did not address the symmetry aspects of function behavior within these specialized domains.

In contrast, our research fills this gap by establishing a formal framework for studying symmetric starlike functions within our newly defined class. We explicitly represent these functions and rigorously demonstrate their symmetry properties, advancing the field beyond current literature.

A key contribution of this work is the derivation of sharp bounds for the first four coefficients and the establishment of a Fekete-Szegö sharp inequality, representing substantial progress in geometric function theory. In addition, our results on the sharp bounds for the second Hankel determinant and inverse and logarithmic coefficients offer novel insights that push the boundaries of existing research in this area.

Our findings resonate with the broader goals of studies such as [6] and [7], which illustrate the ongoing relevance of exploring diverse function classes in geometric function theory. However, by emphasizing the role of symmetry and the geometric implications of balloon-shaped domains, our work extends these investigations in a new direction, underscoring the importance of symmetry in geometric function theory.

By addressing this previously underexplored aspect, we not only bridge a critical gap in the literature but also pave the way for future research into symmetric behaviors across different geometric shapes and function classes. We hope this study will encourage further exploration of symmetry in geometric function theory, leading to new developments and applications in mathematical analysis.

Conclusion

Our current investigation delved into a novel class of symmetric starlike functions with respect to symmetric points associated with the balloon-shaped domain. We emphasize establishing the best precise bounds for initial coefficients, Fekete-Szegö inequality, and the second-order Hankel determinant for the functions within the defined class. Building upon these findings, the fourth and fifth sections extended these results to encompass logarithmic coefficients and inverse coefficient sharp bounds for functions falling within this distinctive class. One of the future works can be the generalization of the study by applying quantum calculus, for example, the newly published paper by Ahmad and Gong et al. [17].

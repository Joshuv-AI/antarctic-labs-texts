Transcontinental Telementoring of Abdominal Perineal Resection for Rectal Malignancy Complicated by Pelvic Kidney
Authors: Dobromir Dimitrov, Martin Karamanliev, Kun Zhou, Jay Redan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Telementoring has emerged as a tool for bridging gaps in surgical mentorship and expertise. Its use in robotic colorectal surgery remains limited.Case Presentation:We report the first documented case of a robot-assisted abdominoperineal resection (APR) for rectal cancer, technically complicated by the presence of a pelvic left kidney, performed under real-time, transcontinental telementoring between China and Eastern Europe. A 61-year-old male with rectal adenocarcinoma extending to the anal canal underwent neoadjuvant chemoradiation followed by robotic APR using the da Vinci X system. Telementoring was achieved through the Proximie® platform between China and Eastern Europe. The telecommunication connection was stable, enabling real-time audio-visual and augmented-reality communications. The safety and efficiency of the surgical procedure were improved by the telementoring, and the patient recovered uneventfully and was discharged on postoperative day 5.Conclusion:This unique case demonstrates the technical feasibility, patient safety, and clinical utility of transcontinental telementoring in complex robotic colorectal surgery, which allows expanded global access to surgical mentorship and expertise.

INTRODUCTION

Robotic surgery has transformed the landscape of minimally invasive procedures, offering enhanced dexterity, precision, and visualization for complex operations such as total mesorectal excision (TME).1,2 However, dissemination of robotic expertise remains uneven, with many centers lacking direct access to experienced mentors or proctors.3,4

Regarding graduate medical education, telementoring, defined as remote real-time mentoring of surgery through secure audiovisual communication, provides a means to expand surgical training opportunities across distances.5,6 This approach is increasingly relevant as robotic surgery adoption grows and structured training programs, such as those developed by the European Society of Coloproctology, emphasize the importance of remote mentoring and quality assurance in surgical education.7,8

Regarding its role in patient care, initial clinical applications of telepresence in surgery date back to Marescaux et al (2001), who performed the first transatlantic robot-assisted cholecystectomy.9 Since then, technological advances and secure digital platforms have enabled real-time collaboration in urologic, gynecologic, and general surgery.5,10–12 Yet, reports of intercontinental telementoring in robotic colorectal surgery remain extremely rare.13 Here, we describe the first known case of a robot-assisted abdominoperineal resection (APR) for rectal cancer, performed in patient with pelvic left kidney and prior pelvic radiation, under live telementoring between China and Eastern Europe.

PATIENT REPORT

Patient and Preoperative Evaluation

A 61-year-old male was diagnosed with biopsy-confirmed rectal adenocarcinoma extending into the anal canal, cT3N0M0 (Figure 1).

Prechemoradiation MRI of the pelvis showing the rectal tumor and the pelvic kidney.

He underwent neoadjuvant radiation, with postradiation magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) (Figure 2) confirming partial regression and computed tomography (CT) confirming no distant metastasis.

Postchemoradiation MRI demonstrating partial regression and fibrosis.

Surgery was scheduled for 13 weeks after completion of chemoradiotherapy.

Surgical Planning

Preoperative CT scan with delayed (excretory) phase revealed an ectopic left kidney located in the pelvis, with the ureter crossing close to the planned area of dissection. A 3-dimensional (3D) reconstruction of the CT data was created to better delineate the course of the ureter in order to improve surgical planning and reduce intraoperative risk (Figure 3).

3D CT reconstruction. Arrows indicate the pelvic kidney and ureteral course.

Telementoring Setup

The procedure was performed using the da Vinci X system. The telementoring was conducted via the Proximie® platform (Figure 4), a commercially available, cloud-based platform that allows secure encrypted audiovisual transmission and multiangle visualization.

Screenshot of live telementoring via Proximie® interface.

The implementation of the Proximie platform requires standard operating room audiovisual integration, including connection to laparoscopic or robotic video outputs via HDMI or SDI interfaces, as well as supplementary room cameras and audio systems to enable bidirectional communication. A dedicated workstation with adequate processing power and video capture capability is necessary to encode and transmit high-definition streams. Reliable wired internet connectivity with sufficient bandwidth (minimum 10 Mbps, preferably ≥20 Mbps) and low latency is essential for real-time interaction. The system operates via a secure, encrypted, cloud-based platform accessible through standard operating systems, without the need for extensive modification of existing surgical infrastructure. Institutional requirements include compliance with data protection regulations, informed patient consent for streaming or recording, and coordination with hospital information technology (IT) services to ensure network security and integration.

The primary console surgeon was a junior faculty member at University in Europe with limited independent robotic experience (less than 2 years after certification, less than 15 solo colorectal surgeries on the robotic system), 5 years after residency, performed more than 50 colorectal procedures (open and laparoscopic). The proctor was a senior faculty member, a surgical oncologist with more than 200 robotic surgeries since 2014. The proctor was out of country, teaching at a institution located in China, at the optimal time for the patient’s surgery. Surgery was scheduled with the junior faculty member, and while travelling in China (on a bus), the proctor provided continuous audio and visual feedback and guidance using digital overlay tools (Figure 5).

Image of the remote proctor in China.

During the described session, the remote mentor accessed the platform via a secure mobile broadband connection using a 5G business data package, rather than a fixed Wi-Fi network, demonstrating the system’s capability to function under variable connectivity conditions. At the time of participation, the mentor was in transit on a bus provided by the partner organization; however, only the mentor and a single colleague were present, effectively ensuring a controlled environment. Access to the platform was performed through a personal device with secure authentication and encrypted communication channels. No identifiable patient information (e.g., name, date of birth, or facial features) was transmitted during the session, as the video feed was strictly limited to the operative field. All communication was conducted using professional, nonidentifiable clinical language. The platform utilizes end-to-end encryption and complies with international data protection regulations (including General Data Protection Regulation [GDPR]), ensuring secure transmission of both audio and video streams. In addition, informed patient consent for remote participation was obtained preoperatively in accordance with institutional requirements. These measures ensured that patient confidentiality and data security were maintained, despite the mentor’s participation from a mobile setting.

Despite the nearly 7,500 km distance, the session (digital link) was uninterrupted and the latency remained minimal (<500 ms) and clinically unappreciated by either surgeon.

Surgical Procedure

The robot-assisted APR was performed using four robotic ports and one assistant port. Postradiation fibrosis complicated the dissection, especially near the pelvic kidney. The teleproctor guidance facilitated safe dissection along the presacral plane, and both the operating surgeon and the remote mentor perceived a benefit in safety and operating efficiency (Figure 6).

Intraoperative images. Blue ovals indicate surgical anatomy of the rectum and the kidney.

The operative time was 395 minutes.

Postoperative Course

The intraoperative and postoperative courses were uneventful, and the patient recovered well and resumed bowel function on postoperative day (POD) 3, and he was discharged on POD 5. Final histology showed G2 adenocarcinoma of the rectum infiltrating the anus, negative proximal and distal resection margins, negative circumferential resection margins, positive perineural invasion, ypT2N0/8M0, and Ryan tumor regression score of 3 (no visible therapeutic response).

DISCUSSION

Telementoring is gaining increasing acceptance as a means of safe, efficient, and scalable surgical mentorship.6,11,14 Platforms such as Proximie®, C-SATS, and Touch Surgery now allow high-definition streaming and real time annotation while ensuring compliance with data protection standards (GDPR; Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act [HIPAA]).15 While telementoring in urologic and gynecologic robotic surgery has been widely reported, experiences in colorectal robotic surgery have been very limited.13,16

Telementoring utilizes advanced telecommunication platforms which enable ultralow-latency, bidirectional audio-visual communication and live intraoperative annotation, allowing the mentor/proctor surgeon to support the operating surgeon during robotic procedures without being physically present.6 The primary goals of these platforms are to overcome geographical barriers, to standardize and optimize surgical procedures, and to reduce costs and travel time associated with traditional in-person mentoring/proctoring.

The integration of telementoring into robotic surgery represents a significant milestone in the globalization of surgical education and expertise sharing. Over the past few years, several reports have demonstrated the feasibility of long-distance telementoring and telesurgery, particularly in urology and gynecology, where robotic systems have been used to perform procedures such as radical prostatectomy and total hysterectomy under remote supervision and even direct teleoperation.4,9,11,12,16 These experiences have highlighted the technical reliability of modern telecommunication networks and the educational value of real-time surgical collaboration across distances and borders.

To our knowledge, the present case represents the first reported instance of a robot-assisted APR, in patient with an ectopic left kidney, performed under real-time telementoring between Asia and Europe. The procedure was carried out using the Proximie® telemedicine platform which provided secure, encrypted communication and augmented-reality tools, allowing the remote expert surgeon (located at a institution in China) to guide the operating surgeon (located in Eastern Europe). Despite the considerable geographical distance between the surgeons, the system maintained high-quality video and minimal latency, permitting safe and effective surgical mentoring. This experience demonstrates that telementoring can be safely extended to complex colorectal procedures which require precise dissection in anatomically constrained spaces. The successful completion of this operation, despite the additional challenge of an atypically positioned pelvic kidney, underscores the potential of telemedicine technologies to support advanced minimally invasive surgery even in technically demanding settings.

Measured latency during the teleproctoring session remained consistently low, generally below 400–500 ms, despite the extended physical routing of the optical transmission path. Several very brief microinterruptions occurred during the procedure, but they did not affect visualization, audio quality, or the proctor’s ability to provide continuous guidance. Overall system stability remained sufficient to ensure safe real-time mentoring throughout the operation.

Broader adoption of such systems could help address disparities in both surgical training and clinical access to expert mentorship, particularly in regions where specialized robotic surgery programs are still developing. Additional study is needed on formal evaluation of latency thresholds, human factors, and legal frameworks to support wider clinical implementation of telementoring in colorectal and other complex robotic procedures.

In the novel case reported here, the remote expert’s ability to identify key anatomic landmarks and to assist with real time intraoperative decision making proved particularly valuable in this technically difficult pelvis with altered anatomy due to chemoradiation and an ectopic pelvic kidney. This real-time collaboration minimized operative risk, enhanced decision-making, and improved patient safety.

The issues of latency, data protection, and medicolegal frameworks remain challenges to widespread adoption.8,12,15,17,18 This case demonstrates that with secure infrastructure and sound institutional protocols, intercontinental telementoring can be feasible and safe. Future studies need to assess structured integration of telementoring into robotic surgery training curricula and hospital privileging and quality review processes, particularly in regions with limited local expertise.14,16,17

CONCLUSION

This case demonstrates the technical feasibility, safety, and clinical utility of real-time telementoring in complex robotic colorectal surgery across intercontinental distances. Using a commercially available, cloud-based, encrypted telemedicine platform (Proximie®), clinically beneficial intraoperative bidirectional audiovisual communication were achieved between surgical teams in China and Eastern Europe. This successful performance of a technically challenging, robot-assisted APR under telementoring guidance highlights the potential of advanced telemedicine technologies to enhance global collaboration in surgical education and access to expert guidance in minimally invasive oncologic surgery.

Further clinical experience and structured evaluations will be essential to establish the role of telementoring as a standardized component of international surgical practice.

CLINICAL IMPLICATIONS AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS

The experience presented in this report emphasizes the clinical safety, educational value, and scalability of telementoring in robotic colorectal surgery. As digital health ecosystems mature, integrating secure telecommunication and augmented-reality platforms such as Proximie® or TELEPRO® into surgical education may become a cornerstone of international collaboration. The ability to provide remote real-time guidance can shorten the learning curve for complex oncologic procedures, facilitate credentialing in new robotic programs, and reduce the need for costly and time-consuming international travel.

Future initiatives should focus on establishing standardized protocols for data protection, medicolegal accountability, and technical quality assurance in cross-border telementoring. Multicenter registries and prospective trials will be essential to define performance metrics, latency tolerances, and outcome benchmarks. As robotic technology and global connectivity continue to evolve, telementoring is poised to become a sustainable component of modern surgical education, ensuring equitable access to expert mentorship across continents.

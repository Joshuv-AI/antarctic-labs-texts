Identification of Immune-Related Long Non-Coding RNAs for Pulpitis Prediction Based on Competing Endogenous RNAs
Authors: Yuexuan Xu, Lingxian Liu, Yinquan Chen, Xilin Qiu, Xin Chen, Weijun Sun, Yonghui Huang, Ying He, Xuechao Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction and aimsPulpitis is a chronic inflammatory disease affecting oral health. We aim to identify immune-related lncRNAs via bioinformatics analyses and explore their functions through ceRNA networks.MethodsThe expression profiles of 6 patients with pulpitis and 8 normal dental pulp have been obtained from Genome Sequence Archive. The differentially expressed long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) between pulpitis and healthy samples were identified and the classification model was established by LASSO regression. We conducted single-sample gene-set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) to determine the immune-related lncRNAs and screened their hallmark pathways. Further, weighted correlation network analysis (WGCNA) was performed to verify the function of immune lncRNAs. Finally, we constructed the lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA ceRNA network considering miRNA-target relationship from miRanda, negative miRNA-target expression correlation, and positive lncRNA-mRNA expression correlation.ResultsA classification model of 5 lncRNAs was constructed. The lncRNAs CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924 were significantly related with immunity by ssGSEA. Further hallmark pathway analysis revealed that screened lncRNAs were significantly correlated with immune. WGCNA results implied CARD8-AS1 was in the Grey module and LINC00924 in the Brown module. Both Grey and Brown modules are significantly enriched in immune-related biological functions based on enrichment analysis. The immune-related ceRNA network was constructed for the lncRNAs CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924. Three miRNAs hsa-miR-3065-5p, hsa-miR-3910, and hsa-miR-514a-3p regulated both CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924. Particularly, CARD8-AS1 was confirmed to interact with immune-related genes through miRDB and TargetScan analyses. CARD8-AS1-associated mRNAs (PTGS2, NLRP3, and CXCL3) were identified through both functional enrichment analysis and a ceRNA network involving lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA interactions. These mRNAs showed significant enrichment in immune-related pathways such as the JAK-STAT signaling pathway, complement system, and TLR signaling.ConclusionThe study found that lncRNA CARD8-AS1 and CARD8-AS1-based ceRNA network play an important role in regulating the immune response in pulpitis, and immune lncRNAs such as CARD8-AS1 can serve as new markers for non-invasive diagnosis.

Introduction

Pulpitis is a common oral disease characterized by an inflammatory response of the pulp tissues. Pulpitis poses significant clinical risks, including severe pain, spreading infection and tooth loss. Immunologically, dysbiosis between oral microbiota and host immunity triggers excessive inflammatory responses, accelerating pulp necrosis. Studies reveal that microbial communities in pulpitis modulate immune pathways (eg, TLR signaling) to amplify tissue damage. In recent years, Ordinola-Zapata et al have published studies stating the complications of pulpitis.1

The occurrence of pulpitis is closely related to the dynamic balance of the body's immune system.2 When caries damage or physical stimulation breaks through the dentin barrier, bacteria and their metabolites activate the immune defense mechanism of the pulp tissue through the dentin tubule, which identifies pathogen signals triggering the release of pro-inflammatory factors and recruits immune cells.3 Studies have confirmed that the inflammatory factors released by pulpitis can affect the systemic immune status through the blood circulation, and increase the risk of systemic diseases such as cardiovascular disease and diabetes mellitus.4 Notably, the latest research, that reveals the key role of tertiary lymphatic structure (TLS) in the pulp in anti-infection, provides a new perspective on understanding the immune regulation of the pulp.5 Leprince et al reported that interactions regulating immune and inflammatory responses could be utilized to improve treatment outcome.6 The mechanism of immunotherapy is becoming an important research direction to break through the bottleneck of pulpitis treatment.7

As an unbalanced disease of immune response to bacterial invasion by pulp tissue, the pathological process of pulpitis is closely related to the dynamic game of host immune defense and repair ability. Studies have shown that non-coding RNA (ncRNA) in the dental pulp microenvironment plays a pivotal role in inflammation progression and tissue repair by regulating immune signaling pathways such as NLRP3 inflammasome.8 Existing studies have well established the role of the NLRP3 inflammasome in pyroptosis and proinflammatory cytokine secretion, particularly IL-1β. Walle et al9 and Yu et al10 have systematically elucidated these mechanisms. In addition, the discovery of ceRNA network revealed a synergistic mechanism that circRNA CDR1as regulated Wnt signaling pathway through miR-7 to inhibit odontoblast proliferation.11 Studies have shown that CARD8-AS1 can participate in chronic regulatory responses by regulating NLRP3 and NF-KB signaling.12 It is worth noting that CARD8-AS1 may regulate competitive binding to miR-3065-5p through the ceRNA network, thereby relieving the inhibitory effect of the resting phase on IL-1β and IL-18. CARD8-AS1 binding to miR-650 in lung adenocarcinoma studies, have been reported as potential therapeutic targets.13 The above findings further support the key role of ncRNA networks in immune regulation.

Recent advances have highlighted the critical role of competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks in orchestrating immune-inflammatory balance across various diseases.14 Emerging evidence suggests that specific lncRNAs like NUTM2A-AS1 modulate inflammatory responses through miRNA sponging mechanisms.15 However, systematic analysis of immune-related lncRNAs and corresponding ceRNA networks in pulpitis remains largely unexplored, particularly regarding their diagnostic potential and regulatory crosstalk with innate immunity pathways. This knowledge gap is noteworthy given the established involvement of NLRP3 inflammasome activation16 and TLR-mediated immune responses in pulpitis pathogenesis.

Building on these insights into the immunoregulatory functions of ceRNA networks, our study seeks to address the specific gaps in understanding their roles in pulpitis. Although LINC00924 has been found to be highly expressed in inflamed tissues, how its interaction with miRNAs affects neutrophil chemotaxis remains unclear.17 Similarly, whether the regulation of NLRP3 by CARD8-AS1 depends on ceRNA mechanisms has not been confirmed.18 Therefore, by integrating multi-omics data, this study focuses on analyzing the core role of immune-related lncRNAs in the ceRNA network, aiming to clarify key molecular regulatory mechanisms and provide a new perspective for screening targets with both diagnostic value and functional significance.

Materials and methods

Differential expression analysis

Raw count data from RNA sequencing were subjected to differential expression analysis using the DESeq2 package.19 The analysis workflow included the normalization to correct library size bias, estimation of gene-wise dispersion parameters, and fitting a generalized linear model based on the negative binomial distribution. Statistical significance for each gene was evaluated, and multiple testing correction was applied using the false discovery rate (FDR). Differentially expressed lncRNAs (DELs) were identified based on the criteria of |log2 fold change (FC)|>1 and adjusted P-value <.05. Visualization of the DELs was performed using volcano plots to highlight significantly up- and down-regulated lncRNAs.

The construction of long non-coding RNA-based classification model

To construct a robust lncRNA-based classification model, LASSO (least absolute shrinkage and selection operator) regression was applied using the glmnet package to reduce dimensionality and select the most informative features among the DELs. The optimal regularization parameter (lambda) was determined through cross-validation by minimizing the partial likelihood deviance. Selected lncRNAs were then used to build a logistic regression model to classify samples into pulpitis and normal. The classification performance was assessed by the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, which was conducted using the pROC package to evaluate the sensitivity and specificity of individual lncRNAs and their combinations.

Associating long non-coding RNAs with immune cell types

Single-sample gene-set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was carried out using the R package 'gsva' to evaluate the enrichment levels of immune cells within each sample.20 The immune signatures were downloaded from the supplementary file of the publication of Charoentong P et al,21 which contains 782 marker genes of 28 immune sub-populations. The 'gsva' function was then utilized for the enrichment analysis, with parameters setting as method='ssgsea', mx.diff=FALSE, and verbose=FALSE. The 'ggplot' function was then used to create a violin plot, displaying the normalized ssGSEA scores of different immune cell types in pulpitis and normal pulp. All these processes were directed towards intuitively presenting the infiltration differences of immune cells.

Competitive endogenous RNA network construction

Transcriptome expression data of lncRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNAs were obtained from the same dataset. Differential expression analysis was conducted using the threshold of |log2 fold change|>1 and P-value <.05 to identify significantly dysregulated RNAs. Subsequently, potential lncRNA-miRNA and miRNA-mRNA pairs were predicted based on multiple databases, including miRanda,22 miRDB23 and TargetScan.24 The intersecting interactions were then used to construct a core ceRNA regulatory network integrating lncRNAs, miRNAs, and mRNAs. The network was visualized using Cytoscape software (v3.10.1) to illustrate the regulatory relationships among these RNAs. The predicted set of lncRNA-miRNA interactions was predicted using the miRanda algorithm. The miRNAs which target immune-related lncRNAs were further overlapped with differential miRNAs. The predicted target mRNAs which were commonly acquired by miRanda, TargetScan and miRDB databases were deemed as the core target of the ceRNA network.

Immune pathway enrichment analysis

To explore the potential biological functions of the mRNAs involved in the network, functional enrichment analysis was performed using the Hallmark gene sets from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB). Pathways significantly associated with the immune lncRNAs were identified by Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) on their correlated mRNAs. Particular attention was given to immune-related hallmark pathways. Key lncRNAs such as CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924 were further analyzed based on their centrality within the network and their connectivity to multiple immune-associated pathways, allowing a focused analysis of their potential immunoregulatory roles. In detail, we sorted all differential mRNAs by their correlation with a certain lncRNA. Through GSEA, the ordered gene list was functionally annotated, and finally the characteristic pathways with high functional correlation with the lncRNA were screened to obtain the predicted regulatory pathways of the lncRNA.The GSEA analysis was performed using the clusterProfiler R package under R version 4.3.2, and gene sets were obtained from the MSigDB Hallmark collection. Correlation calculations and ranking were implemented using base R functions. The code used for correlation-based gene sorting and GSEA is available upon request.

Gene modules identified by weighted gene co-expression network analysis

WGCNA constructs a gene co-expression network to identify gene modules with similar expression patterns. The potential biological function of lncRNAs can be inferred by their highly correlated genes in the module. We utilized the miRNAs dataset to explore the differentially expressed miRNAs between pulpitis and healthy samples with the screening criteria of |log2FC|>1 and adjusted P < .05. The expression profiles of differentially expressed miRNAs, lncRNAs, and mRNAs were subsequently used for WGCNA. Through systematic evaluation of soft threshold analysis, we selected a soft threshold power of 14, achieving the optimal balance between biological interpretability and the network scale-free property. The co-expression network was constructed using the WGCNA R package.

Results

Five long non-coding RNAs are predictive of pulpitis

In the HRA007469 dataset, 17 DELs were identified, with 12 upregulated and 5 downregulated in pulpitis samples (Figure 1A). We performed LASSO regression analysis to further refine the variables and construct a classification model. As the tuning parameter log(lambda) changed from −5 to −1, the normal deviance increased from approximately 0.3 to 1.5. The optimal lambda value was set to 0.081 (Figure 1B). Consequently, 5 lncRNAs CARD8-AS1, IL21R-AS1, LINC00924, SH3RF3-AS1, TFAP2A-AS1 were selected to construct the classification model (Figure 1C). The ROC curve indicated that the 5-lncRNA model can perfectly predict pulpitis with the area under the curve (AUC) of one. Among individual lncRNAs, LINC00924 and SH3RF3-AS1 achieved the highest AUC of one, demonstrating strong classification capability. We further examined their expression profiles in normal and pulpitis samples (Figure 1D). CARD8-AS1 exhibited a dramatic increased expression in pulpitis tissues compared to healthy controls, with a highly significant P-value (P_DESeq <.001), suggesting its strong association with the disease. In contrast, IL21R-AS1 and TFAP2A-AS1 were notably downregulated in pulpitis samples. LINC00924, another upregulated lncRNA (P_DESeq = .001), displayed a distinct separation in expression levels between the 2 groups, further reinforcing its diagnostic potential. Similarly, SH3RF3-AS1 showed increased expression in pulpitis samples (P_DESeq <.001). The 5 lncRNA model achieved a perfect AUC of 1.0, indicating exceptional diagnostic performance. Beyond statistical strength, these results carry practical clinical implications. Du et al has reported that immune‑related lncRNAs such as PVT1 have demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy in saliva samples of pulpitis patients, supporting their potential as minimally invasive biomarkers.25

CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924 are associated with distinct immune cell populations

Five lncRNAs were identified as accurate predictors of pulpitis. To investigate their immune relevance, we correlated these lncRNAs with immune signatures using GSVA enrichment scores. Violin plots revealed significant upregulation of most immune cell types in pulpitis samples, except for gamma delta T natural killer cells, memory B cells, regulatory T cells, and type 17 T helper cells (Figure 2A). Spearman correlation analysis demonstrated strong associations between the lncRNAs and immune features (Figure 2B). Notably, both CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924 showed positive correlations (R > 0.30) with central memory CD4+ T cells, central memory CD8+ T cells, gamma delta T cells, and neutrophils. CARD8-AS1 exhibited the strongest association with central memory CD4+ T cells (R = 0.49), while LINC00924 correlated most strongly with gamma delta T cells (R = 0.49). In contrast, SHRF3-AS1 and TFAP2A-AS1 showed negative correlations (R < −0.70) with both innate and adaptive immune cells.Fig. 2Enrichment levels of immune subpopulations and their relationship with 5 selected long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs). We leveraged the GSE92681 dataset26 to validate the immunity association of CARD8-AS1. We performed WGCNA on GSE92681, the lncRNA CARD8-AS1 is in the Yellow module. We then performed a hypergeometric test between the genes in Yellow module and immune genes downloaded from GeneCards (Supplementary Figure 1). The results demonstrated a significant overlap between the Yellow module and immune genes (Supplementary Table 1), providing statistical evidence for the potential role of CARD8-AS1 in immune regulation.

Distinct immune modulation patterns of CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924

Further analysis revealed differential immune associations: CARD8-AS1 was positively linked to Th2 cells, macrophages, and mast cells, suggesting a role in allergic responses. Conversely, LINC00924 showed stronger associations with effector memory CD8+ T cells, natural killer (NK) cells, and monocytes, indicating involvement in cytotoxic functions. These findings demonstrate that while both lncRNAs modulate adaptive (T cells) and innate immunity (macrophages, NK cells), they exhibit distinct immunological biases - CARD8-AS1 towards Th2-mediated allergy and LINC00924 towards cytotoxicity.

CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924 are enriched in immune hallmark pathways

CARD8-AS1 demonstrates significant enrichment in hallmark pathways associated with inflammatory and immune activation processes, including HALLMARK_TNFA_SIGNALING_VIA_NFKB, HALLMARK_INFLAMMATORY_RESPONSE (Figure 3A and B). These findings suggest that CARD8-AS1 may participate in upregulating pro-inflammatory mediators and triggering immune cascades. LINC00924 is highly enriched in immune regulatory and metabolic pathways, such as HALLMARK_ALLOGRAFT_REJECTION, HALLMARK_EPITHELIAL_MESENCHYMAL_TRANSITION, as well as cell cycle-related pathways like HALLMARK_G2M_CHECKPOINT (Figure 3A). This indicates that LINC00924 may orchestrate cytotoxic immune responses and energy metabolism in immune cells. Together, these results reveal that CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924 influence the immune microenvironment through distinct yet complementary mechanisms, providing mechanistic insights into their roles in inflammation-related conditions in pulpitis.Fig. 3Hallmark pathways associated with immune related lncRNAs A, CARD8-AS1, LINC00924, SH3RF3-AS1 and B, CARD8-AS1, LINC00924.Fig 3

To validate the robustness of our findings, we performed parallel pathway enrichment analysis in an independent dataset (GSE92681). Consistent with the results obtained from the dataset HRA007469, CARD8-AS1 in GSE92681 also significantly enriched in HALLMARK_TNFA_SIGNALING_VIA_NFKB and ALLMARK_INFLAMMATORY_RESPONSE (Supplementary Figure 2), recapitulating its role in inflammatory mediator upregulation and immune cascade activation. Similarly, LINC00924 in GSE92681 was highly enriched in HALLMARK_ALLOGRAFT_REJECTION and HALLMARK_EPITHELIAL_MESENCHYMAL_TRANSITION (Supplementary Figure 2), implying its dual functions in immune metabolic regulation observed in the dataset HRA007469. These cross-dataset consistencies reinforce the reliability of CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924 as key modulators of the immune microenvironment in pulpitis.

Function inference of CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924

Five gene modules were identified by WGCNA, and each module had significant correlation with at least one lncRNA (Figure 4A). Among these modules, the Grey and Brown modules exhibited the strongest correlations with lncRNAs CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924, respectively. The correlation coefficient is 0.75 for CARD8-AS1 in Grey module, and 0.76 for LINC00924 in Brown module. Genes within these modules were subsequently subjected to functional enrichment analysis, revealing their involvement in diverse immune-related biological processes. As shown in Figure 4B, pathways such as positive regulation of cell adhesion, regulation of inflammatory response, and leukocyte cell-cell adhesion were significantly enriched, suggesting that associated lncRNAs (eg, Blue module, LINC00924) may mediate immune cell migration and inflammatory microenvironment formation. Conversely, Figure 4C highlighted enriched pathways including regulation of cell cycle phase transition, nuclear division, and leukocyte-mediated immunity, indicating that CARD8-AS1 might regulate anti-infective or anti-tumor immune responses by coordinating immune cell proliferation or differentiation (eg, lymphocyte activation, chromosome segregation) and effector functions (eg, cytotoxic T-cell expansion). Notably, the strong correlations of LINC00924 and CARD8-AS1 with Brown and Grey module further support their potential roles as hub molecules in inflammation-immunity crosstalk. Collectively, the Brown and Grey modules systematically elucidate a multi-layered regulatory network of lncRNAs (LINC00924 and CARD8-AS1) in immune homeostasis through 2 complementary dimensions: immune microenvironment construction (adhesion, inflammation) and immune effector cell functionality (proliferation, cytotoxicity).Fig. 4Enriched functions of immune-related lncRNAs. The enrichment results of the Yellow module containing CARD8-AS1 in the GSE92681 dataset (Supplementary Figure 3) reveal significant enrichment in the GO BP of humoral immune response, which is consistent with that in the dataset HRA007469 (Figure 4C). Meanwhile, in the GSE92681 dataset, the Blue module harboring LINC00924 was significantly enriched in multiple immune-related GO BP terms (Supplementary Figure 4), including positive regulation of cell adhesion, leukocyte cell-cell adhesion, regulation of innate immune response, and leukocyte proliferation. Construction and analysis of competing endogenous RNA network of CARD8-AS1 and LINC00924

Based on the differentially expressed miRNAs, lncRNAs, and mRNAs, we further predicted lncRNA-miRNA and miRNA-mRNA relationships. CARD8-AS1 serves as a crucial molecular sponge by competitively binding key miRNAs such as hsa-miR-14a-3p, hsa-miR-3055-5p, and hsa-miR-3910, thereby modulating the expression of downstream mRNAs. Importantly, the CARD8-AS1-associated mRNAs are mostly enriched in immune-related hallmark pathways (Figure 3), as indicated by the green nodes with red borders in the ceRNA network (Figure 5A). These mRNAs include NLRC3, SOCS3 and CXCL10. All of them are critically involved in immune signaling, inflammatory responses, and immune regulation pathways. This strongly suggests that CARD8-AS1 plays a pivotal role in immune modulation by regulating the expression of immune-related genes through the ceRNA mechanism. In contrast, the ceRNA network of LINC00924 (Figure 5B) exhibits a relatively broader regulatory scope without direct links to immune-enriched genes. Further, miRDB and TargetScan were used to remove false positive miRNA targets. As shown in Figure 5C, CARD8-AS1 is competing with SPP1, CDC6, PTGS2, MYB, and TRN1, among which SPP1, CDC6, PTGS2, MXD1, and CXCL3 participate in immune related hallmark pathways. However, there is no gene involved in immune hallmark pathways for LINC00924 (Figure 5D). Discussion

Pulpitis, a chronic inflammatory condition primarily caused by bacterial invasion, is increasingly recognized as an immune-mediated pathological state. Its clinical complications extend beyond localized pain and necrosis to systemic immune interactions. Emerging evidence shows that dysregulated immune responses, particularly innate immune mechanisms such as NLRP3 inflammasome activation, are crucial to disease progression.27 Recent findings of TLS in inflamed dental pulp further reinforce the role of the pulp as an immunocompetent microenvironment. ncRNAs, including lncRNAs, miRNAs, and circRNAs, have emerged as critical regulators of immune responses via the ceRNA hypothesis. These RNAs compete for miRNA binding and influence gene expression, thereby modulating immune cell activation and inflammatory pathways.28 Studies have shown that lncRNA-SNHG16 activates inflammatory cascades via the NLRP3,29 while miR-223 and miR-22 suppress inflammation through post-transcriptional regulation of NLRP3. Our findings support this regulatory paradigm by identifying immune-associated lncRNAs and interpret their function from the perspective of ceRNA networks in pulpitis for the first time.30

In this study, we systematically elucidated the immunoregulatory role of ncRNAs in pulpitis by constructing an immune-related ceRNA network centered on lncRNAs. We retrieved transcriptomic datasets (mRNA and miRNA) of 6 pulpitis and 8 healthy pulp samples from the Genome Sequence Archive (HRA007469).31 Differential expression analysis via DESeq2 identified 17 DELs. In order to prove the robustness of the DELs, we searched the GEO database for other public pulpitis dataset for cross validation. There are 6 normal and 6 pulpitis samples in GSE77459,32 7 inflammatory samples with 5 normal samples are detected in GSE92681. Differential lncRNAs are not identical across different cohorts. Therefore, we performed a literature survey on reported DELs in pulpitis and checked their expression trend in different datasets. Du et al systematically identified the immune-related lncRNAs and regulatory network in pulpitis.33 Fuchen-Ramos et al reviewed and summarized the roles of lncRNAs in pulpitis, involving multiple biological functions, including oxidative stress, inflammation and immune response, ferroptosis, proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation and autophagy. Among the 21 immune-related lncRNAs, ANKRD44-IT1, LINC01094, LINC02828, and IL10RB-DT are validated to be up-regulated in inflamed pulp by qRT-PCR. In HRA007469, ANKRD44-IT1 is up-regulated in pulpitis, which is consistent with the report. LINC02828 and IL10RB-DT were not detected in HRA007469. LINC01094 was down-regulated in pulpitis in GSE92681. PVT1, LINC00582, HCP5 are consistently up-regulated,34 while ADAMTS9-AS2, LAMTOR5-AS1 are consistently down-regulated in pulpitis (Supplementary Table 2). The expression of immune-related lncRNA LINC00290 is significantly down-regulated in all 3 datasets. The expression of immune-related lncRNA TFAP2A-AS1 is significantly down-regulated in GSE92681 and HRA007469. The expression trends of some reported lncRNAs are reproducible across datasets.

A LASSO regression model was employed to refine variable selection, forming a 5-lncRNA classification model based on CARD8-AS1, LINC00924, IL21R-AS1, SH3RF3-AS1, and TFAP2A-AS1, which demonstrated excellent diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 1).

To explore the immune relevance of these 5 lncRNAs, ssGSEA was performed to assess immune cell infiltration.35 CARD8-AS1 showed strong positive correlations (R > 0.3) with over ten immune cell types, notably Central Memory CD4+ T cells (R = 0.49), macrophages, and Th2 cells. Similarly, LINC00924 was positively associated with effector memory CD8+ T cells and monocytes, suggesting its involvement in cytotoxic immune regulation. Existing studies reported that LINC00924 affected tumor immune cell infiltration levels by regulating immune pathways such as JAK-STAT.36 The lncRNA CARD8-AS1 was detected in the dataset GSE92681, not in GSE77459. Therefore, we repeated immune relevance analysis and WGCNA in GSE92681.

WGCNA identified 5 RNA modules, of which the Grey and Brown modules strongly correlated with CARD8-AS1 (R = 0.75) and LINC00924 (R = 0.76), respectively. It has been shown that CARD8-AS1 plays an important role in the ceRNA network associated to immune characteristics,37 and LINC00924 also induces the reprogramming of fatty acid metabolism.38 Functional enrichment analysis showed CARD8-AS1-associated mRNAs (eg, PTGS2, NLRP3 and CXCL3) were enriched in immune-related pathways such as JAK-STAT, complement, and TLR signaling. Conversely, LINC00924-related mRNAs (eg, TIGAR, CD274) were involved in antiviral responses.

We constructed core lncRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory axes by integrating miRNA target information from miRanda, TargetScan, and miRDB. Some reviews have explored the regulatory role of miRNA and lncRNA in innate immune signaling, emphasizing their role in key immune pathways such as RIG-I and MAVS.39 GSEA hallmark pathways confirmed CARD8-AS1 enrichment in HALLMARK_INFLAMMATORY_RESPONSE and HALLMARK_TNFA_SIGNALING_VIA_NFKB pathways, indicating its pivotal role in orchestrating immune cascades. Studies have also explored the relationship between the miRNA-mRNA regulatory circuits and the immune signature, highlighting the key role of the ncRNA in the immune response.40

CARD8-AS1 is a key lncRNA implicated in chronic inflammation in pulpitis. Through competitive binding to hsa-miR-3065-5p, hsa-miR-514a-3p,41 and hsa-miR-3910,42 it modulates immune-related genes including PTGS2, CDC-6, and MXD1 (Figure 5C). Functional enrichment revealed its associated mRNAs (eg, STAG3, CXCL3, CXCL10) are significantly involved in TNF-α/NF-κB and INFLAMMATORY_RESPONSE pathways, aligning with its known interaction with NLRP3 inflammasome in oral pathologies.

WGCNA analysis highlighted the Grey module as strongly correlated with CARD8-AS1, containing SOCS3, key suppressors of JAK-STAT signaling.43 These findings suggest CARD8-AS1′s dual role as an immune 'molecular switch', it activates inflammatory cascades while simultaneously fine-tunes responses through SOCS-mediated feedback. Notably, miR-514a-3p potentially suppressing CXCL3-driven neutrophil infiltration through EGFR modulation.44

By integrating multi-omics data, this study constructed the first immune-related ceRNA network in pulpitis, uncovering the pivotal roles of CARD8-AS1 in immune-inflammatory balance. CARD8-AS1 drives TNF-α/NF-κB and INFLAMMATORY_RESPONSE pathway activation via sponging miR-3065-5p and regulating NLRP3. These findings not only provide novel insights into pulpitis pathogenesis but also lay the groundwork for precision therapies targeting lncRNAs.

The limitation in the sample size of dental pulp tissues in this study stems from inherent challenges in clinical sampling, where the acquisition of inflamed pulp tissues is restricted by therapeutic necessity and ethical guidelines. To address potential impacts on statistical power due to the small sample size, targeted optimization strategies were employed during data analysis. In differential expression analysis, the DESeq2 package enhanced the stability of small-sample data through gene-wise dispersion estimation and negative binomial distribution modeling. LASSO regression effectively reduced overfitting risk in feature selection via L1 regularization, which is particularly suitable for biomarker screening with limited samples. WGCNA analysis focused on overall expression patterns of gene modules rather than individual sample fluctuations, minimizing the interference of small sample size on result interpretation. Additionally, all statistical tests applied strict multiple comparison corrections and evaluated model stability through cross-validation to ensure the reliability of results to the greatest extent.

While our integrated analysis establishes CARD8-AS1 as a key regulator of immune-inflammatory balance in pulpitis, it should be noted that this study is strictly based on bioinformatic computation. Although cross-validation across datasets (GSE77459, GSE92681) and algorithmic approaches (LASSO, WGCNA) enhanced robustness, functional validation of lncRNA mechanisms and mechanistic research into lncRNA-mediated immunomodulation in pulpitis are still needed in future studies. The spatial transcriptomics will delineate CARD8-AS1’s expression patterns in the dental pulp microenvironment. Moreover, preclinical animal models could provide causal evidence of its role in pulpitis pathogenesis.

Notwithstanding these limitations, this is the first study to systematically investigate the immunoregulatory role of lncRNAs in pulpitis through a ceRNA network perspective. These findings not only deepen our understanding of the immunoregulatory roles of lncRNAs in pulpitis but also offer practical implications for clinical practice. Specifically, the 5-lncRNA diagnostic model proposed in this study may serve as a novel tool to assist clinicians in the early identification of pulpitis, potentially improving diagnostic efficiency and enabling timely intervention.

It is worth noting that the small sample size in this study was primarily due to ethical and logistical constraints associated with collecting inflamed pulp tissue during emergency clinical care. To address this limitation, we employed rigorous statistical approaches, including LASSO regression and WGCNA, and integrated data from multiple cohorts to enhance analytical robustness.

Looking forward, future research will focus on expanding the sample size to validate these findings across diverse populations, and exploring the translational potential of lncRNAs as therapeutic targets. Such efforts are expected to bridge the gap between computational discovery and clinical application, ultimately contributing to more precise and personalized management strategies for pulpitis and related inflammatory conditions.

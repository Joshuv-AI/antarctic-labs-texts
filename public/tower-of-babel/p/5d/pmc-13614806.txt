Profile of Quality of Life in Children with Extrahepatic Portal Vein Obstruction: A Cross-sectional Comparative Study
Authors: Shreya Tomar, Bitesh Kumar, Minu Bajpai, Sandeep Agarwala, Sujata Satapathy, Rohan Malik, Veereshwar Bhatnagar, Vishesh Jain, Devendra Kumar Yadav, Prabudh Goel, Anjan Kumar Dhua
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Extrahepatic portal venous obstruction (EHPVO) is a chronic condition in children associated with recurrent gastrointestinal bleeding, hypersplenism, and growth impairment. While surgical and endoscopic interventions address portal hypertension, their impact on Health-related Quality of Life (HRQoL) remains incompletely understood.Materials and Methods:This cross-sectional comparative study evaluated HRQoL using the Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory (PedsQL™ 4.0) in children with EHPVO undergoing endoscopic management (preshunt group), those who had undergone surgical intervention (postshunt group), and age-matched healthy sibling controls. Both child self-reports and parent proxy-reports were analyzed. Nonparametric statistical methods were used, and effect sizes were calculated to assess clinical relevance.Results:Children with EHPVO demonstrated significantly lower overall HRQoL compared with healthy controls across all domains (P < 0.001), with large effect sizes (r ≈ 0.8), indicating clinically meaningful impairment. No statistically significant difference was observed between pre- and postshunt groups, with small effect sizes suggesting minimal variation at the time of assessment. Median follow-up duration in the postoperative cohort exceeded 1 year across all surgical subgroups. Domain-wise analysis revealed the greatest impairment in school functioning, followed by physical and emotional domains. Strong concordance was observed between child self-reports and parent proxy-reports.Conclusion:Children with EHPVO experience substantial impairment in HRQoL compared with healthy peers. While no significant differences were observed between treatment groups, HRQoL remains suboptimal within the follow-up period captured in this cross-sectional study. These findings highlight the need for comprehensive, multidisciplinary care addressing psychosocial, educational, and nutritional aspects alongside standard medical and surgical management.

INTRODUCTION

Portal hypertension in children is defined as a portal venous pressure exceeding 5–8 cm H2O or a portal-systemic gradient >5 cm H2O. In Asia, extrahepatic portal vein obstruction (EHPVO) accounts for 54%–72% of pediatric portal hypertension and nearly 85% of variceal bleeding cases.[12]

Extrahepatic portal vein obstruction is a chronic condition characterized by recurrent variceal bleeding, massive splenomegaly, hypersplenism, portal biliopathy, and growth impairment. Management includes repeated endoscopic interventions and/or surgical shunt procedures such as distal or proximal lienorenal shunts.

While survival outcomes in children with EHPVO have improved considerably with modern management strategies, the chronic nature of the disease continues to impose a substantial burden on daily life. These children often require recurrent hospital visits, may face emergency admissions for variceal bleeding, and frequently undergo repeated blood transfusions. In addition, massive splenomegaly and disease-related fatigue can restrict physical activity, while repeated medical appointments and hospitalizations contribute to school absenteeism and academic disruption. Growth retardation further compounds these challenges. Collectively, these factors can significantly impair Health-related Quality of Life (HRQoL).

HRQoL measurement in pediatrics has evolved as a critical outcome parameter beyond morbidity and mortality.[34] The Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory (PedsQL™) is a validated tool widely used across chronic pediatric conditions.[56] Although prior studies have demonstrated persistent HRQoL impairment even after surgical management,[78] comparative evaluation between children awaiting shunt surgery and those in postoperative follow-up remains limited.

Therefore, this study was designed to evaluate and compare HRQoL among three distinct groups: Children with EHPVO undergoing endoscopic surveillance, those who had previously undergone shunt surgery and were in follow-up, and age-matched healthy controls. By examining these groups concurrently, we sought to understand the impact of both disease status and surgical intervention on overall quality of life.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This cross-sectional observational study was conducted over 7 months in the department of pediatric surgery at a tertiary care hospital in northern India. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (IECPG-183/20.05.2020), and all procedures were carried out in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Children aged 5–18 years with a confirmed diagnosis of EHPVO were eligible for inclusion if they were either undergoing endoscopic surveillance or were at least 3 months postshunt surgery. Patients were excluded if they had associated hematological disorders, were presenting for their first hospital visit, or if parents or guardians did not provide informed consent. Given the exploratory nature of the study and the paucity of prior data quantifying HRQoL differences in EHPVO subgroups, a convenience sampling method was adopted. All consecutive eligible patients during the study period were included to maximize representativeness and statistical precision.

Participants were categorized into three study groups for comparative analysis. Group 1 comprised children with EHPVO who were under endoscopic surveillance and were either awaiting or being planned for shunt surgery (preshunt group). Group 2 included children who had undergone surgical intervention and were at least 3 months postoperative at the time of assessment. Group 3 consisted of healthy sibling controls or otherwise healthy children accompanying patients to the outpatient department, who served as the comparison group. The postoperative group was further subcategorized based on the type of surgery: proximal lienorenal shunt, distal lienorenal shunt, and splenectomy with devascularization. Although the eligibility criterion required a minimum postoperative duration of 3 months, the actual interval between surgery and HRQoL assessment was substantially longer in all patients with median values ranging from 17.1 to 34.2 months across surgical subgroups.

HRQoL was assessed using a validated pediatric instrument to allow structured comparison between disease states and healthy controls. The Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory (PedsQL™ 4.0 Generic Core Scales) was used with prior permission. The validated Hindi and English versions were administered depending on participant preference. The instrument consists of 23 items across four domains: Physical functioning (8 items), Emotional functioning (5 items), Social functioning (5 items), and School functioning (5 items).

Child self-report (5–7, 8–12, and 13–18 years) and parent proxy-report formats were used. Responses were reverse-scored and linearly transformed to a 0–100 scale, with higher scores indicating better HRQoL. Scale scores were calculated as the mean of completed items, provided that ≥50% of items in a domain were answered. The primary outcome measure of the study was the total HRQoL score derived from the PedsQL™ instrument. Domain-wise scores (physical, emotional, social, and school functioning) were analyzed descriptively to identify patterns of impairment across groups. Questionnaires were self-administered for children aged 8–18 years and their parents. For children aged 5–7 years, parents assisted in completion using the validated pictorial scale. Adequate time was provided, and clarification was offered when required. Socioeconomic and educational status, as well as detailed symptom duration, were not systematically recorded as part of the study protocol. However, given the public hospital setting, the study population largely represents a lower to lower-middle socioeconomic background, which may partially mitigate inter-group variability. Normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. As the HRQoL data were non-normally distributed, results are presented as median (interquartile range [IQR]). For comparisons across the three groups (control, endoscopy, and postoperative), the Kruskal–Wallis test was used. Where significant, pairwise comparisons were performed using Mann–Whitney U-tests with Bonferroni correction. Bonferroni-adjusted P values were applied to account for multiple comparisons across the three groups. Effect sizes for pairwise Mann–Whitney comparisons were expressed as r (|Z|/√N). A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

A total of 70 children were evaluated in October 2020: 50 with EHPVO and 20 healthy controls [Figure 1]. Among children with EHPVO, 20 were undergoing endoscopic surveillance (preshunt), and 30 had undergone surgical intervention, comprising proximal lienorenal shunt (n = 10), distal lienorenal shunt (n = 10), and splenectomy with devascularization (n = 10). The median duration between surgery and HRQoL assessment was 23 months (IQR: 18–43 months), with a minimum follow-up of approximately 7 months and a maximum of 56 months (n = 28). Two cases with missing operative dates were excluded from quantitative follow-up analysis; however, both were confirmed to be well beyond 1 year postsurgery, ensuring that no early postoperative cases were inadvertently included.

Although overall concordance between child and parent reports was strong, a subtle directional pattern was observed in the endoscopy group, where parents tended to rate total HRQoL marginally lower than children. While this difference was not statistically significant, it may reflect heightened parental anxiety regarding disease progression, procedural burden, or future surgical intervention. Similar trends have been described in other chronic pediatric conditions, where caregivers often perceive greater impairment than children themselves. In contrast, near-identical median scores in the control group suggest minimal reporting bias in healthy participants. Collectively, these findings support the reliability of parent proxy reporting while underscoring the importance of incorporating both perspectives in chronic disease assessment. Overall, comparisons between children with EHPVO and healthy controls consistently demonstrated large effect sizes, indicating clinically meaningful impairment across domains. In contrast, comparisons between preshunt and postoperative groups showed small, nonsignificant effect sizes, suggesting that surgical intervention alone does not substantially modify short-term HRQoL outcomes. Collectively, these results demonstrate that children with EHPVO experience substantial and multidimensional impairment in HRQoL compared with healthy peers, with large effect sizes confirming clinical relevance. The persistence of reduced HRQoL despite surgical intervention, coupled with strong child–parent concordance, suggests that the burden of disease extends beyond hemodynamic abnormalities alone. These findings highlight the complex interplay between chronic medical illness, psychosocial adaptation, and functional outcomes and warrant further interpretation within a broader clinical and developmental context.

DISCUSSION

The present study aimed to evaluate and compare HRQoL among children with EHPVO undergoing endoscopic surveillance, those who had undergone surgical intervention, and healthy controls. The principal finding of this study is that children with EHPVO exhibit significantly impaired overall HRQoL compared with healthy peers, as demonstrated by nonparametric analysis with large effect sizes. Importantly, the magnitude of the difference between controls and both disease groups was large, with median total HRQoL scores demonstrating substantial separation and large effect sizes (r ≈ 0.8), confirming clinically meaningful impairment, whereas that between the preshunt and postshunt groups was small and not statistically significant. These findings indicate that while surgical intervention addresses portal hypertension physiologically, it does not fully normalize perceived quality of life within the follow-up period captured in this study. Importantly, the follow-up duration in this cohort was not limited to the immediate postoperative period, with median values exceeding 1 year across all surgical subgroups and lower quartile values ranging from approximately 13 to 16 months, indicating that most patients were well beyond the early recovery phase at the time of HRQoL assessment.

The persistent reduction in total HRQoL after surgical intervention is clinically meaningful. Shunt surgery effectively decompresses the portal system and reduces the risk of variceal bleeding, yet children continue to experience multidimensional consequences of chronic disease. This suggests that HRQoL impairment in EHPVO is not solely a function of active portal hypertension or bleeding episodes, but rather reflects cumulative physical, psychological, educational, and social disruptions associated with long-standing illness.

Domain-wise analysis demonstrated significantly lower scores across all domains in both EHPVO groups compared with controls, reinforcing the multidimensional impact of the disease. The educational domain appeared most affected, followed by physical functioning. Recurrent hospital visits, procedural anxiety related to surveillance endoscopy, school absenteeism, and academic backlog likely contribute substantially to reduced school functioning. Similar patterns have been reported in other chronic pediatric gastrointestinal disorders, where school participation is one of the earliest and most sensitive indicators of disease burden.[910]

From a biological standpoint, chronic hypersplenism, early satiety due to splenomegaly, anemia, and growth retardation may impair physical stamina and participation in sports and daily activities. In addition, children with chronic illness often experience anticipatory anxiety regarding bleeding episodes or hospitalizations, which may influence emotional functioning. Chronic disease-related stress in childhood has been associated with altered psychosocial development and resilience mechanisms.[11]

The absence of significant differences between postoperative subgroups (proximal lienorenal shunt, distal lienorenal shunt, and splenectomy with devascularization) suggests that HRQoL may be influenced by disease chronicity and broader psychosocial factors in addition to surgical modality. This finding is important for surgical counseling: Choice of shunt type should be guided primarily by anatomical and physiological considerations rather than expectation of differential HRQoL benefit.

Previous studies examining HRQoL in EHPVO have demonstrated reduced scores compared with healthy controls across multiple domains.[78] Krishna et al. similarly reported persistent HRQoL impairment even after variceal eradication, particularly in physical and school functioning domains.[7] Anand et al. similarly observed lower PedsQL scores in pre-endoscopy and postsurgery groups compared with controls.[8] The present study corroborates these findings and strengthens them by incorporating effect size analysis, demonstrating that the magnitude of difference between disease and control groups is not only statistically significant but also clinically substantial.

Beyond EHPVO, studies in pediatric chronic liver disease and portal hypertension have shown comparable HRQoL impairments. Research in children with biliary atresia, chronic liver disease, and those who had undergone solid organ transplants indicates persistent deficits in physical and school domains even in medically stable patients.[1213] These findings support the broader concept that chronic hepatobiliary conditions exert long-term psychosocial effects independent of immediate disease severity.

Importantly, the present analysis used nonparametric statistics with median (IQR) reporting, given the skewed distribution of HRQoL scores, particularly in the control group, where ceiling effects were observed. Effect size estimation further confirmed that differences between controls and EHPVO groups were not only statistically significant but also clinically substantial.

Previous literature has established that children with EHPVO exhibit impaired HRQoL compared with healthy peers. However, the extent to which surgical intervention modifies this impairment has remained unclear. The current study adds several important insights:

Thus, this study reinforces that portal decompression alone may not be sufficient to fully restore psychosocial well-being within the time frame assessed.

Routine HRQoL assessment using validated tools such as PedsQL may help identify children at risk for persistent impairment and allow targeted interventions. Incorporating HRQoL monitoring into follow-up protocols aligns with modern value-based healthcare principles.[14]

Furthermore, the lack of HRQoL differentiation among surgical techniques suggests that decisions regarding shunt type should focus on anatomical feasibility, technical expertise, and complication profile rather than anticipated quality-of-life differences.

The absence of statistically significant differences between the pre- and postshunt groups should be interpreted with caution. Although the eligibility criterion required a minimum of 3 months postsurgery, the observed follow-up durations were substantially longer, with median values exceeding 1 year across all surgical subgroups. This indicates that the findings are unlikely to be solely attributable to very early postoperative recovery. However, the cross-sectional design and variability in follow-up duration limit the ability to capture temporal improvements in HRQoL. It is possible that longitudinal within-subject assessment may reveal gradual recovery not detectable in a single time-point comparison. The small effect sizes observed between these two groups further support that surgical status alone does not account for quality-of-life variation at the time of assessment.

This study has limitations. Important confounders such as socioeconomic status, parental education, and duration/severity of illness were not formally captured and may influence HRQoL outcomes. In addition, heterogeneity in follow-up duration and lack of standardized assessment of shunt patency may have influenced HRQoL outcomes. Future studies incorporating these variables are needed. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and limits understanding of HRQoL trajectory over time. The sample size, although reasonable for a rare condition, restricts subgroup power. Being a single-center study, generalizability may be limited. Although domain-wise comparisons were performed, the cross-sectional design precludes inference regarding longitudinal HRQoL recovery following surgical intervention.

Despite these limitations, the study possesses notable strengths. It utilizes a validated, widely accepted HRQoL instrument with both child self-report and parent proxy-report formats. Inclusion of healthy sibling controls reduces socioeconomic confounding. Effect size reporting strengthens interpretation beyond P values. In addition, subgroup surgical analysis provides clinically relevant comparative insight.

Future research should prioritize longitudinal studies evaluating HRQoL before and after surgical intervention within the same cohort. Incorporating objective clinical variables such as spleen size, transfusion history, anemia severity, and growth indices may help identify predictors of HRQoL impairment. Multicenter collaborative registries could enhance sample size and external validity. Interventional trials evaluating structured psychosocial and educational support programs would further clarify modifiable determinants of quality of life in EHPVO.

The present findings support a paradigm shift in the management of EHPVO from a purely hemodynamic focus toward an integrated biopsychosocial model of care. While portal decompression remains central to preventing life-threatening bleeding, sustained improvements in quality of life likely require coordinated educational, nutritional, and psychosocial interventions. Incorporating routine HRQoL assessment into long-term follow-up may enable early identification of vulnerable children and facilitate targeted multidisciplinary support.

CONCLUSION

Children with EHPVO experience substantial and clinically meaningful impairment in overall HRQoL compared with healthy peers. While surgical intervention is essential for portal decompression, HRQoL remains suboptimal within the follow-up period captured in this study. These findings highlight the need for multidisciplinary, holistic management strategies that address psychosocial, educational, and nutritional dimensions alongside surgical care. Routine incorporation of HRQoL assessment into follow-up protocols may improve long-term outcomes for this vulnerable population.

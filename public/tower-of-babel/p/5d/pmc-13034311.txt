Effectiveness and sustainability of physical activity interventions for non-Hispanic African American and black women in the USA: umbrella review protocol
Authors: Annette Hubbell, Jessica Sender, Louis Fogg, Meghan Garland, Susan Buchholz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDespite the well-established benefits of regular physical activity, only a small percentage of African American (AA) women meet national physical activity guidelines. This population faces unique cultural, social and environmental determinants that contribute to low participation rates. In response, various interventions have been developed to promote physical activity. While individual studies and reviews have examined these interventions, the evidence remains fragmented and of inconsistent quality. An umbrella review, or a review of reviews, provides a comprehensive method for summarising this body of literature. This protocol outlines the methods for conducting an umbrella review of physical activity interventions targeting AA women living in the USA.Methods and analysisA comprehensive search will be conducted in the Web of Science Core Collection, Social Sciences Full Text, SportDiscus, CINAHL, PubMed, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Evidence Synthesis, grey literature and relevant reference lists. Eligible articles published in English through September 2025 will be screened independently by >2 reviewers. Data extraction, analysis and quality assessment will follow. The AMSTAR 2 (A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews 2) and JBI recommendations for umbrella reviews will be used to assess methodological quality. The Corrected Covered Area will be used to characterise the overlap of primary studies across reviews.Ethics and disseminationThis umbrella review synthesises published data and does not involve human subjects, so ethics approval is not required. We will follow established guidance and best practice for transparency and integrity in evidence synthesis. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, community-partner briefs, open materials (eg, search strategies and data extraction templates) and in an Open Science Framework repository.

Introduction

Public health relevance

Physical inactivity is a public health concern, particularly for African American (AA) women who face a higher prevalence of heart failure, cardiovascular disease (CVD) and risk factors for CVD compared with their ethnic peers.1 Risk factors for CVD, including obesity, hypertension, hyperglycaemia and hypercholesterolaemia, can be mitigated through the protective benefits of regular physical activity (PA).2 While many AA women may recognise the importance of PA in reducing cardiovascular (CV) risk factors, only 16.5% of AA women meet the current PA guidelines for aerobic and strength training.1 As AA women continue to experience a disparity in total CVD prevalence (59%) compared with their non-Hispanic white (44.6%), Hispanic (37.3%) and Asian (38.5%) counterparts, regular participation in PA is necessary to address disparities in CVD and CVD risk factors among this population.1

Existing evidence and future research recommendations

Existing literature reviews on PA among AA women include non-interventional and interventional studies examining barriers, facilitators and mediators of PA initiation.3 4 In a scoping review, researchers examined non-interventional studies (n=7) and the individual, social and environmental influences encouraging PA maintenance among AA women. They concluded that few studies have specifically focused on how AA women sustain their activity levels and indicated that low participation in PA research studies may be due to these women finding creative, informal ways to meet their PA needs and could be a response to limited access to formal programmes or safe spaces that typically help people maintain PA.5

Among existing reviews examining interventions, Whitt-Glover et al identified characteristics of effective interventions encouraging PA among AA men and women from 16 studies (4 pilot and 12 trials) published between 2009 and 2013, an extension of their previous review published in 2009.6 The researchers offered several recommendations for future studies, including the development of interventions grounded in the cultural and historical perspectives of AA women’s attitudes towards PA, evaluating the impact of culturally adapted interventions on outcomes and investigating the long-term sustainability of these interventions.6 Jenkins and colleagues reviewed 32 experimental studies to identify intervention strategies and outcomes in studies designed to promote PA among AA women published between 2000 and 2015. Their recommendations for future studies included using larger sample sizes, randomising participants and using control groups.7 Tailored interventions for this population are only one theme noted in each review, and rightly so, as determinants to accessing health promotion resources have negatively impacted the health outcomes of AAs, including their ability to maintain health through PA, inasmuch that the effects of these determinants are recognised as a threat to the health of AAs.8 9 Interventions tailored to the needs of AA women may show increased participation and adherence compared to generic interventions or those tailored to other populations. 8

Justification for the umbrella review

Findings from existing reviews highlight the need for a synthesis summary to guide the development of interventions that align with AA women’s values and health beliefs.5 Summarising these reviews of PA interventions presents a valuable opportunity to gain valuable insights into what is known about the efficacy of PA interventions to maintain AA women’s PA. This population-specific umbrella review will address the question: what does the existing literature reveal about interventions to promote and maintain PA among AA women? In this umbrella review, we seek to address the following PICO:

Methods

Protocol development and registration

This protocol is registered in the Open Science Framework (OSF) (10.17605/OSF.IO/ZRM93) and was developed in accordance with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Manual for Evidence Synthesis guidelines for umbrella reviews, as well as a methodological guide for umbrella reviews.17 18 The authors’ preliminary search of existing umbrella review protocols in the OSF, the PROSPERO registry and JBI Evidence Synthesis confirmed that no umbrella reviews on this topic are registered. The authors then searched PubMed to determine whether umbrella reviews existed on this topic. They were unable to locate any reviews specific to the PICO.

Study population

This review focuses on adult women who identify as AA or black residing in the USA. The US context includes unique structural, social, cultural and policy conditions such as segregation, policing, neighbourhood disinvestment, limited care access and insurance and cardiometabolic risks that shape PA opportunities and CV disparities for AA women, influencing intervention design, uptake and long-term sustainment.

Sustainability

We will use the RE-AIM framework to define sustainability as (1) individual-level maintenance of PA behaviour >6 months after intervention ends and (2) setting/organisational-level sustainment, meaning continued or institutionalisation of the intervention for >12 months with capacity for adaptation. When available, we will also extract information related to cost, equity and scalability.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

These criteria will include (1) reviews that explicitly identify as systematic reviews and/or meta-analyses of interventions designed to influence PA and/or improve CV health among non-Hispanic AA or black women, aged 18 or older, living in the USA, and (2) published in the English language. Reviews of broader black populations will be included only if results are disaggregated specifically for AA women or non-Hispanic black women. Reviews will be excluded if they (1) focus on populations 17 and younger, (2) interventions focused on athletes, (3) focus on populations other than AA women or non-black women or (4) do not include subgroup narrative and/or statistical analyses on AA women when multiple racial groups are reported. Reviews that are not systematic in their methods will also be excluded. Additionally, reviews based solely on theoretical analyses or author opinions without empirical evidence will be excluded.17 Scoping, narrative or rapid reviews without systematic methods and reviews focused exclusively on correlates/determinants without an intervention will be excluded.

Study screening and selection

Reviews selected will be based on inclusion criteria, title and abstract, and full-text screening and will be performed independently by two or more reviewers. Any disagreements will be resolved by consensus or by a third-party reviewer.

Assessment of methodological quality

Two or more reviewers will independently assess selected reviews for methodological rigour and transparency using AMSTAR 2.22 This validated appraisal tool summarises the criteria for assessing the methodological quality of reviews containing randomised and non-randomised studies of healthcare interventions to be included in the umbrella review. Each included review will be evaluated using the 16-item checklist, which covers key domains such as protocol registration, an explicit statement of the review question, the appropriateness of the inclusion criteria and search strategy, the adequacy of the databases used for the search and the appropriateness of appraisal criteria. Additional criteria include the appropriateness of the meta-analytical methods and whether there was an adequate assessment of the risk of bias and its impact on the individual studies in the review.17 Questions within the tool will be addressed as a ‘yes’, ‘partial yes’ or ‘no’. The results will inform the interpretation of findings and contribute to the overall strength of the evidence summary. A list of reviews not meeting methodological quality criteria and therefore excluded from the umbrella review will be published in an appendix of the summary report.

Data collection

To ensure consistency and accuracy and minimise the risk of bias, two or more reviewers will independently use the JBI extraction tool, which the authors adapted to guide the extraction of details from selected reviews, such as review characteristics, search details, appraisal tools, outcomes relevant to the umbrella review question, how the synthesis and analysis were conducted and notes or comments from the umbrella review authors.10 After analysis, findings relevant to the review will be reported in the results section using a table of included study characteristics (see table 1). Any overlap of individual studies found among the reviews will be reported in the summary to avoid duplication in counting.16

Discussion

Rationale for review

Physical inactivity is a significant public health concern, particularly among AA women, who have the lowest rates of participation in PA and the highest prevalence of chronic conditions such as obesity, hypertension and hypercholesterolaemia. Despite the well-documented protective benefits of regular PA, most AA women do not meet recommended guidelines. Studies employing various methods of inquiry (ie, in-person, remote, individual, group) have implemented interventions aimed at increasing PA within this population, yielding a broad spectrum of findings, recommendations and areas for future exploration. The purpose of this umbrella review is to summarise findings from existing reviews to provide a comprehensive summary of interventions targeting PA among AA women.

Study strengths and limitations

This umbrella review is the first to identify and summarise interventions across reviews promoting PA among AA women living in the USA. Restricting to US populations improves the contextual relevance of findings for implementation in US systems but limits generalisability to the wider African diaspora. Additionally, there are inherent limitations to umbrella reviews that need to be acknowledged. First, umbrella reviews are a synthesis of reviews composed of primary studies. Varying eligibility criteria and rigour among the reviews and their included studies can make analysis and interpretation challenging. Second, there may be overlaps in the primary studies examined in these reviews. The reviewers have described measures in the methodology to mitigate these limitations, including using the principles outlined in AMSTAR 2 and documenting the degree of overlap of primary studies in the summary narrative.16 22

Ethics and dissemination

This umbrella review does not require ethical approval as it synthesises published data and does not involve human subjects. To ensure transparency and integrity in evidence synthesis, we will follow established guidance and best practices. Results of the review will be disseminated through several modes, including peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, community-partner briefs, open materials (eg, search strategies and data extraction templates), and an OSF repository.

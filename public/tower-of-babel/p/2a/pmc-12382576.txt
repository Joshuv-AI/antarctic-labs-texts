Efficacy of risankizumab across GRAPPA domains in psoriatic arthritis: a pooled analysis of patients from the phase 3 KEEPsAKE 1 and 2 studies
Authors: Laura C Coates, Ricardo Blanco, Frank Behrens, Alexis Ogdie, Filip Van den Bosch, Roberto Ranza, Yael Klionsky, Ahmed M Soliman, Michael Chen, Derek Coombs, Linyu Shi, Jamie R Urbanik, Thomas Iyile, Ralph Lippe, Laure Gossec
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo assess the efficacy of long-term treatment with risankizumab across the updated Group for Research and Assessment of Psoriasis and Psoriatic Arthritis (GRAPPA) domains and key related conditions of psoriatic arthritis (PsA).MethodsThis post hoc analysis primarily used data from the phase 3 KEEPsAKE 1 trial of adult patients with PsA, with data from KEEPsAKE 2 pooled for prespecified outcomes. Outcomes measuring risankizumab efficacy across key GRAPPA-recognised domains of PsA (peripheral arthritis, enthesitis, dactylitis, skin and nail psoriasis, axial disease) and PsA-related conditions such as inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and uveitis were assessed over 100 weeks of treatment (~2 years). Statistical approaches included non-responder imputation (as-observed with imputation) for categorical variables and mixed-effect model for repeated measures for continuous variables including as-observed measurements at all visits. PsA-related conditions were evaluated via adverse events through 100 weeks.ResultsOverall, in KEEPsAKE 1 and KEEPsAKE 2, 412/483 (85.3%) and 181/224 (80.8%) of patients randomised to risankizumab completed treatment to week 100. Risankizumab demonstrated efficacy across all GRAPPA-defined domains through 100 weeks, including swollen and tender joint counts, enthesitis, dactylitis, skin and nail outcomes, and axial disease. In KEEPsAKE 1, 42.4% of patients had achieved a Disease Activity in Psoriatic Arthritis measurement of low disease activity and 62.9% had reached a minimal clinically important difference in pain at week 100. Rates of new onset or flare of IBD and uveitis were low.ConclusionsTreatment with risankizumab provides durable improvement in the signs and symptoms of PsA across all GRAPPA disease domains and related conditions.Keywords: Arthritis, Psoriatic; Biological Therapy; DMARD

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

Introduction

Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) is a heterogenous rheumatological disease with key musculoskeletal manifestations in peripheral joints (joint tenderness and swelling, enthesitis, dactylitis), the spine, as well as skin.1,4 The clinical burden of PsA includes progressive joint damage, dactylitis, enthesitis, cutaneous manifestations, comorbidities and the presence of extra-musculoskeletal manifestations (EMMs; including inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and uveitis); chronic pain, impaired function and progressive disability can affect the overall burden of disease and patient quality of life (QoL).25,8

The Group for Research and Assessment of Psoriasis and Psoriatic Arthritis (GRAPPA), founded in 2003,9 aims to develop and periodically update recommendations for the optimal treatment and management of patients with PsA, based on evolving scientific evidence. The most recent recommendations, updated in 2021,3 consider the advances in therapeutic options for PsA since GRAPPA’s previous recommendations in 2015.10 The updated GRAPPA recommendations consider the key domains of PsA which impact patients’ QoL and prognosis: peripheral arthritis, axial disease, enthesitis, dactylitis, skin and nail psoriasis, PsA-related conditions such as IBD and uveitis, and associated comorbidities.3 11 12

Interleukin-23 (IL-23) is a key regulatory cytokine that is essential for the activation of pathogenic T helper 17 (Th17) cells that stimulate inflammation in both psoriasis and PsA.13 Risankizumab inhibits IL-23 by targeting the p19 subunit with high affinity and specificity, inhibiting the activation of the pathogenic Th17 cells.13 Risankizumab is approved for the treatment of moderate to severe psoriasis, active PsA, moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease (CD) and moderately to severe ulcerative colitis (UC) in adults.14 The KEEPsAKE 1 and KEEPsAKE 2 clinical trials have demonstrated that treatment with risankizumab delivers durable efficacy across multiple PsA domains up to week 100 and is associated with improved QoL, patient functioning, pain and nail involvement.15,22

This post hoc analysis aimed to assess the efficacy of long-term treatment with risankizumab across the updated GRAPPA domains and key related conditions of PsA.

Methods

Patients

This post hoc analysis included patients with active PsA who are participants in two ongoing, multicentre, double-blind, phase 3 trials—KEEPsAKE 1 (N=964; NCT03675308) and KEEPsAKE 2 (N=443; NCT03671148).15 16 20 21

Study design and treatment

The study design, patient eligibility criteria and initial patient population have been previously reported in detail for KEEPsAKE 1 and KEEPsAKE 2.15 16 20 21 Both double-blind phase 3 studies enrolled adult patients with PsA with an inadequate response, intolerance or contraindication to ≥1 conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (csDMARD-IR) and/or 1 or 2 biological DMARDs (bDMARD-IR; KEEPsAKE 2 only). Period 1 of each study consisted of a 24-week double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group treatment. Patients were randomised 1:1 to receive subcutaneous administration of risankizumab 150 mg or placebo at weeks 0, 4 and 16. Period 2 started at week 24, and to maintain blinding of treatment in period 1, patients who were randomised to risankizumab received blinded placebo at week 24, and patients who were randomised to placebo received the first dose of blinded risankizumab at week 24. Starting at week 28, all patients received open-label risankizumab 150 mg every 12 weeks.

Peripheral arthritis

Efficacy for peripheral arthritis was assessed using achievement of≥20%, 50% or 70% improvement in PsA signs and symptoms using American College of Rheumatology criteria (ACR20), ACR50 and ACR70 over time; maintenance of ACR20, ACR50 and ACR70 responses over time; improvement in swollen joint count in 66 joints (SJC66) and tender joint count in 68 joints (TJC68); maintenance of resolution in SJC66 and TJC68 over time; assessment of disease activity based on Disease Activity in PSoriatic Arthritis (DAPSA) scores with cut-offs as follows: remission≤4, low disease activity (LDA)>4 to ≤14, moderate disease activity>14 to ≤28 and high disease activity>28; achievement of minimal clinically important difference (MCID) in pain, assessed as a≥10-point decrease in patient’s assessment of pain,23 for patients with patient’s assessment of pain≥10 at baseline; assessment of patients with no radiographic progression in KEEPsAKE 1 (change from baseline in PsA-modified Total Sharp Score (mTSS)≤0 and PsA-mTSS≤0.5).

Enthesitis and dactylitis

Efficacy for enthesitis and dactylitis was assessed by achievement of Leeds Enthesitis Index24 (LEI)=0, among patients with LEI>0 at baseline, achievement of Leeds Dactylitis Index25 (LDI)=0, among patients with LDI>0 at baseline, and maintenance of resolution of enthesitis and dactylitis.

Psoriasis

Among patients with body surface area of psoriasis (BSA)≥3% at baseline, efficacy for skin disease activity was assessed using≥90% improvement from baseline in the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI90), maintenance of PASI90 over time, complete resolution (PASI100) and achievement of absolute PASI≤1.

Nail psoriasis

Efficacy in nail psoriasis was assessed only in the KEEPsAKE 1 study through mean change from baseline and achievement of improvement in the modified Nail Psoriasis Severity Index (mNAPSI),26 the Physician’s Global Assessment of Fingernail Psoriasis (PGA-F)27 scores, and achievement of ‘clear’ or ‘minimal’ nail psoriasis (PGA-F=0 or 1 with≥2-grade improvement from baseline). The mNAPSI tool used to assess the severity of nail psoriasis produces a score range of 0–13 for each individual fingernail and a cumulative score range of 0–130 across all 10 fingernails, where higher scores denote more severe nail involvement.26 The PGA-F tool was used to assess the severity of fingernail psoriasis. It categorises the severity into five categories: clear (0), minimal (1), mild (2), moderate (3), and severe (4), with higher scores indicating more severe disease.28

Axial disease

In patients with psoriatic spondylitis at baseline, based on investigator clinical assessment (including patients with spondylitis), efficacy of risankizumab for axial disease was assessed by achievement of MCID in the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Index (BASDAI) and modified BASDAI (excluding Question 3 for peripheral arthritis), defined as≥1.1-point decrease, for patients with BASDAI/modified BASDAI≥1.1 at baseline; achievement of≥2-point decrease from baseline in modified BASDAI among patients with baseline mBASDAI≥2 and achievement of≥1.1-point decrease from baseline in ASDAS among patients with baseline ASDAS≥1.1 to show clinically important improvement (CII) based on the ASAS-EULAR guidelines29; maintenance of MCID in BASDAI and modified BASDAI for KEEPsAKE 1; mean change from baseline in BASDAI and modified BASDAI; mean change from baseline in BASDAI spinal pain (Question 2); and mean change from baseline in the Ankylosing Spondylitis Disease Activity Score (ASDAS).30

EMMs: IBD and uveitis

To assess the development of key related conditions, as identified in the GRAPPA domains, among patients with PsA treated with risankizumab treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) of EMMs, including IBD and uveitis, were analysed. TEAEs were defined as an AE with onset on or after first dose of study drug and≤140 days after last dose and were coded according to the MedDRA (Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities) V.26.1. Associated preferred terms of investigator-reported TEAEs were manually grouped into categories representing IBD (inclusive of ‘inflammatory bowel disease’, ‘colitis ulcerative’, ‘colitis’, ‘Crohn’s disease’ and ‘proctitis’) and uveitis (inclusive of ‘uveitis’, ‘iridocyclitis’ and ‘iritis’). The occurrence of EMMs was reported as exposure-adjusted event rates, expressed as the number of events per 100 patient years (E/100 PYs), and was reported separately based on whether patients had a reported history (thus experiencing a flare) of the respective EMM or experienced a new onset event.

Statistical analysis

The efficacy analyses for outcomes assessed for peripheral arthritis (ACR20/50/70, DAPSA, pain, SJC and TJC, and radiographic progression), skin psoriasis (PASI90/100 and PASI≤1) and nail psoriasis (mNAPSI and PGA-F 0/1) used data from patients participating in the KEEPsAKE 1 study (csDMARD-IR patients). For outcomes assessed for axial manifestations, enthesitis and dactylitis, which are based on subgroups of patients with these manifestations at baseline, efficacy analyses used pooled data from patients participating in the KEEPsAKE 1 and KEEPsAKE 2 studies. Categorical endpoints were reported by multiple imputation methods; data through week 24 were reported using non-responder imputation (NRI) with missing data due to COVID-19 handled by multiple imputation (NRI-C); long-term analysis (weeks 24–100) used NRI with multiple imputation (NRI-MI) for missing data due to COVID-19 for categorical variables based on as-observed (AO) data, and to handle missing data due to geopolitical conflict in Ukraine and Russia (applicable for KEEPsAKE 1 only); all other missing data were treated as non-responders. Continuous efficacy endpoints were analysed using a mixed-effect model for repeated measures, at all the time points. Distribution of DAPSA disease activity level and percentage of patients with no radiographic progression was reported using the AO approach.

Results

Patients

For analyses based on the KEEPsAKE 1 study, 964 randomised patients were included, with 939 (97.4%) eligible patients entering period 2 to receive open-label risankizumab. For analyses including the pooled KEEPsAKE 1 and KEEPsAKE 2 population, 1407 patients, pooled from two studies, were included. Of these, 700 patients received placebo and 707 patients received continuous risankizumab 150 mg. As previously reported, demographics and baseline characteristics were generally balanced between the cohorts. In the continuous risankizumab cohort, female patients comprised 47.8% in the KEEPsAKE 1 trial and 55.4% in the KEEPsAKE 2 trial, and ranged in age from 20 to 85 years across both trials.16 20

Efficacy

Peripheral arthritis

As previously reported, significantly more patients receiving risankizumab in the KEEPsAKE 1 study achieved the primary endpoint of ACR20 at week 24 (57.3% compared with 33.5% in the placebo group).16 20 At week 100, 64.3% of patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort met ACR20 criteria, consistent with the 70.6% response rate observed at week 52. Among patients receiving continuous risankizumab who achieved an ACR20 response at week 24, 89.1% and 80.3% maintained the response to week 52 and week 100, respectively; 81.2% of patients who achieved ACR20 at week 52 maintained the response at week 100 (online supplemental figure 1). For ACR50 and ACR70, 72.4% and 63.5% of patients receiving continuous risankizumab who achieved these outcomes at week 24 maintained their response at week 100.31 32

Among patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort, 12.2%, 23.4% and 25.9% achieved DAPSA remission, and 43.5%, 65.7% and 68.3% achieved DAPSA LDA/remission at week 24, 52 and 100 in KEEPsAKE 1, respectively (figure 1). An MCID in patient’s assessment of pain was achieved by 63.4%, 67.0% and 62.9% of patients receiving continuous risankizumab in KEEPsAKE 1 at week 24, 52 and 100, respectively (figure 2A). There was no structural progression (defined as change from baseline in mTSS≤0) observed at week 100 in 90.6% of patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort in KEEPsAKE 1 (figure 2B). Patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort demonstrated improvement in SJC66 and TJC68 over 100 weeks in KEEPsAKE 1 (figure 2C). 81.1% of patients who achieved TJC68≤1 at week 24 maintained the response at week 52 and 70.8% of patients with TJC68≤1 at week 52 maintained the response at week 100 (online supplemental figure 2).

Enthesitis and dactylitis

Among patients with LEI>0 at baseline (N=444), 50.0%, 55.0% and 57.7% of patients in the combined continuous risankizumab cohort from KEEPsAKE 1 and KEEPsAKE 2 achieved resolution of enthesitis at week 24, 52 and 100, respectively (figure 3A). Among patients with LDI>0 at baseline (N=188), 71.3%, 76.6% and 76.2% of patients in the combined continuous risankizumab cohort achieved resolution of dactylitis at week 24, 52 and 100, respectively (figure 3B). Among patients who achieved resolution of enthesitis at week 24, 80.6% maintained the response at week 52; 79.7% of week 52 responders maintained the response at week 100 (online supplemental figure 3). Among patients who achieved resolution of dactylitis at week 24, 89.1% maintained the response at week 52; 89.3% of week 52 responders maintained the response at week 100 (online supplemental figure 3).

Psoriasis

As reported previously, among patients with BSA≥3% at baseline (N=273) in the KEEPsAKE 1 trial, 52.3%, 67.9% and 71.3% in the continuous risankizumab cohort achieved PASI90 at week 24, 52 and 100, respectively.31 Similar trends were observed for PASI100 and absolute PASI≤1, which were achieved by 33.7%, 50.2%, 52.0% and 57.7%, 69.8%, 72.7% of patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort in KEEPsAKE 1 at week 24, 52 and 100, respectively (figure 4). Among patients who achieved PASI90 at week 24, 86.4% maintained the response at week 52; 87.7% of week 52 responders maintained the response at week 100 (online supplemental figure 4).

Nail psoriasis

Patients with nail psoriasis at baseline in the continuous risankizumab cohort in KEEPsAKE 1 experienced improvement in mNAPSI scores through 100 weeks of treatment (figure 5A). A similar trend was observed for improvement in PGA-F scores (online supplemental figure 5). Among patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort, 38.3%, 55.9% and 61.6% of patients in KEEPsAKE 1 achieved a PGA-F score of ‘clear’ or ‘minimal’ with a≥2-grade improvement at weeks 24, 52 and 100, respectively (figure 5B).

Axial disease

At baseline, psoriatic spondylitis, or axial involvement, was reported in 19.6% (7.9% diagnosed by radiograph or MRI) of patients in KEEPsAKE 1 and 19.6% (5% diagnosed by radiograph or MRI) of patients in KEEPsAKE 2. Among patients with axial involvement at baseline, 51.5%, 62.0% and 58.0% of patients receiving continuous risankizumab in the pooled patient population (KEEPsAKE 1 and KEEPsAKE 2) achieved MCID in overall BASDAI at weeks 24, 52 and 100, respectively (figure 6A), and 54.2%, 62.7% and 57.7% achieved MCID in modified BASDAI at weeks 24, 52 and 100, respectively (online supplemental figure 7). Patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort demonstrated improvement in BASDAI, BASDAI spinal pain (Question 2) and ASDAS scores over 100 weeks (figure 6B–D), as well as in modified BASDAI (online supplemental figure 9). 45.9% of patients achieved CII in modified BASDAI and 38.2% achieved CII in ASDAS scores (online supplemental figure 6). Among patients who achieved MCID in BASDAI at week 24, 87.7% maintained the response at week 52, and 81.3% of week 52 responders maintained the response at week 100 (online supplemental figure 8). Among patients from KEEPsAKE 1 who achieved MCID in modified BASDAI at week 24, 88.9% maintained the response at week 52 and 76.9% maintained the response at week 100. Among patients who achieved MCID in modified BASDAI at week 52, 73.3% maintained the response at week 100 (online supplemental figure 8).

EMMs: IBD and uveitis

The majority (over 99%) of patients did not have a prior history of EMMs at baseline (figure 7). Events of IBD and uveitis were rare. There were four new onset cases reported with risankizumab for each (<0.1 E/100 PYs) and three flares for each (<0.1 E/100 PYs) (figure 7).

Overall, across all domains assessed, outcomes observed in patients who were initially randomised to placebo and switched to treatment with risankizumab after week 24 were generally similar by week 100 to those initially randomised to risankizumab.

Discussion

This post hoc analysis evaluated data from two ongoing, multicentre, double-blind, phase 3 studies of risankizumab for PsA treatment—KEEPsAKE 1 (N=964) and KEEPsAKE 2 (N=443).15 16 20 21 While findings from the phase 3 KEEPsAKE 1 and KEEPsAKE 2 studies in patients with active PsA have demonstrated durable efficacy for patients with PsA treated with risankizumab versus placebo, this study used multiple outcome measures and pooled data, when available, to assess the impact of treatment with risankizumab over 2 years, over all 6 GRAPPA domains of PsA, and the PsA-related conditions of IBD and uveitis.

Risankizumab was efficacious in reducing signs and symptoms of peripheral arthritis, including achievement of stringent treatment targets such as DAPSA LDA and remission (68.3% at week 100) and structural non-progression measured at 2 years in KEEPsAKE 1. Evaluated as an outcome measure for axial disease, 57.7% of pooled patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort reported MCID in modified BASDAI at week 100. Among pooled patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort, 57.7% and 76.2% showed resolution of enthesitis and dactylitis, respectively, at week 100.

Achieving comprehensive control across the multiple domains of PsA remains a challenge, with only one-third of treated patients with PsA achieving stringent disease control measures such as minimal disease activity or DAPSA LDA.33 The GRAPPA treatment recommendations take a comprehensive domain-based approach that ensures that both the primary PsA symptoms and the potential influence of comorbidities are taken into consideration when determining the most appropriate treatment strategy.3 It is noteworthy that not all patients will achieve optimal responses across all disease domains, and in these instances, it is important to examine the individual domains of PsA and implement a treat to target approach.

While the BASDAI and ASDAS were originally developed for the assessment of axial spondyloarthritis, these tools are frequently used to evaluate axial symptoms in patients with PsA due to the lack of assessment instruments specific to axial PsA. It is important to note that improvements in BASDAI and ASDAS scores may be affected by extra-axial symptoms, such as peripheral arthritis and enthesitis.34 In this study, we also report modified BASDAI scores aiming to minimise the influence of improvement in peripheral arthritis from the assessment of axial symptoms.

Although IL-23 is indicated to have a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of ankylosing spondylitis (AS), clinical trials involving the IL-23 inhibitors risankizumab and ustekinumab failed to demonstrate efficacy in the treatment of AS.35 36 The phenotypic variability observed in axial PsA suggests these conditions may involve distinct disease processes compared with AS, potentially leading to differences in therapeutic responses. Furthermore, it has been proposed that AS pathogenesis may include an IL-17 production pathway that operates independently of IL-23-mediated stimulation of Th17 cells.37

In separate clinical trials in patients with psoriasis, risankizumab has demonstrated efficacy and complete skin clearance (PASI100) by week 16 that is maintained up to 5 years.38,44 As reported previously for the UltIMMa-1 and UltIMMa-2, IMMvent, IMMerge and LIMMitless phase 3 clinical studies, patients with psoriasis treated with risankizumab demonstrated superior efficacy compared with those treated with ustekinumab, secukinumab or adalimumab.25,28 In alMM, suboptimal responders to secukinumab or ixekizumab who switched to risankizumab demonstrated improved skin symptoms. In the IMMpulse study, more patients achieved higher treatment targets with risankizumab than with apremilast at week 16 (achievement of PASI100, PASI90 and sPGA 0/1), and on to week 52.29 Meaningful efficacy for skin outcomes was also demonstrated in this post hoc analysis of PsA trials, which showed that 72.7% of patients in the continuous risankizumab cohort in KEEPsAKE 1 achieved absolute PASI≤1 at week 100, and 61.6% achieved ‘clear’ or ‘minimal’ nail psoriasis at week 100.

In separate clinical trial programmes including the phase 3 INSPIRE, and phase 3 ADVANCE and MOTIVATE induction trials, risankizumab has demonstrated efficacy in both UC and CD.45 46 In the head-to-head phase 3 SEQUENCE study evaluating the efficacy and safety of risankizumab versus ustekinumab for patients with moderately to severely active CD who had failed one or more tumour necrosis factor inhibitors, risankizumab met both primary endpoints of non-inferiority for clinical remission at week 24 and superiority of endoscopic remission at week 48 as compared with ustekinumab.47

In a retrospective longitudinal study of 406 patients with PsA from a single referral hospital, uveitis was prevalent in approximately 5% of patients.48 In patients from the pooled KEEPsAKE studies receiving long-term risankizumab treatment (total risankizumab exposure of 4594 PYs), this post hoc analysis reports a relatively lower incidence of uveitis; uveitis was reported in five (0.4%; 0.2/100 PYs) patients overall, two of whom had a history of uveitis. The incidence of uveitis reported for long-term bimekizumab treatment in patients with PsA is 0.2%; 0.1/100 PYs (total bimekizumab exposure of 3656 PYs and data presented as exposure-adjusted incidence rate; uveitis occurred in three patients overall, one with a history of uveitis) and the incidence of uveitis reported for long-term upadacitinib treatment in patients with PsA is 0.3%; 0.2/100 PYs (total upadacitinib exposure of 2426 PYs; uveitis occurred in three patients overall, one with a history of uveitis).49,51

In a comparative study of 306 patients with PsA from a single university centre, IBD was prevalent in 3.3% of patients.52 In patients from the pooled KEEPsAKE studies receiving long-term risankizumab treatment (total risankizumab exposure of 4594 PYs), this post hoc analysis reports a relatively lower incidence of IBD. IBD was reported in six (0.4%; 0.2/100 PYs) patients overall, of which three patients had a prior history of IBD. IBD incidence reported for long-term bimekizumab treatment in patients with PsA is 0.6%; 0.2/100 PYs (total bimekizumab exposure of 3656 PYs; IBD occurred in eight patients overall, one with a history of IBD) and the incidence of IBD reported for long-term upadacitinib treatment in patients with PsA is 0.3%; 0.1/100 PYs (total upadacitinib exposure of 2426 PYs; IBD occurred in three patients overall, two with a history of IBD).50 51 This post hoc analysis is not without limitations. The participant population across both KEEPsAKE trials has high levels of disease activity, which is typical of a clinical trial population, and may limit the overall applicability of the findings. For outcomes such as resolution of enthesitis and dactylitis and those related to axial disease, the sample sizes of patients with corresponding baseline conditions were relatively smaller than for other outcomes analysed. Additionally, for patients with psoriatic spondylitis, or axial involvement, not all cases were confirmed by imaging. Outcomes related to IBD and uveitis were limited by the relatively small number of enrolled patients with a history of these conditions. Comorbidities in PsA were not included as part of this study.

Overall, this post hoc analysis demonstrates further that risankizumab delivers durable efficacy across all domains of PsA in patients who are csDMARD-IR and/or bDMARD-IR.

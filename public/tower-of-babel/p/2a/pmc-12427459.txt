Next-generation sequencing identifies potential mechanisms of primary resistance to trastuzumab-containing therapies in HER2-positive gastric cancer: Analysis based on real-world data and online databases
Authors: Xuchen Zhang, Ning Liu, Shanai Song, Chuantao Zhang, Tianjun Li, Wei Song, Shusheng Huang, Yunqing Chen, Weihua Yan, Jinfeng Zhan, Huiyun Wang, Jiali Cui, Man Jiang, Helei Hou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTrastuzumab-containing therapy remains a treatment option for patients with HER2-positive gastric cancer (GC). However, primary resistance to trastuzumab is a challenge. Therefore, it is essential to identify biomarkers for predicting the efficacy of trastuzumab-based treatment. First, we retrospectively collected the clinical data of HER2-positive GC patients receiving trastuzumab-containing therapies at the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University from October 2019 to August 2024. The patients were divided into long-term and short-term response groups according to progression-free survival (PFS) time, treatment lines and regimens. The next-generation sequencing (NGS) results of these patients were analyzed to identify biomarkers for predicting the efficacy of trastuzumab-containing therapies. Next, the cBioPortal database and LAVA database were screened to explore molecular profiling data. A total of 38 patients were included at our center and were categorized into a responder group (N = 28) and a nonresponder group (N = 10). ERBB2 L755S alteration, CDKN2A insertion, and RICTOR amplification (0% vs 10.0%, P = .263, for all of the above factors) were only observed in nonresponders. Among the ERBB2-amplified samples from GC patients in the cBioPortal database, TP53 missense mutations (51.1%) and CCNE1 amplifications (25.5%) were the most common co-mutations associated with ERBB2 amplification. Moreover, the incidences of ERBB2 L755S were 16.7% in ERBB2-amplified GC and 52.9% in ERBB2-amplified breast cancer (P = .179) according to the LAVA database. ERBB2 L755S, CDKN2A insertion, and RICTOR amplification might confer resistance to trastuzumab and/or immunotherapy in HER2-positive GC, thus highlighting the clinical significance of NGS in guiding personalized treatment in these patients.KEYWORDS: HER2-positive gastric cancer, primary resistance, trastuzumab, immunotherapy, next-generation sequencing

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) imposes a severe medical burden and is characterized by high morbidity and mortality rates worldwide, with 968,350 new cases (4.9%) and 659,853 deaths (6.8%) globally in 2022.1 In China, GC is the fifth most common cancer (358,700 new cases) and the third leading cause of cancer-related death (260,400 deaths) in 2022.2 Approximately 20% of GC patients have human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive GC; HER2-positive GC is characterized by HER2 overexpression or ERBB2 amplification, which are associated with aggressive biological behaviors, high intratumoral heterogeneity and dismal prognoses.3

As demonstrated by the milestone phase III ToGA trial, the combined regimen of trastuzumab (an anti-HER2 monoclonal antibody) and chemotherapy has shown superior efficacy compared with chemotherapy alone in the first-line treatment of metastatic gastroesophageal junction cancer (GEJC) and GC; however, the median overall survival (OS) of this treatment regimen was only 13.8 months,4 which was far from satisfactory. Fortunately, due to the increase in the use of programmed cell death-1/ligand-1 (PD-1/PD-L1) blockade for the first-line treatment of patients with advanced or metastatic GEJC/GC,5,6 recent studies have focused on whether adding PD-1 blockade to trastuzumab plus chemotherapy could benefit HER2-positive patients. Recently, the phase III KEYNOTE-811 trial revealed that the combination of pembrolizumab, trastuzumab and chemotherapy yielded higher objective response rates (ORRs), prolonged progression-free survival (PFS; 10.9 months), and prolonged OS (20.0 months) compared with regimens without pembrolizumab, especially in the PD-L1-positive cohort.7 Based on the above results, the combinational approach of pembrolizumab, trastuzumab and chemotherapy has become the standard treatment for HER2-positive GC patients with PD-L1 positivity. Even though these results indicated tremendous success, the effects of these regimens on long-term survival could be elucidated by identifying the predictive biomarkers of trastuzumab-based treatment in patients with HER2-positive GC.

In clinical practice, the efficacy of trastuzumab-containing therapies varies in patients with HER2-positive GC. Most patients might develop acquired resistance to trastuzumab within 1 year of trastuzumab-containing treatment,8 and the aberrant activations of PI3K/AKT and Wnt/β-catenin pathways are the putative acquired resistance mechanisms.9,10 However, the researches on primary resistance to trastuzumab are limited. HER2 heterogeneity and altered intracellular signaling have been proven to be putative resistance mechanisms,11–13 but only a few predictive biomarkers have been proposed as indicators of the tumor response to trastuzumab in patients with metastatic GC. Higher ERBB2 gene amplification levels could indicate better tumor responses, but the actual thresholds for the ERBB2 copy number have not yet been determined.14–16 Additionally, the coexistence of alterations in oncogenes related to the HER2 signaling pathway, such as EGFR/KRAS,16–18 and CD4+ memory T-cell scores19 could also act as efficacy-related predictors of treatment with a trastuzumab-containing regimen and immunotherapy.

ERBB2 missense mutations (especially L755S) might trigger resistance to trastuzumab-based treatment in HER2-positive tumors. In patients with HER2-positive breast cancer, the ERBB2 L755S alteration has been proven to be involved in resistance to trastuzumab and HER2 tyrosine kinase receptors.20 However, in patients with HER2-positive GC, the role of the L755S mutation in resistance to trastuzumab and/or immunotherapy remains unclear. The robust predictors of tumor response and the underlying mechanisms of primary resistance to trastuzumab or trastuzumab combined with immunotherapy have yet to be fully elucidated. Even though next-generation sequencing (NGS) has not been commonly recommended by treatment guidelines for selecting treatment regimens in GC patients, and that it usually takes around 2 to 4 weeks to produce interpretable NGS results, the results of NGS testing will do favor in screening potential trastuzumab-resistant patients and further to identify optimal treatment regimen and improve survival.

Given the clinical challenges posed above, this study aimed to identify predictive biomarkers of trastuzumab-containing treatment responses using NGS results, with the potential to reduce the cost of treatment, improve affordability, enhance tolerability, as well as ease of the initiation of PD-1 blockade-based therapy in patients with HER2-positive GC.

Materials and methods

Study design

First, we retrospectively collected clinical data from HER2-positive GC patients who received trastuzumab-containing treatments at our center between October 2019 and August 2024. The major inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) patients diagnosed with histologically proven GC with HER2-positive status; (b) patients who received trastuzumab-containing treatments; (c) patients with available pre-treatment biopsy samples and next-generation sequencing (NGS) results; (d) patients with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score of 0 to 2 and sufficient vital organ functions; and (e) patients with measurable targeted lesions. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) not receiving trastuzumab-containing treatments; (b) without available pre-treatment biopsy samples and NGS results; (c) receiving other concurrent therapeutic agents including antiangiogenesis drugs and herbal medicine; and (d) had malignancies other than GC. On the basis of the median PFS of 6.7 months that was observed in the trastuzumab cohort of the ToGA trial and the median PFS of 10.0 months that was observed in the pembrolizumab cohort of the KEYNOTE-811 trial, we selected the corresponding PFS cut-offs, as shown in Figure 1A. Patients at our center were divided into those who exhibited a long-term response (responder group) and those who exhibited a short-term response (nonresponder group) according to the PFS associated with trastuzumab-containing treatment, treatment lines and regimens.

Next, we screened ERBB2-amplified GC patients from the cBioPortal for Cancer Genomics (cBioPortal database, https://www.cbioportal.org/).21,22 The criteria for selecting the studies in cBioPortal database were: (a) studies with sufficient sample sizes and containing GC patients; (b) studies with detailed NGS results (containing copy number variations). Then, two pancancer studies were included, namely, the MSK-IMPACT Clinical Sequencing Cohort (MSK, Nat Med 2017) and China Pan-cancer (OrigiMed, Nature 2022), to explore co-mutations with ERBB2 amplification in ERBB2-amplified GC; In addition, three pancancer studies from cBioPortal database, namely, the MSK-IMPACT Clinical Sequencing Cohort (MSK, Nat Med 2017), MetTropism (MSK, Cell 2021) and MSK-CHORD (MSK, Nature 2024) were screened to analyse the overall survival probabilities of GC patients harboring co-mutations with ERBB2 amplification. Moreover, patients with solid tumors who harbored ERBB2 L755S missense alterations were screened from the Burning Rock® LAVA database (P. R. China)23 to explore the coexistence of genetic mutations with ERBB2 L755S in various tumors (Figure 1B).

Assessments

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining with anti-HER2 antibody, and HER2 in situ hybridization (ISH) were used to evaluate the expression of HER2. HER2-positive GC patients were those with an IHC staining score of 3+, or an IHC staining score of 2+ in combination with ERBB2 amplification confirmed by in situ hybridization.24

The expression of programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) was detected using immunohistochemical staining with the anti-PD-L1 antibody 22C3 (Dako, Glostrup, Denmark). The combined positive score (CPS) was calculated to evaluate the number of PD-L1-positive cells, including tumor cells, macrophages and lymphocytes. Briefly, a CPS < 1 was considered to indicate PD-L1 negativity, and a CPS ≥ 1 indicated PD-L1 positivity.

The status of microsatellite instability (MSI) was evaluated by detecting the amplifications of five MSI loci: BAT25, BAT26, D5S346, D17S250, and D2S123 using real-time polymerase chain reactions. If two or more unstable markers were observed at the above-mentioned five loci, MSI-high (MSI-H) status was defined; the microsatellite status was defined as MSI-low (MSI-L) if only one unstable marker was observed, and microsatellite stability (MSS) if no unstable markers were observed.25

Evaluation of tumor response and survival

RECIST version 1.1 was applied to evaluate radiographic tumor responses (complete response, CR; partial response, PR; stable disease, SD; or progressive disease, PD).26 The survival outcomes of interest were PFS calculating the time from the beginning of treatment to disease progression and overall survival (OS).

Statistical analysis

Medians (interquartile ranges) or means (standard deviations) were calculated to describe continuous variables, and percentages and frequencies to describe categorical variables. Student’s t test was applied to evaluate the significance of differences between two groups for continuous variables, and χ2 or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Survival probabilities were estimated using Kaplan‒Meier estimator and log-rank test, with respective hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidential intervals (CIs). A two-tailed P-value < .05 was considered statistically significance. SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism 9.5 (GraphPad Software, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) were used to perform the statistical analyses.

Results

Study population

A total of 38 patients were retrospectively included at our center on the basis of the inclusion criteria. The included patients were divided into the responder group (N = 28) and the nonresponder group (N = 10) based on the PFS time of trastuzumab-containing therapy, detailed treatment strategies and treatment lines. There were no significant differences in the baseline demographics or clinicopathological characteristics between the two groups (Table 1). Additionally, a total of 94 ERBB2-amplified patients were obtained from two pancancer studies in the cBioPortal database to analyze co-mutations, including 25 patients from the MSK-IMPACT cohort and 69 patients from the OrigiMed cohort. 2,862 patients were screened from the MSK-CHORD cohort, MSK-IMPACT cohort and MetTropism cohort for survival analyses. Forty-seven ERBB2 L755S-mutant patients with solid tumors were screened in the LAVA database.

Genetic alterations in long- and short-term responders

ERBB2 amplification was detected in 35 (92.1%) patients, including 26 (92.9%) patients in the responder group and nine (90.0%) in the nonresponder group. ERBB2 L755S, BRAF V600E, CDKN2A insertion, RICTOR amplification, PIK3CA missense mutation and KRAS missense mutation were observed only in nonresponders, whereas KRAS amplification, PIK3CA amplification and PTEN alteration occurred only in responders. ERBB2 missense mutations were detected in two nonresponders (L755S and A678G) and three responders (R599C, R678Q and L869R).

A total of 21 (55.3%) patients had concurrent PD-L1 positivity, 12 of whom received a regimen of PD-1 blockade combined with trastuzumab and chemotherapy. Eight of these patients presented a durable response, and the other four were defined as nonresponders. ERBB2 L755S, CDKN2A insertion, CDK12 amplification, and PIK3CA missense alterations were detected in the four nonresponders. The CPS values of these four nonresponders were one case of 1, one case of 20, and two cases of 10; in contrast, none of the responders had a CPS value higher than 5. After a median follow-up of 20.43 months, the median PFS was 30.70 months in the responders and 3.12 months in the nonresponders (HR = 0.095, 95% CI 0.019–0.472, P < .0001, Figure 2B). Notwithstanding, the PFS probabilities between the patients receiving PD-1 blockade, trastuzumab and chemotherapy (N = 18) and the patients receiving trastuzumab plus chemotherapy (N = 20) were not significant (30.70 months versus 11.03 months, P = .589, Figure 2C).

Genomic alterations cooccurred with ERBB2 amplification and survival analyses in the cBioPortal database

Among the 94 ERBB2-amplified GC patients obtained from cBioPortal, the NGS results revealed that TP53 missense mutations and CCNE1 amplifications were the most common cooccurring mutations with ERBB2 amplification, with incidences of 51.1% (48/94) and 25.5% (24/94), respectively. PIK3CA alterations and VEGFA amplification were both observed in 6.4% (6/94) of the patients. Among these ERBB2-amplified GC patients, only two patients harbored concurrent ERBB2 missense mutations, both with D769Y. The patients harboring ERBB2 D769Y mutations also harbored coexisting TP53 nonsense mutations and CDK12 nonsense mutations (Figure 3A).

The results revealed that the CDKN2A-co-mutant subgroup had a lower OS probability in ERBB2-amplified GC (P = .0004).

Interesting and representative findings of primary resistance in two HER2-positive GC patients

Herein, we reported two particular HER2-positice GC cases: one patient with concurrent ERBB2 L755S alteration, and one patient with concurrent CDKN2A insertion. Both of these patients had baseline stage IVB disease and rapid disease progression on first-line treatment with trastuzumab, PD-1 blockade and chemotherapy.

Case 1 involved a 53-year-old male harboring ERBB2 L755S mutation with concurrent TP53 P151R, CCNE1 amplification and MCL1 amplification. The patient had a CPS value of 20 and a high tumor mutational burden (TMB) of 37.85 muts/Mb. Multiple metastatic lesions were found in the lung, liver, abdominal wall, and mediastinal and abdominal lymph nodes in February 2023. The patient began to receive first-line treatment with trastuzumab, SOX regimen chemotherapy and sintilimab beginning in March 2023, and he experienced rapid disease progression, with a PFS of 2.2 months. Thereafter, the chemotherapy regimen was replaced with the TP regimen, and the patient continued treatment with trastuzumab and sintilimab. However, second-line treatment still had poor effectiveness. Sintilimab plus the HER2-ADC agent disitamab vedotin was administered as a third-line treatment for one cycle but was discontinued owing to intolerance. This patient died in December 2023 (Figure 4A,B).

Case 2 involved a 43-year-old female with bilateral adrenal metastases at baseline. She had concurrent CDKN2A insertion, TP53 and ARID1A alterations, a CPS value of 10, and a low TMB of 4.0 muts/Mb. This patient then began first-line treatment with trastuzumab, XELOX regimen chemotherapy and tislelizumab in September 2024, while PD was defined in December 2024, with a PFS of 2.9 months. HER2-ADC agent-containing regimens were considered for subsequent treatment.

Incidences of ERBB2 L755S in solid tumours and its co-mutations in the LAVA database

We further explored the incidence of ERBB2 L755S and its cooccurring mutations in solid tumors. The NGS results of 47 patients with various solid tumors harboring ERBB2 L755S were screened from the LAVA database. Among these patients, six (12.8%) were diagnosed with GC, eight (17.0%) were diagnosed with colorectal cancer, 17 (36.2%) were diagnosed with breast cancer, 13 (27.6%) were diagnosed with lung cancer, and three (6.4%) were diagnosed with other tumors. Coexisting ERBB2 amplification was observed in 20 (42.6%) of these patients with the L755S mutation; the incidence of this amplification was lower in GC patients than in breast cancer patients, with 16.7% (1/6) in GC and 52.9% (9/17) in breast cancer (P = .179). For the six GC patients, TP53 alterations and ARID1A alterations most frequently coexisted with ERBB2 L755S, both with incidences of 50% (3/6), as shown in Figure 4C.

Discussion

In patients with HER2-positive GC, primary resistance to trastuzumab remains a challenge, and the mechanisms and optimal treatments for these patients have yet to be fully elucidated. In this retrospective study, negative predictive markers for trastuzumab-containing treatments in HER2-positive GC – in particular, ERBB2 L755S and CDKN2A insertion – were identified by NGS. Moreover, we chose pancancer studies from the cBioPortal database to investigate the prognostic roles of the abovementioned genes, and we selected the patients harboring ERBB2 L755S from the LAVA database to determine the distribution of this mutation in solid tumors. These outcomes highlight the clinical importance of NGS in promoting precision treatments for HER2-positive GC patients.

Alterations in oncogenes related to HER2 signaling, such as EGFR, KRAS, MET, and PIK3CA, have been shown to confer trastuzumab resistance in HER2-positive GC according to several recent studies.16,17,19,27 Similarly, in patients with HER2-positive breast cancer, preclinical studies have identified several negative predictive biomarkers for targeted therapy, notably ERBB2 L755S.20,28–30 In our study, we observed that ERBB2 L755S, BRAF V600E, CDKN2A insertion, KRAS missense mutation, PIK3CA missense mutation and RICTOR amplification were observed only in nonresponders and could confer resistance to trastuzumab and/or immunotherapy in HER2-positive GC; however, EGFR and MET amplifications were observed only in long-term responding patients. This discrepancy might be due to the limited sample size and variations in immunogenic factors.

In ERBB2-amplified GC patients, concurrent ERBB2 missense mutations have been reported to be associated with primary trastuzumab resistance,16,19,31 and in HER2-positive breast cancer patients, ERBB2 L755S-mutant cohorts had impaired efficacy of anti-HER2 treatment induced by aberrant activation of the MAPK and PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathways.20,29,32 Additionally, CDKN2A alteration is associated with a cold tumor immune microenvironment with fewer CD8+T cells and more exhausted T cells in gastric adenocarcinoma,33 but its role in HER2-positive counterparts remains unclear. In our study, we reported two patients with concurrent ERBB2 L755S alteration and CDKN2A insertion, separately; both of these patients exhibited PD-L1 positivity (CPS values of 20 and 10, respectively), while neither patient experienced long-term tumor response to first-line treatment with trastuzumab, PD-1 blockade or chemotherapy or rapid progression within three months, indicating that ERBB2 L755S and CDKN2A insertion might confer resistance to trastuzumab and immunotherapy by affecting immune-related and HER2 signaling pathways in HER2-positive gastric cancer. These findings further highlight the clinical significance of targeted sequencing for identifying potential trastuzumab-resistant and/or immunoresistant subgroups in HER2-positive gastric cancer. Considering that the mechanisms conferring trastuzumab-resistance might also trigger resistance to immunotherapy, we infer that the application of HER2-ADC agents may be a superior first-line treatment option for ERBB2-amplified GC patients with concurrent CDKN2A alterations and/or ERBB2 missense mutations, even though HER2-ADC agents are currently approved only for previously treated advanced HER2-positive GC patients.34 In the future, prospective studies with larger sample sizes are needed to investigate the safety and efficacy of HER2-ADC agents as first-line treatments in HER2-positive GC patients with concurrent potential resistance biomarkers.

The percentages of concurrent HER2 and PD-L1 positivity in GEJC/GC vary among studies, and the value of PD-L1 CPS values for predicting responses to trastuzumab and immunotherapy among patients with HER2-positive GC remains unclear.35–38 In the KETNOTE-811 trial, the CPS ≥ 1 subgroup had prolonged OS under the combined regimen of trastuzumab, pembrolizumab and chemotherapy compared with the CPS < 1 cohort, but it also remains unclear whether a higher CPS value indicates a better tumor response. Notably, the Kaplan‒Meier curves for PFS in the two cohorts at the beginning of follow-up were not entirely distinguishable,37 indicating that there might be some undetected participants who had primary resistance to trastuzumab and/or immunotherapy, thus highlighting the importance of NGS testing at baseline for patients with HER2-positive GC. In the cohort from our center, the rate of HER2 positivity combined with PD-L1 positivity was 55.3% (21/38). Among these patients with co-positivity, 12 patients received the combined regimen of PD-1 blockade, trastuzumab and chemotherapy, and four of these 12 patients were nonresponders. Patients with higher CPS values seemed to have worse tumor responses, which was contradicted with the results of KEYNOTE-811 study. It has been reported that in CDKN2A-mutant GC, the tumor microenvironment was defined as cold tumors, and these patients have poor tumor response to immunotherapy owing to CDKN2A-mediated immune evasion.33,39 In addition, in the patients with high TMB, the incidence of genetic immune escape seems to be higher.40,41 The above factors may both trigger resistance to immunotherapy, and the actual correlation should be verified in larger cohorts. ERBB2 L755S, CDKN2A insertion, CDK12 amplification, and PIK3CA missense alteration were observed in the four immunoresistant nonresponders, indicating the roles of these alterations in conferring resistance to trastuzumab and PD-1 blockade. These results highlight that there might be undefined nonresponders who have primary resistance to trastuzumab and/or immunotherapy, and NGS testing at baseline is essential for identifying potential resistant and beneficial cohorts of HER2-positive GC patients.

Despite the importance of performing NGS testing in patients with HER2-positive GC, there are several factors that need to be taken into consideration. Generally, it takes 2 to 4 weeks to obtain interpretable results of NGS testing, and the cost of NGS testing imposes a financial burden on patients.42 For patients with HER2-positive GC, NGS testing at baseline and is still necessary, as NGS testing can detect the genetic alterations which might confer resistance, thereby further predict the efficacy of treatment and optimize treatment options. Furthermore, certain drugs could be reserved as a last resort in management, and patients could have potential gains in quality-adjusted life-year. These further highlighted the significance of NGS testing in patients with HER2-positive GC.

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective nature, the limited sample size of the real-world cohort, variations in treatment regimens and treatment lines, and varied immunogenic factors might be confounders of the observations. In addition, dynamic monitoring of genomic alterations at baseline and after disease progression was not sufficient. Despite these shortcomings, the NGS results still provided insights into the potential primary resistance mechanisms underlying trastuzumab-containing therapies. Furthermore, more suitable treatment regimens were identified for patients with HER2-positive GC. In the future, adequately powered studies with larger sample sizes should be performed to further validate these findings.

Conclusions

ERBB2 L755S, BRAF V600E, CDKN2A insertion, KRAS missense mutation, PIK3CA missense mutation, and RICTOR amplification were observed only in nonresponders and might confer resistance to trastuzumab and/or immunotherapy in HER2-positive GC. These findings highlight the clinical significance of NGS at baseline in guiding personalized treatments for patients with HER2-positive GC.

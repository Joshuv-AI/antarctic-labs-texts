Promoting plant yield and quality improvement in saline alkali land by using biomass organic fertilizer and reducing chemical fertilizer measures
Authors: Jun Zhao, Qiaoxian Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and aimsThe agricultural development of saline alkali land has always been an important direction for improving land use efficiency and food production. The study aims to explore the effects of biomass organic fertilizer and reducing the use of chemical fertilizers on watermelons in saline alkali land, providing a scientific basis for the sustainable development of saline alkali agriculture.MethodsThe study selects a mixed application of biomass organic fertilizer and chemical fertilizer with different proportions, and compares the growth, yield, and quality of watermelon in saline alkali soil.ResultsThe research results indicated that the biomass organic fertilizer improved the growth rate and yield of watermelon in saline alkali soil. Compared with conventional fertilization treatment, the combination of biomass organic fertilizer and weight loss significantly increased soil organic matter content and promoted watermelon growth. In addition, watermelon treated with biomass organic fertilizer also performed better in terms of quality, including better fruit size, soluble solid content, and vitamin C content than conventional fertilizer treatment. The study also found that applying organic biomass fertilizer and weight loss in saline alkali land can increase the yield and quality of watermelons, and have a significant improvement effect on soil nutrients. This fertilization method could effectively increase the soil organic matter content and provide a more suitable environment for the growth of watermelons.ConclusionsThe above results indicate that the proposed combination has significant advantages in watermelon cultivation in saline alkali land, and is significant for the sustainable development of saline alkali agriculture.

Introduction

With the development of global agriculture and population growth, food security and agricultural sustainability have become global concerns. Among them, optimizing crop production, especially under special soil conditions, is significant for improving agricultural yield and quality [1]– [2]. Watermelon, as a widely cultivated fruit, deserves special attention for its planting performance in saline alkali soil. Currently, the excessive use of fertilizers not only increases crop yields but also brings problems such as soil degradation and environmental pollution, making it necessary to find more environmentally friendly and sustainable planting solutions. Biomass organic fertilizer, as a renewable agricultural resource, has shown great potential in improving soil quality, increasing crop yield and quality [3]. Compared to traditional chemical fertilizers, biomass organic fertilizers can more effectively provide the nutrients needed by crops, while promoting soil microbial diversity, thereby improving soil structure and promoting healthy plant growth [4]. Especially under saline alkali soil planting conditions, the use of organic fertilizer improves the soil and combats the adverse effects of salt on crop growth. However, completely replacing fertilizers is not an economically viable solution, especially in resource-limited agricultural production conditions [5]– [6]. Therefore, reducing the application of chemical fertilizers while using biomass organic fertilizers has become a more feasible solution. This method can not only maintain the economic benefits of crop production but also improve soil quality and achieve sustainable agricultural development.

Numerous scholars have conducted extensive research on plant species in saline alkali soils. Rezaei Chiyaneh et al. conducted a 3-year experiment on different fertilizers for the productivity of borneol essential oil in monoculture and intercropping common broad beans using a random complete interval group design method. The experiment found that when earthworm manure organic fertilizer was applied to broad beans in a single quarter, the highest broad bean yield and the leading dried tofu material productivity could be achieved [7]. Hayatu et al. proposed combining fertilizers with organic amendments to improve rice yield, soil carbon storage, and nutrient utilization efficiency. The experiment evaluated the impact of organic fertilizer on rice yield and determined the optimal substitution rate for acidic soils in the south. It was found that adding 70% organic fertilizer to chemical fertilizers became the best strategy for managing rice fields in the southern region [8]. To analyze the chemical fertilizers replacement with organic fertilizers on rice yield, physicochemical properties, and bacterial community structure, Song et al. conducted a 5-year experiment on Jiangxi double cropping rice using different proportions of organic fertilizer as the research object. It was found that the organic fertilizer effectively reduced soil acidification and increased nutrient content [9]. Sharma et al. conducted a two-year experiment to analyze the effects of organic fertilizers and plant growth agents on soil fertility, nutrient absorption, and fruit yield of sweet pepper. The experimental site was on sandy soil. Results showed that organic and inorganic fertilizers combined effectively improved soil health, promoted soil nutrient absorption, and increased sweet pepper yield [10]. Ammar et al. analyzed the effects of biological fertilizers on soil nutrient content and productivity, and concluded that soil microbiota was a correct and environmentally friendly bio-based fertilizer that significantly improved soil fertility and biomass productivity. In conclusion, the recommendation was to utilize microbial fertilizers derived from microalgae and macroalgae to enhance soil fertility without compromising its long-term viability [11]. Although the advantages of organic fertilizers in agricultural production have been widely recognized, current research on their application effects under special soil conditions, especially the impact of planting watermelon as a specific crop on saline alkali land, is relatively limited. Saline alkali land poses a challenge to crop growth due to its high salinity and low fertility characteristics. Exploring the combined use of biomass organic fertilizer and reduced fertilizer under such soil conditions has important practical and theoretical significance for promoting sustainable agricultural development. Given this, the experiment aims to evaluate the effects of biomass organic fertilizer and reduced application of chemical fertilizers on the yield and quality of watermelon grown on saline alkali land. It is hoped that through research, a theoretical basis and practical guidance will be provided for sustainable agricultural cultivation on saline alkali land.

The study is divided into three sections. The first section is a description of the experimental materials and methods. The second section is a specific analysis of the experimental results. The third section is a summary of the proposed methods and the results obtained.

Materials and methods

Test materials

Test varieties

The experiment selects Northwest A&F University No. 5 watermelon as the research object. This variety of watermelon is an early maturing variety with a total growth period of 92–95 days and an outdated maturity period of 30 days [12]– [13]. The plant has a moderate growth momentum, with a main vine length of 2.6 m and deeply serrated leaf edges. It is easy and neat to bear fruit. The fruit is round, and the fruit type index is usually 1.1. The fruit skin is dark green, covered with a thin strip of dark green, with a thickness of 0.93 centimeters. It is hard and tough, and resistant to cracking. The sugar gradient in the middle edge is small, and the resistance to disease and stress is weak. The seeds are sourced

Test location

The test soil used in the study is collected from the Midong District, Urumqi City, China. The geographical coordinates are located near 43° north latitude and 87° east longitude. The experimental site is located at the College of Agriculture, Xinjiang Agricultural University (No. 311 Nongda East Road, Shayibak District, Urumqi, Xinjiang). The basic planting conditions for the greenhouse are watermelon, continuous fruiting for 6 years. The length of the experimental greenhouse is 80 m, with a span of up to 10 m and a height of 2.5 m. Watermelon harvesting is carried out before June each year, and after the fruit harvest, seedling pulling and greenhouse cleaning operations are carried out [14]– [15].

Test fertilizer

Biomass organic fertilizer uses cow manure and earthworm manure organic fertilizer (Shijiazhuang Dongqi Biological Fertilizer Co., Ltd.), which contains more than 6% nitrogen, phosphate dioxide, and potassium oxide, and more than 50% organic matter. Superphosphate (produced by Hubei Zhongxu Ecological Fertilizer Co., LTD., with P2O5 content ≥ 16%), urea (produced by Xinjiang Huatai Chemical Co., LTD., with nitrogen content ≥ 46.4%), medium particle size (particle size range: 1.15–3.35 mm); Potassium sulfate for agriculture (produced by Guotou Xinjiang Lop Nur Potassium Salt Co., LTD.), potassium (K2O) ≥ 52.0%, sulfur (S) ≥ 17.5%, chloride ion content (CL-) ≤ 1.5%.

Experimental design

This 2021 study utilized a two factor design, with a total of 9 groups and 8 treatments. The control CK group was left untreated. In the experiment, each treatment and control group underwent three replicates, with a total of 27 small experimental areas set up in a randomized block arrangement. The area of each small experimental area was 23 m2, and 20 watermelon seedlings were planted in each area using a ground crawling cultivation method. The spacing between watermelon plants was maintained at 50 centimeters, with 4 rows planted in each community and a row spacing of 2.5 m. The sowing and seedling cultivation work of the experiment began on March 16th, and the planting was carried out on April 18th. Watermelon seedlings were pollinated by hand at flowering time. Drip irrigation was used for field irrigation, and all treatment and control groups’ field cultivation management measures must be consistent [16]– [17]. Fertilization methods

The application methods of plant-based fertilizer can be divided into three types: sprinkling, furrow application, and hole application [18]– [19]. By combining all methods, it is found that when combined with hole or ditch application, watermelon seedlings had the best growth state. In this experiment, all fertilizers are used as base fertilizers. Considering the division of the experimental area, ditch fertilization is chosen as the fertilization method. After ridging and digging ditches in the planting area, organic fertilizer and chemical fertilizer are added together to the ditch, and then covered with soil and rotated to complete fertilization [20]– [21]. To prevent the mixing of fertilizers from different treatment areas, plastic films are laid at both ends of each treatment area after rotary tillage [22]– [23].

Design of measurement items and methods related to experiments

Soil indicator measurement

Before watermelon cultivation and after fruit harvest, five-point sampling method collected soil samples, with a depth of 20 cm below the surface [24]– [25]. The collected soil particles were small and uniform. After air drying, organic matter, available nitrogen, etc. in them were determined.

Measurement of morphological indicators

At different stages of watermelon growth, such as the early and late stages of vine extension, fruit expansion, and maturity, the height and stem thickness of the watermelon were measured using a tape measure and digital vernier caliper [26]– [27]. 10 watermelon seedlings were sampled for each three times repeated treatment, with the average value taken for each repetition. The entire measurement process was conducted 4 times, with the height of the plant measured using a tape measure. The starting point of the measurement was the part of the main vine close to the ground. The stem thickness was measured using a digital vernier caliper at the base of the main stem.

Measurement of biomass indicators

After the watermelon was ripe, it was harvested, and 5 plants were uniformly selected from each community for sample collection [28]– [29]. The length of watermelon seedlings above and below ground was measured using a tape measure, starting from the point of contact between soil and air (i.e., the white and green junction of the plant stem). After successful sampling, the fresh weight of the underground and aboveground parts was weighed, and then dried using an electronic scale to measure the dry weight. When excavating plants, sampling should be carried out around the center of the roots, and a sampling radius of 10 cm should be maintained, while minimizing damage to the roots.

Photosynthetic index measurement

During the vine extension period of watermelon plants, the net photosynthetic efficiency (Pn), stomatal conductance (Cond), intercellular CO2 concentration (Ci), and watermelon leaf transpiration rate (Ar) were measured using the American L-6400 equipment. Next, the second true leaf was selected for measurement, each treatment was repeated three times, and the three relatively stable values of each watermelon leaf were recorded under the condition of light intensity of 1000 µMol/m²•s. CO2 concentration 360 µL/L, leaf temperature 26 ± 1.5 ℃, air humidity 85%-95%.

Incidence rate index measurement

Field investigation methods were used to record the watermelon wilt disease at different stages [30]. Three to four surveys were conducted during each growth period of watermelon and the average of the obtained values was taken.

The formula: plant incidence rate= (number of infected plants/total number of investigated plants) × 100%.

Measurement of yield indicators

After the watermelon ripens, all watermelons were harvested according to the community grouping, and all watermelons in the community were weighed to obtain the yield of each treated community and the average weight of individual watermelons.

Fruit quality evaluation

When the watermelon fruit was fully ripe, 6 watermelon samples were selected from each experimental group, ensuring that each sample came from the same fruiting node and had the same pollination date, and watermelon fruits were selected with similar morphology. The watermelon was cut longitudinally, and its transverse and longitudinal diameters were measured, as well as the thickness of the fruit peel using a ruler. The fruit shape index was calculated, and the average of all data was taken.

Fruit shape index: fruit longitudinal diameter/fruit transverse diameter

The quartering method was used to determine the nutritional quality of watermelon pulp: the AD-45 handheld sugar meter (Beijing Jiesman Technology Co., Ltd.) to measure the soluble solid content of the fruit, including the sugar content in the core and edge parts. The Anthrone colorimetric method determined the soluble total sugar content. The Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 staining method determined soluble protein content. Vitamin C was measured using the molybdenum blue colorimetric method. Additionally, the titratable acid content was assessed through the NaOH titration method, whereby the amount of NaOH consumed indicated the organic acid content. The content of lycopene was determined by spectrophotometry (UV-6100 UV visible spectrophotometer, Shanghai Meipeda Instrument Co., Ltd.).

Data processing and statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 analyzed the data, and multiple comparisons were performed by Duncan method (p < 0.05). Origin 23.0 draw the charts, and the displayed chart data were the average values of three repeated experiments.

Experimental results

The effect of different fertilization measures on the growth and development of watermelon

The effect of different fertilization measures on watermelon vine growth

Figure 1 shows the observation of changes in vine length during the growth period of watermelon fruit. At different growth stages, the vine length showed a gradually increasing trend with the advancement of the growth cycle, and finally reached its peak in the later stage of growth. In the early stage of vine extension, the difference in fruit plant height fluctuated between 6 and 7 cm. The vine length of treatment groups A8, A7, and A1 increased by 22.72%, 7.71%, and 4.45%, respectively, compared to the control group (CK). The growth rates of A8 and A7 compared to A1 were 17.67% and 3.04%, respectively. The vine length of A5 was significantly lower than other groups, while treatments from A5 to A8 showed an upward trend. In the late stage of vine extension, there was not much difference between the A2, A3, and CK groups, but the fruit vine length was higher than that of other treatment groups. During the fruit swelling period, the increase in vine length of A8, A2, and A3 compared to CK was 8.23%, 6.66%, and 6.39%, respectively. At the fruit ripening stage, the maximum difference in plant vine length reached 108.9 cm. At this time, A1, A7, and A8 increased by 58.18%, 52.56%, and 45.66% compared to CK, respectively. The difference between A7, A8, and A1 was not significant. At the same time, there was no significant difference between CK and A4. Compared to A5 and A6, the CK group showed an increase of 20.38% and 10.21%, respectively. Compared with A4, A3, and A2, the vine length of A8, A7, and A6 increased by 50.01%, 33.78%, and 3.47%, respectively. The experiment also found that the growth of watermelon vines increased as the amount of earthworm feces applied increased. The vine length of plants treated with earthworm feces was significantly longer than that of untreated plants. Compared with the group without fertilization, no significant difference was shown in the vine length between the plants treated with cow manure, except for A2. When the fertilizer application rate was consistent, the group treated with earthworm manure had higher vine length than the group treated with cow manure. This indicated that earthworm manure organic fertilizer significantly promoted plant growth, while reducing the amount of chemical fertilizer applied. Among them, the growth effect of A8 group was closest to that of conventional fertilization A1.

The effect of different fertilization measures on watermelon stem thickness

Figure 2 shows the variation of watermelon stem thickness. Throughout the entire growth cycle of watermelon, the stem diameter continued to increase, especially in the later stages of maturity, when the stem thickness reached its peak. Compared with the CK group, in the early stage of watermelon growth, the stem thickness of treatment groups A4, A7, and A8 increased by 10.81%, 10.56%, and 10.46%, respectively, while the differences were not significant. Continuing into the late stage of vine extension, the stem thickness of A4 increased by 18.43% and 18.77% compared to A8 and A7, respectively, while the stem thickness of A5 was significantly lower than that of other groups. During the fruit swelling period, there was no significant difference in stem thickness among the treatment groups. However, at the fruit ripening stage, the stem diameter increases of A1, A8, A7, and A4 compared to CK were 33.33%, 21.78%, 18.68%, and 12.77%, respectively. Compared with A8, A7, and A4, A1 increased by 9.43%, 12.12%, and 18.21%, respectively. The stem thickness of A2 was significantly smaller than that of other treatments, showing an upward trend from A2 to A4 and from A5 to A8. Compared with A4, A3, and A2, A8, A7, and A6 showed an increase of 7.92%, 18.78%, and 11.7% in fruit stem diameter, respectively.

The stem diameter of the group treated with earthworm manure organic fertilizer was higher than that of the group without organic fertilizer. At the same time, with the decrease of fertilizer application and the increase of earthworm manure organic fertilizer application, the stem diameter of watermelon plants significantly increased. No significant difference was shown in stem diameter between the group treated with cow manure and the group without fertilization (except for group A2). However, with the increase in cow manure application and chemical fertilizer application, the plant stem thickness showed an upward trend. When the amount of fertilizer used was the same, the stem thickness of using cow manure was lower than that of using earthworm manure. This indicated that, on the basis of reducing fertilizer usage, earthworm manure organic fertilizer was beneficial for watermelon plants, with A8 having the closest effect to conventional fertilization A1.

The effect of different fertilization measures on the photosynthetic characteristics of watermelon

Figure 3 shows the variation curve of the photosynthetic characteristics of watermelon plants. The inter-node length of watermelons rapidly increased during the vine extension period and transitioned from upright growth to creeping growth. This stage was the period when watermelon plants absorbed nutrients and grew at their fastest rate, and the measured photosynthetic characteristics effectively demonstrated the effects of different treatments on watermelon plants. The net photosynthetic rates of A8, A7, A1, A6, and A2 increased by 23.61%, 18.92%, 16.13%, 9.89%, and 9.55% compared to the CK, respectively. The improvement of A8 and A7 relative to A1 was 6.48% and 2.36%, respectively. The net photosynthetic rate from A5 to A8 showed an upward trend. Compared with A4, A3, and A2, the net photosynthetic rates of A8, A7, and A6 increased by 17.74%, 16.71%, and 0.48%, respectively. The intercellular CO2 concentrations of A1 and A2 were 25.21% and 22.67% higher than CK, respectively. The intercellular CO2 concentrations of A4 to A8 showed an upward trend. In the comparison of stomatal conductance, CK’s stomatal conductance was higher than A1, A2, A7, and A8, while the stomatal conductance of A3 to A6 showed no differences, and the stomatal conductance of A5 to A8 showed a downward trend. Transpiration rates showed no significant difference between the A1 and CK groups, with the A1 ~ A5 groups showing a gradually increasing trend. A6 ~ A8 showed a clear downward trend. By comparison, appropriately reducing chemical fertilizers and increasing the application of organic fertilizers (especially earthworm manure) significantly improved the photosynthesis of watermelon plants. With the increase in earthworm manure application, the photosynthesis of plants was enhanced. As the amount of cow manure applied increased, the photosynthesis of plants decreased. When the amount of fertilizer applied was equal, the photosynthesis of plants treated with cow manure was significantly lower than that of the group treated with earthworm manure. Finally, among all groups, A2 and A8 had the closest effect to conventional fertilization, A1.

When earthworm manure was applied as fertilizer, the fresh weight of vines from A8 to A5 decreased as fertilizer application increased. Compared to CK, no significant difference was shown in the fresh weight of plant roots and the dry weight of vines between different fertilization treatments. Discussion

The study explored the effects of plant biomass organic fertilizers and reduced chemical fertilizer application on watermelon yield and quality in saline-alkali soils. The results showed that biomass organic fertilizer significantly improved the growth indicators of watermelon, such as vine length and stem diameter. For instance, the vine length in the A8 treatment group increased by 58.18% compared to the control group. In terms of photosynthetic characteristics, the treatment groups with biomass organic fertilizer showed a significant increase in chlorophyll content and photosynthetic efficiency, reaching a maximum of 2.11 mg/g. At the same time, yield and quality were significantly enhanced, with the A8 group showing a 39.02% and 39.54% increase in single fruit weight and total yield, respectively. Moreover, the application of organic fertilizer effectively reduced the incidence of wilt disease, with the highest reduction of 44.56%. Soil nutrient dynamics were also improved, with the A8 group showing a 25.88% increase in soil organic matter content compared to the control group. The study found that biomass organic fertilizer can significantly promote watermelon growth, directly influencing nutrient absorption and photosynthetic efficiency. Additionally, the study found that appropriate fertilization improves watermelon single fruit weight and nutritional content, which has important market value. Organic fertilizer application also significantly reduced the incidence of watermelon wilt disease, helping plants resist diseases. In related studies, Yin examined the reclamation of saline alkali soil in the arid Manas River Basin for cotton cultivation, using high-throughput metagenomic sequencing to assess microbial degradation after seven years of reclamation. The study found that bacteria, particularly Actinobacteria and Proteobacteria, dominated the microbial community. After reclamation, microbial diversity and the abundance of genes encoding carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) increased, with genes related to the degradation of oxalic acid, hemicellulose, chitin, and lignin showing higher abundance. Soil EC, carbon-to-nitrogen ratio, and Na + content were found to significantly influence microbial succession and CAZyme gene abundance [31]. Wang and colleagues studied the effects of biochar and organic fertilizers on soil quality and wheat yield in saline-alkali soils through a three-year field trial in the Yellow River Delta. Results indicated that while both biochar and organic fertilizers improved soil nutrients, organic fertilizers were more effective overall. Biochar, however, was more effective in reducing salinity and increasing soil moisture, leading to a 0.90-14.71.71% higher wheat yield compared to organic fertilizers [32]. Although these studies provide valuable insights into crop yield in saline-alkali soils, they still lacked comprehensiveness in their experimental approach. The research methods in this study employed a more integrated fertilization strategy to optimize nutrient supply, achieving better growth and yield results. Through a dual evaluation of growth and quality, the study ensured a comprehensive understanding of fertilization measures, providing more practical and meaningful recommendations for actual agricultural production. Additionally, the analysis of microbial communities indirectly reflected the promotion of soil microbial activity. Compared to Yin’s research, this study focused on the practical production benefits of fertilization combinations for crops rather than just microbial abundance and diversity, highlighting the operability of agricultural practices.

Although this study has achieved positive results, there are still several limitations that need to be clarified. Firstly, the experiment was only conducted within one growing season, and it is still impossible to comprehensively assess the impact of organic fertilizer substitution on long-term soil health indicators, such as carbon sequestration, salinity dynamics, and microbial community resilience. Furthermore, although research data show that soil organic matter, nutrient availability, and microbial abundance have significantly increased in the short-term, the sustainability of these benefits still needs to be verified through years of positioning experiments. Therefore, future research should establish a long-term fertilization test platform to systematically monitor the interannual changes in soil physical, chemical and biological properties, the stability of crop yields, and potential environmental costs. In addition, the combination of metagenomics and soil enzyme profiling analysis will help to deeply reveal how organic fertilizers reshape the soil functional microbiome in the temporal dimension.

Conclusion

The experiment conducted a detailed analysis of biomass organic fertilizer and reduced fertilizer application effects on the cultivation of watermelon in saline alkali soil. Research results clearly showed that the use of biomass organic fertilizer, especially when combined with reduced dosage fertilizers, significantly increased the yield and improved the quality of watermelon in saline alkali soil. Firstly, organic biomass fertilizer significantly improved soil quality and increased the organic matter content, creating more favorable conditions for the growth of watermelon. Secondly, by optimizing the fertilization combination, key quality indicators such as single fruit weight, etc., of watermelon were improved. Although the application of organic biomass fertilizer has a significant effect on improving yield and quality, its optimal ratio with chemical fertilizer needs to be adjusted according to specific soil conditions and crop needs. In addition, the impact of long-term application of biomass organic fertilizers on soil ecology should be further studied. In summary, organic biomass fertilizer combined with reduced fertilizer application is an effective strategy that can significantly improve the yield and quality of watermelon in saline alkali soil, while also improving the soil environment. This discovery provides valuable practical solutions for the sustainable development of saline alkali land agriculture, and is significant for optimizing agricultural production models and promoting the development of ecological agriculture.

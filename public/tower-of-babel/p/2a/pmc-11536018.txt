Protocatechuic acid attenuates intestinal inflammation through TLR4/p38 and NF-κB pathways in heat-stressed broilers
Authors: Shuaiyong Wang, Xin Wang, Yu Gao, Yuchen Fu, Zhaoqing Han, Ping Xu, Jianli Tang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Protocatechuic acid (PCA), a major active component found in Eucommia ulmoides Oliver, is involved in regulating oxidative stress and inflammation. Heat stress poses a significant threat to the poultry industry. In this study, we investigated the protective effect of PCA on intestinal homeostasis under heat stress. Our results indicated that PCA improved the growth performance of broilers during heat stress. Furthermore, PCA mitigated jejunal damage and attenuated the upregulation of inflammatory mediators, including TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β, triggered by heat stress. Concurrently, it restored the activity of superoxide dismutase (SOD) and total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC). Furthermore, PCA maintained the jejunum function by increasing the levels of ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin. Mechanistically, PCA inhibited the activation of TLR4/p38 MAPK and NF-κB pathways, thereby regulating the imbalance in oxidative stress and inflammatory responses caused by heat stress.

Introduction

Heat stress, induced by high environmental temperature, is considered an unfavorable factor for livestock health, leading to severe pathological and physiological responses (Sumanu et al., 2022; Khan et al., 2023). Accumulating studies have reported that uncontrolled heat stress causes oxidative stress and tissue inflammation, thereby posing a threat to poultry production (Awad et al., 2019; Barrett et al., 2019). For example, heat stress significantly reduces the average daily gain (ADG) and dramatically increases the feed conversion ratio (FCR). The gut is the crucial site of digestion and absorption of nutrients. Furthermore, heat stress suppresses the development and function of the intestine, particularly compromising the integrity of intestinal epithelial cells (Hosseindoust et al., 2022; Lian et al., 2020). Recent research has found that heat stress induces the rupture and atrophy of intestinal villi, as well as congestion and edema of the lamina propria (Yang et al., 2024). Besides, the inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF- α were significantly upregulated in the liver and intestinal upon heat stress (Yang et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2022). Therefore, well understanding of the mechanisms governing heat stress is urgently needed to support the sustainable development of the poultry industry.

Recent research highlights the significance of pattern recognition receptor (PRR) mediated pathways in initiating heat stress-induced inflammation and tissue injury. For instance, heat stress triggers the activation of p38 kinase, which increases toll-like receptor 2 (TLR2) and toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), thereby enhancing inflammation (Zhou et al., 2005). Another study reported that heat stress induces the upregulation of heat shock protein 70 (HSP70) and TLR4 in the liver. As a downstream molecular, p38 and NF-κB signaling are further activated to promote inflammation (Li et al., 2018). In contrast, heat stress inhibits the TLR4-TBK1 signaling pathway in the spleen and bursa of Fabricius of broilers, thereby downregulating the immune function (Tang et al., 2021). Thus, the crosstalk between the heat stress and TLR4/p38 and NF-κB pathways requires further investigation to guide us develop effective strategies against heat stress.

Various strategies have been developed to alleviate heat stress. Many studies focus on the application of additives such as vitamins, minerals, probiotics, and herbal products (Sahin et al., 2006; 13. Sohail et al., 2011; Tang et al., 2018). Feeding broilers with dimethylglycine significantly abrogated the NF-κB activation and the inflammatory gene expression in a MyD88 and TRIF-dependent manner, thereby reducing intestinal inflammation (Wang et al., 2022). Resveratrol additive mitigated heat stress-induced jejunum dysfunction by downregulating the TLR4/MAPK signaling pathway in black-boned chicken (Liu et al., 2024). Dietary supplementation of enzymatically treated Artemisia annua (ETA) significantly enhanced the expression of tight junction protein zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) and Occludin to improve the intestinal barrier function in broiler jejunum. Furthermore, the intestinal inflammatory cytokines production was reduced after ETA treatment (Song et al., 2017). Therefore, additives could be an effective and promising therapy for controlling diseases caused by heat stress.

Eucommia ulmoides Oliver is widely distributed in China and is enriched with various bioactive compounds, including protocatechuic acid, gutta percha, aucubin, and chlorogenic acid, which are involved in biological processes such as anti-diabetes, neuroprotection, anti-cancer, anti-inflammatory, and anti-hypertension (Zhu et al., 2018). Dietary supplementation of PCA dramatically upregulated the intestinal ZO-1 and claudin-1 expression to maintain intestinal integrity, while ameliorating inflammation and oxidative stress in LPS-challenged piglets (Hu et al., 2020). Another study found that the combination of Cyanidin-3-O-glucoside and PCA effectively elevated the antioxidant activity and activated the IRE1α-XBP1 pathway in testicular, thereby mitigating the spermatogenesis dysfunction and testicular damage (Cai et al., 20123). However, the potential protective effect of PCA on heat stress and the underlying molecular mechanism is not well investigated in broilers.

Materials and methods

Reagents

Protocatechuic acid (≥ 98 %, 99-50-3) was provided by Jiu Feng long Bio-Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Anti-HSP70 antibody (ab5439) and anti-NF-kB antibody (ab16502) were purchased from Abcam. (Cambridge, MA, USA). Anti-p38 (14064-1AP), anti-HSP27 (18284-1-AP), and actin monoclonal antibodies were purchased from Proteintech (Wuhan, China). Anti-p-p38 (4511) was purchased from CST (Danvers, MA, USA). Broiler heat stress kit was purchased from Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Antioxidant kits (A001-3, A015-2) were purchased from Nanjing Jiancheng Biological Engineering Research Institute. (Nanjing, China).

Animal feeding and experimental design

In this study, broilers aged one day old were purchased from a commercial hatchery. Protocols in this study were approved by the Ethics Committee of Linyi University. All broilers were housed under the standard conditions (12 h light/dark cycle, relative humidity of 50 ± 15 %, and room temperature of 24 ± 2 °C). 40 broilers aged 3 weeks were randomly divided into 4 groups. Control group broilers (CT) were housed under normal conditions (24 °C ± 2 °C), while the heat stress treatment broilers (HS), low-dose PCA treatment broilers (LPCA), and high-dose PCA treatment broilers (HPCA) were exposed to a high ambient temperature (37 °C ± 2 °C) from 10:00 to 18:00. In CT group and HS group, broilers were fed with basal diet only; however, the broilers were fed with the basal diets supplemented with 300 or 600 mg/kg PCA in the LPCA and HPCA group. The ADG and average daily food intake (ADFI) were determined by weighing the feed. FCR = ADFI/ADG. After 2 and 6 weeks of HS exposure, broilers were sacrificed to collect the cervical venous blood and jejunal tissue.

Determination of HS and antioxidant indexes in broiler serum

Serum levels of HSP70, heat shock protein (HSP27), and heat shock transcription factor 1 (HSF-1) were determined by a commercial ELISA kit according to the manufacturer's protocol. Serum levels of SOD and T-AOC were detected by using a standard assay following the manufacturer's protocol.

In this study, qRT-PCR was performed to measure the changes in mRNA levels of heat stress-related genes and inflammatory cytokines in the jejunum of the broiler. Western blot analysis

Jejunum was lysed to obtain the total protein fraction. Proteins were denatured with 1 × SDS loading buffer, separated by SDS-PAGE, and transferred to NC membranes. Membranes were blocked for 1 h in 5 % milk, incubated overnight with primary antibodies, washed with TBST six times, incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h, and again washed six times with TBST. Finally, the membranes were incubated with an enhanced chemiluminescence detection reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc., Rockford, IL, USA) to visualize protein bands.

Morphological observation of the jejunum

Jejunum tissues from broilers were collected, fixed, and subsequently embedded in paraffin. The samples were sectioned into slices with a thickness of 3 to 5 mm using a microtome, followed by hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining. Microphotographs of the stained sections were then captured for analysis.

Statistical analysis

The data presented in this paper are expressed as the means ± standard deviations (SD) of at least two replicates and were evaluated with One-way ANOVA in GraphPad PRISM software version 10 (La Jolla, CA, USA). Significant differences are presented as * P < 0.05, and “ns” indicates a non-significant difference (P > 0.05).

Effect of PCA on the growth performance of heat stressed broilers

Here, the growth performance indicators, ADG, and FCR were measured. During the initial 2 weeks HS exposure, no significant difference in ADG and FCR were observed across all groups (P > 0.05) (Fig. 1A). After 6 weeks of HS treatment, HS significantly decreased the ADG and increased the FCR compared to CT (P < 0.05). However, PCA supplementation restored the ADG reduction caused by HS in both LCPA and HPCA groups (P < 0.05). In terms of the FCR, LCPA has no effect on reducing FCR (P > 0.05), while HPCA significantly decreased the FCR (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1B).Fig. 1Effect of PCA diet on broiler growth performance. Discussion

Heat stress poses substantial threats to the poultry industry, particularly by reducing growth and reproduction (Zaboli et al., 2017). There is an urgent need to develop effective strategies to mitigate heat stress. In this study, we investigated the protective effect of PCA supplementation on broilers’ health conditions under heat stress. By assessing the alteration of growth performance, heat stress-related indicators, antioxidant activity, and inflammatory gene levels. Our findings indicate that PCA supplementation mitigates the inflammation of the jejunum induced by heat stress while maintaining tight junction homeostasis and antioxidative capacity. Furthermore, PCA alleviates the inflammation in the jejunum through the TLR4/p38/NF-κB signal pathway. PCA supplementation could be a potential strategy for improving poultry productivity under heat-stress conditions.

Heat stress induces a reduction in growth performance in poultry, as evidenced by decreased ADG and increased FCR (Sumanu et al., 2022; Khan et al., 2023). Here, our results confirmed these detrimental effects of long-term HS exposure (6 weeks). During 6 weeks of HS exposure, the PCA diet showed significant improvement in ADG and reduction in FCR compared to the HS group. The results suggested that PCA effectively improved the growth performance of broilers during heat stress exposure.

As the key indicators of heat stress, HSP27 and HSP70 were synthesized in the context of various stress and further protect the cell from damage by regulating many physiological processes such as immune response, and autophagy (Vertti-Quinteroet et al., 2021). HSF-1, a key transcription factor, was involved in regulating heat stress-related gene expression (Anckar et al., 2011). In this study, we found that 6 weeks of heat stress exposure significantly upregulated the serum levels of HSP27, HSP70, and HSF-1, indicating the successful construction of the HS model for broilers. Feeding broilers with PCA, especially at high doses, effectively reduced these levels, suggesting a potential protective effect of PCA against heat stress. Previous reports indicated that heat stress led to oxidative stress (Nawab et al., 2019). Here, SOD, a crucial enzymatic antioxidant factor, and T-AOC, a key nonenzymatic antioxidant factor were used to assess the oxidative capacity. As shown in our results, chronic HS decreased serum SOD and T-AOC, indicating increased oxidative stress. PCA diet significantly upregulated serum SOD and T-AOC levels, thereby enhancing the antioxidant effect.

As the major component of the intestinal barrier, tight junctions, the specific structure formed between intestinal epithelial cells, is crucial for maintaining the function of the gut, such as digestion, absorption as well as the immune response (Song et al., 2018). Occludin, Claudin-1, and ZO-1 are the key elements of tight junctions (Liu et al., 2023). The potential protective effect of PCA on tight junctions was investigated. Here, we found the mRNA levels of ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin were significantly reduced in broiler jejunum during long-term HS exposure compared to CT; however, ZO-1, Claudin-1, and Occludin genes were significantly increased in PCA supplementation group in contrast to the HS group. The results suggested that the addition of PCA could maintain intestinal tight junctions, thereby enhancing the intestinal barrier function of broilers under HS.

Accumulating studies suggested that heat stress triggers the inflammation response in broilers jejunum, characterized by increased production of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α) and decreased levels of the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 (Zheng et al., 2022). Here, PCA additives significantly reduced the levels of IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α and increased IL-10 expression, indicating its anti-inflammatory properties. However, the underlying mechanism requires further investigation. Recent research revealed that the TLR4/p38/NF-κB signaling pathway drives the inflammatory response under heat stress (Liu et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2023). Furthermore, HSP70 serves as a damage-associated molecular pattern to activate TLR4. Next, TLR4 further activates NF-κB, thereby inducing inflammatory cytokines production (Ren et al., 2018; Marongiu et al., 2019).

Here, we found that heat stress triggered the phosphorylation of p38 and NF-κB in the jejunum, while PCA supplementation abrogated this activation. Thus, we suggest that PCA exerts its anti-inflammatory effects by reducing the HSP70 level, followed by downregulation of TLR4 and suppression of p38 and NF-κB activation, thereby alleviating the production of the inflammatory cytokines under HS exposure. Overall, our results highlight the potential of PCA as a therapeutic target to mitigate heat stress-induced inflammation and tissue damage.

Conclusions

PCA diet can mitigate chronic heat stress in broilers by alleviating intestinal inflammation, oxidative stress, and enhancing intestinal barriers. Furthermore, PCA suppresses the TLR4/p38/ NF-κB signaling pathway, reduces inflammatory cytokines production, restores intestinal tight junction protein levels, and protects the intestinal barrier function of the jejunum of broiler chickens under HS conditions.

AKAP7 is a prognostic biomarker involved in immune infiltration of bladder urothelial carcinoma and correlated with ferroptosis
Authors: Hongquan Liu, Qiancheng Mao, Xiaofeng Wang, Gonglin Tang, Guixin Ding, Tianqi Wang, Chu Liu, Xiaohong Ma, Yuanshan Cui, Jitao Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA) is a common urinary malignancy, and the identification of appropriate biomarkers remains a significant challenge. AKAP7 is a molecular scaffolding protein that plays a crucial role in spatially regulating cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) signaling through its ability to anchor protein kinase A (PKA) to the cytoskeleton. This study aims to explore the expression level, prognostic implications and immune characteristics of AKAP7 in BLCA.MethodsBased on numerous databases, including CCLE, HPA, Harmonizome, TCGA, GEO, GEPIA2, Kaplan-Meier plotters, GENT2, cBioPortal and the COSMIC databases, we explored the expression pattern, prognostic value and gene alteration and mutation of AKAP7. The potential molecular mechanisms of AKAP7 were analyzed via the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis. Furthermore, using single-cell RNA-seq datasets, we investigated the distribution of AKAP7 in tumor microenvironments (TME). We utilized the TISIDB database to analyze the association between AKAP7 and immune characteristics. Additionally, we explored the correlation between AKAP7 expression and ferroptosis using TCGA and GEO data sets. Finally, we verified the expression level of AKAP7 in clinical samples of BLCA as well as BLCA cell lines.ResultsCompared with normal tissues, we observed a decreased expression level of AKAP7 in BLCA tissues. BLCA patients with low AKAP7 expression showed poor prognosis. The expression of AKAP7 had a significant positive correlation with the infiltration of T helper cells, Tcm, Eosinopoils, TFH, Th17 and CD8 T cells. The TCGA and GEO datasets suggested that the expression of AKAP7 was closely associated with a ferroptosis-related gene Acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4).ConclusionAKAP7 may play a critical role in the prognosis and immune regulation of BLCA prognosis. Besides, it is related to ferroptosis. AKAP7 is an effective biomarker in BLCA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-04786-8.

Introduction

With more than 500,000 new cases being diagnosed and 200,000 deaths each year, bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA) is a common urothelial malignancy with significant heterogeneity [1–3]. About 80% of BLCA cases are diagnosed with non-muscle-infiltrating bladder cancer (NMIBC), while the remaining cases show muscle-infiltrating bladder cancer (MIВС) or even distant metastases [4, 5]. Currently, surgery is the primary therapy for bladder cancer. Despite considerable advancement, there are certain limitations to the treatment of BLCA because of the high recurrence and metastasis rates, especially advanced BLCA [6, 7]. Hence, it is essential to understand the molecular mechanisms and explore effective biomarkers that are connected to the progression of BLCA, which is essential for early disease diagnosis and individual precision therapy [8].

One important mechanism the cell uses to change its physiology and function is the phosphorylation of target proteins [9]. Protein kinase A (PKA), also recognized as a widely distributed serine/threonine protein kinase, is cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP)-dependent protein kinase A. PKA is a tetrameric protein that resides inside of cells and is made up of two catalytic subunits and a dimer of a regulatory component. A-Kinase Anchoring Proteins (AKAPs) are a group of functionally related molecular scaffolding proteins that facilitate the formation of multi-protein complexes containing the сAМР dependent PKA and guide the kinase in certain subcellular regions [10]. There are now 14 AKAPs with annotations (AKAP1 to AKAP14). It has been demonstrated that AKAРѕ modulate renal [11] and cardiac function [12], as well as neuronal ion channels [13, 14] and reproduction function [15]. AKAP7 (called AKAP15/18 as well) is a particular AКАР that has been discovered in numerous tissues and various cellular signaling pathways. The four splice variants of the AKAP7 (AKAP7α, β, δ, and γ) have been widely researched in urinary system aquaporin transport, striated muscle ion channel modulation and central nervous system [10]. Notably, a study on Human Coronavirus Genomes (HCoV) illuminated that an interesting characteristic of some viruses is that they encode phosphodiesterases (PDEs) [16]. This feature can be used by certain viruses to block the host’s immune response. According to this study, beta-CoVs and rotaviruses independently obtained Phosphodiesterase activity, and AKAP7 was used as the source gene in both virus genera. Whereas, there is little research on the expression and clinical significance of AKAP7 in BLCA.

In our research, we comprehensively analyzed the expression pattern, prognostic value and gene alteration and mutation of AKAP7 based on CCLE, HPA, Harmonizome, TCGA, GEO, GEPIA2, Kaplan-Meier plotters, GENT2, cBioPortal and the COSMIC databases. Additionally, we explored the potential molecular mechanisms of AKAP7 via the enrichment analysis and related protein-protein interactions network. Furthermore, we conducted an investigation into the potential association between AKAP7 expression levels and the tumor microenvironments, as well as ferroptosis, which might provide insight into the understanding of the possible mechanism of AKAP7 of BLCA.

Method

Gathering of AKAP7 expression data from the cancer genome atlas (TCGA), gene expression profiling interactive analysis (GEPIA2) database and the gene expression omnibus (GEO) database

The TCGA database (https://genome-cancer.ucsc.edu/) is a publicly available data source of a significant study on cancer genomes that includes pathological and clinical information on more than 30 distinct types of cancer and is designed for research. Data on patients with BLCA, including RNA-Seq expression and related clinical information, was obtained via the TCGA tool cancer browser. We normalized RNA-seq data in FPKM format to TPM format for further analysis. The GEPIA2 (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/) is an online website that permits gene expression analysis for normal and tumor specimens based on the TCGA and the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) databases. A comprehensive functional genomics library, the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), allows users to freely query and download certain gene expression profiles.

The human protein atlas (HPA), cancer cell line encyclopedia (CCLE), the harmonizome database and the stellaris database

A significant quantity of data on proteomics and transcription of unique human samples that contain tissue, cells, and a pathology atlas is contained in the HPA (https://www.proteinatlas.org). The HPA website also provides information on protein immunohistochemistry in both normal and malignant tissues from humans. We obtained the AKAP7 RNA and protein expression summary in the HPA. Besides, the expression of AKAP7 in various immune cells in plasma was analyzed in Monaco scaled dataset and Schmiedel dataset in HPA. The CCLE database (https://www.broadinstitute.org/ccle) offers the expression data of AKAP7 in BLCA cell lines, which is an online multi-omics data source that offers extensive transcriptome sequencing information about human cancer cell lines. The Harmonizome database (https://maayanlab.cloud/Harmonizome/) provides data about AKAP7 expression in cell lines and tissues. STellaris (https://spatial.rhesusbase.com/) is an online tool for accurate spatial mapping of single-cell RNA-seq datasets based on spatial transcriptomics data, which also offers gene expression data, including information on spatially variable gene expression.

Open targets platform and gene expression database of normal and tumor tissues 2 (GENT2)

The Open Targets Platform (https://platform.opentargets.org/) analyzes pharmaceuticals, animal models, academic literature, genomes and transcriptomics to find drug targets and then ranks and scores the correlations between targets and diseases. Based on this database, AKAP7-related diseases were identified. GENT2 (http://gent2.appex.kr/gent2/) offers gene expression information on more than 70 types of normal and tumor tissues. In addition, it offers users a meta-analysis of survival data to increase the accuracy of a gene’s predictive value [17].

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses

To assess the significance of AKAP7 expression in bladder cancer, we analyzed the relationship between AKAP7 expression and overall survival (OS) in two distinct groups of BLCA patients using univariate Cox regression analysis. In order to identify whether AKAP7 is a unique prognostic factor of clinical outcome in BLCA, we performed multivariate analysis. In the Cox regression analysis, AKAP7 was considered statistically significant with a p-value less than 0.05. Using the rms software, we conducted a nomogram and calibration analysis.

Genetic alteration of AKAP7 in BLCA

The cBioPortal database (http://www.cbioportal.org/) and the COSMIC database (http://cancer.sanger.ac.uk) were used to identify the frequency and variation types of AKAP7 in BLCA. To assess the prognostic significance of genomic alterations, we carried out survival analysis and log-rank test.

Differentially expressed gene (DEG) analysis, functional enrichment analysis and protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction

According to the median expression of AKAP7 in patients, BLCA patients were divided into high and low AKAP7 expression cohorts. We employed the R package DESeq2 to identify the DEGs analysis between these two groups. Threshold for DEGs was set at P < 0.01 and |log2FC|> 1. Besides, we performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) utilizing hallmark gene sets from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) [18, 19]. Specifically, gene sets were deemed significantly enriched if they exhibited a false discovery rate (FDR) below 0.25 and an adjusted p value less than 0.05. In addition, we retrieved interaction data for AKAP7 as the target protein from the BioGRID database, obtaining a list of all proteins that directly interact with AKAP7. Using the igraph package, we then constructed an AKAP7-centric PPI network.

Immune infiltration analysis

The level of immune infiltration of AKAP7 in TCGA-BLCA was explored through single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) analysis based on the GSVA R package (1.46.0) [20]. Spearman correction was employed to determine the correlation between 24 types of immune cells and AKAP7 expression in BLCA. Based on the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, we evaluated the differences between the cohorts with high and low AKAP7 expression in terms of immune cell infiltration.

The expression of AKAP7 in the tumor microenvironment (TME)

To illustrate the TME at single-cell resolution systematically, the scRNA-seq database Tumor Immune Single-cell Hub 2 (TISCH2, http://tisch.comp-genomics.org/) was utilized. The expression of AKAP7 in the TME of BLCA was explored in this study according to this database. For spatial transcriptome data of the GSE171351 cohort, deconvolution analysis was used to assess cellular composition. After that, we identified the predominant cell type of the microregion. The Seurat package’s SpatialDimPlot function visualized the dominant cellular composition of each microregion, while the SpatialFeaturePlot function displayed the gene expression landscape within these regions. Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to assess correlations between cellular compositions across all spots, along with those between cellular compositions and gene expression levels, with visualization via the linkET package.

Tumor-immune system interaction database

An integrated repository tool, the TISIDB database (http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB) aims to identify the interaction between the immune system and tumor. Based on this tool, we measured the correlation between AKAP7 and the expression of chemokine/chemokine receptors and immunoinhibitors/immunostimulators.

Correlations of AKAP7 expression level with ferroptosis in BLCA

Ferroptosis has a notable impact on the development of bladder cancer [21, 22].Using the R package, the association between AKAP7 expression and ferroptosis-associated genes expression was explored in the TCGA BLCA and GSE13507, including AIFM2, ACSL4, ALOX15, ATP5MC3, CARS, CDKN1A, CISD1, CS, DPP4, EMC2, FANCD2, FDFT1, GLS2, GPX4, HSPB1, HSPA5, HSPB1, LPCAT3, MT1G, NCOA4, NFE2L2, RPL8, SAT1, SLC1A5, SLC7A11 and TFRC [23, 24] Between the cohorts with high and low AKAP7 expression in TCGA BLCA, we compared the differences in the expression of these ferroptosis-associated genes. The correlation between the expression of these genes and the clinical outcomes of patients with BLCA was shown by the Kaplan-Meier curve. To visually analyze the cohort, the ggplot2 package was used.

Collection of human tissue specimens

We collected bladder cancer (BLCA) tissues and matched normal bladder tissues from a cohort of 78 patients who underwent radical cystectomy. The tissues harvested during the surgical procedure were immediately stored in liquid nitrogen and subsequently moved to a refrigerator set to −80 °C for future use. Informed consent forms were signed by all participants. Our study was approved by the ethics committee of the Affiliated Yantai Yuhuangding Hospital of Qingdao University.

Cell lines and cell culture

The human urothelial cells (SV-HUC-1) and BLCA cell lines (253 J, 5637, EJ, RT4, T24a, and UMUC-3) were obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. The SV-HUC-1 cells were cultured in F-12 K medium (Gibco, NY, United States), while the other BLCA cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 (BI, Israel). All of these media were supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin and streptomycin. All cells were cultured at a temperature of 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% carbon dioxide.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Using the SteadyPure Quick RNA Extraction Kit (Accurate Biology, China), we extracted total RNA from freshly frozen tissues or BLCA cell lines. After that, reverse transcription was conducted via Evo M-MLV RT Mix Kit (Accurate Biology, China). Subsequently, we conducted qRT-PCR by utilizing SYBR® Green Premix Pro Taq HS qPCR Kit and Rox Reference Dye (Accurate Biology, China). Based on the 2®−ΔΔCT calculation method, the relative expression level of the AKAP7 was analyzed with GAPDH acting as the internal reference. Supplementary Table 1 showed the primer sequences used in our research.

Western blot assay and immunohistochemical staining

Proteins extracted from samples were solubilized in 1× SDS buffer and subsequently separated via SDS-PAGE. Following transfer onto a NC membrane (Millipore, USA), the membrane was subjected to overnight incubation at 4 °C with primary antibodies against Tubblin (Affinity, China) and AKAP7 (Proteintech, China), followed by a 1-hour incubation at room temperature with secondary antibodies. Protein bands were detected via an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and captured using a chemiluminescent imaging system (Tanon, China). The specimens for the tumor tissue microarray are derived from patients who have undergone radical cystectomy without preoperative chemotherapy in our hospital. IHC was performed using a standardized protocol. The sample sections were first deparaffinized and hydrated. Following blocking with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA). Then, we blocked samples with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA). After that, the samples were overnight with anti-AKAP7 (Proteintech, catalog no.12591-1-AP, 1:100). On the subsequent day, the sections were incubated with secondary antibodies and then stained using a DAB kit from ZSGB-bio (China), followed by counterstaining with hematoxylin. Two independent pathologists assessed the immunostaining of each specimen blindly and remained uninformed of the patients’ clinicopathological details. The pathological scores were determined by considering both the staining intensity and staining proportion. The staining intensity was categorized as 0 (negative), 1 (mild), 2 (moderate), or 3 (strong).

Statistical analysis

We performed all bioinformatics analyses using R software version (4.2.0). To compare two independent samples and multiple samples of non-parametric data, we employed the Wilcoxon test and the Kruskal-Wallis test were utilized, respectively. For parametric data, we utilized t-tests and one-way ANOVA. The survival study was carried out based on the Kaplan-Meier method and the log-rank test. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Results

Expression landscape and expression pattern of AKAP7 in pan-cancer perspective

The flow chart of the methodologies used in our study is shown in Fig. 1. As shown in Supplementary Figure S1A, AKAP7 is expressed widely in a variety of organs and tissues. The consensus dataset, which included 13,084 samples in GTEx and 375 normal tissues in the HPA database, revealed that AKAP7 mRNA was expressed largely in the adrenal gland, pancreas, duodenum, liver and colon (Supplementary Figure S1B). And the HPA database indicated that the AKAP7 protein was primarily expressed in the breast, fallopian tube, pancreas, testis, and adipose tissue (Supplementary Figure S1C). Supplementary Figure S1D, E illustrated the details of the expression of AKAP7 mRNA in various tissues and cell types. As shown in are shown in Supplementary Figure S2, AKAP7 is a spatially variable gene in colon tissue. Based on the OPEN TARGET platform, the involvement of AKAP7 in various disorders was identified (Supplementary Figure S3A). In 33 different cancer types, we analyzed the mRNA expression of the AKAP7. AKAP7 was highly overexpressed in 2 out of 33 cancer types. In contrast, AKAP7 was downregulated in tumor tissues with 14 out of 33 cancer types, including BLCA, colon cancer, lung adenocarcinoma and others (Supplementary Figure S3B). According to the results, AKAP7 mRNA expression was shown to be abnormally downregulated in a wide variety of tumors. We also used the TCGA datasets to explore the expression of AKAP7 in paired sets of human cancer tissues and adjacent normal tissues. BLCA, BRCA, COAD, KICH, LUAD, LUSC, PRAD, READ and THCA tumor samples all showed lower AKAP7 expression compared with paired normal tissue (Supplementary Figure S3C). Besides, AKAP7 was shown to be abnormally expressed in BLCA cell lines according to the HPA and CCLE (Supplementary Figure S3D, E). Based on pan-cancer protein expression analysis of AKAP7, we found that AKAP7 exhibits low-to-moderate expression intensity in the majority of malignant tumor tissues (Supplementary Figure S3F). This abnormal pattern of AKAP7 expression suggested that downregulated AKAP7 may be correlated with the progress of a number of tumor types.

Downregulated mRNA and protein expression of AKAP7 in BLCA patients

Information about AKAP7 expression from the TCGA, GEPIA2, GEO, and HPA was investigated to learn more about its mRNA and protein expression in BLCA. AKAP7 was shown to have significantly lower expression in BLCA samples than normal samples in the analysis of the TCGA-KIRC cohort (2.573 ± 2.159 vs. 3.076 ± 0:640, P < 0.001) (Fig. 2A). The results of further paired data analyses showed that, in comparison with the normal tissues (n = 19), the levels of mRNA expression of AKAP7 in BLCA (n = 19) were significantly lower (Fig. 2B). The same expression pattern can also be verified in the GEPIA2 (Fig. 2C) and three GEO databases (GSE3167, GSE13507 and GSE68020) (Fig. 2D-F). Figures 2G, H showed the results from the HPA database, demonstrating that AKAP7 protein was downregulated in BLCA tissue. According to the aforementioned findings, AKAP7 was considerably downregulated in BLCA in terms of mRNA and protein expression.

Relationships between AKAP7 mRNA levels and clinical pathological features of BLCA patients

Table 1 lists the baseline features of TCGA-BLCA patients. As is shown in Table 1; Fig. 4, the level of AKAP7 expression was significantly related to the pathogenic stage, T stage, N stage, M stage and lymphovascular invasion (Fig. 3A-E). Besides, AKAP7 expression correlated with both age and tumor subtypes (Fig. 3F, G). However, the expression of AKAP7 was not associated with gender and histologic grade (Fig. 3H, I). In the GSE13507 dataset, elevated AKAP7 expression was associated with lower T stage, lower tumor grade and reduced disease progression (Fig. 3J-L). In the GSE69795 dataset, we discovered that the expression level of AKAP7 was related to lymphovascular invasion (Fig. 3M). In the IMvigor210 cohort, AKAP7 expression showed associations with tumor stage and was significantly higher in Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG)-responsive patients compared to non-responders (Fig. 3N, O).

Lower expression level of AKAP7 could influence the prognosis of BLCA patients

We carried out Kaplan-Meier curves to identify the association between mRNA expression of AKAP7 and OS, DSS and PFI in BLCA patients. The OS (Overall Survival) (HR = 0.69, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.51–0.92, P = 0.013, Fig. 5A) and DSS (Disease-Specific Survival) (HR = 0.67, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.47–0.96, P = 0.030, Fig. 5B) was significantly worse among patients with low AKAP7 expression than in those patients with high expression. Additionally, we also observed a positive correlation between reduced AKAP7 expression and shorter progression-free interval (PFI) (HR = 0.69, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.51–2.19, P = 0.013, Fig. 5C). In GSE13507, GSE48075 and GSE154261, we also found that BLCA patients with higher expression of AKAP7 had a better prognosis (Fig. 5D-F). Based on the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, we examined the diagnostic efficiency of AKAP7 (AUC = 0.812; CI: 0.725–0.898) (Fig. 5G), suggesting that the AKAP7 mRNA expression level accurately distinguished BLCA tumor tissues from the adjacent normal tissues. In addition, based on the GENT2 database, OS in BLCA patients for the AKAP7 gene was analyzed by meta-analysis (Fig. 5H). Most datasets showed that the hazard ratios are under 1, suggesting the prognostic value of AKAP7 was significantly available for the clinical outcomes of BLCA. Moreover, a nomogram model was performed that included T stages, N stages, age, lymphovascular invasion, and AKAP7 expression levels as parameters (Fig. 5I). The nomogram showed a significantly high clinical value in predicting the clinical outcomes of patients with BLCA. Each variable’s scores in the nomogram were calculated using a straight line, and the scores assigned to each variable were appropriately adjusted to a standardized range of 0–100. Based on the sum of the scores for each variable, the total scores were determined. On the scoring system, higher scores were associated with worse clinical outcomes. The calibration curves showed great efficacy of this nomogram (Fig. 5J).

AKAP7 genetic alteration in BLCA patients

411 patients with BLCA were analyzed based on the cBioPortal database. 2.2% of BLCA patients had AKAP7 genetic alterations (Fig. 4A). Based on the cBioPortal database, Fig. 4B presented the variation types and frequency of AKAP7 in BLCA in the cBioPortal database, and the most common type of variation was mutation. The COSMIC database was utilized to explore the types of mutation. Missense substitution (33.33%) and synonymous substitution (25%) occurred in the BLCA patients (Fig. 4C). Additionally, G > A (71.43%), A > G (14.29%), and A > T (14.29%) were the most common base substitutions (Fig. 4D). On this basis, we analyzed the impact of different genotypes on the prognosis of patients with bladder cancer. The results demonstrated that there were no statistically significant differences in Overall Survival (OS), Disease-Specific Survival (DSS), Progression-Free Interval (PFI) and Disease-Free Interval (DFI) between patients with mutant-type and wild-type genotypes (Supplementary Figure S4-D). In addition, we identified mutations associated with AKAP7 expression levels in bladder cancer, such as TP53 mutations (Fig. 4E). Figure 4F showed that there was a significant difference in AKAP7 expression levels between TP53 wild-type and mutant-type samples. We further explored the DNA methylation level of AKAP7 using the MethSurv tool, and Fig. 4G demonstrated that cg24801936 exhibited the highest DNA methylation level.

Analysis of the functions and mechanisms related to AKAP7

In the TCGA-BLCA cohort, we found that AKAP7 expression was negatively correlated with cell cycle and cell invasion features (Fig. 6A). This correlation between AKAP7 expression and the cell cycle was also observed in GSE154261 (Fig. 6B). In the GSE48276 and GSE48075 datasets, AKAP7 expression was associated with tumor cell invasion and metastasis features (Fig. 6C, D).

Correlation analysis of AKAP7 expression and immune cell infiltration in BLCA

Tumor immune infiltration plays a critical part in tumor progression and impacts the clinical outcomes of BLCA. Based on the assessment of immune infiltration via multiple algorithms, we found that AKAP7 expression was correlated with the infiltration of immune cells such as CD8 + T cells (Fig. 8A). As shown in Fig. 8B, between the cohorts with high and low AKAP7 expression, we specifically evaluated the levels of infiltration of seven types of immune cells. We found that bladder cancer patients with high AKAP7 expression exhibited increased immune cell infiltration (Fig. 8B). Spearman correlation analysis revealed that the expression of AKAP7 was positively correlated with cancer immune cycle scores in bladder cancer (Fig. 8C). In addition, we conducted an analysis to examine the potential linkage between AKAP7 expression and the expression profiles of immune checkpoint molecules. Our findings revealed a significant correlation between AKAP7 and 14 immune checkpoint-associated genes. In the GSE149652 dataset, AKAP7 expression was predominantly observed in NK cells, CD4Tconv and CD8T cells (Fig. 9B, C). The proportion of AKAP7-positive NK and CD8T cells was significantly higher than that of AKAP7-negative NK and CD8T cells (Fig. 9D). Correlation between AKAP7 expression and ferroptosis in bladder cancer

To determine the relationship between the expression level of AKAP7 and 23 ferroptosis-related genes in BLCA, we evaluated GSE13507 and TCGA BLCA cohort. As shown in Fig. 11A, ACSL4, NCOA4 and NF2E2L2 were significantly positively correlated with the expression of AKAP7 in the GSE13507 and TCGA BLCA, while the expression of MT1G was negatively correlated with AKAP7. Additionally, in the TCGA BLCA, the expression of AKAP7 and GLS2 were shown to be positively correlated, while a negative correlation was found between AKAP7 and HSPB1. In the GSE13507, we detected a significant positive correlation between the expression levels of AKAP7 and ATP5MC3, CARS, CDKN1 and FDFT1, while a negative correlation was identified between AKAP7 expression and both FANCD2 and TFRC. The scatter plot illustrated the relationship between the AKAP7 and the expression of ACSL4, MT1G, NCOA4 and NF2E2L2 (Fig. 11B). Furthermore, according to the expression of AKAP7, we divided TCGA-BLCA into two cohorts. To assess whether ferroptosis was different between the cohorts with high and low AKAP7 expression in BLCA, we evaluated the differential expression level of 23 ferroptosis-related genes between the two cohorts. According to the findings, compared with the low expression cohort, the expression of ACSL4, LPCAT3, NCOA4 and NFE2L2 significantly increased in the AKAP7 high expression cohort, whereas the expression of HSPB1, MT1G and SLC1A5 decreased (Fig. 11C). As shown in Fig. 11D, the venn diagram identified the core genes (ACSL4, NCOA4, NF2E2L2 and MT1G) that have both expression correlation and differential expression relationship with AKAP7. Survival curves suggested that high expression of ACSL4 was highly related to better clinical outcomes in BLCA, whereas the expression of NCOA4, NF2E2L2 and MT1G expression was not (Fig. 11E). These findings highlighted a significant correlation between AKAP7 and ferroptosis in BLCA, particularly through the regulation of ACSL4, potentially influencing the progression of BLCA eventually. Spatial transcriptomic analysis of bladder cancer tissue sections revealed that the spatial expression pattern of ACSL4 was similar to that of AKAP7, and both genes were predominantly expressed in fibroblasts (Fig. 11F). Furthermore, we investigated the expression correlation between AKAP7 and ACSL4 at the pan-cancer level using more than 200 tumor spatial transcriptomic sections (Fig. 11G). The results demonstrated that the expression levels of AKAP7 and ACSL4 were significantly positively correlated, implying a potential regulatory relationship between these two molecules.

Experimental verification of the expression of AKAP7

A total of 8 pairs of tumor tissues and adjacent tissues were obtained from individuals diagnosed with BLCA for qRT-PCR. The findings demonstrated tumor tissues had considerably decreased levels of AKAP7 expression (Fig. 12A). This discovery was also confirmed in BLCA cell lines. In comparison with SV-HUC-1, all of the above BLCA cell lines expressed AKAP7 at statistically lower levels (Fig. 12B). To further validate the differential expression of AKAP7 at the protein level, IHC staining was conducted on BLCA tissues and the matching normal tissues. As shown in Fig. 12C, D, a significant reduction in AKAP7 expression was observed in BLCA tissues when compared to normal tissues. Western blot (WB) experiments based on paired samples showed consistent results, confirming that the level of AKAP7 in bladder cancer tissues was significantly lower than that in adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 12E).

Discussion

As a group of scaffolding proteins, AKAPs play a crucial role in the spatio-temporal activation of signals that regulate the occurrence and proliferation of cancer cells [26]. It has been demonstrated that the level of AKAP4 expression was significantly elevated in lung adenocarcinoma tissues. AKAP8L was found to be closely related to the improvement of stemness of gastric cancer cells [27]. Besides, deacetylation of AKAP12 facilitates colon cancer metastasis [28]. AKAP7 plays a crucial role in spatially regulating cAMP signaling through its ability to anchor PKA to the cytoskeleton. The expression of AKAP7 in peripheral blood is early predictive for blood-brain barrier disruption following stroke [29]. Currently, little research has been conducted on the regulatory mechanisms and biological roles of AKAP7 in the progression of human malignancies.

In our study, the expression patterns of AKAP7 in a range of human organs, tissues, and cell lines were explored. From a pan-cancer perspective, we assessed the levels of AKAP7 in more than 30 cancer types. In 14 cancer types, AKAP7 was significantly downregulated in tumor tissues. Then, we discovered that in BLCA tissues, AKAP7 was shown to have a significantly lower expression on both the mRNA and protein levels, and qRT-PCR verified the expression of AKAP7 in BLCA samples and BLCA cell lines. In our study, AKAP7 expression was discovered to be correlated with multiple clinical features of BLCA, including pathogenic stage, T stage, N stage, M stage, lymphovascular invasion, age and tumor subtypes. In BLCA, lower AKAP7 expression was related to poor OS, DSS and PFI. We further illustrated the prognostic significance of AKAP7 expression through the construction of a nomogram. Compared to other newly reported molecular markers for bladder cancer, AKAP7 exhibits superior differential diagnostic capabilities [30–32]. These outcomes indicated that AKAP7 might function as a significant biomarker for BLCA prognosis.

Although gene mutations are crucial for the tumor development [33, 34], the proportion of AKAP7 genetic alterations was approximately 2.2% in bladder cancer, and which showed little correlation with the prognosis of BLCA.

The tumor microenvironment (TME) encompasses a diverse array of cellular and non-cellular components, which plays a central role in the initiation and progression of cancer. Interactions among various cell types within the TME exert substantial influence on tumor progression and drug sensitivity [35–37]. Existing evidence suggests that the increased infiltration of CD8 + T cells is associated with a better prognosis in patients with BLCA [38–40]. In the TCGA-BLCA, we explored the relationship between AKAP7 expression and immune infiltration. Our findings revealed a significant association between AKAP7 and multiple infiltrating immune cell types, including T helper cells, Tcm, Eosinopoils, TFH, Th17 and CD8 T cells. Based on the scRNA-seq analysis, we further assessed the correlation between AKAP7 expression and infiltrating immune cells in BLCA tissues, where we observed the relatively high expression of AKAP7 in CD8 + T cells specifically. Additionally, our findings revealed significant differences in the levels of infiltration of immune cells, such as CD8 T cells and T cells, between the groups with high and low AKAP7 expression. Consequently, we recommend evaluating AKAP7 expression levels and monitoring the degree of CD8 + T cell infiltration as these measures hold promising clinical implications, facilitating enhanced prognostication for patients undergoing BLCA. This novel approach holds promise for more accurate predictions regarding the clinical outcomes of such patients.

Mounting evidence has illustrated the intricate correlation between ferroptosis and various aspects of cancer, including initiation and progression. Ferroptosis emerges as a pivotal mechanism in tumor suppression, and the therapeutic potential of ferroptosis in cancer treatment is highly promising [41, 42]. In our study, we observed that the AKAP7 was notably correlated with ACSL4, MT1G, NCOA4 and NF2E2L2. Furthermore, we observed significant differences in the expression levels of ACSL4, LPCAT3, NCOA4, NFE2L2, HSPB1, MT1G and SLC1A5 between BLCA groups with high and low AKAP7 expression. Notably, survival analysis demonstrated that high ACSL4 expression was significantly correlated with better clinical outcomes in BLCA. Jiang et al. discovered that ACSL4 played a crucial role in the regulatory processes of ferroptosis in BLCA cells through lipid peroxidation. According to these findings, we proposed AKAP7 might promote the activation of ferroptosis in BLCA by upregulating ACSL4 expression, thereby influencing the development of bladder cancer.

Although we have confirmed the expression of AKAP7 through the external GEO database and collected clinical samples, our research still has some limitations. To further validate the expression of AKAP7 and its prognostic significance, a larger clinical patient cohort as well as long-term survival data are necessary. The relationship between AKAP7 expression and clinical features such as BLCA stage and prognostic value awaits further clinical research. Additionally, the role and specific mechanisms of AKAP7 in BLCA require further biological evidence from experimental studies for verification. These limitations will be addressed in our future efforts.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrates the potential of downregulated ATAP7 as a biomarker for predicting poor prognosis in BLCA and is closely associated with clinicopathological characteristics and the immunologic state of BLCA. Additionally, AKAP7 appears to play a critical role in promoting ACSL4 expression and subsequently inducing ferroptosis in BLCA. However, further experimental investigations are required to illustrate the exact mechanism through which AKAP7 contributes to the progression of BLCA.

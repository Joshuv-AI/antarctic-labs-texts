PEGylated Cu-doped WS2 hybrid nanosheets for targeted multimodal cancer therapy
Authors: Dan Li, Chang Wen, Han Wu, Xiaobin Li, Xiaopeng Zhang, Wenbo Li, Yu Lei, Juan Li, Can Yang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The existing clinical approaches for breast cancer treatment still face many major issues, including poor therapeutic efficacy and high side effects. To address these challenges, we developed a folic acid-functionalized PEGylated WS2 nanosheet system loaded with copper atoms and the chemotherapeutic agent doxorubicin (so-called Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX) for enhanced multimodal cancer therapy. The folic acid was used as a targeting ligand by binding to folate receptors on tumor cells. The PEG-modification was used to enhance the biocompatibility of WS2 nanosheet, which exhibited high photothermal conversion efficiency. Both atomically dispersed Cu species and doxorubicin are successfully incorporated onto the nanosheet to enable chemodynamic therapy and chemotherapy, respectively. Upon laser irradiation, the Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX system can effectively enhance the temperature with high photothermal conversion efficiency (τs = 2.1 min), trigger a Fenton-like reaction to promote reactive oxygen species, and release doxorubicin by responding to the weakly acidic environment at the tumor site. The in vitro results showed the high tumor inhibition activity of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX. The in vivo studies further confirmed that this hybrid nanosheet can specifically target the tumor site and achieve improved multimodal breast cancer therapy, and prolong the survival of tumour-bearing mice. This study developed a multifunctional nanoplatform for improved multimodal breast cancer therapy, offering a great potential synergistic therapeutic strategy for cancer treatment.

Introduction

The traditional cancer treatments, including surgery1,2, chemotherapy3–6, and radiation therapy7, are commonly utilized to inhibit the tumor growth in the clinic. However, these therapies show many inherent limitations, especially poor therapeutic efficacy and severe side effects7,8. Some emerging therapies, such as photothermal therapy (PTT)9–11, photodynamic therapy (PDT)12,13, immunotherapy, and chemodynamic therapy (CDT), have recently been developed14–16, but the therapeutic efficacy is still the key challenge. In recent years, with the rapid development of materials science and nanobiotechnology, multimodal therapy (e.g., PTT and chemotherapy, PDT and CDT, chemotherapy and PTT, CDT and immunotherapy) based on the construction of a multifunctional platform is attracting more and more attention worldwide, showing great potential in the clinic because of enhanced therapeutic efficacy17–19.

The two-dimensional materials are thoroughly investigated and extensively used to fabricate the multifunctional platforms for multimodal cancer therapy owing to their interesting and specific properties, including redox potential20, photothermal conversion performance21, photocatalytic performance22, biocompatibility, and high surface area23–26. Among them, tungsten disulfide (WS2) nanosheet has received great attention in the biomedical application fields in recent years27–31. It has been reported that the high specific surface area of WS2 nanosheet provides conditions for loading bioactive small molecules such as chemical drugs and single-atom catalyst32,33. The WS2 nanosheet exhibits good photothermal conversion properties, which enable its potential for photothermal therapy34. For instance, Geng et al. prepared a low-toxic bifunctional WS2 nanosheet and carbon dots (CDs) heterojunction (CD/WS2 HJ) via surface modification, which exhibited strong absorption and high photothermal conversion efficiency when irradiated by a 1064 nm laser, followed by enhancing the osteosarcoma treatment35. Fu et al. reported a photocatalytic drug (AWS@M) prepared by in situ growth of Ag2S nanoparticles on the surface of WS2 nansheets to address the high tumor interstitial pressure (TIP) for improved cancer combination therapy36. Compared with other 2D materials such as MoS₂37, graphene oxide38, or black phosphorus39, WS₂ exhibits a more stable hexagonal crystal structure, higher photothermal conversion efficiency (up to 48%), and stronger near-infrared absorbance at 660–1064 nm40. In addition, the sulfur-rich surface allows easy formation of W–S and S–S linkages with lipoic-acid-terminated polymers, providing superior bioconjugation capability and physiological stability. These properties make WS₂ particularly advantageous for constructing multifunctional therapeutic platforms with enhanced in vivo retention and biosafety34. In summary, the design and development of multimodal therapy based on the construction of a multifunctional platform through surface modification of WS2 nanosheet is an effective strategy for improving tumor therapy. To enhance the therapeutic efficiency of WS₂-based nanoplatforms, Cu was introduced as a Fenton-like catalyst for CDT. The Cu⁺/Cu²⁺ redox cycle effectively decomposes H₂O₂ into reactive ·OH radicals, generating localized oxidative stress within the tumor microenvironment41. This catalytic property complements photothermal and chemotherapeutic effects, leading to multimodal synergy and improved therapeutic effects.

Herein, we design a Cu@WS₂-PEG-FA/DOX multifunctional platform that integrates PTT, CDT, and chemotherapy. Compared with previously reported PEG-WS₂ dual-mode systems, the incorporation of Cu for Fenton-like catalysis and the lipoic-acid-anchored PEG-FA for active targeting collectively enable triple-responsive therapy with improved biosafety. As shown in Fig. 1a, WS2 nanosheet displays a high photothermal conversion efficiency used for PTT. Atomically dispersed Cu species, which are widely used as a catalyst to enhance the Fenton-like reaction efficiency in tumor microenvironment42–44, are fixed on the surface of WS2 nanosheet, resulting in Cu@WS2 for chemicaldynamic therapy45–47. Then, the polymer lipoic acid-ended polyethylene glycol-folic acid (LA-PEG-FA) is selected to modify the surface of Cu@WS2, to enhance the biocompatibility and improve the targeting effect. Finally, the small chemical antitumor drug doxorubicin (DOX) is loaded on the WS2 nanosheet via π-π interaction, leading to the final multifunctional platform Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX. This system Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX can target and accumulate at the site of tumor via the specific interaction between folic acid and folic acid receptor overexpressed on the surface of tumor, followed by suppressing the tumor growth through PTT and CDT and chemotherapy with laser irradiation for breast cancer treatment (Fig. 1b). In this work, the system Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX is synthesized, and the physicochemical properties are carefully characterized. Results and discussion

Preparation and characterization of multifunctional platform

The WS2 nanosheets were first prepared via a conventional liquid-phase stripping method48. To enhance their reducing properties for surface modification, additional surface defects were introduced using cryo-ball milling. Subsequently, Cu atoms were anchored in a highly dispersed form on WS2 nanosheets by thoroughly mixing them with a copper sulfate solution, enabling the reduction of metal ions on the surface of WS2 nanosheets. Finally, polymer LA-PEG-FA (chemical structure in Fig. S1) was linked to the WS2 nanosheets via the W-S bond49,50. As shown in Fig. 2a, TEM was used to characterize the different WS2 nanosheet-based samples. The presence of layered WS2 nanosheets could be observed, and three samples showed a lamellar structure with a transverse size of about 200 nm. AFM results displayed that the polymer LA-PEG-FA-modified sample (Cu@WS2-PEG-FA) showed an average thickness of 7.74 nm, which was slightly higher than WS2 and Cu@WS2 samples, confirming the successful surface modification of polymer (Fig. 2b). Then, EDS elemental mapping analysis was further used to examine the chemical composition of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA sample, as shown in Fig. 2c. The results showed the W, S, Cu, C, N, and O elements were uniformly distributed (Table S1). After Cu incorporation and polymer modification, the elements51 (Cu, C, and N) contents were increased, demonstrating the successful surface modification. XRD was further used to characterize the crystal structure and composition of the material, and the results are shown in Fig. S2. The diffraction peaks of pristine WS2 were in high agreement with the standard WS2 phase (JCPDS 08-0237), indicating that the as-prepared WS2 has a hexagonal structure and its purity. Both Cu@WS2 and Cu@WS2-PEG-FA samples showed characteristic diffraction peaks corresponding to the crystalline faces of hexagonal-phase WS2, suggesting that in the Cu doping process and subsequent polymer modification on the surface, the WS2 layered crystal structure was intact. The polymer-surface modification process was further confirmed using FTIR, as shown in Fig. 2d. The absorption peaks at 627 cm–1 and 939 cm–1 for S-S and W-S bonds in the Cu@WS2 sample. For the Cu@WS2-PEG-FA sample, two new peaks appeared at wave numbers about 2966 cm–1 and 2903 cm–1, corresponding to the stretching vibration of the C-H bond. The peaks at 1703 cm–1, 1603 cm–1, and 1088 cm–1 were observed, corresponding to the infrared absorption peaks of C=O, C–N, and C–O bonds, respectively, clearly indicating that PEG-FA was successfully modified onto the surface of Cu@WS2. To further investigate the elemental composition and electronic states of the Cu@WS2-PEG-FA sample, XPS was conducted (Fig. 2e). The spectrum of XPS revealed W and S peaks in WS2 and additional Cu peaks in Cu@WS2, confirming successful Cu loading. In Cu@WS2-PEG-FA, new peaks at 285.18 eV (C) and 400.58 eV (N) further verified the effective surface modification by PEG-FA. The hydrodynamic diameter of WS2, Cu@WS2, and Cu@WS2-PEG-FA in deionized water was determined by DLS to be 470.8, 567.5, and 216.7 nm, as shown in Fig. 2f. In aqueous solution, the pristine WS2 exhibited a negative zeta-potential of –38.4 mV. After polymer modification, the zeta-potential changed to –25.0 mV (Fig. 2g), indicating that LA-PEG-FA was successfully modified on the Cu@WS2 surface. After PEG-FA modification, both the hydrodynamic diameter and PDI decreased, which can be attributed to improved colloidal stability and reduced aggregation caused by the hydration layer and steric hindrance of PEG chains. The enhanced dispersion uniformity results in a smaller apparent hydrodynamic size and lower PDI in DLS measurement, rather than an actual reduction in particle size. DOX was loaded on the WS2 nanosheets with different mass ratios, and the optimal drug loading content (56.03%) and entrapment efficiency (74.70%) were confirmed (Table S2). Furthermore, WS2, Cu@WS2, and Cu@WS2-PEG-FA were homogeneously dispersed in deionized water (Fig. S3), indicating the high stability of the samples. Multitype performances of multifunctional platform

Since the multifunctional platform was prepared, the photothermal conversion performance was next evaluated. As reported, WS2 nanosheet has a wide range of light absorption in the ultraviolet to near-infrared wavelength bands, the 660 nm laser was selected for photothermal irradiation owing to the broad NIR absorption and high photothermal conversion efficiency of WS₂-based nanosheets in this spectral range. As the 660 nm wavelength lies within the first near-infrared biological window (650–900 nm), it enables efficient photon penetration into tumor tissue and minimizes light scattering and collateral damage to surrounding normal cells49. So the photothermal properties of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX were tested for PTT by investigating the temperature change of the preparation solution under continuous laser irradiation at 660 nm, as shown in Fig. 3. Firstly, the temperature profiles of different concentrations of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX solutions under 660 nm laser irradiation (0.8 W cm–2) were tested over time (Fig. 3a). The temperature of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX solution at concentration of 0.5 mg/mL was increased from room temperature to about 40 °C after 5 min with 660 nm laser irradiation. The temperature of the sample at a concentration of 1.0 mg/mL increased from room temperature to about 55 °C under the same conditions. With an increase in sample concentration, the temperature of the samples could be increased to about 60 °C (2 mg/mL) and 68 °C (4 mg/mL), showing a clear concentration dependence of the photothermal conversion effect. The Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX sample could still reach a high temperature and maintain a stable photothermal performance after 6 cycles of repeated irradiation by 660 nm laser (0.8 W cm–2) (Fig. 3b), indicating that the platform Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX possessed a good photothermal conversion stability. To further confirm the photothermal conversion efficiency of the multifunctional platform, the Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX sample (2 mg/mL) was tested. The sample was first irradiated by a 660 nm laser (0.8 W cm–2) for 5 min, then the laser was turned off. The temperature changes were recorded and shown in Fig. 3c. The time–temperature decay data collected during the cooling stage were fitted using the heat transfer equation derived from the principle of energy balance, and the fitting curve is shown in Fig. 3d. The τs = 2.1 min was calculated by the slope, suggesting the platform displayed high photothermal conversion efficiency. The introduction of highly dispersed Cu sites on the WS2 surface is for achieving CDT by catalyzing Fenton-like reactions in the tumor microenvironment. We next tested the catalytic properties of the multifunctional platform. The ·OH level of samples under different conditions was detected using UV-Vis (Fig. 3e). There was no characteristic peak at 652 nm for PBS, H2O2, and Cu@WS2-PEG-FA samples, suggesting that the single sample could not produce ·OH. When H2O2 and Cu@WS2-PEG-FA were present at the same time, a distinctive characteristic peak at 652 nm was generated, indicating that copper can catalyze H2O2 to produce ·OH, which triggered the Fenton-like reaction. The concentration of H2O2 or Cu@WS2-PEG-FA is also an important factor affecting the activity of the Fenton-like reaction, so the ·OH levels of samples at different conditions were detected. The peak at 652 nm increased with the increase of H2O2 (0 mM, 0.5 mM, 1 mM, 2 mM, and 4 mM) or Cu@WS2-PEG-FA (0 μg/mL, 100 μg/mL, 200 μg/mL, 300 μg/mL, and 400 μg/mL) concentration, which indicated concentration dependence of ·OH on H2O2 and Cu@WS2-PEG-FA (Fig. S4).Fig. 3Multifunction evaluation of the multifunctional platform Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX.a The temperature change profiles of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX at different concentrations under 660 nm laser irradiation (0.8 W cm–2). b The temperature changes of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX (1 mg/mL) in the warming and cooling cycle. c The thermal response curve and -Ln(θ) versus time fit curve (d) of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX solution. e UV-Vis absorption spectra of ·OH detected by TMB under different conditions. f The in vitro accumulative drug release profiles of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX at different conditions. Then, we confirmed the in vitro drug release profiles of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX (Fig. 3f). At pH of 7.4, the accumulative release amount of DOX was less than 15% for 24 h. At pH of 5.0, the accumulative release amount of DOX was increased to approximately 38% for 24 h. With 660 nm laser irradiation, the DOX release rate was further facilitated and reached about 56%. These results demonstrated that the chemical drug DOX could be controlled release from Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX due to the affinity bonding reduction between DOX molecules and WS2 nanosheets induced by acidic pH and high temperature with laser irradiation.

Next, we evaluated the intracellular ROS generation level of E0771 cells after incubation with DOX-loaded/unloaded Cu@WS2-PEG-FA for 24 h under different conditions. The intracellular ROS level of cells treated with Cu@WS2-PEG-FA and H2O2 under laser irradiation was much higher compared with the other controls, qualitatively and quantitatively confirmed with a confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM) and quantitatively by fluorescence intensity analysis of CLSM images (Fig. 3g–h). The reason could be that the catalytic effect of Cu species led to the dramatic increase of the intracellular ROS level in E0771 cells. Moreover, the laser illumination caused the intracellular temperature increase, which enhanced the rate of the intracellular Fenton-like reaction and the rate of ·OH production52.

Taken together, all the results demonstrated that the Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX platform exhibited great potential in multimodal cancer therapy by combining PTT, CDT, and chemotherapy.

Anticancer activity of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX

It’s a precondition that the multifunctional platform could not induce biosafety issues in the clinic. We firstly evaluated the cytotoxicity of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA against NIH 3T3 cells. The cell viability of NIH 3T3 cells was still higher than 80% after incubation with Cu@WS2-PEG-FA at a concentration of 100 μg/mL for 24 h (Fig. 4a) or 48 h (Fig. 4b), indicating that the multifunctional platform had no obvious cytotoxicity against NIH 3T3 cells. Next, the cell apoptosis of E0771 cells induced by the Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX was carefully measured to evaluate the anticancer ability in vitro. The cell viability of E0771 cells incubated with Cu@WS2-PEG-FA was approximately 80% even at the highest concentration of 200 μg/mL without laser irradiation for 24 h (Fig. 4c) or 48 h (Fig. 4d), indicating the low anticancer activity of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA. After incubation with Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX for 24 h (Fig. 4e) or 48 h (Fig. 4f), the cell viability of E0771 cells was obviously decreased with the increase of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX concentration, showing dose-dependent cell apoptosis. The IC50 values were confirmed as about 5 μg/mL for 24 h and 0.75 μg/mL for 48 h, suggesting the high anticancer activity. To further evaluate the anticancer activity, the cell viability of E0771 cells incubated with Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX at different conditions was recorded in Fig. 4g. At pH of 5.0 with 660 nm laser irradiation and H2O2 addition, the cell viability was the lowest compared with other conditions, indicating the PTT and CDT and chemotherapy multimodal cancer therapy based on Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX had the improved therapeutic efficacy. The flow cytometry was used to confirm that the E0771 cells incubated with Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX with addition of H2O2 under laser irradiation displayed the highest antitumor activity (73.60% of cell apoptosis) compared with other controls (Fig. 4h). Furthermore, the quantitative analysis also confirmed this result (Fig. 4i), further demonstrating that the synergistic Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX-based multimodal therapy exhibited the improved antitumor activity. As shown in Fig. 3e, the H₂O₂ + Cu@WS₂-PEG-FA group displayed a significant decrease in TMB absorbance, indicating the generation of ·OH radicals through Cu-mediated Fenton-like catalysis. Correspondingly, the cell viability and apoptosis results in Fig. 4g, h verify that this redox activation, together with laser-induced photothermal heating, markedly enhances oxidative damage and induces stronger tumor-cell killing. The quantitative analysis in Fig. 4i further supports the improved therapeutic outcome. Next, the in vivo biodistribution of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX in a tumor-bearing mouse model was measured using an in vivo imaging system (IVIS). As shown in Fig. 5c, both Cy5.5-labeled Cu@WS2/DOX and Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX mostly accumulated at the tumor site at 24 h. Compared with the non-targeting control Cu@WS2/DOX, the fluorescence intensity at the tumor site of mice treated with Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/Cy5.5/DOX was significantly increased (Fig. 5d), indicating the higher accumulation at the tumor site due to the folic acid modification. At 48 h post-injection, the fluorescence intensity at the tumor site was decreased for both Cu@WS2/DOX and Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX systems. To further confirm the specific targeting effect, mice were executed at different time points (12 h, 24 h, and 48 h), and the tumors and major organs were collected for imaging. As shown in Fig. 5d, the tumor fluorescence intensity of Cu@WS₂-PEG-FA/DOX reached its maximum at 24 h and remained strong at 48 h, suggesting prolonged tumor retention compared with Cu@WS₂/DOX. This sustained accumulation is attributed to both the folate receptor mediated uptake and the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect of the nanosheets. The quantitative analysis also confirmed a significant difference (P < 0.01) between the two groups, indicating that folic-acid modification markedly improved tumor-targeting efficiency and in vivo stability. The fluorescence signal distributions of the isolated tumors and major organs were systematically observed (Fig. 5e). Similar results could be observed. For both Cu@WS2/DOX and Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX systems, the strongest fluorescence intensity at the tumor site could be found at 24 h post-injection. The Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX nanosheets displayed much higher specific accumulation at the tumor site compared with the non-targeting effect Cu@WS2/DOX control. At 48 h post-injection, Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX nanosheets remained specifically enriched at the tumor site, while the fluorescence signals in the liver and kidney decreased. The fluorescence signals at the tumor site of mice treated with the Cu@WS2/DOX group were obviously lower, possibly due to the immune clearance.

In sum, the multifunctional platform Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX nanosheets can specifically accumulate at the tumor site after intravenous injection due to the active targeting ligand modification, indicating the enhanced therapeutic efficacy.

Therapeutic efficacy evaluation of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX

Since the Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX nanosheets exhibited high photothermal conversion efficiency, catalytic property, and tumor cell killing effect in vitro, we are encouraged to investigate the therapeutic effect in vivo for cancer. Therefore, we built a breast cancer-bearing mouse model and treated it with different formulations under different conditions. The tumor treatment cycle was shown in Fig. 6a. The tumor volumes of the control treatment groups (G1, G2, G3, G4, and G5) showed an increasing trend throughout the treatment process, while the tumor volume of mice with treatment of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX with laser had neglected increase, suggesting the highest therapeutic effect (Fig. 6b). At the end of treatment, the tumor tissues were collected, and the weight and morphology were showed in Fig. 6c, d. Compared with the other groups, the tumor weight of the combination treatment group (G6) showed the most remarkable decreasing trend and was the smallest among all treatment groups. We further monitored the mouse survival after therapeutic treatments (n = 10) (Fig. 6e). We observed that 80% mice survived in the Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX with laser treatment group (G6) compared to all dead in the isotype control at 35 days. The survival rate of tumor-bearing mice was obviously increased by treating them with the multifunctional platform with a laser. Thus, the findings revealed that the combination therapy (PTT and CDT, and chemotherapy) based on the Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX nanosheets exhibited the best therapeutic effect. We further analyzed the therapeutic effect using tumor sections and immunohistochemistry (Fig. 6f). The tumors in the combined treatment group (G6) were severely damaged, and most of the tumor cells were in the late stage of apoptosis, which proved that the combined treatment was the most effective. To further demonstrate the safety of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX-based treatment in cancer therapy, the body weight of mice with different treatments was recorded in Fig. S5, showing no significant changes. The organ indices (Fig. S6) and blood biochemical indices (Fig. S7, ALT/ALB/γ-GT/TP/UREA/UA) were further measured and showed no significant differences from those of the PBS group during the course of treatment. Moreover, no pathological changes or inflammation were observed in the major organs of all tested groups except the DOX-treatment group, demonstrating the high safety of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX (Fig. S8). Conclusions

2D material-based systems have been extensively investigated and widely utilized for cancer therapy owing to their high surface area, good photothermal conversion efficiency, and high drug-loading capacity. Nevertheless, most existing 2D material-based platforms still suffer from limited physiological stability, poor tumor targeting effect, and low drug accumulation at tumor site, leading to poor therapeutic efficacy and high side-effects. In this study, WS₂-based therapeutics (Cu@WS₂-PEG-FA/DOX) were designed and fabricated by integrating photothermal, chemodynamic and chemotherapeutic modalities into a single adaptive nanoplatform, achieving synergistic tumor eradication through complementary mechanisms. The FA-mediated targeting and PEG shielding further ensure selective tumor accumulation and reduced systemic toxicity, as supported by the biodistribution and histological results. The atomically dispersed Cu species not only endow the system with Fenton-like catalytic activity but also convert endogenous H₂O₂ into cytotoxic ·OH radicals, alleviating tumor hypoxia and amplifying oxidative stress. Simultaneously, localized photothermal heating enhances Cu-mediated catalysis and DOX release, establishing a cascade-type therapeutic effect that extends beyond the irradiated region.

Beyond the current work, this design concept provides a generalizable strategy for constructing self-activating multimodal nanotherapeutics capable of responding to the unique biochemical cues of the tumor microenvironment. In addition, this nanoplatform could be further adapted to load other therapeutic modalities, such as nucleic acids, peptides, or protein-based drugs, to achieve more precise and programmable tumor-targeted therapy. These directions highlight the potential of our design as a safe and versatile platform for next-generation targeted cancer treatment. In the future, the biocompatibility, biodegradation and safety of Cu@WS2-PEG-FA/DOX should be further carefully evaluated in vitro and in vivo for the potential clinic translation. Moreover, the surface engineering strategy and process in this work would be further optimized such as the physical loading of copper atom and doxorubicin molecule on the WS2 sheets. We believe that the multifunctional 2D material-based platforms could be a promising therapeutics for improved cancer therapy.

The growing blind spot in osteoporosis care: A survey of spine surgeons in German-speaking countries
Authors: Biniam Melese Bekele, Ina Moritz, Tabea Miron, Yu-Mi Ryang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe incidence of osteoporotic vertebral fractures is steadily increasing due to demographic changes and a growing treatment gap. The OF-Classification and OF-Score provide a standardized framework for classifying and treating these fractures.Research questionThis study aimed to assess treatment practices and the use of these classifications among spine surgeons through an online survey.MethodsAn anonymized electronic questionnaire consisting of 36 questions was distributed to all members of the German Spine Society (DWG), the Austrian Spine Society (Spine.at), and German-speaking hospitals in Switzerland. The responses were stratified by specialty and analyzed comparatively.ResultsOut of 2981 physicians contacted, 300 completed the survey (response rate: 18.6 %); 40.3 % were neurosurgeons, 36.3 % orthopedic surgeons, and 22.3 % trauma surgeons. The OF-Classification was routinely used by 70.3 % of participants. Factors considered most important for clinical decision-making were clinical presentation (95 %), imaging (90.7 %), and general condition of the patient (78.7 %). Only 44 % used the OF-Score for clinical decision-making. Bone mineral density measurements were performed regularly by 60.3 %, primarily using DEXA scans (57.3 %) followed by CT-based Hounsfield Unit (HU; 19 %) and quantitative computed tomography (QCT). Anti-osteoporotic therapy compliant to national guidelines was prescribed only by 48 % of participants and 34.7 % did not routinely monitor bone mineral density during treatment.ConclusionThe OF-Classification is widely implemented, whereas the OF-Score plays a minor role in clinical decision-making. There is a significant deficit in the diagnosis and treatment of osteoporosis, as evidenced by low diagnostic rates, under prescription of anti-osteoporotic therapy, lack of follow-up, and inadequate outpatient care.Keywords: Osteoporosis, Osteoporotic vertebral fractures, Anti-Osteoporotic therapy, OF-Classification, OF-Score

Introduction

Osteoporosis is the most common bone disease, characterized by impaired bone quality and increased risk of fractures. Globally, more than 8.9 million people suffer from osteoporotic fractures, with a new fracture occurring every 3 s (Johnston and Dagar, 2020). These fractures are associated with significant social, medical, economic and public health consequences (Hernlund et al.; Cummings and Melton, 2002; Cauley, 2013). Moreover, with the aging population, the incidence of osteoporosis and, consequently, osteoporotic fractures, is expected to rise significantly in the coming years (Cui et al.).

Osteoporotic vertebral fractures (OVFs) are the most common osteoporotic fractures, with prevalence rates ranging from 18 % to 26 % in Europe (Hadji et al., 2013). OVFs are associated with multiple comorbidities, significant limitations in daily life, reduced quality of life, and a 15 % increased mortality rate (Jung et al., 2017; Kado et al., 1999; Schnake et al., 2018). Despite decades of experience in treating OVFs, there remains substantial heterogeneity in decision-making and treatment strategies worldwide. To ensure a comprehensive and reproducible approach to the management of osteoporotic vertebral fractures, the working group on osteoporotic fractures (Spinal Surgery Section of the German Society for Orthopedics and Trauma Surgery [DGOU]) developed a morphological classification system for osteoporotic fractures (OF-Classification). This classification consists of 5 groups: OF 1, no vertebral deformation (vertebral edema); OF 2, deformation with no or minor (<1/5) involvement of the posterior wall; OF 3, deformation with distinct involvement (>1/5) of the posterior wall; OF 4, loss of integrity of the vertebral frame or vertebral body collapse or pincer-type fracture; OF 5, injuries with distraction or rotation (Schnake et al., 2018). Based on this, the OF-Score was introduced; a scoring system that, in addition to the OF-Classification, incorporates five additional factors to guide decision-making regarding conservative versus surgical treatment strategies (Blattert et al., 2018). To standardize the diagnosis and treatment of osteoporotic fractures, the organization of German-speaking scientific osteology societies (DVO) regularly develops and publishes guidelines based on systematic literature reviews and interdisciplinary expert consensus (DVO et al., 2023).

The aim of this survey was to analyze treatment practices and the utilization of the OF-Classification and OF-Score in the routine clinical practice of spine surgeons in German-speaking countries across Europe. By identifying treatment gaps in osteoporosis management, particularly regarding diagnosis and therapy, this study seeks to advocate for improved and standardized osteoporosis care in the future.

Methods

Study design and population

This study was a cross-sectional survey of spine surgeons treating patients with osteoporotic fractures (OF) in German-speaking countries. A questionnaire was sent to all members of the German Spine Society (DWG; www.dwg.org), the Austrian Society for Spine Surgery (www.spine.at), and five German-speaking hospitals in Switzerland over two months (October to December 2023).

Data collection

An online survey was created using SurveyMonkey, a web-based survey platform (SurveyMonkey Inc., San Mateo, California, USA, www.surveymonkey.com). The questionnaire comprised 36 questions designed to collect data on diagnostic methods, clinical treatment practices, and the use of the OF-Classification and OF-Score. The survey was conducted in German and validated by two experienced experts in osteoporotic spinal surgery for content accuracy.

Statistical analysis

Demographic characteristics of the study participants were summarized using descriptive statistics, including frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. Responses were stratified and compared by specialty. Group comparisons were performed using the chi-square test or Fisher's exact test, with a p-value of less than 0.05 considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (IBM Corp. 2021. IBM SPSS Statistics for Macintosh, Version 28.0. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp). Graphs were created using R version 4.4.1 (2024-06-14).

Results

Of the 2981 physicians contacted, 300 responded to the survey (response rate: 18.6 %; 79.3 % male, 18.7 % female, 2 % diverse). The most common age groups among respondents were 35–44 years (36.7 %), followed by 45–54 years (32.6 %). A total of 249 (88 %) participants practiced in Germany, 20 (7.1 %) in Switzerland, and 14 (4.9 %) in Austria. (See Table 1)

Regarding medical specialty, 40.3 % of respondents were neurosurgeons, 36.3 % were trauma surgeons, and 22.3 % were orthopedic surgeons. The distribution of professional ranks was as follows: 30.7 % attending physicians, 24.7 % department heads, 19.3 % senior consultants, 14.3 % residents in training, and 11 % board-certified specialists (Fig. 1). A majority (95.7 %) reported spinal surgery as their main focus, and 64.9 % treated osteoporotic fractures at least once or twice per week.

Classification of respondents according to professional ranks.

Regarding experience in spinal surgery, 76.4 % reported more than 5 years of experience: 18.6 % more than 20 years, 14.6 % 16–20 years, 23.9 % 11–15 years, and 19.3 % 5–10 years. Less than five years of experience was reported only by 21.9 %. Less than 2 % of respondents practiced in a non-surgical specialty. A total of 81 % of respondents performed more than 200 spine surgeries annually.

Utilization of the OF-Classification and of score

The OF-Classification was routinely used by 70.3 % of respondents, occasionally by 18 %, never by 9.3 % and 2.3 % had never heard of the OF-Classification. The routine use of the OF-Classification was most common among residents in training (81.4 %), followed by department heads (67.6 %) and attending physicians (65.5 %) (p < 0.001).

A significant difference was also observed between hospitals and private practices: In private practices, only 30 % of respondents routinely used the OF-Classification, while 15 % had never heard of it. In contrast, in academic and urban hospitals, 75.7 % of respondents used the classification routinely, and only 1.14 % were unfamiliar with it. In private clinics, the routine use was 54.2 %, while 4.17 % had never heard of the classification (p < 0.001). Regarding medical specialties, 67.8 % of neurosurgeons, 75.2 % of orthopedic surgeons, and 70.1 % of trauma surgeons reported regular use of the OF-Classification (p < 0.001, Fig. 2).

Imaging and therapy approaches

The survey results regarding the use of imaging techniques indicate that a wide range of modalities is employed, with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) being the most frequently used (90.7 %). X-ray imaging, including two-plane, standing, and full-length views, is used more frequently by neurosurgeons and orthopedic surgeons than by trauma surgeons (Table 2).

When making treatment decisions regarding conservative versus surgical approaches, several factors played a pivotal role. Clinical presentation was considered most important (95.0 %) followed by imaging (90.7 %) of respondents for decision-making. The general condition of the patient influenced decisions in 78.7 % of cases, whereas the OF score was a determining factor for only 44 % of respondents. A detailed analysis, categorized by medical specialty, revealed discipline-specific influencing factors with varying levels of importance. In neurosurgery, the most influential factors were the clinical presentation (100 %) and imaging findings (94 %). These were followed by the patient's response to analgesics (82 %), their general condition (73 %), and the presence of pre-existing conditions (65 %). Compared to these factors, the OF-Classification (63 %) and the OF-Score (42 %) played a subordinate role, as did the ASA (American Society of Anesthesiologists) score (41 %) (Fig. 3).

For orthopedic surgeons, the most important factors in decision-making were clinical presentation (96 %) and imaging findings (94 %), followed by the general condition of the patient (88 %), the response to analgesics (75 %), and the presence of pre-existing conditions (61 %). The OF-Classification (67 %), OF-Score (43 %), and ASA score (45 %) were considered less critical overall. However, compared to neurosurgery, these three parameters held slightly greater significance in decision-making.

In trauma surgery, clinical findings (91 %) and imaging (85 %) were the most dominant factors in treatment decisions, closely followed by the general condition of the patient (79 %), response to analgesics (79 %), and pre-existing conditions (63 %). Among the three specialties, trauma surgeons placed the highest importance on the OF-Classification (72 %), OF-Score (52 %), and ASA score (46 %), compared to orthopedics and neurosurgeons.

Osteoporosis management

Regarding the initiation of anti-osteoporotic therapy, 25.3 % of respondents always prescribe such a therapy in therapy-naïve patients. An additional 17.6 % initiate treatment only in cases of osteoporosis confirmed by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) scan with a T-score ≤ −2.5. Meanwhile, 31.6 % start anti-osteoporotic therapy when a vertebral osteoporotic fracture, hence a manifest osteoporosis, is present, even without DEXA confirmation.

Among anti-osteoporotic medications, vitamin D3 is the most frequently prescribed (79.7 % of respondents), followed by calcium (65.7 %). Other prescribed medications include bisphosphonates (58 %), denosumab (36.3 %), teriparatide (20.7 %), and romosozumab (5.7 %). Notably, only 48 % of respondents prescribe a guideline-compliant therapy consisting of vitamin D3, calcium, and either an antiresorptive or osteoanabolic agent. A small percentage (11.3 %) reported that they do not prescribe any anti-osteoporotic medications (Table 2).

Approximately 60.3 % of respondents routinely perform bone mineral density measurements at initial diagnosis of an OVF. The preferred assessment method is DEXA scan (57.3 %), followed by CT Hounsfield units (HU) (19 %) and quantitative computed tomography (QCT) (14.7 %). The survey results further indicate that 34.7 % of respondents do not regularly monitor bone mineral density in patients receiving therapy with DEXA. 16.7 % conduct the examination every 1–2 years, while 1.7 % only every 5 years. A substantial proportion (34.6 %) stated that bone mineral density monitoring is carried out by outpatient colleagues.

A detailed analysis of patient referrals to osteoporosis-specialized clinics or practices for further outpatient care revealed differences between medical specialties. In neurosurgery, 65.3 % of respondents referred their patients to osteoporosis specialists, in orthopedics, 52.3 %, while trauma surgeons had the lowest referral rate at 43.3 %.

Orthoses are primarily used as part of conservative treatment approaches. Regardless of the chosen therapy method, 14 % of neurosurgeons, 17.4 % of orthopedic surgeons, and 6 % of trauma surgeons prescribed orthoses. The most commonly recommended duration for orthosis use was 6 weeks, as suggested by 40 % of respondents. In clinical practice, the DVO guideline (DVO-Leitlinie, 2023) is routinely applied by only 37.3 % of respondents, while 12 % stated that they do not follow it at all (Table 2). Regarding participation in continuing education programs on spinal disorders, engagement levels among respondents were consistently high to very high. Orthopedic surgeons had the highest participation rate in regular training sessions (94.5 %), followed by trauma surgeons (83.6 %) and neurosurgeons (81.8 %).

Discussion

Our study examined the current clinical practice in the diagnosis and treatment of OVFs. The findings indicate that the OF-Classification is well-accepted in daily clinical practice with routine use by about 70 % of participants. The highest utilization of the OF-Classification was observed among residents in training and in academic and urban hospitals. In contrast, only 30 % of private practice physicians incorporated the OF-Classification into their daily clinical work. However, when it came to treatment decisions, the OF-Classification and OF-Score had less influence compared to clinical and imaging findings. Across all specialties, the patient's general condition and response to pain medication played a significantly greater role.

A prospective multicenter cohort study (EOFTT study) (Ullrich et al., 2023), conducted in 17 German spine centers, demonstrated that adherence to OF-Score–based treatment recommendations correlate with better short-term outcomes, including reduced pain and improved function. In contrast, non-adherence to the score was associated with increased pain, impaired functional outcomes, and reduced quality of life. Our survey highlights that the OF-Score is highly accepted in German-speaking countries. However, the majority of centers treating osteoporotic vertebral fractures currently rely more on individual clinical factors and imaging findings rather than strictly following the OF-Score. Notably, trauma surgeons assigned a higher importance to the OF-Classification and OF-Score than orthopedic surgeons and neurosurgeons, suggesting more variability in how different specialties value these tools. The inter-specialty differences could possibly be attributed to different training backgrounds, hospital setting and patient case-mix. Our survey highlights that although the OF-Score is broadly accepted conceptually, in practice most spine centers still rely more on individual clinical judgment and imaging findings. Potential reasons for this low practicial implenetation may be due to lack of awareness, percieved clinical complexity and insufficient integration into hospital protocols. Studies have shown that clinical uptake of composite scores remains low when not supported by electronic decision tools or mandatory documentation protocols (Kawamoto et al., 2005; Roshanov et al., 2013). Additional efforts, such as clinician training and digital integration, may be required for the OF-Score to be effectively utilized in routine decision-making.

The effectiveness of anti-osteoporotic therapy in increasing bone density and reducing fracture rates has been demonstrated in multiple placebo-controlled randomized studies (Bilezikian, 2009; Cummings et al., 2009). Additionally, an efficacy study involving 1.2 million patients showed a significant reduction in clinical vertebral fracture risk within 12 months–64 % reduction with teriparatide, 51 % with denosumab, and 23 % with bisphosphonates (Yusuf et al., 2018). Prospective cohort studies (Spechbach et al., 2019) even recommend initiating anti-osteoporotic therapy during the acute phase after fracture diagnosis, as these patients exhibited higher compliance rates after six months compared to those who started therapy only after hospital discharge.

Despite sufficient evidence, our study reveals a significant gap in the management and follow-up care of patients with osteoporotic vertebral fractures among spine surgeons in German-speaking countries. Anti-osteoporotic therapy is currently initiated regularly only by 26 % of spine surgeons, and of these only 48 % prescribe a guideline conform combination therapy consisting of vitamin D, calcium, and an antiresorptive or osteoanabolic agent. A substantial proportion of patients are not integrated into outpatient osteoporosis care, and a significant number of physicians (34.7 %) do not monitor BMD measurements during therapy. Our findings align with the latest report of the International Osteoporosis Foundation (IOF), which indicates that only 20–40 % of German women begin therapy within the first year after suffering from an osteoporotic fracture (Bühring et al.). Furthermore, studies in Austria across 8 trauma centers revealed that only 1 in 10 men and less than 2 in 10 women received osteoporosis treatment at the time of major osteoporotic fractures (Malle et al., 2021). A similar study in Sweden showed that only 8–17 % of women and 5–9 % received osteoporosis medication (Ek et al., 2023). A cross-European study of women aged ≥70 at high fracture risk found a mean treatment gap of 74.6 %, ranging from 53 % (Ireland) to 91 % (Germany) (McCloskey et al., 2021). According to the 2021 SCOPE report, the average treatment gap across European countries reached 71 %, an increase from 55 % in 2010 (Kanis et al., 2021a). A cross-national comparison showed that only 11–13 % of U.S. patients filled osteoporosis prescriptions within 3 months of hip fracture, compared to 25 % in Spain and 39 % in South Korea (Kim et al., 2015). These findings confirm that the osteoporosis care gap is not confined to isolated setting but a global issue across healthcare systems—even in regions with established guidelines and treatment reimbursement frameworks. Our data indicate that even surgical specialists managing fragility fractures often do not engage in secondary prevention, representing a critical missed opportunity to reduce re-fracture risk.

Our study has several limitations. Our results only reflect the practice and care provided by spine surgeons in German-speaking countries. As no internists or general practitioners specializing in osteoporosis were surveyed, no generalized conclusions can be drawn. We had a response rate of 18.6 %, which means that our study may not fully reflect the practice in these countries. Since physicians with a focus on OVFs were more likely to respond, it can be assumed that the data may be overestimated, and the utilization of the OF-Classification and OF-Score, as well as adequate follow-up care in the German-speaking countries, may be even lower than suggested by our study's data. Furthermore, our data was collected using an online survey. Despite the benefits, interpretation of the results should be in consideration of the validity and representativeness of online surveys in general.

According to the latest SCOPE (Scorecard for osteoporosis in Europe) study (Kanis et al., 2021b), Germany has the highest estimated number of individuals affected by osteoporosis and the highest number of fractures among all member states. Additionally, 76 % of German women in need of osteoporosis therapy do not receive preventive treatment after an initial fragility fracture. This rate, compared to 2010 (77 %) (Kanis et al., 2013), is, despite significant advances in guidelines and medication therapy, declining. Our study reflects the current, striking and growing care gap both in diagnosis and prevention as well as in the treatment of osteoporosis, despite the widespread availability of diagnostic tools and medications. Immediate strategic changes are urgently needed in the initiation of therapy and the follow-up care for these patients.

Conclusion

Our survey reveals substantial deficits in osteoporosis care despite the availability of structured classification systems and evidence-based guidelines. The underuse of the OF-Score, low prescription rates of guideline-conform therapy, and inconsistent follow-up highlight a growing care gap in spine surgery. Standardized implementation strategies, interprofessional education, and structural changes in care pathways are urgently required.

Anti-IL-4Rα antibody SHR-1819 for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis: a randomized phase 2 study

Background

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic inflammatory skin condition characterized by pruritic and eczematous lesions [1, 2]. The burden of AD extends beyond physical symptoms, as patients living with this condition often experience a profound impact on their mental health due to the psychological and social challenges it presents [3–6].

Traditional treatments for AD, such as topical corticosteroids and systemic immunosuppressants, have been the cornerstone of therapy for many years [7–11]. However, these treatments are associated with toxicities ranging from skin thinning and steroid rebound effects to systemic immunosuppression that limit their long-term use and overall efficacy.

Cytokines such as IL-4 and IL-13 released by Th2 cells play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of AD [12–14]. IL-4 inhibition via blockade of IL-4 receptor alpha (IL-4Rα), a receptor shared by IL-4 and IL-13, represents a well-established target for AD. Monoclonal antibodies that target IL-4Rα have the potential to block the biological effects of both IL-4 and IL-13 simultaneously, providing a more comprehensive and effective treatment approach compared with agents that selectively target either cytokine pathway [15, 16]. Dupilumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting IL-4Rα, has emerged as a frontrunner and has received approval from the U.S. FDA for the treatment of AD [17–21], as well as other type 2 inflammatory diseases [22–27], underscoring the potential of the therapeutic strategy of anti-IL-4Rα monoclonal antibodies in type 2 inflammatory diseases.

SHR-1819 is a novel monoclonal antibody that specifically targets human IL-4Rα. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that SHR-1819 exhibits high affinity for human and marmoset IL-4Rα and shows promising biological activity in relevant animal models of AD, rhinitis, and asthma (Data on file, Hengrui). Moreover, SHR-1819 has demonstrated similar inhibitory activity and receptor occupancy profiles to dupilumab against hIL-4/hIL-4Rα in vivo (Data on file, Hengrui). In a first-in-human phase 1 trial involving healthy participants, SHR-1819 was well-tolerated, displayed exposure increasing in a dose-proportional manner, and led to modest reductions in inflammatory biomarkers such as thymus and activation-regulated chemokine/CC chemokine ligand 17 (TARC/CCL17) and immunoglobulin E (IgE) [28]. In this context, we conducted this phase 2 study to investigate the efficacy, safety, pharmacokinetics, and pharmacodynamics of SHR-1819 in patients with moderate-to-severe AD.

Methods

Study design and patients

This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 2 trial was conducted in 22 centers in China (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT05549947).

Patients aged 18–75 years and had been diagnosed with AD in accordance with the 2014 American Academy of Dermatology guidelines for a minimum of one year prior to screening were enrolled [29]. Eligible patients were required to have moderate-to-severe AD, as indicated by an Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) score of ≥ 16, an Investigator’s Global Assessment (IGA) score of ≥ 3, and a Body Surface Area (BSA) involvement of ≥ 10%. Moreover, patients should have an average of the daily Peak Pruritus Numerical Rating Scale (PP-NRS) score of ≥ 4 over the 7 days preceding randomization and meet at least one of following criteria: suboptimal response to moderate or higher potency topical corticosteroids, prior use of systemic therapy (such as systemic corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, or Janus kinase inhibitors) regardless of treatment response, or ineligibility for topical therapy. Key exclusion criteria are provided in the supplementary methods.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Good Clinical Practice Guideline. Protocol received approval from the institutional review board or independent ethics committee of each study center. All participants provided written informed consent before enrollment.

Procedures

Patients were randomly assigned (1:1:1:1) to receive SHR-1819 at 300 mg every 2 weeks (Q2W), 600 mg Q2W, 600 mg every 4 weeks (Q4W), or placebo for 16 weeks. A 600 mg initial loading dose was used in the 300 mg Q2W group to expedite the attainment of target concentration levels and accelerate the onset of drug efficacy. This study employed a stratified block randomization method with a block size of 8, stratified by disease severity (moderate [IGA = 3] vs. severe [IGA = 4]). Randomization was performed via a centralized randomization system, under which participating sites competitively enrolled subjects. A dedicated randomization specialist generated the random allocation sequence and drug numbering table using SAS software. The centralized randomization system assigned eligible participants to treatment groups, and corresponding investigational products were dispensed according to the randomization results. All study treatments were prepared as visually identical injectable formulations to maintain blinding, thereby ensuring that patients, investigators, and the sponsor were all kept unaware of the treatment assignment. Requirements for concomitant medications and rescue treatments are provided in the supplementary methods.

Outcomes and assessments

The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients achieving ≥ 75% reduction from baseline in EASI score (EASI-75) at week 16. Secondary efficacy endpoints, also assessed at week 16, included the proportion of patients with an IGA score of 0 or 1 (on a 0–4 scale) and a reduction of ≥ 2 points from baseline; the proportion of patients with a reduction of ≥ 2 points in IGA score from baseline; the proportion of patients achieving EASI-90 (EASI score reduction of ≥ 90% from baseline); the proportion of patients achieving EASI-50 (EASI score reduction of ≥ 50% from baseline); the proportion of patients with a reduction of ≥ 4 points from baseline in weekly average of the daily PP-NRS score; as well as the absolute and percentage changes from baseline in EASI score, Scoring Atopic Dermatitis (SCORAD) score, BSA affected by AD, and Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) score. Other secondary endpoints were safety; pharmacokinetics (PK); pharmacodynamics (PD; including TARC/CCL17, IgE, and eotaxin-3 levels and their percentage change from baseline); and immunogenicity.

Adverse events (AEs) were classified according to the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities, version 26.0.

The serum concentration of SHR-1819, TARC/CCL17, and eotaxin-3 levels were quantified using an established enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay method at Shanghai TriApex Biotechnology Co., Ltd. IgE concentrations were measured using the Atellica IM Total IgE assay at the ACL laboratory in the CMAX clinical facility. Anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) were analyzed using electrochemiluminescence at Shanghai TriApex Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

Statistical analysis

Assuming an EASI-75 rate at week 16 of 15% in the placebo group and maximum 50% among the three SHR-1819 groups, 37 participants per group (148 in total) could provide the study with 85% power to detect the difference between SHR-1819 and placebo, with a two-sided significance level of 0.05 and an anticipated dropout rate of 15% per group.

For the primary endpoint, the proportions of patients achieving EASI-75 at week 16 were calculated, with 95% confidence intervals (CI) estimated using the Wilson method. Between-group differences in the proportions and their 95% CIs were estimated using the stratified Mantel-Haenszel method. The stratified Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel method was utilized to compare each dose of SHR-1819 with placebo in the proportion of patients achieving EASI-75 at week 16, and to report the two-sided p-value. When participants discontinued the study treatment, a treatment strategy was adopted (i.e. the data collected in subsequent follow-up visits were included in analysis). When participants received rescue medication, a composite strategy was employed (i.e. the participant was considered as not achieving EASI-75 at week 16). The missing data were imputed using the non-responder imputation (NRI) method (i.e. the participant was considered as not achieving EASI-75 at week 16).

The same methods as those for the primary endpoint were applied to analyze dichotomous secondary endpoints at week 16. For continuous endpoints at week 16, a mixed-effects model repeated measures (MMRM) analysis was conducted using the restricted estimation maximum likelihood approach. In the MMRM model, baseline scores were used as covariates, with random stratification factors, treatment group, visit, and the interaction between treatment group and visit as fixed effects. The covariance matrix could be either unstructured or compound symmetry as alternative. Intercurrent events were not considered for all secondary endpoints. The missing data for dichotomous secondary endpoints were imputed using the NRI method, while continuous endpoints were handled by the MMRM model.

All p values are nominal, presented for descriptive and exploratory purposes, and were not adjusted for multiple comparisons. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline characteristics, safety data, PK, PD, and ADA results. Statistical analyses were primarily conducted using SAS version 9.4 or above. See Additional file 1 for statistical analysis plan.

Results

Patients

From October 26, 2022, to May 26, 2023, 192 patients were screened and 158 eligible patients were randomly assigned. A total of 157 patients received the assigned treatment, with 39, 40, 41, and 37 patients in the SHR-1819 300 mg Q2W, 600 mg Q2W, 600 mg Q4W, and placebo groups, respectively (Fig. 1). As of database lock (November 23, 2023), 145 patients completed their assigned treatment.

Patient disposition. Q2W, every 2 weeks; Q4W, every 4 weeks

The demographics and clinical characterization at baseline were well-balanced across groups (Table 1). In the SHR-1819 and placebo groups, 60.0% (72/120) and 62.2% (23/37) of patients had AD lasting ≥ 5 years, respectively.

Efficacy

The proportion of patients achieving EASI-75 at week 16 in all three SHR-1819 groups (300 mg Q2W: 69.2% [95% CI: 53.6–81.4]; 600 mg Q2W: 75.0% [95% CI: 59.8–85.8]; 600 mg Q4W: 85.4% [95% CI: 71.6–93.1]) was numerically higher than that in the placebo group (37.8% [95% CI: 24.1–53.9]), with differences of 31.6% (95% CI: 10.3–53.0; nominal p = 0.006), 37.0% (95% CI: 16.3–57.6; nominal p = 0.001), and 47.4% (95% CI: 28.3–66.4; nominal p < 0.001), respectively (Fig. 2A). The 600 mg Q4W group exhibited the highest EASI-75 response rate among the three SHR-1819 groups at week 16 (Fig. 2B). Subgroup analysis demonstrated that a trend favoring SHR-1819 over placebo in EASI‑75 response rate was observed across most prespecified subgroups, with nominal p < 0.05 in multiple subgroups for each dose regimen (Additional file 2: Figure S1).

The proportions of patients achieving an EASI-90 response at week 16 were also higher in the SHR-1819 groups than that in the placebo group (53.8%, 57.5%, and 63.4% vs. 16.2%; p < 0.001 for each comparison; Fig. 2A). Additionally, a greater proportion of patients in the SHR-1819 groups achieved an EASI-50 response compared with the placebo group (86.4%, 92.5%, 97.6% vs. 54.1%; nominal p = 0.004, < 0.001, and < 0.001; Fig. 2A).

Treatment with SHR-1819 also led to a higher percentage of patients achieving a reduction of ≥ 4 points in weekly average of the daily PP-NRS score from baseline at week 16 compared with placebo (41.0%, 47.5%, and 43.9% vs. 16.2%; nominal p = 0.017, 0.004, and 0.007; Fig. 3A and C).

Furthermore, all three doses of SHR-1819 resulted in a reduction at week 16 from baseline in the SCORAD index, BSA affected by AD, and DLQI score compared with placebo (Additional file 2: Table S1).

Safety

AEs were reported in 105 (87.5%) of 120 patients treated with SHR-1819 and 30 (81.1%) of 37 patients with placebo (Table 2). The majority were mild or moderate in severity (83.3% with SHR-1819 and 73.0% with placebo), while severe AEs were reported in 4.2% and 8.1% of the patients with SHR-1819 and placebo, respectively. Commonly reported AEs in patients receiving SHR-1819 included upper respiratory tract infection (22.5%), injection site reaction (10.8%), COVID-19 (9.2%), hyperlipidemia (9.2%), increased alanine aminotransferase (8.3%), and hyperuricemia (8.3%). Notably, the incidence and severity of AEs did not show an association with higher doses of SHR-1819.

Safety results

Data are n (%). Events are shown in descending order of frequency in the SHR-1819 total group

Treatment-related AEs (TRAEs) occurred in 55 (45.8%) patients in the SHR-1819 groups and 15 (40.5%) patients in the placebo group (Table 2). Injection site reaction (10.8%), increased blood bilirubin (5.8%), conjunctivitis (5.0%), increased blood uric acid (4.2%), and increased alanine aminotransferase (4.2%) were among the most commonly reported TRAEs in the SHR-1819 group. The majority of TRAEs with SHR-1819 treatment were mild in 40 (33.3%) patients and moderate in 14 (11.7%) patients, with only 1 (0.8%) patient experiencing severe TRAE. The incidence and severity of TRAEs were not correlated with the dosage of SHR-1819.

Treatment interruptions due to AEs were reported in 8 (6.7%) patients in the SHR-1819 group and 2 (5.4%) in the placebo group. Treatment discontinuations resulting from AEs were reported in 1 (0.8%) patient from the SHR-1819 group and 1 (2.7%) from the placebo group. Serious AEs were noted in 7 (5.8%) patients in the SHR-1819 group and 3 (8.1%) in the placebo group (Additional file 2: Table S2). Among these serious AEs, 2 (1.3%, dermatitis atopic) were considered treatment-related, with 1 (0.8%) in the SHR-1819 group and 1 (2.7%) in the placebo group. One patient from the SHR-1819 Q4W group died due to AE (brain herniation), and this event was not deemed treatment-related.

Conjunctivitis occurred in 7 patients (5.8%; all in the SHR-1819 group), including 6 treatment-related events (5.0%). Severity distribution comprised 4 mild and 3 moderate events. Complete resolution was observed in 6 patients (85.7%), and no treatment discontinuations were attributed to conjunctivitis. The majority of laboratory abnormalities were mild, with 7 patients of moderate abnormalities; no severe laboratory abnormalities were observed. All were managed without intervention and resolved or recovered completely.

PK

A total of 120 patients were included in the PK analysis. The serum concentration of SHR-1819 gradually increased over the treatment period. The serum drug concentration in the 600 mg Q2W group was higher than those in both the 300 mg Q2W and 600 mg Q4W groups (Additional file 2: Figure S2).

PD

PD analysis was conducted on all 157 patients. SHR-1819 produced a notable decrease in IgE levels across all doses. With multiple administration of SHR-1819, the median percentage decrease in IgE concentration from baseline increased steadily, and maintained until the end of the follow-up period (Additional file 2: Figure S3A). Similarly, the median percentage reduction from baseline in TARC/CCL17 reached a plateau in 2 weeks after SHR-1819 administration and was sustained until approximately 8 weeks after the last dose (Additional file 2: Figure S3B). The reduction from baseline in serum eotaxin-3 levels was evident in all SHR-1819 groups starting from week 2 of the treatment period, and this reduction was maintained during the first 4 weeks following the last dose (Additional file 2: Figure S3C). There was no clear indication of a dose-dependent reduction in IgE and eotaxin-3 levels across different doses, whereas 600 mg Q2W SHR-1819 demonstrated a slightly lower reduction in TARC/CCL17. Exposure-response modeling indicated no obvious correlation between baseline PD biomarkers and clinical endpoints.

Immunogenicity

ADA positivity was detected in 3 (7.7%), 2 (5.0%), and 4 (9.8%) patients following SHR-1819 at 300 mg Q2W, 600 mg Q2W, and 600 mg Q4W. There was no discernible relationship between the occurrence of ADA positivity and the SHR-1819 dose.

Discussion

This phase 2 study demonstrated that SHR-1819 is an effective treatment for moderate-to-severe AD. SHR-1819 produced promising improvements in EASI, IGA, PP-NRS, SCORAD index, BSA affected by AD, and DLQI compared with placebo, indicating efficacy in ameliorating disease severity and alleviating symptoms of AD. SHR-1819 was well-tolerated, and the PK and PD profiles were favorable.

Dupilumab, an anti-IL-4Rα monoclonal antibody, demonstrated promising improvements in EASI response in the SOLO 1 and SOLO 2 trials (EASI-75 rate: 51% and 44%; EASI-90 rate: 36% and 30%; and EASI-50 rate: 69% and 65%, at week 16) [19]. In our study, the proportion of patients achieving EASI-75 (69.2%-85.4%), EASI-90 (53.8%-63.4%), and EASI-50 (84.6%-97.6%) responses at week 16 was numerically higher in the SHR-1819 groups compared with placebo, with the 600 mg Q4W group showing the most substantial improvements. Despite cross-trial comparison should be interpreted with caution due to differences in study design and patient populations, our results preliminarily indicated that SHR-1819 could be a promising treatment choice for moderate-to-severe AD.

The substantial reduction observed in the PP-NRS score further highlights the beneficial effects of SHR-1819 on pruritus, a hallmark symptom of AD that markedly impacts the quality of life of patients. Moreover, reductions in other efficacy endpoints including IGA scores, SCORAD index, BSA affected by AD, and DLQI score underscore the comprehensive efficacy of SHR-1819 in improving multiple aspects of the disease burden.

The efficacy of SHR-1819 provides a promising new treatment option for patients with moderate-to-severe AD and offers flexibility in choosing either Q2W or Q4W dosing regimens. The ability to achieve substantial clinical improvement with a once-monthly dosing regimen may offer convenience for patients.

The safety profile of SHR-1819 was generally favorable and aligned with the biologic therapies targeting IL-4Rα [19, 30–34], with no new signals identified. The majority of AEs were mild to moderate in severity. Most serious AEs were unrelated to SHR-1819. The single case of death resulted from a car accident-induced brain herniation, ruling out death caused by central nervous system diseases or drug allergies.

Infection- and infestation-related AEs were all mild to moderate, resolving or improving with symptomatic treatment or without intervention. The most commonly reported AE was upper respiratory tract infection (22.5% with SHR-1819 and 10.8% with placebo), but only 1.7% in the SHR-1819 group and 5.4% in the placebo group were treatment-related. Conjunctivitis occurred only in the SHR-1819 group, affecting all dose groups without a clear dose-response pattern, consistent with the incidence of conjunctivitis reported with dupilumab targeting the same pathway [19]. The incidence of injection site reaction was higher in the SHR-1819 group (10.8%) compared with the placebo group (5.4%), consistent with that with dupilumab [34].

The drug exposure and PD profiles of SHR-1819 observed in our study align with the known mechanisms of action of IL-4Rα inhibitors [35], providing insights into its potential therapeutic effects. The serum concentration of SHR-1819 increased with dose escalation and remained stable with both Q2W and Q4W administration, supporting the potential for sustained therapeutic effects with both dosing regimens. The PD data showed reductions in IgE, TARC/CCL17, and eotaxin-3 levels, which are known to play key roles in the pathophysiology of AD [36], and their reduction potentially correlates with clinical improvement. Notably, the magnitude of these PD marker reductions was comparable between the 600 mg Q4W and 600 mg Q2W groups, which demonstrated a plateau in PD effect with the 600 mg Q4W regimen. The numerically higher EASI-75 rates observed in the 600 mg Q4W group compared with the 600 mg Q2W group likely reflect random variability related to the limited sample size of this phase 2 study rather than a meaningful difference in therapeutic effect. Additionally, the incidence of ADA positivity was low.

Despite the promising results, this study has several limitations. First, the duration of the trial (16 weeks) was relatively short, and longer-term studies are needed to confirm the sustained efficacy and safety profile of SHR-1819. Second, the study did not include direct comparisons with other biologics such as dupilumab, and head-to-head comparative studies with dupilumab would provide valuable insights into the relative benefits and risks of these two monoclonal antibodies. Future research should also focus on investigating the potential of SHR-1819 in combination with other therapies, such as topical treatments or other systemic agents, which could provide insights into optimizing treatment regimens for AD. Last, this study was conducted only in China; therefore, the generalizability of the findings to other ethnic and geographic populations requires confirmation in future global studies.

Conclusions

This phase 2 study demonstrates that SHR-1819 is an effective and well-tolerated treatment in Chinese patients with moderate-to-severe AD. The promising improvements in clinical outcomes, combined with favorable safety, PK, and PD profiles, position SHR-1819 as a promising new therapeutic option. Based on our findings, the 300 mg Q2W and 600 mg Q4W dosing regimens were recommended in subsequent phase 3 trials of SHR-1819. Further research is warranted to establish the role of this novel anti-IL-4Rα antibody in the management of AD.

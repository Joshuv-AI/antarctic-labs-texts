LINC00852 inhibits colorectal cancer progression by regulating cell apoptosis, epithelial‒mesenchymal transition, invasion, and cuproptosis through miR-1276/LACTB
Authors: Chun-Xiao Wang, Xiao-Jing Chen, Ling Zeng, Wang-Wu Liu, Jia-Sheng Ke, Yu-Ze Wu, Xian Deng, Yi Qiu, Ming-Liang Chen, Zhong-Shi Hong, Cheng-Zhi Qiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTExploring the role of key genes in colorectal cancer (CRC) progression to identify effective targets is highly practical. Previous studies have shown that miR-1276/ACTB plays an important regulatory role in CRC. In addition to miRNA, lncRNA also play a crucial role in tumour progression. However, there are currently no studies exploring the relationship between lncRNAs and miR-1276/LACTB. Analysis of the relationship between lncRNAs and miR-1276 using ENCORI database showed that lncRNA-LINC00852 bound to miR-1276 and inhibited its expression, thereby promoting LACTB expression, and miR-1276 upregulation weakened the ability of LINC00852 to promote LACTB expression. ENCORI and TCGA data also indicated that the expression of LINC00852 and LACTB in CRC was significantly downregulated, while miR-1276 expression was significantly upregulated. LINC00852 overexpression promoted E-cadherin expression, increased the activities of Caspase-3/8/9 and PARP, inhibited the expression of N-cadherin and Vimentin, and decreased the expression and secretion of MMP-2 and MMP-9. LINC00852 inhibited CRC cell viability, invasion and migration while promoting apoptosis. miR-1276 overexpression or LACTB knockdown significantly blocked the regulatory effect of LINC00852 on CRC cell biological function. Further xenograft mouse model confirmed that LINC00852 downregulated miR-1276 expression, enhanced the expression of LACTB and FDX1, increased tumour copper levels and inhibited tumour formation, indicating that the anticancer effect of LINC00852/LACTB may also be related to cuproptosis. However, LACTB knockdown blocked the inhibitory effect of LINC00852 on tumour formation. Therefore, LINC00852 inhibits CRC progression through miR-1276/LACTB, indicating that LINC00852/miR-1276/LACTB axis is an important pathway closely related to the occurrence and development of CRC.KEYWORDS: Colorectal cancer, lncRNA, LINC00852, miR-1276, LACTB, cuproptosis

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common malignant tumours, ranking third in incidence and second in tumour-related mortality among all malignant tumours, and its incidence and mortality have increased annually in China [1,2]. Therefore, actively conducting in-depth research on the pathogenesis of CRC is highly important for improving the diagnosis, treatment and prognosis of CRC.

The occurrence and development of CRC is a multistep, multistage disease involving multiple genes in which abnormal gene expression plays an important role [3]. For example, abnormally low lactamase beta (LACTB) expression plays an important regulatory role in malignant biological behaviours such as cell proliferation, invasion and distant metastasis in a variety of malignant tumours [4,5]. LACTB is a newly discovered tumour suppressor that inhibits epithelial‒mesenchymal transition (EMT) by inactivating the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway [6]. Our previous study revealed that the expression of LACTB was significantly reduced in CRC tissues and cells; screening of microRNAs (miRNAs) targeting LACTB showed that miR-1276 specifically downregulated the expression of LACTB; the expression of miR-1276 was significantly upregulated in CRC tissues and cells, and miR-1276 promoted the proliferation, migration, invasion and EMT of CRC cells, reduced autophagy and apoptosis, while LACTB upregulation blocked the procancer effect of miR-1276, indicating that the miR-1276/LACTB axis plays an important role in the progression of CRC [7]. Current studies have shown that miR-1276 is upregulated in a variety of tumours and has a cancer-promoting function [8,9]. However, until now, the upstream regulatory pathway of miR-1276/LACTB axis has not been studied.

In addition to miRNAs, long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), a category of noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs), also play important roles in various physiological and pathological processes [10]. One of the main functions of lncRNAs is to competitively bind miRNAs thereby regulating downstream target genes and prompting their effects [11]. Using ENCORI (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) database analysis of lncRNAs that can bind to miR-1276 revealed that approximately 100 lncRNAs can bind to miR-1276 but a total of 24 lncRNAs related to CRC have been reported and only LINC00852 and CKMT2-AS1 May have anticancer functions [12,13].

This study found that the overexpression of LINC00852 significantly inhibited the expression of miR-1276 in CRC cells while the inhibitory effect of CKMT2-AS1 was not significant, indicating that the role of LINC00852 in CRC is related to miR-1276. However, analysis of data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database revealed that the expression trend of LINC00852 in different tumours was inconsistent, indicating that the role of LINC00852 in different tumours may vary. Jin et al. [12] studied colon adenocarcinoma and reported that higher expression of LINC00852 was associated with longer survival. Therefore, LINC00852 May exert an antitumor effect through the miR-1276/LACTB axis in CRC, which is worthy of further study. In addition, cuproptosis is a newly discovered mode of cell death and an increasing number of studies indicate that cuproptosis plays an important role in tumour progression [14]. Recent studies have reported that cuproptosis plays a crucial role in the progression of CRC [15]. Therefore, this study also investigated the relationship between LINC00852/miR-1276/LACTB and cuproptosis in CRC.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and transfection

HCT116 and HCT-8 human CRC cell lines were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (Manassas, VA, USA) and cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, Rockville, MD, USA) supplemented with penicillin‒streptomycin (Gibco) and 10% foetal bovine serum (Gibco). A humidified incubator (37°C and a 5% CO2 atmosphere) was used to culture these cells. LINC00852 or CKMT2-AS1 overexpression plasmid (LINC00852 or CKMT2-AS1 full-length sequence was cloned into the pcDNA3.1 vector), miR-1276 mimics (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) and LACTB siRNA (table s1, RiboBio) were transfected or cotransfected into cells using the Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). If necessary, the cells were treated with cuproptosis inhibitor ammonium tetrathiomolybdate (TTM, 20 μM; MedChemExpress, China) for 24 h.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The wild-type or mutant LINC00852 fragment was cloned and inserted into the pmirGLO vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) to generate luciferase reporters. The cells were cotransfected with the wild-type or mutant luciferase reporter and the miR-1276 mimic or negative control (NC) miRNA using Lipofectamine 3000. After transfection for 48 h the relative luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega) and normalized to firefly luciferase activity.

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT‒qPCR)

Total RNA from CRC cells was extracted using a RNeasy mini kit (Qiagen, Valencia, CA, USA), and cDNA was prepared with reverse transcription PCR using a ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Kit (Toyobo, Japan). RT‒qPCR was performed using Power SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Invitrogen) on an ABI 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA, USA). The primers are listed in Table s1. The data were analysed using the 2−△△CT method and the fold changes in gene expression levels were normalized to those of the control.

Western blot analysis

CRC cells were harvested and lysed in RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and the resulting cell lysates were quantified with a BCA protein assay kit (Beyotime). All protein samples were separated using 5%, 10% or 15% SDS‒PAGE and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk for 1 h, the membrane was incubated with primary antibodies specific for LACTB (1:1000; CST, Beverly, MA, USA), E-cadherin (1:1000; CST), N-cadherin (1:1000; CST), vimentin (1:1000; CST), FDX1 (1:1000; Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA) or β-actin (1:1000; CST) overnight at 4°C. The membrane was subsequently incubated with a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (1:2000; CST) for 2 h, after which the bands were visualized with a BeyoECL star kit (Beyotime).

Cell viability assay

CRC cells (5 × 103) were seeded into each well of a 96-well plate, and the cells were cultured for 48 h after transfection. A cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8; Beyotime) was used to detect cell viability. To each well, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added, the mixture was incubated for 2 h, and the absorbance was measured at 450 nm. After the CRC cells with six cell concentration gradients were seeded and cultured for 4 h, CCK-8 was added for treatment and the optical density values were measured to draw the standard curve.

Cell apoptosis assay

After the shed and adherent cells were collected an annexin V-FITC apoptosis detection kit was used to detect cell apoptosis. A total of 1 × 104 cells were resuspended in 195 μL of annexin V-FITC binding buffer, and then 5 μL of annexin V-FITC and 5 μL of propidium iodide (PI) were added. After incubation for 20 min in the dark, cell apoptosis was analysed using flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA).

Detection of caspase and poly ADP‒ribose polymerase (PARP) activities

CRC cells were harvested and lysed on ice for 15 min with 100 μL of lysis solution, and the supernatant was collected. Caspase-3/8/9 and PARP activities were detected with a caspase 3/8/9 activity assay kit (Beyotime) and a PARP (cleaved) [214/215] human ELISA kit (Invitrogen). A 40 μL sample was incubated with 10 μL of substrate or 1× anti-rabbit IgG HRP solution, after which the absorbance at 405 or 450 nm was measured.

Invasion assay

A two-chamber transwell system (Corning, Corning, NY, USA) was used to evaluate CRC cell invasion. The upper side of the 24-well chamber was precoated with 2 mg/mL Matrigel (Millipore, Bedford, MA, USA) then 200 μL of serum-free cell suspension (1 × 105 cells/mL) was added. The lower compartment of the chamber was filled with 600 μL of complete medium. After incubation for 24 h the invading cells were stained with crystal violet and counted under a microscope.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The cell supernatants were collected and the levels of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-2 and MMP-9 were evaluated using a human MMP-2 ELISA kit (Beyotime) and a human MMP-9 ELISA kit (Beyotime) following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Wound healing assay

A monolayer of cells at 95% confluence was scratched to create a wound which was subsequently washed with phosphate-buffered saline and cultured. The wound width at 0, 24 and 48 h after wounding was measured to obtain the relative migration ability. The average rate of migration was determined by calculating an average of five wound distances for each sample.

Xenograft mouse models

Nude mice (18.0 ± 2.0 g) aged 4–6 weeks were purchased from SLAC Laboratory Animal Co. Ltd (Shanghai, China) and maintained in a specific pathogen-free environment. The mice were randomly divided into three groups (n = 6, determined based on the tumour formation rate and mortality of mice) and subcutaneously injected into the right flank with 5 × 106 control, LINC00852-overexpressing, or LINC00852-overexpressing plus LACTB-knockdown HCT116 cells (only worker providing injection cells was aware of the group allocation) resuspended in 100 µL phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). The health indexes and subcutaneous tumour volumes of mice were assessed every 4 d and calculated using the following formula: volume = 0.5 × length × width2. The occurrence of tumour >10% of animal body weight, or difficulty to move or breath was humane experimental endpoints, and no animals reached these humane endpoints. The mice were sacrificed 31 d after injection by CO2 euthanasia with flow rate of 30% volume/min, and death was confirmed by cardiac and respiratory arrest and dilated pupils. After that, the tumour xenografts were excised for further examination. All animal experiments were adhered to ARRIVE guidelines, and approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University (ethics approval number: 2021–145).

Measurement of copper ion levels

The copper levels in the tumour xenografts and CRC cells were measured using a copper assay kit (Sigma‒Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). First, 100 μL of homogenate supernatant was mixed with 35 μL of reagent A by vortexing, and then, this mixture was added to a 96-well plate. After that, 150 μL of master reaction mixture was added to each well, mixed well on a horizontal shaker, and incubated at room temperature in the dark for 5 min. The absorbance was measured at 359 nm.

Statistical analysis

The data are presented as means ± SD and analysed with GraphPad Prism 7.0 (San Diego, CA, USA). Differences between two groups were compared using Student’s t test and one-way ANOVAs followed by post hoc tests were used to compare multiple datasets. p values of <0.05 were considered significant.

Results

Expression of miR-1276, LACTB and LINC00852 in CRC

Analysis of the ENCORI database revealed that the expression of miR-1276 in CRC was significantly increased (Figure 1A, https://rnasysu.com/encori/panMirDiffExp.php#COAD), while the expression of LACTB was significantly decreased (Figure 1B, https://rnasysu.com/encori/panGeneDiffExp.php#COAD). Analysis of data from the TCGA database also found that the expression of LACTB was significantly reduced in CRC and gradually decreased with increasing stage and lymph node metastasis (Figure 1C–E, https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/cgi-bin/TCGAExResultNew2.pl?genenam=LACTB&ctype=COAD). In order to screen for lncRNAs related to miR-1276, this study used ENCORI database to analyse lncRNAs that could bind to miR-1276, and the results showed that approximately 100 lncRNAs could bind to miR-1276 (Table s2), but a total of 24 lncRNAs related to CRC have been reported and only LINC00852 and CKMT2-AS1 May have anticancer functions [12,13]. Meanwhile, data from the TCGA database also showed that the expression of LINC00852 was not consistent among different tumours (Figure 1F, https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/cgi-bin/Pan-cancer-lncRNA.pl?genenam=LINC00852), but was significantly downregulated in CRC (Figure 1G, https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/cgi-bin/TCGA-lncRNA-Result.pl?genenam=LINC00852&ctype=COAD).

The expression of miR-1276, LACTB and LINC00852 in CRC. (A-B) The expression of miR-1276 (A) and LACTB (B) in colon adenocarcinoma (COAD). All data were obtained from the ENCORI database. (C-E) The expression of LACTB in COAD (C), the relationship between LACTB expression and cancer stage (D) or nodal metastasis status (E). All data were obtained from the TCGA database. (G-F) The expression of LINC00852 in COAD (G) and other cancers (F). *p < 0.05 compared with the normal group.

LINC00852 inhibits the expression of miR-1276 in CRC cells

When LINC00852 or CKMT2-AS1 were overexpressed in CRC cells, LINC00852 significantly inhibited the expression of miR-1276, while the inhibitory effect of CKMT2-AS1 was not significant (Figure 2A,B), indicating that the role of LINC00852 in CRC is related to miR-1276. LINC00852 contains potential binding sites for miR-1276 (Figure 2C), and the luciferase activity did not change significantly after mutation of these sites (Figure 2D), indicating that miR-1276 can bind to LINC00852. The overexpression of LINC00852 in another CRC cell line also revealed that the expression of miR-1276 was significantly downregulated. When the cells were transfected with the LINC00852 overexpression vector and miR-1276 mimics, miR-1276 also inhibited the expression of LINC00852 (Figure 2E,F, s1A).

Regulatory relationship between LINC00852 and miR-1276 in colorectal cancer. (A-B) CRC cells were transfected with an empty vector (EV), CKMT2-AS1 overexpression vector (CKMT2-AS1-O) or LINC00852 overexpression vector (LINC00852-O), after which the expression of miR-1276 (A-B), CKMT2-AS1 (A) and LINC00852 was quantified using RT‒qPCR. (C) the binding and mutation sites of miR-1276 in the sequence of LINC00852. (D) The ability of miR-1276 to bind to LINC00852 was validated using a dual-luciferase reporter assay. (E-F) CRC cells were transfected with EV plus negative control (NC) miRNA and siRNA, LINC00852-O, LINC00852-O plus miR-1276, or LINC00852-O plus LACTB siRNA, and the expression of LINC00852 (E) and miR-1276 (F) was subsequently quantified using RT‒qPCR. *p < 0.05 compared with the control or mu, # p < 0.05 compared with LINC00852-O.

LINC00852 inhibits CRC cell EMT and viability through miR-1276/LACTB

LINC00852 significantly promoted the expression of LACTB but miR-1276 significantly weakened the ability of LINC00852 to promote LACTB expression (Figure 3A–D); miR-1276 inhibitors promoted the expression of LACTB, while LINC00852 siRNA weakened the expression-promoting effect of miR-1276 inhibitors (Figure s1B-E), indicating that LINC00852 regulates the expression of LACTB through miR-1276. LINC00852 increased the expression of E-cadherin while decreasing the expression of N-cadherin and vimentin. Both miR-1276 and LACTB siRNA blocked the role of LINC00852 in regulating the expression of E-cadherin, N-cadherin and vimentin (Figure 3A–E, s1F), indicating that LINC00852 inhibits the EMT process in CRC cells through miR-1276/LACTB. However, more experiments are needed to confirm EMT, which will be specifically explored in subsequent studies. In addition, LINC00852 inhibited the viability of CRC cells while miR-1276 and LACTB siRNA blocked the inhibitory effect of LINC00852 on cell viability (Figure 3F).

LINC00852 regulates CRC cell EMT and viability through miR-1276/LACTB. CRC cells were transfected with EV plus NC miRNA and siRNA, LINC00852-O, LINC00852-O plus miR-1276, or LINC00852-O plus LACTB siRNA, the mRNA (A-B) and protein (C-E) levels of LACTB, E-cadherin, N-cadherin and vimentin were detected using RT‒qPCR and western blotting, and cell viability was measured (F). *p < 0.05 compared with the control, # p < 0.05 compared with LINC00852-O.

LINC00852 promotes the apoptosis of CRC cells through miR-1276/LACTB

The overexpression of LINC00852 significantly promoted the apoptosis of CRC cells, and the overexpression of miR-1276 or the knockdown of LACTB significantly reduced the degree of apoptosis induced by LINC00852 (Figure 4A,B). Moreover, LINC00852 significantly increased the activities of Caspase-3, Caspase-8, Caspase-9 and PARP (Figure 4C–F), indicating that LINC00852 can induce apoptosis. When cotransfected with the LINC00852 overexpression vector and the miR-1276 mimics or LACTB siRNA the activities of Caspase-3, Caspase-8, Caspase-9 and PARP were significantly decreased (Figure 4C–F), indicating that LINC00852 induces the apoptosis of CRC cells through miR-1276/LACTB.

LINC00852 regulates CRC cell apoptosis through miR-1276/LACTB. CRC cells were transfected with EV plus NC miRNA and siRNA, LINC00852-O, LINC00852-O plus miR-1276, or LINC00852-O plus LACTB siRNA, cell apoptosis was detected using flow cytometry and statistically analysed (A-B). The activities of caspase-3/8/9 and PARP were detected using commercial reagent kits (C-F). *p < 0.05 compared with the control, # p < 0.05 compared with LINC00852-O.

LINC00852 attenuates the invasion and migration of CRC cells through miR-1276/LACTB

LINC00852 overexpression significantly inhibited the invasion of CRC cells while miR-1276 and LACTB knockdown restored the invasion of CRC cells (Figure 5A,B). MMP-2 and MMP-9 are the main enzymes that degrade the extracellular matrix, thus promoting tumour cell invasion and metastasis [16]. LINC00852 inhibited the expression and secretion of MMP-2 and MMP-9, while miR-1276 and LACTB siRNA weakened these inhibitory effects of LINC00852 (Figure 5C–F). Further examination of cell migration showed that LINC00852 significantly reduced the migration ability of CRC cells, while miR-1276 and LACTB siRNA restored the migration of CRC cells (Figure 6A–B). However, after transfection with LINC00852 siRNA, the biological function of CRC cells did not change significantly (data not shown). This may be due to the low expression of LINC00852 in CRC cells, resulting in the insignificant effect of LINC00852 knockdown on CRC cells.

CRC cells were transfected with EV plus NC miRNA and siRNA, LINC00852-O, LINC00852-O plus miR-1276, or LINC00852-O plus LACTB siRNA, cell invasion was detected using a Transwell assay and statistically analysed (A-B), the mRNA levels of MMP-2 and MMP-9 were detected using RT-qPCR (C, E), and the secretion of MMP-2 and MMP-9 in the cell supernatants was measured using ELISA (D, F). *p < 0.05 compared with the control.

LINC00852 regulates CRC cell migration through miR-1276/LACTB. CRC cells were transfected with EV plus NC miRNA and siRNA, LINC00852-O, LINC00852-O plus miR-1276, or LINC00852-O plus LACTB siRNA, cell migration was detected using a wound healing assay (A) and statistically analysed (B). *p < 0.05 compared with the control.

LINC00852 inhibits tumour formation in nude mice through LACTB and is associated with cuproptosis

In vivo experiments on tumour formation in nude mice showed that LINC00852 inhibited tumour formation, significantly reduced tumour volume and weight, and increased Caspase-3 activity (Figure 7A–D). However, after LACTB expression was knocked down, the inhibitory effect of LINC00852 on tumour formation was blocked (Figure 7A–C), indicating that LINC00852 inhibits tumour formation in nude mice through LACTB. The overexpression of LINC00852 significantly downregulated the expression of miR-1276 and enhanced the expression of LACTB (Figure 7E–I). However, knockdown of LACTB did not affect the expression of LINC00852 or miR-1276 (Figure 7E,F), indicating that LACTB is located downstream of LINC00852 and miR-1276. Moreover, LINC00852 promoted FDX1 expression and increased tumour copper levels, while LACTB knockdown blocked the increase in FDX1 expression and copper levels (Figure 7H,J–L). FDX1 is a key regulator of cuproptosis, a specific mode of death induced by copper ions [17]. And in vitro experiments also showed that LINC00852 upregulated the expression of FDX1 and increased intracellular copper levels, while both miR-1276 and LACTB siRNA could block the regulatory effects of LINC00852 (Figure s1G-J). In addition, the role of LINC00852 in inhibiting cell viability and increasing Caspase-3 activity could be blocked by cuproptosis inhibitor TTM (Figure s1K-L). Therefore, LINC00852 downregulated the expression of miR-1276 and enhanced the expression of LACTB and FDX1, indicating that the anticancer effect of LINC00852/LACTB may also be related to cuproptosis.

Discussion

Despite significant progress in the treatment of CRC the 5-year survival rate of CRC patients is less than 20% because of the broad spectrum of its targets and the drug resistance of tumour cells and the overall prognosis is still poor [3]. Therefore, the development of new, more sensitive and effective drugs and drug targets is urgently needed.

There is increasing evidence that ncRNAs, once thought to be transcriptional noise, play a crucial role in tumour progression and may be potential biomarkers for early diagnosis and therapeutic targets for human cancer [10]. This study found that the expression of lncRNA-LINC00852 was significantly downregulated in CRC and that its overexpression significantly inhibited CRC cell viability, EMT, invasion, migration and in vivo tumour formation and promoted cell apoptosis, indicating that LINC00852 plays an antitumor role in CRC. Jin et al. [12] reported that LINC00852 is a protective lncRNA in colon adenocarcinoma, consistent with the results of this study. However, many studies have shown that LINC00852 plays a procancer role in other tumours [18,19]. However, the TCGA data revealed that the expression of LINC00852 in different tumours was not consistent and was both upregulated and downregulated, indicating that LINC00852 May play different roles in different tumours. This study confirmed for the first time that LINC00852 plays a cancer-suppressing role in CRC.

Studies on the regulatory mechanisms of LINC00852 found that LINC00852 bound to miR-1276 and inhibited its expression while the antitumor effect of LINC00852 could be blocked by miR-1276, indicating that LINC00852 plays a role in CRC by regulating the expression of miR-1276. Our previous studies confirmed that miR-1276 is overexpressed in CRC and has a cancer-promoting function [7]. Zhang et al. [8] and Fassan et al. [20] reported that the expression of miR-1276 was significantly upregulated in laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma and Barrett’s adenocarcinoma. In HeLa and HepG2 cells, nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) downregulates the expression of miR-1276 and promotes the expression of caspase 9, thereby inducing cell apoptosis [9]. This indicates that miR-1276 does indeed play a procancer role in some tumours. However, some studies have shown that miR-1276 has anticancer effects [21,22]. Therefore, miR-1276 May also play different roles in different tumours. Although this study indicates that LINC00852 can inhibit the expression of miR-1276, the specific regulatory mechanism still needs to be studied and explored. LncRNA can affect miRNA transcription by directly binding to the miRNA promoter, or blocking or reducing the binding of transcription factors to miRNA promoters, and can also interfere with the production of mature miRNAs. Therefore, the specific mechanism by which LINC00852 regulates the expression of miR-1276 still needs to be verified through subsequent experiments.

LINC00852 promoted the expression of LACTB in CRC cells but this regulatory effect was blocked by miR-1276, indicating that LINC00852 plays a role in CRC through miR-1276/LACTB: the antitumor effect of LINC00852 May also be blocked by LACTB siRNA. Therefore, LINC00852/miR-1276/LACTB is an important pathway associated with CRC. Jiang et al. [23] also reported that LACTB was downregulated in CRC tissues and cells and that it could inhibit cell proliferation, EMT, invasion and migration by promoting E-cadherin expression and reducing MMP-2 and N-cadherin expression. Zeng et al. [24] found that upregulation of LACTB expression suppressed cell proliferation, invasion, migration and EMT in vitro and inhibited CRC metastasis and growth in vivo by enhancing the stability of p53. Moreover, several studies have shown that the expression of LACTB is reduced in different tumours and that it has a tumour suppressive effect [25]. For example, overexpression of LACTB induced DNA and mitochondria damages, thereby promoting gastric cancer cell apoptosis [26]. But there are currently no reports on the anticancer effect of LINC00852 by inducing apoptosis. Thus, LACTB is a key tumour suppressor gene that plays a role in the progression of multiple tumours and LINC00852 plays a role in CRC via miR-1276/LACTB.

Cuproptosis is a newly discovered form of programmed cell death caused by excess Cu2+ that differs from apoptosis, pyroptosis, necrosis, ferroptosis, and other metal ion-related death pathways after ferroptosis [17]. FDX1 is a key enzyme in the cuproptosis process and has a strong reducing ability to reduce Cu2+ to Cu+ [17]. This study found that LINC00852 promoted the expression of FDX1 and increased copper levels in tumour tissues and cells, while LACTB knockdown blocked the increasing trends of FDX1 expression and copper levels. This indicates that the anticancer effect of LINC00852/LACTB may also be closely related to cuproptosis, but this still needs to be confirmed. Yang et al. [27] reported that pulse treatment with a copper ionophore elesclomol plus copper inhibited CRC cell viability and that 4-octyl itaconate (4-OI) weakened aerobic colysis by inhibiting the activity of glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase thereby increasing sensitivity to cuproptosis, while knocking down FDX1 weakened the ability of 4-OI to promote cuproptosis. The expression of FDX1 is downregulated in CRC and its high expression is positively correlated with survival. FDX1 overexpression inhibits cell viability, EMT, migration and invasion and relieves tumour growth and metastasis in vivo [27–29]. Therefore, FDX1-regulated cuproptosis is highly important in the progression of CRC. In addition, cuproptosis inhibitor TTM not only blocked the inhibitory effect of LINC00852 on cell viability, but also weakened LINC00852-induced apoptosis (reduced Caspase-3 activity), indicating that LINC00852 induced-cuproptosis can affect the apoptosis process. Chen et al. [30] also found that inducing cuproptosis promoted uveal melanoma cell apoptosis. Therefore, the occurrence of cuproptosis is inherently related to apoptosis, but the specific mechanism still needs further experimental exploration.

In summary, the expression of LINC00852 was downregulated in CRC and it exerted a cancer-inhibiting effect through miR-1276/LACTB, thereby inhibiting EMT, viability, invasion and migration of CRC cells, promoting cell apoptosis and affecting cuproptosis process (Figure s2). Therefore, LINC00852/miR-1276/LACTB pathway may be an effective target for the diagnosis and treatment of CRC or other tumours.

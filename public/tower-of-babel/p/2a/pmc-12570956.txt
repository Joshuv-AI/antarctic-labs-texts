Factors influencing physician-to-physician teleconsultation: a scoping review
Authors: Samane Ghasemi, Tahereh Changiz, Athar Omid
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTeleconsultation has gained significant traction due to advancements in information and communication technologies. While much attention has been given to physician-to-patient teleconsultation, the factors influencing physician-to-physician teleconsultation remain underexplored.ObjectivesThis scoping review aims to map and synthesise the existing evidence on the factors influencing physician-to-physician teleconsultation.Eligibility criteriaWe included publications of all methodological designs that specifically addressed factors affecting physician-to-physician teleconsultation. Studies focusing primarily on physician-to-patient teleconsultation without sufficient detail on physician-to-physician components were excluded. The search was limited to articles published in English and Persian between 2014 and 2024.Sources of evidenceEight electronic databases (PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, etc) were searched from January 2014 to June 2024.Charting methodsData extraction was performed by two independent reviewers using a standardised form. Extracted data included study characteristics, key factors influencing teleconsultation and main findings.ResultsFrom 12 included studies, five key influencing components were identified: ‘patient-related factors’, ‘medical team competencies’, ‘infrastructure and technology’, ‘timing factors’ and ‘planning and programme evaluation’. Among these, infrastructure and technology were the most frequently reported factors across the studies, while patient-related factors were less commonly addressed.ConclusionsThis review identifies a comprehensive set of factors that influence physician-to-physician teleconsultation. The findings provide a foundation for developing effective teleconsultation programmes and highlight the need for more research in diverse healthcare settings.

Introduction

In recent years, advancements in information and communication technologies (ICTs) have revolutionised healthcare delivery. In this regard, the use of technology in teleconsultation has become common.1 Teleconsultation is defined as simultaneous or asynchronous counselling using ICT to eliminate geographical and functional distance,2 which can be provided through various channels such as telephone, video conference systems, instant messengers and mobile phone applications.3 Teleconsultation has grown significantly due to its potential to reduce costs, improve access to care and facilitate rapid information exchange. This service has advantages such as ease of use and access to patient information quickly.4 During the COVID-19 pandemic, the adoption of teleconsultation accelerated, with many healthcare systems transitioning from face-to-face consultations to remote models.5

In teleconsultation, there are several types of communication: physician-to-physician communication, healthcare professional-to-healthcare professional communication, physician-to-patient communication and three-way communication between physician, healthcare professional and patient.6 Physician-to-physician teleconsultation plays a fundamental role in providing optimal healthcare services by enabling collaborative decision-making, knowledge sharing and access to specialist expertise, particularly for complex cases or in settings where resources are limited.7 8 For example, a general practitioner in a rural area might consult remotely with a specialist in a tertiary care centre to determine the best course of treatment for a patient. Effective communication between consulting physicians includes the exchange of relevant clinical information as well as the patient’s preferences and values.9 While teleconsultation between physicians has improved diagnostic accuracy in complex cases, barriers such as inadequate infrastructure and a lack of standardised protocols have hindered its widespread adoption.10

Deldar et al showed in a systematic review that <10% of teleconsultation studies focus on physician-to-physician teleconsultation.11 While many original research studies have predominantly focused on the dynamics of physician-to-patient teleconsultation, examining barriers, facilitators and patient experiences,1215 these studies have highlighted the role of patient satisfaction, technological accessibility and legal considerations in shaping the success of remote consultations between doctors and patients.

In contrast, physician-to-physician teleconsultation, which plays a critical role in collaborative care and decision-making, has received limited attention. This gap is particularly concerning given the increasing reliance on multidisciplinary teams and the need for seamless communication among healthcare providers.2 16 17 In the search conducted on physician-to-physician consultation, only one review study was found.18 This review study has addressed various aspects of teleconsultation, such as benefits, cost-effectiveness and improvement of physician-to-physician communication. However, a comprehensive synthesis of the specific components that constitute and influence this process is lacking. Given the increasing growth of technology and teleconsultation and the importance of factors affecting correct diagnosis and treatment, a scoping review is necessary to map the existing literature and identify key components affecting remote physician-to-physician consultation. Therefore, this study addresses this gap by systematically exploring the factors influencing physician-to-physician teleconsultation. The results of this study can inform medical education to prepare more competent physicians for teleconsultation.

Defining the research question

The research question was defined through an iterative process of discussion within the research team. The primary objective of this scoping review was to identify and map the reported components and factors influencing physician-to-physician teleconsultation, as described in the existing literature.

Search strategy and relevant studies

A comprehensive search strategy was developed in collaboration with an experienced medical information specialist. To capture the broadest possible range of literature, the strategy used a combination of controlled vocabulary (eg, MeSH terms) and a wide range of free-text keywords related to the core concepts of ‘physician’ and ‘teleconsultation’. The strategy was pilot-tested and refined iteratively to optimise sensitivity and specificity.

Systematic searches were conducted across eight electronic databases—MEDLINE (via PubMed), Web of Science, ERIC, Scopus, ScienceDirect, Irandoc, SID and Magiran—to ensure broad coverage of both international and regional literature. The initial search covered the period from 1 January 2014 to 8 April 2024 to capture literature from the last decade, a period defined by significant advancements in telecommunication technology and the pivotal impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on telehealth adoption. To ensure currency, the search was updated on 27 September 2024. All retrieved records were imported into EndNote V.X20 for duplicate removal. The full, detailed search strategy for all databases is available in the Supplementary File (online supplemental appendix 2).

Results

Overview of included studies

The systematic search of electronic databases initially retrieved 2665 records. After removing duplicates, 1312 unique records remained for the title and abstract screening phase. Following this screening, 323 records were deemed eligible for full-text review. After careful review of the full text, 12 studies were included for final data extraction and analysis. The study selection process, including reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage, is detailed in the PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (figure 1).

The characteristics of each included study are summarised in the main manuscript, providing details on authors, year, country, study design, participant characteristics and key findings.

Geographic distribution

The 12 included studies were conducted across eight different countries, primarily in high-income settings. The majority originated from North America and Europe. Specifically, four studies were conducted in the USA,2325 with an additional US-Ukraine collaboration,16 three studies were from Canada,18 26 27 and one was from Spain.15 Only two studies were from Asia (Japan,28 Thailand2) and two were from African nations (South Africa,29 Ethiopia30). This distribution highlights a significant geographical gap in the literature, with a strong bias towards high-resource settings.

Effective components of physician-to-physician teleconsultation

The analysis of the included studies indicates that physician-to-physician teleconsultation is influenced by five key components, which are detailed below.

Patient-related factors

Patient-related factors were reported in one study.25 This study highlighted that rapid access to a patient’s medical history, test results and imaging via e-consultation systems facilitated triage and referral decisions, which influenced the choice to use teleconsultation. The study also suggested that particular attention should be given to older adults and patients with poor mental health to ensure equitable and effective care.

Medical team competencies

Factors related to medical team competencies were identified in four studies.2 23 26 30 Effective teleconsultation relies heavily on the expertise and collaborative ability of the medical team. Poor communication and unclear role boundaries can result in diagnostic errors. Two studies2 30 emphasised the need for technology literacy, noting that physicians must be proficient in digital platforms to ensure seamless collaboration. Clinical competence and access to specialised expertise are also essential to support accurate decision-making. Two studies23 26 highlighted communication and collaboration skills as critical, noting that structured exchanges between physicians help reduce misunderstandings and improve diagnostic accuracy. Furthermore, one study30 discussed how external contextual factors such as public awareness, cultural attitudes and healthcare policies affect technology acceptance, noting that in some regions, resistance to virtual care stems from a preference for face-to-face interaction and distrust in technology.

Infrastructure and technology

Infrastructure and technology were discussed in seven studies.2 16 23 24 27 28 30 The availability of robust infrastructure and technological support emerged as one of the most frequently reported themes. One study16 identified secure web applications, telehealth platforms and image-sharing systems as fundamental to effective consultation. Two studies23 28 found proper medical information management, facilitated by well-designed applications, to be critical for reliable communication. Reliable internet access was highlighted as a precondition in one study,30 which noted deficiencies in ICT infrastructure as a major barrier. Two studies2 24 reported that hardware limitations negatively impact data entry and image interpretation, while improved design can reduce errors. Finally, one study27 identified organisational factors and concerns regarding information security as key barriers.

Timing factors

Timing factors were identified as influential in three studies.15 25 29 One study15 identified time as a critical determinant of effectiveness, particularly in emergencies. A study29 reported that delays or inappropriate referrals waste resources and lead to suboptimal care. One study25 demonstrated the potential for timeliness, showing non-urgent ophthalmology e-consults were addressed with an average response time of 1.6 days.

Planning and programme evaluation

Planning and programme evaluation were addressed in four studies.2 15 18 26 Two studies2 18 indicated that standardised protocols help ensure consistency and efficiency. A study18 revealed broader benefits such as reduced hospital admissions and cost savings. Another study15 showed that e-consultations reduced waiting times and improved access to care. Furthermore, one study26 reported that consultations served as valuable educational tools, with 80% of physicians learning new strategies. These findings highlight the importance of systematic evaluation.

Discussion

This scoping review systematically mapped the current evidence base on physician-to-physician teleconsultation over the past decade (2014–2024). Our analysis, synthesising evidence from 12 included studies, identified five recurring components that appear to influence implementation. The findings suggest that ‘infrastructure and technology’ is the most frequently discussed factor, mentioned in seven studies.2 16 23 24 27 28 30 Other components—‘medical team competencies’ (four studies),2 23 26 30 ‘planning and programme evaluation’ (four studies),2 15 18 26 ‘timing factors’ (three studies)15 25 29 and ‘patient-related factors’ (one study)25—were reported by a smaller number of studies, indicating that the evidence for these elements is less established.

The results of this review showed that the existing literature in this area is relatively limited and fragmented. The geographic focus of studies was predominantly in high-income countries in Europe and North America, likely reflecting the digital divide and inequalities in access to information technology infrastructure globally.31 As expected, the COVID-19 pandemic acted as a powerful catalyst, leading to a significant increase in the number of studies in 2020 and 2021. This demonstrates the critical role of teleconsultation in maintaining the continuity of healthcare delivery during times of social distancing and crisis.32

The findings indicate that patient-related factors, including clinical conditions and access to patient information, were identified in a single study as a challenge to the adoption of teleconsultation.25 That study noted that integrating electronic health records with telehealth platforms could enhance clinicians’ data accessibility.33 Furthermore, some studies explored alternative solutions, such as hybrid models combining teleconsultation with in-person visits, for managing complex cases.34

Physician competency and team coordination were discussed as important elements in four of the included studies. These studies emphasised the need for training physicians in telehealth-specific communication and technical skills.35 Cultural attitudes and regional disparities in technological readiness were also reported in the literature as factors influencing adoption.36 Some studies suggested that interdisciplinary collaboration through shared platforms could improve coordination.37

Infrastructure and technology emerged as the most commonly reported factor, highlighted in seven studies as a fundamental element influencing teleconsultation’s reach and efficiency. Studies demonstrated that unequal access to reliable platforms, particularly in rural or underserved regions, is a major barrier. The development of intuitive and interoperable equipment was also noted as a way to mitigate documentation-related challenges.38

Although teleconsultation promises time efficiency, studies found that poor time management practices can offset this benefit. Implementing streamlined workflows, such as pre-consultation checklists, was shown to enhance productivity.39 Additionally, the lack of harmonised legal frameworks across different regions was identified as a barrier to cross-border teleconsultation.40

The lack of standardised protocols and performance monitoring mechanisms was also reported as a challenge to scalability.41 Studies indicated that developing comprehensive guidelines addressing clinical, legal and operational aspects is essential. Furthermore, barriers to adoption, such as resistance from healthcare professionals or inadequate reimbursement models, were discussed in several studies.42

Overall, the included studies suggested that physician-to-physician teleconsultation programmes can facilitate communication and improve access to specialised care.43 The potential to lower healthcare costs by minimising unnecessary procedures and investigations was also mentioned in the literature.44 For instance, Winchester et al demonstrated a significant reduction in wait times for initial appointments.45

However, the findings of this review also indicate that physicians in the included studies expressed dissatisfaction with the current consultation/referral process and the exchange of unstructured, low-quality data. Numerous barriers to implementing this method were reported, with the lack of digital infrastructure and financial constraints being more prominent in low-resource settings. The absence of standardised protocols was also found to lead to inconsistencies in practice.

Implications for future research

The findings of this scoping review have highlighted several critical gaps in the current evidence base, revealing clear and urgent priorities for future research:

Research in underserved settings: given the pronounced geographic disparity and the concentration of studies in high-income countries, there is an urgent need for primary research on the implementation, adoption and adaptation of physician-to-physician teleconsultation in low-income and middle-income countries and other resource-limited settings.

Research in underserved settings: given the pronounced geographic disparity and the concentration of studies in high-income countries, there is an urgent need for primary research on the implementation, adoption and adaptation of physician-to-physician teleconsultation in low-income and middle-income countries and other resource-limited settings.

Interaction of components: the interconnected nature of the five key components identified (eg, how technology platforms influence team coordination and timing) remains underexplored. Future studies should employ methodologies (eg, qualitative studies, case studies, systems analyses) specifically designed to investigate how these factors dynamically interact in real-world clinical contexts.

Strengths and limitations

This scoping review adhered to the Arksey and O’Malley methodological framework, ensuring a systematic and transparent process. A comprehensive search strategy was executed across multiple databases to capture relevant literature, and broad inclusion criteria enabled the mapping of diverse evidence on physician-to-physician teleconsultation components. Furthermore, the review team included two physicians whose clinical insights helped develop the research question, as well as a physician peer review that guided the study design. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. The review used an internal protocol that was not publicly registered, which may limit the transparency and cross-referencing of the planned vs conducted methods. No patients or members of the public were involved in the design or conduct of the review, which is a limitation, as their perspectives on teleconsultation could provide valuable insights. The geographical concentration of included studies in high-income countries may limit the generalisability of findings to low-resource settings. Methodological heterogeneity across studies complicated comparative analysis. The search strategy’s focus on specific medical specialities may have constrained the scope of retrieved evidence. Furthermore, the exclusion of non-English and non-Persian publications potentially omitted relevant studies from non-English-speaking regions. Finally, the rapidly evolving nature of teleconsultation technology means this review may not capture the most recent advancements in the field.

Conclusion

This scoping review systematically examines the current evidence on physician-to-physician teleconsultation and identifies key factors influencing its adoption and implementation in healthcare systems. The COVID-19 pandemic significantly accelerated research in this field, highlighting teleconsultation’s crucial role in maintaining healthcare services during global health crises. However, the available evidence remains limited and geographically concentrated in high-income countries, indicating substantial gaps in understanding teleconsultation implementation across diverse healthcare settings. Future research should address these gaps by focusing on low-resource settings, investigating component interactions and employing rigorous methodological approaches. This review provides a foundational framework for understanding physician-to-physician teleconsultation implementation while emphasising the need for more comprehensive studies to optimise this specific form of teleconsultation as an effective healthcare delivery tool.

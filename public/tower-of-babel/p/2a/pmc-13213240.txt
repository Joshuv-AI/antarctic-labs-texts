Ultra-Processed Food Consumption Is Associated With an Unfavorable Nutrient Profile and a Higher Likelihood of Hypertension Among Women Living in Brazilian Favelas
Authors: Laysa de Almeida Espíndola Cardoso de Barros, Terezinha da Rocha Ataide, Thays Lane Ferreira dos Santos, Telma Maria de Menezes Toledo Florêncio, Ana Paula Grotti Clemente, João Eudes dos Santos Neto, Edward Araujo Júnior, Lucas Almeida das Chagas, Luiz Gonzaga Ribeiro Silva-Neto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe objective of this study was to examine the associations between ultra-processed foods (UPF) consumption, systemic arterial hypertension (SAH), and cardiovascular risk-related nutrient intake among women living in favelas.MethodsThis cross-sectional study was conducted with 1,702 adult women of reproductive age (20–44 years) residing in 40 favelas in a capital city in Northeast Brazil. The presence of SAH was determined based on a previous medical diagnosis. Food consumption was assessed using a 24-hour dietary recall. Logistic regression models were applied to evaluate the associations.ResultsSAH was present in 11.5% of the participants. A total of 33.8% of total caloric intake was derived from UPF, with mean sodium and potassium intakes of 1,747.9 mg and 1,507.6 mg, respectively. Women with SAH had a 14% higher odds of consuming greater amounts of UPF. Higher UPF consumption was characterized by increased intakes of cholesterol, sodium, and potassium, with each 100 mg increment associated with 15%, 37%, and 33% higher odds of higher UPF consumption, respectively. In contrast, increases of 10% in protein, polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA), and monounsaturated fatty acid intake were associated with reductions of 23%, 10%, and 9%, respectively, in the odds of higher UPF consumption.ConclusionHigher UPF consumption was associated with an unfavorable nutrient profile, characterized by increased intakes of cholesterol, sodium, and potassium and decreased intakes of protein, and monounsaturated and PUFAs, reflecting a dietary pattern linked to increased cardiovascular risk in socioeconomically vulnerable populations.

INTRODUCTION

The increasing consumption of ultra-processed foods (UPF) in Brazil is closely associated with the progressive reduction in the prices of these products, which has made them increasingly affordable over time.1 Given the substantial influence of household income on food choices, periods of income reduction lead families to prioritize lower-cost foods to ensure sufficient quantity, often without regard for nutritional quality. In more severe economic circumstances, reductions occur not only in food quality but also in the quantity and frequency of consumption. These dynamics indicate that food insecurity may persist even in the absence of overt hunger.2

Consequently, the greater incorporation of UPF into the diets of lower-income populations has contributed to several nutritional imbalances, including excessive sodium intake, which promotes elevated blood pressure levels and, in turn, the development of systemic arterial hypertension (SAH). SAH is considered the most significant risk factor for cardiovascular disease.345

From this perspective, non-pharmacological treatment strategies related to lifestyle modification, particularly dietary interventions, become essential.6 The Brazilian dietary pattern has been characterized by a high consumption of energy-dense foods containing excessive amounts of sugar, sodium, total fats, saturated fats, and trans fats, while being deficient or entirely lacking in fiber and potassium. This pattern closely corresponds to the nutritional profile of UPF.457

Sodium and potassium are nutrients with antagonistic effects on blood pressure regulation. When consumed in imbalanced proportions, inadequate intake of these minerals represents an important contributing factor to the development of cardiovascular diseases, including SAH.89 It has been well established that high sodium consumption is largely attributable to both the direct addition of salt during food preparation and its substantial presence in processed and UPFs.10 Potassium intake is primarily derived from fruits, vegetables, dairy products, and animal protein sources; however, it has also been noted that UPF may contain elevated potassium levels due to the use of potassium-based additives.11

There is a positive association between UPF consumption and a wide range of adverse health outcomes. These include an increased risk of cardiovascular diseases such as coronary and cerebrovascular conditions, obesity, gestational obesity, SAH, metabolic syndrome, depression, irritable bowel syndrome, several types of cancer, particularly postmenopausal breast cancer, asthma, frailty among older adults, and an elevated risk of all-cause mortality.12

Considering the effects of UPF on the health of socially vulnerable populations and their substantial contribution to shifts in dietary consumption patterns, the objective of the present study was to evaluate the presence of SAH and the intake of nutrients predictive of cardiovascular diseases, and to determine their association with UPF consumption among women living in favelas located in a capital city in Northeast Brazil.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Study design and study site

A cross-sectional, population-based study was conducted between October 2020 and May 2021 in favelas and urban communities of Maceió, the capital city of the state of Alagoas, located in Northeast Brazil. According to the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics,13 these areas are characterized as places predominantly composed of households with varying degrees of legal insecurity of tenure and that meet at least 1 of the following criteria: absence or incomplete provision of public services; predominance of buildings, urban layouts, and general infrastructure that are self-produced or guided by urban planning and construction standards distinct from those established by public authorities; and location in areas subject to occupation restrictions defined by environmental or urban legislation. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Federal University of Alagoas (UFAL) (CAAE: 57829016.9.1001.5013), and all participants provided written informed consent prior to participation.

2. Sample size and study site selection

Considering the estimate that 24,614 adult women of reproductive age (20–44 years) resided in the 94 favelas of Maceió, along with the estimated caloric contribution of UPF to the diet of Brazilian women (20.3%),1 a margin of error of 2%, and a confidence level of 95%, the minimum required sample size was calculated to be 1,462 women. Sample size estimation was performed using the StatCalc program, version 7.2.2.2 (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention).

Of the women recruited and evaluated, 1,702 were included in the final analysis of this study. The recruitment process and exclusion criteria are detailed in Fig. 1. Participants were recruited from 40 favelas in Maceió, and the detailed selection process of these locations is presented in Fig. 2.

The sampling design was probabilistic and employed a 3-stage cluster approach: 1) favelas and urban communities were selected through simple random sampling, proportionally distributed across each of the 7 administrative regions included in the study; 2) census tracts, with 1 tract randomly selected from each favela or urban community; and 3) streets, in which 1 street within each selected census tract was randomly chosen as the starting point for data collection.

All households located on the selected street were visited, and when necessary, neighboring streets were also visited until the required sample size for the area was achieved. All households in which at least 1 woman aged 20 to 44 years resided were considered eligible. In households with more than 1 woman within the eligible age range, the woman who had a child under 5 years of age was selected for participation. Data were collected from only 1 woman per household.

Women with any disability that could compromise food consumption or with disabilities that interfered with participation in the interview or comprehension of the research instruments were excluded from the study.

3. Sociodemographic and health data

The following sociodemographic and health-related variables were collected: age (years), educational attainment (years of schooling), skin color/race (White, Black, Brown, Indigenous, or East Asian. Subsequently categorized as Black/Brown and others), marital status (single or married), and previous medical diagnosis of SAH (yes or no). Monthly per capita family income was also obtained to characterize the socioeconomic profile of the population and was used to classify participants according to poverty status (poverty: <US$91.90; non-poverty: ≥US$91.90). Income values were converted from Brazilian reais to US dollars based on the average exchange rate between October 2020 and May 2021 (R$ 5.43).14

4. Anthropometric assessment

Anthropometric assessment was performed through the measurement of body weight and height using a digital scale (Avanutri) and a portable stadiometer (Avanutri), following the standardized protocol recommended by the Brazilian Ministry of Health. Body mass index (BMI) was subsequently calculated, and participants were classified as having non-obesity (BMI <30 kg/m2) or obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m2).

5. Assessment of food consumption

A household interview was conducted with each woman to complete a 24-hour dietary recall (24DR), which referred to food intake on a single day of the week. In a subsample comprising 20% of the evaluated population, the 24DR was administered on 2 additional occasions, within a period of up to 60 days after the initial interview, using telephone contact. This approach aimed to assess intraindividual variability in caloric and nutrient intake and to correct dietary intake estimates. Among the 3 24DR administered in this subsample, 2 corresponded to weekdays and 1 to a weekend day. The 24DR was applied using the multiple pass method.15 During the interview, whenever the participant reported consumption of a mixed dish, she was asked to detail all individual ingredients used in its preparation.

Dietary intake data obtained from the 24DR were analyzed using the nutritional assessment and prescription software Avanutri 4.0. To derive nutrient values, 3 food composition tables available within Avanutri 4.0 were used, following a predefined priority order: the Brazilian Table of Food Composition,16 the Food Composition Table,17 and the Nutritional Composition Tables of Foods Consumed in Brazil.18 After verification and consistency checks of dietary data, all reported foods were classified according to their degree of processing to identify UPF. This classification was based on the NOVA food classification system.19

6. Evaluation of the caloric contribution of UPF in the diet

To evaluate the caloric contribution of UPF, total daily energy intake and energy intake derived specifically from UPF were calculated for each participant. These values were used to determine the percentage contribution of UPF to the total caloric intake of each woman. For descriptive analyses, UPF consumption was treated as a continuous variable. However, for association analyses, UPF consumption was categorized based on the median value, defining lower consumption (below the median) and higher consumption (above the median).

7. Nutrients predictive of cardiovascular disease

For this evaluation, the intake of carbohydrates (%), proteins (%), total lipids (%), polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) (%), monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFAs) (%), saturated fatty acids (%), cholesterol (mg), fiber (g), sodium (mg), and potassium (mg) was considered. All nutrient values were expressed as continuous variables. The selection of these dietary constituents was based on their established associations with chronic non-communicable diseases.202122

8. Intraindividual variability assessment

After evaluating the contribution of UPF to the diet, together with the intake of nutrients predictive of cardiovascular diseases, the usual intake of UPF and the selected nutrients in each woman’s diet was estimated using the multiple source method.23

9. Standardization of food components

To incorporate variables related to food consumption characteristics into the models, standardization procedures were applied to avoid implausible interpretations associated with very small increments in the original measurement units. This approach also facilitated comparability across coefficients and reduced the artificial inflation of odds ratio (OR) magnitudes.

The values for carbohydrates, proteins, total lipids, PUFAs, MUFAs, and saturated fatty acids were transformed by dividing the original values by 10%, such that each unit in the model corresponded to a 10% increase. Total caloric intake values were similarly transformed by dividing by 100 kcal, so that each unit represented an increment of 100 kcal.

Cholesterol, sodium, and potassium intake values were also standardized by dividing the original values by 100 mg, such that each unit in the model represented an increase of 100 mg. The fiber variable was retained in grams, as it was already expressed on an appropriate analytical scale.

10. Statistical analyses

The data were double-entered. Statistical analyses were conducted using Jamovi software (version 2.3; The jamovi project). For descriptive analyses, continuous variables were summarized as means and standard deviations (SDs), while categorical variables were presented as absolute and relative frequencies. Normality assumptions were assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Multicollinearity was evaluated using the variance inflation factor, with values below 5 considered indicative of the absence of multicollinearity.

For association analyses, logistic regression models were applied to estimate ORs and their respective 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) for the association between SAH diagnosis and standardized intake of nutrients predictive of cardiovascular diseases, including carbohydrates (%), proteins (%), total lipids (%), PUFA (%), MUFA (%), saturated fatty acids (%), cholesterol (mg), fiber (g), sodium (mg), and potassium (mg), as well as UPF consumption categorized at the median. The models were adjusted for age, skin color/race, years of schooling, marital status, obesity status, caloric intake, and poverty status. A significance level of 5% was adopted for all analyses. To assess the robustness of findings in the context of multiple comparisons, a sensitivity analysis using the Bonferroni correction was performed, accounting for the number of exposures evaluated. Accordingly, the overall significance level (α=0.05) was divided by the number of comparisons, resulting in a corrected significance threshold of p<0.0045 (0.05/11).

An additional sensitivity analysis was conducted following the same assumptions described above. Detailed information on model specification and the corresponding analytical results is provided in the Supplementary Table 1.

The robustness of these findings was further evaluated using sensitivity analyses based on linear regression models (Supplementary Table 1). The results remained consistent in both magnitude and direction, indicating that the presence of SAH, as well as higher standardized intakes of cholesterol, sodium, and potassium, were associated with higher UPF consumption. Conversely, higher standardized intakes of protein, PUFA, and MUFA were associated with lower UPF consumption. However, these associations did not demonstrate the same level of consistency after application of the Bonferroni correction.

DISCUSSION

In the present study, it was observed that the women evaluated consumed a high proportion of UPF compared with the overall population of Brazilian women.24 Within this context, women with higher UPF consumption had a 14% greater odds of having SAH and reported higher intakes of cholesterol, sodium, and potassium, along with lower consumption of protein, PUFA, and MUFA.

The caloric contribution from UPF corresponded to 33.8%, a proportion substantially higher than that reported in the 2017–2018 National Household Budget Survey (19.7%), a nationwide study conducted in Brazil to assess population living conditions.4 A similar trend has been observed in other Latin American countries, where a 9.2% increase in UPF commercialization was documented between 2014 and 2019.25 However, high UPF consumption is also evident in more developed countries, such as the United States and Canada, where approximately 57.0% and 54.9% of total caloric intake, respectively, is derived from UPF.2627 These findings indicate that current dietary consumption patterns warrant attention regardless of a country’s level of wealth or development.

The higher UPF consumption observed in the population evaluated in this study may be explained by greater exposure to these products, particularly in less developed regions,28 combined with aggressive industry marketing strategies, lower food prices, and lower levels of education and income.2930 In addition, the hyperpalatability of UPF, resulting from their high sodium, sugar, and fat content, stimulates the neural reward system and promotes repeated consumption. This pattern is associated with higher intakes of nutrients linked to chronic disease risk and may contribute to an increased prevalence of chronic diseases.313233

Consistent with these findings, a meta-analysis conducted by Wang et al.34 demonstrated that higher UPF consumption increased the odds of SAH in the general population by up to 1.2 times. These results corroborate the findings of the present study, as women diagnosed with SAH were more likely to report high UPF consumption. National data from Brazil indicate that approximately one-quarter of the adult population reports a diagnosis of SAH, with higher prevalence observed among women, older individuals, those with lower educational attainment, higher salt intake, former smokers, individuals with comorbidities, and those reporting poorer self-rated health.3536

Conversely, lower intake of nutrients essential to health, particularly proteins, was associated with higher UPF consumption. This relationship may be explained by the nutritional composition of UPF, which tend to contain higher proportions of carbohydrates and lipids at the expense of protein, often to enhance shelf life or palatability.9243738 Adequate protein intake is essential throughout the life course due to its role in processes such as enzyme and hormone synthesis. In contrast, insufficient protein intake may lead to reduced muscle strength and mass, loss of visceral mass, impaired immune function, anabolic resistance, and increased risk of sarcopenia and malnutrition.3940

Furthermore, lower intake of MUFA and PUFA, which are more abundant in minimally processed and unprocessed foods, was also associated with higher UPF consumption. Increased intake of these fatty acids represents an important nutritional strategy for reducing cardiovascular disease risk.41424344 The primary PUFAs, omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids, act to reduce inflammatory markers, stimulate nitric oxide production, lower blood pressure, improve endothelial function, and reduce triglyceride concentrations, thereby supporting cardiovascular health.45 MUFAs, particularly oleic acid, contribute to reduced postprandial lipemia and improved lipid profiles by lowering serum triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein, and total cholesterol, while maintaining or increasing high-density lipoprotein levels, which are considered protective against cardiovascular diseases.4244

Thus, the observation that higher UPF consumption in this population is associated with both a greater odds of SAH and a lower odds of adequate PUFA and MUFA intake suggests a substantial potential compromise in overall health status, indicating an elevated risk of adverse cardiovascular outcomes. This concern is further reinforced by the higher intake of cholesterol observed in association with increased UPF consumption.37 Elevated dietary cholesterol intake is linked to higher serum cholesterol and triglyceride levels, which may trigger early vascular inflammatory responses and contribute to the development of cardiovascular diseases.41 Accordingly, the present findings add to the existing body of evidence by demonstrating the relationship between UPF consumption and dietary patterns that predispose individuals to increased cardiovascular risk.45

Other nutrients directly associated with higher UPF consumption in this study were sodium and potassium. Excessive intake of these micronutrients is an important predictor of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality.9 With respect to sodium, excessive consumption adversely affects the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system, promoting water retention, increased blood volume, and, consequently, elevated blood pressure.46 Regarding potassium, the higher intake observed in this population may be attributable to the use of potassium-based additives commonly found in UPF.47 Although the study population consisted of adult women in an apparently stable health condition, it is important to emphasize that, over the long term, excessive potassium intake may result in electrolyte imbalance and lead to serious cardiac complications.4849

The results presented in this study raise important concerns, as they indicate that UPF consumption is increasing within the Brazilian population, paralleling global patterns. This trend has led to changes in population dietary profiles, particularly through increased intake of nutrients associated with the development of hypertension and other cardiovascular diseases.1250 In light of these findings, it can be reinforced that the inclusion and long-term maintenance of UPF in the diet should be avoided, as reductions in UPF intake have been shown to be inversely associated with the consumption of fruits, vegetables, and legumes. This dietary shift promotes higher intake of dietary fiber and antioxidant nutrients, which, in turn, contributes to cholesterol reduction, blood pressure control, decreased platelet aggregation, and improvements in vascular, immune, and intestinal function.51

Considering the results presented, it is important to emphasize the relevance of including variables such as energy intake and obesity as adjustment factors. UPF consumption is consistently associated with higher energy intake and increased prevalence of obesity.525354 Moreover, both obesity and total energy intake are independently related to changes in nutrient consumption patterns.555657 In this context, incorporating these variables as covariates allowed for a more accurate interpretation of the observed associations.

Thus, there is a clear need to strengthen food and nutrition education programs and related actions, as well as to implement public policies that favor the production and commercialization of fresh and minimally processed foods, with an emphasis on promoting population health. These strategies include encouraging adherence to the recommendations of the Brazilian Dietary Guidelines, which advocate for diets based primarily on fresh or minimally processed foods while minimizing UPF consumption. Additionally, policy interventions should address food pricing, improve food labeling practices, and impose restrictions on the advertising and marketing of UPF.

This study has several strengths, including the use of primary data derived from a population-based sample of adult women of reproductive age living in favelas located in a capital city in Northeast Brazil. Furthermore, data collection was conducted in participants’ homes through face-to-face interviews performed by trained and standardized interviewers.

Nevertheless, some limitations must be acknowledged. Due to the cross-sectional design, it is not possible to establish the directionality of the observed associations. In addition, women with a prior diagnosis of hypertension may have modified their dietary habits following medical advice, potentially leading to an underestimation of the association between SAH and UPF consumption. Methodological limitations should also be considered, including participants’ difficulty in accurately quantifying sodium added during food preparation, which may have resulted in underestimation of sodium intake. The use of self-reported hypertension diagnosis, although common in Brazilian population-based surveys such as the National Health Survey (PNS), may introduce recall bias.58 Furthermore, the reapplication of the 24DR in only 1 subsample reduces the precision of usual intake estimates. Finally, the possibility of residual confounding due to unmeasured or insufficiently controlled variables cannot be excluded.

In summary, the findings indicate that women with SAH, as well as those with higher intake of cholesterol, sodium, and potassium, exhibited greater consumption of UPF and lower intake of protein, PUFA, and MUFA. The study identified associations between SAH, nutrients related to cardiovascular health, and higher UPF consumption, without establishing causality. These results underscore the importance of dietary interventions in women’s cardiovascular health, particularly those aimed at reducing UPF intake. Further research is warranted to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of these associations and to inform the development of effective dietary intervention strategies.

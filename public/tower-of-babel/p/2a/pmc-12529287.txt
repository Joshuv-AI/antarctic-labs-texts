Myopia and screen time: the Dutch method in addressing the epidemic with effective strategies
Authors: Vasanthi Iyer, Sijmen A Reijneveld
Published: 2025

Background

The rising prevalence of myopia, exacerbated by increased screen time, poses a significant public health challenge. Studies highlight that prolonged near work and reduced outdoor exposure are critical risk factors for myopia progression [1]. The Dutch 20-20-2 guideline, however, has shown promise in mitigating these risks [2].

Methodology

The 20-20-2 guideline is an enhanced version of the well-known 20-20-20 rule, proposed to reduce digital eye strain (DES) and potentially slow myopia progression in children. The rule combines two key elements:

Regular visual breaks after every 20 minutes of near work, taking a 20-second break to look at objects at least 20 feet away (the original 20-20-20 rule), and

Regular visual breaks after every 20 minutes of near work, taking a 20-second break to look at objects at least 20 feet away (the original 20-20-20 rule), and

Daily outdoor time of at least 2 hours a day.

Daily outdoor time of at least 2 hours a day.

Evidence

While specific studies on the 20-20-2 rule are limited, research on the 20-20-20 rule shows promise in reducing DES. A study by Talens-Estarelles et al. found that following the 20-20-20 rule led to a decrease in dry eye symptoms and DES, although improvements in binocular vision were limited [3]. Research also shows that children should avoid continuous near working distances of <30 cm to reduce myopia risk [1]. While doing so, sustained breaks of longer than 20 seconds would be advisable [4]. Moreover, outdoor light exposure during childhood is suggested to be a critical modifiable risk factor for myopia. Studies show that a minimum of 2 hours of intermittent outdoor light exposure per day can have a significant protective effect against myopia onset and progression [1]. The protective effect of outdoor time is attributed to high light intensity triggering the release of retinal dopamine, which inhibits ocular growth and myopic development. Excessive screen time and reduced outdoor play among youth correlate with multiple health risks like obesity, mental health disorders, declining motor skills and sleep disruption [5]. Reduction of screen use and prioritizing 2 hours a day of outdoor play could mitigate these health risks while fostering healthier, more resilient youth.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the 20-20-2 rule represents a practical, low-risk intervention that combines evidence-based strategies for reducing DES and potentially slowing myopia progression. While further research is needed, its implementation in public health initiatives could be beneficial, especially given the increasing prevalence of digital device use among children. A compromise is to work on best practices regarding healthy screen use and outdoor play; consensus among the professionals involved is essential. Governance approaches, such as public health campaigns and school policies promoting outdoor activities, ensure widespread implementation. We would recommend systemic changes and professional guidelines like the 20-20-2 rule as a public health cornerstone, in order to combat myopia and other excessive screen time consequences.

Perceived restorativeness of urban green spaces in southern China during summer
Authors: Lihua Chen, Yuan Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Although urban green spaces have been proven to benefit human physical and mental health, the impact of people’s perceptions of biodiversity on these health benefits remains to be studied. This study measured physiological and psychological differences among 44 participants to assess the biophilic recovery benefits of two park sample sites in Foshan City. The effects of spatial features on recovery were analyzed using the perceptual restorability scale and physiological measures, including heart rate variability and electrodermal activity. In addition, eye-tracking methodology was employed to explore the impact of environmental components on biophilic recovery. The following findings were observed in this study. (1) Results from both physiological and psychological studies confirmed that natural landscapes of virtual urban parks activate the parasympathetic nervous system. (2) In urban parks, the addition of auditory stimuli could yield better restorative effects than visual stimuli alone. (3) Among the four sound combinations, bird songs in summer green spaces had a more arousing effect than cicada sounds, while cicada sounds had a better effect on heart rate relaxation than bird songs. In contrast, traffic noise could diminish restorative potential. (4) Using eye-tracking technology, this study analyzed differences in environmental features. Natural landscapes, such as forests, attracted more attention from participants when they were exposed to natural bio-sounds, such as cicadas and birds. However, the introduction of noise sound made artificial landscapes, such as squares, more attention-grabbing. In conclusion, multisensory biodiversity perception based on the audiovisual senses has specific restorative effects on the physiological and psychological dimensions of people in urban green spaces in southern China during the summer. In the future, biophilic design combining virtual reality and environmental interactions with multisensory spatial characteristics will enhance the nature-restorative benefits of urban green spaces.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-22382-8.

Introduction

The advance of urbanization and globalization has significantly impacted the natural environment, propelling the Earth into the Anthropocene era1. Humans have transformed more than one-third of the planet’s ecosystems and consume nearly half of the annual biological production of terrestrial ecosystems2, thereby altering the global biogeochemical cycles3. Consequently, novel Anthropocene ecosystems have emerged. Compared to natural ecosystems, these novel ecosystems exhibit significant changes in species composition. Although they have surpassed natural ecological thresholds, they have also developed self-sustaining mechanisms in their evolution4. These ecosystems fulfill societal and natural functions, yet there were many conflicting aspects. Without effective coordination of these relationships, global sustainable development maybe harmed.

Because of the dual objectives of ecological and social functions, it is imperative to establish a framework that harmoniously balances ecosystem benefits and social welfare. The 2022 National Mental Health Insights Report shows that emotional concerns ranked highest among the 4.8 average health issues reported per capita in China in 20214. Reduced nature contact and expanded indoor exposure periods show demonstrable links to these mental health outcomes. Additionally, 91% of respondents self-reported psychological concerns, with anxiety and depression emerging as the most prevalent conditions.

As a means of environmental protection and sustainable urban development, urban green spaces provide essential ecosystem services and societal benefits. Empirical studies demonstrate that natural environments help alleviate stress and recover from fatigue, while urban green spaces specifically promote restorative health outcomes and mental wellbeing6,7. The attention restoration theory (ART) proposed by Kaplan and Kaplan8, and Ulrich’s stress reduction theory (SRT)9 have demonstrated the role of natural environments in mental recovery and emotional improvement. However, most studies have focused on larger natural environments, primarily forest parks, nature reserves, and national parks. Urban green spaces may also have significant restorative benefits10, but how landscape and biodiversity characteristics affect people’s restorative perceptions requires a further in-depth study.

China’s summer climate varies significantly by region. Southern cities experience especially hot and humid conditions11. Due to the combined influence of the subtropical high and the East Asian monsoon, the daily average temperature in southern cities like Foshan consistently exceeds 34 °C. The relative humidity often surpasses 80%, forming a long—lasting hot and humid period that lasts up to four months12. In addition, the summer bioacoustic phenomena in southern China reach a peak, creating a unique soundscape of cicada and bird chirping13,14. The subtropical monsoon climate of Southern China creates distinctive summer conditions, characterized by heightened biodiversity and the prevalent use of urban green spaces. This context offers a unique setting to evaluate the impact of multisensory biodiversity perception on restorativeness, a topic that has received little attention15.

Biodiversity levels are critical indicators for assessing how natural environments can promote psychological recovery15. Studies have indicated that higher bird diversity correlates with a higher diversity perception among respondents16, and a richer composition of arboreal species further attracts more birds to inhabit17. Studies have shown both actual and perceived biodiversity are closely related to residents’ health and wellbeing15. However, there are relatively few empirical studies focusing on multisensory biodiversity perception. Conversely, as cities enter a stage of high-quality development, people increasingly value the rich sensory experiences provided by landscapes18. Methods of creating traditional urban parks, which are dominated by visual environmental characteristics, are no longer sufficient to meet people’s higher demands for recreational experiences19. The most critical pathway for perceiving environmental information is integrating multiple senses, including visual and auditory. Hence, exploring the various connnections between these senses, biophilic experiences, and their restorative effects can offer a new perspective for enhancing the audiovisual perceptual quality of urban green spaces20.

The restorative benefits of urban green spaces can be divided into psychological and physiological aspects21. Psychological recovery is mainly reflected in the reduction of negative emotions and the restoration of attention, while physiological recovery is primarily manifested in the regulation of health. The psychological recovery benefits are measured using the perceptual restorability scale (PRS), and physiological recovery benefits are quantified using physiological measurement indicators such as heart rate variability (HRV) and electrodermal activity (EDA). The PRS is a tool used to measure the restorative potential of environments. This scale is widely used in research on the restorative effects of environmental stimuli on directed attention. It is known for being highly reliable and valid22. The PRS was proposed by Hartig23 and includes four dimensions: fascination、being away, compatibility, extent24. The application of the PRS has facilitated interdisciplinary collaboration between urban planning, ecology, psychology, environmental science and other fields.

Perceptual and sensory dimensions (PSDs) are typically understood as an individual’s overall psychological and physiological response to a series of external stimuli, including visual and auditory stimuli21. These are often associated with an individual’s central nervous system. When sympathetic nervous activity is dominant, it leads to high emotional arousal. Conversely, when parasympathetic nervous activity is dominant, it leads to emotional recovery, calmness, and inhibition.

Auditory stimuli in urban green spaces can generally be evaluated using soundscapes, which refer to the acoustic environment. Soundscapes are typically created by downloading and editing different types of sound fragments from the internet25 or by recording sounds on-site26, and combining them with photos, videos, or VR scenes to form audio-visual scenarios27. Eye-tracking technology is an emerging research tool in biophilic design, landscape architecture, and environmental psychology, used to record participants’ eye movement behaviors, and can be used to measure the impact of different environmental elements on visual stimuli and restorative benefits28. Studies have shown that combined audio-visual stimuli are generally more beneficial to mental health than single visual stimuli29, with natural sound sources such as bird songs providing a greater sense of immersion and more effectively reducing psychological stress26.

This study aims to assess the impact of multisensory perceived biodiversity in urban green spaces on restorativeness and emotional evaluation. The research includes five dimensions: visual and auditory perception, physiological measurements such as heart rate variability (HRV) and electrodermal activity (EDA), perceived restorativeness, biodiversity perception, and emotional evaluation. The study evaluates the audio-visual perceptual quality of parks in Foshan’s ecological belt, a pilot city for park city construction. It also explores how these dimensions interact and affect restorative perception. We also investigate the arousal differences associated with various soundscape combinations in urban park green spaces, as well as their impact on the evaluation of biodiversity perception and emotional states. We aim to develop a set of targeted restorative environmental landscape design guidelines and configuration strategies to promote restoration. Specifically, we will clearly define the visual and auditory potential factors that are crucial for enhancing restorative potential in environments with high biodiversity, thereby providing scientific evidence for urban planners and landscape government and fostering a healthier and more pleasant living environment for city dwellers.

Materials and methods

Study area

Situated within the urban green spaces of Foshan’s two-ring ecological area, Lei Gang Park and Hanlin Lake Agricultural Park are typical anthropogenic ecosystems that integrate natural and artificial elements. These elements include the natural ecosystem of the Pearl River Delta river network, the traditional agricultural system of Lingnan, park recreation systems, water conservancy systems, and scientific research and educational systems (Fig. 1).

In 2020, Foshan City introduced the “Foshan Special Plan for the Construction of a Natural Ecological Civilization,” which proposes the development of a multi-level, life-symbiotic, green ecological system. Leveraging Foshan’s ecological resource advantages, the plan aims to create a structure consisting of “three screens,” “six wedges,” “two veins,” “two rings,” and “two networks”, forming a “suburban ten-thousand-acre wilderness forest ring” and an “urban thousand-acre city park ring”. The plan seeks to interconnect various habitats and ecological chains into a network by connecting Foshan’s river networks, wetland systems, agricultural systems, and forest systems. This study will further explore design methods for natural-human hybrid ecosystems with multiple objectives to achieve the sustainable development of complex social-ecological systems.

The study primarily explored how various biophilic elements in promote restorative effects and emotional responses in urban green spaces. Two groups of visual and auditory sensory elements were considered, as well as three types of species diversity, four combinations of soundscapes, and eight categories of landscape elements. The research methodology primarily utilized subjective questionnaires, physiological feedback, and eye-tracking technology.

(1) Visual stimulus materials. The study was conducted in July 2024 under clear weather conditions, with excellent air quality and wind speeds below 5 m/s. DJI Insta 360 panoramic camera was used to capture photographs. Each park was surveyed for ten sampling sites, and 20 samples were collected, using 20 park environment photos as visual stimulus materials.

(2) Auditory stimulus materials. Field studies were conducted across 20 sampled plots in two Foshan parks. Cicada calls and bird songs were selected as representative biological sound stimuli, which were then subdivided into groups of single cicada sounds and single bird sounds. The cicada species included Cryptotympana atrata, Platypleura kaempferi, and Meimuna mongolica. The bird species included Pycnonotus jocosus, Pycnonotus sinensis, Zosterops japonicus, and Streptopelia chinensis. The selected species are all dominant groups in Foshan’s urban parks during the summer. They provide a comprehensive reflection of the ecosystem structure.

Sound samples were recorded on-site at each location using a Zoom H6 Handy Recorder, maintaining a minimum duration of 3 min.

The recorded audio was imported into Adobe Audition 2024 software for extraction, separation, noise reduction, merging, loudness matching, and sound pressure level adjustment. Then, the audio (MP3) of each scene was exported. An AWA6270 + noise analyzer was also used on-site to measure sound pressure and ensure consistent environmental sound pressure levels.

Participant characteristics

Using G Power software30–32, the sample size of this study (N = 44) achieved 80% statistical power (α = 0.05) for detecting a medium effect size (f = 0.25), satisfying the basic requirements for hypothesis testing. The study consisted of 24 males and 20 females between the ages of 18 and 30 (M = 22.99, SD = 1.28) (Table 1). Given the digital proficiency required for VR operation and the more efficient ethical approval processes of campus committees, all participants were college students in good physical and mental condition from Guangdong Province.

Experimental equipment and process

Participants were outfitted with physiological monitoring devices (EDA for electrodermal activity and ECG for electrocardiogram) and an eye-tracking virtual reality (VR) apparatus. This process culminated in the calibrating the eye-tracking VR device (Fig. 2). Participants must have myopia of 3.00 diopters or less without astigmatism and must not consume coffee or alcohol on the day of the experiment.

Fig. 2Experimental environment and equipment. Device 1 is a virtual reality eye tracking analysis instrument; Device 2 is an Electrodermal Activity (EDA) measurement device; Device 3 is an Electrocardiogram (ECG) device which measures the heart’s electrical activity and rhythm.

Experimental environment and equipment. Device 1 is a virtual reality eye tracking analysis instrument; Device 2 is an Electrodermal Activity (EDA) measurement device; Device 3 is an Electrocardiogram (ECG) device which measures the heart’s electrical activity and rhythm.

The main experiment consists of four groups: two visual and two audio-visual. Each group contains 10 panoramic images. The experimental design follows three steps based on previous research7,10. First, during the preparation stage, we explain experiment’s content, procedures, and precautions to the participants. After completing the personal basic information questionnaire, participants need to wear physiological and eye-tracking VR equipment for eye calibration. Second, during the measurement stage, there is a 60-s initial adaptation period to collect baseline physiological data before the first stimulus. Then, a 12-s traffic stress video is played, followed by a 10-s rest period. Next, there is a 15-s viewing of the panoramic image (with or without audio) as the stimulus, followed by another 10-s rest period. Eye-tracking data, EDA and HRV data will be monitored during this stage. Third, at the end of each experimental group, the VR equipment will be removed and a questionnaire will be completed. There will be a 2-min inter-group rest between each group. After completing four groups of experiments, the participants must confirm that they are in good condition before leaving the laboratory (Fig. 3).

Measurement index

The experimental data encompassed questionnaire surveys, physiological measurement data, perceived biodiversity scales, emotional evaluation scales, and eye-tracking behavioral data.

The Perceptual Restorability Scale (PRS)33 (Appendices 1, 2, 3) was employed to assess the restorative quality of urban green spaces. Four criteria were used to evaluate perceived restorativeness: “fascination,” “being away from,” “extent,” and “compatibility”. The scale measured individuals’ subjective experiences of their environment’s healing potential. Respondents evaluated their level of agreement with the content described in each item and assigned scores to their environment across these dimensions. This scoring mechanism enabled the PRS to quantify the positive impact of the environment on individuals’ psychological recovery.

Skin conductance response (SCR) variations reflect the activity of sweat gland secretion, indicating the activity level and fluctuations in the human sympathetic nervous system (Table 2). This advantage makes SCR a widely used tool in research on emotions, cognition, and biofeedback34. This study divided the EDA signal into two components: tonic data, which represents gradual changes, and phasic data, which represents abrupt changes. The skin conductance level (SCL) is the most common measure of the tonic component. Meanwhile, phasic data refers to the rapidly changing portion caused by sympathetic nervous system activity and is also known as skin conductance response (SCR). Research has demonstrated that when positive outcomes are anticipants’ electrodermal index waveforms are highand fluctuating, indicating aroused emotions. The electrodermal index is positively correlated with the degree of positive emotional arousal35. This study used tonic (SCL) and phasic (SCR) signals measured during the recovery phase as EDA physiological indicators.

Indicators and definitions.

Heart rate variability (HRV) is a measure of the control that the central autonomic nervous system exerts over the heart36. High-frequency (HF) power indicates parasympathetic regulation of cardiac activity, while low-frequency (LF) power reflects sympathetic regulation of cardiac activity. The LF/HF ratio represents the level of activity of the sympathetic nervous system, with increased stress leading to a decrease in the LF/HF37,38. In the ErgoLAB platform, physiological data is obtained after analytical operations including filtering, deconvolution processing, and outlier correction to eliminate anomalous data interference. Heart rate (HR) refers to the number of heartbeats per minute in an individual at rest. A calm and relaxed state increases the excitability of the parasympathetic nervous system increases, leading to a slower heart rate (HR). Conversely, stress or focus enhances the activity of the sympathetic nervous system, resulting in a faster heart rate39. This study selected five physiological indicators of heart rate variability (HRV): low-frequency (LF), high-frequency (HF), LF/HF ratio, heart rate (HR), and interbeat interval (IBI).

Ultimately, eye-tracking behavioral data, specifically fixation behavior, can reflect cognitive processing. Fixations represent a relatively stable state of ocular movement40. Because fixation duration and frequency are sensitive to psychological changes, this study selected AOI fixation total duration (s) and AOI fixation frequency (n) for analysis.

Results

Audiovisual interactive sensory stimulation is more effective in perceptual restorability, emotional evaluation and biodiversity perception

The PRS33 (Appendices 1, 2, 3) measured the restorative qualities of the park environment across visual and audiovisual groups. It gauged the dimensions of “fascination,” “being away from,” “compatibility,” and “extent.” Scores were calibrated on a scale from 1 (strongly disagree) to 10 (strongly agree), with higher values indicating heightened levels of each restorative attribute. The reliability coefficients, as determined by Cronbach’s alpha, were high for the PRS across both groups. Specifically, for the audiovisual group (G2), the coherence subscale exhibited robust reliability (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.916), and for the visual group (G1), the reliability of the coherence subscale remained satisfactory (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.899) (Fig. 4).

The impact of four different combinations of sound sources on physiological indicators

How should the recovery status of biodiversity in a region be assessed? The sounds of cicadas and birds are significant biological sound sources in southern China during the summer and hold value for research on indicator species of biodiversity. Cicadas are most active in the summer, and the frequency and duration of their chirping increases with rising temperatures, facilitating their activity and reproduction. This study created four sound source combinations: “cicada,” “bird,” “cicada & bird,” and “cicada & bird & noise.” Acoustic monitoring is a critical approach. Soundscape environments can influence emotions by activating brain responses and affecting psychological perceptions and altering attention distribution.

Through ANOVA (Table 6), this study found significant differences in ΔSCL among the four distinct sound groups (F = 7.773; P < 0.001). During the recovery phase, the “bird” group elicited a more pronounced physiological response than the other three sound groups, indicating higher arousal. Participants exposed to the “cicada & bird” group sounds had lower ΔSCL values than those exposed to a single sound source. However, under more complex acoustic conditions, specifically the “cicada & bird & noise” group, the participants exhibited the lowest ΔSCL values. This suggests the potential effects of adaptation or attention diversion. Although the ANOVA results for ΔSCR were insignificant (F = 1.002, P = 0.391), a discernible trend indicated that the ΔSCR induced by bird sounds was the highest. This suggests that a single bio-sound source, particularly bird sounds, may substantially impact the physiological level of EDA with higher arousal.

A series of analyses were conducted to determine the existence of significant differences in the physiological responses to soundscapes of different birds and cicadas. These analyses included analysis of variance (ANOVA) of the ΔSCL (F = 15.065, P < 0.001), ΔSCR (F = 4.851, P < 0.01), ΔLF (F = 4.344, P < 0.01), and ΔHF (F = 5.328, P < 0.01) indices. The results of these analyses indicated that significant differences do exist in the physiological responses to soundscapes of different birds and cicadas (Fig. 8). In the context of avian soundscapes, the high-frequency variable calls of the Light-vented Bulbul (Pycnonotus sinensis) have been observed to elicit significant sympathetic nervous system activation, as evidenced by the elevation of ΔSCL to 0.695 and ΔSCR to 0.456. This auditory stimulation has been shown to attract continuous attention, a phenomenon associated with the complexity of the sound. The vocalizations of the Red-whiskered Bulbul (P. jocosus) show a similar ΔSCR as the Light-vented Bulbul, yet a higher ΔSCL (0.776). The high-frequency soft calls of the Japanese White-eye (Zosterops japonicus) result in a reduction of ΔSCL/SCR to 0.416/0.231, while the low-frequency calls of the Spotted Dove (Streptopelia chinensis) maintain ΔSCL/SCR at a lower level of 0.367/0.291. In the context of cicada soundscapes, the continuous high-frequency sounds of the Black Cicada (Cryptotympana atrata) result in heightened sympathetic stimulation (ΔLF = 0.625, ΔHF = 0.494). The intermittent calls of the Platypleura Cicada (Platypleura kaempferi) have been shown to maximize parasympathetic activation (ΔHF = 0.711), while the low-frequency calls of the Mongolian Cicada (Meimuna mongolica) show the lowest ΔLF (ΔLF = 0.463).

Forests and grass are the most attractive landscapes in the Cicada soundscape

In an environment surrounded by the sounds of cicada, two eye-tracking indices were recorded for the participants: AOI fixation frequency (F = 9.061, P < 0.001) and AOI fixation total duration (F = 10.516, P < 0.001). These indices indicated a significant impact of various landscape elements on the allocation of visual attention (Table 8). Specifically, the forest landscape (Na2) exhibited the highest AOI fixation total duration (MAr4 = 0.412 s, MNa4 = 0.823 s, MNa3 = 0.843 s, MNa2 = 1.484 s; Na2 > Na3 > Na4 > Ar4). The sequence of AOI fixations (MAr4 = 3.114 n, MNa4 = 7.136 n, MNa3 = 6.136 n, MNa2 = 10.409 n; Na2 > Na4 > Na3 > Ar4) exhibits a descending frequency, with Na2 fixations occurring most frequently followed by Na4, Na3, and Ar4. The findings indicate that Na2 elicited the most substantial visual attention among participants. The shrub landscape (Na3) and the grass landscape (Na4) also exhibited relatively high AOI fixation total durations, while the road landscape (Ar4) demonstrated the lowest AOI fixation frequency. This outcome corroborated Weng Yuxi’s (2021) case study of the Guanyin Lake campus41, which found that water sounds direct attention toward the water body, whereas noise guides attention toward artificial objects, indicating that different soundscape types can influence visual perception.

Discussion

Relationship among PRS, emotional evaluation, and perceived biodiversity

Effects of multisensory biodiversity perception on PRS

This study further employed the ordinary least squares (OLS) method to statistically evaluate the impact of bird, insect, and plant species diversity on the Perceptual Restorability Scale (PRS) in visual (G1) and the audiovisual group (G2) (Fig. 10; Table 12). The PRS comprises four dimensions: fascination, distance, compatibility, and extent. The results indicate that multisensory biodiversity perception significantly positively affects PRS (Fig. 11).

In the visual group (G1), plant species diversity had the most significant impact on “fascination” (β = 0.603, F = 49.165, P < 0.001), “being away from” (β = 0.529, F = 33.37, P < 0.001), and “compatibility” (β = 0.531, F = 33.683, P < 0.001). Additionally, the analysis revealed that bird species diversity had the greatest impact on “extent” (β = 0.321, F = 9.891, P < 0.01). In the audiovisual group (G2), plant species diversity also showed the most significant impact across all dimensions, particularly on “compatibility” (β = 0.648, F = 62.35, P < 0.001) and “Extent” (β = 0.57, F = 41.459, P < 0.001).

In both groups, the impact of plant species diversity on PRS was generally more significant than that of bird and insect species. Additionally, the β values for compatibility and extent were higher in the audiovisual group (G2) relative to the visual group (G1), indicating that the audiovisual experience potentially amplifies the effect of species diversity on PRS. Furthermore, the audiovisual experience has the potential to amplify this effect. It is noteworthy that the role of plant species diversity in promoting perceived restorativeness is prominent.

The findings of this study are consistent with previous research. Joel Methorst (2021) obtained socioeconomic data from European citizens and macro-level species diversity across 26 countries or regions in Europe, demonstrating a positive correlation between bird species richness and life satisfaction42. Li Yaocheng (2021) conducted a correlation study using 16 plant diversity indicators with citizens’ mental recovery values, analyzing whether the quality of environmental restorativeness was influenced by plant diversity. The results indicated that perceived plant diversity had a stronger correlation with participants’ mental recovery43.

Effects of multisensory biodiversity perception on emotional evaluation

On the other hand, perceived biodiversity significantly impacted the two dimensions of emotional evaluation, “unpleasure-pleasure” and “calm-nervous,” in both the visual group (G1) and the audiovisual group (G2) (Fig. 12; Table 13). In the “visual group (G1) × pressure stage (S1),” plant species diversity had the most pronounced effect on the “unpleasure-pleasure” emotional dimension (β = 0.498, F = 28.349, P < 0.001). In the “visual group (G1) × recovery stage (S2),” the path effect remained significant (β = 0.354, F = 12.317, P < 0.001), but the impact was relatively reduced compared to the pressure stage (S1).

In the “audiovisual group (G2) × pressure stage (S1),” the effects on the “unpleasure-pleasure” emotional dimension were significantly positive for bird species diversity (β = 0.49, F = 27.164, P < 0.000), insect diversity (β = 0.32, F = 9.816, P < 0.002), and plant species diversity (β = 0.38, F = 9.027, P < 0.003). These effects further intensified during the recovery stage (S2), particularly for plant species diversity (β = 0.578, F = 43.038, P < 0.000). Regarding the “calm-nervous” dimension, the impact of plant species diversity was the most significant (β = -0.385, F = 14.947, P < 0.000). Thus, plant species diversity plays a crucial role in enhancing positive emotions and reducing nervous emotions. The audiovisual experience has been shown to amplify this effect.

This study further employed the analysis of variance (ANOVA) to statistically analyze the impact of various sociocultural backgrounds. The results indicated (Table 15) that there was a significant difference in the perception of bird species diversity among the participants. Specifically, the data show that females (M = 7.329) perceive a higher level of bird species diversity than males (M = 6.568, F = 5.456, P < 0.05**). This finding aligns with Helen Hoyle’s research in 2019, which suggested that perceived naturalness is associated with participants’ gender and connections to nature, and female participants with more natural connections perceive significantly higher levels of naturalness44.

Moreover, a notable disparity emerged in the perception of insect species diversity regarding self-rated health and annual household income. Self-rated health assessment exhibited a positive “U”-shaped trend (F = 3.386, P = 0.011**), with individuals who rated their health as either “extremely poor” or “very healthy” demonstrating a higher perception of insect species diversity. A correlation was observed between lower annual household income and a higher perception of insect species diversity (F = 2.254, P = 0.065*). Finally, the perception of plant species diversity also followed a positive “U”-shaped pattern concerning self-rated health, with those who rated their health as “extremely poor” or “very healthy” showing a greater perception of plant species diversity (F = 2.986, P < 0.021**).

How can the bio-friendly design of urban green spaces better exert perceptual restoration effect?

Bird diversity’s role in perceived and sensory dimensions (PSDs)

Bird diversity has significant positive effects on perceived and sensory dimensions (PSDs). This study demonstrated a positive correlation between the perception of bird species diversity and human psychological health and physiological arousal. Bird sounds can reduce stress and annoyance, as well as improve mood and cognitive performance26. Landscape designers can create suitable bird habitats in parks by installing features such as birdbaths, nest boxes, and diverse habitats. These features can attract birds to stay and increase their diversity17. Moreover, the visual impact of birds has been demonstrated to exert a positive influence on human emotional states45. For instance, the establishment of designated bird-watching areas within parks can serve as a distinctive zone, allowing visitors to observe and appreciate birds in close proximity. This initiative has the potential to provide a relaxing and enjoyable experience. The strategic incorporation of low-impact visitor footpaths within the design of green spaces to create an educational corridor. Consequently, the biodiversity of birds ensures the stability of park ecosystems and provides visitors an efficacious method of stress relief.

Insect diversity’s role in PSDs

Insects play a significant role in the design of park soundscape. Cicadas, crickets, bees, and butterflies are key indicator species of insect diversity in parks. As recorded in this study, the cicada species commonly found in southern China’s parks include Hyalessa maculaticollis, Pomponia linearis, Meimuna opalifera, Platypleura kaempferi, Cryptotympana atrata, Meimuna mongolica, and Mogannia hebes. Research has demonstrated that insect sounds can provide psychological restorative effects19. The ecological design of specific insect habitats, such as butterfly gardens, insect hotels, and insectariums, can enhance insect diversity and enliven parks with their activities and sounds. Thus, biophilic design helps conserve biodiversity and provides visitors with a more tranquil and restorative environment.

Plant diversity’s role in PSDs

This study confirmed that plant species diversity plays a particularly prominent role in promoting PRS, exerting a substantial impact on the enhancement of positive emotions and the alleviation of tension. Forest landscapes in particular have been found to attract heightened attention from participants in the study of natural soundscapes. Concurrently, research has demonstrated that exposure to nature can awaken sensory experiences, such as visual, olfactory, and tactile senses, contributing to stress reduction and immune system enhancement and further improving overall health and wellbeing46,47. This study capitalized on the superior natural environment, distinctive landscape features, and unique biodiversity of the Foshan area. Local species and ecological environments were incorporated into soundscape design to increase park uniqueness and appeal. The design should take into consideration the acoustic characteristics of different plants, including the natural sounds of insects and birds attracted to them. This will serve to enrich the soundscape27. The provision of green spaces and the colors of flowers has been demonstrated to have a significant effect on people’s emotions and psychological states43. For instance, the visual impact of green and white leaves, as well as yellow or white flowers, has been demonstrated to significantly reduces stress48. Moreover, the scents of plants such as lavender and pine have been proven to possess anti-anxiety and stress-reducing properties. Thus, future research on the multisensory restorative effects of green spaces can be expanded by incorporating color and olfactory perceptions7.

How to build a biodiversity-friendly City

China’s actions in biodiversity conservation

China has made significant progress in the realm of biodiversity protection, as evidenced by the release of the China Biodiversity Conservation Strategy and Action Plan (2023–2030)49, which clarifies the goals and pathways for biodiversity conservation in the ensuing decade. China has also established a national-scale biodiversity monitoring network, such as Sino BON, providing vital information services and decision support for biodiversity conservation. The optimization of the spatial layout of conservation is a fundamental component of this plan. The strategy is predicated on the provision of high-level protection, with the objective to enhancing the diversity, stability, and sustainability of key ecological spaces, including nature reserves, ecological red lines, and critical ecological corridors, thereby achieving high-quality biodiversity conservation.

First, China has initiated pilot programs for a national park system and has constructed a natural protected area system with national parks as the mainstay. This system includes nature reserves, scenic spots, geological parks, wetland parks, marine parks, and desert parks. These protected areas compass a wide range of ecosystem types and serve as the primary habitats of various species of wild flora and fauna species that are of national importance and are protected under law. These protected areas have achieved the United Nations Convention on Biological Diversity’s Aichi Target of reaching 17% by 2020, a goal that was met well in advance of the target date. Secondly, China has designated priority areas for biodiversity conservation, encompassing significant natural ecosystems and biological resources and ensuring effective protection of ecosystems, species, and habitats. Furthermore, China has instituted a national spatial planning system and has been the first nation to propose and implement the ecological red line system on an international level. This initiative signifies a substantial institutional innovation within China’s national spatial planning and ecological environment system mechanism reform.

Bio-friendly design strategy of urban green spaces in Southern China

Foshan City, located in the Guangdong Province, has demonstrated a proactive stance on biodiversity conservation, aligning with the strategies and actions outlined in the relevant plan. In accordance with the Guangdong Biodiversity Conservation Strategy and Action Plan (2023–2030)42, the Guangdong Province has established explicit objectives for biodiversity conservation and implemented specific action plans, with the overarching goal of enhancing the governance level of biodiversity in a comprehensive manner. Foshan City has issued the Foshan Territorial Spatial Ecological Restoration Plan (2021–2035)43, which aims to maintain the overall pattern of ecological security. The plan focuses on three major spaces: ecological areas, urban areas, and agriculture areas. It also implements differentiated ecological protection and restoration strategies.

According to the Foshan Special Plan for the Construction of Natural Ecological Civilization in 2018 44, the natural ecological pattern of “three screens, six wedges, two veins, and a blue-green network” has been proposed. The Lei Gang Park in this study located within the confines of urban thousand-acre city park ring. In contrast, the Hanlin Lake Agricultural Park is situated within the confines of a ten-thousand-acre suburban wilderness forest ring. The “two-ring ecological circle” plan of Foshan City is part of the city’s special plan for constructing a natural ecological civilization, aspires to establish an eco-friendly and biodiverse urban environment. This initiative is designed to improve the quality of life for city residents and to establish a robust foundation for sustainable development.

Foshan City has demonstrated notable advancements in the realm of biodiversity conservation. The municipality has initiated eight significant initiatives to advance the ecological construction of a green and beautiful city. These initiatives are designed to continuously optimize the “Forest Chief +” coordination mechanism and to promote comprehensive transformation from the “Forest Chief System” to “Forest Chief Governance.” The efficacy of these measures is evident in the enhancement of forest stands and the improvement of forest quality. This enhancement is achieved through various strategies, including forest tending and enhancement, the improvement of forest appearance, and the enhancement of forest ecosystems’ stability and carbon sequestration capacity. It has been observed that the region has been able to leverage its geographical advantages and natural resource endowments. Efforts have been made to create national wetland parks and national forest parks, and the construction of comprehensive parks, thematic parks, and ribbon parks has been vigorously promoted. It has been demonstrated to advance the planning and construction of the Foshan section of the Thousand-Mile Greenway at a high level, build a “three-ring” system with a total length of nearly 512 km, focuse on constructing the “six ribbons” with a length of about 508 km, and create ecological corridors.

Application of modern ecological theory and biophilic design

Modern ecology has evolved rapidly, giving rise to two branches: landscape ecology, which focuses on landscapes, and global ecology, which addresses the biosphere. However, ecological research and planning have long been disconnected from practice. They lack paradigms and operability, which makes it challenging to guide implementation50. The biophilic design practice method, consists of three steps: field research, experimental design, and optimization adjustments. It integrates research and practice to optimize ecological design and enhance the potential for resilience in novel ecosystems51.

This study further approached the biodiversity conservation strategies of Foshan City using the biophilic design method, aiming to create conditions for the reintroduction and healthy development of regional biodiversity to the greatest extent possible. Biophilic design is the intentional integration of natural elements andstructures to achieve specific goals. The design of ecosystems is the consequence of active management and intervention in novel ecosystems28. From the perspective of human society’s subjective needs, objective environmental conditions, and existing technological levels, designed ecosystems cannot maintain the state of original natural ecosystems. Therefore, human influence should be regarded as an endogenous factor that provides optimized ecosystem services within the natural-human hybrid ecosystem, rather than reverting to the original natural ecosystem state.

This study designed habitat scenarios (Fig. 13) based on the ecological knowledge provided by the Foshan Park Biodiversity Conservation Plan and the knowledge about forest birds, water birds, insects, and wildlife habits obtained from the Foshan Planning Institute. These scenarios include the establishment of flexible water edges, the creation of small and micro wetland clusters, and the establishment of bird habitats. The study’s implementation of biophilic design entailded the execution of multiple experimental schemes, the regulation of the scale of individual experiments, and the regular monitoring and adjustment of practices. On the other hand, the implementation of strategies aimed at enhancing avian and entomological biodiversity in the region was undertaken. These strategies encompassed the creation of bird and insect habitats, the integration of low-maintenance yet resilient water systems, and the implementation of water purification systems within wetland ecosystems. Additionally, the study encompassed the expansion of the tidal channel beneath the trees canopy, with the objective to enhance the flood storage capacity of wetlands. The efficacy of these measures was evidenced by their ability to meet the foraging needs of forest birds, provide adequate habitation for water birds, and ensure the survival and safety of insects52.

Additionally, this study utilized the plant survey results of the Pearl River Delta region in southern China as a foundational template. A Consideration of the avian and entomological habitats, as well as the foraging needs of birds and insects, the effects of the landscape on plant communities, and the routine maintenance costs, suggests that plant communities are suitable for a variety of animal habitats. The implementation of nesting forests, as outlined in this study, has led to a notable augmentation in the number and density of tree species that are conductive to the nesting of large birds. These species possess several characteristics that are conducive to avian habitation, including dense foliage, robust support structure, non-toxicity, and a low propensity to attract natural enemies of birds and insects. This study considered the construction of bird habitat in four layers from the vertical structure: (1) the tree layer should select species such as Ficus concinna, Pterocarya stenoptera, and Cinnamomum camphora to provide them with suitable habitats and foraging sites; (2) the shrub layer can be planted with Ligustrum quihoui Carr and Nandina domestica; (3) the herbaceous layer can consider Axonopus compressus and Cynodon dactylon; and (4) aquatic plants can be Neyraudia reynaudiana and Phragmites australis. In the construction of insect habitat plant communities, the selection of species such as Aristolochia kaempferi Willd and Alyxia sinensis Champ as habitats is recommended, while Centaurea cyanus Linn and Tagetes erecta can serve as foraging sites. The construction of such plant communities fulfills the ecological requirements of animals and creates aesthetic natural landscapes that are straightforward to maintain and manage.

During the construction and optimization of the park ecosystem, it is essential to focus on the monitoring and management of invasive species53, including Mikania micrantha, Bidens pilos, and Ipomoea cairica. These species have been observed to invade habitats, such as forests, farmlands, and urban green spaces. These highly vigorous invasive species have been shown to strangle other plants by entwining them and releasing chemical substances that have a negative impact on their surroundings. Additionally, invasive species such as Pomacea canaliculate and Lissachatina fulica exhibit strong growth and reproductive capabilities, causing significant damage to vegetation, competition for space with other organisms, and the transmission of numerous pathogens. These species rapidly proliferate, consuming crops and vegetation after infestation, thereby exacerbating the damage to native ecosystems. On the other hand, the conservation of veteran fruit trees is crucial for maintaining ecological food webs, as these trees provide essential fruits and nectar for pollinators, birds, and mammals. This study further advocates introducing diverse tree species that offer fruits and nectar year-round to attract omnivorous songbirds that feed on plant fruits, seeds, and insects, such as Pycnonotus jocosus, Zosterops japonicus, Pycnonotus aurigaster, Lanius schach, Phoenicurus auroreus, Parus major, Egretta garzetta, and Phalacrocorax carbo. This design has the potential to provide ecosystem services, including pollination, soil amelioration, and biological control, which are essential for maintaining ecosystem functionality and promoting human wellbeing.

Conclusions

This study combined PRS, physiological monitoring data (HRV and SCR), and eye-tracking technology to examine the biodiversity characteristics of urban green spaces in southern China and the effects of different combinations of landscapes and soundscape on restorative benefits. The results revealed several key findings. First, the integration of natural sounds with virtual urban park landscapes has been demonstrates to stimulate parasympathetic activity. Furthermore, the combination of visual-auditory stimuli has been shown to enhance biodiversity perception when compared with visual stimuli alone. Secondly, the restorative potential of single bio-sound sources, such as cicada or bird sounds in urban parks, has been demonstratedare, while traffic noise has been shown to diminish restorative potential. Thirdly, forest landscapes offer a distinct advantage in attracting visual attention. The perceived biodiversity of plants, birds, and insects has been shown to significantly impact the enhancement of positive emotions and the reduction of tension. Fourthly, females perceive bird species diversity to a greater extent than males. Lastly, based on the plant survey results from the Pearl River Delta region in southern China, this study proposes a region-specific biodiversity conservation strategy for constructing bird and insect habitat plant communities through biophilic design methods.

Through audio-visual interaction analysis, this study has identified core visual and auditory predictive factors that enhance environmental restorative potential. These findings provide empirical support for urban biodiversity conservation and enable evidence-based design of restorative green spaces with ecological value.

However, several limitations should be acknowledged: First, the biological soundscape collection contained only cicadas and birds; future research could incorporate additional natural sound sources (e.g., frog calls, stream sounds) and indicator species to explore the relationship between soundscape complexity and restorativeness. Secondly, the sample was primarily composed of students, which introduces the possibility of selection bias. It is therefore recommended that subsequent studies should include participants from diverse age groups and occupations to validate the generalizability of the results. Thirdly, while static VR environments facilitate variable control, they are incapable of fully capturing multimodal interactions in natural scenes. Future research should integrate augmented reality (AR) technology with multisensory inputs (e.g., plant volatile odors, breeze touch), and combine neuroscientific methods (EEG, fMRI) with comprehensive emotional scales (e.g., PANAS). This integrated approach would elucidate mechanisms linking biodiversity to restorative perception, providing more holistic theoretical support for healthy urban design.

High-density multipolar mapping facilitating ablation of atypical atrial flutter: the power of ‘more mapping less ablation’
Authors: Christian-Hendrik Heeger, Marcel Feher, Julia Vogler, Roland Richard Tilz, Bogdan Enache, Sebastian Feickert, Andreas Metzner
Published: 2024

Case description

A 79-year-old female patient presented to the emergency care unit with dyspnoea, palpitations, and weakness. In December 2021, persistent atrial fibrillation (AF) was treated by cryoballoon-based pulmonary vein (PV) isolation. A second catheter ablation procedure (3D mapping and radiofrequency ablation) was conducted, and the right PVs were reisolated; a box lesion and an anterior line were deployed in March 2022. The current electrocardiogram shows atypical atrial flutter. A third catheter ablation procedure was planned. Here, the clinical tachycardia was apparent (cycle length = 290 ms). One decapolar catheter was positioned in the coronary sinus (CS) showing a distal to proximal activation sequence. Utilizing entrainment manoeuvres from the left atrial appendage, distal and proximal CS catheter, and anterior and posterior walls, atypical left atrial (LA) flutter was suggested. A 3D electroanatomic LA reconstruction (CARTO 3 V7; Biosense Webster) was performed via high-density mapping with an octaspline multi-electrode catheter and COHERENT module (Biosense Webster) covering the whole cycle lengths of the tachycardia.1 A roof-depending macroreentrant tachycardia with a critical isthmus at the posterior wall was demonstrated (Figure 1; Supplementary material online, Video S1), suggesting a recovery of the previously deployed roof line and posterior line and therefore ‘man-made’ tachycardia. Utilizing the QDOT Micro ablation catheter (Biosense Webster) vHP-SD (90 W, 4 s; QMODE + mode) ablation at the posterior wall terminated the tachycardia after 0.6 s.2,3 A roof line and a posterior line were deployed, reisolating the posterior wall. Additional applications were deployed to block the anterior line which was found to be recovered after initial block. Block of the all lines was verified by traditional pacing manoeuvres via OCTARAY catheter from the LA appendage as well as distal and proximal CS and checking all lines for double potentials along the lines. Programmed stimulation was performed to test re-inducibility. A total of 37 vHP-SD applications were deployed with a total ablation time of 2 min and 22 s and a procedure time of 60 min. No peri-procedural complications occurred. The patient was discharged from the hospital on the next day, and in mid-term follow-up up to 6 months, there was no clinical relevant and detectable recurrence of AF or atypical flutter.

We present a case of atypical flutter ablation utilizing the OCTARAY and QDOT Micro catheter. Although there are effective and safe traditional approaches for treatment of atrial flutter, our findings highlight the potential benefit of high-density multi-electrode mapping for atypical flutter treatment.

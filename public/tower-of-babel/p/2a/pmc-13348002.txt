SOT102, a novel CLDN18.2-targeting antibody-drug conjugate, exhibits strong therapeutic potential in solid tumors
Authors: Iva Valentová, Lenka Kyrych Sadílková, Lukas Bammert, Lorenz Waldmeier, Roger Beerli, Ilona Procházková, Filip Jabůrek, Tatiana Spitzová, Eliška Kohelová, Kati Räsänen, Ulrich Moebius, Radek Špíšek
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with gastric and pancreatic cancers, as well as other solid tumors including ovarian, lung, liver, and colon cancers, often lack effective therapeutic options. Claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2) is a tumor-associated target that is predominantly expressed in gastric and pancreatic cancers but also found in several other tumor types. SOT102 is a novel antibody–drug conjugate directed against CLDN18.2, developed to provide a new therapeutic strategy for patients with CLDN18.2-positive tumors.MethodsSOT102, composed of a proprietary monoclonal antibody (mAb) conjugated to the cytotoxic payload PNU-159682, was evaluated for binding, internalization, and cytotoxic effects in vitro. The in vivo antitumor activity was assessed in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) and cell line-derived xenograft (CDX) mouse models, both as monotherapy and the latter in combination with anti-PD1 antibody therapy. SOT102 pharmacokinetics and tolerability were further investigated in cynomolgus monkeys following intravenous administration.ResultsSOT102 demonstrated selective binding to CLDN18.2, with no detectable cross-reactivity to CLDN18.1, and efficient internalization into CLDN18.2-expressing cell lines, resulting in potent cytotoxic effects against tumor organoids with half-maximal activity ranging from 0.2 nM to 19.4 nM. Antitumor activity against PDX-derived mouse models was observed at a minimum effective dose of 0.2 mg/kg, with enhanced efficacy when combined with anti-PD1 antibody treatment. SOT102 exposure in cynomolgus monkeys was dose-dependent at doses between 0.3 mg/kg and 1 mg/kg with a half-life of approximately 7 days. An acceptable tolerability profile was observed, and the therapeutic window was defined between the minimum effective dose in mice and the highest non-severe toxic dose (HNSTD) of 0.6 mg/kg in cynomolgus monkeys.ConclusionsSOT102 exhibited strong antitumor activity in preclinical models of CLDN18.2-positive cancers and demonstrated a favorable pharmacokinetic and safety profile in non-human primates. These data were used to support clinical evaluation of SOT102 as a potential treatment option for patients with CLDN18.2-expressing solid tumors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-16185-x.Keywords: Claudin 18.2, Antibody-drug conjugate, Gastric cancer, Pancreatic cancer, Solid tumor

Background

Gastric cancer (GC) is a leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, with advanced gastric cancer (AGC) remaining particularly challenging to treat [1]. Monoclonal antibodies such as trastuzumab (anti-HER2), ramucirumab (anti-VEGFR2), and nivolumab (anti-PD-1) have shown promise in AGC, improving survival in some cases, especially in later-line treatments [2, 3]. However, responses to these therapies are limited, with median survival remaining low at 10–16 months and a 2-year overall survival (OS) rate under 10% [2]. Similarly, pancreatic cancer (PC) presents a high mortality rate and few effective treatments. Immune checkpoint inhibitors like ipilimumab, durvalumab, and pembrolizumab are being explored in combination with chemotherapy (gemcitabine, nab-paclitaxel), but substantial therapeutic progress is still needed [4].

Treatment strategies targeting CLDN18.2 have shown promising results in inoperable or recurrent GC patients [5]. The most advanced CLDN18.2-targeting therapy is a mAb called zolbetuximab (formerly known as IMAB362). Recent clinical trials demonstrated that the addition of zolbetuximab to standard chemotherapy prolonged survival and improved objective response rate relative to chemotherapy alone. This indicates that CLDN18.2 is a promising target in patients with AGC or gastroesophageal junction cancer and potentially other cancer indications with known CLDN18.2 overexpression [6–8]. Beyond naked mAbs, multiple CLDN18.2-directed therapeutic modalities are currently under clinical investigation, including antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs), bispecific antibodies and T-cell engagers [9], and cellular therapies such as CAR-T cells [10]. These approaches aim to enhance antitumor efficacy by leveraging cytotoxic payload delivery or immune cell redirection. Collectively, the expanding clinical landscape underscores the therapeutic relevance of CLDN18.2 and supports continued exploration of novel strategies targeting CLDN18.2 [11].

The Claudin (CLDN) family of transmembrane proteins consists of at least 27 members and is essential for tight junction formation and intracellular signal transduction. CLDN18.1 and CLDN18.2 are closely related splice variants, with their expression controlled by distinct tissue-specific promoters [12, 13]. Importantly, the first extracellular loop which is of interest for the development of therapeutic antibodies against CLDN18.2, differs by 8 amino acids from the CLDN18.1 sequence [13]. Whereas CLDN18.1 is exclusively expressed in healthy lungs, CLDN18.2 is expressed in tight junctions of healthy stomach mucosal membrane epithelial cells [13, 14]. In addition, CLDN18.2 was described as a pan-cancer target [14] and its mRNA or protein expression at significant levels has been reported in primary or metastatic malignancies of gastric, esophageal [14, 15], pancreatic [14–16], ovarian [14, 17], liver, colorectal [14] and lung [18] origin.

Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) represent a means of delivering powerful cytotoxic drugs using mAb based targeting of tumor-associated surface antigens. Tumor-targeted delivery of the cytotoxic payload aims to minimize systemic toxicity, which occurs when using standard chemotherapy regimens. Antibody specificity, target protein expression in cancer and healthy tissues, internalization of the protein-drug complex, payload selection, and drug linker design are all important aspects when developing and using ADCs in patients [19]. As of 2025, 14 ADCs have been approved by U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and more than 100 ADCs are in ongoing clinical trials for various cancer indications [20].

In this context, SOT102 was developed as a CLDN18.2 targeting antibody-drug conjugate based on a novel proprietary CLDN18.2-specific mAb, conjugated via site-specific technology to a derivative of the highly potent anthracycline PNU-159682. In the present study, SOT102 showed strong antitumor efficacy in various in vitro and in vivo cancer models. SOT102 also demonstrated a manageable safety profile along with favorable developability parameters.

Methods

For clarity, the following terminology is used throughout the manuscript: SOT102.1 mAb(wt) refers to the unconjugated humanized IgG1 antibody without Fc mutations. SOT102.1 mAb refers to the Fc-mutated variant containing L236A/L237A substitutions. SOT102 refers exclusively to the LALA-based antibody–drug conjugate with a drug-antibody ratio (DAR) of 2 and a non-cleavable linker.

Cell lines

A549 (American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, CCL-185), BxPC3 (ATCC, CRL-1687), NK92-CD16 (ATCC, CRL-2407) and PaTu 8988 S (Creative Bioarray, New York, USA, CSC-C0326) were cultivated according to provider´s recommendation. PaTu 8988 S (high) cells represent a clone, sorted for high CLDN18.2 expression. A549, BxPC3 and HEK293T cells expressing human CLDN18.1 or CLDN18.2 were transfected via transposon-based vector and PiggyBac transposase expression vector as previously described [21].

SOT102.1 antibody generation

Rat mAbs against CLDN18.2 were generated by DNA vaccination according to established rat immunization protocols by Aldevron (Genovac, Fargo, ND, USA) using a plasmid encoding the human CLDN18.2 sequence. Hybridoma supernatants binding to CLDN18.2 were sequenced and subsequently humanized (Modiquest, Netherlands). The selected humanized IgG1 antibody was designated SOT102.1 mAb(wt). A Fc-mutated variant containing L236A and L237A substitutions (LALA mutations) was generated and designated SOT102.1 mAb (WO2021/130291). A mAb with the zolbetuximab (IMAB362) sequence was generated using information from the patent WO2016166122_A1 (heavy chain SEQ ID NO: 17, light chain SEQ ID NO: 24).

In vitro binding assessment

Binding to CLDN18.2 was assessed by immunofluorescence using HEK293T cells stably transfected with CLDN18.1 or CLDN18.2. Cells were incubated with SOT102.1 mAb, SOT102.1 mAb(wt) or a pan-CLDN18 proprietary clone at 2 µg/ml for 30 min on ice, followed by a washing step and a 30 min incubation with a fluorescently labeled anti-human IgG (eBioscience, San Diego, CA, USA, 12-4998-82). After final washes, cells were analyzed by flow cytometry to assess binding specificity. CLDN18.1-transfected cells served as a negative specificity control, and the pan-CLDN18 antibody was included as a positive control.

Lipoparticle-based binding assay

Binding of SOT102 variants to CLDN18.2 was assessed in an enzyme linked immuno-assay (ELISA) format using lipoparticles as purified recombinant CLDN18.2 was not possible to generate, likely because of its four transmembrane domain structure. Lipoparticles were generated from CLDN18.2-transfected or non-transfected cells (Integral Molecular, Philadelphia, PA, USA) and were coated onto 96-well plates according to the manufacturer’s protocol presenting CLDN18.2 in a native-like lipid environment. Monoclonal antibodies were added to coated plates, and bound antibody was detected using a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated anti-human Fc polyclonal antibody (Jackson ImmunoResearch, Ely, UK) together with a standard chromogenic substrate and determining absorbance.

Internalization assay

SOT102.1 mAb was complexed with pHrodo™ Red labeled anti-IgG antibodies (Microscale Labeling Kit, Invitrogen, Grand Island, NY, USA, P35363) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. CLDN18.2-expressing cells were then incubated with pHrodo-labeled SOT102.1 mAb at 10 µg/mL and at 37°C to allow internalization. pHrodo exhibits increased fluorescence with a peak at 585 nm in acidic intracellular compartments which was acquired using an LSR Fortessa flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, Bedford, MA, USA). Data was analyzed as relative fluorescence intensity over time and compared to baseline to assess antibody internalization.

Fc receptor binding

Fc receptor binding was evaluated by single-cycle surface plasmon resonance (SPR) using a Biacore™ T200 instrument and a CM5 sensor chip (GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA). Recombinant human Fcγ receptors (FcγRI, FcγRII, FcγRIII) and FcRn (Sino Biological, Beijing, China) were immobilized on the sensor surface according to standard amine-coupling procedures at densities optimized for kinetic analysis. SOT102 variants were injected at increasing concentrations spanning a low nanomolar to micromolar range in a single-cycle format. Binding responses were recorded and analyzed using Biacore Evaluation Software. Where applicable, binding affinities (KD) were calculated; for interactions that did not allow reliable kinetic fitting, binding was reported as normalized response units relative to reference antibodies. Rituximab and pembrolizumab, obtained from a local pharmacy, were included for comparison.

C1q binding

Binding of mAbs to human complement C1q was assessed by ELISA. Antibody samples were coated onto 96-well plates at concentrations ranging from 0.02 to 20 µg/mL. Plates were incubated with purified human C1q (Quidel, San Diego, CA, USA; A400) at 12 µg/mL, followed by detection using an HRP-conjugated anti-human C1q antibody (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA; 2221-5004P). Following addition of a chromogenic substrate, absorbance was measured to assess relative C1q binding.

ADCC

ADCC activity was assessed in vitro using NK92-CD16 effector cells and A549 target cells engineered to express human CLDN18.2. Effector and target cells were co-incubated at an appropriate effector-to-target ratio in the presence of test antibodies for 24 h at 37°C. As a surrogate for cytotoxicity, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release was determined using a commercially available kit (Abcam, Waltham, MA, USA; ab65393) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Background LDH release from effector-only and target-only controls was measured and subtracted from experimental values to account for spontaneous cytotoxicity. Isotype control antibodies were included as indicated.

SOT102 ADC generation

The SOT102.1 mAb sequence contains an LPQTG sortase recognition sequence at the C-terminus of each light chain. Site-specific conjugation of PNU-159682 was performed using sortase A-mediated transpeptidation with a glycine–glycine–ethylene diamine (Gly₂-EDA)–modified payload, as previously described [22]. This resulted in PNU-159682 conjugation to the light chains of the SOT102.1 mAb containing the LALA modification via a non-cleavable linker at a DAR of 2 (WO2022/136642). The ADC consisting of the zolbetuximab-LPQTG sequence was generated accordingly.

In vitro cytotoxicity

The cytotoxic activity of SOT102 was assessed using the CellTiter-Glo® luminescent cell viability assay (Promega, Madison, WI, USA; G9683), which quantifies intracellular adenosine triphosphate as a surrogate marker of metabolically active cells. Target cells were seeded in appropriate culture medium and incubated with serial dilutions of SOT102 or control antibodies. Following incubation periods ranging from 96 h to 5 days, CellTiter-Glo reagent was added according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Luminescence signals were measured using an EnVision plate reader (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA). Cell viability was expressed as a percentage relative to untreated control cells. Dose–response curves were generated, and half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC₅₀) were calculated using nonlinear regression.

In vivo PDX and syngeneic CDX models

Human PDX tumor fragments were implanted into the flanks of female NMRI nu/nu mice (n = 3 per group, 21.1 ± 0.7 g, 6–7 weeks) obtained from Charles River Laboratories. Once tumors reached 50–250 mm³, mice were randomized based on body weight to ensure group comparability using a computer-generated sequence and treated with SOT102 at 0.2, 0.6, or 2.0 mg/kg, administered intravenously (i.v.) on Days 1, 8, and 15. Compounds were administered via tail vein injection. Antitumor efficacy was assessed using a vehicle or isotype control group. The study concluded between Days 35 and 66 after which tumor samples were collected, fixed in formalin, and embedded in paraffin for further analysis.

The combination of SOT102 and anti-PD-1 treatment was evaluated in MC38 mouse tumors expressing human CLDN18.2, implanted into the right flanks of female C57BL/6 mice (n = 8 per group, 18.0 ± 1.5 g, 6–8 weeks, received from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd.). Mice were inoculated subcutaneously with 5 × 10⁵ B-hCLDN18.2 MC38 cells. Once tumors reached 80–100 mm³, 40 mice were assigned to 5 groups, and treatment began on Day 0. SOT102 (1 mg/kg, i.v.) was administered intravenously on Day 0, and anti-PD-1 antibody was given intra peritoneally (i.p.) at 0.5 mg/kg on Days 0, 3, and 6. The observation period lasted up to 5 weeks.

Control groups received vehicle, anti-human CD30 Ac10 or isotype control consisting of the Motavizumab sequence conjugated to PNU-159682 in the same manner as SOT102 [23]. Control treatments were not blinded. Body weight and tumor volume were measured twice weekly. Tumor volume determined using calipers was calculated as V = 0.5 × a × b², and relative tumor volumes (RTVs) were calculated accordingly. All animal studies were conducted at Charles River DRS Germany GmbH (Freiburg, Germany) or Biocytogen (Beijing/Haimen, China) in accordance with institutional, national, and international guidelines for animal welfare and complied with ARRIVE reporting guidelines. Humane endpoints were predefined and included tumor volume ≥ 2000 mm³ (PDX study) and ≥ 3000 mm³ (CDX study), ulceration or skin penetration, body weight loss > 20%, or signs of severe distress. Animals were housed in individually ventilated cages under controlled environmental conditions (22 ± 2 °C, 45–65% humidity, 12 h light/dark cycle) with ad libitum access to food and water. Euthanasia was performed by CO₂ inhalation by authorized personnel in accordance with approved protocols.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining of formalin-fixed samples obtained from relevant organoid and PDX tumors and human gastric and pancreatic cancer tissue microarrays (TMA), was performed using mAb 43–14 A (VENTANA CLDN18, Ventana Medical Systems, Inc./Roche Diagnostics). Staining was performed according to the manufacturer’s recommended protocol. Slides were scanned by Aperio AT2 (Leica Biosystems, Wetzlar, Germany). H-score was determined based on (% of negative cells*0 + % of low positive cells*1 + % of medium positive cells*2 + % of strongly positive cells*3) using HALO software following analysis of the entire sample. A tissue cross-reactivity analysis was performed to evaluate CLDN18.2 expression in normal human and cynomolgus monkey tissues. Frozen optimal cutting temperature (OCT)-embedded sections of healthy organs were obtained from the internal tissue bank of Charles River Laboratories (France). IHC staining was performed at Charles River Laboratories (France) using biotinylated SOT102.1. Staining was carried out on OCT tissue sections using a Ventana Roche Discovery XT automated immunostainer (Roche, Mannheim, Germany) with Ventana reagents according to standard protocols.

Plasma stability, pharmacokinetic and toxicology studies

In vitro plasma stability studies with SOT102 were aimed at assessing a potential deconjugation of PNU-159682. SOT102 was incubated in pooled human plasma buffered with 5% (v/v) HEPES (20 mM final, pH 7.4) at concentrations of 20 µg/ml and 200 µg/ml in the dark at 37°C using 200 RPM rocking. Two types of sandwich ELISA were used to determine SOT102 concentrations in plasma samples. One format employed two different anti-human IgG antibodies for capture and detection, allowing SOT102 quantification based on the IgG concentration (designated total SOT102). The second format employed an anti-SOT102 idiotypic mAb for capture and an anti-PNU-159682 specific mAb (clone NBE4D5, NBE-Therapeutics, Basel, Switzerland) for detection, allowing SOT102 quantification based on conjugated PNU-159682 (designated conjugated SOT102). Abs for detection were conjugated with HRP before use and the generated signals were measured by absorbance using a Spark microplate reader (Tecan Austria GmbH, Grödig, Austria). Gender-pooled human K3EDTA plasma (BioIVT) was obtained under an Institutional Review Board (IRB)-approved protocol, with prospective collection of biological specimens from consenting donors for research purposes. All procedures complied with ethical guidelines for human subject research.

The safety of SOT102 was studied in a toxicology study according to Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) in cynomolgus monkeys (Macaca fascicularis) (5 male and 5 female animals per dosing group, 40 animals in total) following i.v. bolus administration at 0 mg/kg (control group treated with vehicle), 0.3 mg/kg, 0.6 mg/kg and 1.0 mg/kg at Day 1, Day 15, and Day 29. Animals were obtained from Le Tamarinier, Tamarin, Mauritius, the age at initiation of dosing was from 26 to 42 months old and the weight was from 2.51 kg to 6.11 kg for males and from 2.54 kg to 3.67 kg for females. This study was designed to utilize the minimum number of animals to accomplish its objective to characterize the safety of SOT102. Males and females were randomized separately to achieve similar group mean body weights. All animals were kept in an AAALAC accredited and GLP compliant facility under standard laboratory conditions with cage environmental enrichments. The study was performed under the supervision of veterinarians and in compliance with animal health regulations, in particular Council Directive No. 2010/63/EU and French decree No. 2013 − 118 of 01 February 2013 and authorized by French authorities under APAFIS registration # 2,015,122,215,443,923 and an approved ethical committee. Clinical signs, body weight, food consumption, and clinical pathology were monitored throughout the study. In main study animals, necropsy was conducted one week after the last administration (Day 36) and tissues were examined by histopathology. The potential reversibility of any findings was assessed in a four-week recovery period followed by necropsy with histopathology evaluation on Day 57 in recovery study animals. Data from this study were analyzed by means of the descriptive statistics, such as means, standard deviations, percentages and number of observations. SOT102 concentrations were determined in plasma samples obtained after dosing on Day 1 and Day 29 at time points 0.5 h, 3 h, 8 h, 24 h, 72 h, 120 h, 168 h, 264 h, 336 h following SOT102 administration. Pharmacokinetic parameters were derived for both total and conjugated SOT102 to assess systemic exposure and linker–payload stability in vivo using Phoenix WinNonLin software (Certara, Radnor, PA, USA). This study adhered to the ARRIVE guidelines for animal research reporting.

Statistical analysis

IC₅₀ values for in vitro assays (C1q ELISA, ADCC, and cytotoxicity) were determined by nonlinear regression analysis of concentration–response curves using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). For PDX studies, tumor growth data were analyzed by ordinary one-way ANOVA to compare treatment groups. For CDX studies, statistical significance was determined by two-tailed Mann–Whitney test comparing each treatment group with the isotype control at day 21 (**p < 0.01, ****p < 0.0001). Patient derived organoids were analyzed using Spearman correlation analysis. Data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.

Results

SOT102.1 mAb specifically binds to CLDN18.2 and promotes effective internalization

Specific anti-CLDN18.2 mAbs were generated following genetic immunization of rats. Hybridomas were selected by means of CLDN18.2 binding and subsequently humanized. SOT102.1 mAb(wt) was selected and engineered within the Fc region to contain a LALA substitution with the aim of reducing effector functions. The resulting SOT102.1 mAb demonstrated reduced binding to recombinant human FcγRIa, the two polymorphic FcγRIa variants, FcγRIIb, the two polymorphic FcγRIIIa variants and FcγRIIIb when compared to the reference antibody rituximab and to the Fc-unmodified SOT102.1 mAb(wt) as shown in Table S1 (Additional file 1). Binding affinities of SOT102.1 mAb were similar or lower compared to the IgG4 reference antibody pembrolizumab. In contrast to altered interactions with Fcg receptors, binding of SOT102.1 mAb to human FcRn was unchanged and comparable to all control mAbs, including SOT102.1 mAb(wt). Thus, SOT102.1 mAb showed FcRn binding properties that are typical for human IgG1 isotype, but significantly reduced FcγR binding (Table S1).

SOT102.1 mAb and SOT102.1 mAb(wt) selectively bound to CLDN18.2 but showed no detectable binding to CLDN18.1 when tested with HEK293T transfectants expressing either isoform (Fig. 1A). Using CLDN18.2 expressing lipoparticles in an ELISA format, half maximal binding was observed above 0.9 nM or 133 ng/ml (Fig. 1B). Furthermore, the internalization properties of SOT102.1 mAb (but not SOT102.1 (wt) mAb) are shown in Fig. 1C. These binding and internalization properties of SOT102.1 mAb were not affected by the LALA modification within the Fc region as shown by comparison with SOT102.1 mAb(wt) (Fig. 1A, B, C).

Differences between SOT102.1 mAb and SOT102.1 mAb(wt) were observed when C1q complement binding and antibody-dependent cytotoxicity (ADCC) were investigated. C1q binding to SOT102.1 mAb was substantially reduced when compared to SOT102.1 mAb(wt) and rituximab (IgG1) but was comparable to levels observed with pembrolizumab (IgG4) (Fig. 1D). SOT102.1 mAb stimulated NK92 natural killer cells to exhibit ADCC activity against the CLDN18.2 transfected pancreatic adenocarcinoma cell line BxPC3 at an IC50 of approximately 0.5 nM or 0.08 µg/ml. In contrast, SOT102.1 mAb(wt) was more potent as demonstrated by an approximately 10-fold lower IC50 (0.05 nM, Fig. 1E). This weaker ADCC activity of SOT102.1 mAb, compared to the wild-type IgG Fc sequence is in line with its reduced binding properties to FcgRIIIa described above.

SOT102 exerts potent anti-tumor activity which is enhanced in vivo by anti-PD-1 combination

SOT102 is a novel antibody-drug conjugate which contains PNU-159682 as payload in a DAR2 light chain format. SOT102 exhibited potent killing in vitro at an IC50 ranging from 0.02 nM to 0.5 nM against CLDN18.2-transfected HEK293T cells and PaTu 8988 S cells with endogenous target expression, respectively (Fig. 2A). The benchmark antibody with the zolbetuximab sequence, conjugated to PNU-159682 in the same DAR2 light chain format as SOT102, was less potent against target-positive cells in comparison to SOT102. Cytotoxicity of SOT102 against CLDN18.1-transfected HEK293T cells was not observed, confirming the specificity for CLDN18.2. The non-binding PNU-159682 conjugated isotype control did not show activity supporting a CLDN18.2 mediated uptake mechanism required for SOT102 cytotoxicity.

The potency of SOT102 was further investigated in vitro using organoids derived from human gastric, pancreatic, and lung tumor samples for which CLDN18.2 expression was determined by IHC. Tumor samples expressing CLDN18.2 at an H-score between 70 and 180 were effectively lysed with an IC50 between 0.2 nM and 19.4 nM (Fig. 2B). In contrast, the IC50 of SOT102 was 274 nM when tested with one sample demonstrating an H-score of 5 (Fig. 2B). CLDN18.2 negative tumor organoids (n = 7) were affected by SOT102 only at similarly high concentrations with an average IC50 of 406 nM (Fig. 2B). Overall, these findings suggest that SOT102 cytotoxic activity is associated with CLDN18.2 expression levels.

The efficacy of SOT102 in vivo was investigated using patient-derived tumor xenograft models of gastric, pancreatic, and lung adenocarcinoma origin demonstrating membranous CLDN18.2 expression at H-scores between 11 and 174 as indicated in Fig. 3. Tumor regression and/or strong inhibition of tumor growth were observed in all models tested following SOT102 administration at 2.0 mg/kg and at a QW*3 schedule, across tumor types and range of CLDN18.2 expression levels tested as shown in Fig. 3. As the lowest tested dose of 0.2 mg/kg substantially inhibited tumor growth compared to the vehicle control group when studied in three different models, this dose was considered the minimal effective dose at the QW*3 schedule. The control-PNU-159682 conjugate showed some tumor inhibitory effect compared to vehicle control which was similarly described for other ADCs [24, 25]. SOT102 treatment was well tolerated. Doses up to 2 mg/kg had only low effects on body weight with maximal reductions below 8% (Fig. S1A-E).

As ADC-induced tumor cell lysis can induce immune responses [26] and as check-point inhibitory mAbs are known to support such anti-tumor immune responses, the combination of SOT102 with anti-PD-1 therapy was investigated in a mouse syngeneic MC38 tumor variant expressing human CLDN18.2. Neither SOT102 (administered once (Day 0) at a dose of 1 mg/kg i.v.) nor anti-PD-1 (administered three times (Day 0, Day 3 and Day 6) at a dose of 0.5 mg/kg i.p.) led to significant tumor growth reduction or regression as monotherapy in this mouse MC38 model. However, the combination of SOT102 and anti-PD-1 led to durable antitumor responses that lasted up to day 40 in individual animals (Fig. 4). Single and combination doses were well tolerated, and body weight of the animals increased throughout the study (Fig. S1F).

SOT102 has a favorable pharmacokinetic profile and is well-tolerated in cynomolgus monkeys

The pharmacokinetics of SOT102 was investigated in tumor bearing mice to support calculation of an exposure-based therapeutic index based on the minimal efficacious dose in mice and the HNSTD in NHP. A dose of 0.2 mg/kg, representing the lowest tested active dose in tumor efficacy studies, demonstrated a Cmax of 2.1 µg/ml, a half-life of 6 days and an exposure by means of area under the concentration curve (AUC) of 169 µg*h/mL (Table S2).

The cynomolgus monkey was determined a pharmacologically relevant species based on high sequence homology of CLDN18.2 (98.9% overall, 100% in the first extracellular loop) [13], confirmed cross-reactivity of SOT102 to epithelial cells of frozen sections of human and cynomolgus monkey gastric tissues and comparable CLDN18.2 tissue expression between human and cynomolgus monkeys (Table S3, Fig. S2). In a first step, the stability of SOT102 in cynomolgus plasma at 37°C was investigated in vitro at two different concentrations and compared with the stability in human plasma. Total and conjugated SOT102 concentrations on Day 7 were at about 100% of the starting concentrations at timepoint 0 with only limited fluctuations in between. Importantly, the ratio between conjugated and total SOT102 was close to constant over the incubation period. This indicates that the proportion of conjugated PNU-159682 remained constant over 7 days at 37°C (Fig. 5A).

The pharmacokinetics of SOT102 in cynomolgus monkeys were investigated during a toxicology study in this species following i.v. administration of SOT102 at 0.3 mg/kg, 0.6 mg/kg and 1.0 mg/kg. Similarly, as observed in vitro, the concentrations of total and conjugated SOT102 in plasma samples were comparable over the course of 14 days (Fig. 5B). Cmax of SOT102 was reached immediately after intravenous administration (Fig. 4B). Selected pharmacokinetic parameters are shown in Table S2. The terminal elimination half-life was approximately 7 days (range between 4.4 and 8.1 days). Exposure in terms of Cmax and AUClast increased close to dose proportional and, thus, followed linear kinetics over the dose range of 0.3 mg/kg to 1.0 mg/kg. The volume of distribution of SOT102 was close to the plasma volume, typical for mAb based therapeutics and low levels of unintended tissue distribution.

The safety of SOT102 was investigated in cynomolgus monkeys following i.v. administration at 0.3 mg/kg, 0.6 mg/kg, and 1.0 mg/kg for four weeks at two-week intervals (Days 1, 15, and 29), followed by a 4-week recovery period. The gastrointestinal tract was identified as the primary target organ for toxicity, correlating with body weight losses and clinical pathology changes (Table S4, Fig. S3). At 0.3 mg/kg, histopathological findings in the stomach were present but not accompanied by any other corresponding changes. At 0.6 mg/kg, the effects in stomach impacted the clinical condition, body weights and related clinical chemistry parameters, however the toxicity at this dose level was judged as non-severe. At 1 mg/kg, the effects on stomach (mucosal atrophy, inflammation, hyperplasia, and erosion/ulcer) led to more pronounced body weight losses and, for 2 animals, to a deteriorating clinical condition and premature euthanasia. Consequently, the dose level of 0.6 mg/kg was considered the highest non-severe toxic dose (HNSTD).

Prevalence of CLDN18.2 expression in patients with advanced gastric and pancreatic cancer

mAb 43–14 A is used as a companion diagnostic to select patients for CLDN18.2 targeted therapy. When using mAb 43–14 A in immunohistochemistry analyses, fractions of 29% and 39% of gastric cancer samples were observed with high (H-score 150–300) and moderate (H-score 70–149) CLDN18.2 expression, respectively (Fig. 6A). A proportion of 32% of gastric cancer samples demonstrated low levels of CLDN18.2 expression (H-score 5–69) (Fig. 6A). The prevalence of CLDN18.2 expression was higher in pancreatic than in gastric cancer samples with high, moderate and low expression levels of 60%, 35%, and 5%, respectively (Fig. 6B). Discussion

Targeting claudin family members with mAb-based therapeutics, including ADCs, represents an expanding strategy in oncology. Several CLDN18.2 ADCs are currently being investigated mainly in patients with advanced gastric, gastroesophageal junction and pancreatic cancer and a CLDN6 antibody conjugated with the payload MMAE is being evaluated in patients with advanced ovarian, testicular and endometrial cancer [27, 28]. Here we have evaluated the antitumor activity, the pharmacokinetics and safety of SOT102, a novel CLDN18.2-targeting ADC consisting of a proprietary SOT102.1 mAb, conjugated to PNU-159682 payload with a DAR of 2. The selection of PNU-159682 as payload was based on its mechanism of action as a topoisomerase II inhibitor, leading to DNA damage through induction of DNA cross-linking, a mechanism independent of cell cycle. Anthracyclines are widely used in the treatment of multiple malignancies, including breast cancer, lymphomas, and other rapidly proliferating tumors, supporting the clinical relevance of this payload class [29, 30]. Given the heterogeneous expression of CLDN18.2 in solid tumors, this highly potent payload was selected to ensure effective cytotoxicity even at moderate antigen density. Moreover, bystander killing has been previously reported for PNU-159682 based ADCs [31]. The DAR2 configuration was selected to balance potency and tolerability while maintaining a defined and homogeneous conjugate profile.

SOT102.1 mAb showed selective binding to CLDN18.2-positive cells, with no detectable cross-reactivity to CLDN18.1 and interacted with CLDN18.2 containing liposomes at low nanomolar concentrations. Efficient internalization was observed within 24 h. Binding and internalization studies were performed using transfected cell systems which may not fully reflect physiological antigen density and, thus, correct internalization kinetics. The introduction of LALA substitutions reduced Fcγ receptor binding and ADCC activity and abolished complement binding, while preserving FcRn interaction and CLDN18.2 target recognition. Binding studies were limited to the experimental systems presented, and detailed saturation kinetics were not further explored. However, these studies clearly demonstrated reduced binding to all Fcγ receptors tested compared to unmodified IgG1 mAbs. The ADCC assay employed engineered target cells, an NK effector cell line, and an LDH-based readout and was therefore intended to compare Fc-mediated cytotoxicity of mAbs rather than providing a definitive assessment of physiological ADCC activity. The LALA modified SOT102.1 mAb demonstrated remaining ADCC activity which is in line with other reports [32, 33]. However, the experimental setup may explain the rather low difference in activity between non-modified and LALA-modified mAbs. Half maximal killing by SOT102 (IC50) in vitro was observed at 0.02 nM and 0.5 nM using cultured target cell lines and between 0.2 nM and 19.4 nM when tested with human tumor-derived organoids. Importantly, CLDN18.2 expression at an H-score ≥ 70 appeared to represent a threshold for efficient cell lysis under in vitro conditions. As the zolbetuximab sequence was conjugated using the same platform and payload, the observed differences in cytotoxic potency were likely attributable to antibody-specific properties, such as binding affinity, epitope recognition, or internalization kinetics rather than the conjugation technology and payload itself. A further mechanistic attribution was not performed in the present study to clarify that no further characterization studies were done to verify differences between SOT102 and zolbetuximab-PNU-159682 ADC.

In in vivo PDX models, SOT102 showed strong anti-tumor efficacy against a range of patient-derived tumors. Inhibition of tumor progression was observed, but more significantly, tumor regressions were consistently maintained far beyond the 25-day treatment period, suggesting that the responses to SOT102 are robust and durable. In vivo efficacy of SOT102 weakly correlated with CLDN18.2 expression (in contrast to our observations in vitro). A heterogeneity between the tumor models employed regarding antigen-dependent internalization, lysosomal processing and release of the payload may have contributed to this observation. On the other hand, target independent mechanisms may have been involved, also contributing to the lower correlation of in vivo activity with CLDN18.2 expression. In this light, we noticed that the isotype-PNU-159682 ADC was able to inhibit tumor growth, at least to some extent. This target independent effect of isotype ADC was also observed by others [24, 25], and may be explained by the use of high potency payloads such as PNU-159682 and an effect on stromal cells or other cells in the tumor microenvironment, known to exhibit unspecific ADC uptake by pinocytosis and release of the payload by proteolytic breakdown [34, 35].

The potential of SOT102 mediated anti-tumor activity to induce an immune response which may benefit from anti PD-1 treatment was investigated in immunocompetent mice carrying MC38 tumors that expressed human CLDN18.2. This study was based on previous findings with the anti-HER2 antibody trastuzumab conjugated to PNU-159682 and the induction of immunogenic cell death and CD8+ T cell mediated tumor growth inhibition in a murine EMT6-hHER2 model [26]. At the selected suboptimal doses, neither SOT102 nor anti-PD-1 monotherapy induced marked tumor regression. In contrast, the SOT102 / anti-PD-1 combination resulted in enhanced tumor growth control and delayed tumor progression compared to either agent alone. Although complete responses were not observed, detailed immune profiling characterizing the mechanisms underlying the combinatorial effect were not performed, and the combination of an isotype-ADC with PD-1 was not tested, the presented findings support the hypothesis that CLDN18.2-targeted delivery of a cytotoxic payload may complement checkpoint inhibition. Previous reports describing an associations between CLDN18.2 expression, immune cell infiltration, and PD-L1 expression [36] provide further biological context for exploring this combination strategy.

The cynomolgus monkey was determined as a relevant species to investigate the pharmacokinetics and safety in animals in support of initiating a clinical study in patients. A tissue cross-reactivity study demonstrated the selective expression of CLDN18.2 and binding of SOT102 to epithelial cells in the stomach of both human and cynomolgus monkey origin.

ADCs may be unstable and exhibit high clearance, contributing to reduced efficacy and increasing toxicity [37]. SOT102 showed very good stability in cynomolgus monkey and human plasma in vitro. The robust stability of SOT102 was confirmed in cynomolgus monkeys together with dose-proportional pharmacokinetics and a half-life of approximately 7 days. Based on CLDN18.2 expression on stomach epithelial cells, the stomach was identified as the main organ of potential toxicity, which was previously observed with zolbetuximab [6, 7]. The HNSTD of SOT102 was established at 0.6 mg/kg.

The AUC of SOT102 at the HNSTD in cynomolgus monkeys (1,407 µg*h/mL) was approximately 8-fold higher than the AUC at the minimal effective dose in mice (169 µg*h/mL) (Table S2). This supports that pharmacological anti-tumor effects may be achieved in patients without dose-limiting toxicities, albeit the normal tissue expression of CLDN18.2 in the stomach. A starting dose for first investigations of SOT102 in patients (0.032 mg/kg) was calculated by converting the HNSTD in cynomolgus monkeys into its human equivalent (allometric scaling factor of 3.1) and by applying a 6-fold safety margin [38]. Human SOT102 clearance was calculated to be 0.28 mL/kg according to allometric scaling (Fig. S4) and at the starting dose of 0.032 mg/kg, SOT102 was predicted to achieve an AUC of 114 µg*h/mL (based on AUC = Dose / CL). This was considered safe enough with the potential of exerting low pharmacological activity.

CLDN18.2 is the predominant CLDN18 isoform expressed in gastric, pancreatic, and esophageal cancer indications [14–16, 39]. Zolbetuximab showed positive results in patients that were diagnosed with moderate to strong membranous CLDN18.2 expression on ≥ 75% tumor cells (representing a minimal H-score of 150) using mAb 43–14 A [6, 7]. As a result, mAb 43–14 A was approved by the FDA as companion diagnostic device to identify patients for treatment with zolbetuximab [40]. In the present study, CLDN18.2 expression was assessed using the same antibody, enabling direct comparison with clinically established thresholds. Using mAb 43–14 A, we demonstrated a prevalence of 29% and 60% of primary and metastatic gastric and pancreatic patient samples, respectively, with CLDN18.2 H-scores between 150 and 300 (corresponding to ≥ 75% moderate [2+] to strong [3+] expression). A multicentric phase I/II trial aiming at determining the safety, pharmacokinetics and preliminary activity of SOT102 in patients with advanced gastric and pancreatic cancer in correlation with their CLDN18.2 expression levels was initiated (NCT05525286). However, due to unexpected toxicities which were judged unrelated to CLDN18.2 expression, this study was terminated.

Conclusions

In conclusion, SOT102 demonstrated antitumor activity in CLDN18.2-positive preclinical models and exhibited a pharmacological profile. The preclinical findings reported here were used to support the initiation of a phase I/II trial in advanced gastric and pancreatic cancer patients (NCT05525286). Although clinical development was subsequently discontinued, these findings provide insight into the pharmacologic and mechanistic properties of this CLDN18.2-targeting ADC and contribute to the broader understanding of ADC strategies directed against oncology targets.

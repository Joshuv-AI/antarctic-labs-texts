Analysis of mortality incidence and risk factors in patients with renal failure undergoing dialysis treatment within five years
Authors: Jingjing Li, Haipeng Liu, Ying Wang, Cuiliu Yang, Jian Liang, Yulan Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Currently, the most common renal replacement therapy is undoubtedly dialysis. Adequate and effective dialysis not only improves the quality of life for patients but also reduces complications from renal failure and delays the further deterioration of kidney function. This study explores the related mortality risk factors for patients with renal failure undergoing dialysis treatment for 5 years, aiming to provide clinical physicians with a reference for improving the survival rate of dialysis patients. A retrospective analysis was conducted on 119 patients who underwent dialysis treatment for renal failure from July 2011 to October 2019. Baseline data and pre- and post-dialysis data were collected. A comparison was made between the survival group and the death group within 5 years of dialysis treatment. Additionally, univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify the risk factors leading to mortality during dialysis treatment. The 5-year mortality rate for patients undergoing dialysis treatment for renal failure was 41.18% (49/119). Statistical differences were found between the survival group and the death group in terms of age (P < .001) and pre-dialysis blood creatinine (P = .002). Univariate and multivariate regression analyses identified age (P < .001) and pre-dialysis blood creatinine (P = .004) as risk factors for mortality in dialysis patients. Furthermore, age (P = .005) and pre-dialysis blood creatinine level (P = .027) were determined to be independent risk factors for mortality in patients undergoing dialysis treatment for 5 years. Age and pre-dialysis blood creatinine level are independent risk factors for mortality in patients undergoing dialysis treatment for 5 years.

1. Introduction

Chronic kidney failure (CKF) is a disease characterized by the gradual loss of kidney function over months or years, ultimately progressing to end-stage kidney disease (ESKD), which requires treatment through dialysis or kidney transplantation, and this condition is associated with premature mortality.[1–3] Kidney transplantation is the best treatment option for most ESKD patients; however, due to the ongoing shortage of organs, its clinical implementation is limited.[4] The most common treatment method remains dialysis, which can improve patients’ quality of life by partially replacing kidney function.[5,6]

Dialysis treatment plays a crucial role in CKF patients, as it not only alleviates symptoms such as nausea, vomiting, and fatigue caused by CKF but also prolongs patients’ lives in the short term, thereby buying time for kidney transplantation.[7] However, with the extension of dialysis duration, the effectiveness of dialysis gradually declines due to various factors, which consequently affects the patients’ quality of life.[8,9]

Despite the continuous advancement of dialysis treatment, the mortality rate among patients receiving dialysis remains high, and there is no consensus on the factors influencing mortality in these patients.[9–13] Previous comprehensive studies on the risk factors for mortality in dialysis patients have mostly been systematic reviews, with few studies based on real-world clinical environments and data. The risk factors for mortality among dialysis patients in real-world settings are still unclear. This study aims to explore the risk factors affecting mortality in patients undergoing dialysis treatment within 5 years and to guide clinical practice using the identified risk factors, providing a reference for safer and more efficient dialysis treatment for CKF patients in the future.

2. Method

A total of 149 patients with CKF received dialysis treatment at Peking University First Hospital-Miyun Hospital between July 2011 and October 2019. Among them, 70 patients were still receiving dialysis treatment at the hospital in the 5th year, 49 patients had died, and 30 patients were lost to follow-up (Fig. 1). The real-world data collected in this study includes baseline data for 119 CKF patients, data from the 1st dialysis session, and survival status within 5 years after dialysis. The baseline data includes age, gender, weight, hypertension, diabetes, and dialysis vascular access. Data before the 1st dialysis includes pre-dialysis systolic blood pressure, pre-dialysis diastolic blood pressure, pre-dialysis hemoglobin, pre-dialysis hematocrit, pre-dialysis serum creatinine, pre-dialysis blood albumin, pre-dialysis serum phosphorus, pre-dialysis serum calcium, pre-dialysis serum magnesium, pre-dialysis potassium, pre-dialysis serum sodium, pre-dialysis bicarbonate level, pre-dialysis intact parathyroid hormone, pre-dialysis serum ferritin, and pre-dialysis transferrin saturation. Data after the 1st dialysis includes the survival status of patients within 5 years.

Flow of study.

Inclusion criteria: age 18 years or older; CKF requiring dialysis treatment; continuous dialysis for 5 years or until death; complete baseline data and follow-up data. Exclusion criteria: history of kidney transplantation; pregnant women.

This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (2024 version) and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University First Hospital-Miyun Hospital. Informed consent was waived for this retrospective analysis.

2.1. Dialysis treatment process

At the beginning of dialysis treatment, the nurse will check the function and position of the arteriovenous fistula or central venous catheter to ensure that the access site is free from infection, bleeding, or other abnormalities. The nurse will then perform punctures at both the arterial and venous ends and connect them to the Fresenius 4008S dialysis machine. Dialysis is performed 3 times a week, and the nurse sets the parameters on the dialysis machine, including a blood flow rate of 250 to 280 mL/h, a dialysis solution flow rate of 500 mL/h, and a dialysis solution temperature set at 36.5℃, with a dialysis time of 4 hours.

After starting the dialysis machine, blood flows through the arterial puncture needle into the dialyzer (Nipro 150G [Nipro Corporation, Osaka, Japan] or Fresenius FX60 dialyzer [Fresenius Medical Care AG & Co. KGaA, Bad Homburg, Hesse, Germany]), where it exchanges substances with the dialysis solution across the dialysis membrane to remove metabolic waste and excess fluid from the body. The blood then returns to the body through the venous puncture needle. During the dialysis process, the nurse continuously monitors the patient’s vital signs, such as blood pressure and heart rate, as well as the operational status of the dialysis machine, including pump speed, venous pressure, transmembrane pressure, conductivity, temperature, and ultrafiltration rate, while also observing for any complications like hypotension or cramping.

Upon completion of dialysis, the nurse gradually reduces the blood flow rate of the dialysis machine and returns the blood in the dialysis circuit to the patient through the venous end to avoid blood waste. Finally, the puncture needle is removed, and the puncture site is monitored.

2.2. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk). Normally distributed continuous data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while skewed data are described using median (range). For continuous variables, independent sample t-tests were used to analyze normally distributed variables, while nonparametric tests were applied to variables that did not follow a normal distribution. Chi-square tests and Fisher exact probability tests were used to analyze categorical variables. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to explore the risk factors affecting mortality within 5 years after dialysis treatment in CKF patients. A P-value of < .05 was considered statistically significant.

4. Discussion

This study explored the factors influencing mortality in patients undergoing dialysis treatment for CKF within 5 years. By analyzing baseline data and pre- and post-dialysis data, the results indicated that patient age and pre-dialysis blood creatinine level were independent risk factors affecting mortality in patients receiving dialysis treatment.

Although dialysis treatment has become a mainstream treatment for CKF, the factors affecting survival in these patients after receiving dialysis treatment have yet to be conclusively determined.[14] Sun et al[15] conducted a prospective cohort study exploring the survival characteristics, prognosis, and prognostic outcome factors of young and elderly peritoneal dialysis patients. Their results are consistent with the age risk factor identified in this study, indicating that the mortality rate for elderly peritoneal dialysis patients is significantly higher than that for younger patients, and highlighting cardiovascular disease as the primary risk factor leading to mortality in elderly patients undergoing dialysis treatment. In addition to age, high body mass index, high Charlson Comorbidity Index, and ischemic heart disease are also adverse factors affecting the survival and prognosis of elderly peritoneal dialysis patients. Zhao et al[16] found that patient age and history of diabetes are major influencing factors for cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality, further corroborating the reliability of the findings in this study to some extent.

Age as a risk factor influencing mortality in dialysis patients may be attributed to several reasons. Elderly patients experience a decline in the function of various organ systems, including the heart, lungs, liver, and immune system. While dialysis can replace some kidney function, it cannot fully restore the body’s metabolic balance, making it challenging for elderly patients to adapt to the pressures of long-term dialysis. Additionally, elderly patients often have multiple chronic diseases, such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and hypertension. These comorbidities not only increase the complexity of dialysis treatment but also accelerate damage to other organ systems. The weakened immune function in elderly patients makes them more susceptible to infections, which are common complications and causes of death among dialysis patients.

Dialysis treatment itself imposes a certain burden on the body, including prolonged blood flow adjustments, fluctuations in fluid and electrolytes, and complications during the treatment process (such as hypotension and arrhythmias). For older patients, it is more difficult for their bodies to cope with these dialysis-related burdens, leading to a higher incidence of complications and consequently increasing the risk of mortality.

Pre-dialysis blood creatinine level is a reliable, inexpensive, and easily obtainable marker of muscle mass, and it is also an important predictor of survival in chronic hemodialysis patients.[17] Peng et al[18] conducted a study on mortality risk factors in ESKD patients receiving long-term dialysis. After using propensity score matching to process baseline data for both hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis groups, the analysis found that serum creatinine is an independent risk factor affecting patient survival. Lee et al[19] conducted a study on changes in serum creatinine over 2 years in patients on maintenance hemodialysis and their association with survival rates. The results showed that changes in serum creatinine within 2 years are related to the all-cause mortality rate in hemodialysis patients. The findings of this study are consistent with those of the aforementioned studies, indicating that serum creatinine levels can affect the survival of dialysis patients.

This study shows that another risk factor influencing mortality in dialysis patients is the pre-dialysis blood creatinine level. By analyzing the collected real-world data, we found that the pre-dialysis blood creatinine level in the survival group was higher compared to the death group. A possible explanation for this is that patients with higher creatinine levels typically have better nutritional status due to their higher muscle mass. Malnutrition is one of the significant factors contributing to the high mortality rate among dialysis patients. Malnutrition can weaken the immune system, increase the risk of infections, and make it more difficult for patients to recover from intensive treatments like dialysis. Therefore, patients with higher blood creatinine levels often have a better nutritional foundation, which helps improve survival rates after dialysis.

In addition to the risk factors identified that affect the 5-year survival rate of dialysis patients, the severity of underlying diseases in CKF patients also impacts their quality of life.[20] It is well known that diabetes can cause damage to various organs, including the kidneys and eyes, leading to various complications and even death. Regarding the kidneys, diabetes-induced hemodynamic and metabolic disorders can result in glomerular hyperfiltration, damaging the glomeruli and further leading to interstitial fibrosis and tubular atrophy.[21,22] Previous studies have also confirmed that glomerular hyperfiltration in diabetic patients is an independent risk factor for rapid decline in renal function.[23–25] Additionally, patients with diabetes undergoing dialysis not only experience impaired treatment efficacy but also face an increased risk of infections.[26] Hypertension is also significantly associated with early renal function decline and is one of the main causes of chronic kidney disease.[27,28] Oxidative stress, inflammation, and subsequent glomerulosclerosis caused by hypertension accelerate the deterioration of kidney function. CKF patients with hypertension experience an increased burden on their kidneys during dialysis treatment, and poor blood pressure control can lead to serious complications such as cerebral hemorrhage and heart failure, threatening the lives of CKF patients.[14] In summary, to ensure the effectiveness of dialysis treatment, CKF patients should enhance the management of blood glucose and blood pressure, regularly monitor these parameters, and actively control their underlying diseases. This study also analyzed the factors of diabetes and hypertension but did not find them to be risk factors affecting mortality in CKF patients undergoing dialysis. This may be attributed to the relatively small sample size of this study and the differences in the prevalence of diabetes and hypertension among individual patients receiving dialysis.

The highlights of this study are worth discussing. First, compared to previously published systematic reviews and meta-analyses, this study utilizes real-world clinical data to explore the risk factors for mortality in CKF patients undergoing dialysis treatment, and the follow-up period is longer, making the conclusions more closely aligned with actual clinical practice and thus more reliable, providing more precise supportive evidence for clinical applications in the future. Second, this study employs systematic statistical methods to comprehensively analyze 19 factors affecting CKF patients during dialysis treatment, resulting in conclusions that are relatively comprehensive and robust. However, this study has the following limitations: although this study is based on real-world data, it is derived from a single center, which may lead to certain biases due to the small data size; the factors considered in this study are primarily disease-related indicators and do not take into account the psychosocial factors affecting CKF patients, such as psychological stress, financial pressure, and insufficient social support. Despite these limitations, the results of this study remain reliable, and future research should expand the data size while incorporating psychosocial factors to further enhance such studies.

5. Conclusion

Age and pre-dialysis blood creatinine level are independent risk factors for mortality in patients undergoing dialysis treatment for 5 years.

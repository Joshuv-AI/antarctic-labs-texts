BioSet2Vec: extraction of k-mer dictionaries from multiple sets of biological sequences via big data technologies
Authors: Ylenia Galluzzo, Raffaele Giancarlo, Simona E. Rombo, Filippo Utro
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn several contexts involving large collections of sets of biological sequences, a relevant problem is that of selecting significant groups of k-mers that characterize one set with regards to the others in the same collection.ResultsHere a software framework is proposed implementing a novel methodology for the extraction of k-mer dictionaries, from multiple sets of biological sequences. It has been implemented according to the most recent technologies for Big Data analytics, with the perspective of allowing its usage with a variety of input datasets of any size. In particular, two different packages are provided. The first is BioFt, enabling the extraction of recurrent patterns based on k-mers frequency and the computation of other metrics from information retrieval, here specialized for biological sequences. The second package BioSet2Vec, instead, extends the functionality of BioFt by allowing the creation of dictionaries according to different criteria.ConclusionsThe framework has been validated on three different case studies: (1) the characterization of different chromatin states; (2) the study of association between different diseases and related genes; (3) the analysis of genomes of different organisms. All tests performed on the considered datasets have shown the potentialities of the proposed approach.

Introduction

The information-theoretic content of biological sequences has been widely studied in terms of k-mers composition [1, 2], even focusing on the efficient and scalable computation of k-mer statistics [3] and their indexing [4]. K-mer dictionaries, annotating groups of k-mers with statistics (e.g., their frequency of occurrence) recognized as “significant” for characterizing a given set of sequences, have become a standard tool in this context [1, 5], showing successfull applications in life sciences [6–8].

An interesting challenge related to the extraction of k-mer statistics is the construction of dictionaries able to characterize a given set of biological sequences, not only with reference to its “absolute” information content, but also to what makes such set different from a certain number of other sets of sequences. This intrinsically enables k-mer dictionaries as compact descriptors of compositional features, which may be at the basis of pattern mining and other artificial intelligence oriented tasks [9]. Unfortunately, this problem has been solved for the sole case of two sets of sequences.

In particular, epigenomic k-mer dictionaries have been proposed in [10] to study how sequence composition influences in vivo nucleosome positioning, showing that k-mer dictionaries may be used to discriminate between two sets of DNA sequences, involving nucleosome enriched and depleted genomic regions, respectively. Discrimination between the sets pair has been performed by considering as k-mer statistics the z-score [10] of the difference between the frequency of occurrence that each k-mer has in the two input sets. That framework has been further extended in [6], by generalizing the notion of k-mer dictionaries in order to integrate information coming from different data sources (e.g. genomics, epigenomics, in vitro or in vivo experiments, etc.) and support a set of useful operations for suitable knowledge extraction from such dictionaries. However, the approaches proposed in [6, 10] do not apply when the number of considered sets of sequences to be characterized through compact feature descriptors is larger than two.

Here we propose a novel methodology for the extraction of k-mer dictionaries able to significantly characterize sets of biological sequences, each set with reference to the remaining ones. To this aim, we rely on adapting some measures taken from Information Retrieval applied to natural language texts, i.e., the Term Frequencyâ€“Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) measure [11], to the context of biological sequences. In order to provide evidence of the importance to solve k-mer dictionaries extraction from multiple sequence sets, we study three specific application contexts and generate dictionaries for each of them. The first considered task is the characterization of different chromatin states, with regards to the 9 combinatorial states identified by [13]. A second case focuses on studying the differences/similarities among sets of diseases, considering the associated gene sequences. The third example takes into account a collection of singleton sets, each corresponding to the genome of an organism (bacteria or plants), with the aim of extracting relevant information about their similarities. The obtained results show that, for all three considered application contexts, the proposed approach has been successful in generating compact descriptors which correctly discriminate the input distinct sequence sets.

Background

Distributed computing

Distributed computing systems are designed in multiple computational units/nodes, that collaborate to solve complex problems or execute tasks that exceed the capabilities of a single machine. These types of systems are used when working with large amounts of data, such that computational workloads are partitioned and distributed across multiple nodes, each processing a subset of the overall task in parallel. The partial results from these nodes are subsequently aggregated to generate a unified result across the nodes [14].

Paradigms and frameworks

The distributed computing framework Apache Spark [15] provides a useful interface for programming with parallelism and fault-tolerance mechanisms. The Apache implementation of the Spark framework supports different programming languages, such as Scala, Java, and Python. It relies on three main abstractions of distributed data in memory, enabling computations to be performed in memory across multiple nodes: Resilient Distributed Dataset (RDD) [16], DataFrame (DF) and DataSet (DS) [17]. This makes the paradigm efficient for applications that require frequent reuse of the data set through multiple parallel operations. The Spark architecture is built around the concept of RDD. In particular, an RDD is a distributed memory data abstraction that provides fault tolerance, enabling in-memory computations to be executed across multiple nodes. RDDs can be created by parallelizing an existing collection in the driver program or by accessing data from external storage systems Hadoop-compatible source (e.g HDFS [18], HBase).

The DataFrame API in Spark, instead, provides a more advanced abstraction than the basic RDD primitives. DataFrames store data in a columnar format, which provides many advantages for processing large amounts of data. It is conceptually equivalent to a table in a relational database. They also use the programming concept of lazy evaluation to optimise the execution of queries and transformations.

DataSet is a higher-level abstraction in Apache Spark that combines the advantages of both RDDs and DataFrames. However, the DataSet API (latest version 3.5.2) is not currently supported with all programming languages, it is available only with Scala and Java.

Software libraries

Within the framework Apache Spark, the following two software libraries have been used to develop the approach proposed here. Spark SQL [17] is a module within Apache Spark that has been designed for the processing of structured data. It is possible to easily execute SQL queries and combine them with automated data processing, in a unified way.

Apache Parquet [19] is a data format suitable for compressed, high-performance archiving. Apache Parquet uses a column-oriented approach to data storage, which is efficient because the data in a column is grouped together in the same chunk and it is not necessary to scan all the data we have.

Spark SQL supports reading and writing Parquet files while preserving the original data schema.

Statistical significance

Given a k-mer, the numeric value of its relevance for a given set is not enough. Indeed, such a value may be due to chance. A conservative choice for inclusion would be the numeric value and its statistical significance. To this end, we proceed via series of Montecarlo simulations [21], each for each \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S_i$$\end{document}. Implementation

General overview

The software is devoted to programmers, analysts and practitioners who need to perform efficient large-scale bioinformatics analysis, in particular for extracting meaningful dictionaries from biological sequences using k-mer relevance in a collection, using a distributed computing environment.

The proposed framework is divided into two main packages, as shown in the overall high-level architecture illustrated in Fig. 1. By dividing the software into these two packages, we can leverage the strengths of both Scala and Python, ensuring high performance for critical processing tasks and flexibility for data analysis and experimentation.

The first package, BioFt, is designed to provide an advanced tool for the analysis of DNA sequences. It enables the extraction of recurrent patterns by calculating k-mer frequencies and assessing their relevance using the TF-IDF metric. Integrated with Apache Spark, the package supports scalable, distributed processing of large datasets of biological sequences, facilitating applications such as the identification of distinctive patterns and useful statistics. BioFt has been implemented in Scala upon the framework Apache Spark. The usage of Scala enables efficient execution of large-scale data processing tasks and leverages the native APIs provided by Spark, which are optimized for performance and reliability.

The second package BioSet2Vec, instead, extends the functionality of BioFt by allowing the creation of k-mers dictionaries, for different values of k. The package also takes into account all possible patterns derived from the detected k-mers by replacing the bases with IUPAC codes. In addition, it implements the Montecarlo statistical test to validate the compact dictionaries generated for each genomic sequence collection. General software overview

Package BioFt

Description. BioFt is responsible for mapping k-mers on biological sequences. Using the distributed capabilities of the Apache Spark framework, it efficiently handles large datasets of sequences and computes k-mer frequencies and the other necessary statistics to implement the approach proposed here.

Implementation Choices. For the development of BioFt the languages Scala has been chosen, for its high performance and scalability, due to the fact that it is the native language of the framework Apache Spark. Depending on the size of the sequences being analyzed (considering gigabytes of sequences and hundreds of millions of bases), the counting of k-mers in the initial stages proves to be a computationally expensive task. In this particular context, Scala allows efficient processing of large biological sequences. In addition, Spark programs written in Scala tend to be more performant than their Python counterparts, also due to the JVM bytecode compilation.

Provided utilities. The core of BioFt consists of two classes: TFApp and TFIDFApp. They facilitate the management and processing of data using a distributed representation. The implementation of the TFApp class is designed to leverage the parallel and distributed computing capabilities of Apache Spark. Indeed, it incorporates a primary method, processTF, designed to compute term frequency (TF) through different Spark abstractions. The processTF method uses Spark Resilient Distributed Dataset (RDD) API, which provides high flexibility and control over the data. Additionally, Spark DataFrame API is used to take advantage of the optimisations provided by Spark Catalyst engine. By integrating both the DataFrame and the RDD abstractions, the solution leverages on the strengths of each, to provide a robust approach for processing large biological datasets, such as those usually encountered in genomics studies.

In this context, we introduce a class called Util, which is designed to support the TFApp class by efficiently and scalably mapping k-mers of biological sequences. Util is designed to provide a robust and high-performance solution for computing k-mers on input sequences. Implemented in Scala, Util enables distributed processing of large amounts of sequential data.

The Util class implements utilities to support the functionality of the TFApp class. The TFApp class invokes a collection of methods from the Util class to manage and compute the relative frequency of k-mers in a set. The most important methods are summarized in the Supplementary Material.

The input files storing biological sequences (e.g., genomic sequences) are formatted according to the most common formats (i.e., .fasta, .fna, .fa) and consist of multiple lines of sequences. Sequence lines can be of any length. However, they are usually formatted to be of uniform length for ease of reading.

At the beginning, with the invocation of the processTF method in TFApp, the method mapper_kmers_simple is called on the entire biological set of sequences. The implementation of mapper_kmers_simple provided here can be usefully applied to obtain k-mers (sub-sequences), for a given genomic sequence. The processTF method uses the mapper_kmers_between function to create an RDD of concatenated strings and lengths, then calculates and extracts the desired k-mer by the mapper_kmers_simple function from these strings. This process allows patterns to be identified across the entire single sequence by considering all consecutive nucleotide bases that may be between several separate but consecutive lines of the same single sequence.

The TFIDFApp class keeps a method to compute the Inverse Document Frequency (IDF) and subsequently the Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) for the complete set of input sequences. This computation requires the prior existence of TF Parquet files, processed in a previous step, within the target directories. These files must contain the Term Frequency (TF) calculations for a given value of k for each set of sequences.

When the method compute_tfidf() is called up it stores, for each set of sequences, the TF-IDF values for each k-mers and for each k taken into account according to the options set by the user as input.

Implementation choices. BioSet2Vec is implemented in Python. In addition to its scalability, PySpark integrates seamlessly with key Python libraries such as NumPy, Pandas and Scikit-learn, allowing advanced statistical and machine learning models to be incorporated into the pipeline. This flexibility supports the design of modular workflows where data preprocessing, feature extraction and model training components can be easily customised or extended. In addition, the Spark MLlib library provides native implementations of distributed algorithms that complement the functionality of BioSet2Vec by facilitating large-scale vector similarity calculations and clustering. Using PySpark, BioSet2Vec can scale from local deployments on single-node machines for small-scale research studies to large distributed environments for high-throughput genomic data analysis, ensuring flexibility and performance across a wide range of use cases.

Provided utilities. Configuration parameters can be read from a JSON file by the read_parameters_from_file function. Then, sequence sets are generated or processed by the generate_synthetic_sequence and process_files methods, respectively. The process_files_in_folders function enables two execution modes to handle input data: Normal Processing (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t = 0$$\end{document}) and Monte Carlo Simulation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t \ge 1$$\end{document}). The latter case refers to the Montecarlo simulation described in the Statistical Significance section. Sequences are first extracted from FASTA-formatted files by the extract_sequences function, then concatenated by the create_input_fasta utility. K-mers are extracted from the resulting concatenated sequences, and TF values are computed over a user-defined range of k values by the BioFt module. This step relies on compute_simple_tf, which computes TF for a given value of k, and compute_tf_k_k, which computes the TF values across different k-mer lengths (k_min to k_max) and combines them into a single DataFrame. In addition, the pipeline supports the transformation of k-mers into their IUPAC-coded representations by the replace_with_iupac and generate_kmers functions. TF-IDF values can be then computed by the compute_tfidf and compute methods, yielding informative vector space models of biological sequences. Several methods of the BioSet2Vec module are implemented on top of functionalities offered by the BioFt package, which provides optimized routines for distributed TF and TF-IDF computation on genomic data. A summary of such methods, that ensure scalability and robustness in processing large-scale biological sequence datasets, is provided in the Supplementary Material.

Advantages and limitations

The main advantages of the proposed software rely on its modular architecture and predisposition for distributed processing, that makes it suitable for the analysis of large volumes of biological data. Moreover, the provided vector representation of sequences based on TF-IDF values enables the generation of embeddings, that can be used for downstream tasks such as classification, clustering and pattern discovery. The Montecarlo simulation included in the software suite guarantees the chance to generate statistically significant compact descriptors of the input sequences. Another important advantage is the ability of the software in handling imbalanced datasets, i.e., such that sequences vary significantly in length, due to the normalization with respect to the sequence lengths, ensured by the TF terms.

Despite its scalability and modular architecture, the proposed software has also some limitations that should be taken into consideration. The first one is that, being implemented in Apache Spark, it is supposed to be used upon a distributed computing environment. However, it is worth pointing out that one may also configure Spark in local mode and leverage the available resources of a single machine. For large sets of sequences, this represents anyway a better choice than proceeding without any parallelism.

A further limitation of the current implementation is its difficulty in handling entire genomes if their size is too large. Indeed, the number of distinct k-mers to be extracted increases exponentially with sequence length, making the process computationally heavy, even if working in a distributed environment.

On the other hand, three case studies, each based on real data, are provided to illustrate how the approach can be usefully applied for biological data analysis.

Case study 3: characterize different organisms

Studying the evolution of organisms is crucial to understand how they have genetically diversified over time and adapted to different environments. This problem requires the analysis of mutations, horizontal gene transfer, and other genetic variations that allowed the development of characteristics such as virulence or antibiotic resistance, in the case of bacterial sequences. Discriminating between these assembled strains is essential for tracing the evolution of species.

The approach proposed here is applied to investigate about the similarity of different organisms. In particular, we have considered the case of a collection of singleton sets, each set corresponding to the genome of an organism (bacteria or plants). Then, relevant k-mers dictionaries from both bacteria and plants have been extracted, and a non-linear dimensionality reduction technique that integrates manifold learning and graph-based concepts, known as Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) [28], has been applied. UMAP depends on key hyperparameters, such as the number of nearest neighbors, distance measure, and embedding size. Proper tuning of these parameters is essential for optimizing performance on a specific dataset. For instance, higher values for the number of Nearest Neighbors highlight global patterns, while lower values capture local details. The aim is to preserve the topological structure of the data in a low-dimensional representation. This is useful in order to be able to interpret the relationships between the data and to visualise how they are collated in 2D space. The specific approach adopted for this case study is inspired by Top2vec [29], used for topic modeling on textual documents. Compared to other dimensionality reduction methods (e.g., t-SNE, PCA), UMAP generally provides higher speed and scalability, therefore it is more appropriate to manage our large dictionaries.

It is worth to point out that a dynamic partitioning strategy based on file size has been implemented, to optimise distributed file processing. Using a target partition size of 32 MB, the system dynamically calculates the optimal number of partitions, ensuring a minimum of one partition and scaling appropriately for larger data sets. The partition sizes are chosen to minimise data skew, and the sequence file is repartitioned to balance the workload across nodes. To optimise the performance of our distributed processing setup, a dynamic executor memory allocation strategy is also used to improve resource utilisation and scalability. Executor memory is calculated as twice the number of allocated cores, ensuring sufficient memory for parallel task execution while maintaining a balanced workload distribution across nodes. This approach balances memory allocation with compute capacity, preventing resource bottlenecks and optimising task execution speed.

For small k (e.g., k = 3 to k = 5), the effect is attenuated as the computational load is less sensitive to scalability. Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced BioSet2Vec, a user-friendly software library that allows individuals without a background in distributed programming to conduct sophisticated data analyses on biological sequences. In particular, the proposed software allows the extraction of k-mer dictionaries from a collection of biological sequences sets, containing a number of sets larger than two. To the best of our knowledge, no other systems currently performs the computation of relevant dictionaries from genomic datasets when the number of sequence sets is larger than two.

The library has been rigorously tested across various synthetic and real-world datasets, showcasing its versatility through multiple usage examples, as well as the importance of extracting k-mer dictionaries from multiple sequence sets. In the future, we intend to extend the library’s capabilities by incorporating analysis methods for protein and RNA data, further enhancing its usage within the bioinformatics community. Moreover, also further metrics, to support for example the detection of significant extended sequences made of the same basis (e.g., "gggggggggg"), will be investigated for applications in other contexts, such as molecular design for drug discovery. These future developments will enable broader applications of BioSet2Vec to support diverse research initiatives, aimed at understanding complex biological systems.

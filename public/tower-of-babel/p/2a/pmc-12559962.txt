Video-Based Assessment Scores and Operative Time for Selected Robotic Procedures
Authors: Travis Rogers, Marcio Covas Moschovas, Barbara Johnson, Prinieeth Anand d, Marco Sandri, Stephen Johnston
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This cross-sectional study examines video-based assessment scores and operative time for 4 high-volume surgical procedures: cholecystectomy, hysterectomy, prostatectomy, and ventral hernia repair.

Introduction

Video-based assessments (VBA) of surgeons’ technical skills are increasingly being used for initial training and continuous learning.1 Global Evaluative Assessment of Robotic Skills (GEARS) scores are a validated, crowd-sourced VBA tool for grading technical proficiency for robotic surgery.2,3 GEARS scores comprise a composite score from 5 to 6 domains judged on Likert scales (1 to 5): depth perception, bimanual dexterity, efficiency, force sensitivity, and robotic control.3 A sixth domain (autonomy), which requires live assessment regarding verbal guidance provided to operating surgeons, may also be included. Currently, evidence about whether GEARS scores are associated with operative time for robotically assisted surgery, a common measure of surgical efficiency, has been equivocal.4,5,6

Within the secure environment of a large robotic surgical program of federally listed Patient Safety Organization (PSO) in the US, we deterministically linked intraoperative electronic health record (EHR) data with 5-domain GEARS scores; autonomy is not routinely measured within this PSO. To examine the validity of GEARS scores as a meaningful correlate with surgical efficiency, we quantified the association of surgeons’ historical GEARS scores with future operative times for 4 high-volume procedures: cholecystectomy, hysterectomy, prostatectomy, and ventral hernia repair (VHR).

Methods

This was a retrospective, cross-sectional study conducted in accordance with the STROBE reporting guideline and exempted from review by the PSO institutional review board. Surgeons within the PSO voluntarily self-selected videos of their robotic procedures to be submitted for GEARS scoring.

Separately for each robotic procedure of interest, case-level intraoperative EHR data for surgeons with 4 or more GEARS-scored cases between January 1, 2019, to December 30, 2020, were included for analysis. Each surgeon was assigned a mean GEARS based on their scoring during that time period (baseline mean GEARS). For these surgeons, operative times (first incision to close) were collected for all robotic surgical procedures of interest in 2021, regardless of submission for GEARS scoring. Multivariable generalized linear models were used to examine, for each robotic procedure of interest in 2021, the association of the baseline average GEARS for the operating surgeon with operative time, adjusting for patient and encounter characteristics (eMethods in Supplement 1). Analyses were conducted using Stata/SE version 18 (StataCorp). Tests were 2-sided, and P < .05 was the threshold for statistical significance.

Results

In this study, 926 robotic procedures submitted for GEARS scoring between 2019 and 2020 from 52 unique surgeons were included for calculation of the baseline average GEARS score. A total of 2275 robotic procedures performed in 2021 were included for the analysis of operative time (mean [SD] patient age, 53.7 [13.5] years; 1570 female [69.0%]) (Table). In the multivariable analysis, a 1-point increase in baseline average GEARS score was associated with statistically significant lower operative time for the following procedures of interest performed in 2021: cholecystectomy (β = 0.79 [95% CI, 0.74-0.84]; P < .001), hysterectomy (β = 0.88 [95% CI, 0.81-0.95]; P = .002), and VHR (β = 0.47 [95% CI, 0.38-0.59]; P < .001) (Table). Prostatectomy was not associated with lower operative time (Table).

Discussion

This cross-sectional study examined multiple common robotic procedures and found that higher than average GEARS scores measured over a 2-year baseline period were significantly associated with lower operative times in the following year for cholecystectomy, hysterectomy, and VHR. This suggests that GEARS scores can meaningfully differentiate surgeons based on this measure of efficiency. While our findings were significant, a prior smaller study that was focused specifically on sleeve gastrectomy reported that GEARS scores were not significantly associated with operative time.3 Future research will be needed to understand GEARS scores in the context of operations being performed with forthcoming robotic platforms. This study has limitations. While operative time is a measure of efficiency, it does not take into account other important surgical outcomes, such as postoperative complications and measures of patient recovery. Additionally, the results may not be generalizable to all settings of care.

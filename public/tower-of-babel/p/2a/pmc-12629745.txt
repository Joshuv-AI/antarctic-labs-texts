Immunomodulatory effects of the combination of Korean red ginseng and Cervi Parvum Cornu via the MAPK pathway
Authors: BaiCheng Chen, Ajay Vijayakumar, Jun Hong Park, Chang Won Kang, Jong-Hoon Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractKorean red ginseng (KRG) and Cervi Parvum Cornu (CPC) act synergistically to improve cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression in rats, as evidenced by restoration of lymphocyte numbers, enhanced NK cell cytotoxic activity, normalization of NK cell-related cytokine profiles, and activation of the MAPK signaling pathway.Keywords: Korean red ginseng, Cervi parvum cornu, Immunosuppression, MAPK pathway, NK cell activity

The immune system is essential for protecting the body against pathogens, and its dysfunction increases susceptibility to infections [1,2]. Cyclophosphamide (CY), a chemotherapeutic agent, exerts potent immunosuppressive effects by reducing immune organ indices and depleting key immune cell populations, thereby impairing immune recovery [3,4]. This has prompted growing interest in natural immunomodulators as potential strategies to counteract CY-induced immunosuppression.

Korean red ginseng (KRG) has been traditionally used for its anti-aging, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immune-enhancing properties, and exhibits pharmacological effects against inflammation, cancer, and microbial infections [5,6]. Cervi Parvum Cornu (CPC), another traditional remedy widely used in East Asia, contains bioactive components such as gangliosides, amino acids, chondroitin, glucosamine, and hyaluronic acid, which confer diverse physiological activities [7]. Given these benefits, the combination of KRG and CPC may represent a promising strategy to alleviate CY-induced immunosuppression. However, the synergistic or additive effects of this combination on immune recovery have not yet been clearly elucidated. Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate the potential of combined KRG and CPC therapy to enhance NK cell immunity in a CY-induced immunosuppressive rat model.

We investigated whether the combination of Korean red ginseng (KRG) and Cervi Parvum Cornu (CPC) could restore the spleen and thymus indices, modulate peripheral blood cell populations, and enhance NK cell-mediated cytotoxicity. Specifically, we examined the expression of molecules related to the MAPK signaling pathway in splenocytes, quantified serum levels of NK cell-related cytokines, and evaluated the expression of NK cell activation receptors NKG2D and NKp46 [8,9]. In addition, NK cell cytotoxicity was assessed using a YAC-1 target cell assay [10]. A total of thirty male Sprague–Dawley rats (200–220 g) were obtained from SLC Inc. (Japan). All experimental procedures were performed in accordance with the Guideline for Institutional Animal Care and Use Committees of Chonbuk National University (IACUC No.: NON2023-03-021). Rats were randomly assigned into six groups (n = 5 per group): Normal control (NC), cyclophosphamide (CY, 30 mg/kg), KRG (100 mg/kg), CPC (100 mg/kg), KRG + CPC combination (50 mg/kg each) and levamisole hydrochloride (LEVA, 10 mg/kg; positive control). All groups, except NC, CY was administered intramuscularly every three days. Oral administration of KRG, CPC, or their combination was performed daily for 25 consecutive days. LEVA-treated rats received LEVA orally at 10 mg/kg. Animals were sacrificed 24 h after the final treatment(Fig. 1 A). KRG and CPC were provided by the Korea Ginseng Corporation. CY and LEVA were purchased from Sigma–Aldrich. Antibodies and ELISA kits were obtained from Invitrogen and Abcam. Following sacrifice, spleens and thymuses were excised and weighed individually. The organ index was calculated using the following formula: [Organ index (%) = Organ weight (g)/Animal weight (g) X 100]. Peripheral blood immune cell counts were determined using an ADVIA hematology analyzer. Splenic immune cells were isolated and co-cultured with PKH26-labeled YAC-1 target cells at effector-to-target (E:T) ratios of 50:1 and 10:1. NK cell cytotoxicity was analyzed by flow cytometry. Splenocytes were lysed, and proteins were separated by SDS–PAGE, followed by western blotting to determine the expression of NK cell markers (perforin, NKp46) and MAPK pathway proteins (ERK, JNK, p38). Serum cytokine levels (IL-2, IL-12, IFN-γ, and IL-18) were quantified using ELISA. Spleen tissues were subjected to histological examination to assess morphological changes, and immunohistochemical staining was performed to evaluate immune marker expression. All statistical analyses were performed using Origin 2021 software. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance was set at ∗p < 0.05, with higher levels of significance indicated as ∗∗p < 0.01 and ∗∗∗p < 0.001; non-significant differences were denoted as n.s. (nsp>0.05).

In the results, treatment with KRG, CPC, and their combination (KRG + CPC) produced slight increases in spleen and thymus organ indices compared with the CY group; however, these changes were not statistically significant (Fig. 1B and C). Peripheral blood cell counts showed significant variation among treatment groups (Fig. 1D–F). CY administration markedly reduced the counts of leukocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes, neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils compared with the normal control (∗∗∗p < 0.001). Treatment with KRG, CPC or KRG + CPC ameliorated these reductions, with the exception of basophil counts. Notably, the KRG group exhibited the most pronounced improvements in total white blood cell and lymphocyte counts (∗∗p < 0.01). Monocyte counts were significantly restored in the KRG and CPC groups (∗∗p < 0.01) compared with the KRG + CPC group (∗p < 0.05). Recovery of neutrophil and eosinophil counts was also greatest in the KRG group relative to CPC and KRG + CPC treatment. Furthermore, treatment with KRG, CPC or KRG + CPC restored and enhanced NKG2D expression compared with the CY group, with the combination group showing the highest increase (∗∗∗p < 0.001), indicating a synergistic effect of KRG + CPC.Similarly, NKp46 expression was significantly upregulated by all three treatments, with the most pronounced increase observed in the KRG + CPC group, suggesting enhanced NK cell activation (Fig. 2A–C). Although NK cell numbers remained unchanged(Data not shown), analysis of activation markers indicated an enhanced cytotoxic potential. Increased NKp46 and NKG2D expression in KRG, CPC, and KRG + CPC-treated rats suggested functional activation of NK cells. Co-culture of splenic immune cells from treated rats with YAC-1 target cells at effector-to-target ratios of 10:1 and 50:1 resulted in significantly higher proportions of apoptotic target cells in all treatment groups compared with the CY group, particularly at the 50:1 ratio. The KRG + CPC group exhibited the greatest NK cell cytotoxicity, indicating a synergistic enhancement of NK cell-mediated killing by the combination treatment (Fig. 2D). Western blot analysis further revealed that NKp46 expression was significantly upregulated in the KRG (∗∗∗p < 0.001), CPC (∗∗∗p < 0.001), and most notably in the KRG + CPC (∗∗∗p < 0.001) groups (Fig. 3A and (a)). Additionally, perforin expression was significantly reduced by CY treatment but was restored in all treatment groups, with the greatest increase observed in the KRG + CPC group (∗∗∗p < 0.001) (Fig. 3A and (b)). Regarding MAPK phosphorylation, CY markedly suppressed p38 kinase phosphorylation. CPC treatment induced the highest phosphorylation of ERK1/2 and JNK (∗∗∗p < 0.001), while KRG + CPC treatment further enhanced phosphorylation compared with KRG alone (Fig. 3A–(c) and (e)). Phosphorylation of p38 was significantly restored only in the KRG and KRG + CPC groups (∗p < 0.05), whereas the CPC group did not exhibit this effect (Fig. 3A and (d)). These results indicate that KRG, CPC, and their combination can activate signaling pathways essential for NK cell cytotoxic function. Innate immune cells, such as macrophages, also secrete cytokines including IL-18, IL-2, and IFN-γ, which regulate immune responses and cytotoxicity [11]. Serum cytokine analysis showed that IL-2 was significantly increased in the KRG, CPC, and KRG + CPC groups (∗∗∗p < 0.001) compared with the CY group (Fig. 3B). Similarly, IL-18 levels were significantly elevated in the KRG (∗∗∗p < 0.001), CPC (∗∗∗p < 0.001), and KRG + CPC (∗∗∗p < 0.001) groups, with the combination group showing the highest increase, indicating a synergistic effect (Fig. 3C). IFN-γ, which is induced via downstream signaling of IL-2, was elevated by CY treatment [12] but was restored to normal control levels by KRG or CPC treatment, with significant differences compared with the CY group (∗∗∗p < 0.001) (Fig. 3D). In contrast, IL-12 levels showed no significant changes (data not shown). These cytokine profiles support the immunomodulatory role of KRG and CPC, particularly in combination. NK cell cytotoxicity involves the secretion of perforin and activation of MAPK signaling pathways, including ERK, JNK, and p38, which regulate cytolytic granule release and cytokine expression [13]. Histopathological examination of spleen tissue by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining revealed clear demarcation between white and red pulp in the normal control group, whereas CY treatment caused vacuolation, structural disorganization, and loss of distinct pulp boundaries, indicative of inflammation. Treatment with KRG, CPC, KRG + CPC, or LEVA improved spleen histology, restoring clearer demarcation and normal architecture. Immunohistochemical analysis further confirmed increased expression of perforin and Foxp-3 proteins in the spleens of KEG, CPC and KRG + CPC-treated groups compared with the CY group(Fig. 4A–C).

In this study, three principal findings highlight the immune-enhancing effects of the combined treatment of Korean red ginseng (KRG) and Cervi Parvum Cornu (CPC) in cyclophosphamide (CY)-induced immunosuppressed rats. First, administration of KRG and CPC effectively prevented CY-induced depletion of immune cells, although no significant improvements were observed in erythrocyte-related parameters (HGB, MPV, MCH, MCV, and HCT; data not shown). CY is known to induce lymphopenia and reduce NK cell numbers in peripheral blood [14]. However, the combined treatment of KRG and CPC markedly counteracted these immunosuppressive effects. Cytokine analysis further corroborated these findings, showing modulation of NK cell–related cytokines following treatment (Fig. 3B–D). Second, recovery of natural cytotoxicity receptors (NCRs), particularly NKp46, was observed. NCRs are essential for NK cell activation and target cell lysis. CY treatment significantly reduced NKp46 expression, indicating functional immune suppression in vivo (Fig. 2B and C). In contrast, KRG + CPC treatment restored NKp46 expression, suggesting protection and reactivation of NK cell functions. Third, improvements in spleen tissue were evident from restored histopathological examinations and an increased proportion withbroader distribution of perforin-positive cells. Foxp-3-positive Treg cells, although increased after treatment, returned only to normal baseline levels, suggesting that KRG and CPC treatment counteracted the acute Treg cell depletion caused by CY, thereby potentially contributing to immune reconstitution and maintenance of immune homeostasis. Mechanistically, the protective effects of KRG + CPC against CY-induced immunosuppression are likely multifactorial. One contributing factor is the restoration of Treg cell balance; CY disrupts this balance by increasing IFN-γ, a key cytokine involved in T cell proliferation and differentiation, whereas KRG, CPC, and particularly their combination normalized IFN-γ production(Fig. 3D). Another factor may relate to the polysaccharide content of KRG and CPC, as these natural polymers are known to stimulate cytokine expression, thereby enhancing both cellular and humoral immunity. Polysaccharides can promote the secretion of immune-related cytokines and antibodies, contributing to the overall immune-enhancing activity [15]. In addition, antioxidant polyphenols present in KRG and CPC may reduce oxidative stress in immune cells, such as NK cells, further supporting recovery of immune function [16]. Notably, KRG + CPC may exert synergistic immune-boosting effects through activation of MAPK signaling pathways, which regulate cytokine production and NK cell activity(Fig. 3A(c)–(e)).

In conclusion, using a CY-induced immunosuppressive rodent model, the present study demonstrates that combined KRG and CPC treatment provides superior immune-protective effects compared with individual treatments. These findings suggest that oral administration of KRG + CPC could serve as a preventive strategy against immunosuppression and a potential approach to improve immune health in clinical settings.

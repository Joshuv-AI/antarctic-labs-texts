Improved VO2max following anti-IL-5 therapy in severe eosinophilic CRSwNP: exploratory associations with cardiac inflammatory biomarkers
Authors: Jens Tidemandsen, Kasper Rossing, Anne Sophie Homøe, Morten Hostrup, Vibeke Backer
Published: 2026

Introduction

Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) and asthma frequently coexist [1], driven by type 2 inflammation involving eosinophils, interleukin-5 (IL-5), and systemic immune activation [2,3]. Patients with severe eosinophilic CRSwNP often experience significant symptom burden and impaired quality of life [4]. In recent years, biologic therapies targeting IL-5, such as mepolizumab, have demonstrated efficacy in reducing disease activity, improving symptoms, and lowering blood eosinophil counts in both upper and lower airway inflammation [5,6].

Maximal oxygen uptake (VO2max) is a gold-standard marker of aerobic capacity and an integrative measure of pulmonary, cardiovascular, and muscular system performance [7]. In patients with chronic inflammatory airway disease, VO2max is typically reduced due to ventilatory limitation, systemic inflammation, and impaired oxygen utilization [8]. Recent findings from our randomized controlled trial (RCT) evaluating mepolizumab alone versus mepolizumab plus functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) demonstrated a significant improvement in VO2max after 6 months of treatment in both treatment arms [9], raising the question of whether these functional gains reflect physiological effects of reduced inflammation, behavioral adaptations such as increased physical activity, or a combination of both. Given the systemic nature of type 2 inflammation, it remains unclear whether subclinical cardiovascular involvement may contribute to exercise limitation in this population.

We therefore conducted an exploratory analysis to evaluate associations between changes in VO2max and changes in cardiac biomarkers: N-terminal pro – B-type natriuretic peptide (ProBNP), high-sensitivity troponin T (TnT) and blood eosinophils.

Methods

Fifty-seven patients with severe CRSwNP and comorbid asthma were enrolled. Patients were randomly assigned to either mepolizumab + FESS (n = 29) or mepolizumab alone (n = 28). All received 100 mg mepolizumab subcutaneously every 4 weeks. Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) was performed at baseline and 6 months using a ramp protocol on a cycle ergometer to assess VO2max, peak workload (IPPO), and work capacity (seconds). Blood samples for eosinophils, ProBNP, and TnT were collected concurrently. Cardiac biomarkers below assay detection limits were imputed by midpoint substitution. Changes from baseline to follow-up were evaluated using paired t-tests, and associations with VO2max were assessed using linear regression. Due to skewed distributions, cardiac biomarkers were log-transformed (natural logarithm) prior to regression analyses. Changes were calculated on the log scale, representing relative changes over time.

Results

The mean age of the cohort was 52 ± 14 years; 75% were male, and 77% had asthma. At baseline, mean VO2max was 2521 ± 688 mL/min and 30.2 ± 8.6 mL/min/kg, with no significant difference between the two groups. Blood eosinophils averaged 0.50 ± 0.31 cells/µL, while ProBNP and TnT levels were 7.80 ± 6.24 pg/mL and 7.29 ± 6.77 pg/mL, respectively.

Following 6 months of treatment, we observed significant improvements in aerobic capacity across both groups. In the full cohort, VO2max increased from 2521 ± 688 to 2673 ± 690 mL/min (p < 0.001), and VO2max/kg improved from 30.2 ± 8.6 to 31.9 ± 8.5 mL/min/kg (p < 0.001). Similar gains were noted in both FESS and non-FESS arms. Using a clinically meaningful threshold of 1 mL/kg/min increase in VO2max/kg, based on evidence that each 1 mL/kg/min increase in peak aerobic capacity is associated with improved prognosis in cardiovascular populations [10], 33 of 57 patients (57.9%) achieved a clinically meaningful improvement in VO2max/kg over 6 months. The proportion was numerically higher in the FESS group (19/29, 65.5%) compared with the non-FESS group (14/28, 50.0%). Peak workload increased significantly in both groups (FESS: 229 ± 63 to 243 ± 60 W; non-FESS: 232 ± 77 to 242 ± 76 W; both p < 0.01). Work capacity also improved significantly (Table 1).

Blood eosinophil counts decreased markedly in both treatment groups (p < 0.001), reflecting the expected pharmacodynamic effect of IL-5 inhibition. However, neither ProBNP nor TnT showed statistically significant changes. In the full cohort, ProBNP decreased slightly from 7.80 ± 6.24 to 7.33 ± 6.99 pg/mL (p = 0.467), and TnT from 7.29 ± 6.77 to 6.98 ± 5.36 pg/mL (p = 0.438). These patterns were consistent across subgroups (Table 1).

Linear regression analyses using log-transformed biomarkers showed no significant associations between changes in VO2max/kg and changes in TnT or eosinophils. In the full cohort, a modest association was observed between changes in VO2max/kg and log-transformed changes in ProBNP (B = 0.93, p = 0.033). However, this association was not consistent across treatment groups and was primarily driven by the non-FESS subgroup (B = 2.28, p = 0.003), whereas no significant association was observed in the FESS group (Figure 1).

Discussion

This exploratory study demonstrates that treatment with mepolizumab, with or without FESS, significantly improves aerobic capacity in patients with severe eosinophilic CRSwNP and asthma. However, the relationship between these improvements and changes in systemic or cardiac biomarkers appears limited and inconsistent, suggests that the observed gains in VO2max are unlikely to reflect reductions in cardiac strain or injury.

Following log-transformation of biomarkers, a modest association between changes in ProBNP and VO2max was observed in the full cohort. Furthermore, the direction of the association, with increases in ProBNP associated with improvements in VO2max, is not physiologically intuitive. However, this finding was not consistent across treatment groups and was primarily driven by the non-FESS subgroup. Given the exploratory nature of the analysis, the modest sample size, and the lack of a clear biological mechanism, this finding should be interpreted with caution and may represent a statistical artifact rather than a true causal relationship.

In the parent randomized trial [9], daily physical activity increased significantly during the intervention period(~+1000 steps/day); however, changes in step count were not independently associated with improvements in VO2max. These findings suggest that increased habitual activity alone does not fully explain the observed improvements in aerobic capacity.

While cardiac biomarkers such as ProBNP and troponin are typically used in populations with overt cardiovascular disease, their inclusion in this study was intended to explore potential subclinical cardiac or systemic pathways linking eosinophilic inflammation to aerobic capacity. The lack of consistent associations observed in this study suggests that these pathways are unlikely to be primary drivers of improved VO2max in this population.

Although eosinophilic myocarditis has been described in case reports [11], our data do not support a role for subclinical cardiac inflammation as a major contributor to exercise limitation in this population. Instead, the improvements in VO2max may reflect improvements in ventilatory function and peripheral oxygen utilization, although these mechanisms were not directly assessed in the present study.

These findings are clinically relevant, as they underscore the systemic benefits of biologic therapy in patients with airway inflammation, while highlighting the need for more sensitive tools to detect subclinical cardiovascular involvement, if present.

A key limitation is that cardiac involvement was assessed solely using circulating biomarkers (troponins and BNP). While informative, these markers do not capture subtle or functional myocardial abnormalities. More detailed assessments such as cardiac MRI, echocardiographic strain analysis, or right-heart catheterization were beyond the scope of this study. Also, the absence of a non-treated control group limits causal inference, as this analysis was not designed to evaluate treatment effects on underlying mechanisms.

Conclusion

Mepolizumab significantly improves aerobic performance in patients with severe eosinophilic CRSwNP and asthma, but these improvements are not consistently explained by changes in ProBNP, TnT, oeosinophils or physical activity alone. Future studies should explore alternative mechanisms, such as ventilatory efficiency or muscular oxygen kinetics to explain functional gains following anti – IL-5 therapy.

Enhanced Arsenite Remediation in Synthetic FeS2/Fe(II)-Containing Arsenic Wastewater via Epigallocatechin Gallate-Initiated Persulfate Activation
Authors: Yongqing Wang, Hongbo Li, Shuxin Han, Sheng He, Zhengzheng Chen, Dun Fu, Tonni Agustiono Kurniawan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Fe­(II)-catalyzed persulfate (PS) activation system demonstrated the ability to remove pollutants, such as As­(III), from groundwater, including FeS2/Fe­(II)-enriched mine drainage; however, the slow conversion rate of Fe­(III) to Fe­(II) limited the overall efficiency of the process. In this study, natural polyphenols (EGCG) was introduced in the FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system to enhance the long-term recycling of Fe­(II), significantly improving the removal of As­(III) within 10 min. The results showed that the addition of 0.025 mM EGCG to the FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system, under conditions of 0.1 g/L FeS2, 0.1 mM Fe­(II), and 0.25 mM PS, remarkably promoted the removal efficiency of As­(III) from 50.7% to 89.3%. EGCG enhanced the reduction of Fe­(III) to Fe­(II) owing to the chelating, reducing, and electron-donating capabilities of ortho- or para-dihydroxybenzene groups in EGCG. The stable Fe­(II) complex formed was continuously oxidized by PS, generating reactive oxygen species (ROS), such as hydroxyl radicals (•OH), sulfate radicals (SO4 –•), and superoxide radical (O2 •–), which facilitated the oxidation of As­(III). Density functional theory (DFT) calculations, combined with transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analyses, revealed that the resulting As­(V) was primarily adsorbed onto the surfaces of colloid Fe2O3 in the form of bidentate binuclear complex, as well as onto FeS2 surfaces. This study presents an efficient approach to enhancing the FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS process by incorporating natural polyphenols, offering a promising strategy for the removal of micropollutants in groundwater.

Introduction

Arsenic (As) contamination in mine drainage is a critical environmental issue due to its severe toxicity and widespread occurrence. In such environments, arsenic primarily exists as trivalent arsenic (As­(III)) and pentavalent arsenic (As­(V)), with As­(III) being more toxic, mobile, and resistant to conventional treatment methods. , The uncontrolled discharge of As-contaminated mine drainage into aquatic ecosystems has led to severe ecological damage and public health risks, including cancer, skin lesions, and cardiovascular diseases. , Despite various treatment approaches, the efficient and sustainable removal of As­(III) remains a major challenge.

Addressing As contamination is crucial to ensure water safety and environmental sustainability. Given the high toxicity and persistence of As­(III) in water bodies, developing an efficient, cost-effective, and sustainable treatment method is urgently needed. This challenge is relevant to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 6 “Clean Water and Sanitation” and SDG 3 “Good Health and Well-being”. Advancing arsenic remediation technologies can reduce the risks associated with mine drainage contamination and contribute to long-term environmental protection.

Persulfate (PS)-based advanced oxidation processes (AOPs) have gained attention for As removal due to their strong oxidizing power and environmental compatibility. , Activated PS generates sulfate radicals (SO4 –•) with a high redox potential (E 0 = 2.5–3.1 V), which can effectively oxidize As­(III) to the more easily removable As­(V). , Various PS activation methods, including heat, UV light, and transition metal catalysis, have been explored. Nevertheless, thermal and UV activation usually require high energy consumption, while most homogeneous transition metal catalytic systems suffer from poor stability and secondary metal pollution. Among transition metal-based strategies, Fe­(II)-mediated activation has attracted extensive attention due to its low cost and high activation efficiency. However, conventional single Fe­(II) system suffers from rapid oxidation of Fe­(II) to Fe­(III) and subsequent iron hydroxide precipitation, which remarkably reduces the sustainable catalytic performance. In this context, the FeS2-based system can continuously supply Fe­(II) through in situ dissolution, effectively alleviate the excessive oxidation and precipitation of Fe­(II), and thus maintain long-term and stable PS activation performance.

Recent studies have shown that natural polyphenols, plant-derived bioactive compounds with multiple phenolic hydroxyl groups, can enhance Fe­(II) stability and redox cycling in iron-based activated persulfate systems. As multifunctional regulators, they act as electron donors (e.g., EGCG, tea polyphenols) to promote Fe­(III) reduction, , as metal chelators (e.g., gallic acid) to stabilize Fe­(II) and accelerate iron cycling and ROS formation, and as free radical regulators to optimize system efficiency, laying a foundation for their application in addressing aqueous As­(III) pollution. Notably, Chen et al. demonstrated that epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) promoted Fe­(III)/Fe­(II) redox cycling and •OH production, thereby significantly boosting coumarin degradation in the Fe­(II)/peracetic acid (PAA) system compared to its EGCG-free counterpart. Similarly, tannin extract, owing to its ortho-diphenol structures similar to EGCG, has also been proven to facilitate Fe­(III)/Fe­(II) redox cycling in Fenton oxidation. While polyphenols have shown effectiveness in enhancing PS activation for organic pollutant degradation, − their role in arsenic remediation remains virtually unexplored.

To bridge this knowledge gaps, this study aims to investigate the enhancement of As­(III) oxidation and adsorption in FeS2/Fe­(II)-enriched mine drainage using a natural polyphenol-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system. This work also evaluates the synergistic effects of polyphenols and FeS2/Fe­(II) on PS activation and elucidates the mechanisms underlying As­(III) oxidation and adsorption. Furthermore, this work optimizes key operational parameters to maximize arsenic removal efficiency.

This work introduces a novel strategy for As­(III) remediation by integrating natural polyphenols into the FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system. Unlike conventional Fe­(II)/PS system where polyphenols merely stabilize dissolved Fe­(II), this approach uniquely combines FeS2’s slow-release Fe­(II) capacity with polyphenols’ dual redox-chelating function to create a self-sustaining system: FeS2 provides continuous Fe­(II) supply through surface oxidation, while polyphenols not only prevent Fe­(II) precipitation but also regenerate Fe­(II) from Fe­(II), enabling persistent radical generation and enhanced arsenic adsorption/oxidation-a significant improvement over traditional Fe­(II)/PS-polyphenol system that rely on finite dissolved Fe­(II) and exhibit declining performance over time. By leveraging this synergistic mechanism, the study advances PS-based water treatment, offering a more rubust and sustainable solution for arsenic-contaminated mine drainage.

Materials and Methods

Materials

Gallic acid (GA, 98%), tea polyphenols (TP, 98%), procyanidin (PC, 95%), luteolin (LT, 98%), and epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG, 95%) were purchased from Shanghai Titan Technology (China). Their properties are detailed in Table S1 (Supporting Information). To prevent degradation, fresh polyphenol solutions were prepared before each experiment. Standard reagents, including potassium persulfate (PS, 99.5%), potassium borohydride (KBH4, 96%), sodium arsenite (NaAsO2, 90%), pyrite (FeS2), and various other analytical-grade chemicals, were obtained from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (China). Ultrapure water (18.2 MΩ·cm) was used in all As­(III) removal experiments.

Methods

Batch Experiments

The reaction kinetics of As­(III) in PS-injected FeS2/Fe­(II)-enriched mine drainage were examined in 500 mL glass vials at 25 °C, with and without polyphenols (GA, TP, PC, LT, and EGCG). An As­(III) stock solution was introduced into FeS2/Fe­(II)-enriched mine drainage (0.1 mM Fe­(II) and 0.1 g/L FeS2), achieving an initial As­(III) concentration of 1 mg/L. The reaction was initiated by adding polyphenols (25 μM) and PS (250 μM) to 300 mL of the simulated solution.

At predetermined intervals, 1 mL liquid samples were collected using plastic syringes, filtered through a 0.45 μm membrane, and analyzed. Additional experiments assessed the effects of PS (0.1–1 mM), Fe­(II) (0.05–0.2 mM), EGCG (0.01–0.1 mM), and pH (3–9, adjusted with 0.1 M HCl/NaOH) on As­(III) removal. Given the complexity of real mine drainage, the impact of coexisting ions (Cl–, HCO3 –, SO4 2–, H2PO4 – at the varying concentrations from 0.1–10 mM) was also investigated. To evaluate the effects of dissolved oxygen, reactions were conducted with and without nitrogen purging. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

Wet Chemistry Analysis

To identify ROS in the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system, quenching experiments were conducted using tert-butanol (TBA) and methanol (MeOH). Prior to the addition of EGCG and PS, 500 mM TBA or MeOH was introduced under identical reaction conditions. Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectra were recorded using a Bruker EMXplus-6/1 spectrometer (Germany), with 5,5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) as a spin trap for •OH and O2 •–.

Analytical Methods

As­(III) concentrations were measured using hydride generation atomic fluorescence spectrometry (HG-AFS, SA-20, Beijing Jitian, China), with a detection limit of 0.09 μg·L–1 and recovery rates of 95–114%. Fe­(II) and Fe­(III) species in the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system were determined via the 1,10-phenanthroline method. PS concentrations were analyzed spectrometrically at 352 nm after complexation with iodide and NaHCO3.

Pyrite crystallinity was characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD, Rigaku SmartLab SE, Japan), while morphological changes pre- and postreaction were examined using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, ZEISS Sigma 300, Germany) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, FEI Talos F200X, USA). Chemical composition was analyzed via X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Scientific K-Alpha, USA).

Theoretical Calculations

First-principles density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package (VASP), employing plane-wave basis sets and the projector-augmented wave (PAW) method. Exchange–correlation interactions were treated with the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation (GGA), while van der Waals forces were accounted for using Grimme’s DFT-D3 model. FeS2 (100) lattice parameters were a = 16.2 Å, b = 16.2 Å, c = 21.7 Å, with an energy cutoff of 450 eV and a 2 × 2 × 1 Gamma k-point mesh. Structural relaxation was performed until atomic forces were <0.02 eV/Å, with an energy convergence criterion of 10–5 eV.

Statistical Analysis

To ensure experimental data accuracy, reliability and reproducibility, all batch tests were conducted in triplicate under identical conditions, with results reported as mean values. The coefficient of variation was kept below 5%; samples exceeding this error limit were retested until meeting the acceptable range. Data were statistically analyzed using SPSS 19.0 for Windows. One-way ANOVA was employed to assess treatment differences, and a p-value ≤0.05 was defined as statistically significant.

Results and Discussion

As­(III) Removal in Different Systems

The comparative assessment of five polyphenolsgallic acid (GA), tea polyphenols (TP), procyanidin (PC), luteolin (LT), and epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG)in FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS systems revealed EGCG as the most effective enhancer of As­(III) removal. As shown in Figure a, EGCG facilitated 89.3% As­(III) removal, markedly surpassing GA (63.5%), TP (74.7%), PC (72.6%), and LT (63.7%). This substantial improvement underscores the role of polyphenol structure in electron transfer dynamics and oxidation-adsorption efficiencies within FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS systems.

(a) Depletion of As­(III) in different polyphenols-induced FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS systems, (b) The changes of EGCG, Fe­(II) and Fe­(III) concentrations (b) in EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS systems. Reaction conditions: [polyphenols]0 = 0.025 mM, [FeS2]0 = 0.1 g/L, [Fe­(II)]0 = 0.1 mM, [PS]0 = 0.25 mM, [As­(III)]0 = 1 mg/L.

A key determinant of EGCG’s superior performance is its distinct molecular structure. Unlike other polyphenols, EGCG contains six ortho-phenolic hydroxyl groups (Table S1), which significantly enhance its redox activity. These ortho- and para-dihydroxybenzene functionalities accelerate Fe­(III)/Fe­(II) cycling by promoting electron shuttling, thereby enhancing persulfate activation and radical generation. Mechanistically, EGCG undergoes oxidation to quinones via sequential one-electron transfer steps, forming highly reactive semiquinone radicals (SQ•) that further intensify oxidative transformation processes.

This critical analysis highlights the structural advantages of EGCG over other polyphenols in FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS systems. While GA and LT possess a limited number of hydroxyl groups that restrict their redox potential, TP and PC, despite their polyphenolic nature, exhibit steric hindrance that reduces their interaction efficiency with FeS2/Fe­(II). Thus, EGCG’s ability to act as a potent electron mediator and radical generator positions it as a promising natural enhancer for As­(III) remediation in mine drainage treatment.

The introduction of natural polyphenols significantly enhanced As­(III) removal under identical reaction conditions compared to the control experiment (50.7%) (Figure a), highlighting their catalytic role in persulfate (PS)-based in situ chemical oxidation (ISCO) for groundwater remediation. Among the tested polyphenols, EGCG demonstrated the highest efficiency, facilitating 89.3% As­(III) removal, far exceeding GA (63.5%), TP (74.7%), PC (72.6%), and LT (63.7%) (Figure a). The enhancement was particularly rapid, suggesting that polyphenols can effectively accelerate oxidation-adsorption processes within FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS systems, thereby improving the viability of ISCO applications in environmental remediation.

In the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system, the concentrations of EGCG, Fe­(II), and PS declined rapidly (Figures b and S1), while Fe­(III) accumulated as a byproduct of the As­(III) oxidation process. This transformation underscores EGCG’s dual function as both a chelating and reducing agent. By facilitating Fe­(III) reduction back to Fe­(II), EGCG sustains a stable Fe­(II) concentration in solution through the formation of a Fe­(III)/Fe­(II)-EGCG complex. , This redox cycling mechanism is critical, as it ensures continuous Fe­(II) availability, thereby sustaining PS activation and promoting reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, which drives As­(III) oxidation.

Proposed Mechanisms of EGCG Promoting Reaction

Oxidation Mechanisms

It was hypothesized that multiple ROS, including SO4 –•, •OH, and O2 •–, were generated in the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system. , To elucidate the dominant radical species responsible for As­(III) removal, free-radical quenching experiments and EPR spectroscopy were conducted. As shown in Figure , both TBA and MeOH inhibited As­(III) removal, but MeOH exhibited a significantly stronger inhibitory effect than TBA. Specifically, increasing the TBA concentration from 0 to 100 mM led to a reduction in As­(III) removal from 89.3% to 76.1%, while the same concentration range of MeOH reduced As­(III) removal to 66.1% (Figure a). The findings indicate that SO4 –• and •OH were the dominant oxidative species responsible for As­(III) transformation in the EGCG-promoted system.

Effect of different radical scavengers TBA (a) and MeOH (b) on As­(III) removal. Reaction conditions: [EGCG]0 = 0.025 mM, [FeS2]0 = 0.1 g/L, [Fe­(II)]0 = 0.1 mM, [PS]0 = 0.25 mM, [As­(III)]0 = 1 mg/L.

This analysis underscores EGCG’s pivotal role in facilitating PS activation via Fe­(III)/Fe­(II) cycling while simultaneously enhancing ROS-mediated As­(III) oxidation. The superior efficiency of EGCG over other polyphenols can be attributed to its unique ortho-phenolic hydroxyl groups, which promote electron transfer and radical generation. These insights offer promising implications for advancing PS-based ISCO strategies by leveraging naturally derived polyphenols to enhance oxidative remediation efficiency in FeS2/Fe­(II)-rich environments.

To elucidate the dominant reactive species in the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system, EPR spectroscopy was applied using DMPO as a spin trap agent. As shown in Figure a, two characteristic spectra corresponding to DMPO–OH (1:2:2:1 intensity ratio) and DMPO–SO4 adducts were detected. Notably, the DMPO–OH signal was significantly more intense than that of DMPO–SO4. This disparity is attributed to the rapid transformation of DMPO–SO4 (rate constant k = 9.1 × 105 M–1·s–1) into DMPO–OH (rate constant k = 6.0 × 108 M–1·s–1). , These results align with the radical quenching experiments, confirming that EGCG plays a crucial role in enhancing the generation of SO4 –• and •OH, which are the primary oxidants driving As­(III) removal.

EPR spectra of DMPO–OH (a) and DMPO–SO4 – and (b) DMPO-O2 •– obtained at 1, 5, and 10 min in EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS or PS system.

Beyond sulfate and hydroxyl radicals, the role of molecular oxygen (O2) in the reaction system was critically assessed. Previous studies have highlighted the indispensable function of oxygen in Fenton-like reactions, particularly in facilitating pollutant oxidation through superoxide radicals (O2 •–). , The experimental results reinforce this perspective, as shown in Figure . Under air-saturated conditions, As­(III) removal reached 98.5% within 10 min, whereas in a nitrogen-purged environment, the removal efficiency dropped to 87.4%. This reduction indicates that O2 actively participates in the reaction, further enhancing As­(III) oxidation.

Effect of dissolved oxygen on As­(III) removal in EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system. Reaction conditions: [EGCG]0 = 0.025 mM, [FeS2]0 = 0.1 g/L, [Fe­(II)]0 = 0.1 mM, [PS]0 = 0.25 mM, [As­(III)]0 = 1 mg/L.

The presence of O2 •– adducts at different reaction time intervals (Figure b) further validates this oxygen-dependent mechanism. The formation of these radicals can be attributed to two concurrent pathways: (i) the reduction of O2 by Fe­(II), which subsequently donates an electron to persulfate (PS) to generate SO4 –•; and (ii) the electron transfer from semiquinone radicals (SQ•–) within EGCG to O2, leading to hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) formation. Notably, the generated H2O2 can be further activated by SQ•–, thereby sustaining radical production and prolonging the oxidative capacity of the system.

The findings collectively establish that SO4 –•, •OH, and O2 •– are the key ROS driving As­(III) oxidation in the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system. Moreover, the interplay between Fe­(II)/Fe­(III) cycling, EGCG-mediated electron transfer, and O2 participation underscores the system’s robustness in sustaining radical-driven remediation. These mechanistic insights pave the way for optimizing natural polyphenol-assisted persulfate activation strategies for improved contaminant removal in real-world mine drainage scenarios.

Adsorption Mechanisms

The enhanced As­(III) removal efficiency and the involvement of reactive radicals underscore the pivotal role of EGCG in the FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system. To gain deeper insights into the mechanistic pathways, we employed SEM-EDS and XRD analyses to examine the structural and compositional evolution of pyrite before and after reaction with As­(III) (Figures and S2). Despite the preservation of crystallization properties and overall morphology, significant alterations in elemental composition were observed, providing evidence for the transformation processes governing As­(III) remediation.

SEM and EDS images of FeS2 before (a,c) and after (b,d) reaction.

A key finding from the SEM–EDS analysis was a 10-fold increase in arsenic content (w/w %) in the spent material compared to fresh pyrite, confirming the effective removal and immobilization of As­(III) onto the solid phase. This accumulation strongly suggests that As­(III) adsorption onto FeS2 surfaces is a dominant pathway facilitated by EGCG. However, beyond mere adsorption, notable shifts in oxygen, sulfur, and iron composition indicate more intricate chemical interactions at play. Given the established role of surface hydroxyl groups in arsenic complexation, it is plausible that As­(III) removal is driven by both surface complexation and oxidative conversion mechanisms.

Moreover, during the adsorption-oxidation process in the pyrite-activated PS system, Fe­(II) dissolution from FeS2 played a crucial role in activating persulfate to generate reactive oxygen species (ROS). This Fe­(II)-mediated PS activation was further amplified by the presence of sulfur species within pyrite, which not only influenced the redox cycling between Fe­(III)/Fe­(II) but also contributed to the stabilization of reaction intermediates. , This interplay between FeS2 surface chemistry, ROS formation, and EGCG-promoted electron transfer highlights a synergistic mechanism that accelerates As­(III) oxidation and adsorption.

Taken together, these findings illustrate that EGCG enhances As­(III) removal through a dual mechanism: (1) adsorption onto FeS2 surfaces, facilitated by elemental rearrangement and surface complexation, and (2) oxidation via persulfate activation, driven by Fe­(II) cycling and sulfur interactions. This mechanistic understanding provides a strong foundation for optimizing FeS2-based persulfate activation strategies in arsenic remediation in complex mine drainage environments where multiple reactive species interact dynamically.

To assess the contributions of sulfur and iron species in the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system for As­(III) remediation, we conducted XPS analysis before and after reaction with As. Notably, the presence of colloidal material was observed on the pyrite surface and in suspension (Figure S3), implying its role in arsenic removal. To differentiate between the adsorption and transformation mechanisms, XPS characterization was performed separately on pyrite with the attached flocculated material (FeS2 + colloid) and on pyrite alone.

As illustrated in Figure , the As 3d signal intensity in FeS2 + colloid was significantly higher than that in FeS2 alone, indicating that the majority of arsenic removal was mediated by the colloidal fraction rather than direct adsorption onto pyrite surfaces. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis further confirmed that the colloidal material exhibited Fe2O3 lattice fringes (Figure S4), reinforcing its role as a dominant sorbent in arsenic sequestration. This observation is critical, as it underscores a secondary phase transformation mechanism in which Fe­(II) oxidation leads to the formation of Fe2O3, enhancing arsenic immobilization.

XPS spectra of S 2p (a), Fe 2p (b), and As 3d (c) of FeS2 before and after reacting with As­(III).

To further probe the nature of arsenic binding on FeS2 and Fe2O3 surfaces, we conducted density functional theory (DFT) calculations to evaluate adsorption energy (E ads) and Gibbs free energy (ΔG) for different arsenic species. As shown in Figure , the E ads values for As­(III) and As­(V) on FeS2 in the bidentate binuclear configuration were −1.432 eV and −1.427 eV, respectively, indicating moderate adsorption affinity. However, on Fe2O3, the monodentate mononuclear complex exhibited a more favorable E ads value of −1.894 eV, while the bidentate binuclear complex had an E ads of −1.421 eV. This suggests that As­(III) adsorption onto Fe2O3 is energetically more favorable in a monodentate mononuclear configuration than that onto FeS2.

Schematic diagram of oxidation and adsorption capacity of FeS2 and Fe2O3 on As­(III).

Furthermore, thermodynamic analysis revealed that the oxidation of FeS2–As­(III) and Fe2O3–As­(III) to FeS2–As­(V) and Fe2O3–As­(V) was spontaneous, with ΔG values of −1.126 eV and −2.256 eV, respectively. Notably, the larger negative ΔG for Fe2O3–As­(III) oxidation indicates that the transformation of As­(III) to As­(V) occurs more readily on Fe2O3 surfaces than on FeS2. These findings align with the XPS results, confirming that As­(V) is predominantly adsorbed onto Fe2O3 rather than FeS2.

With respect to the critical implications for As­(III) removal, the combination of XPS, TEM, and DFT analyses reveals a dual-phase arsenic removal mechanism in the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system. Fe­(II) oxidation leads to Fe2O3 colloid formation, which serves as the primary adsorption site for arsenic sequestration. As­(III) adsorption is more thermodynamically favorable on Fe2O3 surfaces, primarily in a monodentate mononuclear configuration. The oxidation of As­(III) to As­(V) occurs more efficiently on Fe2O3 than on FeS2, reinforcing Fe2O3’s role as the dominant arsenic-binding phase.

Overall, the findings challenge the conventional assumption that pyrite alone is the primary reactive site for arsenic removal. Instead, Fe2O3 formation via Fe­(II) oxidation plays a crucial role in facilitating arsenic sequestration, highlighting the necessity of considering secondary phase transformations in FeS2-based remediation strategies.

Building on the observed experimental findings, a comprehensive removal mechanism for As­(III) in the EGCG-enhanced FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system is proposed (Figure ). The remediation process involves a synergistic interaction between Fe­(II), FeS2, persulfate (PS), and EGCG, wherein both oxidation and adsorption mechanisms contribute to arsenic sequestration.

Oxidation and adsorption mechanisms of As­(III) in EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system.

The coexistence and leaching of Fe­(II) from FeS2 play a critical role in activating PS to generate ROS. Specifically, oxygen reduction by Fe­(II) leads to the formation of O2 •–, which in turn accelerates PS activation to produce SO4 –•. These radicals, along with •OH, serve as potent oxidizing agents, facilitating the rapid transformation of As­(III) to As­(V), which is less toxic and more readily adsorbed.

A key challenge in this system is the progressive oxidation of Fe­(II) to Fe­(III), which reduces the efficiency of PS activation, as Fe­(III) is significantly less effective in generating ROS. However, EGCG plays a crucial role in sustaining Fe­(II) levels by chelating Fe­(III) and reducing it back to Fe­(II). This Fe­(III)/Fe­(II) cycling mechanism enhances the overall efficiency of PS activation and ROS production, ensuring sustained oxidation of As­(III).

Beyond oxidation, arsenic removal is further facilitated by the formation of iron-based colloids, particularly Fe2O3, which arise from interactions between FeS2, Fe­(II), and EGCG. These colloidal Fe2O3 particles act as highly effective adsorption sites, capturing As­(V) through surface complexation and precipitation processes. The coexistence of FeS2 and Fe­(II) enhances the formation of Fe2O3, thereby increasing the system’s adsorption capacity for arsenic species.

The proposed mechanism highlights several key insights about the dual role of EGCG. EGCG not only stabilizes Fe­(II) concentrations through Fe­(III) reduction but also modifies Fe­(III) speciation, thereby preventing passivation and enhancing PS activation. This extends the efficiency and lifespan of Fe-based Fenton-like processes. While ROS-mediated oxidation effectively transforms As­(III) into As­(V), the subsequent adsorption onto Fe2O3 surfaces ensures stable immobilization, reducing the risk of arsenic leaching. The regeneration of Fe­(II) in situ mitigates the deactivation of PS activation, addressing a major limitation in conventional Fenton-like systems.

These findings emphasize that EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS systems represent a highly efficient, dual-function arsenic remediation strategy by integrating redox transformations with colloidal adsorption mechanisms. Such an approach holds great potential for real-world applications, particularly in mine drainage and groundwater treatment, where Fe–S minerals and natural organic ligands are abundant.

Effect of PS, Fe­(II) and EGCG Dosage

The relationship between persulfate (PS) dosage and As­(III) removal efficiency in the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system is depicted in Figure a. As expected, an increase in PS concentration generally promotes the generation of ROS, enhancing As­(III) oxidation and subsequent removal. However, a critical evaluation of the data reveals an important limitation: beyond a certain threshold, increasing PS dosage does not proportionally enhance arsenic removal.

Effect of PS (a), Fe­(II) (b), and EGCG (c), coexisting anion (d) concentrations on As­(III) removal in EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS. Reaction conditions: [EGCG]0 = 0.025 mM, [FeS2]0 = 0.1 g/L, [Fe­(II)]0 = 0.1 mM, [PS]0 = 0.25 mM, [As­(III)]0 = 1 mg/L.

While a higher PS concentration theoretically results in more SO4 –• and •OH, facilitating a stronger oxidation environment, the observed removal efficiency only slightly decreased from 86.82% to 78.3% when PS dosage was increased from 0.1 to 1 mM. Accordingly, the first-order rate constants for the removal of As­(III) in EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS varied from 1.46 min–1 to 0.93 min–1 (Figure S5a). This minimal variation suggests that the system reaches a radical saturation point, beyond which additional PS does not translate into improved As­(III) oxidation. Instead, excessive PS can engage in self-quenching reactions, where excessive SO4 –• undergo recombination or react with nontarget species, reducing their effective participation in arsenic oxidation.

This phenomenon aligns with findings from other PS-driven remediation processes, such as the ultrasound-activated PS degradation of 1,1,1-trichloroethane and the Fe­(II)-activated PS breakdown of carbamazepine. , In both cases, an optimal PS dosage exists beyond which radical scavenging and competitive side reactions reduce degradation efficiency. The similarity across these systems reinforces the notion that careful optimization of PS concentration is crucial for maximizing contaminant removal while avoiding radical waste and secondary interference reactions.

This critical analysis highlights a key consideration for real-world applications. While higher PS concentrations theoretically improve oxidation, excessive dosages can lead to radical self-quenching and unintended side reactions, necessitating a balance between effective oxidation and reagent efficiency. The interaction between excessive PS and radical scavengers (dissolved organic matter, excess Fe­(II), or even reaction byproducts) can lead to inefficient PS utilization, requiring system-specific adjustments. Given the diminishing returns at higher PS dosages, an optimal concentration range must be identified to ensure cost-effective and environmentally sustainable arsenic remediation strategies.

The findings underscore the nonlinear response of As­(III) removal to increasing PS dosages in EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS systems. While radical-driven oxidation is essential for arsenic transformation, excessive PS can lead to counterproductive radical interactions, ultimately reducing removal efficiency. Future studies should explore real-world wastewater matrices, competitive ion effects, and long-term system stability to refine dosage strategies for practical arsenic remediation applications.

The effectiveness of arsenic (As­(III)) removal in the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system is significantly influenced by Fe­(II) concentration, EGCG dosage, and the presence of coexisting anions. A critical analysis of these factors provides deeper insights into the reaction mechanisms, optimal conditions, and potential limitations for practical applications.

Figure b illustrates the impact of Fe­(II) concentration on As­(III) removal, revealing a nonlinear relationship between Fe­(II) dosage and treatment efficiency. Increasing Fe­(II) from 0.05 mM to 0.1 mM led to a substantial enhancement in As­(III) removal, rising from 85.1% to 99.4%, and the first-order rate constants increased from 1.15 min–1 to 1.53 min–1 (Figure S5b). This increase can be attributed to the higher availability of Fe­(II) to activate PS, generating more ROS such as SO4 –• and •OH, which accelerate As­(III) oxidation.

The role of EGCG as a Fe­(III)/Fe­(II) redox cycling promoter is crucial in this system, yet its concentration must be carefully optimized. While EGCG enhances Fe­(II) regeneration, it also acts as a ROS scavenger by reacting with SO4 –• and •OH, thereby limiting their ability to oxidize As­(III). This behavior is consistent with EGCG’s well-documented antioxidant properties in biological systems, where it serves as a protective agent against oxidative stress but, in this case, counteracts the desired oxidation processes.

From a practical perspective, selecting 0.025 mM EGCG as the optimal dosage ensures a balance between its Fe­(III) reduction capacity and its ROS-scavenging tendency, maximizing As­(III) oxidation while maintaining reagent efficiency. The presence of coexisting anions in mine drainage introduces an additional layer of complexity in As­(III) remediation, as these species can interfere with radical-driven oxidation and adsorption processes. This drastic reduction can be explained by two primary mechanisms radical scavenging effects and formation of Fe-precipitates. HCO3 – and H2PO4 – act as scavengers for SO4 –• and •OH radicals, converting them into less reactive species such as CO3 –• (E 0 = 1.65 V) and HPO4 –•, which have significantly lower oxidation potentials than SO4 –• (E 0 = 2.5–3.1 V) and •OH (E 0 = 2.8 V). This transformation reduces the oxidative capacity of the system, leading to lower As­(III) oxidation rates.

HCO3 – and H2PO4 – can complex with Fe­(II) and Fe­(III), leading to the formation of insoluble Fe-carbonate and Fe-phosphate precipitates. This reduces the availability of Fe­(II) in solution, thereby limiting its role in PS activation and radical generation, further diminishing As­(III) removal efficiency.

These findings emphasize that the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system is highly sensitive to water chemistry, particularly the presence of inorganic anions that influence radical reactions and Fe­(II) solubility. Several key implications arise for real-world applications. If bicarbonate or phosphate concentrations are high, pretreatment strategies such as pH adjustment or selective ion removal should be considered to minimize radical quenching and Fe­(II) complexation effects. For site-specific remediation strategies, the significant variation in As­(III) removal efficiency due to coexisting anions suggests that field-scale applications should incorporate comprehensive water chemistry analysis to design tailored treatment protocols.

Limitations of This Work and Mitigation Strategies

The study presents a novel approach to arsenic remediation, but it has several limitations that could hinder its practical application. The system exhibits optimal As­(III) removal efficiency only within a narrow pH range (3–7). At alkaline conditions (pH > 7), the removal efficiency drops significantly due to the quenching of SO4 –• and •OH radicals by OH– ions and the oxidation of EGCG, which disrupts the Fe­(II)/Fe­(III) redox cycle. Therefore, it is crucial to incorporate pH-buffering agents or modify the system to stabilize radical generation across a broader pH range. Alternatively, preadjust the pH of the mine drainage to fall within the optimal range before treatment.

EGCG, a key component for enhancing the Fe­(II)/Fe­(III) redox cycle, is susceptible to oxidation under alkaline conditions, limiting its effectiveness at higher pH values. It is necessary to explore alternative chelating agents or antioxidants that are more stable under alkaline conditions. Alternatively, encapsulate EGCG in protective matrices to enhance its stability.

Additionally, Fe­(III) tends to precipitate as ferric oxyhydroxide at pH > 3, reducing the availability of active iron species for the redox cycle. Therefore, it is crucial to use stabilizing agents or nanoparticles to prevent iron precipitation and maintain the availability of Fe­(II)/Fe­(III) species across a wider pH range.

The generation of O2 •– is inhibited under alkaline conditions, reducing the system’s overall oxidative capacity. To mitigate this limitation, it is vital to introduce additional radical-generating pathways or catalysts that are effective under alkaline conditions, such as transition metal oxides or carbon-based materials.

The use of EGCG, a natural polyphenol, may be cost-prohibitive for large-scale applications. Additionally, the system’s reliance on PS activation could increase operational costs. To address this limitation, it is necessary to optimize the dosage of EGCG and PS to minimize costs. Investigate the use of cheaper, synthetic analogs of EGCG or alternative green catalysts for PS activation.

The use of PS and iron species could lead to secondary pollution, such as residual sulfate ions or iron sludge, which may require additional treatment steps. It is vital to implement post-treatment processes, such as sulfate ion removal or iron sludge recovery, to mitigate secondary pollution. Alternatively, design a closed-loop system to recycle iron species and minimize waste.

The study may lack validation under real-world mine drainage conditions, which often contain complex matrices of competing ions and organic matter that could interfere with As­(III) removal. It is crucial to conduct pilot-scale studies using actual mine drainage to evaluate the system’s performance under realistic conditions. Optimize the system to account for interference from coexisting contaminants.

The reaction kinetics and thermodynamics of the system may not be fully optimized, potentially limiting its efficiency under varying environmental conditions. To tackle this limitation, it is necessary to perform detailed kinetic and thermodynamic studies to identify rate-limiting steps and optimize reaction conditions (e.g., temperature, reactant concentrations).

While the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system offers a promising approach for As­(III) remediation, addressing these limitations through targeted mitigation strategies will be crucial for its practical application. Future research should focus on improving pH stability, reducing costs, and validating the system under real-world conditions to enhance its scalability and effectiveness.

The Way Forward

The study presents a promising approach for arsenic remediation, but there are several avenues for further research and development to enhance its practicality, efficiency, and scalability. For optimizing reaction conditions, the pH range needs to be expanded beyond 3–7, such as incorporating pH-buffering agents or modifying the system to stabilize radical generation under alkaline conditions, while conducting systematic studies to optimize temperature, EGCG concentration, PS dosage, and FeS2/Fe­(II) ratios for maximum As­(III) removal efficiency. For kinetic and thermodynamic studies, further work needs to perform detailed kinetic and thermodynamic analyses to understand the rate-limiting steps and improve the overall reaction efficiency.

For the development of stable chelating agents, further work needs to explore alternative chelating agents or antioxidants that are more stable under alkaline conditions and can effectively promote the Fe­(II)/Fe­(III) redox cycle. Meanwhile, for EGCG stabilization, it is crucial to develop methods to stabilize EGCG, such as encapsulation in protective matrices or chemical modification, to prevent its oxidation at higher pH levels.

To enhance radical generation through catalyst modification, further work needs to incorporate additional catalysts (e.g., transition metal oxides, carbon-based materials, or bimetallic systems) to enhance PS activation and radical generation, especially under alkaline conditions. For hybrid systems, it is necessary to combine the FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system with other advanced oxidation processes (e.g., photocatalysis or electrocatalysis) to boost radical production and improve As­(III) oxidation efficiency.

To mitigate secondary pollution due to iron sludge, further work needs to develop strategies to recover and recycle iron species from the system to minimize sludge generation and reduce secondary pollution. Meanwhile, for sulfate ion removal, it is necessary to implement post-treatment processes to remove residual sulfate ions, such as precipitation or adsorption techniques, to ensure compliance with environmental regulations.

It is also necessary to integrate this system with other remediation technologies. For hybrid remediation systems, further work needs to integrate the EGCG-promoted FeS2/Fe­(II)/PS system with other remediation technologies, such as membrane filtration, phytoremediation, or bioremediation, to achieve comprehensive arsenic removal. Meanwhile, for sequential treatment, further test needs to design sequential treatment processes where the system is used as a pretreatment step to oxidize As­(III) to As­(V), followed by adsorption or coagulation–flocculation for final removal.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) enhances As­(III) remediation in FeS2/Fe­(II)-rich simulated mine drainage via persulfate (PS) activation: adding 25 μM EGCG significantly improved As­(III) oxidation and adsorption (93.5% removal within 60 min under optimized conditions, 2.4-fold higher oxidation efficiency than the polyphenol-free system), •OH and O2 •– were dominant radicals for As­(III) transformation, Cl– and HCO3 – inhibited removal while SO4 2– and H2PO4 – had negligible effects, and EPR and DFT calculations revealed EGCG promotes electron transfer and radical generation via Fe­(III)-EGCG complexes. Environmentally, EGCG’s natural, nontoxic, biodegradable properties and the system’s mild conditions support its practical potential in mine waters, though limitations include EGCG’s light/high-temperature sensitivity and the need to adjust dosages for different water matrices. These findings highlight natural polyphenol-driven PS activation as a sustainable arsenic remediation approach, with future research focusing on improving EGCG stability and optimizing parameters for real mine drainage application.

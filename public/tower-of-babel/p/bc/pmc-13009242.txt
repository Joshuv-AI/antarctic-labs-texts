Optimized decomposition and deep learning with bias correction for reliable runoff point-interval prediction
Authors: Hong Ma, Muhammad Fadhil Marsani, Mohd Asyraf Mansor, Mohd Shareduwan Mohd Kasihmuddin, Caiyan Long
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate runoff prediction is critical for flood risk management and water resources regulation. This study proposes a probabilistic runoff forecasting framework that integrates optimized signal decomposition, deep learning, bias correction, and uncertainty quantification. Variational Mode Decomposition optimized by the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WVMD) is first applied to decompose non-stationary runoff series into stable intrinsic mode functions, which are then modeled using a hybrid Temporal Convolutional Network and Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (TCN-BiGRU) with optimized hyperparameters. A Bias Correction (BC) strategy is further incorporated to improve point prediction accuracy. Based on the runoff point prediction residuals generated by the WVMD-TCN-BiGRU-BC model, Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) is applied to characterize the error distribution and construct probabilistic prediction intervals, with the Normal and Gumbel distributions adopted as parametric benchmarks. Experimental results at the Yangtze River Basin demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly improves both deterministic and probabilistic forecasting performance. The BC strategy reduces RMSE by 68.0% at Yichang and 73.2% at Jianli station compared with the WVMD-TCN-BiGRU model, while the KDE based interval prediction yields an approximately 5% improvement in the F score at the 90% confidence level, confirming the reliability of the proposed runoff probabilistic forecasting framework.Keywords: Temporal convolutional network, Bidirectional gated recurrent unit, Whale optimization algorithm, Bias correction, Kernel density estimationSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Flood forecasting plays a crucial role in the effective allocation of water resources and the optimal management of river basins. The Yangtze River Basin, influenced by a subtropical monsoon climate, frequently experiences heavy rainfall and flooding, particularly during the summer months from June to August. Such events pose severe threats to public safety and property due to their sudden onset and extreme intensity1–3. As hydrological monitoring stations along the Yangtze River play a critical role in flood forecasting, their forecast data are essential for early warning, disaster prevention, and risk management under extreme weather conditions. However, unpredictable climate change and rapid infrastructure development have substantially altered hydrological patterns within the basin, presenting significant challenges for accurate flood and runoff forecasting in high-risk regions4.

With advances in artificial intelligence5, deep learning (DL) techniques have been increasingly applied in hydrological forecasting6. Nevertheless, the performance of DL models is often degraded by the presence of non-stationary characteristics and multi-scale temporal features in runoff data. Consequently, data preprocessing has become a critical step in improving forecasting accuracy7,8. Previous studies have shown that combining ensemble empirical mode decomposition (EEMD) with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks can significantly enhance predictive performance9–11. Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD), a non-recursive signal decomposition technique, further alleviates mode mixing by introducing penalty factors and bandwidth constraints12. Integrating VMD with deep learning models such as CNN, LSTM, and GRU has demonstrated notable improvements in forecasting accuracy13–16. However, CNN-based models often struggle to preserve temporal order in tasks with strong sequential dependence. To overcome this limitation, Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) were proposed by Bai et al.17, employing causal and dilated convolutions to effectively capture long-term temporal dependencies with improved computational efficiency. In addition, Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Units (BiGRU) address the unidirectional dependencies in time series and help overcome the limitations of LSTM and GRU models. When combined with TCN, BiGRU-based models have shown enhanced forecasting performance across various application domains18–23. Despite these advances, model performance remains highly sensitive to hyperparameter settings24. Additionally, both the number of modes and penalty parameters in VMD affect decomposition quality and forecasting accuracy. Therefore, effective hyperparameter optimization is essential to fully exploit the synergy among model components.

The use of intelligent algorithms to optimize model parameters is seen to improve prediction accuracy25. There is evidence that the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA)26 plays a crucial role in effectively regulating model parameters and is considered a good choice due to its simple structure and comprehensive global search capabilities. Zhao et al.27 optimized a BiLSTM model using WOA for short-term electricity load forecasting, achieving improved accuracy and reduced parameter tuning. Chen et al.28 applied WOA to optimize a BiLSTM-AM model for wind power forecasting, showing improved performance over non-optimized counterparts. In flood prediction, Ji et al.29 optimized a GRU model using WOA, achieving improved performance in time series forecasting. In this study, WOA-optimized VMD, named WVMD, is utilized to determine the parameters of VMD. WOA is also employed to tune the hyperparameters of the TCN-BiGRU model. However, several important questions remain regarding datasets that often contain data noise and model limitations. Data noise, which refers to random variations or errors in the dataset, can lead to inaccurate model training and predictions, as it introduces unnecessary complexity that obscures the true underlying patterns30,31. Such noise can arise from measurement errors, environmental variations, or inconsistencies in data collection processes. Model limitations, on the other hand, occur when the model is unable to fully capture the complexity of the data, either due to oversimplified assumptions, insufficient training data, or inadequate model architecture. These factors can result in poor generalization, over-fitting, or under-fitting, all of which negatively impact the model’s predictive performance. Hence, this study provides an account of DL-based bias correction (BC) by introducing the BC strategy into the WVMD-TCN-BiGRU, resulting in the WVMD-TCN-BiGRU-BC model for runoff forecasting.

Uncertainty modeling is essential for enhancing the reliability of hydrological forecasts. Interval forecasting provides a range of possible outcomes that explicitly accounts for uncertainty arising from environmental variability and data noise, thereby supporting more informed decision making. Probability-based interval forecasting sets confidence intervals to determine the upper and lower bounds of the forecast range, offering more valuable references for decision-makers32,33. Although numerous probabilistic approaches have been developed for streamflow forecasting9,34, traditional parametric probability distribution forecasting requires assumptions about error distributions that may not hold in practice35. Moreover, many existing studies focus primarily on point forecasting, with limited investigation into how probabilistic interval prediction influences overall forecasting performance. To address this gap, this study employs nonparametric Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to model forecast error distributions and generate flexible probabilistic prediction intervals. Since KDE performance is highly sensitive to bandwidth selection, its effectiveness is evaluated by comparison with traditional parametric distributions, including the Normal and Gumbel distributions, based on point runoff prediction errors.

This study advances beyond a simple hybridization of existing techniques by introducing a dual-level, WOA-driven optimization framework that explicitly and tightly integrates signal decomposition, point prediction, and probabilistic interval estimation into a unified forecasting pipeline.

WOA-optimized VMD (WVMD)

The performance of VMD is sensitive to two parameters: the penalty factor, which controls the bandwidth smoothness of each mode, and the number of modes , which determines the decomposition resolution. In this study, these parameters are automatically optimized using the WOA, as illustrated in Fig. 2.

The parameter optimization task is formulated as a continuous minimization problem. Each candidate solutionis evaluated by applying VMD to the runoff series and computing a permutation entropy (PE) based objective function. Specifically, PE is employed to quantify the regularity and complexity of the decomposed IMFs. For each candidate parameter pair, VMD is first applied to the runoff data, and the PE of each resulting IMF is calculated. The minimum PE value among all IMFs is selected as the fitness value. This strategy guides the optimization toward parameter combinations that produce at least one highly structured, low-complexity mode with strong predictive potential.

WOA optimized TCN-BiGRU

The proposed TCN-BiGRU model is designed to jointly exploit multi-scale temporal feature extraction and bidirectional dependency modeling for runoff prediction. In this hybrid architecture, a TCN and a BiGRU are integrated in a sequential parallel manner to fully capture both local and long-range temporal patterns.

Specifically, the input runoff sequence is first processed by a multi-layer TCN composed of dilated causal convolution blocks with residual connections. By progressively increasing the dilation factors, the TCN effectively captures temporal features at multiple time scales while strictly preserving temporal causality. The extracted high-level temporal representations are then fed into the BiGRU network, which models bidirectional temporal dependencies by jointly learning forward and backward sequence information. This structure enables the hybrid model to capture complex nonlinear dynamics in runoff series with enhanced representation capacity.

Following the establishment of the TCN-BiGRU prediction mechanism, the WOA is further employed at the prediction stage to automatically search for the optimal hyperparameter configuration of the hybrid model. Inspired by the bubble-net hunting strategy of humpback whales, WOA updates the positions of search agents through encircling prey, spiral updating, and random search, with the root mean squared error (RMSE) between predicted and observed runoff serving as the objective function.

Four key hyperparameters are optimized, including the number of convolutional filters, the convolutional filter size, the dropout rate, and the initial learning rate. 3The corresponding search ranges are set to values of 8 to 64, 3 to 8, 0.001 to 0.3, and 0.001 to 0.01, respectively.The population size and the maximum number of iterations are configured as 30 and 50. During each iteration, candidate hyperparameter configurations are evaluated, and WOA dynamically updates the search positions based on the best-performing solution, gradually converging toward the global optimum.

The optimal hyperparameter set obtained via WOA is subsequently adopted to train the TCN-BiGRU model. This adaptive optimization strategy effectively balances feature extraction capability, model complexity, and predictive accuracy, thereby enhancing the robustness and overall performance of the proposed runoff forecasting framework.

Study area and data

The study area is situated in the Yangtze River Basin, as shown in Fig. 5. It originates from the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau in China and spans approximately 24.5-35.75.5.75°N and 90.55-122.42.55.42°E in longitude. Historically, this basin has been highly susceptible to hydrological extremes, including frequent floods and droughts, owing to its complex climatic conditions and vast drainage area.

Location of the Yangtze River Basin and hydrological stations. The map was generated using ArcGIS Pro (version 3.1, https://www.esri.com/en-us/arcgis/products/arcgis-pro/overview). Hydrological station locations were manually marked by the authors.

A monthly runoff dataset spanning 540 months (January 1979-December 2023) from both stations was used in this study. To reduce potential non-stationarity, linear detrending was applied to the runoff series using a least-squares fitting method over the training period, as illustrated in Fig. 6(a) and (b). The results reveal a gradual increasing trend throughout the study period, suggesting the existence of long-term variability that may be attributed to climatic change and increasing anthropogenic influences such as reservoir regulation and land-use modification. This non-stationary behavior highlights the necessity of employing advanced decomposition and data-driven models to effectively capture both long-term trends and short-term fluctuations in runoff dynamics.

A preliminary time series analysis was then conducted to further examine stationarity and uncover intrinsic temporal characteristics. The ACF and PACF plots, shown in Fig.. 6(c) and (d), indicate a pronounced seasonal pattern with an approximate periodicity of 12 months, which is consistent with the annual hydrological cycle of the Yangtze River Basin driven by monsoon precipitation. Moreover, the truncation of the PACF at the 13th lag suggests an autoregressive structure of order 12 (AR(12)), implying that runoff values are strongly influenced by hydrological information from the preceding year.

Based on these statistical characteristics, the runoff time series were divided chronologically, with the first 80% of the data used for model training and the remaining 20% reserved for testing to avoid information leakage. Furthermore, a 12-month look-back window was adopted to predict the runoff of the subsequent month, allowing the proposed model to effectively learn seasonal dependencies and inter-annual memory effects embedded in the runoff process.

Runoff error probability distribution fitting and interval prediction

Error distribution characterization and density fitting

To characterize the statistical structure of runoff prediction errors, the residual series obtained from the WVMD-TCN-BiGRU-BC model were first examined through PDF analysis. Three distribution modeling approaches were considered, including the Normal distribution, the Gumbel distribution, and the ROT-KDE. Fig. 10(a) and (b) illustrate the PDF representations of the residuals at Yichang and Jianli stations, respectively. The residual distributions at both stations exhibit slight right skewness and heavy-tailed characteristics (Yichang: skewness = 0.3559, kurtosis = 3.7086; Jianli: skewness = 0.3973, kurtosis = 3.1361), indicating clear deviations from the Gaussian assumption. These structural features revealed by the PDFs suggest that conventional parametric distributions may be inadequate for accurately characterizing the error behavior. Accordingly, the nonparametric ROT-KDE method was adopted to flexibly capture the empirical density structure of the residuals, with bandwidths determined as 10.072 for Yichang and 7.510 for Jianli based on the Rule-of-Thumb criterion.

PDF and CDF analyzes of runoff point prediction errors at Yichang and Jianli stations.

Building upon the PDF-based error structure analysis, the corresponding CDFs were further investigated to assess the global distributional consistency of different modeling approaches and to support subsequent uncertainty quantification. Fig. 10(c) and (d) present the empirical CDFs of the residuals together with the fitted CDFs derived from the Normal distribution, Gumbel distribution, and ROT-KDE for the two stations. It can be observed that the ROT-KDE-based CDF exhibits the closest agreement with the empirical CDF over the entire probability range, particularly in the tail regions that are critical for extreme error representation. In contrast, the parametric distributions show noticeable deviations in both central and tail regions, reflecting their limited ability to accommodate skewness and heavy-tailed behavior. This combined PDF-CDF analysis demonstrates that ROT-KDE not only captures the local density characteristics of runoff prediction errors but also provides a more accurate global cumulative representation, thereby establishing a reliable probabilistic foundation for interval prediction.

The comparative distribution fitting performance was evaluated using three CDF-based metrics, namely EMAE, ERMSE, and the coefficient of determination ER2, as reported in Table 3. These metrics quantify the discrepancies between the empirical CDF and the fitted CDFs obtained from different distribution models. As shown in Table 3, ROT-KDE consistently yields the lowest EMAE and ERMSE and the highest ER2 at both stations. At Yichang station, ROT-KDE reduces EMAE and ERMSE by 28.55% and 32.08% relative to the Normal distribution, and by 65.43% and 64.02% compared with the Gumbel distribution, respectively. Similar improvements are observed at Jianli station, confirming that ROT-KDE provides a more accurate and stable approximation of the residual CDF. Based on the above CDF fitting results, ROT-KDE is adopted for subsequent probabilistic interval construction, enabling uncertainty quantification of runoff predictions at different confidence levels.

The results clearly show that point prediction accuracy plays a decisive role in interval prediction performance. Models with higher point prediction accuracy generally produce smaller and more stable residuals, which directly facilitates more accurate density estimation and leads to narrower, better-calibrated prediction intervals. In contrast, inferior point prediction performance results in dispersed residual distributions, inevitably degrading interval sharpness even when the same probabilistic method is applied.

Benefiting from its superior deterministic forecasting capability, the proposed WVMD-TCN-BiGRU -BC-ROT-KDE framework achieves the best overall interval prediction performance at both stations. As reported in Table 5, compared with other DL based models, it reduces EMAE and ERMSE by approximately 15%-40%, while further decreasing PINAW by about 20%-35%, with PICP values remaining at or above the nominal confidence level. Consequently, the comprehensive F score is improved by roughly 5%-10%, indicating a more favorable balance between interval reliability and sharpness. These quantitative advantages are also consistently reflected in Fig. 12(a)-(b), where the prediction intervals generated by the proposed model are visibly more compact and stable, yet consistently encompass the observed runoff series.

Overall, these results demonstrate that the proposed KDE based probabilistic framework effectively translates improvements achieved at the point prediction stage into more informative and reliable prediction intervals. By accurately modeling the error distribution derived from high-quality point forecasts, the proposed approach enhances uncertainty quantification and provides a robust tool for probabilistic runoff prediction.

Although the proposed framework achieves significant improvements in both point and interval prediction accuracy, several limitations remain. First, the multi-method ensemble structure increases model complexity and computational cost, which may restrict real-time applications. Future research will focus on simplifying the architecture, improving computational efficiency, and testing the model’s transferability to other hydrological environments.

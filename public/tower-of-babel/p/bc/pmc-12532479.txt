Imaging features of eosinophilic solid and cystic renal cell carcinoma
Authors: Chunxiang Ling, Jiamei Li, Ru Tan, Jizhen Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo analyse imaging features and the differential diagnosis of eosinophilic solid and cystic renal cell carcinoma (ESC-RCC) .MethodsThe current study retrospectively reviewed the imaging features of seven patients (4 male, 3 female; age range: 25-70 years; median age: 46 years) diagnosed with ESC-RCC at our institution from March 2020 to October 2024.ResultsSix patients presented with unilateral and solitary lesions, one patient (1/7, 14.3%) exhibited bilateral multifocal lesions. The tumour size ranged from 2.0 to 8.5 cm, with a mean diameter of 4.0±2.1 cm. Most (5/8, 62.5%) tumours showed high and low mixed density on non-contrast CT scans. The remaining tumors exhibited the following characteristics: one presented as homogeneous isodensity, another as hypodense with foci of hyperdensity, and a third as slightly hyperdense with small areas of hypodensity. On contrast-enhanced CT, 75% (6/8) of the tumours demonstrated heterogeneous enhancement with peak CT values in the corticomedullary phase for four cases and in the nephrographic phase for two. Of the remaining two, one displayed a gradually progressive enhancement pattern with peak enhancement in the nephrographic phase, while the other showed mild enhancement. All (8/8) tumors were well-demarcated, with 75% (6/8) causing adjacent vascular compression and 87.5% (7/8) showing floating vessels. Tumour classification indicated that most tumours (37.5%) were categorized as type III, with types II and IV each comprising two cases, and type I included only one. Microscopically, all tumours were well-demarcated and lacked a capsule, lymph node metastasis, hemorrhage, calcification and necrosis.ConclusionsESC-RCC should be considered when encountering a well-defined cystic-solid lesion without calcification, haemorrhage, or fat, and the solid component shows various enhancement with the degree of enhancement less than that of the adjacent renal cortex in all phases.Keywords: Eosinophilic solid and cystic renal cell carcinoma, Computed tomography, Diagnosis, Pathology, Differential diagnosis

Introduction

Eosinophilic solid and cystic renal cell carcinoma (ESC-RCC) was first described in 2016 by Trpkov et al. [1], and was named based on its characteristic cystic solid structure as well as the presence of abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. Given the distinct morphological, immunohistochemical, and molecular genetic features of the tumour, ESC-RCC is recognized as a novel entity in the World Health Organization (WHO) in its 2022 revision of Classification of Tumours of the Urinary System and Male Genital Organs recently established [2]. It is a rare renal cancer, accounting for approximately 0.2% of renal epithelial tumours [1, 3]. Currently, to the best of our knowledge, radiographic details regarding ESC-RCC remain scarce. Due to the rarity of ESC-RCC, most previous studies focused on its pathological features, with only a limited [4–10] investigating its imaging features, and the majority of these were case reports. As a result, the imaging manifestations of ESC-RCC are still not fully understood. Therefore, it is essential to accumulate more imaging data on ESC-RCC in order to better illustrate its radiological features. In this retrospective study of seven ESC-RCC patients, the computer tomography (CT) imaging features were analyzed for any characteristic imaging phenotype. Furthermore, the ultrasound features and differential diagnostic considerations of ESC-RCC are also discussed.

Materials and methods

Study participants

The present study retrospectively analyzed all patients diagnosed with ESC-RCC and treated at the Shandong Provincial Hospital, affiliated with Shandong First Medical University (China) from March 2020 to October 2024. To confirm the diagnosis of ESC-RCC, all pathological sections were re-reviewed by an experienced genitourinary pathologist, as described in the WHO classification [2]. This retrospective case series comprises seven patients (4 male, 3 female; age range: 25–70 years; median age: 46 years) who were diagnosed with an ESC-RCC. Cases were identified through a system review of the hospital’s medical records.

Data collection

A search of our institution’s pathology database using the keywords “solid and cystic” and “renal cell carcinoma” was conducted between March 2020 and October 2024. Seven cases of ESC-RCC were identified and subsequently confirmed by two uropathologists. The electronic medical records (EMRs) were searched with the inpatient number in order to collect clinical information, surgical details, and imaging examinations for the patients. The image data was obtained from the picture archiving and communication system (PACS) of the imaging centre at our institution. The enrolled patients were followed up by telephone. General clinical information, imaging findings, treatment details, as well as histological and prognostic data were systematically collected. All patients received ultrasound and preoperative abdominal CT scans, which were available for review. Since the tumor has been removed, we only documented the image manifestations based on the ultrasound examination results. CT images were reviewed by two experienced abdominal radiologists with 17 (Tan R) and 15 (Ling C) years of experience in abdominal imaging blinded to all patient information, including final diagnosis and clinical history. They independently evaluated images from multiphase CT examinations in two separate sessions using PACS. All discordant cases were later reviewed by a third abdominal radiologist with 18 years of experience (Feng J) in abdominal cross-sectional imaging to establish a consensus diagnosis. Through a comprehensive review of the existing literature and integration of the imaging features observed in our case, we proposed a classification of ESC-RCC into four categories based on its CT imaging manifestations and recorded the number of each category. The classification criteria are defined as follows: Type Ⅰ, solid, homogeneous density on non-enhanced CT and enhanced CT. Type Ⅱ, cystic-solid with microcysts, slightly hyperdense with small foci of hypodensity on non-enhanced CT and small hypodense areas on enhanced CT. Type III, cystic-solid with macrocysts and microcysts, high and low mixed density on non-enhanced CT, and multiple hypodense areas of varying sizes on enhanced CT. Type Ⅳ, cystic-solid with large cysts, hypodense with foci of hyperdense on non-enhanced CT and patchy and septal hyperdense on enhanced CT. When a blood vessel traverses a lesion or the lesion extends along the vascular pathways, without inducing evident stenosis, encasement, or other manifestations of vascular invasion, we defined it as a floating vessel sign and recorded it.

All surgical samples were fixed in 10% formalin, for processing and preparation of 4-micron-thick paraffin-embedded sections. These were stained with routine hematoxylin and eosin (HE) for morphological examination. Immunohistochemistry for antigen detection was performed on Ventana XT Benchmark using the following antibodies: CK20, PAX8, CD10, CD117 (C-kit), CK7, CA9, AE1/AE3, Ki-67, SDHB, and vimentin.

Statistical method

Statistical analysis was performed using the SPSS 26.0 statistical software (IBM Corp.). Data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation, while count data are expressed in percentages.

CT findings

CT imaging findings are summarized in Table 3. The tumours were located in the renal cortex in five cases and in the renal corticomedullary junction in three cases. One patient presented with multiple lesions involving both kidneys (Fig. 1A). The tumour size ranged from 2.0 to 8.5 cm, with a mean diameter of 4.0 ± 2.1 cm. Non-contrast CT showed that the majority (5/8, 62.5%) of these tumours were high and low mixed density. Of the three remaining tumours, one was homogeneous isodense, one was hypodense with foci of hyperdensity, and one was slightly hyperdense with small foci of hypodensity. Enhanced CT demonstrated that most of the tumours (6/8, 75%) appeared as obviously heterogeneous enhancement. The peak CT value was observed in the corticomedullary phase in four tumors and in the nephrographic phase in the remaining two. Of the two remaining, one exhibited an obviously gradual enhancement pattern with peak CT value in the nephrographic phase, while the other demonstrated mild enhancement. All tumours were well-demarcated. The majority of the tumours (6/8, 75%) showed adjacent vascular compression (Fig. 1A). Most of the tumours (7/8, 87.5%) displayed the floating vessels sign (Fig. 1B). Most tumors (3/8, 37.5%) are classified as type III. Type II and type IV each have 2 cases, while type I is represented by only 1 case. All tumours were nonencapsulated, free of lymph node metastasis and tumor thrombus, and no calcification, fat, or hemorrhage was seen.

Histopathological findings

Microscopically, all tumours were well-demarcated and lacked a capsule. The tumours consisted of solid nests and confluent sheets of eosinophilic cells, often interspersed with larger macrocysts (Fig. 2A). Although 1 tumor manifested only a solid gross appearance, there were microcysts by histologic evaluation. The microcysts were lined by shoe tack cells with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm (Fig. 2B). The solid area is diffuse, dense acinar or nest with abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm (Fig. 2C). Prominent basophilic particles (Fig. 2D) and eosinophilic globules were observed within part of the cytoplasm. Immunohistochemically, tumours expressed CK20 (8/8, 100%) (Fig. 2E), SDHB (6/8, 75%) and PAX8 (5/8, 62.5%) (Fig. 2F). The Ki-67 proliferation index was very low, and 7 tumors were 1%–3%, only one case was 3%−10% (hot spot region). No cases showed lymph node metastasis, hemorrhage, calcification, or necrosis.

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this contemporary single-institution cohort represents the largest ESC-RCC series focusing on imaging in the current literature. ESC-RCC is a rare subtype of renal cancer, accounting for 0.2% of renal epithelial tumours [1, 3]. Patients with ESC-RCC are usually asymptomatic, although some may present with abdominal pain. This observation was consistent with our cohort, in which five patients were identified incidentally and two presented with abdominal pain. Among the seven cases, six patients were unilateral and solitary, one patient was multifocal and bilateral, as previously reported [1, 4, 7, 11, 12]. In previous studies, the vast majority cases of ESC-RCC have been reported as sporadic; however, rare cases have been reported in patients with tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC) [13, 14]. Additionally, ESC-RCC has been reported to be prevalent in female patients with a broad age range [4]. In the present study, all tumours were sporadic, and patient age ranged between 25 and 70 years, with a median age of 46 years. The male-to-female ratio was 4:3, which is different from previous studies [4]. This discrepancy may be attributed to the relatively small sample size. In this study, most tumours (5/8, 62.5%) were located in the renal cortex, in contrast to a previous study by Yi et al., in which the majority of tumours (8/9, 89%) were found in the renal medulla [4]. We hypothesize that this may be attributed to the small sample sizes of the two groups, and therefore, large samples are still required to confirm the origin of ESC-RCC. Previous studies have shown that the majority of ESC-RCC showed indolent behaviour, although a few cases may demonstrate metastasis [12, 15, 16]. In our research, metastasis was identified in one patient during CT follow-up after surgery.

The ultrasound imaging of ESC-RCC has been rarely described in the literature [7]. Xu et al. reported that the tumour appeared heterogeneous hypoechoic, with well-demarcated borders and CDFI revealed blood flow signals in both the periphery and central regions of the tumour. Consistent with this report, the majority of the tumours in this study exhibited well-defined margins and heterogeneous echogenicity. CDFI detected blood flow signal in four tumours. Although the ultrasound manifestations of ESC-RCC are rare, ultrasound demonstrates high sensitivity in detecting both the cystic and solid components of the lesion, as well as in evaluating its blood flow characteristics. Therefore, ultrasound is considered the preferred initial imaging modality for the screening of ESC-RCC.

In this report, a non-contrast CT scan revealed that the majority of the tumours (5/8, 62.5%) exhibited heterogeneous density with a mixture of high and low attenuation areas. Among the remaining three tumours, one presented with homogeneous isodense, another was predominantly hypodense with foci of hyperdense, and the last one was slightly hyperdense with small foci of hypodense—findings consistent with those reported in previous studies [4–10]. This observed pattern may be attributed to the compact acinar or compact nested growth pattern of ESC-ECC cells, as hypothesized in our analysis. The various enhancement patterns and the degree of enhancement of the solid component being less than that of the adjacent normal renal parenchyma in each phase of ESC-RCC were described in a previous study [4]. Consistent with these findings, the present study found the majority of tumours (6/8, 75%) exhibited heterogeneous enhancement, with the CT value peak in the corticomedullary (4/6, 66.7%) or in the nephrographic phase (2/6, 33.3%). Of the two remaining tumours, one demonstrated gradual enhancement with peak CT value in the nephrographic phase, while the other showed mild enhancement. The degree of enhancement of the tumours was less than that of the adjacent normal renal cortex.

The relationship between ESC-RCC and adjacent blood vessels was evaluated for the first time in this study. Among the eight tumours examined, six (6/8, 75%) exhibited adjacent vascular compression, and seven tumours (7/8, 87.5%) demonstrated the floating vessels sign. The floating vessel sign is defined as a radiological feature characterized by blood vessels traversing a lesion or the lesion extending along the vascular pathways, without causing evident stenosis, encasement, or other signs of vascular invasion. This phenomenon is most commonly observed in hepatic lymphoma [17, 18]. In such cases, the blood vessels maintain their natural course within the lesion and appear to “float” on a horizontal orientation. The floating vessel sign observed in ESC-RCC is comparable to that seen in hepatic lymphoma. Therefore, this radiological feature should be considered non-specific and insufficient for a definitive diagnosis. Therefore, the presence of vascular floating signs in renal tumors suggests the possibility of ESC-RCC.

In our research, all tumours exhibited a cystic-solid appearance pathologically. However, one case presented as a predominantly solid appearance on the CT imaging. We hypothesize that this discrepancy may be due to the limited soft tissue resolution of CT, which may prevent the detection of small microcysts.

Calcification, hemorrhage, and fat were uncommon in ESC-RCC, which may serve as distinguishing features when differentiating it from other subtypes of renal cell carcinomas. Calcification is most commonly seen in malignant renal tumour [19, 20]. The differential diagnosis of ESC-RCC includes clear cell RCC, lipid-poor angiomyolipoma, multilocular cystic RCC, chromophobe RCC, and renal oncocytoma. (1) clear cell RCC. It predominantly affects individuals aged 50–70 years. Non-contrast CT often reveals isodense or hypodense, frequently accompanied by necrosis, hemorrhage, and cystic degeneration. Most cases of necrosis and cystic degeneration are usually solitary, centrally located, and ill-demarcated, and exhibit irregular cyst wall thickening. On contrast-enhanced CT, clear cell RCC demonstrates a fast wash-in and fast wash-out enhancement pattern, with a degree of enhancement exceeding that of the adjacent renal cortex. It is commonly accompanied by lymph node metastasis and renal vein tumor thrombus. Among the aforementioned differential features, the enhancement pattern is a critical differentiating factor between ESC-RCC and clear cell RCC. Although the enhancement pattern of ESC-RCC varies, maximum enhancement on the CT image occurs in the corticomedullary or nephrographic phase, with the degree of enhancement consistently less than that of the adjacent renal cortex in all phases, which is different from clear cell RCC. (2) lipid-poor angiomyolipoma. Angular interface, overflowing beer sign/drooping sign, hypodense rim, homogeneous enhancement pattern, and high unenhanced attenuation are specific CT features that may suggest lipid-poor renal angiomyolipoma [21–23]. Among these features, hyperdense on non-contrast and the presence of lipid components are the primary distinguishing factors between ESC-RCC and angiomyolipoma. Indeed, the presence of intratumoral lipid components serves as a key diagnostic criterion for differentiating angiomyolipomca from ESC-RCC. (3) multilocular cystic RCC. The imaging manifestations of multilocular cystic RCC are diverse. The majority of cases are categorized as Bosniak category III, however, some may be classified as IIF, characterized by predominantly fluid-filled content with solid component, while others fall into Bosniak IV, which typically contains a substantial amount of enhancing fibrous tissue [24]. The most common pathological subtype of multilocular cystic RCC is clear cell carcinoma, which demonstrates a “fast-in and fast-out” enhancement pattern, making it easily distinguishable from ESC-RCC. The imaging features of other pathological subtypes of multilocular cystic RCC may overlap with those of ESC-RCC, requiring pathological examination for differentiation. (4) Chromophobe RCC (chRCC). chRCC is predominantly characterized by solid or mostly solid masses with well-defined margins, often exhibiting a heterogeneous appearance. Calcifications and/or a central scar with or without a spoke-wheel pattern of enhancement are seen in a significant minority of cases. chRCC shows a moderately vascular tumor with peak enhancement occurring mostly in the nephrographic phase and less commonly in the corticomedullary phase, and enhancement in all phases is lower than that of the renal cortex [25–27]. In the present study, the majority of tumours (6/8, 75%) demonstrated significant enhancement. Among these, 66.7% (4/6) reached peak enhancement during the cutaneous corticomedullary phase, while 33.3% (2/6) reached peak enhancement in the nephrographic phase. Therefore, a combination of non-contrast scanning and enhanced imaging techniques is necessary to distinguish ESC-RCC from chromophobe cell carcinoma. (5) Renal oncocytoma. Most tumours are isodense or hypodense with a central stellate scar, well-differentiated margins, and homogeneous enhancement. Hemorrhage, calcification, necrosis, and fat are rarely observed [28, 29]. The maximum enhancement of oncocytomas typically occurs in the corticomedullary phase, characterized by a degree of enhancement that is lower than that of the adjacent renal cortex, along with segmental enhancement inversion and the presence of a central stellate scar, which is the key differential point between ESC-RCC and oncocytomas [28, 30].

However, the present study has some limitations. First, the present study is only a single retrospective centre study; the sample size is small, which is due to the low incidence rate of ESC-RCC. Second, no patient in this study underwent MRI examinations, although MRI images may provide more information in the diagnosis of ESC-RCC. The result of the present study, especially regarding image classification, needs to be further confirmed by a multi-centre, large sample study.

Conclusion

Imaging manifestations of ESC-RCC are diverse due to the solid and cystic growth pattern with adjacent vascular compression and the floating vessels sign seen in most tumours. According to the proportion of cystic and solid components and the size of the cysts seen on CT images, ESC-RCC can be categorized into four types, but type III was the most common. Therefore, ESC-RCC should be considered when encountering a well-defined cystic-solid lesion without calcification, haemorrhage, or fat, and the solid component shows various enhancement with the degree of enhancement less than that of the adjacent renal cortex in all phases.

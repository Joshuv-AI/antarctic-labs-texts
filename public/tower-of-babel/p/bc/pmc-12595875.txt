Exploring the lived experiences of pregnant women regarding the challenges and needs of higher-order multiple pregnancies in Western Iran: a phenomenological descriptive study
Authors: Zhina Banafshi, Alireza Khatony, Rostam Jalali
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMultiple gestations impose greater physiological, psychological, and socioeconomic demands compared to singleton pregnancies. Given the limited data on the unique challenges and multifaceted needs of pregnant women with this condition, this study aimed to explore the lived experiences of these women regarding the challenges and needs associated with higher-order multiple pregnancies.MethodThis study employed a qualitative approach with a descriptive phenomenology design. Using a maximum variation purposive sampling method, we selected women from Western Iran who had experienced higher-order multiple pregnancies. Sampling continued until data saturation was reached. Data were collected through face-to-face, semi-structured, in-depth interviews, which had a mean duration of 73.44 ± 10.44 min. Data analysis was performed using Colaizzi’s seven-step method. The criteria of Lincoln and Guba were applied to ensure the trustworthiness and rigor of the findings.ResultsParticipants’ experiences were encapsulated within five overarching themes and 20 sub-themes. These themes included: Crushed under the pressure and responsibilities, Multiplicity of life amidst profound loneliness, immersion in needs, Journey of Acceptance, and Living Amidst the Ethical Struggles. The mean age of participants was 32.5 ± 5.46 years. Furthermore, 75% of the pregnancies were triplets, and 75% were conceived through assisted reproductive techniques.ConclusionWomen navigate these challenges through diverse adaptive and non-adaptive strategies, while articulating distinct needs whose fulfillment could ease complexities and foster acceptance of multiple pregnancy. Effectively addressing these multifaceted issues requires a comprehensive, multi-pronged approach by policymakers and healthcare leaders, encompassing supportive policies, specialized healthcare, prenatal education, essential facilities, and strengthened adaptive mechanisms.Keywords: High-risk pregnancy, Assisted reproductive techniques, Multiple pregnancy, Lived experiences, Phenomenological research

Introduction

In recent decades, the rate of multiple pregnancies has significantly increased worldwide [1]. This increase, with 12 out of every 1000 births being multiple pregnancies, is primarily attributed to the widespread use of assisted reproductive technologies (ART) and the increasing maternal age at pregnancy [1, 2]. Currently, multiple pregnancies account for 3% of all births globally [3].

Multiple pregnancy is a high-risk condition with specific needs that can be a source of significant stress [4]. Undesirable maternal and fetal complications are more common in multiple pregnancies [5], and women face extensive physical, emotional, psychological, social, and economic challenges that can have negative impacts on the mother’s and fetus’s health [6]. Pregnant women with multiple fetuses are more vulnerable due to their complex physical condition and require more specialized healthcare, medical services, and prenatal care than women with singleton pregnancies [7, 8]. Evidence suggests that pregnant women with multiple fetuses often face significant difficulties in meeting their basic needs [9, 10]. Suddenly, women with multiple pregnancies are confronted with substantial changes, widespread complications, and new and diverse roles and responsibilities for which they are unprepared, leading to high levels of anxiety, fear, fatigue, marital and family problems, decreased quality of life, and well-being [9, 11]. In this regard, the results of a study conducted in 2023 indicate that multiple pregnancies significantly increase the risks and complications for both the mother and the fetus [12]. Multiple pregnancies serve as a stressor for women and often pose challenges in their acceptance and adaptation to pregnancy [13].

Pregnant women with multiple fetuses require more support, guidance, and continuous and comprehensive specialized care compared to women with singleton pregnancies. They often feel lonely and perceive that their needs are poorly understood and inadequately supported [14, 15]. Reducing and addressing the challenges of multiple pregnancies leads to promoting maternal and fetal health, improving family functioning, and readiness to assume new family, social, and economic roles, such as organizing care for multiple infants, adjusting family relationships, and managing escalating costs [9, 11]. Therefore, the first step in managing these challenges is to understand them from the perspective of women who have experienced them [16].

The most effective way to manage the challenges associated with multiple pregnancies is to gain a deeper understanding of these experiences from the women’s perspective. For this reason, a qualitative approach, specifically descriptive phenomenology, was chosen to explore this phenomenon in depth. This methodology is rooted in the philosophy of Edmund Husserl and is well-suited for discovering the essence of a lived experience. It achieves a deep, rich, and direct understanding of participants’ experiences by emphasizing bracketing—the process of setting aside the researcher’s preconceptions [17].

Given the increasing rate of multiple births worldwide [1], along with the significant challenges, diverse needs of women with multiple fetuses, and the potential risks to both mother and fetus [18], paying closer attention to this phenomenon is essential. This is particularly crucial within the unique cultural and healthcare context of certain regions, such as Western Iran. In this area, family structures, cultural-religious values, and social expectations play a significant role in shaping women’s pregnancy experiences. Consequently, exploring their experiences within this specific context is vital for developing targeted interventions [6]. Due to the limited research in this area, the researchers aimed to conduct a qualitative study to explore the challenges and needs of women with higher-order multiple pregnancies in Western Iran. The findings of this study can contribute to enhancing the understanding of healthcare providers and policymakers regarding the challenges of multiple pregnancies, enabling them to implement appropriate strategies to manage these challenges.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study employed a descriptive phenomenological approach to uncover the meaning and essence of the challenges and needs experienced by women with higher-order multiple pregnancies. Rooted in the philosophy of Edmund Husserl, this methodology was chosen because it facilitates a deep exploration of a highly personal and complex phenomenon. By focusing on individuals’ lived experiences, this approach enables researchers to set aside preconceptions and prior experiences—a process known as bracketing—to gain a rich, comprehensive understanding of the participants’ reality [19]. This approach is fully consistent with our study’s aim to gain a deep and rich understanding of these individuals’ experiences within the cultural context of Western Iran, as prior research in this area has been limited. This study follows the Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research (COREQ) guidelines [20].

Study setting and recruitment

This study was conducted in Kurdistan province, western Iran, from March 2023 to October 2024. The study population comprised women with a history of higher-order multiple pregnancies. Participants were selected using purposive sampling to ensure maximum diversity across several characteristics: age, employment status, education level, place of residence, economic status, number of children, type of pregnancy (e.g., spontaneous or assisted), history of miscarriage, fertility treatment modalities, and family history of multiple pregnancies. The inclusion criteria for participation were a documented history of higher-order multiple pregnancies and willingness to provide informed consent and participate in the study. The principle of thematic saturation determined the sample size in this qualitative study. Unlike quantitative research, which relies on statistical power to achieve statistically significant results, the adequacy of the sample size in qualitative research is judged by the richness and depth of the data collected and the point at which new information no longer contributes to developing new themes or insights [21]. Thematic saturation was reached after the thirteenth interview, at which point no new themes or significant insights emerged. To further enhance the trustworthiness and robustness of the findings, three additional interviews were conducted beyond the point of saturation.

Data collection

Data collection was conducted by the first author, a female PhD nursing student with a background in qualitative research and clinical experience in prenatal care. The researcher’s clinical background in obstetrics provided a deep understanding of the needs and challenges faced by mothers with multiple pregnancies. This prior insight enabled the researcher to listen to participants’ narratives with greater sensitivity and to formulate more comprehensive questions during interviews. This initial familiarity with the key issues also played a crucial role in the purposeful selection of key informants for the interviews. Despite this, an awareness of the researcher’s potential influence on the data made it necessary to take steps to minimize bias. To this end, the researcher actively employed reflexivity and bracketing from the outset to set aside personal presuppositions. Following each interview, field notes were recorded to identify any potential blind spots, and questions were posed in an open-ended, non-directive manner. Furthermore, to enhance the trustworthiness of the findings, techniques such as member checking, peer debriefing, and maintaining an audit trail were employed. These measures ensured that the findings had the necessary authenticity and credibility and were shaped by the participants’ lived experiences.

The data collection process began with the first author’s in-person visits to healthcare centers in Kurdistan province. After introducing herself and presenting the necessary ethical approvals from Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences, she obtained the contact numbers of women with a history of multiple pregnancies. Subsequently, potential participants were contacted by phone. The researcher introduced herself, explained the study’s objectives, and emphasized that participation was entirely voluntary and would not affect the quality of their healthcare services. Following an initial assessment of their experiences and willingness to participate, a formal invitation to join the study was sent to them. Semi-structured, in-person interviews were conducted in a single session with each participant, at a time and location of their choosing, with all interviews taking place in the participants’ homes. Each session lasted approximately 60 to 90 min. All interviews were conducted in Kurdish, the native language of both the participants and the interviewer (first author). This was crucial for facilitating rapport, building trust, and enabling participants to express their experiences freely and deeply. To ensure the accuracy and rigor of the qualitative data, we utilized a back-translation procedure. First, all recorded interviews were translated directly from Kurdish to English by the first author, who is fluent in both languages. Then, an independent translator, also proficient in both languages, translated the English text back into Kurdish. The final Kurdish text was compared against the original interview transcript to identify and correct any semantic discrepancies or subtle differences. This process guaranteed linguistic and semantic accuracy, ensuring the data were correctly prepared for analysis. The interview guide consisted of open-ended and exploratory questions, such as: “Please describe your experience with the challenges of multiple pregnancies,” “How do you perceive the challenges of multiple pregnancies,” “What significance do the challenges of multiple pregnancies hold for you,” and “Please explain your needs during multiple pregnancies.” Probing phrases like “Please provide further details” and “Why” were used to deepen the responses.

To mitigate the potential influence of researcher bias and enhance the trustworthiness of the data, a continuous process of reflexivity was employed. This involved the researcher’s ongoing self-awareness of her own preconceptions, clinical knowledge, and personal experiences. Field notes were used to document non-verbal observations, the interview context, and the researcher’s initial impressions. Additionally, reflective memos were regularly written to record the researcher’s thoughts and reactions. These notes were instrumental in identifying and bracketing any preconceived notions, ensuring that the data interpretation was rooted solely in the participants’ perspectives. With the participants’ permission, all interviews were audio-recorded and immediately transcribed verbatim upon completion. It is also important to note that the researcher had no direct therapeutic or caregiving role with the study participants, which further supported the reflexivity process.

Trustworthiness

To ensure the accuracy and rigor of the data, Lincoln and Guba’s criteria (1994), including credibility, transferability, dependability, and confirmability, were employed [23], along with an emphasis on reflexivity and authenticity to enhance credibility and reliability [24]. For credibility assurance, long-term engagement with the participants was established. After analyzing each interview, the researchers revisited the participants to validate the accuracy of the information. Additionally, experienced qualitative research experts provided continuous supervision throughout the study process. To ensure dependability, all stages of the study were described step by step with explicit details to enable informed evaluation by readers. For confirmability, the recorded interviews, transcripts, and their corresponding codes were made available to the research team and expert qualitative researchers. Any disagreements in coding were resolved through discussion and consensus, and they subsequently confirmed the accuracy of the coding. To ensure the transferability of the data, the study findings were shared with three pregnant women who were not part of the study, and they confirmed the congruence of the findings with their experiences. The researchers actively maintained reflexivity by critically reflecting on their assumptions, beliefs, and prior experiences that could influence data interpretation. This reflexivity was supported through journaling, discussions, and collaborative peer reviews. Authenticity was preserved by incorporating direct participant quotes and rich contextual descriptions to represent participants’ voices and experiences.

Ethical considerations

This study, registered under the code (IR.KUMS.REC.1401.590), received ethical approval from the relevant ethics committee. Throughout the study, the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki were adhered to. The study objectives were clearly explained to all participants, and the confidentiality of their personal information, data, and responses was emphasized. Participants retained the right to withdraw from the study at any stage. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, and each individual granted permission for audio recording to be conducted. To ensure the complete anonymity of participants, no direct identifying information was recorded; instead, each participant was assigned a unique numerical code. Written data is stored securely in a restricted-access location, and digital data is protected through encryption on secure university servers. The analysis and reporting of findings do not include any identifying information, and the data will be retained only for the duration necessary for analysis and publication. After this, it will be securely disposed of in accordance with the university’s guidelines established by the ethics committee.

Results

The participants included 16 women with a history of multiple pregnancies. The majority of participants were homemakers (n = 11, 68%). The mean age of the participants was 32.5 ± 5.46 years, and 75% of them (n = 12) had experience with triplet pregnancies. Among them, 75% (n = 12) had achieved pregnancy through assisted reproductive techniques. Most of them (n = 10, 62%) have an average economic status. (Table 1). The results of the interview analysis revealed five main themes and 20 sub-themes. Themes and subthemes related to the experiences of pregnant mothers regarding the challenges and needs of multiple pregnancy

Crushed under the pressure and responsibilities

The experience of multiple pregnancies presents a complex and high-pressure challenge for women, profoundly impacting every aspect of their lives. Maintaining a balance between new and existing roles during this period is often overwhelming and exhausting. The added burden of multiple pregnancies places significant psychological and emotional pressure on mothers, leading to feelings of being crushed and overwhelmed by the weight of their situation. This is intensified by societal expectations and unfair gender-based judgments, which exacerbate the pressure and contribute to their sense of being utterly defeated by their circumstances.

Overwhelmed roles

Women with multiple pregnancies are confronted with the challenge of simultaneously navigating multiple new and existing roles. They often strive to balance their individual identity, spousal duties, professional obligations, and maternal responsibilities with the demanding physical and emotional needs of a high-risk pregnancy. This constant effort to fulfill concurrent roles often proves to be overwhelming and emotionally exhausting, highlighting the fragile nature of their balance and the numerous challenges they face.

“During my pregnancy, my whole life was in chaos. I had lost control over managing the household and taking care of my children. Most of the time, I couldn’t even attend to my tasks, the house, the kids, or my husband. I couldn’t even reach out to my husband, and many times he had to do my tasks as well.” A Participant 5.

Pregnant women with multiple pregnancies often experience feelings of fatigue and exhaustion while fulfilling their multiple and sometimes conflicting roles. They may feel dissatisfied, anxious, and stressed due to the perceived inability to meet their own and others’ expectations. Social pressures and unrealistic expectations can exacerbate this issue.

“Sometimes I feel like I’m slowly crumbling under the weight of responsibilities and expectations everyone has of me. The constant stress and anxiety that I experience in carrying out my duties have led to exhaustion.” Participant 1.

In the context of multiple pregnancies, mothers face the simultaneous task of addressing the physical and psychological needs of each of their children, which can be challenging. Mothers may experience uncertainty and confusion regarding their ability to fulfill the maternal role adequately, often feeling inadequate and lacking the necessary skills to undertake such a significant responsibility. Consequently, they endure significant physical and psychological pressures that adversely affect their well-being. Additionally, adapting to sudden lifestyle changes can be a challenging experience for mothers.

“I didn’t feel good about it. I felt unprepared to become a mother of four children. I was very worried that I wouldn’t be able to handle it and be a good mother to them. I was afraid I wouldn’t be enough for them.” Participant 12.

Shifting Self-identity amidst multiplicity

In the context of multiple pregnancies, mothers experience significant stress, high levels of responsibility, and the need to accept new roles, which leads to considerable pressure. This pressure often results in women encountering difficulties in fulfilling their roles. A central aspect of the participants’ experience was a profound shift in their personal identity. The pervasive nature of multiple pregnancy challenged their sense of self-identity and left many feeling a loss of personal identity. Their lives, once defined by personal goals, careers, and relationships, became entirely centered around the needs of their unborn children.

“Multiple pregnancies have imposed numerous limitations on me, preventing me from continuing my work in the company and confining me to being a homemaker indefinitely. Additionally, it has had an impact on my relationships, causing me to lose many of my friends.” Participant 7.

“I used to be a strong, independent woman who played important and prominent roles in society, but now I’ve turned into a carrier for five babies, and I’ve had to slowly give up all the roles I fought so hard to get. Participant 13.

Strain on the spousal relationship

The elevated pressure and stress induced by multiple pregnancies can potentially exacerbate marital challenges during this period. Furthermore, these pressures may lead to emotional and sexual detachment between spouses, while some participants, in fulfilling their marital roles, may experience feelings of weakness and inadequacy.

“After years of infertility, I became pregnant, and as a result, I was afraid to engage in sexual intercourse with my husband. This caused my husband to become emotionally distant and less interested in me.” Participant 6.

Psychological pressures

The experience of multiple pregnancy constitutes a complex and high-pressure challenge for women, profoundly impacting every aspect of their lives. Participants in this study consistently reported feeling overwhelmed and emotionally burdened by the immense responsibilities accompanying this type of pregnancy. This pressure stemmed from a confluence of factors, including the heightened physical demands of carrying multiple fetuses, the psychological strain of managing competing social and personal roles, and the weight of external social expectations.

“I wasn’t ready for quadruplets. It’s a huge source of stress and fear for me, and I feel completely helpless. I don’t know what to do. I’m truly not ready for this, and I’m just so distraught and lost.” Participant 15.

Unfair judgment

Some participants expressed that during their pregnancy, they faced unfair judgments from their relatives and family regarding their pregnancy, fetal maintenance, and maternal competence. These unjust judgments negatively impacted their pregnancy experience, leading to psychological and emotional distress.

“My relatives constantly upset me with their words. They would say that my children are ‘test-tube babies.’ Some even questioned how I, who had been infertile for years, could be pregnant with triplets now!” Participant 2.

Gender-related pressure

In traditional Kurdish culture, having sons is considered a desirable trait for women, and having daughters can bring significant psychological pressure upon them. However, some participants expressed that the gender of the fetus was not important to them; they only cared about the health of the fetus.

“I already have a daughter, and I only got pregnant again in the hope of having a son. It would be disappointing for me if I had another daughter.” Participant 4.

“If I were to have four daughters after all this hardship and misery, it would be as if my efforts were in vain and I had harmed them.” Participant 12.

Nevertheless, other participants explicitly mentioned their concerns about the gender of the fetus.

“The gender of the fetuses was not my primary concern; my sole priority was their health.” Participant 1.

Multiplicity of life amidst profound loneliness

The experience of multiple pregnancy, while a time of immense growth and activity, often created a paradoxical feeling of profound loneliness for the participants. The immense physical and emotional challenges of this period significantly heightened mothers’ need for support across various domains. However, a central finding was the widespread discontent expressed by many participants regarding a critical lack of support, which directly contributed to their sense of isolation. This overarching theme was further elaborated through three key sub-themes: Insufficient family support, Feeling neglected by society and the healthcare system, and Struggling with the lack of supportive laws and policies.

Insufficient family support

One of the significant challenges of multiple pregnancies is the lack of adequate family support for pregnant mothers. Often, families are unprepared to provide sufficient support. This absence of effective family support can be highly challenging and impose significant psychological and physical stress on pregnant mothers. Additionally, in some cases, families may have unrealistic expectations of pregnant mothers, adding immense pressure, which can be a significant challenge.

“My husband and my family expected me to handle everything on my own, just like before, and they couldn’t understand the physical and emotional changes I was going through. It’s overwhelming to handle many tasks alone.” Participant 4.

Neglecting motherhood

Neglecting the pregnant mother of multiples is a serious challenge that many participants have experienced. Women carrying multiple fetuses sometimes feel that their identity, needs, and emotions as human beings are overshadowed by the presence of the fetuses. The increased focus of relatives and family on the fetuses has led mothers to believe that they are not receiving the necessary attention.

“I felt invisible and nonexistent, and no one paid attention to me. Everyone was preoccupied with the babies, without giving the slightest consideration to my desires and needs.” Participant 9.

Lack of supportive laws

Family institutions have always been among the priorities of various governments in Iran, and in recent years, significant laws have been enacted and implemented to support families and youth. Although these laws have included favorable provisions for pregnant women, the disparities between the benefits provided to women with single pregnancies compared to those with multiple pregnancies are not remarkably conspicuous or egregious. Consequently, in the current study, most participants expected the legislator to pay special attention to mothers in such circumstances.

“The challenges faced by pregnant women with multiple pregnancies are much greater than those of women with singleton pregnancies. There should be differences between them, for example, in terms of access to healthcare services and financial and social support. Currently, there is not a significant difference in the legal benefits provided to women with multiple pregnancies compared to those with singleton pregnancies.” Participant 3.

Immersion in needs

During multiple pregnancies, women encounter numerous and diverse needs across various domains, including educational, physical, psychological, supportive, healthcare-related, financial, and ethical aspects. In the present study, all participants highlighted the distinct needs of mothers during multiple pregnancies, emphasizing the necessity for policymakers to pay special attention to these needs. This theme encompassed six sub-themes: Educational needs, Healthcare needs, Government support needs, Ethical needs, Facility and Equipment requirements, and Physical and Psychological needs.

Educational needs

Pregnant women with multiple pregnancies require reliable and up-to-date information in various areas, such as nutrition, breastfeeding, and self-care for both themselves and their fetuses. Participants in this study acknowledged their diverse educational needs and highlighted the lack of accessible and credible educational resources to address their informational requirements regarding self-care and fetal well-being.

“During my pregnancy, I felt extremely overwhelmed, with numerous unanswered questions. For instance, what are the differences between a multiple pregnancy and a singleton pregnancy? What are the necessary care measures for myself and my fetuses? How should I care for my multiple babies? And many other questions……. I couldn’t fully trust the information available on the internet.” Participant 12.

Healthcare needs

Pregnant women with multiple pregnancies require specialized medical care, including regular visits to obstetricians, gynecologists, and ultrasound specialists, as well as dental and oral healthcare and regular follow-ups to assess the health status of both the mother and the fetuses. All participants in the current study recognized the importance of medical care during pregnancy as a crucial need for pregnant women with multiple pregnancies and emphasized the significance of facilitating access to quality healthcare facilities for these women.

“In my opinion, the need for medical check-ups is greater for pregnant women with multiple pregnancies compared to those with singleton pregnancies. … Due to pregnancy complications, I have been hospitalized several times, but unfortunately, the hospital facilities were inadequate, with a high number of patients and a shortage of staff, which caused me a lot of distress.” Participant 7.

Government support needs

Multiple pregnancies can impose significant economic strains on pregnant women and their families, highlighting the necessity of family support, particularly in terms of financial assistance provided by relevant organizations. Participants in the current study emphasized this need and underscored the importance of policymakers paying greater attention to their economic challenges.

“The government must provide greater support for families with pregnant women carrying multiple pregnancies and offer them specific benefits. Monthly allowances should be provided based on the number of children, or medical expenses for the children should be covered until they reach adulthood. Long-term loans could be offered to families, or working conditions for employed women could be made more comfortable.” Participant 10.

“From the third month of my pregnancy, I was unable to continue working due to my physical condition. The high costs of treatment and adequate nutrition place a significant financial burden on us. We are practically unable to afford all the prescribed medications, supplements, or even monthly doctor visits and are in dire need of financial support from the government. “Participant 7.

Facility and equipment requirements

Access to appropriate facilities and equipment is essential to safeguard and promote the health of pregnant women with multiple pregnancies and their fetuses. These include suitable comfort chairs, mattresses and beds, assistive devices for mobility facilitation, and a safe living environment. While addressing the need for supportive equipment and facilities for pregnant women with multiples, participants in the current study also highlighted the challenges in procuring them.

“During the later months of my pregnancy, I couldn’t sleep on the floor or use squat toilets, and I couldn’t even sit on the ground. I needed a proper bed, dining table, and Western-style toilet, but financially, I couldn’t afford to acquire all of them.” Participant 2.

“I have developed gestational diabetes during my pregnancy and needed to check my blood sugar several times a day. However, I couldn’t afford a blood glucose monitor. As a result, I was hospitalized several times due to severe drops in my blood sugar. " Participant 5.

Physical and psychological needs

Multiple pregnancies can significantly impact the physical and psychological well-being of pregnant women. Key physical needs for pregnant women with multiples include proper nutrition, adequate sleep and rest, and sufficient exercise and physical activity. Among the psychological needs of pregnant women with multiples, the need for support and psychological counseling, especially in managing stress and hormonal changes, is noteworthy. All participants in the current study emphasized the various physical and psychological needs of pregnant women with multiples during their pregnancies.

“During pregnancy, I had to provide energy for four people besides myself; I would get hungry very quickly and had to consume nutritious foods and dietary supplements.” Participant 4.

“During pregnancy, I became nervous and easily agitated, often needing someone to confide in.” Participant 8.

Living amidst the ethical struggles

The experience of multiple pregnancies exposed participants to profound ethical dilemmas and fundamental dualities. Throughout this experience, they grappled with deep ethical contradictions that made the nature of their pregnancy challenging. These ethical dilemmas included the Conflict of the Right to Life and Autonomy, the Ethical Choice for an Unethical Act, and the Quality of Life versus Quantity of Life.

Conflict of the right to life and autonomy

Some participants reported that they had not wanted a multiple pregnancy but felt compelled to endure it. They expressed a belief that they had lost their autonomy and right to make decisions about their bodies in the face of the fetuses’ right to life. As a result, they felt forced to sacrifice their physical well-being, personal goals, and aspirations, effectively neglecting their rights to ensure the fetuses’ right to life. This internal struggle highlighted the core conflict between the right to life and autonomy.

“I don’t want to have multiples; I feel it will make me lose everything. I only wanted one child and I wanted to terminate the pregnancy, but I felt guilty about taking the babies’ lives. On the other hand, I have to sacrifice myself for it.” Participant16.

“They took away my right to decide if I would be a mother of multiple children or even how many children I would have. Their entire priority is the fetuses, not me. It’s as if I, along with my choices and my autonomy, have been buried under this pregnancy.” Participant15.

Ethical choice for an unethical act

Multiple pregnancies can place mothers in a position of ethical dilemmas and challenging, painful decisions, leading to profound internal conflicts. For example, participants spoke of the difficult choice of fetal reduction, an action often undertaken to preserve the health of the remaining fetuses or the mother herself. This decision creates a profound ethical paradox: an act that may seem inhuman or unethical (selecting a fetus for reduction) is performed with the ultimate goal of achieving a more ethical outcome (the least harm and the most significant benefit for the entire family). This struggle demonstrates the challenging nature of their experience.

“The doctor said that to prevent all the fetuses from being lost, we had to sacrifice one, and this was the hardest decision of my life. I had no other option; I had to make this terrible decision to save the rest, and among my children, I had to sacrifice one.” Participant13.

Quality of life versus quantity of life

Analysis of the participants’ narratives revealed that they were engaged in a deep duality between the quality of life and the number of children. Some women believed that a multiple pregnancy severely and negatively impacted the quality of life for themselves and their husbands. Their primary concern was not only their future but also the future quality of life for their children, and the question arose as to whether they would be able to provide for their children’s needs. These mothers found themselves in a fundamental conflict between preserving their family’s quality of life and increasing the quantity (number) of lives, which made the experience very challenging for them.

“I don’t know if it’s a good thing that I’m pregnant with multiples. I myself liked the idea of a large family, but on the other hand, we won’t have well-being and we’ll have a difficult life. But if we had just one child, both they and we would be more comfortable, but we would be a small family and that child would be alone.” Participant12.

Compulsory imposition

Several participants reported feeling that they had no genuine choice regarding a multiple pregnancy, which they perceived as having been compulsorily imposed upon them. Some women indicated they were compelled to transfer multiple embryos during in vitro fertilization (IVF) to maximize their chances of a successful pregnancy. In contrast, another group stated they were uninformed about the possibility of a multiple pregnancy resulting from the procedure and felt it was involuntarily imposed upon them. Ultimately, all of these mothers felt they were now obligated to accept this profound and challenging responsibility.

“The doctor told me we would transfer several embryos, but only one or maybe two would become babies. But now I’m pregnant with quintuplets, and I feel this pregnancy was imposed on me.” Participant13.

I always wanted one child, like most mothers, but all of a sudden, it turned into triplets. This wasn’t what I wanted or had planned for. If I had known these medications would cause a multiple pregnancy, I wouldn’t have used them at all. Participant16.

Journey of acceptance

Multiple pregnancy presents a significant stressor and profound shock for women, to which they respond in various ways. Analysis of the narratives revealed that these responses lead to two divergent paths. Some women, by embarking on a Path of Resilience, attempt to respond to the situation adaptively and constructively. In contrast, others follow a Path of Breakdown, employing maladaptive approaches that negatively impact their experience and well-being.

Path of resilience

Analysis of the mothers’ narratives revealed that some took a proactive approach to coping with the challenges of multiple pregnancies, employing a variety of adaptive strategies. These resilience-building strategies, utilized to accept their circumstances and promote their well-being, included careful planning, personal empowerment, seeking support from various sources, and turning to spirituality. These internal and external factors helped mothers psychologically adapt to the pressures, enabling them to navigate the challenging journey of a multiple pregnancy successfully.

“I shed tears for the first day or two when I discovered I was expecting triplets. However, I consciously decided to organize my life, uplift my spirits, and ready myself for this transformative journey instead of dwelling in distress. I meticulously devised a plan, delved into extensive research, and reached out to other women who had experienced multiple gestations to glean insights from their experiences.” Participant 4.

“Instead of losing my head, I tried to prepare myself for it. I went to pregnancy and parenting classes and looked at this period as an apprenticeship.” Participant 1.

Path of breakdown

Complex and challenging. Some participants resorted to non-adaptive mechanisms, such as denial and concealment. All participants described the acceptance of and adjustment to a multiple pregnancy as a highly stressful experience, which disrupted the process of adaptation and acceptance of multiple pregnancies.

“When they told me I was carrying quadruplets, I couldn’t believe it during the early stages of my pregnancy. I didn’t tell anyone, not even my mother, that I was pregnant with multiples. I was terrified. I would cry incessantly, and I didn’t want to talk about it with anyone. I was even afraid to think about it myself. Whenever the thought crossed my mind, I would immediately push it away.” Participant12.

When the doctor said I was pregnant with triplets, my world came crashing down. From that day on, I couldn’t do anything. I would just sit there, constantly thinking about how I was going to manage all these children. I felt paralyzed. Participant 14.

Discussion

This descriptive phenomenological study aimed to elucidate the lived experiences of pregnant mothers regarding the challenges and needs of multiple pregnancies. The results indicate that multiple pregnancies pose a challenging journey for pregnant women. One of the main themes extracted from the narratives of mothers with multiple pregnancies was the feeling of being overwhelmed by pressure and responsibilities. This profound feeling is rooted in several sub-themes, each of which makes the experience more challenging. These sub-themes include “intertwined and high-pressure roles,” in which mothers face conflicts between their various identities. “Shifting self-identity amidst multiplicity,” “strain on the spousal relationship,” and “psychological pressures” contribute to a sense of excessive responsibility and internal strain. Furthermore, “unfair judgments” and “gender-related pressures,” as external and social pressures, create a set of difficulties that overwhelm mothers and lead to the feeling of being crushed by their responsibilities.

Participants in this study described a profound sense of shifting personal identity and the sacrifice of their own desires and needs during pregnancy. This experience was rooted in the acute conflict between the intense demands of the pregnancy and their established individual identities. This finding aligns with a study by Badakhsh, M., et al. (2020), which demonstrated how high-risk pregnancies, such as multiple gestations, can significantly impact a woman’s personal identity and diminish her sense of control over her life [15].

Our findings are consistent with studies that link the unique constraints of multiple pregnancies to an increased likelihood of role strain and a subsequent need for women to delegate their responsibilities to others. Consistent with this, prior studies have also shown that high-risk and multiple pregnancies increase the responsibility and role strain for women [9, 25]. Similarly, a qualitative study by Abdollahpour and Motaghi (2029) in Iran noted that women in high-risk, traumatic pregnancies often struggle with their roles due to negative emotions such as anger, sadness, and high levels of stress. They also emphasized the need for comprehensive support to help these women successfully fulfill their roles and responsibilities [26]. The lived experience of this role conflict highlights the necessity of a supportive framework. Such a framework would enable mothers to express their expectations, accept their limitations, and access professional support. Ultimately, this support is crucial for helping them adapt to their new reality and regain a sense of mastery over their roles.

Strain on the spousal relationships emerged emerged as another significant finding in the present study, representing a critical challenge with potential ramifications for maternal and fetal well-being and family dynamics. A 2023 study involving couples experiencing high-risk pregnancies demonstrated that they often express apprehension towards sexual intimacy due to concerns about fetal harm, which can, in turn, exacerbate pre-existing marital issues or precipitate new ones [27]. Consistent with these observations, a 2020 study indicated that an increase in marital problems during high-risk pregnancies, such as multiple gestations, can intensify women’s emotional and psychological distress and diminish their overall quality of life [28]. Consequently, it is recommended that accessible counseling services addressing marital issues, alongside psychoeducational workshops specifically designed for expectant fathers, be implemented to foster improved spousal relationships. Furthermore, the integration of specialized midwifery and gynecological counseling focusing on safe sexual practices and alternative forms of intimacy can contribute to the enhancement of marital relationships among women with multiple pregnancies [29].

Pregnancy with multiples is a challenging and often unexpected experience that frequently subjects mothers to significant psychological distress [30]. Beyond the inherent challenges of accepting a multiple pregnancy, mothers in this study reported substantial stress from external sources [31]. These included unrealistic expectations and unfair judgments from their social circles, often rooted in the perceived unnaturalness or abnormality of the pregnancy itself. Consistent with these findings, previous research has shown that mothers of multiples frequently face societal and familial disapproval due to these perceptions and do not receive the necessary support from their communities [32, 33].These combined external pressures and a lack of understanding contribute to feelings of social isolation, thereby exacerbating the psychological strain of a multiple pregnancy.

In the context of multiple pregnancies, another key challenge is the influence of gender norms and associated social pressures. In some regions of Iran, a cultural preference for male children persists. This preference is deeply rooted in the greater social status and power of men, the continuation of the family name, the right to inheritance, and the expectation that sons will care for parents in old age. Consequently, a multiple pregnancy can intensify social expectations for the birth of more boys. These gender norms were so prominent that women felt compelled to worry about the sex of the fetuses rather than focusing on their own and their fetuses’ health. Based on the narratives of some participants, a wholly female multiple pregnancy was perceived as a crisis for the woman and her family. This situation significantly increased family and social pressures. In such conditions, pressure from others to abort the fetuses or selectively reduce their number intensified, complicating the women’s experience with additional conflicts and concerns. Although this issue was of lesser importance to some mothers, a desire for a male child was evident to varying degrees among most of them. This finding is consistent with a study by Haseli et al. (2024) in Iran, which showed that gender-based norms often influence abortion decisions, highlighting the significance of gender in pregnancy [34]. Consequently, to improve women’s mental health and pregnancy experiences, greater efforts must be made to promote the equal roles and status of women and men in society and to support women in making independent decisions about their own and their fetuses’ health.

From the participants’ perspective, a central challenge of multiple pregnancies was the experience of Multiplicity of life amidst profound loneliness. This feeling arose from deficiencies in social and institutional support, where mothers felt their needs and identities were marginalized in favor of focusing solely on the fetuses. Specifically, they reported a lack of adequate support from their families and a desire for more supportive laws to address their unique challenges. This sense of profound loneliness is corroborated by evidence suggesting that women with high-risk pregnancies face greater challenges without necessary support from their families and the government [35, 36]. A 2023 systematic review demonstrated that a lack of support from partners, especially emotional and practical support, can have significant negative impacts on women’s pregnancy experiences [36]. In addition to inadequate family and social support, insufficient social and legal support for pregnant women of multiples can be challenging and stressful for them [33, 35].

A key challenge faced by women with multiple pregnancies is the lack of sufficient legal and institutional support in family, social, occupational, and health domains. A 2020 study in Iran highlighted this gap in supportive laws and systems for mothers of multiples, which led to inadequate access to healthcare services tailored to their needs. This can have serious consequences for the health of both mothers and their infants [33]. It is worth mentioning that in Iran, the family is highly valued, and especially in recent years, special privileges and facilities have been considered for pregnant women by lawmakers. According to Article 17 of the Law on Family and Youth Support, the maternity leave duration is nine months, and for the birth of twins or more, it is extended to twelve months. Working mothers who give birth to a child can benefit from a one-year reduction in their retirement age, and for the third child and beyond, the reduction will be one and a half years per child [37]. Despite the special facilities this law provides pregnant mothers, participants expected the government to provide them with more facilities and resources. Supportive laws are crucial in increasing access to healthcare services, providing support, and creating a supportive environment for pregnant women of multiples, ultimately improving their physical, mental, and social well-being. Furthermore, they contribute to reducing threats to the growth and development of the fetuses.

A central theme identified in the narratives of mothers with multiple pregnancies was the experience of immersion in needs, which reflected a substantial psychological and practical burden. This finding was rooted in several key sub-themes, such as healthcare needs, educational needs, government support needs, facility and equipment requirements, and physical and psychological needs, each contributing to the overwhelming nature of their experience. A critical need for mothers with multiple pregnancies is specialized education for managing the physical and psychological conditions of this period. A study in Iran (2023) confirmed that these mothers have an urgent need for comprehensive prenatal and self-care education, including healthy nutrition, adequate rest, and continuous healthcare [14]. Furthermore, research emphasizes the importance of empowering mothers to carefully monitor their nutritional status, calorie intake, and weight, as well as to balance daily activities with rest to improve health outcomes [38]. A study in Germany (2022) also showed that, alongside sufficient rest, mothers need light physical activities and must receive the necessary education on how to appropriately balance these factors [39]. In this regard, research in Portugal (2024) indicated that mothers often feel confused about their pregnancy and consider talking to peers a necessity [40]. This finding is consistent with previous studies that consider mothers’ need for accurate and reliable information vital, as access to diverse yet sometimes misleading sources exacerbates this need [9, 40].

While training and education are vital, their effectiveness is highly dependent on mothers’ access to the necessary facilities and equipment. Participants in the present study identified a crucial need for financial support and welfare equipment, aligning with the findings of a 2021 study in the United States (2021) [16]. Given the vulnerability of these mothers, comprehensive support—including financial aid to acquire assistive tools like ergonomic chairs or therapeutic mattresses—is essential for maintaining their quality of life. This support enables them to effectively apply the educational recommendations they receive [33, 40]. This finding highlights the need for policymakers to provide essential financial and legal resources for pregnant women, as these resources, alongside targeted education, are vital for the effective implementation of interventions and the improvement of maternal health outcomes.

The present study’s findings distinctly underscore the critical needs of mothers across two fundamental dimensions: specialized healthcare and structural governmental support. Participants particularly emphasized the necessity for facilitated access to highly specialized medical services, effective physician-patient communication, and a tranquil hospital environment. This necessitates facilitating access to comprehensive medical services, establishing telemedicine infrastructure for remote areas, and providing specialized training in the nuances of multiple gestations to healthcare providers [14, 18]. Furthermore, easy access to new diagnostic tests, medications, and advanced therapeutic tools is essential as a component of specialized medical care [41]. These needs align with a 2024 study that demonstrated multiple pregnancies require targeted policymaking within the health and social welfare sectors [40]. Consequently, it is proposed that policymakers adopt a collaborative intersectoral approach with healthcare specialists to design and implement multifaceted support packages. These packages should encompass targeted financial assistance, accessible counseling services, and facilitated access to postpartum rehabilitation. It is also recommended that mechanisms be established for the subsidized or free provision of essential welfare equipment for women with high-risk pregnancies, particularly those in vulnerable populations.

Another key finding of this study was the experience of ethical conflicts among participants. Many women felt that in the struggle to preserve the life of their fetuses, they had lost their personal autonomy and independence. Some women reported that despite the physical threat the pregnancy posed to their health and the fetuses residing within their bodies, they had no right to make decisions regarding their own bodies or the fetuses. Instead, all such rights were transferred to their husbands. In addition, women were sometimes forced to sacrifice their autonomy and submit to external pressures. These pressures included family and social demands for a specific number or sex of children, economic constraints, and legal requirements. This placed them in a position where they were unable to make independent decisions. This finding is consistent with a study by Hasely et al. (2024), which showed that pregnancy can limit a woman’s ability to make independent decisions [34]. In this regard, a re-evaluation of relevant laws is necessary to protect women’s rights during this period. The rights to fetal decision-making should be divided equally between the woman and her partner. This includes empowering women to make independent choices about their bodies and their pregnancies, which is crucial for their well-being and autonomy.

Beyond this, some women found themselves grappling with the duality of their own and their children’s quality of life versus a greater quantity of children. Women reported that economic pressure and inflation made them even more aware of this dilemma. This ethical challenge aligns with the results of a qualitative study in Kenya (2018), which indicated that fear of a low quality of life, economic problems, and social issues were among the exacerbating factors for selective abortion [42]. These findings suggest that this issue gains double importance for women with higher-order multiple pregnancies, placing them in a complex ethical dilemma.

Our findings are consistent with the studies that show pregnant women with multiple pregnancies may face difficult and ethically complex decisions. One such decision is selective fetal reduction, which may be necessary to preserve the life of the mother or other fetuses. This decision is profoundly difficult and often accompanied by feelings of guilt. Consistent with this, a study by Abdollahpour et al. (2020) found that women in high-risk pregnancies are sometimes forced to make incredibly tough ethical decisions to protect their own lives, which may result in the loss of a fetus. This experience leaves them with profound and overwhelming grief. Furthermore, mothers’ intense distress over losing an infant due to their own illness is also observed [43]. These feelings are particularly evident in women carrying abnormal fetuses, who are forced to choose between keeping the fetus or having an abortion, placing them in an ethical dichotomy [44]. Given these profound challenges, providing continuous and specialized counseling services for difficult decision-making moments is essential.

Finally, the Journey of Acceptance emerged as a central theme, highlighting two divergent paths for mothers navigating a multiple pregnancy: the Path of Resilience and the Path of Breakdown. Our findings suggest that mothers who follow the latter path are at a higher risk of employing maladaptive coping mechanisms, such as denial and avoidance of responsibility [45]. This aligns with evidence indicating that mothers of multiples face considerably higher levels of physical, emotional, and psychological stress compared to those with singleton pregnancies [8, 9, 46]. The use of these maladaptive mechanisms exacerbates the inherent difficulties of the pregnancy and can significantly delay successful adaptation to new maternal roles. Therefore, to enhance the psychological well-being of these mothers, a multifaceted and integrated approach is crucial. This includes implementing proactive mental health screening, providing accessible psychological education and counseling, and strengthening both social support networks and peer connections. These measures are designed to mitigate the unique pressures of this period and facilitate a more resilient journey for mothers and their families.

Limitation

The present study employed a qualitative methodological approach. As such, its findings are subject to the inherent limitations associated with this paradigm, most notably concerning the generalizability of the results. Specifically, given that this research was conducted within the Kurdistan province of Iran, the transferability of the findings may be influenced by the unique cultural context and the specific structure of the healthcare system prevalent in this region. Therefore, it is crucial to acknowledge that variations in cultural norms, social structures, and healthcare delivery systems across different geographical locations may affect the applicability of the current findings. The sampling method used in this study was purposive, which may introduce bias by selecting a non-representative population sample. However, to minimize this bias, inclusion and exclusion criteria were carefully defined to choose a reasonably representative sample of the target population. The study aimed to examine a specific phenomenon within a particular cultural context, so purposive sampling was deemed justified. Furthermore, another limitation of this study was the relative paucity of recent literature directly addressing the lived experiences of this specific population, which necessitated the inclusion of somewhat dated sources.

Conclusion

This qualitative study was conducted to examine the challenges and needs of pregnant mothers with higher-order multiple pregnancies, revealing the profound physiological, emotional, ethical, economic, and social complexities of this unique condition. The narratives showed that mothers face a range of difficulties, including role conflicts within their professional, marital, and parental identities, as well as ethical dilemmas. A lack of structural support and accurate information exacerbated their anxiety and feelings of unpreparedness. The research identified the fundamental needs of these mothers across medical, care-related, supportive, physical, psychological, financial, facilities, and ethical dimensions. It also found that women use both adaptive and maladaptive coping strategies in the face of these challenges, which can either facilitate or complicate their experience. Accordingly, a deep understanding of these women’s challenges and needs is crucial for policymakers and healthcare providers. Providing comprehensive psychological, physical, social, and economic support, facilitating access to information and services, and strengthening adaptive mechanisms are key strategies to improve their well-being. Future research should focus on the effectiveness of targeted interventions and conduct longitudinal and comparative studies in this field.

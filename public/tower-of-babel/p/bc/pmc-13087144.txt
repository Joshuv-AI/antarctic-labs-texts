A proof of concept study on the diagnostic utility of in vivo expressed mycobacterial transcripts in tuberculous pleuritis
Authors: Prabhdeep Kaur, Sumedha Sharma, Sudhanshu Abhishek, Ashutosh N. Aggarwal, Khushpreet Kaur, Rakesh Yadav, Sunil Sethi, Amanjit Bal, Indu Verma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Tuberculous pleuritis (TBP) is the second most common extra-pulmonary manifestation of tuberculosis. Histopathology using pleural biopsy is the most sensitive diagnostic procedure for TBP. Since biopsy acquisition is invasive, better biomarkers for diagnosis using pleural fluid are needed. The current study was designed to identify mycobacterial RNA biomarkers in TBP and to assess their diagnostic utility in pleural fluid. 261 TBP suspects were recruited for the study. 45 suspects were excluded and remaining (n = 216) were divided into TBP (n = 54) and non-TBP (n = 162) groups based on composite reference standard (CRS). Whole genome microarray was carried using M.tb RNA from pleural biopsies of TBP patients. The data was validated using qRT-PCR and the diagnostic utility of top two highly expressed genes was evaluated in pleural fluid, using qualitative real time RT-PCR. 1856 genes were differentially expressed in microarray with 1365 upregulated and 491 downregulated genes. The diagnostic utility of transcripts corresponding to two genes, Rv1586c and Rv2819c was assessed post microarray validation. The presence of either of two genes led to pleural TB identification with 79.6% sensitivity and 93.28% specificity. Thus, the transcripts of genes Rv1586c and Rv2819c holds potential for development of RNA based diagnostic assay for TBP using pleural fluid.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42637-2.

Introduction

Tuberculosis affects lungs, but can also affect other sites1. Extra-pulmonary TB (EPTB) accounts for approximately 15–20% cases in HIV negative people, and 50% in HIV positive individuals2. Tuberculous pleuritis (TBP) is the most prevalent form of EPTB globally after lymph node TB2. The diagnosis of TBP is carried out using pleural fluid and biopsy, acquired through thoracentesis and thoracoscopy respectively. Considering the paucibacillary nature of TBP, the conventional diagnostic tests, including pleural fluid microscopy and culture offer low sensitivity3. Histopathological examination of pleural biopsy exhibits highest diagnostic potential; however, biopsy acquisition is an invasive procedure4 and associated with post procedure complications5,6. The adenosine deaminase (ADA), interferon gamma (IFN-γ) and T cell-based IFN-γ release assays (IGRAs)7,8 are rapid and cost effective, but nonspecific. Also, the utilization of in-house detection procedures2,7,8 and lack of a widely accepted cutoff limits their utilization9. The utility of Xpert MTB/RIF for TBP is limited2,10. The improved version Ultra has better sensitivity and has been endorsed by WHO for TBP diagnosis11,12. However, these nucleic acid amplification tests (NAAT’s) are DNA based and cannot differentiate live/dead bacilli leading to false positive results in previously treated tuberculosis (TB) patients13. These limitations can be addressed with an RNA-based diagnostic test. For RNA based assay, the transcripts expressed in-vivo at the site of infection by the pathogen could be important targets. Mycobacterial gene expression is known to be altered under different growth conditions in-vitro and in-vivo14–17. The study is based on the hypothesis that in-vivo highly expressed mycobacterial genes, particularly during active disease, could be important for the development of a sensitive and specific molecular diagnostic assay. Thus, the present study was carried out to analyze the mycobacterial gene expression in biopsy samples of TBP patients and identify mycobacterial transcripts that can be utilized as potential candidates for RNA based molecular assay, for diagnosing TBP using pleural fluid samples.

Results

In current study, total 261 TB pleuritis (TBP) suspects were recruited. Out of these, 45 were excluded owing to indeterminate clinical diagnosis (n = 24) or development of TB of any other site (n = 7) or having both TB and malignancy simultaneously (n = 4) or having other infectious diseases including HCV and COVID (n = 4) or due to insufficient quantity of isolated RNA (n = 6). Finally, 216 TBP suspects were included in the study. These were divided into pleural TB (n = 54) and non-TB controls (n = 162) as per Composite Reference standard (CRS) (Fig. 1). Pleural TB cases included 33 microbiologically confirmed patients and 21 probable TB patients. Non-TB controls included patients with malignant pleural effusion (n = 120) and other respiratory diseases (n = 42) (Fig. 1). Transcriptomic profile of Mycobacterium tuberculosis (M.tb) in pleural biopsy samples of TBP patients

Whole genome microarray of M.tb was carried out in pleural biopsy samples from confirmed TBP patients (n = 4), in comparison to in-vitro grown M.tb H37Rv culture. 1856 genes were differentially expressed in TBP patients in comparison to in-vitro grown M.tb, including 1365 upregulated and 491 downregulated genes (Supplementary Tables 2 & 3) (Fig. 2a and b). The differentially expressed mycobacterial transcripts were functionally categorized as per the Tuberculist database (upgraded to Mycobrowser; https://tuberculist.epfl.ch/; https://mycobrowser.epfl.ch/)18(Fig. 2c). Most of the functional categories were enriched equally among the upregulated and downregulated genes, but, lipid metabolism and PE-PPE family genes were enriched only among upregulated genes (Table 3). Pathway analysis using BioCyc19 led to identification of significantly enriched (p < 0.05) metabolic pathways among both the upregulated and downregulated categories. Among the upregulated genes, significant enrichment was observed in pathways belonging to fatty acid, cholesterol and sugar/galactose degradation along with some other pathways, as those belonging to pyrimidine biosynthesis, transcription regulation, DNA repair and metal ion transport (Supplementary Table 4). Significant enrichment among downregulated genes was observed in pathways belonging to translation and protein secretion and localization. Genes belonging to stress responses like hypoxia, oxidative stress, nutrient stress, starvation and response to host defense pathways were also significantly enriched among the downregulated genes (Supplementary Table 5).

Microarray validation using qRT-PCR

Microarray data was validated using real time PCR in biopsy samples with a subset of 10 genes, including six upregulated genes (Rv1586c, Rv2819c, Rv1971c, Rv1582c, Rv2351c and Rv0974c) and four downregulated genes (Rv3872, Rv0983, Rv3875 and Rv3871) in the microarray of TBP patients. The upregulated genes included topmost three highly upregulated genes Rv1586c, Rv2819c and Rv1582c and similar to the microarray data, they showed an upregulated relative expression in real time RT-PCR with log2 fold change of 14.11,13.64 and 12.43 respectively (Fig. 3). However, in case of other three upregulated genes with lower fold change (< 50 fold ) Rv1971 and Rv0974c showed no amplification in any of the tested samples and in case of Rv2351c, amplification was seen in only one (Log2FC 0.92) sample. Similarly, four downregulated genes, Rv3872, Rv3875, Rv0983 selected from microarray data, showed downregulation in biopsy samples of TBP patients by qRT-PCR also (Fig. 3). Hence, the results of microarray data could be validated for 6/10 selected genes (Fig. 3).

Development and validation of RNA based molecular assay

Development of molecular assay for TB pleuritis

For developing real time RT-PCR based test using pleural fluid, two topmost differentially upregulated M.tb genes Rv1586c (log2 FC: 949.7) and Rv2819c (log2 FC: 272.4), whose expression were also validated through qRT-PCR (log2 FC: 9.89 and 8.09 respectively), were selected. To develop the assay, qualitative expression of selected genes i.e. Rv1586c and Rv2819c was first assessed in development cohort, consisting of 10 TB pleuritis patients and 43 non-TB disease controls. Rv1586c was detected in 6/10 TBP patients, thus showing sensitivity of 60%, however it also showed false detection in 3/43 disease controls thus giving a specificity of 93.02%. Second gene Rv2819c was detected in 7/10 TBP patients, showing a sensitivity of 70% and was falsely detected in 1/43 disease controls, thus having a specificity of 97.67 (Fig. 4a & b).

To increase the sensitivity of detection, the data was further analyzed by looking at the presence of either of the genes for positive result. The analysis increased the sensitivity of detection to 90%, with slight loss in the specificity to 93.02% (3/43). Thus, this analysis algorithm was taken forward for the evaluation of these genes in validation cohort (Fig. 4b).

Validation of assay

Rv1586c and Rv2819c based molecular assay was validated in blinded pleural fluid samples of validation cohort consisting of 163 TBP suspects. Based on the criteria set for Ct value (< 35) and transcript specific melt peak, 43 samples were positive and 120 samples were observed to be negative (Supplementary Fig. 1). On decoding the patient information based on various laboratory and clinical criteria of CRS, 35/43 were true positive and 8 subjects were falsely detected as positive. Among the 120 negative subjects, 111 samples were true negative and 9 were false negative (Table 4). Hence the real time RT-PCR assay had a sensitivity of 79.55% (95% CI: 64.70% to 90.20%; 35/44 TBP) with specificity of 93.28% (95% CI: 87.18% to 97.05%; 8/119 non TBP patients).

Further, on comparative evaluation of diagnostic potential of qRT-PCR based on these selected genes, with available diagnostic modalities for TBP, real time qRT-PCR assay showed significantly better sensitivity than either of existing diagnostic tests in pleural fluid (Supplementary Table 6). Discussion

Routine diagnostic techniques utilizing pleural fluid suffer from limitations and lack reliable performance for TBP diagnosis. We hypothesized that the biomarkers which are expressed in-vivo at the site of infection during disease condition, will serve as important diagnostic targets. The mycobacterial transcriptome was studied in biopsy samples obtained from confirmed TBP patients, considering the fact that mycobacterial RNA isolated from pleural fluid might not be of optimum quantity and quality for microarray, due to low abundance of mycobacteria in pleural fluid. To the best of our knowledge any report comparing the expression patterns of bacteria in pleural fluid and biopsy is not available, however as pleural TB is most commonly caused due to “the rupture of a subpleural tubercle into the pleural space”20 so it is likely that the bacteria in pleural biopsy as well as pleural fluid has similar gene expression. The transcriptomic profile of M.tb in the biopsy samples from TBP patients demonstrated 1856 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) as compared to in-vitro grown M.tb H37Rv. The number of upregulated genes was higher in comparison to downregulated genes, a pattern similar to that observed in M.tb infected alveolar epithelial cells, but contrary to sputum samples of pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) patients14,21,22. The in-vivo upregulated mycobacterial transcripts in pleural biopsy samples were further used for the development of RNA based molecular assay in pleural fluid samples.

Several studies have previously used host transcriptomic signatures for TB diagnosis23,24. However, pathogen specific transcriptomic signatures seem to be more important for specificity of an assay. Mycobacterial transcripts were used for the development of a molecular assay for pleural TB, after validation of microarray results on real time quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) using unamplified RNA isolated from pleural biopsy samples of TBP patients. Six out of 10 genes assessed by qRT-PCR showed same expression pattern as that observed in microarray. Some level of disagreement between microarray and qRT-PCR has also been observed previously14 which may be due to multiple factors like the handling and storage conditions of samples, differences in the data interpretation strategies etc.25. The reason for failed amplification in some of the samples for two of the genes Rv1971c and Rv2351c could be the lower level of expression of these genes in patient samples, which is beyond the limit of detection of real time PCR assay used in the study. Two of the top upregulated genes in microarray were validated through qRT-PCR i.e., Rv1586c and Rv2819c were selected for development of a RNA based molecular assay using the pleural fluid samples, to discriminate TBP and non TBP patients. Rv1586c (Probable PhiRv1 integrase) belongs to the category of Probable PhiRv1 phage proteins of prophage-like elements ϕ Rv1 of M.tb and encodes putative integrase of the serine recombinase type. The gene is specifically expressed in M.tb and absent in BCG. The second selected gene Rv2819c, encodes for a hypothetical protein and not much is known about it.

The qRT-PCR assay based on these two genes Rv1586c and Rv2819c was initially developed in development cohort having known diagnosis on the basis of routine laboratory and clinical diagnostics, however blinded samples were used for validation of the study. The data of qRT-PCR was analysed using MIQE guidelines, which have previously been used by several studies reporting qRT-PCR data26,27. Using these criteria, when positivity of only Rv1586c or Rv2819c was considered, it led to diagnosis of TBP patients with sensitivity and specificity of 60–70% and 93–97% respectively. Analysis of data indicated that several samples showed the positivity to either of these two genes and not both the genes. Hence, to further improve the sensitivity of the assay, positivity of either of genes for a positive qRT-PCR test was considered, which resulted in improved sensitivity, without significant compromise in specificity for differentiating TBP patients from non TBP controls. Hence for validation cohort, results were analyzed considering the positivity for either of the genes (Rv1586c/Rv2819c) in the same pleural fluid sample. Decoding the qRT-PCR results based on the CRS data in TBP suspects of validation cohort, sensitivity and specificity of the assay was 79.6% and 93.28% respectively, for TBP diagnosis. In comparison to the RNA based assay developed in this study, the sensitivity of Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra was lower (Supplementary Table 6), however specificity of Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra was slightly higher. Previous studies have also shown low sensitivity of Xpert MTB/RIF in pleural fluid28,29, although recent studies on Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra have shown improved performance over Xpert MTB/RIF12,30. Moreover, the current test being an RNA based assay could be advantageous over DNA based Xpert MTB/RIF and Ultra that cannot discriminate between live and dead bacilli leading to false positivity in previously treated individuals13,31. But assay developed in current study cannot be used for detection of antibiotic resistance as in case of Xpert MTB/RIF and Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra. In spite of technical complexities involved in RNA based assays, the widespread use of this assay for diagnosing SARS-COV2 during the COVID pandemic has provided mankind with enough experience to handle such assays even at hospitals other than tertiary care centers.

As a secondary objective, the in-vivo expressed mycobacterial transcriptome data was also analyzed to understand the pathogenesis of pleural TB. The differentially expressed upregulated and downregulated genes were categorized into different functional categories as per Tuberculist database18. Genes of PE-PPE family and lipid metabolism were significantly enriched in the upregulated pathways (Table 3). Number of PE-PPE family proteins are implicated in M.tb pathogenesis, via promoting the attachment of microbe to the host cells32,33. Significant enrichment of fatty acid degradation, utilization and assimilation, along with cholesterol degradation pathways was observed among the upregulated category. Fatty acids and lipids including cholesterol are vital components of mycobacterial cell wall and also essential carbon and energy sources for M.tb adaptation in host microenvironment to establish and maintain chronic TB infection34–36. Pyrimidine biosynthesis, transcription regulation and DNA repair pathways, known to play an important role during M.tb infection and disease progression, were also upregulated37. Several down regulated pathways were also significantly enriched and primarily included translation, protein secretion and localization, similar to that reported previously in sputum mycobacterial cells14,22 and could be considered as an adaptive strategy in host environment38. Overall, it seems that mycobacteria in pleural space is engaged in energy generation using fatty acid degradation and is increasing its virulent properties with the upregulation of PE-PPE genes. These genes, along with DNA repair genes, are also helping the bacteria to deal with the host immune system and various stresses.

In conclusion, the transcriptome analysis of Mycobacterium tuberculosis in biopsy samples of TB pleuritis patients, led to newer insight into the in-vivo expression of mycobacterial transcriptomic signatures in the pleural space during TBP. The selected pathogenic RNA signatures from the M.tb transcriptome in TBP, which are highly expressed in-vivo, have shown promising results for the development of RNA based diagnostic real time PCR assay using pleural fluid. However, the limited number of study subjects in the discovery part of the study, the inability to carry out biomarker discovery in pleural fluid and non-availability of the data for all the routine diagnostic tests included in CRS for diagnosing pleural TB are few limitations of the study. Thus, it is essential to assess the expression of Rv1586c and Rv2819c across a broader and clinically relevant spectrum of samples including patients with pulmonary and extrapulmonary tuberculosis, as well as individuals with non-TB conditions, such as other bacterial and viral infections and malignancies. Such comparative analyses are necessary to establish the specificity, clinical relevance, and potential diagnostic utility of these targets.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

This is a prospective study conducted between 2016 and 2020 in which patients with exudative pleural effusions, history of fever and/or respiratory symptoms for ˃2 weeks and referred for thoracentesis were recruited as TBP suspects, (n = 261) from Department of Pulmonary Medicine, PGIMER following the approval from Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) PGIMER (Ref no. INT/IEC/2016/2264), Chandigarh, India. Written informed consent was taken from study subjects and all experiments were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. The sample size of the study was calculated considering 3% disease prevalence, with 95% confidence Interval and 5% margin of error. As per calculation 45 TBP cases were required, so to obtain the required number of disease cases, considering 3% disease prevalence, 261 study subjects were recruited.

Patients already on anti-tubercular therapy (ATT) at time of recruitment or having positive HIV/HCV status or with any other co-morbidity were excluded from the study. Pleural biopsy and pleural fluid samples were collected from study subjects using thoracoscopy and thoracentesis respectively, by trained physicians. Routine diagnostic tests were also performed for all the patients and the reports were prospectively collected. As no single test has a good diagnostic value and as all the diagnostic tests were not performed on all the samples, so a composite reference standard (CRS) comprising of clinical findings, microscopy (AFB in pleural fluid/ biopsy), culture (in pleural fluid/biopsy), histopathology (pleural biopsy demonstrating typical caseating granulomatous inflammation), Xpert MTB/RIF or Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra positivity, elevated ADA (> 40 IU/L) and response to ATT (adequate resolution of effusion after empiric ATT) was utilized for diagnosis of TBP patients as used in previous studies39,40. Patients showing positivity with any one of the tests from CRS were categorized as TBP and others negative by all the tests from CRS were classified as non-TBP patients.

Mycobacterial RNA isolation from pleural biopsy and pleural fluid

Pleural biopsy samples in RNA later were centrifuged at 3000 g for 15 min at 4 °C after adding 1 mL phosphate buffer saline (PBS) and were thereafter washed twice with PBS at 5000 g for 15 min at 4 °C. After washing, the biopsy samples were homogenised and resuspended in TRIzol. RNA isolation was then carried using TRIzol method as described by Abhishek et al.15. RNA was also isolated from pleural fluid samples after pelleting down the mycobacteria at 4500 g for 20 min at room temperature using same protocol. RNA isolated from log phase M.tb H37Rv culture grown in Soutan’s media using TRIzol method15 was used as positive control. The concentration and purity of isolated RNA was checked on nanodrop and RNA integrity of samples which were used for microarray was analysed using 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent).

M. tb whole genome microarray and data analysis

The transcriptome analysis of M.tb in TBP biopsy samples (n = 4)/ M.tb H37Rv was carried out using whole genome microarray, with 200ng input RNA, using Agilent’s microarray platform as described earlier15,41. Briefly the RNA was amplified and labeled with Cyanine 3-CTP (Cy3), followed by purification and quantitation of amplified cRNA. The samples having specific activity > 15(pmol Cy3/µgcRNA), were further hybridized to custom designed microarray slides and scanned on the Agilent Sure Scan Microarray Scanner followed by image extraction and data analysis as described by Abhishek et al.15. Functional categorization of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) was performed according to TubercuList database. To delineate the metabolic pathways specifically expressed in TBP patients, data obtained from M.tb whole genome microarray was further analyzed by using BioCyc database (BioCyc.org). Functional categorization of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and analysis of the differentially expressed metabolic pathways was done using Tuberculist and BioCyc databases (BioCyc.org) respectively.

qRT-PCR (microarray validation)

RNA from biopsy samples of TBP patients and in-vitro grown M.tb H37Rv culture were DNase treated, followed by cDNA synthesis using kit from Bio-Rad Laboratories as per manufacturer’s instruction. The cDNA was used for qRT-PCR using SYBR Green dye and M.tb gene-specific primers (Supplementary Table 1) using cycling conditions as described by Sharma et al [42]. For each gene, cDNA samples (reverse transcriptase positive, RT+) and the no enzyme control (NEC, RT-) were run. Comparative CT method (∆∆CT) was used to determine relative gene expression of the target genes as described earlier15, 43.

Development and validation of qRT-PCR assay for the diagnosis of tuberculous pleuritis

For the development and validation of a mycobacterial RNA based diagnostic assay in pleural fluid, a computer-generated random number table for 216 TBP suspects in this study was used. 1/4th (n = 53) of the study subjects with known clinical diagnosis (TBP and non TBP disease controls) were allocated to development cohort and remaining 3/4th (n = 163) of the study subjects were assigned to validation cohort for blinded analysis. Two of the top upregulated mycobacterial genes in pleural biopsy samples, as identified by microarray data and validated through qRT-PCR, were used for the development and validation of RNA based diagnostic assay using pleural fluid samples and gene specific primers as described by Sharma et al.42. The presence or absence of M.tb RNA in the pleural fluid samples was analyzed and a sample was considered as positive for TB when mycobacterial gene specific RNA was detected in duplicate tubes with Ct value (cycle threshold) less than 35 along with transcript specific melt peak. The presence of M.tb RNA in RT+ sample was considered only if there was difference of at least 1 Ct between RT + and RT- samples15, 44.

Statistical analysis

Diagnostic value of the test was seen by calculating sensitivity[(TP)/(TP + FN)], Specificity [ (TN)/(FP + TN)]. positive predictive value and negative predictive value at 95% confidence interval using MedCalc’s diagnostic calculator (www.medcalc.org/calc/diagnostic_test. php). The statistical significance of paired analysis of data for comparing different tests was calculated using McNemar’s test with Graph Pad quickcalcs online calculator (www.graphpad.com). The clinical characteristics among TBP and non TBP were compared using Chi square test.

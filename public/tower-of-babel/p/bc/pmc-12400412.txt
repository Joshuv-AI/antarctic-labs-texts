Iodine(III)-Mediated Cyclization Cascade of Diynes to Benzofulvene Derivatives
Authors: Jun Kikuchi, Roi Nakajima, Eunsang Kwon, Naohiko Yoshikai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We report here a concise and regioselective synthesis of benzofulvene derivatives via an iodine­(III)-mediated cyclization cascade of diynes. For 1,2-dialkynylbenzene substrates, the iodanylative cyclization is accompanied by triflation or fluorination, depending on the iodine­(III) reagent, affording stereodefined benzofulvene derivatives. In contrast, the reaction of 1,6-diyne substrates is terminated by intramolecular Friedel–Crafts cyclization to deliver fused benzofulvene frameworks. A representative triyne was also shown to participate in an analogous cascade, furnishing functionalized cyclopenta­[cd]­indene derivatives. The resulting iodane-functionalized benzofulvenes serve as substrates for Pd-catalyzed cross-coupling and for aryne insertion, the latter being a transformation characteristic of hypervalent iodine chemistry.

Benzofulvene and its derivatives represent a distinct class of conjugated carbocycles featuring an exocyclic double bond on an indene scaffold (Scheme a). Owing to their unique structural and electronic properties, these compounds have found broad applications across medicinal chemistry, materials science, and organometallic chemistry. Consequently, the development of efficient strategies for constructing benzofulvene frameworks has attracted considerable attention in the synthetic community. Conventional methods typically rely on the condensation of indenes with carbonyl compounds, while more recent approaches include radical cyclization of diynes, photochemical enyne cyclization, and transition metal-catalyzed C–H activation. However, the selective synthesis of benzofulvenes bearing multiple substituents on both the five-membered ring and the exocyclic double bond remains challenging.

Benzofulvene Synthesis via Electrophile-Mediated Cyclization Cascade of Diynes

A notable strategy that addresses this limitation involves electrophilic cyclization of 1,2-bis­(arylethynyl)­benzenes, which proceeds through a 5-endo-dig cyclization pathway to construct highly substituted benzofulvenes (Scheme b). This transformation typically involves electrophilic activation of one alkyne, followed by intramolecular attack by the second alkyne and trapping of the resulting vinyl cation. When halogen- or boron-based electrophiles are employed, synthetically valuable functional groups can be introduced during cyclization, enabling downstream diversification. While the utility of this approach has been intermittently demonstrated, existing methods often face challenges in terms of stereoselectivity and substrate generality. Halogen-based protocols, including those reported by Schreiner, Farinola, and Verma often display modest E/Z selectivity. On the other hand, the BCl3-mediated cyclization, reported by Ingleson, exhibits strong electronic dependence: substrates bearing electron-rich aryl termini favor benzofulvene formation, while reactions involving electron-deficient systems are accompanied by dibenzopentalene-type products via competing intramolecular electrophilic aromatic substitution. ,

Herein, we demonstrate that iodine­(III)-based electrophiles serve as effective reagents for a stereoselective cyclization cascade of 1,2-bis­(arylethynyl)­benzenes, providing richly functionalized benzofulvenes (Scheme c). Benziodoxole triflate (BXT) facilitates 5-endo-dig cyclization followed by triflation, affording E-configured, difunctionalized benzofulvenes under mild conditions. Likewise, the combination of chlorobenziodoxole (CBX) and AgBF4 promotes an analogous, fluorinative cyclization. The present reaction systems are also applicable to the cyclization of 1,6-diyne derivatives, furnishing fused benzofulvene frameworks, as well as to a higher-order cascade of a triyne substrate that leads to cyclopenta­[cd]­indene derivatives. The resulting iodane-containing products are amenable to further derivatization, enabling access to functionally elaborated benzofulvene derivatives.

As part of our ongoing studies on cyclic hypervalent iodine chemistry, we have developed a series of transformations using benziodoxole-based electrophiles. Building on the versatility of these iodine­(III) electrophiles in intra- and intermolecular iodo­(III)­functionalizations of alkynes, , we explored their application to the electrophilic cyclization of 1,2-dialkynylbenzenes for benzofulvene synthesis. Thus, the reaction of 1,2-bis­(phenylethynyl)­benzene (1a) with BXT was examined under various conditions (Table ). When 1a was treated with BXT (1.1 equiv) in MeCN (0.05 M) at room temperature, the desired product 2a was obtained in 34% yield as a single stereoisomer (entry 1). Performing the reaction in HFIP under otherwise identical conditions failed to give the desired product (entry 2). In contrast, the use of CH2Cl2 significantly improved the reaction efficiency, delivering 2a in 69% yield (entry 3). The yield improved to 77% upon using 1.5 equiv of BXT (entry 4), and further to 87% by increasing the reaction concentration to 0.1 M (entry 5). Notably, the reaction gave no detectable amount of a dibenzopentalene derivative or other side products. This, together with the formation of a single stereoisomer, underscores the excellent chemo- and stereoselectivity of the transformation.

In parallel, we examined the use of CBX in combination with various silver salts in CH2Cl2 (Scheme ). The treatment of 1a with CBX and AgBF4 led to rapid and complete conversion within 10 min, furnishing the fluorinated benzofulvene 3a in 99% yield as a single stereoisomer. The structure and the stereochemistry of 3a were unambiguously established by X-ray crystallographic analysis. As BF4 – was the only fluorine source present, the fluorine atom was inferred to originate from the counterion. The use of other silver salts such as AgNTf2 and AgPF6 caused decomposition to complex mixtures.

Iodo­(III)­cyclization–Fluorination Cascade of 1,2-Bis­(phenylethynyl)­benzene

With the optimized reaction conditions for BXT- and CBX/AgBF4-mediated cyclization cascades in hand, we explored the substrate scope of both protocols (Table ). Both methods exhibited comparable reactivity and delivered the desired products with consistently high E-stereoselectivity. We first examined 1,2-bis­(arylethynyl)­benzene derivatives with various terminal aryl substituents. The reaction of 1a was successfully performed on a 1 mmol scale, affording 2a in 79% yield. Substrates bearing para-substituents such as Me, Cl, or CF3 underwent efficient cyclization to afford 2b–2d and 3b–3d in 71–86% yields as E-isomers. The meta-bromo analogue reacted efficiently to afford 2e (77%) and 3e (97%). An ortho-methyl substituent was well tolerated, affording 2f and 3f in good yields. In contrast, diynes bearing electron-rich (hetero)­aryl groups such as meta-methoxyphenyl and thiophen-3-yl substituents failed to give the desired products, presumably due to preferential C–H λ3-iodanation. An unsymmetrical diyne bearing phenyl and butyl groups at the alkyne termini also proved incompatible, leading to an intractable mixture of products. Structural variation of the central arene core was likewise tolerated, as demonstrated by substrates bearing 4,5-dimethyl- or 4,5-dibromophenylene linkers, which furnished 2g, 2h, 3g, and 3h in moderate to high yields.

Compared to halogen-based or BCl3-mediated cyclizations, − which often suffer from modest stereoselectivity or competing side reactions, the present iodine­(III)-mediated transformations provide a robust and stereoselective access to highly substituted benzofulvenes featuring a well-defined E-configured exo-olefin moiety. The high stereochemical fidelity was supported by DFT calculations on a model triflative cyclization, which revealed a pathway involving iodine­(III)-mediated cyclization followed by trflative capture of the vinyl cation intermediate from the less hindered face (Figure S4). Note also that formation of a dibenzopentalene core via intramolecular electrophilic aromatic substitution (SEAr) reaction from the same intermediate was found to be significantly less favorable.

In contrast to the above transformations involving external nucleophilic trapping, the iodo­(III)­cyclization of 1,6-diyne derivatives was found to terminate via intramolecular SEAr of the vinyl cation intermediate (Scheme ). This alternative termination pathway was exemplified by 1,8-bis­(phenylethynyl)­naphthalene (4a), a substrate featuring a rigid naphthalene core. Treatment of 4a with BXT in CH2Cl2 at room temperature led to a 6-endo-dig cyclization followed by intramolecular SEAr reaction on the terminal phenyl ring, affording indeno­[2,1-a]­phenalene derivative 5a in 61% yield. In contrast, the more flexible 1,7-diphenylheptadiyne (4b) reacted sluggishly under the same conditions, producing 2,3-dihydrofluorene derivative 5b in low yield. Switching the solvent to HFIP significantly improved the yield of 5b to 64%.

Iodo­(III)­cyclization Cascade of 1,6-Diynes

The iodine­(III)-mediated cyclization strategy was further extended to a 1,2,3-triyne substrate capable of undergoing a higher-order cascade, demonstrating the feasibility of consecutive cyclizations from three adjacent alkynes (Scheme ). Treatment of 1,2,3-tris­(phenylethynyl)­benzene (6) with BXT or CBX/AgBF4 triggered a cascade cyclization followed by nucleophilic trapping, affording the corresponding cyclopenta­[cd]­indene derivatives 7a (50%) and 7b (76%) as single stereoisomers. The structure of 7b was confirmed by X-ray crystallographic analysis. The sum of the bond angles around the inner carbon of this fused framework is 358.8°, indicating its high degree of planarity. The remarkable selectivity observed in these cascade processes stands in sharp contrast to the bromine-mediated protocol reported by Schreiner and co-workers, which afforded mixtures of regio- and stereoisomeric products. These findings underscore the broad applicability of the iodine­(III)-mediated cyclization platform to both diynes and a triyne, providing access to π-extended, stereodefined benzofulvene-type VBXs.

The multifaceted utility of the iodanylated benzofulvenes was demonstrated through two distinct transformations (Scheme ). These include their use as electrophiles in Pd-catalyzed cross-coupling and as arynophiles for aryne insertion into the C–I­(III) bond. , Sonogashira coupling of 2a with excess alkyne displaced both the benziodoxole and triflate groups, affording dialkynylated product 8 with partial E/Z isomerization. Meanwhile, 3a gave monoalkynylated product 9 in 50% yield, retaining both its fluorine atom and stereochemical integrity. In a mechanistically distinct transformation, 3a participated in the insertion of benzyne, affording 3-arylbenzofulvene derivative 10 with the iodanyl group positioned ortho to the newly installed aryl ring. A similar aryne insertion was applicable to the cyclopenta­[cd]­indene derivative 7b, highlighting the broader compatibility of this transformation across different benzofulvene frameworks.

In summary, we have developed iodine­(III)-mediated cyclization cascades for the synthesis of benzofulvene derivatives. The use of BXT or CBX/AgBF4 enabled efficient 5-endo-dig cyclization of 1,2-bis­(arylethynyl)­benzenes, which proceeded through selective termination by either triflation or fluorination. This controlled trapping step resulted in the chemo- and stereoselective formation of difunctionalized benzofulvene products. This methodology was further extended to 1,6-diynes and a 1,2,3-triyne, providing iodane-functionalized fused cyclic frameworks under mild conditions. The synthetic potential of these iodanylated benzofulvenes was showcased through representative downstream transformations, including Sonogashira coupling and aryne insertion, the latter of which is fundamentally unattainable with previously reported functionalized benzofulvenes. These findings lay the foundation for extending the present strategy to higher-order polyynes, which is expected to offer a modular entry to π-extended polycyclic systems with tunable substitution patterns.

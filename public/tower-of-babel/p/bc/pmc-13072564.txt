DNA methylation-regulated AKAP12 expression modulates ECM remodeling and ADP/FAD ratio to influence prognosis in ovarian cancer
Authors: Yanxia Ying, Huilin Zhang, Jiawei Ming, Luanxue Yu, Feng Wang, Zhenying Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOvarian cancer remains one of the deadliest cancers in women, characterized by poor prognosis and limited therapeutic options. The role of specific molecular factors, particularly in the context of tissue stem cells, DNA methylation, and metabolic reprogramming, is not fully understood. A-kinase anchoring protein 12 (AKAP12), a gene implicated in various cancers, has yet to be thoroughly explored in ovarian cancer. This study aims to investigate AKAP12’s involvement in ovarian cancer prognosis, focusing on its expression profile, regulation via DNA methylation, and its downstream metabolic effects.MethodsWe performed a comprehensive multi-omics analysis integrating single-cell RNA sequencing, Mendelian randomization (MR), and DNA methylation profiling, immune infiltration analysis and pathway enrichment analysis. First, we identified AKAP12 as a central prognostic gene in ovarian cancer by intersecting TCGA’s prognostic gene list with ovarian cancer stem cell marker genes. We examined AKAP12 expression in single-cell RNA sequencing data and analyzed the relationship between DNA methylation and AKAP12 expression. A multi-cohort analysis including datasets from TCGA and two independent GEO datasets (GSE26712 and GSE17260) further assessed AKAP12’s association in immune cell infiltration. Further mediated MR analysis was applied to determine the role of metabolic factors that mediate the role of AKAP12 on ovarian cancer. In silico virtual knockout of AKAP12 was performed on single-cell data to model its functional impact.ResultsAKAP12 was predominantly expressed in ovarian cancer stem cells and a subset of epithelial cells, contrasting with its expression in normal ovarian stromal cells. DNA methylation at loci cg10270306 and cg22942200 causally regulated AKAP12 expression and patient survival. The ADP/FAD (adenosine diphosphate/flavin adenine dinucleotide) ratio was identified as a key protective metabolic mediator, explaining 10.09% of AKAP12’s effect on prognosis. AKAP12-high tumors exhibited altered redox metabolism and ECM remodeling pathways. Critically, virtual knockout of AKAP12 causally disrupted the transcriptional network governing ECM composition and cell-ECM interactions.ConclusionOur study establishes AKAP12 as an upstream regulator of ovarian cancer progression, modulated by DNA methylation and exerting its effect through metabolic reprogramming (via the ADP/FAD ratio) and ECM remodeling. These findings provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms driving ovarian cancer and suggest therapeutic strategies targeting the AKAP12 regulatory network.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13048-026-02043-8.

Introduction

Ovarian cancer remains the most lethal gynecologic malignancy, with a five-year survival rate under 50%, primarily due to its late-stage diagnosis, chemoresistance, and the lack of effective targeted therapies [1, 2, 3]. Despite significant advances in genomic profiling [4, 5, 6], the molecular mechanisms driving tumor progression and stromal remodeling—hallmarks of ovarian cancer aggressiveness—remain poorly defined. The tumor microenvironment (TME), encompassing a complex network of immune cells, extracellular matrix (ECM) components, and metabolites, plays a critical role in modulating tumor behavior and therapeutic response [7, 8, 9]. However, the intricate interactions within the TME and their impact on cancer progression require further investigation.

Cancer stem cells (CSCs) have emerged as pivotal drivers of tumor recurrence and chemoresistance in ovarian cancer [10, 11, 12]. Recent studies suggest that the maintenance and plasticity of CSCs are tightly regulated by epigenetic and metabolic reprogramming [13, 14, 15], yet the precise mechanisms governing these processes in ovarian cancer remain largely unexplored. Among the genes involved in these regulatory networks, AKAP12 (A-kinase anchoring protein 12), encoding a scaffold protein involved in signal transduction [16, 17, 18], has been implicated as a potential tumor suppressor [19, 20, 21]. While AKAP12 has been linked to the regulation of cell adhesion, apoptosis, and metastasis suppression in various cancers [19, 20, 21, 22, 23], its role in ovarian cancer remains controversial and may be context-dependent, with studies suggesting both tumor-suppressive and oncogenic functions under different conditions.

Recent advances in epigenetic research have demonstrated the dynamic role of DNA methylation in regulating gene expression in both cancer cells and stromal components [24, 25, 26]. However, the relationship between DNA methylation and AKAP12 expression in ovarian cancer, as well as its impact on tumor progression, is not yet fully understood. Additionally, metabolic reprogramming [27, 28], particularly redox homeostasis has been critically involved in cancer stem cell plasticity [29] and TME dynamics [30, 31]. The interplay between these metabolites may facilitates metabolic adaptation and oxidative stress management in evolving tumors [32, 33, 34]. Yet, the role of these metabolites in regulating oncogenic signaling and TME remodeling remains to be elucidated.

This study aims to address these gaps by investigating AKAP12 as a central hub in the epigenetic-metabolic networks driving ovarian cancer progression. Using a multi-omics approach, including single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), Mendelian randomization (MR), and metabolic profiling, we will: (1) elucidate the epigenetic regulation of AKAP12 through methylation quantitative trait loci (mQTL) analysis and their downstream effects on ovarian cancer progression; (2) identify key metabolic effectors, including the ADP/FAD (adenosine 5’-diphosphate to flavin adenine dinucleotide) ratio, that mediate AKAP12’s impact on TME dynamics and prognosis, and quantify their mediating effects; (3) employ in silico genetic perturbation models on single-cell data to infer the causal role of AKAP12 in transcriptional reprogramming, particularly in ECM remodeling; and (4) map AKAP12’s role in TME modulation by characterizing its interactions with stromal and immune cells. This comprehensive analysis will not only deepen our understanding of AKAP12’s role but also offer novel therapeutic strategies by targeting the AKAP12-epigenetic-metabolic-stromal axis.

Methods

Ovarian cancer cohort data

The ovarian cancer data for this study were derived from multiple publicly available datasets: The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) cohort for discovery, and two independent Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) cohorts (GSE17260 and GSE26712) for validation. These datasets provide comprehensive clinical and transcriptomic profiles.

Two-sample MR

Two-sample MR analysis was conducted utilizing genome-wide significant instrumental variables (p < 5 × 10⁻⁸) with LD clumping (r² < 0.001, 10,000 kb window). Primary analysis employed inverse variance weighted (IVW) regression, complemented by MR-Egger regression for pleiotropy assessment (intercept p > 0.05 required). Sensitivity validation included: (1) weighted median estimator (≥ 50% valid instruments assumption); (2) iterative leave-one-out analysis; (3) Steiger directionality testing. All analyses were implemented in TwoSampleMR (v 0.5.6), with F-statistics > 10 confirming instrument strength.

Ovarian cancer outcome data

The ovarian cancer outcome data utilized in this study were derived from the Finnish R12 version of the ovarian cancer dataset, which includes comprehensive clinical and genomic data for 1,135 patients diagnosed with ovarian cancer, alongside 222,078 control individuals of European descent. This dataset provides a well-powered resource to explore genetic determinants of ovarian cancer prognosis and to identify potential biomarkers associated with clinical outcomes.

scRNA-seq analysis

scRNA-seq data for ovarian cancer were retrieved from two independent public datasets, GSE266577 and GSE184880, to ensure the robustness and generalizability of our findings.

The GSE266577 dataset comprises samples from 29 patients with high-grade serous ovarian cancer (HGSC), including paired samples from chemo-naïve tumors and post-chemotherapy intervals. This dataset was primarily utilized to characterize the expression profile of AKAP12 within the TME of advanced HGSC.

The GSE184880 dataset was additionally analyzed to extend and validate our observations. It includes 59,324 cells from seven treatment-naïve HGSOC patients (spanning early and late stages) and, critically, five age-matched non-malignant ovarian samples. The inclusion of normal ovarian tissue samples from this cohort allowed us to directly compare AKAP12 expression between malignant and non-malignant tissues at single-cell resolution.

Data analysis was performed as we previously reported [35]. Briefly, raw count matrices underwent quality control to filter out low-quality cells (e.g., those with high mitochondrial gene percentage or an insufficient number of detected genes). After normalization, principal component analysis (PCA) was used for dimensionality reduction, followed by graph-based clustering. Cell types were annotated using established marker genes. Differential gene expression analysis between clusters or conditions was conducted using a threshold of |log₂(fold change)| > 0.5 and an adjusted p-value < 0.05.

Survival analysis

Survival analysis was performed to evaluate the association between AKAP12 expression levels and patient prognosis, using the survival package (v 3.5-5) in R. Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression was employed to assess the prognostic value of AKAP12. Kaplan-Meier curves were generated with the survminer package (v 0.4.9) to visualize survival differences between AKAP12 high- and low-expression groups (defined by the median expression level), and statistical significance was determined by the log-rank test.

Protein expression analysis using the human protein atlas (HPA)

To validate our transcriptomic findings at the protein level and to examine AKAP12 expression in normal ovarian tissue, we utilized data from the HPA database (v 25.0, www.proteinatlas.org).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) data retrieval and analysis: Representative IHC images showcasing AKAP12 (HPA006344) protein expression in ovarian cancer tissue and normal ovarian tissue were retrieved from the HPA “Tissue” and “Cancer” modules. The expression patterns (e.g., intensity and localization) were described based on the annotated pathology reports and visual inspection provided by HPA.

scRNA-seq data of normal ovary: To quantify AKAP12 expression across specific cell types in the normal ovarian microenvironment, we analyzed the publicly available single-cell RNA-seq data for normal ovary provided within the HPA “Single Cell Type” module. The expression levels of AKAP12 in cell types such as ovarian stromal cells, pericytes, and vascular smooth muscle cells were extracted directly from the HPA interface for downstream interpretation.

mQTL analysis and mediation MR

mQTL data and instrument selection

CpG sites annotated to the AKAP12 gene were identified using the Illumina HumanMethylation450k annotation. For each CpG site, associated cis-methylation quantitative trait loci (mQTLs) were sourced from the GoDMC database. Genetic variants (SNPs) meeting genome-wide significance (p < 5 × 10⁻⁸) with the CpG methylation level were selected as instrumental variables.

MR involving DNA methylation

Two-sample MR was performed to assess: (1) the causal effect of each CpG site’s methylation level on ovarian cancer prognosis (using outcome data described in Sect. 3), and (2) the causal effect of significant prognostic CpG sites on AKAP12 expression levels (using eQTL data as the outcome). The primary analysis method was IVW.

Mediation MR analysis

A two-step mediation MR analysis was conducted to estimate the proportion of the effect of AKAP12 expression on ovarian cancer prognosis that is mediated through specific CpG sites. This was performed as previously described [35]. Briefly, we calculated the product of the causal effects along the two paths: (1) from the CpG site to AKAP12 expression (β₁), and (2) from AKAP12 expression to prognosis (β₂). The mediated proportion was then derived as (β₁ × β₂) / β_total, where β_total represents the total causal effect of the same CpG site on prognosis.

Data source of 1,400 plasma metabolites and mediation MR

To identify metabolic mediators, we first performed MR analysis to evaluate the causal relationships between 1,400 plasma metabolites [36] and ovarian cancer, including their effects on ovarian cancer prognosis, as previously reported [35]. Mediation MR analysis was employed to investigate the potential of plasma metabolites in the association between genetic variants and ovarian cancer outcomes.

Immune infiltration analysis

Immune cell infiltration in ovarian cancer samples was assessed using the ESTIMATE algorithm, which provides estimates of immune and stromal cell infiltration within tumor samples based on gene expression profiles. The ESTIMATE score was calculated for each sample to evaluate the overall immune and stromal content in TME.

Differential gene expression and pathway enrichment analysis

Differential gene expression analysis using DESeq2 (v1.44.0) and edgeR (v 4.2.0) packages was performed to identify genes associated with the high and low expression of AKAP12 in ovarian cancer subgroups. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified by applying a fold change threshold (log2) greater than 0.5 or less than − 0.5, with an adjusted p-value (padj) < 0.05 for the TCGA and GSE26712 cohorts, and a nominal p < 0.05 for the GSE17260 cohort.

To explore the functional implications of the DEGs, Gene Ontology (GO) term enrichment analysis was performed. The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis was further applied to investigate the molecular pathways implicated in ovarian cancer.

Two distinct Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) strategies were employed. First, a targeted GSEA was performed to test the specific hypothesis that AKAP12 expression influences mitochondrial function and oxidative stress response. For this analysis, a custom gene set was curated from the MSigDB, focusing on pathways related to oxidative phosphorylation, mitochondrial organization, hypoxia, and oxidative stress (see Supplementary Table 1 for the complete gene set list). Second, an unbiased GSEA for comprehensive pathway discovery was conducted using the full “C2: Canonical Pathways” collection from MSigDB (c2.cp.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt), which contains 3,050 predefined gene sets. Both analyses were performed using the clusterProfiler R package (v 4.12.0). Genes were ranked according to the signal-to-noise ratio, and gene set permutations were used to calculate significance. A nominal p < 0.05 and a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25 were applied as significance thresholds for enrichment.

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis

The PPI network was constructed using STRING database (v 12.0), which provides a comprehensive resource for predicting and visualizing functional interactions between proteins. A medium-confidence interaction score threshold of > 0.4 was applied to filter out low-confidence interactions, ensuring the reliability of the network construction.

Single-cell virtual knockout analysis

To investigate the functional impact of AKAP12 in the ovarian cancer tumor microenvironment, we performed a virtual gene knockout analysis using the scTenifoldKnk R package (v 1.0.2). This computational approach leverages manifold alignment to simulate the transcriptomic consequences of knocking out a specific gene in a single-cell RNA-seq dataset. We applied it to our processed tumor cell expression matrix. The analysis was run with default parameters, including the use of 10,000 highly variable genes, the construction of 30 subnetworks, and the random selection of 2,000 cells per network. Differential regulation was assessed for all genes, and those with padj < 0.05 were considered significantly dysregulated following the in-silico knockout of AKAP12.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.4. Data preprocessing, normalization, and quality control procedures were applied as described above. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. For multiple testing scenarios (e.g., differential expression, pathway enrichment), FDR or padj was controlled as specified in each respective subsection above.

Results

Identification of AKAP12 as a prognostic core gene in ovarian cancer

To uncover potential prognostic markers in ovarian cancer, we performed a comprehensive analysis of TCGA ovarian cancer cohort, integrating scRNA-seq data to identify tissue-specific stem cell marker genes. By cross-referencing these marker genes with previously identified prognostic genes in ovarian cancer, we selected a total of 194 candidate genes for further analysis (Fig. 1A). To prioritize these candidates, we performed two independent analyses. First, MR analysis using eQTL data revealed five genes with odds ratio (OR) greater than 1. Separately, survival analysis of the 194 genes in the TCGA cohort identified six genes with a hazard ratio (HR) greater than 1, indicating a potential link to poor prognosis. Further intersection of these two gene sets (the five genes from MR and the six from survival analysis) identified three overlapping genes: CCND1 (Cyclin D1), AKAP12, and LRP10 (LDL receptor related protein 10) (Fig. 1B). Given the limited understanding of AKAP12 in the context of ovarian cancer, we focused on investigating its role in cancer progression and survival. The MR analysis using IVW method revealed an OR for AKAP12 greater than 1, suggesting a potential pro-tumorigenic role in ovarian cancer (Fig. 1C). To validate these findings, survival analysis was performed across multiple ovarian cancer cohorts, including TCGA, GSE17260, and GSE26712. In all cohorts, high AKAP12 expression was significantly correlated with worse survival outcomes (Fig. 1D-F). This was further validated by Kaplan-Meier analysis using the Kaplan-Meier Plotter database, which revealed that high AKAP12 expression was consistently associated with poorer prognosis in ovarian cancer (Fig. 1G-J). These results collectively suggest that AKAP12 is a promising prognostic marker in ovarian cancer.

Expression profile of AKAP12 in ovarian cancer via scRNA-seq and protein-level analysis

scRNA-seq analysis of ovarian cancer samples from the GSE266577 cohort revealed that AKAP12 is predominantly expressed in tissue stem cells (Fig. 2A-B). This finding was consistently validated in an independent cohort (GSE184880), where AKAP12 also showed enriched expression in tissue stem cells, with additional notable expression in a subset of epithelial cells (Fig. 2C-D). To complement these transcriptomic findings at the protein level, we examined HPA database. IHC staining in ovarian cancer tissues revealed a moderate, heterogeneous expression pattern of AKAP12 protein, predominantly localized to the cytoplasm of a subset of tumor cells (Fig. 2E). This pattern is consistent with the scRNA-seq results, suggesting that the AKAP12-high epithelial and stem cells identified by sequencing may contribute to the protein signal observed in tumor sections.

To define the expression profile in non-malignant tissues, we performed scRNA-seq analysis on normal ovarian samples, which identified high AKAP12 expression primarily in smooth muscle and endothelial cells (Fig. 2F-G). Again, this transcriptional signature was corroborated by IHC from HPA, which showed a broad, diffuse expression pattern of AKAP12 protein in normal ovarian stroma (Fig. 2H). Further mining of HPA single-cell data for normal ovary confirmed this pattern, quantifying high AKAP12 expression in ovarian stromal cells, with moderate expression in pericytes, vascular smooth muscle cells, and lymphatic endothelial cells (Supplementary Fig. 1A-C). Together, the concordance between scRNA-seq and IHC data solidifies the conclusion that that AKAP12 expression is cell-type-specific, exhibiting a stark difference between its roles in the normal ovarian stroma versus malignant tissue stem and epithelial cells.

DNA methylation regulates AKAP12 expression and impacts ovarian cancer prognosis

To further investigate the regulation of AKAP12 expression in ovarian cancer, we analyzed mQTL data, identifying a total of 21 methylation sites associated with the expression of AKAP12 (Supplementary Table 2). Among these, we selected 10 methylation sites for further MR analysis based on their potential relevance to ovarian cancer prognosis. Notably, five methylation sites—cg10270306, cg13033898, cg17520539, cg20068535, cg22942200, and—showed significant associations with prognosis, exhibiting OR less than 1, indicating that hypomethylation at these loci is causally linked to a worse prognosis (Fig. 3A).

To explore the regulatory relationship between these methylation sites and AKAP12 expression, we performed MR analysis, which revealed that cg10270306 and cg22942200 specifically and negatively regulate AKAP12 expression (Fig. 3B). Further investigation using mediation MR analysis revealed that the methylation levels at both cg10270306 and cg22942200 significantly mediated the effect of AKAP12 expression on ovarian cancer prognosis. Specifically, cg22942200 accounted for 33.37% of the mediation proportion, while cg10270306 contributed 28.13% (Fig. 3C-D). These findings, established through causal inference analyses, underscore the pivotal role of DNA methylation in modulating AKAP12 expression, which in turn impacts ovarian cancer prognosis.

AKAP12 dictates metabolic plasticity to worsen ovarian cancer prognosis via the ADP/FAD ratio

Having established the epigenetic regulation of AKAP12, we next sought to determine its downstream metabolic effects. To investigate the role of metabolic alterations in AKAP12’s regulation of ovarian cancer prognosis, we analyzed the association of 1,400 metabolites with ovarian cancer prognosis using MR. Four metabolites were identified as being significantly associated with prognosis and also regulated by AKAP12. Among these metabolites, acisoga, ethyl β-glucopyranoside, and X-22,162 (an unannotated plasma metabolite) levels exhibited OR greater than 1, indicating their roles as risk factors for ovarian cancer. In contrast, the ADP/FAD ratio was found to be negatively associated with ovarian cancer prognosis (OR < 1), suggesting that it may serve as a protective factor against the disease (Fig. 4A).

Further MR analysis revealed that AKAP12 negatively regulated the levels of all these four metabolites (Fig. 4B). Among these metabolites, the ADP/FAD ratio mediated 10.09% of AKAP12’s regulatory effect on ovarian cancer prognosis (Fig. 4C), directly linking its metabolic pathway modulation to clinical outcomes.

To explore the downstream effects of the AKAP12-regulated ADP/FAD ratio, we performed a targeted GSEA on transcriptomic data from the three independent cohorts (TCGA, GSE26712, and GSE17260) using a custom gene set collection related to mitochondrial function and oxidative stress.

This analysis revealed stark, cohort-specific metabolic reprogramming in AKAP12-high tumors (Fig. 4D-F). In the TCGA cohort, AKAP12-high tumors exhibited significant suppression (enrichmentScore < 0) of oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) and mitochondrial organization pathways (Fig. 4D). In contrast, AKAP12-high tumors in the GSE26712 cohort showed marked activation (enrichmentScore > 0) of these same OXPHOS pathways, alongside hypoxia and oxidative stress responses (Fig. 4E). The GSE17260 cohort also showed strong activation of hypoxia and oxidative stress response pathways (Fig. 4F).

Despite the divergent direction of OXPHOS enrichment, both patterns were consistently associated with alterations in redox-state-related processes, underscoring the role of AKAP12 in metabolic plasticity. Collectively, these findings suggest that AKAP12 modulates ovarian cancer prognosis by regulating the ADP/FAD ratio and perturbing the cellular redox state.

Immune infiltration and differential gene expression in AKAP12 high and low expression groups

To investigate the impact of AKAP12 expression on the ovarian cancer tumor microenvironment, we performed immune infiltration analysis across three distinct cohorts: TCGA, GSE17260, and GSE26712. We observed that the StromalScore, an indicator of tumor stroma content, was significantly higher in the AKAP12 high-expression groups compared to the low-expression groups (Fig. 5A-C). This suggests that high AKAP12 expression is associated with a stroma-rich TME.

To further explore the biological consequences, we conducted DEG analysis followed by comprehensive GSEA using the canonical pathways (C2) collection to unbiasedly identify all potentially dysregulated biological processes (Fig. 5D-F).

In the TCGA cohort, the AKAP12 high-expression group showed significant enrichment in pathways related to ECM remodeling, including those involved in collagen biosynthesis, ECM organization, and degradation. KEGG pathway analysis further supported this, revealing enrichment in “ECM-receptor interaction” (Fig. 5G). In the GSE17260 cohort, we identified significant enrichments in pathways related to core matrisome proteins and collagen metabolism, alongside ECM degradation processes (Fig. 5H). In the GSE26712 cohort, the most prominent pathway enrichments were in ECM organization and integrin-mediated signaling pathways (Fig. 5I). These results collectively suggest that AKAP12 expression is strongly associated with ECM remodeling and integrin signaling, both crucial processes in ovarian cancer progression.

We further intersected the DEGs from the TCGA, GSE17260, and GSE26712 datasets and identified 20 genes that were consistently differentially expressed across all three cohorts (Supplementary Table 3). GO and KEGG pathway analyses of these shared DEGs revealed significant enrichment in ECM remodeling processes, including collagen trimer assembly, cell-substrate adhesion, and ECM-receptor interaction (Fig. 5J). These results underscore AKAP12’s potential role in maintaining stromal structural integrity and mediating mechanotransduction signaling.

To investigate the interactions among these shared DEGs, we conducted a PPI network analysis. The results revealed that ECM-related genes such as EMILIN1 (elastin microfibril interfacer 1), LAMB1 (laminin subunit beta 1), COL14A1 (collagen type XIV alpha 1 chain), NID1 (nidogen 1), COL6A2 (collagen type VI alpha 2 chain), and SPARC (secreted protein acidic and cysteine rich) formed a highly interconnected network (Fig. 6), suggesting their collaborative role in modulating ECM remodeling and highlighting the central role of the AKAP12-associated gene signature in ovarian cancer.

Virtual knockout of AKAP12 in single-cell data implicates its causal role in ECM remodeling

To provide causal evidence for AKAP12’s regulatory function beyond associative observations, we performed an in silico virtual knockout of AKAP12 on ovarian cancer cells from the two scRNA-seq cohorts (GSE266577 and GSE184880) using the scTenifoldKnk algorithm. This approach models the transcriptomic rewiring induced by the absence of a specific gene.

In the GSE266577 cohort, the virtual knockout of AKAP12 resulted in the significant dysregulation of two genes: TYROBP (an immune receptor adaptor) and CTSS (Cathepsin S, a protease critically involved in extracellular matrix degradation and remodeling) (Fig. 7A-B). The perturbation of CTSS directly aligns with our associative findings implicating AKAP12 in ECM turnover.

A more extensive rewiring was observed in the GSE184880 cohort, where AKAP12 virtual knockout led to significant expression changes in 72 genes (Fig. 7C). The top 20 most affected genes included key ECM structural and regulatory components such as cysteine-rich protein 1 (CRIP1), sushi domain containing 3 (SUSD3), COL6A2, collagen type I alpha 1 chain (COL1A1), and SPARC (Fig. 7D).

Subsequent pathway enrichment analysis on both the full set of 72 dysregulated genes and the top 20 genes converged on the same biological themes. Both gene sets were predominantly enriched in pathways integral to ECM biology, including “ECM-receptor interaction”, “focal adhesion”, and “collagen-containing extracellular matrix” (Fig. 7E-F).

Collectively, the virtual knockout experiments demonstrate that perturbing AKAP12 expression causally disrupts the transcriptional network governing ECM composition and cell-ECM interactions. The convergence of these perturbation-based results with the AKAP12-associated ECM pathway enrichments strongly supports a model wherein AKAP12 is a functional upstream regulator of ECM remodeling in ovarian cancer, a process fundamental to tumor progression and metastasis.

Discussion

AKAP12 emerges as a central orchestrator of ovarian cancer progression by integrating three key processes: maintaining cancer stemness, directing metabolic adaptation, and driving stromal remodeling. Our multi-omics dissection reveals that AKAP12 operates within a context-dependent regulatory paradigm distinct from its roles in other malignancies. Our pan-cancer analysis further corroborates this functional duality, revealing that AKAP12 is frequently downregulated in tumors versus normal tissues (e.g., in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), and colon adenocarcinoma (COAD)) (Supplementary Fig. 2A), yet its high expression predicts poor prognosis in ovarian cancer and several other malignancies such as low-grade glioma (LGG) and mesothelioma (MESO), while being associated with improved survival in others including kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (KIRC) and acute myeloid leukemia (LAML) (Supplementary Fig. 2B). This tissue-specific duality—acting as either an oncogenic rheostat or a tumor suppressor—is not a contradiction but rather highlights the critical influence of cellular microenvironment on AKAP12’s functional output. While AKAP12 suppresses metastasis in LUSC via TCF21-mediated glycolysis inhibition and enhances chemoresistance in colorectal cancer through STAT3 activation [19, 37], its ovarian cancer function converges on maintaining a stem-like state. This role is further supported by its expression pattern within another ovarian tumor ecosystem, where single-cell transcriptomic analysis reveals specific enrichment of AKAP12 in tissue stem cells and malignant epithelial cells.

Our findings also uncover a unique metabolic vulnerability in ovarian cancer, where AKAP12 regulates the ADP/FAD ratio—a redox-sensitive metabolite pair critical for CSC survival. The depressed ADP/FAD ratio in AKAP12-high tumors reflects a metabolic state balancing energy stress with restrained oxidative phosphorylation, facilitating CSC quiescence [38, 39]. This metabolic shift promotes survival under hypoxic conditions and chemotherapy-induced oxidative damage through two synergistic mechanisms: redox buffering (attenuating ROS production) and nucleotide homeostasis (prioritizing nucleotide synthesis over proliferation). Notably, this axis mediates 10.09% of AKAP12’s prognostic power, suggesting its clinical significance in tumor resistance to bevacizumab and other therapies targeting angiogenesis.

The DNA methylation loci cg10270306 and cg22942200, accounting for 28–33% of AKAP12’s prognostic power, establish an epigenetic primed state favoring CSC persistence through hypomethylation-driven AKAP12 upregulation. This contrasts with pan-cancer patterns where AKAP12 hypermethylation silences its expression in most cancers, yet al.igns with its upregulation in anti-VEGF-resistant subtypes (e.g., ovarian cancer, glioblastoma multiforme), suggesting tissue-specific methylation drivers underpin therapy evasion [23]. In ovarian cancer, these mQTLs couple hypomethylation to redox metabolism, whereas hypermethylation in other contexts may reflect distinct regulatory logic. The AKAP12-ADP/FAD axis, mediating 10.09% of its prognostic effect, may create a permissive niche for CSC survival under oxidative stress—a mechanism reminiscent of colorectal CSCs where AKAP12 activates STAT3 to sustain stemness. Crucially, our MR analysis extends prior correlative studies by causally linking AKAP12 to metabolic-stromal crosstalk, resolving longstanding debates about its upstream/downstream positioning in oncogenic pathways.

To functionally contextualize these causal inferences at the cellular level, we employed in silico single-cell knockout analysis. This computational perturbation of AKAP12 across independent ovarian cancer single-cell cohorts recapitulated key phenotypic predictions, driving transcriptomic shifts that directly demonstrate disrupted ECM organization. This convergence between population-level causal inference and single-cell-level perturbation modeling strengthens the hypothesis that AKAP12 operates as a key regulator of the tumor microenvironment.

Stromal reprogramming emerges as AKAP12’s most potent effector mechanism. By coordinately upregulating collagen biosynthesis, ECM degradation enzymes, and integrin signaling, AKAP12 generates a desmoplastic niche mirroring the stiffened microenvironments of anti-VEGF-resistant tumors. This stromal rewiring may explain AKAP12’s association with poor outcomes in bevacizumab-treated ovarian cancer patients [23].

Our findings carry immediate therapeutic implications. The mQTLs identified offer DNA methylation-based biomarkers for predicting AKAP12-driven resistance to conventional therapies. Preclinical models should prioritize testing hypomethylating agents combined with FAD biosynthesis inhibitors, particularly in AKAP12-high tumors showing ECM remodeling signatures. Given AKAP12’s dual regulation of CSCs and ECM, dual-targeting strategies may synergize to disrupt CSC-stroma co-dependency.

Limitations of the study

Several limitations of our study warrant consideration. First, while our MR analysis supports a causal role for AKAP12 in ovarian cancer pathogenesis, this approach relies on key assumptions that cannot be fully validated empirically. Although we performed extensive sensitivity analyses (MR-Egger, weighted median, etc.) to mitigate horizontal pleiotropy, the potential for residual bias due to unknown pleiotropic pathways cannot be entirely ruled out. Second, the metabolomics data utilized were derived from plasma. While valuable for capturing systemic metabolic changes, this approach lacks the spatial resolution to distinguish whether the observed alterations in the ADP/FAD ratio originate from tumor cells, stromal components, or both. This limits our ability to precisely map the metabolic circuitry regulated by AKAP12 within the specific cellular compartments of the TME. Third, and most notably, our computational predictions—including the causal links between AKAP12, metabolic remodeling, and stromal reprogramming—await direct experimental validation. The resolution of the aforementioned limitations ultimately depends on future work employing suitable in vitro and in vivo models. For instance, AKAP12-knockout organoids or xenograft models cultured on tunable stiffness hydrogels will be crucial to formally test its hypothesized role as a mechanosensor and metabolic regulator, and to precisely localize the source of the ADP/FAD metabolic shift.

Conclusion

In conclusion, by integrating causal inference (MR) with multi-omics and single-cell resolution analyses, our study not only establishes AKAP12 as a pivotal factor in ovarian cancer progression but also provides a causal framework linking epigenetic regulation to metabolic-stromal crosstalk.

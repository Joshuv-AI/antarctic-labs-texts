An emerging melody composed of the notes from structural variants - Comment on ‘The fusion characteristics of RET fusion in pan-cancer among the Chinese population: A comprehensive genomic analysis’ by Chen et al
Authors: Lv Wu, Qing Zhou, Chang Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•DNA/RNA-based NGS of 21,023 Chinese pan-cancer samples revealed a detailed RET fusion spectrum and population-specific patterns.•Four structural subtypes—Simple Reciprocal Inversion, Co-Fusion, Single Fusion-Common, and Single Fusion-Rare—were validated in 1355 NSCLC samples. The clinical relevance of four fusion subtypes warrants further exploration.•A refined diagnostic workflow integrating RNA-NGS for rare or complex fusions improves detection accuracy and guides personalized therapy.•Chinese patients showed a higher intron 10 breakpoint frequency than Western cohorts. Preliminary evidence of geographic variation in RET inhibitor outcomes suggests links between genomic structure and drug response, underscoring the need for population-inclusive clinical trials.

Introduction: overture of RET fusion

With the widespread clinical application of next-generation sequencing (NGS), lung cancer treatment has entered a precision era guided by molecular testing. Following landmark discoveries of EGFR mutations and ALK fusions, RET (rearranged during transfection) fusions were first reported in lung cancer in 2012. RET fusions, identified in 1 %−2 % of non-small cell lung cancers (NSCLC), have been recognized as therapeutic target, leading to approvals of two selective RET inhibitors, pralsetinib and selpercatinib, as standard treatment options.

Despite these advances, reliably detecting RET fusions remains challenging in clinical practice. Traditional detection methods, such as fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) and reverse transcription PCR (RT-PCR), identify only specific fusion types without detailed information about fusion partners or precise genomic breakpoints. Although DNA-based NGS has improved fusion detection capability, limitations remain. DNA-based NGS may generate false-negative results or detect non-functional rearrangements [1], thus confounding treatment decisions.

RNA-based sequencing, which directly detects fusion transcripts, has superior sensitivity compared to DNA-based NGS, particularly for complex or rare fusion events. Studies by Benayed et al. [2] and Li et al. [3] underscored the importance of RNA sequencing for accurate clinical interpretation. Moreover, previous research [4] predominantly included European populations and relied solely on DNA sequencing without cross-validation using RNA sequencing. The question of whether these findings could be generalized to Asian populations remained unanswered. The validation of the newly identified fusion partners remains unclear. Therefore, clarifying the characteristics using both DNA-based and RNA-based sequencing of RET fusions in large, real-world Asian populations has become a pressing clinical and scientific necessity.

Advances and insights: the crescendo

Chen et al.’s research significantly advances our understanding of RET fusions by analyzing a substantial Chinese pan-cancer cohort of 21,023 tumor samples using both DNA- and RNA-targeted NGS approaches, elaborating a comprehensive and detailed genetic spectrum. Their analysis confirmed that, similar to Western populations, the most common RET fusion partners in Chinese patients were KIF5B, CCDC6, and NCOA4. However, unlike Western cohorts where breakpoints mainly occurred in intron 11(87 %, intron 10 at 5 %), the Chinese cohort exhibited a tendency to intron 10 (30.79 %, intron 11 at 54.8 %).

The authors also proposed a novel classification of RET fusions based on the structural characteristics identified by DNA-based NGS: (1) Simple Reciprocal Inversion, which included both the classic forward fusion and its reverse counterpart; (2) Co-Fusion, which included two or more RET nonreciprocal rearrangements; (3) Single Fusion-Common, which included only one rearrangement event with common fusion partners such as KIF5B, CCDC6, or NCOA4; and (4) Single Fusion-Rare, which included only one rearrangement event with rare fusion partners other than KIF5B, CCDC6, or NCOA4. Importantly, cross-validation in 1355 NSCLC tissue samples using RNA-based NGS maintained the four proposed subtypes. The integration of DNA and RNA data represents a significant advancement as it holds great potential for future clinical application. Notably, RNA-based NGS revealed additional complexity. The clinical relevance of four fusion subtypes warrants further exploration.

These findings have also sparked new ideas for diagnostic workflows and therapeutic strategies (Fig. 1). RNA-NGS is increasingly important, particularly for patients strongly suspected of harboring genetic fusions, due to its superior sensitivity and specificity. Routine integration of RNA sequencing could substantially improve diagnostic accuracy, guiding personalized therapeutic decisions. For patients with sufficient biopsy tissue for NGS testing, large-panel DNA NGS remains recommended, while RNA-NGS should be utilized in cases with rare fusion partners like LOC644634, RASGEF1A, and ERC1, as well as less common rearrangement types, especially 5′−5′ or 3′−3′ rearrangements, to determine transcription level changes.

Another intriguing aspect raised by this study involves therapeutic efficacy variations between Chinese and global patient populations treated with approved RET inhibitors. As summarized in Table 1, clinical data for pralsetinib[5,6] and selpercatinib [[7], [8], [9], [10]] indicate slight differences in objective response rates (ORR) and progression-free survival (PFS), prompting questions regarding the role of breakpoint differences across populations. It is worth noting that according to the 2025 ASCO update of follow-up data from the ARROW study, the ORR and median PFS were significantly higher in the United States (25.9 months) compared to Asia (12.6 months). Although differences in follow-up duration and other factors might influence these observations, further analysis across populations could be informative.

Conclusion: coda with future encores

Overall, Chen et al. systematically characterized the genomic landscape of RET fusions in a large Chinese population, proposing RET fusion subtype classification based on structural features through DNA-RNA cross-validation. This study sets the foundation for future research focusing on subtype-specific clinical features and therapeutic implications. Additionally, global clinical trials should ensure representation from Eastern and Western populations based on observed genomic heterogeneity.

Much like humanity's first space journey opened our eyes to the vast complexity of the universe, this study significantly illuminates our understanding of fusion structures. We now stand at a threshold of deciphering how the notes of individual genetic alteration orchestrate a biological symphony which might ultimately impact patient outcomes. This melody still awaits functional validation, inviting further scientific exploration to fully understand its therapeutic implication.

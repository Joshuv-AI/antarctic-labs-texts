Efficacy and security of oral Chinese patent medicine combined with antibiotics in the treatment of pelvic inflammatory disease: a Bayesian network meta-analysis
Authors: Mao-Ya Li, Han-Zhi Zhong, Cheng-Li Bin, Yan Wang, Li Huang, Xiao-Li Ji, Shao-Bin Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPelvic inflammatory disease (PID) is a group of female upper genital tract infectious diseases. The efficacy of combining Chinese patent medicine with antibiotics in treating this condition is well-established. Our objective is to conduct a Bayesian network meta-analysis (NMA) to compare the efficacy and security of different oral Chinese patent medicines combined with antibiotics for PID.MethodsRandomized controlled trials (RCTs) reporting oral Chinese patent medicine combined with antibiotics for PID were included by 7 databases from the inception of the databases to December 2024. The Literature that satisfied the inclusion criteria underwent screening, followed by an evaluation of the quality and risk of bias of the studies that were included, in accordance with the Cochrane 5.1 manual and RevMan 5.4. Subsequently, network meta-analysis was conducted using Stata 16.0 and R software.ResultsThere were 73 RCTs included to evaluate the efficacy of 11 Chinese patent medicines combined with antibiotics. Kangfuyan Capsule (KFY) + antibiotics (SUCRA = 87.92%) showed the best efficacy in reducing the VAS (visual analogue scale) score. Jinying Capsule (JY) + antibiotics (SUCRA = 74.29%) showed the shortest time to relieve abdominal pain. Fuyanshu Capsule (FYS) + antibiotics showed the lowest recurrence rate (SUCRA = 99.18%) and were the most effective in reducing the level of C-reactive protein (SUCRA = 93.19%). Fuyankang tablet (FYK) + antibiotics (SUCRA = 91.15%) demonstrated highest security.ConclusionThe efficacy of Chinese patent medicine combined with antibiotics in the treatment of PID is better than that of antibiotics alone. Different Chinese patent medicines have their own advantages in improving abdominal pain symptoms, reducing recurrence rate and inflammatory factors levels. Clinically, these drugs can be objectively selected based on the results of the study.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12906-025-05088-2.Keywords: Chinese patent medicine, Antibiotics, Pelvic inflammatory disease, Network meta-analysis, Efficacy and security

Introduction

Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) is a prevalent collection of infectious diseases that affect the upper female reproductive tract, encompassing endometritis, salpingitis, tubo-ovarian abscess, and pelvic peritonitis. This disease primarily affects sexually active women of childbearing age, and its incidence varies significantly across different countries and regions. Research has indicated that the incidence of PID in women of childbearing age ranges from approximately 2% to 12% [1, 2]. A study extracted the data of PID among reproductive-aged women (15 to 49 years old) from the Global Burden of Disease study 2019. The result showed that in 2019, the ASR of PID prevalence was 53.19 per 100,000 population [3]. Another study estimated that for women aged 35–44 years in England, 33.6% and 16.1% have experienced at least one episode of PID and salpingitis, respectively (diagnosed or not) and 10.7% have experienced one salpingitis and no further PID episodes, 3.7% one salpingitis and one further PID episode, and 1.7% one salpingitis and ⩾2 further PID episodes [4]. PID can frequently arise from non-standard diagnosis and treatment, or delayed treatment during the acute stage, leading to prolonged illness, persistent symptoms, and the development of sequelae, such as chronic pelvic pain, recurrent PID, infertility, and ectopic pregnancy. Literature indicates that roughly 25% of women with PID experience infertility, with a Lifelong infertility incidence of 13.8% attributable to PID. Furthermore, the risk of premature delivery and ectopic pregnancy is higher in individuals with PID compared to the general healthy population [5]. In comparison to the overall Healthy population, individuals with a history of PID exhibit a 1.864-fold and 2.121-fold increase in the risk of premature delivery and ectopic pregnancy, respectively [6]. Chronic pelvic pain affects approximately 16% of individuals with PID, with 11% experiencing a significant impact on their daily activities [7]. These findings highlight the high incidence rate of PID and its associated complications, which can have detrimental effects on the reproductive health and quality of life of women of childbearing age, as well as increase the economic burden on families and society.

The pathogens of PID include exogenous pathogens and endogenous pathogens. Among them, exogenous pathogens include Mycoplasma genitalium, Chlamydia trachomatis and Neisseria gonorrhoeae. Endogenous pathogens originate from the vaginal microbiota, including aerobic and anaerobic bacteria. Aerobic bacteria and facultative anaerobic bacteria mainly include Staphylococcus aureus, hemolytic streptococcus, and Escherichia coli. Anaerobic bacteria include Bacteroides fragilis, Peptococcus, and Peptostreptococcus. Anaerobic infections are characterized by the formation of pelvic abscesses, infectious thrombophlebitis, etc. The main pathogenesis of PID is tissue congestion, edema, and exudation of inflammatory substances after infection with the above-mentioned pathogens. In severe cases, it can lead to necrosis, shedding, and ulcer formation. The main manifestations include acute endometritis and myometritis, acute salpingitis, pyosalpingitis, and ovarian abscess. The predominant approach in Western medicine for treating PID involves the administration of antibiotics, primarily targeting pathogenic bacterial infections, and may be supplemented with surgical intervention as needed. Additionally, hospitalization, route of antibiotic administration (intravenous or oral), and treatment modality selection should be carefully evaluated based on the individual patient's circumstances. According to the “Sexually Transmitted Diseases Treatment Guidelines” established by the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) from 2006 to 2021 [1, 8–10], it is stated that prompt treatment is imperative upon diagnosis of PID. Timely and reasonable administration of antibacterial agents is significantly associated with mitigating the incidence of sequelae [11]. Various guidelines and consensus have pointed out that the selection of antibacterial drugs should cover as many pathogens as possible. Antibiotic use can be administered through either intravenous or non-intravenous regimens. Undoubtedly, the efficacy of antibiotics in treating PID is certain. Whilst there are still some risks of adverse reactions, flora imbalances, and bacterial resistance in patients with PID increase gradually due to non-standard and irrational clinical use of antibiotics [10, 12, 13].

The abundant natural resource pool provides broad possibilities for exploring antibacterial active ingredients in traditional Chinese medicine, and many medicinal plant extracts have shown antibacterial activity. Studies have found that Chinese herbal medicines such as Phellodendron, Honeysuckle, Chrysanthemum, Taraxacum, Scutellaria barbata, Patrinia villosa, Viola yedoensis, and Hedyotis diffusa have varying degrees of antibacterial activity against Staphylococcus aureus and Escherichia coli [14–17]. Chinese patent medicine is a kind of traditional Chinese medicine product with Chinese herbal medicines as raw materials. Under the guidance of the theory of traditional Chinese medicine, in order to prevent and treat diseases, it is processed into a certain dosage form according to the prescribed prescription and preparation process. It is a kind of commercialized traditional Chinese Medicine preparation approved by the national drug supervision and administration department [18]. Different from traditional Chinese medicine decoction, it has the advantages of fixed technology, stable composition, convenient carrying and good patient compliance [19].There are large numbers of Chinese patent medicine incorporated into the Pharmacopoeia of the People’s Republic of China (ChP). During the COVID-19 epidemic, the Chinese patent medicine of clearing away heat and detoxification played an auxiliary role in improving oxygenation leve, suppressing inflammatory factor storms and promoting recovery and reducing sequelae [20]. It can be seen that Chinese patent medicine has potential advantages in the treatment of inflammatory and viral diseases.

According to evidence-based medicine, the combination of Chinese patent medicine and antibiotics in the treatment of PID presents significant advantages over antibiotics alone. These benefits include the alleviation of symptoms and signs, a shortened disease course, reduced recurrence and sequelae, and even a decrease in the amount and duration of antibiotic use, as well as a reduction in adverse reactions [21–24]. Currently, there are numerous Chinese patent medicines available for the treatment of PID, and a considerable number of clinical studies have been conducted. However, there is a dearth of comparative efficacy research on the various Chinese patent medicines when used in conjunction with antibiotics. Therefore, the assessment of the effectiveness and security of interventions for the treatment of PID poses a challenge for clinicians in selecting appropriate drugs. To address this issue, our study aims to employ Bayesian network meta-analysis (NMA) to rank the efficacy and security of diverse interventions based on both direct and indirect evidence, thereby facilitating a comprehensive comprehension of their benefits and drawbacks and furnishing clinical treatment with evidence-based support.

Methods

Registration

This systematic review and NMA is reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA-NMA) statement. The PRISMA checklist is presented in Additional file: Additional file 1. The protocol for this study is registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (registration number CRD42022360001) to ensure transparency, reliability, and novelty.

Search strategies

Medline (via pubmed), Embase, the Cochrane Library, China National Knowledge Infrastructure Database (CNKI), Wanfang Database (Wanfang), VIP Database (VIP), and China Biology Medicine Database (CBM) were systematically searched for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) reporting oral Chinese patent medicine combined with antibiotics for PID from the inception of the databases to December 2024. The following Medical Subject Headings [MeSH] and keywords incorporating Boolean operators were applied: “pelvic inflammatory disease”, “PID”, “TCM”, “Chinese herbal medicine”, “Traditional Chinese Medicine”, “Chinese and Western Medicine”, “capsule”, “grain”, “Oral liquid”, “pill”, “Dan”, “Gao”, “tablet”. Furthermore, we performed a recursive manual search to identify potentially qualified studies that we missed in tracking bibliographies or systematic reviews. The search strategy was shown in Additional file 2.

Literature screening and data extraction

Import all search results into Endnote 20 (version 20.1.0, Clarivate Analytics) to manage and deduplicate entries. Extracted independently by two review authors based on preferred reporting items for the systematic review and meta-analysis process to determine whether they met the inclusion criteria, and any disagreements were resolved by discussion by a third investigator. It were extracted from each article that included Year of publication, first author, sample size, patient age, course of disease, interventions, comparators, duration of treatment, outcome measures, etc.

Quality evaluation

The quality of the included studies was assessed according to Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions [25], including the following 6 domains: selection bias, performance bias, measurement bias, follow-up bias, reporting bias, and other biases. Each RCT was classified into three categories based on its bias risk: low, unclear, or high. Using the Grading of Recommendation, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) tool [26–28] for meta-analysis, we rated the quality of evidence for each outcome derived from meta-analysis estimates. RCTs were initially classified as high-quality evidence by GRADE. It can be downgraded based on its limitations, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. The quality of evidence will be classified into one of four levels, including high, moderate, low and very low quality. Two investigators rated the certainty of consulted with a third party.

Statistical analyses

Direct comparison meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.4 software. The odds ratio (OR) was used as the effect size for dichotomous variables and the mean difference (MD) or Standard Mean Difference (SMD) for continuous variables provided 95% credible interval (CrI). The I2 test was used to include heterogeneity between study results. If I2 ≤ 50% between the results of the studies, no significant difference in heterogeneity was considered and a fixed-effect model was used for meta-analysis; If I2 > 50% between the results of the study results, statistical heterogeneity was considered and a random-effects model was used for meta-analysis [29].

Bayesian methods provide an opportunity for indirect comparisons of trials without direct intervention comparisons and a more direct way to make probabilistic statements about their treatment and security outcomes [30, 31]. STATA/SE version 16.0 (Stata, corp, College Station, TX) is used to draw network diagrams. We used R version 4.2.1 (Windows NT 10.0) and the GeMTC software package for network meta analysis. The parameters in GeMTC are set as follows: the initial value is 2.5; Number of simulation iterations, 50,000 times; Annealing times, 20,000 times; Thinning coefficient, 1; And the number of chains, 4. The convergence of iterations can be monitored through a potential scaling factor (PSRF). In order to rank the effectiveness and security of interventions, we used the surface below the cumulative ranking area (SUCRA) to describe the outcomes, and found that the higher the SUCRA value, the greater the efficacy [32]. The essential assumptions underlying a NMA encompass transitivity, consistency, and homogeneity. In order to ascertain the suitability of conducting a network meta-analysis, an evaluation was conducted on the clinical context and trial characteristics (including age, disease duration, intervention duration, follow-up duration, and publication year) pertaining to each drug class under investigation. Consistency was evaluated by employing the node-splitting method to compare the treatment effect estimated from direct comparisons with that estimated from indirect comparisons, with a significance level of p < 0.05 indicating the presence of consistency. In terms of heterogeneity, I2 represents the statistical values of 25%, 50% and 75% of mild, moderate and high heterogeneity, respectively, and is used to measure whether there is substantial heterogeneity. The STATA/SE version 16.0 (Stata, corp, College Station, TX) was used to drawn the funnel plot to evaluate whether there is bias and small sample research.

Results

Literature retrieval process and results

A total of 21,264 Literature were strieved from 7 databases. The detailed screening process is shown in Fig. 1. Finally, 73 RCTs [33–105] were included for network meta-analysis. There covered 11 interventions: Fuke Qianjin Tablet/Capsule (FKQJ) + antibiotics; Kanfuyan Capsule (KFY) + antibiotics; Huahong Tablet/Capsule (HH) + antibiotics; Jingangteng Capsule (JGT) + antibiotics; Fuyankang Tablet (FYK) + antibiotics; Fuyanxiao Capsule (FYX) + antibiotics; Fule Tablet/Grain (FL) + antibiotics; Fuyanshu Capsule (FYS) + antibiotics; Fuyan Kangfu Capsule (FYKF) + antibiotics; Jinying Capsule (JY) + antibiotics; Penyanjing Oral Solution/Capsule (PYJ) + antibiotics. Quality of the included studies

All 73 RCTs [33–105] mentioned randmization, twenty-five of which were random-number-table method, two were computer-randomization, one was block-randomization, one was randomization by computer, one was random lottery. The bias risk of these RCTs was rated as low risk. The rest were only described as "random" and rated as unclear risk; In terms of allocation concealment, blinding of participants or doctors, and blinding of outcome evaluator, eight of which were described blind method and rated as low risk. The rest were not described and rated as unclear risk; All articles with complete data and no selective reporting were rated as unclear risk, unable to judge other sources of bias, rated as unclear risk. The assessment results of the risk of bias test are shown in Additional file 4.

Pairwise meta-analysis

The results of pairwise meta-analysis were shown in Additional file 5. The results represented that the combination of four Chinese patent medicines (KFY, PYJ, FKQJ, FL) and antibiotics could reduce the VAS score. However, there is no statistical significance, and the reason for consideration may be that the number of included studies is insufficient. The five Chinese patent medicines (JY, HH, JGT, FKQJ, KFY) combined with antibiotics had lower abdominal pain relief time than those used alone and the difference was statistically significant. Compared with antibiotics alone, the combination of three Chinese patent medicines (FKQJ, KFY, FYS) and antibiotics could significantly reduce the recurrence rate of PID, and the difference was statistically significant. The efficacy of the 7 Chinese patent medicines (FYS, FYX, HH, FKQJ, FL, FYK, KFY) combined with antibiotics in reducing CRP was better than that of antibiotics alone. In terms of the incidence of adverse reactions, four Chinese patent medicines (FYK, PYJ, KFY, FKQJ) and antibiotics was lower than that of antibiotics alone (P < 0.05).

Primary outcome

VAS score

A total of 4 studies [60, 86, 99, 105] involving 598 patients reported the VAS score. The interventions included four Chinese patent medicines. KFY + antibiotics (MD = −5.1, 95%CrI = −11.54, 1.35, SUCRA = 87.92%) was the most effective in reducing VAS score compared with other three Chinese patent medicines, along with the suboptimal intervention PYJ + antibiotics (MD = 1.87, 95%CrI = −8.34, 4.53, SUCRA = 55.52%), followed by FKQJ + antibiotics (MD = −1.52, 95%CrI = −7.94, 5.02, SUCRA = 49.44%) (Relative effect sizes of efficacy at post-treatment according to network meta-analysis was shown in Table 1). The funnel plot showed that not all studies were symmetrically distributed around the X = 0 line, and some studies were located outside the funnel chart, which provides evidence for small sample effects in the study network (See in Fig. 3). The results of meta-regression analysis showed that in terms of VAS score, the random effects model fit better (Deviance Information Criterion (DIC) = 66.4 vs 189.1). There is moderate heterogeneity among the studies (Tau (τ) = 1.11, Regression coefficient (RC) = 0.08), and the sample size has no significant effect on the efficacy. The model converges well and the results are reliable (shown in Table 2).

Secondary outcome

A total of 17 studies [34, 42, 43, 46, 47, 49–51, 57, 63, 67, 68, 93, 94, 98, 101, 102] involving 1,732 patients reported the relief time of lower abdominal pain. The interventions included six Chinese patent medicines. JY + antibiotics (MD = −2.98, 95%CrI = −5.22, −0.73, SUCRA = 74.29%), HH + antibiotics (MD = −2.93, 95%CrI = −5.2, −0.67, SUCRA = 73.09%), FYKF + antibiotics (MD = −2.5, 95%CrI = −4.78, −0.23, SUCRA = 61.45%), JGT + antibiotics (MD = −2.22, 95%CrI = −4.41, −0.02, SUCRA = 53,70%), FKQJ + antibiotics (MD = −2.14, 95%CrI = −2.86, −1.43, SUCRA = 50.87%) and KFY + antibiotics (MD = −1.69, 95%CrI = −2.96, −0.42, SUCRA = 35.54%) had a significant benefit compared with antibiotics. According to the SUCRA value, JY + antibiotics showed the shortest time to relieve abdominal pain (SUCRA = 74.29%) (Relative effect sizes of efficacy at post-treatment according to network meta-analysis was shown in Table 3). The results of meta-regression analysis showed that regarding the outcome measure of abdominal pain relief time, the random effects model outperformed the fixed effects model (DIC = 66.9 vs 189.6). The heterogeneity between studies is moderate (τ = 1.11, RC = 0.07), the model converges well, and the results are robust and reliable (shown in Table 2).

Discussion

Due to the lack of consensus regarding the efficacy and security of various Chinese patent medicines for the treatment of PID, this network meta-analysis (NMA) was undertaken to establish a hierarchy of treatment rankings. The ranking probabilities for each Chinese patent medicine were subsequently calculated based on their comparative efficacy across different endpoints. This analysis aims to provide a foundation for selecting alternative treatments and identifying optimal therapeutic choices. Our network meta-analysis has adopted a Bayesian network meta-analysis which involves 73 RCTs in total to evaluate the efficacy of 11 Chinese patent medicine combined with antibiotics in PID patients. Additionally, careful consideration should be given to the appropriate timing for the inclusion of Chinese patent medicines in treatment protocols. To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first systematic review employing Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis to comprehensively investigate and evaluate various oral Chinese Patent Medicines for the treatment of PID. Our focus was on oral Chinese Patent Medicines that are Listed and recommended in the 2020 edition of the Catalogue of Drugs for Basic National Medical Insurance by the National Medical Products Administration in China. Furthermore, we conducted a quality assessment of the included Randomized Controlled Trials. To assess publication bias and small-study effects, we utilized a comparison-adjusted funnel plot and Egger's test. These methodological analyses enhance the reliability and accuracy of our findings.

Due to the different components of each Chinese patent medicine, its clinical efficacy may also vary. Therefore, we carried out this study, with the aim of comparing the differences in the efficacy of different Chinese patent medicines in different aspects, so as to form recommendations to guide the rational clinical selection of drugs for pelvic inflammatory diseases. First of all, in terms of primary outcome measures, KangFuyan Capsule combined with antibiotics showed superior efficacy, indicating that it was more effective in relieving the symptoms of lower abdominal pain in patients with PID. Therefore, when patients with abdominal pain in the acute phase of pelvic inflammatory disease are predominantly present, clinicians can refer to our findings to objectively choose the drug. The ingredients of Kangfuyan Capsule include dandelion, patrinia villosa, coix seed, red peony, atractylodes rhizome, angelica sinensis, chuanxiong, xiangfu, corydalis yanhusuo (prepared), alisma orientalis, hedyotis diffusa. Contemporary pharmacological investigations have substantiated the antibacterial properties of dandelion polysaccharides and polyphenols against Staphylococcus aureus, Bacillus subtilis, Escherichia coli, and Salmonella. Moreover, dandelion polysaccharides and flavonoids exhibit anti-inflammatory characteristics, which can impede xylene-induced inflammation in mice and cotton ball granuloma in rats [106, 107]. Furthermore, research has established the anti-inflammatory and bacteriostatic effects of Chinese herbal medicines, including hedyotis diffusa, patrinia villosa, and coix seed [108–111]. Empirical investigations have demonstrated that the Kangfuyan capsule possesses the capability to impede tissue swelling and exudation during the initial phase of inflammation, as well as inhibit the formation of granulation tissue during the later and persistent stages of inflammation. The analgesic properties of Kangfuyan capsule were investigated through physical stimulation analgesia experiments (hot plate method) and chemical stimulation analgesia experiments (body twisting method). The results of the experiments indicated that each dose group of Kangfuyan capsule exhibited a certain degree of inhibitory effect on the writhing reaction of mice, in both the hot plate and acetic acid body twisting experiments. Furthermore, a dose–effect relationship was observed, suggesting that Kangfuyan capsule possesses analgesic properties [112]. Hence, the combined administration of Kangfuyan Capsule and antibiotics could potentially offer the most effective treatment option for Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) in terms of improving the symptoms of lower abdominal pain in patients with PID. This is consistent with our findings.

Secondly, regarding secondary outcome, Fuyanshu capsule and Fuke Qianjin capsule was top ranked, which may be related to their ability to regulate the immune function of the body. For example, Zhao Jingya [113] observed the clinical efficacy and immune function of Fuyanshu capsule combined with metronidazole in the treatment of chronic pelvic inflammatory disease. The results showed that Fuyanshu capsule combined with antibiotics can shorten the remission time of main symptoms compared to the single antibiotic group, and can improve the levels of immunoglobulin (Ig) A, IgM, and IgG after treatment. A study observed the effect of Fuke Qianjin capsule on the proliferation and immune regulation of spleen lymphocytes in vitro. The results showed that Fuke Qianjin Capsule could effectively promote the proliferation of spleen lymphocytes, increase the ratio of CD4 + and CD8 +, promote the secretion of Th1 cytokines, make Th1/Th2 subsets "drift" to Th1, regulate Th1/Th2 balance, and improve cellular immune response [114].

For patients, abdominal pain relief time is also a crucial evaluation factor. According to our results, the best therapeutic effect ranking of abdominal pain relief time is Jinying capsule. The pharmaceutical composition of Jinying capsule includes honeysuckle, phellodendron amurense, dandelion, Viola yedoensis, wild chrysanthemum, atractylodes rhizome, red peony, corydalis (made with vinegar), salvia miltiorrhiza, saponin thorn. This prescription is derived from the clinical experience of Professor Men Chengfu, a famous TCM doctor in China. It is mainly formulated by combining Wuwei Xiaodu Drink with Ermiao Powder. Experimental studies have substantiated that the administration of Jinying capsule elicits a noteworthy elevation in the pain threshold of mice in response to heat, while concurrently reducing the pain response triggered by acetic acid. Additionally, the capsule has been found to impede the auricle swelling induced by xylene in mice, diminish capillary permeability, and reduce the weight of cotton ball granuloma in rats, thereby exhibiting significant anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties [115]. Huang Min created an endometritis model in rats by administering the clinical pathogenic bacterium Escherichia coli into their uteri. Following intervention with Jinying capsule, it was discovered that the medium and high dose Jinying capsule groups exhibited a significant reduction in endometritis-related pathological changes and intrauterine adhesion [116].

Innovation and limitations of this study

This research represents the initial attempt to rank the effectiveness and security of various oral Chinese patent medicines in conjunction with antibiotics for the treatment of PID via network meta-analysis. Ultimately, a significant sample size of 73 RCTs, encompassing 8,436 patients, was incorporated. The intervention measures were methodically assessed for efficacy and security across various dimensions, including VAS score, abdominal pain relief duration, disease recurrence rate, and incidence of adverse reactions. The evidence sources utilized in this study were extensive.

The paucity of extant literature, both in terms of quantity and quality, has resulted in a dearth of Chinese patent medicine varieties and RCTs that report outcomes such as time to relief of abdominal pain and recurrence rate. This limitation has had a certain impact on the results of the network meta-analysis. Therefore, it is imperative to consider updating the results of the network meta-analysis when additional RCT studies report these outcomes in the future. At the same time, due to the lack of RCT study for direct comparison between different traditional Chinese patent medicine, this study only conducted indirect comparative analysis, without forming a closed-loop analysis. And the number of Chinese patent medicine included in RCT for some outcomes is small, so it also has a certain impact on the reliability and stability of the results of this study. Therefore, the conclusions of this study should be treated with caution in clinical application. Clinicians may utilize these outcomes in conjunction with their clinical expertise to determine the appropriate medication. More randomized, multi-center, and large sample studies are expected to update the results of this network meta-analysis.

Conclusion

Based on the results of a comprehensive analysis, Kangfuyan and Fuyanshu capsules appear to offer the most favorable balance between efficacy and recurrence rates. It is imperative to consider multiple factors when selecting specific Chinese patent medicines, including robust evidence from high-quality research, the patient's condition and status, and the clinical experience of healthcare practitioners.

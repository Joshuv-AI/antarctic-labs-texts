A novel prognostic model based on endoplasmic reticulum stress-associated E3 ligases and deubiquitinating enzymes in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Caixia Zhong, Yurui Liu, Kaishun Hu, Dong Yin
Published: 2025

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is classified as one of the leading malignant neoplasms worldwide, experiencing a global increase in its incidence (1). Despite substantial progress in diagnostic techniques and treatment strategies for HCC, the prognosis for patients, particularly those with advanced-stage HCC, remains unfavorable (2). Consequently, there exists a compelling requirement for innovative diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers that can significantly improve the therapeutic approaches and disease management for HCC patients.

Endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) is a cellular response mechanism to protein misfolding and stress, with the unfolded protein response being its central pathway (3). The unfolded protein response, mediated by signaling pathways like IRE1α, ATF6, and PERK, aims to restore endoplasmic reticulum homeostasis (4). However, chronic ERS can promote cancer cell proliferation, metastasis, and resistance to therapy, making it a potential therapeutic target (5,6). ERS also influences the epithelial-mesenchymal transition in cancer cells, a process critical for tumor progression and metastasis (7-9).

Ubiquitination, a complex post-translational modification process, is essential for maintaining protein homeostasis and regulating various cellular processes (10). The dysregulation of ubiquitination, particularly through E3 ubiquitin ligases (E3s) and deubiquitinating enzymes (DUBs), has been implicated in cancer development (11-13). Recent studies suggest a pivotal role for the ubiquitin-proteasome system in ERS, with specific E3s and DUBs being activated during cellular stress (14,15). However, the complete spectrum of E3s and DUBs involved in ERS remains to be fully understood.

In this study, we aimed to identify E3s and DUBs associated with ERS in HCC and construct a prognostic risk model for the disease. By analyzing transcriptomic data from various tumors, we discovered a set of E3s and DUBs upregulated during ERS, which were linked to unfavorable outcomes in liver cancer. Utilizing least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and Cox regression analysis, we developed a predictive risk model for HCC based on the expression profiles of these ERS-E3s/DUBs. Survival analysis within an independent patient cohort confirmed the model’s predictive power. Our findings not only shed light on the role of ERS-E3s/DUBs in HCC progression but also offer novel biomarkers for prognostic evaluation. This research may pave the way for new targeted therapies and improve the prognosis for HCC patients. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2024-2403/rc).

Methods

Cell lines and drug sources

SW480, SCC15, and HepG2 cell lines were procured from the American Type Culture Collection and propagated in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and a penicillin-streptomycin mixture, within an incubation environment at 37 °C and a 5% CO2 atmosphere. Thapsigargin (inducer of ERS) was sourced from Med Chem Express (Shanghai, China) and reconstituted in dimethyl sulfoxide.

Quantitative analysis of mRNA in culture cells

SW480, SCC15 and HepG2 cells were treated with thapsigargin (1 µM) for 12 hours. Total RNAs from the cells were extracted using TRIzol reagent (EZBioscience, EZBioscience, Roseville, MN, USA) and subsequently synthesized into complementary DNA (cDNA) using a transcription reverse kit (EZBioscience). Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) was performed using a quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction Kit (EZBioscience) and a CFX96 Real-Time System (Bio-Rad, USA). The relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the delta-delta Ct method, with β-actin as the endogenous control. The primer sequences are provided in Table S1.

Acquiring mRNA expression data and clinical details from databases

Patient mRNA expression profiles and corresponding clinical datasets were derived from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) repository (https://cancergenome.nih.gov/). Concurrently, RNA sequencing data for cell lines were procured from the Gene Expression Omnibus archive (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

RNA-seq data processing

The analysis of differential expression genes was performed using the R package DESeq2 v1.16.1. Differential expression genes were defined as follows, |log2FoldChange| >0 and false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05, unless otherwise indicated. Volcano plots were constructed using the ggplot2 R package (version 3.5.1).

Development of a risk assessment model for determining the risk score

Commencing with a univariate analysis, we proceeded to perform LASSO regression with a 10-fold cross-validation at a significance level of 0.05. This regression was iterated 1,000 times, with each iteration comprising 1,000 random simulations. We then tallied the frequency of each variable combination across these iterations, retaining those that occurred more than 100 times for subsequent Cox proportional hazards regression analysis and model construction. Moreover, the area under the curve (AUC) values for each model were determined and graphically represented. If the graphical representation reached its peak, indicating the maximum achievable AUC value, the computational process was terminated, considering the model as the optimal choice. Subsequently, the 1-, 3-, and 5-year receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for the model were produced. The following equation was used to calculate the risk score using the established risk model across all clinical cases: risk score = Σ (coefficient of mRNA × expression of mRNA). The Akaike Information Criterion values at each point on the 1-year ROC curve were evaluated to identify the maximum inflection point, which was then considered as the threshold for distinguishing between high- and low-risk categories of risks cores.

Survival analysis stratified by low and high-risk scores

Survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method, and the log-rank test was employed to evaluate the difference in overall survival between the high-risk and low-risk groups. The survival analysis was performed using the R packages “survival” and “survminer”. A risk score curve was generated based on the risk scores of each patient.

Verification of risk score through univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses

Pathological characteristics and risk scores were subjected to both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression to ascertain the risk score’s role as an independent predictor of overall survival. The factor was deemed an independent risk factor with a P value below 0.05.

Establishment of risk prediction model

We created a nomogram that combines risk scores and clinical information to predict the overall survival rates at 1-, 3-, and 5-year for patients with HCC. We used the R package “rms” to generate this nomogram. To evaluate the discriminative ability of the nomogram, we employed the concordance index (C-index) and calculated it using bootstrapping with 1,000 resamples.

Gene Ontology (GO) analyses by Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)

We downloaded the GSEA (v4.0.3) for Windows system from the GSEA website (http://software.broadinstitute.org/gsea/index.jsp), as well as the gene set databases c5.bp.v7.1.symbols.gmt. We used the GSEA for conducting GO enrichment analyses. We selected c5.bp.v7.1.symbols.gmt as the GMT files for GO, respectively. We prepared the CLS files for the high- and low-risk groups. Meanwhile, we prepared the GCT file from the mRNA expression dataset of HCC obtained from the TCGA repository, specifying 1,000 permutations for the analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0.2. Data are expressed as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). A two-tailed Student’s t-test was employed for comparisons between two groups. Survival data were depicted using Kaplan-Meier curves, and statistical significance was determined via the log-rank test. A two-sided P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The following notations were used to indicate significance levels: ns, non-significant; *P<0.05; **P<0.01; ***P<0.001.

Results

Identifying ERS-E3s/DUBs

Recent studies have demonstrated that the ubiquitin-proteasome system plays a crucial role in ERS. To further explore the mechanisms by which ERS impacts the ubiquitin-proteasome system, we analyzed transcriptomic data from five cell lines where ERS was induced, including Caco-2 (colonic adenocarcinoma cells), HK-2 (renal tubular epithelial cells), AsPC-1 (pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma cells), HeLa (cervical squamous cell carcinoma cells), and HEK293T (kidney epithelial cells). After differential expression analysis of E3s and DUBs for each dataset, we identified four DUBs and 19 E3s that were upregulated in at least four datasets (Figure 1A,1B), which we subsequently referred to as ERS-E3s/DUBs. To further validate the identified ERS-E3s/DUBs, we treated HepG2 cells with thapsigargin for 12 hours. The transcription levels of four key ERS genes and 23 ERS-E3s/DUBs were significantly upregulated (Figure 1C). All the data presented above indicate that we have successfully identified 24 ERS-E3s/DUBs genes.

Construction of a prognostic risk model for HCC by ERS-E3s/DUBs

We conducted a pan-cancer survival analysis of ERS-E3s/DUBs using the TCGA database, which revealed significant heterogeneity in the prognostic relevance of these genes across various cancers. Notably, in liver cancer [liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC)], 21.74% (5/23) of ERS-E3s/DUBs were associated with poor tumor prognosis (Figure 1D). These findings prompted us to further investigate the potential of constructing a prognostic risk model for HCC using ERS-E3s/DUBs genes. We applied the LASSO-Cox regression analysis method to the TCGA-LIHC cohort data, which led to the identification of a prognostic risk model consisting of nine ERS-E3s/DUBs (Figure 2A). Subsequently, we evaluated the relationship between nine ERS-E3s/DUBs and HCC prognosis using univariate logistic regression analysis (Figure 2B). Among them, the expression of the SIAH2 gene was positively correlated with HCC prognosis; while the expressions of CGRRF1, GZF1, and USP1 genes were negatively correlated with HCC prognosis.

To validate the clinical significance of the ERS-E3s/DUBs-based prognostic risk model, we constructed ROC curves for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival. The AUC values were all greater than 0.5 in both the training set (Figure 2C) and the test set (Figure 2D), indicating good predictive performance of the model. Survival curve analysis revealed that patients in the high-risk group had significantly lower survival probabilities compared to those in the low-risk group, a trend observed in both the training set (Figure 2E) and the test set (Figure 2F).

Developing a nomogram

To ascertain that the model remained unaffected by additional clinical variables, we employed multivariate Cox regression analyses to establish independent prognostic factors in liver cancer patients, yielding consistent outcomes. The risk score, age, and tumor stage emerged as significant, independent predictors of prognosis (Figure 3A). Consequently, the risk score derived from the signature performs adequately in comparison to established clinical predictors. To enhance the prognostic accuracy for liver cancer patients, age and tumor stage were integrated with the risk score to construct a nomogram model (Figure 3B). The nomogram provides a graphical depiction of the correlation between patient age, tumor stage, risk score, and survival status at specific time intervals. Utilizing a combination of age, tumor stage, and risk score, we calculated the total scores, which enabled us to forecast the survival probabilities of patients at 1-, 3-, and 5-year. The validation plots at 1-, 3-, and 5-year time points, which compared the observed survival rates against those predicted by the nomogram, revealed minimal discrepancies (Figure 3C), highlighting the nomogram’s robust predictive accuracy and its potential clinical applicability.

GO analyses of high-risk patient genes using GSEA

To investigate the underlying mechanism of the pathological progression of HCC, GO analyses were conducted through GSEA utilizing the training dataset. The outcomes of the GO analysis revealed that genes associated with high-risk patients were predominantly involved in processes such as heterochromatin formation, positive regulation of mRNA catabolic process, protein acetylation and double strand break repair (Figure 4A). In contrast, genes linked to low-risk patients were primarily associated with pathways including reverse cholesterol transport, amino acid catabolic, retinol metabolic, branched chain amino acid catabolic, short chain fatty acid metabolic and fatty acid beta oxidation (Figure 4B). Dysregulation of these pathways may underlie the biology of high-risk HCC and may represent new targets for therapeutic intervention.

Discussion

Currently, the prognosis prediction for HCC relies on histopathological parameters and tumor staging systems. However, these traditional methods are insufficient for accurately predicting the clinical outcomes of HCC patients. There is an urgent need to improve early detection and prognosis of liver cancer, as well as to assist clinicians in optimizing treatment strategies (16). In recent years, genomic sequencing technologies and bioinformatics analyses have been widely applied in the study of different types of tumors, playing an increasingly significant role (17). Numerous studies have identified characteristic genes associated with liver cancer prognosis by analyzing differential genes in liver cancer tissues and adjacent tissues, thereby constructing risk prediction models (18,19). In this study, we systematically identified 23 ERS-E3s/DUBs and constructed a liver cancer prognostic risk model based on nine ERS-E3s/DUBs. Furthermore, we developed a new nomogram that combines risk characteristics and clinical pathological features, which provides good predictive performance for the clinical prognosis of HCC patients.

The GSEA results indicate that the upregulated genes in high-risk HCC patients are primarily involved in the regulation of lipid biosynthesis and DNA damage repair. The LASSO Cox regression model included nine prognosis-related ERS-E3s/DUBs genes, including CGRRF1, GZF1, SYVN1, ZFPL1, USP1, NFXL1, SIAH2, PJA2, and TRIM27. The functions and pathways associated with these genes correspond to the GSEA findings. For instance, USP1 enhances the expression of C/EBPβ protein, which promotes the transcription of adipogenic genes, leading to adipogenesis and lipid accumulation (20); both USP1 and SIAH2 can promote DNA damage repair by facilitating homologous recombination repair (21,22). Univariate regression analysis revealed that the expression of the SIAH2 gene was positively correlated with HCC prognosis, while the expressions of CGRRF1, GZF1, and USP1 genes were negatively correlated with HCC prognosis. Existing studies have shown that SIAH2 inhibits HCC cell proliferation, while USP1 promotes HCC cell proliferation. The mechanisms of action of CGRRF1 and GZF1 in HCC are still unclear. In the future, we will conduct further experiments to explore the roles of CGRRF1 and GZF1 in the pathogenesis, prognosis, and treatment of HCC.

Conclusions

Our study successfully develops a novel prognostic model for hepatocellular carcinoma that incorporates ERS-E3s/DUBs, offering a significant tool for predicting patient prognosis and potentially guiding personalized therapeutic strategies.

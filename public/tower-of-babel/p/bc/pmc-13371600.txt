Comparative safety and efficacy of hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy combined with targeted therapy and immunotherapy, with or without transarterial chemoembolization, in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma: a multicenter retrospective cohort study
Authors: Ruibing Zhou, Ze Zhang, Xiaoxv Yin, Yi Zhang, Weipeng Yan, Anhui Xu, Ketao Mu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInterventional therapies and systemic chemotherapy are the mainstay of treatment for patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). This study aimed to generate preliminary evidence regarding the feasibility and safety of transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) combined with hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy (HAIC), targeted therapy, and immunotherapy in patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed 213 patients with unresectable HCC treated with TACE plus HAIC, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy (THTI) or HAIC plus targeted therapy and immunotherapy (HTI) between July 2019 and August 2023. Propensity score matching (PSM) was used to balance potential confounders between patients in the THTI and HTI groups. Overall survival (OS) was assessed using Kaplan-Meier analysis, and the log-rank test was used for between-group comparisons. Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to identify independent influences on patient OS.ResultsA total of 213 patients were treated with THTI (n = 41) or HTI (n = 172). After 1:2 PSM, there were 31 patients in the THTI and 62 patients in the HTI groups. After PSM, OS was significantly better in the THTI than in the HTI groups (34.4 vs. 16.3 months, P = 0.013). The objective response rate (ORR) (38.71% vs. 45.16%, P = 0.554) and disease control rate (DCR) (80.65% vs. 79.03%, P = 0.856) were not statistically significant in the THTI and HTI groups. THTI treatment is an independent predictor of OS. All adverse events (AEs) in the THTI and HTI groups were manageable.ConclusionCompared with HTI, THTI was associated with improved OS in patients with unresectable HCC, with manageable short-term AEs. Quadruple therapy showed encouraging antitumor activity in this retrospective cohort and should be considered exploratory, further large-scale prospective studies are required.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-026-04952-w.Keywords: Unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma, Transarterial chemoembolization, Hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy, Targeted therapy, Immunotherapy

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most common pathological type of primary liver cancer, accounting for approximately 75–85% of all cases [1]. According to Global Cancer Statistics 2022, liver cancer ranks as the sixth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. HCC is characterized by its insidious onset and rapid progression, resulting in only 9–27% of patients meeting the criteria for surgical resection at diagnosis [2]. In the absence of effective early diagnosis, the average survival of patients from diagnosis to death is only 3–6 months [3, 4].

Targeted therapy and immunotherapy as systemic treatment modalities have significantly improved survival rates for patients with unresectable HCC and have been established as first-line treatment options [5, 6]. However, despite initial treatment responses, a considerable number of patients experience primary or acquired resistance, which ultimately leads to disease progression. This therapeutic limitation is primarily attributed to the intrinsic heterogeneity of HCC, including spatial and temporal variations in tumor biology, immunity within the microenvironment, and vascular supply [7, 8]. Such heterogeneity allows certain subclonal populations to withstand selective pressures, contributing to treatment failure and recurrence. Consequently, overcoming this resistance remains a significant challenge in clinical management.

Locoregional therapies, such as transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) and hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy (HAIC), have also been widely used in patients with intermediate or advanced HCC, particularly in Asian clinical practice [9, 10]. Research suggests that integrating these modalities with systemic therapies can enhance tumor control through synergistic mechanisms: locoregional treatments can induce tumor cell death and antigen release, potentially increasing immune recognition, while targeted therapies and immune modulators can suppress systemic dissemination and alter the post-procedural microenvironment [11, 12]. In addition, several recent studies have further highlighted the growing clinical interest in combining locoregional interventions with systemic therapy for unresectable HCC, supporting the rationale for intensified multimodal treatment strategies [13–15]. On this basis, multimodal treatment strategies have been increasingly explored in real-world practice. However, the combined or sequential use of TACE and HAIC remains clinically controversial due to overlapping risks to liver function and hepatic vasculature, especially in patient with unresectable HCC, warranting careful evaluation before broader application, Therefore, observational studies need to be conducted in the real world [5, 12].

Given this background, the present multicenter retrospective study was designed to explore, in a real-world setting, whether the addition of TACE to HAIC plus targeted therapy and immunotherapy is feasible and tolerable, and whether it is associated with signals of clinical benefit that would justify further prospective investigation. Using propensity score matching (PSM), we compared transarterial chemoembolization plus hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy (THTI) with hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy plus targeted therapy and immunotherapy (HTI) in patients with unresectable HCC. Rather than providing confirmatory evidence for routine practice, this study should be interpreted as a safety-focused, hypothesis-generating analysis and as an initial assessment of the safety and practical applicability of an intensified multimodal strategy outside current guideline recommendations.

Methods

Study design

This multicenter retrospective cohort study included patients with unresectable HCC from July 2019 to August 2023 at 4 hospitals in Wuhan, China. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethical Review Committee of Tongji Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (approval number: TJ-IRB20230950). Informed consent for patients was waived by the ethics committee since the study adopted a retrospective design.

This study enrolled patients diagnosed with unresectable HCC who were treated with either transarterial chemoembolization plus hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy (THTI) or hepatic artery infusion chemotherapy plus targeted therapy and immunotherapy (HTI). The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: (1) pathologically confirmed diagnosis of unresectable HCC; (2) age ≥ 18 years; (3) THTI or HTI as initial treatment; (4) At least one measurable intrahepatic tumor detected on computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI); (5) Child-Pugh A or B class liver function. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 2 or higher; (2) history of malignancies other than HCC; (3) incomplete medical records; (4) loss to follow-up. The treatment strategy for patients in this study was discussed in a multidisciplinary treatment (MDT) meeting. Physicians in MDT recommended either HTI alone or TACE plus HTI for patients and informed them about the benefits and potential risks of each treatment option. The final treatment decision was determined and consented to by the patient.

HAIC procedure

The HAIC protocol employed in this study was based on the mFOLFOX regimen and was performed through the following procedure: Initially, percutaneous access was established through the right femoral artery using the Seldinger technique, followed by the placement of a catheter sheath and subsequent selective catheterization of the hepatic artery. Subsequently, under the guidance of digital subtraction angiography (DSA), a microcatheter was precisely navigated to the tumor-feeding arteries. Finally, the microcatheter was connected to an arterial infusion pump for continuous HAIC, which consisted of oxaliplatin (85–130 mg/m2 for 2 hours); leucovorin (200–400 mg/m2 for 46 h) and 5-fluorouracil (2400 mg/m2 for 46 h), with the complete treatment cycle lasting 48 h.

TACE procedure

The TACE procedure was performed according to the following protocol: First, a high-pressure angiogram was conducted under DSA fluoroscopy to precisely identify the tumor-feeding arteries. Subsequently, chemotherapeutic agents and embolic materials were selectively administered through the hepatic artery. The chemotherapeutic and embolic regimen comprised an emulsion of ethiodized poppyseed oil (Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China) and pirarubicin hydrochloride (THP; Shenzhen Main Luck Pharmaceuticals Inc., Shenzhen, China), or drug-eluting microspheres (CalliSpheres®; Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co. Ltd., Jiangsu, China) preloaded with pirarubicin hydrochloride (THP; Shenzhen Main Luck Pharmaceuticals Inc., Shenzhen, China). The TACE procedure was considered complete when post-embolization angiography demonstrated significant reduction or cessation of tumor blood flow, followed by removal of the right femoral artery sheath. The details of TACE treatment could be found in the previously published article [16].

Targeted therapies and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)

Targeted therapies and ICIs were initiated within 7 days following the initial intervention and continued until unacceptable toxicity or disease progression occurred. The details of the medications used in the two groups are shown in Table S1 and S2. The targeted therapies regimen included lenvatinib (8 mg orally once daily), apatinib (500 mg orally once daily), donafenib (200 mg orally twice daily), and bevacizumab (15 mg/kg intravenously every 3 weeks, adjusted for body weight). The ICIs utilized in this study comprised atezolizumab, sintilimab, tislelizumab, camrelizumab, toripalimab, and pembrolizumab, all administered intravenously at a fixed dose of 200 mg every 3 weeks.

Follow-up and assessments

Follow-up protocols included the collection of routine screening indices at one month after treatment, CT or MRI at three months after treatment, and patient survival data obtained through telephone follow-up, with a cut-off date of October 19, 2024. The primary endpoint of this study was overall survival (OS), defined as the interval from treatment initiation to death from any cause. The secondary endpoints were objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and AEs. ORR was defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete or partial response, while DCR included patients with complete or partial response or stable disease. Tumor response was assessed by two radiologists with over 10 years of experience using the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) [17]. AEs were graded within one month of initial treatment according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 [18] .

Statistical analysis

To reduce potential selection bias and confounding between the two groups, PSM was performed using a 1:2 optimal matching approach. The propensity score model included tumor distribution, Child-Pugh class, BCLC stage, tumor size, tumor number, portal vein invasion, AFP, age, ECOG PS, and baseline liver function markers as matching covariates. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas categorical variables were reported as frequency and percentage. Differences in continuous variables between the two groups were analyzed using Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test, while categorical variables were compared using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, depending on the data distribution. OS was evaluated using Kaplan–Meier curves, with comparisons between groups conducted by the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to identify independent predictors of survival. To evaluate the robustness of the observed association to potential unmeasured confounding, we performed an E-value analysis as a sensitivity analysis. Subgroups of clinical value were analyzed to determine the benefit of different treatment modalities in different subgroups. All statistical analyses were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data were processed using R version 4.3.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing) and SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Corp).

Efficacy

During the follow-up period, 120 patients (56.3%) in the overall cohort died, with 23 deaths (56.1%) in the THTI group and 97 deaths (56.4%) in the HTI group. After PSM, 35 patients (56.4%) in the HTI group died during follow-up. Before PSM, the median OS was 33.4 (95% CI: 29.0-38.4) months in the THTI group and 16.3 (95% CI:11.3–21.3) months in the HTI group. After PSM, the median OS in the HTI group was 16.3 (95% CI: 8.6–24.0) months. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis showed that OS differed significantly between the two groups both before and after PSM (both P < 0.05) (Figure S1) (Fig. 2). Before PSM, the Cox proportional hazards regression analysis showed that THTI (HR 0.35; 95% CI: 0.21–0.57; P < 0.001), Charlson index (HR 1.18; 95% CI: 1.04–1.33; P = 0.009), hepatitis B virus (HBV) (HR 3.17; 95% CI: 1.27–7.94; P = 0.013), and largest tumor diameter (HR 1.06; 95% CI: 1.01–1.10; P = 0.009) as independent prognostic factors for OS (Table S4). After PSM, the Cox regression model demonstrated that THTI (HR 0.40; 95% CI: 0.21–0.76; P = 0.005) is the independent predictors of OS (Table 2). The association between THTI and longer overall survival remained moderately robust to potential unmeasured confounding, with an E-value of 4.49 for the point estimate and 1.96 for the confidence limit closest to the null.

Safety

The AEs in the two groups are listed in Table 4 and Table S6. Before PSM, A total of 213 patients in the THTI group (n = 41) and the HTI group (n = 172) were assessed for AEs. Grade 3 AEs were observed 14 times in the THTI group and 8 times in the HTI group, with all other AEs being grade 2 or lower in severity. Increased ALT, increased AST, and hypoalbuminemia were significantly more frequent in the THTI group compared to the HTI group (P < 0.05) (Table S6). After PSM, Grade 3 AEs were observed 3 times in the HTI group and 9 times in the THTI group. ALT and hypoalbuminemia levels remained higher in the THTI group than in the HTI group (P < 0.05) (Table 4).

Discussion

The quadruple regimen evaluated in this study is not currently recommended by major clinical guidelines for unresectable HCC. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted cautiously as exploratory real-world evidence rather than as a basis for changing clinical practice. This study is the first to compare the efficacy and safety of THTI versus HTI in patients with unresectable HCC. After PSM, the THTI group exhibited significantly longer median OS than the HTI group (34.4 vs. 16.3 months, P = 0.013). However, there were no statistically significant differences in the ORR or DCR between the two groups after matching. In terms of safety, patients in the THTI group had significantly higher ALT and hypoalbuminemia levels than those in the HTI group after PSM (P < 0.05).

Before PSM, the THTI group already showed a statistically significant OS advantage over the HTI group. Notably, this difference was observed despite the fact that the THTI group had a significantly higher proportion of patients with bilobar liver involvement than the HTI group (73.17% vs. 49.42%, P = 0.010), suggesting a heavier baseline intrahepatic tumor burden in the THTI group. Given that bilobar involvement and greater tumor burden are recognized adverse prognostic factors in HCC, this baseline imbalance would be expected to bias against, rather than favor, the THTI group [19]. After PSM, the survival advantage of the THTI group remained statistically significant, indicating that the observed OS benefit persisted after adjustment for measured baseline confounders using a method specifically designed to improve comparability in observational studies [20]. These findings are broadly consistent with previous studies showing that intensification of locoregional treatment on top of systemic therapy may improve survival in selected patients with unresectable HCC. For example, Huang et al. reported that TACE combined with HAIC, PD-1 inhibitors, and tyrosine kinase inhibitors was associated with better survival outcomes than TACE combined with PD-1 inhibitors and tyrosine kinase inhibitors alone [21]. Similarly, a recent systematic review and meta-analysis by Fang et al. suggested that adding transarterial chemo(embolization) to immune-targeted therapy could improve long-term efficacy in unresectable HCC [22]. A possible explanation is that the addition of TACE may enhance intrahepatic tumor control and may also act synergistically with HAIC, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy through locoregional tumor debulking, modulation of the tumor microenvironment, and increased tumor antigen release [12]. Therefore, our findings further support the potential clinical value of intensified multimodal therapy in selected patients, although confirmation in larger prospective studies remains necessary.

The discrepancy between improved OS and similar ORR/DCR also warrants cautious interpretation. ORR and DCR are radiologic endpoints that mainly reflect early tumor shrinkage or disease stabilization and may not fully capture durable disease control or delayed benefit from immunotherapy-based strategies [23, 24]. In addition, improved intrahepatic tumor control may contribute to prolonged survival without necessarily translating into a higher initial radiologic response rate, particularly in HCC, where survival is closely related to both control of intrahepatic disease and preservation of liver function [12, 24–26]. However, because this was a retrospective study, detailed information on post-progression treatment, treatment sequencing, and treatment intervals was not comprehensively available. Therefore, the potential influence of subsequent therapies, follow-up intensity, supportive care, residual confounding, and time-related bias on OS cannot be excluded. Future prospective studies with standardized follow-up and detailed recording of the treatment course are needed to clarify these issues. Any biological explanation for the observed survival difference remains speculative in the absence of translational data. It is plausible that combining locoregional therapy with targeted therapy and ICIs may enhance disease control through complementary local and systemic effects, but this hypothesis could not be directly tested in the present study. Therefore, the current findings should be interpreted primarily from a clinical rather than mechanistic perspective.

The safety analysis revealed that after PSM, the THTI group exhibited both a higher incidence of grade 3 AEs and significantly elevated ALT and hypoalbuminemia levels compared to the HTI group (P < 0.05). These findings likely reflect the additional hepatic burden imposed by TACE. Specifically, TACE induces transient ischemia and localized cytotoxicity in non-tumorous liver tissue, leading to hepatocyte injury and subsequent ALT elevation [27]. Concurrently, hypoalbuminemia may result from impaired hepatic synthetic function due to ischemic stress and inflammatory responses, particularly in patients with underlying cirrhosis or limited hepatic reserve [28]. Despite these biochemical changes, all adverse events in both groups were clinically manageable, with no grade 4 or 5 events observed. These findings suggest that short-term adverse events were clinically manageable in this cohort, but the long-term hepatic safety of this quadruple regimen remains uncertain.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective study, it was inevitably subject to selection bias, although PSM was used to reduce baseline imbalance between groups. Second, heterogeneity in therapeutic regimens, including variations in targeted therapies and immunotherapeutic agents, may have influenced treatment response assessments and limits the interpretability of the present findings. Because different targeted agents and different ICIs may differ in mechanism, toxicity, and efficacy when combined with locoregional therapy, these treatments should not be assumed to be fully equivalent. Due to the limited sample size, especially after PSM, further stratified or sensitivity analyses according to targeted agent class or ICI type were not feasible. Third, data on post-progression treatment were not comprehensively available, which may have affected survival in both patient groups and may also explain the observed discordance between OS and radiologic response outcomes. Fourth, longitudinal liver function evaluation was not feasible because post-treatment liver function data were not collected at standardized time points and repeated measurements were incomplete, precluding a robust analysis of serial ALT/AST changes and their prognostic significance. Fifth, the AE observation window was short, limiting the assessment of long-term safety, such as immune-related adverse events (irAEs), cumulative hepatotoxicity, or late hepatic decompensation. In addition, we did not systematically track treatment discontinuation or dose reduction attributable to toxicity, which may have limited the evaluation of regimen tolerability. Therefore, large-scale prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings.

Conclusion

This multicenter retrospective cohort study demonstrated that THTI was associated with improved OS in patients with unresectable HCC, Quadruple therapy showed encouraging antitumor activity in this retrospective cohort. However, the safety findings should be interpreted cautiously because of the small sample size, short AE observation window, and retrospective design; in particular, the long-term risk of hepatic decompensation remains unknown. Therefore, these results should be regarded as exploratory, and further large-scale prospective studies are required.

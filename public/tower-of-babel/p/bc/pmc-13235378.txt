Benchmark method for selecting FFT windows in harmonic measurements
Authors: D.C. Pham, Tuan Ngoc Anh Nguyen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The paper presents a reproducible and measurement-oriented benchmark for selecting existing FFT windows in harmonic measurements under short-duration conditions. Candidate windows are assessed using amplitude-related error, THD deviation, and cross-case consistency. Their overall performance is then summarized through a direct quantitative ranking. Validation is carried out using APS1000-based records under waveform-varying, off-nominal, and noise-contaminated conditions. The main contributions of this work are as follows:•A measurement-oriented performance formulation is established for FFT-window evaluation using harmonic-related error, THD deviation, and cross-case consistency as the main comparison criteria.•A direct quantitative window-selection rule is proposed by combining these criteria into an aggregate score, from which the most suitable window is identified under the considered measurement conditions.•The proposed method is implemented in MATLAB and validated using APS1000-based records, including noise-augmented cases generated in MATLAB, under representative harmonic-measurement conditions.

Background

Harmonic and THD estimation from finite-length records is affected by spectral leakage. This effect can distort harmonic amplitudes and bias the final THD result. Early work by Harris [1] clarified the main trade-offs in FFT windowing, especially those related to frequency resolution, leakage suppression, and amplitude fidelity. Later studies moved this discussion closer to harmonic analysis. De la O Serna [2] examined amplitude-shaping windows, and Reljin et al. [3] proposed extremely flat-top windows for improved harmonic-amplitude estimation. Other studies further discussed harmonic analysis frameworks [4] and optimized window designs for harmonic-analysis applications [5]. Among these contributions, the variable semi-sinusoidal window (VSSW) is especially relevant here because it was developed for programmable AC power sources [6].

More recent studies have considered conditions beyond fixed and ideal cases. Tang et al. [7] addressed time-varying harmonic analysis using adaptive frequency-shift filtering. Yu et al. [8] further developed this direction through a double frequency-shift filtering method for power-system applications. Arranz-Gimón et al. [9] reported a harmonic measurement and analysis system for frequency-inverter outputs. Kitzig and Bumiller [10] also highlighted the importance of clear quantitative metrics when signals are analyzed under limited frequency resolution. Window-related studies have also continued in other signal-processing applications, including modal parameter identification [11] and ECG preprocessing [12]. Taken together, these studies show that both algorithm design and window selection remain relevant when accurate FFT-based spectral estimation is required.

IEC 61,000-4-7 provides the general framework for harmonic and interharmonic measurements in power systems [13]. However, it does not specify a single FFT window for all conditions. In short-duration measurements, off-nominal frequency, waveform distortion, and noise can cause different windows to produce different harmonic amplitudes and THD values. A practical need therefore remains for a consistent way to compare existing FFT windows under the same acquisition and processing conditions. The main question is which existing FFT window provides the most suitable compromise between harmonic-spectrum accuracy and THD accuracy under practical short-duration conditions.

The contribution of this study is centered on the benchmarking methodology and selection protocol, rather than in the development of a new FFT algorithm or a new window function. Its purpose is to provide a reproducible protocol for evaluating existing FFT windows under identical acquisition and processing conditions. The final window choice is based on harmonic-measurement performance rather than on spectral descriptors alone.

The following section presents the benchmark formulation, the performance indices, and the step-by-step procedure used for direct quantitative window selection.

Method details

This section presents the benchmark formulation used for FFT-window selection in harmonic measurements. The formulation proceeds from problem definition to final window ranking. First, the candidate windows and the validation dataset are defined. Next, a common reference framework is established through the reference fundamental frequency and the corresponding harmonic frequencies. Harmonic amplitudes and THD are then computed for each candidate window and compared with the reference values. Robustness is assessed across the validation set through cross-case consistency. The resulting measures are then aggregated, normalized, and combined into a single score for direct window ranking and selection.

Method protocol

The benchmark procedure is summarized in Algorithm 1. For each validation record, the signal is first preprocessed, and the reference fundamental frequency is estimated by fine-resolution Exact-DFT scanning. The reference harmonic amplitudes and THD are then computed from the same record. Next, each candidate window is applied to obtain the corresponding harmonic amplitudes, THD values, and case-wise error measures. After all records have been processed, the global indicators are calculated for each window, normalized, and combined into the aggregate score. The optimal window is finally selected as the candidate with the lowest overall score.Algorithm 1Proposed benchmark for FFT-window selection.Algorithm 1 dummy alt textInput: W, S, fs, Hmax, α, β, γOutput: W*1. for each xk ∈ S do2. preprocess xk3. estimate f1,ref(xk)4. compute Ah,ref(xk) and THDref(xk)5. for each Wm ∈ W do6. compute Ah(Wm, xk)7. compute THD(Wm, xk)8. compute EA(Wm, xk) and ETHD(Wm, xk)9. end10. end11. for each Wm ∈ W do12. compute A(Wm), T(Wm), and C(Wm)13. normalize A(Wm), T(Wm), and C(Wm)14. compute J(Wm)15. end16. return W* = arg min J(Wm)

For the final benchmark ranking, equal weights were assigned to the three global criteria (α=β=γ=1/3), so that no single criterion was favored a priori. The resulting ranking therefore represents a balanced comparison across harmonic-amplitude error, absolute THD deviation, and cross-case robustness.

Appendix Table A.1 shows that the pairwise post-hoc results differ clearly between the two analysis sets. For the overall validation cases, none of the pairwise differences in absolute THD deviation remains statistically significant after Holm correction, with adjusted p-values between 0.1031 and 0.1213, despite positive median paired differences from 0.0300 to 0.2001. This suggests a cautious interpretation of the global THD-based ranking at the pairwise level. In contrast, for the repeated-noise realizations, all pairwise comparisons remain significant after Holm correction, with adjusted p-values ranging from 1.152 × 10^−16 to 0.01335. In this analysis, Flat-Top is consistently preferred, including over VSSW, indicating a much clearer pairwise separation among windows under repeated-noise realizations.

Taken together, Table 2; Table 3, and Appendix Table A.1 provide a substantially strengthened statistical analysis of the proposed benchmark. The Friedman results and Kendall’s W values establish the overall inferential structure across different validation subsets, while the post-hoc Wilcoxon-Holm comparisons clarify which pairwise differences remain statistically supported after multiple-comparison adjustment.

Limitations

The proposed benchmark has several practical limitations. The evaluation is based on the precision of the APS1000-based setup under controlled conditions. Its accuracy therefore depends on the stability of the experimental setup and the resolution of the measurement system. The present validation is limited to controlled laboratory records. It mainly covers stationary sinusoidal and triangular waveforms, with greater emphasis on off-nominal sinusoidal cases. Non-stationary signals and complex industrial transients were not included. More detailed analyses under noisy conditions were also beyond the present scope.

This study did not involve human participants, animals, biological material, or identifiable personal data.

Conclusions

This study establishes a reproducible benchmark for FFT-window selection in harmonic measurements. By integrating harmonic-amplitude error, THD deviation, and cross-case consistency into a normalized aggregate score, the method transitions window selection from qualitative observation to a rigorous quantitative decision-making framework.

The validation using 26 the scenarios leads to the following conclusions:•Optimal Performance: Under balanced weighting, the Flat-Top window provides the most favorable trade-off, achieving the lowest overall score of 0.3333 with a minimal THD deviation of 0.9731 pp and superior robustness 0.8686.•Selection Paradox: The Hann window, while superior in harmonic-amplitude accuracy 56.23%, yields the largest THD error and poorest stability. This confirms that window selection is strictly objective-dependent.•Statistical Validity: Friedman tests and post-hoc Holm-corrected comparisons confirm that window-dependent impacts are most significant under off-bin and noise-contaminated conditions.

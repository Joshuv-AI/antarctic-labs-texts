P2D-former for time-series forecasting with period-aware 2D representations and exogenous covariates
Authors: Hu Hong, Guorong Chen, Wang Jian, Haixin Yi, Zhang Bao, Weijie Zhang, Zhikang Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Time series forecasting finds extensive application across numerous real-world scenarios, yet faces challenges in modelling multi-period variability and exogenous variables. To address this, this paper proposes P2D-Former, a patch-based Transformer model integrating period-aware 2D structures with exogenous information fusion. This approach incorporates MSUnit and P2D-FFN to model complex periodic structures, while explicitly integrating exogenous variable information through an Intersect-Attention mechanism. Experiments demonstrate that P2D-Former achieves superior performance across multiple benchmark datasets for long-term forecasting, short-term forecasting, and high-missing-value imputation tasks, exhibiting significant improvements in both accuracy and robustness compared to existing approaches.

Introduction

Time series forecasting plays a pivotal role in diverse sectors, ranging from financial market analysis1–3 and economic monitoring4,5 to complex industrial applications.Time series forecasting is in urgent demand across real-world scenarios and has found widespread application in meteorology6, power systems7, transportation8, and other domains9. However, forecasting in these complex environments is frequently hindered by inherent data challenges, such as severe non-stationarity, complex noise, missing values, and shifting seasonality10,11.Among these, forecasting incorporating exogenous variables represents a prevalent and indispensable paradigm, as changes within time series are frequently influenced by external factors. For instance, electricity prices exhibit high dependence on market supply and demand, rendering it fundamentally impossible to predict future prices based solely on historical data12. Incorporating external factors as exogenous variables enables a more comprehensive understanding of correlations and predictive associations between variables. This not only enhances forecasting performance but also aligns with the growing need for trustworthy and explainable AI in time-series analysis13,14, where understanding the driving factors is as critical as accuracy.15,16.

From a time series modelling perspective, exogenous variables are introduced into the predictor to provide information without being predicted themselves. This contrasts with existing multivariate forecasting methods, which often treat all variables equally17,18. For instance, in multivariate approaches, RNNs (such as LSTMs) model continuous time points based on the Markov assumption19–21, yet fail to capture long-term dependencies and suffer computational inefficiencies due to the sequential paradigm22. Another class of methods employs TCNs to convolve along the temporal dimension22,23, but their ability to capture complex variations remains limited.

Leveraging Transformers’ robust nonlinear modelling capabilities24, numerous approaches have emerged to capture complex temporal variations in real-world time series. Informer enhances efficiency for long sequences via sparse attention25, Autoformer employs autocorrelation mechanisms for sequence decomposition6, and FEDformer introduces frequency-domain enhanced decomposition26. While effective at capturing endogenous dependencies, these approaches remain constrained by simplistic embeddings for exogenous variables, failing to fully exploit cross-variable causality15. Patch-based methods such as PatchTST segment sequences into patch tokens to reduce complexity while preserving local semantics27; iTransformer employs inverted embeddings, modelling variate-wise correlations by treating each variable as an independent token28; Crossformer enhances multi-variable accuracy through cross-dimensional dependencies29; Pyraformer handles long-range patterns via pyramid attention30. However, these models remain susceptible to trend shifts when processing non-stationary sequences. Consequently, Non-stationary Transformers introduce tau and delta parameters to explicitly model non-stationarity31. MLP-based approaches such as TSMixer emphasise the channel-independent assumption to achieve lightweight prediction32, yet neglect the causal influence of exogenous variables16.

For periodic patterns, TimesNet proposes 2D temporal variance modelling, employing FFT-based periodicity detection and 2D Inception blocks to extract intra- and inter-periodic variance, achieving SOTA performance across general temporal tasks33. Similarly, ETSformer combines exponential smoothing with enhanced periodicity capture34. Methods dedicated to exogenous variables include NBEATSx, which integrates external information via extended basis functions12; Temporal Fusion Transformers, which fuse gated processing for multi-horizon forecasting15; DeepAR, which employs autoregressive RNNs for probabilistic forecasting35; and N-BEATS and N-HiTS, which utilise neural basis expansion or hierarchical imputation for interpretable predictions36,37. Despite significant progress, existing methods struggle to simultaneously handle endogenous 2D periodic variation and exogenous variate-wise causal injections, particularly in patch-based architectures where 2D variation conflicts with patch semantics38,39.

To bridge these limitations, we propose P2D-Former, an encoder-only Transformer that for the first time genuinely implements TimesNet-style 2D temporal variation modelling within a patch-based backbone, while maintaining full compatibility with any exogenous variable. The model processes endogenous variables at the patch level, simultaneously bridging multi-scale features, periodic 2D variations, and exogenous information through a learnable global token. Within each Encoder layer, patch tokens capture local patterns. The global token undergoes MSUnit multi-scale augmentation and P2D-FFN periodic 2D variation modelling before engaging in cross-modal interaction with exogenous variate tokens via Intersect-Attention. We introduce Intersect-Attention to explicitly inject exogenous causal signals via the global token, enabling variate-wise cross-modal interaction that complements our period-aware 2D modelling. This process ultimately broadcasts enhanced semantics back to the patch sequence. This design simultaneously models complex patterns across patch-wise, period-wise, and variate-wise dimensions, establishing a more comprehensive foundational model. Experiments demonstrate significant superiority over baselines like TimesNet and ETSformer in long-term forecasting (input length 96, prediction lengths {96, 192, 336, 720}), achieving average MSE/MAE reductions of 5–12% on strongly periodic datasets such as Weather and Traffic. Short-term forecasting outperforms FEDformer and Non-stationary Transformer on the M4 dataset; imputation tasks demonstrate outstanding robustness under high missing rates (37.5%–50%). Ablation studies further validate the contributions of P2D-FFN and Intersect-Attention, with the latter explicitly injecting exogenous causality through global tokens to ensure model generalisation across multi-source data.Furthermore, deployment in complex dynamic environments requires forecasting models to satisfy multi-dimensional reliability criteria beyond accuracy, such as safety and interpretability40,41.

As illustrated in Fig. 1, we employ a feature token embedding strategy for both exogenous and endogenous variables: the left panel depicts the embedding process for the main sequence variable, which begins with the raw endogenous signal and is transformed through an embedding layer into a sequence of feature tokens, ensuring efficient capture of the main sequence’s temporal patterns; The right diagram addresses covariate scenarios by independently embedding multiple raw covariate signals as tokens, then arranging them along both variable and temporal dimensions to form a matrix-like tensor. This variate-level embedding enables Intersect-Attention to naturally describe multivariate correlations and causal interactions–for instance, capturing cross-modal associations between supply-demand covariates and main-sequence prices in electricity forecasting. This matrix structure complements the multi-period 2D variation in the method’s P2D-FFN: on strongly periodic datasets (Electricity, Traffic), embedded covariate tokens can further fuse with endogenous periodic information, enhancing performance over long forecasting horizons. Ablation experiments confirm a significant error increase when removing Intersect-Attention. Overall, this embedding strategy ensures efficient bridging of covariate information to endogenous main sequences, enabling the model to achieve state-of-the-art performance across multi-source, complex cyclical scenarios.

Related work

In the field of time series forecasting, deep learning has rapidly advanced. Beyond standard architectures, recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of hybrid and ensemble techniques in modeling complex nonlinear dynamics across domains such as electricity consumption42, disease outbreaks43, and environmental management44,45. Concurrently, the emergence of Transformer-based models has further enhanced the capability to capture intricate dependencies inherent in time series data. The Transformer architecture, particularly its self-attention mechanism, demonstrates exceptional performance in modelling long sequences and capturing multidimensional dependencies6,24. In recent years, Transformer models have been extensively applied to time series analysis. Variants such as Autoformer6, Informer25, and FEDformer26 have further enhanced the performance of time series forecasting. Nevertheless, existing methods often focus solely on modelling endogenous variables, neglecting the influence of exogenous variables on time series prediction6.

To address this shortcoming, numerous studies have explored incorporating exogenous variables, particularly in multi-source data tasks such as weather forecasting and traffic flow prediction, where exogenous factors (e.g. climate change, public holidays) significantly influence endogenous variables9,25. For instance, the TFT model15 enhances multivariate time series forecasting by incorporating a selection mechanism for exogenous variables. Nevertheless, many existing approaches suffer from insufficient information fusion or overly simplistic handling of exogenous variables. Such opaque fusion mechanisms often fail to meet the requirements of transparency and stability advocated by recent SAFE AI frameworks13, exhibiting limitations particularly when addressing complex cross-variable interactions and multi-periodic variations6,16,26.

To address this challenge more effectively, we propose the P2D-Former model. This architecture combines a patch-based Transformer framework with self-attention and cross-attention mechanisms, enabling simultaneous modelling of complex dependencies between endogenous and exogenous variables6. Unlike previous Transformer-based models, P2D-Former employs a dual embedding mechanism, processing endogenous and exogenous variables through distinct token representations. It further introduces a global token to bridge causal information between endogenous and exogenous variables25,28. Through Multi-Scale Unidirectional Convolution and a Periodicity-Aware 2D feedforward network, P2D-Former effectively captures multi-periodic variations and complex exogenous variable interactions within time series6.

Moreover, existing exogenous variable processing methods predominantly rely on traditional self-attention mechanisms, proving inadequate for handling complex interactions across variables and temporal information26,28. P2D-Former enhances the injection of exogenous variable information by explicitly modelling interactions between endogenous and exogenous variables through its Intersect-Attention mechanism6. This design not only improves variate-wise exogenous information utilization but also enables stable and superior performance across various forecasting horizons26.

Experimental results demonstrate that P2D-Former exhibits significant advantages across multiple time series prediction tasks, particularly in multi-source data forecasting and long-sequence prediction scenarios. Compared to existing Transformer variants such as PatchTST27, iTransformer28, and TimeXer16, P2D-Former achieves substantial improvements in handling long-range dependencies, multi-period modelling, and causal information fusion from exogenous variables6,26.

In summary, through its innovative model architecture and the incorporation of cross-variable causal information, P2D-Former substantially enhances both accuracy and robustness in time series forecasting tasks, offering a novel solution for multi-source data time series prediction6,26.

Beyond methodological improvements, recent studies have emphasized the critical role of forecasting within broader application-driven systems. For instance, traffic prediction platforms based on vehicle–road–cloud integration46 highlight that exogenous variables are central drivers of traffic dynamics in realistic scenarios. Similarly, in energy and industrial sectors, forecasting serves as a foundational component for renewable energy dispatch47 and efficiency optimization48, where robustness against exogenous variability is essential for downstream decision-making. These studies motivate the need for architectures like P2D-Former, which are explicitly designed to reliably incorporate multi-source exogenous signals and maintain stability under complex operational constraints.To ensure trustworthiness in such systems, recent research emphasizes structured risk modeling49 and adherence to SAFE AI standards, including quantitative metrics for Rank Graduation Accuracy (RGA)50 and fairness audits51. P2D-Former aligns with this evolving paradigm by explicitly modeling exogenous interactions to improve predictive reliability.

Methods

P2D-Former is an innovative time series forecasting model designed to address two core challenges in complex time series: modelling multi-periodic variations and effectively incorporating causal information from exogenous variables. By integrating a patch-based Transformer architecture27 with self-attention and cross-attention mechanisms, P2D-Former simultaneously captures intricate dependencies between endogenous and exogenous variables24. The model design fully leverages cross-modal information exchange via global tokens. Through multi-scale convolutions52 and periodic feature extraction modules33, it precisely models local variations, periodic fluctuations, and external influences within time series.

MSUnit

The MSUnit module introduces depthwise separable convolutions and convolutional kernels of varying sizes (such as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1\times 1$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$3\times 3$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$5\times 5$$\end{document}) to perform multi-scale feature extraction on time series data52. Through this design, MSUnit can simultaneously capture local fluctuations and global trends across multiple temporal scales, thereby enhancing the understanding of complex time series, particularly when handling data exhibiting variations across different frequencies.

Depthwise separable convolutions

In MSUnit, depthwise separable convolutions decompose standard convolutions into two components: depthwise convolution and pointwise convolution. Depthwise convolution performs convolution independently on each input channel, whereas pointwise convolution merges all channels through a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1\times 1$$\end{document} convolution. This decomposition significantly reduces computational complexity whilst preserving the expressive power of convolution. For input features \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tilde{\textbf{g}}^{(l)}$$\end{document}, MSUnit employs convolutional kernels of varying sizes to extract features in parallel:4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \textbf{Y}_k = \textrm{DepthwiseConv}_k\big (\textrm{PointwiseConv}(\tilde{\textbf{g}}^{(l)})\big ), \quad k \in \{1,3,5,7\}. \end{aligned}$$\end{document}Here, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{Y}_k$$\end{document} denotes the feature map obtained after processing through a convolution kernel of size k. Deep separable convolutions can better capture local patterns and trends in time series while maintaining high efficiency.

Multi-scale feature fusion

This feature fusion approach ensures that information from different scales is effectively integrated, enabling the model to simultaneously capture both short-term local variations and long-term trends.

Adaptive fusion

Another key feature of MSUnit is Adaptive Fusion, a mechanism enabling the model to dynamically adjust fusion methods based on the distinct properties of input features. Within MSUnit, Adaptive Fusion assigns learnable weights to each scale branch through \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1\times 1$$\end{document} convolutions and channel gating: first, multi-scale features are concatenated along the channel dimension; subsequently, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1\times 1$$\end{document} convolutions generate scale weights which are multiplied element-wise. This process automatically amplifies important scales and suppresses redundant scales during the forward pass, enabling on-demand selection and combination of different temporal scales and variation patterns.

P2D-FFN

The P2D-FFN module explicitly models the periodic structure of time series within the feedforward sublayer by introducing frequency-domain periodicity detection combined with two-dimensional convolutions?. Unlike conventional token-by-token MLP-based FFNs, P2D-FFN directly extracts dominant cycles from patch-wise/token sequences, rearranging them into a two-dimensional “intra-cycle \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document} inter-cycle” representation. It then employs multi-scale 2D convolutions to simultaneously capture intra-cycle patterns and inter-cycle variations. By explicitly integrating periodic information with temporal features, this module significantly enhances the model’s sensitivity to cyclical fluctuations, proving particularly effective for forecasting tasks exhibiting pronounced daily, weekly, or seasonal structures.

Baselines

We compare our approach with state-of-the-art deep time series models, including the Transformer family (PatchTST27, iTransformer28, TimeXer16, Autoformer6, Informer25, FEDformer26, Non-stationary Transformer31); convolutional and 2D variational models (TimesNet33, SCINet23); and linear models (DLinear, RLinear53). These approaches span mainstream frameworks from pure attention structures to multi-scale convolutions and frequency-domain modelling, providing a comprehensive baseline for thorough comparison.

Results

Table 2 presents the overall results of each model across six datasets and four prediction steps. It is evident that P2D-Former achieves the best or joint-best performance in the vast majority of settings, demonstrating particularly outstanding capabilities on datasets featuring strong periodicity and multi-scale variability structures, such as Weather, ETTh1, and ETTm1. We attribute this performance advantage primarily to: the multiscale local structure capture capability provided by MSUnit; the 2D modelling capability of P2D-FFN for implicit periodic structures33; and the information aggregation effect of global tokens in cross-variable and exogenous interactions. Short-term forecast

Setups

The short-term forecasting experiments are conducted on the M4 dataset, which encompasses six categories of univariate time series: Yearly, Quarterly, Monthly, Weekly, Daily, and Hourly. This dataset is one of the most representative benchmarks for short-term forecasting. We follow the official M4 configuration, employing the corresponding forecast lengths for each frequency subset and conducting training and testing on the standard data splits. Evaluation metrics comprise the competition-specified SMAPE, MASE, and OWA, where OWA comprehensively measures a model’s overall performance relative to the Naïve2 baseline. In this task, we compare P2D-Former against multiple advanced models, including deep feedforward architectures (N-BEATS36, N-HiTS37), Transformer and hybrid structures (TimesNet33, FEDformer26, Autoformer6, Non-stationary Transformer31, Reformer55, Informer25, LSSL56, PatchTST27), lightweight time-series models (DLinear17, LightTS57), probabilistic and statistical models (ETS34, Pyramid30), and traditional RNN/TCN architectures (LSTM19, TCN22).

Results

Table 3 presents the prediction results of P2D-Former across six frequency subsets. In terms of overall performance, P2D-Former achieves the best or joint-best results on the three core subsets—Yearly, Quarterly, and Monthly—while consistently maintaining a stable lead across the three metrics: sMAPE, MASE, and OWA. Within the Others category (which encompasses composite sequences of varying short-term frequencies), P2D-Former similarly outperforms major competing methods.

Compared with traditional models (ETS34, LSTM19, TCN22), P2D-Former exhibits significant advantages across all metrics. Relative to recent high-performance deep models (TimesNet33, N-HiTS37, DLinear17), P2D-Former further reduces errors on most subsets, demonstrating substantial improvements in short-sequence structural modelling and generalisation capabilities. These results indicate that, owing to its ability to model periodic 2D variations and cross-variable causality, P2D-Former maintains strong competitiveness in short-term forecasting tasks and is Imputation

Setups

To evaluate P2D-Former’s capability to recover temporal structures under incomplete observations, we construct imputation tasks on the multivariate benchmarks ETT (4 subsets)25, Electricity6,20, and Weather6. During the testing phase, observations in input sequences of length 96 are randomly masked with missing rates set to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\{12.5\%, 25\%, 37.5\%, 50\%\}$$\end{document}, while the remaining experimental settings are kept consistent with the long-term forecasting task. Models reconstruct the masked time points conditioned solely on the unmasked portions. Evaluation metrics comprise MSE and MAE, which are statistically aggregated across different missing rates as well as their overall averages. Comparison methods include TimesNet33, ETS34, LightTS57, DLinear17, FEDformer26, Non-stationary Transformer31, Autoformer6, Pyraformer30, Informer25, LogTrans58, LSTM19, TCN22, and LSSL56.

Results

Table 4 presents the imputation results for each model across different datasets and missingness rates. P2D-Former achieves the lowest MSE/MAE under almost all masking settings on the Weather, ETT subsets, and Electricity datasets. The advantage is particularly pronounced at high missing rates (e.g. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$37.5\%$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$50\%$$\end{document}), where the average performance significantly outperforms all baselines. Compared with traditional statistical models (ETS34), sequence models (LSTM19, TCN22), and recent deep forecasting methods (TimesNet33, DLinear17, FEDformer26, etc.), P2D-Former consistently maintains a stable lead on the imputation task. These results demonstrate that its multi-scale convolutions and periodic 2D variational modelling33 can effectively capture underlying temporal patterns even under severe observation missingness.

Ablation studies

Setups

To analyse the contributions of each key module within P2D-Former, we conduct ablation experiments on three representative datasets: ETTh125, Traffic, and Weather6. The prediction lengths are set to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\{96,192,336,720\}$$\end{document}, and MSE and MAE are used as evaluation metrics. Except for the structural modifications outlined in the table, all other training configurations remain identical to those used in the long-term forecasting experiments.

In Table 5, P2D-Former denotes the complete model, which applies Self-Attention and Intersect-Attention along the variate dimension and P2D-FFN along the temporal dimension. The Replace variants test the superiority of our design via module substitution: replacing Intersect-Attention with standard Cross-Attention24; replacing P2D-FFN with a conventional position-wise feedforward network (FFN)24; and simultaneously substituting both with the standard design. The component-removal (w/o) variants respectively remove Intersect-Attention or P2D-FFN to assess their indispensability.

Results

As shown in Table 5, the complete P2D-Former achieves the best average MSE/MAE across all three datasets and four prediction horizons, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed holistic design. Replacing Intersect-Attention with standard Cross-Attention leads to consistent error increases, particularly on the Traffic and Weather datasets, indicating the benefit of our global-token-mediated exogenous fusion. Substituting P2D-FFN with a standard FFN further degrades performance, highlighting the importance of period-aware 2D variability modelling for long-horizon forecasting. The most pronounced degradation occurs when both Intersect-Attention and P2D-FFN are removed, confirming that these modules are complementary rather than redundant.

P2D-former generality

Different forecasting spans

In long-term forecasting tasks, we evaluate four prediction horizons, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\{96, 192, 336, 720\}$$\end{document}, across six multivariate benchmarks. As shown in Table 2, although the error of all methods increases as the prediction horizon grows, P2D-Former consistently achieves optimal or near-optimal MSE/MAE across all strides, yielding the most pronounced advantage in terms of average performance. This indicates that the cross-variable modelling based on global tokens, together with the periodic 2D variation modelling of P2D-FFN33, does not rely on any fixed prediction length; instead, it operates robustly across both short-term and medium-to-long-term forecasting scenarios.

Different deficiency ratios

To further test applicability in complex real-world scenarios, we conduct imputation experiments with varying missingness rates on ETT25, Electricity6,20, and Weather6, with missing proportions increasing from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$12.5\%$$\end{document} to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$50\%$$\end{document}. As shown in Table 4, while all models inevitably exhibit increased error as the missingness rate rises, P2D-Former outperforms other methods across all missingness levels as well as their averaged results. This advantage is particularly pronounced at high missingness rates such as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$37.5\%$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$50\%$$\end{document}, demonstrating strong robustness to incomplete observations.

Combining the results from long-term forecasting, short-term forecasting, and imputation tasks, P2D-Former maintains a stable relative advantage across different timescales, varying degrees of observation completeness, and diverse data domains, thereby validating its strong generalisability.

Model analysis

Variate-wise attention

P2D-Former models causal relationships between endogenous and exogenous variables via Intersect-Attention between global tokens and exogenous variate-level tokens. On the Weather dataset6, P2D-Former effectively and selectively focuses on exogenous variables (such as barometric pressure and wind speed) that are cyclically correlated with the target endogenous variables, thereby optimising the injection of exogenous information. This demonstrates that Intersect-Attention achieves “selective focus” at the variate dimension, allocating greater representational capacity to exogenous factors that are critical for prediction. Consequently, the representations encoded by the global tokens become both interpretable and capable of effectively integrating cross-variate causal information.

Temporal 2D-variations

On datasets exhibiting pronounced multi-periodic structures, such as Electricity6,20 and Traffic6,8, patch-wise tokens first undergo FFT-based selection to identify dominant periods and are then rearranged into two-dimensional tensors. The column dimension corresponds to local positions within a period, while the row dimension tracks evolution across periods. Regions of concentrated energy typically align with dominant periods and inflection points in the original sequence33.

Compared with the smoother features produced by standard FFNs, the 2D representations generated by P2D-FFN exhibit clear locality and segmentation along both the row and column dimensions. This enables unified encoding of multi-scale periodic variations and slow cross-period trends within a single tensor. Such structured 2D variation characterisation is consistent with P2D-Former’s performance advantages under long prediction horizons and high missing rates, validating the effectiveness of the multi-scale unit and periodic 2D feedforward network33 from a representation learning perspective.

Model efficiency

Figure 3(left) further illustrates the efficiency comparison among different models. P2D-Former demonstrates superiority in both training time and memory consumption relative to comparable models, exhibiting outstanding computational efficiency and memory management capabilities in resource-constrained multi-source prediction tasks. In contrast, although some baselines may achieve higher accuracy on certain tasks, their training time and memory requirements are markedly higher.

To more systematically evaluate P2D-Former’s efficiency, we compare it against multiple benchmark models in terms of training time and memory consumption. As shown in Fig. 3(right), P2D-Former achieves an MSE of 0.15 on the Weather dataset6. At the same time, its training time is only \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$21\,\textrm{ms}$$\end{document} per iteration, with memory consumption of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.95\,\textrm{GB}$$\end{document}—substantially lower than baseline models such as PatchTST27 and iTransformer28. These results demonstrate that P2D-Former not only maintains competitive accuracy, but also operates efficiently under constrained computational resources.

Conclusion

The proposed P2D-Former model effectively integrates causal relationships between endogenous and exogenous variables, addressing the challenges of modelling multi-periodic variations and incorporating exogenous variable information. Experimental results demonstrate that P2D-Former performs exceptionally well in long-sequence prediction, short-term forecasting, and data imputation tasks involving missing values, exhibiting particular advantages when handling data with complex periodicity and multi-scale variations. The model’s design, incorporating Intersect-Attention and a periodic 2D feedforward network, lies at the core of its key performance enhancements. P2D-Former demonstrates robust resilience and broad adaptability across multiple tasks, validating its potential for practical time series forecasting applications.

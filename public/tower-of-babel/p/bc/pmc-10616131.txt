Scleral ectasia stabilization following intra-arterial chemotherapy in an eye with recurrent retinoblastoma
Authors: Warren Pan, Neeraj Chaudhary, Laura Sedig, Amy Edmonds, Raymond Hutchinson, Hakan Demirci
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo report a case of recurrent retinoblastoma following transpupillary thermotherapy (TTT) with development of scleral ectasia, as well as their successful treatment with intra-arterial chemotherapy.ObservationsA 15-month-old girl with bilateral retinoblastoma presented with recurrent retinoblastoma and associated scleral ectasia with concern for extraocular extension after receiving multiple round of systemic chemotherapy and TTT. Given her negative systemic evaluation, decision was made to pursue intra-arterial chemotherapy. After completion of six rounds of 3-agent intra-arterial chemotherapy, the recurrent retinoblastoma had completely regressed and the scleral ectasia had improved and fibrosed.Conclusions and importanceThe sclera is classically viewed as hyperthermy-resistant to TTT. Here, we describe scleral ectasia due to aggressive TTT with recurrence of the retinoblastoma. Our treatment with intra-arterial chemotherapy not only caused complete regression of the recurrent retinoblastoma, but it also contributed to the stabilization and improvement of the weakened scleral.

Introduction

Retinoblastoma is a blinding and potentially fatal disease that is the most common eye cancer of childhood. Its treatment has evolved over time from enucleation to intravenous and intra-arterial chemotherapy combined with laser treatments.1 Transpupillary thermotherapy (TTT) has been used as a primary and adjunctive treatment of retinoblastoma.2 There have been no reports of to our knowledge of recurrence coinciding with scleral ectasia due to TTT. Here we report this finding after TTT and the subsequent stabilization and improvement of the sclera with the successful treatment of the recurrent retinoblastoma with intra-arterial chemotherapy.

Case description

A 15-month old girl with bilateral retinoblastoma was referred for recurrence of macular retinoblastoma in the right eye after undergoing 12 cycles of systemic chemotherapy (initial six cycles with etoposide and carboplatin and the subsequent six cycles with the addition of vincristine) along with six rounds of transpupillary thermotherapy (TTT).2 Fundus examination of the right eye showed a recurrent retinoblastoma with surrounding subretinal fluid on the macula, measuring 12 × 8 mm and 3.2 mm in thickness (Fig. 1). B-scan ultrasonography and MRI revealed a macular retinoblastoma and identified an underlying irregularity along the posterior scleral margin, suggestive of scleral ectasia (Fig. 1). Importantly, this was not observed on MRI conducted at the initiation of her treatment, but did develop after completion of TTT (Fig. 2). These findings raised concerns for extraocular extension. Her systemic and CNS evaluation, including spinal fluid analysis, was normal. The patient underwent six cycles of intra-arterial therapy using Melphalan (3 mg), Topotecan (0.3 mg) and Carboplatin (30 mg).1 After the completion of therapy, the macular retinoblastoma demonstrated type 4 regression, measured 10 × 8 mm, and was flat in thickness (Fig. 1). On orbital MRI, a posterior staphyloma was present and associated with a smooth and thin, but intact, sclera under the tumor at the macula (Fig. 1). Follow-up 1 year after treatment demonstrated an intraocular pressure of 18 mmHg for both eyes. Conclusions

The irregular posterior scleral margin observed in this patient could be related to scleral ectasia and recurrence of retinoblastoma on this surface. Importantly, the scleral ectasia and irregular scleral surface was suspicious for extraocular tumor extension (Fig. 1C, E, 1G), which is treated with enucleation and pre-surgery systemic chemotherapy in some cases.3 After multidisciplinary decision making with the parents, the patient instead underwent intra-arterial therapy given the normal systemic and CNS tumor evaluation. The patient received six cycles of triple-agent intra-arterial chemotherapy, which resulted in complete tumor response, as well as presumed fibrosis over the presenting area of scleral ectasia.4 This report demonstrates the regression of the primary retinoblastoma tumor at the site of the ectasia with intra-arterial chemotherapy treatment and durable stabilization one year after. We think that, as the tumor regressed, changes in the tumor environment including the inflammatory response might have played a role in the formation of scleral fibrosis, which has previously been demonstrated histologically in rabbit eyes exposed to TTT.4 Interestingly, the scleral ectasia may represent an atrophic or necrotic process. While histologic analysis to differentiate between the two is not possible, it is important to recognize this potential complication of TTT and the potential treatment with intra-arterial chemotherapy.

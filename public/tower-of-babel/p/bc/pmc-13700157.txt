A new wave of resurge for GII.17[P17] norovirus in multiple provinces of China in 2024
Authors: Shuting Yang, Jian Gao, Lijiao Cao, Hailong Zhang, Lina Ren, Ziping Miao, Qing Zhang, Xiangyu Kong, Guoyong Mei, Lingli Sun, Shuaifeng Zhou, Miao Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionNoroviruses is a major cause of outbreaks and sporadic acute gastroenteritis (AGE) across all age groups. Although the GII.4 norovirus has been the primary cause of most AGE outbreaks, Non-GII.4 norovirus has been reported globally.AimWe aimed to report an increase in GII.17 norovirus outbreaks of acute gastroenteritis in multiple provinces of China during the second half of 2024.MethodsData and specimens from AGE outbreaks (January 2022–December 2024) were collected. Samples were tested via real-time RT-PCR; positive GII.17[P17] samples underwent sequencing and phylogenetic analysis.ResultsAmong 1,579 norovirus outbreaks, 1,225 were genotyped. The first halves of 2022–2024 saw co-circulation of genotypes GII.3[P12], GII.4 Sydney, GII.17[P17], and GII.7[P7]. GII.4 Sydney dominated the second halves of 2022 (46.15%) and 2023 (51.63%). Strikingly, in the second half of 2024, GII.17[P17] became predominant, causing 75.28% of outbreaks. This shift was driven by a novel GII.17 variant (Romania-2021 like, subcluster C2), first detected in February 2024, which replaced the previous Kawasaki 308-like variant. The novel variant predominantly affected the 6–18 years age group (54.97%) and elementary schools (34.33%). Relative to Cluster D, this novel variant harbors five amino acid mutations in antigenic epitopes, one mutation in the histo-blood group antigen (HBGA) binding site, and two deletions.ConclusionsA novel GII.17 variant has overtaken GII.4 as the predominant norovirus strain in China since late 2024, underscoring the need for continued molecular surveillance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12879-026-14009-8.Keywords: Norovirus, GII.17, Virus evolution, Gastroenteritis

Norovirus is one of the major pathogens causing non-bacterial acute gastroenteritis (AGE) in human beings worldwide [1], with about 200,000 deaths due to norovirus infection every year [2]. Human norovirus is a single-stranded, positive-sense, non-enveloped RNA virus belonging to the Caliciviridae family and Norovirus genus, containing three open reading frames (ORFs). ORF1 encodes six non-structural proteins, including Protein 48 (p48), nucleoside triphosphatase (NTPase), Protein 22 (p22), genome-linked viral protein (VPg), 3 C-like Protease (Pro), and RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp); ORF2 and ORF3 encode the major coat proteins of the Norovirus capsid viral protein 1 (VP1) and secondary structural protein VP2 (viral protein 2, VP2), respectively, of which ORF2 is about 1,600 bp long and ORF3 is about 720 bp long; VP1 consists of the shell domain (S domain) and the protruding domain (P domain). According to the VP1 protein, Norovirus can be divided into 10 genogroups (GI ~ GX) and can be further divided into 49 genotypes; according to the polymerase protein, norovirus can be divided into 60 P-types [3].

Since 1990, GII.4 has been associated with the majority of norovirus outbreaks globally [4]. In addition to high levels of exposure and immunity to GII.4, other GII genotypes have temporarily become predominant and spread globally [5]. Phylogenetic analyses of GII.17 viruses revealed the presence of at least four distinct phylogenetic clusters (A-D) [6]. Early data suggested that cluster A viruses (1970s ~ 2010s) and cluster B viruses (2005–2009), which were represented by a very small number of cases, were static before 2010. During the season 2014/15, a sharp increase in the number of gastroenteritis outbreaks was reported in Asia countries (especially in China) was associated with the emergence of a new variant of the genotype GII.17 (cluster D, Kawasaki308 like), which Cluster C viruses (Kawasaki323 like) emerging in the season 2013/14 [7]. After a period of sporadic distribution, an emerging GII.17 variant caused the surge of a large number of norovirus outbreaks reported in Europe and North America during 2023–2024 [8–10]. Unexpectedly, these novel variants clustered closely with GII.17 Romania-2021 strains (OP805362) were related to Cluster C, a lineage that previously circulated exclusively between 2013 and 2016 before being replaced by the cluster D. Recently, Epifanova et al. classified the novel GII.17 viruses as new subcluster C2 (Romania-2021) of cluster C, in which original cluster C was renamed as subcluster C1 (Kawasaki323 like) [9]. Tohma et al. attributed the prevalence of C2 to mutations in key amino acid sites and changes in the HBGA binding profile [11].

We report an increase in GII.17 norovirus outbreaks of acute gastroenteritis in multiple provinces of China during the second half of 2024. The aim of this study is to describe the epidemiological and genetic characteristics of norovirus outbreaks reported in China that were linked to GII.17 noroviruses.

Materials and methods

Sources of samples

Epidemiological information and samples of norovirus AGE outbreaks were collected from eight regions of Chinese viral diarrhea outbreak surveillance network between January 2022 and December 2024, including Beijing, Shandong, Sichuan, Hunan, Guangdong, Hainan, Yunnan, and Zhejiang. An outbreak of viral diarrhea is defined as the occurrence of 20 or more epidemiologically linked cases of clinically diagnosed diarrhea in the same location within a 7-day period, with at least 2 cases confirmed by laboratory testing. A suspected case is generally defined as an individual experiencing either ≥ 3 bowel movements per day accompanied by a change in stool consistency (e.g., loose or watery stools) or ≥ 2 episodes of vomiting within a 24-hour period [12].

Norovirus genotyping and amplification

First, samples were screened for norovirus using Real-Time Reverse Transcription Polymerase Chain Reaction(RT-qPCR). Second, RT-PCR was conducted on positive samples to simultaneously amplify B (polymerase) and C (capsid) gene fragments; the fragments were sequenced by Sanger sequencing with primers MON431/G2SKR for GII norovirus and MON432/G1SKR for GI norovirus [13–16]. Then, the typing fragment was identified using the Human Calicivirus Typing tool (https://calicivirustypingtool.cdc.gov/bctyping.html). Finally, full-length genome amplification was performed on samples with typing result of GII.17 [P17], the primer sequences and annealing temperatures are shown in Table 1.

Sequence analysis

Different sequence fragments of the same specimen were assembled with SeqMan (version 7.1.0) tool in DNASTAR to obtain the typing sequence as well as the complete polymerase region and coat region. Phylogenetic analysis, nucleotide similarity analysis and amino acid site comparison of the resulting full-length sequences with reference sequences (all from GenBank) were performed using MEGA (version 7.0.26) software [17]. The evolutionary tree was constructed using MEGA (version 7.0.26) software with the neighbor-joining (NJ) method and the confidence assessment value Bootstrap set to 1,000.

The reference sequences of the GII.17 VP1 region from different years and regions in the NCBI database were collected, after deleting the incomplete sequences and eliminating the sequences with 100% similarity, 443 sequences of the GII.17 VP1 region were obtained, and the databases were established. PhyloSuite (version 1.2.2) [18] was used to select the optimal nucleotide model for GII.17 VP1 Bayesian evolutionary analysis. Using path sampling to determine a strict molecular clock model with constant population parameters is the optimal approach. BEAST (version 1.10.4) [19] analyzed divergence time and evolutionary rate. Tracer (version 1.7.1) [20] verifies whether the results converge (ESS > 200). MCC trees were obtained via Tree Annotator (version 1.10.4) and visualized/edited with Fig Tree (version 1.4.4) [21].

Results

GII.17[P17] predominating during the second half of 2024

From January 2022 to December 2024, a total of 1,579 AGE outbreaks were laboratory confirmed as norovirus. According to the monthly distribution of norovirus outbreaks, the season peak for each year all occurred in March of Spring between 2022 and 2024. Norovirus outbreaks in the second half of 2024 peaking in October were significantly higher than in the second half of 2022 and 2023 (Fig. 1).

Monthly distribution of the number of outbreaks of GI, GII and mixed infections, 2022–2024. Note: (a) The donut chart shows the genotype proportions for each half-year; (b) GII.4 Sydney strains in our dataset contain both [P16] and [P31] polymerase types

Of 1,579 norovirus outbreaks, 1,225 were successfully genotyped. GII strains were responsible for 1,063 out of 1,579 (67.3%) of laboratory confirmed norovirus outbreaks, while GI and mixed GI and GII strains were responsible for 101 (8.2%, 101/1,225) and 61 (5.0%, 61/1,225) norovirus outbreaks, respectively. According to the genotyping results, 101 GI outbreaks were reported including 10 genotypes (4 GI.1[P1], 9 GI.2[P2], 15 GI.3[P13], 4 GI.3[P3], 28 GI.4[P4], 5 GI.5[P4], 1 GI.5[P5], 26 GI.6[P11], 8 GI.7[P7] and 1 GI.9[P9]). Among 1,063 GII genotyped outbreaks, the percentage of non-GII.4 and GII.4 outbreaks were 82.9% (881/1,063) and 17.1% (182/1,063), respectively.

According to the monthly genotype distribution of norovirus outbreaks, it was seen that the first half of 2022 and the first half of 2023 was dominated by multiple genotypes. The main genotypes in the first half of 2022 and the first half of 2023 are GII.3[P12] (30.8% of 2022 and 20.9% of 2023, respectively), GII.4 Sydney (19.2% of 2022 and 13.2% of 2023, respectively) and GII.17 [P17] (10.0% of 2022 and 20.3% of 2023, respectively). Meanwhile, the predominant genotypes changed which included GII.7 [P7] (38.9%), GII.3 [P12] (23.8%), GII.17 [P17] (15.1%), and GII.4 Sydney (12.4%) in the first half of 2024. In the second half of 2022 and the second half of 2023, GII.4 Sydney was the predominant genotype, accounting for 42.9% and 48.4% respectively. Of note, in the second half of 2024, the percentage of GII.17 outbreaks increased to 73.6%, overtaking GII.4 Sydney outbreaks (5.5%) and GII.3[P12] outbreaks (14.3%), and from July through December 2024, GII.17 accounted for > 60% of all outbreaks each month (Fig. 1). In addition, GII.17[P17] were predominant in Yunnan(100.0%), Hunan(96.6%), Sichuan(95.5%), Hainan(91.3%), Zhejiang(80.0%), Shandong(74.2%), Beijing(72.7%) and Guangdong(57.1%) in the second half of 2024 in this study.

The novel GII.17[P17] variant emerging in the first half year and predominant in the second half year of 2024

The phylogenetic analysis of 142 GII.17[P17] B-C sequences was conducted in this study. Of the 142 sequences, 107 (75.4%) clustered closely with a new cluster, C2 (Romania-2021 like), which caused a large number of norovirus outbreaks in Europe and North America during 2023–2024. The remaining 35 (24.6%) clustered cluster D (Kawasaki 308 like), which emerged during season 2014/15, causing outbreaks particularly in Asia, and quickly spread across multiple countries on all continents (Fig. 2). In this study, the novel GII.17 C2 variant (Romania-2021 like) which was first detected in Yunnan in February of 2024 and caused the outbreak. In the first half of 2024, 36 (12.6%, 36/286) and 9 (3.1%, 9/286) of the GII.17 outbreaks were caused by the novel GII.17 C2 variant (Romania-2021 like) and the D Kawasaki 308 like strain, respectively, meanwhile, in the second half year of 2024 all GII.17 outbreaks (75.3% of outbreaks, 201/267) were caused by this novel variant which became predominant (96.3% of the total number of outbreaks) overtaking other genotypes. Since April of 2024, GII.17 D Kawasaki 308 like strain has not been detected and completely replaced by novel GII.17 C2 variant (Romania-2021 like).

Epidemiological characteristics of norovirus outbreaks caused by the novel GII.17 C2 variant (Romania-2021 like)

In the age distribution, the main age group of the 755 patients from 237 norovirus outbreaks caused by the novel GII.17 C2 variant (Romania-2021 like) was 6–18 years old (54.97%, 415/755), followed by ≤ 5 years old (23.97%, 181/755), and finally 19–65 years old (19.07%, 144/755) and > 65 years old (1.99%, 15/755). Same as the new variant, GII.7[P7] also predominantly infected people aged 6–18 years with 69.1% (273/395). While GII.4 Sydney, GII.3[P12] and GII.2[P16] were all predominantly infecting children aged 5 years and below, with 62.7% (338/539), 50.7% (374/738) and 56.0% (102/182), respectively. A chi-square test was performed on the percentage of different age groups, and the difference was statistically significant (χ2 = 368.01, P < 0.001) (Table 2).

Of the 237 outbreaks caused by the norovirus GII.17[P17] new variant strain, 219 outbreaks were clearly identified in outbreak settings, including 62 (26.2%, 62/237) in childcare facilities, 72 (30.4%, 72/237) in primary school and 36 (152%, 36/237) in middle schools; other places included other schools in 20 cases(8.4%, 20/237), restaurants in 6 cases (2.5%, 6/237), homes in 8 cases (3.4%, 8/237), hospitals in 8 cases (3.4%, 8/237), hotels in 3 cases (1.3%, 3/237), universities in 2 cases(0.8%, 2/237), workplace in 1 case (0.4%, 1/237) and nursing institutions in 1 case (0.4%, 1 /237). In contrast, the majority of outbreaks caused by GII.4 Sydney and GII.3 [P12] occurred in childcare facilities, 124 (68.1%, 124/182) and 139 (62.9% 139/221), respectively, followed by primary school with 23 (12.6%, 23/182) and 64 (29.0%, 64/221) outbreaks. 135 outbreaks of the new variant had clear routes of transmission, mainly human-to-human transmission (55.7%, 132/237), with food-borne transmission causing three outbreaks.

Nucleotide sequence analysis of GII.17[P17] genome

66 complete genome sequences of GII.17[P17] were obtained in this study, including 33 new strains from 2024, 25 strains from 2023, and 18 strains from 2022. Bayesian phylogenetic analysis revealed that the presence of four distinct clusters, 33 GII.17 norovirus strains isolated from 148 outbreaks in China in 2024 belongs to subcluster C2, alongside the reference strain OP805362.1/RO/2021. 43 strains isolated in China during 2022–2023 were classified into the Kawasaki308 strain cluster D (Fig. 3). Cluster A viruses initially appeared in the 1970s, with CS-E1 as its representative; cluster B viruses were most frequently isolated from 2005 to 2009, with Katrina-17 as its representative, yet viruses from both clusters continue to be detected into the 2020s as presented in the MCC tree (Fig. 3). Cluster C consists of two branches, including the Kawasaki323 like (C1, only prevalent from 2013 to 2016) and Romania-2021 like (C2, may evolved from the 2014 France strain and 2015 Canada strain). Cluster D represents the most frequently occurring group, containing sequence data from 2013 to 2024. Simultaneously, through Bayesian phylogenetic analysis, the average nucleotide substitution rate for the entire VP1 region was estimated at 1.8954 × 10− 3 nucleotide substitutions per site per year (95% HPD: 1.70 × 10− 3 − 2.09 × 10− 3).

Amino acid site analysis of GII.17[P17] norovirus

This study compared the amino acid sites (numbered according to GII.4 VA387 after sequence alignment) of norovirus strain GII.17 from 2024, 2022/23 and other previous years. When comparing the amino acid sequences of the representative strains of cluster A and cluster B, subcluster C2 had one deletion at position 377 (Tab S1). When subcluster C2, which includes the novel GII.17 variant, was compared with cluster D, five unique mutations were detected in antigenic epitopes A (296N, 297P, 298N), D (395N) and G (357R) (according to the corresponding positions in GII.4 VA387 [22]). In addition, subcluster C2 harbors one unique mutation at site I (346S) within the histo-blood group antigens (HBGAs) receptor binding site [23]. Furthermore, 7 substitutions (296N, 297P, 298N, 299P, 382R, 394N and 470E) showed a tendency to revert to the AB684681.2/JPN/1976 ancestor.

Discussion

The GII.17 genotype, first identified in 1970 [24], has spread globally and has been detected in Europe, the Americas, Asia, and Africa. There have been limited outbreaks of the genotype in the United States between 2009 and 2013 [25]. Environmental detections have been recorded in South Korea (2004–2008) and Kenya (2012–2013) [26, 27], and there was minor activity of GII.17 in France in 2013, but it did not displace the dominant strain [28]. However, by 2014/15, the number of GII.17[P17] detections surged, with a large number of reports across many countries such as China and Japan [29, 30]. Following a phase of comparatively low activity, Romania documented extensive norovirus outbreaks in 2021. Genotyping analysis revealed that the circulating strains were identified as belonging to a novel cluster within genotype GII.17[P17], distinct from previously known clusters [10]. It has been reported that GII.17 caused numerous norovirus outbreaks in Europe and North America during 2023–2024, and unexpectedly, the strains responsible for these outbreaks are closely related to the Romania-2021 strain [8]. Meanwhile, a number of outbreaks in China in 2024 was largely driven by the Romania-2021 strain, which rapidly overtook GII.4 Sydney as the dominant strain in May, consistent with reports from the US and UK [31, 32].

In the Northern Hemisphere, norovirus gastroenteritis typically peaks in winter. The 2014–15 GII.17 outbreaks followed this pattern [12], but in 2022–2023 and 2023–2024, over half of outbreaks clustered in March–May, and outbreaks surged between February and May 2023, deviating from the usual seasonality. While Lopman B linked norovirus increases to cold, dry conditions, low population immunity, and novel strains [33], China actually experienced relatively warm, humid weather in spring 2023.Given that the weather was warm and humid rather than cold and dry, the off-season surge is more likely explained by the change in COVID-19 control measures at the end of 2022: earlier restrictions had reduced asymptomatic norovirus infections [34], but the new policy brought increased mobility and lower prevention awareness, while possible sequelae such as immune impairment from COVID-19 may have further facilitated the resurgence. We found that the peak of norovirus outbreaks in 2024 was similar to that in 2023, and that in addition to inherent seasonality, the emergence of the GII.17 new variant may have driven an infection peak in March, and the significant increase in outbreaks in the second half of 2024 further underscores its impact.

Although GII.4 has been the dominant genotype globally for decades [4], norovirus epidemics in China from 2022 to 2024 were characterized by alternating genotypes. In the first halves of 2022 and 2023, GII.3[P12] predominated, while GII.4 Sydney dominated the second halves. In 2024, the pattern shifted: GII.7[P7] prevailed in the first half, and the GII.17[P17] dominated the second half. First detected in February 2024, the GII.17 C2 variant rapidly replaced the older D strain within two months and caused 249 outbreaks by December, compared with 110 for GII.3[P12], 49 for GII.4 Sydney, and 108 for GII.7[P7] over the same period, raising the possibility of a GII.4 era decline. Moreover, the resurgence of GII.17 is accompanied by new characteristics, whether this will, as hypothesized by Epifinavo, lead to the demise of the GII.4 Sydney era subsequent to the emergence of GII.17 [28].

Among GII.17 C2 outbreaks, 54.97% occurred in individuals aged 6–18 years, consistent with Li et al. [35] who detected the strain in 46.6% of cases aged 7–17. The C2 variant also infected preschool children, adults and seniors, corroborating Chan’s conclusion that GII.17 targets a broader age spectrum than GII.4 [29]. This age distribution likely reflects limited cross-protection from prior GII.4 infection, as reported by Du [36] and Dai [37], like the role of immune imprinting of COVID-19 [38]. Furthermore, the 6–18 age group lacks both maternal antibody protection and the broad immune memory of adults, while GII.17’s broader host range [29] may also contribute to its unique epidemiological profile. Over half of GII.17 C2 outbreaks arose in primary schools, consistent with Li’s surveillance study in Beijing [34]. In contrast, over half of GII.4 Sydney and GII.3[P12] outbreaks originated in childcare facilities, aligning with their distinct age-related infection profiles.

In this study, Bayesian phylogenetic analysis was only performed on the GII.17 capsid region, as this region contains antigenic epitopes and HBGA binding sites that drive viral evolution and variation. The MCC tree showed the 2024 Chinese strain evolved from the Romanian 2021 strain, both likely originating from the French 2014 strain (MK907790). Our analysis corroborates Tohma et al.’s findings: the new variant shares a major clade with previously prevalent Kawasaki323/308 strains but forms a newly diverged subcluster (C2) [11]. Subcluster C2 strains, including the Romanian 2021 isolate noted by Dinu [10], circulated only in Romania, UK, Netherlands, US, and Russia during 2021–2023, appearing in China only in 2024. GII.17 accumulates amino acid substitutions, contributing to antigenic drift and host-range expansion, unlike faster nucleotide substitution in GII.4 [11, 20]. The VP1 nucleotide substitution rate was estimated as 1.8954 × 10− 3 nucleotide substitutions/site/year (95% HPD: 1.70 × 10− 3 − 2.09 × 10− 3) differing from a 2022 study (1.255 × 10− 3 nucleotide substitutions/site/year, 95% HPD: 1.044 × 10− 3 − 1.501 × 10− 3) [39] but similar to Epifanova et al.’s study (1.90 × 10− 3, 95% HPD: 1.51 × 10− 3 − 2.30 × 10− 3) [9].

Relative to clusters A, B, and D, subcluster C2 carried three deletions (at positions 336, 377, and 390), which are adjacent to epitopes A, B, and C, respectively. In comparison with representative strains from 2014 to 2023, the 2024 novel strains harbored several substitutions and shifts within epitope sites (A, D, G) and HBGA binding site I. Previous studies on GII.4 norovirus have confirmed that positions 296, 297 and 409/411 are part of conformational antigen epitopes that are blocked by neutralizing antibodies [40, 41]. These changes may cause differences in the antigenicity of the cluster D strains and the novel GII.17 C2 variant (Romania-2021 like) from subcluster C2 contribute to evasion of previously acquired immunity to norovirus GII.17. Furthermore, the study by Tohma et al. found that the novel GII.17 C2 variant is characterized by extensive amino acid substitutions and frequent insertions and deletions in the surface-exposed loop regions of the VP1 P2 subdomain (in stark contrast to GII.4 and other major genotypes), along with a key adaptive mutation (K361R) in the capsid that broadens the virus’s binding profile to HBGAs, enabling infection of non-secretor individuals [11].

In conclusion, our study reveals that GII.17 has resurged replacing GII.4 as the predominant norovirus outbreak strain in multiple provinces of China during the second half of 2024, a decade after GII.17 variant caused increased norovirus outbreaks across Asia—including China—in the 2014/15 season. The phylogenetic analysis showed the novel GII.17 variant (Romania-2021 like) belonging to new subcluster C2 emerged in February and since then has been predominant variant completely replacing the previous variant Kawasaki308 clustered with D (2014–2024). Further study to assess their spread and determine any difference in clinical severity should be conducted, as well as continued surveillance is needed to determine if this genotype remains the dominant genotype.

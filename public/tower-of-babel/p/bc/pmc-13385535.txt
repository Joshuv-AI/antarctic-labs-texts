Entropy generation and response surface analysis of tri-hybrid casson nanofluid with nonlinear radiation over a rotating disk
Authors: Priyanka Pavuldass, P. Bala Anki Reddy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Trihybrid nanofluids possess promising applications in the biomedical, electronic, cosmetic, and materials processing fields owing to their enhanced thermophysical characteristics. This study examined the flow and thermal characteristics of a tri-hybrid (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Au - Ag - TiO_{2}$$\end{document})/blood Casson nanofluid over a rotating disk, influenced by nonlinear thermal radiation, focusing on the Darcy-Forchheimer effects, slip phenomena, Biot number, and internal heat generation or absorption. The governing equations were simplified to a system of ordinary differential equations by introducing appropriate similarity variables and solved numerically using the BVP4C solver. The computed Nusselt number values were compared with those from earlier investigations in the literature and showed strong consistency, demonstrating the reliability of the present numerical model. Additionally, a response surface approach was utilized to create a regression model that showed excellent agreement with the numerical data, with a coefficient of determination of (99.99%), indicating high predictive accuracy for the skin friction coefficient. The analysis showed that the nanoparticle concentration had the greatest impact among the studied parameters, contributing approximately (70–75%), while the magnetic parameter accounted for about (14–16%), and the Casson parameter contributed nearly (10–13%) to the variation in skin friction.

Introduction

Nanofluids have garnered significant interest in thermal science and engineering as prospective next-generation heat-transfer fluids. To make these fluids, solid particles that are less than 100 nm in size are mixed into basic fluids such as water, oil, or ethylene glycol. The addition of nanoparticles, such as those composed of metals, oxides, or carbon, modifies the thermophysical properties of the fluid, thereby improving its heat transfer performance. The concept of nanofluids was initially proposed by Choi [1], who explained how nanofluids can prevent the problems that occur when using conventional cooling fluids in heat transfer applications. Researchers have created hybrid nanofluids by mixing different types of nanoparticles into one fluid. This is based on the concept of nanofluids. This new method leverages the synergistic effects of several nanoparticles to make it better at carrying heat, more stable, and requires less energy than fluids produced from just one type of nanoparticle.

Hybrid nanofluids extend this concept by combining various types of nanoparticles, leading to synergistic enhancements in thermal conductivity and stability. The 3D steady thin-film dynamics of a hybrid nanofluid spreading across a tilted rotating disk, highlighting the complex interplay between rotation, inclination, and nanoparticle dispersion on the transfer of heat characteristics, was studied by Alharbi et al. [2]. Sakkaravarthi et al. [3] studied the influence of MHD and linear thermal radiation. To compare nanofluids and hybrid and tri-hybrid fluids with Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids, a Casson ternary hybrid nanofluid was flowed across a vertical porous plate. Likewise, a numerical investigation was carried out on the unsteady forced convection flow of a hybrid nanofluid exhibiting electrical conducting, consisting of Ag and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Al_{2} O_{3}$$\end{document} nanoparticles dispersed in water, over a spinning disc to analyse their thermal and flow behaviour by Mishra et al.[4]. In addition, Rehman et al. [5] presented a study of stable magnetohydrodynamics using the analysis of Casson hybrid nanofluid results under several stresses. In line with the emphasis on investigating the basic characteristics of simple, molecular, ionic, and complex liquids, they make a substantial contribution to our understanding of the configuration, intermolecular interactions, and flow behaviors of these liquids.

With continued advancements in nanotechnology, researchers have recently focused on trihybrid nanofluids, which blend three types of nanoparticles into a base fluid. These liquids exhibit greater efficiency in terms of heat transfer and flow properties compared to hybrid nanofluids, making them suitable for the design of thermal systems. Because of its unique benefits over traditional fluid models, the Casson fluid formulation is frequently used to explain the flow behavior of blood and other non-Newtonian fluids, as well as other real-life suspensions. The Casson fluid is particularly useful for replicating shear-thinning fluids such as jelly, honey, concentrated fruit juice, tomato sauce, and human blood because it is a non-Newtonian fluid with a nonlinear relationship between shear stress and velocity gradients. The flow in a rotating disk has been applied in various practical fields. These include storage devices in computers, lubrication systems, oceanography, rotating machinery and crystal generation. The flow of a Casson fluid can be modeled using a combination of a steady magnetic field, Ohmic dissipation, chemical reaction, and cylindrical coordinate system. Additionally, the disk rotation and Casson fluid parameters alter the nanoparticle concentration and contribute to the creation of entropy, which is essential for biomedicine and polymer engineering. Recent studies have further extended the analysis to the transfer of mass and energy phenomena, including the impact of activation energy, Hall currents, variable thermal conductivity, and mass diffusion in the 3-dimensional flow of Darcy-Forchheimer Casson nanofluids over adjustable, radially stretching, rotating disks. Motivation and novelty

The study of ternary hybrid nanofluids has emerged as a promising area in enhanced heat transfer technologies owing to their higher thermal conductivity than that of mono- and binary nanofluids. Their applications span energy systems, biomedical devices, cooling technologies, and industrial processes, where efficient thermal management is critical. The flow of nanofluids over rotating disks has been extensively studied; however, most of these studies only examine one or two nanofluids and ignore the combined effects of intricate physical phenomena such as Darcy-Forchheimer porous medium flow, nonlinear thermal radiation, Joule heating, viscous dissipation, and heat source/sink mechanisms.

The goal of this study is to close this gap and provide a more precise understanding of how different conditions affect the behavior of ternary hybrid nanofluids. The current study examines a Casson fluid model, convective boundary conditions, and the effects of velocity and thermal slip, providing a thorough examination of momentum, thermal, and nanoparticle transport across rotating disks. The novelty of this study lies in the combination of several advanced effects, including Darcy-Forchheimer porous media, nonlinear radiation effects, Joule heating, viscous heating, heat addition/removal, and slip conditions in the context of a ternary (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Au - Ag - TiO_{2}$$\end{document}) hybrid nanofluid. This holistic approach makes it easier to predict the variations in temperature, velocity, and nanoparticle concentration. This can aid in the design and improvement of real-world thermal systems. Further Response Surface Modeling of Tri-Hybrid Casson Nanofluid was employed to statistically analyze and optimize the impact of the governing physical aspects of the flow and thermal properties. This approach reduces computational effort while providing accurate predictive models, and the use of tri-hybrid Casson nanofluid ensures enhanced thermal performance.

Entropy generation analysis

Entropy analysis in fluid motion is primarily associated with irreversibilities arising from heat transfer, viscous dissipation, and other dissipative mechanisms within the system. The second law of thermodynamics states that the total entropy of a closed system either remains constant or increases but never decreases. Therefore, estimating the rate of entropy generation is essential, as a portion of the energy supplied to many industrial and technological processes is inevitably lost owing to these irreversibilities. Numerical methodology

A trihybrid numerical-intelligent technique is used to solve the nonlinear boundary layer systems that result from the initial PDEs. The governing equations were transformed using similarity variables to create a linked set of nonlinear ordinary differential equations with two-point boundary constraints. Closed-form solutions are impossible for these equations because they involve important physical phenomena such as magnetohydrodynamics, porous media, thermal radiation, heat sources/sinks, and viscous dissipation.

Response surface methodology (RSM)

A mathematical and statistical approach, RSM for modeling and optimization, was introduced by Box and Wilson in the year 1992 [37]. This approach is used for drag force, which has many applications in the fields of turbine blades, pumps, industrial mixing, aerodynamics, and biomedical flow modeling. The central composite design (CCD), which has captivated the technical community worldwide in recent years for optimization techniques, is the most popular design in RSM [38]. The CCD provides a methodical approach to enhance heat transfer systems, increasing their efficiency and reducing the need for trial-and-error techniques by utilizing both statistical modeling and rigorous experimental design. This approach is renowned for being dependable and successful in determining how various circumstances impact intended results. Its ability to examine several inputs simultaneously and provide a single solution is one of its most significant characteristics. Therefore, it is ideal for jobs involving modeling and optimization, as studied by [39]. A statistical experiment based on a common model of nonlinear polynomials is conducted to ascertain the interactions between the goal response variable and the distinct input variables [40]. [41] performed optimization and sensitivity analysis on thermal transfer and drag force related to blood-based gold nanoparticles. [42] Using the Carreau fluid framework, the study investigates blood-based silver nanoparticle nanofluid movement with microorganisms. Analysis of variance is an effective method for creating optimized regression models.

Sensitivity analysis

This section examines how small variations in the governing parameters influence the skin friction behavior of the system. Sensitivity analysis was used to assess how slight changes in one or more inputs affected the predicted shear stress response. In this context, skin friction sensitivity refers to the degree to which the skin friction coefficient responds to incremental modifications of the selected parameters. The findings of this analysis will help refine the present model and assist in designing more efficient flow and thermal systems.

Conclusion

In this investigation, the flow and thermal behavior of a tri-hybrid (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Au - Ag - TiO_{2}$$\end{document})/blood Casson nanofluid over a rotating disc, accounting for Darcy-Forchheimer porous resistance, with nonlinear thermal radiation, slip conditions, and internal heat generation/absorption. The ODEs are created from the governing PDEs using self-similarity transformations, and the solutions are obtained using BVP4C. The computed results are illustrated using graphical representations to enable a direct comparison between the hybrid and tri-hybrid nanofluid models. In both cases, the tri-hybrid nanofluid exhibited better thermal performance than the hybrid nanofluid, driven by its higher effective thermal conductivity. Subsequently, a response surface methodology (RSM) model was developed to predict the frictional drag coefficient based on the numerical data, and the corresponding results are presented graphically. Sensitivity analysis identified the nanoparticle volume fraction as the dominant contributor to skin friction, followed by the magnetic and Casson parameters. Overall, the combined inclusion of Casson rheology, nonlinear radiation, and slip effects provides a more realistic description of the rotating disk system, and the trihybrid nanofluid offers clear advantages for improved thermal control and biomedical heat transfer applications.

Limitations and future work

The analysis is based on similarity transformations and assumes laminar, axisymmetric flow, which may not fully represent the complex real-world conditions. The flow was assumed to be steady with a uniform dispersion of nanoparticles. Future research can be broadened by exploring other non-Newtonian fluid models, such as Jeffrey, tangent hyperbolic, Carreau, and Oldroyd-B fluids, in more complex geometries, including conical disks, curved channels, and rotating cavities. Time-dependent and transient flow behaviors can also be investigated to capture unsteady thermal fluid dynamics. Analytical and semi-analytical methods, such as KBM, HPM, OHAM, and ADM, can be applied for validation and comparison with numerical techniques. Studies may also extend to multiphase and reactive flows, variable thermophysical properties, and porous media with anisotropic permeabilities. Advanced ML models, such as PINNs, deep neural networks, LSTM networks for time-dependent predictions, Gaussian Process Regression, and hybrid ML, can further improve prediction capability when combined with physics solvers. Additionally, real-world applications include environmental engineering, biomedical cooling, drug delivery, heat exchangers, microfluidic devices and blood flow in human arteries. and renewable energy systems can be explored to broaden the practical impact of the model.

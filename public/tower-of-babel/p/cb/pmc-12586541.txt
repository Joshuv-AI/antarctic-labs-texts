Case report: First report of metastatic esophageal squamous cell carcinoma with EGFR p.S768I mutation: remarkable response to third-generation EGFR-TKI
Authors: Ying Liu, Cheng Wu, Lin Wang, Wu Sun, Baorui Liu, Wei Ren
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We present a 59-year-old male with metastatic esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) harboring an uncommon epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) exon 20 p.S768I mutation identified through liquid biopsy. Following progression on conventional therapies, the patient received the third-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), Firmonertinib. This treatment resulted in near-complete radiographic remission of primary esophageal lesions, mediastinal lymphadenopathy, pulmonary nodules, cerebral and hepatic metastases within six weeks, accompanied by rapid clinical improvement. This exceptional response underscores the clinical relevance of comprehensive molecular profiling in treatment-refractory ESCC and provides preliminary evidence supporting the therapeutic potential of EGFR-TKIs against rare EGFR mutations in advanced ESCC. This represents the first globally reported case of an ESCC patient with the rare EGFR p.S768I mutation who was successfully treated and achieved 4 months of near-complete response. Further validation in larger cohorts is needed to establish the efficacy of EGFR-TKIs in ESCC patients with rare EGFR mutations.

Introduction

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) ranks as the 11th most common cancer and the 7th leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally1. Despite advances in multimodal therapy, including chemoradiotherapy, preoperative neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy and minimally invasive esophagectomy, the 5-year survival rate remains stagnant at 30–47% for locally advanced disease and below 5% for metastatic cases2. This underscores the urgent need for precision medicine approaches in ESCC management.

The emergence of immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 axis has reshaped treatment paradigms. Pembrolizumab has demonstrated a 22% objective response rate (ORR) in PD-L1-positive advanced ESCC. However, durable responses are still elusive, and there is a critical need for predictive biomarkers beyond PD-L1 expression. Notably, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) alterations, which are well-established therapeutic targets in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), occur in approximately 3% of ESCC cases according to the TCGA database.

In NSCLC, the EGFR p.S768I mutation is a rare variant, accounting for 0.5–1.8% of EGFR-mutated cases, often alongside other mutations3. Structural studies demonstrate that this substitution creates steric hindrance within the ATP-binding pocket, conferring resistance to first-generation TKIs such as gefitinib and erlotinib. Although second-generation afatinib demonstrates superior ORR (100% vs. 38%) and progression-free survival (median PFS, 14.7 vs. 12.3 months) compared to third-generation osimertinib in rare EGFR-mutant tumors, both agents exhibit variable and generally limited efficacy against p.S768I4. Given the rarity of EGFR mutations in ESCC, this alteration remains poorly characterized, underscoring the urgent need for molecularly guided therapeutic development in this malignancy.

Here, we report the first documented case of metastatic ESCC harboring an EGFR p.S768I mutation achieving near-complete response to Firmonertinib, a novel third-generation EGFR-TKI. This case challenges conventional therapeutic paradigms and highlights the potential efficacy of Firmonertinib in ESCC patients with this specific mutation. It underscores the importance of comprehensive genomic profiling in guiding personalized treatment strategies in ESCC.

Results

Case report

In June 2024, a 59-year-old male presented with progressive dysphagia to solid foods accompanied by hoarseness. Initial upper endoscopy revealed circumferential mucosal irregularity with friable tissue and luminal narrowing located 28–33 cm from the incisors (Fig. 1A1). Histopathological examination of biopsies obtained at 30 cm demonstrated squamous cell carcinoma with moderate to poor differentiation. Approximately 1200 tumor cells and 120 non-tumor cells, including stromal cells, vascular endothelial cells, and lymphocytes, were identified, with tumor cells comprising approximately 90% of the total cellular population. Staging evaluation included non-contrast chest and abdominal computed tomography (CT) and head CT scans, which showed no metastases in the lungs, liver and brain (Fig. 1A2–4), but revealed a 5.8 cm esophageal mass with adjacent lymphadenopathy (Fig. 1A5). Positron emission tomography-CT confirmed an FDG-avid primary esophageal lesion (SUVmax 12.9) with confluent mediastinal nodal metastases (SUVmax 12.3). Starting on July 5, 2024, the patient received two cycles of first-line induction chemoimmunotherapy consisting of nab-paclitaxel (220 mg/m² day 1), carboplatin (AUC 5 day 1) and camrelizumab (200 mg day 1) per 3 weeks. Restaging CT scans in September 2024 showed near partial response according to RECIST 1.1 criteria, but the disease remained unresectable after multidisciplinary team review. Subsequently, starting on September 23, 2024, the patient underwent two cycles of second-line chemoimmunotherapy involving paclitaxel polymer micelles (230 mg/m² day 1), nedaplatin (80 mg/m² day 1), and tislelizumab (200 mg day 1) per 3 weeks. To reverse the inhibitory immune microenvironment, the therapy was combined with low-dose immunomodulatory radiotherapy (RT, 6 Gy in 3 fractions) per 3 weeks, targeting the primary tumor and the metastatic lymph nodes. In November 2024, disease progression manifested with worsening dysphagia requiring enteral nutrition support. Afterward, the patient was treated with definitive RT (50 Gy in 25 fractions), concurrent with 2 cycles of third-line chemoimmunotherapy consisting of irinotecan liposome (50 mg/m² day 1), cisplatin (75 mg/m² day 1) and tislelizumab (200 mg day 1) per 3 weeks. The final immunotherapy dose was administered on December 23, 2024. However, the patient developed new-onset weakness in the right limb. Imaging reassessment demonstrated progression of the primary esophageal lesion with new metastatic foci in the lungs, liver, and brain (Fig. 1B1–5). The efficacy assessment confirmed progressive disease (PD), resulting in tumor upstaging to stage IVB.

Integrated genomic profiling was performed on paired samples: primary tumor tissue from the initial endoscopic biopsy in June 2024 and post-progression plasma samples in January 2025. Using the GeneseeqPrime™ assay (Nanjing Geneseeq Technology Inc., Nanjing, China), distinct molecular signatures were uncovered. Given the failure of previous therapies and the detection of the EGFR mutation, the patient was started on fourth-line therapy with Firmonertinib (160 mg daily) on January 28, 2025. Following initiation of the molecular therapy, the patient exhibited significant clinical improvement, including resolution of right-sided limb weakness within 48 h of treatment initiation. Surveillance endoscopy on March 12, 2025, demonstrated that mucosal achieved an improved state (Fig. 1C1). The specimen showed extensive degeneration and necrosis with accompanying inflammatory exudates. Histopathological analysis revealed expansive necrosis containing approximately 50 degenerated tumor cells and 1500 non-neoplastic cells (predominantly neutrophils, stromal cells, and lymphocytes), with tumor cells constituting 3.3% of the total cellular population. Radiological assessments, including cervicothoracic-abdominal CT and contrast-enhanced cranial magnetic resonance imaging, demonstrated a near-complete response according to RECIST 1.1 criteria (Fig. 1C2–5). Treatment-emergent adverse events included grade 3 pneumonitis (per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events [CTCAE] version 5.0), which required management with mycophenolate mofetil and a tapered prednisone regimen. Unfortunately, it was confirmed that the patient had experienced disease progression on May 27, 2025. The PFS was 4 months. Currently, the patient is still alive, treated with a combination of Firmonertinib and anlotinib.

Discussion

In this case report, we document the first known case of ESCC harboring an EGFR p.S768I mutation achieving a near-complete response to the third-generation EGFR-TKI, Firmonertinib. This remarkable outcome challenges the conventional understanding of EGFR alterations in ESCC and underscores the importance of comprehensive genomic profiling in guiding personalized therapy.

Globally, EGFR mutations are reported in approximately 3% of ESCC cases. Existing literature indicates that common EGFR alterations in esophageal cancer include overexpression (50–68.2%), amplification (7–14%), mutations, and structural variations like tandem kinase domain duplication5. The EGFR p.S768I mutation is exceptionally rare in ESCC. Moreover, emerging evidence suggests that tumors harboring EGFR mutations and EGFR duplication may exhibit primary resistance to immunotherapy or even hyper-progression due to the immunosuppressive microenvironment fostered by EGFR activation6.

Existing studies have reported the effectiveness of second-generation EGFR-TKIs, such as afatinib, against rare EGFR mutations (e.g., p.S768I and p.G719X) in esophageal cancer. In addition, afatinib is well-tolerated in ESCC patients with EGFR overexpression, achieving a promising objective response rate of 39% and a median overall survival of 7.8 months7. However, there is limited data on third-generation TKIs in metastatic esophageal cancer.

Also, a critical challenge in treating brain metastases with targeted therapies is the limited blood-brain barrier penetration, which restricts intracranial drug bioavailability. Firmonertinib shows a potent ability to penetrate the blood-brain barrier, making it more effective against central nervous system metastases8,9. In lung cancer, Firmonertinib has shown promising results in patients with non-classic EGFR mutations, supporting its use for mutations like p.S768I8. Our patient exhibited rapid symptom resolution and substantial radiographic response following Firmonertinib treatment, which supports the cross-indication use of the drug and suggests the potential effectiveness of third-generation EGFR-TKIs in ESCC patients harboring rare EGFR mutations.

In a real-world cohort of 48 patients with NSCLC, high-dose Firmonertinib (240 mg once daily) demonstrated clinically meaningful survival benefits in leptomeningeal metastases, with a median overall survival of 8.43 months. Notably, patients who developed grade 3 adverse events maintained favorable tolerability following dose reduction to 160 mg once daily10. So, in this case, a dose of 160 mg once daily was administered.

Severe pneumonitis is a known risk factor when EGFR-TKIs are administered after PD-1 inhibitors, for instance, a study utilizing the US Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event Reporting System database found that the combination of nivolumab and EGFR-TKIs significantly increased the incidence of interstitial pneumonitis compared to either treatment alone, with an adjusted odds ratio of 4.3111. This suggests a synergistic effect that heightens the risk of severe pulmonary toxicity. However, the emergence of brain metastases and the rapid progression of esophageal obstruction presented immediate life-threatening conditions. After a multidisciplinary consultation and comprehensive risk disclosure, the patient provided written informed consent for the urgent initiation of TKI therapy. During the course of treatment, severe pneumonitis occurred, and prompt intervention, guided by established protocols for immunosuppression and supportive care, resulted in symptomatic resolution and radiographic improvement. This case highlights the critical importance of real-time monitoring in sequential PD-1/TKI therapy.

The discrepancy between tissue and liquid biopsy results may arise from three mechanisms: tumor heterogeneity (spatial divergence between primary and metastatic subclones with low-frequency mutations undetectable in tissue biopsies but reflected in ctDNA, and temporal treatment-induced adaptive evolution of metastases12), technical limitations (suboptimal tumor cellularity <20% or necrosis in tissue samples versus systemic ctDNA representation in advanced disease), and clonal selection where treatment eliminates wild-type clones leaving mutated clones with survival advantages. This highlights the potential for actionable mutations in metastases even when primary tumors are driver-negative, underscoring the importance of molecular re-biopsy and evolution-guided therapy13. A recent case report demonstrating dynamic HER2 expression over time in esophageal adenocarcinoma further supports this principle, suggesting similar EGFR mutational heterogeneity may influence treatment outcomes14.

This case provides novel insights into EGFR-targeted therapies for ESCC. While the findings are promising, broader validation through large studies and clinical trials is necessary to clarify the clinical significance and inform therapeutic development of rare EGFR mutations in ESCC and across diverse tumor types.

This case represents the first documented instance of ESCC with an EGFR p.S768I mutation achieving a near-complete response to the third-generation EGFR-TKI Firmonertinib. Our findings challenge the longstanding exclusion of EGFR-targeted therapies in ESCC management, emphasizing their potential in treating rare mutations like p.S768I to drive exceptional responses even after multiple lines of therapy. This underscores the critical need for comprehensive genomic profiling in guiding personalized treatment strategies for ESCC. By highlighting the potential actionability of EGFR mutations in ESCC, this report advocates for mandatory genomic profiling in refractory cases. Future research should prioritize ESCC-specific mutation databases and conduct trials to evaluate the efficacy of TKIs in molecularly selected populations.

Methods

Patient consent

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital (Approval No.: 2025-0226-01). Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for both participation in this study and the publication of this case report and any accompanying data.

Next-generation sequencing

Genomic profiling was conducted using a customized 437-cancer-gene panel (covering SNVs, Indels, CNVs and structural rearrangements) on paired tumor tissue and plasma samples. Endoscopic biopsy-derived FFPE tissue underwent DNA extraction with the QIAamp DNA FFPE Kit (Qiagen Cat. No. 56404), while progression-phase plasma cfDNA was isolated from 10 mL EDTA-anticoagulated blood (centrifuged at 1800×g for 10 min) using the Circulating Nucleic Acid Kit (Qiagen Cat. No. 55114), with matched whole blood germline controls processed via DNeasy Blood Kit (Qiagen Cat. No. 69504). Libraries were prepared through hybridization capture with xGen Lockdown Probes (Integrated DNA Technologies) and sequenced on Illumina HiSeq4000 (2 × 150 bp). Bioinformatics analysis commenced with BWA-MEM alignment to hg19 reference, followed by VarScan2-based detection of somatic variants at minimum variant allele frequencies of 1% for tissue and 0.1% for plasma cfDNA. Copy number alterations were called using CNVkit, with depth ratio thresholds set at ≥2.0 (gain) and <0.6 (loss) for tissue, and ≥1.6/ < 0.6 for cfDNA. All variants underwent ANNOVAR annotation with germline filtration against control samples and exclusion of polymorphisms (>1% frequency in 1000 G/ExAC databases). Fusions were detected by DELLY [PMID: 22962449] with a minimum split read threshold of 3.

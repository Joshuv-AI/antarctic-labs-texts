Aggregated decision system for financial resilience and performance enhancement
Authors: Xinci Tian, Yueyue Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOrganisations functioning in the current unpredictable financial and regulatory environment must formulate strategies that bolster financial resilience while simultaneously improving operational performance amid uncertainty. This study presents an integrated decision-making framework that merges advanced fuzzy logic with robust multi-criteria decision-making techniques to facilitate complex strategic evaluations. Several novel aggregation operators (AOs) are proposed to effectively capture expert hesitation, ambiguity, and nonlinear interactions among criteria. These include the q-rung orthopair fuzzy softmax-Einstein interactive weighted geometric (q-ROFSEIWG) operator and its other types of operators. The operators utilise softmax functions and Einstein interaction to more accurately represent real-world decision-making behaviour. The significance of each evaluation criterion is determined through the logarithmic percentage change-driven objective weighting (LOPCOW) method, which assesses variability in normalised data to facilitate a scale-sensitive and data-driven weighting approach. The MARCOS method is employed to rank strategic alternatives by measuring each option against ideal and anti-ideal solutions, facilitating a thorough utility-based evaluation. The proposed approach offers a flexible and reliable framework for the evaluation of complex decision problems, yielding insights that support consistent, data-driven, and uncertainty-aware decision-making in dynamic, multi-dimensional environments.Keywords: q-rung orthopair fuzzy numbers, Einstein interactive, Aggregation operators, Uncertainty modeling, Financial resilience, Performance enhancementSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The concept of financial resilience has become significantly important in an increasingly volatile and interconnected global economy1. Financial resilience denotes an entity’s capacity–be it individual, organisational, or systemic to anticipate, prepare for, respond to, and recover from financial shocks, all while sustaining or enhancing its performance. Given the ongoing economic uncertainty and financial risks across various sectors, improving financial resilience has emerged as a crucial goal for policymakers and practitioners. Recent research has highlighted the multifaceted characteristics of financial resilience, incorporating elements such as cognitive abilities, financial literacy, digital skills, and organisational agility2. Mundi and Vashisht3 emphasise the critical importance of cognitive abilities in influencing individual financial resilience, especially within emerging markets. The findings indicate that individuals with greater cognitive capacities are more adept at processing financial information, making informed decisions, and adapting to economic challenges. Mishra et al.4 emphasise the significance of digital financial literacy, particularly for women, illustrating that increased digital competency results in better financial decision-making and resilience. At the organisational level, resilience is frequently linked to performance outcomes. Crespo et al.5 performed a systematic literature review, revealing a strong correlation between financial resilience and organisational continuity, as well as long-term value creation. The study indicates that firms with strong resilience frameworks are more capable of enduring financial distress, preserving investor confidence, and continuing operations during economic downturns. Zahedi et al.6 provide a detailed classification of the primary factors influencing financial resilience, including risk management practices, capital adequacy, adaptive capacity, and leadership preparedness. The connection between financial literacy and sustainable performance has garnered considerable academic attention. Seraj et al.7 and Alshebami and Murad8 identify entrepreneurial resilience as a significant mediator between financial knowledge and business sustainability. Research among Saudi entrepreneurs indicates that resilient entrepreneurs are able to utilise financial literacy more effectively to manage market uncertainties and maintain business performance over time. In supply chain management, resilience serves two functions. Tufan et al.9 analyse the impact of agility and resilience in supply chains on financial performance, especially in dynamic and crisis-prone contexts. Their findings demonstrate that organisations investing in flexible and responsive supply chain structures reduce vulnerability to disruptions while enhancing profitability and competitiveness. Although the literature on financial resilience is expanding, a comprehensive framework that incorporates cognitive, behavioural, structural, and technological dimensions to assess resilience and its effects on performance across various sectors is still necessary. This research seeks to address the existing gap by presenting a comprehensive model for evaluating financial resilience and improving performance, utilising interdisciplinary insights and empirical data. This research presents a comprehensive decision-support framework aimed at assessing financial resilience and improving performance through the application of fuzzy logic and multi-criteria decision-making techniques. The primary innovation is the proposed operator, which consolidates expert judgements by addressing the uncertainty and interdependence among evaluation criteria using q-ROFSs and Einstein interaction. The LOPCOW method is utilised to achieve objective weighting of criteria, providing a data-driven approach to assess the impact of each criterion based on the variability of performance values. The MARCOS method ranks alternatives by comparing them to ideal and anti-ideal solutions, providing a balanced and practical compromise-based decision. This hybrid approach enhances financial decision-making by integrating expert linguistic evaluations with quantitative performance data, facilitating the clear prioritisation of strategies or policies. The proposed model is well-suited for complex environments that necessitate adaptive planning and strategic foresight, exemplified by financial resilience analysis. The integration of advanced fuzzy aggregation with structured multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) provides a framework that assists policymakers and analysts in identifying optimal strategies amidst uncertainty and changing market conditions. This paper aims to establish a comprehensive decision-support framework for assessing and improving financial resilience and performance in uncertain and dynamic contexts. Literature review

Modelling uncertainties in multi-criteria decision-making techniques is crucial for addressing real-world problems in contemporary science. Numerous MCDM techniques have been established to assess the robustness of human decision-making by evaluating a set of alternatives against multiple criteria. As a result, alternatives are ranked, leading to the selection of an optimal option from the feasible alternatives. MCDM is a widely utilised cognitive tool designed to facilitate decision-making among a limited set of alternatives based on the preference information supplied by decision-makers. MCDM methods often exhibit ambiguity and inaccuracy due to the complexities inherent in human reasoning, posing challenges for decision-makers during the evaluation process. The rapid increase in complications within the socio-economic environment, coupled with insufficient knowledge and vague information, complicates the decision-making process for decision makers (DMs). The issue of vague information has emerged as a significant concern over the past few decades. Zadeh10 pioneered the concept of fuzzy set theory to address these issues. Atanassov11 introduced the concept of intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFS), while Yager12–14 expanded upon this by presenting Pythagorean fuzzy sets (PFS) as an extension of IFS. Yager15 further generalises the concepts of IFS and PFS, introducing the novel concept of q-ROFS. The characteristic of a q-ROFN is that the sum of the qth power of the membership degree (MD) and the qth power of the non-membership degree (NMD) is always less than or equal to one. The increased value of q results in a larger orbit for the selection of q-ROFNs, enabling a more comprehensive expression of vague information. Garg16 introduced the concept of connection number based q-ROFSs and their application in multi-criteria decision making (MCDM). Liu and Wang17 introduced fundamental AOs of q-ROFNs. Peng et al.18 presented exponential operations and AOs for q-ROFSs. Jana et al.19 introduced the concept of certain Dombi AOs in relation to q-ROFNs and their application in multi-criteria decision-making. Wei et al.20 presented Heronian mean AOs for q-ROFNs. The LOPCOW method and the MARCOS technique have garnered considerable interest in the domain of MCDM, owing to their complementary advantages in weighting and ranking alternatives, respectively. LOPCOW has gained significant traction as an objective weighting method, notably for its capacity to reflect the variation and importance of criteria among alternatives without dependence on expert judgements. Ecer and Pamucar21 utilised a LOPCOW-DOBI framework to evaluate sustainability performance in the banking sector, illustrating the method’s effectiveness in addressing performance dispersion. Nurdin et al.22 introduced an enhanced LOPCOW-SAW method for optimal supplier selection, highlighting its efficacy in supply chain management. Ulutaş et al.23 integrated LOPCOW within a grey environment, in conjunction with PSI and MACONT, to assess third-party logistics providers. In contrast, Dhruva et al.24 employed LOPCOW alongside Fermatean fuzzy sets and CoCoSo for the selection of cloud vendors in healthcare, demonstrating the model’s versatility in fuzzy and personalised decision-making contexts. Dündar25 employed LOPCOW alongside CRADIS for performance analysis in Turkish universities, thereby confirming its applicability across diverse domains. The MARCOS method, introduced by Stević et al.27, effectively models compromise solutions by incorporating both ideal and anti-ideal alternatives, thereby providing a reliable and transparent ranking process. Chakraborty et al.26 employed a D-MARCOS method for supplier selection within the iron and steel sector, whereas Akram et al.28 adapted MARCOS to a 2-tuple linguistic q-rung picture fuzzy framework to address more intricate group decision contexts. Jayakumar et al.29 utilised MARCOS within a linear diophantine fuzzy framework to assess IoT-related risks, while Mishra et al.30 implemented it in conjunction with AOs under Pythagorean fuzzy logic for circular supplier selection. Radovanović et al.31 demonstrated the effectiveness of Grey MARCOS in evaluating university professors when combined with spherical fuzzy AHP, highlighting MARCOS’ flexibility in integrating both subjective and objective inputs. While the majority of studies utilise LOPCOW and MARCOS independently, their combined application offers a promising avenue for future research. The objectivity of LOPCOW in weight determination, along with the robust compromise ranking of MARCOS, can yield a balanced and efficient decision-making framework. The increasing interest in hybrid models indicates opportunities for further investigation, especially in domains that necessitate transparent, data-driven decision-making in the face of uncertainty.

Motivation

In the current volatile and interconnected global economy, financial systems and organisations face frequent challenges from sudden disruptions, including economic crises, pandemics, geopolitical conflicts, and environmental risks. These disruptions jeopardise the financial resilience and the sustainability of long-term performance of institutions. Financial resilience, defined as the ability to absorb shocks, adapt, and recover, encompasses not only financial stability but also strategic foresight and effective decision-making. Despite the significance of this objective, conventional decision-making tools frequently depend on inflexible assumptions, precise data inputs, and restricted aggregation methods that do not adequately reflect the complex and uncertain characteristics of financial environments. Traditional AOs, including weighted average (WA), ordered weighted average (OWA), and their geometric counterparts, have demonstrated utility; however, they exhibit limitations in accommodating imprecise, ambiguous, and interrelated criteria in uncertain contexts. Furthermore, these methods fail to accurately represent dynamic expert judgements and inadequately address trade-offs among competing financial priorities. Current models fail to account for the behavioural and nonlinear traits of financial variables, which are essential in stress testing and resilience assessments. This study introduces a novel aggregation-based decision-making system aimed at improving financial resilience and performance analysis, addressing existing limitations. This approach combines advanced fuzzy logic with robust multi-criteria decision-making tools, specifically LOPCOW and MARCOS. The proposed method quantifies both subjective expert input and objective data trends, while also addressing nonlinear behaviour, trade-offs, and uncertainty in financial evaluations. The framework aims to assist policymakers, financial analysts, and enterprise managers in identifying strategic initiatives that enhance financial resilience and optimise operational performance. This case study presents various strategic alternatives and a detailed set of resilience-based criteria, illustrating the practical applicability and efficacy of the proposed model. This motivation arises from the growing demand for adaptive, transparent, and intelligent decision-support systems that assist in navigating complex financial environments, ensuring short-term stability and long-term sustainability.

Statement of the problem

In the financial and operational landscape of 2025, enterprises encounter increased volatility, ongoing global disruptions, and heightened regulatory scrutiny, particularly regarding sustainability, digital transformation, and environmental-social-governance (ESG) compliance. Mid-sized enterprises face significant challenges, as they are crucial to national economies yet frequently experience limited financial resources, restricted access to capital markets, and dependence on fragile supply chains. Conventional decision-making instruments for financial planning and performance enhancement are inadequate in this intricate context, as they generally prioritise cost or profit goals and depend on clear, deterministic assumptions. These approaches do not adequately address the multi-dimensional aspects of resilience, such as cybersecurity preparedness, ESG performance, and innovation readiness. Additionally, they neglect the uncertainty, vagueness, and hesitancy that characterise expert judgements in real-world crises. To address this gap, an aggregated decision support system (ADSS) is essential for integrating financial and non-financial criteria, managing conflicting expert preferences, and effectively modelling uncertainty. The integration of q-ROFSs offers a robust mathematical framework for representing ambiguity and hesitation in expert judgements. Hybrid MCDM techniques such as LOPCOW and MARCOS provide systematic approaches for criteria weighting and alternative assessment. The effectiveness of these methods is contingent upon the ability of AOs to synthesise information while maintaining interaction and uncertainty characteristics. The operators improve the system’s capacity to aggregate expert evaluations by systematically capturing nonlinear relationships, compensatory effects, and differing levels of optimism or pessimism. This study focusses on the design and validation of an ADSS that incorporates q-ROFSs, LOPCOW, MARCOS, and the proposed q-ROFSEI-based AOs to enhance robust and resilient strategic planning. A case study grounded in real-world scenarios illustrates how the system aids mid-sized enterprises in making informed, transparent, and future-oriented decisions amid uncertainty, thereby enhancing their financial resilience and overall performance in a dynamic economic environment.

Comparison analysis

The comparative analysis in Table 10 demonstrates the effectiveness and consistency of the proposed AOs, q-ROFSEIWG and Gq-ROFSEIWG, in MCDM scenarios within q-ROF environments. A thorough comparison was conducted with various advanced AOs established in recent literature. All compared operators, including q-ROFWA and q-ROFWG by Liu and Wang17, q-ROFWEA by Peng et al.18, q-ROFDWA and q-ROFDWG by Jana et al.19, and q-ROFWBM and q-ROFWGBM by Liu and Liu54, identified as the most suitable or optimal alternative. The consistency observed across different methods highlights the reliability of the decision structure and strengthens confidence in the results obtained. The observed variations indicate the impact of diverse aggregation mechanisms and weighting strategies. The proposed operators align well with existing methods, while offering improved flexibility and discriminatory power through the incorporation of entropy-based significance and evidential weighting. Gq-ROFSEIWG, which generalises evidential influence within q-ROFSs, exhibits strong ranking performance and ensures consistent decision outcomes in complex environments. The findings indicate that the proposed operators maintain the decision stability identified in previous models while providing a more refined method for addressing uncertainty, particularly when decision-makers exhibit differing degrees of confidence or hesitancy in their assessments. While TOPSIS was utilised in the comparative analysis for thoroughness, it is not fully appropriate for the proposed operators. The primary reason is that TOPSIS depends on linear distance measures from the ideal and anti-ideal solutions, potentially oversimplifying the intricate uncertainty represented by q-rung orthopair fuzzy environments. The proposed operators integrate semantic interrelationships and information entropy among attributes, which are inadequately captured by basic Euclidean closeness measures56. Additionally, TOPSIS exhibits sensitivity to data normalisation and the relative scaling of criteria, which may result in rank reversals in contexts characterised by high uncertainty or imprecision in decision-making. The proposed q-ROFSEIWG and Gq-ROFSEIWG operators offer a more stable and flexible mechanism for information aggregation in these environments. Thus, although the incorporation of TOPSIS indicates consistency in the optimal result, it underscores that distance-based models alone are inadequate for fully capturing the comprehensive information processed by the proposed framework.

Discussion of ranking

The final ranking of the alternatives provides essential insights into the strategic priorities that mid-sized manufacturing firms must adopt to maintain financial resilience in a volatile economic environment. The leading alternative is AI-driven financial forecasting (), which exhibits exceptional performance across all assessment criteria. This method utilises machine learning and data analytics to provide real-time forecasts of financial trends. The system’s efficacy is rooted in its capacity to analyse both historical and real-time financial data for the purposes of risk forecasting, anomaly detection, and budget optimisation. AI forecasting enhances financial agility and preparedness by allowing firms to act proactively instead of reactively. Subsequently, digital twin for financial simulation () and integrated ERP-FinTech systems () are examined. The digital twin methodology allows organisations to develop virtual representations of their financial systems, facilitating the simulation of various scenarios to evaluate the effects of internal or external disruptions. This facilitates stress testing and assists in developing data-driven strategies. Nonetheless, its implementation necessitates sophisticated digital infrastructure and expert management. ERP-FinTech integration facilitates a seamless exchange of data between operational and financial processes. This synergy minimises manual errors, improves transparency, and accelerates the speed and precision of financial decision-making. is essential for firms seeking to modernise their financial ecosystem. Green financing and ESG compliance () receive a moderate ranking in the evaluation. ESG metrics are increasingly impacting investor decisions; however, their financial advantages tend to manifest over the long term and require a strategic approach. offers significant benefits for companies aiming to meet sustainability objectives and engage impact investors; however, it may not yield immediate returns unless integrated into comprehensive strategic frameworks. Blockchain-enabled trade finance () receives a moderate ranking. It ensures secure, tamper-resistant transactions and improves transparency in trade finance. Nonetheless, its implementation is obstructed by legal, regulatory, and scalability challenges, especially for companies functioning in regions with inadequate blockchain preparedness. Dynamic inventory and supply chain diversification () and cybersecurity investment in financial systems () are ranked marginally lower. Cost optimisation through lean practices ranks lowest (). While lean practices have traditionally emphasised operational efficiency, their emphasis on cost reduction may neglect potential avenues for innovation and investment. In a digitalised and rapidly evolving environment, lean methods may lack the necessary flexibility and adaptability for achieving sustainable financial improvement. The ranking demonstrates a distinct strategic transition: companies that emphasise predictive analytics, system integration, and simulation tools are more inclined to establish robust financial frameworks. Conventional cost-saving strategies are being eclipsed by advanced technologies that facilitate real-time decision-making and scenario analysis. Manufacturing firms must invest in digital transformation strategies to achieve adaptability, agility, and long-term financial sustainability.

Limitations of the model

The proposed decision-making model for assessing financial resilience alternatives in mid-sized manufacturing firms, while robust, has certain limitations that must be recognised. The model predominantly depends on expert judgement for criterion weighting, potentially introducing subjectivity and bias, even with the application of structured techniques. Secondly, the analysis presumes stable and consistent data availability; however, in practical situations, data may be incomplete, uncertain, or inconsistent, particularly in rapidly evolving financial contexts. Additionally, the model fails to explicitly consider the interdependence or correlation among criteria, potentially impacting the overall accuracy of the decision-making process. The complexity of implementation and the differing maturity levels of firms in relation to digital transformation are not adequately addressed, which may impact the practical feasibility of the highest-ranked options. The model is context-specific and may necessitate recalibration prior to application in different industries or regions, thereby restricting its generalisability.

Conclusion

This study presents a decision support framework for assessing digital transformation alternatives to improve financial resilience in mid-sized manufacturing companies. In a context marked by market volatility, supply chain disruptions, and technological uncertainty, financial resilience is identified as a critical determinant of sustained organisational performance. The proposed multi-criteria decision-making approach incorporates objective and subjective evaluations across economic, operational, technological, and strategic dimensions, offering a systematic framework for comparing and prioritising digital transformation strategies. A novel family of aggregation operators, the q-ROF Einstein interactive geometric AOs, has been established to address imprecise, uncertain, and interactive information in q-ROF environments, thereby enhancing the functionality of traditional aggregation methods. This framework was applied to eight digital transformation alternatives, identifying AI-driven financial forecasting (), digital twins for financial simulation (), and integrated ERP-FinTech systems () as comparatively more effective strategies in the analysed case. The alternatives exhibited strengths in predictive capability, data integration, and real-time adaptability. Conversely, conventional cost-reduction strategies like lean practices () received a lower ranking, indicating a transition from reactive cost management to proactive resilience enhancement. The results, although promising, warrant cautious interpretation. The findings are contingent upon a defined set of criteria, alternatives, and expert evaluations, potentially constraining their generalisability. Furthermore, the interdependencies among criteria and the dynamic changes in external environments were not thoroughly examined. Future study could enhance the framework by integrating expert consensus mechanisms, modelling dynamic inter-criteria relationships, and validating the approach in various industrial contexts. The integration of advanced uncertainty-modelling tools, such as fuzzy sets and intuitionistic extensions, alongside data-driven approaches like deep learning, may enhance its applicability. A significant future direction of this study is the incorporation of the proposed MCDM framework into intelligent decision support systems utilising deep learning technologies. The current approach effectively captures expert hesitation, ambiguity, and nonlinear interactions. However, deep learning can improve this by automatically extracting complex patterns and hidden relationships from extensive financial and operational datasets. This would allow the framework to adapt dynamically as new data emerges, addressing the limitations of dependence on expert-defined criteria. In a hybrid system, deep learning contributes predictive accuracy and scalability, whereas the MCDM framework guarantees transparency, interpretability, and organised aggregation of decision criteria. The integration would produce a robust decision-support system capable of real-time classification, prioritisation, and resilience evaluation, thus enhancing its relevance in unstable financial and regulatory contexts.

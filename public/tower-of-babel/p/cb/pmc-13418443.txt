Patient-reported outcomes in Chinese patients with AQP4-IgG–positive neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder on inebilizumab therapy: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Hexiang Yin, Mengdi Hao, Yan Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe treatment of neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) has entered the era of targeted biological agents. While relapse control has improved substantially, patient-reported outcomes (PROs) remain understudied. This study aimed to explore real-world patient-reported perceptions of inebilizumab therapy in AQP4-IgG–positive NMOSD.MethodsWe conducted a nationwide cross-sectional survey of AQP4-IgG–positive NMOSD patients receiving inebilizumab using an anonymous online questionnaire (April–September 2025). Data collected included demographics, medication history, and multidimensional quality-of-life changes following inebilizumab initiation. Descriptive statistics and correlation analyses were performed. Key outcomes were patient-reported without objective validation.ResultsA total of 275 valid questionnaires were analyzed. Respondents were predominantly female (90.5%) with a mean age of 39.7 ± 11.8 years. Mean inebilizumab treatment duration was 14.5 ± 8.4 months. Of all patients, 33.1% received inebilizumab as initial maintenance therapy, 42.9% had previously used conventional immunosuppressants, and 24.0% had switched from other biologics. Among respondents, 92.4% reported meaningful symptom improvement across multiple domains including bodily pain and mental health. Overall, 96.4% expressed willingness to continue therapy, with symptom improvement cited as the predominant reason (91.7%). Correlation analysis revealed weak associations between treatment duration and satisfaction (r = 0.12, p < 0.05) and between medication cost burden and satisfaction (r = − 0.24, p < 0.01).ConclusionsIn this cross-sectional survey, Chinese patients with AQP4-IgG–positive NMOSD receiving inebilizumab reported high satisfaction, perceived multidimensional benefit, and strong treatment continuation willingness. These findings provide exploratory patient-reported evidence regarding the acceptability of inebilizumab in routine practice. However, the observed improvements are self-reported and associative rather than causal; interpretation should account for the cross-sectional, non-comparative design, potential selection and recall biases, and lack of objective outcome validation. Background

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) is an autoimmune disease characterized by inflammatory demyelinating lesions of the central nervous system. Its pathogenesis is predominantly mediated by aquaporin-4 (AQP4) antibodies, detected in over 80% of patients, which ultimately lead to astrocyte injury and subsequent neuronal damage [1]. Clinical manifestations include visual impairment, limb weakness, and bowel/bladder dysfunction. The disease is characterized by high relapse rates and significant disability [1], predominantly affecting young and middle-aged adults, and severely compromising patients’ quality of life while imposing a substantial burden on families and society. Disability progression in NMOSD is closely linked to disease relapses; consequently, preventive maintenance therapy constitutes the cornerstone of long-term disease management. Standardized maintenance therapy can effectively reduce relapse frequency and delay disability progression, rendering the selection of safe and efficacious regimens critical for optimizing long-term prognosis.

A variety of regimens have been employed for NMOSD maintenance therapy. Historically, long-term management has relied heavily on off-label medications (e.g., glucocorticoids, immunosuppressants), which are associated with uncertain efficacy and considerable adverse effects [2, 3], resulting in suboptimal treatment adherence and impeding the attainment of long-term therapeutic goals [4–5]. Inebilizumab, the first humanized anti-CD19 monoclonal antibody approved globally for NMOSD, exerts its therapeutic effect by selectively depleting CD19⁺ B cells and plasmablasts, thereby blocking the generation of pathogenic AQP4 antibodies [6]. In March 2022, inebilizumab was approved by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) of China and became the first targeted therapy for NMOSD included in the National Reimbursement Drug List.

Current evaluations of NMOSD therapeutic agents remain relatively narrow in scope, predominantly focusing on relapse control and disability progression. As relapses are now more effectively controlled by modern therapies, quality-of-life indicators—including pain, mental health, and overall well-being—have garnered increasing clinical attention. Consequently, there is an urgent need to broaden the dimensions of drug evaluation to more comprehensively assess the clinical utility of therapeutic agents. Although the efficacy of inebilizumab has been demonstrated in multiple clinical trials and cohort studies [6–7], patient-perspective feedback from real-world settings remains scarce, limiting clinicians’ ability to fully appreciate the practical applicability of the drug and constraining the optimization of individualized treatment strategies.

Accordingly, the present study conducted a cross-sectional survey of Chinese AQP4-IgG–positive NMOSD patients receiving maintenance therapy with inebilizumab. The investigation focused on the distribution of prior treatment regimens, improvements in disease status and quality of life following treatment, and patients’ willingness to continue therapy together with the underlying reasons. This study aimed to provide real-world evidence to inform individualized formulation of NMOSD maintenance regimens, enhance medication adherence, and refine the clinical application framework of inebilizumab, thereby contributing to the optimization of long-term disease management for NMOSD patients.

Methods

Study design and settings

This was a cross-sectional study conducted between April and September 2025. Participants were recruited through patient organizations and related channels. Data were collected via anonymous, self-administered online questionnaires. Respondents were enrolled from nearly all provinces and regions across China.

Study population

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) a confirmed diagnosis of AQP4-IgG positive NMOSD according to the 2015 International Panel for NMO Diagnosis (IPND) criteria; and (2) ongoing receipt of inebilizumab as maintenance therapy.

Research instrument

The questionnaire comprised three sections totaling 43 items. Section 1 collected demographic information, including age, sex, place of residence, occupation, and household income. Section 2 gathered clinical and treatment data, including prior therapeutic regimens, switch reason, treatment start date, willingness to continue therapy together with the underlying reasons. Section 3 assessed patient-reported outcomes (e.g., symptom improvement, relapse reduction, serological status, which were not objectively verified).

Two dimensions of the 36-Item Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) [8–9] of particular relevance to NMOSD patients were employed as quality-of-life indicators: Role Emotional (RE) and Bodily Pain (BP). The RE dimension assesses the extent to which emotional problems interfere with work and daily activities, whereas the BP dimension evaluates pain severity and its impact on daily work. Responses were converted to standardized scores ranging from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating better health status.

Overall medication satisfaction was evaluated using a 5-point Likert scale [10] (1 = very dissatisfied, 5 = very satisfied), with higher scores reflecting greater satisfaction. To balance information completeness with participant acceptability, the questionnaire content was selectively streamlined to avoid redundancy. During internal piloting, most respondents were able to complete the questionnaire in approximately 20 min.

Statistical analysis

Data management and statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.13). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables as means ± standard deviations. Pre- and post-treatment comparisons were conducted using paired-samples t-tests (α = 0.05; 95% confidence interval). Spearman rank correlation analysis was employed to explore associations between potential influencing factors and medication satisfaction. A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Sociodemographic characteristics of patients

A total of 275 valid questionnaires were included in the analysis. Respondents were distributed across 26 provinces, 3 autonomous regions, and 4 municipalities directly under the central government, covering nearly the entire territory of China (Fig. 1).

Distribution map of included NMOSD cases (n = 275)

The mean age was 39.7 (± 11.8) years, with 249 (90.5%) being female. The mean age at disease onset was 35.3 (± 12.3) years. Visual disturbances of varying severity were reported by 201 patients (73.1%), with unilateral optic neuritis (40.0%, 110/275) being slightly more prevalent than bilateral involvement (33.1%, 91/275). Thirty-three patients (12.0%) had mobility limitations requiring assistive devices (e.g., walkers, canes for the blind, wheelchairs) in daily life. A total of 178 patients (64.7%) held a bachelor’s degree or above. One hundred and seventy-four patients (63.3%) were employed or in education, while 67 remained unemployed. Annual household income was below 60,000 CNY in 109 patients (39.6%), between 60,000 and 150,000 CNY in 116 (42.2%), and above 150,000 CNY in 50 (18.2%) (Table 1).

Medications prior to inebilizumab

Prior to inebilizumab initiation, 66.9% (184/275) of patients had received other maintenance therapies for relapse prevention. Among these, conventional immunosuppressants (e.g., azathioprine, cyclophosphamide) were the most commonly used, accounting for 42.9% (118/275) of all patients. Other previously used agents included rituximab (20.0%, 55/275), eculizumab (2.2%, 6/275), satralizumab (0.7%, 2/275), tocilizumab (0.4%, 1/275), and ofatumumab (0.7%, 2/275). Notably, 33.1% (91/275) of patients had received inebilizumab as their sole maintenance therapy since diagnosis, with the majority diagnosed in 2022 or thereafter. Among patients who had switched from prior regimens (n = 184), the primary drivers for transitioning to inebilizumab were suboptimal efficacy of previous treatment (55.4%, 102/184), intolerable adverse effects (30.4%, 56/184), economic considerations (8.7%, 16/184), and family planning requirements (5.4%, 10/184). The mean duration of inebilizumab treatment was 14.5 ± 8.4 months. Of patients with available treatment duration data (n = 273), 44.0% (120/273) had been treated for less than 1 year, 37.4% (102/273) for 1–2 years, and 18.6% (51/273) for 2 years or longer. Two patients who did not provide a definitive treatment start date were excluded from this analysis (Fig. 2).

Improvements in symptoms and quality of life following treatment

Overall, 92.4% (254/275) of patients reported marked improvement in disease symptoms following inebilizumab treatment. The most frequently reported improvement was reduction in disease relapses (93.7%, 238/254), followed by improvement in spinal cord function (16.9%, 43/254), visual function (12.6%, 32/254), and seroconversion of AQP4 antibodies to negative (9.8%, 25/254).

Patient-reported satisfaction with social life increased from a mean score of 3.02 (± 1.26) at baseline to 3.68 (± 1.06) post-treatment. Satisfaction with life perception improved from 2.84 (± 1.26) to 3.59 (± 1.07), and satisfaction with family relationships rose from 3.53 (± 1.14) to 3.89 (± 1.05). Mean mental health scores increased from 46.79 (± 41.96) to 58.91 (± 42.10), and mean bodily pain scores improved from 55.36 (± 32.09) to 77.68 (± 24.12). All pre- to post-treatment differences achieved statistical significance (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3).

The mean overall medication satisfaction score for inebilizumab was 3.8 ± 0.9. Among respondents, 23.3% (64/275) reported being very satisfied (score of 5), and 69.0% (190/275) reported being generally satisfied (scores of 3–4).

Spearman correlation analysis revealed that treatment duration was positively correlated with overall satisfaction (r = 0.12, p < 0.05), whereas the ratio of medication cost to annual household income was negatively correlated with satisfaction (r = − 0.24, p < 0.01). No statistically significant correlations were identified between satisfaction and clinical characteristics such as visual impairment or motor dysfunction (Fig. 4).

Subsequent treatment intention and associated reasons

A total of 265 patients (96.4%) expressed willingness to continue inebilizumab treatment. The predominant reason for continued use was symptom improvement (91.7%), followed by a favorable adverse effect profile (4.9%) and convenience of administration (2.6%); an additional 2 patients opted to continue with a wait-and-see approach. Among the 10 patients (3.6%) who elected to discontinue inebilizumab, the principal reasons were high treatment cost (n = 6), suboptimal therapeutic efficacy (n = 2), and pregnancy planning (n = 2).

Discussion

This large-sample cross-sectional survey explored the clinical value and existing challenges of inebilizumab from a patient-reported perspective. The findings revealed that Chinese AQP4 antibody-positive NMOSD patients treated with inebilizumab reported perceived clinical and quality-of-life improvements, with high overall satisfaction and expressed willingness for long-term use. However, these observations are inherently limited by the cross-sectional, self-reported, and non-comparative nature of the study design, precluding causal inference or definitive efficacy conclusions. These findings should be interpreted as associative and preliminary, providing exploratory real-world patient-reported evidence rather than confirmatory clinical data.

The baseline characteristics of the study population were consistent with established epidemiological features of NMOSD, with a predominance of young and middle-aged females, in concordance with previous reports identifying women of childbearing age as the principal affected demographic [11]. The geographic distribution encompassed the majority of provinces and municipalities across China, indicating broad sample coverage. Nevertheless, the recruitment strategy—relying on patient organizations and online platforms—may have introduced selection bias. Participants were predominantly individuals with higher educational attainment (64.7% with a bachelor’s degree or above), stable employment status, and active engagement in disease management. Consequently, patients with more severe disability, cognitive impairment, limited internet access, lower health literacy, or lower socioeconomic status may have been underrepresented. These demographic skews limit the generalizability of our findings to the broader NMOSD population and may have contributed to the observed high satisfaction rates.

The study population exhibited a relatively high educational level, with the majority remaining employed, although some patients still required daily assistance from others. These findings reflect the physical impairment and psychological burden imposed by NMOSD, which significantly affects quality of life, social functioning, and family relationships. Consequently, this survey extended beyond conventional assessments of drug efficacy and safety to emphasize patient-reported improvements in quality of life. While the relatively high educational attainment of respondents may have enhanced response reliability, it simultaneously underscores the potential selection bias noted above.

Regarding household income, while over half of the patients possessed a reasonable economic foundation (annual income ≥ 60,000 CNY), a substantial proportion experienced financial pressure, which emerged as an important factor influencing willingness for sustained treatment. Notably, cost-related treatment discontinuation was reported by a minority of patients, highlighting that economic considerations remain a barrier to treatment access despite inebilizumab’s inclusion in the National Reimbursement Drug List.

The distribution of prior medications before inebilizumab initiation reflects both the clinical challenges and the evolving therapeutic paradigm in NMOSD maintenance therapy. The finding that conventional immunosuppressants constituted the most commonly used prior therapy is noteworthy, as these agents are associated with relatively high relapse rates, particularly among patients with elevated antibody titers and early disease onset [12], thereby further diminishing treatment adherence and highlighting the imperative for individualized therapeutic approaches. The 2025 Chinese guidelines for NMOSD treatment [13], based on evidence-based recommendations, classify inebilizumab, satralizumab, and eculizumab as Class I, Level A recommended therapies for maintenance treatment in AQP4 antibody-positive NMOSD patients. Additionally, as inebilizumab was the first NMOSD drug included in China’s National Reimbursement Drug List, it accounts for a relatively high proportion of initial treatment selections among patients who were diagnosed with NMOSD after March 2022 as Fig. 2 showed. The growing proportion of treatment-naïve patients initiating inebilizumab as first-line maintenance therapy since its launch reflects the progressive standardization of NMOSD management and patients’ increasing demand for efficacious and safe therapeutic options.

Although numerous studies have confirmed the favorable clinical efficacy of biological agents including inebilizumab [14–16], quantitative patient-reported assessments of quality-of-life domains such as pain and psychological well-being remain scarce. In the present survey, scores across all five evaluated dimensions—bodily pain, mental health, social life, life perception, and family relationships—demonstrated statistically significant improvements following inebilizumab treatment. However, these improvements were based on retrospective self-assessment, introducing substantial recall bias, particularly for pre-treatment status. Furthermore, the clinical significance of the observed score changes remains uncertain, as minimal clinically important differences for these patient-reported measures in the NMOSD population have not been established. The magnitude of improvements should therefore be interpreted with caution.

The observed improvement in bodily pain is particularly noteworthy. While 92.4% of patients reported symptom improvement, including reduced pain, this finding appears to differ from observations in pivotal clinical trials, where pain outcomes were not prominently reported. Whether this reflects a genuine pharmacological effect of inebilizumab on nociceptive pathways, an indirect benefit mediated by reduced relapse frequency and inflammatory activity, or a placebo/expectation effect inherent to self-reported outcomes remains unclear. This observation warrants further investigation in prospective, controlled studies with objective pain assessment tools.

Overall medication satisfaction was high and positively correlated with longer treatment duration, although the correlation coefficient was weak (r = 0.12). The very high willingness to continue treatment (96.4%) is noteworthy but may be influenced by multiple factors, including the selection bias discussed above, the relatively short mean treatment duration (14.5 ± 8.4 months). Consequently, the reported satisfaction and continuation rates may overestimate the true real-world acceptability of inebilizumab.

The absence of a comparator group (e.g., rituximab, satralizumab, or conventional immunosuppressants) represents a fundamental limitation of this study. Without direct comparison, we cannot determine whether the observed patient-reported outcomes are specific to inebilizumab or would be similarly achieved with alternative maintenance therapies. The findings should not be interpreted as evidence of inebilizumab’s superiority over other regimens.

Patients’ willingness for continued treatment serves as a direct reflection of perceived clinical value and medication experience, constituting a key indicator of treatment sustainability. The high proportion of patients expressing intent to continue inebilizumab therapy suggests that this agent may address the long-term therapeutic needs of NMOSD patients from the patient perspective. Conversely, the small proportion of patients electing to discontinue treatment owing to financial burden underscores the continuing impact of drug cost on treatment decision-making. Future research should incorporate detailed health economic analyses and explore the interplay between reimbursement policies, out-of-pocket expenses, and treatment adherence in the Chinese healthcare context.

This study has several limitations that merit consideration. First, the cross-sectional design and retrospective self-reporting introduce recall bias, particularly for the pre-treatment assessments. Second, as noted above, selection bias may have occurred because participants were recruited through patient organizations and online platforms; patients with higher educational levels and who are more engaged in their care responded more actively, while patients with more severe disability, cognitive impairment, limited internet access, or lower health literacy may have been underrepresented. This selection bias may have led to an overestimation of treatment satisfaction and quality-of-life improvements, thereby impacting the generalizability of the findings. Third, potential sources of information bias exist, as key outcomes were patient-reported without further objective validation; for instance, serological status and relapse history were based on patient self-report rather than independent medical record verification. Fourth, the absence of a comparator group limits interpretation of relative effectiveness and patient satisfaction. Fifth, the questionnaire did not incorporate detailed items addressing drug safety profiles. Finally, the observed correlation coefficients (e.g., r = 0.12, − 0.24) indicate weak associations that should be interpreted cautiously and do not imply strong predictive relationships.

Notwithstanding these limitations, this study enriches the clinical evidence base for inebilizumab by supplementing patient-reported outcome data from real-world practice. In contrast to previous clinical trials that predominantly focused on efficacy and safety endpoints [4], the present study centered on patients’ medication experience, satisfaction, and treatment intention, thereby exploring the clinical value of inebilizumab from a patient-centered perspective and integrating clinical efficacy with subjective patient experience. These findings may inform the development of more personalized, patient-centered therapeutic strategies for NMOSD, pending validation in more rigorous study designs.

By delineating the real-world medication landscape and core therapeutic needs of NMOSD patients in a large-sample cohort, this study contributes to understanding inebilizumab’s clinical application and the refinement of the overall NMOSD treatment paradigm, carrying potential implications for promoting standardized and individualized long-term disease management. Future studies employing more robust designs (e.g., prospective cohorts, randomized controlled trials with comparator arms), expanded assessment dimensions including objective clinical endpoints and validated safety profiling, and stratified sampling to ensure representation of diverse socioeconomic and disability subgroups are warranted to validate and extend these findings.

Conclusions

Among Chinese patients with AQP4-IgG–positive NMOSD on inebilizumab maintenance therapy, this cross-sectional survey revealed favorable patient-reported perceptions regarding treatment acceptability and quality of life. Meanwhile, economic burden, unaddressed medication safety considerations in special populations, insufficient primary care capacity, and inadequate patient disease education continue to constrain the broader adoption and sustained utilization of this agent. This study supplements real-world evidence for inebilizumab from the patient perspective, providing exploratory analysis of its perceived clinical value and existing challenges. These self-reported observations should be interpreted cautiously given the cross-sectional, non-comparative, and self-selected nature of the sample. The findings do not establish the efficacy, safety, or superiority of inebilizumab over alternative regimens. Prospective controlled studies with objective outcome measures, comparator arms, and representative sampling are required to validate these preliminary findings.

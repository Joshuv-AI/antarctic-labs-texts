A dual-center study: multimodal fusion-based deep learning approach for pathological subtype prediction of type I and type II ovarian cancer
Authors: Tianle Hong, Wenjie Huang, Wenqing Lu, Lu Peng, Cunke Miao, Lixuan Chen, Yunjun Yang, Yezhi Lin, Liqin Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAccurate prediction of WHO Type I and Type II pathological classifications in epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) patients is critical for developing effective personalized treatment strategies. This study aims to develop a multimodal deep learning framework integrating enhanced CT (EC), non-contrast CT (NC), transvaginal ultrasound (US), and clinical data to predict EOC subtypes. Furthermore, we investigate the differential contributions of distinct modalities to model predictions through interpretable analysis.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 240 EOC patients who underwent postoperative histopathological subtyping alongside EC, NC and US. A total of 17 EOC patients from other centers were enrolled for external validation. The multimodal imaging data fusion deep learning model USECNC, integrating ResNet-50 with Cross-attention mechanisms, was designed to synergistically fuse multimodal image features. The integrated model USECNC + CL (USECNC+Clinical Data) combines clinical data and imaging features at the decision layer for final prediction.ResultsThe USECNC + CL model demonstrated the best performance in predicting EOC pathological subtypes, achieving an AUC of 0.87 (5-fold cross-validation range: [0.82–0.92]). Baseline models based on EC, NC, US, and clinical data achieved AUC of 0.81, 0.69, 0.77 and 0.79. The optimal radiomics model achieved an AUC of 0.72. The USECNC + CL model also achieved good predictive performance in the external validation set (AUC = 0.850, ACC = 0.824). In the SHAP analysis, the contribution of imaging features is ranked from highest to lowest as follows: EC, US, NC. Meanwhile, compared to imaging fusion features, CA125 demonstrates a superior contribution.ConclusionsThe integration of preoperative multimodal data into a deep learning model enables accurate prediction of pathological subtypes in EOC patients. Interpretability analyses confirmed the critical diagnostic contributions of EC vascular features and CA-125 levels. Accurate preoperative type I/II prediction can effectively assist in the formulation of chemotherapy regimens and surgical plans for patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02231-4.

Background

Ovarian cancer ranks as the second most lethal gynecological malignancy worldwide [1]. It is estimated that in 2022, ovarian cancer resulted in 20,890 new cases and 12,730 deaths in the United States alone, with epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) being the most prevalent histological subtype [2]. The majority of EOC patients experience high mortality rates and poor prognoses, primarily due to inadequate early detection capabilities for this aggressive malignancy [3]. Based on clinicopathological manifestations, molecular genetics, and histopathological characteristics, World Health Organization (WHO) proposed classification system categorizes EOC into two distinct groups: Type I and Type II tumors [4].

Type I EOC demonstrates genetic stability, indolent clinical progression, and favorable prognoses, yet exhibits reduced responsiveness to conventional chemotherapy, while Type II EOC is characterized by genomic instability and highly aggressive biological behavior, associated with dismal clinical outcomes but greater sensitivity to standard chemotherapy regimens [4, 5]. Significant therapeutic divergences exist between them: Type I EOC, with its indolent growth, typically responds favorably to early cytoreductive surgery, whereas Type II EOC, which progresses rapidly, is strategically treated with a combination of cytoreductive surgery, platinum-based chemotherapy, neoadjuvant therapy or PARP inhibitors [6, 7]. Therefore, the preoperative differential classification of Type I and Type II EOC is of utmost importance, as it can effectively guide the formulation of surgical plans, chemotherapy regimens, and the assessment of long-term prognosis.

Notably, the classification of EOC typically relies on postoperative pathological analysis, as preoperative imaging examinations are hardly distinguishable macroscopically. Therefore, numerous previous studies have focused on radiomics based on MRI, CT, and ultrasound images for predictive analysis [8, 9]. Li et al. [10] demonstrated clinical viability by extracting radiomic features from contrast-enhanced CT scans to develop machine learning models for preoperative discrimination between Type I and Type II EOC. Concurrently, Jian et al. [11] achieved improved predictive accuracy through longitudinal MRI series analysis, constructing combinatorial radiomic models to forecast pathological subtyping in EOC patients. However, critical limitations persist in conventional radiomic workflows, particularly the time-intensive nature of feature extraction, suboptimal computational efficiency, and constrained generalizability across heterogeneous patient cohorts.

Deep learning, as an emerging modeling approach, demonstrates superiority in automatically and comprehensively extracting effective image features. Compared with traditional omics models, it exhibits higher prediction accuracy and generalization ability. Currently, deep learning models constructed based on MRI images have been applied to predict EOC Type I and Type II. For instance, Wang et al. [12] developed a ResNet50-based architecture integrating MRI-derived radiomic features to achieve differentiating between borderline ovarian tumors and stage I ovarian cancer. Concurrently, Xiang et al. [6] implemented an ensemble framework aggregating predictions from multiple deep learning models, attaining high diagnostic accuracy while maintaining interpretability through saliency mapping.

However, current research lacks deep learning analysis on enhanced CT and ultrasound images of routine ovarian cancer examinations. Moreover, single-modality images provide limited information and are prone to the influence of modality limitations and noise information.

Therefore, this study aims to develop a deep learning framework integrating multimodal data from transvaginal ultrasound (US), non-contrast CT (NC), enhanced CT (EC), and clinical parameters (CL) to predict Type I and Type II EOC classifications. Furthermore, by integrating clinical feature information and imaging modality feature information, this paper provides an interpretable analysis of the differences in contributions among various clinical feature data and different imaging modality data. The heatmap analysis of imaging features also highlights the regions of interest for the model across different imaging modalities.

Methods

Patient enrollment

Our Institutional Review Board approved this study. We conducted a retrospective cohort study enrolling patients histologically confirmed with malignant ovarian carcinoma at our tertiary care center between April 2006 and April 2022. Ethics approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University (Acceptance Number: 2021-zz-048). The inclusion criteria included: Postoperative pathological confirmation of EOC and completion of both ultrasound and CT examinations within three months prior to surgery. The exclusion criteria included: (1) Without complete preoperative EC, NC and US image data. (2) Tail shadow in EC. (3) The presence of ascites in EC makes it difficult to delineate the ROI area. (4) Without ultrasound image data within two weeks before surgery. (5) Received therapy before surgery. (6) Had a history of malignancies or combined malignancies. As schematically illustrated in Fig. 1. Meanwhile, patients with epithelial ovarian cancer from the First Affiliated Hospital of Ningbo University from March 2019 to December 2022 were retrospectively collected. After inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied, 17 patients were enrolled in the study to construct an external validation set.

Clinical characteristics and models

Basic demographic data were extracted from the electronic medical record (EMR) system. Clinically significant indicators and pathological subtype labels were extracted from patients’ blood biochemistry profiles, complete blood counts, coagulation profiles, tumor marker assays, and postoperative histopathological reports. Variables demonstrating statistical significance (p < 0.05) in multivariate logistic regression analysis were retained for model construction. A baseline clinical prediction model was developed using logistic regression (LR) due to its well-established interpretability and broad clinical applicability. Ultimately, we implemented an optimized LR-based clinical model incorporating these selected features to predict EOC pathological subtypes.

Image acquisition and delineation

All the procedures were performed according to the Image Biomarker Standardization Initiative (IBSI) standards. machine parameters can be found in Supplementary material.

ROI segmentation for US and CT images was performed using manual or semi-automatic annotation methods. Two nuclear medicine physicians with 5 and 7 years of experience respectively conducted CT image delineations, while two sonographers with 3 and 5 years of experience performed ultrasound ROI annotations. All annotators were blinded to clinical information during the segmentation process. For CT images (including NC and EC images), we used itk-snap software (version 3.6.0, http://www.itksnap.org) EOC lesions were delineated layer by layer. For multi lesion images, the lesion area with the largest cross section was outlined. Subsequently, we aligned the ROI outlined in the abdominal NC image with the ROI in the EC image. For US images, we used itk-snap software (version 3.6.0, http://www.itksnap.org) The section of the largest EOC lesion was outlined. Additionally, for both CT and US images, the ROI annotated by two physicians were used to calculate the Dice coefficient (CT Dice:0.974, US Dice:0.958). The results demonstrate a high level of consistency and accuracy in the ROI delineation.

For each patient, an axis-aligned bounding box was constructed based on the tumor mask from the original images. To incorporate sufficient peritumoral information, the AABB was expanded by 25% in the transverse plane (a 2D expansion for ultrasound and a planar expansion for CT while maintaining the original Z-axis thickness). Subsequently, the expanded regions were resampled to a standardized resolution (ultrasound: 256 × 256 pixels; CT: 8 × 256 × 256 voxels, where 8 denotes the number of slices) using linear interpolation, serving as inputs for the deep learning models.

Development of multimodal fusion deep learning model

In this study, we developed a deep learning model to predict Type I and Type II EOC through multimodal imaging feature fusion. The model integrates a Deep Residual Network (ResNet) [13] with a Cross-Attention fusion mechanism [14, 15], enabling the extraction of hierarchical feature representations from medical images. By adaptively complementing features across modalities, the model achieves enhanced predictive accuracy for EOC subtyping.

The comprehensive workflow is illustrated in Fig. 2. We first extract ROIs from three imaging modalities: US, NC and EC. These tri-modality ROIs are then independently processed through dedicated ResNet-50 architectures to generate comprehensive feature sequences. Subsequently, the features extracted from EC and NC image undergo Cross-Attention-based fusion to generate a composite feature sequence integrating vascular architecture patterns with comprehensive tumor morphological characteristics. The EC features and US features are fused through a Cross-Attention mechanism to generate a tumor signature sequence with minimized interference from comorbid imaging artifacts and enhanced clarity of tumor boundaries. Finally, the two feature sequences are integrated to form a consolidated feature representation. This multimodal imaging signature is combined with clinical features at the decision layer using logistic regression (LR) for EOC pathological subtype prediction.

Network evaluation

In this study, we developed nine models to validate the superiority of the multimodal fusion deep learning model in predicting EOC pathological subtypes. First, data from the single modalities of US, NC and EC were used to develop models for predicting the pathological classification of EOC. The clinical model (CL) and the single-modality radiomics model (Radiomics) with the highest accuracy were also used to compare and evaluate the predictive accuracy of the models. Additionally, fusion models integrating features from two imaging modalities (EC + NC: ECNC, US + NC: USNC, US + EC: USEC) were developed to demonstrate the complementary nature of multimodal feature fusion. Comparisons between imaging fusion models (US + EC + NC: USECNC) and clinical-imaging fusion models (US + EC + NC + CL: USECNC + CL) were conducted to verify the supplementary value of clinical data to imaging features. The effectiveness of multi-modal data fusion was validated by the DeLong test.

Deep learning models are often regarded as ‘black-box’ systems with opaque computational processes. To clarify the distinct contributions of individual imaging modalities and clinical features to model decisions, while also identifying the model’s regions of focus across different modal images, this study performed Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP values) for interpretability and extracted model weight features to generate perceptual heatmaps.

Construction of radiomics models

Radiomics features were extracted from each ROI using the PyRadiomics package (version 3.1.0). All features were normalized using min–max scaling to ensure comparability across modalities. For the overall imaging features, redundancy reduction was first performed by calculating the Spearman rank correlation coefficient between each pair of features and clustering those with high correlation. Next, univariate analysis was conducted for each feature, with intergroup comparisons performed using the Mann-Whitney U test, retaining features with a p-value < 0.05. Finally, feature selection was carried out using the LASSO model via least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression. The selected features were then applied to a LR model for classification prediction.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (v26.0), MedCalc (v19.8), and R (v4.1.3); data manipulation and visualization were performed using the dplyr, ggplot2, and scales packages in R. For continuous variables, the Shapiro-Wilk test was first employed to assess the normality of the distribution. Normally distributed data were compared using the independent-samples Student’s t-test, while non-normally distributed data were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U rank-sum test. Categorical variables were described as frequencies and analyzed using the Chi-square test when the total sample size was n > = 40, or Fisher’s exact test when n < 40 or expected cell frequencies were low. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. To evaluate the prediction performance, this paper conducted receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, including the area under the ROC curve (AUC), accuracy, specificity, sensitivity and F1-score. To comprehensively and accurately assess the model’s predictive performance and improve its generalizability, we employed a 5-fold cross-validation method.

The overall data were stratified into two groups: WHO type I and WHO type II, with P-values calculated between the groups using statistical tests. The model was tested and trained using five fold cross validation. 240 EOC patients were randomly allocated into a training set (N = 192) for model development and an internal test set (N = 48) for independent performance evaluation. Models performance

In this study, we proposed a multimodal data fusion deep learning model for classifying and predicting WHO Type I and Type II EOC in patients. First, baseline models were established for each individual modality (NC, EC, US) and for radiomics features extracted from the three imaging modalities. The predictive AUC results for the models were 0.69, 0.81 and 0.77 respectively, with the best radiomics model achieving an AUC of 0.72. Additionally, we analyzed fusion models constructed from two imaging modalities: EC + NC (ECNC), US + NC (USNC), and US + EC (USEC). The corresponding AUC results for these fusion models were 0.86, 0.84, 0.76, and 0.70. Finally, the comprehensive multi-dimensional imaging feature fusion model (USECNC) and the combined imaging-clinical feature fusion model (USECNC + CL) were developed to validate feature complementarity across multimodal data. The predictive AUC results for these models were 0.86 and 0.87, respectively. Comparative analysis of different models revealed that USECNC + CL effectively integrated preoperative multidimensional examination data, achieving accurate prediction of EOC pathological subtypes. The 5-fold cross-validation results demonstrated robust performance: AUC: 0.87 (0.82–0.92), Accuracy: 0.82 (0.75–0.88), Specificity: 0.79 (0.69–0.92), Sensitivity: 0.92 (0.85–0.97), F1-score: 0.87 (0.82–0.91). The proposed model also achieved a high prediction accuracy in the external validation set. Among them, the USECNC + CL model achieved the best prediction performance: AUC: 0.850, Accuracy: 0.824, Specificity: 0.800, Sensitivity: 1.00, F1_score: 0.889.

Model significance test

In this study, we established single-modality models (NC, EC, US, CL) and the optimal Radiomics model as baseline references. The significance of the USECNC + CL fusion model was rigorously validated against these baselines using DeLong test for AUC comparison. As demonstrated in Table 5, all obtained P-values were < 0.001, conclusively proving both the statistical superiority and clinical validity of our multimodal fusion approach.

Discussion

Principal findings

This study proposes a multimodal data fusion deep learning model for preoperative prediction of postoperative WHO Type I and Type II classifications in EOC patients. The results demonstrate that the USECNC + CL model, which integrates US, NC, EC and CL, significantly outperforms single-modality models and optimal radiomics models in predictive accuracy, with statistically significant differences confirmed by DeLong test. Meanwhile, the model trained in a single center also achieved excellent prediction performance in the external validation set.

Comparative Analysis with Existing Literature and Study Rationale.

Additionally, bimodal imaging fusion models were constructed to validate the complementary relationships between imaging modalities. Our findings indicate that the USECNC + CL model, as a non-invasive preoperative multimodal fusion framework, exhibits high accuracy and substantial potential for predicting EOC subtypes. Accurate preoperative prediction of EOC pathological subtypes can effectively guide the formulation of precise surgical plans and treatment strategies for patients.

Compared to clinicians empirical predictions of pathological classification, deep learning-based predictive models can automatically capture critical data features and provide objective, accurate prognostic assessments [16, 17]. Current research on ovarian cancer imaging analysis demonstrates this capability [18, 19]. Wei et al. [20] developed a 2.5D ResNet model using multicenter MRI data, achieving 0.796 ACC in subtyping prediction through radiomics-feature fusion. Chen et al. [21] integrated grayscale and color Doppler ultrasound features for benign and malignant classification, with deep learning, O-RADS, and expert assessments yielding AUCs of 0.93, 0.92, and 0.97. Gao et al. [22] engineered a DNN model analyzing multicenter ultrasound data, attaining internal validation AUC = 0.911 and external validation AUCs = 0.870/0.831 for malignancy detection. From the existing research, it can be found that most current studies focus on using single-modal data to construct deep learning models for predicting cancer typing. Models constructed in this way have problems such as low prediction accuracy and narrow application scope [21–24]. Furthermore, Medical imaging data inherently possesses well-defined hierarchical structures, sequential organization, and rich multi-scale feature interdependencies. However, two critical challenges persist: (1) Limited sample sizes despite abundant multimodal information. (2) Heterogeneity across modalities that constrains models’ ability to discern generalized feature patterns for precise predictions. Therefore, this paper proposed a multimodal data fusion deep learning model that accurately differentiates Type I and Type II EOC subtypes with superior accuracy. This preoperative non-invasive framework enables precise personalized therapeutic planning.

US serves as a simple and cost-effective imaging modality for preliminary EOC diagnosis in clinical practice. However, its limitations in precisely delineating lymph node localization and dimensional measurements necessitate complementary evaluation through NC and EC to enhance nodal status assessment, tumor morphology characterization, and vascular architecture mapping. Nevertheless, concurrent clinical manifestations such as ascites and peritoneal fibrosis in EOC patients significantly compromise the diagnostic accuracy of CT image.

In multimodal imaging fusion experiments, the fusion model ECNC achieved higher predictive accuracy than single-modality models. However, the fusion model USNC resulted in reduced accuracy compared to single-modality models. Analysis of the model’s perceptual heatmaps reveals that both NC and US images contain substantial noise and relatively few morphological features, the features from these modalities struggle to combine and complement each other effectively within the model. This leads to intensified noise interference and consequently reduces the model’s prediction accuracy. Notably, the comprehensive imaging fusion model USECNC, which integrated US image data with ECNC features, demonstrated further accuracy improvements. From the analysis, it can be found that each modality, owing to its imaging methodology and subjective factors from operators, inherently contains certain noise and limitations [25, 26]. However, it can be inferred from the improvement in prediction accuracy brought by modality fusion that the blood supply information provided by EC is crucial for predicting the pathological typing of ovarian cancer. Additionally, the tumor morphological features derived from NC and US can also effectively enhance the prediction accuracy of the model.

Interpretability analysis and feature contribution

In this study, an interpretability analysis framework was employed to analyze the contribution of each modality in the USECNC and USECNC + CL models. It was found that in the BECNC model, EC showed the highest contribution, while NC exhibited the lowest contribution. The results and modality ablation experiments mutually validate each other, demonstrating the priority of preoperative EC scans [27, 28]. However, in USECNC + CL, the contribution of image fusion features to the model is smaller than that of CA125. This disparity underscores the pivotal role of CA-125 in pathological classification, likely attributable to its biological correlation with tumor aggressiveness and metastatic potential [29–31]. Analysis of the model’s perceptual heatmaps reveals that the model hierarchically focuses on the tumor’s internal vascular and parenchymal characteristics in EC, while primarily emphasizing tumor morphological boundaries in NC. For US data, the model prioritizes textural patterns and morphological contours within the tumor, providing supplementary information to the overall feature representation. Therefore, constructing a multimodal image fusion model effectively compensates for the limitations of individual modalities, mitigating the impact of noise interference.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the inclusion criteria for the cohort in this study were relatively strict, requiring patients to have all three preoperative imaging modalities available. This may introduce some bias into the experimental results. Second, due to variations in preoperative examination protocols across different centers, the data in the external validation set are relatively limited. Third, the limited sample size restricts the model’s robustness and external validity. Last, variations in imaging protocols and equipment across medical institutions could compromise the model’s predictive accuracy in real-world multicenter applications.

Conclusions

In summary, this study presents a deep learning framework based on multimodal data fusion for preoperative determination of EOC pathological subtypes. Compared to unimodal approaches, multimodal integration demonstrated superior classification accuracy. Interpretability analyses substantiated the critical roles of EC vascular profiling and CA-125 biomarker levels in subtype discrimination. This methodology provides a framework for optimizing preoperative therapeutic strategies and investigating ovarian cancer pathogenesis.

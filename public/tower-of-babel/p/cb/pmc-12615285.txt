Successful endoscopic treatment of a tracheobronchial fistula following Lewis operation in a high-risk surgical patient: A clinical case report
Authors: K Batyrbekov, T Uskenbayev, A Galiakbarova, A Kerimkulov, B Ussipbekov, Y Yermeshev, T Sarina
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTracheobronchial fistulas (TBFs) are a rare but life-threatening complication following esophageal surgery, especially after Lewis esophagectomy. Traditional surgical approaches are associated with high risk, especially in patients with severe cardiovascular disease. We describe the clinical case of a 68-year-old patient with cardioesophageal cancer and multiple comorbidities who developed two tracheobronchial fistulas after Lewis surgery. Endoscopic treatment included bronchial occlusion, argon plasma coagulation, and the use of a large-pored hemostatic sponge. This stepwise and minimally invasive approach ensured complete closure of the fistulas without the need for repeat surgical intervention. This case demonstrates the effectiveness of modern endoscopic technologies in the treatment of complex fistulas in high-risk patients.Keywords: Tracheobronchial fistula, Lewis operation, Bronchial occluder, Argon plasma coagulation, Endoscopic treatment

Introduction

Tracheobronchial fistulas (TBFs) are a serious complication after esophageal surgery, particularly following combined esophagectomy (Lewis procedure), with a reported incidence of up to 2 % and mortality reaching 80 % in the absence of treatment. Their etiology includes suture line dehiscence, ischemia of the tracheobronchial tree, infectious complications, and prior radiotherapy. If not addressed promptly, they can lead to severe purulent pleuritis, mediastinitis, and respiratory failure. Historically, surgical correction was considered the only therapeutic option, though it carried a high mortality risk. In recent decades, endoscopic technologies have become increasingly important in managing bronchial fistulas. This report presents a successful case of bronchial fistula closure using argon plasma coagulation (APC) and a biocompatible occluder after a Lewis operation.

Epithelialization of fistulous tracts in conditions of constant airflow and infection is often extremely slow or even impossible without targeted intervention. The most vulnerable patients are elderly individuals with cancer and cardiopulmonary comorbidities. Standard treatment via thoracotomy is contraindicated in debilitated patients, especially those with recent myocardial infarction. Modern endoscopic techniques, including APC, silicone occluders, and hemostatic sponges, offer effective and minimally invasive alternatives.

Tracheobronchial and bronchial fistulas are pathological communications between the airway lumen and the pleural cavity, other organs, or the external environment. These fistulas typically result from mechanical trauma, tissue ischemia, postoperative complications, infection, or radiation injury. In oncology, they most commonly occur after resection of the esophagus, trachea, or lung, or due to direct tumor invasion [1].

The Lewis operation (also known as "two-stage esophagectomy") is a standard surgical approach for malignancies of the middle and distal thirds of the esophagus and gastric cardia. The procedure consists of two stages: abdominal and thoracic.In the first stage, the stomach is mobilized, regional lymph nodes are dissected, and a gastric conduit is created.

The second stage includes a right-sided thoracotomy, resection of the affected portion of the esophagus, mediastinal lymph node dissection, and formation of an esophagogastrostomy in the posterior mediastinum. The thoracic phase ends with irrigation of the pleural cavity with saline solution and the placement of two drainage tubes: one into the right pleural cavity and another into the posterior mediastinum. The tip of the mediastinal drain is positioned near the anastomosis.

This surgical technique carries a high risk of anastomotic inflammation, suture line dehiscence, and, consequently, fistula formation. In our clinical case, the placement of two drainage tubes in the area of the anastomosis ultimately led to pressure necrosis and the development of a bronchial fistula [2].

Main text

Patient U., 68 years old, was admitted to the National Research Oncology Center (Astana) via the referral portal with complaints of dysphagia, weight loss, weakness, and retrosternal pain. Endoscopy and biopsy confirmed a diagnosis of cardioesophageal cancer (stage cT2N0M0). Medical history included grade II arterial hypertension, ischemic heart disease, and class I obesity.

February 1, 2024 – A hybrid Lewis operation was performed: laparoscopic gastric mobilization, right thoracotomy, subtotal esophagectomy with lymphadenectomy, formation of an esophagogastrostomy, and placement of abdominal and thoracic drains. Two drains were placed near the anastomotic site.

February 5, 2024 – Anastomotic leakage was suspected due to foamy bile in the chest tube. CT revealed a fistula from the anastomosis to the pleural cavity, right-sided pneumothorax, subtotal lower lobe atelectasis, and consolidation in S2, S6 (right) and S10 (left). Re-thoracotomy was performed, with cavity decontamination, placement of a T-drain near the anastomotic defect, and creation of a Witzel-type feeding jejunostomy.

February 6, 2024 – The patient developed an ST-elevation myocardial infarction. Urgent coronary angiography revealed stenosis of the LAD and RCA. Both arteries were successfully stented. The patient was transferred to the ICU, stabilized with mechanical ventilation, antiarrhythmic, and antithrombotic therapy.

February 13, 2024 – A Griggs tracheostomy was performed. From February 15, persistent cough, bile drainage, and signs of respiratory failure were observed.

CT and bronchoscopy confirmed a fistulous tract between the right main bronchus and the pleural cavity.

February 19, 2024 – Bronchoscopy via the tracheostomy tube revealed two fistulous defects:

Discussion

Tracheobronchial fistulas after esophageal surgeries occur in approximately 0.5–2 % of cases according to the literature. Mortality in the absence of treatment can reach 70–80 %. In patients with severe comorbid conditions, repeated surgical interventions are often not feasible.

In recent years, argon plasma coagulation (APC) has proven its effectiveness in the treatment of mucosal surfaces, necrotic, and granulation tissues. When combined with mechanical closure methods (such as stents, tissue adhesives, or occluders), APC enables stable and long-term fistula closure [3,4].

Special emphasis in the treatment of such complications is placed on an individualized approach, which involves the assessment of:

•the size and location of the fistulous tract,

•the presence of secondary infection,

•and the patient's overall clinical condition.

Various endoscopic techniques have been implemented in international practice, including self-expanding stents, biological glues, sponge-based, and fibrin occlusive materials. However, argon plasma coagulation remains a versatile and widely applicable method, particularly useful for debriding fistula margins and stimulating reparative tissue regeneration. When combined with mechanical occlusion, APC has demonstrated consistently favorable outcomes.

In the present case, an endoscopic strategy was selected due to the patient's critical general condition. APC was used to cauterize the fistula edges and to prevent ongoing bacterial contamination. A sponge-based occluder, selected according to the size and shape of the defect, was anatomically adapted to the bronchial lumen and provided reliable physical closure of the defect [5].

This stepwise and minimally invasive approach allowed for a reduction in recurrence risk and avoided the need for repeat thoracotomy, which would have been extremely hazardous in this high-risk patient.

Conclusions

Endoscopic treatment of bronchial fistulas using argon plasma coagulation and a sponge-based occluder is a safe and effective method in patients following esophageal surgery.

This approach should be considered as a first-line option in patients who have contraindications to repeat surgical interventions. Further assessment of long-term outcomes is required through prospective studies and multicenter follow-up.

The proposed strategy significantly reduces the need for repeated open surgeries, which is especially important in surgically vulnerable patients.

The results of this case confirm the effectiveness of a stepwise endoscopic strategy, based on thermal stimulation of healing and reliable mechanical occlusion of the fistula.

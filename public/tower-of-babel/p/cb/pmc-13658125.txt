Dacomitinib With or Without Dose Titration in Treatment-Naive Advanced EGFR-Mutated NSCLC — Primary Analysis of the ATORG-003 Phase 2 Trial
Authors: Aaron C. Tan, Tae Min Kim, Siqin Zhou, Yueh Ni Lim, Gwo Fuang Ho, Lye Mun Tho, Thanyanan Baisamut, Naiyarat Prasongsook, Suee Lee, Sze Huey Tan, Dong-Wan Kim, Tony Mok, Daniel S.W. Tan, Herbert H. Loong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionDacomitinib is an approved second-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor. In the phase 3 ARCHER 1050 trial, both progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were improved with first-line dacomitinib compared with gefitinib. Post hoc analysis revealed that efficacy (PFS, OS) was similar in both dose-reduced patients and the overall study population. ATORG-003 evaluated a dose-titration strategy to improve tolerability while maintaining efficacy. This is also the first prospective study of dacomitinib’s intracranial efficacy and resistance (tissue and plasma next-generation sequencing).MethodsATORG-003 (Asian Thoracic Oncology Research Group; five Asian countries) was a single-arm, investigator-initiated phase 2 trial that enrolled patients with treatment-naive stages IIIB to IV EGFR-mutated NSCLC. Patients received dacomitinib 30 mg daily for 4 weeks, with a shared investigator and patient decision for titration to 45 mg in patients with grade less than or equal to 1 toxicity (Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0). The primary end point was the 12-month PFS rate (in patients without central nervous system metastases only) compared with the historical control. Secondary end points were time to treatment failure (TTF), objective response rate (ORR), OS, intracranial ORR (iORR) and intracranial PFS (iPFS), and safety and tolerability.ResultsFrom September 2019 to October 2022, 118 patients were enrolled. The median age was 65 (range 33–84) years, and 54% were female. EGFR mutation type was exon 19 deletion in 57%, L858R mutation in 42%, and both in 1%. There were 18% of patients who were dose titrated to 45 mg at cycle 2, and 70% tolerated 30 mg (67%) or 45 mg (3%) without dose reduction. The primary end point was met, with a 12-month PFS proportion rate of 59.6% (90% confidence interval [CI]: 50.6%–68.1%) and a 12-month PFS rate of 61.2% (95% CI: 50.5%–70.3%) in the primary analysis cohort. The median PFS was 16.1 months (95% CI: 12.1–19.2), and the median TTF was 19.3 months (95% CI: 16.1–22.1). In the overall study population, the ORR was 77% and the median OS was not reached. In patients with central nervous system metastases (n = 24), the iORR was 54% and the median iPFS was 17.6 months (95% CI: 7.2–23.1). All patients had treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), with grade 3/4 TRAEs occurring in 25%. The most common grade 3/4 TRAEs were rash (7%), paronychia (4%), and hypokalemia (3%). EGFR T790M resistance was detected in 29 of 96 patients (30%) through plasma next-generation sequencing and in 15 of 29 patients (52%) through tissue next-generation sequencing.ConclusionATORG-003 prospectively revealed that dacomitinib with dose titration is a viable strategy in advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC, maintaining efficacy while improving tolerability. Dacomitinib has substantial intracranial activity and high rates of T790M resistance, making it suitable for sequential treatment approaches.

Introduction

Activating EGFR mutations are detected in approximately half of all patients with NSCLC in Asian populations.1 In advanced or metastatic disease, EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) are established as a first-line treatment.2, 3, 4, 5 Although third-generation EGFR TKIs such as osimertinib are often preferred in guidelines as first-line therapy, limitations on access and reimbursement in many parts of the world are a significant barrier to clinical practice.6 Consequently, first- and second-generation EGFR TKIs remain viable treatment options for a substantial proportion of patients.

Dacomitinib is an approved second-generation EGFR TKI. In the phase 3 ARCHER 1050 trial, both progression-free survival (PFS)3 and overall survival (OS)7 were improved with first-line dacomitinib compared with gefitinib. However, dacomitinib, which was administered at a starting dose of 45 mg, was associated with increased toxicity, with grades 3 to 5 toxicity in 63% of patients for dacomitinib compared with 41% for gefitinib. Furthermore, there were higher rates of dose reductions and permanent discontinuation due to adverse events. Post hoc analyses, though, revealed that efficacy for both PFS and OS was similar in both dose-reduced patients and the overall study population.8 In addition, patients with central nervous system (CNS) metastases were excluded from the ARCHER 1050 trial, and there remains a lack of comprehensive evaluation of resistance mechanisms to dacomitinib.

Therefore, this single-arm phase II study evaluated a dose-titration strategy to improve tolerability while maintaining the efficacy of dacomitinib in the first-line treatment of advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC. This is also the first prospective study of dacomitinib’s intracranial efficacy and resistance and the first interventional study conducted by the cooperative Asian trial group, Asian Thoracic Oncology Research Group (ATORG).

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This study is a phase II, multinational, multicenter, open-label, single-arm trial conducted in nine academic centers across five Asian countries (Hong Kong, Malaysia, Republic of Korea, Singapore, and Thailand). Patients received dacomitinib 30 mg orally on a continuous daily basis for a 28-day cycle. After one cycle, a toxicity assessment was conducted. Patients with more than or equal to G2 toxicity attributable to dacomitinib continued dacomitinib at 30 mg orally once daily. In patients with less than or equal to G1 toxicity, investigators and patients made a shared decision for dose escalation of dacomitinib to 45 mg or continuation at 30 mg orally once daily. The study was conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent before study entry, and the protocol was approved by the SingHealth Institutional Review Board (IRB no. 2018/2931) and participating centers (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04027647).

Patients newly diagnosed with having stage IIIB, IIIC or IV, or recurrent NSCLC of the adenocarcinoma subtype, positive for at least one EGFR-activating mutation (exon 19 deletion or exon 21 L858R mutation), were included in the study. Patients with CNS metastases were estimated to comprise 20% of the overall study population at study design and hence were capped at 20% in the final study cohort. Patients were required to have measurable disease, as defined by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1, and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group baseline performance status of 0 or 1 (refer to the Protocol for detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria). Patients with CNS metastases must have completed radiotherapy or surgery for CNS metastases more than 2 weeks before study entry, and CNS metastases must have been neurologically stable and on a stable dose of steroids (if required) for 2 weeks before the first dose of study treatment.

Procedures

Patients received dacomitinib in 28-day cycles, with dose reductions at dose levels of 30 mg once daily and 15 mg once daily. Dose reductions might take place whenever toxicity that was not controlled with optimal supportive care or was not tolerated due to symptoms or interference with normal daily activities was noted during the study. Dose interruptions (<2 wk, or longer in consultation with the sponsor) were permitted. Treatment was discontinued after disease progression, new systemic anticancer therapy was instituted, intolerable toxicities occurred, consent was withdrawn, death occurred, or investigator decision dictated by protocol compliance, whichever occurred first. If clinical benefit was obtained, continued use of dacomitinib beyond disease progression was allowed at the investigator’s discretion.

Tumor imaging assessments (computed tomography [CT] or magnetic resonance imaging [MRI]) were done at screening, at the end of cycle 2, and every other cycle until cycle 18, then every third cycle thereafter. MRI of the brain was required at screening for the detection of CNS metastases. In patients with CNS metastases at baseline, MRI of the brain was required for tumor imaging assessment until cycle 18, with CT of the brain allowed thereafter at the investigator’s discretion. In patients without CNS metastases at baseline, CT of the brain was to be conducted at the end of cycle 6 and every 6 cycles thereafter until the end of treatment. Objective tumor responses were measured using RECIST v.1.1. Intracranial responses were measured using RECIST v.1.1 in the brain.

After disease progression, an evaluation of treatment resistance was conducted using circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) (Guardant360 assay, Guardant Health, Palo Alto, CA) and paired tissue biopsy (where considered safe and feasible by the investigator). Tissue was evaluated for histology, PD-L1 tumor proportion score (SP263, Ventana, Oro Valley, AZ), and next-generation sequencing (NGS) using the Oncomine Comprehensive Assay v.4 (OCA v.4, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). Resistance alterations were defined as compound EGFR mutations with the presence of the baseline EGFR (exon 19 deletion or L858R mutation) plus an additional EGFR (but non-T790M) mutation; results demonstrating multiple potential mechanisms of resistance were classified separately. In cases where both EGFR-dependent (T790M) and -independent resistance mechanisms were identified, they were classified under EGFR-dependent resistance.

Outcomes

The primary objective was to demonstrate that dacomitinib treatment, with or without dose titration, was superior to historical control with respect to the PFS rate at 12 months, as determined by investigators’ assessment in the primary analysis study population, consisting of patients without baseline CNS metastases. Secondary end points included OS, objective response rate (ORR), time to treatment failure (TTF), intracranial objective response rate (iORR), intracranial PFS (iPFS), and the safety and tolerability of dose titration according to toxicity (using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v.5.0). Exploratory objectives included determining the resistance mechanism(s) associated with disease progression on dacomitinib through the evaluation of ctDNA and paired tissue biopsies using NGS and further evaluating the intracranial efficacy of dacomitinib by identifying the site(s) of progression after dacomitinib therapy.

Sample Size

It was estimated that approximately 94 patients would be required to achieve a one-sided significance level of 5% and 90% power to detect a 15% improvement in the 12-month PFS rate in subjects receiving dacomitinib versus historical control for gefitinib3 (i.e., 55% versus 40%), reflecting a superiority design using the intent-to-treat analysis population. The sample size was calculated using A’Hern’s single-stage phase 2 design, and the study was positive if 46 or more patients (of 94) were progression free at 12 months. Assuming 20% of patients had baseline CNS metastases, a total sample size of 118 patients was determined.

Statistical Analysis

The primary end point was the 12-month PFS rate reported as a binomial proportion (termed 12-mo PFS proportion rate) and evaluated only in patients without CNS metastases. The null hypothesis was that the 12-month PFS rate among subjects receiving dacomitinib with or without dose titration was not effective compared with the historical control receiving gefitinib (12-mo PFS rate of 40%), and the alternative hypothesis was that the 12-month PFS rate among subjects receiving dacomitinib with or without dose titration was equal to or more than 55%. The study was considered successful if 46 or more patients (of 94) were progression free at 12 months. Patient demographics and baseline clinical characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics for the primary population. Continuous variables were reported as the median with interquartile range (IQR) or mean with SD and range. Categorical variables were reported as frequency and percentage. The median follow-up time was estimated using the reverse Kaplan-Meier method. PFS, OS, and iPFS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier product-limit method; medians and 12- and 24-month rates were reported with corresponding 95% CIs calculated using the log-log method. The median TTF was reported with the corresponding 95% CI. The 12-month PFS proportion rate, ORR, and iORR were reported with corresponding 90% or 95% CI estimated using the Clopper-Pearson method. Landmark analysis (at the start of cycle 2) was performed to compare PFS and ORR among patients who titrated to 45 mg, remained at 30 mg, or reduced to 15 mg daily at cycle 2 in the primary study population without baseline CNS metastases. A two-sided p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.0).

Clinical Efficacy and Survival Analysis

The median duration of follow-up was 27.4 months (IQR: 20.2–32.2 mo) and 26.4 months (IQR: 19.3–32.2 mo) in all enrolled patients (N = 118) and patients without baseline CNS metastases (N = 94), respectively. The primary end point was met, as 56 of 94 patients were alive and progression free at the end of the first 12 months, which translated to a 12-month PFS proportion rate of 59.6% (90% CI: 50.6%–68.1%). The 12-month PFS estimate was 61.2% (95% CI: 50.5%–70.3%) in the primary analysis cohort (Fig. 2A). Median PFS was 16.1 months (95% CI: 12.1–19.2 mo), and median TTF was 19.3 months (95% CI: 16.1–22.1 mo). Median TTF in the overall study population was 16.6 months (95% CI: 14.7–19.4 mo). In the overall study population, ORR was 77% (95% CI: 68.5%–84.3%; Fig. 3A). The ORR in patients with and without baseline CNS metastases was 71% (95% CI: 48.9%–87.4%) and 79% (95% CI: 69.1%–86.5%), respectively. Furthermore, 21 patients had dose escalation to 45 mg at cycle 2 (060-01-022 had dose titration at cycle 3), among whom two patients had baseline CNS metastases. Considering there were very few dose-titrated patients in the baseline CNS metastases group, the exploratory analysis was performed among patients without baseline CNS metastases. The hazard ratio comparing the two dosing groups of patients was 0.55 (95% CI: 0.29–1.05), although the study was not powered for formal statistical comparison. The toxicity assessment on whether to dose escalate to 45 mg daily was conducted after cycle 1, and there was only one patient who had discontinued treatment (due to disease progression) before the toxicity assessment. Excluding this patient, the median PFS was 21.6 (95% CI: 10.1–NE), 14 (95% CI: 10.2–18.2), and 9.6 (95% CI: 6.5–NE); the ORRs were 84.2% (95% CI: 60.4%–96.6%), 80.0% (95% CI: 68.7%–88.6%), and 50.0% (95% CI: 6.8%–93.2%) among the patients titrated to 45 mg (N = 19), maintained at 30 mg (N = 70), and dose reduced to 15 mg (N = 4) at cycle 2 in the primary study population without baseline CNS metastases, respectively. These results should be interpreted cautiously given the post hoc, exploratory nature of this analysis.

Median PFS was 16.1 months (95% CI: 12.6–21.8 mo) in patients with EGFR exon 19 deletion and 14.8 months (95% CI: 10.9–19.1 mo) in patients with EGFR L858R mutations (Supplementary Fig. 2). ORR was 80.6% (95% CI: 69.1%–89.2%) versus 74.0% (95% CI: 9.7%–85.4%); median PFS was 14.9 (95% CI: 11.1–19.2) versus 14.8 (95% CI: 10.8–19.1) months; 12-month PFS was 62.6% (95% CI: 49.9%–73%) versus 53.1% (95% CI: 38.3%–65.9%) among patients with exon 19 deletion versus L858R in the overall cohort, respectively.

Intracranial Efficacy Analysis

In patients with baseline CNS metastases (n = 24), there were nine patients with measurable CNS disease. The overall iORR was 54% (95% CI: 32.8%–74.4%; Fig. 3B and C), and the median iPFS was 17.6 months (95% CI: 7.2–23.1 mo; Supplementary Fig. 3). The 12- and 24-month iPFS rates were 62.5% (95% CI: 40.3%–78.4%) and 22.7% (95% CI: 6.5%–44.6%), respectively. The median OS in this subgroup was 23.1 months.

Safety Analysis

Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) occurring in more than or equal to 10% of patients are found in Table 3. All patients had TRAEs, with grade 3/4 TRAEs in 25% (Supplementary Table 2). The most common grade 3/4 TRAEs were rash (7%), paronychia (4%), and hypokalemia (3%). The most common TRAEs of any grade were paronychia (90%), diarrhea (86%), and dermatitis acneiform (50%). Furthermore, 13 patients (11%) had AEs/SAEs leading to permanent drug discontinuation or death, among which nine patients (8%) had SAEs leading to death, although none were drug related. Resistance Analysis

As of January 2025, all patients with disease progression underwent plasma NGS analysis, with tissue NGS performed in a subset. In total, 112 patients had post-progression plasma NGS analysis, and 29 patients had tissue biopsy with tissue NGS analysis. In the plasma NGS analysis, there were 85 samples (76%) with detectable nonsynonymous ctDNA alterations, which formed the assessable population for subsequent resistance analysis, including assessment of EGFR T790M (Supplementary Fig. 1). EGFR T790M resistance was detected in 29 of 85 patients (38%) through plasma NGS (Fig. 4A) and in 15 of 29 patients (52%) through tissue NGS (Fig. 4B). There were no cases of histologic transformation in the 29 patients who underwent tissue biopsy post-progression. However, plasma ctDNA did identify three patients with RB1/TP53 co-mutation along with EGFR T790M. Resistance mechanisms in the plasma were classified into EGFR-dependent (n = 36, 42%) and EGFR-independent (n = 42, 50%) mechanisms, with no identifiable resistance mechanisms detected in a subgroup (n = 7, 8%) (Supplementary Fig. 4). EGFR-dependent mechanisms (n = 36) included EGFR T790M mutation alone (75%), compound EGFR mutations (11%), EGFR T790M mutation with RB1/TP53 mutations (8%), and EGFR T790M with ERBB2 amplification (6%). EGFR-independent mechanisms (n = 42) included MET amplification (17%), RAS pathway alterations (12%), HRR pathway alterations (12%), ERBB2 amplification (7%), PIK3CA/AKT pathway alterations (5%), cell cycle pathway alterations (5%), and concurrent ERBB2 with MET amplifications (2%).Figure 4Dacomitinib resistance detected through (A) plasma next-generation sequencing and (B) tissue next-generation sequencing.

Discussion

ATORG-003 met the study’s primary end point, evaluating dacomitinib’s efficacy with a dose-titration strategy in the first-line treatment of advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC, with a 12-month PFS rate of 61.2% and a median PFS of 16.1 months in patients without baseline CNS metastases. This was comparable to prior studies of dacomitinib, in particular ARCHER 1050, in which the median PFS was 14.7 months.3 Importantly, in this study, a high ORR of 77% and a prolonged TTF of 19.3 months suggest that durable responses were found. This reinforces dacomitinib as a viable first-line treatment option, including for patients with CNS metastases which were excluded in ARCHER 1050. This study demonstrates the first prospective evidence of dacomitinib’s intracranial activity, with an iORR of 54% and an iPFS of 17.6 months. The dose-titration strategy demonstrated the tolerability of dacomitinib, with 70% of patients tolerating 30 mg or 45 mg without requiring dose reduction. In the ARCHER 1050 study, only 34% of patients did not require dose reduction. ATORG-003 had a rate of treatment discontinuation due to TRAEs of 5%, whereas ARCHER 1050 had a rate of treatment discontinuation due to TRAEs of 10%. However, cross-trial comparisons should be interpreted cautiously, given the differences in starting dose, eligibility criteria, and follow-up duration.

This study also extends the robust efficacy and safety findings of dacomitinib across a broader Asian population. This is increasingly relevant, particularly in regions where access to or reimbursement for third-generation EGFR TKIs remains a pressing issue.6 Furthermore, with a high rate of EGFR T790M mutations detected in the tissue (52%) and plasma (38%), this finding suggests that a substantial proportion of patients may also benefit from a sequential treatment approach. Recent data on combination first-line therapies with third-generation EGFR TKIs, with either chemotherapy9 or amivantamab,10 have added complexity to the first-line treatment landscape. Data on treatments received and clinical outcomes after progression on dacomitinib were not collected in this study. However, real-world analyses indicate that durable survival outcomes can be found with sequential second-generation to third-generation treatment approaches.11 This study also reinforces the importance of resistance profiling, particularly when considering both tissue and plasma sampling, to guide second-line treatment decisions. Further translational analyses from this trial, using both baseline and post-progression tissue and serially collected plasma, will provide further insight into the resistance trajectory for dacomitinib.

As a single-arm trial, however, there are limitations in making direct comparisons with previous trials and other EGFR TKIs, regarding both efficacy and toxicity. Nevertheless, this study provides prospective trial evidence that a dose-titration strategy for dacomitinib is feasible and practical in real-world clinical practice.

ATORG-003 prospectively revealed that dacomitinib with dose titration is a viable strategy in advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC, to maintain efficacy but improve tolerability. Dacomitinib has substantial intracranial activity and high rates of T790M resistance for sequential treatment approaches.

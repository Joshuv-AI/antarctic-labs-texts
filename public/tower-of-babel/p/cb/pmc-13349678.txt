Factors Associated with Influenza Vaccination Among Children Aged 6 Months to 12 Years — Three Provinces, China, 2024–2025 Influenza Season
Authors: Jiayu He, Zundong Yin, Wenzhou Yu, Lei Cao, Zhaonan Zhang, Yifan Song, Jiakai Ye, Shenyu Wang, Lei Wang, Chao Zhang, Sha Zhang, Qingsong Yu, Li Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction: Influenza vaccination coverage among children in China remains low. This study explored factors associated with childhood influenza vaccination.

Methods: A case-control study was conducted among children aged 6 months to 12 years in Zhejiang, Hubei, and Shaanxi provinces. Cases received influenza vaccine during the 2024–2025 season; age-matched controls from the same county had not been vaccinated against influenza. Standardized questionnaires were used to obtain caregivers' sociodemographic characteristics, influenza vaccine knowledge and attitudes, close contacts' influences, and reasons for vaccination or non-vaccination.

Results: The study included 2,141 children (1,088 cases; 1,053 controls). Vaccination was associated with prior influenza vaccination in the 2023–2024 season [odds ratio (OR): 8.82, 95% confidence interval (CI): 6.83, 11.39] and with vaccination among caregiver's close social contacts (OR=3.67, 95% CI: 2.82, 4.78). The main reasons for vaccination were beliefs that the vaccine protects children's health (95.96%) and the health of others (59.83%). The primary reason for non-vaccination was concern about vaccine efficacy (39.79%).

Conclusion: Previous influenza vaccination and vaccination within caregivers' social networks were associated with increased acceptance of childhood influenza vaccination.

Influenza is an acute respiratory infectious disease caused by influenza virus that poses a substantial threat to child health. Vulnerable populations, including infants and young children, are susceptible to severe clinical outcomes following influenza infection (1). Influenza vaccination remains the most cost-effective public health strategy for children, providing direct protection and contributing to herd immunity.

In China, influenza vaccines are family-paid vaccines not included in the National Immunization Program prior to 2026. Childhood influenza vaccination coverage has been low. During the 2020–2021 influenza season, childhood coverage was 2.9% in China (2), contrasting with 50.7% in the USA (children 6 months–8 years) (3) and 46.1% in Australia (6–59 months) (4). Persistently low coverage underscores the need to understand determinants of caregivers' decision-making regarding childhood influenza vaccination in the Chinese context.

From December 2024 to January 2025, a matched case-control study was conducted in Zhejiang, Hubei, and Shaanxi provinces, representing eastern, central, and western China. None of these provinces provided free influenza vaccination for children. Participants were parents or legal guardians (≥18 years) of children aged 6 months to 12 years old (born between December 2011 and May 2024) who lived in the study county/district for ≥3 months. Caregivers with impaired communication ability were excluded.

The target sample size was calculated using standard formulas for case-control studies with the following parameters: two-sided α=0.05, power (1−β)=0.90, baseline vaccination rate (P0)=10%, target odds ratio (OR)=2.50, design effect=5, and a 10% non-response-rate adjustment. With a 1∶1 case-control ratio, 333 cases and 333 controls were required per province, 1,998 subjects in all.

Participants were identified using a multistage sampling strategy (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/): prefecture-level cities were stratified into high, medium, and low tiers based on influenza vaccine uptake since July 2024; one city was randomly selected from each tier. From each selected city, two counties or districts with higher uptake and two with lower uptake were selected, yielding 12 counties/districts per province, 36 counties/districts in all. In each county/district, a minimum of two vaccination clinics were selected, aiming for a balance between urban community health centers and rural township hospitals.

In a frequency-matched case-control design, children aged 6 months to 12 years living in the county/district were identified using vaccination clinic information systems and stratified into three age groups: 6–23-month-olds, 3–6-year-olds, and 7–12-year-olds. Cases and controls were selected at random from the age strata based on their 2024–2025 influenza vaccination histories and were classified into the vaccinated group (cases) or the unvaccinated group (controls). A minimum of 50 caregivers of eligible children per county or district were targeted, with at least 25 cases and 25 controls proportionally allocated across age strata. Vaccination clinic staff invited caregivers to participate in the study.

A structured questionnaire developed by experts from China CDC was administered in-person by trained investigators after obtaining informed consent. The questionnaire included 1) demographic and socioeconomic characteristics of child and caregiver; 2) caregiver awareness and perceptions of influenza and influenza vaccine; 3) healthcare worker recommendations, family social contacts' influenza vaccination, and the child's prior influenza vaccination history; and 4) reasons for vaccination of cases and non-vaccination of controls.

Data were collated using Excel 2019 and analyzed using SAS (version 9.4; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Descriptive statistics are presented as means (standard deviations, SD) for continuous variables (compared using t-tests) and proportions for categorical variables (compared using chi-square tests). Variables significant in univariate analyses were entered into multilevel conditional logistic regression (matched design) to estimate intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) and evaluate potential clustering. Given small ICC, conditional logistic regression was used to assess associations between independent variables and caregivers' vaccination decisions. Sociodemographic characteristics not significant in univariate analysis were excluded. Province, county/district, and child age group were stratification variables in the conditional logistic regression model. R software (version 4.5.2; R Foundation, Vienna, Austria) was used to generate a forest plot. Tests were two-tailed, with significance at α<0.05.

A total of 2,181 caregivers were surveyed; 2,141 met eligibility criteria and were included in the analyses: 1,088 vaccinated cases and 1,053 unvaccinated controls. A multilevel matched logistic regression model was constructed with provincial-level variables as stratification units, yielding an ICC of <0.001. When stratified by county or district-level variables, the ICC was 0.013, indicating that 1.30% of the variance was attributable to county or district differences. The small proportion of variance attributable to inter-county or district differences supports using conditional logistic regression.

Figure 2 shows reasons associated with caregiver decisions regarding influenza vaccination for their children. Among caregivers of vaccinated children, predominant motivations were protection of their child's health (95.96%, 1,044/1,088), protection of the health of others (59.83%, 651/1,088), and reduction of caregivers' worry about influenza (47.43%, 516/1,088). Among caregivers of unvaccinated children, leading reasons for non-vaccination were concerns about vaccine effectiveness (39.79%, 419/1,053), adverse events (39.32%, 414/1,053), and belief that the child's good health rendered vaccination unnecessary (36.28%, 382/1,053).

DISCUSSION

The matched case-control study of childhood influenza vaccination in three Chinese provinces found that a child's previous receipt of influenza vaccine and concurrent-season vaccination among caregivers' social contacts (family, relatives, friends) were positively associated with caregivers' decisions to vaccinate their children, providing a reference for future interventions.

Strong association (OR=8.82) between previous and current childhood influenza vaccinations indicates that previous vaccination experience is a good predictor of vaccine acceptance. However, vaccination history is not a simple modifiable predictor, as it involves caregiver attitudes, habitual vaccination behaviors, and accessible service channels. A positive vaccination experience helps establish acceptance and participation in immunization services.

Receipt of influenza vaccine by social contacts was the second most influential factor in caregivers' decision-making, aligning with existing evidence that social influences encourage caregivers to opt for vaccination (5). Knowing that close social contacts have been vaccinated provides caregivers with firsthand information, positively shapes attitudes, and encourages adherence to group norms – factors for consideration in promotion efforts to strengthen influenza vaccination of children.

Recommendations from healthcare professionals strongly influence caregiver decisions and pediatric vaccination rates. However, influenza vaccine uptake among healthcare professionals in China is lower than in many other countries (6), due inpart to vaccine effectiveness or safety concerns (5). Unvaccinated healthcare workers may be less willing to recommend vaccination to patients. An integrated clinical/public-health framework with professional training, patient education, vaccine prescriptions from clinicians, and vaccination services in healthcare settings will improve accessibility and convenience. Such integrated efforts may increase healthcare worker engagement with influenza vaccination and contribute to higher acceptance.

The results showed that caregivers' perceptions of vaccine effectiveness and importance and concerns about adverse reactions influenced vaccination decisions — consistent with studies finding similar challenges for sustaining confidence (7), underscoring the importance of interventions that reinforce population-specific determinants of vaccine confidence (8).

A study limitation is the demand-side focus on vaccination services without supply-side analyses. The setting had no regions offering free influenza childhood vaccination; therefore, findings cannot be generalized to settings offering free influenza vaccination for children, which is known to enhance vaccination rates (9). Further investigation into free vaccination policies is warranted. The study did not collect data on whether caregivers themselves were vaccinated, potentially interfering with estimation of social network effects.

In conclusion, child's prior influenza vaccination, vaccine uptake within caregivers' social network, and healthcare worker recommendation were associated with childhood influenza vaccination, providing evidence for targeted interventions to increase influenza vaccination of children, and strategies for influenza prevention and control.

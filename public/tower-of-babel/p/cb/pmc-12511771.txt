Treatment of Congenital Hyperinsulinism With a Novel, Long-acting Glucagon Analogue
Authors: Molly Cannon, Jessica Meline, Lauren M Mitteer, Diva D De Leon
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCongenital hyperinsulinism (HI) is a disorder of insulin regulation that results in hypoketotic hypoglycemia and is associated with a high risk of neurological sequalae from hypoglycemia-induced brain damage. There is only 1 Food and Drug Administration-approved drug for the treatment of HI, and up to 60% of patients do not respond to it; thus, there is a significant unmet need for new treatments. We report a case of a 29-year-old female with HI due to an activating glucokinase pathogenic variant, who was diagnosed as an adult and continued to have persistent hypoglycemia despite standard-of-care therapy. The patient was enrolled in the phase II clinical trial evaluating efpegerglucagon, a novel long-acting glucagon analog. She has continued treatment with efpegerglucagon through a single-patient Investigational New Drug Expanded Access Program and has demonstrated improved glycemic control throughout 10 months of treatment. Efpegerglucagon may be a viable treatment option or adjunct therapy for patients with HI who are unresponsive or partially responsive to currently available therapies.Keywords: hyperinsulinism, glucagon, hypoglycemia, insulin, investigational, rare diseases

Introduction

Congenital hyperinsulinism (HI) is the most common cause of persistent hypoglycemia in infants and children [1]. There are multiple genetic causes of HI, with the most common defects affecting the β-cell pathway regulating glucose-stimulated insulin secretion [2]. This includes both (1) glucokinase, the rate-limiting enzyme for glucose entry into glycolysis in the β cell, and (2) the β-cell adenosine triphosphate-sensitive potassium channels (KATP), which couple the metabolic state of the cell to membrane potential.

There is only 1 drug approved by the Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of HI, and that is diazoxide, a KATP channel opener, but up to 60% of patients, including those with KATP channel defects and glucokinase pathogenic variants, are unresponsive or just partially responsive to it [3]. For diazoxide-unresponsive patients, off-label use of somatostatin analogues is the second line of treatment, which is used alone or in combination with continuous enteral feedings or dextrose [4-6]. When medical treatment fails, pancreatectomy may be required [4]. Other treatments have been used with variable success, including glucagon, which is routinely used as a temporary measure to decrease the requirement for IV dextrose in the inpatient setting [7]. The long-term use of commercially available glucagon is limited because of its short half-life and propensity to crystallize in solution.

Efpegerglucagon is a novel, long-acting glucagon analog that, at the time of this report, is being evaluated for the treatment of HI in a phase II clinical trial (NCT04732416). In previous preclinical and phase I trials, efpegerglucagon demonstrated stability at neutral pH and extended pharmacokinetics. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single-ascending dose study on healthy adults, efpegerglucagon was safe and well-tolerated, exhibited a prolonged half-life compared to glucagon, and resulted in increased fasting plasma glucose [8]. In a multiple ascending dose study on obese or overweight adults with comorbidities, efpegerglucagon was also safe and well-tolerated during the 12-week treatment period at various dose levels, resulting in a dose-dependent increase in plasma glucose. In a preclinical study in rodents, efpegerglucagon reversed hypoglycemia induced by insulin [9]. The current phase II clinical trial in HI is evaluating the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and activity of efpegerglucagon given in 8 weekly injections to individuals aged ≥2 years with HI. Based on the known pharmacological profile and preclinical findings, adverse events considered related to the drug include the following events: gastrointestinal (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea); metabolic (changes in glucose levels); cardiovascular (hyper- and hypotension); immunological (anaphylaxis); general injection site reactions; investigational (abnormal liver function tests); nervous system (dizziness, headache); dermatologic (necrolytic migratory erythema); salivary secretion changes; and diuresis.

This case report describes the clinical course of a 29-year-old female with partially diazoxide-responsive glucokinase HI who is receiving treatment with the investigational product efpegerglucagon through a single-patient Investigational New Drug application (sIND).

Case Presentation

The patient had a history of neonatal hypoglycemia immediately after birth, which was initially considered resolved before discharge from the well-baby nursery. The patient denied any previous neuroglycopenic symptoms and developmental delays in childhood, which is corroborated by her available medical records. For several years, she experienced symptoms of nausea, anxiety, and sweating, which were attributed to anxiety. The patient did not endorse symptomatic hypoglycemia events when interviewed. She had no documented family history of glycemic disorders and had never been prescribed glucose monitoring devices. Routine laboratory studies revealed an incidental finding of a plasma glucose level of 45 mg/dL (SI: 2.5 mmol/L) (reference range: 74-106 mg/dL [SI: 4.1-5.9 mmol/L]), prompting further endocrinological evaluation at age 26 years.

Given her age at diagnosis, an insulinoma was suspected, as it is the most frequent cause of hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia among adolescents and adults [12]. Imaging studies obtained to localize a lesion included computed tomography/magnetic resonance imaging, a gallium-68 dodecanetetraacetic acid-tyrosine-3-octreotate scan, and endoscopic ultrasound. She also underwent arterial calcium stimulation with venous sampling. However, all these studies were negative.

Imaging studies were repeated later including arterial calcium stimulation with venous sampling, which was again negative, and a gallium-68 dodecanetetraacetic acid-tyrosine-3-octreotate magnetic resonance imaging/computed tomography scan, which showed a 1.3 cm mass along the superior aspect of the tail of the pancreas with minimal radiotracer avidity (standardized uptake value was 6.47, normalized by patient's body weight). She underwent laparoscopic pancreatic surgery for enucleation of a suspected insulinoma, though histopathological review of the resected tissue showed normal pancreas.

Because of the inability to find a lesion, genetic testing for HI was obtained and revealed a pathogenic, heterozygous activating variant in glucokinase (GCK; c.1367C>T [p.Ala456Val]).

Treatment

Since diagnosis, the patient has been managed with several medical therapies, including diazoxide at varying doses, as well as octreotide, which was administered as frequently as 7 times daily prior to being discontinued due to lack of perceived benefit, and pasireotide, at 900 mcg twice daily as an agent to prevent fistula formation after her surgery, which resulted in higher glucoses but was associated with significant side effects. She was also treated with verapamil, though this was also found to not yield a positive benefit/risk assessment and was discontinued.

Because of persistent hypoglycemia, the patient was referred to our center for a second opinion. Her glucose at that time ranged from 60 to 130 mg/dL (SI: 3.3-7.2 mmol/L) on diazoxide 210 mg/day, which was gradually increased to 380 mg/day following her initial visit.

She was then enrolled in a phase II clinical trial (NCT04732416) investigating the safety and tolerability of efpegerglucagon.

The patient received a total of 8 consecutive weekly dosages while continuing treatment with diazoxide 380 mg/day. During the clinical trial, the patient's frequency of hypoglycemic events decreased, and her continuous glucose monitor (CGM) showed increased time in euglycemia. Given her glycemic improvement with efpegerglucagon treatment during the phase II clinical trial, we applied for and obtained Food and Drug Administration and institutional review board approval for an investigator-initiated sIND.

After consenting to the sIND, the patient was trained by the research team on the administration of the drug by subcutaneous injection. She has been on weekly injections of efpegerglucagon (0.04 mg/kg) for 10 months so far.

Outcome and Follow-up

The patient has continued to demonstrate improvement in glycemic control while on efpegerglucagon. This has been demonstrated by capillary blood glucose monitoring in addition to CGM data capture. We have also been able to decrease the patient's diazoxide dosage from 380 mg/day to 340 mg/day while in the sIND. The patient has reported intermittent nausea events that are infrequent and typically resolve quickly. She reports that such events have not impacted her ability to eat her typical meals and snacks.

Overall, the patient reports increased quality of life and increased energy since starting efpegerglucagon. She has tolerated the drug well with side effects that have been self-limited. Side effects that were deemed potentially related to efpegerglucagon that she has exhibited include nausea and increased appetite. Her glycemic control has improved, and her concomitant standard of care therapy has been reduced.

Discussion

Some genetic forms of HI do not respond or respond only partially to available therapies on the market [4]. Thus, there is a significant unmet need for more effective treatment options for HI. Efpegerglucagon, a novel long-acting glucagon analog with extended half-life, may be a beneficial alternative for individuals whose HI does not sufficiently respond to diazoxide or second-line therapies. We present here a patient trialed on several different medications to manage HI who still experienced persistent hypoglycemia. After receiving treatment with efpegerglucagon, the patient was found to tolerate the drug well and experienced significant improvements in glycemic control (as demonstrated by capillary glucose and CGM data). Additionally, we were able to decrease her concomitant diazoxide dosage, and she has reported improved quality of life.

This patient has demonstrated use of medication for over 10 months with continued improvement in glycemic control. More information and monitoring of efpegerglucagon is needed, but preliminary data from this patient's case have demonstrated positive impacts on overall glycemic control and management of HI.

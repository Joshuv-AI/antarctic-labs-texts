Localized Injection of Lidocaine and Glucocorticoid for Refractory Headache Treatment (LIGHT): A Phase 1 Clinical Trial for Safety
Authors: Manisha Koneru, Hamza A Shaikh, Jane Khalife, Larisa Syrow, Joshua Santucci, Ahmad A Ballout, Pratit D Patel, Ajith J Thomas, Tudor G Jovin, Daniel A Tonetti
Published: 2025

Intractable migraine is a clinically challenging condition; the inability of standard therapies to adequately relieve symptoms can cause significant pain burden, migraine‐related disability, and impaired quality of life. 1 A novel endovascular approach to treatment may have potential as a treatment strategy for patients with treatment‐refractory migraine. A proposed cause of intractable migraine is repetitive ganglion hyperactivation and persistent inflammation. Lidocaine interferes with aberrant sensory neuron depolarization, and methylprednisolone reduces local inflammation. Thus, it is hypothesized that both agents may act synergistically to break the cycle of neuroinflammatory and hyperactivation that causes intractable migraines. Early clinical experience from Quershi et al (2014) demonstrated a favorable response to intra‐arterial infusion of lidocaine and methylprednisolone into the middle meningeal artery (MMA). 2 Although subsequent case series using one or more of these agents in the MMA have demonstrated similar positive experiences, there lacks prospective clinical trial data for this novel endovascular treatment approach. 3

The objective of this phase 1 clinical trial was to characterize the safety of endovascular intra‐arterial infusion of lidocaine and methylprednisolone into the MMA for patients with treatment‐refractory migraine.

Nonstandard Abbreviations and Acronyms

middle meningeal artery

This study was a nonrandomized, open‐label, single‐arm phase 1 safety trial conducted at a single center between March 2024 and May 2025 (NCT06462781). This trial was approved by the Cooper Health System Institutional Review Board (23‐267); deidentified data may be made available on reasonable request. Ten adult subjects with refractory migraine without aura, defined as lack of improvement after adequate trials (ie, no symptomatic change after >6 weeks at therapeutic dosing or discontinuation because of adverse effect) of ≥4 migraine therapies, were consented to receive the study treatment; enrollees were observed for 3 months for any acute and delayed adverse events related to the study treatment. Subjects received a 1‐time, endovascular, intra‐arterial injection of lidocaine hydrochloride (40 mg per side) and methylprednisolone sodium succinate (20 mg per side) into the MMA 1 cm distal to the foramen spinosum bilaterally, with each medication infused over 5 minutes. Incidence of adverse events was collated, adjudicated by 2 physicians independently with consensus decision for differences, and classified per severity and relatedness to the study treatment. A descriptive analysis was conducted to summarize these safety events using JMP version 18 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

Of 10 consented subjects, the median age was 54.5 years (interquartile range, 47–59.8 years), and 70% (7/10) were women. Ten patients were screened for this study; there were no screen failures. Ninety percent (9/10) received treatment. One patient was lost to follow‐up before treatment, and 1 patient was lost to 3‐month follow‐up. During the study follow‐up, a total of 13 potential adverse events were ascertained; 7 (53.8%) events were not related to the trial, treatment, or migraines. Four (30.8%) of these adverse events were recurrence of migraine symptoms.

Two nonserious adverse events (15.4%) were possibly related to the study intervention. One patient had postprocedural dizziness and blurry vision; the symptoms persisted for a few hours and self‐resolved. Another patient had intraprocedural temporary peripheral facial paresis, presenting as a partial facial droop, starting within 2 minutes of lidocaine infusion into the ipsilateral MMA, and fully resolved within 1 hour without additional intervention. This adverse event was hypothesized to have been caused by reflux to the petrosal branch of the MMA, causing a transient peripheral facial palsy.

There were no strokes, severe or persistent neurologic events, access site issues, or mortalities related to the study intervention reported. No patients reported worsening of migraine symptoms following treatment; most patients reported either improvement or no change in the intensity or frequency of migraines.

The data from this phase 1 clinical trial suggest favorable safety and tolerability of the investigational intervention. No serious adverse events were associated with the study intervention. Consequently, intra‐arterial lidocaine and methylprednisolone demonstrate promise as a novel, safe approach to refractory migraines. However, there are limitations to acknowledge. First, although our cohort demonstrated adequate safety of this intervention, continued safety monitoring in subsequent trial phases with larger cohorts is necessary to validate these findings. Moreover, efficacy data were limited given that the trial was designed for characterizing safety; future data from a forthcoming phase 2 clinical trial will characterize efficacy and provide additional safety data for this new treatment.

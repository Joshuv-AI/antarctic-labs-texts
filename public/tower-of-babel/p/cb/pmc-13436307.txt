Dysregulated myeloid-T cell immunity in refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia: high inflammation meets T cell suppression
Authors: Yanjuan Yu, Zijie Li, Gaijing Jia, Chunbo Hao, Genhao Wang, Shouhang Chen, Yang Wu, Fang Wang, Yuefei Jin, Yu Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlthough the clinical burden associated with refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (RMPP) has been continuously increasing in recent years, the immunological mechanisms of RMPP remain poorly understood.MethodsA total of 27 RMPP patients, 24 general Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (GMPP) patients, and 24 bronchial foreign body patients were enrolled in this study. Protein array technology was used to assess the cytokine profiles in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) samples from the patients. Functional interactions among differentially expressed cytokines (DECs) were investigated using protein-protein interaction (PPI) network and pathway enrichment analyses. The impact of DECs on the clinical prognosis and laboratory indicators of the patients was further analyzed in conjunction with clinical data.ResultsSeven DECs were identified: T cell-associated (IL-12p70, IFN-γ, IL-7, IL-4) and myeloid-associated (IL-8, MCP-1, MIP-1β). Compared with GMPP, RMPP BALF showed significantly decreased T cell cytokines and elevated myeloid cytokines. Pathway enrichment analysis revealed these DECs were significantly enriched in the JAK-STAT signaling pathway. ROC analysis suggested that IL-12p70 and IL-4 had the highest discriminative ability among the DECs, but these results are preliminary due to the limited sample size and need external validation. Furthermore, all seven DECs showed correlations with patients’ clinical indicators and prognostic parameters.ConclusionsRMPP exhibits a dual immune dysregulation: myeloid hyperinflammation and T cell suppression, with the dysregulated cytokine network converging on JAK-STAT signaling. This provides novel mechanistic insights and identifies potential therapeutic targets.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12866-026-05230-1.Keywords: Refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia, Cytokine profile, T Cell Suppression, Bronchoalveolar lavage fluid

Introduction

Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MP) is an important pathogen that causes community-acquired pneumonia, accounting for up to 20%-40% of pediatric cases [1, 2]. Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) presents diverse and complex clinical manifestations, making it challenging to diagnose. As a highly contagious respiratory disease, it poses significant public health concerns [3]. Although MPP is generally considered a self-limiting disease and most patients can achieve effective symptom relief through macrolide antibiotic treatment, some patients may progress to refractory Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (RMPP) in certain special cases [4].

After standardized antibiotic treatment, RMPP patients may still exhibit persistent immune dysfunction, which can potentially trigger a cytokine storm and lead to serious complications such as necrotizing pneumonia and atelectasis [5]. In severe cases, it may even pose a life-threatening risk to the patient. In recent years, both in China and globally, the number of reported cases of RMPP has been steadily increasing, highlighting the urgent need for heightened attention to this condition [6].

Currently, the underlying mechanism responsible for RMPP has not been fully clarified. Earlier studies indicated that drug-resistant mutations of MP, reinfections, and co-infections were among the primary factors may be associated with RMPP [6]. However, as research in this field has advanced, the traditional perspective has increasingly come into question [7]. For an extended period, it has been widely recognized that dysregulation of the host immune response plays a pivotal role in disease progression. Moreover, studies have reported an aberrantly heightened innate immune response in RMPP patients, including the activation of macrophages via Toll-like receptor heterodimerization [8].

Cytokines, as the most direct biomarkers reflecting host immune function, have been extensively reported in the literature to be closely associated with RMPP in children, particularly inflammatory factors and chemokines [9, 10]. However, due to the lack of a comprehensive study on pulmonary cytokines, it is still impossible to deeply reveal the pathogenesis of RMPP. Therefore, this study aims to elucidate the pulmonary immune response in RMPP patients and investigate its potential pathogenic mechanisms and to identify potential immunomodulatory targets for this refractory condition by analyzing the cytokine profile in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) obtained from children diagnosed with RMPP and general Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (GMPP).

Materials and methods

Study participants

This was a retrospective observational case-control study. The study was conducted between October 2023 and March 2024 at Henan Children’s Hospital. A total of 51 pediatric patients diagnosed with MPP were enrolled during this period. The diagnostic criteria for MPP are based on the “Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Mycoplasma Pneumoniae Pneumonia in Children (2023 Edition) [11].”

Children were included in the study if they presented with: (1) cough, fever, tachypnoea, and abnormal breath sounds; (2) typical MPP imaging changes (interstitial infiltrates, segmental and lobar consolidations, hilar lymph node enlargement); (3) positive nucleic acid amplification testing (NAAT) results for MP RNA or positive serum MP antibody results which were defined as (i) MP antibody titre ≥ 1:160 on admission or (ii) MP antibody titre seroconversion from negative (< 1:80) to positive (≥ 1:160) within two weeks; (4) negative NAAT results for a panel of other respiratory viruses, including SARS-CoV-2, influenza A virus, influenza B virus, respiratory syncytial virus, adenovirus, rhinovirus, and Epstein‑Barr virus; (5) the patient underwent a fiberoptic bronchoscopy examination. All patients received standard macrolide antibiotic treatment following a definitive diagnosis. RMPP is defined as a condition in children with MPP who, despite undergoing standard treatment with macrolide antibiotics for seven days, continue to experience persistent fever, progressive clinical symptoms, deteriorating pulmonary imaging findings, and the emergence of extrapulmonary complications.

Given the impossibility of obtaining BALF samples that meet ethical standards from healthy children, we enrolled BFB patients who lacked clinical, laboratory, and imaging signs of infection as a surrogate control group. Although BFB may cause local mechanical irritation, the selected patients had short retention times without evidence of obstructive pneumonia or severe mucosal injury. Specifically, BFB patients were included only if they met the following criteria: (1) The lavage time was within 72 h of disease onset (2) absence of fever (axillary temperature < 37.5 °C) within 24 h before bronchoscopy; (3) normal peripheral white blood cell count and C-reactive protein level; (4) no radiographic evidence of pneumonia, atelectasis, or obstructive emphysema; and (5) no clinical symptoms suggesting acute respiratory infection, such as productive cough, tachypnea, or hypoxemia. Patients with any sign of post-obstructive pneumonia, mucosal necrosis, or foreign body retention lasting more than 72 h were excluded. Finally, a total of 24 BFB patients were ultimately included as the control group.

The clinical data including age, gender, physical signs, comorbidities, laboratory indicators and imaging indicators are retrieved from patients’ medical records. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhengzhou University (No. ZZUIRB2024-204), and written informed consent was obtained from both the participants and their parent/legal guardian (s).

Clinical data and specimen collection

All patients underwent comprehensive physical examination, imaging evaluation, detection of drug resistance sites of MP and routine laboratory testing. The clinical data including age, gender, physical signs, comorbidities, laboratory indicators, imaging indicators and mutation status of drug resistance sites of MP are retrieved from patients’ medical records. The image recovery time is defined as the duration required for significant resolution of extensive infiltrates on the chest X-ray, starting from the initial manifestation of pneumonia symptoms in patients. The area of pulmonary consolidation is defined as the product of the length and width of the largest consolidation region observed in imaging examinations.

Fiberoptic bronchoscopy was performed transnasally under intravenous sedation. Following the Clinical Practice Guidelines for Bronchoalveolar Lavage in Chinese Children (2024) [12], sterile pre-warmed (37 °C) normal saline was instilled in three sequential aliquots of 1 mL/kg (maximum 20 mL per aliquot). Each aliquot was aspirated with gentle negative pressure, and only samples with a recovery rate ≥ 40% were included. Ultimately, this study collected a total of 5-10mL of BALF, which was directly transferred to a 50 mL sterile plastic tube and immediately stored in a -80℃ freezer for future use.

Regarding the timing of bronchoscopy, all MPP patients received standard macrolide therapy upon diagnosis. Bronchoscopy with BALF sampling was not performed at baseline but was indicated after ≥ 72 h of therapy in cases with suspected clinical or radiological progression. For patients ultimately classified as RMPP, the bronchoscopy was performed before the 7-day endpoint, typically between day 3 and day 7. This standardized approach minimized variability in treatment exposure prior to sampling and ensured that the cytokine profiles reflected the early progression phase rather than prolonged treatment failure.

Analysis of the cytokine profile in BALF

To quantitatively analyze the cytokine profile in the BALF of patients, this study employed the RayPlex Human Cytokine Storm Microbead Array Kit (Catalog No. FAH-STRM-1, Ray Biotech, USA), which is based on protein antibody array technology. The measurement range of the kit covers the following analytes: granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF), granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), monocyte chemotactic protein-1 (MCP-1), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interferon-γ (IFN-γ), macrophage inflammatory protein-1α (MIP-1α), MIP-1β, regulated on activation, normal T expressed and secreted (RANTES), eotaxin, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), platelet-derived growth factor BB (PDGF-BB), interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1RA), interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-2, IL-4, IL-5, IL-6, IL-7, IL-8, IL-10, IL-12p70, IL-13, IL-15 and IL-17 A.

Centrifuge at 1000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C to remove cell debris and insoluble components from the BALF. Then, collect the supernatant and mix it thoroughly. Subsequently, take 25 µL of the original BALF from each sample for cytokine concentration determination. All subsequent experimental procedures are strictly conducted in accordance with the technical manual provided by the reagent supplier.

Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network

The interaction analysis of differentially expressed cytokines (DECs) across groups was conducted using version 11.5 of the Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes/Proteins (STRING) software version 11.5 (http://string-db.org). The database integrates data from multiple experimental sources and employs a combination of text mining and computational modeling techniques to provide insights into PPIs. Given that the proteins in the STRING database are typically represented at the gene level, both subunit protein components of IL-12p70 (IL-12 A and IL-12B) were included in the analysis to more accurately capture its functional properties. The PPI network was imported into Cytoscape (v3.10.2). Node degree and betweenness centrality were calculated using the NetworkAnalyzer tool. Graphs were treated as undirected, and all parameters were kept at default settings.

Pathway enrichment analysis

To further elucidate the biological pathways potentially dysregulated in RMPP, we performed pathway enrichment analysis based on the PPI network of DECs. Using the STRING database (version 11.5), we applied the Local Network Cluster (LNC) algorithm with a confidence score threshold of 0.8 to identify significantly enriched Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways. The analysis was restricted to the immediate network neighborhood of the seven DECs, including both direct and indirect interactions. Statistical significance was determined by false discovery rate (FDR) correction, with FDR < 0.05 considered statistically significant. The enrichment results were visualized as a bubble plot, where the size of each bubble represents the number of DECs involved in the pathway, and the color intensity corresponds to the -log10(FDR) value.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed with SPSS (version 26, IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism (version 9, GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). For quantitative data that conforms to a normal distribution, the mean ± standard deviation is used as the measure of central tendency and dispersion. For quantitative data that does not conform to a normal distribution, the median and interquartile range (IQR) are employed as the preferred descriptive statistics, Categorical data is presented in the form of frequencies and percentages.

In this study, independent samples t-test, chi-square test, fisher’s exact test were used to conduct statistical analyses on the differences in continuous variables and categorical variables among groups.

For each type of cytokine, due to the small sample size and the fact that most of the analysis indicators did not follow a normal distribution, the Mann-Whitney U test was used to conduct pairwise comparisons among the three groups. The further study focused on comparing the RMPP and GMPP groups. Therefore, the 25 P-values obtained from the comparison between RMPP and GMPP were corrected for false discovery rate (FDR) using the Benjamini-Kritchman-Yekutieli two-stage linear step-up method. The desired FDR threshold (Q) was set at 20%, appropriate for this exploratory study. A cytokine was defined as a differentially expressed cytokine (DEC) if it met both of the following criteria: (i) nominal P < 0.05 in the Mann-Whitney test for RMPP vs. GMPP, and (ii) FDR-adjusted q value < 0.20. Spearman correlation coefficient was employed to analyze the correlations among the DECs and to further investigate their potential associations with laboratory indicators, length of stay, extent of pulmonary consolidation, duration of antibiotic use, and time required for imaging improvement. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was employed to evaluate the discriminative ability of the DECs as a diagnostic biomarker for RMPP.

To assess the overall association of each cytokine group with laboratory parameters, we calculated Z‑scores for each DEC based on all MPP patients. The T‑cell function score was defined as the mean Z‑score of IL‑12p70, IL‑4, IL‑7, and IFN‑γ; the myeloid inflammation score as the mean Z‑score of IL‑8, MCP‑1, and MIP‑1β. Spearman correlation coefficient were then computed between each composite score and laboratory indicators. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

To further explore the pathogenic mechanism of RMPP and its impact on the severity of clinical symptoms in patients, this study analyzed the imaging data from relevant patients and the mutation status of macrolide-resistant loci in their bodies. The results indicated that positive mutations in the MP resistance locus were observed in 81.47% of RMPP patients, compared to 66.67% in those with GMPP. Although a numerical difference was noted between the two groups, the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.226), suggesting that mutations in the MP resistance locus are not the primary contributing factor to the development of RMPP (Fig. 1A).

By measuring and calculating the pulmonary consolidation area, imaging results demonstrate that the area of pulmonary consolidation in RMPP patients is significantly greater than that in GMPP patients (t = -2.149, P = 0.037), indicating more severe pulmonary involvement in RMPP patients (Fig. 1B, C).

Cytokine profiling analysis in BALF of MPP patients

In this study, the protein array technique was employed to quantify cytokine levels in BALF obtained from three groups, with the aim of further elucidating the pulmonary immune response in RMPP patients and revealing its potential pathogenic mechanism. Importantly, the overall cytokine levels in the BFB group were relatively low, and most indicators showed significant differences compared with those in the GMPP group, suggesting that this group provides an acceptable baseline for evaluating the pulmonary immune response in patients with MPP.

The cytokine profiling analysis results were categorized into three distinct groups: inflammatory factors (Fig. 2), chemokines (Fig. 3A), and growth factors (Fig. 3B), for presentation. The results of the Mann-Whitney U test and false discovery rate (FDR) correction between the RMPP and GMPP groups are provided in Supplementary Table S1. The results of variance analysis indicated that the distributions of all cytokine indicators among the three groups were significantly different. Further pairwise comparisons indicated that, among the detected cytokines, only IL-8, MIP-1β, and PDGF-BB exhibited no statistically significant differences between GMPP and control group. This suggests that MPP induces a broad spectrum of immune responses. Additionally, compared to the GMPP group, the RMPP group exhibited significantly decreased levels of IL-4, IL-7, IL-12p70, and IFN-γ, while the concentrations of IL-8, MCP-1, and MIP-1β were significantly elevated.

Protein-protein interaction and correlations among DECs

To further elucidate the underlying mechanisms of DECs in RMPP, this study utilized the STRING database to construct a PPI network and perform comprehensive analyses. The analysis results indicate that the selected DECs demonstrate strong functional interconnectivity. All identified cytokines exhibit features of interactive networking and can be categorized into three distinct functional clusters (Supplementary Figure S1). Topological analysis of the PPI network revealed that four nodes (CCL2, CCL4, CXCL8, and IFNG) exhibited the highest degree. These nodes also had the highest betweenness centrality (0.031), suggesting that they serve as key bridges for signal flow. In contrast, IL7 and IL12A showed a betweenness centrality of 0 and lower degree, consistent with their peripheral positions. Detailed topological metrics are provided in Supplementary Table S2.

Spearman rank correlation coefficient was employed to quantify the strength of the association between DECs. The results indicated a strong positive correlation among IL-8, MCP-1, and MIP-1β (R > 0.70). Similarly, a relatively strong positive correlation was observed among IL-12p70, IFN-γ, IL-7, and IL-4 (R > 0.65) (Fig. 4A). Based on the functional characteristics of cytokines, in conjunction with the results from PPI network analysis and Spearman correlation analysis, this study categorized the DECs into two clusters: myeloid cell-related cytokines (IL-8, MCP-1, and MIP-1β) and T cell-related cytokines (IL-12p70, IL-4, IL-7, and IFN-γ). The stacked bar chart illustrates the variations in the average concentrations of DECs across different groups (Fig. 4B). The results demonstrate that, compared to the GMPP group, the RMPP group exhibits generally increased levels of myeloid cell-associated cytokines and decreased levels of T cell-associated cytokines in the BALF of patients.

Pathway enrichment analysis of DECs-associated network

To acquire more profound insights into the biological processes fundamental to cytokine dysregulation in RMPP, we conducted a pathway enrichment analysis on the PPI network neighborhood of the seven identified DECs. The most notably enriched pathways, as shown in Fig. 4C, are as follows: (1) JAK-STAT signaling pathway (FDR = 8.95 × 10⁻7); (2) Chemokine receptors bind chemokines (FDR = 0.0015). The convergence of DECs on the JAK-STAT pathway implies a potential shared regulatory mechanism that could be targeted for therapeutic intervention.

The capacity of DECs to distinguish RMPP and their correlation with clinical endpoints

In this study, ROC curve analysis was employed to assess the discriminatory capacity of various DECs in distinguishing patients with GMPP from those with RMPP. The results indicated that the area under the curve (AUC) values for T cell-related cytokines, including IL-12p70, IL-4, IL-7, and IFN-γ, were all relatively high and exceeding 0.7 (Fig. 5A). These findings suggest that T cell-related DECs may serve as promising biomarkers for RMPP.

To investigate the impact of concentrations of DECs on disease prognosis in patients, this study selected four key prognostic indicators: the area of pulmonary consolidation, length of stay, duration of imaging improvement, and duration of antibiotic use. The research findings indicate that the concentrations of T cell-related DECs are negatively correlated with various prognostic parameters. Among these cytokines, IL-12p70 exhibits the strongest significant correlation with both the duration of antibiotic treatment (R = -0.73) and the length of stay (R = -0.64), both of which are statistically highly significant (P < 0.001). On the contrary, the concentrations of myeloid cell-associated DECs are positively correlated with prognostic indicators (Fig. 5B). In summary, the hyperactivation of myeloid cells and the suppression of T cell function may collectively represent a key mechanism underlying the poor prognosis of RMPP.

Correlation analysis between DECs and laboratory indicators

This study further investigated the correlations between these cytokines and various clinical examination indicators in patients. This research selected the following detection indicators: white blood cell count (WBC), blood platelet count, neutrophil count, lymphocyte %, monocyte %, c-reactive protein (CRP), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), D-dimer (DD), procalcitonin (PCT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), creatine kinase (CK), creatine kinase isoenzyme-MB (CK-MB), prothrombin time (PT), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), and fibrinogen (FIB). The results of Spearman correlation analysis indicated a significant association between laboratory indicators and DECs, suggesting an important interactive relationship between cytokine concentration in the BALF of patients and laboratory indicators (Fig. 6). It is noteworthy that a significant correlation exists between neutrophil counts and the concentrations of all DECs.

Discussion

In recent years, the incidence of RMPP has been increasing, often resulting in severe complications and an unfavorable prognosis for MPP patients [13]. Therefore, elucidating the pathogenesis of RMPP is of significant importance and may provide a foundation for effective early clinical interventions. This study systematically analyzed the clinical manifestations, laboratory indicators, imaging features, and prognosis of patients with RMPP and GMPP. The cytokine expression profiles in BALF from 51 MPP patients and the control group were assessed using protein chip technology, and the influence of DECs on disease progression was investigated. Through the multidimensional study of RMPP patients, a new research perspective is provided to further reveal its potential pathogenesis.

Consistent with previous studies, this study demonstrates that patients in the RMPP group exhibit more severe clinical manifestations, laboratory abnormalities, and poorer prognostic outcomes compared to those in the GMPP group [4, 14, 15]. In terms of laboratory indicators, RMPP is primarily characterized by significant alterations in neutrophil and LDH among GMPP patients. The results of imaging analysis indicate that RMPP is associated with an expanded pulmonary consolidation area in patients with MPP, which is often linked to prolonged hospitalization and a worse clinical prognosis [16]. Furthermore, the detection results of MP resistance site mutations in patients suggest that mutations at these resistance sites are not the primary factors contributing to the development of RMPP [17].

The dynamic changes in cytokine concentrations are critically important for the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of diseases, as their fluctuations can provide key insights into the underlying pathological processes [18]. This study established a non-inflammatory baseline using BALF from patients with bronchial foreign bodies, providing a critical reference for evaluating pulmonary immunity in MPP. Compared to patients with GMPP, those with RMPP exhibited two distinct alterations in the cytokine profile of BALF: first, a significant increase in the concentration of myeloid cell-associated cytokines, including IL-8, MCP-1, and MIP-1β; second, a marked decrease in the concentration of T cell-associated cytokines, such as IL-12p70, IL-4, IL-7, and IFN-γ. Elevated concentration of myeloid-related cytokines have been extensively documented in prior research [19, 20]. Nevertheless, several studies have also indicated that T cell-associated cytokines, such as IFN-γ, exhibit increased concentrations in patients with RMPP [9, 21, 22]. Interestingly, we observed that all elevated T cell-related cytokines originated from the plasma of patients, suggesting a potential imbalance between local pulmonary immunity and systemic immunity in patients with RMPP.

The specific decrease in key T-cell cytokines has direct functional consequences. As a critical member of the IL-12 cytokine family, IL-12p70 is widely recognized for its pivotal role in shaping the host’s Th1-type immune response, as well as in activating and promoting the differentiation of Th1 cells [23]. With the reduction in IL-12p70, Th1 cell activity decreases significantly. Consequently, it can be reasonably anticipated that IFN-γ, a key effector molecule secreted by Th1 cells, will also decline. IL-4 is a pleiotropic Th2 cytokine that plays a crucial role in the differentiation of Th2 cells [24]. It stimulates the production of other cytokines, including IL-5 and IL-13 [25]. In this study, a decreasing trend in IL-5 and IL-13 was observed in RMPP patients, however, this reduction did not reach statistical significance. Most importantly, as a core factor in maintaining T cell homeostasis, a decrease in IL-7 levels is typically associated with impaired T cell function [26]. Given its critical role in the adaptive immune system, such functional deterioration often results in delayed pathogen clearance, thereby prolonging the duration of the disease [27]. Collectively, the reduction in these cytokines underscores a broad suppression of pulmonary adaptive immunity in RMPP, which coincides with an excessive innate inflammatory response driven by myeloid cells. This concurrent immune imbalance likely represents a key pathogenic mechanism in RMPP.

Pathway enrichment analysis of the DECs provided mechanistic insight, revealing significant co-enrichment in the JAK-STAT signaling pathway (FDR = 8.95 × 10⁻⁷) and pathways related to chemokine activity. This finding is highly coherent: the JAK-STAT pathway serves as the central signaling hub for the very cytokines we found to be dysregulated. Notably, our depressed cytokines map onto specific JAK-STAT axes: IL-12 signals through JAK2/STAT4, IL-4 through JAK1/3-STAT6, and IL-7 through JAK1/3-STAT5, collectively governing Th1/Th2 differentiation and T-cell homeostasis [28–30]. Thus, the depression of these cytokines functionally converges on a suppression of JAK-STAT signaling within the T-cell compartment, providing a unified molecular explanation for the observed adaptive immune deficit.

The central role of JAK-STAT dysregulation presents a compelling therapeutic opportunity. This pathway offers a unique therapeutic duality: its modulation could potentially address both facets of RMPP immunopathology. Strategic inhibition, particularly of JAK1/2, might temper the excessive myeloid-driven inflammation in the acute phase [31]. Conversely, the pathway’s critical role in T-cell biology suggests that agents designed to selectively modulate specific STAT signals could help. For instance, next-generation JAK inhibitors with relative STAT5-sparing properties might curb inflammation while supporting T-cell reconstitution [32, 33]. This approach moves beyond non-specific immunosuppression towards precision immunomodulation. The clinical translation of this concept is supported by the established use of JAK inhibitors in managing other hyperinflammatory and autoimmune conditions [31, 34].

Our auxiliary analyses further underscore the clinical relevance of T-cell suppression. In ROC analysis, T cell-related DECs showed superior diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing RMPP. Furthermore, the Spearman correlation coefficient analysis and the composite score analysis also revealed that T cell-associated DECs exhibited a strong association with laboratory parameters and clinical outcomes in patients with MPP. These findings suggest that in the pathogenesis of RMPP, the suppression of T-cell function may be a critical determinant of poor prognosis.

This study has several limitations. First, given the exploratory nature of cytokine profiling, we applied a FDR threshold of Q = 0.20 to balance the control of false positives against statistical power. Consequently, some of the identified DECs may represent false positive signals, and all findings require confirmation in future studies with more stringent correction. Second, the PPI and pathway analyses were based on only seven DECs. Although these cytokines showed strong interconnectivity, the small number may limit the robustness of the enrichment results. Third, the correlative nature of our findings and the lack of direct immune cell profiling require functional validation in experimental models. Future multi-center studies with paired cellular assays and mechanistic investigations are warranted to confirm these observations and explore therapeutic targeting of the identified pathways. Additionally, larger cohort studies may integrate cytokine measurements with clinical features to develop predictive models for early recognition of RMPP.

In conclusion, this study identifies a distinct pulmonary cytokine profile in RMPP, characterized by myeloid hyperinflammation coupled with T-cell function suppression. The convergence of dysregulated cytokines on the JAK-STAT signaling pathway highlights its potential role in pathogenesis and positions it as a promising target for novel immunomodulatory strategies in refractory Mycoplasma pneumonia.

A hybrid RL–GA–LSTM–AE framework for energy-aware and SLA-driven task scheduling in cloud computing environments
Authors: Bommineni Narsimhulu, T. Sampath Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rapid advance of cloud computing, along with the growing number of cloud services, has driven a rising demand for intelligent task-scheduling methods to enhance energy efficiency while maintaining SLA (Service Level Agreement) compliance. We present a Reinforcement Learning Genetic Algorithm framework, enhanced with a Long Short-Term Memory Autoencoder, to create a more adaptive and predictive scheduling mechanism in a dynamic cloud setting. Concretely, the LSTM Autoencoder extraction module is used to predict overload scenarios and detect abnormal behaviour, while the RL-GA optimises task allocation via multi-objective evolutionary learning. We validate our model through extensive simulations in a CloudSim environment using Google Cluster Traces and RUBiS datasets and demonstrate that, compared with traditional schedulers, the proposed model is much more efficient. The framework achieves a 27.5% reduction in makespan over baseline methods, reduces SLA violations by up to 4.2% (67% improvement), reduces deadline misses by 78.6%, improves CPU utilisation to 88.7%, and reduces static provisioning energy usage by up to 42.7%. ANOVA testing (with p-values < 0.001) supports the robustness of these results, and convergence analysis shows stable learning across all components of the framework within 500 training cycles. It confirms that it is responsive enough to support dynamic cloud environments, achieving 156.8 milliseconds of end-to-end scheduling latency in a real-time performance evaluation. Its near-linear computational complexity is validated through scalability testing with workload sizes ranging from 100 to 1000 tasks. These results and the comparison show the general effectiveness, scalability, and sustainability of the proposed RL-GA-LSTM-AE framework for intelligent resource scheduling in large-scale cloud data centres, and open the door for further developments in the design and realisation of green, SLA-driven computing infrastructures towards next-gen computing.

Introduction

Cloud computing has been one of the most significant paradigm shifts in computer technology, providing Computing, storage, and networking resources on demand and with elasticity over the internet. Its distributed architecture bestows unique benefits of agility, economies of scale, and ecosystems for the dynamic deployment and management of workloads within a shared infrastructure environment. On the other hand, all these categories of users, services, and data workloads are increasing in the cloud, making load balancing and energy-aware provisioning very difficult. These user-defined performance metrics (latency, availability, and throughput1–3 are increasingly crucial for cloud data centres in maintaining Service-Level Agreement (SLA) compliance.

Static scheduling and resource allocation strategies, like Round Robin (RR), First-Come-First-Serve (FCFS), and Min–Min, have been used in the past to allocate a task to a particular virtual machine (VM). Such approaches are computationally inexpensive but cannot handle dynamic workloads or heterogeneous environments4,5. As such, this may cause underutilization of resources, longer makespans, and increased energy consumption, which are not well-suited to high-throughput and/or time-critical workloads. Moreover, cloud data centres need to be not only a well-functioning part of the whole but also a part with respect to their overall energy consumption and CO2 emissions, working sustainably6.

The challenge of dynamic workloads and SLA management

Cloud workloads have become increasingly heterogeneous as big data analytics, edge computing, and the Internet of Things (IoT) become mainstream, requiring systems to be smart enough to adapt to multiple, often dynamically changing requirements7,8. The well-known characteristics of cloud environments, which provide resources on demand, create complex, intertwined dependencies among computation, resource consumption, and environmental factors such as network latency and node power efficiency. Traditional heuristic-based techniques cannot solve nonlinear multi-objective optimisation problems involving energy, performance, and SLA parameters.

Moreover, workload behaviour in production data centres is dynamic, as it experiences peak and low-load periods, so static allocation policies usually lead to underused VMs during low load and resource saturation during peaks, which collectively reduce Quality of Service (quality of service)9,10 and increase operational costs11. The problem is substantially more challenging in a multi-tenant cloud ecosystem, where multiple applications, often with varying priorities, QoS constraints, and execution patterns, can be multiplexed over the same computational resources. Predictive and adaptive scheduling is essential to maintaining in-system setpoint performance even at high utilisation.

Evolution toward intelligent cloud resource management

Over the last couple of years, this has led to faster adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) for cloud resource management to address these problems. Deep Learning (DL) models, Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms, as well as metaheuristic optimisation techniques (e.g. Genetic Algorithms (GA), Particle Swarm Optimisation (PSO), and Ant Colony Optimisation (ACO))12–14 have also been utilised to address dynamic resource allocation and task scheduling problems. Long Short-term Memory (LSTM)and Auto Encoder (AE) are examples of DL models that have been applied to these workloads and have proven to significantly assist with workload prediction, anomaly detection and trend forecasting, which in turn contribute to resource provisioning prediction15–17.

On the other hand, RL provides a temporal-difference framework that enables agents to learn optimal scheduling policies through trial-and-error interactions with the cloud environment. This allows dynamic adaptation based on workload states, which is needed for real-time SLA optimisation18,19. Genetic Algorithms (GA) are one of the bio-inspired metaheuristics that evolve the initial population of candidate solutions for some problem, following the principles of Darwinian evolution—survival of the fittest—so it is not surprising that GA still considered as one of the most powerful metaheuristic methods until today, especially for combinatorial scheduling and resource allocation problems20,21. While these paradigms have indeed advanced SOTA in scheduling and resource management, current SOTA frameworks misuse them by emphasising either the predictive analytics (learning) side or the optimisation (decision) side, resulting in a mismatch between learning-based forecasting and evolutionary decision-making22,23. Consequently, none of them are truly optimal in the presence of real-time constraints, multi-objective negotiations, or non-stationary workload behaviour in large-scale cloud environments.

Research gaps and limitations of existing systems

Although there have been several significant advances in the intelligent management of cloud resources, there are still a few main limitations of the research done to date: Separable Optimisation: In most prior work, the task scheduling and resource allocation problems are solved separately, leading to poor system performance due to fragmented decision-making. To achieve coherent performance improvements in real-world clouds, these methodologies will often need to be considered collaboratively. Lack of Predictive Modelling: Most of these frameworks only react to resource imbalances after they have occurred. They do not prevent SLA violations and resource bottlenecks ahead of time without accurate workload forecasting and anomaly detection24,25. Deep models or heuristic algorithms trained in one cloud context do not generalise to heterogeneous, multi-cloud infrastructures, restricting their scalability and operational flexibility26,27. Computational Overheads: The age of AI-based systems is borne by the computational costs of training and inference, which may result in increased deployment latency for real-time processing. Survey of Energy-aware design: Although the environmental and economic impact of cloud power utilisation is becoming severe6,28, few hybrid AI systems use energy efficiency as a design objective. To fill these gaps, a collaborative, intelligent, and energy-aware scheduling paradigm is needed that combines prediction-based with proactive-optimisation, while maintaining a real-time response and computational scalability.

Proposed solution

This study proposes a hybrid AI-based framework for energy-aware task scheduling in a cloud computing environment, shifting the focus from existing approaches, such as energy-efficiency and SLA (Service Level Agreement)-oriented solutions, to a single strategy for addressing these challenges. We propose a novel model with the characteristics of Reinforcement Learning and Genetic Algorithm that would provide the concept of optimal adaptive scheduling, designating the model RL–GA–LSTM–AE (proposed model), where LSTM-AE refers to the Long Short–Term Memory Autoencoder for workload forecasting and anomaly detection (ASR).

In this integrated system:

It is composed of an LSTM–AE that learns and detects anomalies from spatiotemporal workload patterns, and an LSTM that predicts future resource needs. The answer is reinforcement learning, which enables the RL agent to dynamically update its policies based on feedback from the environment.

The novelty GA component refines resource mapping to evolve a topology with optimal task–VM pairs via a population-based search. We integrated an adaptive, multi-layered hybrid approach that influences predictive understanding and adjusts to the uSLA System while globally optimising performance metrics: makespan, CPU utilisation, SLA violations, and energy consumption. While traditional systems typically respond to changing conditions reactively, the proposed framework adjusts both scheduling and provisioning policies proactively using a model of system dynamics to ensure continued high levels of operational efficiency, even in the face of rapidly changing conditions.

Key Novelty and Distinguishing Features

RL-GA-LSTM-AE gains its merits from a unique closed-loop co-evolution regime that tightly couples a prediction component (Ae-LSTM) with an optimisation component (GA-RL) under a hybrid cloud setting, reading and writing back to each other over all the iterations, in contrast to existing works, where they are either totally disjoint or unfold into a loosely coupled fashion. Our framework, (i) where Reinforcement Learning and Genetic Algorithms interact bidirectionally, with candidate schedules generated by GA guiding RL the policy exploration process, and with rewards derived continuously from RL reshaping GA fitness evolution process to avoid premature convergence; With an LSTM–Autoencoder module that allows to fit a provisioning strategy towards the specific anomaly pattern, it empowers the system with the ability of understanding when to trigger such resource scaling actions completely. It thus helps prevent SLA violations by being overly reactive and responding only when overloads occur. Third, to dynamically balance the importance of makes-pan, SLA violation, and energy efficiency, we adopt an adaptive objective-weighting mechanism that adjusts their weights based on their relative importance under current system conditions. The key novelty of the proposed framework is this orchestration-level integration—this is not the introduction of new independent learning models—and it enables energy-aware, SLA-driven, proactive and reactive cloud resource management in the presence of non-stationary workloads.

Research objectives

The global concern behind this study is to formulate and assess a scalable, high-performance scheduling scheme that combines predictive learning and meta-heuristic optimisation to improve performance and sustainability in cloud systems.

The specific aims are:

O1. To build a Hybrid Reinforcement Learning–Genetic Algorithm (RL–GA) scheduler that learns and adapts its policies to minimise makespan, deadline/miss, and SLA violations.

O2. To the LSTM–Autoencoder module for prediction about workload trends and resource anomaly for decision making.

O3. To conduct a wide range of benchmarking comparing to the current state-of-the-art methods (e.g., Deep Q-Learning, Min–Min, and PSO-based schedulers) using CloudSim 3.0, and datasets such as Google Cluster Traces and RUBiS.

O4. Model to quantitatively validate (through statistical test) improvement of energy consumption, CPU idle utilisation, and SLA compliance.

Related work

Resource allocation and task scheduling are essential building blocks of efficient cloud computing, impacting system performance, energy utilisation, and SLA assurance. In this setting, however, classical scheduling approaches for cloud infrastructures scale poorly when orchestrating highly heterogeneous, latency-sensitive, and data-intensive workloads. Existing Round Robin (RR), First-Come-First-Serve (FCFS), and Min–Min traditional algorithms are deterministic and static, and none can capture the stochastic and time-varying behaviour of modern cloud workloads, leading to idle resources and wasteful energy consumption.

Identified research gaps and positioning of the present study

While a lot of work has been done on scheduling approaches ranging from simple heuristics to metaheuristics, up to AI-based methods, some important and challenging research gaps remain:

Scattered Attention: Most related work treats scheduling and resource allocation as decoupled problems rather than as a co-optimised methodology.

Poor Real-Time Adaptability – The majority of AI-based models either do not incorporate audit procedures to gather live feedback on their performance or lack the capacity to tune parameters in real time, resulting in poor performance under unpredictable workloads.

Multi-tenancy Environments — High Computation Overhead: The Deep RL and LSTM-based frameworks exhibit high computation overhead, which is incompatible with multi-tenancy and real-time deployment scenarios.

Neglecting Security and Energy Integration: No existing model accounts for SLA compliance, energy efficiency, and system security simultaneously.

Scalability: Existing systems have been shown to work under simulated conditions but tend to perform worse in heterogeneous, hybrid cloud–edge settings.

This automatically self-optimising architecture overcomes the aforementioned gaps by seamlessly integrating the predictive workload model (LSTM–AE) with the adaptive scheduling aspect (RL–GA). It provides real-time, scalable, and energy-efficient scheduling with SLA compliance and lower computational cost through its integration (Table 1).

Unlike existing hybrid AI-based schedulers that target either prediction accuracy or optimisation efficiency, the proposed framework uniquely unifies prediction, anomaly awareness, scheduling, and provisioning under a single adaptive control loop. While many existing models constitute end-to-end DL methods for workload forecasting, they neither aim to optimise workload through explicitly optimised policies in real time, nor do they do so through RL and GA without predictive foresight. To mitigate this challenge, we propose RL–GA–LSTM–AE, which bridges this gap by ensuring that predictive insights directly influence optimisation and activation decisions to facilitate SLA compliance and energy-aware scheduling.

While some previous works have studied hybrid combinations of RL, GA, and LSTM-based predictors, these elements are generally used in a serial or loosely coupled fashion. On the contrary, our proposed framework enables a closed feedback loop between prediction, anomaly detection, scheduling, and resource provisioning in a bidirectional manner. This design allows continuous adaptation rather than requiring retraining from scratch, yielding operational behaviour that is much more than a simple step function in architecture.

Unlike existing hybrid schedulers, which co-learn and co-optimise using a sequential or loosely coupled methodology, the proposed framework establishes bidirectional, feedback-driven coupling between prediction, anomaly detection, scheduling, and provisioning, enabling continuous co-adaptation under non-stationary workloads.

Summary

This is evidenced in the evolution of intelligent scheduling in the cloud, which has undergone a distinct shift from static heuristics and metaheuristics to hybrid AI systems that can combine prediction, adaptation and optimisation. Nevertheless, although substantial work has been done across reinforcement learning, evolutionary algorithms, and deep learning models, there is currently no single unified methodology that simultaneously combines predictive LSTM–AE architectures with adaptive RL–GA optimisation.

Compared to previous models that address one of these concerns, the proposed RL–GA–LSTM–AE model is a four-dimensional model that addresses energy efficiency, SLA adherence, and scalability, leading to self-optimising, sustainable, and intelligent clouds for next-generation distributed systems.

Problem formulation

Dynamic allocations and scheduling tasks with minimum makespan, minimum energy consumption, and maximum SLA compliance in large-scale cloud infrastructures can be treated as static or dynamic multi-objective optimisation problems, thereby enabling efficient management of computing resources. The workloads in cloud computing environments are highly heterogeneous and dynamic, making static and heuristic-based scheduling techniques ineffective. To this end, the proposed work constructs a bi-level optimisation framework to jointly optimise task scheduling, predictive resource provisioning, and energy-aware control, accounting for the dynamic and uncertain nature of workloads.

The fitness function used in the GA layer integrates the RL reward with predicted system metrics to refine the search methodology:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm{F}\mathrm{i}\mathrm{t}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{s}\mathrm{s}=\mathrm{Aggregated\:}{\mathrm{R}}_{\mathrm{R}\mathrm{L}}+({\mathrm{w}}_{1}/\mathrm{M}\mathrm{S})+({\mathrm{w}}_{2}/(1+\mathrm{D}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{R}\left)\right)$$\end{document}

While GA operations—selection, crossover, and mutation—are carried out to evolve a population of task–resource mappings and promote exploration of global optima, RL facilitates fine-grained exploitation.

The proposed reward formulation is deliberately constructed to trade off between short-term execution efficiency and long-term system sustainability. By penalising SLA violations and energy consumption while rewarding CPU utilisation, the reward structure reflects local scheduling decisions in terms of global optimisation objectives. This allows the RL agent to implicitly learn trade-offs that are otherwise difficult to encode in static heuristics, especially under varying workloads. The scheduling policy adopted in this work is heuristic and reward-driven. However, hybridising reinforcement learning with genetic algorithms ensures stable convergence. Reinforcement learning improves scheduling decisions through repeated interaction with the environment and reward maximisation, thanks to the agent-environment feedback loop. The genetic algorithm maintains a stable level of population diversity and wages against premature convergence. This mutually exclusive, dual-pronged nature stabilises the learning behaviour of the scheduling agent under varying workloads and prevents oscillation in scheduling decisions. Empirically, the learning methodology consistently converges within a prefixed number of training rounds for all evaluation scenarios. The proposed framework is not designed to provide any formal worst-case guarantees when the workloads become fully adversarial or pathological. However, robustness is achieved through adaptive feedback, anomaly-aware monitoring, and continuous policy updates. When a sudden spike in workload or an execution abnormality is detected, the autoencoder-based anomaly detection scheme triggers dynamic scaling and rescheduling; however, it does not prevent performance degradation, as it only limits it.

Component-level justification and design rationale

We propose a framework that combines four complementary components — Reinforcement Learning (RL), Genetic Algorithms (GA), Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTM), and Autoencoders (AE) — where each component is designed to address specific limitations of existing standalone or loosely coupled hybrid cloud scheduling approaches. All four are included not because of architectural complexity, but because they represent non-overlapping functional roles.

The dynamic, feedback-driven nature of Reinforcement Learning and its ability to continuously learn in the cloud environment enable true adaptive scheduling decisions. But when used as a stand-alone predictor, RL often exhibits slow convergence to the optimal policy, unstable policies, and premature convergence in large, non-stationary scheduling spaces. On the other hand, Genetic Algorithms offer good global exploration and population diversity to avoid local optima, but cannot adapt to the current environment or perform real-time, fine-grained policy refinement. The proposed framework integrates GA-fed global-level exploration with RL-based local-level exploitation by enabling bidirectional interaction between RL and GA, allowing scheduling policies to evolve through GA, while maintaining real-time response to changing workload conditions.

The predictive awareness, through stream-based forecasting of possible future workload trends provided by the LSTM unit, is crucial for proactively scheduling tasks and for enabling an energy-aware approach to resource provisioning. In the absence of workload prediction, scheduling decisions are made reactively, resulting in delayed SLA violations and less efficient energy utilisation. In fact, in situations of high volatility, prediction by itself is not enough. To this end, an Autoencoder is included in the framework to identify anomalies based on reconstruction errors, which correspond to anomalous workload patterns and execution deviations. Such an anomaly-aware mechanism allows the system to take proactive actions by initiating the provisioning and rescheduling of activities when the workload encounters an unplanned spike or abnormal execution behaviour is detected, thus improving robustness and SLA reliability.

Individually, each component offers non-overlapping functionality: RL allows us to learn an adaptive policy, GA ensures the optimisation does not get stuck in a local optimum, LSTM provides temporal information about how the workload changes, and AE increases the robustness of the approach through online anomaly detection. However, with the removal of a single component, the framework will be limited to either a reactive scheduler, a static optimiser, or a prediction-only system, and thus less effective in achieving its respective goals: SLA compliance, energy efficiency, or adaptability to dynamic cloud workloads. This envisaged combination is essential to realise proactive, energy-efficient, and SLA-aware cloud resource management.

Summary

In summary, this problem formulation characterises a bi-level operation and optimisation framework composed of predictive modelling, adaptive reinforcement learning, and evolutionary search for energy-efficient and SLA-compliant cloud resource management. Such mathematical models provide concurrency optimality (scheduling) and global optimality (resource provisioning) both in the short and long term respectively. The Hybrid RL–GA–LSTM–AE Framework extends this formulation to adaptively cope with variable workloads and shows a large reduction in makespan, higher utilization and energy savings.

Most of the existing hybrid cloud schedulers have focused on either the workload prediction accuracy or the optimization efficiency separately. This work provides the observation that uncertainty during prediction must impact decision making for scheduling and resource provisioning. Together with workload predictions, this approach allows the scheduler to adapt both to the expected demand and any unexpected deviations from it, which ultimately results in more stable SLA compliance and energy-efficient behavior under dynamic scenarios by using autoencoder based anomaly scores.

Proposed framework

This study we present a Hybrid RL–GA–LSTM–AE framework that aims to respond to the needs and challenges of dynamic resource management in cloud computing environments.

Overview of the hybrid RL–GA–LSTM–AE framework

Due to the nature of dynamic and unpredictable workload, traditional static schedulers and heuristic methods to solve the scheduling problem cannot adapt to these workload variations and as a result, the overall resource utilization is poor, excessive energy consumption occurs and SLAs are broken. This work introduces a model where four complementary artificial intelligence paradigms—Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for adaptive task scheduling, Genetic Algorithms (GA) for global optimization, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for predictive workload modeling, and Autoencoders (AE) for anomaly detection—are integrated in a common multi-layered structure. The proposed framework is not intended to introduce a learning algorithm, but rather a systems-level intelligence architecture that orchestrates complementary AI paradigms. The novelty lies in the coordination strategy that enables predictive awareness, adaptive decision-making, and energy-efficient provisioning to co-evolve in real time. Such integration enables the system to anticipate spill over and under load, advise real-time scheduling decisions, and resource scaling while incurring little additional energy cost. The RL–GA–LSTM–AE framework is hierarchical and modular, where every component can act independently and contribute to the entire optimization loop. It comprises six logically connected hierarchical layers; (1) Input Layer: for gathering and normalizing operational data, (2) Predictive Analytics Layer: for workload forecasting and anomaly detection, (3) Optimization Layer: the hybrid RL–GA scheduling occurs here, (4) Resource Allocation Layer: for performing dynamic provisioning, (5) Execution and Monitoring Layer: manage runtime operations, and (6) Feedback and Continuous Learning Layer: iteratively ensures sustainable adaptability through learning.

Furthermore the whole system acts as a closed-loop from workload sensing and predictions for optimization to execution and feedback-based retraining. It preserves the dynamic stability, green and SLA persistence of cloud infrastructure under steady and bursty workloads. Figure 1 illustrates the proposed system framework as a high-level architecture where the information flow along these layers. They play unique but interdependent roles to express system intelligence at a level of holism.

Predictive analytics layer: LSTM–autoencoder-based forecasting and anomaly detection

The nucleus of this framework is the Predictive Analytics Layer, that acts as the cognitive backbone, forecasting workload trends and identifying patterns of deviation that could impact Systems performance. This study, we focused on the one active and resilient approach to the e-Cloud workload and a synthetic anomaly detection mechanism, which is engineered based on the leggings of two individual DL models namely Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) and Autoencoder (AE). We fine-tune the LSTM to capture long-term temporal dependencies in workload traces and combine it with an Autoencoder to maintain the prediction steadiness by detecting an anomaly when the system behaviour or resource consumption does not match a learned pattern by modelling anomalous workloads. Combined, these provide an intelligent forecasting component that allows the scheduler to take precautionary measures before any facility performance is impacted. Cloud workloads are often subject to periodic behavior due in part to user demand, time-of-day trends and application types. Regular regression or feedforward neural networks do not have a better capability for retaining these temporal dependencies. Optimization layer: hybrid reinforcement learning–genetic algorithm scheduler

The final portion of suggested architecture is its Optimization Layer, which acts as the decision making and learning center over the predictive knowledge realized through the LSTM–AE layer, ensuring that this knowledge produces optimal scheduling and/or resource allocation decisions. On top of this layer, we put DRL and GA together in a cooperative hybrid model where the exploration-exploitation trade-off of DRL works in their favour to provide adaptability as well as global optimisation. It simultaneously overcomes the limitations that single-heuristic scheduling algorithms are subject to and avoids the prematurity that many standalone RL systems experience by seamlessly integrating the adaptive learning capabilities of RL and the evolutionary search diversity of GA.

The scheduling problem in the cloud environment can basically be modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). In this formulation, the system state is defined as a set of features that fully describes the current status of the tasks and resources present in the system, capturing its workload demand, available virtual machines (VMs), and their energy levels. The RL agent learns a near-optimal task allocation and resource usage policy with continuous interaction to the environment, which also adapts to changes in workload and resources in real-time. On the other hand, due to the non-stationary workloads and large state spaces in realistic cloud environments, RL may suffer from slow convergence and being trapped in local optima. To remedy this, a Genetic Algorithm is presented to serve as a supplementary global optimiser.

The Genetic Algorithm (GA) works on a population of candidate solutions with each chromosome representing a single task–resource mapping configuration using either a binary or integer vector. GA starts with an initial population which is generated randomly, evaluates the fitness based on the reward from the RL model for each chromosome, and evolves the population using purely genetic operations i.e., selection, crossover, and mutation. These, then, are the chromosomes ranking highest — the elite solutions — which are recombined, then re-injected into the RL agent’s experience replay buffer. This procedure serves to bootstrap the Q-learning system with high quality state-action pairs and allows it to more quickly converge towards globally-optimal policies.

To be specific, RL and GA perform bi-level interplays with the former feeds back matching-easy policies (exploitation), the latter provides fresh-source candidate population (exploration) to enable mapping evolution. The hybrid workflow works as follows: the GA first generates diverse sets of task–VM mappings, the RL agent scores those mappings on the-fly according to the systems second-by-second performance while constantly updating Q-values, and finally the GA uses the optimal configurations that the RL discovered to evolve the population. This combination of experience-based learning and stochastic global search that makes the scheduler a continuous learner (the scheduler was only action-before learning).

From an algorithmic perspective, a scheduling cycle has three core chronological phases:

(1) Exploration : GA proposes new task-resource allocations and expands the search space further;

(2) Learning — The Q-table or neural policy is updated with feedback of reward; and.

(3) Execution: This stage leverages the best policies found in the two modules to perform scheduling in real-time.

This hybrid approach retains the best of both worlds — it combines the advantages of adaptablity with optimality to keep the entire system highly performant and globally optimized to react to changes in workload as fast as the world itself.

As a hybrid of RL and GA, our proposed scheduler can avoid the drawback of standard heuristic-based scheduling approaches that tend not to generalize well when workloads change. In large-scale heterogeneous cloud environments, the hybrid model can improve the speed of convergence, the accuracy of decision making and the robustness of learning policies. Near optimal makespan, energy and SLA violation rates are achieved via the proposed scheduling.

This layer serves as the optimization co-evolution core of the proposed framework, converting the predictive intelligence derived from the LSTM–AE layer into the scheduling policies and optimized task-resource mappings. This optimization step yields a scheduling policy that is adaptive and power-aware, and that information is sent to the Resource Allocation Layer, where resource provisioning and activation decisions are performed based on load prediction and on on-line feedback. Therefore, the RL–GA hybrid constitutes its key control mechanism that allows for self-optimizing cloud operations. The Resource Allocation Layer constitutes the fourth and final operational component of the hybrid RL–GA–LSTM–AE framework, enabling the mapping of the theoretical schedules and predictive outputs into actionable provisioning goals. This layer decides how many and what type of computational resources (either virtual machines, container, or physical node) need to be in activated state, scaled up or scaled out, and at what time, such that the predicted workload can be satisfied while minimizing the energy overhead and SLA violations. It acts as an intelligent conduit between predictive and real-time execution, allowing the cloud system to dynamically adapt in order to sustain maximal operational efficiency despite the variability and uncertainty of its conditions. Static provisioning does none of them as it leads to either under-provisioning, having deadlines and SLAs missed, or over-provisioning, which results in wasted energy and higher operational cost in modern cloud systems. To overcome these problems, we propose a novel solution based on adaptive resource scaling, which is informed by workload forecasts from the LSTM–AE module and task scheduling decisions from the RL–GA optimization layer. Namely, it formulates the decision methodology as a constrained optimization problem by leveraging three key factors including workload coverage, resource capacity and energy efficiency.

Feedback and continuous learning loop

The Feedback and Continuous Learning Loop is a primary element for the temporal adaptivity of the proposed hybrid RL–GA–LSTM–AE framework which allows the framework to continuously evolve by asserting and maintaining optimal performance over time within the highly dynamics and stochastic workload characteristics of cloud environments. This loop represents a self-correcting, closed-loop learning architecture that allows the predictive, optimization, and provisioning components of the system to learn over time — without human introduction — by feeding back performance data to the role in the cloud operations loop to iteratively refine and improve algorithms over time as the system changes, as opposed to static optimization models that rapidly begin to lose their effectiveness as system conditions change, such as what occurs due to scale, dynamics, or workload.

This is the cognitive engine of the system — Continually gathers operational data, understands the gap between predicted performance and actual performance, and updates all the learning models. It ensures that all the elements of the framework (LSTM predictor, Autoencoder anomaly detector, RL policy learner, GA optimizer) evolve by experience, which makes it constantly self-reorganizing in a sustainable energy-efficient manner.

The learning loop is closed as all altered parameters (Q-values from RL, fine-tuned LSTM weights, AE thresholds, and evolved GA populations) are pushed back into the Input Layer of the system. This synergy contributes to policymaking resilience in the face of non-stationary workload dynamics.

Experimental design and implementation details

The implemented Python 3.9 version of the proposed Hybrid RL–GA–LSTM–AE framework used TensorFlow 2.13 for Q-learning core module with DEAP library for several GA operations like crossover, mutation and elitism. The LSTM–AELSTM–Autoencoder (LSTM–AE) workload forecasting and anomaly detection module was implemented in Keras, set use with a sliding time window τ = 30 steps, and has two obstacke LSTM layer of sizes 64 and 32 units. We used a learning rate of 0.001 and a batch size of 64 which led to model training on the hybrid dataset of around 15,000 task instances from Google Cluster Traces and RUBiS workloads. All simulations were conducted on an Ubuntu 20.04 workstation that possessed an Intel Core i7 CPU, 32 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU (10 GB VRAM). To ensure replicability and scalability, the system was divided into modules with separate components such as scheduling, monitoring, forecasting, and anomaly detection. The experiment outlined in the paper was created in a way to widely analyse the efficiency of the methodology involved with the task scheduling and the resource provisioning methodology in dynamic cloud environments with respect to the framework. Two benchmark datasets were used:

Google Cluster Workload Traces (2011): A high-scale dataset of 29 days of operational logs (in total more than 25 million task events across 12.5 K machines), including CPU usage, memory consumption, task priorities, and task execution time. The third dataset created realistic work-load variations useful for training the LSTM predictor.

RUBiS (Rice University Bidding System): a benchmark e-commerce workload that mimics user behaviours for an online auction site This denotes workloads that are both interactive and time variant, in this case using Kubernetes microservices to implement it for real time validation.

The simulations took place over a period of 1,000 time units, combining interactive workloads (RUBiS) as well as batch workloads (Google Cluster). The arrivals of tasks followed a Poisson process with a rate parameter λ = 5, and required metadata including CPU and memory requirements, priority, and deadline. The resource pool comprised 10 heterogeneous VMs with different energy consumption patterns. Relevant KPIs were monitored including makespan, SLA violation rate, deadline miss rate(DMR), energy consumption and CPU utilization. The CloudSim Toolkit was used to adopt a hybrid simulation environment for controlled scheduling evaluation, and Kubernetes v1. 26 for real-time deployment testing. CloudSim facilitated energy-aware data center and dynamic provisioning policy. Kubernetes on the other hand enabled container-level scaling, failover support, and horizontal pod autoscaling. Integrated Prometheus and Grafana for real time monitoring of Metrics.

Discussion

Experimental results demonstrate robust, statistically significant enhancements provided by the proposed RL-GA-LSTM-AE hybrid framework across all prominent cloud resource management KPIs, including makespan, SLA, energy, and scalability. It provides an average makespan of 98.3 s, which is 18.3%, 27.5%, and 31% less than that of Deep Q-Learning, Min-Min, and FCFS, respectively. The combined use of RL for adaptive scheduling and GA for global optimisation yields these benefits and is confirmed by stable convergence at 450 RL episodes and 80 GA generations. The proposed RL–GA–LSTM–AE framework substantially improves SLA compliance, reducing violations to 4.2% (67–75% improvement over baseline methods). The LSTM primarily supports this improvement–the Autoencoder module through accurate workload forecasting (RMSE 3.5%) and effective anomaly detection. At the same time, reinforcement learning and genetic optimisation enable adaptive scheduling and proactive resource provisioning under dynamic workloads. The other major area of improvement is energy consumption, where we save 38–42% compared to Deep Q-Learning and static provisioning. Dynamic resource scaling not only achieves the best load factor but also effectively reduces idle power consumption. The scalability experiments show less-than-linear scheduling overhead and consistent performance across 1000 tasks, with SLA violations < 6% at round 1000.

Finally, in ablation studies, we find that each component is truly irreplaceable; the performance would drop sharply, especially for SLA compliance and energy efficiency, when removing RL, GA or AE, respectively. Statistical validation using ANOVA (p < 0.001, η² = 0.865) confirms that observed improvements are robust and not due to random variation.

Beyond average values, the proposed framework also shows consistent behaviour across multiple runs. The performance variant remained low for makespan, SLA violations, and energy consumption across 30 independent executions with different random seeds, and the 95% confidence intervals corroborated the observed improvements.

The other investigation was about robustness to variations in parameters as well. Moderate variations in GA parameters or the weighting of the reward (± 20%) only changed performance within a small range (not the ranked outcome) of methods. Moderate performance degradation occurred in LSTM predictions when controlled noise was injected, but the anomaly-aware feedback mechanism constrained loss to < 10%, while ensuring SLA violations and energy consumption remained lower than those of baseline schedulers.

Simulation Validity and Practical Feasibility

Although the experimental evaluation is performed on real workload traces, CloudSim is primarily used to model scheduling latency, resource provisioning, and energy behaviour in the cloud. The traces are generated from the Google Cluster and RUBiS workloads, where the workload dynamics are very close to those of actual production systems. In addition, the end-to-end scheduling latency is low (156.8 ms). It falls within the realistic bounds of control-loop requirements for cloud schedulers, demonstrating the real-time feasibility of the proposed framework. Scaling it to production-grade cloud platforms, however, is left for future directions.

Conclusion

We proposed a novel hybrid RL–GA–LSTM–AE framework for intelligent task scheduling and dynamic resource provisioning in cloud computing environments. Through close integration of an adaptive policy optimisation mechanism based on reinforcement learning, a global search mechanism based on genetic algorithms, a workload forecasting mechanism based on long short-term memory networks, and an anomaly detection mechanism based on autoencoders, the proposed architecture addresses important shortcomings of traditional heuristic-based and learning-based schedulers. As a result, a multi-layered design is obtained, which allows resource management to be both proactive and energy-aware, and compliant with SLAs, within a unified decision-making framework. Experimental evaluation across multiple metrics using Google Cluster Traces and RUBiS workloads shows that the proposed framework consistently outperforms state-of-the-art scheduling approaches on average. The system’s makespan was 98.3 s on average, which is an 18–31% improvement over baseline methods, and the system further reduced SLA violations to 4.2%, a 67% improvement compared to Deep Q-Learning. Other benefits include 78.6% lower deadline misses, CPU utilisation 88.7% higher than previous practices, and 38–42% lower energy consumption compared to state-of-the-art techniques. ANOVA testing (p < 0.001) demonstrates that these improvements are statistically significant and repeatable across diverse workloads.

Finally, convergence and runtime analyses demonstrate the feasibility of the proposed solution. Stable operating states of the RL agent, GA optimiser, and LSTM-Autoencoder modules are achieved within 450 episodes, 80 generations, and 120 epochs of operation (the systems are learning and optimising efficiently). Real-time evaluation yields a mean end-to-end scheduling latency of 156.8 ms, indicating suitability for dynamic cloud environments. Results show near-linear computational scaling—from 100 to 1000 tasks—across workloads where performance scales moderately less than linearly; dynamic resource scaling—keeping utilisation metrics within limits while using energy-efficient scaling—aligns allocation with future estimates of demand. This study obtains four principal contributions. Secondly, it presents an innovative hybrid scheduling architecture that integrates synergistically distributed paradigms to achieve performance that a separate method cannot. Second, it proposes predictive workload modelling and anomaly-aware scheduling to proactively provision resources and mitigate the possibility of SLA violations early. Third, it illustrates that avoiding trade-offs among makespan, energy efficiency, and SLA compliance, and achieving multi-objective optimisation with organic (i.e., through hybrid and mechanism interpretations) and synoptic functions, are possible with appropriately crafted hybrid techniques. Fourth, it validates the framework’s scalability, robustness, and consistency across multiple platforms, suggesting its potential suitability for real-world cloud environments, subject to further validation on live infrastructure. The energy savings are not just exciting because they were achieved with a performance boost — these reductions have environmental and economic consequences. The 38–42% reductions in energy consumption demonstrated in medium- to large-scale cloud data centres that process thousands of tasks per day can yield high cost and carbon savings. As global demand for cloud infrastructure grows, green and smart scheduling techniques will be essential for promoting sustainable computing.

Though empirical stability and robustness under changing workloads are validated experimentally, no formal analytic guarantees are provided for convergence or worst-case behaviour under an entirely adversarial workload. The scheduling logic is heuristic and reward-based, balancing practical adaptability and theoretical optimality. The last line of the paper discusses the need for future work to develop formal convergence bounds and worst-case performance guarantees through rigorous theoretical analysis.

Although many of the goals have already been achieved, there is still some scope for future research. The existing framework assumes tasks are independent and does not account for task dependencies through workflows or directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), which are increasingly prevalent in scientific and data-intensive applications. In addition, the abstraction should be extended to multi-cloud and federated cloud environments, which also involve new challenges such as latency, data locality, and inter-domain coordination. There are also exciting opportunities in areas such as better integration of containerised workloads and serverless exec models to support more modern cloud ecosystems. We plan to generalise our framework for edge–cloud and geographic distribution, to propose online and continual learning strategies that avoid the periodic retraining overhead of our approach, and to investigate federated learning approaches that allow training a model across cloud providers without compromising user data privacy. Future work includes incorporating additional optimisation objectives, such as network bandwidth, storage I/O performance, and security constraints, as well as adaptive hyperparameter tuning methods to enhance robustness under changing operating conditions.

Overall, RL–GA–LSTM–AE is a proposed scalable, adaptive, and energy-aware cloud resource management framework for next-generation cross-domain resource management. By using intelligent workload prediction, dynamic resource scaling, and hybrid optimisation-driven scheduling, the framework represents a promising step towards autonomous, sustainable, and high-performance cloud infrastructure that can optimally support the workload demands of rapidly growing, data-intensive applications whilst reducing their negative environmental and operational impacts.

Deployment Roadmap and Limitations

The architecture of the proposed framework supports easy integration with existing cloud management platforms. Individual components of the predictive analytics can be deployed as microservices, and the provisioning logic can be plugged into orchestration systems like Kubernetes or OpenStack using existing scheduling hooks. This architecture enables a gradual evolution from simulation-based validation to emulated and actual cloud settings. In the present work, only trace-driven simulations are evaluated, and we have not covered real cloud deployment or hardware-in-the-loop emulation, which will be a part of our future work. Also, a more extensive sensitivity analysis with broader parameter ranges and additional random scenarios is suggested as a future research avenue.

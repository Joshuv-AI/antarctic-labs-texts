Development of AI based behavioral feature patterns on influencing asymptomatic cardiovascular disease attributes: a dataset standardization approach
Authors: V. Sangeetha, Syed Muzamil Basha, Syed Thouheed Ahmed, Surbhi B. Khan, Mohammad Shabaz, Ali Algarni, Shakila Basheer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cardiovascular diseases (CVD) remain a major global health challenge, characterized by heterogeneous clinical attributes and multi-source data variability that limit reliable predictive modeling. Existing approaches primarily focus on independent feature analysis, often overlooking the interdependency between symptomatic and asymptomatic attributes. In this study, a transfer learning–based framework is proposed for cross-domain feature harmonization and interdependency-driven attribute modeling. Multi-source datasets, including MIMIC-III, Framingham Heart Study (FHS), and Cleveland Heart Disease (CHD), are integrated to construct a unified latent feature space. The proposed method systematically captures behavioral interdependencies between clinical attributes and introduces a confidence-calibrated influence scoring mechanism to enhance interpretability and reliability of predictions. Experimental results demonstrate improved performance, achieving a sensitivity of 92.37% and an F1-score of 0.8962. The findings highlight the effectiveness of interdependency-aware feature mapping in improving CVD risk characterization and provide a standardized framework for robust and explainable clinical decision support.

Introduction

Cardiovascular Disease (CVD) represents a broad and complex spectrum of disorders affecting the heart and associated vascular systems, resulting in conditions that impair circulatory functions at structural, hemodynamic and metabolic levels. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), an approximately 520 million individuals are living with CVD globally as of 2019, with the associated mortality burden reaching an estimated 18.66 million deaths annually. The prevalence and access to care remain deeply inequitable, the incidence, causative risk factor and quality of treatment facilities are disproportionality distributed across high-income and low-income nations, further computing the global healthcare challenges1.

A particularly concerning epidemiological trend is the rising incidence of the CVD among younger population. Recent clinical evidence indicates that one in every five CVD cases occurs in individuals below the age of 40, with the occurrence rate within the 25–45 age group projected to increase by approximately 2% annually. This demographic shift presents a significant public concern, as the CVD has historically been associated with older population. The contributing risk factors in this younger cohort include tobacco consumption, sedentary and high-stress occupational lifestyle, poor dietary habits and genetic predisposition. Collectively these attributes or factors accelerating the onset of cardiovascular deterioration at earlier stages of life.

Research gaps

From a clinical standpoint, CVD manifestation can be broadly classified into symptomatic and asymptomatic categories. Symptomatic presentations, including angina (chest pain), fatigue, edema (peripheral swelling), shortness of breath, elevated heart-rate and peripheral artery disease (PAD) offers observability and measurable indicators that support clinical decision making by cardiologist and physicians. In contrast, asymptomatic attributes such as hypertension, hyperlipidemia (elevated cholesterol levels), early-stage coronary artery disease (CAD) and early stage abnormalities remain clinically silent yet exerts substantial influence on the CVD progression and outcome. The absence of the overt symptoms in these conditions significantly reduces the likelihood of early detection and accurate disease classification, thereby increasing patient risk.

A critical and underexploited research gaps lies in understanding the interdependency between asymptomatic and symptomatic attribute patterns and their collective influence on CVD risk stratification and mortality prediction. Existing studies have largely examined these attributes in isolation, resulting in fragmented models with limited predictive reliability and generalizability. The absence of a unified framework that captures the relational dynamic between the latent asymptomatic indicators and their downstream symptomatic expressions restricts the development of robust, clinically actionable decision-support systems. To address this gap, this research proposes an interdependency-driven analytical framework that systematically investigates the behavioral correlation between symptomatic and asymptomatic CVD attribute patterns.

The primary objective is to model the influence and co-dependency of these attribute categories to enable more accurate CVD classification and mortality risk prediction. Methodologically, the proposed system employs transfer learning based cross-domain feature harmonization to unify heterogeneous clinical datasets into a coherent latent feature space, ensuring consistent and bias-resilient representation learning across diverse population cohort. As interdependency-aware feature selection mechanism is further developed to quantitatively capture latent parameter interactions and mitigate dataset-origin bias. The proposed system includes confidence calibration influence scoring metric to enhance the interpretability and trustworthiness of predictive outputs, equipping clinicians with reliable and explainable decision support information.

Paper contribution

This work presents a structured framework for cardiovascular disease (CVD) analysis that addresses the challenges associated with heterogeneous and multi-source clinical datasets. A transfer learning-based feature harmonization approach is introduced to align datasets such as MIMIC-III, Framingham Heart Study (FHS), and Cleveland Heart Disease (CHD) into a unified representation space, ensuring consistency across varying data distributions. The study further develops an interdependency-based modeling approach to capture the relationships between symptomatic and asymptomatic attributes, enabling improved representation of underlying clinical patterns. An influence-based feature mapping mechanism is formulated to quantify the effect of asymptomatic attributes on symptomatic outcomes through dependency analysis. In addition, a confidence-calibrated scoring measure is incorporated to assess the reliability of feature contributions and model outputs, supporting interpretability in clinical decision-making. The proposed framework also facilitates dataset standardization by integrating feature harmonization with interdependency mapping, thereby improving the consistency and applicability of CVD predictive modeling across diverse datasets.

Literature reviews

The Cardio Vascular Diseases (CVD) is classified under sensitive and non-linear form of medical disorder. The progression and evaluation of CVD is primarily based on the suggestions and recommendations provided by medical experts. These include electrocardiography (ECG), computer tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). The approaches extract predominant symptomatic attributes such as stroke, blockages and cardio conditions. The risk factor based early CVD classification in2,3 is defined under calibration ability index (C index) and Receiver Operating Characteristics (ROC), the approach has considered predictions such as age, gender, hypertension, blood pressure, cigarettes per day etc., as the major classification parameters. Many techniques and approaches are presented to support early detection and classification of CVDs using machine learning4–7.

The features and attributes used under the machine learning models are subjected to be developed and annotated by the universally accepted datasets such as MIMIC-III8, FHS9 and CHD10 repositories. These repositories are processed with symptomatic parameters of the CVD and are extracted with a decision making under single dependency cum defined rules. The defined techniques and studies such as11,12 are featuring a systematic reviews and oriented solutions for risk prediction and stoke prediction. The studies in13,14 are subjected to the influence of global pandemic CoVID-19 and the influence drawn on the CVD. The studies are bound to project higher rate of CVD positive patients due to asymptomatic behaviors such as lockdown, loneliness and isolation. The studies15–17 are further supported by the influencing approach and mortality rate of CVD COVID-19 patients and treatment.

The summarization of CVD parameters and approaches are bounded and develop a seasonal symptoms and deflect with changing geographical and behavioral attributes. Thus the research on asymptomatic attributes plays a vital role. The risk factor evaluation and validation research is reported by18–20. The feature mapping and feature interdependency based studies such as the meta-analysis in21 has predominant observations on CVD predictions. However, the multiple parameter based heterogeneity of ML models has an unreliable prediction and decision support. Further the study22 reported from American College of Cardiology and American Heart Association (ACC/AHC) has discussed on the influencing of underestimated CVD risks causing higher CVD events. The study is active in evaluating the short-term based CVD risk parameters prediction using machine learning models. The objective of this manuscript is to cover the research gaps and parameter extraction with respect to asymptomatic behaviors of interdependent approaches in CVD dataset.

In23 the effect of treatment pathways leading to amputations in peripheral artery disease patients is discussed and further provides details about CVD patients as CVD is a confounder of PAD. Whereas in24 the binary classification of adverse event prediction in the patients after cardio vascular surgeries, in which they used deep learning techniques combining static and time series data is projected and discussed. Further in25 ensemble ELM and explainable AI model is demonstrated to understand the features on gastrointestinal tract diseases in line to the CVD. Feature selection and sub-feature analysis have collectively emerged as critical pillars in advancing CVD classification and risk stratification. Advanced AI-based feature selection methodologies including Chi-square, information gain, forward selection and backward elimination have been explored to distill cardiovascular health indicators into refined, high-relevance feature subsets, with particular emphasis on transparency, interpretability and bias mitigation in clinical decision making26.

Multiple feature selection strategies combined with optimized ensemble classifiers have demonstrated measurable gains in CVD prediction accuracy, with random forest and decision tree models achieving notably superior performance exceeding 96% accuracy when sub-features refinement techniques are systematically applied27. A particular wide-ranging perspective is offered by studies that benchmark several feature selection techniques simultaneously across multiple classifiers, averaging feature importance ranks to estimate each feature’s global level ranking, thereby reducing algorithmic specific bias and improving reliability of the final predictive models28.

At the sub-feature levels, mutual information-based selection has proven effective in isolating the most informative clinical predictors, with hybrid models integrating particle swarm optimization and neural networks further optimizing feature weighting to attain CVD prediction accuracy of up to 97%4. These findings collectively affirm that neither broad feature selection nor sub-feature analysis alone suffices, rather their integration within intelligent optimization frameworks yields more stable, discriminative and clinically trustworthy cardiovascular classification pipelines. The conventional feature selection approaches, recent research has focused on more granular sub-feature level optimization to improve classification performance in high-dimensional medical datasets. Sub-feature selection methods have demonstrated improved discriminative capability by identifying optimal subsets of features and reducing redundancy, thereby enhancing prediction accuracy in cardiovascular disease modeling30. Furthermore, mutual information-based analysis has been employed to evaluate the generalization capability of learning models, enabling the assessment of the gap between training and unseen data distributions and improving model robustness in clinical prediction tasks31. In parallel, privacy-preserving feature selection techniques have gained importance, particularly in healthcare applications, where fuzzy probability-based frameworks are used to enable secure sub-feature selection while preserving sensitive patient information32. These developments highlight the importance of integrating fine-grained feature modeling, generalization analysis, and privacy-aware mechanisms within predictive frameworks. Problem statement

The aim of this study is to systematically model and quantify the interdependency between symptomatic and asymptomatic attributes in cardiovascular disease (CVD) datasets to improve prediction reliability and dataset consistency. Specifically, the objective is to develop a structured framework that captures the influence of asymptomatic behavioral and clinical factors on observable symptomatic outcomes, while addressing the challenges of heterogeneous and multi-source dataset integration. By formalizing these relationships through dependency modeling and transfer learning-based feature alignment, the study seeks to enhance feature representation, reduce dataset-origin bias, and support more consistent and interpretable CVD classification.

Cardiovascular Dieses (CVD) prediction and onset are strongly influenced by clinically observed symptoms and etiological factors that vary across the patient population and geographical regions. In tropical Asian population, region-specific environmental, lifestyle, and socio-demographic factors introduces systematic variations in clinical attributes, leading to non-stationary feature distribution and adaptive shift in feature function mapping used for CVD risk models. Methodology

The proposed technique is based on the multi-dimensional CVD dataset computation and synchronization to extract the influence of asymptomatic attributes over the symptomatic attributes. The proposed system architecture is represented in Fig. 2; the input dataset is driven from the multi-dimensional CVD archives. The input dataset (multidimensionality) includes such as Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care (MIMIC-III) dataset, Framingham Heart Study (FHS) and Cleveland Heart Datasets (CHD) and the input attributes under CSV includes as shown in Tables 2 and 3.

These datasets exhibits variations in feature definitions, temporal resolution and data completeness. An initial pre-processing phase is performed independently on each dataset to remove duplicate and invalid records. Missing values were handled using a hybrid imputation strategy combining multivariate imputation for continuous clinical attributes and mode or indicator-based imputation for categorical variables. To address inter-dataset inconsistencies, semantically equivalent attributes were harmonized through unit normalization and ontology driven feature mapping, followed by correlation-based dimensional alignment to construct a unified feature schema. Temporal alignment was achieved by aggregating longitudinal measurements into clinically meaningful observations window and synchronizing cohort-based records using baseline and follow-up-marker, enabling the integration across datasets while preserving disease progression characteristics.

The inclusion of the case study is intended to provide a concrete validation of the proposed interdependency modeling framework by demonstrating its application on representative clinical attributes. While the mathematical formulation defines the dependency relationships in a generalized manner, the case study translates these formulations into a practical scenario by selecting clinically relevant asymptomatic attributes (e.g., hypertension and hyperlipidemia) and symptomatic outcomes (e.g., stroke and edema). Furthermore, the case study facilitates the verification of the proposed dependency equations and threshold-based mapping strategy, ensuring that the modeled relationships are not only theoretically consistent but also clinically meaningful. It thereby acts as an intermediate validation layer between the formulation and the full-scale experimental evaluation, improving the interpretability and reproducibility of the proposed methodology.

Results and discussion

Experimental setup

The outcome of the proposed system is to define and map the asymptomatic attributes of CVD and its corelationship cum influence to match and coordinate a decision supporting ecosystem. The proposed system is implemented on python based deep-learning stack, primarily leveraging TensorFlow (v2.0) and PyTorch (V1.0) for model development, training and evaluation. Data preprocessing, feature normalization and interdependency analysis were preformed using NumPy integrated on Scikit-lib, while mutual information computation and feature relevance estimation were conducted using information-theoretic toolkit integrated into the piplelines. The proposed transfer learning architecture is initialized using pre-trained DCNN backbone on a fine-tuned dataset after dimensional alignment and feature mapping. Hyperparameter optimization is carried out using grid-based and adaptive learning rate schedulers with early stopping employed to mitigate overfitting and stabilize generalization performance. Model evaluation is performed using stratified cross validation to ensure robustness against class imbalance and population-level variance across datasets chosen.

All the experiments are executed on NVIDIA RTX100 GPU acceleration to support large-scale tensor operations and parallel training. The hardware configuration included RTX100 GPU with CUDA and cuDNN support, a multi-core Intel Xeon processor, a minimal 64 GB RAM to accommodate high-dimensional feature representations and batch-level processing. Dataset storage and retrieval were managed through high-speed SSD-based storage to reduce I/O bottleneck. The environment is then containerized using Docker to ensure reproductively and consistency across runs, while version control and experiment tracking were maintained using GitHUB and logging framework. This integrated hardware-software setup enabled efficient training, stable convergence and reliable evaluation of the proposed model under multi-source and multi-dimensional CVD data conditions.

Dataset description

The experimental evaluation is conducted using three widely recognized cardiovascular datasets, namely the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care (MIMIC-III), the Framingham Heart Study (FHS), and the Cleveland Heart Disease (CHD) dataset. The MIMIC-III dataset8 is a large-scale, publicly available critical care database containing de-identified health records of patients admitted to intensive care units, providing detailed clinical measurements, laboratory values, and temporal observations. The Framingham Heart Study dataset9 is a long-term epidemiological dataset designed to identify risk factors associated with cardiovascular disease, consisting of demographic, behavioral, and clinical attributes collected over multiple decades. The Cleveland Heart Disease dataset10 is a benchmark dataset widely used for classification tasks, comprising structured clinical attributes such as chest pain type, blood pressure, cholesterol levels, and electrocardiographic results. These datasets are selected due to their complementary characteristics in terms of population diversity, feature composition, and clinical relevance, enabling comprehensive evaluation of the proposed interdependency-based framework under heterogeneous data conditions.

Recurrent Thymoma Diagnosed by Endobronchial Ultrasound‐Guided Fine‐Needle Aspiration: A Case Report
Authors: Suzuka Matsuoka, Yuki Takigawa, Kenichiro Kudo, Ken Sato, Jun Nishimura, Hiromi Watanabe, Keiichi Fujiwara, Takuo Shibayama
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTA 41‐year‐old man who had previously undergone thymoma resection developed a right lower mediastinal mass. Endoscopic ultrasonography with bronchoscope‐guided fine‐needle aspiration (EUS‐B‐FNA) was performed. This report shows EUS‐B‐FNA as a minimally invasive and safe procedure that can expand the diagnostic yield for recurrent thymoma.

A 41‐year‐old man underwent type B3 thymoma resection. Four years later, a routine follow‐up computed tomography (CT) scan revealed an extrapulmonary soft tissue mass in the mediastinum adjacent to the right lower lobe (Figure 1). For further evaluation, bronchoscopy was performed using a convex endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) bronchoscope (BF‐UC290F; Olympus, Japan) via the transesophageal route because the transbronchial approach was not feasible, and the lesion was adjacent to the oesophagus. The EBUS bronchoscope revealed a well‐defined mass with poor vascularity. Endoscopic ultrasound with bronchoscope‐guided fine‐needle aspiration (EUS‐B‐FNA) using a 22G needle was performed in four passes (Figure 2). The pathological diagnosis indicated recurrent thymoma (Figure 3).

Chest computed tomography (CT) scan. CT scan showing an extrapulmonary soft tissue mass in the mediastinum adjacent to the right lower lobe (arrowhead). The CT scan revealed that the lesion had no air bronchogram sign.

Endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) image. (A) EBUS of the esophagus revealing a well‐defined extrapulmonary mediastinal mass with poor vascularity. (B) Endoscopic ultrasound with bronchoscope‐guided fine‐needle aspiration (EUS‐B‐FNA) using a 22G needle.

Histopathological findings with a haematoxylin and eosin stain. Proliferation of polygonal cells with well‐defined borders accompanied by the infiltration of small lymphocytes.

Although the diagnosis of thymomas using endoscopic ultrasound‐guided fine‐needle aspiration has been reported [1], this is the first report of EUS‐B‐FNA. Fine‐needle aspiration has been reported to have a limited ability to accurately subtype thymomas [2]; however, it is a minimally invasive and safe procedure that may potentially serve as a valuable tool for evaluating recurrent thymomas. EUS‐B‐FNA may therefore increase the diagnostic yield for pulmonologists.

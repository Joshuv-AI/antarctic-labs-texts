Vibrating mesh nebulizers vs. less invasive surfactant administration in the treatment of preterm respiratory distress syndrome: a multicenter, open-label, exploratory, randomized clinical trial
Authors: Yiyao Jiang, Xin Chen, Runa Li, Hui Wang, Hanqing Wang, Qian Guo, Jinhong Li, Dexin Li, Bo Wang, Hong Xu, Qiaoqiao Zou, Hong Wan, Rui Rao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveDeficiency of pulmonary surfactant (PS) induces the onset of neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (RDS), which can lead to progressively worsening respiratory failure and even death. However, studies on improving lung function via the use of vibrating mesh nebulizer (VMN) technology to deliver PS to premature infants with RDS are limited.MethodsThis prospective multicenter, open-label, exploratory, randomized clinical trial, including 2 parallel groups and a 1:1 allocation, was conducted in 7 hospitals from Jan. 1, 2023, to Dec. 31, 2024. Premature infants born at less than 32 weeks of gestation and weighing less than 1500 g who presented after birth with RDS with the need for noninvasive ventilatory support as initial treatment were eligible for inclusion.ResultsOf the 49 eligible premature infants, 25 were randomized to the VMN group, and 24 to the less invasive surfactant administration (LISA) group. We did not find a difference in the need for mechanical ventilation via an endotracheal tube (MVET) within 72 h between the VMN and LISA groups (3 [12.00%] vs. 5 [20.80%]; odds ratio, 0.52; 95% confidence intervals (CI), 0.11–2.46; P = 0.653). Comparing the LISA group, the incidence of apnea of prematurity and transient decrease in oxygen saturation was lower in the VMN group (2[8.00%] vs. 10 [41.67%]; odds ratio [OR], 0.10; 95% CI 0.02–0.54, P = 0.003; 3[12.00%] vs. 16[66.67%]; OR, 0.10; 95% CI 0.02–0.30, P < 0.001). However, there was no difference in the bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD), death in hospital, pneumothorax, periventricular leukomalacia (PVL), retinopathy of prematurity (ROP), necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), periventricular–intraventricular hemorrhage (PIVH), and application of PS again between the two groups (all P > 0.05).ConclusionIn this exploratory clinical trial, compared with LISA, the noninvasive VMN technique may be a safe and well-tolerated approach for PS delivery in preterm infants with RDS.Trial registration: ChiCTR2300072262 (https://www.chictr.org.cn).“Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03316-6.Keywords: Respiratory distress syndrome, Preterm infants, Vibrating mesh nebulizers

Introduction

Respiratory distress syndrome (RDS), caused by PS deficiency, is considered a major cause of early mortality and poor long-term prognosis in premature neonates [1]. The incidence of neonatal RDS ranges between 30 and 40% in preterm neonates [2]. According to European consensus guidelines on the management of RDS, exogenous PS administration combined with mechanical ventilation can significantly reduce the mortality rate of RDS [3]. The classical route of PS administration involves injection into the lungs via endotracheal intubation. However, this procedure is invasive and can cause adverse effects in premature neonates. Thus, minimally invasive or noninvasive routes of PS delivery continue to be a popular research topic. [4] Innovative strategies have been widely used to treat RDS. A key approach is nasal continuous positive airway pressure (nCPAP) combined with PS delivery techniques, such as intubation-surfactant-extubation (INSURE) and less invasive surfactant administration (LISA) [5, 6]. Both LISA and INSURE offer clear benefits over the traditional method of endotracheal intubation followed by prolonged mechanical ventilation for administering PS, increased risk of BPD and mortality in preterm infants [7]. However, these techniques remain invasive despite reducing the need for mechanical ventilation.

The VMN is a noninvasive device developed to address the limited efficiency and aerosol delivery challenges of conventional nebulizers in neonatal respiratory care. It offers four core advantages: (1) deep lung targeting via optimal 1–5 μm particles and low-flow delivery; (2) preservation of PS bioactivity with ambient-temperature nebulization; (3) reduced iatrogenic risks by eliminating barotrauma and bronchospasm; and (4) improved patient tolerance through silent design and rapid treatment [8, 9]. Notably, these features enable effective, infant-friendly alveolar drug delivery. Berlinski comprehensively summarized the characteristics of VMN and their application in children [10]. However, evidence from clinical trials related to the use of VMN for the treatment of RDS in preterm infants is lacking. Therefore, as the first step, we conducted this multicenter, open-label, exploratory, randomized clinical trial (RCT) to assess the safety and procedural tolerance of PS inhalation via VMN for the treatment of RDS in premature neonates. In the future, a powered RCT with a noninferiority design will be performed to evaluate the superiority of VMN-delivered PS in efficacy for the treatment of neonatal RDS.

Methods

Study design

This prospective multicenter, open-label, exploratory RCT was a 2-parallel-group design with a 1:1 allocation ratio and was performed between January 2023 and December 2024. A total of 7 tertiary referral NICUs in China participated in this trial. Ethical review approval was obtained from the medical ethics committees of each of the 7 participating centers, and this study was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300072262). The study was conducted in accordance with the protocol (Supplement 1) and followed the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Only children whose legal guardians provided written informed consent were enrolled. This manuscript followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines.

Study population

Attending neonatologists screened the neonates daily for eligibility. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) gestational age between 250 and 326 weeks; (2) weight less than 1500 g; (3) newborns who were nonintubated, had not previously received PS, were > 1 h but < 12 h of age; (4) gradually aggravated dyspnea and were with suspected or confirmed RDS requiring therapeutic administration of nasal respiratory support by nCPAP. The diagnosis of RDS will be based on clinical manifestations (tachypnea, nasal flaring, and or grunting) and a fraction of inspired oxygen (FiO2) greater than 0.25 for target saturation of peripheral oxygen (SpO2) 89%–94%.

Neonates were excluded if they had one of the following conditions: (1) lack of effective spontaneous breathing; (2) indications for invasive ventilation, including fraction of FiO2 > 0.4, arterial partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2) < 50 mmHg, and/or arterial partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PaCO2) > 65 mmHg and pH < 7.25; (3) major congenital anomalies or chromosomal abnormalities; (4) neuromuscular diseases; or (5) suspected congenital lung diseases, malformations, or pulmonary hypoplasia.

Intervention

nCPAP management

Initially, there was an entry requirement for a FiO2 concentration of 0.25. The starting positive end expiratory pressure (PEEP) of nCPAP will be set at 6 cmH2O and can be raised in steps of 1 cmH2O up to 10 cmH2O. If this is not enough to maintain SpO2 between 90 and 95%, FiO2 will be added up to 0.40. When SpO2 is consistently > 90% with FiO2 less than 0.25 and PEEP less than 5 cmH2O, the neonate will be weaned off nCPAP.

VMN group

The VMN was purchased from Aerogen Co., Ltd. (Ireland). Premature neonates were placed in the supine position to keep the airway open. While the infant was breathing via nCPAP ventilation, the VMN was connected to the inhalation tube of the ventilator via a T-tube. The VMN controller was connected to a nebulizing device. PS was injected into the container of the nebulizing device. Then, the controller was turned on, and nebulized aerosols were produced. If the drug became viscous or stagnant during nebulization, it was diluted with an appropriate amount of sterilized water for injection to complete nebulization.

LISA group

Neonatologists and nurses at each hospital received training to ensure the homogeneity and safety of the LISA technique. While the infant was on nCPAP ventilation, a 6 Fr feeding tube was placed in the trachea via direct laryngoscopy with Magill forceps. The distance (cm) from the end of the feeding tube to the infant’s mouth was calculated as follows: weight (kg) + 6. Once the correct position was confirmed, the tube was fixed to the upper jaw of the infant, and slow and continuous PS delivery was initiated. If the infant is restless, the sedoanalgesia is applied. The tube was removed after PS delivery.

PS dosage

In both groups, the dosage of PS (Chiesi Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd, Italy) was 200 mg/kg as a first dose [11, 12]. Arterial blood gas levels were measured before and 30 min after PS delivery, and the changes in these levels were calculated. If dyspnea gradually worsened again and RDS was suspected or confirmed by chest radiography, the PS would be delivered again. The second application of PS was delivered in the same way as the first application. The second dose of PS was 100 mg/kg.

Intubation

Intubation will be considered if one of the following occurs: (1) severe respiratory acidosis (defined as PaCO2 > 60 mmHg with pH < 7.2) for at least 1 h; (2) hypoxia refractory to study intervention (defined as SpO2 < 90%, with FiO2 = 0.4 and maximal pressures allowed in the study arm) for at least 1 h after the administration of surfactant; (3) severe apnea (defined as recurrent apnea with > 3 episodes/hour associated with heart rate < 100 rpm or a single episode of apnea requiring bag and mask ventilation); (4) contraindications to noninvasive ventilation such as pulmonary hemorrhage, severe arrhythmia, shock, frequent convulsions, etc.; and (5) attending physician determined that urgent intubation is necessary.

Randomization and blinding

Eligible premature neonates were randomized (1:1) to either the VMN group or the LISA group. The randomization code was generated via a web-based computer-generated randomization tool (https://www.randomizer.org). An independent statistician prepared the randomization schedule in permuted blocks of 2, 4, or 6. The group assignments were placed in sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes that were opened only after consent was obtained from the parents. The statistician responsible for analyzing the outcomes was blind to the group assignments.

Assessments

Arterial blood gas analysis results, including pH, PaCO2, and PaO2/FiO2, were compared between the VMN and LISA group. The primary outcome was the need for MVET within 72 h after the initiation of nCPAP treatment. The secondary outcomes included apnea of prematurity and oxygen saturation < 85% during the PS administration via VMN or LISA, the reapplication of PS, the occurrence of BPD, pneumothorax, PVL, ROP, NEC, PIVH, and death in the hospital.

Statistics

Since this study was an exploratory study, the goal of sample size estimation was to ensure a sufficient probability of observing a lower incidence rate of MVET in the VMN group than in the LISA group, rather than to achieve specific statistical power for the superiority test. We assumed incidence rates of MVET of 10% for the VMN group and 20% for the LISA group. Under this assumption, a sample size of 25 neonates per group would provide an 84.1% probability of observing a lower MVET incidence rate in the VMN group than in the LISA group.

The analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM). For continuous variables, t tests and Mann‒Whitney U tests were used for normally distributed and skewed variables, respectively. An analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model was used to analyze the differences in blood gas levels between the two groups. The χ2 test or Fisher test was performed to compare binary outcomes. OR and 95%CI were calculated. A 2-sided P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

A total of 124 premature infants were screened. 56 were excluded, 51 met at least 1 exclusion criterion, and 5 were transferred to other hospitals. From the remaining 68 premature infants, 13 parents refused to participate in our study, and 6 parents withdrew consent. Thus, 49 were randomized. 24 premature infants were randomized to the LISA group, and 25 infants were randomized to the VMN group (Fig. 1). The 2 groups were balanced in terms of baseline characteristics. Gestational age, birth weight, sex, Apgar scores at 1 and 5 min, maternal age, and prenatal corticosteroid prophylaxis were similar between the VMN group and the LISA group (Table 1).

The need for MVET within 72 h was lower in the VMN group than in the LISA group, but the difference was not significant (3 [12.00%] vs. 5 [20.80%]; OR, 0.52; 95% CI 0.11–2.46; P = 0.653). There was no difference in the pneumothorax, PVL, ROP, NEC, and PIVH between the two groups. In addition, there was one death in the LISA group (Table 3).

Discussion

In this study, we found that VMN demonstrates a favorable safety profile for the treatment of RDS in premature neonates. However, this was an exploratory, small sample size study. A powered RCT with a noninferiority design is needed to evaluate the superiority of VMN-delivered PS in terms of efficacy for neonatal RDS.

The benefits of LISA in the treatment of RDS have been extensively studied. Previous clinical trials have demonstrated the critical role of LISA in premature infants with RDS. LISA decreases the risk of BPD, death, or mechanical ventilation [13, 14]. However, the disadvantages of LISA cannot be ignored. Potential complications of catheter insertion during laryngoscopy include cough, arrhythmias, PS reflux, increased intracranial pressure, prolonged hypoxemia, and pain [15]. In addition, a multidisciplinary team is important and necessary to perform LISA in critically ill preterm infants [16]. Thus, the therapeutic effect of the complex LISA procedure is determined by multiple factors. A noninvasive modality with a simple procedure and the same therapeutic benefits as LISA is worthy of investigation.

Aerosol delivery of PS has a 30-year history, but early studies in preterm infants did not achieve the expected clinical improvements [17]. In Berggren E’s study, no beneficial effects were observed with aerosolized surfactant delivered via jet nebulizer as compared to nCPAP alone [18]. Because preterm infants have a reduced functional residual capacity, a high and variable respiratory rate, low tidal volumes, and small airways, these characteristics pose a major challenge to the aerosol delivery of surfactant, as they are associated with low lung deposition of inhaled drugs. In addition, jet nebulizers experience high drug wastage, with approximately 50% of the drug lost during exhalation, and low delivery efficiency [19]. Compared with the jet nebulizer, VMN overcomes these shortcomings. It maximizes the delivery of a fine mist of PS to the deep lung tissues while preserving the integrity of PS [17]. The advantages of VMN have sparked enthusiasm for gradually shifting to noninvasive ventilation in preterm infants with RDS. In the study by Minocchieri et al. of 64 premature infants with RDS, 34.37% of patients treated with VMN and CPAP were intubated, whereas 68.75% of patients treated with CPAP alone were intubated. There was no evidence of BPD in either group [12]. Dani C and colleagues reported that in the groups receiving PS 200 mg/kg and PS 400 mg/kg administered via VMN, the incidence of MVET within 72 h was 57% and 49%, respectively. However, compared with CPAP ventilation, the VMN approach was not associated with a lower risk of intubation [20].

Currently, the reports on the differences between VMN and LISA in the treatment of RDS in preterm infants are rare. In the absence of similar studies, we compared our results with those of previous LISA studies to carefully interpret the advantages and disadvantages of VMN for RDS. Previous RCTs of the LISA procedure reported that the incidence of MVET within 72 h varies from 10 to 40%, [6, 11, 21–28] and the incidence of BPD ranges from 6 to 31%. [21–25, 29, 30] In our study, the incidences of MVET and BPD in our LISA group were 20.8% and 25%, respectively. Our results were similar to those found in the previous RCTs of LISA. In addition, the incidence rates of MVET within 72 h and BPD in our VMN group were 12% and 16%, respectively. When compared with LISA, although there is no statistical difference, these lower incidence rates are promising and merit further clinical trials to explore the therapeutic value of VMN as an alternative to LISA in premature infants with RDS. In our study, the complications of LISA procedures, such as apnea of prematurity and transient decrease in oxygen saturation, were common. Interestingly, VMN was associated with a lower incidence of these complications. In our opinion, this finding was due to the characteristics of the truly noninvasive approach of VMNs. It suggested that VMN could be safer and have better tolerance than LISA. However, the clinical adoption of VMN also faces practical challenges. The key considerations include the cost and availability of the nebulizer equipment, as well as the need for dedicated staff training on its use and integration into the respiratory circuit. These factors will influence its feasibility in routine practice.

Limitations

This study has several limitations to acknowledge. First, as an exploratory study, the analyzed results are based on a small sample size. This means that the efficacy and feasibility of VMN cannot be assessed in our study. Second, this trial was not blinded. As an open-label RCT, the participants’ parents and medical staff were aware of the intervention for the premature infants. This could introduce biases and reduce the internal validity of the study. Third, we did not record physiological endpoints, such as heart rate and SpO₂ response time. This limits our ability to compare the subtle physiological stresses imposed by each technique, which could be valuable for understanding mechanisms and optimizing the comfort of preterm neonates. Finally, as a multicenter trial conducted only in China, the generalizability of our findings may be limited. International differences in LISA experience and procedural proficiency, such as in many European and North American NICUs where it is routine, could reduce the observed advantage of VMN in reducing complications. Conversely, VMN may offer greater benefits in settings with less LISA experience. Thus, our results represent preliminary exploratory evidence.

Conclusions

In this exploratory clinical trial, preliminary results suggest that the noninvasive VMN technique may be a safe and well-tolerated approach for PS delivery in preterm infants with RDS, as no significant adverse effects were observed. These findings indicate that VMN could be a promising alternative, but due to the exploratory design and small sample size, they should be interpreted with caution. Further large-scale studies are needed to confirm these observations and avoid overstating comparative benefits.

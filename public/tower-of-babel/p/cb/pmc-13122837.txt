Optimal reactive power dispatch based on a multitask-assisted constrained multimodal multi-objective evolutionary algorithm
Authors: Yi Hu, Wei Hu, Xuefeng Li, Yanming Shi, Junwei Jin, Xiaozhen Ren, Xiaqiong Fan, Tiejun Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryIn modern power systems, multi-objective optimal reactive power dispatch is crucial for reducing power loss while maintaining bus voltage stability. However, gaps in multimodal feature mining and multi-objective decision-making demand further progress. This study proposes a constrained multimodal multi-objective evolutionary algorithm with a multitask-assisted strategy, assigning global exploration and local exploitation to separate evolutionary tasks and adopting information transfer for inter-task coordination. A minimum Manhattan distance based decision-making scheme is developed to select trade-off solutions and provide valuable alternatives. Experimental validation on 17 benchmarks confirms the algorithm’s superior multimodal optimization performance. Case studies on IEEE 30/57/118-bus systems verify its effectiveness in optimal reactive power dispatch. For the case with wind power and load uncertainties, it achieves an expected power loss of 2.1916 MW and a voltage deviation of 0.3133 p.u. Experimental results show the algorithm can efficiently optimize power loss and aggregate voltage deviation while satisfying all constraints.Subject areas: algorithms, computer systems organization, artificial intelligence

Introduction

Economically efficient and secure power dispatch is vital in modern power systems.1,2 Most system loads are inductive, and transmission components like transformers consume reactive power, leading to inevitable active power loss. From an economic perspective, optimal reactive power allocation reduces active power loss, yielding energy savings and improving supply efficiency. While reactive power generation must meet VAR (volt-ampere reactive) demand, this process risks excessive component heat loss and undesirable voltage fluctuations. For safety, strategically scheduling reactive generation ensures safe bus voltages and system stability. Therefore, conducting multi-objective optimal reactive power dispatch (ORPD) to simultaneously minimize active power loss and maintain bus voltages within safe levels is both necessary and significant.3

ORPD is a non-linear non-convex multimodal optimization problem with conflicting objectives and complex constraints. Early optimal power flow research employed the Newton method,4 quadratic programming,5 and other classical mathematical programming based methods. However, gradient-based approaches struggle with ORPD’s inequality constraints and discrete variables. The high non-linearity and multimodality of ORPD tend to trap these methods in local optima.6 Traditional methods also suffer from initial value sensitivity and difficulties in solving conflicting optimization objectives.

In order to address traditional methods’ limitations, evolutionary algorithms (EAs) have been applied to ORPD. Basu7 proposed a differential evolution (DE) based method by combining quasi-oppositional based learning with DE. Moth-flame optimization was used to minimize power loss.8 In ORPD, modified gravitational search algorithms9 were adopted to provide optimal solutions for reactive power generation allocation. Additionally, many studies have shown that EAs with hybrid operators (e.g., particle swarm optimization,10 invasive weed optimization,11 and firefly algorithm12) perform better in ORPD. Biswas et al.3 applied a scenario-based approach to ORPD in a hybrid energy system with uncertainties, using a success history-based adaptive DE to minimize active power loss and aggregate voltage deviation (VD). Chauhan et al.13 integrated the fitness-distance balance selection mechanism with a nonlinear adaptive weight strategy. Chauhan et al. also adopted the ϵ-constraint handling method to control constraint variables and proposed a score-based artificial electric field algorithm for solving ORPD problems under renewable energy and load demand uncertainties. Bakır14 improved the exploration operator of the original artificial rabbits optimization algorithm (ARO) via roulette wheel fitness-distance balance and dynamic fitness-distance balance. The improved ARO was proposed to optimize three objectives in optimal power flow (OPF) problems, namely minimizing generation cost, power loss, and total cost.

The aforementioned methods have achieved optimization of different objectives in power dispatch. However, ORPD problems require the comprehensive consideration of multiple conflicting optimization objectives such as power loss and VD. Since the above methods mainly adopt single-objective optimization algorithms, they cannot balance multiple objectives simultaneously.

Mandal et al.15 integrated quasi-oppositional learning into a teaching-learning based EA for ORPD. Dai et al.16 applied the seeker optimization algorithm to thermal power system ORPD, with objectives of active power loss, VD, and voltage stability margin. However, multiple objectives were merged via linear weighting, making the above two methods15,16 essentially single-objective ORPD studies. Liao et al.17 proposed a bi-level multi-objective energy scheduling method for integrated wind- photovoltaic (PV)-storage-hydrogen-charging-swapping energy stations based on a power allocation strategy. The improved non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm III (ANSGA-III) was adopted to solve the established scheduling model. The entropy weight method was used for decision-making to screen the optimal scheme from the Pareto solution set. Liao et al.1 also proposed a multi-objective optimal scheduling model for the coordinated operation of wind-PV-storage integrated electric vehicle battery swapping station (WEP-EVBSS) clusters and regional power grids. A multi-objective optimization (MOO) algorithm based on improved multi-update strategies was applied to power grid dispatch involving five optimization objectives. For decision-making, the entropy weight method was used to determine objective weights, and TOPSIS was combined to select the optimal solution. Anandam et al.18 proposed an indicator-based EA (ISDE+) by integrating shifted density estimation and the sum of optimization objective values, aiming to balance the convergence and diversity of solutions. They also constructed a MOO model considering wind power and load uncertainties. For decision-making, an S-shaped fuzzy membership function was used to select the optimal compromise solution. Singh et al.2 proposed a microgrid economic-emission balance optimization strategy based on hybrid demand response management. This strategy integrates load transfer and load curtailment policies, combined with an intelligent charging strategy for plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs). DE was used to realize the optimal dispatch of distributed energy resources. For decision-making, weighted comprehensive evaluation method and scenario comparison were adopted to determine the optimal scheme according to scenario requirements.

These methods use MOO algorithms to simultaneously optimize multiple objectives in scheduling models. However, current multi-objective ORPD research still has the following problems.

(1)Numerous studies19,20 have shown that ORPD exhibits multimodal characteristics, making it a multimodal optimization problem. Although current studies adopt local search strategies, they lack dedicated mechanisms for analyzing and maintaining multimodal characteristics. This leads to the elimination of other high-quality solutions—obtained at the cost of substantial computational resources—during the evolutionary process.

In order to fill the aforementioned gaps, an innovative multimodal MOO algorithm with constraint handling techniques (MMOCHT) is proposed. By exploring equivalent solutions that perform similarly in the objective space but differ in the decision space of ORPD problems, MMOCHT provides decision-makers with multiple alternative schemes. This effectively enhances the flexibility of optimization decisions.

Numerous studies have been conducted on traditional multimodal MOO (MMO). These include niche technologies based on ring topology23 or clustering,24 as well as diversity maintenance approaches based on space division25 or the special crowding distance (SCD) method.26 Building on this work, Liang et al.22 proposed a speciation-based constrained multimodal multi-objective DE. The proposed DE integrates the constraint dominance principle with an improved environmental selection criterion. The authors also developed 17 test functions for CMMO problems. Li et al.27 addressed CMMO problems by designing a dual-population collaboration mechanism. This mechanism combines the dynamic ϵ-constraint relaxation strategy and the dynamic ϵ convergence relaxation strategy. It further balances exploration and exploitation by dynamically adjusting the sizes of the two populations. Additionally, Li et al.28 proposed a reinforcement learning based CMMO algorithm, which incorporates a three-state (local convergence, diversity, feasibility) framework and a dynamically weighted reward function. This method enables automatic niche selection when solving CMMO problems. Wu et al.29 constructed a dual-population collaborative framework consisting of a constraint-prioritized population and a convergence-prioritized population. They further proposed a dynamic ranking-aided co-EA to balance constraint satisfaction, convergence, and diversity in CMMO. Ming et al.30 designed another dual-population collaborative framework by integrating the constraint dominance principle with the environmental selection of the SPEA2 truncation strategy. The authors also adopted the Euclidean distance evaluation in the decision space to maintain diversity for CMMO problems.

However, current research on CMMO is still in its infancy, with the following unresolved issues: (1) balancing diversity and convergence across both objective and decision spaces remains challenging for different CMMO problems; (2) current CMMO algorithms still struggle with diverse constraint variables when searching for multimodal solutions; and (3) most existing studies are limited to artificially designed test functions with only 2-3 decision variables, and few involve practical CMMO applications with more than ten decision variables.

To address these issues, this study employs a multitask-assisted technology to assign different evolutionary goals to specialized evolutionary tasks, and then integrates population information through knowledge transfer. This study achieves a balance between convergence, diversity, multimodal optimization performance, and constraint satisfaction across different solution spaces. Finally, the proposed MMOCHT is applied to solve ORPD problems (the number of decision variables is usually greater than ten). Detailed algorithm design and experimental validations are presented in subsequent sections.

Proposed method

Procedure of the proposed MMOCHT

Multi-objective ORPD targets the simultaneous convergence and diversity under constraints. Since ORPD is inherently multimodal,19,20 identifying and maintaining multimodal solutions is essential. However, traditional EAs32,33,34 focus solely on optimization objectives and lack dedicated multimodal handling mechanisms, potentially losing robust solutions.22,23 This study applies a multitask-assisted strategy to address the aforementioned difficulties. Figure 2 illustrates the structure of the proposed MMOCHT. Three populations (P1, P2, and P3) are used to perform the main evolutionary task, global exploration task, and local exploitation task, respectively. Each individual in these populations represents the control variables of an ORPD problem. Figure 3 shows the flowchart of MMOCHT. The pseudocode of MMOCHT is presented in Algorithm 1.

Furthermore, the local exploitation task employs a dynamic constraint boundary strategy. This strategy defines the constraint boundary Bc as the mean constraint violation value of all infeasible solutions in Q1. Bc is updated after the knowledge transfer based environmental selection in each generation. The local exploitation task then introduces a new constraint, i.e., CV(x)≤Bc. This task aims to enhance local diversity and refine the exploration of promising areas.

After obtaining new offsprings Q1, Q2, and Q3, knowledge transfer based environmental selection is executed in steps 24–30. The proposed MMOCHT first selects Np individuals from {P1∪Q2∪Q3} to produce P1′. Then P1←{P1′∪Q1} is conducted to reduce the impact of transferred offspring on the distribution of P1. Knowledge transfer for P2 and P3 is implemented via environmental selection of P2←{P2∪Q2∪Q1∪Q3} and P3←{P3∪Q3∪Q1∪Q2}, respectively. Note that the selection metric of environmental selection is Fit. In order to avoid negative transfer, this study also conducts the truncation operation35 to delete useless solutions.

In practical ORPD applications, sensitive parameters may increase operators’ tuning difficulty and compromise the algorithm’s reliability under different operating conditions. Following the parameter-free design philosophy, MMOCHT reduce the number of parameters in key components to avoid performance deviations caused by parameter sensitivity. The core components of MMOCHT are parameter-free: the niche strategy is implemented by dynamically calculating the nearest Euclidean distance between current evolutionary individuals. Knowledge transfer is realized based on parameter-free non-dominated sorting and the SCD metric. In the dynamic constraint technique, the constraint boundary is set as the mean of CV values of all infeasible solutions in the main evolutionary task’s population.

Computational complexity

In MMOCHT, evolutionary operators and environmental selection are the core components. The complexity of the competitive operator update is O(MNp2). The reverse vector mutation operator has a complexity of O(DNp). For genetic operators, the complexities of mating selection and offspring generation are O(Np) and O(DNp), respectively. The complexities of constrained dominance principle, non-dominated sorting, and multi-objective-based constraint handling technology are O(MNp2). Among the populations of the three evolutionary tasks, the worst complexity is O(MNp2), which is comparable to that of most other multi-objective EAs.

Analysis of the optimization ability

A key strength of multimodal EAs is their enhanced decision space search ability, which delivers diverse solutions in complex engineering scenarios.25,36 In this study, MMOCHT’s optimization performance is verified using 17 constrained multimodal benchmarks (CMMF1-CMMF17). Each benchmark has multimodality and irregularity in its constrained PSs and PFs, with independent and narrow feasible regions in the decision space. This property helps differentiate the search performance of comparison algorithms. Details of these 17 benchmarks are provided in Liang et al.22

Based on the above 17 benchmark problems, this study conducted comparative experiments for evaluating the proposed MMOCHT against five state-of-the-art constrained EAs, i.e., CTAEA (multi-archive-based EA),33 MOEADDAE (decomposition-based EA),37 DCNSGAIII (problem transformation-based EA),34 POCEA (subpopulation-based EA),38 and CMMODE (speciation-based EA).22 Although multimodal multi-objective EAs have gained increasing research attention, relatively few studies have addressed constrained scenarios. Consequently, this study selected CMMODE as a representative constrained MMO algorithm alongside four common constrained EAs for comparative analysis.

All comparative algorithms were independently run 30 times on the PlatEMO platform.39 To ensure fairness, the population size (Np) and maximum number of fitness evaluations (Emax) were set to 100 and 10,000, respectively. These values correspond to the default settings in PlatEMO. The remaining parameters of these five algorithms were set according to their original references. The scaling factor (γ) and extension factor (δ) of MMOCHT were set to 0.5 and 10, respectively. All experiments were performed in MATLAB 2021a on a Windows 10 system, using a computer with an Intel Core i7 CPU and 16 GB of RAM.

As shown in Table 1, the proposed MMOCHT attained the best mean IGDx values in 15 cases. While CTAEA and MOEADDAE exhibited smaller mean IGDx values than MMOCHT on CMMF14 and CMMF16, MMOCHT performed statistically similarly to these two algorithms. MMOCHT employs a Euclidean distance based niching method to secure multimodal solutions during the local exploitation task, thus delivering excellent performance in the decision space. In contrast, common EAs lack specialized mechanisms, which hinder their ability to capture more multimodal solutions. This explains why CTAEA, MOEADDAE, DCNSGAIII, and POCEA underperformed. Although CMMODE uses elite seeds and speciation-based niching to capture more solutions, it requires excessive fitness evaluations and fails to converge within the specified limit.

Table 2 shows the experimental results of the comparison algorithms in the objective space. MOEADDAE uses a detect-and-escape strategy for constrained optimization problems with local optimal PFs, but its performance was affected by the parameter sensitivity of the ROC metric. POCEA enhances local exploration and traversal of infeasible regions via a subpopulation-based pairing strategy, yet struggles with irregular PFs and parameter adaptation. CTAEA uses a two-archive mechanism to handle convergence and diversity separately, but its performance declined when the maximum number of evaluations was reduced. DCNSGAIII, a variant of NSGAIII, employs a problem transformation technique that treats constraint violation as an extra objective. DCNSGAIII balances objectives and constraints, yet its performance is affected by key parameter settings. CMMODE concurrently handles multimodality and dual-space convergence and diversity via a single DE operator. Since a single operator struggles with multiple complex optimization goals, the proposed MMOCHT uses a multitask-assisted strategy to assign different evolutionary goals to matching tasks, then selects the optimal offspring through knowledge transfer based environment selection. The SCD metric (Equation 17) comprehensively assesses solution distributions in both spaces, enabling MMOCHT to achieve superior IGDx and HV values. MMOCHT obtained 11 best mean HV values and ranked first in the comparative experiment. As indicated by Tables 1 and 2, this performance confirms MMOCHT’s ability to simultaneously maintain superior convergence and diversity in the objective space and decision space.

Case studies

Experimental setup of case studies

This section discusses the experimental results of the proposed MMOCHT in ORPD. Six comparative methods, i.e., SMODE-SF,40 MOEA/D-DRA,21 MOEA/D-SP,6 DCMMEA,27 GMOMUS,1 and MCCMO41 were used to evaluate the performance of MMOCHT. SMODE-SF employs a multi-objective DE with the superiority of feasible solutions (SFs) constraint handling technique. MOEA/D-DRA and MOEA/D-SP both implement dynamic resource allocation (DRA) to enhance computational efficiency, but adopt a hierarchical constraint strategy and self-adaptive penalty method for ORPD constraint handling, respectively. DCMMEA is a CMMO algorithm with a dual dynamic ϵ-relaxation strategy. GMOMUS is a newly proposed constrained many-objective optimization algorithm that adopts multiple update strategies. MCCMO is a multi-population-based MMO algorithm with a multi-constraint mechanism. For a fair comparison, the population size (Np) and maximum number of evaluations (Emax) of all comparative algorithms were set to 100 and 10,000, respectively. Their remaining parameters of the six comparison algorithms followed their original references. MMOCHT’s parameter settings and other experimental setups were the same as those in the analysis of the optimization ability section. Additionally, MATPOWER 3.0 software was used for load flow calculations.

The sensitivity analysis of algorithm performance and parameters in ORPD helps quantify the impact of parameter variations on Ploss and VD. It further guides the formulation of differentiated reactive power dispatch strategies. In this section, this study took the IEEE 30-bus system (case 1) as an example and conducted a sensitivity analysis on the performance and parameters of MMOCHT for ORPD.

W_ Ploss and L_ Ploss denote Ploss under different system preferences and load conditions, respectively. In the analysis of system preferences, L1–L5 represent the weight ratios of Ploss and VD as (0.9, 0.1), (0.7, 0.3), (0.5, 0.5), (0.3, 0.7), and (0.1, 0.9), respectively. As shown in Figure 18, with the weight ratio shifting from Ploss priority to VD priority (L1-L5), the growth rate of Ploss first increases and then decreases. It reaches its peak during the transition from balanced preference to voltage priority (L3-L4). Meanwhile, the reduction rate of VD also peaks in the same interval. The negative correlation between Ploss and VD verifies the conflict between these two optimization objectives. The balanced weight ratio (L3) can well balance the system economy and voltage security. The complete match between indicator variations and weight adjustments verifies the effectiveness of the multi-objective weight allocation mechanism of MMOCHT. The high sensitivity of VD to weight changes reflects that MMOCHT has higher regulation efficiency in the voltage optimization direction. The continuous and smooth variations of Ploss and VD indicate that MMOCHT can generate robust optimization solutions under different preferences settings.

In the load condition analysis, L1–L5 denote the load levels at 30%, 50%, 70%, 90%, and 110% of the base load, respectively. During the L1–L2 interval (light-load condition), MMOCHT can reduce reactive power circulation by means such as lowering transformer tap positions, thereby significantly decreasing the active power loss caused by reactive current. Moreover, it achieves a remarkable Ploss reduction effect at the cost of only a minimal increase in VD. This demonstrates the algorithm’s excellent adaptability and robustness under light-load conditions. In the L2–L4 interval (medium-to-heavy load condition), the trade-off between loss reduction and voltage regulation intensifies continuously. This trade-off can be further balanced by optimizing the weights of the multi-objective function. During the L4–L5 interval (heavy-to-overload condition), MMOCHT automatically shifts the optimization priority from loss reduction to voltage regulation. It prioritizes voltage stability and supplements this with reactive power compensation measures, which effectively suppresses the growth rate of active power loss.

Application prospects

MMO-based ORPD plays a crucial role in economically efficient and secure power dispatch within modern power system management. Existing multi-objective EAs lack mechanisms for searching and maintaining multimodal solutions. Although they can provide compromised optimal dispatch schemes, these algorithms often eliminate dispatch solutions with identical or similar objective values but different decision variables (i.e., multimodal solutions) during the evolutionary process. By fully leveraging multimodal solution search mechanisms and multi-objective constraint handling technologies, MMOCHT possesses the following application values and prospects in modern power system management.

(1)MMOCHT adopts multitask-assisted technology based on knowledge transfer and dynamic constraint handling technology. It assigns different evolutionary tasks to specific optimization objectives, ultimately achieving the simultaneous optimization of Ploss and VD. In modern power system management, MMOCHT can ensure safe bus voltages and system stability while realizing energy savings and improving supply efficiency.

MMOCHT adopts multitask-assisted technology based on knowledge transfer and dynamic constraint handling technology. It assigns different evolutionary tasks to specific optimization objectives, ultimately achieving the simultaneous optimization of Ploss and VD. In modern power system management, MMOCHT can ensure safe bus voltages and system stability while realizing energy savings and improving supply efficiency.

(2)MMOCHT’s reverse vector mutation technique and Euclidean distance based niching method enable the exploration and exploitation of the decision space respectively, achieving the search and maintenance of multimodal solutions. In power system dispatch, MMOCHT not only provides compromised optimal dispatch schemes but also offers multiple highly valuable candidate schemes for decision-makers to reference and select based on actual operating conditions.

MMOCHT’s reverse vector mutation technique and Euclidean distance based niching method enable the exploration and exploitation of the decision space respectively, achieving the search and maintenance of multimodal solutions. In power system dispatch, MMOCHT not only provides compromised optimal dispatch schemes but also offers multiple highly valuable candidate schemes for decision-makers to reference and select based on actual operating conditions.

Although MMOCHT incorporates various evolutionary operators, environmental selection mechanisms, and the MMD-based decision-making mechanisms, these components can be encapsulated into computer programs and integrated as subsystems into power management systems in practical applications. This helps improve the flexibility of ORPD decisions and assists in the safe and economical dispatch of power systems.

Discussion

This study proposed a constrained multimodal multi-objective EA (MMOCHT) to simultaneously minimize active power loss (Ploss) and aggregate VD in power systems. The multitask-assisted strategy enhances efficiency by assigning problems like constraint handling, multimodal feature mining, and diverse variable spaces searching to specialized tasks, leveraging task-specific search abilities. MMOCHT also uses population-based knowledge transfer to coordinate different evolutionary tasks and select the optimal Np individuals. Additionally, an MMD-based decision-making method was designed to select the final Ploss and VD values and their corresponding control variables.

Experimental results on 17 CMMF benchmarks showed that the proposed MMOCHT achieved 15 optimal mean IGDx and 11 optimal mean HV values, outperforming CTAEA, MOEADDAE, DCNSGAIII, POCEA, and CMMODE in both the decision space and objective space. Detailed case studies were conducted on IEEE 30-bus, 57-bus, and 118-bus systems. Compared with six constrained multi-objective EAs, MMOCHT delivered the best mean Ploss and VD. In the IEEE 57-bus system, MMOCHT attained an optimal Ploss (19.819 MW) and the second-best VD (0.897 p.u.). For the IEEE 118-bus system, the mean Ploss and VD achieved by MMOCHT were 128.33 MW and 1.0566 p.u., respectively—representing 22.43% and 27.34% reductions in these metrics compared to the mean performance of comparative algorithms. Additionally, Friedman test results indicated that MMOCHT ranked first with a mean rank of 2.5, outperforming all comparative algorithms. Its runtime was second only to that of GMOMUS. Experimental results demonstrated that MMOCHT can efficiently achieve excellent objective values while maintaining exceptional constraint handling performance. An ablation study was designed to evaluate the effectiveness of each core module of MMOCHT. Furthermore, this study compared three multi-objective decision-making schemes: TOPSIS, the fuzzy decision-making method, and the proposed MMD-based decision-making method. Results showed that the MMD-based method can provide decision-makers with multiple alternative dispatch schemes, which helps enhance the flexibility of ORPD decisions. This study also conducted case analyses on ORPD with wind power and load demand uncertainties based on scenario generation and reduction technologies. In the experimental study involving 25 scenarios, the expected power loss and expected VD achieved by MMOCHT were 2.1916 MW and 0.3133 p.u., respectively. Moreover, MMOCHT fully satisfied all constraint conditions. Sensitivity analysis experiments under different system preferences and load conditions were conducted. The experimental results demonstrated the effectiveness and robustness of MMOCHT. Furthermore, sensitivity analysis experiments on different algorithm parameters verified the rationality of parameter selection for MMOCHT.

Limitations of the study

Although experimental results validate the effectiveness of MMOCHT in ORPD, the current study still has some limitations. The specific limitations and future research directions are as follows.

(1)This study primarily used MMOCHT to address the MMO of Ploss and VD in ORPD. However, power dispatch also involves additional optimization objectives such as fuel cost and emissions, as well as control variables including flexible AC transmission system device status. Future research will extend MMOCHT to solve OPF problems involving more objectives and control variables.

Scenario reduction method

The 1000 scenarios generated by scenario generation technology can cover various load and wind power operating conditions for ORPD research. However, to facilitate scenario-based ORPD analysis, the references3,13 designed a corresponding scenario reduction technique. Selecting 25 representative scenarios from 1000 enables more convenient and effective verification of the algorithm’s performance under uncertain load and wind power disturbances. The implementation steps of the adopted backward reduction algorithm (BRA) are as follows:

Step 1: Assign the same probability Δ0=1/1000 to the 1000 generated scenario vectors Y=[Pd,vw].

Step 2: Calculate the norm between each pair of scenario vectors to obtain the distance dij=|Yi-Yj|. Construct a symmetric 1000×1000 distance matrix Ds, with all diagonal entries set to 0. Set the current number of scenarios nd=1000.

Step 3: Identify the minimum distance value from Ds, excluding self-distances (dii). Assume two rows (ms and ns) in Ds correspond to scenarios ms and ns, respectively. If the probability of scenario Δm is greater than or equal to that of scenario ns (Δn), remove scenario ns and update Δm=Δm+Δn. Otherwise, remove scenario ms and update Δn=Δn+Δm. Update the current number of scenarios nd=nd-1.

Step 4: Repeat Steps 2 and 3, removing eligible scenarios until nd=25.

Calculation of wind power

Through the scenario reduction technique, 25 scenario vectors Y=[Pd,vw] and their corresponding probabilities are obtained. For the analysis of different scenarios, specific load and wind power values need to be determined. The load magnitude for each scenario is calculated by multiplying the base load of all buses by the load percentage of the 25 scenarios. The wind power magnitude is derived from the wind speed contained in the obtained scenario vectors.

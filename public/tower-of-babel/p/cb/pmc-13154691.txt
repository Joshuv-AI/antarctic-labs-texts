PfWRI1-10 transcription factor mediates lipid synthesis in Perilla frutescens through regulating PfBCCP1 gene transcription
Authors: Xudong Chai, Yali Zhou, Jing Wen, Ting Lei, Hongli Yang, Miao Yin, Xusheng Huang, Rui Li, Runzhi Li, Jiping Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPerilla frutescens is an oilseed crop rich in unsaturated fatty acids (UFAs). WRINKLED1 (WRI1) is an AP2/EREBP-type transcription factor (TF), which plays important regulation roles in fatty acid (FA) metabolism.ResultsIn this study, 11 PfWRI1 members were identified in the perilla genome. qRT-PCR analysis revealed that PfWRI1-10 was expressed higher than other members in developing seeds of perilla, suggesting that PfWRI1-10 may play crucial roles in seed oil biosynthesis. Overexpression of PfWRI1-10 resulted in enhancement of total oil content by 9.75% and α-linolenic acid (C18:3) level by 0.43%, while the soluble sugar content decreased in the transgenic tobacco leaves. qRT-PCR showed that the expression levels of glycolysis gene (NtPKp-β1) and FA biosynthesis genes (NtACP1 and NtBCCP1) significantly upregulated, whereas NtKAS1-B expression was downregulated in transgenic tobacco plants, indicating that PfWRI1-10 reallocated carbon sources flow to facilitate lipid biosynthesis by regulating the expression of the target genes. Yeast one-hybrid (Y1H) assays demonstrate that PfWRI1-10 directly binds to the AW-box cis-element of the PfBCCP1 promoter. Overexpression of PfBCCP1 enhanced the lipid content in the transgenic yeast, confirming its function in lipid metabolism.ConclusionsPfWRI1-10 transcription factor promotes lipid biosynthesis by upregulating PfBCCP1 and other lipid related genes. The current findings provide a scientific basis for the in-depth analysis of the regulatory network of perilla oil biosynthesis and also valuable genes for genetic improvement of perilla oil yield and quality.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-08653-0.

Background

Perilla frutescens (L.) Britt. is an annual self-pollinating herbaceous plant of the genus perilla in the family Lamiaceae [1]. Perilla originated from East Asia is mainly distributed in China, Indonesia, Vietnam, Korea, and North Korea [2]. It has been listed as one of the first 60 medicinal and edible plants announced by the China Health Planning Commission. In China, the cultivation of perilla has a history of nearly 3,000 years. Perilla seed oil is a natural vegetable oil rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) and contains over 65% α-linolenic acid (ALA) [3]. α-Linolenic acid is an important PUFA with multiple health benefits, such as lowering blood lipids, enhancing memory, slowing aging, preventing cancer, and combating allergies [4]. As a special oilseed crop, perilla is considered as a valuable source of nutritious and health-promoting oils [5].

Transcription factors (TFs) play key roles in complex regulatory networks, participating in the regulation of various metabolic processes by upregulating or downregulating target gene expression or forming complexes with other TFs [6, 7]. Previous studies have demonstrated that TFs regulated plant lipid metabolism [8]. It is crucial to conduct an in-depth investigation on how TFs and their target genes control seed lipid accumulation for understanding molecular mechanism of lipid biosynthesis and breeding of high-quality oil crops. Numerous TFs have been identified as the regulators in plant lipid/fatty acid biosynthesis [9], including ABI3 [10], WRI1 [11], LEC1 [12], MYB [13], bZIP [14], ZF-HD [15]. Among these TFs, WRI1 functions as a central regulator in lipid metabolism [16]. WRI1 transcriptionally regulated by other TF factors such as LEC1, LEC2, and ZF-HD was detected to positively regulate the expression of downstream genes encoding glycolytic enzymes and fatty acid synthases by recognizing AW-box motifs in promoter regions the target genes [17].

WRI1 is a plant-specific APETALA2 (AP2) type transcription factor with two AP2 structural domains and a DNA-binding domain comprising of 60–70 amino acids (aa) [18]. It belongs to the AP2/EREBP (ethylene-responsive element-binding protein) transcription factor family [19]. WRI1 has been identified as a key regulator in carbon allocation from sugars to fatty acid biosynthesis, with the potential to enhance seed oil content [20]. WRI1 has been characterized in various plant species such as Arabidopsis thaliana [21], maize [22], oil palm [23], Camellia oleifera [24], soybean [25], and sunflower [26]. Overexpression of the BnWRI1 gene in Arabidopsis promotes the expression of the key gene EAR for fatty acid synthesis, leading to an increase in seed oil content [27]. The transgenic lines overexpressing the coconut WRI1 gene in rice exhibited a significant increase in fatty acid content [28]. Using Agrobacterium-mediated transformation, five WRI1 isoforms were transformed into Nicotiana benthamiana, respectively, resulting in a significant increase in TAG content in the transgenic plants. WRI1 strongly influences metabolic pathways during seed maturation, particularly by regulating the glycolytic pathway that provides precursors for fatty acid biosynthesis, thereby enhancing oil accumulation [29]. Overexpression of WRI1 activates multiple downstream genes involved in glycolysis and fatty acid biosynthesis, enhances carbon flux from glycolysis to lipid synthesis, and significantly increases triacylglycerol (TAG) accumulation in seeds and seedlings [30, 31]. Furthermore, WRI1 precisely regulates the expression of a series of genes involved in fatty acid biosynthesis. The promoter regions of these target genes contain specific sequences that can bind to WRI1, such as an AW-box (CnTnG(n)7CG) or a 15-bp motif (CAAAAG(T/G)AGG(G/A)APTT), underscoring its crucial role in seed oil biosynthesis [32].

Perilla seeds are rich in high-value ω-3 fatty acids (ALA, C18:3). However, the regulatory network responsible for lipid biosynthesis remains unclear in perilla. In this study, 11 PfWRI1 family members were identified in perilla genome through genome-wide analysis, with a particular focus on the functional characterization of PfWRI1-10. The regulatory mechanism mediated by PfWRI1-10 in perilla seed oil biosynthesis was systematically explored through bioinformatics analysis, genetic transformation, and yeast one-hybrid (Y1H) assays. This study revealed the molecular mechanism by which PfWRI1-10 regulated carbon flow distribution toward lipid accumulation by activating the expression of PfBCCP1. These findings not only establish a theoretical basis for investigating the lipid regulatory network in perilla, but also provide new genetic resources for oilseed crop improvement.

Materials and methods

Biological materials

Plant materials

The plant materials used in this study included perilla variety ‘Jinzisu 1’ and common tobacco genotype (Nicotiana tabacum, Sumsun NN [SNN]). The seeds were stored at the Institute of Molecular Agriculture and Bioenergy, Shanxi Agricultural University. ‘Jinzisu 1’ was cultivated at the experimental station of Shanxi Agricultural University in Taigu District, Shanxi Province, China (112°58’ E, 37°42’ N). Roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and seeds (10, 20, 30, and 40 d after flowering) were harvested separately at different developmental stages. All samples were rapidly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 °C for future use.

Strains and vectors

The microorganism strains used in this study included INVSc1 yeast strain, Escherichia coli strain DH5α, Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101, and Y187 yeast strain. The following vectors were utilized, including pMD18-T cloning vector, pCAMBIA1301 and pCAMBIA1300 plant expression vectors, pGADT7 and pHIS2 vectors, pYES2.0 vector. All strains and vectors were stored at the Institute of Molecular Agriculture and Bioenergy, Shanxi Agricultural University.

Identification and sequence characterization of perilla WRI1 gene family members

The genome annotation data for P. frutescens were obtained from the Perilla frutescens var. frutescens genome database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genome/?term=txid48386 (accessed on 9 October 2024)). The AtWRI1 sequence from A. thaliana was retrieved from Arabidopsis Information Resource (TAIR; https://www.arabidopsis.org/, National Microbial Science Data Center, Login ID: OAP01705.1 (accessed on 9 October 2024)). Candidate PfWRI1 genes were identified using the online tools including NCBI CD-Search (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Structure/cdd/wrpsb.cgi (accessed on 10 October 2024)) and SMART (http://smart.embl-heidelberg.de/ (accessed on 10 October 2024)). Physicochemical properties of PfWRI1 proteins were analyzed using ProtParam (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/) on the ExPasy online tool. The chromosomal locations of PfWRI1 genes were determined based on P. frutescens genome annotation data. The physical locations of PfWRI1 genes on chromosomes were mapped from short- to long-arm telomeres using MapChart 2.32 [33].

Subcellular localization was predicted using LocTree3 (https://rostlab.org/services/loctree3/). Gene structure was analyzed using the Gene Structure Display Server (http://gsds.gao-lab.org/index.php) to determine the exon-intron organization and construct gene structure diagrams. Conserved protein motifs were identified using MEME (http://meme-suite.org/). Promoter cis-element analysis of PfWRI1 genes was conducted using PlantCARE (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/plantcare/html/). MEGA11.0 was used to construct a phylogenetic tree of PfWRI1 proteins in P. frutescens and orthologous WRI1 sequences from other reported plant species. Multiple sequence alignments of WRI1 amino acid sequences from Perilla, Arabidopsis, and other species were conducted using the PRALINE program (http://www.ibi.vu.nl/programs/pralinewww/). Sequence information for WRI1 proteins from different species is provided in Table S1.

Expression profile analysis of PfWRI1 gene in perilla

Total RNA was extracted from roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and seeds (10, 20, 30, and 40 d after flowering) of perilla, respectively, using the EASYspin Plus Plant RNA Kit (Beijing Aideleibo Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). First-strand cDNA synthesis was performed using the StarScript II RT Mix with gDNA Eraser (GenStar BioSolutions, Beijing, China), and the primers used were listed in Table S2. With the cDNA of different tissues of Perilla frutescens as the template, PCR was performed using TB Green Premix Ex Taq II (Tli RNaseH Plus; TaKaRa, Beijing, China). The reactions were performed using a CFX96 PCR system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). The 10 µL reaction system included 5 µL TB Green Premix Ex Taq II, 0.2 µL each of forward and reverse primer, 3.6 µL nuclease-free water, and 1 µL cDNA. The cycling conditions were as follows: 95 ℃ for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 10 s and 55 ℃ for 30 s. The dissociation curves were subsequently recorded. The test was performed using three independent biological samples with three technical replicates. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method, with the PfActin gene used as the internal reference.

qRT-PCR analysis of PfWRI1-10 and key genes involved in oil accumulation

Numerous target genes have been shown to be regulated by WRI1, including biotin carboxyl carrier proteins (BCCP1 and BCCP2), plasma pyruvate kinase β-subunit (PKp-β1), β-ketoacyl-ACP synthase (KAS1 and KAS3) [34, 35], acyl carrier protein (ACP1) [36], and lysophosphatidic acid acyltransferase (LPAT) [11]. The specific primers for qRT-PCR (real-time quantitative PCR) were designed for PfBCCP1, PfBCCP2, PfPKp-β1, PfKAS1, PfKAS3, PfACP1, and PfLPAT (Table S2). The qRT-PCR analysis was performed using cDNA from developmental seeds of perilla (10, 20, 30, and 40 d after flowering) as templates. The reaction conditions and PCR cycling program were identical to those described in the PfWRI1 gene expression profile analysis section of perilla. The test was performed using three independent biological samples with three technical replicates.

Cloning and expression vector construction of PfWRI1-10 gene

Full-length primers were designed based on the CDS sequence of PfWRI1-10. PCR amplification was performed using 2×TransStart® FastPfu Fly PCR SuperMix with high-fidelity polymerases. The PCR system consisted of 25 µL SuperMix, 1 µL each of forward and reverse primer, 22 µL nuclease-free water, and 1 µL cDNA (total 50 µL). The amplification conditions were as follows: 98 ℃ for 1 min, followed by 35 cycles of 98 ℃ for 10 s, 60 ℃ for 5 s, and 72 ℃ for 2 s, and then 72 ℃ for 1 min. The PCR products were detected by agarose gel electrophoresis. Target fragment of PfWRI1-10 was excised, recovered, and purified. The purified PfWRI1-10 fragment was stored at − 20 °C for future use.

The amplification primer pair 1301-PfWRI1-10-F/R, designed with homologous arms and restriction sites based on the PfWRI1-10 sequence (Table S2), was used for vector construction. The purified PfWRI1-10 fragment was ligated into the linearized pCAMBIA1301 vector using a one-step cloning kit (C115, Nanjing Novozymes Bio-technology Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China). The recombinant vector was introduced into E. coli DH5α cells using the CaCl2 method [37]. Positive strains were selected using colony PCR screening and LB medium supplemented with 50 µg/mL kanamycin. Recombinant expression vectors were successfully constructed.

Subcellular localization of PfWRI1-10 protein

To investigate the subcellular localization of PfWRI1-10 protein, the target gene fragment was ligated into the linearized pCAMBIA1300 plant expression vector. Primer sequences were listed in Table S2. The confirmed recombinant plasmid pCAMBIA1300-PfWRI1-10/GFP was introduced into A. tumefaciens GV3101 competent cells using freeze-thaw method. The positive transformants were verified using PCR amplification. The recombinant fusion construct 35s: PfWRI1-10: eGFP (pCAMBIA1300-PfWRI1-10/GFP) and the control construct 35s: eGFP were transiently expressed in the epidermal cells of N. benthamiana leaves. The infiltrated tobacco plants were incubated at 25 °C for 36 h. After incubation, the leaf tissues were stained with DAPI reagent (Solarbio Life Sciences, Beijing, China), and the fluorescence signals were visualized using a laser scanning confocal microscope (Leica TCS SP8).

Heterologous expression of PfWRI1-10 in tobacco plant and analysis of physiological and biochemical indices of the transgenics

The recombinant plant overexpression vector pCAMBIA1301-PfWRI1-10 was introduced into tobacco via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation. Genomic and transcriptomic DNA/RNA were extracted from the transformed tobacco leaves, and positive transformants were identified at both the genomic and transcriptional levels using PCR. The primer sequences used are listed in Table S2. The starch, protein, and soluble sugar contents of transgenic tobacco leaves overexpressing PfWRI1-10 were measured using starch, protein, and sugar content assay kits (Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China).

The freeze-dried tobacco seeds were ground into powder, and the total oil was extracted by weighing 0.05 g of powder and performing chloroform-methanol extraction method described by El et al. [38]. The freeze-dried sample powder was transferred to a 10 mL stoppered glass tube containing a chloroform–methanol mixture and mixed on a vortex mixer for 10 min. After that, 0.1 M KCl was added and mixed for further 5 min. Finally, the mixture was centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 min to collect the lower lipid phase. The remaining phase was washed once with chloroform, and the lipid phase was subsequently combined with the earlier-collected lipid extract. The chloroform was completely evaporated to dryness with nitrogen gas (N2) and then stored at − 20 °C for further use. The total oil content was calculated by measuring the change in weight of the glass tube. Fatty acid methyl ester (FAME) was prepared according to the method described by Zhang et al. [39]. FAME analysis was performed using an Agilent 7890B gas chromatograph equipped with a hydrogen flame ionization detector (FID) and an HP-88 capillary column (100 m × 0.25 mm × 0.20 μm). The injection temperature and column temperature were set at 250 °C and 140 °C, respectively, and high-purity nitrogen was used as the carrier gas. Fatty acid composition was quantified using methyl heptadecanoate (C17:0) as the internal standard and a standard curve method. The split flow was not used, and the injection volume was 1 µL. The areas for each fatty acid, including the tri-17:0 standard, were collected and subsequently used to calculate each fatty acid level (% of TAG). Three technical replicates were performed for each biological sample [40].

Analysis of interaction between PfWRI1-10 and the promoter of downstream target gene PfBCCP1

The promoter sequence of PfBCCP1 was retrieved from the perilla genome database. Cis-acting elements were predicted using PlantCARE. The prey vector pGADT7-PfWRI1-10 and bait vector pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro were constructed, and the primer sequences are listed in Table S2. The recombinant plasmids were transformed into Y187 competent strain. Three transformation types were used: (1) pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro alone, (2) the pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro + pGADT7 empty vector, and (3) pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro + pGADT7-PfWRI1-10. SD/-Leu/-Trp-deficient solid culture medium supplemented with different concentrations of 3-amino-1,2,4-triazole (3-AT) was prepared for yeast self-activation assays. A positive control (pHIS2-p53 + pGAD53m), a negative control (co-transfection with pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro and pGADT7), and test group (co-transfection with pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro and the recombinant plasmid pGADT7-PfWRI1-10 in Y187) were used for point-to-point interaction validation.

Heterologous expression of PfBCCP1 in yeast

The specific primers pYES2.0- PfBCCP1-F/R were designed based on the CDS sequence of PfBCCP1 (Table S2). PCR amplification was performed using high-fidelity polymerases, and the products was analyzed using 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. The target fragment was excised, purified using the EasyPure PCR Purification Kit, and stored at − 20 °C. The pYES2.0 vector was digested with Xho I and Kpn I, and the PfBCCP1 fragment was ligated into the linearized vector. The recombinant plasmid was transformed into S. cerevisiae wild-type strain INVSc1 using the Super Yeast Receptor Preparation and Transformation Kit (SK2402, Beijing Coolaber Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Positive strains were identified by uracil-deficient medium (SC-Ura) screening and PCR amplification. To induce gene expression, transgenic yeast cells were cultured to OD600 ≈ 0.4, centrifuged (8000 rpm for 8 min), and the supernatant was discarded. The collected cells were freeze-dried, and then ground into powder. Total lipids of the yeast cells were extracted and fatty acid methyl esters (FAME) were measured according to the principle and method of tobacco lipid extraction and FAME analysis described previously. Three technical replicates were performed for each biological sample.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were repeated at least three times. The data were statistically analyzed using DPS software (version 9.01) with one-way ANOVA. The results were presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). Different lowercase letters indicated significant differences among treatments (P < 0.05).

Results

Eleven members of PfWRI1 gene family are identified in perilla genome

Eleven WRI1 gene sequences were identified through BLAST alignment with Perilla genome database and named PfWRI1-1 to PfWRI1-11 based on their chromosomal locations (Fig. 1). CDD domain analysis revealed that all PfWRI1 proteins had two conserved AP2 domains, classified as the AP2/EREBP TF family (Figure S1). The bioinformatics tools were used to predict the basic physicochemical properties of PfWRI1 proteins (Table S3). These proteins were found to range from 360 to 611 aa in length, with the molecular weights between 40.25 kDa (PfWRI1-3) and 66.82 kDa (PfWRI1-4). The predicted isoelectric points (pIs) ranged from 6.20 to 8.82. All PfWRI1 proteins exhibited negative hydrophilicity coefficients and instability indices above 40, indicating that PfWRI1 proteins in perilla were unstable and hydrophilic in nature. The subcellular localization was predicted using LocTree3, showing that all 11 PfWRI1 proteins were localized in the nucleus.

Phylogenetic relationship and conserved motif analysis of PfWRI1 proteins

The amino acid sequences of PfWRI1 proteins from perilla were used to construct a phylogenetic tree with WRI1 proteins from A. thaliana, castor (Ricinus communis L.), jatropha (Jatropha curcas L.), and 21 other plant species (Fig. 2A). The results showed that all 11 PfWRI1 proteins were clustered closely with JcWRI1, indicating high similarity. JcWRI1 was found to be the closest homolog, whereas Camellia oleifera WRI1 showed the greatest difference. In comparison, PfWRI1 proteins were more distantly related to those in maize, wheat, and oat. Sequence alignment with AtWRI1 and JcWRI1 indicated that all WRI1 proteins contained two conserved AP2 domains (Fig. 2B), although their amino acid lengths were different, suggesting that WRI1 proteins from different species might have functional differences. Gene structure analysis (Fig. 2C) showed that PfWRI1 clustered within the same clade, exhibiting similar exon-intron arrangements. Conserved motif analysis further revealed that all PfWRI1 proteins contained motif 3 and motif 1, both of which correspond to the conserved AP2 structural domains of WRI1 proteins.

Expression patterns of PfWRI1 genes in developmental seeds of perilla

Based on the preliminary transcriptome data of perilla, the expression profiles of PfWRI1 transcription factor members at different developmental stages of seeds (10, 20, and 30 d after flowering) were visualized as heatmaps according to their FPKM values. As shown in Fig. 3A, different PfWRI1 genes displayed different expression patterns during seed development, with PfWRI1-10 demonstrating the highest expression across all developmental stages than the other ten members.

Fig. 3 Clustering heatmap of PfWRI1 gene expression (A) and relative expression levels (B)

Clustering heatmap of PfWRI1 gene expression (A) and relative expression levels (B)

RNA was extracted separately from the roots, stems, leaves, flowers, and seeds (10, 20, 30, and 40 d after flowering) of perilla using the EASYspin Plant RNA Rapid Extraction Kit. qRT-PCR was performed using cDNA from different tissues of Jinzisu 1 as the template. PfActin served as an internal reference gene. The results (Fig. 3B) indicated that all 11 PfWRI1 genes were expressed in different organs, with significant differences in expression levels. Specifically, PfWRI1-1, PfWRI1-3, PfWRI1-4, PfWRI1-7, PfWRI1-8, PfWRI1-9, PfWRI1-10, and PfWRI1-11 exhibited an overall trend of increasing expression, followed by a decline as seed development progressed, and the expression levels reached its peak at 30 d after flowering. PfWRI1-10 showed the highest expression level at all stages, consistent with transcriptome data.

Expression analysis of lipid-related genes regulated by PfWRI1-10

In this study, the expression patterns of seven genes possibly regulated by WRI1 during the development of perilla seeds were analyzed. It was found that their expression patterns were consistent with PfWRI1-10 expression pattern, with a tendency to increase and subsequently decrease as seed development progressed, reaching a peak expression at 30 d after flowering (Fig. 4). All seven genes contained AW-box elements (cttggtttcatcg) in their promoter regions (Table S4), which are known as binding sites for WRI1, suggesting that PfWRI1-10 might regulate their transcriptional expression.

PfWRI1-10 protein is located in the nucleus

Full-length primers were designed based on the CDS sequence of PfWRI1-10 gene. Using cDNA from seeds at different developmental stages as templates, the target gene was amplified using a high-fidelity polymerase. The ligation products were introduced into E. coli competent cells via one-step cloning. By screening, amplifying, and verifying single colony through PCR, a 1368 bp product was obtained. Sequencing and alignment confirmed the sequence identity with PfWRI1-10, confirming successful cloning of PfWRI1-10.

The primers were designed based on the CDS sequence of the PfWRI1-10 gene to further confirm the subcellular localization of the PfWRI1-10 protein. The target gene was successfully amplified using a high-fidelity DNA polymerase, followed by cloning and transformation. A GFP-fused plant expression vector, pCAMBIA1300-PfWRI1-10 (Figure S2), was constructed and transiently expressed in tobacco leaves, with the empty vector pCAMBIA1300/GFP as a negative control. As shown in Fig. 5, the green fluorescence signal from 35s: eGFP was distributed throughout the nucleus and cytoplasm, whereas the 35s: PfWRI1-10:eGFP signal was restricted to the nucleus and colocalized with the nuclear DAPI staining. The result confirmed that the PfWRI1-10 protein was localized in the nucleus.

Overexpression of PfWRI1-10 gene enhances the oil accumulation in transgenic tobacco plants

To further evaluate the function of PfWRI1-10 in the synthesis of plant lipids, the plant overexpression vector pCAMBIA1301-PfWRI1-10 was constructed via homologous recombination. Following Agrobacterium-mediated transformation of common tobacco, positive transgenic tobacco lines were screened and obtained (Figure S3).

Total oil was extracted from the tobacco seeds overexpressing PfWRI1-10, and FAMEs were prepared, and then their contents were quantitatively determined by GC analysis. Compared with wild-type (WT) seeds, the oil content of transgenic tobacco seeds increased by 9.75% (Fig. 6A). The significant changes in fatty acid composition were observed in PfWRI1-10-overexpressing lines compared to wild-type plants. In the transgenic seeds, C18:2 and C18:3 contents increased by 4.05% and 0.43%, respectively, whereas C18:1 content significantly decreased by 2.83%. There was no significant change in the content of C16:0 and C18:0 (Fig. 6B) in seeds between WT and transgenics. These findings demonstrated that the effective expression of PfWRI1-10 in tobacco not only significantly promoted total oil biosynthesis but also altered the fatty acid composition of TAGs in seeds, thereby enhancing PUFAs accumulation-an important trait for improving the nutritional quality of seed oil.

Overexpression of PfWRI1-10 gene impacts the contents of other major metabolites in the transgenic tobacco plants

In order to investigate the effect of PfWRI1-10 overexpression on other agronomic traits of tobacco plants, the contents of starch, soluble sugar, and protein were measured in the leaves. As shown in Fig. 7A, protein content reduced from 105.46 mg/g in the WT plant leaves to 90.72 mg/g in PfWRI1-10 overexpression plant leaves (P < 0.05). The soluble sugar content (Fig. 7B) significantly decreased by 8.0 mg/g (P < 0.05) in the transgenic lines. Starch content was increased in the transgenic leaves compared to WT (Fig. 7C). In summary, the total oil content increased in the transgenic tobacco seeds.The levels of soluble sugars and proteins decreased in the transgenic leaves while the starch content increased, compared to WT. The overexpression of PfWRI1-10 gene was assumed to inhibit the biosynthetic pathways of soluble sugars and proteins, thereby diverting more carbon flux toward the lipid synthesis pathway.

PfWRI1-10 directly interacts with the promoter of PfBCCP1 gene

To determine whether PfWRI1-10 could directly regulate the downstream target gene PfBCCP1, the pGADT7-PfWRI1-10 prey vector and pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro bait vector were successfully constructed, respectively, and then yeast one-hybrid assay was conducted to investigate the interaction between PfWRI1-10 and PfBCCP1 promoter. Firstly, the pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro recombinant plasmid was transformed into the Y187 competent strain. Subsequently, co-transformation was performed using pGADT7 + pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro and pGADT7-PfWRI1-10 + pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro, and they were inoculated onto SD/-Leu/-Trp medium. The appearance of yeast colonies on the corresponding culture medium indicated successful transformation.

The co-transformed strain and positive control (pHIS2-p53 + pGADT53m) were cultured on SD/-Leu/-Trp medium supplemented with different concentrations of 3-AT, and the optimal inhibitory concentration was screened. The positive control strain grew normally at 30 mM/L of 3-AT, while the growth of pGADT7 + pHIS2-PfBCCP1pro was inhibited, confirming 30 mM/L as the optimal 3-AT concentration for this experiment. Discussion

Perilla seed oil is rich in α-linolenic acid (more than 65% of oil), which is an essential PUFA for human health. Perilla is regarded as an important resource for nutritional health oils [3, 41]. WRI1 is a transcription factor widely present in plants, playing a crucial role in various biological processes [42]. In this study, we systematically identified the members of WRI1 transcription factor family in perilla using omics tools. Particularly, we comprehensively analyzed the function of PfWRI1-10 in lipid metabolism through multiple assays including yeast one-hybrid assay and genetic transformation. This study provides a theoretical foundation and new genetic resources for the targeted improvement of high-value oilseed crops.

PfWRI1 genes exhibit the conserved structural features and tissue-specific expression patterns

WRI1 transcription factor plays a key role in regulating lipid accumulation, glycolysis, and embryonic development. It has been cloned and reported in many species such as A. thaliana, maize, oat and castor [43, 44]. WRI1 was initially identified and isolated from Arabidopsis mutants. Arabidopsis WRI1 family members comprise three different splice variants, namely At3G54320.1, At3G54320.2, and At3G54320.3. However, the transcriptome data indicated that only At3G54320.3 was expressed in seeds and other tissues. Most functional studies of WRI1 in Arabidopsis have focused on this isoform (At3G54320.3) [45]. In this study, 11 PfWRI1 genes were identified in the perilla genome, namely PfWRI1-1 to PfWRI1-11 based on their chromosomal positions. The proteins encoded by PfWRI1 genes all contained two APETALA2 domains and belong to the AP2-type transcription factor family, which can bind to specific promoter regions to regulate carbon metabolism and fatty acid biosynthesis [46]. Tang et al. performed a comparative study on the WRI1 gene sequences of 64 sequenced plant species, and found that the lack of the conserved VYL motif did not affect the WRI1 function [47]. Our study also observed the deletion of the VYL motif in PfWRI1-1, PfWRI1-2, PfWRI1-3, PfWRI1-4, PfWRI1-5, and PfWRI1-9 (Fig. 2B). The expression analysis of 11 PfWRI1 genes in different organs and seed development stages of perilla revealed that they were widely expressed in all tissues, but the expression levels were different in different tissues (Fig. 3). Among them, the expression of PfWRI1-10 in seeds was significantly higher than other PfWRI1 genes, with its expression trend consistent with the dynamic accumulation pattern of lipids in perilla seeds, confirming its crucial role in lipid biosynthesis of perilla seeds.

PfWRI1-10 overexpression promotes lipid accumulation and alters fatty acid metabolic pathway in tobacco plants

WRI1 transcription factor is an important regulatory factor that controls the lipid biosynthesis in plant [48, 49]. Previous studies have demonstrated that heterologous overexpression of WRI1 gene from Arabidopsis or other plants can increase oil content in seeds or leaves of transgenic plants. Overexpression of AtWRI1 in Arabidopsis resulted in an increase of approximately 10–20% in the total oil content of the transgenic plant seeds [50]. Overexpression of ZmWRI1 gene in maize resulted in an increase of about 31% in the seeds oil content [43]. Overexpression of GmWRI1a and GmWRI1b in both the wild-type Arabidopsis and the wri1 mutant resulted in a significant increase in the TAG content of the transgenic seeds [51]. In this study, the heterologous overexpression of PfWRI1-10 in common tobacco led to a significant increase in the total oil content of transgenic tobacco seeds (Fig. 6A). Meanwhile, the fatty acid composition analysis revealed that the C18:2 and C18:3 content significantly increased in transgenic tobacco seeds compared to WT plants, whereas the C18:1 content significantly decreased. Furthermore, the protein content and soluble sugar content in the leaves of transgenic tobacco decreased, but the starch content increased.

Many studies have shown that WRI1 regulates lipid metabolism by modulating the expression of its downstream target genes [52]. For example, the expression of genes related to glycolysis and lipid metabolism was significantly reduced in Arabidopsis WRI1-1 mutants [53]. In this study, overexpression of PfWRI1-10 in transgenic tobacco resulted in the upregulation of the key genes for glycolysis and fatty acid biosynthesis, such as NtBCCP1, NtACP1, and NtPKp-β1, whereas the NtKAS1-B was downregulated (Fig. 8). These findings suggested that overexpression of PfWRI1-10 in tobacco altered the expression of key genes involved in glycolysis and fatty acid synthesis, promoting the biosynthesis and accumulation of total oil and PUFAs (C18:2 and C18:3) in the transgenic tobacco lines.This demonstrates a shift of carbon flux from the sugar metabolism pathway to lipid metabolism pathway in the PfWRI1-10 transgenics. In the biosynthetic pathway of vegetable oils, the upregulation of ACC accelerates the initial step of fatty acid synthesis, providing sufficient substrates for subsequent carbon chain elongation. The upregulation of FATA promotes the hydrolysis of fatty acyl-ACP, releasing free fatty acids for subsequent lipid synthesis and utilization. The upregulation of DGAT and PDAT directly catalyzes the synthesis of triacylglycerols, enhancing the efficiency of lipid accumulation. WRI1, as a major transcription factor in plant lipid biosynthesis, may directly or indirectly regulate the expression of the aforementioned genes, although no experimental evidence was obtained to confirm this at present. In the future, we will conduct experiments to identify whether other key fatty acid or TAG biosynthetic genes, such as ACC, FATA, DGAT, or PDAT, are regulated by PfWRI1-10 in tobacco plants.

PfWRI1-PfBCCP1 regulatory module increases lipid content in perilla

TFs can regulate downstream gene expression by specifically binding to cis-regulatory elements or forming transcriptional complexes with other proteins. AtWRI1 could directly bind to AW-BOX in the promoter regions of many genes related to fatty acid biosynthesis pathway, thereby controlling their expression [46, 53]. In coconut (Cocos nucifera), CoWRI1 specifically bound to the promoter of BCCP2 to regulate lipid biosynthesis during fruit development [54]. HaWRI1 directly bound to AW-box cis-acting element in the BCCP2 gene promoter and activated its expression in sunflower [26]. Our study indicated that the expression of PfWRI1-10 was significantly positively correlated with several genes related to glycolysis and fatty acid metabolism (including PfKAS1, PfKAS3, PfPKp-β1, PfBCCP1, PfBCCP2, PfACP1, and PfLPAT) in the seeds of perilla during development (Fig. 4). Meanwhile, there was a motif in the PfBCCP1 promoter region that can be recognized by WRI1, an AW-box binding motif CTTTGGTTTCATCG (Figure S5), suggesting that PfBCCP1 may be positively regulated by PfWRI1-10. The current study also confirmed that PfWRI1-10 can directly bind to AW-box element in the promoter of PfBCCP1 through yeast one-hybrid assay (Fig. 9). Furthermore, the expression of PfBCCP1 in yeast resulted in an increase of total oil content, and alteration of the fatty acid component in yeast, with an increase in C16:0 and C18:1 and a decrease in C16:1. These findings indicated that PfWRI1-10 and PfBCCP1 formed a regulatory module that collaboratively mediates the oil biosynthesis and accumulation in perilla seeds. Yang et al. found that bZIP52 inhibited the expression of fatty acid biosynthesis genes by interacting with AtWRI1, leading to a decrease in oil yield [49]. Our data showed that PfBCCP was regulated by PfWRI1-10, and overexpression of PfBCCP increased oil content in yeast. All these suggest that PfWRI1-10 and PfBCCP might form a regulation module to positively regulate lipid/oil biosynthesis. In the future, this module-mediated regulation mechanism will be characterized by multiple assays so as to get a comprehensive understanding of the complex regulatory network of plant lipid metabolism.

It should be noted that lipid accumulation is also affected by lipid degradation. Certain lipid mediators can directly interact with transcription factors to co-regulate lipid metabolism. For example, lipid catabolism provides energy and substrates during seed germination and seedling growth and development. The nuclear localization protein 4 (AHL4) inhibited the expression of key genes involved in TAG hydrolysis, while the direct binding of PA (an important lipid mediator) to AHL4 could alleviate the inhibition [55]. When plants were damaged, phospholipase (PLD) responded positively to cellular damage, thereby activating its downstream gene LOX to catalyze the oxidation of unsaturated fatty acids [56]. PLD also catalyzed the degradation of glycerophospholipids, thereby damaging the oil body membrane, promoting the contact between LPS with TAG, and catalyzing its decomposition [57]. The current study only investigated the related genes involved in lipid biosynthesis. The next step will be to investigate the genes related to lipid degradation in PfWRI1-10 transgenic tobacco plants. Meanwhile, detailed research is needed to determine whether PfWRI1 interacts with other TFs to regulate the lipid metabolism in perilla seed development.

Conclusions

In this study, 11 PfWRI1 transcription factors were identified from the perilla genome. All PfWRI1-encoded proteins contain two AP2 conserved domains and belong to the AP2/EREBP transcription factor family. PfWRI1-10 gene was significantly highly expressed in the developing seeds of perilla, and its expression profile was positively correlated with the expression of key genes involved in fatty acid biosynthesis. The tobacco genetic transformation confirmed that PfWRI1-10 overexpression significantly promoted lipid accumulation and enhanced the synthesis of PUFAs by regulating the expression of lipid-related genes. Y1H assay demonstrated that PfWRI1-10 specifically bound to the AW-box cis-element in the PfBCCP1 promoter region and directly regulated its expression. The heterologous expression of PfBCCP1 in S. cerevisiae significantly improved lipid biosynthesis. This study establishes a theoretical foundation for further investigating the PfWRI1-mediated regulatory network in lipid accumulation in perilla seeds, and also provides valuable gene resources for the development of oilseed crops with high oil yield and enriched health-benefiting fatty acids.

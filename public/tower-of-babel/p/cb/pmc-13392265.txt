Pathology-prior driven substructure-aware graph neural network for whole slide image classification
Authors: Jiyang Wu, Dongxun Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate detection of sentinel lymph node metastasis in whole slide images (WSIs) is critical for breast cancer staging, yet existing graph neural network (GNN) based methods lack pathological prior features that characterize diagnostically meaningful tissue configurations, leaving the model without an effective guidance for message passing or node importance evaluation. We propose a pathology-prior driven substructure-aware graph neural network for whole slide image classification. The pathology-prior substructure encoding (PPSE) module characterizes four substructure descriptors. The substructure-aware message passing (SAMP) module uses these descriptors to guide message passing via edge-level attention weights computed from incident node substructure features, concentrating information flow on diagnostically coherent connections. The substructure-aware graph readout (SAGR) module uses substructure-enriched node representations to evaluate node importance by deviation from the slide-level tissue distribution, shifting node scoring from absolute semantic evaluation to slide-level anomalousness detection. Experiments on the SLN-Breast dataset demonstrate AUC of 0.9479±0.0133 and ACC of 0.9462±0.0188, outperforming most baselines with substantially reduced cross-fold variance. Independent validation on the TCGA-ESCA dataset further demonstrates AUC of 0.9610±0.0295 and ACC of 0.9050±0.0284, confirming the generalizability of the proposed framework across cancer types. Code is available at https://github.com/jyatgithub/Pathology-Prior-Driven-Substructure-Aware-Graph-Neural-Network-for-WSI.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most prevalent malignancy among women worldwide, and the metastatic status of the axillary sentinel lymph node (SLN) is a decisive factor in TNM staging and treatment planning1,2. Pathological diagnosis of SLN metastasis requires examining gigapixel-scale hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained whole slide images (WSIs), a process that is both time-consuming and subject to inter-observer variability1. The fundamental diagnostic challenge is that metastatic involvement manifests through complex spatial morphological patterns: tumor cells form densely packed nests with high local cellular connectivity, the tumor-stroma interface exhibits abrupt transitions in morphological composition, and metastatic foci expand from the subcapsular sinus inward, producing characteristic spatial gradients that integrate information across multiple tissue regions simultaneously. These are inherently relational and topological phenomena, and their reliable automated detection demands models that explicitly encode such structural configurations.

Early deep learning approaches framed WSI classification within the Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) paradigm, treating each WSI as a bag of independently extracted patches3,4. Attention-based MIL methods3 demonstrated that discriminative regions could be identified without pixel-level annotation, and subsequent work such as CLAM5 further improved efficiency through instance-level clustering. More recent MIL variants including CA-MIL6, RRT-MIL7, AEM-MIL8, TDA-MIL9, TransMIL10, and MICO-MIL11 have introduced context-aware aggregation, feature re-embedding, and clustering-based mechanisms to capture inter-patch dependencies. However, these methods treat patches as conditionally independent instances and are therefore fundamentally unable to model the topological tissue configurations—cluster connectivity, neighborhood heterogeneity, and spatial role within the tissue graph—that constitute the primary morphological evidence for SLN metastasis.

Graph neural network (GNN) based methods address this limitation by representing WSIs as graphs over tissue regions and modeling their spatial relationships through message passing12,13. SlideGraph+12 constructs WSI-level graphs via spatial agglomerative clustering and Delaunay triangulation, using EdgeConv message passing to capture local tissue geometry. GTP13 combines graph representations with vision transformers to capture both local topology and long-range dependencies, and KMG-Mamba14 incorporates knowledge-guided multi-scale graph representations. However, these methods remain entirely data-driven: the graph representation carries no explicit encoding of which topological configurations are diagnostically meaningful, leaving the model to discover relevant structural patterns from limited labeled data alone.

Despite these advances, existing WSI-GNN methods share a fundamental deficiency: they lack pathological prior features that characterize diagnostically meaningful tissue configurations, leaving the model without an effective guidance for message passing or node importance evaluation. At the message passing stage, without explicit characterization of diagnostically relevant substructures, the model cannot differentiate boundary nodes at the metastatic-normal tissue interface from interior nodes, causing their representations to average out the diagnostic signal. At the graph readout stage, absolute semantic scoring without reference to the global tissue distribution systematically suppresses focal metastatic regions whose topological deviation from the background is precisely the diagnostic evidence being sought.

To address these limitations, we propose a Pathology-Prior Driven Substructure-Aware Graph Neural Network for WSI classification. The key insight is that the morphological diagnostic criteria for SLN metastasis correspond directly to measurable topological substructures in the WSI graph. By first characterizing these substructures as explicit node-level descriptors, the model gains a principled basis for both guiding message passing and evaluating node importance. The proposed framework consists of three modules. The Pathology-Prior Substructure Encoding (PPSE) module characterizes four diagnostic tissue morphologies as explicit topological descriptors: neighbor semantic heterogeneity describes tissue boundary transitions where metastatic and normal cells are in immediate proximity; local clustering coefficient represents the cohesiveness of cellular neighborhoods; triangle participation characterizes the dense mutual connectivity of tumor cell nests; and degree centrality reflects the spatial role of each node within the tissue graph. The Substructure-Aware Message Passing (SAMP) module uses these substructure descriptors to guide message passing: rather than treating all edges equally, it computes edge-level attention weights from the substructure features of incident nodes, concentrating message flow along diagnostically coherent connections and suppressing signal cancellation at boundary nodes through layer-wise substructure residual connections. The Substructure-Aware Graph Readout (SAGR) module uses substructure-enriched node representations to evaluate node importance by computing each node’s deviation from the slide-level tissue distribution, so that the rare but diagnostically critical tumor nodes are weighted by how much they deviate from the global background rather than by their absolute semantic magnitude.

Related work

Multiple instance learning for WSI classification

Weakly supervised WSI classification has been predominantly approached through the Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) paradigm, where each slide is treated as a bag of independently extracted patches with only slide-level labels available3,4. Attention-based MIL3 introduced learnable aggregation weights to identify discriminative instances, and CLAM5 extended this with instance-level clustering to improve data efficiency. Subsequent works have sought to model inter-patch dependencies: TransMIL10 introduced correlated MIL via transformer self-attention, DSMIL15 proposed a dual-stream architecture with multi-scale embeddings, and DGMIL16 leverages feature distribution statistics for bag-level discrimination. More recent methods including CA-MIL6, RRT-MIL7, AEM-MIL8, TDA-MIL9, and MICO-MIL11 have further advanced context-aware aggregation and feature modeling. However, MIL-based methods fundamentally treat patches as conditionally independent instances and cannot characterize the topological tissue configurations—cluster connectivity, neighborhood heterogeneity, and spatial gradients between metastatic and normal tissue—that constitute the primary morphological evidence for SLN metastasis.

Graph neural networks for WSI analysis

GNN-based methods address the spatial relationship limitation of MIL by representing WSIs as graphs and performing message passing to learn structure-aware representations12,13. SlideGraph+12 constructs WSI-level graphs via spatial agglomerative clustering and Delaunay triangulation, capturing local tissue geometry through EdgeConv message passing. GTP13 integrates local topology with global context through graph-transformer architectures, while KMG-Mamba14 incorporates knowledge-guided multi-scale representations. However, these methods are entirely data-driven and encode no explicit pathological prior knowledge, which produces two specific deficiencies. Standard message passing aggregates neighbor features without awareness of topological substructure, so nodes at tissue boundaries receive mixed signals from morphologically heterogeneous neighbors and the diagnostic signal is averaged out rather than preserved. Attention-based pooling17 scores node importance from isolated semantic features without reference to the global slide distribution, underweighting focal metastatic regions against the dominant normal tissue. Both limitations stem from the same underlying gap: without an explicit characterization of which topological configurations are diagnostically meaningful, the model has no principled basis for either guiding message passing or evaluating node importance.

Topological substructure encoding in graph learning

Enhancing GNN expressiveness through explicit substructure encoding has been explored in general graph learning. GSN18 counts subgraph isomorphisms to enrich node representations with higher-order structural patterns, and higher-order GNNs19 operate on k-tuples of nodes to go beyond the 1-WL expressiveness bound. These works demonstrate that explicitly encoding topological substructures into node features is an effective strategy for overcoming the expressiveness limitations of standard message passing. However, they are entirely task-agnostic: substructures are selected based on graph-theoretic criteria without domain knowledge, and node importance at readout is not considered in relation to global graph context. In the WSI setting, the topological substructures that matter for diagnosis are not arbitrary graph motifs but specific configurations corresponding to established morphological criteria, and their diagnostic significance is inherently relative to the global tissue distribution of the slide rather than an absolute property of individual nodes.Fig. 1Overview of the proposed pathology-prior driven substructure-aware GNN framework for WSI classification.

Method

Overview

We propose a pathology-prior driven substructure-aware GNN framework consisting of three dedicated modules. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the Pathology-Prior Substructure Encoding (PPSE) module characterizes four diagnostic tissue morphologies as four explicit descriptors and appends them to each node’s feature vector at graph construction time, providing a shared substructure representation that guides all subsequent stages. The Substructure-Aware Message Passing (SAMP) module uses these descriptors to compute edge-level attention weights from the substructure features of incident nodes, concentrating message flow on diagnostically coherent connections. The Substructure-Aware Graph Readout (SAGR) module uses substructure-enriched node representations to evaluate node importance by deviation from the slide-level tissue distribution, producing a pathologically weighted graph-level representation for classification.

Experimental results

Datasets and experimental settings

We evaluate our method on the SLN-Breast dataset4, which contains 130 whole slide images (WSIs) of sentinel lymph node biopsies, including 36 metastasis-positive and 94 metastasis-negative cases.The SLN-Breast dataset is relatively small (130 WSIs), which may limit the statistical power of individual fold evaluations. To assess generalizability, we additionally evaluate on the TCGA-ESCA dataset, which comprises 158 esophageal carcinoma WSIs (92 squamous cell carcinoma and 66 adenocarcinoma cases) from a larger and more diverse multi-site cohort.All experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 GPU. Patch-level features are extracted using a ResNet-50 backbone pretrained on ImageNet, producing 2048-dimensional feature vectors per patch. Spatial graphs are constructed by first computing a combined spatial-semantic distance for each patch pair, with spatial decay \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _d = 3\times 10^{-3}$$\end{document} and feature decay \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _f = 1\times 10^{-3}$$\end{document}, followed by hierarchical agglomerative clustering with average linkage at distance threshold \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda _h = 0.8$$\end{document} to obtain cluster nodes. Cluster centroids are then connected via Delaunay triangulation, with edges exceeding 1500 \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu$$\end{document}m pruned to remove spurious long-range connections. We adopt a 5-fold stratified cross-validation strategy: each fold reserves 10% of the data as the test set, and the remaining 90% is further split into 90% for training and 10% for validation, yielding an overall training/validation/test ratio of 81%/9%/10%. The validation set (9%) is used exclusively for early stopping and model checkpoint selection based on validation AUC; the test set (10%) is strictly held out and used only for final performance evaluation after all model selection decisions are completed, with no test set information influencing training or model selection at any stage. To address class imbalance, stratified oversampling is applied during training. For optimization, we use the Adam optimizer trained with early stopping. Model selection is based on validation AUC, and final performance is reported using an ensemble of the last 10 checkpoint models per fold with Platt scaling calibration, averaged across all 5 folds.

Overall performance

We compare our method against a comprehensive set of baselines on both the SLN-Breast dataset4 and the TCGA-ESCA dataset23. Simple aggregation baselines include MEAN-MIL and MAX-MIL, which apply mean and max pooling over patch features without learnable aggregation. MIL-based methods include AB-MIL3 (ICML ’18), CA-MIL6 (ICLR ’24), AEM-MIL8, RRT-MIL7 (CVPR ’24), TDA-MIL9 (MICCAI ’25), TransMIL10 (NeurIPS ’21), MICO-MIL11 (MICCAI ’25), DSMIL15 (CVPR ’21), DGMIL16 (MICCAI ’22), and HIPT22 (CVPR ’22). SlideGraph+12 (MIA ’22) serves as the primary GNN-based comparison target. Quantitative results are summarized in Table 1.

Our method achieves the best performance across all metrics on both datasets. On SLN-Breast, our method obtains AUC of 0.9479±0.0133, ACC of 0.9462±0.0188, and F1 of 0.8940±0.0407, outperforming SlideGraph+12 by +2.37% in AUC and +5.44% in F1. On TCGA-ESCA, our method achieves AUC of 0.9610±0.0295 and ACC of 0.9050±0.0284, surpassing the strongest baseline TDA-MIL by +0.97% in AUC, demonstrating generalizability across cancer types. Among all baselines, AB-MIL achieves the highest AUC on SLN-Breast (0.9293) but relies solely on patch-level attention without topological modeling, and TransMIL and MICO-MIL perform notably worse on both datasets. DSMIL, DGMIL, and HIPT achieve AUC values of 0.8537, 0.7628, and 0.8594 on SLN-Breast and 0.8964, 0.8964, and 0.8894 on TCGA-ESCA respectively, all of which are outperformed by the proposed method on both datasets. The consistently low standard deviation of our method (AUC std \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$=$$\end{document} 0.0133 on SLN-Breast, 0.0295 on TCGA-ESCA) compared to all baselines further demonstrates its robustness across folds. To assess statistical significance, we conduct one-sample t-tests against dataset-specific chance-level null hypotheses (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H_0$$\end{document}: a method performs no better than random), with chance levels defined as AUC = 0.500, majority-class ACC (0.723 and 0.582), and expected random F1 (0.356 and 0.455) for SLN-Breast and TCGA-ESCA respectively. Removing SAGR while retaining SAMP and all four descriptors yields an immediate drop across all metrics (AUC −0.0135, ACC −0.0154, F1 −0.0392) accompanied by a substantial increase in cross-fold variance (AUC std +0.0203). This confirms that slide-level deviation scoring is necessary for identifying focal metastatic regions: without SAGR, node importance is evaluated from absolute semantic features alone, and the small metastatic foci are systematically underweighted against the dominant normal tissue background, leading to both degraded performance and unstable generalization across folds.

Further removing SAMP produces an additional consistent decline (AUC −0.0102, ACC −0.0077, F1 −0.0152), validating that substructure-driven edge attention is essential for preserving boundary node signals. Without topologically-guided aggregation, all edges are treated equally regardless of the substructure roles of incident nodes, causing boundary nodes at the metastatic-normal tissue interface to receive mixed messages from morphologically heterogeneous neighbors and suppressing the diagnostic signal before it reaches the readout stage.

Discussion: effect analysis of substructure

Statistical analysis

To assess whether the proposed substructure descriptors capture discriminative tissue patterns at the population level, we conduct a WSI-level distribution analysis comparing positive (metastasis, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n=36$$\end{document}) and negative (normal, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n=94$$\end{document}) cases. For each WSI, the mean value of each descriptor across all graph nodes is used as the representative statistic. As shown in Fig. 4, positive cases consistently exhibit higher values across all four descriptors, with the most pronounced separation observed in Neighbor Heterogeneity (feat2, mean \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.746 \pm 0.074$$\end{document} vs. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.703 \pm 0.075$$\end{document}) and Clustering Coefficient Inverse (feat1, mean \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.579 \pm 0.039$$\end{document} vs. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$0.572 \pm 0.043$$\end{document}), reflecting greater morphological heterogeneity and reduced local cohesion in metastatic tissue consistent with the tumor-stroma interface and invasion front. Triangle Participation (feat0) and Degree Centrality (feat3) also show consistent trends in the expected direction, with positive cases exhibiting higher median values and broader interquartile ranges. The relatively large overlap between groups for feat0 and feat3 is attributable to the WSI-mean aggregation, which dilutes spatially concentrated signals from small metastatic foci across the entire nodal graph. These descriptors are better utilized at the node level within the GNN, as confirmed by the ablation results.

Pathological analysis

The substructure descriptors employed in the proposed method are grounded in established histopathological features of sentinel lymph node (SLN) metastasis in breast cancer. Metastatic carcinoma cells arriving via afferent lymphatics preferentially colonize the subcapsular sinus, which serves as the primary entry point into the lymph node parenchyma24. Macrometastatic invasive ductal carcinoma characteristically forms dense tumor cell nests that expand inward from the subcapsular sinus, producing a distinctive spatial pattern: high local cellular connectivity, elevated packing density, and abrupt transitions between tumor and residual lymphoid tissue at the invasion front. These spatial configurations directly correspond to the substructure descriptors encoded in our framework.

As illustrated in Fig. 5, triangle participation (feat0) and degree centrality (feat3) capture regions of high local cellular connectivity and dense packing, which are hallmarks of established tumor cell nests. The center node in Fig. 5(a) participates in six triangles within its local neighborhood, with all triangle-forming nodes located within the densely packed tumor parenchyma. Triangles in the tissue graph arise when spatially proximate patches share mutual neighbors, a configuration that emerges specifically in regions of high and uniform cellular density consistent with invasive carcinoma nests24. Figure 5b shows the hub node (degree = 7) and its seven neighbors, all residing within the tumor parenchyma, where high degree centrality reflects a patch centrally located within a compact tumor mass rather than a peripheral or isolated position25.

The local clustering coefficient (feat1) quantifies the cohesiveness of local cellular neighborhoods; its inverse encodes low-clustering transitional zones where tissue architecture shifts from dense tumor parenchyma to surrounding stroma or adipose tissue, corresponding to the active tumor invasion front. The neighborhood semantic heterogeneity descriptor (feat2) is sensitive to regions where cellular composition changes abruptly, corresponding to the tumor-stroma interface pathologically characterized by desmoplastic stromal reaction–excessive deposition of cross-linked collagen matrix by cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs)–which has been shown to promote tumor invasion and metastatic behavior25. The co-occurrence of dense tumor cell nests and reactive desmoplastic stroma thus produces a compound topological signature simultaneously captured by multiple substructure descriptors. Conversely, in tumor-negative lymph nodes, the parenchyma exhibits spatially homogeneous architecture comprising follicular structures, paracortical T-cell zones, and medullary sinuses24. This uniform organization yields low triangle participation, low degree centrality, and minimal neighborhood heterogeneity throughout the tissue graph, providing a natural discriminative contrast against tumor-bearing regions and directly explaining the globally suppressed prediction scores observed in the negative case visualization.

Conclusion

We propose a pathology-prior driven substructure-aware GNN for sentinel lymph node metastasis detection in WSIs, addressing the signal cancellation problem in message passing and the lack of global context awareness in graph readout through three dedicated modules: Pathology-Prior Substructure Encoding Module encodes four topologically grounded descriptors as node-level pathological fingerprints, Substructure-Aware Message Passing Module injects these fingerprints via edge-level attention and residual connections to concentrate information flow on diagnostically coherent connections, and Substructure-Aware Graph Readout Module evaluates node importance by z-score deviation from the slide-level tissue distribution, making the readout sensitive to the relative anomalousness of focal metastatic regions. Experiments on SLN-Breast demonstrate AUC of 0.9479±0.0133 and ACC of 0.9462±0.0188, outperforming all baselines with substantially reduced cross-fold variance. Independent validation on TCGA-ESCA further yields AUC of 0.9610±0.0295 and ACC of 0.9050±0.0284, demonstrating that the proposed framework generalizes beyond the training domain to a different cancer type and tissue context. Future work will explore learnable substructure encoding to extend the framework beyond manually designed pathological priors.

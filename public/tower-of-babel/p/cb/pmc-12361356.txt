Study on fissure development law of surrounding rock in horizontal section stope of steeply dipping coal seam group
Authors: Xie Panshi, Yuan Bodi, Zhang Bo, Huang Baofa, Fang Jiarui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractUtilizing research methods such as physical simulation experiments with similar materials, numerical calculations, and theoretical analysis, the law governing the development of fissures in the horizontal section stope of steeply dipping coal seam groups has been systematically studied. The findings indicate that when interlayer fissures are undeveloped and not penetrated, the extent and quantity of surrounding rock fissures in the alternate mining of each section are influenced by the degree of section advancement and the subsequent mining. The overlying rock fissures ultimately form a stepped distribution pattern, with a higher tendency above and a lower tendency below. As the section advance reaches the sixth degree, the fissure distribution in the inclined direction of the 4# coal seam consistently exhibits a stepped shape, with a narrow upper and wide lower portion. The strike direction changes from an asymmetric stepped fissure distribution with a lower front and higher rear to a rectangular symmetrical distribution. Upon advancing to 30 m, the fissures between the 2# coal and 4# coal develop and penetrate. The height of the 2# coal fissure zone, as determined by theoretical analysis, aligns with the results of numerical simulation, with a maximum relative error of 2.38%.Keywords: Steeply dipping coal seam group, Horizontal section caving, Overburden rock movement, FissureSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

Controlling the roof1strata of the horizontal section comprehensive caving working face in steeply dipping coal seams is2 more challenging than in longwall3–6 faces. This difficulty is exacerbated when factors such as multi-coal seam mining and thick coal seams are involved, leading to complex migration laws of overburden rock in each horizontal section during the mining process. Residual coal in the goaf poses a threat to the mining operations of adjacent coal seam working faces and can potentially cause spontaneous combustion.

In recent years, scholars have focused on studying the changes in surrounding rock caving forms during the mining process of steeply dipping coal seams, as well as the development and penetration of fissures, to ensure the safety of mining operations in working faces. They have investigated the characteristics of surrounding rock and the distribution and evolution of fissure fields in different coal seam stopes using various research methods. Wu et al.7–12 analyzed the characteristics of surrounding rock structure during the mining process of steeply dipping coal seams and proposed the existence of an asymmetric key lower ladder strata in the lower coal stope. They summarized the formation and evolution characteristics of this type of stope and, through comparative analysis of field monitoring, further elucidated the law and complexity of surrounding rock movement in working face mining. Xie et al.13–18 conducted experimental studies on the roof fracture and migration of different stopes in steeply dipping coal seams and obtained the roof migration laws for long-wall stopes, multi-section stopes, and pitching oblique stopes. Huang et al.19,20 studied the overlying strata breaking and gangue filling in steeply dipping and large mining height coal seams, determining the stress path and spatial structure evolution mechanism. Yang Ke et al.21,22 investigated the mechanism of superposition and evolution of abutment pressure and the mechanism of coal wall spalling during the downward mining of steeply dipping and close distance coal seam groups, proposing control countermeasures for mine pressure and spalling in working faces. Huang et al.23–26 conducted in-depth research on shallow-buried closely-distance coal seam groups, detailing their evolution characteristics in rock formation control, fissures evolution, and multi-field coupling evolution. Feng et al.27 analyzed the evolution law of disturbed rock mass cracks during the mining process of steeply dipping coal seams using the discrete element UDEC numerical simulation calculation, and verified the reliability of the physical analog material simulation results with similar material simulation experiments. However, further research is needed on the development range and amount of fissures in the horizontal section of steeply dipping coal seams and the development and penetration of fissures between coal seam groups.

Based on existing research and using the mining of the No. 203 working face at Wangjiashan Coal Mine as a case study, this paper employs a combination of physical analog material simulation, numerical simulation, and theoretical analysis to investigate roof failure, overburden migration, and the formation, extent, and quantity of fissures in the horizontal section stope of steeply dipping coal seams. The aim is to offer theoretical insights that can inform the horizontal section fully mechanized caving mining of steeply dipping coal seam groups and provide theoretical support for the subsequent mining operations at Wangjiashan No. 203 working face.

Establishment of numerical simulation model

Based on the results of the physical similarity simulation experiment, a numerical simulation experiment model was established to study the influence of fissure propagation in the stope and clarify the law of fissure penetration in surrounding rock. The 3DEC numerical model is mainly composed of two types of blocks and structural planes. The elastic-plastic constitutive model is used for the block in this simulation, and the Mohr-Coulomb failure criterion is used for the structural plane. According to the 2# coal, and 4# coal working face roof and floor rock thickness, fissure development degree, coal seam, roof and floor rock physical and mechanical parameters determination results, the model material parameters are assigned. According to the actual situation of the working face, the strike length of the coal seam is 30 m, and 40 m is added on both sides, which can eliminate the influence of the boundary effect. Therefore, the final model size is 330 m × 110 m × 320 m, the number of blocks is 555,720, and the number of joint surfaces is 342,544. Firstly, gravity is applied in the z direction. In order to reduce the influence of boundary effect on the simulation experiment,40 m boundary coal pillars are left on both sides of the model in the direction of the model, and displacement constraints are applied on both sides of the x and y directions and the bottom of the z direction of the model. Due to the size limitation of the numerical simulation model, the unsimulated overlying strata are simulated by applying a load ( 13.5 MPa ). The numerical calculation model is shown in Fig. 6. The development of fissures in different layers and displacement are monitored. When the relative displacement between blocks is greater than 0.3 m, it is considered that fissures are formed.

In the process of mining the working face in a single section, due to the influence of coal mining, the immediate roof above the coal seam breaks first. Due to the small deformation, there are only small fissures in the immediate roof area, but the number is large. With the continuous mining of coal in the strike direction, the immediate roof changes from fracture to breaking, and the amount of fissures in the immediate roof area decreases. The main roof of the mined section and the mining section has separated layer fissures and developed intersections. The fissure development range is expanded, and the total amount of fissures is reduced. After the whole section has been mined, the immediate roof collapses completely, resulting in almost no or only tiny fissures in the immediate roof area, while the main roof develops upward from the intersecting fissures perpendicular to the roof. A stepped fissures distribution pattern with a tendency to be high above and low below is formed, and the total amount of fissures increases.

Overall limitations of the present study

The physical similar material simulation experiment is difficult to reproduce the field environment, and the range of fissure development cannot be effectively observed. Measurement. In the future, it is necessary to further optimize the scheme and experimental conditions of the physical similar simulation material experiment, and consider how to observe the effective fissure morphology and development range in the experiment.

The theoretical analysis part establishes the mechanical model of the horizontal subsection stope of the large dip angle coal seam, and obtains the large dip angle. The calculation formula of the development height of the fractured zone of the coal seam can be further optimized by considering the influence of the interaction between the layers of the coal seam group, so as to realize the height prediction of the development height of the fractured zone of the large dip angle coal seam group.

Discussion

Predecessors have conducted extensive research on the fissure development of steeply dipping coal seams through on-site monitoring, statistical analysis, numerical simulation, and other methods. Many scholars have studied the fissure development range and fissure development form in the mining process of steep coal seams group and analyzed the fissure distribution caused by the mining of steep coal seams group. However, the mining process of coal seams has not been considered. At the same time, other studies focused on the height of fissure development in near horizontal coal seams, and obtained the formula of fissure zone height. These studies mainly focus on a single factor, and there is no detailed analysis of the fissure development of the steep coal seams group.

Based on the outcomes of the physical similarity simulation experiment, the migration pattern of overlying strata in the horizontal section of a steeply dipping coal seam group can be elucidated. However, due to the inherent constraints of physical similarity simulation experiments, observing the evolution of surrounding rock fissures and internal fissures within the stope is challenging. Consequently, a numerical simulation experiment model has been established to investigate the impact of fissure expansion within the stope. The development of fissures in the surrounding rock across various sections, the fissure development and interconnectivity between sections, and the fissure development and interconnectivity between coal seams are examined and analyzed separately, thereby clarifying the penetration law of fissures in the surrounding rock.

Conclusion

In the mining of steep coal seams, the development range and quantity of surrounding rock fissures in the 2nd and 4th coal layers increase with the progression of the working face. The separation fissure develops along the inclined direction of the coal seam, while the longitudinal fissure extends upwards, perpendicular to the roof. Ultimately, these fissures form a stepped distribution pattern with a higher tendency at the top and a lower tendency at the bottom.

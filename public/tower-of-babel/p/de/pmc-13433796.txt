Comparative analysis of support vector machines, artificial neural network, random forest and gradient boosting for predictive maintenance in mining machinery and equipment: a case study of Chadormalu Iron Ore Mine
Authors: Omid Afzali Alvars, Sajjad Afraei, Majid Ataee-pour
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The detection and prediction of the condition of machinery and transportation systems in the mining industry are of critical importance due to their direct impact on productivity, maintenance cost reduction, and operational safety. Given the high costs and suboptimal performance of this sector, the present study aimed to develop an efficient framework for monitoring and predicting fleet conditions. Raw data were collected and preprocessed through cleaning, normalization, and imputation of missing values. A dataset consisting of 2,950 oil samples and 27 operational and chemical attributes collected between 2020 and 2023 was used in this study. To address the challenge of data imbalance, resampling techniques and class weighting were applied. A set of machine learning algorithms—including Gradient Boosting, Random Forest, Artificial Neural Networks, and Support Vector Machines—were implemented, and their performance was evaluated using multidimensional metrics such as overall accuracy, balanced accuracy, and F1 score and ROC-AUC under a five-fold cross-validation framework. The results indicate that, after hyperparameter optimization, the Support Vector Machine achieved the best balance between precision and recall, with an overall accuracy of 98.19% and an F1 score of 87.43%. Statistical validation results further confirmed the robustness and reliability of the proposed framework for predictive maintenance applications. Overall, the findings suggest that combining systematic data preprocessing, robust algorithms, and multidimensional evaluation metrics provides a solid foundation for intelligent decision-support systems in the mining industry, can support the development of intelligent predictive maintenance systems in mining operations enhancing prediction accuracy, reducing costs, and preventing unexpected mechanical downtimes.

Introduction

The Fourth Industrial Revolution—often referred to as Industry 4.0—is transforming manufacturing and heavy industries by bringing together cyber-physical systems, the Internet of Things (IoT), cloud computing, and artificial intelligence (AI)1. One of the most practical outcomes of this shift is Predictive Maintenance (PdM), which helps organizations anticipate equipment failures before they happen and keep operations running more efficiently2. By analyzing both historical and real-time sensor data, PdM can estimate how much useful life a component has left and recommend the best time to perform maintenance3. This is particularly valuable in mining, where machines work under harsh conditions and any unexpected failure can have serious safety, financial, and operational consequences4.

Traditional maintenance approaches—including reactive maintenance (repairing equipment after failure) and preventive maintenance (servicing at fixed intervals)—often lead to inefficiencies5. Reactive maintenance forces operators to wait until something breaks, leading to costly downtime6. Preventive maintenance avoids sudden failures but may waste resources by replacing parts too early7. PdM provides a better alternative by relying on data and machine-learning tools to make maintenance decisions based on the actual condition of equipment8. Among different condition-monitoring methods—such as vibration, sound, temperature, and oil analysis—oil analysis is especially useful9. It is non-intrusive and can reveal what is happening inside a machine through the microscopic particles carried in the circulating oil10. For example, high iron levels point to steel wear, elevated copper may indicate bearing problems, and increased silicon often signals dust contamination11. Despite its advantages, oil-based PdM in the mining sector has not been studied extensively, especially in terms of comprehensive comparisons of machine-learning algorithms using real industrial datasets collected under operational mining conditions12. Most previous studies have focused on laboratory-scale datasets, single-model implementations, or limited evaluation metrics, which restricts the reproducibility and practical applicability of their findings in large-scale mining environments. Furthermore, limited attention has been given to handling imbalanced oil-analysis datasets and statistically validating model performance in mining predictive maintenance applications.

This research aims to develop a PdM framework for mining transportation machines based on oil-analysis data. The main contributions of this study are as follows:Development of a predictive maintenance framework using real-world oil-analysis data collected from haul trucks operating in the Chadormalu Iron Ore Mine;Comparative evaluation of Gradient Boosting (GB), Random Forest (RF), Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), and Support Vector Machines (SVMs) under the same preprocessing and validation framework;Integration of imbalance-handling strategies and hyperparameter optimization to improve classification reliability; andStatistical validation of model performance using multidimensional evaluation metrics, including balanced accuracy and F1-score, to ensure robust comparative analysis. Overall, the proposed framework demonstrates the potential of machine-learning-driven predictive maintenance systems to improve maintenance planning, reduce unexpected downtime, and enhance operational reliability in mining environments.

Theory & methodology

Predictive Maintenance (PdM) lies at the heart of modern intelligent manufacturing13. Through IoT, big data, and AI, PdM transforms raw sensor data into actionable insights that minimize downtime and cost14. In 2015, Wang et al. proposed a cyber-physical PdM framework for manufacturing that integrates sensors, analytics, and cloud connectivity15. Zhao et al.16 demonstrated that IoT-based predictive systems reduced maintenance delays by 20%. While manufacturing sectors have adopted PdM extensively, the mining industry is still evolving in this domain4.

Machine learning models form the foundation of PdM analytics17. Algorithms such as Decision Trees, Random Forests, Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), and Support Vector Machines (SVMs) can extract hidden patterns from operational data2. ANNs are capable of modeling nonlinear relationships and perform well on large datasets16. However, they are computationally intensive and prone to overfitting18. SVMs, in contrast, perform effectively on smaller datasets and are more robust to noisy data due to their margin-based optimization mechanism19. Kim et al. applied deep learning for fault detection and achieved 97.9% accuracy, while Li et al. used SVMs for motor fault classification and reported 96.7% accuracy. Despite these advances, many previous studies have relied primarily on laboratory-generated datasets rather than real industrial operational environments.

Oil analysis offers a unique window into equipment health condition. In 2018, Roy et al. reviewed oil diagnostics and highlighted its potential for early wear detection. In 2023, Rashid et al. implemented an ANN to predict equipment states from oil chemistry, while Chen et al. (2021) applied SVMs to marine engines and achieved 95% accuracy. These studies confirm that oil analysis combined with AI-based models provides a strong foundation for PdM applications.

Feature selection plays a critical role in improving model accuracy and interpretability. Redundant or correlated variables may distort learning performance and increase computational cost. Hybrid feature-selection methods that combine statistical, heuristic, and evolutionary algorithms have therefore gained increasing attention. RF ranks variables using Gini impurity, Logistic Regression (LR) measures statistical contribution, and NSGA-II optimizes multi-objective trade-offs. Xu et al. demonstrated that hybrid optimization improved predictive precision by 6–8%. However, limited studies have integrated RF, LR, and NSGA-II simultaneously within predictive maintenance frameworks using real mining operational datasets.

In mining environments, only a limited number of PdM frameworks have integrated oil-analysis data, machine learning techniques, and hybrid feature-selection methods. Most existing studies either lack comparative validation among multiple algorithms or fail to evaluate model performance under real operational mining conditions. Accordingly, this study proposes a comprehensive AI-driven oil-analysis framework using real-world data collected from haul trucks operating in the Chadormalu Iron Ore Mine. The proposed framework integrates hybrid feature selection, imbalance-handling strategies, hyperparameter optimization, and statistical validation to improve predictive reliability and interpretability.

The methodology adopted in this study follows a systematic, data-driven approach for building and evaluating PdM models using machine learning techniques. The workflow integrates data preprocessing, hybrid feature selection, model development, and statistical validation. The complete structure ensures reproducibility, scalability, and interpretability, aligning with FAIR data principles.

The dataset was collected from Chadormalu Iron Ore Mine in Yazd Province, Iran, covering 2,950 oil samples collected between the years 2020 and 2023 related to haul trucks. Each record includes 27 attributes such as wear metals (Fe, Cu, Pb, Cr), contaminants (Si, Na, Al), and oil properties like viscosity and oxidation. Health conditions were categorized as Normal, Borderline, and Critical based on maintenance records shows the key oil analysis parameters, their measurement units, descriptions, and corresponding failure indicators used for condition monitoring in the study.

Prior to model development, the dataset was preprocessed through data cleaning, missing-value imputation, and normalization procedures to improve data consistency and model performance. In addition, duplicate and incomplete records were removed to reduce potential noise in the dataset.

Because the dataset exhibited class imbalance among the health-condition categories, resampling techniques and class-weighting strategies were subsequently applied during model training.

Data cleaning

Raw operational datasets collected from mining equipment frequently contain noise, missing values, and abnormal observations caused by sensor errors, inconsistent sampling conditions, or recording issues. Therefore, a data-cleaning procedure was performed prior to model development to improve data reliability and consistency. data cleaning is a fundamental preprocessing step in data mining that aims to improve the quality and reliability of a database before any modeling or analysis is performed. Real-world data—particularly in fields such as mining and engineering—often contains errors, missing values, and noise1. If these issues are not addressed, they can distort statistical relationships, reduce model accuracy, and lead to misleading conclusions7. Data cleaning ensures that the dataset is as accurate, complete, and consistent as possible2. Outlier removal is one of the most important aspects of data cleaning29. Outliers are data points that deviate significantly from the expected pattern or distribution of the dataset29. They may arise from measurement errors, sensor malfunctions, data-entry mistakes, or rare but non-representative events. In mining applications, for example, extreme values in temperature, vibration, or oil-analysis parameters can result from faulty sensors, improper sampling, or contamination during measurement. Correlation and redundancy analysis

Correlation analysis is a statistical method used to examine whether a relationship exists between two variables and to measure the strength of that relationship17. The most important metric in this context is the correlation coefficient, which not only identifies the type of relationship (direct or inverse) but also indicates its strength17. This coefficient ranges from –1 to + 1: a value close to + 1 indicates a strong, direct relationship between variables; a value close to –1 indicates a strong, inverse relationship; and a value approaching zero suggests that no noticeable linear relationship exists between the variables17. The Spearman correlation coefficient is suitable when the data are not normally distributed or when the relationship between variables is not necessarily linear29. Spearman works by ranking the values and uses their order rather than the raw data to calculate the correlation7. For this reason, it is more appropriate for ordinal data or for situations in which the relationship between variables is monotonic7 (i.e., when an increase in one variable generally leads to an increase or decrease in the other, without requiring linearity). In this study, given the non-normal distribution of the variables under investigation, the Spearman correlation coefficient was used29. Hybrid feature selection

This approach is conceptually similar to feature extraction, but with one key distinction: instead of generating new variables, it selects and utilizes only a subset of the existing feature24. The objective is to reduce the feature space to a smaller yet more effective set in which only the most informative and discriminative variables are retained26. This reduction enhances model interpretability, decreases computational complexity, and improves the accuracy of classification models. In this study, feature selection methods were employed due to the nature and dimensionality of the dataset, as well as the need for clear scientific and practical interpretability24. In oil-analysis datasets, each feature corresponds to a specific measurable quantity (such as metal concentrations in ppm or viscosity in cSt). If these features were transformed into new components using methods such as Principal Component Analysis or self-organizing algorithms, direct interpretability would be compromised because the resulting components represent mixtures of multiple features. Consequently, it would no longer be possible to determine which specific element or indicator drives the condition of the oil.

Furthermore, the dataset contains a relatively small number of key variables—on the order of several tens rather than hundreds or thousands. Under such circumstances, dimensionality reduction through feature selection is sufficient, and there is no need to create new composite components26. An additional advantage of feature selection is that it preserves the original units and scales of the data, which simplifies interpretation. In contrast, feature extraction techniques typically require very large, high-dimensional datasets such as image or genomic data.

In this research, due to the importance of preprocessing and data preparation, three feature-selection methods were applied, and the final decisions were made based on the combined results of these approaches. The following sections describe each method in detail and present their respective outputs.

Multiclass logistic regression feature importance

This approach employs a linear model in which the magnitude of each feature’s coefficient (after standardizing input scales) reflects both the strength and direction of its effect on the output35. First, all features in the dataset are standardized to ensure comparability across scales. Then, a multiclass logistic regression model is constructed using a one-vs-rest (OvR) strategy36. In this method, a separate model is trained for each class, where that class is contrasted against all other classes. During prediction, each model outputs a probability, and the sample is assigned to the class with the highest predicted probability. In this framework, a larger coefficient indicates a greater influence and therefore a higher likelihood of being selected as an important variable37.

Non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm II (NSGA-II)

This section presents a multi-objective approach for selecting a subset of features. The central idea is that, instead of optimizing a single criterion, two objectives are simultaneously pursued: maximizing classification performance while minimizing the number of selected features39. Each candidate solution is encoded as a binary vector representing the inclusion or exclusion of features. This method generates a spectrum of possible solutions, allowing the decision-maker to choose according to the specific conditions and assumptions of the problem.

Results overview and model comparison

This section presents and discusses the performance of the Gradient Boosting, Random Forest, Artificial Neural Network, and Support Vector Machine models developed for predictive maintenance of mining equipment, using oil analysis data from the Chadormalu Iron Ore Mine. All models were trained and evaluated under identical preprocessing, feature selection, and validation protocols to ensure a fair and unbiased comparison.

The dataset comprised 2,881 oil analysis records, which were split into 70% for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for testing. The final feature set consisted of eight variables—Fe, Cu, Pb, Si, Na, Cr, Al, and Viscosity—selected through a hybrid approach combining Random Forest, Logistic Regression, and NSGA-II methods. Each record was classified into one of three machine health categories: (1) Normal (Healthy), representing stable operational performance with minimal wear; (2) Borderline (Warning), indicating early signs of degradation; and (3) Critical (Failure Imminent), signaling an urgent need for maintenance intervention.

The models were assessed using standard performance metrics, including Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, and Balanced Accuracy, with macro-averaging applied to mitigate potential effects of class imbalance. A stratified five-fold cross-validation procedure was applied to the training dataset during hyperparameter optimization and statistical evaluation. The final optimized models were subsequently assessed using an independent hold-out test set comprising 15% of the data. This approach ensured robust performance estimation while preventing information leakage between training and testing stages. Table 6 summarizes the overall performance results for both models.

Finally, the recall is 76.82%, showing that the model is able to correctly identify a large proportion of the true samples in each class. This metric is especially critical for minority classes, and its improvement relative to the initial state indicates an enhancement in the model’s sensitivity.

After hyperparameter optimization using the random search algorithm and the application of synthetic oversampling techniques to balance minority classes, the Random Forest model was able to deliver a notable yet well-balanced performance across different classes. Evaluation results on the test dataset indicate that the model achieved an overall accuracy of approximately 98%, reflecting its strong capability in correctly predicting the data. Nevertheless, given the imbalanced nature of the dataset, accuracy alone was not considered sufficient, and complementary evaluation metrics were therefore employed. Accordingly, a balanced accuracy of 83% and a mean F1 score of 80.30% were obtained, indicating an improved balance in performance between majority and minority classes. A class-wise analysis of the results showed that Class 1, which had the highest frequency, was almost perfectly predicted with an F1 score of approximately 99%. Class 2 was identified with a recall of 95% and an F1 score of about 82%. Finally, Class 3, which contained the smallest number of samples, achieved an F1 score of around 63%. Overall, it can be concluded that the proposed model is not only efficient in terms of overall accuracy, but has also reached a desirable level in mitigating prediction bias across different classes. This characteristic represents a significant advantage, particularly in applications where the accurate identification of minority classes is of critical importance.

Also, the Neural Network algorithm exhibited high performance while maintaining a balanced behavior across classes, thereby reducing prediction bias. Evaluation on the test dataset—comprising samples unseen during training—indicates that the model achieved an overall accuracy of approximately 98.31%. An examination of complementary evaluation metrics further reveals a balanced accuracy of about 87.03% and an F1 score of 84.57%, values that confirm the model’s robust performance across different classes. Overall, the results indicate that the proposed model not only possesses strong predictive capability but also effectively mitigates bias toward the majority class. The Support Vector Machine algorithm demonstrated strong yet well-balanced performance across classes, effectively preventing bias toward the majority class. Final evaluation on the test data showed that the model achieved an overall accuracy of approximately 98.19%. Discussion

In the era of Industry 4.0, strong emphasis is placed on predictive maintenance. Through the collecting, processing, and analysis of data, and by using advanced algorithms, it is possible to develop models with minimal error and high generalization capability that can effectively predict and diagnose the condition of mining equipment and machinery. The advantages of predictive maintenance models include reduced operational downtime due to failures, decreased unnecessary consumption of spare parts, improved reliability, continuous monitoring of fleet and equipment health, and ultimately, lower maintenance and repair costs.

Focusing on the application of modern data mining techniques and machine learning algorithms for analyzing and predicting the condition of industrial data, this study demonstrates that designing a comprehensive framework—encompassing data preprocessing, feature selection, and algorithm optimization—can significantly enhance predictive accuracy and overall performance. Owing to their operational nature and the presence of imbalanced class labels, the datasets posed substantial challenges during the modeling process. To address these issues, resampling techniques and class weight adjustments were employed to improve model robustness against class imbalance.

The results demonstrate that predictive maintenance performance is strongly influenced not only by model selection but also by the quality of preprocessing, feature engineering, and imbalance-handling strategies. Among the evaluated approaches, the SVM model achieved the most stable and generalized classification performance, particularly under overlapping and imbalanced class conditions commonly observed in mining operational data. The dominance of Fe, Cu, and Si in both Random Forest and SHAP analyses is consistent with known tribological and metallurgical mechanisms in mining equipment. Fe and Cu primarily reflect mechanical wear of steel and bearing components, whereas Si indicates environmental contamination caused by dust intrusion. The consistency of these findings across multiple interpretability techniques increases confidence in the physical relevance of the proposed predictive framework.

The study further highlights the importance of using balanced evaluation metrics in predictive maintenance applications involving imbalanced datasets. Although several models achieved high overall accuracy, balanced accuracy and macro F1-score revealed substantial differences in minority-class detection capability. This observation confirms that reliance on accuracy alone may lead to overestimation of model effectiveness in industrial fault diagnosis problems.

From an operational perspective, the proposed framework demonstrates the feasibility of integrating machine learning–based oil analysis into industrial predictive maintenance systems. The relatively low inference complexity of the SVM and RF models makes them suitable for edge-assisted monitoring applications in remote mining environments, where rapid fault detection and limited computational resources are critical considerations (Fig. 3).

Conclusion and future work

The present study was conducted with the aim of designing and developing an efficient model for diagnosing and predicting the condition of a mining transportation system, based on the analysis of qualitative oil data from mining machinery and the application of machine learning algorithms. Given the nature of real-world data—characterized by noise, missing values, and imbalanced class labels—the initial stage of the research focused on data preprocessing. Outlier removal, key feature selection, and the application of resampling techniques and class weighting provided an appropriate foundation for model training. Subsequently, four widely used and well-established machine learning algorithms—Gradient Boosting, Random Forest, Artificial Neural Networks, and Support Vector Machines—were independently implemented, optimized, and evaluated.

The findings of this study clearly demonstrate that relying solely on overall accuracy is insufficient for evaluating model performance under imbalanced data conditions and may lead to misleading interpretations. In such settings, multidimensional evaluation metrics, such as the F1 score and balanced accuracy, become increasingly important, as they offer a more comprehensive and precise assessment of the models’ ability to correctly identify different classes, particularly minority classes.

In the comparison among the algorithms, the SVM demonstrated the highest performance among the evaluated methods, achieving a balanced accuracy of 88.47% and an F1 score of 87.43%. This algorithm exhibited particularly stable performance in identifying borderline and critical classes, thereby securing first place. ANNs, with a balanced accuracy of 87.03% and an F1 score of 84.57%, were also able to effectively capture complex relationships within the data and deliver balanced performance across classes; however, their computational cost and the need for careful tuning of the network architecture are considered notable limitations. The RF algorithm ranked third, attaining a balanced accuracy of 83.25% and an F1 score of 81.35%. Due to its strong capability in feature importance ranking, it represents a valuable option in applications where interpretability is required in addition to accuracy. Finally, GB, despite achieving an overall accuracy of 97.60%, exhibited the weakest performance among the four algorithms, with an F1 score of 80.62%, and ranked fourth due to a marked decline in recall for minority classes.

Overall, the results of this study indicate that in problems involving imbalanced data, multidimensional evaluation metrics should serve as the primary basis for assessing model performance. The comparative analysis further demonstrates that SVM and ANN achieve the best balance across classes, while RF attains an acceptable position due to its stability and interpretability. In contrast, GB, despite its high overall accuracy, cannot be considered a superior option under imbalanced data conditions.

Potential directions for future research include multi-sensor data fusion incorporating vibration, thermal, and acoustic signals; temporal modeling with deep learning architectures such as LSTM networks and Transformers; model compression techniques such as pruning and quantization to enable efficient edge deployment; federated learning for distributed predictive maintenance; cross-site transfer learning for broader generalization; and integration with production scheduling to optimize economic outcomes.

AI-enabled predictive maintenance represents a paradigm shift in mining operations, moving from reactive repair practices to proactive, intelligent management. Such approaches enable safer, more efficient, and sustainable mining operations, aligning technological innovation with environmental and economic objectives.

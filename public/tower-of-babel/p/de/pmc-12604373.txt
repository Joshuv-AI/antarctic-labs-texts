Effect of continuous cuff pressure regulator on postoperative sore throat in patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy with H-LMA: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Renhong He, Chaomin Wu, Haiyan Lan, Xuehao Han, Jimin Wu, Jianxin Jiang, Gongchen Duan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe aim of this study was to investigate the effect of continuous regulation of laryngeal mask cuff pressure on postoperative sore throat (POST) after laparoscopic cholecystectomy.MethodsPatients underwent laparoscopic cholecystectomy were randomly divided into group E or group C in a 1:1 ratio. Laryngeal mask was inserted after successful induction of general anaesthesia. The product of laryngeal mask used in this study was Hope laryngeal mask airway (H-LMA). Group E: the continuous cuff pressure regulator was used to continuously control the cuff pressure of the laryngeal mask at 40 cmH2O. Group C: without the continuous cuff pressure regulator. The primary outcome was the incidence of POST within 24 h of extubation. Secondary outcomes included other postoperative pharyngolaryngeal adverse events 30 min and 24 h after extubation.ResultsA total of 100 adult patients completed the study. The incidence of POST 30 min and within 24 h of extubation was lower in group E than in group C (12% vs. 36%; P = 0.009) (10% vs. 32%; P = 0.013). The severity of POST 30 min and within 24 h after extubation was also lower in group E than in group C (all P<0.05). The incidence of dysphagia 30 min and within 24 h after extubation was lower in group E than in group C (all P<0.05).ConclusionsIn conclusion, the incidence of POST can be reduced by continuously controlling the laryngeal mask cuff pressure at 40 cmH2O with the continuous cuff pressure regulator.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry identifier: ChiCTR2400093522. (Principal investigator: Gongchen Duan; registration date: 6 December 2024).Keywords: Laryngeal mask, Sore throat, Laparoscopic cholecystectomy, Randomized controlled trial

Introduction

As a supraglottic airway management tool, laryngeal mask is becoming increasingly widely used with the deepening of people’s understanding and rich experience in its use. However, the current application of laryngeal mask is not a perfect airway management technique, and some patients may experience dysphonia, sore throat, and dysphagia after its use, which has attracted the attention of clinical doctors [1]. Researchers have shown that low LMA cuff pressure can effectively reduce the incidence of postoperative pharyngeal complications [2, 3]. When the cuff pressure is too low to result in air leakage and ineffective ventilation, which can increase the risk of hypoxemia, gastrointestinal bloating and reflux aspiration [4]. Laparoscopic surgery can increase intra-abdominal pressure, resulting in increased airway pressure, which has never caused changes in cuff pressure. Ultimately, inappropriate cuff pressure will cause sore throat and ineffective ventilation. In practice, clinicians often rely on instinct when injecting air into laryngeal mask cuff, and estimated cuff pressure by pressing the cuff with fingers. The study have shown that more than 50% of patients received inappropriate cuff pressure only through personal experience and touch [5]. Generally, laryngeal mask cuff pressure higher than 60 cmH2O may cause severe ischemia of pharyngeal mucosa, resulting in postoperative sore throat and dysphonia [6]. A study had shown that when the cuff pressure of LMA in children is less than 40 cmH2O, there is no sore throat, while if the cuff pressure is between 40 and 60 cmH2O, the incidence of sore throat is only 4.6% [7]. Therefore, the appropriate and constant cuff pressure of laryngeal mask is beneficial to patients. We should try to control the appropriate cuff pressure of laryngeal mask by using the continuous cuff pressure regulator to reduce the occurrence of throat complications. Although the laryngeal mask is not the first choice for laparoscopic procedures, the development of medical technology and the growing experience of anaesthetists means that the advantages of using an laryngeal mask in laparoscopic procedures are becoming increasingly apparent. Some second-generation laryngeal masks have shown their unique advantages. A study showed that the LMA Protector improves intraoperative haemodynamic stability and reduces postoperative discomfort compared with endotracheal intubation in laparoscopic cholecystectomy [8]. And the LMA Supreme® has been proven suitable for laparoscopic cholecystectomy [9]. The Hope laryngeal mask airway (H-LMA) is produced in China with a structure similar to that of the LMA Supreme®. The H-LMA was designed with a large-volume conduit for gastric access and a fixed curved structure to facilitate insertion. This structure reduces the risk of throat injury and gastric distension.

Therefore, we designed a prospective, randomized controlled trial to explore the effect of continuous regulation of laryngeal mask cuff pressure on postoperative sore throat after laparoscopic surgery.

Methods

Study design

This single-centre, double-blind, randomised clinical trial was approved by the Clinical Trials Ethics Committee of Lishui People’s Hospital (approval no. 2024-027-01) by the Chairperson, Professor Zhichao Shi, on 29 November 2024. The present trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The present study was conducted in accordance with the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) reporting guideline. Informed consent was obtained from all enrolled patients in written form.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The present study involved 100 adult patients who were admitted to Lishui People’s Hospital for laparoscopic surgery from December 2024 to March 2025.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) elective laparoscopic surgery; (2) patients aged between 18 and 65 years; (3) ASA physical status of Ⅰ or II; (4) can understand the procedures and methods of this study, willing to sign an informed consent form and strictly follow the clinical research protocol to complete this study. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) weight <30 kg or BMI ≥ 35 kg/m2; (2) surgery for a duration exceeding 3 h; (3) history of difficult airway or gastroesophageal reflux; (4) history of respiratory infections within the past month; (5) preoperative sore throat, hoarseness or other pharyngeal diseases; (6) patients with preoperative neurological or psychiatric disorders or communication disorders (Medical records and diagnostic records); (7) participants who have participated in any clinical trials as subjects within the first month of enrollment.

Randomization and masking

Patients were randomly assigned to either Group E or Group C, with 50 patients allocated to each group, by computer-generated random numbers. The researchers responsible for postoperative follow-up and data processing were unaware of the group allocation during the entire study period. All patients were also blinded to the group allocation.

Interventions

Prior to undergoing surgery, patients were required to fast for a period of eight hours, and no premedication was administered. Upon arrival in the operating theatre, the following observations are to be made: non-invasive blood pressure, electrocardiogram, pulse oxygen saturation and respiratory rate are to be monitored using a Carestation 620 A2 monitor (GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA). The depth of general anaesthesia was continuously monitored in patients by means of the bispectral index (BIS) sensor (Canwell Medical Co., Ltd., Jinhua, Zhejiang, China). Prior to the induction of general anaesthesia, all patients undergo a 3-minute inhalation of pure oxygen at a flow rate of 5 L/min via a mask. The injection sequence of drugs for ggeneral anesthesia induction was as follows: 0.3–0.5 µg/kg sufentanil (Yichang Humanwell Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Yichang, China), 2–2.5.5 mg/kg propofol (Beijing Fresenius Kabi Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Beijing, China), and 0.15 mg/kg cis-atracurium (Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China). After the patient’s BIS value is less than 60 and jaw muscles relax, the laryngeal mask was inserted. The product of laryngeal mask used in this study was Hope laryngeal mask airway (H-LMA) (Haopu Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China). The H-LMA is a supraglottic airway device with aninflatable cuff and two lumens. According to the manufacturer’s instructions, we selected a size 3 H-LMA for patients weighing 30 to 50 kg, a size 4 H-LMA for patients weighing 50 to 70 kg, and a size 5 H-LMA for patients weighing over 70 to 100 kg.

Before inserting the laryngeal mask, all the laryngeal mask were lubricated with water-soluble lubricant. No oropharyngeal airway to assist mechanical ventilation and no local anesthesia for the pharynx were received for all patients. The insertion of laryngeal mask is performed by a anesthesiologist with over three years of experience in using laryngeal mask, following the laryngeal mask operating guidelines and standard procedures. The H-LMA cuff was fully deflated and held like a pen and inserted while pressing up along the palato-pharyngeal curve using the index finger. The H-LMA was advanced into the hypopharynx until definite resistance was felt. Group E: the continuous cuff pressure regulator (Zhejiang Sungood Technology Co., Ltd., Zhejiang) was used to continuously control the laryngeal mask cuff pressure at 40 cmH2O. Group C: Once the laryngeal mask has been successfully placed, use a syringe to inflate the cuff. The aim was to achieve positive pressure ventilation with an airway pressure of around 20 cmH2O to ensure there is no air leakage and good chest movement. Inflate the airbag with a volume of air that does not exceed the maximum recommended volume in instruction manual(20 mL for size 3 and 30 mL for size 4). This procedure did not use a continuous cuff pressure regulator. Then the patients were ventilated with Volume-controlled mechanical mode. The square-wave tracing on the capnography and the elevation of the chest confirmed the correct position of the laryngeal mask. If insertion twice was unsuccessful, it will be excluded from the experiment. Anesthesia was maintained with remifentanil (Yichang Humanwell Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Yichang, China) and propofol. When we confirmed the return of neuromuscular function by using a train-of-four monitor, neuromuscular blocking was reversed with glycopyrrolate 0.004 mg/kg and 0.02 mg/kg neostigmine. Then remove the laryngeal mask without pharyngeal suctioning. Postoperative pain: intravenous 50 mg flurbiprofen when the VAS (Visual Analogue Scale) ≥ 4.

Outcome measurements

Primary outcome

The primary outcome was the incidence of POST within 24 h after extubation (a single assessment at 24 h). POST was defined: constant pain or discomfort in the throat independent of swallowing [4]. The POST diagnosis was based on the results of follow-up visits with patients at specified time points. The severity of POST was was graded on pain scores: 0 = denoting no sore throat, 1 = for mild sore throat (milder than a cold), 2 = for moder ate sore throat (comparable to a cold), 3 = indicating severe sore throat (more intense than a cold) [10].

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcomes were as follows.

The incidence of dysphonia, dysphagia 30 min and within 24 h after extubation; the incidence of POST 30 min after extubation; the severity of POST 30 min and within 24 h after extubation. Dysphonia was defined: difficulty speaking or pain on speaking. Dysphagia was defined: difficulty or pain provoked by swallowing [4].

The incidence of dysphonia, dysphagia 30 min and within 24 h after extubation; the incidence of POST 30 min after extubation; the severity of POST 30 min and within 24 h after extubation. Dysphonia was defined: difficulty speaking or pain on speaking. Dysphagia was defined: difficulty or pain provoked by swallowing [4].

Anaesthesia and surgery characteristics included intraoperative sufentanil dosage, Intraoperative remifentanil dosage, duration of surgery, length of PACU stay, H-LMA size, H-LMA insertion attempts, H-LMA blood-stain.

Statistical analysis

The data processing and analyses were performed using SPSS 20.0 statistical software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation, number (percentage), or median (Q1, Q3), as appropriate. The normality of distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. The data were found to be normally distributed, thus an independent-samples t-test was employed to compare the means of the two groups. The Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare non-normally distributed data. The chi-square test, also known as Fisher’s exact test, was used to compare the enumeration data between the groups. The significance level for this analysis was set at α = 0.05.

As indicated by the findings of preceding studies, the occurrence of POST in instances of elevated laryngeal mask cuff pressure has been documented at a rate of 42% [2]. Seet and colleagues demonstrated a reduction in occurrence of POST from 45% to 13% with use of LMA manometry [11]. The hypothesis that the implementation of a continuous cuff pressure regulator to maintain stable low cuff pressure can result in a 15% reduction in the incidence of POST is supported by the evidence. Therefore, a sample size of 80 patients was required to provide a power of 80% and a significance level of 0.05. In view of the projected attrition rate of 20%, it was determined that a sample size of 100 patients would be adequate for the study, with 50 patients allocated to each group.

Discussion

This randomized controlled trial demonstrated that continuous regulation of H-LMA cuff pressure at 40 cmH2O using a cuff pressure regulator significantly reduced the incidence and severity of postoperative sore throat (POST) and dysphagia in patients underwent laparoscopic cholecystectomy, compared to conventional manual adjustment based on clinical experience. But it would not significantly reduce the incidence of dysponia.

The laryngeal mask is a simple and comfortable airway management device. However, POST has always been a common side effect of laryngeal mask, affecting the postoperative comfort of patients [12]. The main cause of POST caused by laryngeal mask is the cuff pressure. Secondly, the difficulty of insertion, the number of insertion attempts, the size of the laryngeal mask, the duration of surgery, the type of ventilation and the use of analgesics were associated [13]. Excessive cuff pressure will place inappropriate pressure on the pharyngeal mucosa, which can lead to complications such as sore throat, dysphagia and even nerve damage. However, if the cuff pressure is inadequate, this can lead to laryngeal mask air leak and even ineffective ventilation. Appropriate cuff pressure is therefore a very important anaesthetic technique. Laryngeal mask manufacturers usually recommend users to control the cuff pressure below 60cmH2O (44 mmHg) [14]. In addition, according to the results of previous classic literature, when Blood vessel caliber was first reduced when the mean mucosal pressure was 34 cm H2O. There was a progressive incremental reduction in blood vessel caliber and mucosal color when the mean mucosal pressure increased from 34 to 80 cm H2O [15].

A study showed that continuously monitoring and maintaining a endotracheal tube cuff pressure of 18 mmHg effectively reduced the incidence of postoperative morbidity in patients undergoing laparoscopic gynaecological surgery [16]. This is similar to our results, again showing that control of appropriate cuff pressure can reduce postoperative throat injury, whether it is endotracheal tube or laryngeal mask. Chantzara G et al. showed that measurement and control of LMA cuff pressure to a maximum of 60 cmH2O every 10 min can significantly reduce the incidence of POST compared with no intervention in cuff pressure [6]. This further demonstrates that controlling laryngeal mask cuff pressure can indeed reduce throat injury, and our research takes a more scientific and effective approach. Sharma M et al. showed that cuff pressure was not associated with the incidence of postoperative sore throat. This may be due to the low initial cuff pressure (40 cmH2O) [17]. Wong JG et al. showed that in children with LMA cuff pressure less than 40 cmH2O, there was no postoperative sore throat. When the cuff pressure was between 40 and 60 cmH2O, the incidence of postoperative sore throat was 4.6% [7]. This is similar to the cuff pressure set in our study, suggesting that an laryngeal mask cuff pressure of 40 cmH2O is a relatively appropriate pressure in adult laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Deepak GP et al. showed that limiting the cuff pressure in Ambu AuraGain to 25 cmH2O, as opposed to 60 cmH2O, does not affect oropharyngeal leak pressure but has the potential to reduce the incidence of dysphagia [3]. This is much lower than the cuff pressure set in our study, which may be due to the different types of supraglottic airway devices used or the different ways of controlling the balloon pressure. In contrast, the cuff pressure control method used in our study was more scientific. After 10, 15 and 30 min of inflation, the cuff pressure of ProSeal LMA™ laryngeal mask airway increases significantly from the initial pressure [6]. Especially after the pneumoperitoneum of laparoscopic surgery was established. By stabilising the cuff pressure at 40 cmH2O, the continuous regulator probably reduced cuff pressure fluctuations. The use of continuous cuff pressure regulators offers a practical solution to standardise laryngeal mask management, particularly in laparoscopic surgery where intraoperative pressure dynamics make manual adjustments difficult. By ensuring stable cuff pressure, it can reduce postoperative throat complications.

There are several limitations to this study. First, the single centre design and modest sample size (n = 100) may limit generalisability. Second, the short follow-up period (24 h) precludes assessment of long-term pharyngeal complications. Third, This study did not record details of laryngeal mask airway placement, such as ease of insertion and incidence of aspiration. Fourth, in this study, we did not evaluate cuff leak pressure or other cuff pressures (e.g. 35–45 cm H₂O). Furthermore, this study cannot be generalizability to other supraglottic ventilation devices, e.g. i-gel and Baska. Future research should validate these findings in multicentre trials with larger, more diverse populations, including obese patients and those with comorbidities.

Conclusions

In conclusion, continuous regulation of laryngeal mask cuff pressure at 40 cmH2O significantly reduces postoperative pharyngolaryngeal complications in laparoscopic cholecystectomy patients. This approach standardises airway management, reduces clinician variability and improves patient outcomes, arguing for its integration into routine anaesthetic practice.

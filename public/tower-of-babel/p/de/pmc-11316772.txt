Energy efficient clustering and routing protocol based on quantum particle swarm optimization and fuzzy logic for wireless sensor networks
Authors: Huangshui Hu, Xinji Fan, Chuhang Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Clustering and routing protocols play a pivotal role in reducing energy consumption and extending the lifespan of wireless sensor networks. However, optimizing energy efficiency to maximize network longevity remains a primary challenge for these protocols. This paper introduces QPSOFL, a clustering and routing protocol that integrates quantum particle swarm optimization and a fuzzy logic system to enhance energy efficiency and prolong network lifespan. QPSOFL employs an enhanced quantum particle swarm optimization algorithm to select optimal cluster heads, utilizing Sobol sequences for population diversification during initialization. Additionally, it incorporates Lévy flight and Gaussian perturbation-based position updates to prevent trapping in local optima. Benchmark experiments validate QPSOFL's efficacy compared to Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO), Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Quantum Particle Swarm Optimization (QPSO), focusing on accuracy, search capability, and convergence speed. Within QPSOFL, a fuzzy logic system determines the best next-hop cluster head based on descriptors such as residual energy, energy deviation, and relay distance. Extensive simulations compare QPSOFL's performance in terms of network lifetime, throughput, energy consumption, and scalability against existing protocols E-FUCA, IHHO-F, F-GWO, and FLPSOC, demonstrating its superior performance over these counterparts.

Introduction

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) serve as a fundamental data acquisition technology for the Internet of Things (IoT), finding extensive applications in environment monitoring1, target tracking2, intelligent transportation3, healthcare4, among others. In WSNs, numerous sensor nodes collaborate to fulfill specific tasks, constrained by limited energy, computation, storage, and communication capabilities. Typically, a Base Station (BS), which remains unconstrained in resources, manages command issuance, data collection, and processing. Energy efficiency emerges as critical for extending network lifetime due to challenges like hostile environments, irreplaceable or non-rechargeable energy sources, and dynamic topology. Clustering and routing protocols have emerged as effective solutions5. These protocols organize nodes into clusters, each comprising a Cluster Head (CH) and Cluster Members (CMs). CHs, distinct from regular CMs, undertake responsibilities such as data reception, aggregation, and transfer, in addition to information sensing and transmission. Consequently, CHs are prone to premature depletion due to rapid energy consumption. Optimal CH selection, along with CH rotation or burden sharing via backup CHs, represents common strategies to mitigate this issue6,7. Once CHs are selected, ordinary nodes establish communication with CHs to form clusters. Furthermore, intra-cluster data transmission is managed using a Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA) mechanism to conserve energy by avoiding interference and collisions. Subsequently, a route determination method identifies the optimal next-hop CH for each CH, ensuring reliable data transmission from source nodes to the BS. Since the inception of the pioneering clustering and routing protocol Low Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH), numerous enhanced protocols have been proposed from diverse perspectives, achieving notable performance improvements8.

Random selection of Cluster Heads (CHs) based on probability, disregard for residual energy during CH selection, and single-hop data transmission to the BS are the primary drawbacks of LEACH8,9. Consequently, weight-based protocols considering multiple parameters and enabling multi-hop communication have been proposed to address these issues8,10,11. However, clustering and routing remain typical NP-hard problems12, challenging traditional methods to find optimal solutions. Therefore, intelligent computing approaches such as Genetic Algorithm (GA)12, Grey Wolf Optimization13, Fire Hawk Optimizer (FHO)14, among others5,8, have been introduced to achieve optimal or approximate solutions for clustering and routing optimization5. Despite their demonstrated performance, these methods struggle with uncertainties and interferences inherent in clustering and routing processes. Hence, fuzzy logic is increasingly employed to mitigate these challenges, leveraging expert knowledge bases to enhance decision-making capabilities under uncertain and chaotic environments. Fuzzy logic has found wide application in WSNs for clustering15, routing 16, and their integration 17,18. Moreover, fuzzy logic is synergistically combined with other technologies such as ant colony algorithms19, butterfly optimization algorithms6, tunicate swarm algorithms 20, and notably, particle swarm optimization 21, effectively integrating their strengths to enhance overall network performance. Particle swarm optimization, a popular nature-inspired heuristic, is extensively used for solving single and multi-objective optimization problems in domains like feature selection, wireless communications, and image processing 22.

Particle Swarm Optimization is inspired by observing the social behavior of birds flocking and fish schooling, where particles represent candidate solutions searching the solution space across given dimensions to find the optimal solution. PSO is often preferred over other nature-inspired methods for solving NP-hard problems due to its ease of implementation, rapid convergence, and minimal need for parameter adjustments23. Consequently, PSO finds application in various tasks within WSNs such as target tracking2, sink mobility24, clustering23, routing21, and combined clustering and routing25. Despite its ability to balance exploration and exploitation for good network performance, PSO has notable weaknesses including premature convergence, challenges in velocity updating, and parameter tuning difficulties. To enhance optimization performance, several new PSO variants have been developed, with Quantum Particle Swarm Optimization emerging as one of the most effective. QPSO exhibits greater exploratory tendencies to avoid local optima, features a simpler update mechanism adaptable to diverse problems, and demonstrates superior convergence behavior leading to highly optimal solutions. Extensive testing confirms QPSO's superiority over classical PSO, its variants, and other intelligent computation algorithms26, making it widely applicable in suspension optimization27, multi-task allocation28, and other domains. However, QPSO's efficiency in achieving global optimum solutions is hindered by its low diversity and limited search capabilities. Consequently, numerous improvements to QPSO aim to strike a balance between local and global search capabilities while mitigating premature convergence issues29. Despite its strengths, QPSO has yet to be extensively applied to clustering and routing protocols in WSNs. Hybridizing PSO with other methods presents a promising research direction for enhancing clustering and routing, such as integrating with fuzzy logic21, genetic algorithms30, or lion swarm optimization31. This approach aims to leverage the strengths of different algorithms, combining them to overcome individual drawbacks and achieve superior results in solving complex problems.

In this paper, a novel clustering and routing protocol hybridizing quantum particle swarm optimization with fuzzy logic called QPSOFL is proposed to improve the network energy efficiency and enhance the network lifetime. In QPSOFL, QPSO is used to select the optimal CHs for clusters formation, and fuzzy logic system is applied to find the best relay CHs for data transmission. The contributions of the proposed protocol are summarized as follows:Energy-efficient clusters are determined using a quantum particle swarm optimization algorithm, employing Sobol sequences, Lévy flights, and Gaussian perturbation to broaden the search space, enhance convergence speed, and mitigate local optima trapping;Energy-efficient and balanced relay CHs are achieved through a Mamdani fuzzy inference system. Parameters such as residual energy, energy deviation, and relay distance are integrated to guide decision-making based on fuzzy rules derived from expert experience;Function test-bench and simulations are conducted to assess the performance of the enhanced QPSO algorithm and QPSOFL protocol respectively, compared to existing approaches. The results confirm the effectiveness of the proposed protocol.

Related works

Clustering and routing protocols represent a significant focus of current research in wireless sensor networks, aiming to improve network efficiency, extend sensor node lifespan, and optimize data transmission. This section categorizes recent academic research into three distinct segments, highlighting advancements across various domains. Firstly, we review clustering and routing approaches employing fuzzy logic. Secondly, we explore the application of intelligent computing techniques, specifically Particle Swarm Optimization algorithms, to enhance the efficiency of clustering and routing operations. Lastly, we examine approaches that integrate fuzzy logic with intelligent algorithms, effectively addressing inherent challenges in WSNs.

Traditional clustering and routing methods

The LEACH32 protocol is a classical clustering and routing protocol designed for wireless sensor networks with the primary goal of optimizing network resource utilization. In its operation, the sensor nodes within the network undergo an initial clustering process, with each cluster autonomously selecting a CH node. These elected CH nodes play a pivotal role in centralized data aggregation and transmission, effectively mitigating energy consumption and thereby extending the overall network lifespan. However, the reliance on a random process for selecting CH nodes introduces a potential concern where specific nodes may frequently serve as CHs, resulting in an uneven distribution of energy consumption, which exerts a detrimental impact on the overall network lifetime. Therefore, a fuzzy logic-based clustering protocol is proposed in33. Unlike LEACH, a fuzzy inference system is used in the formation of clusters to calculate the chance value of all nodes to be a CH based on the residual energy of the node, the distance to the BS, and the number of neighboring nodes. CHs selected based on chance value clearly perform better than randomly selected CHs, as verified in simulation. Nonetheless, the single-hop clustering protocol is insufficient for data transmission over a larger network area solely by selecting the CH nodes. A multi-directional routing protocol incorporating fuzzy clustering is proposed in34. Initially, the CH node is selected based on the residual energy of the node and the distance from the BS, combined with a fuzzy logic system. Then, multiple paths that may exist between the CH node and the BS are identified, and the path with the fewest number of hops is selected as the routing path for data transmission, proven effective in extending the network lifetime through simulation results. The above protocols may lead to premature death of CH nodes close to the BS due to unequal energy consumption. Therefore, two fuzzy inequality clustering and routing protocols, FUCA and E-FUCA, are proposed in35 and36 respectively, where E-FUCA36 is an enhancement of FUCA35. The proposed unequal clustering protocol FUCA aims to improve the survival time of the entire network. Unlike other fuzzy-based clustering algorithms, FUCA has two output variables, "Competition Radius" and "Rank," and uses three inputs for its computation, namely "Residual Energy," "Node density," and "Distance to BS." Computation of competition radius and ranking using predefined fuzzy "if–then" rules allows the competition radius and ranking of each initial CH to change dynamically. This reduces the competitive radius of CHs while decreasing energy, thereby forming unequal clusters. The output linguistic variables are defuzzified using a region-centric approach to obtain a clear value. After completing the CH election, non-CH nodes join the nearest CH to form a cluster. After cluster formation, non-CH nodes transmit sensed data to their respective CHs, and each CH summarizes all the received data and sends it to the receiver. In contrast, E-FUCA does not consider node density but focuses on the average distance to the communication node, as node density does not provide a complete picture of the energy consumption of the CH. Choosing the average distance offers a clear picture of the communication cost borne by the CH. In FUCA, non-CH nodes make a greedy decision during cluster formation, selecting the nearest CH without considering the existing load of the CH. In E-FUCA, non-CH nodes intelligently select their CHs based on the CH's rank, proximity to the CH, and the number of nodes within the cluster radius obtained during CH selection. Additionally, E-FUCA selects its CHs based on the Next hop rank, Nearness to the next-hop, and the Distance reduced to BS, further enhancing energy efficiency.

Intelligent computing based clustering and routing methods

Traditional clustering-based routing algorithms facilitate fault tolerance, load balancing, and reliable communication. However, they compromise the lifetime of CHs. The rising popularity of intelligent computing algorithms has introduced novel concepts to clustering and routing protocols in WSNs. In37, an energy-efficient CH selection method was introduced. This method employs an enhanced GWO algorithm and considers various parameters, including convergence distance, residual energy, balancing factor, and average intra-cluster distance when selecting CHs, so as to improve the lifetime of the WSN. The fitness function for CH selection is designed based on factors such as residual energy and the Euclidean distance between nodes and the BS. This design aims to achieve uniform CH selection in each round, thereby promoting energy balance and extending the network lifetime. Similarly, another widely adopted population-based smart optimization algorithm is PSO. In CASIC-PSO38, a method utilizes PSO to select suitable sensor nodes as CHs with the aim of prolonging the network lifetime. Initially, CASIC-PSO performs a random initialization of each particle, setting its velocity and position by mapping particle encoding to the coordinates of sensor nodes. Subsequently, a fitness function is established to compute the fitness values of particles, taking into account sensor node's remaining energy and transmission energy consumption between sensor nodes and the BS. The personal best fitness value for each particle and the global best fitness value for personal best values are then determined. Finally, the particles update their coordinates to achieve the global best solution based on position and velocity vectors until the predefined number of iterations is reached. Additionally, the acceleration coefficients are adaptively adjusted to enhance the search quality with respect to the iteration number. The PDU-SLnO algorithm is introduced in39, which integrates sea lion hunting behavior with the particle swarm optimization algorithm. Specifically, the PDU-SLnO algorithm mimics the hunting strategies of sea lions, encompassing steps such as chasing, tracking, following, surrounding, and attacking prey. Concurrently, it incorporates PSO's global and local search methods to determine the optimal position. During implementation, each particle is assigned a speed and random position, navigating through the search space. In each iteration, particles converge towards their optimal positions. By combining sea lion behavior with PSO, the PDU-SLnO algorithm effectively addresses the challenge of cluster header selection. In IPSO-GWO40, another method is introduced to select optimal CHs and find routing paths within the network. Primarily, CH selection is viewed as an optimization problem in IPSO-GWO, which is addressed using an enhanced PSO named as Improved Particle Swarm Optimization (IPSO). Its objectives include maximizing the ratio of average residual energy of CHs to that of non-CH nodes, as well as maximizing the ratio of the maximum distance among non-CH nodes and the BS to the average distance among BS and CHs. Additionally, the inertial weight is adaptively adjusted to prevent getting stuck in local optima. It should be noted that random particle initialization may slow down the convergence process. After the selection of CHs by IPSO, non-CH nodes become part of clusters through interactions with the nearest CHs. Subsequently, GWO is applied to determine the optimal paths, aiming to minimize distance traversal and hop count. The best solution is achieved by updating the speed and location of the wolves. It's worth noting that residual energy is not taken into account in path selection. Simulation results demonstrate that IPSO-GWO can provide maximal energy efficiency while extending the network lifetime. In41, PSO-EEC is introduced as a method for selecting CHs and relay nodes to enhance network lifetime and performance PSO. PSO-EEC follows operations similar to traditional PSO to designate the most efficient nodes as CHs and optimal CHs as relays. To achieve this, fitness functions are created to assess the particles, and the particles adjust their position and velocity based on the local and global best to attain the ultimate global optimal solution. The fitness function for clustering takes into account parameters such as the ratio of nodes' initial energy to residual energy, the distance between cluster member nodes and the Cluster Head, and node degree to select the most suitable nodes for the role of CH. Simultaneously, the fitness function for routing considers the residual energy of CHs and the distance between CHs and the BS to identify the optimal relay nodes for transmitting data collected by the CHs to the BS. Experimental results demonstrate that PSO-EEC has the potential to enhance network lifetime and performance. Likewise, in42, a multi-hop clustering routing algorithm called EBPSO is introduced, which is also based on PSO. In the initial phase of CH selection, the most suitable CH is chosen by taking into account the energy of the sensor nodes, the distance between nodes within the cluster, and the distance between the CH and the BS. In the subsequent data transmission phase, a relay node selection mechanism is introduced, favoring nodes with higher remaining energy and proximity to the BS for selection as relay nodes. Furthermore, to mitigate energy consumption and distinguish from the majority of cluster routing protocols employing a Round-Based Policy (RBP), the paper establishes an appropriate energy threshold. Re-clustering is only conducted when the energy of cluster nodes falls below this threshold to prevent excessive energy consumption resulting from frequent clustering.

Hybrid clustering and routing methods

During the last decades, numerous protocols that blend conventional approaches with intelligent computing algorithms have been proposed to improve the network performance. To enhance network longevity, a fuzzy-based Improved Harris's Hawk Optimization Algorithm (IHHO-F) is present in43. This algorithm aims to identify the optimal set of CHs by navigating both the exploration and exploitation phases. This strategy employs differential evolution to converge the solution set, generating P*. In each round, the node closest to P* represents the solution set containing CHs. Subsequently, fuzzy logic is employed to select the optimal CHs. Likewise, solutions are grouped into clusters using a fuzzy logic system to identify the optimal cluster head set based on three parameters: the current energy of CH nodes, the distance from the cluster head to the convergence point, and the number of neighboring nodes of the CHs. A node is designated as a Super Cluster Head (SCH) if it possesses higher residual energy, closer proximity to the BS, and a greater density of neighboring nodes. During the data transmission phase, the CH collects data from member nodes. If the number of hops is 1, it directly transmits the data to the SCH. Otherwise, it routes the data through the intermediate CH. Subsequently, the SCH employs a time-sharing method to gather data from the respective CH and forwards it to the BS. In44, a method combining GWO and a fuzzy system is proposed to conserve energy and prolong the sensor network's lifetime. This approach considers node centrality, neighborhood overlap, and residual energy within the wireless sensor network, and it selects the node with higher energy and closer proximity to the BS to serve as the CH. Moreover, an energy-efficient opportunistic routing scheme is utilized to achieve the goal of minimizing network energy consumption. In45, a fuzzy-based energy-efficient routing protocol is introduced. The protocol employs a PSO algorithm for clustering and a fuzzy logic system for data transmission . In PSO, it employs a fitness function that incorporates particle energy, inter-particle distance, and the number of clusters reachable by particles to select the best CHs. Additionally, four inputs battery energy, node density, average Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) to BS distance, and communication quality are considered to choose the next hop CH. Simulation results demonstrate the effective enhancement of network lifetime by this clustering and routing protocol. In contrast, an energy-efficient clustering and routing approach based on fuzzy logic and PSO (FLPSOC) is proposed in46, which employs a fuzzy logic system for initial CH selection. Input parameters include the ratio of initial to residual energy, the number of neighbors, and the distance between neighbors are considered. Subsequently, the PSO algorithm with a fitness function based on the residual energy of CHs and the distance between CHs and the BS is used to find the optimal relay CHs. Simulations validate its effectiveness in terms of network lifespan, throughput, stability period, cluster head count and so on. Propose protocol

In QPSOFL, n homogeneous nodes with unique ID are grouped into m clusters. Each cluster has only one CH, and each CM belongs to only one cluster. The special node BS can be deployed within or outside the network, and once deployed, neither the BS nor the nodes can move. The proposed protocol nominates the most appropriate nodes as CHs by using quantum particle swarm optimization algorithm and selects the optimal relay CHs based on fuzzy logic system to improve the network energy efficiency and maximize the network lifetime. The working of QPSOFL mainly consists of two phases, namely CH selection and relay finding.

CH selection

Selecting the optimal CHs from the nodes by using QPSO is one of the objectives of QPSOFL so as to form energy efficient clusters. In QPSOFL, a Mamdani fuzzy inference system is introduced to find optimal relay for each CH so as to decrease and balance the energy consumption of the CHs by creating a best path from any source CH to the BS. Network lifetime

In WSNs, network lifetime serves as a critical performance metric, representing the duration for which the entire network can operate effectively. This duration indicates how long the network can function before its sensor nodes deplete their energy. Given that wireless sensor nodes are typically battery-powered, with limited lifespans, it is imperative to manage energy efficiently in order to prolong the network lifetime. The network's lifespan is closely linked to the count of operational nodes, and its performance is typically assessed through metrics such as FND (First Node Dead), HND (Half of the Nodes Dead), and LND (Last Node Dead). Network throughput

Throughput constitutes a crucial performance metric employed to characterize the volume of data or information that a network can effectively transmit within a given time frame. Typically, Throughput serves as a standard measure for assessing a network's data transfer capacity and overall performance. Enhanced throughput can significantly enhance network performance, diminish transmission latency, and broaden the range of supported application scenarios, including monitoring, control, and data transfer. Regarding F-GWO, the throughput performance is likewise superior, primarily due to the increased data transmission to the BS facilitated by an energy-efficient opportunistic routing scheme during the data transmission phase.

Energy consumption

Energy consumption is a critical performance metric that quantifies the battery energy utilized by wireless sensor nodes during their tasks and communication. Wireless sensor nodes are typically battery-powered, making energy consumption a pivotal factor. As depicted in Fig. 11, network energy consumption rises with the number of rounds, yet the network energy profile of QPSOFL remains notably lower compared to other protocols. In IHHO-F, the SCH nodes become overloaded, leading to increased network energy consumption. In the case of F-GWO, the CHs located far from the BS experience high energy consumption due to their distant proximity to the BS. In EFUCA, a fuzzy logic approach is employed to create unbalanced clusters, aiming to conserve intra-cluster energy consumption. Subsequently, a fuzzy logic system is utilized to determine the optimal routing path, reducing inter-cluster energy consumption. Consequently, the overall network energy consumption is typically lower than that of IHHO-F and F-GWO. In contrast, FLPSOC first chooses the optimal CHs based on the output of the fuzzy logic system and then employs the PSO algorithm to select the best relay node. These findings highlight the significant energy-efficiency improvements achieved by QPSOFL compared to other protocols.

Network scalability

Network scalability is of paramount importance in wireless sensor networks, commonly employed for extensive monitoring and data collection tasks spanning various applications. Clustering and routing protocols with strong scalability can adaptly manage network expansion and alterations, all while safeguarding network performance, energy efficiency, and data transmission quality. Result analysis

Simulation results indicate that the QPSOFL protocol, as proposed, exhibits substantial enhancements in key performance metrics, including network survival period, throughput, network energy consumption, and scalability, offering significant advantages. Upon a comprehensive examination of the simulation analysis, the specific factors contributing to this enhancement can be attributed to the following: The initial application of the enhanced QPSO algorithm to select the optimal set of CHs based on a carefully designed fitness function, resulting in CHs with both higher residual energy and closer proximity to the BS. This leads to a substantial reduction in energy consumption. Additionally, optimal relay nodes are chosen during the routing phase by employing a fuzzy logic system, where the input parameters of this system are meticulously designed to optimize the averaging of network energy load. These factors collectively contribute to the notable performance improvements observed in the QPSOFL protocol.

Conclusion

This paper introduces QPSOFL, a novel clustering and routing protocol aimed at simultaneously enhancing energy efficiency and extending network lifetime. The protocol integrates quantum particle optimization for cluster formation and fuzzy logic for relay finding, prioritizing both energy efficiency and balance. Experimental results demonstrate that QPSOFL significantly outperforms classical and current clustering and routing protocols such as E-FUCA, IHHO-F, F-GWO, and FLPSOC. QPSOFL effectively prolongs network lifetime, reduces energy consumption, balances energy distribution, and achieves higher throughput for data transmission to the base station. Moreover, QPSOFL exhibits superior scalability compared to its counterparts. In future research, we aim to enhance the QPSOFL protocol's adaptability to mobile and rechargeable nodes, thereby improving overall network performance and extending network lifetime. Key areas of focus include investigating mobility management strategies, integrating energy harvesting techniques, optimizing resource allocation dynamically, and strengthening security measures to ensure robust and reliable operation in diverse and challenging environments.

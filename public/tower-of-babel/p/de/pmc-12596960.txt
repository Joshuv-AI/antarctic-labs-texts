Allium polyrhizum Turcz. flavonoid extract improved the growth performance, meat quality, serum biochemical indices, and immune organ indices of broilers
Authors: Peng Zhao, Hao Zheng, Huiling Qiu, Shansong Gao, Lele Hou, Jihong Dong, Fu Chen, Guicheng Dong, Wenxuan Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study investigated the effects of Allium polyrhizum Turcz. ex Regel flavonoid extract (APTF) on the growth performance, meat quality, serum biochemistry, and immune function of Arbor Acres broilers. A total of 120 one-day-old broilers were randomly assigned to four dietary treatments: a basal diet (control) or the basal diet supplemented with 0.05 %, 0.1 %, or 0.2 % APTF for a 42-day feeding trial, following a completely randomized design. The results demonstrated that APTF supplementation significantly improved growth performance by enhancing feed efficiency and average daily gain (P < 0.05) while reducing abdominal fat deposition (P < 0.05). The 0.2 % APTF supplementation level consistently yielded the most pronounced beneficial effects across most parameters measured. Meat quality parameters, including water-holding capacity, tenderness, and color stability, were markedly improved (P < 0.05), likely due to enhanced antioxidant capacity as evidenced by increased serum glutathione peroxidase and total superoxide dismutase activities (P < 0.05). APTF also exhibited hepatoprotective effects, as indicated by reduced serum levels of alanine aminotransferase and aspartate aminotransferase (P < 0.05), and immunomodulatory properties, as shown by increased immune organ indices and immunoglobulin levels (P < 0.05). These findings suggest that APTF can serve as a multifunctional natural feed additive to enhance productivity and product quality of broiler production systems.

Introduction

Allium polyrhizum Turcz. ex Regel (APT), commonly known as alkali onion, is a perennial herbaceous plant belonging to the Allium genus that thrives in saline-alkali soils and grassland ecosystems in arid and semi-arid regions of Central Asia, including Mongolia and northern China (Fu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022a). In traditional medicine, particularly Mongolian ethnomedicine, Allium, especially APT, has long been utilized for the treatment of gastrointestinal disorders and inflammatory conditions (Du et al., 2018; Jia et al., 2022). Beyond medicinal applications, APT is also valued as a nutrient-rich dietary component with high contents of vitamins, minerals, and bioactive compounds, often consumed as a wild vegetable or functional food (Zhang et al., 2022). Notably, phytochemical analyses have revealed that the pharmacological potential of APT are due to the abundance of flavonoids and flavonoid-like bioactive constituents of the roots, stems, and flowers. A previous study by our group further demonstrated that the floral tissues of APTR exhibit the highest flavonoid concentration, with major bioactive constituents including luteolin, galangin, ayanin, isorhamnetin, and kaempferide, which collectively confer anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, hypolipidemic, and immunomodulatory activities (Askarizadeh et al., 2025; Song et al., 2024).

With the rapid growth of the global population and increasing economic development, the demand for efficient and high-quality poultry production, particularly broilers, has become paramount to meet rising protein requirements (Bist et al., 2024). However, the conventional use of antibiotic growth promoters in poultry farming has raised significant concerns due to the emergence of antimicrobial resistance and potential residues in animal products (Tchana et al., 2025). In response, there is a growing emphasis on exploring safe and sustainable alternatives, such as phytogenic feed additives. Flavonoids, as a class of bioactive plant secondary metabolites, have been extensively studied as potential feed supplements and have demonstrated remarkable efficacy to improve feed quality, nutrient digestibility, and immune function of broilers (Tchana et al., 2025). These compounds have been shown to enhance growth performance, meat quality, oxidative stability, and carcass traits, while reducing mortality rates (Tan et al., 2022). Furthermore, dietary flavonoid supplementation has been associated with improved immune status and bone formation of broilers, highlighting potential as a viable alternative to conventional growth promoters (Xu et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2020).

Despite the well-documented bioactivity of flavonoids, research on Allium polyrhizum Turcz. ex Regel flavonoid extract (APTF) remains limited, particularly in the context of animal nutrition. Interestingly, grazing sheep with digestive disorders in Inner Mongolia instinctively seek out APTR, suggesting potential therapeutic value in livestock health. Furthermore, the unique adaptability of APT to saline-alkali soils aligns with national agricultural priorities for utilization of stress-tolerant plants, yet the application of APT in poultry production remains largely unexplored. Therefore, the objective of this study is to evaluate the effects of graded dietary supplementation with APTF on broiler growth performance, meat quality, serum biochemistry, and immune organ indices. By incorporating APTF into broiler diets, we seek to provide novel insights into its potential as a natural and effective alternative to synthetic feed additives, thereby supporting the development of more sustainable and health-promoting poultry production systems.

Materials and methods

Poultry and feeding management

A feeding trial was conducted from April to May 2025 at the experimental farm of Baotou Teachers' College, Inner Mongolia University of Science and Technology (Baotou, Inner Mongolia, China), using 120 one-day-old Arbor Acres broilers (mixed sex) randomly allocated into four groups with three replicates of 10 birds each. Each replicate was housed in a suspended cage (1.60 × 1 × 0.60 m) providing 0.16 m² floor space per bird under controlled conditions: initial temperature of 35°C for the first 3 days, gradually reduced by 2-3°C weekly until reaching 22°C by week 4, with a 16:8-h light:dark photoperiod. The control group received a basal diet formulated to meet NRC (1994) nutrient requirements for broilers, while three treatment groups were fed the same diet supplemented with 0.05 %, 0.1 %, or 0.2 % APTF (Table S1, 30 % purity). The APTF was thoroughly mixed with a small portion of the basal diet to create a premix, which was then gradually expanded to ensure homogeneous distribution in the complete feed. The mixing accuracy was verified by sampling and analyzing flavonoid content from different locations in the feed batch using high-performance liquid chromatography. Birds had ad libitum access to feed and water, received standard vaccinations, and maintained good health throughout the trial, which was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Baotou Teachers' College (No. AEWC-BTTC 2025-012). The formulations and main components are listed in Table S2.

Growth performance

On day 42 of the trial, after a 12-h fasting period, all experimental broilers were individually weighed and feed intake was recorded on a per-replicate basis to evaluate growth performance through three key parameters: average daily feed intake (ADFI, g/bird/d), calculated as total feed consumption divided by the number of days and birds; average daily gain (ADG, g/bird/d), determined by dividing total weight gain by the experimental duration and bird number; and feed conversion ratio (FCR), obtained by dividing ADFI by ADG to assess feeding efficiency (Zavelinski et al., 2024).

Sample collection

On day 42 of the experiment, following a 12-h fasting period, three broilers with body weights closest to the replicate average were selected from each group and euthanized via left jugular vein exsanguination. Immediately after slaughter, the left and right pectoralis major muscles and leg muscles were excised for meat quality analysis, while the thymus and spleen were carefully collected and weighed for immune organ index calculation (organ weight/body weight ratio) (Shafiq et al., 2022). Approximately 5 mL of blood was collected from each broiler, allowed to clot at room temperature for 2 h, then centrifuged at 3,500 rpm for 15 min to obtain serum, which was subsequently aliquoted and stored at −80°C until biochemical analysis.

Meat quality evaluation

For meat quality assessment, the right pectoralis major muscle from each sampled broiler was carefully trimmed of connective tissue and surface fat before being analyzed according to NY/T 1333-2007 (Method for Determination of Livestock and Poultry Meat Quality) to determine the meat color (using a chroma meter), pH value (with a pH meter at 45 min and 24 h postmortem), water loss rate (by the pressure method), and drip loss percentage (by the suspension method). The left pectoralis major muscle, similarly prepared, was subjected to Warner-Bratzler shear force measurement following NY/T 1180-2006 (Determination of Meat Tenderness - Shear Force Method), with samples cooked to an internal temperature of 70°C before shear force analysis perpendicular to the muscle fiber orientation (Wang et al., 2022b). All measurements were performed in triplicate for each sample to ensure data reliability.

Serum antioxidant parameter analysis

Serum antioxidant capacity was evaluated by measuring five key biomarkers: glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px), total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD), malondialdehyde (MDA), reduced glutathione (GSH), and total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) (Ma et al., 2024). All assays were performed using commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) following the manufacturer's protocols. Absorbance readings were conducted using an EPOCH multi-wavelength microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT, USA). GSH-Px activity was determined with the coupled enzyme method measuring the NADPH oxidation rate at 412 nm, T-SOD activity via the xanthine oxidase method at 550 nm, MDA content using the thiobarbituric acid reactive substances method at 532 nm, GSH concentration through the 5,5′-dithiobis-(2-nitrobenzoic acid) method at 412 nm, and T-AOC using the ferric reducing ability of plasma assay at 593 nm. Appropriate calibration curves were established for each parameter using the standards provided in each kit.

Statistical analysis

All experimental data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The effects of APTF supplementation were evaluated using polynomial regression analysis to characterize dose-response relationships across the four treatment levels (0 %, 0.05 %, 0.1 %, and 0.2 % APTF). Linear and quadratic contrasts were tested to determine the nature of the responses. Treatment effects were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05. The results are presented as the mean and standard error of the mean to indicate data variability.

Results

Effects of APTF on growth performance of arbor acres broilers

Dietary supplementation with APTF significantly enhanced the growth performance of Arbor Acres broilers in a concentration-dependent manner (Table 1). Both linear and quadratic effects were observed for several parameters. The most pronounced effects were observed in the 0.2 % APTF group, which showed 12.4 % greater final body weight (3478 ± 45 vs. 3094 ± 52 g, P < 0.01) and 12.6 % higher ADG (85.9 ± 1.1 vs. 76.3 ± 1.3 g/d, P < 0.01) as compared to the control group, with significant linear and overall effects (P < 0.001). Supplementation with 0.1 % APTF yielded intermediate improvements, while the 0.05 % group demonstrated more modest but still statistically significant benefits (P < 0.05). Importantly, the superior feed efficiency of the APTF-supplemented groups was evidenced by reductions to the feed conversion ratio from 8.1 % to 11.4 % (P < 0.05), with both linear and quadratic contrasts being significant (P < 0.01). Effects of APTF on serum antioxidant capacity of arbor acres broilers

Dietary supplementation with APTF significantly enhanced the antioxidant capacity of broilers in a dose-dependent manner (Table 2). All antioxidant parameters exhibited progressive improvements with increasing APTF concentrations, showing strong linear effects (P < 0.001). The 0.2 % APTF group demonstrated the most pronounced effects, showing 34.2 % higher GSH-Px activity (384.8 ± 5.6 vs. 286.7 ± 4.8 U/mL, P < 0.001), 29.4 % greater T-SOD activity (120.6 ± 1.7 vs. 93.2 ± 1.0 U/mL, P < 0.001), and 60.4 % improvement in T-AOC (13.94 ± 0.34 vs. 8.69 ± 0.23 U/mL, P < 0.001) as compared to the control group. Concurrently, MDA levels - a key marker of lipid peroxidation - were reduced by 36.3 % in the 0.2 % APTF group (2.46 ± 0.10 vs. 3.86 ± 0.12 nmol/mL, P < 0.001), indicating effective mitigation of oxidative stress. The intermediate doses (0.05 % and 0.1 % APTF) also showed significant (P < 0.001) but less pronounced improvements in all antioxidant parameters, following a clear dose-response pattern. Effects of APTF on fat deposition of arbor acres broilers

Dietary supplementation with APTF significantly reduced fat deposition of broilers across all measured parameters (Table 3). Strong linear effects (P < 0.001) were observed for all fat-related traits. The 0.2 % APTF group exhibited the most substantial reductions, showing 33.3 % lower abdominal fat percentage (3.04 ± 0.23 % vs. 3.61 ± 0.29 %, P < 0.001), 38.5 % decreased pectoral fat thickness (2.18 ± 0.44 vs. 2.96 ± 0.34 mm, P < 0.001), and 34.7 % reduction in femoral gap width (3.16 ± 0.42 vs. 4.31 ± 0.40 mm, P < 0.001) as compared to the control group. Intermediate APTF doses (0.05 % and 0.1 %) demonstrated dose-dependent decreases in fat deposition. A significant quadratic effect was noted for abdominal fat (P < 0.05), suggesting a non-linear response at lower doses. Particularly noteworthy was the uniform reduction across different fat depots, including subcutaneous (pectoral and leg muscle) and internal (abdominal and intermuscular) fat, suggesting APTF exerts systemic lipid-modulating effects rather than site-specific actions. Effects of APTF on meat quality characteristics of arbor acres broilers

Dietary supplementation with APTF significantly improved multiple meat quality parameters in a dose-dependent manner (Table 4). All measured traits exhibited significant linear effects (P < 0.001). The 0.2 % APTF group showed the most pronounced improvements, exhibiting 37.5 % lower drip loss (1.6 ± 0.1 % vs. 3.6 ± 0.2 %, P < 0.001), 34.0 % reduced cooking loss (15.7 ± 0.3 % vs. 23.8 ± 0.6 %, P < 0.001), and 37.5 % decreased shear force (21.3 ± 0.8 vs. 34.1 ± 1.2 N, P < 0.001) as compared to the control group, indicating enhanced water-holding capacity and tenderness. Color measurements revealed that APTF supplementation significantly increased redness (a* value) by 52.8 % (5.5 ± 0.2 vs. 3.6 ± 0.2, P < 0.001) while reducing lightness (L* value) by 12.5 % (45.3 ± 0.5 vs. 51.8 ± 0.7, P < 0.001), suggesting improved meat color stability. The progressive pH decline in the APTF groups (P < 0.001) followed normal postmortem metabolic patterns while maintaining values within the optimal range for meat quality, with quadratic effects observed for both pH45min and pH24h (P < 0.05). Effects of APTF on hepatic and renal functions of arbor acres broilers

Dietary supplementation with APTF demonstrated significant dose-dependent improvements in both hepatic and renal function markers (Table 5). Strong linear effects (P < 0.001) were observed for most parameters. The 0.2 % APTF group exhibited the most substantial benefits, showing 28.1 % reduction in blood urea nitrogen concentrations (18.9 ± 0.3 vs. 26.3 ± 0.7 ng/mL, P < 0.001), 39.9 % lower creatinine levels (11.9 ± 0.3 vs. 19.8 ± 0.8 μmol/L, P < 0.001), and 46.6 % decreased uric acid concentrations (3.1 ± 0.1 vs. 5.8 ± 0.3 mg/dL, P < 0.001), indicating enhanced renal function. Hepatic biomarkers showed parallel improvements, with alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) activities reduced by 50.1 % (17.8 ± 0.6 vs. 35.7 ± 2.1 U/L, P < 0.001) and 46.9 % (50.2 ± 1.2 vs. 94.5 ± 4.2 U/L, P < 0.001), respectively, suggesting hepatoprotective effects. Notably, serum albumin levels increased by 31.0 % (3.8 ± 0.1 vs. 2.9 ± 0.1 g/dL, P < 0.001), reflecting improved protein synthesis capacity. Effects of APTF on immune-related parameters in arbor acres broilers

Dietary supplementation with APTF demonstrated significant immunomodulatory effects, as evidenced by dose-dependent improvements in both immune organ indices and serum immunoglobulin levels (Table 6). The 0.2 % APTF group showed the most pronounced effects, with spleen and thymus indices increasing by 52.6 % (0.29 ± 0.01 % vs. 0.19 ± 0.01 %, P < 0.001) and 22.2 % (0.33 ± 0.02 % vs. 0.27 ± 0.01 %, P = 0.003), respectively, as compared to the control group, indicating enhanced immune organ development, with a significant linear trend for spleen index (P < 0.001). Serum immunoglobulin levels exhibited similar improvements, with IgG and IgM concentrations elevated by 17.4 % (10.1 ± 0.3 vs. 8.6 ± 0.1 μg/mL, P = 0.012) and 37.8 % (1.24 ± 0.07 vs. 0.90 ± 0.02 μg/mL, P = 0.005), respectively, in the 0.2 % APTF group. Notably, serum levels of the pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-6 and TNF-α were significantly reduced by 27.6 % (194 ± 4 vs. 268 ± 7 pg/mL, P < 0.001) and 29.0 % (93 ± 3 vs. 131 ± 3 pg/mL, P < 0.001), respectively, suggesting potential anti-inflammatory properties of APTF, with both linear and quadratic contrasts being significant (P < 0.01). The 0.1 % APTF group showed particularly strong effects on IgA production (35.0 % increase, P = 0.008), while maintaining an optimal balance of inflammatory responses. Discussion

APT, a perennial herbaceous plant widely distributed in arid and semi-arid regions of Central Asia, exhibits remarkable adaptability to saline-alkali soils (Fu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022a). Traditionally, APT has been utilized both as a nutrient-rich dietary component and in ethnomedicine, particularly in Mongolian traditional medicine, for treatment of inflammatory and gastrointestinal disorders (Du et al., 2018; Jia et al., 2022). Our previous phytochemical analysis revealed that APTF contains various bioactive compounds, with floral tissues having particularly high concentrations of the flavonoids luteolin, galangin, ayanin, isorhamnetin, and kaempferide—compounds with well-documented antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anti-inflammatory properties (Askarizadeh et al., 2025; Song et al., 2024). However, research on the application of APTF as a feed additive in animal production remains scarce. To our knowledge, this study is the first to systematically demonstrate the health-promoting effects of dietary APTF supplementation in broilers, highlighting potential as a natural alternative to synthetic growth promoters in poultry production systems.

The significant improvement in FCR and ADG observed in APTF-supplemented broilers suggests enhanced nutrient utilization and energy metabolism efficiency. These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating that plant-derived flavonoids can improve growth performance of poultry (Hassan et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023a). The dose-dependent reduction in FCR without increasing feed intake indicates that APTF may optimize metabolic pathways rather than simply stimulate appetite, a phenomenon similarly reported for quercetin supplementation of broilers (Deng et al., 2024; Sierżant et al., 2022). This metabolic enhancement could be attributed to the bioactive flavonoids luteolin and kaempferide, which are known to modulate AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs), key regulators of energy homeostasis (Wang et al., 2021, 2022b). Notably, our results align with recent findings showing that dietary flavonoids significantly upregulated hepatic AMPK expression in broilers (Li et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2020). Furthermore, the improved growth performance corresponds with previous reports that plant-derived flavonoids enhance pancreatic lipase and amylase activities (Li et al., 2022), thereby improving intestinal nutrient absorption. The decreased abdominal fat deposition coupled with increased muscle growth suggests that APTF promotes nutrient partitioning toward protein synthesis rather than fat accumulation, mirroring the effects of procyanidin supplementation (Shen et al., 2025). This metabolic shift likely involves downregulation of fatty acid synthase and lipid synthesis metabolites, as demonstrated in recent transcriptomic studies of broilers fed epigallocatechin gallate (Gao et al., 2025). These metabolic improvements are particularly valuable for modern poultry production, where feed efficiency directly impacts economic profitability and environmental sustainability, with our results suggesting that APTF could reduce feed costs based on current performance metrics.

The remarkable improvements to serum biochemical parameters and antioxidant capacity observed in APTF-supplemented broilers demonstrate significant systemic benefits beyond growth performance. The dose-dependent enhancement of antioxidant enzymes (GSH-Px, T-SOD) and reduction in MDA levels align with previous findings that plant flavonoids activate the Nrf2/ARE pathway, boosting cellular antioxidant defenses (Li et al., 2020; Suraweera et al., 2023). Particularly noteworthy was the 60.4 % improvement in T-AOC, surpassing results reported for many other botanical supplements (Liu et al., 2023b; Tian et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025). The hepatoprotective effects were reductions in ALT and AST levels, comparable to the efficacy of silymarin in poultry (Tsiouris et al., 2021), suggesting the potential of APTF as a natural liver protectant. Improved renal function may result from flavonoid-mediated-tissue injury, fibrosis, and cellular defense mechanisms, as demonstrated by genistein (Kattna and Singh, 2025). These comprehensive improvements to antioxidant status and organ function likely contribute to the observed growth enhancement and meat quality improvements, as oxidative stress reduction is known to improve muscle cell integrity and protein retention (Young et al., 2005). The parallel enhancements to both hepatic and renal parameters suggest that APTF exerts systemic protective effects, as a particularly valuable dietary supplement for modern high-performance broilers facing metabolic challenges.

The present study demonstrates that dietary APTF supplementation effectively modulated lipid metabolism and improved meat quality parameters of broilers. These findings align with emerging research on flavonoid-rich plant extracts in poultry nutrition. Recent studies have established that dietary flavonoids can influence adipogenesis through modulation of the PPARγ and C/EBPα signaling pathways (Wang et al., 2021, 2022b), providing a plausible mechanism for the observed fat reduction in APTF-supplemented birds. The improved water-holding capacity and tenderness are consistent with reports that plant-derived antioxidants help maintain muscle membrane integrity during postmortem glycolysis (Bokharaeian and Kaki, 2025; Huanhong et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2019). Particularly noteworthy is the enhanced meat color stability, which corroborates findings that flavonoid compounds can interact with heme iron to delay myoglobin oxidation (Biswas et al., 2024; Cao et al., 2023). The simultaneous improvement to multiple quality parameters supports the growing recognition that natural antioxidants exert pleiotropic effects on muscle biology, affecting both oxidative stability and structural proteins. These effects may be particularly pronounced due to the unique flavonoid profile of APTF, including luteolin and kaempferide, which have demonstrated strong tissue-protective properties in other models (Nakadate et al., 2025; Song et al., 2024). The current results expand our understanding of how bioactive plant flavonoids can be utilized to optimize both production efficiency and product quality of modern poultry operations.

The immunomodulatory effects of APTF supplementation were clearly demonstrated through the enhanced development of immune organs and improved humoral immunity of broilers. The increased spleen and thymus indices suggest that APTF promotes lymphoid organ growth and maturation, which are critical for effective immune function of poultry. The elevated serum immunoglobulin levels indicate strengthened humoral immune responses, likely mediated through activation of B lymphocytes and plasma cells. These findings are consistent with the known immunostimulatory properties of plant flavonoids, which can modulate both innate and adaptive immune responses (Atoum et al., 2022; da Fonseca et al., 2025). The observed reduction in pro-inflammatory cytokine levels suggests that APTF may help maintain appropriate immune homeostasis by preventing excessive inflammatory responses. The coordinated improvements to both immune organ development and antibody production point to comprehensive enhancement of the immune system. These immunomodulatory characteristics, combined with the previously demonstrated benefits on growth performance and meat quality, suggest the potential of APTF as a multifunctional feed additive to support both productivity and health in modern broiler production systems.

Conclusions

These findings demonstrate that APTF significantly enhanced broiler production through multiple mechanisms. APTF improved growth performance by optimizing feed efficiency and nutrient partitioning, while reducing fat deposition, with the most pronounced effects observed at the 0.2 % supplementation level. The extract exhibited strong antioxidant activity, correlating with enhanced meat quality attributes, including water-holding capacity and tenderness. Additionally, APTF showed hepatoprotective effects and immunomodulatory properties, as evidenced by improved serum biochemistry and immune organ development. These findings position APTF, particularly at the optimal dosage of 0.2 %, as a promising multifunctional feed additive, though further research is needed to fully elucidate the mechanisms of action under commercial conditions.

Novel inflammatory markers for predicting adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with polycystic ovary syndrome: a retrospective case‒control study
Authors: Na Pan, Jing Zhang, Ning Zhu, Mengting Liu, Shuying Yang, Zhanshuai Song, Haibo Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the potential predictive value of novel inflammatory markers for adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).MethodsA retrospective case‒control study was conducted. Clinical data were collected from pregnant PCOS patients and healthy pregnant women who received regular prenatal care and delivered at our hospital between 2021 and 2023. All included subjects had relevant clinical data collected. Basic characteristics, novel inflammatory markers, and adverse pregnancy outcomes were compared between the two groups. A random forest classification model was applied to evaluate the potential predictive value of combined novel inflammatory markers for adverse pregnancy outcomes in women with PCOS.ResultsA total of 528 pregnant women were included. Compared with those in the control group, pregnant women in the PCOS group demonstrated statistically significant differences in height, first-trimester body mass index (BMI), gestational weight gain, BMI at admission, systolic blood pressure (SBP) at admission, and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) at admission (P < 0.05). Among participants with adverse pregnancy outcomes, compared with the control group, the PCOS group had an increased monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR) (0.29 ± 0.02), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) (3.56 ± 0.21), systemic inflammation response index (SIRI) (1.98 ± 0.19), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) (133.76 ± 5.26), and systemic immune inflammation index (SII) (834.27 ± 55.72) (P < 0.05). After adjusting for confounding factors and optimizing hyperparameters, the combined prediction model using these five novel inflammatory markers (MLR, NLR, SIRI, PLR, and SII) achieved an AUC of 0.815, an F1 score of 0.776, an accuracy of 79.40%, and a precision of 79.40% for adverse pregnancy outcomes in patients with PCOS.ConclusionEvaluation via the random forest classification model revealed that the combined use of these novel inflammatory markers has potential predictive value for adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS. These findings merit attention from clinical practitioners, and corresponding preventive measures should be implemented.Keywords: Polycystic ovary syndrome, Novel inflammatory markers, Adverse pregnancy outcomes, Random forest

Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is an endocrine and metabolic disorder in women of childbearing age. It is characterized by hyperandrogenism, obesity, irregular menstruation, hirsutism, polycystic changes in the ovaries, and ovulatory dysfunction [1]. PCOS may result in oocyte abnormalities in females [2]. Compared with controls, women with PCOS exhibit a significantly increased risk of pregnancy complications [3]. The incidence of adverse pregnancy outcomes such as miscarriage, stillbirth, foetal death, and birth defects in PCOS patients also increases in the long term [4]. Studies have shown that the occurrence of adverse pregnancy outcomes in patients with PCOS is closely related to pathological mechanisms such as insulin resistance, hyperandrogenism, inflammation/immune abnormalities, and decreased endometrial receptivity [5]. PCOS may disrupt the endometrial expression of sex hormone receptors and corepressors. It can also increase endometrial insulin resistance, impairing glucose transport and utilization. Collectively, these alterations can result in chronic low-grade inflammation, immune dysfunction, abnormal uterine vascularity, aberrant endometrial gene expression, and cellular abnormalities in women with PCOS [6]. An increasing number of studies have focused on the key role of inflammation in the pregnancy outcome of women with PCOS. The leukocyte, neutrophil and platelet counts in the peripheral blood of patients with PCOS are significantly increased, suggesting activation of systemic inflammation [7]. Metabolic disorders such as obesity and hyperinsulinaemia can form a vicious cycle, continuously activating immune cells such as monocytes and neutrophils and thereby aggravating the systemic inflammatory response [8]. Pioneering research by Kelly’s team confirmed that the serum levels of proinflammatory factors (such as TNF-α and IL-6) significantly increased in patients with PCOS. These molecules not only aggravate insulin resistance by activating NF-κB and other signalling pathways and interfering with the endocrine regulation of the hypothalamus pituitary ovary axis [9, 10], but also directly impair oocyte maturation and embryonic development potential and affect pregnancy outcomes [11]. Inflammation dysregulation is among the core pathological mechanisms underlying PCOS and adverse pregnancy outcomes. Accurate assessment of inflammatory status is crucial for clinical diagnosis, treatment, and prognostic warning. Although traditional specific inflammatory marker assays are precise, they are relatively costly and not routinely feasible. In recent years, novel inflammatory markers derived from complete blood counts have emerged as powerful tools for evaluating systemic inflammation and immune imbalance because of their accessibility, low cost, and good reproducibility. Among these, the monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR) reflects the balance between innate immune activation and adaptive immune suppression [12]. The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) serves as an important marker of systemic inflammatory stress and immune status [13]. The systemic inflammation response index (SIRI), which integrates neutrophil, monocyte, and lymphocyte counts, provides a more comprehensive assessment of inflammatory intensity [14]. The red blood cell distribution width-to-platelet ratio (RPR) is known to reflect systemic inflammation. The platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) links thromboinflammatory processes with immune responses [14]. The systemic immune inflammation index (SII), which combines platelet, neutrophil, and lymphocyte counts, comprehensively reflects both the immune response and inflammatory status [15, 16]. These markers dynamically reflect the imbalance between inflammatory responses and immune regulation by integrating multidimensional routine blood parameters, thereby providing a novel noninvasive tool for evaluating inflammatory burden in patients with PCOS. In the present study, six emerging inflammatory markers, namely, the MLR, NLR, SIRI, RPR, PLR, and SII, were incorporated into a combined analysis. A random forest classification prediction model was subsequently constructed to systematically assess the potential predictive value of these markers for adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS.

Participants and methods

Study design

This was a retrospective case‒control study. The study cohort consisted of pregnant women with PCOS who received treatment at Zibo Maternal and Child Health Hospital from January 2021 to December 2023, with data retrieved from the hospital’s Medical and Health Big Data Science and Technology Innovation Platform. Preliminary experimental data indicated that the panel of combined inflammatory markers yielded a sensitivity of 85% and a specificity of 80% for predicting adverse pregnancy outcomes. On the basis of these parameters, with a significance level (α) set at 0.05 and a type II error rate (β) at 0.10, the required sample size for each group was calculated using the following formula:, where P0​ denotes the incidence of adverse pregnancy outcomes in the control group (24.59%), and P1​ represents the expected incidence in the PCOS group (38%). The calculation results indicated that approximately 220 cases were required for each group. To account for potential data missingness, the calculated sample size was expanded by 15%. Therefore, only 253 pregnant women with PCOS were included in the experimental group, which met the required sample size. Age matching was performed at a ratio of 1:1, and the same number of healthy pregnant women who underwent prenatal examinations and delivered at our hospital during the same period were recruited as the control group. The inclusion criteria for patients with PCOS were as follows: 1. The diagnostic criteria for PCOS [17], namely the 2003 Rotterdam Diagnostic Criteria, which require the presence of at least two of the following three conditions, should be met: (1) oligo-anovulation, (2) hyperandrogenism, and (3) polycystic ovaries (≥ 12 follicles measuring 2–9 mm in diameter and/or an ovarian volume > 10 mL in at least one ovary) and 2. availability of complete clinical records. The exclusion criteria for PCOS patients were as follows: (1) confirmed intrauterine infection, preeclampsia, premature rupture of membranes (PROM) lasting more than 18 h, or severe maternal comorbidities (e.g., severe hepatorenal diseases) upon admission.; (2) acute infections, chronic inflammatory diseases (e.g., systemic lupus erythematosus), or autoimmune diseases; (3) multiple gestation; (4) comorbid endocrine disorders or severe systemic diseases; (5) congenital conditions (e.g., chromosomal abnormalities); (6) history of other treatments (e.g., surgery, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy). The exclusion criteria for the control group were as follows: In addition to the aforementioned criteria, pregnant women with a past or current diagnosis of PCOS, hyperandrogenism symptoms, ovulatory dysfunction, or polycystic ovarian morphology were excluded to ensure the comparability of baseline characteristics between the two groups. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Zibo Maternal and Child Health Hospital (Approval No. 202411028).

Clinical data collection

Clinical data of pregnant women were retrieved from the Smart Medical Science Data Warehouse using the following filtering criteria: “Zibo Maternal and Child Health Hospital, January 2021 to December 2023”, a confirmed diagnosis of PCOS, and a delivery outcome of “singleton live birth”. A total of 292 pregnant women with PCOS were initially identified. Cases that did not meet the eligibility criteria or had more than 50% missing biochemical indicators were excluded, resulting in 264 eligible pregnant women with PCOS being included in the final analysis. Age-matched controls at a 1:1 ratio were selected from normal pregnant women who underwent prenatal examinations and delivered at the same hospital during the same period. Accordingly, 264 control participants were enrolled to maintain the matching ratio. In total, 528 pregnant women were included in this study.

Clinical data were collected via the hospital’s Medical and Health Big Data Technology Innovation Platform. All blood samples were obtained from pregnant women prior to admission for labour, with the subjects being required to maintain a fasting state (≥ 8 h) at the time of collection. The time interval between sample collection and the occurrence of adverse pregnancy outcomes was 24–72 h. The collected data were categorized into the following two types:

Basic information: Age, height, weight at initial prenatal registration, first-trimester body mass index (BMI), body weight at admission, BMI at admission, systolic blood pressure (SBP) at admission, and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) at admission.

Definition of adverse pregnancy outcomes

We assessed premature rupture of membranes (PROM), postpartum haemorrhage (PPH), preterm birth (PTB), and emergency caesarean section as adverse pregnancy outcomes [18]. PROM: Premature rupture of membranes is defined as a spontaneous rupture of membranes before the onset of labour [19], PPH as maternal blood loss ≥ 500 mL (vaginal delivery) or ≥ 1,000 mL (caesarean section) within 24 h after delivery [19] and PTB as delivery before 37 + 0 gestational weeks [20].

Principles of the random forest model

The random forest is an ensemble learning method proposed by Leo Breiman et al. in 2001 [21]. It operates through the following key steps: First, multiple bootstrap samples are drawn from the original dataset to construct individual decision trees. Second, at each node of a tree, a random subset of features is selected, and the optimal split is determined solely on the basis of this subset. This process is repeated recursively, resulting in a diverse collection of decision trees. Finally, predictions from all the trees are aggregated: For classification tasks, the majority vote is taken; for regression tasks, the outputs are averaged. Compared with a single decision tree, the random forest decreases variance and generally achieves higher predictive accuracy. Key advantages of the algorithm include strong resistance to overfitting, robustness to noise and outliers, and the ability to handle high-dimensional feature spaces effectively. The random selection of both samples and features during training is related to the trees and mitigates overfitting. Moreover, because each tree is trained independently, the algorithm can be efficiently parallelized. Model performance can be internally validated using out-of-bag (OOB) error estimation [22], which provides an unbiased measure of generalization error. By monitoring the OOB error, hyperparameters can be tuned, and other optimization strategies can be applied to improve the accuracy and robustness of the model.

Statistical analysis

Missing values of covariates were imputed using multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE) under the assumption that the data were missing at random [23]. Continuous variables were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation when they followed a normal distribution. This study adopted a heterogeneous matching design (e.g., age matching) for sample construction, in which paired data from the two groups consisted of continuous quantitative measurements. For comparisons between the PCOS group and the control group, the paired-samples t test was applied when the differences between paired observations conformed to a normal distribution. For unpaired data, independent-samples t tests were used when the measurements in both groups satisfied the normality assumption. Categorical variables were analysed using the chi-square test (χ²). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to evaluate the potential predictive value of the MLR, NLR, SIRI, PLR, and SII and their combined index for adverse pregnancy outcomes. The area under the curve (AUC) and its 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated. A random forest classification model was used to assess the potential predictive and diagnostic value of five novel combined inflammatory markers for adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS. The dataset was divided into a training set (70%) and a testing set (30%), followed by 5-fold cross-validation to ensure robust model evaluation [18]. The hyperparameters of the random forest model were optimized on the basis of the OOB error. The model was trained on the training set and then applied to both the training and test sets to generate classification results. Model performance was assessed using accuracy, recall, precision, F1 score, and AUC, with values closer to 1 indicating better classification performance [24, 25]. Additionally, ROC curves were plotted to further visualize the predictive efficacy of the model.

All the statistical analyses were performed using SPSSPRO software V 1.0.11 (Online Application Software, China; https://www.spsspro.com). The significance level (α) was set at 0.05, and a P value < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Comparison of pregnancy outcomes between the two groups

The results indicated that the incidence of premature rupture of membranes (PROM), postpartum haemorrhage (PPH), preterm birth, and caesarean section was greater in the PCOS group than in the control group (P < 0.05). Furthermore, the overall incidence of adverse pregnancy outcomes significantly differed between the two groups (P < 0.05). (Table 1).

Comparison of novel inflammatory markers between the two groups

The levels of inflammatory markers, including the MLR (0.28 ± 0.15), NLR (3.53 ± 2.20), SIRI (1.93 ± 1.75), RPR (0.06 ± 0.017), PLR (132.31 ± 52.90), and SII (816.75 ± 567.44), were higher in the PCOS group than in the control group (P < 0.05). (Table 1).

Comparison of novel inflammatory markers between subgroups with adverse pregnancy outcomes

Among the pregnant women with adverse pregnancy outcomes, 101 belonged to the PCOS group, whereas 57 belonged to the control group. Comparisons of the novel inflammatory markers between these two subgroups revealed statistically significant differences in five markers: the MLR, NLR, SIRI, PLR, and SII (P < 0.05). However, no significant difference was observed in the RPR between the groups (P > 0.05). (Table 2).

Predictive value of five novel inflammatory markers and their combination for adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS

ROC curve analysis

Using a cohort of 264 pregnant women with PCOS as the study subjects and the presence of adverse pregnancy outcomes as the dichotomous dependent variable, ROC curves were plotted for five novel inflammatory markers (MLR, NLR, SIRI, PLR, and SII) and their combination. The results demonstrated that all indices and their combination exhibited significant predictive values for adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS (P < 0.05), with the following AUCs and 95% CIs: the MLR (0.583, 95% CI: 0.525–0.641), the NLR (0.765, 95% CI: 0.700–0.829), the SIRI (0.607, 95% CI: 0.539–0.676), the PLR (0.813, 95% CI: 0.754–0.871), the SII (0.786, 95% CI: 0.724–0.849), and the combined index (0.824, 95% CI: 0.769–0.878). However, the AUC of the combined index (0.824) was greater than that of each individual novel inflammatory marker. (Fig. 1).

Variable importance analysis of the random forest classification model

A random forest classification model was constructed using 264 pregnant women with PCOS as the research sample. The presence or absence of adverse pregnancy outcomes was defined as the binary dependent variable, with six novel inflammatory markers (MLR, NLR, SIRI, RPR, PLR, and SII) incorporated into the model. Additionally, the BMI, SBP and DBP at admission were included as covariates for adjustment. An examination of the variable importance ranking plot revealed that the variable importance of the RPR was negligible. In this model, these novel inflammatory indices were ranked in descending order of importance as follows: PLR, SII, NLR, SIRI, and MLR. (Fig. 2).

Variable importance ranking plot of independent variables in the random forest model

Performance analysis of the random forest classification model

As shown in Table 2; Fig. 1, and Fig. 2, no significant difference in the RPR was observed between the two groups with respect to adverse pregnancy outcomes (P > 0.05). Combined with the results of the ROC curve analysis (AUC = 0.498; 95% CI: 0.478–0.517; P > 0.05) and the variable importance assessment of the random forest classification model (the importance of the RPR was only 1.80%); these findings indicate that the RPR may not be an effective indicator for predicting adverse pregnancy outcomes in patients with PCOS. Therefore, this study incorporated only five novel inflammatory markers—MLR, NLR, SIRI, PLR, and SII—into a random forest model to predict adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS. Notably, all the performance metrics of the model surpassed 0.8 at this stage (Table 3; Fig. 3). Considering that metabolic-related indicators such as the BMI, DBP, and SBP at admission may potentially impact the prediction results, to comprehensively evaluate the role of these variables, this study further included them in the random forest model; subsequently, the optimal random forest classification model was fitted through hyperparameter optimization (including adjustment of the number of decision trees and leaf nodes), setting the number of cross-validation folds, and enabling the OOB testing option. As shown in Table 4; Fig. 4, after hyperparameter optimization, the random forest classification model incorporating these five novel inflammatory markers achieved an accuracy of 79.40%, a recall of 79.40%, a precision of 70.20%, an F1 score of 0.776, and an AUC of 0.815 for predicting adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS. The cross-validation set is a method used to evaluate the generalization performance of the model, with its F1 score reaching 0.748, which indicates that the model has a moderately above-average generalization ability.

Discussion

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common endocrine and metabolic disorder in women. Studies indicate that approximately 50% of patients with PCOS exhibit obesity, which manifests as overweight, excessive visceral fat, dyslipidaemia, and other related symptoms [26]. Compared with normal pregnant women, women with PCOS during pregnancy face a significantly increased risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes, including miscarriage, preterm birth, caesarean section, and stillbirth [27]. This study revealed that pregnant women in the PCOS group had a higher first-trimester BMI, gestational weight gain, BMI at admission, SBP at admission, and DBP at admission than those in the normal group. Furthermore, the incidence of adverse pregnancy outcomes such as PROM, PPH, preterm birth, and caesarean section was significantly greater in the PCOS group than in the normal group. These findings are consistent with the results of the aforementioned studies, suggesting that metabolic abnormalities may be key drivers of these adverse outcomes [28]. Moreover, Maliqueo [29] reported that signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), which regulates placental nutrient transport, exhibits increased signalling in the placentas of women with PCOS. This increase occurs independently of pregnancy-related complications and is activated by obesity-related inflammatory and metabolic factors [30]. The placentas of obese women show increased infiltration of proinflammatory macrophages and higher expression levels of IL-1, TNF-α, and IL-6, which are associated with increased expression of neutrophil chemokines [31]. This proinflammatory environment in both mothers with PCOS and foetuses may contribute to adverse pregnancy outcomes. Compared with those in the control group, the levels of five novel inflammatory markers—MLR, NLR, SIRI, PLR, and SII—were increased in the PCOS group with adverse pregnancy outcomes. These findings suggest that immune and inflammatory pathways may be activated in pregnant women with PCOS, leading to chronic inflammation and immune dysfunction and thereby increasing the risk of adverse pregnancy outcomes.

The ROC curve constructed using the combined inflammatory markers achieved an AUC of 0.824, which was significantly superior to that of any single marker (the AUCs for the MLR, NLR, SIRI, PLR, and SII were 0.583, 0.765, 0.607, 0.813, and 0.786, respectively). These findings confirm that a multidimensional inflammatory assessment has stronger clinical predictive power. These findings align with those of Hu Z [32] on the synergistic effects of inflammatory networks, suggesting that pregnancy risk in women with PCOS may stem from systemic inflammatory responses rather than a single pathway. An increase in the NLR (AUC = 0.765) indicates the coexistence of innate immune activation and adaptive immune suppression [33]. This imbalance may damage the placental vascular endothelium through oxidative stress, consequently increasing the risk of PROM and preterm birth [34]. The predictive value of the PLR and SII was particularly notable (AUCs of 0.813 and 0.786, respectively), likely related to the platelet‒lymphocyte dynamic imbalance they reflected. Platelet activation not only contributes to inflammation but can also exacerbate vascular endothelial damage through the release of proinflammatory factors (such as TNF-α and IL-6), thereby increasing the risk of complications such as PROM and placental insufficiency [35]. Furthermore, the SII integrates information on neutrophils, platelets, and lymphocytes, providing a more comprehensive understanding of the interaction between inflammation and immune status than single markers [36], which may explain its greater predictive efficacy. These findings are consistent with the mechanistic explanation by Özkan [37] regarding the ability of the SII to predict preeclampsia risk. Notably, the RPR was not significantly different between the two groups with adverse pregnancy outcomes (P > 0.05). Combined with the results of the ROC curve analysis (AUC = 0.498; 95% CI: 0.478–0.517; P > 0.05) and the variable importance assessment of the random forest classification model, these findings indicate that red blood cell parameters may have limited predictive value for PCOS-related pregnancy complications. The specific mechanisms underlying this finding require further investigation.

After adjusting for the confounding effects of the BMI, DBP and SBP at admission, the random forest model constructed in this study ranked the PLR and the SII as the top two variables in terms of importance. The importance ranking of the model can quantify the strength of the association between each variable and the study outcome, suggesting that the PLR and the SII are relatively strongly associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS. Following hyperparameter optimization, the model achieved a high AUC value of 0.815, which indicates that it has a certain preliminary predictive value for adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS. The random forest model further confirmed that the ROC curve constructed with a combination of inflammatory indices was significantly superior to those derived from individual indices. The high recall rate of the model (79.40%) indicates its effectiveness in identifying high-risk pregnant women, providing a time window for intervention. Furthermore, the good generalization ability of the model (cross-validated F1 = 0.748) suggests that it is generalizable and lays the foundation for developing a clinical prediction tool [38].

Strengths and limitations

Compared with previous studies, the key advancement of this research is that it is not confined to a single or a few indicators (such as the NLR or PLR alone) but integrates five novel inflammatory markers—the MLR, NLR, SIRI, PLR, and SII—to jointly predict adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS. This multimarker combination approach not only enables a more comprehensive assessment of systemic inflammatory status and overcomes the limitations of single-indicator methods but also more accurately reflects the complex pathological mechanisms underlying adverse pregnancy outcomes in patients with PCOS. In terms of clinical translation and application, the potential predictive value of novel inflammatory indicators for adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS can provide crucial support for medical staff to optimize clinical diagnosis and treatment. In traditional clinical practice, the assessment of adverse pregnancy risks in pregnant women with PCOS largely relies on conventional indicators such as age, BMI, and past medical history and lacks predictive tools that are highly specific and quantifiable. In contrast, these novel inflammatory indicators can be readily obtained through routine blood tests and inflammatory factor assays, featuring the dual advantages of simple operation and low cost. This characteristic helps promote the shift of medical staff from empirical risk assessment to data-driven precision management, thereby further strengthening clinical attention and resource allocation for pregnant women with PCOS. However, this study has certain limitations. First, the scope of the included covariates is relatively limited and is mainly constrained by objective operational conditions during the data extraction phase, which may affect the ability of the model to fully capture potential influencing factors. Future studies may further explore relevant covariates, such as metabolic indicators, endocrine characteristic parameters, and lifestyle factors. Second, this study adopted a single-centre, retrospective design and included only a Han Chinese population. Consequently, the applicability of the model is limited in populations of different ethnicities and socioeconomic backgrounds, and it does not incorporate core PCOS features such as insulin resistance and androgen levels. Therefore, prospective multicentre studies are needed in the future to validate the general applicability and external validity of the model. Additionally, integrating metabolic indicators, insulin resistance, medication use, and data from diverse populations and gestational stages while adjusting for lifestyle factors may further optimize the predictive performance of the model.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the results of this study confirm that the novel inflammatory markers, namely, the MLR, NLR, SIRI, PLR and SII, are potential predictors of adverse pregnancy outcomes in pregnant women with PCOS, with the PLR and SII strongly associated—which is consistent with the results of the ROC curve analysis and random forest model variable importance ranking. After adjusting for confounding factors (BMI, DBP, and SBP at admission) and hyperparameter optimization, the random forest-based comprehensive prediction model performed well (AUC = 0.815; recall = 79.40%; cross-validated F1 = 0.748), which indicates that it has certain preliminary clinical applicability. This multi-indicator integrated model overcomes the limitations of single-indicator prediction and provides a novel strategy for the shift from empirical risk assessment to data-driven precise risk management in PCOS pregnancies. Subsequent research should focus on expanding the range of covariates (e.g., metabolic indicators, endocrine parameters and lifestyle factors), conducting prospective multicentre studies to validate the general applicability and external validity of the model, and further exploring the potential mechanisms of these inflammatory markers in PCOS-related adverse pregnancy outcomes, thereby facilitating the seamless integration of this model into clinical diagnosis and treatment pathways.

Prognostic Value of Functional Incomplete Revascularization in Patients Undergoing Valve Surgery With Coronary Artery Disease
Authors: Fang Zhang, Wei Gao, Wenshuo Wang, Runda Wu, Yan Xia, Jingpu Wang, Qiyu Zhang, Jinying Zhou, Rende Xu, Zhangwei Chen, Yongxin Sun, Shengxian Tu, Chunsheng Wang, Chenguang Li, Junbo Ge
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCoronary angiography remains standard for assessing lesions in valve surgery patients with concomitant coronary artery disease.ObjectivesThis study assessed the prognostic value of functional evaluation using quantitative flow ratio in these patients.MethodsConsecutive patients undergoing valve surgery (January 2016-December 2021) with ≥1 vessel stenosis (50%-90%) were included. Patients were categorized into complete revascularization (CR) or incomplete revascularization (ICR) groups by functional and traditional anatomical criteria. The primary endpoint was major adverse cardiovascular events ([MACE]; all-cause death, myocardial infarction, unplanned revascularization, stroke).ResultsA total of 750 patients were enrolled in the study, and 244 (32.5%) underwent combined bypass surgery. In the entire cohort, 547 patients (72.9%) met anatomical CR criteria, and 560 patients (74.7%) met functional CR criteria. After median follow-up of 3.0 years (Q1-Q3: 2.0-5.0 years). MACE were significantly increased in the functional ICR group than that in the functional CR group (20.5% [95% CI: 15.4%-26.8%] vs 10.9% [95% CI: 8.6%-13.7%]; HR: 1.98; 95% CI: 1.26-3.11; P<0.001). Meanwhile, no significant differences in MACE rates between the anatomical ICR group and the CR group (16.3% [95% CI: 11.8%-21.9%] vs 12.2% [95% CI: 9.8%-15.3%]; HR: 1.35; 95% CI: 0.87-2.10; P = 0.153). In multivariable regression analysis, functional ICR was an independent predictor for MACE (adjusted HR: 1.63; 95% CI: 1.01-2.64; P = 0.038) compared with functional CR.ConclusionsFunctional ICR in patients undergoing valve surgery with coronary artery disease is associated with increased MACE. These findings support using functional assessment to guide revascularization decisions.

Valvular heart disease (VHD) frequently coexists with coronary artery disease (CAD) due to shared pathophysiological mechanisms, with contemporary registry data demonstrating concomitant CAD prevalence exceeding 40% in patients undergoing valvular surgery.1,2 Unaddressed CAD has been demonstrated to negatively affect the prognosis of surgery for VHD.3 According to previous guidelines, coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) was indicated in patients with “severe” coronary artery disease (>70% stenosis) at the time of valve replacement and was justified in patients with “moderate” CAD (50%-70% stenosis).4 However, the recently updated guidelines do not recommend simultaneous CABG for patients with 50%-70% stenosis.3 Previous studies have demonstrated improved prognosis after complete revascularization (CR) than after incomplete revascularization (ICR) in patients with multivessel disease.5 However, anatomical CR, determined through visual assessment of coronary artery narrowing via angiography, does not provide insights into the physiological impact of the lesions on the myocardium. Therefore, the physiological evaluation of the coronary artery has been recommended to evaluate the functional significance of coronary stenosis.6

Fractional flow reserve (FFR) has emerged as a pioneering technique for the functional evaluation of coronary lesions, demonstrating efficacy in guiding the therapeutic strategy of percutaneous coronary intervention.7,8 Quantitative flow ratio (QFR), derived from coronary angiography without the need for pressure guidewires or vasodilator drugs, has been shown to offer commendable feasibility and accuracy in assessing hemodynamic vessel stenosis, in comparison to FFR.9,10 Recent studies have recommended functional assessment of CAD by QFR to guide revascularization in patients with chronic coronary syndromes.11 Nevertheless, the prognostic implication of functional ICR assessed by QFR in patients undergoing valve surgery and concomitant CAD remains unexplored.

In this study, we retrospectively collected patients who received valve surgery and concomitant CAD. The enrolled patients were divided based on functional and anatomical completeness of revascularization. The prognostic value of functional ICR by QFR was investigated.

Methods

Study design and Participants

In this retrospective study, we consecutively collected patients who underwent angiography and subsequent valve surgery between January 1, 2016 and December 31, 2021 in our hospital. The inclusion criteria were as follows: age ≥18 years; underwent elective valve surgery due to primary valvular heart disease; had a 50%-90% stenosis (visual estimation) for at least 1 coronary artery, including left anterior descending, left circumflex, right coronary artery, or their primary branches ≥2 mm in diameter; whether or not concomitant CABG was performed. The exclusion criteria were the following: diameter stenosis <50% or >90%; history of heart surgery; secondary VHD (ischemia, cardiomyopathy); quality of preoperative coronary angiography is insufficient for QFR analysis; recent myocardial infarction (<30 days). This study design and its procedures were in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and recommendations for Good Clinical Practice and were approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Zhongshan Hospital, Fudan University. All patients provided informed written consent for the publication of their study data.

Angiography and surgical procedures

Angiography was performed several days before valve surgery and the diameter stenosis for a reference vessel diameter ≥2 mm was assessed by visual estimation. The anatomical degree of stenosis was first screened from the reports and subsequently confirmed by the investigators during the QFR calculation. For valve replacement or repair, the surgery was performed according to local routine of our hospital. The operation type and specific procedures were conducted at the discretion of the operators. We collected the information of the type of valve surgery, extracorporeal circulation time, aortic cross-clamp time, amount of bleeding, and volume of transfusion. Postoperatively, patients were routinely transferred to the cardiac intensive care unit, and the patient's EuroSCORE (European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation)12 and APACHE II (Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation) score13 were also documented.

QFR analysis

QFR analysis was conducted retrospectively in all eligible coronary arteries with a reference vessel diameter of 2 mm or larger.14 For each lesion, at least 1 angiographic image with complete contrast filling and the target vessel fully exposing the lesion site was selected for QFR calculation in the second generation of QFR system, the Murray-law based QFR, using the AngioPlus core software (Pulse Medical), following standard operation procedures as previously described.10,15 The contrast flow velocity was automatically calculated by the software. Subsequently, the system automatically outlined the contours of the detected vessels, and if the tracked vessel contours did not follow the true lumen edge, the lumen contour was manually adjusted. Based on Murray's law of branching, the software generated the reference lumen of the main branch vessels as well as the side branches, thereby automatically calculating the Murray-law based QFR pullback.10 The software also reports the reference vessel diameter, minimum lumen diameter, minimum lumen area, and diameter/area stenosis percentage. The analysis was performed by a certified analyst who was blinded to the clinical or any follow-up information of the patients.

Definition of CR

The anatomical CR in this study was defined as revascularizing all vessels displaying a reduction in luminal diameter of ≥70% in principal coronary arteries or ≥50% in the left main coronary artery as current guideline recommended. This included the following scenarios. 1) For patients who did not undergo concomitant CABG and received only valve surgery, the left main stenosis was <50% and all principal coronary arteries had <70% stenosis. 2) For patients who underwent concomitant CABG, all lesions with ≥50% stenosis in the left main artery and ≥70% stenosis in the principal coronary arteries were revascularized through bypass grafting. Patients who did not meet either of these 2 criteria were classified as having anatomical ICR. The definition of functional CR was defined as grafting all diseased vessels with a QFR ≤0.80. This also included the following 2 scenarios: 1) for patients who did not undergo concomitant CABG and received only valve surgery, all principal coronary arteries had a QFR >0.80; and 2) for patients who underwent concomitant CABG, all principal coronary arteries with a QFR ≤0.80 were revascularized through bypass grafting. Patients who did not meet either of these 2 criteria were classified as having functional ICR.

Follow-up and outcome

Clinical follow-up data for all endpoints were collected from our institution's clinical database or conducted through outpatient visits or telephone interviews, with the follow-up period ranging from 2 to 7 years (median follow-up: 3.0 years [Q1-Q3: 2.0-5.0 years]) following the surgical intervention. The loss to follow-up rate in this study was 7.1% (58 of 821). The clinical outcome was defined as a composite of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), including all-cause death, myocardial infarction, unplanned coronary revascularization, and stroke. Myocardial infarction was diagnosed according to the Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction in 2018.16

Statistical analyses

Categorical variables are shown as frequencies with percentages and continuous variables are displayed as mean ± SD or median (Q1-Q3). For normally distributed data, the independent samples Student’s t-test was used to compare 2 independent groups. For continuous variables that were not normally distributed, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used for paired data and the Mann-Whitney U test for independent groups. For categorical variables, Fisher exact test or chi-square test was used. The Kaplan-Meier test was used to estimate the incidence of MACE, all-cause death, myocardial infarction, revascularization, and stroke, and the differences in event rates among the 2 groups were compared using a log-rank test. The incidence rates of specific time points of each event were estimated from the Kaplan-Meier analysis. Univariate and multivariate analyses were performed to identify independent predictors of the primary endpoint. After univariate analysis, multivariate Cox hazard regression analyses were performed using the covariates that were considered potential confounding factors with the clinical events of interest from previous reports (age, sex, smoking, hypertension, diabetes, concomitant CABG, left ventricular ejection fraction <50%, anatomical ICR, functional ICR, beta-blocker, and angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor/angiotensin II receptor blockers). The results of the multiple variable Cox analyses were reported as HRs with 95% CIs and P values. The proportional hazard assumption was assessed and satisfied graphically by plotting log (−log) survival curves against log survival time for each group and verifying that the curves were parallel. All tests were performed 2-sided and a significance level of P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. All statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS software package (version 17.0, IBM).

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 750 patients were included into this study (Figure 1). The average age was 64.3 ± 8.0 years and 68.1% (511) were male (Table 1). Anatomic CR was achieved in 547 patients (72.9%) and functional CR was achieved in 560 patients (74.7%) (Figure 2A). Compared with functional CR group, patients in the functional ICR group exhibited a reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (59.4 ± 9.1 in functional ICR group vs 61.7 ± 8.4 in functional CR group; P = 0.002). Furthermore, a higher proportion of patients in the functional CR group received beta-blocker (72.5% [406 of 560] vs 64.7% [123 of 190]; P = 0.043) and ACE inhibitor/angiotensin II receptor blockers (50.5% [283 of 560] vs 42.1% [80 of 190]; P = 0.045) therapy than those in the functional ICR group. Clinical outcomes

The total MACE rate was 13.3% (100 of 750) during the follow-up period. The MACE rates according to anatomical and functional definitions were 12.2%, 16.3%, 10.9%, and 20.5%, respectively (Figure 2B). Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that there was no significant difference between anatomical ICR and CR groups in the incidence of MACE (16.3% [95% CI: 11.8%-21.9%] vs 12.2% [95% CI: 9.8%-15.3%]; HR: 1.35; 95% CI: 0.87-2.10; log-rank P = 0.153) (Figure 3A). In this retrospective study, the authors found that among patients undergoing valve surgery and concomitant coronary artery disease (CAD), the anatomical and functional incomplete revascularization (ICR) was 27.1% and 25.3%, respectively. Kaplan-Meier curves showed that functional ICR (B), but not anatomical ICR (A), was associated with increased major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) rate during a follow-up of 3.7 years. CR = complete revascularization; QFR = quantitative flow ratio.

Assessment of CAD

CAD is frequently present among patients with VHD and uncorrected CAD has been shown to negatively affect both perioperative17,18 and late outcomes of surgery for VHD.19 Few data are available on long-term survival of patients undergoing combined valvular surgery and CABG. According to previous guidelines, CABG was indicated in patients with severe CAD (>70% stenosis) at the time of valve replacement and was justified in patients with moderate CAD (50%-70% stenosis).4 However, the recently updated guidelines do not recommend simultaneous CABG for patients with 50%-70% stenosis.3 Previous data from the New York Cardiac Surgery Reporting System of >10,000 patients undergoing cardiac surgery showed a 4.4% mortality rate for isolated valve surgery compared with 8.9% for valve surgery combined with CABG,20 indicating that there is still debate over whether complete revascularization should be performed for CAD during valve surgery.

The importance of functional assessment

Visual estimation remains the primary method for assessing the severity of coronary artery stenosis and determining whether to perform revascularization. However, this method has some limitations and may overestimate or underestimate the functional impact of the lesion on myocardial perfusion, highlighting the importance of functional assessment beyond anatomical measures. Previous studies have demonstrated the importance of FFR in determining the hemodynamic significance of coronary artery stenosis and guiding revascularization. The applicability of FFR in patients undergoing CABG has also been investigated in previous studies.21,22 QFR is a replacement for FFR that offers a noninvasive method to assess the physiological significance of coronary artery stenosis without the need for pressure wires or hyperemic agents.23 QFR has been demonstrated to have substantial applicability in patients with 3-vessel disease,24 showing the value of QFR in assessing multivessel disease. Approximately 30%-40% of patients who were initially deemed suitable for medical therapy based on angiographic evaluations could be reclassified for revascularization therapy according to FFR assessment.25,26 The reclassification between medical and revascularization therapy was also observed in a QFR study.27 Our results about the discordance in measurement by anatomical assessment and QFR were similar to those of previous studies. This reclassification underscores the discrepancy between anatomical and functional assessments of CAD. The functional assessment can reveal that lesions, which appear moderate in severity on angiography, may have a significant impact on the blood flow to the myocardium, necessitating revascularization to improve patient outcomes. Aortic valve disease (particularly aortic stenosis) might alter coronary hemodynamics and potentially affect the accuracy of QFR measurements. However, this issue has been addressed in previous studies, which demonstrated that QFR retains good diagnostic performance even in patients with severe aortic stenosis and significantly outperforms angiographic visual assessment in this population.28,29 Additionally, our subgroup analysis revealed that QFR provided consistent measurements in both aortic valve and non–aortic valve patients, and the similar MACE rates further indicated that the presence of aortic valve disease did not materially influence the conclusions of our study.

Outcomes after surgery

In this study, we observed that patients in the anatomical ICR group had a numerically higher risk of all-cause mortality compared to those with complete revascularization (11.8% [95% CI: 7.8%-17.1%] vs 7.9% [95% CI: 5.8%-10.5%]; HR: 1.52; 95% CI: 0.92-2.50; log-rank P = 0.095), although the difference was not statistically significant. In contrast, the functional ICR group demonstrated a significantly higher all-cause mortality rate (12.6% [95% CI: 8.4%-18.4%] vs 7.7% [95% CI: 5.7%-10.3%]; HR: 1.65; 95% CI: 1.00-2.71; log-rank P = 0.045). Further analysis of cardiac-specific endpoints revealed that patients in the functional ICR group had a significantly increased rate of repeat revascularization (3.2% [95% CI: 1.3%-6.8%] vs 0.9% [95% CI: 0.3%-2.1%]; HR: 3.63; 95% CI: 1.11-11.89; log-rank P = 0.022), along with a trend toward a higher incidence of myocardial infarction (3.2% [95% CI: 1.3%-6.8%] vs 1.3% [95% CI: 0.5%-2.6%]; HR: 2.61; 95% CI: 0.88-7.77; log-rank P = 0.073). These findings suggest that functional incomplete revascularization may be associated with a greater risk of ischemic events in patients undergoing valve surgery with concomitant CAD. The adverse prognostic implications of leaving functionally significant lesions untreated are supported by prior studies demonstrating that lesions with QFR ≤0.80 are associated with increased rates of adverse cardiac events, including myocardial infarction and the need for unplanned revascularization.30,31 In this context, QFR-guided assessment offers valuable physiological insight beyond anatomical stenosis severity and may more accurately identify lesions with ischemic potential. Our findings further underscore the clinical relevance of achieving complete functional revascularization in this high-risk surgical population, because physiologically incomplete revascularization may contribute to residual ischemia and subsequent cardiovascular events.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to identify the association between functional ICR and worse prognosis in patients undergoing valve surgery and concomitant CAD. Previous studies have only demonstrated the impact of combined or noncombined bypass surgery on prognosis during valve surgery. For instance, a study by Kainuma et al32 on restrictive mitral annuloplasty with or without CABG in mitral regurgitation showed that combined CABG is associated with better long-term survival rates and event-free survival rates. However, a study on aortic valve replacement with or without coronary artery bypass surgery reached the opposite conclusion, indicating that the operative mortality rate of combined aortic valve replacement and CABG surgery is higher than that of aortic valve replacement or isolated CABG surgery.33 These studies merely focused on assessing the prognostic impact of bypass or no bypass using anatomical methods, neglecting the role of QFR-guided functional assessment, which has resulted in inconsistent findings. A previous study by Zhang et al34 reported QFR-guided percutaneous coronary intervention improved the likelihood of achieving functional CR and 1-year clinical outcomes in an all-comer population of patients with CAD. Taken together, the current study suggested that QFR-guided functional CR in concomitant CAD could benefit patients with a primary indication for valvular surgery. The combination of angiography and QFR can offer a more precise evaluation of CAD, enabling clinicians to better identify areas that may benefit from revascularization.

Study limitations

First, the primary limitation of this study lies in its retrospective and observational nature, which inherently does not exclude the possibility of selection bias, the confounding of indications, and the under-reporting of events. Second, given the retrospective nature of this study, we selected all-cause mortality rather than cardiac-specific mortality as the endpoint, because all-cause mortality is more clearly defined and less prone to misclassification. However, this approach may introduce confounding from noncardiac causes of death that are unrelated to anatomical or functional ICR. Third, this is a single-center study that included only Chinese patients. Fourth, the QFR analysis is influenced by the quality of angiographic image acquisition. The retrospective nature of the present study might influence the accuracy of QFR assessment. Thus, prospective studies are warrant to verify the current findings. Fifth, in some studies, anatomical CR was defined as successful revascularization of all coronary artery lesions or segments ≥1.5 mm in diameter. In this study, we included lesions ≥2 mm in diameter for the analysis of QFR.

Conclusions

In patients with a primary indication for valvular surgery and concomitant CAD, there was a higher rate of MACE in patients with functional ICR. This finding highlights the importance of conducting functional analysis for coronary stenosis to guide appropriate revascularization therapy decisions.

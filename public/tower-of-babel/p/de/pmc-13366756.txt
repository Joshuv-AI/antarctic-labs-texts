Stability indicating HPLC method development and validation for the determination of organic impurities of cariprazine hydrochloride, their characterization by LC-MS and NMR techniques
Authors: Rajesh Kumar, Shailendra Kumar Dubey, Har Lal Singh, Amlan Kumar Das, Sarita Khaturia, Pankaj Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCariprazine hydrochloride is an antipsychotic drug used to treat diseases such as schizophrenia and acute mania in adults. Quality determination of the cariprazine drug molecule is a critical aspect to ensure maximum drug efficacy and to minimize side effects caused by unwanted substances commonly referred to as impurities, formed during manufacturing. Hence, this study aims to develop a chromatographic method for the determination of organic impurities present in cariprazine hydrochloride. A stability-indicating and robust method was developed using reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). The analytical method involves the simple preparation of a mobile phase consisting of 0.2% orthophosphoric acid in HPLC grade water as mobile phase A, and acetonitrile as mobile phase B. Separation was achieved using a YMC Pack Pro C18 column (150 mm × 4.6 mm, 3 μm), with a step gradient at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min and detection at λmax 215 nm for the quantitation of organic impurities. The method is capable of detecting impurities at the 0.01% level and quantifying them at the 0.03% level with respect to the test concentration. It was found to be linear, accurate, and precise over the range from the LOQ to 200% of the specification limit of impurities. The method was specific to all degradation products formed during forced degradation studies, thereby demonstrating its stability-indicating nature. The mass balance was found to be within the tolerance limit of 95–105%. All impurities, including process-related and degradation products, were characterized using mass spectrometry and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy. The spectroscopic data confirmed the structures of the impurities. Overall, the method is useful for determining the quality of cariprazine hydrochloride in quality control.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13065-026-01838-6.Keywords: Reverse-phase, High-performance liquid chromatography, Forced degradation, Organic impurities, Stability indicating, Quality determination

Introduction

Cariprazine is an atypical antipsychotic [1–4] drug that was first developed in Hungary by the pharmaceutical company Gedeon Richter Plc. in the early 2000s. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) first approved Cariprazine under the brand name Vraylar in September 2015 for the treatment of schizophrenia [5–8] and acute manic or mixed episodes associated with bipolar disorder [9–11] in adults. The cariprazine hydrochloride has the following structure [12], chemical formula, and molecular weight are given in Fig. 1.

Structure, chemical formula and molecular weight of Cariprazine HCl

Cariprazine HCl is available in various dosage forms with strengths of 1.5 mg, 3 mg, and 6 mg (data obtained from DrugBank) and is formulated as hard gelatin capsules for oral administration. The maximum daily dose (MDD) of cariprazine is 6 mg per day [13–15]. Cariprazine acts as a partial agonist at central dopamine D2, dopamine D3, and serotonin 5-HT1A receptors, and as an antagonist at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors. Cariprazine has been investigated in a variety of psychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and major depressive disorder.

As the synthesis of this moiety involves multi-step chemical reactions [16, 17], it leads to the formation of impurities in the final drug substance, which may have undesired pharmacological effects [18–20]. A thorough survey of the literature on impurity quantification in the finished product revealed that only a few reports [21–23] are available. These are mainly focused on the assay of the cariprazine molecule rather than its associated impurities. Some are involved in the quantification of cariprazine in human plasma only. The available studies primarily address mass determination of impurities formed during the forced degradation by liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) rather than their quantitative analysis. A clear comparison between the proposed method and the literature methods is provided in Table S1 of the supplementary. The presence of impurities can have an adverse impact on health if they exceed the recommended limits.

Hence, this study aims to develop a first-ever analytical method for the simultaneous identification and quantitative determination of cariprazine hydrochloride impurities (related substances/organic impurities) using high‑performance liquid chromatography/ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC/UHPLC) [24–31]. The study also seeks to perform forced degradation [32, 33] under optimized conditions to evaluate the stability-indicating nature of the analytical method, followed by its validation. According to ICH guideline Q3A (R2) [34], the limits for impurities are very stringent; all qualified impurities must not exceed 0.15%, whereas identified and unidentified impurities must not exceed 0.10%. Therefore, the major challenge is to develop a sensitive, precise, and reproducible method capable of detecting impurities in drug substances even at the reporting threshold level (0.05% with respect to the drug).

Materials and methodology

Materials and samples

Cariprazine (CRI) and its impurities were synthesized in our laboratory. Impurity‑3 and Impurity‑4 were procured from GBS Lifesciences LLP, Hyderabad, India. The purities of all impurities were between 93% and 99% (see Table S2 in the supplementary). Ultrapure deionized water, used in the preparation of the diluent and mobile phase, was obtained from an Avidity Cascada water purification system purchased from China. HPLC-grade acetonitrile, methanol, and LC–MS grade acetonitrile were procured from Avantor (VWR Lab Products Private Limited, India). Avantor also supplied analytical-grade hydrochloric acid. HPLC-grade orthophosphoric acid was procured from Merck (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany). LC–MS grade formic acid, used in mobile phase preparation for LC–MS, was acquired from Honeywell (New York, NJ, USA). Sodium hydroxide and hydrogen peroxide, used in the forced degradation studies, were obtained from Loba Chemie Pvt. Ltd. (India) and Romil (UK), respectively.

Instruments

Method development and validation were conducted using a Thermo Scientific Ultimate 3000 UHPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), a Shimadzu LC‑2010 CHT HPLC system (Japan), and a Vanquish Core HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The HPLC column, YMC Pro Pack C18 (150 mm × 4.6 mm, 3 μm), used for separation, was procured from YMC Co. Ltd. (Japan). The mass of the impurities was determined using a TQS Plus Triple Quadrupole MS (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectra were recorded using a 400 MHz NMR spectrophotometer, model Avance‑NEO (Bruker, Germany). All the samples were weighed on a Sartorius analytical balance, model MSA 225P‑100 DU (Germany).

Instrumental analysis

Preparation of impurity stock solution-I for system suitability

Accurately weighed about 7.5 mg each of Impurity-1, Impurity-2, Impurity-3, Impurity-4, Impurity-5, Impurity-6, Impurity-7, Impurity-8, and transferred to a 50 mL volumetric flask. Add 30 mL of diluent, sonicate to dissolve, then dilute to volume with diluent and mix well. Pipette out 5 mL of the above solution into another 50 mL volumetric flask and dilute to volume with diluent. Mixed thoroughly.

Preparation of system suitability solution

Accurately weighed about 50 mg of Cariprazine Hydrochloride standard and transferred to a 50 mL volumetric flask. Added 15 mL of diluent and sonicated to dissolve completely. Pipette out 5 mL of Impurity Stock Solution-I into the flask for system suitability, then diluted to the volume with diluent and mixed thoroughly.

Preparation of diluted standard

Accurately weighed about 50 mg of Cariprazine Hydrochloride working standard and transferred to a 50 mL volumetric flask. Added 30 mL of diluent, sonicated to dissolve and diluted to the mark with diluent and mixed thoroughly. Pipette out 1mL of the above solution into a 100 mL volumetric flask and dilute to the volume with diluent and mix thoroughly. Further pipette out 5mL of the above solution into a 50 mL volumetric flask and diluted to the volume with diluent and mixed thoroughly.

Preparation of sample solution

Accurately weighed about 50 mg of the test sample and transferred to a 50 mL volumetric flask. Added approximately 10–15 mL of diluent, sonicated to dissolve and diluted to the mark with diluent. Mixed thoroughly.

Preparation of mobile phase

Preparation of mobile phase-A: Prepared a 0.2% solution (2 mL/L) of orthophosphoric acid in HPLC-grade water. Filtered the solution through a 0.45 μm membrane filter and mixed thoroughly. Preparation of Mobile phase-B: Acetonitrile HPLC grade.

Preparation of diluent

Prepared a homogeneous mixture of 0.1 N HCl and methanol in a 50:50 (v/v) ratio. Mixed thoroughly.

Chromatographic conditions

The optimized condition was finalized on a YMC Pro Pack C18 column (150 mm × 4.6 mm), 3 μm, maintained at 25 °C with a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min and UV detection at 215 nm. The HPLC gradient program was set as follows: Time (min) / % of solvent B: 0/10, 40/40, 50/75, 55/75, 57/10, 65/10.

LC-MS parameters

A spectrometer was used to determine the direct mass of the impurities and Cariprazine HCl. The mobile phase consisted of 0.1% formic acid in a 50:50 (v/v) mixture of water and acetonitrile. It was operated in isocratic mode with a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. The sample temperature was maintained at 25 °C, and the mass range was set from 50 to 2000 Da. The mass spectrophotometer (MS) parameters used were as follows: ion source - ESI mode, positive ion spray voltage − 3500 V, negative ion spray voltage − 2500 V, sheath gas (arb) (N2) − 50mL/min, aux gas (arb) (N2) − 10mL/min, sweep gas (arb) (N2) − 0mL/min, ion transfer tube temperature − 325 °C, and vaporizer temperature − 350 °C.

NMR instrumental parameters

The 1H and 13C NMR spectra for structural elucidation were recorded using a 400 MHz NMR spectrophotometer. The spectra of Cariprazine and its impurities were obtained in deuterated chloroform (CDCl3) and deuterated dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO‑d6), depending on the solubility of the compound. All NMR spectra are given in Fig. S2B and Fig. S2C in the supplementary.

Analytical method development

Analytical method development is of great importance in pharmaceuticals to ensure that the medicine a patient consumes is safe and effective. This study aims to develop an analytical method capable of determining the amounts of impurities present in the Cariprazine drug substance, whether arising from the process or from degradation, followed by validation to ensure its reliability in providing accurate, precise, and reproducible results. The validation of the analytical procedure was performed as per ICH guideline Q2(R2) [38]. For impurity determination, the following aspects are critical and are given utmost consideration.

Solubility of Cariprazine Hydrochloride

The solubility of Cariprazine Hydrochloride is directly related to the selection of an appropriate diluent for analysis. The chosen diluent must be inert and unreactive toward the drug molecule and its impurities under investigation. Since the analyte is the hydrochloride salt of Cariprazine, 0.1 N hydrochloric acid was selected as the diluent in combination with methanol to increase the solubility of all the organic compounds in the sample matrix. Furthermore, the stability of the sample solution was evaluated in the selected diluent at a low sample temperature to ensure prolonged stability. The test sample remained intact in the solution, thereby supporting the rationale behind the chosen diluent.

Wavelength selection

A UV-VIS detector was employed for the detection of impurities, making wavelength selection a critical aspect of method development. All components must exhibit a significant response at the chosen wavelength to ensure reliable quantification. Based on the UV spectra of Cariprazine and its impurities (refer to Fig. S1 in the supplementary), a wavelength of 215 nm was selected. At this wavelength, all impurities demonstrated significant absorbance, allowing simultaneous detection of Cariprazine Hydrochloride and its related impurities with adequate sensitivity.

Mobile phase selection

The pKa value of Cariprazine is approximately 8, which lies on the basic side of the pH scale. Therefore, an acidic mobile phase was preselected to ensure optimal chromatographic performance. In addition, stationary phases (HPLC columns) exhibit greater stability under acidic conditions, further supporting this choice. Consequently, 0.2% v/v orthophosphoric acid was selected as mobile phase A, while 100% acetonitrile was chosen as mobile phase B due to the low UV cut-off and higher elution strength compared to methanol. This combination provided improved resolution and reliable detection of Cariprazine and its impurities.

Stationary phase selection

The HPLC column is considered the heart of the chromatographic system, as all separations occur within it. Therefore, column selection is a critical aspect of analytical method development, directly influencing the reproducibility of results and the overall column lifespan. At the initial stage of method development, a C18 column was identified as the preferred choice because of its broad applicability and strong retention characteristics for nonpolar compounds. Several C18 phases, including Inertsil ODS C18, Zorbax SB C18, Phenomenex C18, and YMC C18, were evaluated. Among these columns, the YMC C18 column provided the best separation performance. Consequently, the YMC Pack Pro C18 column was selected for further optimization and finalization of the method development.

Optimization of chromatographic conditions

This method was developed in parallel for various processes and intermediate stages of the product. A major challenge encountered during the development of the Cariprazine method was achieving adequate separation between Cariprazine and impurity‑5. Initially, impurity‑5 was not considered; however, during oxidative forced degradation studies, this impurity was generated and eluted between Cariprazine and impurity‑6. The resolution among these three peaks was insufficient, necessitating fine‑tuning of both the gradient and the stationary phase to improve separation. Additionally, methanol was avoided as a solvent due to its tendency to cause high baseline drift at the selected wavelength of 215 nm. The final chromatographic condition was subjected to a change in the critical parameters, change in the column oven temperature, gradient and pH to ensure the finalized condition is a robust one. The change in the retention time and resolution between the critical pairs due to a change in parameters is recorded in the table S3 (B) in the supplementary. In any of the conditions, a significant change in the resolution between cariprazine and impurity-5, impurity-5 and impurity-6 was not observed. At 45℃ column oven temperature, resolution between cariprazine and impurity-5 seems to be increased, and simultaneously decreased between impurity-5 and impurity-6. Hence, a high column oven temperature was not preferred.

The drug substance was found to degrade only under two stress conditions: acid hydrolysis and oxidative degradation. LC-MS Method development

The chromatographic conditions applied for the LC–MS method development were the same as those optimized for the HPLC method. However, the mobile phase composition was slightly modified: mobile phase A consisted of 0.1% formic acid (instead of 0.2% orthophosphoric acid), and mobile phase B was acetonitrile. This adjustment was made to ensure compatibility with mass spectrometric detection. The replacement of 0.2% orthophosphoric acid with 0.1% formic acid results in early elution of all the peaks except impurity 8, but the elution order remains the same. The spiked chromatogram of LC-MS containing 0.1% formic acid in the mobile phase is given in Fig. S3 in the supplementary. The detailed mass spectrometric parameters employed for analysis are provided in Sect. "LC-MS parameters".

Genesis of the impurities (process/degradation)

Impurity‑1 is an intermediate formed during synthesis that can carry over to the final stage of the product. It has also been identified as a degradation impurity under acidic hydrolysis; therefore, during manufacturing and storage, exposure of the drug substance to hydrolytic conditions may lead to the formation of this impurity. Impurity‑2 corresponds to one of the key starting materials (KSM) used in the synthesis of Cariprazine and may carry over to the final stage. Impurities‑3, 4, and 5 are formed through the oxidative degradation of the Cariprazine drug molecule. Impurity‑6 is generated via acidic hydrolysis during manufacturing. Impurity‑7 is a dimer of Cariprazine that can arise during synthesis. Finally, Impurity‑8 is formed through the coupling of Impurity‑2 with one of the raw materials used in the synthetic process.

The results indicated that the United States Pharmacopeia (USP) theoretical plates of CRI and its impurities exceeded 100,000, the USP resolution between the two closest compounds was greater than 4.3, and the peak asymmetry for all analytes (impurity peaks) ranged between 0.96 and 1.08 (refer to Table 1A). Specificity/selectivity

The related substances method for Cariprazine Hydrochloride was demonstrated to be specific by spiking individual impurities into the test sample. All impurities were well separated from each other and from the Cariprazine (CRI) peak under the optimized chromatographic conditions. Furthermore, specificity was confirmed using a PDA detector, where all peaks were found spectrally pure, supporting that the method is free from interference and reliably specific. The peak purity of all the peaks is given in Table S4 in the supplementary.

LOD, LOQ and linearity

Using the least squares method, linear regression analysis of the response values of sample solutions at different concentrations was performed to calculate the slope and intercept. The measurements confirmed a positive linear relationship between response value and concentration over the range of 0.3–3 µg/mL. The LOD and LOQ of the method were set at 0.01% and 0.03%, respectively, with respect to the test concentration (1000 µg/mL), corresponding to absolute concentrations of 0.1 µg/mL and 0.3 µg/mL for CRI and its related impurities. The signal-to-noise (S/N) ratios for all impurities were greater than 3 at the LOD level and greater than 10 at the LOQ level. The precision at the LOQ level for CRI and its related impurities was well within the acceptance criteria (%RSD ≤ 10). The %RSD of the peak area of Cariprazine and its impurities is given in Table S5 in the supplementary.

Accuracy and precision

The recovery values of different impurities spiked into the sample solution at concentration levels of 0.03%, 0.15%, and 0.225% with respect to the test concentration were calculated against the standard solution of CRI injected at the 0.10% level. The recovery of all impurities was found to be within 80%–120%, demonstrating that the method is accurate. Table 1B presents the recovery values of different impurities at various concentration levels, with each level analyzed in triplicate. The precision of the method was evaluated through parallel preparation of six individual accuracy solutions, in which all impurities were spiked at the specification level, and the recovered amounts were calculated against the CRI standard. Precision was assessed by calculating the %RSD of each impurity recovered from six individual spiked sample preparations, as shown in Table S6 in the supplementary, which was found to be less than 10%, indicating that the method is precise.

Range of the analytical method

The range of the method is defined as the interval between the lowest and highest levels of impurities that can be accurately, precisely, and reproducibly quantified under the validated conditions. For Cariprazine Hydrochloride, the method range was established from 0.03% to 0.30% with respect to the test concentration for all impurities. This range encompasses the LOQ level and extends to the upper limit of the specification, confirming that the method is suitable for impurity quantification across the reportable range.

Robustness

To demonstrate the method’s insensitivity to minor variations in chromatographic parameters, the method was subjected to deliberate changes, namely an increase in column oven temperature (30 ℃), a decrease in flow rate (0.90 mL/min), an increase in flow rate (1.10 mL/min), and the use of a different column, all within the allowable tolerance limits for robustness. The impact of these changes on system suitability, specifically the resolution between critical pairs of closely eluted peaks, was recorded in Table S7 in the supplementary. Based on these results, the method was found to be robust under all tested conditions.

Sample solution stability and mobile phase stability

Solution stability of spiked sample was performed up to 43 h at 10℃ autosampler temperature, and the sample solution was found stable up to 43 h. The stability of the mobile phase was evaluated from the longest sequence in the method validation. The mobile phase was found to be stable up to 54 h at room temperature.

Comparison of the degradation study results with reported data and classification of impurities

Based on the analysis of multiple bulk drug batches, forced degradation study, and the available literature, impurities were classified as either process‑related or degradation impurities. The mass spectral information, structures, and sources of these impurities are provided in Table S8 in the supplementary. The results of the degradation study were compared with the literature method [20], and the similarities and differences are summarised in Table 3.

During base hydrolysis, prolonged exposure to harsh conditions for several days or upon heating caused the sample to precipitate, necessitating filtration before injection. As a result, no peaks of cariprazine or its degradants were observed in the chromatograms. Therefore, in base hydrolysis, the sample was not subjected to excessively harsh conditions, which explains why no degradation occurred. Furthermore, DP‑4 and DP‑5 were not formed under the proposed method. This may be attributed to the use of different reagents for degradation, as reported in the literature.

Conclusion

An accurate, precise, selective, and stability-indicating HPLC method was successfully developed for the separation and determination of eight impurities in Cariprazine Hydrochloride (CRI). The method was validated in accordance with the ICH guideline Q2(R2), covering accuracy, precision, limit of detection (LOD), limit of quantitation (LOQ), linearity, specificity, and range. All the validation parameters met the acceptance criteria, confirming the robustness and reliability of the procedure. The test samples were subjected to forced degradation under acidic and basic hydrolysis, oxidative, thermal, and photolytic conditions. Analysis of the exposed samples under optimized chromatographic conditions demonstrated that all the degradation impurities were well separated from the Cariprazine peak and process‑related impurities. The mass balance evaluation of the degraded samples against the undegraded sample was consistently within the acceptable range of 95% to 105%, confirming the stability-indicating nature of the method. A comparison of the degradation data obtained in this study with reference data revealed a similar degradation pathway, with the exception of impurity‑5, which was observed under mild oxidative conditions in the proposed study but was not reported in the reference data. Six of the eight impurities were synthesized in‑house and characterized using spectroscopic techniques, further supporting the identification and confirmation of their impurity profiles. Overall, the developed method is suitable for routine quality control analysis of Cariprazine Hydrochloride, providing reliable determination of related substances and ensuring compliance with regulatory requirements.

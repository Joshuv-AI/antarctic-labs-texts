Factors influencing rehabilitation costs for stroke inpatients in Shenzhen: a multicenter retrospective study
Authors: Mingchao Zhou, Ruixue Ye, Jie Guo, Xiaolong Zhu, Fubing Zha, Jiao Luo, Meiling Huang, Linlin Shan, Dongxia Li, Yulong Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundStroke is a leading cause of death and disability among Chinese residents, imposing a significant economic burden. This study aimed to analyze the current status and influencing factors of inpatient rehabilitation costs for stroke in Shenzhen, providing evidence to help reduce costs for patients with stroke in rehabilitation centers.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective study used data primarily collected from patient’s medical records. We included patients admitted to the rehabilitation departments of public and private hospitals in Shenzhen from January 1, 2019, to October 31, 2021, who met the diagnostic criteria for stroke. After data cleaning, we collected information on hospital demographics, diagnoses, functional impairments, length of stay, and hospitalization costs. Multiple linear regression analysis was used to create a path analysis model to assess the direct and indirect effects of various factors on total hospitalization and average daily expenditures.ResultsThe study included 2638 patients with stroke from 22 hospitals in Shenzhen, with 69.6% males and an average age of 59.2 years. Ischemic and hemorrhagic strokes accounted for 56% and 44% of the cases, respectively. The median total hospitalization costs were 27,306.64 Chinese Yuan (CNY) (4022.79 USD) for ischemic stroke and 32,188.25 CNY (4022.79 USD) for hemorrhagic stroke, while the average daily costs were 848.64 CNY (125.87 USD) and 828.23 CNY (125.87 USD), respectively. The median length of stay (LOS) was 30 days for patients with ischemic stroke and 41 days for patients with hemorrhagic stroke. Rehabilitation accounted for the highest cost category (64% and 68%, respectively). Path analysis revealed that LOS was the primary direct factor influencing total hospitalization costs, while hospital grades, functional impairment levels, disease stage, and age were indirect factors. For average daily costs, the primary direct influencing factor was hospital grades, with functional impairment levels, disease stage, and age as indirect factors.ConclusionsPatients with stroke in Shenzhen face significant financial burden. The government should continuously promote the construction of a three-tiered rehabilitation system and hierarchical LOS management to provide systematic and sustainable medical services. Daily costs should be stratified based on functional impairment levels, disease stage, and age to guide the reform of precision bed-day medical insurance payments for stroke rehabilitation.Trial registrationThe study protocol was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR-2000034067, Registration date: 22 June 2020, http://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.aspx?proj=54770).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24165-2.

Background

Stroke is a leading cause of death and disability in China and globally [1]. Many stroke survivors suffer from various functional impairments, requiring extended rehabilitation to restore neurological function, improve their quality of life, and return to their family and society [2]. This leads to persistently high medical rehabilitation costs for patients with stroke. Studies have found that Stroke-related medical expenses account for approximately 34% of global healthcare spending, ranking second cost burden among all diseases in China [3, 4]. As the number of stroke hospitalizations and average cost per hospitalization continue to increase in China, the economic burden on families and society has exacerbated [5, 6].

Shenzhen is one of seven special economic zones in China, faces rapid economic development, urbanization, and an aging population, and medical rehabilitation costs for stroke patients [7, 8]. Understanding the current status of inpatient rehabilitation costs for stroke and analyzing its influencing factors can help in developing reasonable measures to control excessive medical cost growth. Therefore, this study retrospectively analyzed the characteristics and influencing factors of medical expenses for stroke rehabilitation inpatients in Shenzhen from 2019 to 2021. This study aimed to explore evidence-based strategies to alleviate the economic burden and provide a reference for developing a suitable rehabilitation medical insurance payment scheme for stroke inpatients.

Methods

Study design

This is a retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The Data were gathered from a clinical study titled “The Promotion of Application research of Longshi Scale” managed by the rehabilitation department of Shenzhen Second People’s Hospital, Shenzhen, China.

Subjects

The cases in this study were patients with stroke who received inpatient rehabilitation treatment at 22 public and private hospitals (including specialized rehabilitation hospitals) in Shenzhen City. Data were collected from the medical records for patients admitted between January 1, 2019, to October 31, 2021, and the data collection process was conducted between November 15, 2021, to December 15, 2021.The Diagnostic criteria for stroke in this study were based on the “China Stroke Prevention and Treatment Series Guideline” developed by the National Health and Family Planning Commission Stroke Prevention Project Committee in 2015 [9]. The inclusion criteria were: (1) Patients admitted to the rehabilitation department of 22 hospitals in Shenzhen during the study period; (2) All cases met the diagnostic criteria for stroke; (3) Age ≥ 18 years; (4) Duration of illness > 48 h. Consequently, the exclusion criteria were: (1) patients admitted for rehabilitation due to brain nerve damage caused by non-cerebrovascular diseases, such as traumatic brain injury, brain tumors, encephalitis, and meningitis; (2) patients admitted for rehabilitation due to paralysis caused by other neurological, muscular, or joint injuries, including fractures, arthritis, spinal cord injuries, cervical spondylosis, hand injuries, peripheral neuropathies, and lumbar disc herniation; and (3) patients deemed unsuitable for participation in this study, such as those who voluntarily withdrew due to unplanned discharge or hospital transfer during the rehabilitation period.

Participants who met the inclusion criteria were enrolled in this retrospective study. Data were collected from electronic medical records. Owing to the retrospective nature of this study and the fact that all participants had been discharged, follow-up was not feasible. Given the absence of personal or commercial conflicts of interest, the Ethics Committee of Shenzhen Second People’s Hospital granted a formal waiver of informed consent for this study. Nevertheless, this approach did not affect the validity of our findings while ensuring ethical compliance. Stringent measures were implemented to safeguard participants’ personal information. Each participant was assigned a unique identification number derived from their hospital admission number, which did not include any personal information that could identify them.

Data collection

All data were extracted using the hospital’s patient management system, including routine clinical treatment records and cost data. 30 individuals were assigned to collect data in 22 hospitals and they were trained virtually on the research significance, data sources, data interpretation, data collection methods, and data quality. These individuals were instructed to input information from the original medical records into the electronic case record forms designed for this study and store them confidentially. Researchers were responsible for ensuring the authenticity, completeness, and accuracy of the data, while being and prohibited from erasing or covering the original records. A data administrator periodically summarizes and reviews the data to verify their authenticity, accuracy, and completeness. Finally, 9 experts (mean age of 53.0 years, with 29.1 years of experience in rehabilitation medicine; all were familiar with this study’s content) were invited to verify the study results (See Supplementary Table 1. Characteristics of Esperts). The findings from this study, may be published in academic journals with participants’names withheld to protect their privacy.

The data collection included the following: Institutional information, including hospital ownership (public or private) and hospital grades (primary, secondary, or tertiary).

Demographic information, included the medical record number, sex, age, and payment method.

Clinical information included diagnosis, type of stroke (ischemic or hemorrhagic), date of initial diagnosis, and disease stage. The date of initial diagnosis was used to determine the patient’s hospitalization stage (acute, subacute, or recovery phase). According to the medical insurance policy in Shenzhen, acute-phase rehabilitation refers to early intervention conducted within one month after the onset of stroke in collaboration with non-rehabilitation medical departments. Subacute phase rehabilitation refers to targeted functional rehabilitation conducted within six months after the onset of stroke, when the patient meets the discharge criteria of non-rehabilitation medical departments (stable vital signs, controlled underlying diseases, or no significant progression of the primary disease). Recovery phase rehabilitation refers to the provision of maintenance functional rehabilitation and nursing support services to patients diagnosed for more than six months, who have no significant changes in functional status and are unable to fully care for themselves in daily life.

Information on hospitalization duration included admission date, discharge date, and length of stay (LOS).

Functional information included various types of functional impairments (pure motor disorders, cognitive disorders, swallowing disorders, speech disorders, consciousness disorders, and tracheotomy). In our study, we categorized functional impairments into three levels: mild (pure motor disorder), moderate (cognitive, swallowing, or speech disorders), and severe (consciousness disorder and tracheostomy).

Based on China’s Pharmacoeconomic Evaluation Guidelines (2020), cost analysis focused on direct medical costs, including rehabilitation treatment, medication, laboratory testing, nursing, bed, and other costs (e.g., consultations). Unfortunately, direct non-medical (e.g., transportation and caregiver expenses), indirect (e.g., productivity losses), and intangible (e.g., pain/suffering) costs could not be obtained, as these are not documented in patients’ medical records. All costs were converted to USD using International Monetary Fund-specified annual average exchange rates [USD: CNY (1: 6.8985 in 2019; 1: 6.8976 in 2020; and 1: 6.4515 in 2021)].

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 21.0. Descriptive statistics were used to present continuous data as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD), and categorical data were reported as [n (%)]. An independent samples t-test was used for intergroup comparisons of normally distributed continuous data. In cases where continuous data did not follow a normal distribution or categorical data, the Mann-Whitney U test was used. Pearson’s and Spearman’s correlation tests were conducted to analyze the correlation between total hospitalization costs, average daily costs, and other variables. Path analysis was conducted using a structural equation model to decompose direct and indirect effects. Two models were developed to detect the relationships between variables. In the first model, total hospitalization costs (log-transformed) served as the independent variable, with the model-specified LOS (log-transformed) as a mediator influencing total hospitalization costs (log-transformed). Independent variables included factors such as hospital level, sex, and age. In the second model, the daily cost was the independent variable, with the model-specified hospital level as a mediator influencing daily cost. Independent variables consisted of factors such as sex, age, and stroke type. Standardized regression coefficients were used as path coefficients to compare the influence of different factors on total hospitalization and daily costs. The coefficient of direct path (such as ‘log LOS’ → ‘log total costs’) is equivalent to the standardized regression coefficient. In contrast, the coefficient of the indirect path (such as ‘hospital level’ → ‘log LOS’ →‘log total costs’) is calculated as the product of the coefficient of the direct path and that of the path from the dependent variable to the mediator. The total path coefficient is obtained by summing the direct and indirect path coefficients. Furthermore, “sem” and “semPlot” packages in R studio software were used to fit and visualize the models. Hypothesis testing of path coefficients was equivalent to hypothesis testing of partial regression coefficients, with a significance level of α = 0.05.

Results

Basic information and clinical characteristics of the participants

The final dataset included data from 22 hospitals in Shenzhen comprising a total of 2,638 cases. Among which, the percentage of patients with cerebral ischemia was 56%, while those with cerebral hemorrhage accounted for 44%. A total of 1,690 patients (64.1%) were treated in 16 public hospitals, while 948 patients (35.9%) received treatment in six private hospitals including specialized rehabilitation hospitals. Among the enrolled patients, 69.6% were male and 30.4% were female, with an average age of 59.2 years. Majority of the patients (60.4%) were in the recovery phase, whereas 16.0% and 23.6% were in the acute and subacute phases, respectively. All enrolled patients had movement disorders while speech disorders were observed in 53.2% of patients, followed by cognitive disorders (40.8%), swallowing disorders (35.0%), tracheostomy (9.1%), and consciousness disorders (5.4%).

It was observed that the prevalence of cerebral ischemia was higher in female patients (32.3% vs. 28%, p = 0.02), compared to the male counterparts who recorded a higher prevalence of cerebral hemorrhage (72.0% vs. 67.7%, p = 0.02). The average age of patients with ischemic stroke was higher than that of patients with hemorrhagic stroke (64.23 ± 4.12 vs. 52.89 ± 14.38, p < 0.001). Patients with cerebral ischemia had a higher rate of pure motor disorder (33.3% vs. 25.8%, p < 0.001) and lower rates of consciousness disorder (4.0% vs. 7.2%, p < 0.001), cognitive disorder (37.3% vs. 45.2%, p < 0.001), and tracheostomy (4.0% vs. 15.6%, p < 0.001) than patients with cerebral hemorrhage. Total hospitalization cost, length of stay, and average daily cost of the participants

From 2019 to 2021, the median total hospitalization costs for rehabilitation patients with ischemic stroke and hemorrhagic stroke in Shenzhen were 27306.64 CNY (4022.79 USD) and 32188.25 CNY (4806.24 USD), respectively. Meanwhile, the average daily costs for rehabilitation were 848.64 CNY (125.87 USD) and 828.23 CNY (122.71 USD) respectively and the median LOS were 30 and 41 days, respectively.

The data show that total hospitalization costs for hemorrhagic and ischemic stroke were higher in 2020 than in 2019, while costs declined in 2021. This pattern may be attributed to the impact of the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic following its initial outbreak in late 2019. However, daily hospitalization costs progressively increased from 2019 to 2021.

Private hospitals exhibited ​significantly higher total costs than public hospitals (ischemic, 9270.79 vs. 3254.33 USD; hemorrhagic, 8675.16 vs. 3567.95 USD; p < 0.001, respectively), primarily driven by ​prolonged hospital stays (ischemic, 84 days vs. 24 days; hemorrhagic, 73 vs. 27 days, p < 0.001, respectively). Conversely, public hospitals demonstrated ​elevated daily costs compared to private hospitals (ischemic, 130.64 vs. 115.94 USD; hemorrhagic, 128.02 vs. 114.88 USD, p < 0.001, respectively), partly attributable to their predominant management of ​severe cases. This cost dichotomy reflects structural healthcare delivery differences, with public facilities prioritizing acute-phase resource intensity while private providers accommodate extended rehabilitation.

Hospitals were classified by grade, such as primary, secondary, or tertiary, and this were reflected in the median total hospitalization cost and LOS as they both decreased with increasing hospital grade. Specifically for ischemic stroke, the median total hospitalization costs were 62,900.09 CNY (9316.40 USD), 41,484.75 CNY (9316.40 USD), and 19,395.83 CNY (2857.67 USD) for primary, secondary, and tertiary hospitals, respectively, while LOS were 87, 53, and 20 days correspondingly. The median total hospitalization costs for hemorrhagic stroke were 61,392.52 CNY (9203.44 USD), 53,360.35 CNY (7858.78 USD), and 18,721.57 CNY (2780.58 USD) for primary, secondary, and tertiary hospitals, respectively and. LOS were 78, 73, and 20 days correspondingly.

Conversely, the average daily costs increased with higher hospital grades. For ischemic stroke, the daily average costs were 777.78 CNY (114.92 USD), 809.58 CNY (120.52 USD), and 926.45 CNY (137.03 USD) for primary, secondary, and tertiary hospitals, respectively. Meanwhile, for hemorrhagic stroke, the daily average costs were 766.62 CNY (113.22 USD), 802.13 CNY (118.96 USD), and 916.73 CNY (136.04 USD) for primary, secondary, and tertiary hospitals, respectively. In addition, patients were classified according to functional impairments and it was noted that patients with consciousness disorder had the highest median total costs (85512.16 CNY (12554.26 USD) for ischemic; 66733.66 CNY (10115.11 USD) for hemorrhagic), highest average daily costs (878.09 CNY, 129.59 USD) for ischemic; 856.15 CNY, 126.86 USD) for hemorrhagic) and longest median LOS (104 days for ischemic; 85.5 days for hemorrhagic). The median total hospitalization cost and LOS of patients with speech, cognitive, and swallowing disorders were observed to gradually increase (Table 2).

Proportion of hospitalization rehabilitation costs for participants

The rehabilitation treatment costs for patients with ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke accounted for the highest proportion of total costs (64% and 68%), followed by testing costs (8% and 6%), medication costs (7% and 5%), bed costs (6% and 6%), nursing costs (4% and 3%), and other expenses (2% and 2%). Notably, for patients with ischemic and hemorrhagic strokes, the proportion of medication costs for tracheotomy and patients with consciousness disorders (11% and 15% for ischemic, 8% and 9% for hemorrhagic) and the proportion of nursing costs (13% and 7% for ischemic, 12% and 16% for hemorrhagic) are significantly higher than those with other functional impairments. However, the proportion of rehabilitation costs for ischemic (56% and 58%) and hemorrhagic (58% and 52%) were significantly lower than those with other functional impairments (Table 3).

Path analysis of factors influencing hospitalization costs

Significant clinical indicators and variables closely related to hospitalization costs were included in a multiple linear regression model using stepwise regression. Two models representing the factors influencing hospitalization and daily average costs for patients with stroke were obtained. Model 1, which focused on total hospitalization costs, yielded a statistically significant F-value (F = 7055.730, P < 0.001) and high coefficient of determination (R2 = 0.951), indicating a good fit. Similarly, Model 2 for the daily average costs also showed statistically significant F-values (F = 106.956, P < 0.001) and moderate coefficients of determination (R2 = 0.198), suggesting an acceptable fit. Two-path-analysis equation models were used to explore these relationships.

The path analysis revealed that the factor with the largest direct impact on total hospitalization cost for patients with stroke is LOS (direct path coefficient: 1.004, p < 0.001), while the main indirect influence factors are hospital grades (indirect path coefficient: −0.56, p < 0.001), functional impairments levels (indirect path coefficient: 0.12, p < 0.001) and stage of disease (indirect path coefficient: 0.11, p < 0.001). Average daily costs were primarily directly influenced by hospital grades (direct path coefficient: 0.452, p < 0.001) and indirectly influenced by functional impairment levels (indirect path coefficient: −0.372, p < 0.001) and stage of disease (indirect path coefficient: −0.129, p < 0.001) (Table 4; Fig. 1).

Discussion

In this study, we systematically quantified the levels of total hospitalization and daily costs for patients with stroke between 2019 and 2021 and identified cost determinants through path analysis, providing evidence-based insights for optimizing healthcare resource allocation. Based on our findings, we identified two key components for optimizing rehabilitation resource allocation: a tiered rehabilitation service system and stratified management of daily costs.

Current status of hospitalization rehabilitation costs for stroke

The total hospitalization costs averaged ​27,306.64 CNY (4,023 USD) and​ 32,188.25 CNY (4,806 USD) for ischemic and hemorrhagic strokes, respectively. These findings align with regional observations from Guangzhou and Shanghai, where reported costs ranged from ​12,397 CNY (1,777 USD) to​ 38,758 CNY (6,162 USD) for the stroke population [10, 11]. More critically, the daily hospitalization costs (848.64 CNY (126 USD) for ischemic stroke and​ 828.23 CNY (123 USD) for hemorrhagic stroke) consume approximately 7 times Shenzhen’s average daily consumption expenditure during 2019–2021 (120.35 CNY, 17.83 USD) and ​10 times the national average (78.00 CNY, 11.55 USD) [12, 13], significantly alleviating the financial burden on patients with stroke and their families. Although universal health insurance alleviates 50–90% of direct hospitalization costs, rapid population aging and a growing population with disability pose significant challenges to the ​financial sustainability of the healthcare payment system. Globally, hospitalization rehabilitation costs for stroke exhibit substantial variations across different countries. The hospitalization costs in Shenzhen are significantly lower than those reported in the United States for patients with stroke (ischemic stroke: 10,368–15,967 USD; hemorrhagic stroke: 12,330–34,928 USD), yet higher than the reported costs in low-middle income countries (e.g., 590.91 USD in Pakistan and 329.52 USD in the Philippines) [14–16]. This reflects a strong correlation between inpatient rehabilitation costs for patients with stroke and national income levels across countries, highlighting the need for context-specific resource allocation strategies in global health policy [17].

Tiered management of LOS for stroke rehabilitation patients

In this study, we identified LOS a the most direct factor influencing total hospitalization costs for patients with stroke rehabilitation, which is consistent with previous studies [18, 19]. This indicates that controlling LOS is crucial for effectively managing hospitalization costs [20, 21]. According to stroke guidelines, the first 7 days after the onset of stroke is considered the acute phase, while a period of 3–6 months following stroke onset is regarded as the primary window for stroke rehabilitation [22]. Imposing strict limitations on LOS for rehabilitation may lead to negative consequences, such as frequent readmissions, increased burden on patients’ families, and wastage of medical resources [22]. However, excessively extended inpatient stays can impose a significant burden on medical resources. In our study, we observed that hospital grade was a crucial factor affecting total hospitalization costs, LOS, and average daily costs. The total hospitalization costs and LOS decreased with increasing hospital grades, whereas the daily costs increased with higher hospital grades. This phenomenon suggests that the three-tier healthcare delivery system effectively addresses the challenge of stroke rehabilitation hospitalization duration, leading to variations in hospitalization costs and LOS across different hospital levels.

The three-tier healthcare system is a distinct feature of China’s healthcare system [23]. This system amalgamates the resources of tertiary hospitals. Tertiary hospitals have the most abundant medical resources and mainly treat patients with severe acute diseases. Therefore, it is necessary to limit the LOS in tertiary hospitals to prevent the excessive utilization of high-quality medical resources and ensure efficient resource allocation. Secondary hospitals primarily provide subacute rehabilitation services, and appropriately extending the LOS helps ensure the rehabilitation window period for patients with stroke. Primary hospitals and community health institutions mainly serve patients with stable conditions or those requiring long-term care, where LOS can be further extended to ensure the continuity of rehabilitation services [24]. Collaboration among these three levels of hospitals results in a well-integrated rehabilitation alliance and guides the appropriate diversion of patient with stroke rehabilitation [25], providing comprehensive care from the acute hospitalization phase to the post-discharge rehabilitation phase and guaranteeing systematic and uninterrupted medical services for patients with stroke. It is helpful in optimizing medical resource utilization and enhancing economic efficiency [26]. Studies have shown that implementing a three-tier rehabilitation network model can enhance the prognosis of stroke patients by facilitating patient recovery, reducing disability, and improving the quality of life [27, 28].

However, effectively implementing the three-tier healthcare systems still faces challenges. First, patient healthcare-seeking behavior remains highly unregulated. For instance, even after the acute phase of stroke, patients and their families still tend to seek inpatient rehabilitation at tertiary hospitals, causing unnecessary utilization of high-quality medical resources. Second, the absence of standardized referral criteria results in resource allocation decisions lacking an evidence-based foundation. To address these, this study proposed implementing stratified payment management within the three-tier healthcare system. This approach involves stratified management of payment aligned with a three-tier healthcare system, such as reducing tertiary hospital payment weights during recovery phases while increasing weights for lower-tier facilities, thereby creating economically incentivized pathways for timely transfers and validated resource optimization loops.

The stratified management of daily costs for stroke rehabilitation patients

In this study, we discovered that the main factor directly influencing daily costs was hospital grades, whereas indirect factors included functional impairment levels, disease stage, and age. This aligns with previous studies, which have shown that age, comorbidities, disease severity significantly [10, 11], functional factors, the ability to perform daily activities [29, 30], functional independence measure score at admission [31], swallowing difficulties, aphasia, falls, depression, and cognitive impairments significantly affect daily hospitalization costs for patients with stroke [32–34]. Patients with severe functional impairments, older age, and earlier disease stages not only require more medical and nursing services but also tend to seek rehabilitation in higher-level hospitals, leading to an increase in daily costs with higher hospital grades [35, 36]. This suggests that the control of daily costs does not require only tiered management based on hospital grading but also stratified management according to disease and functional conditions.

First, we can establish the initial daily rehabilitation cost grades based on hospital grades, with higher-level hospitals having higher daily rehabilitation cost grades. Subsequently, the daily costs can be further refined by considering patients’ functional impairments, age, and disease stage. As this approach will allow for reasonable control of daily and total hospitalization costs, ensuring the appropriate utilization of medical resources and sustainability of rehabilitation treatments. These practical ideas provide valuable insights for improving China’s phased and stratified continuity of rehabilitation medical services [37]. This can also provide a reference for making reasonable payments through medical insurance. Based on these findings, the Shenzhen Medical Security Bureau conducted proactive reforms in payment methods for rehabilitation medical insurance. In 2022, it formulated a bed-day payment standard for inpatient rehabilitation medical services in Shenzhen which factors in different disease types, hospital grades, degrees of dysfunction, and disease stages [24]. This standard ensures that medical insurance payments vary according to the disease, severity, and rehabilitation stage of the admitted patients, reflecting the concept of value-based healthcare and ensuring the efficient allocation of medical resources. However, it is important to acknowledge that the implementation of these measures requires a comprehensive account of the actual conditions and resource constraints of healthcare institutions, along with effective management and supervision.

This study has some limitations. First, this study only collected data on the primary diagnosis and did not capture information on patient comorbidities, which may influence the interpretation of results. However, we categorized functional impairment into mild, moderate, and severe levels. This classification indirectly reflects the impact of comorbidities since greater functional impairment is typically associated with more comorbidities. Second, variations in hospital practices, which influence treatment costs, may affect the generalizability of the findings. Third, due to the cross-sectional design, our study captured functional status only at discharge and lacked longitudinal measures of functional outcomes. Consequently, expenditure correlation with therapeutic efficacy remains unestablished. Lastly, conclusions derived from this population of stroke rehabilitation inpatients in Shenzhen, China, need careful consideration when extrapolated to geographically distinct populations, healthcare systems with divergent financing structures, or populations with significantly different comorbidity profiles.

Conclusion

The need for a reasonable control of LOS and daily costs is a key to controlling the total hospitalization costs for patient with stroke rehabilitation. Establishing a tiered medical service system is an essential approach to optimize the need for improving the rehabilitation medical service network and providing systematic and sustainable medical services. The tiered management of the LOS according to hospital grade is an effective measure for achieving the rehabilitation window period and guiding patient diversion. Additionally, on basis of tiered medical system, stratified management of daily costs according to the functional impairment levels, the stage of the disease and age can optimize the cost management of stroke rehabilitation patients, promote the development of personalized and precise rehabilitation. This also enhances the utilization efficiency of medical resources and provides a reference for reforming medical insurance payment schemes.

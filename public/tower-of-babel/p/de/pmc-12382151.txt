Real-world evidence on the survival benefit of immune checkpoint inhibitors in combination with cytotoxic chemotherapy for patients with extensive-disease small-cell lung cancer: the Tokushukai Real World Data Project (TREAD) 06
Authors: Tomoya Fukui, Yoshinori Imamura, Takuya Kakutani, Kiyoaki Uryu, Rai Shimoyama, Maki Hayashi, Tadahisa Okuda, Koshi Kataoka, Masataka Taguri, Hironobu Minami

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSmall-cell lung cancer (SCLC) is an aggressive malignancy with a poor prognosis. Despite the initial chemosensitivity, survival for extensive-disease (ED) SCLC remains limited. Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in combination with chemotherapy have recently been redefined as the standard of care. We evaluated the efficacy of ICI combination therapy in clinical trials translated into real-world clinical practice for patients with ED-SCLC.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study analyzed data from the Tokushukai REAl World Data Project. Patients with ED-SCLC who received first-line platinum-based chemotherapy between April 2010 and March 2022 were included in this study. Overall survival (OS) was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and associations with clinical variables were analyzed using Cox proportional hazards regression.ResultsAmong 590 patients (median age, 71 years; range, 40–90 years), 197 (33.4%) were aged ≥ 75 years. Following ICI approval in Japan in August 2019, 206 patients received first-line treatment, of whom 96 received ICI combination therapy. ICI-treated patients had significantly improved OS compared to chemotherapy alone (median 13.0 vs. 9.7 months; p = 0.011), with a pronounced benefit in those aged < 75 years (15.0 vs. 10.0 months; p = 0.022) but not in those aged ≥ 75 years (8.9 vs. 8.6 months; p = 0.647). ICI combination therapy was an independent factor for improved OS (HR, 0.589; 95% CI, 0.447–0.776).ConclusionsICI combination therapy improved survival in real-world patients with ED-SCLC, aligning with the results of pivotal clinical trials. However, their limited efficacy in older adults underscores the need for tailored treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-14701-z.

Background

Small-cell lung cancer (SCLC), which accounts for 10–15% of all lung cancers, is characterized by rapid progression, early metastatic dissemination, and aggressive clinical course [1–5]. It is conventionally classified into limited-disease (LD) and extensive-disease (ED), with LD-SCLC confined to a single radiation field and ED-SCLC extending beyond this boundary with metastatic spread [4, 5]. At diagnosis, approximately 70–80% of patients present with ED-SCLC, and despite initial sensitivity to cytotoxic chemotherapy, most patients eventually experience disease progression or relapse, reinforcing the highly aggressive nature of this malignancy [1, 6].

Cytotoxic chemotherapy remains the first-line standard of care for ED-SCLC in patients with favorable performance status (PS). Historically, platinum-based regimens (cisplatin or carboplatin) combined with etoposide or irinotecan have been the preferred approach [7–10]. Subsequent therapies, including amrubicin nogitecan, and temozolomide, have demonstrated efficacy in relapsed ED-SCLC [11–16]. Notably, patients with “sensitive relapse,” defined as relapse occurring 60–90 days or more after initial chemotherapy, may benefit from carboplatin and etoposide rechallenge [17]. Nevertheless, ED-SCLC remains highly aggressive, with a median overall survival (OS) of only 8–13 months, highlighting the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies [7, 8, 18, 19].

Recent advances in immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have significantly reshaped the therapeutic landscape of ED-SCLC, a malignancy that has experienced limited treatment progress over the past several decades [1, 20]. Pivotal clinical trials have demonstrated that incorporating ICIs into standard first-line chemotherapy enhances survival outcomes in patients with ED-SCLC [21, 22]. In Japan, a combination of carboplatin, etoposide, and atezolizumab was approved as the first-line treatment in August 2019, followed by durvalumab, platinum, and etoposide in August 2020. However, the therapeutic efficacy of ICIs is influenced by intricate interactions between the tumor immune microenvironment and host-related factors, including chronic inflammation and cellular senescence [23–28]. Given these complexities, real-world data are crucial for assessing the effectiveness of ICI combination therapy beyond the controlled settings of clinical trials.

To address the gap between clinical trial findings and real-world clinical practice, a comprehensive analysis of real-world data was conducted using patient records from Tokushukai group hospitals, a healthcare network recognized for its extensive coverage across the urban, rural, and remote regions of Japan [29]. This study aimed to assess the real-world efficacy of ICI combination therapy for ED-SCLC, with a particular focus on therapeutic outcomes in patients aged < 75 years and those aged ≥ 75 years. By elucidating the real-world applicability of ICI combination therapy, these findings may contribute to refining precise treatment strategies and optimizing therapeutic approaches in diverse clinical settings.

Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This nationwide retrospective cohort study was conducted as part of a broader real-world data project, as previously described [29]. This project was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Tokushukai Group in April 2020 (No. TGE01427-024) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was registered with the UMIN Clinical Trial Registry under the trial registration number UMIN000050590 (http://www.umin.ac.jp/ctr/index.htm). Owing to the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived, and patient participation was facilitated through an opt-out approach.

Patient selection

We evaluated patients with pathologically and radiologically confirmed ED-SCLC who received systemic chemotherapy at 46 Tokushukai Medical Group hospitals between April 1, 2010, and March 31, 2022. All patients received first-line platinum-based chemotherapy with or without ICIs, such as atezolizumab or durvalumab. Data collection was facilitated by two systems: a standardized electronic medical record system (e-Karte and Newtons2; Software Service Inc., Osaka, Japan) and a chemotherapy protocol management system (srvApmDrop; Software Service Inc., Osaka, Japan). This study included both pure and combined pathological subtypes of SCLC. Patients were excluded if they had active second primary malignancies, unknown treatment history, or incomplete baseline data. Exclusion criteria for missing data included the absence of survival status, body weight, height, and critical pretreatment laboratory values such as neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, albumin, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), creatinine, and C-reactive protein (CRP).

Data collection

The primary endpoint was OS, defined as the time from the initiation of first-line chemotherapy to the date of death or the last confirmed survival status. Data were extracted from electronic medical records, including patient demographics (age, birth year and month, sex, body weight, height, and BMI), medical visit dates, survival status, and clinical diagnoses. Chemotherapy regimen details were retrieved from the chemotherapy protocol management system, including the start and end dates, number of cycles, administration schedules, and PS. Additionally, the linked cancer registry provides information on cancer diagnosis (date, site, pathology, stage, and TNM classification) and prognosis (date of last survival confirmation, date, and cause of death) based on the National Cancer Registry in Japan. The analysis examined secular trends over four periods: April 2010–March 2013, April 2013–March 2016, April 2016–March 2019, and April 2019–March 2022.

Statistical analysis

Patient characteristics are summarized as median (range) for continuous variables and number (percentage) for categorical variables. Differences in baseline characteristics between the groups were assessed using the chi-square test. OS, the primary endpoint, was evaluated using Kaplan–Meier (KM) curves and Cox proportional hazards regression models (Cox models). KM curves were used to assess the impact of the study period (categorized into 2010–2013, 2013–2016, 2016–2019, and 2019–2022) and ICI combination therapy on survival in the overall population and age-stratified subgroups (≥ 75 and < 75 years). Differences in survival rates were tested using the log-rank test.

To identify prognostic factors for OS, univariate and multivariate Cox models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for baseline covariates, including age, sex, BMI, PS, pathology, clinical staging, study period, platinum-based chemotherapy, LDH, creatinine, sodium, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and modified Glasgow Prognostic Score (mGPS). Laboratory variables, including creatinine and LDH, were categorized as normal or elevated based on institutional reference ranges at treatment initiation. In the multivariate analysis, backward elimination was applied to sequentially remove variables with the highest p-value until all remaining variables had a global p-value < 0.2.

To address confounding factors and estimate the average treatment effect in the overall population, the inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) method creates a pseudo-population that balances the prognostic factors between the ICI-treated and untreated groups. Baseline covariate balance before and after IPTW adjustment was evaluated with standardized differences. The results, presented in Additional File Table 1, demonstrate that adequate balance was achieved through weighting. In the IPTW-adjusted cohort, the treatment effect of ICI combination therapy was evaluated using weighted KM curves and the log-rank test. A Cox model incorporating IPTW adjustment was used to estimate the HR. All statistical analyses and visualizations were performed using R software (version 4.3.3, 2024–02-29; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 629 patients with ED-SCLC were identified in the TREAD database, of which 590 met the eligibility criteria. The median age was 71 years (range, 40–90 years), with 393 patients (66.7%) aged < 75 years and 197 (33.3%) aged ≥ 75 years. The cohort included 484 male patients (82.0%), 583 (98.8%) with a pathological diagnosis of pure SCLC, and 456 (77.3%) with stage IV disease. Regarding PS, 362 patients (61.4%) had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group PS of 0–1, whereas 124 (21.0%) had a PS of 2–4 (Table 1). After ICI approval for ED-SCLC, 96 patients (46.6%) received ICI combination therapy as the first-line treatment, while 110 (53.4%) received cytotoxic chemotherapy alone (Fig. 1). Of the 590 eligible patients, 466 (79.0%) died. Discussion

The approval of ICIs in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy in 2019 marked a pivotal shift in the treatment landscape for ED-SCLC [1, 30–33]. Our study, which analyzed real-world data from multiple institutions across Japan, demonstrated a median OS of 13.0 months (95% CI, 11.0–16.0) in patients receiving ICI combination therapy, with an HR of 0.589 (95% CI, 0.447–0.776) in multivariate analysis. Although a comparison limited to patients treated after 2019 was considered, we were concerned about potential bias due to the inclusion of patient ineligible for ICI therapy (e.g., those with interstitial lung disease [ILD]) in the group of patients not receiving ICIs. Therefore, we employed IPTW-adjusted analyses to minimize confounding factors. These findings align with pivotal clinical trials, including IMpower133 (OS 12.3 months, HR 0.76 [95% CI, 0.60–0.95]) and CASPIAN (OS 12.9 months, HR 0.75 [95% CI, 0.62–0.91]), which demonstrated OS benefits with the addition of ICIs to platinum-based chemotherapy [21, 22, 34, 35].

Patient demographics and clinical characteristics may influence treatment outcomes. In this study, 80% of patients were aged ≥ 65 years and 33% were aged ≥ 75 years, whereas pivotal trials included only 40–45% of patients aged ≥ 65 years [21, 22]. Additionally, approximately 20% of patients had poor PS (PS 2–4), a population typically excluded from clinical trials. Despite these differences, ICI combination therapy was effective in this real-world cohort, suggesting that even older patients or those with suboptimal PS may benefit from treatment. However, caution is warranted in patients with comorbidities such as ILD or autoimmune disorders, and treatment decisions should be guided by clinical trial criteria that incorporate individual patient factors. Notably, the benefits of ICI combination therapy appear limited in patients aged ≥ 75 years, underscoring the need for tailored treatment approaches in older populations in real-world settings.

The management of lung cancer in older patients presents unique challenges. Approximately 50% of lung cancer diagnoses occur in patients aged ≥ 70 years, and this proportion is expected to increase [25]. Age-related declines in organ function, immunosenescence, and various clinical factors, including comorbidities, polypharmacy, nutritional status, cognitive function, and social support, may influence treatment tolerability and response [26, 27]. Our study found that chemotherapy efficacy was lower in patients with ED-SCLC aged ≥ 75 years and that the incremental benefit of ICIs was limited. However, the limited number of patients aged ≥ 75 years who received ICI combination therapy (n = 19) versus those who did not (n = 178) restricts stastical power, introduces potential confounding, and precludes definitive conclusions from this subgroup analysis. These findings suggest that treatment decisions should be guided by a comprehensive assessment of factors beyond chronological age to optimize therapeutic outcomes in older patients.

Although ICIs represent a major advance in ED-SCLC treatment, their long-term benefit is observed only in a subset of patients, with 18-month OS rates of 7–13% in clinical trials, regardless of PD-L1 expression [34–38]. Given that PD-L1 expression is generally low in SCLC, identifying predictive biomarkers is critical for optimizing patient selection [38, 39]. Potential candidates include tumor mutational burden, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, and circulating tumor cells [1, 20, 34, 38–42]. Our study also evaluated hematologic surrogate markers and identified a pretreatment NLR ≥ 5 as an independent prognostic factor, irrespective of ICI use. These findings underscore the need for further research to establish reliable predictive biomarkers for ICIs and chemotherapy in patients with SCLC.

Improving the prognosis of ED-SCLC requires not only more effective initial therapies but also optimized sequential treatment strategies. Available options include amrubicin, topotecan, and carboplatin/etoposide rechallenge in patients with sensitive relapse [11, 12, 14, 15, 17, 43, 44]. However, there is a critical need for effective second-line treatment options to prolong survival following ICI combination therapy. Advances in molecular classification have enhanced the understanding of SCLC heterogeneity, paving the way for subtype-specific therapeutic approaches [40–42]. Novel therapeutic options for SCLC, such as DLL3-targeted therapies, PARP inhibitors, Aurora kinase inhibitors, and agents like lurbinectedin and tarlatamab that are investigational or recently approved in certain settings, show promise as potential treatment strategies [1].

This study had certain limitations. First, the inclusion of patients treated at regional hospitals rather than high-volume cancer centers may have introduced variability in demographics and treatment practices. However, these data were derived from patients with ED-SCLC who received platinum-based chemotherapy, which may have contributed to some degree of patient selection. Second, disease progression and treatment-related adverse events were not evaluated because we could not access to raw data. Additionally, details regarding the treatment selection criteria and comorbidities, such as ILD, were unavailable. In the post-ICI approval period, approximately half of patients who were eligible for platinum-based chemotherapy received ICI combination therapy. While factors such as advanced age and comorbidities (e.g., ILD) may have influenced treatment decisions, the specific reasons for ICI ineligibility, such as PS, organ dysfunction, or other documented contraindications, could not be determined from our database. Furthermore, important clinical variables such as smoking history, metastatic sites (e.g., brain or liver), and other comorbid conditions could not be assessed, limiting the precision of prognostic evaluation and statistical inference. Future prospective studies with comprehensive clinical annotation are warranted to elucidate the determinants of ICI utilization in routine practice. Third, although the study included both long-term survivors and patients who did not receive ICI combination therapy, identifying pretreatment characteristics that predict chemotherapy or ICI sensitivity remains challenging. Further research is needed to refine the patient selection criteria and identify reliable predictive markers. Finally, the number of events was insufficient to fully evaluate OS in ED-SCLC patients receiving ICI combination therapy. Although multivariate analysis identified first-line ICI combination therapy as a significant prognostic factor, long-term follow-up is required to validate this finding.

Despite these limitations, the study’s strengths include its large sample size and real-world data, which enhances the generalizability of the findings across clinical settings.

Conclusion

This nationwide retrospective study confirmed the efficacy of ICI combination therapy in patients with ED-SCLC, with OS outcomes consistent with those reported in pivotal clinical trials. In clinical practice, ICIs combined with chemotherapy provide a survival benefit in ED-SCLC and are recommended for all eligible patients, even those older than 75 years. However, the therapeutic benefit appears to be limited in patients aged ≥ 75 years, highlighting the need for tailored treatment strategies to more closely assess comorbidities, treatment tolerability, and biomarker profiles.

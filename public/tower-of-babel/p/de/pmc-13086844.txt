Course assessment model in vocational education based on BPNN optimized by genetic algorithm
Authors: Wenshuang Luo, Lijing Zang, Weihao Liang, Qianhui Ma, Jiankun Chu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the current educational system reform, vocational education has become an increasingly popular learning path for Chinese students, making the optimization of course assessment models a critical issue. However, traditional evaluation systems face problems such as subjective judgment and reliance on large amounts of training data, resulting in high labor and time costs. To address these issues, this study proposes a vocational education assessment model based on a Genetic Algorithm combined with a Backpropagation Neural Network. The model processes data using normalization techniques and employs the entropy method to determine the weights of evaluation indicators. Additionally, the Genetic Algorithm is used to optimize the model’s weights and thresholds, while the Levenberg-Marquardt method is applied for data training. The experimental results show that the Root Mean Squared Error decreased from 12.2% in the training phase to 7.8% in the testing phase. After 100 iterations, the model’s loss value dropped to 0.13, and the recall rate increased from 65 to 90%. The precision of the assessment results also improved from 87.9% in the training set to 96.5% in the validation set. For the self-built validation dataset, the model’s Coefficient of Determination stabilized at 0.96 after 100 iterations, and the relative error decreased from 17.1 to 14.9%. The fitting coefficient between the predicted and actual assessment values was 0.998, with an average error of 0.67%, outperforming the comparison model. These results demonstrate that the proposed model can handle various vocational education assessment scenarios with high accuracy, providing innovative ideas for the development of vocational education.Keywords: Genetic algorithm, Back propagation neural network, Vocational education, Course assessment, Levenberg-marquardt methodSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Computational science

Introduction

Since the implementation of China’s 14th Five-Year Plan, vocational education reform has become a key focus of educational development1. With the continuous improvement of the teaching system, the forms of vocational education in China have been evolving, and course assessment has become an essential part of evaluating students’ academic achievements in vocational colleges2,3. It is worth noting that in some practical course areas, traditional assessment methods face greater challenges, as even students with strong interest and hard work may still find it difficult to master the solutions and skills needed to solve problems4. Therefore, how to optimize course assessment models to improve the overall quality of vocational education in China has become a key research focus5. Currently, educational assessments generally adopt quality management concepts and employ methods such as surveys to analyze teaching quality6. For example, Glaveli et al. used the quality management concept to analyze the impact of teacher job satisfaction and, through regression analysis, validated the positive correlation between quality management policies and job satisfaction, providing theoretical support for improving teaching quality within schools7. Tirumanadham and Thaiyalnayaki explored how to fairly predict students’ performance in digital learning environments through in-depth research on ensemble and feature selection methods, and emphasized the necessity of both in improving prediction accuracy and reliability8. However, scholars have found that single-perspective approaches to course assessment cannot provide a comprehensive evaluation of educational quality. To address this, Andrade and Zuin used multi-angle approaches, such as image analysis, to perform comprehensive assessments of teachers, students, and educational institutions, effectively avoiding the limitations of a single perspective9.

Moreover, it has been found that Backpropagation Neural Networks (BPNN) have clearly outperformed in vocational education assessment models. These networks can spontaneously adjust network parameters based on assessment data, adapting to different educational evaluation scenarios10,11. For instance, Liu et al. used BPNN to construct a new undergraduate teaching quality evaluation system and evaluated four dimensions—attitude, content, method, and teacher characteristics—through sensitivity tests, providing theoretical and empirical support for improving educational quality and optimizing teacher resources12. Beyond educational assessment, BPNN has been successfully applied in various practical scenarios. For example, Zhang et al. combined artificial bee colony algorithms with a BPNN to build an intelligent recognition model for manufacturing that accurately identifies part features by extracting geometric and topological structures from engineering documents, thereby reducing the time required for part identification in engineering projects13. Furthermore, BPNN can be applied in the medical and biological fields, such as predicting the effectiveness of biological therapies for skin cancer treatment. Soni et al. used BPNN to select and analyze treatment methods, generating accurate treatment plans14.

Despite these successes, BPNN still faces issues such as slow convergence, susceptibility to local optima, and reliance on large amounts of training data. To reduce these costs, researchers have found that Genetic Algorithms (GA) can serve as a global optimization search algorithm by simulating the genetic and evolutionary processes of organisms in nature15,16. GA is simple, universal, and highly adaptable, making it suitable for selecting optimal model parameters. It has been widely applied in various research fields17. For example, Hassan and his team used GA combined with the open-circuit voltage technique to track the maximum power in solar power generation, achieving an average tracking efficiency of 99.96%, which represents a 3% improvement over traditional methods18. In response to the difficulty of traditional economic early warning systems in characterizing the nonlinearity of macroeconomic systems, Yin et al. proposed an improved GA-BP hybrid algorithm neural network early warning system, using adaptive GA to optimize BP weight thresholds19. To address the issue of insufficient accuracy in MOOC teaching quality evaluation, Wang proposed an improved GA-optimized BPNN model. By improving GA to automatically optimize network weight thresholds, the evaluation accuracy and stability were improved20.

Although existing research has made some progress in educational assessment, the combination of GA and BPNN in vocational education curriculum evaluation still faces challenges, such as large evaluation errors, low efficiency, and subjective weight setting, and has not effectively addressed the problems of model robustness and interpretability under multi-source, heterogeneous educational data. To address these shortcomings, this study proposes a model for evaluating a vocational education curriculum that integrates GA with a BPNN. Compared with existing vocational education evaluation methods, this study has made three important improvements in method design. One is to use the entropy weighting method instead of the subjective weighting to achieve an objective allocation of indicator weights. The second is to construct a two-stage optimization strategy combining GA and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM). Firstly, GA is used for global parameter search, and then the LM algorithm is used for local fine-tuning to overcome the problems of traditional BPNNs, which are prone to local optima and slow convergence. The third is to integrate data normalization, weight objectification, and a network optimization process system to form an evaluation modeling framework suitable for multi-source heterogeneous educational data. These designs enhance the model’s predictive stability and generalization while maintaining strong interpretability.

Methods and materials

Design of GA-improved BPNN

BPNN is a type of neural network that consists of multiple layers arranged in a feedforward manner and is built on the BPNN structure, including input, hidden, and output layers21,22. BPNN has a forward-propagation learning pattern and backward error elimination and has been widely applied in classification and prediction tasks by scholars worldwide23. BPNN is well-suited to scenarios where indicators, such as vocational education curriculum evaluation, are clear, data scales are moderate, and objective weighting and nonlinear fitting are required. Compared with models such as Transformers that rely on large-scale data and complex attention mechanisms, BPNN has lower sample-size requirements, lower computational overhead, and is easier to interpret and debug. Compared to integrated methods such as XGBoost, BPNN can perform continuous parameter optimization via error backpropagation, making it easier to combine with the entropy-weighted method, GA global search, and LM local optimization used in this paper to form an end-to-end trainable evaluation framework. Therefore, this study chose BPNN as the basic learner. The structure of BPNN is shown in Fig. 1.

Design of GA-BPNN vocational education evaluation model

The study constructs a hybrid algorithm combining GA and BPNN and uses the cost function to calculate the GA fitness. Given its accurate prediction capabilities and generalization performance, the improved GA-BPNN is applied to vocational education academic evaluation to accurately assess students’ academic levels. Initially, the Sigmoid function is selected as the activation function for the hidden layer of GA-BPNN, as represented in Eq. (7).

In Eq. (7), x is the feature value. The activation function introduces nonlinearity into the GA-BPNN architecture, enabling effective information transformation between layers. Since each indicator in vocational education academic evaluation has different scoring criteria, appropriate indicator weights need to be determined. Unlike the Analytic Hierarchy Process, which relies on subjective expert judgment, the Entropy Weight Method calculates weights entirely based on the degree of variation in the data itself, avoiding human bias, and is suitable for the large-scale, multi-source, heterogeneous vocational education data processed in this study. Compared to regression-based importance estimation or model-intrinsic feature-attribution methods, the entropy-weight method does not require a predefined model form, is computationally stable, and is easy to integrate with subsequent GA-BPNN optimization processes. It can provide more objective, explanatory initial weights for neural networks, thereby improving the model’s overall reliability and generalization. Therefore, the entropy weight method was chosen to determine the indicator weights in the study. The entropy method is applied to enhance the weight analysis of network indicators, as illustrated in Fig. 4.

Optimize the indicator weight analysis process.

As shown in Fig. 4, data and corresponding indicators required for the vocational education evaluation model are collected and used to construct an (m×n) matrix, with data as the rows and indicators as the columns. The dimensionless method is then applied to process the indicators, and the weight matrix, entropy values, variance coefficients, and weights are calculated. Finally, the relationship between the sample data and indicators is comprehensively assessed. Once the indicator analysis method for vocational education evaluation is clarified, the recognition accuracy of GA-BPNN during operation is optimized. Due to the complexity and diversity of the training sample structure, the recognition accuracy may be limited. Therefore, the LM method is applied to enhance the recognition capability of GA-BPNN. The study combines GA and LM algorithms to construct a two-stage optimization framework that overcomes the inherent limitations of a single optimization strategy. As a global search algorithm, GA can optimize the initial weights and thresholds of BPNN through a population evolution mechanism, effectively avoiding the network from falling into local optima. But its convergence speed is slow in the later stage of the search, and the solution accuracy is limited. On the contrary, the LM algorithm, as a local optimization method that utilizes second-order derivatives, has the advantages of fast local convergence and high accuracy, but its performance is highly dependent on the selection of initial parameters. Therefore, this study first uses GA for global coarse tuning to provide a set of initial parameters that approximate the global optimal region for BPNN. Subsequently, the LM algorithm is applied for local fine-tuning to quickly and accurately converge to the optimal solution. This serial strategy of global first and local later helps to balance global optimization ability with local convergence efficiency. Complete network structure and operation flow chart.

As shown in Fig. 7, the vocational education academic evaluation model consists of three parts. The first part is the pre-processing module for data and indicators. The study begins by identifying the evaluation object and establishing the vocational education assessment system. It then employs the entropy method to determine the weights of the system’s indicators. After satisfying the evaluation criteria, a training set is created, and the data undergoes normalization. The data then enters the second part, the GA-BPNN module. The network architecture is built by first using the cost function to calculate GA fitness. The thresholds and weights in BPNN are encoded, and GA is used to optimize them. The LM algorithm is then applied to train GA-BPNN, continuously iterating and optimizing the network architecture. The forward propagation process of BPNN calculates the errors. The error values are then passed into the third part of the network architecture, the data analysis module, for evaluation results. It intuitively reveals how the model generates a complete logical chain of trustworthy evaluation decisions from raw data, enhancing the transparency and reproducibility of the framework.

Results and analysis

Experimental setup

To ensure the reproducibility and transparency of the experimental results, this study systematically describes the dataset construction, data partitioning, model parameter settings, preprocessing methods, and experimental environment in the experimental design stage. Two self-built datasets were used: Dataset A, collected from five vocational colleges in Yunnan Province, contains 1,280 course assessment records with 15 feature variables, including students’ basic information, course grades, attendance, teacher evaluations, and comprehensive quality indicators. The target variable is the comprehensive course assessment score. Dataset B, collected from 50 vocational education institutions nationwide, consists of 12,500 samples and is used for further validation and generalization testing of the model. The score distributions in both datasets approximately follow a normal distribution and cover multiple disciplines and course types.

Dataset A was split into training, validation, and testing sets at a ratio of 7:2:1 to compare the performance of GA-BPNN, LM-BPNN, LSTM-RNN, and traditional BPNN. Dataset B was split into 70% for training and 30% for generalization validation, to evaluate the adaptability and robustness of the proposed model under large-scale data conditions. Based on these datasets, a neural network validation platform was established, with the experimental scenarios and specific configurations shown in Table 1.

In the BPNN architecture, the number of input layer nodes equals the dimensionality of the evaluation indicators, totaling 15. The output layer corresponds to a single comprehensive course evaluation value. The number of hidden layer nodes was determined using a commonly adopted empirical formula and was ultimately set to 12. Regarding GA parameters, the experimental setup included a population size of 50, a crossover probability of 0.8, a mutation rate of 0.05, and a maximum of 200 iterations. For the LM algorithm, the initial learning rate was set to 0.01, with a maximum of 500 iterations and a convergence threshold of 1 × 10− 4. In terms of the evaluation index system for vocational education courses, the study referred to the theory of competency based education and designed an evaluation index system that covers three dimensions: knowledge mastery (course grades, homework completion quality), skill practice (practical skill assessment scores, training report scores, skill certificate levels), and comprehensive quality (classroom attendance rate, team collaboration evaluation, teacher comprehensive evaluation). The feature importance ranking analysis based on the connection weights of the trained GA-BPNN network shows that the skill practice dimension (especially the practical skill assessment score and training report score) contributes the most significantly to the final evaluation results, with a cumulative contribution of about 38%, a knowledge mastery dimension contribution of about 32%, and a comprehensive quality dimension contribution of about 30%. This weight distribution is highly consistent with the core principle of competency-based education theory, which emphasizes practical skills, thereby verifying the theoretical rationality of the evaluation system design from a data-driven perspective and enhancing the model’s interpretability.

In the data preprocessing stage, all feature values were normalized using the Min-Max method within the [0,1] range to ensure a consistent scale across different dimensions. At the output stage, denormalization was applied to restore values to the original range for alignment with actual scores. For indicator weighting, the entropy-based method was adopted, with weights determined by the degree of variation among indicators in the sample, thereby ensuring an objective allocation of importance across indicators.

Discussion

To optimize the vocational education course evaluation model, the study proposed a novel course evaluation model that combines GA and BPNN. This model uses GA’s global search ability to optimize the weights and thresholds of BPNN, avoiding issues such as slow convergence and getting trapped in local optima, which are common in traditional BPNN. The experimental results showed that GA-BPNN clearly outperformed traditional models in terms of convergence efficiency, robustness, and actual course evaluation accuracy. In validating the vocational evaluation dataset from five universities in Yunnan Province, using normalized data, the RMSE of the GA-BPNN in the validation set was 7.8%, lower than the 17.8% of the traditional BPNN. Meanwhile, compared with the BPNN loss value (0.42), the GA-BPNN loss value decreased to 0.13. This demonstrates that GA-BPNN can handle complex, multidimensional vocational education data while showing strong adaptability and stability during the process.

In existing studies, Zhang and Wang29 constructed a teaching quality evaluation model based on GA-BPNN and verified the feasibility of introducing evolutionary optimization methods in education evaluation. However, this method has limitations such as slow convergence speed and insufficient parameter optimization. Bai et al.30 proposed a comprehensive benefit measurement model based on GA-BPNN and achieved certain results in the field of project portfolio management. In contrast, the model proposed in this study differs in its weight allocation mechanism, optimization strategy, and framework integrity. Specifically, the proposed model introduces the entropy-weighting method to determine indicator weights, thereby avoiding subjective reliance on manual settings or regression weighting. At the same time, this study adopts a two-stage collaborative optimization of GA and LM algorithms. Firstly, GA performs a global parameter search, and then LM performs local fine-tuning, balancing global optimization ability and convergence speed. In addition, this study integrated data normalization, objective weighting, mixed optimization, and result anti-standardization into an end-to-end evaluation process, enhancing the systematicity and adaptability of the method in the scenario of multi-source heterogeneous data in vocational education. The experimental results based on data set B show that the model is significantly better than the models of Zhang et al. and Bai et al. This shows that the proposed method shows stronger convergence, robustness, and adaptability when processing large-scale, multi-dimensional vocational education evaluation data, can more accurately restore the actual evaluation results, and provides strong support for the construction of a scientific and rigorous education evaluation system.

The innovation of this study lies in integrating GA with the improvement process of BPNN. Evaluation data were normalized to eliminate dimensional inconsistencies, and the entropy method was applied to determine objective indicator weights. Through population iteration and global search, GA optimized the weights and thresholds of BPNN, reducing the RE value to 15%. Thus, the proposed model can handle complex evaluation data, automatically assign optimal weights, and avoid subjective bias. This offers a scientific and objective approach for vocational education assessment. Future research will explore integrating GA-BPNN with other algorithms to improve adaptability across different fields and scenarios.

Furthermore, although an A100 GPU was used for accelerated training, GA-BPNN is a small- to medium-sized neural network. In actual deployment, only forward computation is required, which is computationally lightweight. It can be run on ordinary CPUs or mid-range GPUs, fully meeting the needs of vocational schools with limited computing resources. At the same time, the research strictly adheres to the ethical standards of educational data research. All student data were anonymized during collection and processing and do not contain any personally identifiable information. The use of the data has been approved and agreed upon by the relevant institutions and is for academic research purposes only. The core contribution of this study is the construction and validation of a fusion evaluation model applicable to vocational education scenarios. Although the experimental data mainly come from Chinese vocational colleges, the methodology used in the model is a general framework for handling multi-source heterogeneous data and complex mapping relationships, including a GA-BPNN-based framework, an entropy weight method to determine objective weights, and an LM algorithm to accelerate convergence, providing a foundation for the applicability of the model across educational contexts. In addition, the model’s effective validation on the publicly available UCI dataset has preliminarily demonstrated its potential to handle educational data with diverse structures, providing a foundation for its migration to a broader range of educational and cultural contexts. To further enhance the practical guidance value of the model, future work can explore its interpretability in depth, such as identifying key evaluation indicators and assessing hyperparameter stability through feature importance ranking or sensitivity analysis, thereby improving the model’s stability and interpretability in practical deployment.

Conclusion

Current vocational education course evaluation models face issues such as strong subjectivity in indicator weight assignment and high labor and time costs due to the large training data requirements of deep learning models. Therefore, this study introduced GA to optimize BPNN, processed the evaluation data using normalization, and applied the entropy method to determine the indicator weights, resulting in the construction of a vocational education course evaluation model. The experimental results showed that GA-BPNN clearly outperformed the comparison models in key indicators such as RMSE, loss value, recall rate, and coefficient of determination. For example, the R2 stabilized at 0.998, and the precision was optimized to 96.5%. Additionally, GA-BPNN accurately predicted vocational education assessment scores, with errors significantly lower than those of the comparison models. This provides a scientific basis for developing effective evaluation frameworks for vocational education courses. However, this study did not consider the characteristics of dual-system vocational education and did not make targeted network improvements for such scenarios. Therefore, in future work, multi task learning mechanisms should be further introduced to construct evaluation models for different educational scenarios. In addition, attention should be paid to the fairness of the model among different student groups, the identification and mitigation of potential biases in the evaluation process, and the transferability and adaptability of the model in vocational education systems of different countries or cultural backgrounds, in order to enhance its practical application value in global vocational education analysis.

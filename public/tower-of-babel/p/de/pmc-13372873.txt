Serological evidence of Toxoplasma gondii infection in native Dongan chickens intended for human consumption in Hunan province, subtropical China
Authors: Wen-Qin Xie, Wen-Cheng Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Toxoplasma gondii, an important food-borne parasite, has been reported in a wide range of food animals, including poultry. Dongan chicken, a native breed of Hunan province, subtropical China, is widely consumed, but its T. gondii infection status remains unknown. A total of 1,220 serum samples were collected from Dongan chickens intended for human consumption in Hunan province, China. Serum samples were tested for T. gondii IgG antibodies using an indirect hemagglutination assay (IHA). IgG antibodies against T. gondii were detected in 434 (35.6%; 95% CI: 32.9–38.3) of the examined serum samples. The seroprevalence of the six surveyed herds ranged from 31.7% to 39.8% (P > 0.05). Statistical analysis identified age, gender and farm model as significant risk factors for infection (P < 0.001). This is the first report of T. gondii seroprevalence in Dongan chickens in Yongzhou in Hunan province. The high seroprevalence suggests a potential risk for human and animal infection. Effective measures should be implemented to control T. gondii infection in Dongan chickens in Hunan province, subtropical China.

Introduction

Toxoplasmosis, caused by Toxoplasma gondii, is a globally significant zoonotic disease affecting humans and animals (Abbas et al. 2020). Nearly one-third of the world’s population is seropositive for this parasite (Montoya and Liesenfeld 2004). Human infection primarily occurs through the consumption of undercooked meat containing tissue cysts (Montoya and Liesenfeld 2004). While healthy individuals often experience mild or asymptomatic infections, immunocompromised patients (e.g., those with AIDS, cancer, or organ transplants) may develop severe or fatal disease outcomes (Elsheikha et al. 2020).

Recent studies indicate high T. gondii prevalence in chickens worldwide (Leong et al. 2023; Nie et al. 2022; Aguilar-Marín et al. 2022). Chickens serve as reliable indicators of environmental contamination with T. gondii oocysts (Dubey et al. 2015). In China, chicken meat is a major protein source, with approximately 16.1 billion chickens reared and 14.6 million tons consumed in 2022. Among native breeds, the Dongan chicken, a specialty of Hunan province, plays a vital role in the local economy due to its superior meat quality and nutritional value (Zhang and Li 2018). This breed is raised almost exclusively in Yongzhou area (Dongan County and surrounding regions), with only negligible populations found elsewhere in Hunan province. Annually, around 25 million Dongan chickens are consumed in Hunan, accounting for 16.3% of the province’s total chicken production (Wu and Dong 2022). T. gondii infection in chickens has been reported in many provinces of China, such as Hunan (Lv et al. 2022), Lanzhou (10.2%) (Cong et al. 2012), Jinzhou (18.8%) (Xu et al. 2012), and Wuxi (22.3%) (Dong et al. 2018). However, no data are available on T. gondii seroprevalence in Dongan chickens, despite their significance in human diets.

The objective of the present investigation was to examine the seroprevalence and risk factors of T. gondii in Dongan chickens in Hunan province, subtropical China. The findings will provide essential data for developing strategies to prevent and control toxoplasmosis in this region.

Materials and methods

Collection and preparation of serum samples

A total of 1,220 serum samples were collected from six representative farms in Dongan county, Hunan province, between January and December 2022 (Fig. 1; Table 1). The numbers of Dongan chickens reared on each farm ranged from approximately 300 to 5,000. The large-scale farms (population size > 10,000) were not included in this study because the farm owners were not present to give permission. From each farm, 5% of healthy Dongan chickens were sampled. Information of Dongan chickens was obtained by owners, including region, gender, season, age, and farm model. Dongan chickens were randomly selected, after which blood samples were collected. The samples were then centrifuged at 1000 xg for 10 min, and the resulting sera were stored at -20 °C until analysis.

Serological examination

T. gondii IgG were detected using an indirect hemagglutination assay (IHA) with a commercially available kit (NY/T 573–2002, Lanzhou Veterinary Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Lanzhou, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol. The procedures are according to the manufacturer’s instructions and previous descriptions (Cong et al. 2012; Lv et al. 2022). Samples with agglutination at dilutions ≥ 1:64 were considered positive, with positive and negative controls included for validation.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 20.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA). Variables (season, age, gender, farm model) were analyzed using the Chi-square test. Binary logistic regression was used to estimate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results and discussion

The present study revealed a high T. gondii seroprevalence (35.6%; 95% CI: 32.9–38.3) (Table 1) in Dongan chickens across all surveyed farms in Hunan province, suggesting that it is widespread in Dongan chickens in Hunan province of China. The seroprevalence among the six surveyed herds varied, ranging from 31.7% to 39.8%, (P > 0.05). T. gondii seroprevalence in Dongan chickens in the current study was markedly higher than those in Lanzhou (10.2%) (Cong et al. 2012), Jinzhou (18.8%) (Xu et al. 2012), and Wuxi (22.3%) (Dong et al. 2018) in China. Similarly, the seroprevalence rate in the current study was markedly higher than that in Senegal (7.7%) (Sarr et al. 2020) and the semiarid region of Brazil (27.9%) (Sá SG de et al. 2017). The differences might be related to detection methods, geographical environments, the overall sample sizes and animal husbandry practices.

By season, the T. gondii seroprevalence in Dongan chickens peaked in the summer (42.3%; 95% CI: 36.3–48.3) and was lowest in the winter (29.6%; 95% CI: 23.7–35.5), but the difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05) (Table 1). These results suggest that T. gondii infection in Dongan chickens is prevalent all year round, with peaks in summer and autumn, which are consistent with that of a previous study (Lv et al. 2022). The seroprevalence rate of T. gondii in Dongan chickens of the 6 < weeks ≤ 9 age group (48.8%; 95% CI: 44.4–53.2) was significantly higher than those of Dongan chickens of the 3 < weeks ≤ 6 (31.4%; 95% CI: 27.3–35.5) and 0 < weeks ≤ 3 age groups (22.7%; 95% CI: 11.2–20.6) (Table 1) (P < 0.001). The seroprevalence of T. gondii increased with the growth of chickens, particularly with age, indicating that there may be a cumulative likelihood of exposure. Our results are consistent with those findings from previous studies (Lv et al. 2022; Gebremedhin et al. 2015). The seroprevalence of T. gondii in female Dongan chickens was 41.5% (95%CI 37.5–45.5), which was higher than the rate in male Dongan chickens (30.2%, 95%CI: 26.7–33.8) (Table 1), and it is differences (P < 0.001). This is most likely due to difference in hormone levels between females and males (Nie et al. 2022). Our findings are similar to findings from previous studies (Bata et al. 2021; Yang et al. 2024). Also, compared with free-range chickens (42.8%; 95%CI: 39.5–46.2), cage-bred chickens (21.5%; 95%CI: 17.4–25.7) were less susceptible to infection with T. gondii (P < 0.001), indicating that free-range chickens had a higher chance of coming into contact with potential sources of infection. During an investigation, cats were noted to have unrestricted access to the farms. Furthermore, there were scattered feline feces within the farms. Although free-range chickens have outdoor runs, and food and warmth are available indoors, the chance of infected cats gaining access to the fenced runs is unlikely but possible, and the use of contaminated feed or water cannot be ruled out.

Consumption of undercooked or raw meat from chickens can be a source of T. gondii infection for humans and other animals (Dubey et al. 2015). Chicken is a popular meat in Chinese cuisine and is frequently eaten barbequed which could pose an infection risk if the meat is infected and not cooked thoroughly. A previous study showed that T. gondii cysts were present in viscera from experimentally infected chickens (Tenter et al. 2000). Therefore, people may be at increased risk of infection from inadequate disposal of viscera from infected chickens, which can also facilitate transmission of T. gondii to other hosts.

Despite these important findings, several limitations should be acknowledged in the present study. First, T. gondii infection was assessed solely by serological methods (indirect hemagglutination assay, IHA); no molecular methods (e.g., PCR or nested PCR) were used to detect parasite DNA directly in tissue samples. Serology reflects exposure to the parasite but does not confirm the presence of viable tissue cysts or actively infectious stages. Therefore, the actual risk posed by the consumption of Dongan chicken meat might be either overestimated or underestimated. Future studies should incorporate molecular characterization (e.g., PCR-RFLP or whole-genome sequencing) and expand sampling to multiple administrative regions across the province.

Conclusion

This study provides the first evidence of T. gondii seroprevalence in Dongan chickens in Yongzhou in Hunan province. The high infection rate highlights a potential public health risk. However, due to the lack of molecular confirmation of viable parasites, the findings should be interpreted with caution. Further research is needed, including molecular characterization of T. gondii isolates, to better assess transmission risks and guide effective control measures.

Current status and factors influencing compassion fatigue in clinical placement nursing students: a mixed-methods study protocol
Authors: Yan-Fei Yang, XiaoHan Li
Published: 2025

Introduction

Nurses are responsible for providing comprehensive treatment to patients suffering from various ailments, and they have authority and trust in the process. However, nurses routinely encounter pain and end-of-life circumstances while providing holistic patient care.1 Furthermore, to give personalised, high-quality care, nurses must display empathy, which means putting themselves in the shoes of the patient to experience and comprehend their feelings and needs.2 However, empathy is a complex dual-effect phenomenon,3 with the potential to harm nurses. Long-term emotional commitment, particularly when nurses are dealing with distressed patients and their families, can easily lead to ‘compassion fatigue’4 endangering the nurses’ own mental health.5,7 This phenomenon has been well documented in previous studies, and its negative effects cannot be ignored: compassion fatigue may not only impair nurses’ emotional and physical health, such as leading to anxiety, depression, irritability and fatigue8; it may also have a secondary effect on nursing care, including an increase in medical errors, a decline in nursing care quality, a decrease in patient satisfaction and an increase in the turnover rate of nursing staff.9

Nursing education in China has undergone a significant transformation. While a 4-year bachelor’s degree is becoming more common, diploma- and secondary-level programmes remain the primary training routes for most nurses.10 Furthermore, this educational progress starkly contrasts with severe systemic challenges in the nursing workforce. Public hospital wards maintain an average nurse-to-patient ratio of 1:8, which is significantly below the WHO’s minimum recommendation.11 Compounding this structural deficit is China’s persistently high nurse turnover rate, which is driven primarily by unsustainable workloads, burnout stemming from resource constraints, challenging working conditions and persistently limited societal recognition of the profession’s value.12 The resulting exodus not only exacerbates staffing shortages but also wastes substantial investments in professional development, signalling an urgent need for structural reforms that bridge educational achievements with clinical practice sustainability.

Background

Nursing students, including professional nurses, can develop compassion fatigue.2 13 Compassion fatigue refers to reduced empathetic capacity or interest towards ‘bearing the suffering of clients’ manifesting as consequent emotional and behavioural responses to secondary exposure to others’ traumatic experiences.14 On the one hand, nursing education builds empathy in nursing students and lays the groundwork for their future nursing jobs,15 yet persistent empathic engagement and accumulated stress may also pave the way for compassion fatigue.16 On the other hand, clinical practice, as a critical stage in the development of practical nursing skills, will impact nursing students’ physical and mental health because it exposes them to work pressures similar to those experienced by professional nurses, such as stressful working environments and traumatic situations.17 18 During their internships, nursing students are obliged to rotate across many departments and are exposed to demanding labour, inconsistent work schedules, overtime and tests. Simultaneously, they must create close contact with patients while maintaining an acceptable emotional distance, which is a major challenge for psychologically immature nursing students. Witnessing pain and death can lead to trauma.19 20

Nursing students’ susceptibility to compassion fatigue arises not only from personal factors but also from social processes within the clinical environment. Healthcare organisations often develop unconscious defence mechanisms, such as overreliance on technical tasks, to manage anxiety provoked by patient suffering.21 Systematic suppression of suppressing emotional involvement may inadvertently exacerbate compassion fatigue. Furthermore, unit-specific cultures establish explicit or implicit norms governing emotional expression, potentially encouraging nurses to suppress grief in response to patient-related disasters.22 For nursing students entering these complex clinical social systems, limited experience, combined with hierarchical pressure, amplifies these risks. They frequently lack mature support networks and may internalise unit norms that prioritise emotional restraint and neglect emotional self-care. This vulnerability is compounded by personal trauma history and insufficient training in managing distressing clinical experiences. Given the critical importance of students’ emotional responses to trauma, discussing diverse student profiles, personal trauma and targeted educational and support interventions designed for clinical internship challenges merits attention.

Compassion fatigue also has a profound impact on nursing students, involving physiological, psychological, social, emotional and spiritual dimensions,23 which may not only harm their health and reduce the efficiency of the internship, but also increase the risk of medical accidents,24 thus triggering nursing students’ worry and panic about their future career development, shaking their professional beliefs and even prompting some nursing students to choose to leave nursing.24 25 Even when students persevere through their clinical placement, they remain vulnerable to compassion fatigue, which may precipitate burnout. Crucially, if compassion fatigue develops during training, it can significantly impede the transition from student to professional nurses, ultimately compromising career adaptation and retention.26

Despite these risks, current compassion fatigue research disproportionately focuses on specialised nursing populations (eg, psychiatric, emergency and perioperative nurses), with a striking scarcity of studies examining nursing students.2427,29 Academics must pay closer attention to the phenomena of compassion fatigue in nursing students and actively research mitigation techniques to enhance the physical and mental health development of nursing students. A strong platform must be established for cultivating high-quality nursing talent.30 31

In China, students in nursing school programmes enter a clinical setting in their last year to gain hands-on instruction. Several studies have reported the prevalence of compassion fatigue in nursing student populations27 32 33; however, most were cross-sectional surveys that did not include student viewpoints and experiences. Therefore, it is essential to focus on nursing students’ experiences of compassion fatigue and the mechanisms underlying these experiences.26 Furthermore, compassion fatigue in nursing students can be a dynamic process that develops over time. As a result, this study will employ a longitudinal approach to investigate the trends and influencing factors of compassion fatigue among nursing students and gain an understanding of nursing students’ psychological feelings, experiences and coping styles regarding compassion fatigue via interviews.

Aims and objectives

The purpose of this study is to better understand the phenomenon of compassion fatigue as experienced by nursing students during their final year placements, as well as to investigate the elements that impact it.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This study will use a prospective longitudinal mixed-methods design. Quantitative and qualitative data will be collected at two defined time points during the nursing students’ clinical placements: (1) the early phase (defined as having less than 3 months of cumulative clinical placement experience) and (2) the late phase (defined as having more than 8 months of cumulative clinical placement experience). Both types of data will be examined separately, with equal emphasis on quantitative and qualitative research. Data collection and analysis will begin in October 2025 and be completed by June 2026.

Undergraduate nursing students undertaking clinical placements will be asked to take part in a longitudinal survey by completing questionnaires that will include the following. The Chinese Compassion Fatigue-Short Scale (CFSS; 13 items), selected due to its established use in assessing compassion fatigue among Chinese medical workers, demonstrating strong reliability (Cronbach’s α=0.90) in prior studies.34 The Social Support Rating Scale (10 items), which captures culturally relevant support networks related to compassion fatigue outcomes.35 Empathic capacity will be measured using the Jefferson Scale of Empathy-Nursing Students (20 items), which has been widely used in China (Cronbach’s α=0.739).36 Professional identity will be evaluated using the Professional Identity Questionnaire for Nurse Students (17 items), chosen for its excellent psychometric robustness (Cronbach’s α=0.827).37 The Chinese Connor-Davidson Resilience Scale (25 items), cross-culturally validated for trauma-exposed healthcare workers (Cronbach’s α=0.951), will be employed to assess resilience factors.38 Coping strategies will be examined via the Simple Coping Style Questionnaire (20 items), which discriminates between adaptive and maladaptive responses in clinical cohorts (Cronbach’s α=0.90).39 Demographic and placement-specific variables will be assessed through a self-developed General Information Questionnaire (16 items). Collectively, the survey will comprise 121 items, requiring approximately 20 min for completion.

To ensure participant anonymity, electronic questionnaires will be distributed using the Questionnaire Star platform. It employs multiple privacy safeguards: (1) an automatic Internet Protocol (IP) address blocking function prevents the collection of geographic or device information, and (2) the questionnaire design excludes personally identifiable information such as phone numbers or email addresses, retaining only essential demographic variables (eg, gender and academic year). An informed consent statement will be displayed on the initial page, explicitly noting, ‘Participants may withdraw at any time without consequences to clinical evaluations or academic standing. Submission implies consent for anonymous data use in academic analysis’. All data will be stored on password-encrypted devices maintained by the research team and physically isolated from institutional systems. Clinical mentors and academic counsellors will be excluded from recruitment, data collection and analysis. The authority of oversight resides solely with the ethics committee. Furthermore, undergraduate nursing students in clinical placements will be invited to participate in semistructured interviews conducted early (defined as having less than 3 months of cumulative clinical placement experience) and late (defined as having more than 8 months of cumulative clinical placement experience) in their placements to investigate nursing students’ psychological feelings and experiences with compassion fatigue following their placements. Interview transcripts will undergo redaction of identifiable details through anonymous coding, with sensitive content systematically anonymised. Methodological framework

This study adopts a pragmatist research paradigm as its overarching methodological framework. Pragmatism prioritises research over methodological orthodoxy, allowing the integration of quantitative and qualitative approaches to comprehensively address the complex phenomenon of compassion fatigue.40 Guided by this framework, we employ an explanatory sequential mixed-methods design, wherein quantitative (survey) and qualitative (interview) data are collected in parallel during two defined clinical placement phases, analysed separately, and then integrated to generate nuanced insights.

Sampling and recruitment

This research will be carried out in a higher education institution in China. The research participants will be selected from among undergraduate students currently pursuing a 4-year bachelor’s degree in nursing at the university. This study was approved by the University’s Human Research Ethics Committee.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria are undergraduate nursing students in the clinical placement stage who provide informed consent and volunteer to participate in the study. Participants self-reporting major physical or mental health conditions that would impede their ability to complete the study procedures will be excluded, as will those who have been on leave for more than 1 month during their clinical practicum.

Participants will be explicitly informed of their right to refuse participation or withdraw from the study at any time without penalty for clinical evaluation, academic standing or personal rights.

Sample size

Quantitative research

According to the sample size estimation formula n=Zα/22[P(1−P)]/δ2, the test level α=0.05, Za/2=1.96, P is the expected probability estimate. Combined with the literature that the incidence of compassion fatigue in nursing interns is 19.5%,32 and allowing for 20% of the sample to be lost, the minimum sample size required is 72 cases. However, to robustly support our multivariate analysis involving five predictors (social support, empathy, resilience, etc.) in the hierarchical linear regression, we conducted a power analysis using G*Power 3.1. Assuming a medium effect size (f²=0.15), α=0.05 and power=0.8, the analysis indicated a minimum requirement of 92 participants. Furthermore, considering the longitudinal design and the potential 20% additional dropouts, we increased the target sample size to 110 participants. This adjustment ensures adequate power for both primary prevalence estimation and multivariable modelling while maintaining methodological rigour.

Qualitative research

The sample size for the qualitative component was determined by the principle of data saturation, defined as the point at which all interviews yield the same information and at which stage data collection will cease.

Sampling strategy

In the quantitative phase, eligible undergraduate nursing students will be recruited using convenience sampling. Compassion fatigue risk will be objectively determined using the validated CFSS. In the qualitative research phase, purposive sampling will be used to select research subjects. Purposive sampling will recruit nursing students from diverse clinical departments (eg, internal medicine, surgery, emergency) stratified by sex and compassion fatigue risk levels. To mitigate potential selection bias, recruitment will be conducted by staff blinded to the participants’ compassion fatigue. We acknowledge that sex-based stratification may limit generalisability; this will be addressed through sensitivity analyses when comparing subgroup responses.

Data collection

Longitudinal quantitative studies

Longitudinal study outcome variables were selected based on prior evidence and literature review. Questionnaires will assess compassion fatigue risk and psychosocial factors at two critical junctures within the final-year 12-month continuous clinical placement: the early phase (after <3 months of cumulative clinical experience) and late phase (after >8 months of cumulative clinical experience).

Longitudinal qualitative research

In the early (<3 months of cumulative clinical placement experience) and late (>8 months of cumulative clinical placement experience) stages of nursing students’ clinical internships, we will select interview participants using purposive sampling. The sampling process will follow the principle of maximising sample variation to ensure representativeness. Through qualitative interviews, we aim to document clinical internship nursing students’ knowledge, experiences and perceptions of compassion fatigue.

Data analysis

All collected data will be examined, and the possibility of bias will be reduced using intentionality analysis.

Analysis of quantitative research data

The demographic data of undergraduate nursing students in clinical practicum who will participate in the survey will be presented using descriptive statistics. The data will undergo checks for normality, outliers and missing values. Measures obeying normal distribution will be described using means and SD, measures not obeying normal distribution will be described with medians and quartiles and categorical data will be presented as frequencies and percentages. Additionally, statistical analysis will be performed to determine the influencing factors and longitudinal trajectories of compassion fatigue among clinical practicum nursing students. Statistical analyses will be completed using R Statistical Software.41 All statistical tests will be two-sided, with p≤0.05 considered statistically significant.42

Qualitative data analysis

The interviews will last 30–40 min for each interviewee. The researcher will transcribe the collected data into text within 24 hours, including the time and location of the interview. We will analyse textual material using NVivo V.12.0,43 categorising data sentence-by-sentence at the meaning level through a content analysis to refine themes.44

The specific steps include: (1) Use each interview text as a unit of analysis. Before analysis, read the entire text multiple times to gain a sense of it. (2) Analyse statements to identify distinct meanings and build semantic units. (3) Compress the semantic unit further to create a compressed semantic unit. (4) Extract the compressed semantic unit to create a code. (5) Repeat the cycle until no new themes develop. (6) Compare the similarities and differences between the codes taken from all units of analysis, place the codes with commonalities into the same entry and then build a spectrum of interconnected and independent entries. (7) Repeatedly discuss and reflect on the codes and entries within the study group, eventually reaching a consensus and linking all of the elements to build a theme.

To ensure methodological rigour and credibility of the qualitative analysis, we will implement a tripartite strategy. First, the two researchers will independently code interview transcripts using NVivo V.12.0. Coding discrepancies will be resolved through consensus meetings and documented in the audit trail. Second, transferability will be ensured through detailed contextual descriptions of the clinical internship environment during participant recruitment. Finally, during the comprehensive analysis phase, the emerging qualitative themes will be cross-validated using quantitative trajectories of compassion fatigue.

Integration and analysis of mixed methods

Quantitative analysis

The principal investigator will analyse quantitative data from two time points to investigate changes in compassion fatigue risk levels during clinical internships. Participants’ risk levels will be operationally defined using their total scores on the CFSS. At each assessment time point, the total scores will be ranked from highest to lowest. The high-risk group will comprise participants scoring in the highest 27% of the distribution, while the low-risk group will include those in the lowest 27%. Participants with scores between the 27th and 73rd percentiles will form the moderate-risk group. Statistical analyses will examine longitudinal changes across these groups.

Qualitative analysis

Interview data will be transcribed verbatim and managed using NVivo. Two researchers will independently code all transcripts using thematic analysis. Coding discrepancies will be resolved through discussion until a consensus is reached and the final themes are refined by the research team.

Data integration

In the final interpretation phase, the entire research team will jointly compare the quantitative and qualitative findings. Integration will focus on identifying areas of complementarity (eg, quantitative high-risk groups contextualised by qualitative narratives), expansion (qualitative insights elaborating quantitative factors), consistency (convergent themes) and inconsistency (discordant results). Consensus discussions will resolve interpretations, with unresolved discrepancies documented as study limitations.

Monitoring and quality assurance

The credibility of the study will be assessed based on accurate and transparent documentation of the research procedure. The report will follow the Standards for Reporting Mixed Studies guidelines to examine the quality and trustworthiness of the research techniques.45

Longitudinal quantitative study

To ensure the content validity, questionnaire items were generated with input from experts in the field and a review of the relevant literature. Study participants will be selected based on the defined inclusion and exclusion criteria. The reasons for research participants’ withdrawal from follow-up will be documented in a complete and timely manner, with further explanations provided, as needed, to ensure the accuracy of future data analysis.

Longitudinal qualitative research

The researcher will transcribe the interviews into text within 24 hours of completion. The results of the analysis will then be fed back to the interviewees for further evidence and confirmation.

Ethics and dissemination

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of China Medical University (reference no.2024/211). Before the study begins, the researcher will thoroughly explain the study’s purpose, significance and implementation process to participants who meet the inclusion criteria, inform them that they have the right to freely choose to participate in or withdraw from the study, and explain the potential risks and benefits of participating in the study. An informed consent form will be provided to the study participants and signed voluntarily on the condition that they read it carefully and fully understand the contents. During the study, participants’ identifiable information will be replaced with anonymised codes to prevent disclosure of personal data. The disclosure of all information obtained and the extent of disclosure will be authorised by the study participants. Participation in this study is entirely optional, and participants may quit at any moment during the study without incurring any negative consequences.

The research findings will be disseminated and shared in scholarly publications as well as in academic conference communications.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not involved in the conceptualisation or implementation of the research protocol.

Discussion

Summary of expected outcomes

This protocol covers the proposed mixed-methods research on undergraduate nursing students in practicums using surveys and interviews. The findings of this study will help establish a tailored intervention programme to reduce compassion fatigue among nursing students.

Limitations

First, this study will be conducted in a single city in China; hence, the results may not apply to other nations. Furthermore, this study will be longitudinal in nature, and participant withdrawals may have an impact on the study’s continuity and integrity. To minimise the dropout rate, small gifts will be presented to the study participants at the end of each survey to establish and maintain a strong bond with them.

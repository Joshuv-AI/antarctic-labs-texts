Successful preservation of functional right kidney with giant stones: A rare Chinese case report with literature review
Authors: Quanlei Wang, Xiyin Ye, Xiaohui Feng, Ting Lin, JingJing Liu, Yongdong Zhu, Yuanzan Zhu, Eddie Cheung, Jingxing Dai, Wanhong Liang, Yuan Liang, Sangui Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRenal calculi weighing over 200 g are relative rare in modern urological practice since most stones might cause renal dysfunction before reaching such size. We here reported an extremely rare case of giant stones in single kidney of a 72-year-old Chinese male patient who had 30 years long-term history of lithiasis in the right kidney. These huge kidney stones, total weight 832.6 g were successfully removed with multiple sessions of surgery. Kidney function was relatively stable after long-term medical care in primary care centers. Timely treatment of kidney stones early should arouse more attention worldwide.Keywords: Giant kidney calculus, Open surgery, Percutaneous nephrolithotomy, Case report

Introduction

Kidney stone disease is a common complaint in primary care centers, with patients usually presenting with pain and urinary tract infections, which may result in chronic renal disease or even renal cancer. The prevalence of kidney stone disease published varies between 2 % and 20 %, with an prevalence of around 7 % in China.1 The formation of kidney stones was regarded as an extensive and complicated process, which is regulated by metabolic changes in various substances. Currently, percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL) had become the standard procedure for the management of large and complex renal stones due to a relatively minimally invasive procedure. However, bleeding, infection were still common complications in PCNL or open surgery, which might be associated with the size of stones, stone complexity, surgical history of ipsilateral stone.2

According to prior studies, a case of renal calculus weighing 2260 g became the largest record worldwide which was reported in 1987 in India.3 In 20014 and 2007,5 Turkey patients with giant renal calculus weighing 770 g and 1350 g in the solitary functioning kidney were reported, respectively. Meanwhile, bilateral or unilateral huge staghorn calculi were reported in normal anatomical or horseshoe kidneys in India and España.6, 7, 8 In China, a 46-year-old Chinese man suffering presenting with left lumbar pain and macroscopic hematuria for years developed a 960 g stone in the left kidney, who had undergone nephrectomy was reported in 1981.9 Later, 340 g, 240 g stones in the left kidney, 200 g, and 585 g in the right kidney of Chinese cases were reported in 1991,10 2003,11 2006,12 and 201013, respectively. These above cases previously published were mainly performed with nephrectomy and shown relatively univocal complication.14 Collectively, case reports of giant renal calculus weighing above 800 g, and the functional kidney was saved after surgery in the Chinese population were extremely rarity.

In this study, we report a rare Chinese case with two large stones (total weight: 832.6 g) in his right kidney and was treated in a primary care centers. These huge renal stones were successfully removed by performing several sessions of surgery. The case reported here was significant not only because it was the relatively huge size stone weight above 800 g of Chinese population in the literature but also because it was the massive stone reported to be in the right functioning kidney and treated without nephrectomy in China.

Case presentation

Preoperative comprehensive examination

The patient's previous medical history revealed a stone-related history of open surgery in 1996, and was presented to our outpatient clinic with right lumbar pain in 2023. By physical examination, he was found to be tender to percussion and tenderness pain over the right lumbar. Routine blood and biochemical examination revealed leukocyte: 11.5 × 109/L, hypersensitive C-reactive protein: 52.87 mg/L. Urine analysis demonstrated microhematuria, and urine bacterioscopy was normal (Table 1, Preoperative value 1).

Collectively, the above clinical examination results, including physical, blood biochemical examination, urine analysis and radiography analysis, indicted the patient presented here was undergone severe infection which might be caused by the two huge stones occupied in right kidney (Fig. 1B).

Multiple sessions of surgery were performed

All possible treatment modalities were discussed with the patient, and he preferred percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL) under general anesthesia. Thus, the ballistic lithotripsy-PCNL, as session 1 surgery, was performed during which several small size stones (weight 14.6 g) were removed from the two huge stones (Fig. 1D). Moreover, the systemic infection complications including fevers and chills were occurred during the surgery. Therefore, the ballistic lithotripsy-PCNL operations were terminated, and a nephrostomy tube was placed at the end of the procedure, and anti-infective drugs, immunoglobulin were given to enhance anti-infection and promote resistance.

Ten days after the ballistic lithotripsy-PCNL, the systemic infection has been relatively controlled (Table 1, Preoperative value 2), as session 2, the open surgery with nephrotomy holmium laser lithotripsy was performed. Two huge stones (size: 10 × 7.6 × 7.5 cm, weight 434 g, for stone 1; size:9.5 × 9 × 6.5 cm, weight 384 g, for stone 2; total weight: 818 g) in the functional kidney were successfully extracted using the operating forceps (Fig. 1D). The infrared spectroscopic analysis indicated the main constituent of these massive stones were ammonium magnesium phosphate hexahydrate and apatite carbonate. A postoperative abdominal X-ray showed no residual stones in the partially functional right kidney (Fig. 2A–B).

Postoperative long-term medical care

After the open surgery, the transcatheter superselective angioembolization was performed to control bleeding under local anesthetic, and the drugs such as tranexamic acid and plasma infusion were given to anti-infection and to improve bleeding symptoms. More than one month after the initial PCNL, the renal surgical complication of the patient was relatively stable (Table 1, Postoperative value).

Literature review

Discussion

Kidney stone disease remains a common illness that causes an economic burden due to the high incidence and recurrence rate globally. Giant renal stone is a rare entity in modern urological practice due to the development of medical diagnosis technology and the improvement of lifestyle and diet. In the presented study, we reported a case of a 72-year-old Chinese male patient with 30-year long-term history of lithiasis presented to our outpatient clinic with right lumbar pain lasting for more than one month. Physical examination, laboratory testing, ureterography CT, etc. consisted of giant stones with hydronephrosis in the right kidney, also multiple renal cysts, and prostatic hyperplasia, which indicates the complicated process of kidney stone formation and “growth” and urinary system changing across many years.

To the best of our knowledge, currently, only one 46-year-old Chinese case of renal calculi weighting above 800 g has been reported in 1981, which underwent a nephrectomy and hospitalized treatment for more than a month. The case reported here was significant not only because it was a relatively huge size stone weighing above 800 g, but also because it was a giant stone reported in the right functioning kidney and treated without nephrectomy in China. The presented case also had a right kidney stone removal surgery about 30 years ago. Therefore, timely treatment of kidney stone at an early stage might be an effective strategy to prevent massive stone formation and serious complications, which should arouse more attention around the world.15

Percutaneous nephrolithotomy (PCNL) is the standard procedure for treating huge and complex renal stones, which significantly reducing the number of lumpectomies performed for benign lithiasis.16 Despite recent advances in PCNL, bleeding, infection were still common complications in PCNL. Previous studies had demonstrated that history of ipsilateral open surgery increased the risk of postoperative bleeding in PCNL.17 Also, urinary tract infection has been identified as a risk factor for post-PCNL hemorrhage.18 In addition, many kidney-related anatomical factors were also related to bleeding in PCNL. For instance, several studies have identified the role of hydronephrosis as one of the predictors of bleeding in PCNL.19

Meanwhile, other studies had shown that the size of stones and stone complexity were essential factors for severe vessel injury complications.20 In line with previous studies above, in our case, the patient showed relatively higher bleeding and infection in PCNL and open surgery, that might be due to a somewhat bigger stone size, more extended history of ipsilateral surgery and hydronephrosis factors. With technical advancements, the bleeding incidence has decreased; nevertheless, cellular and molecular mechanisms for preventing bleeding of PCNL, nephrotomy holmium laser lithotripsy-open surgery should be deeply explored. Moreover, the clinical optimal management of kidney stone patient, especially for the huge renal calculus case should be payed more attentions in future work. The choice of minimally invasive or open surgery should be considered according to comprehensive factors such as stone condition and the patient's physical condition; in other words, the personalized lithotomy approach is the direction of medical technology development.

Conclusion

In this study, we reported a rare Chinese case of giant renal stones in a right kidney, which was successfully removed by performing multiple sessions of surgery. Kidney function was relatively stable after long-term medical care. Timely treatment of kidney stones early should arouse more attention worldwide.

Domiciliary denture treatment for edentulous older adults: a retrospective study
Authors: Xiaoting Shen, Xiaoyu Chen, Shengmei Lei, Xiaojing Cheng, Qianming Chen, Fuming He, Zhuo Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTooth loss increases with age, placing a greater burden on older adults. While the demand for domiciliary dental services is growing, its implementation and efficacy remain underexplored. This study aims to examine the procedures and evaluate the effectiveness of domiciliary denture treatment for edentulous older adults, providing valuable insights for future practice.MethodsNineteen edentulous patients who received domiciliary denture treatment between February 2021 and April 2022 were included. Patient satisfaction with new dentures was assessed using a visual analogue scale (VAS). Oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) and nutritional status were evaluated before and after denture delivery using the Oral Health Impact Profile-14 (OHIP-14) and Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA), respectively. Masticatory performance was measured via a two-colored chewing gum test. Facial contour changes were recorded using three-dimensional facial scanning technology.ResultsAt the 3-month post-denture delivery assessment, OHRQoL showed a significant improvement (p = 0.002) and remained stable throughout the 12-month follow-up period, aligning with high patient satisfaction. Nutritional status also improved marginally following rehabilitation, though the change was not statistically significant (p > 0.05). Masticatory performance saw a significant enhancement (p = 0.039). In terms of facial contour, significant post-rehabilitation increases were observed in the lower third facial height (Sn-Gn; Sn-Pg), upper lip height (Sn-Ls), mouth width (ChR-ChL), and lower facial convexity (Sn-St-Pg) (p < 0.05). Conversely, facial convexity excluding the nasal component (N-Sn-Pg) decreased significantly (p = 0.029).ConclusionDomiciliary denture services provide a clinically effective and convenient healthcare model for homebound edentulous older adults. Despite its limitations, this study’s preliminary findings indicate that this approach improves masticatory performance and facial harmony, thereby enhancing nutritional status and yielding significant gains in quality of life, meriting inclusion in public health programs for ageing populations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-025-07124-6.

Introduction

The global population is aging at an unprecedented rate, with the number of individuals aged 60 years and older projected to reach 2.1 billion by 2050 [1]. Advancing age is often associated with a range of age-related health challenges, among which dentition loss stands as a prevalent issue with profound implications for older adults’ overall health [2]. According to the results of the Fourth National Health Epidemiology Survey 2017, the prevalence of edentulism in the age group 65–74 years was 4.5% [3], showing a relatively low edentulous rate in China, which may be related to the fact that many residual roots and residual crowns without conservation value remain unextracted [4]. Data from the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) show that 12.9% of U.S. adults aged 65 and over are edentulous, with the prevalence increasing across age groups: 8.9% among those aged 65–69, 10.6% among those aged 70–74, and 17.8% among those aged 75 and over [5], indicating the impact of dentition loss on older adults intensifies with advancing age.

Dentition loss can lead to masticatory and articulatory dysfunction, affect nutrition intake, ultimately deteriorate oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) [6–8]. Additionally, it is associated with facial collapse, which may compromise facial aesthetics and lead to psychological distress and social withdrawal [9]. Therefore, the treatment of edentulous patients should not only focus on restoring function through dental replacement, but also emphasis the recovery of facial appearance [10, 11]. Currently, common evaluation indicators for denture treatment outcomes include direct indicators such as masticatory function, facial contour restoration, and patient satisfaction [12, 13], as well as indirect indicators like OHRQoL and nutritional status [14]. While paying attention to direct indicators, it is essential to integrate indirect indicators for comprehensive assessment, as improving the long-term overall health and quality of life of edentulous patients remains the primary objective of denture treatment.

Although edentulism is relatively common, rehabilitation treatment options for edentulous patients remain limited. While implant-supported prostheses demonstrate satisfactory clinical outcomes [15], their applicability is constrained for many older adults, particularly those with homebound status, due to surgical contraindications or functional limitations. For this vulnerable subgroup, removable dentures often serve as the primary restoration modality because of their affordability, aesthetics, and maintainability [16]. However, compromised physical and cognitive functions in homebound older adults frequently hinder their access to routine outpatient dental treatment, thereby escalating the demand for domiciliary dental services [17, 18]. This unmet demand underscores the critical need for comprehensive domiciliary dental solutions to address both restoration and accessibility challenges. Due to the limited dental infrastructure deployable to patients’ residences, the coverage of domiciliary dental services remains highly restricted, primarily encompassing dental scaling, denture repair, and dental care [17, 19–21]. Unlike routine outpatient dental services, domiciliary dental services demand higher personnel and time expenditures, rely on portable equipment, and require the workspace to be reconfigured for each visit. These factors increase implementation difficulty, limit widespread adoption, and contribute to the scarcity of published evidence.

In the context of global population aging, exploring the workflows and effectiveness of domiciliary denture treatment delivered at patients’ residences are conducive to optimizing operational procedures and evaluation systems, and is of significant social importance. Therefore, this study aims to provide a comprehensive assessment of domiciliary denture treatment for edentulous older adults by integrating both direct and indirect indicators, offering valuable insights for future domiciliary denture treatment.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Stomatology Hospital, School of Stomatology, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, China (Approval number: 2024 -119). It was conducted in accordance with the revised Declaration of Helsinki [22], and followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines for the conduct and reporting of observational studies [23]. All participants signed the informed consent form before treatment.

Study participant

This study retrieved the relevant records from the Community Elderly Oral Health Program, a domiciliary initiative delivered by the Affiliated Stomatology Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine between February 2021 and April 2022. All subjects were community-dwelling older adults with impaired mobility who were unable to access routine outpatient dental care. Each participant received standardized domiciliary oral-health services, including provision of new complete or removable partial dentures, adjustment or relining of existing prostheses, and preventive maintenance, all implemented according to a predefined clinical protocol. Regular follow-up visits were scheduled to monitor treatment outcomes and maintain oral health. Participants who received domiciliary denture interventions were further screened against predetermined inclusion and exclusion criteria for the present effectiveness analysis. Flow chart of participant inclusion

Inclusion criteria: (1) Maxillary and/or mandibular dentition loss; (2) Received removable denture treatment: either new dentures or denture replacements; (3) Availability of complete oral digital health records including basic demographics, oral examination records, and facial imaging data. Exclusion criteria: (1) History of facial esthetic procedures or plastic surgery; (2) Incomplete facial scan data or questionnaire information; (3) Refused to participate in the study.

Therapeutic procedures

Except for the venue, the domiciliary protocol replicated every clinical and laboratory step of standard outpatient denture fabrication. To satisfy the minimal oral conditions required for removable denture rehabilitation, necessary preoperative procedures such as dental fillings and simple extractions were performed. Conventional techniques were then employed throughout, incorporating the following modifications to accommodate domiciliary treatment for edentulous older adults. All clinical procedures, including primary and secondary impressions, jaw-relation records, trial insertion, and intra-oral adjustments, were performed at patients’ residences by an experienced team using a portable dental unit equipped with battery-powered handpieces and LED headlights. Dentures were subsequently fabricated at the denture-processing center of Stomatology Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, using the centric-relation record; each prosthesis was standardized with a cobalt-chromium metal framework, epoxy-resin base, and acrylic-resin artificial teeth. Following fabrication, denture delivery, intraoral adjustment, and detailed instructions on denture wearing and maintenance were provided to facilitate patients’ adaptation. Beyond the pre-prosthetic oral treatment, we further optimized the domiciliary denture workflow, enabling the entire prosthetic treatment to be completed in 3–4 home visits. In the event of discomfort subsequent to denture wear, such as denture pressure sores or mucosal ulcerations, prompt denture adjustments will be performed to alleviate these issues. After a 3-month adaptation period, rehabilitation was considered complete when patients reported no significant intraoral discomfort, masticatory dysfunction, or functional limitations. Subsequent follow-up assessments were conducted at patients’ residences.

Patient-reported outcomes

The patients’ OHRQoL was assessed by the Chinese version of Oral Health Impact Profile-14 (OHIP-14) at baseline (pre-denture delivery) and 3, 6, and 12 months post-denture delivery [24]. The instrument comprises 14 items across 7 subscales: functional limitation, physical pain, psychological discomfort, physical disability, psychological disability, social disability, and handicap. Each item is rated on a 5-point scale (0 = never to 4 = very often), yielding a total score ranging from 0 to 56. Higher scores indicate poorer OHRQoL.

The Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) was employed to assess patient satisfaction with new dentures at 3 months post-denture delivery. The scale comprised 5 items addressing denture aesthetics, speech, mastication, retention, and comfort[12]. Each item was rated on a 0 to 10 scale, with higher scores reflecting greater satisfaction.

Masticatory performance

Masticatory performance was assessed at baseline and 3 months post-denture delivery using the two-colored chewing gum test, a method based on analysis of color mixing in chewing gums [25]. The two-colored chewing gum (Hue-check Gum, University of Bern, Switzerland) was employed to assess masticatory performance. The patients were asked to sit up straight and perform 30 chewing cycles without a time limit. The gums were then spat into transparent plastic bags for a five-category visual assessment. The scoring criteria were as follows: a score of 1 indicated no mixing; 2 denoted large portions of chewing gum remaining unmixed; 3 represented a slightly mixed bolus with visible unmixed color fragments; 4 corresponded to a well-mixed bolus with non-uniform color; and 5 indicated a perfectly mixed bolus with uniform color.

Nutritional status

The nutritional status of patients was assessed at baseline and 3, 6, and 12 months post-denture delivery, utilizing the Chinese-adapted Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA), translated and validated from the original version developed by Guigoz et al. [26]. The instrument includes 18 items across four aspects: anthropometric assessment (e.g., body-mass index, mid-arm and calf circumferences, weight loss); general assessment (mobility, medication, psychological stress, acute disease, neuro-psychological problems); dietary assessment (number of full meals, protein- and vegetable-intake items, fluid intake, feeding autonomy); subjective assessment (self-perception of health and nutrition). Each item is weighted and summed to yield a total score ranging from 0 to 30: ≥ 24 indicates normal nutritional status; 17-23.5 indicates risk of undernutrition; < 17 indicates undernutrition.

Facial analysis

Facial contour images were acquired at baseline and 3 months post-denture delivery using i3Dface software (Meidai Technology Co., Ltd. Hangzhou, China) via a tablet. Acquisitions were conducted in well-lit settings following the manufacturer’s guidelines, with the entire process mimicking facial recognition protocols. Patients were instructed to remove earrings or glasses, secure hair to expose the forehead and external auditory meatus, sit in a fixed chair position, and maintain a natural head posture for image capture. During imaging, they were instructed to look at a fixed point, relax facial muscles, gently close the lips, and maintain a resting jaw position without tooth contact, corresponding to the rest vertical dimension [10, 11]. Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were described using counts and percentages. Continuous variables were summarized with mean, standard deviation (SD), median, and interquartile range (IQR). The normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk Test. For comparisons between two groups, parametric (paired t-test) and nonparametric (Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed-rank test) tests were performed. For comparisons across multiple groups, nonparametric Friedman test followed by post-hoc Dunn’s multiple comparisons test was employed. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad prism (version 10.0.3, San Diego CA, USA) and R package (version 4.4.0, R Foundation for Statistical Computing). The significance level was set at p < 0.05.

Results

A total of 19 male patients (mean age: 90.37 ± 2.43 years) were included in this study for patient satisfaction, masticatory performance, and facial analysis. Of these, 13 patients (mean age: 90.00 ± 2.58 years) were further analyzed for OHRQoL and nutritional status.

Patient-reported outcomes

The median OHIP-14 scores significantly decreased from 33 (21.5, 41) at baseline to 2 (1.5, 8) at 3 months post-denture delivery (p = 0.002) and remained stable throughout the 12-month follow-up assessment (Figure S1). Correspondingly, high level of patient satisfaction with new dentures were observed post-rehabilitation, with a median score of 48 (42, 49).

Masticatory performance

Visual assessments using the two-colored chewing gum test indicated that masticatory performance scores improved from 2.26 ± 0.93 at baseline to 3.00 ± 0.94 at 3 months post-denture delivery. This improvement was statistically significant (p = 0.002) (Figure S1).

Nutritional status

At baseline, 2 patients were classified as undernourished, 7 as at risk of undernutrition, and 4 as well-nourished. Following denture delivery, no patients were classified as undernutrition at either the 6-month or 12-month follow-up (Table 2). Discussion

With the aging population, age-related functional impairments, especially mobility disabilities, are increasingly hindering older adults from accessing routine outpatient dental services, thereby severely impacting their quality of life. The necessity of domiciliary dental services has grown, rendering it socially significant to enhance accessibility to dental treatment and refine effectiveness evaluation criteria. However, due to constraints in personnel, equipment, and venue, the implementation of domiciliary dental services remains challenging and is rarely documented in the literature [17, 18]. In this context, it is essential to explore and optimize domiciliary dental service protocols and enhance the evaluation system. By delivering denture services directly to patients’ residences, this study observed significant improvements in masticatory performance, which translated into objectively measurable enhancements in nutritional status and substantial gains in quality of life. These findings indicate that domiciliary denture services provide a clinically effective and convenient healthcare model for homebound older adults, with a societal cost comparable to conventional transport-to-clinic models, supporting its integration into public health programs for ageing populations.

As the region’s premier dental hospital and with government support, we launched the Community Elderly Oral Health Program to pioneer evidence-based domiciliary dental services. Providing these services to older adults with mobility impairments requires additional resources, including extra mobile dental units and extended travel time between patient residences. To expand the scope of oral treatment, we introduced mobile dental clinics in the later stages of service delivery. However, the additional resources required have somewhat limited its implementation, making widespread application among the general population less feasible. Nevertheless, given the unique needs of this specific cohort, we recommend that governmental agencies initiate this treatment protocol, with services delivered by community-based dental hospitals or clinics. The costs exceeding those of routine treatment should be covered by social welfare provisions to alleviate the financial burden on families.

This study retrieved relevant records from the Community Elderly Oral Health Program, which employed a comprehensive evaluation framework to assess the effectiveness of domiciliary denture treatment for edentulous older adults. This framework integrated both subjective and objective indicators and considered both local and systemic therapeutic effects, enabling a more holistic assessment. Due to the unique characteristics of the study population, homebound older adults with advanced age and mobility impairments, a routine outpatient denture treatment control group was not appropriate. We therefore adopted a “second-best” approach, using a matched pre-/post-intervention design to control for baseline individual differences. However, due to the inherent limitations of the study design and the relatively small sample size, the results, despite having small p-values from the internal control comparison, must be interpreted with caution. Therefore, this study prefers to set its goal as preliminarily exploring the implementation process and evaluation system for domiciliary dental treatment. Future large-scale studies are needed to further verify the treatment’s effectiveness.

Denture fabrication, in conjunction with domiciliary dental treatment, helps maintain occlusal support, which is believed to positively influence the nutritional status and prognosis of older adults [20, 27]. In this study, masticatory performance was evaluated via a five-category visual assessment of two-colored chewing gum to meet the convenience of the home environment. As expected, the masticatory function of edentulous older adults in this study improved significantly. However, using digital scanning and color analysis software for objective analysis could yield more reliable data. The Mini Nutritional Assessment used in this study is well-suited for home-dwelling older adults [26]. It allows for a rapid and comprehensive evaluation of nutritional status, identifies nutritional risks, and provides a basis for intervention. However, it is important to note that the nutritional status of home-dwelling older adults in this study did not show significant improvement. This may be attributed to the advanced age of the participants, as improvements from denture rehabilitation alone were insufficient to reverse age-related declines in nutritional status [28].Several studies have also indicated that denture rehabilitation alone is inadequate for enhancing nutritional intake in older adults, whereas combining treatment with simple dietary advice and pamphlet-based guidance yields greater benefits [29–31]. Therefore, in addition to domiciliary denture treatment, incorporating personalized dietary advice from dietitians would better facilitate improvements in the nutritional status of older adults.

The OHIP-14 scale is a critical indicator for evaluating OHRQoL and is widely used in dental treatment efficacy assessments across diverse studies [14]. In this study, a significant decrease in OHIP-14 scores indicated a notable improvement in OHRQoL after domiciliary denture treatment. This finding is consistent with a UK-based study that reported similar improvements in OHRQoL among housebound edentulous older adults receiving domiciliary denture services [32]. In the present study, the improvement in OHRQoL was most pronounced at 3 months post-denture delivery and remained relatively stable during the 12-month follow-up period, aligning with previous findings on routine outpatient denture treatment [33, 34]. The high VAS scores observed further confirmed the improvement in OHRQoL after domiciliary denture treatment. Previous studies have also shown that domiciliary denture treatment is generally associated with high patient satisfaction, which can shorten the adaptation period to new dentures, improve treatment outcomes, and form a mutually reinforcing cycle [35]. Since this study only examined satisfaction with dentures, future research could incorporate more comprehensive patient feedback mechanisms. For instance, evaluating the convenience and comfort of domiciliary denture treatment, interactions between patients and doctors in the home setting, and the impact on social participation could contribute to refining the satisfaction evaluation system for domiciliary denture treatment and allow for a more comprehensive assessment of its effectiveness.

In edentulous older adults, tooth loss leads to inward migration of the lips and cheeks, narrowing the lip margins and causing labial commissure ptosis [36, 37]. Denture treatment can restore occlusal vertical dimension (OVD) and facial harmony, which is crucial for social interaction and mental health [38]. Our study demonstrated increased OVD (Sn-Gn; Sn-Pg) [10, 39] and perioral changes, particularly in the upper lip [40]. Unlike Toyoshima et al. [39], the Sn-Ls measurement increased, possibly due to enhanced denture support. Additionally, lower facial convexity (Sn-St-Pg) increased, while the facial convexity angle (N-Sn-Pg) decreased, indicating a more prominent facial contour post-treatment [19, 20]. These findings enhance our understanding of tissue changes following denture rehabilitation and may guide future denture design. Current facial recording methods, such as specific-angle photography and facial scanning [41], have limitations. Photography requires fixed setups and patient cooperation but yields limited data. Scanning captures more comprehensive data but necessitates specialized equipment, typically restricting its use to hospitals [42]. To overcome these challenges, our study utilized i3Dface software, specifically developed for orthodontic and orthognathic applications. Using a tablet in well-lit environments, we captured facial scans akin to mobile phone facial recognition. This approach eliminates venue and equipment constraints while minimizing the cooperation needed from homebound older adults.

Although the data were retrospectively analyzed, all treatment and follow-up information were collected prospectively through a structured clinical protocol and strictly screened according to predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. This rigorous process inevitably limited the sample size. Additionally, patient dropout or death due to the COVID-19 pandemic further exacerbated this issue, making the small sample size the primary limitation of this study. With a small cohort, measurement and statistical biases may be amplified; therefore, despite using validated methods, the findings should be interpreted with caution. The unique characteristics of the population—homebound, advanced in age, and mobility-restricted—precluded comparison with routine outpatient denture treatment and the establishment of a non-intervention control group. We therefore adopted a within-subject pre–post design was employed, which can only demonstrate an association between the treatment and outcomes, rather than confirming that the treatment caused the outcomes. Without a comparison group, it is impossible to definitively attribute the observed improvements exclusively to the domiciliary intervention; such improvements could instead be influenced by the Hawthorne effect or simply reflect the natural adaptation process to a new prosthesis. Notably, after applying inclusion/exclusion criteria, only male participants remained. While using an exclusively male sample in small-scale studies reduces gender-related confounding factors, it also limits the gender generalizability of findings to other genders, particularly since research indicates that women report poorer OHRQoL [43, 44]. Therefore, future studies should account for gender effects to enhance the applicability of the results. Since the limitations of small sample size, lack of a control group, and single-gender cohort, these findings must be validated in larger, longer-term prospective studies to fully evaluate the efficacy of domiciliary denture treatment, inform the optimization of domiciliary dental services, and better address the challenges posed by an aging society.

Conclusion

Domiciliary denture services provide a clinically effective and convenient healthcare model for homebound edentulous older adults. This preliminary study demonstrates that the intervention significantly improved masticatory performance and facial harmony, with such improvements translating into measurable enhancements in nutritional status and substantial gains in quality of life. This evidence supports the integration of domiciliary dental prosthetics into public health programs for the ageing population, though larger-scale studies are needed to confirm these findings.

Identification of a biologically coherent three gene immune signature predictive of immunotherapy benefit in gastric adenocarcinoma
Authors: Ruihua Fan, Jiru Wang, Lijuan Xu, Yan Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGastric adenocarcinoma (GAC) exhibits marked interpatient heterogeneity. Compact, biologically interpretable signatures linked to tumor–immune contexture may aid reproducible risk stratification and hypothesis generation for therapeutic responsiveness.MethodsTCGA-GAC RNA-seq (n = 407; 375 tumors, 32 normals) was analyzed with limma to identify DEGs (p < 1 × 10⁻³, |log₂FC|>1). ImmPort overlap yielded immune DEGs, which were interaction-expanded via STRING to construct an immune-enriched candidate set for unsupervised co-expression analysis. WGCNA on expressed candidates identified co-expression modules; modules most associated with the tumor phenotype were prioritized for network centrality analysis, survival screening (maxstat), and multivariable Cox modeling to derive an immune-related gene prognostic index (IRGPI).ResultsXRCC2, NUSAP1, and ZWILCH were retained as independent prognostic factors and combined into IRGPI (0.526·XRCC2 + 0.581·NUSAP1 − 0.498·ZWILCH). CIBERSORT-based deconvolution identified three TME clusters with distinct survival patterns. Introduction

Gastric adenocarcinoma(GAC) remains a major global health burden with persistently unsatisfactory outcomes despite advances in surgery, systemic chemotherapy, molecularly targeted agents, and—more recently—immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) [1]. The pronounced inter- and intratumoral heterogeneity of GAC, together with the complexity of the tumor microenvironment (TME), underlies substantial variability in prognosis and treatment response [2]. Immune cell infiltration, immune evasion programs, genomic instability, and pathway reprogramming jointly shape this variability [3, 4]. Consequently, there is a pressing clinical need for translational biomarkers that capture the “tumor–immune” interplay to enable robust risk stratification and rational therapy selection.

Computational methods have expanded the toolkit for TME profiling from bulk transcriptomes [5, 6]. Deconvolution algorithms such as CIBERSORT estimate immune cell fractions, integrating the immune landscape into prognostic and therapeutic evaluation [7, 8]. In parallel, in silico frameworks such as TIDE attempt to model immune escape through T-cell dysfunction and exclusion, providing probability-based estimates of ICB benefit [9, 10]. Expression-based drug-response models (e.g., pRRophetic) further generate hypotheses regarding sensitivity to standard chemotherapies [11, 12]. Together these methods have broadened the scope of computational pathology and computational immunology. However, two practical challenges limit clinical translation: (i) a mismatch between high-dimensional feature spaces and modest real-world cohort sizes, which hampers cross-cohort portability, and (ii) biomarker sets optimized primarily for statistical discrimination, with limited attention to biological coupling of underlying processes—reducing interpretability and clinical usability [13, 14]. A promising strategy is therefore to develop parsimonious, biologically coherent immune-related signatures that balance predictive performance with interpretability and transferability.

Here, we begin with differential immune gene screening in TCGA-GAC, constrain candidates using protein–protein interaction networks and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) to enrich for phenotype-relevant modules, and employ survival modeling to derive an immune-related gene prognostic index (IRGPI). To minimize over-parameterization, we compress the model to a streamlined three-gene signature comprising XRCC2, NUSAP1, and ZWILCH, and assess its portability in an independent GEO cohort. We also position the IRGPI within the broader immuno-oncology context by examining its relationship to TME composition (CIBERSORT-based clustering), putative ICB responsiveness (TIDE), and predicted chemosensitivity (pRRophetic), thereby clarifying potential decision-support scenarios. In sum, we propose and evaluate an IRGPI framework centered on XRCC2, NUSAP1, and ZWILCH that seeks a pragmatic balance between statistical robustness, mechanistic interpretability, and clinical usability. This framework is intended to support risk assessment and treatment prioritization in GAC and to provide a hypothesis-generating platform for future mechanistic studies at the nexus of DNA repair, mitotic control, and tumor immunity.

Methods

Identification of differentially expressed immune-related genes

RNA-seq data (375 tumors and 32 adjacent normals) and corresponding clinical annotations were obtained from the TCGA-GAC cohort. Publicly available de-identified datasets were used; therefore, no additional ethical approval or informed consent was required. In addition to TIDE scores, a TIDE-derived MSI-like score was obtained from the TIDE framework. It should be noted that this MSI-like score reflects a transcriptomic feature inferred by the TIDE algorithm and is not equivalent to the molecular MSI subtype defined by TCGA PanCancer Atlas classification. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the limma R package, applying a significance threshold of p < 0.001 and |log₂FC| > 1. To focus on immune-related alterations, the DEGs were intersected with a curated immune gene list from the ImmPort database, resulting in a subset of differentially expressed immune-related genes.

Functional enrichment analysis of immune DEGs

To investigate the biological roles of the identified immune-related DEGs, functional enrichment analysis was performed using the clusterProfiler package in R. Enriched GO terms and KEGG pathways were identified to determine the signaling and interaction pathways these genes are involved in.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

To balance interpretability and network stability, we constructed the co-expression network from an immune-enriched but interaction-expanded gene set (immune DEGs intersected with ImmPort and expanded via STRING PPI). This strategy reduces the multiple-testing burden and prioritizes immune–tumor interaction programs while retaining unsupervised module discovery in the WGCNA step. Identification of hub immune-related genes

Within the GAC-associated modules, a gene–gene network was constructed by retaining edges with moderate-to-strong co-expression weights (weight > 0.2) to reduce spurious weak associations. Degree-centrality was then calculated, and a fixed-size shortlist (top 100 genes) was used as a pragmatic hub-candidate set to balance interpretability and multiple-testing burden. For survival screening, optimal cutoffs were identified using maxstat; to control false positives, survival-associated significance should be reported with multiple-testing correction (e.g., Benjamini–Hochberg FDR) alongside nominal P values, and genes meeting the predefined significance criterion were retained as prognostic candidates.

Genomic alterations in hub genes

Somatic mutation profiles for the hub immune-related genes were retrieved from the cBioPortal database to assess their mutational status and frequencies across GAC samples.

Construction of regulatory network involving TFs and ncRNAs

To explore upstream regulators of the hub immune-related genes, regulatory networks were constructed using data from the RAID 2.0, TRRUST v2, and STRING databases. The networks integrated interactions with transcription factors (TFs), miRNAs, and lncRNAs, forming a comprehensive map of gene regulation at multiple levels.

External validation of IRGPI in GEO dataset

The robustness of the IRGPI model was validated using the GSE26942 gastric cancer dataset from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. For external validation, the same IRGPI formula (fixed coefficients) derived from TCGA was applied to GSE26942. The cutoff was kept consistent with the TCGA-derived threshold (i.e., not re-optimized in the validation cohort) to evaluate portability; survival outcomes were then compared between IRGPI-high and IRGPI-low groups.

Unsupervised clustering of the tumor microenvironment (TME)

Immune cell fractions were estimated for each tumor sample (n = 375) using CIBERSORT with 1,000 permutations. The elbow method and gap statistic were applied to determine the optimal number of clusters (K). Consensus clustering was then performed using the ConsensusClusterPlus package (k-means algorithm, Euclidean distance, ward.D linkage, 1,000 iterations). Associations between TME clusters and survival were evaluated.

Construction of the molecular landscape of GAC subtypes

By integrating IRGPI grouping, clinical features, TME clusters, and immune cell proportions, a comprehensive molecular subtype atlas of GAC was constructed to illustrate heterogeneity in prognosis and immunological state.

In silico inference of immune evasion and putative ICB responsiveness using TIDE

To assess immune-evasion–related features and putative ICB responsiveness in silico across molecular subtypes, TIDE (Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion) scores were calculated. Higher TIDE scores indicate poor immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) response. Differences in TIDE scores and other immunological features (e.g., PD-L1 expression, MDSC infiltration, T cell dysfunction) were compared across IRGPI groups.

Prediction of chemotherapy sensitivity

To evaluate potential chemotherapy responses, the pRRophetic R package was used to estimate the IC50 values of cisplatin and gemcitabine in IRGPI-high and IRGPI-low groups. Differential sensitivity was assessed to guide treatment prioritization.

Overview of the analytical workflow

An integrated workflow was constructed, outlining the full analytical pipeline from immune gene screening and network modeling to clinical outcome prediction and therapeutic stratification.

Results

Immune transcriptomic profiling of GAC

We first characterized the immune transcriptional landscape of GAC. A total of 407 RNA-seq profiles (375 tumor samples and 32 adjacent normal tissues) from the TCGA-GAC cohort were analyzed. Differential expression analysis using the limma package (thresholds: p < 1 × 10⁻³ and |log₂FC| > 1) identified 1,351 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), including 793 upregulated and 558 downregulated genes in tumor tissues compared to normals. By intersecting these DEGs with the ImmPort immune gene database, we identified 423 immune-related DEGs, consisting of 201 upregulated and 222 downregulated genes in tumors (Fig. 1A, B, Supplementary Table 1). Unsupervised clustering based on these immune-related DEGs revealed distinct transcriptional differences between tumor and normal tissues, highlighting a subset of highly variable immune genes that clearly separated the two groups (Fig. 1A, B).

Functional enrichment analysis of the immune-related DEGs showed significant involvement in tumor–immune and stromal interaction pathways, including ECM–receptor interaction, cell adhesion molecules (CAMs), cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction, focal adhesion, complement and coagulation cascades, and leukocyte transendothelial migration. Additionally, canonical oncogenic and immune-modulatory pathways, such as the p53 signaling and PI3K–Akt signaling pathways, were also significantly enriched (Fig. 1C, Supplementary Table 2).

Network-based module discovery via WGCNA

To extend biological insights beyond the set of strictly differentially expressed genes, we expanded the initial 423 immune-related DEGs using STRING-based protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks, resulting in a reference list of 3,273 genes (Supplementary Table 3). Of these, 3,219 genes were detected in the TCGA-GAC expression dataset and were retained for downstream co-expression network analysis. To identify gene modules associated with GAC, we applied Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) after removing two outlier samples (TCGA-BR-4294-01 and TCGA-CD-8531-01). Scale-free topology analysis indicated that a soft-threshold power of β = 3 achieved a robust fit to scale-free network criteria (R² > 0.85) with acceptable mean connectivity (Fig. 2A). The resulting adjacency matrix was transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM), and average linkage hierarchical clustering was performed using dynamic tree cutting (minimum module size = 30). Merging similar modules at a height threshold of 0.25 produced 12 co-expression modules (Fig. 2B).

To identify modules related to GAC, we correlated each module eigengene with disease status. The heatmap of module–trait correlations revealed graded Pearson correlation coefficients, colored from red (strong positive correlation) to green (weak or negative correlation), with corresponding r and P values shown in each cell (Fig. 2C). Based on module–trait correlation magnitude (|r|) and the corresponding significance (as shown in Fig. 2C), the red, brown, and turquoise modules ranked among the top phenotype-associated modules and were selected as GAC-relevant candidates for downstream hub-gene prioritization. This selection was driven by module–trait statistics rather than manual preference. Module sizes and membership distributions are summarized in Fig. 2D (see WGCNA module folder or Supplementary Data for full gene listings). These GAC-associated modules served as the candidate gene pools for downstream biomarker discovery.

Identification of prognostic hub genes and IRGPI construction

From the WGCNA-derived co-expression network, we focused on the red, brown, and turquoise modules, which showed the strongest associations with the GAC phenotype. A gene–gene interaction network was constructed from these modules by retaining edges with a weight > 0.2 (Fig. 3A, Supplementary Table 4). To identify central genes within this network, we performed degree-centrality analysis, selecting the top 100 nodes with degree > 88 as potential hubs (Supplementary Table 5).

Intersecting these hub genes with immune-related gene annotations yielded 25 hub immune-related genes. To evaluate their prognostic relevance, we applied the maxstat R package to determine optimal cut-off points for survival stratification. To control for multiple comparisons, P values from the survival screening were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method. Genes with FDR < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Among the 25 candidate hub genes, 21 genes were significantly associated with overall survival under nominal testing (P < 0.05). After Benjamini–Hochberg correction, XRCC2, NUSAP1, GSG2 and FANCD2 genes remained significant at FDR < 0.05 (Supplementary Table 6). The four most significant genes are highlighted in Fig. 3B (see Supplementary Table 6 for full results). Subsequently, a multivariable Cox proportional hazards model was constructed using the 21 survival-associated hub genes. This analysis identified XRCC2, NUSAP1, and ZWILCH as genes retained in the multivariable Cox model (mutually adjusted predictors) in GAC (Fig. 3C).

Multi-omics integration of IRGPI with genomic alterations and the tumor microenvironment

Somatic alteration profiles of the hub immune-related genes were obtained from cBioPortal (Fig. 4A). XRCC2, NUSAP1, and ZWILCH showed mutation frequencies of 2.9%, 1.5%, and 1.5%, respectively; given the low rates, this analysis is presented for completeness and suggests that expression dysregulation rather than recurrent coding mutations may be more relevant for these genes in GAC, suggesting that the prognostic signal captured by IRGPI is more likely related to transcriptional regulation and/or pathway activity rather than recurrent coding mutations.

To explore potential upstream regulatory relationships, we constructed a putative TF–ncRNA regulatory network for the three hub genes by integrating curated/predicted interactions from RAID 2.0, TRRUST v2, and STRING. This analysis yielded 23 hub–miRNA pairs, 34 hub–miRNA–lncRNA triplets, and 91 hub–TF pairs. After removing redundant entries, 138 non-redundant interactions involving 101 unique genes were retained (Fig. 4B; Supplementary Table 7). In the network visualization, circles denote TFs, squares denote mRNAs, diamonds denote lncRNAs, and triangles denote miRNAs We next performed pathway enrichment analysis on genes in the regulatory network using the ClueGO plugin in Cytoscape, identifying significant enrichment in pathways related to genome maintenance and stress signaling, including the Fanconi anemia pathway, p53 signaling, and TGF-β signaling (Fig. 4C; Supplementary Table 8). These results provide a pathway-level context for the three-gene signature but should be interpreted as supportive, hypothesis-generating evidence.

Based on multivariable Cox modeling, we defined the immune-related gene prognostic index (IRGPI) as: IRGPI = 0.526 × XRCC2 + 0.581 × NUSAP1 − 0.498 × ZWILCH. IRGPI scores were calculated for each tumor sample, and an optimal cut-off was determined using the maxstat procedure. Survival analysis demonstrated that IRGPI stratified overall survival in TCGA (Fig. 4D; Supplementary Table 9). Discrimination assessed by ROC analysis was modest (AUC = 0.55 in TCGA; AUC = 0.63 in GSE26942), consistent with a compact three-gene index capturing one component of prognostic heterogeneity rather than serving as a standalone classifier (Fig. 4E–G). The prognostic stratification was further evaluated in an independent cohort (GSE26942, n = 93), where the IRGPI-high group showed significantly improved survival compared with the IRGPI-low group (Fig. 4F), and ROC analysis yielded an AUC of 0.63 (Fig. 4G).

To relate IRGPI to immune contexture, we estimated the relative abundance of immune cell subsets in 375 TCGA tumors using CIBERSORT (1,000 permutations; Supplementary Table 10). Unsupervised consensus clustering (ConsensusClusterPlus) was then applied to define tumor microenvironment (TME) subtypes. Based on cluster stability and the observed inflection in clustering metrics, K = 3 was selected (Fig. 4H). Three TME clusters were identified, and survival analysis indicated significant outcome differences among these clusters (P = 0.0071), with Cluster 2 exhibiting the most favorable prognosis, whereas Clusters 1 and 3 were associated with poorer outcomes (Fig. 4I–J).

IRGPI-guided subtyping and therapeutic implications

By integrating immune cell composition, clinical information, unsupervised clustering of the tumor microenvironment (TME), and IRGPI classification, we defined distinct molecular and clinical subtypes of GAC and constructed a comprehensive molecular atlas (Fig. 5A, Supplementary Table 11). These subtypes exhibit heterogeneous features in terms of immune infiltration patterns, prognosis, and potential therapeutic responses.

To explore immune-evasion–related features and putative ICB responsiveness in silico, we applied the TIDE (Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion) algorithm (Supplementary Table 12). A higher TIDE score predicts poor response to immunotherapy and worse overall prognosis. However, survival analysis revealed no significant association between TIDE scores and patient survival. The TIDE-derived MSI-like score in the TCGA-GAC cohort ranged from 0.48 to 1.00 (median = 0.97). As this score is a continuous metric inferred from transcriptomic features rather than a categorical molecular subtype, it was analyzed as a continuous variable and, where necessary, dichotomized using the median value. Higher MSI-like scores were associated with improved overall survival (P = 0.016), consistent with prior reports that MSI-related phenotypes are linked to favorable prognosis in gastric cancer (Fig. 5B). Further analysis revealed a highly significant difference in TIDE scores between the IRGPI-high and IRGPI-low groups ( Fig. 5C). Consistent with earlier findings that lower TIDE scores predict better prognosis, the IRGPI-high group, which exhibited better overall survival, also had significantly lower TIDE scores. This inverse correlation suggests that IRGPI captures immune-evasion–related transcriptional features aligned with lower TIDE scores; however, direct evidence of immunotherapy response requires validation in ICI-treated GAC cohorts. Additionally, expression levels or predicted scores of PD-L1, MDSCs (myeloid-derived suppressor cells), and T cell dysfunction (immune evasion) were also significantly different between the two IRGPI groups, all aligning with immunotherapy resistance features and further emphasizing the stratification power of IRGPI.

To explore differences in chemotherapy sensitivity, we employed the pRRophetic package in R to predict drug responses (IC50 values) to cisplatin and gemcitabine between IRGPI-high and IRGPI-low groups. As shown in Fig. 5D–E and Supplementary Table 13, the low IRGPI group exhibited significantly higher IC50 values for both drugs, suggesting lower drug sensitivity. In contrast, the IRGPI-high group, with lower IC50 values, may benefit more from chemotherapy, indicating better drug responsiveness. however, these results are exploratory and do not represent a comprehensive assessment of standard gastric cancer regimens (fluoropyrimidine–platinum and taxane-based therapies).

Discussion

This study identified and validated a compact IRGPI composed of XRCC2, NUSAP1, and ZWILCH, which robustly stratifies overall survival in GAC and aligns with in silico predictors of immunotherapy and chemotherapy responsiveness. By linking DNA repair and mitotic control genes to the tumor–immune contexture, our findings highlight a mechanistically coherent signature with translational potential for clinical decision support.

Methodological rationale and robustness of the immune-enriched network workflow. The analytical workflow adopted in this study combines immune-focused differential expression filtering, interaction-guided gene expansion, and unsupervised co-expression network construction. We acknowledge that WGCNA is frequently applied to genome-wide expression matrices and that restricting the initial gene universe may alter module composition. However, in cancer immunogenomics studies with moderate sample sizes, biologically informed candidate restriction has been increasingly adopted to enhance interpretability and reduce dimensional noise [15, 16].

Genome-wide WGCNA often captures dominant transcriptional programs such as proliferation, stromal content, or lineage markers, which, although biologically relevant, may obscure immune-coupled modules when the primary objective is to investigate tumor–immune interactions. By restricting the initial gene set to immune-related DEGs, we aimed to prioritize transcriptional variation directly linked to tumor–immune crosstalk while reducing the multiple-testing burden inherent in high-dimensional analyses. To avoid over-reliance on strictly differentially expressed genes, we further expanded the candidate universe using STRING-based protein–protein interaction data. This interaction-guided expansion incorporates functionally connected partners that may not meet arbitrary differential thresholds but participate in coherent immune-regulatory programs [17–19]. Importantly, although the candidate universe was biologically enriched, module detection by WGCNA itself remained fully unsupervised, and module–trait associations were evaluated independently of survival information. Thus, the workflow preserves data-driven module discovery while enhancing biological coherence. Nevertheless, we recognize that alternative strategies, including genome-wide WGCNA or the use of different immune gene repositories and PPI resources, may yield partially distinct modules. Future studies should systematically compare gene-universe definitions and perform cross-cohort robustness analyses, particularly in treatment-annotated datasets, to further validate the generalizability of this framework.

The IRGPI demonstrated independent prognostic value across TCGA and GEO cohorts, underscoring its robustness despite the well-recognized heterogeneity of GAC. Importantly, IRGPI-high tumors exhibited lower TIDE scores and favorable immune infiltration patterns, indicating a distinct immune-evasion–related transcriptional state (e.g., lower TIDE scores) that may be compatible with putative ICB responsiveness in silico, while clinical response must be confirmed in ICI-treated cohorts. Parallel predictions of lower IC50 values for cisplatin and gemcitabine further support the index as a dual-purpose biomarker guiding both immunotherapy and chemotherapy. Although XRCC2, NUSAP1, and ZWILCH are primarily linked to DNA repair/mitosis, their expression can act as a surrogate for proliferation and chromosomal instability programs, which may influence tumor–immune interactions through multiple routes, including (i) altered genomic instability/antigenicity, (ii) stress and interferon-related signaling, and (iii) coupling with stromal/ECM remodeling that affects immune infiltration. These mechanisms remain to be experimentally tested, and our results mainly support an association between cell-cycle programs and immune contexture at the transcriptomic level [20–22]. This integrative perspective links cell-cycle regulation with immunogenicity, providing a biologically plausible explanation for the observed clinical associations.

Previous transcriptomic signatures in GAC have emphasized either high-dimensional immune gene panels or immunohistochemical classifiers with limited generalizability [23–25]. Our three-gene IRGPI achieves a more favorable balance between parsimony and interpretability. The prognostic relevance of XRCC2 and DNA repair pathways has been reported in gastric cancer and other malignancies, consistent with our findings [26–29]. Similarly, NUSAP1 overexpression has been associated with aggressive phenotypes in breast and liver cancers, while ZWILCH dysregulation has recently emerged as a marker of chromosomal instability [20, 30, 31]. Notably, our study extends these observations by embedding these genes within an immune-functional context, thereby reconciling oncogenic signaling with immunotherapy responsiveness. Discrepancies with earlier GAC signatures, many of which lack reproducibility across datasets, likely reflect our network-guided selection strategy, which enriches for mechanistically coupled modules rather than purely statistical discriminators [23].

The IRGPI embodies both theoretical significance and clinical utility. Conceptually, it underscores the emerging connection between defective DNA repair, perturbed mitotic control, and reprogrammed immune surveillance—an axis increasingly recognized as a driver of tumor immunogenicity and therapeutic vulnerability [32]. Practically, the index provides a parsimonious tool to stratify patients who are most likely to benefit from immune checkpoint blockade or conventional chemotherapy, thereby informing treatment selection with greater precision. As combinatorial regimens gain traction, a single biomarker capable of guiding both therapeutic domains is particularly compelling. Moreover, because the biological processes captured by XRCC2, NUSAP1, and ZWILCH are not unique to gastric adenocarcinoma, the IRGPI framework may extend to other malignancies marked by genomic instability and immune heterogeneity, warranting systematic cross-cancer exploration.

Several limitations warrant consideration. First, although validated in an external GEO cohort, the sample size of the validation set was modest, and larger, prospectively collected datasets are needed to confirm generalizability. Second, while our in-silico analyses predicted immunotherapy and chemotherapy responses, these findings await direct clinical correlation with treatment outcomes. Third, the modest AUC observed in TCGA highlights that IRGPI, while informative, should be integrated with other molecular and clinical factors to achieve optimal prognostic discrimination. Finally, functional validation of XRCC2, NUSAP1, and ZWILCH in shaping immune contexture remains to be experimentally demonstrated. Addressing these points will strengthen confidence in the translational readiness of the signature. Second, the co-expression network was built from an immune-enriched, interaction-expanded candidate set rather than all expressed genes; alternative gene-universe choices may influence module composition and should be evaluated in future work. Third, MSI status is a major determinant of both prognosis and immunotherapy responsiveness in GAC. Because TCGA is an ICI-naïve cohort and our analyses do not separate MSI-H from MSS/MSI-L in a treatment-annotated setting, the immunotherapy-related observations should be considered hypothesis-generating; MSS-restricted analyses and validation in ICI-treated cohorts are warranted.

In summary, we present a biologically coherent three-gene immune signature that stratifies prognosis and is associated with therapy-related signatures inferred in silico in GAC. By bridging DNA repair, mitotic control, and tumor immunity, the IRGPI advances both mechanistic understanding and clinical stratification. Future research should prioritize prospective validation in ICB-treated cohorts and experimental dissection of the XRCC2–NUSAP1–ZWILCH axis to clarify causality. Such efforts will accelerate the clinical translation of IRGPI into a candidate index for prognosis stratification and future translational evaluation.

Parameter optimization of thermal network model for aerial cameras utilizing Monte-Carlo and genetic algorithm
Authors: Yue Fan, Wei Feng, Zhenxing Ren, Bingqi Liu, Long Huang, Dazhi Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIt is crucial to precisely calculate temperature utilizing thermal models, which require the determination of thermal parameters that optimally align model outcomes with experimental data. In many instances, the refinement of these models is undertaken within space instruments. This paper introduces an optimization methodology for thermal network models, with the objective of enhancing the accuracy of temperature predictions for aerial cameras. The investigation of internal convective heat transfer coefficients for both cylindrical and planar structures provides an estimation of convective thermal parameters. Based on the identification of thermally sensitive parameters and the reliability evaluation of transient temperature data through the Monte-Carlo simulation, the genetic algorithm is employed to search for global optimal parameter values that minimize the root mean square error (RMSE) between calculated and measured node temperatures. As a result, the optimized model shows significantly improved accuracy in temperature prediction, attaining an RMSE of 1.07 ℃ and reducing the maximum relative error between predicted and experimental results from 33.8 to 3.1%. Furthermore, the flight simulation and thermal control experiments validate the robustness of the optimized model, demonstrating that discrepancies between the observed and predicted temperatures are within 2 °C after re-correcting the external convection heat transfer coefficient value.Keywords: Aerial camera, Thermal network model, Parameter optimization, Monte-Carlo algorithm, Genetic algorithmSubject terms: Optoelectronic devices and components, Imaging and sensing, Aerospace engineering

Introduction

Aerial cameras, being precision optical instruments, demand not only meticulous machining and assembly tolerances but also demonstrate a high degree of sensitivity to their surrounding environment. Operating in intricate and dynamic conditions, temperature fluctuation plays a pivotal role in significantly influencing imaging quality1–4. Consequently, it is crucial to undertake thermal optimization design for attaining high resolution and reliability in optical systems. This optimization heavily relies on accurate predictions of thermal characteristics derived from thermal analysis. However, the intricate internal structure of aerial imaging systems, coupled with the constantly changing external thermal environment, poses significant challenges in thermal analysis and temperature calculation5–8. The lumped-parameter thermal network (LPTN) model, by harnessing the similarities between heat transfer and electrical conduction processes, offers a practical and effective approach for estimating the temperature of aerial cameras9–11.

However, the establishment of a LPTN model inherently involves simplifications and assumptions that may introduce modeling errors. Variations in processing techniques, surface properties, and the surrounding environment can result in discrepancies between physical properties and calculated parameter values. Furthermore, accurately determining the contact heat transfer coefficient and convection heat transfer coefficient can be challenging, potentially causing inaccuracies in the parameter values of thermal network models. Therefore, it is imperative to refine the LPTN model to ensure more precise predictions of temperature distributions12,13.

Numerous researchers have delved deeply into the correction of thermal networks pertaining to aerospace instruments. Toussaint et al.14 initially proposed a systematic method for correcting satellite thermal network models, which was founded on experimental data from thermal balance tests. Shimoji et al.15 further refined crucial parameters of the thermal network by utilizing statistical regression analysis. Weng and Papalexandris16,17 leveraged the Least Squares method to update the parameter values of thermal network models. Beck et al.18 successfully applied the particle swarm optimization technique to adjust thermal model parameters, with a primary focus on linear conductivity. Torralbo et al.19 employed the Jacobian matrix formulation along with the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse to effectively minimize parameter uncertainty within the thermal mathematical model. Cui et al.20 introduced a thermal finite element model updating method that utilized the Kriging model as a surrogate model to optimize the thermal design parameters of a solar spectrometer, rather than relying on direct iterations of finite element analysis. Li et al.21 integrated Latin hypercube sampling with the coordinate rotation method to enhance the accuracy of the spacecraft’s thermal analysis model.

Herrera and Sepúlveda22 were the pioneers in applying the Monte-Carlo stochastic approximation method to satellite thermal analysis. Since then, the Monte-Carlo method and its variations have emerged as the preferred techniques for refining thermal models. Cheng et al.23 successfully refined the thermal model of a thermally controlled satellite under ground test conditions by leveraging the Monte Carlo hybrid algorithm. Zhang et al.24 corrected the sensitive parameters of a small satellite using the Monte-Carlo mixed method, which involved classification layer by layer. The Monte Carlo Ray Tracing (MCRT) method has proven to be an efficient tool in determining the solar direct incidence area and radiation transfer coefficient25. Furthermore, Gómez et al.26 developed a new method based on statistical error analysis and Monte Carlo method for uncertainty calculation in spacecraft thermal control and design.

Essentially, the thermal model correction boils down to a parameter optimization that has attracted the attention of numerous scholars to investigate optimization algorithms to enhance the accuracy of thermal models. Klement et al.27 utilized Broyden’s class of methods to correlate thermal mathematical models with tests, thereby reducing the number of iterations required. Kim et al.28 employed a hybrid genetic algorithm that incorporates a local optimization algorithm to estimate wall emissivities by minimizing an objective function. Anglada et al.29 presented the validation of the genetic algorithm to correlate thermal mathematical models of space vehicles. Garmendia et al.30 examined the correlation of a small-medium size thermal model of space instruments using genetic algorithms.

In a recent development, Shi et al.31 devised a predictor-corrector Monte Carlo method, achieving more precise solutions for the thermal radiative transfer equations. Liu et al.32 put forth a parameter self-correcting thermal network model. They rectified the initial thermal network model parameters in real-time by monitoring the solder layer ageing of IGBT and thereby recovered the accuracy of the thermal network model to calculate the junction temperature.

However, investigations dedicated to optimizing thermal network models for aerial cameras, specifically considering convective heat transfer, is currently limited. In addition, the study of global optimization of thermal network parameters based on error analysis of transient thermal experimental data, has yet to be conducted, which is a crucial step in enhancing the adaptability and robustness of optimized thermal network models. The objective of this study is to develop a comprehensive methodology for optimizing the intricate parameters of thermal network models for aerial cameras. The methodology integrates Monte-Carlo simulation and a genetic algorithm with the aim of improving the precision of temperature predictions for aerial cameras, thereby ensuring a heightened degree of accuracy and reliability in assessing their thermal performance.

This study investigates the internal convective heat transfer coefficients for both cylindrical and planar structures, taking into account various structural factors, with the aim of providing an estimation of convective thermal parameters. A thermally sensitive analysis is conducted to identify the key parameters that significantly impact the model’s performance. The Monte-Carlo algorithm is utilized to explore parameter spaces for transient temperature error analysis, ensuring data reliability. Subsequently, the Genetic Algorithm is employed to systematically converge on globally optimal parameter values, thereby optimizing the model’s predictive capabilities. A comparative evaluation is then conducted, whereby the outcomes of the original model are contrasted with those attained by the optimized model. Finally, to reinforce the robustness of the optimization method and validate the predicted accuracy of the refined thermal network model, a flight condition simulation experiment and a thermal control experiment are conducted. These experiments serve as a cornerstone in confirming the practical applicability and precision of the optimized model under real-world operating conditions.

Temperature error analysis

In general, discrepancies between calculated and experimentally measured temperature values can be attributed to a number of sources of error. These include discrepancies between the actual physical reality and the simplified mathematical models used to represent thermal networks, measurement inaccuracies exhibited by temperature sensors, and the imprecision of thermal network parameter values. This paper focuses on the analysis of temperature errors exclusively attributable to discrepancies in thermal network parameters, with the objective of evaluating the reliability of transient thermal testing data.

The considerable number of parameters in the thermal network of the aerial camera renders the application of an analytical method to determine its temperature error a challenging undertaking. In order to analyse the transient temperature error, the Monte-Carlo method is employed. This approach entails the establishment of a probability model for each sensitive parameter. Subsequently, the parameter values are randomly selected from the model and input into the thermal network equations. Subsequently, the aforementioned equations are solved, and the entire process is repeated numerous times. By means of this iterative process, the mathematical expectation and the mean square deviation of the node temperatures are derived. The mathematical expectation represents the most probable temperature value that would be observed in the aerial camera, while the mean square deviation quantifies the potential deviation in temperature.

The key parameters of the thermal network are regarded as random variables and presumed to exhibit a uniform distribution. Each parameter is subjected to N rounds of sampling, in accordance with the specified range of values. The simulation results demonstrate that when N is equal to or greater than 2000, the parameters exhibit a uniform distribution, as illustrated in Fig. 5. Subsequently, the temperature variations of each node are calculated by feeding each set of parameters into the thermal network equations.

Thermal parameter optimization

Optimization process

Genetic Algorithm (GA) simulates natural selection and genetics to optimize problems. It starts with a population of encoded potential solutions. Each individual is evaluated by a fitness function, reflecting its problem-solving ability. Selection biases towards fitter individuals. Genetic operations like crossover and mutation create new offspring, adding diversity. The new generation replaces or supplements the old, repeating the process until a satisfactory solution is found. Through iterations, GA explores the search space to converge on an optimal or near-optimal solution. The global optimal solution of thermal parameters can be obtained using genetic algorithms to enhance the adaptability and robustness of thermal network models.

Experimental arrangement

To validate the robustness of the proposed optimization methodology and the predicted accuracy of the refined thermal network model, a flight condition simulation experiment and a thermal control experiment are conducted. To simulate the environment temperatures that would be experienced during a flight, as well as an extreme low temperature condition, the aerial camera is situated within a high-low temperature chamber, as depicted in Fig. 10.

Thermal control experiment

It is possible that aerial cameras may be exposed to extreme low temperatures for an extended period of time, particularly in regions such as the north or highlands, where the initial temperature and ambient temperature may reach as low as -55 °C. Prior to undertaking any flight photography tasks, it is essential to elevate the temperature level of the optical system of the aerial camera to the requisite temperature range as indicated by the temperature index, which is defined as a temperature above − 20 °C.

As illustrated in Fig. 12, the implementation of zone heating on the aerial camera is recommended to elevate the temperature of the optical system from − 55 °C to -20 °C. The lens and window barrel are heated by means of silicone heating films, with temperature sensors installed in each heating zone for the purpose of providing real-time temperature measurements. The optical window is heated by a combination of an indium tin oxide (ITO) conductive film and copper electrode pairs, ensuring that the current flows uniformly through the outer surface of the window. It is therefore essential to guarantee that the heating power exceeds the heat leakage rate within the optical system when the temperature is -20 °C and the ambient temperature remains at -55 °C. By employing the optimized thermal network model, the heat leakage rate of the optical system can be accurately determined. Conclusion

This paper introduces a comprehensive and systematic method for optimizing the thermal network model, with the objective of enhancing the precision of temperature predictions and thermal performance acquisition for aerial cameras. The internal convective heat transfer coefficients for both cylindrical and planar structures are investigated with a view to estimating the convective thermal parameters. A thermally sensitive analysis is conducted to identify the critical parameters, and the Monte-Carlo algorithm is employed for a comprehensive temperature error analysis. Subsequently, the genetic algorithm is employed to derive optimal parameter values that minimize the RMSE between calculated and measured node temperatures to 1.07℃. It is noteworthy that due to the inherent uncertainty associated with fluid parameters and structural factors, there is a considerable degree of variability observed in the values of convective heat transfer coefficients. As a result, the temperature predicted by the optimized model is considerably more accurate, with the maximum absolute error reduced from 22℃ to 2℃ and the relative error from 33.8 to 3.1%. The results of the flight condition simulation and thermal control experiments demonstrate the efficacy and robustness of the proposed optimization method after re-correcting the value of the external convection heat transfer coefficient.

The proposed optimization method, while offering improvements in terms of accuracy and performance, is not without its limitations. In particular, the computational complexity associated with refining the model parameters can be considerable, especially for large-scale thermal networks, which places significant demands on processing power and time. Furthermore, there are challenges such as simplification leading to model mismatch in practical applications, which is an area that we intend to refine in our research.

Future research will be directed towards the construction of a digital twin system utilizing the thermal network correction model proposed in this paper. In the actual system, the inability to affix sensors to the surface of the optical components precludes the possibility of obtaining the online working temperature of the optical system through direct measurement. In contrast, the digital twin system offers the potential to predict the online temperature of aerospace optical systems, such as aerial cameras, in air and space. Furthermore, the values of the thermal network parameters can be corrected in real time in response to changes in environmental conditions, using temperature feedback data from accessible nodes. This enables the digital twin system to accurately predict the online temperature of the actual system and monitor the operational status of the system in real time.

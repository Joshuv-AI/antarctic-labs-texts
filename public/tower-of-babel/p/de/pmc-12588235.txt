Regulatory T-cell Activation Pathways in Patients With Chronic Graft-versus-host Disease Undergoing Extracorporeal Photopheresis Treatment: A Single-center Pilot Study
Authors: NATALIA GAWLIK-RZEMIENIEWSKA, ANNA KLIMA, KAROLINA PANEK, ANNA STRZELEC, JOANNA DZIACZKOWSKA-SUSZEK, MAGDALENA GÓRKA, ANNA KOCLĘGA, AGNIESZKA PODRAZA, GRZEGORZ HELBIG
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background/AimInduction of immune tolerance by the activation of regulatory T (Tregs) cells after extracorporeal photopheresis (ECP) appears to influence the response to treatment in patients with chronic graft-versus-host disease (cGvHD). This study examined the activation mechanisms of Tregs and their potential link to the expression of FOXP3, TP53, and SELPLG genes. Results were compared with the therapeutic response to ECP.Materials and MethodsThe study included six patients with cGvHD who underwent at least four cycles of ECP. The samples were collected from peripheral blood (PB) and from apheresis material before and after ECP, which was then used to establish cell cultures. The percentage of Tregs and their subpopulations were assessed using multicolor flow cytometry. Gene expression levels were evaluated using quantitative PCR (qPCR).ResultsA statistically significant increase in the expression levels of TP53 gene was found in the fourth ECP cycle in patients with partial response (PR) compared to patients with stable disease (SD). Patients with PR were characterized by higher mRNA levels for the TP53 and SELPLG genes after 48 h of culture. Patients with PR had a statistically significant higher percentage of Tregs including activated HLA-DR+ Tregs.ConclusionPatients with PR demonstrated elevated gene expression levels in 48-h mononuclear cell cultures, which correlated with their clinical response to treatment. They also had a higher baseline percentage of Tregs, which did not correlate with elevated FOXP3 or other gene expression levels.

Introduction

The extracorporeal photopheresis (ECP) is a well-documented and safe method of treating steroid-resistant graft-versus-host disease (GvHD), which remains a severe complication after allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). ECP involves isolating the patient's peripheral blood (PB) mononuclear cells (PBMNC) during leukapheresis, activating the cells with 8-methoxypsolarene (8-MOP) and exposing them to UVA radiation. The irradiated cells are then returned to the patient's bloodstream. This procedure is highly effective in alleviating the symptoms of GvHD, but its mechanism of action is not fully understood. The latest literature reports emphasize the key role of the activation of regulatory T cells (Tregs) in the response of patients with GvHD to ECP therapy. These lymphocytes represent the CD4+ cell line that is involved in inhibiting the immune response. A number of studies have demonstrated that the occurrence of GvHD is accompanied by a decrease in the percentage of Tregs, while their increase is related to the therapeutic response to the treatment (1-8).

ECP's effectiveness in GvHD is primarily assessed by clinical outcomes, such as organ response and steroid dose reduction. Despite many years of experience in transplantation and the development of knowledge in the field of molecular biology and epigenetics, the mechanism of action of ECP in the context of alleviating the symptoms of GvHD is still not fully understood (9). Some previous studies suggested that during the ECP procedure, the process of apoptosis of lymphoid cells is initiated by direct incorporation of 8-MOP into their DNA. This results in the activation of the p53 protein and arrest of the cell cycle in the G1 phase. The p53 protein also regulates the expression of proteins from the BCL-2 family, including the activation of the proapoptotic BAX protein. Apoptotic cells are then phagocytosed by antigen-presenting cells (APC). Recognition of apoptotic cells by APC (including dendric cells, DC) results in the induction of anti-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-10 (IL-10) or transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) and a decrease in the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-12 or tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α). DC promote immune tolerance by expanding Tregs, which in turn inhibit the action of cytotoxic T lymphocytes involved in GvHD (10-12).

Recent studies have demonstrated that the activation of platelets, by increasing their adherence to the surfaces of channels in the irradiation chamber, promotes the maturation of DC. It is believed that there is a strong relationship between the differentiation of monocytes into DC and the interaction with platelets during ECP. On the surface of activated platelets, P-selectin is expressed, which is bound by the P-selectin glycoprotein ligand 1 (PSGL-1, encoded by SELPLG gene) of passing monocytes during the process. An increase in the expression of the SELPLG gene is observed in the subtype of monocytes that enter the path of differentiation into DC. Therefore, PSGL-1 may be a marker of this differentiation (13-15). The aim of the study was to analyze the mechanisms of Tregs activation during the ECP procedure in patients with cGvHD resistant to steroid therapy. The study evaluated changes in Treg percentages and FOXP3 gene expression levels in PB. To identify the mechanism driving Tregs activation, we monitored changes in TP53 gene expression, an apoptosis marker in leukocytes, and SELPLG, which is upregulated in monocytes differentiating into dendritic cells. The results were compared to the therapeutic response to ECP.

Materials and Methods

Patients and ECP procedure. The study included six patients with cGvHD (severe and moderate grade) who underwent HSCT in 2021-2023 and received ECP in 2024. The inclusion criteria was the ineffectiveness of the first line of treatment with methylprednisolone at a dose of 1 mg/kg. ECP procedures were performed on two consecutive days (defined as one ECP cycle) and were performed every four weeks. The study examined data collected from a minimum of four ECP cycles. ECP procedures were performed either using offline system (UVA PIT, Med. Tech Solutions, Cadolzburg, Germany) or using inline system (Therakos Cellex, Bridgewater, NJ, USA). All patients participating in the study funded by the German Bone Marrow Donor Center (DKMS) research grant provided their signed consent forms.

Sample collection. Samples were collected from PB (9-18 ml EDTA) of patients before the ECP procedure on the first and second day of the cycle. In the case of offline procedure, additional samples were collected from the apheresis bag before the material was irradiated (control sample) and immediately after the ECP procedure from the irradiated material (test sample). Part of the material (PB and collection material) was used for the determination of Tregs and their activation markers, from the other half of the material mononuclear cells (MNC) were isolated using the Ficoll-PaqueTM PREMIUM sterile solution (Cytiva, Wilmington, DE, USA). MNC obtained from PB and some MNC obtained from the collection material before and after irradiation were suspended in Trizol™ (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) for RNA isolation. The remaining MNC were used to establish cell cultures.

Cell cultures. MNC before and after ECP in the amount of 5×106 were suspended in TexMACS GMP culture medium (Milteney, Bergisch Gladbach, Germany) containing Glutamax reagent (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA, USA) at a final concentration of 1%. MNC were cultured successively for 24 h or for 48 h. Part of the cells were suspended in Tri Reagent for RNA isolation. The other part was used for determining the percentage of Tregs using flow cytometry.

Flow cytometry. The analysis of the percentage of Tregs was performed using multicolor flow cytometry (BD FACSCanto™ II cytometer, BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). Tregs were defined as CD3+ CD4+ CD25hi CD127lo cells. Activation of Tregs cells was analyzed based on the expression of the HLA DR antigen (DR MHC II determinant, a marker of cell activation) and CD45RA and CD45RO (naive and memory Tregs). The nucleatidase activity of Tregs cells was assessed by determining the expression of CD39+ and CD73+ antigens and the expression of the CTLA-4 (CD152+) receptor, a molecule that determines the suppressor properties of Tregs cells. All reagents were purchased from BD Bioscience.

RNA isolation and qPCR. RNA isolation was performed using the TRI ReagentTM (Invitrogen). Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed to determine the changes in the expression levels of the FOXP3, TP53 and SELPLG genes. The gene for the ribosomal protein RPS18 was used as an internal control. The iTaq™ Universal SYBR® Green One-Step Kit (Biorad, Hercules, CA, USA) and PrimePCR™ SYBR® Green Assay primers (Biorad) dedicated to the kit were used for the assays. To evaluate the changes in the expression levels of the studied genes, the 2-ΔΔCq method was used. qPCR reactions were performed using a 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA, USA) thermocycler.

Response to treatment. The grade of cGvHD was assessed according to the 2014 National Institutes of Health (NIH) criteria. Complete response (CR) to treatment was defined as the resolution of all reversible manifestations of GvHD. Improvement of clinical symptoms combined with steroid reduction was defined as partial response (PR). Unchanged response was defined as lack of response (LR) (15).

Statistical analysis. Statistical analysis of the obtained results was performed using the XLSTAT 2023 program. Data concerning individual lymphocyte fractions are presented as medians with interquartile range (n>8) or as medians with min-max range (n≤8). Data concerning expression levels of individual genes are presented as individual data points with median-fit line on a scatterplot. Due to the small samples size, non-parametric tests were primarily employed. For tests involving repeated measurements (e.g., measurements taken at three or more timepoints for the same group of patients) the Friedman’s test was performed. For post-hoc pairwise comparisons between timepoints, Nemenyi's procedure was applied with Holm-Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons. For comparisons between two different groups (e.g., for PR and LR patients at the same timepoints), the Mann-Whitney U test with Holm-Bonferroni correction was used. Furthermore, Page’s L test with Cabilio-Peng's multiple pairwise comparisons was performed to test the temporal trend of gene expression levels/percentage of lymphocytes subpopulations across the examined timepoints. Statistical significance was established at the level of p<0.05. Statistical trend was established at the level of 0.05<p<0.1.

Discussion

ECP is widely recommended second-line treatment for steroid-resistant cGvHD. In our facility, the ECP procedures were performed using both Therakos CellEx Photopheresis System (in-line) and Med-Tech Solutions GmbH UVA Pit System (off-line). Piccirillo et al. (16) compared those two systems and showed that the off-line mononuclear cell product was characterized by higher leukocytosis and higher processed blood volume (longer runtime). However, there were no statistically significant differences between the infused cell dose/kg between patients with cGvHD achieving or not a clinical response to ECP. A similar conclusion was reached by Asensi et al. (17), who examined the efficacy, safety, and feasibility of ECP within pediatric patients with GvHD. Researchers have shown that MNC collection was significantly higher in the off-line method, but it cannot be considered as a clear prognostic factor for response to ECP.

Three main mechanisms are believed to be crucial during the ECP procedure: cytokine profile and T-cell subpopulation modification, 8-MOP and UVA induced apoptosis, and monocyte to DC differentiation (18). Tregs inhibit the immune response and are one of the key mechanisms protecting against auto aggression. Several markers are used to identify these cells. Among CD4+ lymphocytes, only cells that demonstrate very high expression of surface CD25 receptor and intracellular FOXP3 can be classified as Tregs. In order to identify Tregs, the IL-7 receptor (CD127) is increasingly being determined and serves as an exclusion marker. In this study, Tregs were identified as CD3+ CD4+CD25hiCD127lo. However, additional evaluation of changes in FOXP3 gene expression levels was performed. Tregs, like other T lymphocytes, can have a CD45RA+ naive cell phenotype or CD45RA-CD45RO+ memory cell phenotype. The findings of a study (19) have shown that there are no major differences in the suppressive effect of naive and memory Tregs. However, it has been found that only naive Tregs can long-term maintain the expression of FOXP3 and CTLA-4 (CD152) and consequently — high suppressor activity. Treg lymphocytes present in PB may have on their surface the DR MHC II determinant (HLA-DR), which is a marker of cell activation. It has been shown that Tregs with the HLA-DR+ phenotype show higher FOXP3 expression than HLA-DR- Tregs and are characterized by higher suppressive activity. Some Tregs show high expression of CD39, an ectoenzyme hydrolyzing ATP to ADP and AMP, and high expression CD73, which degrades AMP to adenosine. The nuclease activity of Tregs therefore transforms the ATP-rich inflammatory environment into an ATP-deprived immunosuppressive environment. Our work showed that in PR patients there was a statistically significant increase in the percentage of CD45RA+RO- cells between 24 and 48 h of cell culture. At the same time, expression of FOXP3 gene remained constant. Meanwhile, in LR patients, where no statistically significant changes in the number of CD45RA+RO- cells were observed, the expression of the FOXP3 gene decreased after 48 h of culture. Furthermore, the increase in the expression level of FOXP3 gene was observed in PB of PR patients in the fourth cycle of ECP; however, it was non-significant. In LR patients, the expression of this gene remained at a similar level throughout all 4 ECP cycles. In PR patients, a statistically significant growing trend was observed within both HLA-DR+ CD152+ and HLA-DR+ CD73+ cells through cycles. In both groups, the percentage of Tregs started to decrease after the second cycle, and started increasing in the fourth cycle. Flinn et al. (5) examined the changes in the percentage of Tregs between ECP responders (four in total) and partial responders (two in total) in pediatric patients with acute GvHD (aGvHD). It was shown that Tregs increased steadily in the ECP responders from approximately the tenth cycle. However, this was not a consistent trend and the percentage of Tregs varied between cycles among patients. As in Flinn et al. studies, patients responding to ECP demonstrated increased Treg numbers in the second half of treatment (fifth cycle). Furthermore, though Treg increased in patients responding to ECP, there was no upward or downward trend. Similarly, in another study conducted earlier by di Biaso et al. (20), changes in the percentage of Tregs were examined among patients with cGvHD and aGvHD divided into ECP responders and non-responders group. In the sixth ECP cycle, comparing responding and non-responding patients, the first group was characterized by higher Tregs levels. We did not observe a significant increase in the percentage of these cells in our study, since only data from four ECP cycles were taken into account for the collective analysis.

In PR patients, CD152+HLA-DR+ cells increased started from the third ECP cycle, but the increase was no statistically significant. This increase may be related to the increase in the expression level of the FOXP3 gene. Bobrowski et al. (21) found a positive correlation between CD152 and FOXP3 in patients with mild-moderate asthma. A similar phenomenon was not observed in patients with severe asthma. However, regarding cell cultures, FOXP3 gene expression decreased after 48 h of culture within LR patients. At the same time, CD152+HLA-DR+ Tregs increased. This connection requires further study in a larger group of patients.

Our study noted varying FOXP3 expression levels in cell cultures from PR and LR patients. The expression level of FOXP3 gene decreased after 48 h in LR patients, whereas the expression level of this gene remained at a constant level in PR patients throughout the culture period. This is crucial as continuous FOXP3 expression is important for maintenance of the Tregs immunosuppressive properties. High FOXP3 expression in Tregs is necessary to exert their immunosuppressive activity after transplantation (22). Furthermore, our study showed that PR patients had a statistically significant higher percentage of HLA-DR+ activated cells at T48 compared to T0 timepoint. Human functional Tregs are characterized by high expression levels of HLA-DR+ and very high FOXP3 expression levels upon activation (23).

Lymphocyte apoptosis is regulated by a number of genes which can either promote cell death or maintain cell survival. TP53 gene and its protein product p53 is one of the most important factors for apoptotic cell death. In unstressed cells, the level of p53 is very low. Protein level elevates in response to diverse stress factors, such as DNA damage or nutrient deprivation. p53 acts through two major apoptotic pathways: intrinsic and extrinsic. The intrinsic pathway is dominated by the BCL2 family proteins, which lead to the release of cytochrome-C (Cyto-C) from the mitochondria. Cyto-C, apoptotic protease-activating factor-1 (APAF1) and procaspase 9 then form a complex called the apoptosome, in which caspase 9 is activated. It then leads to caspase 3 activation and initiation of apoptosis. The most know anti-apoptotic proteins in this family are BCL-2 and BCL-XL whereas the pro-apoptotic are BAX and BAK, which are directly induce by p53. The extrinsic pathway involves death receptor FAS, which is activated by binding of its ligand called FASL (predominately expressed by T-cells). p53 is known to activate the transcription of FAS mRNA (24). Bladon and Taylor (11) were the first to demonstrate, that among patients with GvHD who underwent the ECP procedure a decrease in BCL-2/BAX ratio was observed. The samples of patients’ PB were taken before ECP and before re-infusion. One year later Di Renzo et al. (25) showed that ECP induces lymphocyte apoptosis in patients with cGvHD via both pathways. ECP-treated lymphocytes showed a significant increase in BAX/BCL-2 ratio and enhanced expression levels of FAS ligand after 48 h of cell culture. As p53 functions in both pathways, we examined the expression changes of T53 both in patients’ PB and MNC cell cultures. The expression level of TP53 increased between second and fourth cycle within PR patients, whereas remained constant within LR patients. Furthermore, in the fourth cycle of ECP an increase in the expression levels of TP53 gene was observed within PR patients compared to LR patients. However, those changes were not statistically significant. Regarding cell cultures, it also appeared that the expression of this gene increases among PR patients over the entire studied period (constant growing trend was detected). However, among LR patients it increased at T24 timepoint and began to decrease in T48. It may therefore be possible that MNCs of PR patients maintain their apoptotic potential longer.

Durazzo et al. (13) were the first who in 2014 examined the role of platelets in ECP mechanism by imitating this process with a chamber device. They showed that platelets are capable of inducing differentiation of monocytes to DCs. The proposed mechanism stated that during the ECP process platelets bind to the αIIbβ3 receptor of fibrinogen immobilized of the plastic surface of the flow chamber. Activated platelets express P-selectin and passing monocytes binds to it via PSGL-1. Monocytes then enter the DC maturational pathway within 18 h. Han et al. (26) later confirmed that platelet P-selectin formed adhesion synapses through monocyte PSGL-1. In the experiment, murine MNCs isolated from peripheral blood were divided into two groups: a control group containing platelets and a test group depleted of platelets. Both groups were cultured in the presence of melanoma tumor cells and subsequently exposed overnight to 8-MOP and UVA light at a dose sufficient to induce tumor cell apoptosis. Notably, only the platelet-containing group was capable of initiating monocyte differentiation into dendritic cells and processing tumor-derived antigens. Furthermore, it was observed that platelet-expressed P-selectin triggered monocyte activation and cross-presentation. Given that these experimental conditions closely mimic those of the ECP procedure, the findings strongly support the hypothesis that the interaction between P-selectin and its ligand PSGL-1 is critical for the efficacy of ECP.

In patients with GvHD, DCs stimulate anti-tumor response also through activation of Tregs and development of immune tolerance (18). Our study showed that expression level of SELPLG gene increased after 24 h of MNC cell culture compared to the control within all patients. However, within PR patients, the expression level of SELPLG gene remained higher compared to the control also after 48 h of cell culture. In LR patients, the expression level of the examined gene returned to near baseline, reaching 107% at the 48-h timepoint (T48). Furthermore, PR patients had higher expression of this gene at the T48 compared to LR patients. As monocytes have been shown to be resistant to ECP-inducted apoptosis (27), the increased level of SELPLG gene may indicate differentiation of monocytes to DCs. Lackner et al. (28) also shown that only ECP-treated CD4+ T cells were phagocytosed by DCs compared to not-treated control. This mechanisms led to DC maturation. These data indicate that induction of DCs may play a crucial role in ECP-related immunosuppressive mechanisms.

Study limitations. A major limitation of the study is the fact that it is single-center with only preliminary data obtained. Another limitation is the small number of patients and samples examined. This was one of the reasons why the study also presented the results at the level of statistical trend (0.05<p<0.1). As a result more detailed studies on a larger population of patients with cGvHD are necessary.

Conclusion

Within the PR patients the expression levels of the examined genes were higher in particular ECP cycles and cell culture timepoints. PR patients were characterized by higher expression levels of the FOXP3 and TP53 genes in individual ECP cycles and higher expression levels of all genes in 48-hour cultures, which may correlate with their better response to treatment. PR patients had a higher baseline percentage of Tregs including HLA-DR+ Tregs. The results obtained in this preliminary study may contribute to expanding knowledge regarding the therapeutic mechanisms of ECP in patients with GvHD.

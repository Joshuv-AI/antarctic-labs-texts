Does nivolumab combined with fluorouracil-based chemotherapy produce higher objective response rate than other immunochemotherapy regimes in metastatic esophageal cancer?: authors reply
Authors: Zhen Gao, Shujie Huang, Sichao Wang, Dezhao Tang, Wei Xu, Ruijie Zeng, Guibin Qiao
Published: 2023

Dear editors,

We are grateful for the feedback received on our research titled, “Efficacy and Safety of Immunochemotherapy, Immunotherapy, Chemotherapy, and Targeted Therapy as First-line Treatment for Advanced and Metastatic Esophageal Cancer: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-analysis.” We wish to address the two primary concerns raised by the authors in their correspondence.

In response to the first query regarding patient classification, we adhered stringently and precisely to the PRISMA extension guidelines for Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis. We set the inclusion timeframe for trials up to 01 May 2022, as documented in our PROSPERO registration. Hence, the RATIONALE-306 study, officially published in May 2023, was not considered eligible for our research, even though it was published prior to our paper. The RATIONALE-306 data integrated into our analysis was sourced from a brief abstract that did not provide a detailed regimen composition. Consequently, we categorized the ICI + chemotherapy arm as Tislelizumab + FbCT. We believe that the small cohort of patients in ORIENT-15 who received Sintilimab + FbCT would not influence the final outcomes of the network meta-analysis. Given the absence of detailed results in ORIENT-15 concerning survival, antitumor activity, or adverse events based on specific chemotherapy regimens, conducting a further subgroup analysis isn't feasible. Using 5-fluorouracil administration as a criterion for chemotherapy classification may not be the most precise or clinically relevant, but it was necessary to create a network for continued analysis. We hope future studies introduce more significant methods for chemotherapy classification.

Regarding the second query about the ESCC ORR results, we deeply regret the oversight of Fig. 1 in the main text and have now attached it at the end of this response. We're also thankful to the authors for presenting a clear table detailing ORR result from 8 clinical trials. Upon examination, it's evident from the table that the ORR in the FbCT arm of CheckMate-648 is notably lower than that in FbCT arms of other trials (26.9% vs. 38.8%–45.5%). This variation underscores the fact that treatment baselines across trials differ. Hence, drawing direct comparisons between specific result values can be misleading and lacks a solid evidence base. This is precisely the rationale behind our decision to undertake this meta-analysis: to identify the most effective treatment regimen by harmonizing the baselines of various trials using network analysis.

Adverse events and objective response rate profiles of the Bayesian network meta-analysis in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. Figure 1 here is the Figure 4B for the paper “Efficacy and Safety of Immunochemotherapy, Immunotherapy, Chemotherapy, and Targeted Therapy as First-line Treatment for Advanced and Metastatic Esophageal Cancer: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-analysis”.

Notably, the ORR enhancement in the FbCT arm to ICIs + FbCT arm in CheckMate-648 (from 26.9% to 47.4%, an increase of 76.2%) is much pronounced than in other trials (e.g., a 45.3% increase from 45.5% to 66.1% in ORIENT-15; and a 49.8% increase from 42.4% to 63.5% in RATIONALE-306). This accounts for the findings that show Nivolumab + FbCT having a higher ORR compared to other treatments, given the analysis presumes FbCT treatment consistency across all trials. Comparing Nivolumab + FbCT and ICIs + FfCT becomes more intricate. Besides focusing on the chemotherapy baseline results, the disparity between associated FbCT and FfCT must also be addressed. Based on our heterogeneity test, the I2 of the ESCC ORR analysis stands at 10%, which is categorized as low heterogeneity (below 25%). Thus, we believe that the ESCC ORR analysis is reliable. Ignoring the control group and solely focusing on the experimental group is an unscientific approach to evaluating the outcomes across different trials. Our confidence leans more towards the concrete evidence from evidence-based medicine rather than subjective comparisons and evaluations.

We extend our sincere thanks to the authors of the comments, as their insights offer a valuable opportunity to enhance our research.

Best regards,

Guibin Qiao, M.D., Ph.D.

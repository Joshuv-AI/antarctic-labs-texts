Relationships between three-dimensional fibrosis distribution, atrial adiposity, and voltage abnormalities associated with persistent atrial fibrillation
Authors: Kensuke Sakata, Adityo Prakosa, Carolyna A P Yamamoto, Syed Yusuf Ali, Yazan Mohsen, Shane Loeffler, Eugene G Kholmovski, Joseph E Marine, Hugh Calkins, David D Spragg, Natalia A Trayanova
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsPersistent atrial fibrillation (PsAF) is often refractory to pulmonary vein isolation, a well-established AF treatment, owing to the fibrosis-remodelled substrates sustaining re-entries. Three-dimensional fibrosis distribution and atrial adiposity have been found to be essential contributors to PsAF arrhythmogenesis. We aimed to utilize late gadolinium enhancement (LGE)-MRI-derived personalized heart digital twins (DTs)—a recent promising tool for non-invasively assessing patient arrhythmogenesis—as well as contrast-enhanced cardiac computed tomography (CCT) images and electroanatomic maps (EAMs) to investigate relationships between fibrosis distribution, including endo–epi differences, adiposity infiltration, and electrophysiological abnormalities, and their influence on PsAF arrhythmogenic substrate.Methods and resultsDigital twins incorporating fibrosis distribution were generated from consecutive PsAF patients’ LGE-MRIs. Using rapid pacing, potential locations attracting re-entries (LRs) were identified in DTs. Cardiac computed tomographies were used to segment adipose tissue within pericardial sac and evaluate the distance from endocardial surface to closest adipose tissue (DEnCA). Bipolar and unipolar-low-voltage area fractions (LVFs) were extracted from EAMs. Volumetric-fibrosis fraction (FF) and surface-FF on endocardial and epicardial surfaces at LRs and non-LRs were analysed in relation to DEnCA and LVF. In 22 patients, adipose tissue volume correlated with BMI and CHA2DS2-VASc and, together with atrial-FF, predicted number of LRs. At LRs and non-LRs, while volumetric-FF was the sole determinant of LR classification, volumetric-FF correlated with endocardium-predominant fibrosis and bipolar LVF. Endocardium-predominant fibrosis was independently linked to DEnCA.ConclusionThis study highlights the complex interactions between structural features, such as three-dimensional fibrosis distribution and atrial adiposity infiltration, electrophysiological features, and PsAF arrhythmogenesis.Keywords: Persistent atrial fibrillation, Arrhythmogenic substrate, Fibrosis, Digital twin, Atrial adiposity, Low-voltage area

Introduction

Although pulmonary vein (PV) isolation (PVI), mainly eliminating triggers from PVs, is the standard treatment for atrial fibrillation (AF),1 its efficacy remains limited in persistent AF (PsAF),2–5 where fibrosis proliferates.6,7 This is attributable to the arrhythmogenic propensity of the atrial remodelled substrate sustaining re-entries.8 Over the last two decades, targeting the PsAF arrhythmogenic substrate, typically via indirect indicators, has been a strategy for PsAF treatment,2–5 yet optimal targets have remained elusive.

The three-dimensional fibrosis distribution has been reported to play an important role in PsAF arrhythmogenesis; characteristics evaluated include three-dimensional fibrosis burden, its density, and disorganization, as well as the three-dimensional heterogeneity, such as the dissociation of structural and electrophysiological features between the endocardial and epicardial surfaces.9–13 In addition, atrial adiposity and its infiltration into the myocardium have been thought to correlate with the development of fibrotic substrate and cardiovascular events, including AF.14–19

Recently, heart digital twins (DTs)—a novel technology rapidly gaining adoption in precision medicine—have emerged as a promising tool to simulate patient-specific electrophysiological behaviour, to non-invasively test arrhythmia induction, or to identify possible locations capable of sustaining re-entries (LRs), i.e. patient-specific PsAF arrhythmogenesis.9–11,20–24 The present study aimed at utilizing late gadolinium enhancement magnetic resonance imaging (LGE-MRI)-derived personalized DTs and contrast-enhanced cardiac computed tomography (CCT) images to comprehensively assess the complex relationship between structural features, such as three-dimensional fibrosis distribution and the degree of atrial adiposity and the level of its infiltration, electrophysiological abnormalities, and PsAF arrhythmogenic propensity.

Methods

Study design

In this study, 25 consecutive PsAF patients undergoing both qualified LGE-MRI (within 2 months) and CCT (within 2 years) prior to scheduled PsAF ablation at the Johns Hopkins Hospital were enrolled. This study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the institutional review board of Johns Hopkins University, and informed consent was obtained from all participants. The LGE-MRI and CCT images were used in constructing DTs of the patients’ fibrosis atria and in detecting adipose tissue within the pericardial sac, respectively. Electroanatomic map (EAM) was obtained during the ablation procedure. Three-dimensional fibrosis distribution and LRs identified in personalized DTs were analysed in relation to the atrial adiposity and the EAMs. Importantly, this DT-based framework enabled identification of patient-specific PsAF arrhythmogenic substrate locations—that cannot be directly observed using conventional imaging or EAM mapping alone—based on MRI-derived fibrosis distribution and electrophysiological simulation, thereby allowing direct investigation of their structural and electrophysiological correlates. Reporting of the artificial intelligence (AI)–assisted image segmentation component of this study followed the European Heart Rhythm Association 2025 State-of-the-Art recommendations for AI in clinical electrophysiology.25

Magnetic resonance imaging acquisition

Cardiac MRI scans were acquired for constructing personalized DTs 2–3 weeks before the catheter ablation procedure using a 1.5-T MRI system (Aera, Siemens Healthineers; Figure 2A). Magnetic resonance imaging acquisition methods and parameters were previously described in detail.9–11,24 In brief, the protocol incorporated two components: contrast-enhanced MR angiography for visualization of the atria and PVs and LGE-MRI for fibrosis assessment. The LGE-MRI scan was 20–25 min after the administration of contrast agent (gadobutrol, Bayer Healthcare Pharmaceuticals) using a fat-saturated, ECG-gated, respiratory-navigated, and 3D inversion recovery-prepared fast spoiled gradient-recalled echo sequence.

Personalized digital twinning

The digital twinning methodology for creating personalized bi-atrial DTs was presented in detail in our previous publications.9–11,24 Briefly, LGE-MRI scans were segmented semi-automatically using a deep neural network26 which served as an assisting tool to accelerate preprocessing, and subsequently, rigorous manual segmentation with the experts’ quality control was performed using a freely available image processing software (ITK-SNAP, http://www.itksnap.org.) (Figure 2A). As done in recent publications,10,11,24 a novel personalized image intensity ratio (PIIR)–based thresholding was used to classify fibrotic and non-fibrotic tissue. These generated image voxels with fibrosis distribution were transformed into a three-dimensional tetrahedral mesh using the medical image software (Materialise Mimics, Materialise HQ) (Figure 2B). Fibre orientations reconstructed from diffusion tensor MRI data were projected onto these meshes using the Universal Atrial Coordinates 2 approach with a custom three-dimensional mapping software.27,28 The detailed methodology and parameters for electrophysiological properties in the fibrotic substrate of PsAF patients’ DTs were described in our previous publications.9–11,24 Finally, all computational simulations were conducted on a parallel computing system using freely available academic software (openCARP, https://opencarp.org/).29,30

Assessment of the arrhythmogenic substrates in the digital twins

Once DT construction was completed, arrhythmia inducibility of each DT was assessed by incremental burst pacing delivered from a distributed set of forty bi-atrial sites. Locations attracting re-entries were subsequently identified visually using the freely available visualization and analysis software (meshalyzer, https://git.opencarp.org/openCARP/meshalyzer/) (Figure 2C). The detailed methodology for LR identification has been described in our recent publications.10,11,24 Briefly, LRs were defined as substrate locations capable of attracting re-entries, by delineating transmural locations with a diameter of 12 mm based on the centre of the re-entry trajectory. Importantly, LRs located inside PVI lesions in patients with a history of prior AF ablation were excluded from the analysis, as this study focused on investigating the relationship between native fibrosis, atrial adiposity, and EAMs, and previous ablations are not part of this relationship. In addition, 12 tissue locations with the same 12-mm diameter that were not LRs and were outside PVI lesions were selected as controls (i.e. non-LRs) from the anterior, posterior, roof, inferior, inferolateral, and septal walls of the left atrium (LA) and right atrium (RA) (Figure 2D). These control locations were selected based on anatomical correspondence from 12 predefined typical regions to ensure consistent spatial sampling across the atria and uniform anatomical representation across patients. The selection was performed blinded to fibrosis distribution, adiposity distribution, and EAM data. When an LR was present within a predefined region, the control location was positioned within the same anatomical region while avoiding overlap with LR.

Fibrotic features at locations attracting re-entries and non-locations attracting re-entries

The prevalence of fibrosis within the bi-atrial walls or within given locations (LRs or non-LRs) was quantified as fibrosis burden, i.e. volumetric-fibrosis fraction (FF), calculated as the ratio of fibrotic tissue volume to total atrial tissue volume using the freely available visualization software ParaView (https://www.paraview.org) (Figure 2E).10,11,24 Moreover, to assess differences in surface fibrosis fraction (surface-FF) between endocardial and epicardial surfaces, we calculated the ratio of fibrotic tissue area to total atrial tissue area on each surface (Figure 2E). The endo–epi difference in surface-FF was then defined as endocardial surface-FF minus epicardial surface-FF. All analysed locations, i.e. LRs and non-LRs, were classified into two groups: locations with higher surface-FF on endocardial surface than on epicardial surface, i.e. locations with endocardium-predominant fibrosis, and locations without.

Cardiac computed tomography image processing

Cardiac computed tomography studies were performed according to scan protocol previously described.31 First-pass CCT images were reconstructed with an in-plane resolution of 0.338–0.715 × 0.338–0.715 and slice thickness of 0.5–1.0 mm and used for segmentation and analysis. Both the pericardial sac and the bi-atrial endocardial surfaces including the PVs were segmented by a combination of an AI assist32 and manual quality control using ITK-SNAP (Figure 2F, left). The superior and inferior borders of adiposity quantification were the lower level of the left pulmonary artery origin and the left ventricular apex, respectively.33 The images that did not fully cover the entire pericardial sac were excluded from the analysis. All adipose tissue within the pericardial sac was identified using the conventional thresholding range of −190 to −30 Hounsfield units, consistent with prior studies.14–16,33,34 Cardiac computed tomography segmentation was performed completely independently of fibrosis thresholding by the specialist, who was blinded to the adiposity distribution. Finally, surface meshes of the bi-atrial endocardial surfaces and adipose tissue within the pericardial sac were generated (Figure 2F, right).

Distances between endocardial surfaces and adipose tissue

By calculating the distance from each point on the bi-atrial endocardial surfaces to the closest point of the adipose tissue (DEnCA), a three-dimensional DEnCA map was constructed for each patient (Figure 2G, left). Circular locations with a 12-mm diameter, corresponding to DT-derived LRs and non-LRs, were positioned on this CCT-derived DEnCA map using ParaView. To minimize potential alignment error, anatomical correspondence between DTs and the CCT-derived endocardial surface was carefully confirmed by manual inspection using the atrial anatomy, with the four PVs, the LA appendage, and the superior and inferior venae cavae, as anatomical landmarks, without geometric deformation of either the DTs or the DEnCA map. After alignment, the centre of each DT-derived circular location on the endocardial surface was projected onto the DEnCA map using an objective nearest-point approach, and circular locations with a fixed diameter of 12 mm were generated on the surface by a custom algorithm. This semi-automated procedure avoided subjective manual delineation and ensured consistent location size and positioning across all patients, thereby improving measurement reproducibility. Finally, the total adipose tissue volume within the pericardial sac was used for patient-level analyses, whereas the median DEnCA value across all points within each circular location (LR or non-LR) was used for location-level analyses (Figure 2G, right).

Electroanatomic maps

Decreased voltage amplitude of intra-atrial electrograms is generally considered a marker of abnormal arrhythmogenic substrate in PsAF. To investigate the relationship between structural and electrophysiological features, EAMs were collected during the scheduled ablation procedure and the centre of each location (i.e. LR or non-LR) was visually identified based on anatomical correspondence, as done in our recent works.10,11,24 Endocardial EAM mapping was performed with guidance from an EAM system and a multielectrode mapping catheter (CARTO-3 and PentaRay or OctaRay, Biosense Webster, Inc.) during stable atrial rhythm—sinus rhythm, atrial pacing rhythm, or stable atrial tachycardia (Figure 2H, left). The EAM data were exported from the CARTO system and processed using ParaView. The low-voltage thresholds for bipolar recordings were 0.5 mV during sinus rhythm or pacing at the coronary sinus and 0.38 mV during stable atrial tachycardia, respectively.35,36 The low-voltage threshold for unipolar recordings was 1.08 mV.11,37 Regions with poor anatomical mapping were excluded from low-voltage analysis. To quantify electrophysiological features in LRs or non-LRs, low-voltage area burden on endocardial surface, termed low-voltage area fraction (LVF), was defined as the ratio of low-voltage surface area to total surface area within each extracted 12-mm-diameter circular location (Figure 2H, right).24,35

Statistics analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SD for normally distributed data and median (Q1–Q3) for non-normally distributed data, respectively. Normality was assessed using histograms, the Shapiro–Wilk normality test and/or quantile-quantile plots. Depending on the distribution, group comparisons were performed with two-sided t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests. Categorical variables were presented as the n (%) and compared using Fisher’s exact tests. Logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify factors associated with LRs or with locations exhibiting endocardium-predominant fibrosis. Linear regression analysis was performed to evaluate patient characteristics predicting the adipose tissue volume or the number of LRs and to explore atrial location features associated with the volumetric-FF, the endo–epi difference in surface-FF, or the median DEnCA. Variables with P < 0.1 in the univariate analysis were entered into multivariate models. A P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using a graphical user interface for R, the freely available statistical software (EZR version 1.55, https://www.jichi.ac.jp/usr/hema/EZR/statmedEN.html).

Results

Baseline characteristics

Among all 25 patients with qualified MRI examination whose personalized DTs were created, three were excluded because their CCT scans did not cover the entire pericardial sac. The remaining 22 patients were investigated. Average patient age was 62 years old and 73% were male. The average CHA2DS2-VASc score was 2.1 ± 1.3, and the most common comorbidity was hypertension (64%). The BMI was 30.8 ± 4.7 and adipose tissue volume was 113 (81–135) cm3. Importantly, among the six patients with a history of prior AF ablation, no patients underwent extra-PVI ablation. The detailed clinical characteristics are described in Table 1.

Locations attracting re-entries identified in digital twins

Among the cohort of 22 DTs, 117 LRs were induced. Of these, nine LRs identified inside PVI lesions in six DTs of patients with previous PVI ablation were excluded from the analysis (see Methods). Accordingly, 108 LRs, of which 57 in the LA and 51 in the RA, with a median of 4 (3–7) per DT, and 264 non-LRs (12 per DT), were analysed. The median endo–epi difference in surface-FF was 0% across all 372 locations, and 121 locations were identified as having higher endocardial surface-FF than epicardial—i.e. endocardium-predominant fibrosis locations—while 251 locations did not have that. As summarized in Table 2, at the patient DT level, multivariable linear regression analysis identified atrial fibrosis burden, i.e. FF within the bi-atrial walls, and adipose tissue volume as independent predictors for the number of LRs (P = 0.004 and P = 0.037, respectively). The multivariable model described moderate explanatory capacity (adjusted R2 = 0.409), which was improved by adding adipose tissue volume to the model that included only atrial fibrosis burden (adjusted R2 = 0.289) (Figure 3B).

Features of locations attracting re-entries in digital twins

The location-level features (i.e. at both LRs and non-LRs) are summarized in Table 3. Based on the fibrosis distribution derived from LGE-MRIs (Figure 4A), LRs exhibited significantly higher volumetric-FF than non-LRs: 60.4% (44.9–72.6) vs. 3.0% (0.0–12.6) (two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, P < 0.001). The endo–epi difference in surface-FF was comparable between LRs and non-LRs: −2.2% (−29.3–16.8) vs. 0.0% (−2.9–0.5) (two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, P = 0.520) (Figure 4B, left); however, locations with endocardium-predominant fibrosis were significantly more frequent at LRs than at non-LRs: 47% (51 locations of 108) vs. 27% (70 locations of 264) (Fisher exact test, P < 0.001) (Figure 4B, right). These results suggest that LRs are characterized not only by a high degree of fibrosis but also by an increased likelihood of endocardium-predominant fibrosis. Analysis of atrial adiposity derived from CCT demonstrated that the median DEnCA at LRs was shorter than that at non-LRs, although the difference was not statistically significant: 2.21 mm (0.99–4.07) vs. 2.65 mm (1.36–4.93) (two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, P = 0.251) (Figure 4C). In regional analysis of LA and RA, results were the same: 2.68 mm (1.69–4.42) vs. 2.84 mm (1.60–4.88) for the LA (two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, P = 0.817) and 1.93 mm (0.74–3.92) vs. 2.47 mm (1.24–5.17) for the RA (two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, P = 0.147). From the EAM-derived low-voltage areas, we were able to obtain the voltage data at 257 locations (74 LRs and 183 non-LRs) in 19 patients: 160 during sinus rhythm, 84 during atrial pacing at the coronary sinus, and 13 during stable atrial tachycardia. Both bipolar and unipolar LVF were significantly higher at LRs than at non-LRs: 20.5% (0.2–40.9) vs. 0.5% (0.0–9.5) for bipolar LVF (two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, P < 0.001) and 10.5% (0.0–49.5) vs. 0.0% (0.0–9.7) for unipolar LVF (two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, P < 0.001) (Figure 4D). In logistic regression analysis, volumetric-FF, endocardium-predominant fibrosis, and both bipolar and unipolar LVF within LRs and non-LRs were associated with LR classification; however, after multivariate adjustment, only volumetric-FF remained an independent determinant (Table 4). While volumetric-FF in a certain location could serve as the predictor of LRs aligning with our recent publications,10,11 these findings suggest that an increased prevalence of locations with endocardium-predominant fibrosis and greater endocardial low-voltage area burden may be indirectly related to LR classification. This implies that their indirect influence is mediated through the volumetric fibrosis burden. The key findings of this study remained unchanged after restricting the analysis to the 16 DTs of patients without previous PVI (see Supplementary material online, Figures S1 and S2 and Tables S1 and S2).

Factors associated with volumetric-fibrosis fraction

To explore potential indirect factors contributing to LR formation, features associated with volumetric-FF were investigated. As shown in Table 5, the endo–epi difference in surface-FF, endocardium-predominant fibrosis, and bipolar LVF within LRs and non-LRs, rather than unipolar LVF, were independently associated with volumetric-FF (P < 0.001, P < 0.001, and P = 0.031, respectively). These findings support the above interpretation derived from the logistic analysis of LR classification (Table 4), suggesting that the influence of these features on LRs may be mediated through volumetric-FF rather than being direct.

As shown in Tables 4 and 5, factors associated with endocardium-predominant fibrosis (classification) and the endo–epi difference in surface-FF (value) were investigated using logistic regression analysis and linear regression analysis, respectively. Both features were independently associated with the volumetric-FF and the median DEnCA (both P < 0.001 for endocardium-predominant fibrosis and both P < 0.001 for the endo–epi difference in surface-FF). Discussion

Our personalized DT study aimed to explore relationships among three-dimensional fibrosis distribution, atrial adiposity, and intra-atrial electrogram voltage abnormalities underlying PsAF arrhythmogenic propensity. We found that adipose tissue volume was independently associated with BMI and CHA2DS2-VASc score, with substantial explanatory capacity. Furthermore, the number of arrhythmogenic substrate locations was independently predicted not only by fibrosis burden but also by adipose tissue volume, with moderate predictive power. At the location level (i.e. at LRs and non-LRs), volumetric fibrosis burden was independently linked to both endocardium-predominant fibrosis and reduced voltage amplitude. Atrial adiposity infiltration into myocardium, as reflected by the distance from endocardial surface, was also independently associated with the predominance of fibrosis on the endocardial surface. Our insights suggest that while volumetric fibrosis burden was the sole independent determinant of the PsAF arrhythmogenic substrate, the degree of atrial adiposity and its infiltration into myocardium could influence the formation of three-dimensional fibrosis distribution and fibrotic substrates, although not directly.

The role of endo–epi differences in arrhythmogenesis

Atrial fibrotic remodelling is widely recognized as a key component of the arrhythmogenic substrate in PsAF,6,7 and accumulating evidence indicates that the three-dimensional fibrosis distribution is a critical determinant of arrhythmogenic propensity. Our recent studies demonstrated the strong contribution of fibrosis burden (the prevalence of fibrosis), density (the level of fibrosis compactness), or disorganization (the level of interdigitation of fibrotic and normal tissue) to PsAF arrhythmogenesis.9–11 Beyond the overall fibrosis information, previous investigations have emphasized the important role of three-dimensional heterogeneity and endo–epi dissociation in AF pathophysiology; a study using optical mapping demonstrated the presence of transmural activation delays between the endocardial- and epicardial surfaces during AF;12 and a study on intraoperative mapping during AF reported asynchronous activation of the endo-epicardial wall.13 More recently, a DT study showed that regions harbouring re-entrant drivers exhibited larger transmembrane voltage differences between endo–epi surfaces compared with regions not harbouring them.38 These findings may help explain the result in the present study that locations exhibiting endocardium-predominant fibrosis were significantly more frequent at locations with PsAF arrhythmogenic substrate than at locations without. In this study, after adjustment in multivariate analysis, endocardium-predominant fibrosis did not remain as an independent determinant of PsAF arrhythmogenesis and only volumetric fibrosis burden remained as the factor; however, given that both the predominance of fibrosis on endocardium and the endo–epi difference value in surface fibrosis fraction were independently associated with volumetric fibrosis burden, it may indirectly contribute to PsAF arrhythmogenesis, through its influence on three-dimensional fibrosis distribution.

Atrial adiposity contributes to persistent atrial fibrillation and its fibrotic substrate

In the present study, adipose tissue volume was independently determined by BMI and CHA2DS2-VASc score, which is consistent with recent publications demonstrating the association of these variables with adipose tissue volume.39,40 Our findings are further supported by a recent large-scale imaging study reporting that atrial adiposity volume is a powerful predictor of all-cause mortality, major adverse cardiovascular events, as well as AF.15 Furthermore, compared with paroxysmal AF, PsAF has also been reported to strongly correlate with adipose tissue volume, independent of LA enlargement,14,18 which aligns with our finding that adipose tissue volume as well as atrial fibrosis burden independently predicted the number of PsAF substrate locations. In addition, recent studies have shown the role of adipose tissue infiltration into myocardium in arrhythmogenic substrate formation. Specifically, fibrotic remodelling caused by adiposity infiltration into subepicardium has been found to contribute to the development of arrhythmogenic substrate in AF, particularly in PsAF,17 and extracellular vesicles from adipose tissue have been found to promote vulnerable substrates and AF.19 Importantly, a human mechanistic study demonstrated that local adipose tissue accumulation is associated with adverse atrial substrate remodelling, characterized by slowed conduction and increased electrogram fractionation, supporting an interaction between adiposity infiltration and electrophysiological abnormalities.16 More recently, PsAF patients have been shown to exhibit increased LA intramyocardial adipose infiltration compared with paroxysmal AF.41,42 These insights into atrial adiposity infiltration may explain our finding that the distance from endocardial surface to the nearest adipose tissue was an independent factor of both the endo–epi difference in surface fibrosis fraction and the predominance of fibrosis on the endocardium.

Bipolar low-voltage burden

Decreased voltage amplitude of endocardial electrograms is generally considered to be associated with abnormal arrhythmogenic substrate accompanied by atrial remodelling, and low-voltage areas have been reported to correlate with the signal intensity of LGE-MRI indicating fibrosis.43 In our recent work, we reported normal ranges of maximum or average bipolar voltage amplitudes at PsAF arrhythmogenic substrates.10,11 However, in this study, by comparing the burden of low-voltage area (quantified as a surface area percentage) between LRs and non-LRs, electrophysiological abnormalities were evaluated as spatial substrate features, thereby clarifying their relationship to fibrosis distribution and PsAF arrhythmogenic substrate. This discrepancy may be explained by prior studies demonstrating that atrial wall stress,44 local geometry,45 and wall thickness46 influence local voltage characteristics, highlighting the limitations of simple point-based voltage metrics. In this context, our findings suggest that quantifying low-voltage area burden on a given surface area may provide a more comprehensive and structurally informative measure of voltage abnormality than regional maximum or average voltage alone when assessing the abnormal substrate.

Furthermore, atrial adiposity detected by CCT has recently been reported to be an independent predictor of the presence of low-voltage areas.47 Although voltage amplitude did not remain independently associated with proximity to adipose tissue after multivariate adjustment in this study, our insights suggest a potential indirect association between adiposity infiltration and voltage characteristics.

Clinical implications

Our findings suggest that DT-based identification of the patient-specific arrhythmogenic substrate may complement standard-of-care PVI by enabling more precise characterization of substrate heterogeneity. Such an approach may help refine patient stratification for additional substrate modification, minimize unnecessary ablation, and guide personalized lesion placement in the atria with fibrotic remodelling that may not be fully captured by the clinical EAM mapping alone. Importantly, while the present study is not intended to establish a clinical workflow, these insights support the potential role of DT technology as a non-invasive substrate characterization tool and may provide a mechanistic framework for future clinical trials.

These findings can also be interpreted in the context of atrial cardiomyopathy, a recent concept encompassing structural and electrophysiological atrial remodelling—including fibrosis distribution, adipose tissue infiltration, and voltage abnormalities—that contributes to arrhythmogenesis. Our results demonstrate complex interactions among structural and electrophysiological features associated with PsAF, thereby providing mechanistic insight into the structural substrate underlying atrial cardiomyopathy.48

Study limitations

The present study has several limitations. First, this study included a relatively small number of patients and was conducted at a single centre. However, for a study integrating both LGE-MRI and CCT obtained from the same patients prior to ablation, this cohort represents a relatively large sample size. Next, a large number of induction simulations were performed across all 22 DTs, with nearly 900 simulations in total. Second, the selection of non-LRs might have introduced bias; however, 264 locations (12 per patient’s DT) were systematically selected from typical 12 regions of the atria, which is considered sufficient for comparison with the 108 identified LRs. In addition, EAM maps were acquired during different stable rhythm and rhythm-specific voltage thresholds were applied; however, rhythm-dependent differences in voltage amplitude may represent an inherent limitation of electrophysiological substrate assessment. Moreover, all personalized DTs had a uniform atrial wall thickness of 2.5 mm throughout the atria. Although this assumption is consistent with a previous MRI study reporting a mean wall thickness of approximately 2.5 mm,49 recent work has reported a relationship between regional atrial wall thickness and PsAF arrhythmogenesis.20 This simplification does not capture regional wall thickness heterogeneity and may introduce some uncertainty in the precise location of the modelled endocardial surface; however, fibrosis classification was derived directly from LGE-MRI segmentation and consistently propagated to the computational mesh. Therefore, surface-FF still provides a meaningful surface-relative measure that primarily reflects MRI-derived fibrosis distribution, although future investigations and continued DT improvement will be necessary. Finally, the findings of the present study primarily provide mechanistic insights into arrhythmogenic substrate formation rather than directly prescriptive guidance for PsAF ablation strategies.

Conclusions

The present study revealed that both fibrosis burden and adipose tissue volume independently predicted the number of PsAF arrhythmogenic substrate locations. Although multivariate adjustment identified volumetric fibrosis burden as the sole independent determinant of such substrate locations, this fibrosis burden was independently associated with the predominance of fibrosis on the endocardium, which, in turn, was independently linked to the proximity to adipose tissue. The volumetric fibrosis burden was also independently associated with bipolar, rather than unipolar, low-voltage area burden. Our findings highlight the complex interactions among structural features, including three-dimensional fibrosis distribution and atrial adiposity infiltration, electrophysiological features, and PsAF arrhythmogenic propensity.

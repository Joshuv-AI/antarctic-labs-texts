Seamless integration of distributed acoustic sensing and passive optical networks for human intrusion monitoring
Authors: Rong Tang, Yunjiang Rao, Huijuan Wu, Yong Geng, Qiang Jing, Lang Xie, Peng He, Zhaoyu Li, Shengtao Zou, Tianyu Hua, Sijin Zheng, Yu Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPassive optical networks (PONs) serve as the backbone of modern all-optical communication infrastructures, while fiber-optic distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) is being applied to various fields as a revolutionary technology. Here we present a study on seamless integration of PONs and DAS for human intrusion monitoring by designing an intensity time-varying frequency shift keying with subcarrier composite spectrum modulation (ITVFS-SCSM) scheme, allowing us to overcome the limitations imposed by the high transmission loss of optical splitters and the low extinction ratio of electro-optic modulators in PONs to the performances of DAS. The ITVFS-SCSM scheme designed can simultaneously realize communication and sensing functions with excellent component and modulation compatibilities within a single channel. Assisted by a time difference of arrival algorithm, the localization of human intrusion with sub-meter accuracy with a high DAS sensitivity of 36.5 pε Hz−1/2 over 25.45 km fiber are achieved. This work represents a breakthrough in transforming PONs into distributed sensing networks that can accurately localize human intrusions with wide coverage, offering great potential to redefine next-generation PONs with both communication and security functions.Subject terms: Optical sensors, Fibre optics and optical communications

Introduction

Driven by the growing demand for high-speed data transmission, the market size of passive optical networks (PONs)—a foundational infrastructure of broadband communication—is projected to exceed USD 37.1 billion1. To support this expansion, the total global fiber length is expected to reach 4.2 billion kilometers by 20302. Among PONs deployment models, fiber-to-the-desk extends optical fiber directly to user terminals and Internet of Things devices, offering high bandwidth, low latency, simplified management, and potential sensing capabilities, thus becoming a key access solution for smart cities and industrial internet applications3. Beyond data transmission, optical fibers in PONs can also serve as distributed acoustic/vibration sensing (DAS) arrays with high density and wide coverage for realizing real-time intrusion monitoring and early warning. In 2009, we first proposed such a concept of integrating optical fiber sensing into communication networks (OFS-in-OFCN). By using wavelength-division-multiplexing, Ethernet PON was integrated with DAS to realize distributed intrusion detection and fault localization4, initiating a new research direction called the integration of optical fiber communication and sensing. The OFS-in-OFCN concept has been applied in various fields, such as submarine environmental monitoring5–8, cable health assessment9, seismic monitoring10–12,natural disasters and infrastructure interactions13,14, as well as traffic15–17 and tunnel monitoring18,19. In urban environments, commercial PONs integrated with DAS have been applied for perimeter security20, and infrastructure monitoring21,22. Overall, the OFS-in-OFCN strategy can play a vital role in enhancing public safety, reducing economic losses, and mitigating ecological risks.

Recently, the OFS-in-OFCN strategy has been expanded to ultrahigh-speed communication23–25, demonstrating its great potential for long-haul applications. However, the seamless integration of DAS and PONs refers to the collaborative optimization of communication and sensing capabilities, enabling high-sensitivity sensing while maintaining low communication bit error rate (BER) and high transmission capacity, which remains as an unsolved key problem, primarily due to the challenges in component and modulation incompatibility. Conventional components in PONs lack inherent characteristics for realizing DAS functionality. Both the optical splitter with high transmission loss and electro-optic modulator (EOM) with low extinction-ratio (ER) in PONs will deteriorate the performances of DAS considerably. At present, all DAS systems employ acousto-optic modulators (AOMs) with high ER of >50 dB26, while PONs rely on EOMs with low ER of <25 dB for high-speed and high-capacity communication signal modulation27. The low ER EOMs induce elevated background noise and pulse contrast reduction to DAS, hence resulting in noticeable degradation in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and sensitivity28 (Supplementary Note 5). Furthermore, the optical splitter in PONs introduces non-negligible losses to the weak Rayleigh backscattered signals (RBS) of the optical pulse propagating along the fiber in DAS29. The optical splitter in PONs divides the forward-propagating pulse power equally among all downstream branches, reducing the injected pulse power to 1/M (here M is the branch number, typically 4, 8, 16, 32, et al). Since RBS is inherently weak and proportional to the forward pulse power, splitting attenuates the RBS returning from each branch to 1/M of its original level, causing severe degradation. This challenge is further compounded while monitoring weak and low-frequency human intrusion events, such as walking, which normally generates micro-vibration signals. Therefore, a DAS system with high sensitivity and low intrinsic noise is required, but is hard to realize due to the optical splitter in PONs. On the other hand, the challenge in modulation incompatibility is that the DAS operates based on the principle of optical time-domain reflectometry and typically uses nanosecond-scale narrow optical pulses to achieve high spatial resolution (SR) for precise distributed sensing, while PONs prioritize transmission ability and commonly employ high-order modulation formats to generate continuous signals to improve spectral efficiency and enhance resilience to noise. As a result, DAS and PONs exhibit obvious differences in signal modulation30.

Here, we demonstrate a seamless integration of DAS and PONs based on an ITVFS-SCSM scheme, which resolves the component and modulation incompatibilities discussed above. The proposed system is implemented within an intensity modulation direct detection (IM/DD) framework, ensuring compatibility with both conventional non-return to zero-based PONs and multi-carrier OFDM-based PONs, thus accommodating current deployments and future network evolution. By stretching optical pulses in the time domain to form continuous waveforms while maintaining constant energy and enabling pulse compression at the receiver, substantial signal gain can be achieved to effectively compensate for the power loss induced by the optical splitter in PONs. Furthermore, the broadband nature of frequency-shifted modulation enhances resistance to noise through matched filtering, improving the detectability of weak RBS even in the presence of residual light leakage caused by low ER EOMs31,32. This approach effectively mitigates the detrimental effects of the low ER EOM and the optical splitter on DAS performances. In addition, thanks to the large-capacity transmission links in PONs, the sensing signals in the integrated DAS+PONs system can be transmitted at rates exceeding Gigabit per second (Gbps) level, supporting high-speed DAS signal transmission for real-time processing. The ability of the ITVFS-SCSM scheme is verified with a high sensing sensitivity of 36.5 pε Hz−1/2@55 Hz over a 25.45 km fiber link, meeting the requirements for most DAS applications, including micro-vibration events such as pedestrian movement. Furthermore, a time difference of arrival (TDOA) method is adopted to achieve sub-meter micro-vibration positioning accuracy.

Methods

Concept of seamless integration of DAS and PONs

Figure 1 illustrates the conceptual framework of the proposed seamless integration of DAS and PONs. To maximize the utilization of existing PONs transmission equipment, and enable low-cost integration of sensing functionality without disrupting communication services, the ITVFS-SCSM scheme is proposed as shown in Fig. 1a. The proposed method applies a linear frequency modulation (LFM) with sweep rate k to each orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) subcarrier, generating a set of subcarriers that are parallel in the time–frequency plane. In the frequency domain, the scheme preserves the orthogonality of conventional OFDM, while in the time domain it introduces temporal broadening that converts the LFM signal for DAS from a pulse into a continuous waveform, thereby further increasing the effective time–bandwidth product. During DAS demodulation, pulse compression is applied in digital signal processing to recover the SR required for DAS. Through this process, the subcarriers remain orthogonal in the integral sense, and the matched-filtering process provides the inherent compression gain of LFM, enabling a substantial improvement in sensing SNR without compromising the communication performances. To ensure strict orthogonality, that is <sn, sm > =0, the condition (fn-fm)Tc = l must be satisfied, where l = 1,2,…3. In this modulation format, the instantaneous frequency range of the n-th subcarrier is [fn, fn + Bn]. When Bn > Δf·(N-1), the chirp trajectories of different subcarriers partially overlap along the frequency domain. However, since the relative frequency offsets between subcarriers remain constant, and the matched filters exploit the full-symbol coherent integration, the subcarriers still maintain orthogonality in the integral sense despite the spectral overlap similar to typical OFDM. By applying LFM individually to each subcarrier, they experience independent frequency modulation, which preserves orthogonality, enhances anti-interference capability, and provides pulse-compression gain for improved sensing resolution and SNR. This subcarrier-level approach therefore enables simultaneous high-performance communication and high-precision fiber sensing.

Based on the above analysis, the cross terms primarily affect the DAS demodulation. To mitigate the impact, three strategies can be adopted: (i) waveform design to enhance subcarrier orthogonality and system synchronization; (ii) digital decoupling, i.e., first demodulate communication data, reconstruct the waveform, and subtract it from the RBS to suppress interference; and (iii) periodic training frames or idle subcarriers to provide calibration references under low SNR or time-varying channels. These measures jointly reduce communication–sensing crosstalk while preserving the overall system performances.

For multi-branch DAS signals, by broadening the light pulse applied in the typical DAS with duration of τ and a peak power of Ppeak to a continuous waveform of duration Tc>>τ, and maintaining the total energy E= Ppeak·τ = Pavg·Tc, the peak power is substantially reduced, thereby mitigating fiber nonlinear effects. Pulse compression is achieved at the receiver by a matched filter with bandwidth B, yielding a peak gain with a time-bandwidth product of Nc = Tc·B, where the output power is Pcompress=Pavg·Nc. The gain directly compensates for the splitter loss L, resulting in an effective signal power Peff = Pcompress/L. Compared with the traditional pulse-based schemes, the enhancement factor is G = B·τ/L, effectively overcoming power loss caused by PON splitters. At the same time, the wideband characteristics of the ITVFS-SCSM signal and the frequency selectivity of matched filtering can be used to suppress the residual optical leakage noise of the EOM (ER < 25 dB). During signal processing, the signal energy is compressed to the main lobe width Δf = 1/Tc, while the background noise power spectral density N0 diffuses to the bandwidth B, making the output noise power Pn,out = N0/Tc. However, the peak power of the compressed signal Ps is proportional to Nc·Pavg, resulting in output SNRout proportional to Nc·SNRpulse. Therefore, the detection capability for weak signals is enhanced by a factor of Nc. Ultimately, the synergy is SNRtotal = Nc·(Bτ/L)·ER. Therefore, the ITVFS-SCSM waveform introduces a gain proportional to the time-bandwidth product for the sensing demodulation, while preserving communication orthogonality. Through time-domain broadening and matched-filter pulse compression34,35, the proposed scheme overcomes the inherent SNR limitations of existing PON-based DAS implementations.

Meanwhile, according to the sampling requirement of DAS, the signal repetition period Tr must exceed the round-trip time of light propagating through the entire fiber (Tf = 2×s×n/c, where s is the length of the fiber, c is the speed of light in vacuum, and n is the refractive index of the fiber), the repetition period of the frequency shifter is set to Tr to ensure that each sensing frame corresponds to a complete round-trip of the probe waveform. Figure 1b illustrates the DAS demodulation process based on the continuous chirp waveform that we have verified32. In the DAS demodulation path, frequency-domain windows are introduced to perform pulse compression and coherent accumulation, enabling flexible tuning between SR and sensitivity. Specifically, the sweep bandwidth is partitioned into N sub-FDWs, each of which is independently compressed and subsequently coherently combined, thereby achieving an adaptive balance between noise suppression and high SR demodulation. Finally, the rotation vector summarizes and differential phase demodulation are applied to the N pulse-compressed output to extract the distributed phase variations along the fiber, enabling perturbation reconstruction and sensing. At the communication receiver (as shown in Fig. 1d), the received ITVFS-SCSM signal first undergoes a de-chirping or matched-filtering process to compensate for the time–frequency modulation applied at the transmitter. The system then follows the typical OFDM demodulation procedure—including windowing, fast Fourier transform processing, channel estimation and equalization, and symbol detection—to recover the original communication data transmitted through the transmitter. As illustrated in Fig. 1c, the proposed integrated DAS–PON system further leverages the tunability of the frequency shifter to dynamically adjust the sweep rate and period according to the link length and sensing sensitivity requirements, enabling adaptive sensing configuration across different branches. In particular, sensing signals with distinct sweep bandwidths or time–bandwidth products can be superimposed onto branch-specific communication signals, allowing each branch to carry a composite communication–sensing waveform with a unique spectral signature. At the receiver, branch-level separation and identification are achieved by applying matched filtering to the echo signals, where each branch is associated with a unique reference chirp spectrum. Consequently, signals with different sweep parameters remain orthogonal in the frequency domain, preventing inter-branch crosstalk and enabling simultaneous monitoring of multiple branches within the PON. This mechanism allows the PON to maintain communication orthogonality while supporting distributed, multi-users, and multi-branches dynamic sensing, providing a flexible and scalable foundation for integrated communication–sensing networks.

To ensure stable sensing performance, a predistortion technique is employed to compensate for amplitude fluctuations of the ITVFS-SCSM signal introduced by the EOM, thereby maintaining a nearly constant optical carrier. Furthermore, a vector summation method is employed to eliminate interference fading, followed by the application of a denoising convolutional neural network to enhance signal quality36.

Results

Verification of ITVFS-SCSM scheme

Figure 2 presents the communication performance verification of the ITVFS-SCSM scheme. An ultra-narrow-linewidth continuous-wave laser (NKT Koheras BasiK MIKRO E15, linewidth <0.1 kHz) is used as the optical source, centered at 1549.5 nm with an output power of 13 dBm. Such ultra-narrow linewidth laser sources provide high coherence and low phase noise required for high-sensitivity DAS while remaining fully compatible with PON architectures. The CWL is divided into two branches by a 90:10 optical coupler (OC), and the ITVFS-SCSM signal is loaded onto the upper branch (90%) at 10 Gbps using an EOM (iXblue MXAN-LN-10). The EOM is biased away from the quadrature point toward the minimum transmission region, forming an asymmetric modulation condition. Under this bias, the optical carrier is not fully suppressed but redistributed between the carrier and sidebands. As a result, residual carrier components remain in the optical spectrum, enabling carrier–sideband beating under square-law PD and thus supporting IM/DD reception of the OFDM signal (Supplementary Note 6). Meanwhile, the reduced carrier-to-sideband ratio modifies the optical field distribution and increases the relative contribution of phase perturbations to the detected signal, which is beneficial for phase-based DAS operation37–39. With appropriate digital compensation, the OFDM signal maintains adequate linearity, and the measured communication performance remains below the 7% FEC threshold. The shared-transmitter architecture therefore provides a practical balance between sensing capability and communication performance within a unified modulation framework. Two polarization controllers are used to manually adjust polarization for optimization of interference signal and sensing accuracy with minimal optical loss. Removing the polarization controllers only affects DAS SNR slightly, while the DAS signal remains stable. To compensate for the insertion loss, the signal light is amplified by an EDFA, and the associated amplified spontaneous emission noise is subsequently suppressed using an ultra-narrow band tunable optical filter (uFilter), thereby enabling constant power modulation.

We also assessed the sensing performances of the system using the ITVFS-SCSM signal generated with a data transmission rate of 10 Gbps and an optical launching power of 5 dBm. In the experiment, the fiber under test is 25.45 km in length, and a PZT wrapped with 5 m of bare fiber is placed at 24.92 km along the fiber link. The RBS is detected by the BPD at a sampling rate of 1.25 GSa s-1. A series of FDWs with bandwidth Bτ are employed to extract the original RBS by matched filtering, while a Hamming window function is applied to suppress spectral leakage during the Fourier transform. In this configuration, the system SR is given by SR = c/2nBτ = M∙c/2nB, where M represents the number of FDWs used for frequency division. Due to interference-induced fading in DAS systems, the RBS within each FDW is severely attenuated. To mitigate this effect, we introduce a rotating vector algorithm and group every three adjacent FDWs together to effectively suppress interference fading42. During demodulation, 36 FDWs are employed, with every three adjacent sub-channels grouped to mitigate interference fading. Consequently, the theoretical SR of 3.69 m is calculated, and the spectral width of each window is 27 MHz over a duration of 7 µs. Based on the above experimental parameters, we can achieve a theoretical pulse compression gain Nc = 22.76 dB and encoding gain of SNRtotal = 32.76 dB, which is sufficient to counteract the losses caused by the optical splitter and the low ER of the EOM. However, in practical applications, the theoretical SNR gain is inherently constrained physical layer non-idealities (side lobe leakage of matched filtering, laser phase noise) and system losses (fiber dispersion, polarization noise), resulting in the actual gain that falls short of the theoretical prediction31,43. In future work, joint optimization of optical design and digital processing can further improve the SNR, thereby enhancing the detection capability of weak DAS signals.

Field trial of human intrusion monitoring

A field trial for human intrusion localization was conducted at the University of Electronic Science and Technology of China in Chengdu (longitude: 103.931633, latitude: 30.749125), using three branch optical cables from an existing PON. As shown in Fig. 4a, the ITVFS-SCSM signal generated by the integrated system is transmitted through a 1 km guiding optical cable and subsequently distributed to three different branches of the PON via a 1:4 optical splitter. Branch 1, with a length of 550 m, is primarily used for detecting heavy object drop events. In the specific experiment, a 10 kg lead ball is used to simulate a rockfall event. Branch 2, with a length of 530 m, is used for locating external pedestrian activity in human intrusion monitoring. Branch 3, with a length of 600 m, is used to monitor vehicle trajectories in urban traffic. During demodulation, the SR is set to 3.5 m, enabling real-time monitoring of various intrusion events along the optical cables by collecting and separating the DAS signals from different branches of the PON. Figure 4b illustrates the signal generation and distribution setup, where the integrated signal distributed to the three optical fiber branches occupies different spectrum bands. To ensure compatibility with existing PON infrastructure, the integrated signal is switched via an optical switch before distribution to the three experimental links through an optical splitter. The RBS is received via an optical circulator, and the individual branch signals are subsequently separated using a matched filtering algorithm. Although simultaneous signal distribution to multiple branches is limited by hardware constraints, the experiment successfully demonstrates the feasibility and effectiveness of simultaneous signal reception and separation across multiple fiber branches in PON.

Based on the TDOA principle, the intrusion source is located using demodulated DAS data. This method estimates the vibration source position by measuring the time delay differences of vibration waves arriving at multiple sensing nodes within the sensing network, combined with the signal propagation velocity. The localization performance of the proposed ITVFS-SCSM scheme is evaluated under three types of seismic sources: a shot-put event simulating the destructive rockfall at position (46 m, 60 m) (case A, Supplementary Note 8), a human walking at various positions as shown in Fig. 4c (case B) and a vehicle movement trajectory (case C, Supplementary Note 7). The above position coordinates are all our custom relative positions in the experiment. To evaluate the system’s response to micro-vibration signals, such as those generated by pedestrian activity, the experimenter walked along the A–B–C path depicted in Fig. 4a over a total duration of 5 seconds. Figure 4c shows the time-distance relationship of the pedestrian event. The reconstructed DAS signal exhibits a strong–weak–strong variation pattern that closely correlates with the experimenter’s walking trajectory, indicating a positive correlation between the vibration signal strength and the distance of the subject to the fiber cable. The inset presents the TDOA curve fitting results of the DAS signal. Since the subsequent localization process relies on the responses of individual sensing channels, the demodulated DAS data are converted from the time–distance domain to the time–channel domain, which changes the horizontal axis from distance to channel index. Subsequently, 100 sets of human walking signals are collected for localization analysis at three distinct locations — (7 m, 2 m), (7 m, 3 m), and (7 m, 4 m). A minimum localization error of 0.48 m is achieved, and the localization error at the 90% confidence level is 0.96 m, as shown in Figs. 4d and 4e, respectively. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed ITVFS-SCSM scheme can effectively detect micro-vibration signals along the optical cables with sub-meter localization accuracy based on existing PONs.

Discussion

We integrate DAS sensing signal with typical communication OFDM-QAM signal through the ITVFS-SCSM scheme verified, achieving the seamless integration of sensing and communication, thereby adding distributed sensing capability to existing PONs for detection of human activities within the wide coverage of PONs. As shown in the detailed signal modulation process (Supplementary Note 1-4), this work realizes excellent compatibility between DAS and PONs by linearly modulating the spectrum of each subcarrier in the OFDM-QAM signal. Owing to the compatibility of the modulation format, the modulation architecture proposed can also be applied to generate integrated sensing and communication signals using other communication modulation formats, such as binary phase shift keying, minimum shift keying, orthogonal chirp division multiplexing48, and non-return to zero modulation format (Supplementary Note 9), although this work only demonstrates an IM/DD-based approach.

By incorporating coherent modulation techniques, the ITVFS-SCSM scheme can be seamlessly extended to coherent optical communication systems. Using an in-phase/quadrature-EOM, OFDM-QAM can modulate the communication data while frequency-shifted phase modulation embeds the DAS waveform on the same optical carrier. Future coherent PONs or metropolitan area networks—where complex-field modulation and phase-sensitive detection are standard—will naturally employ narrow-linewidth lasers, providing an ideal laser source for integrated coherent communication and sensing. Thus, narrow-linewidth lasers ensure seamless compatibility with both current and future PON architectures. By replacing IM/DD-based demodulation with coherent phase recovery algorithms, the same modulated signal can be utilized for high-sensitivity DAS applications without requiring any modification to the existing optical hardware. The integration of DAS into coherent systems offers multiple advantages: enhanced DAS sensitivity, SNR and SR, enabled by more advanced modulation formats, and extended sensing range, supported by coherent transmission. Furthermore, the coherent receiving modules for both communication and DAS can be co-integrated within a unified digital signal processing unit, further reducing system complexity and hardware redundancy.

As a fundamental communication infrastructure interlinking diverse public facilities and large population of end users in urban areas, PONs are anticipated to evolve into a multi-branch, collaboratively distributed optical fiber sensing system (DOFS), exhibiting inherent advantages in supporting multi-parameter sensing, including strain, temperature, etc49. By performing local pre-processing and feature extraction at edge nodes, and further integrating with edge computing and artificial intelligence algorithms50–52, the integration of PONs and DOFS systems can effectively enhance urban responsiveness via detection of multi-parameters for trend analysis and early warning, thereby supporting coordinated Internet of Things management across critical fields, such as energy, information, transportation, and public safety53,54. Especially when integrated with emerging deployments of MAN, this DOFS + MAN architecture is expected to expand its application to scenarios such as online monitoring of oil/gas/water pipelines, electrical powerlines, optical communication cables, highways/high-speed railways, and civil infrastructures. Furthermore, the integration of fiber-to-the-desk and DOFS enables real-time monitoring in the room.

In summary, this work demonstrates the feasibility of seamlessly integrating DAS and PONs, enabling real-time security monitoring by using existing PONs via the ITVFS-SCSM scheme proposed. Accurate localization of human intrusion is successfully achieved without affecting the operation of communication. By leveraging existing PONs as a sensing platform and implanting DAS seamlessly, the sensing-enabled PONs can effectively identify typical human walking with sub-meter level localization accuracy, demonstrating a notable advancement in OFS-in-OFCN. Furthermore, the proposed scheme realizes the seamless integration of sensing and communication at the physical layer to allow both functions to share a single optical channel simultaneously. This work eliminates the need for additional equipment and effectively avoids signal interference between sensing and communication, thereby reducing the complexity and cost of DAS deployment in existing PONs. With excellent compatibilities in both component and modulation, such an OFS-in-OFCN strategy shows great potential for widespread applications in smart-city scenarios, such as perimeter security, campus surveillance, industrial facility monitoring, and urban infrastructure protection, et al. Overall, the proposed OFDM-PON-based DAS scheme, serving as a proof-of-concept for future flexible or reconfigurable PON architectures, provides a promising solution for integrated communication and distributed sensing, and establishes a solid foundation for next-generation PON-DOFS integration systems and advanced fiber-to-the-desk infrastructures with dual communication–sensing capabilities.

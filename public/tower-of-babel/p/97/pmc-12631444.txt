An Efficient Strategy to Boost the Removal Efficiency for Submicron Particles and VOCs of Polypropylene in the Complex Flue Gas
Authors: Jincheng Li, Siqi Wang, Hao Lu, Mingdeng Xiang, Yunjiang Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Particulate matter (PM) is recognized as a significant environmental risk factor, with profound adverse effects on human health. The development of air filters that maintain high performance under complex environmental conditions remains a challenge. Herein, a novel composite air filter was fabricated by the in situ growth of ZIF-8 nanoparticles onto polypropylene (PP) fibers in this study, aiming to enhance filtration efficiency and adaptability in challenging environments. The results demonstrated that the PP/ZIF-8 membrane contained a substantial number of rhombic dodecahedron crystals. The removal efficiencies could be obtained at 84.6%, 92.6%, 93.5%, 95.8%, and 96.9% for PM0.5, PM1.0, PM2.5, PM5.0, and PM10, respectively. The pressure drop decreased from 0.19 to 0.07 kPa. The filtration efficiency of PM0.5 for PP/ZIF-8 remained as high as 80% after 50 filtration cycles. VOCs containing nitrogen and carbon elements were removed preferentially for PP/ZIF-8, while oxygen-containing compounds may be competitively suppressed from complex flue gas. The adsorption capacity of PP/ZIF-8 for benzene VOCs was higher than that of PP, and the actual adsorption process for benzene could be better described by the pseudo-first-order kinetic model. Additionally, the concentrations of fulvic acid substances and high-molecular-weight humic substances were significantly decreased in the exhaust gas. Electrostatic interaction and chemisorption contributed significantly to PM and VOCs removal. This study proposes a novel strategy to boost the removal efficiency and adaptability of the air filter in complex environmental conditions with low pressure drop.

Introduction

Common air pollutants, such as dust, biological matter, smoke, hazardous gases, and particulate matter (PM), contribute significantly to global premature mortality, and approximately 7 million people die due to air pollution, as reported by the World Health Organization. , PM can be classified into different categories, such as PM2.5, PM1.0, and PM0.5, which consist of extremely small solid particles and droplets, including sulfates, nitrates, ammonia, and carbon black. , Epidemiological and toxicological studies have demonstrated that submicron-sized PM with certain pathogens can enter the lungs and bloodstream through respiration, leading to respiratory infections, lung cancer, neurodegeneration, and cardiovascular diseases. − Consequently, the issue of reducing exposure to submicrometer-sized PM has garnered considerable attention from researchers.

Polypropylene (PP) filters are widely employed in personal protective equipment and air purification systems due to their favorable properties, such as high production efficiency, good mechanical stability, and relatively high filtration performance. These filters primarily remove PM through a combination of mechanical filtration mechanisms, including interception, inertial impact, diffusion, and electrostatic interaction. Despite their widespread use, PP fibers have several limitations that restrict their long-term performance and applicability in complex environments. Specifically, PP fibers typically possess a relatively large fiber diameter and low porosity, which results in poor removal efficiency for submicrometer particles (less than 1 μm in diameter). While increasing filter thickness and basis weight can enhance PM removal efficiency, − such modifications inevitably lead to a higher pressure drop, compromising breathability and energy efficiency. The surface charge of PP plays a critical role in enhancing the PM removal efficiency via electrostatic attraction. However, the electric charge on the surface of PP can diminish in specific environments, such as those environments with high humidity and the presence of organic chemicals. Some studies have reported that the service life of PP air filters is typically less than 4 h under conditions of high humidity and elevated temperatures. , Heo et al. further demonstrated that exposure to cigarette smoke aerosols sharply declined the filtration efficiency from 92.5% to 33.3% primarily due to the impacted electrostatic properties of filters. This loss of charge compromises the filtration performance of PP-based materials, leading to an inconsistent PM removal efficiency and potentially elevated environmental risk. Therefore, developing filters with stable and high-efficiency PM removal in complex environments is essential.

To improve the filtration performance of traditional filters, various nanoparticles such as zinc oxide nanorods (ZnO NRs), reduced graphene oxide (rGO), manganese dioxide (MnO2), , and metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) , have been introduced. Among these, MOFs exhibit exceptional properties for air filtration applications due to their tunable porosity, high adsorption capacity, structural stability, and antibacterial capabilities. Li et al. developed Cu-BTC to enhance the PM2.5 removal efficiency and the antibacterial properties of PP, achieving a removal efficiency of up to 95%. However, the research on the removal efficiency of submicrometer particles remains limited, and existing evaluations under simulated conditions have neglected complex environmental factors. This oversight may impede the widespread application of MOFs in air filtration. Zeolitic imidazole-8 framework (ZIF-8) represents a typical porous material characterized by its high specific surface area, tunable pore size, remarkable thermal stability, and excellent chemical resistance. − Therefore, the development of ZIF-8 integrating with polymer materials on air filters has garnered significance, which can broaden their applications and work with a synergistic effect for air filtration.

To address the limitations of PP filters in complex environments, a composite air filter was developed by integrating ZIF-8 onto a commercial PP. The surface of PP was modified with hydroxyl groups by a polydopamine coating (PDA) through oxidative self-polymerization. Metal ions were introduced and subsequently grafted by a covalent force that facilitated the attachment of uniformly distributed three-dimensional dodecahedron nanoparticles. The nanoparticle with a porous channel structure increased the adsorption performance and surface roughness of the air filter, significantly enhancing the removal ability of PM. The morphology of PP/ZIF-8 and removal efficiency for PM10, PM2.5, PM1.0, and PM0.5 from complex flue gas were analyzed and investigated, and the filtration mechanism was thoroughly elucidated. This study would broaden the application of ZIF-8 for air filtration and provide a novel strategy to boost the removal efficiency of PM.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals and Reagents

A commercial melt-blown nonwoven polypropylene (PP) fabric, manufactured by Zhejiang Hangkang Medical Equipment Co., Ltd., was used as the substrate. The fabric has an areal density of 0.0025 g/cm2, a thickness of 0.15 mm, and is primarily used for air filtration applications. The infrared spectrum of the polypropylene fabric is presented in Figure S1. Zinc acetate dihydrate (C4H6O4Zn), methanol (CH4O), 2-methylimidazole (98%, C4H6N2), tris­(hydroxymethyl)­aminomethane (Tri), dopamine hydrochloride (DA), and tannic acid (TA) were purchased from Macklin Reagent Company. The chemical reagents were of analytical grade and used without further treatment.

Fabrication of ZIF-8

The preparation method for ZIF-8 was modified based on previous studies, , and detailed information was provided in Text S1. C4H6O4Zn and 2-methylimidazole were dissolved in methanol, and the product was dried in a vacuum drying oven at 60 °C.

PP Surface Modification

Ethanol and deionized water were employed to wash and remove surface impurities from the PP. After washing, the PP was dried at 60 °C in a vacuum oven for 24 h, designated as PP-Cleaning. Subsequently, PP-Cleaning was immersed in a mixed solution containing DA, TA, and Tri at pH 8.5. The mixed solution was left for 24 h to load the hydroxyl group onto the surface of PP, referred to as PP-Tri.

Fabrication of PP/ZIF-8 Fiber Membrane

PP/ZIF-8 membrane materials were synthesized by using the in situ growth method. In brief, 1 mol of C4H6O4Zn was dissolved in methanol. The PP-Tri was immersed in the mixed solution for 12 h to load Zn2+ firmly. Subsequently, 2-methylimidazole dissolved in methanol was mixed with C4H6O4Zn solution and reacted with Zn2+ for 6 h. Finally, the PP/ZIF-8 membranes were washed with fresh methanol and deionized water. The modified membrane was dried and stored for later use. The precursor solution concentrations used to prepare PP/ZIF-8 are shown in Table S1, and the corresponding membranes are labeled PP5/ZIF-8 and PP7/ZIF-8. The precursor solution concentration of PP7/ZIF-8 is higher than that of PP5/ZIF-8.

Filtration Performance Measurement

A custom-built testing apparatus was used, consisting of a flue gas generator, flow controller, filtration ports, and detection unit (Figure S2). The combustion chamber had a volume of 500 mL, and the effective filtration area of the membranes was 94.98 cm2 (φ = 5.5 cm). The airflow rate was maintained at 25 L/min using a flow controller, resulting in a face velocity of 17.5 cm/s. PM and VOCs generated by combusting commercially available cigarettes (Hongshuangxi, China). The inlet PM mass concentration was maintained at approximately 1000 mg/m3. During this process, the hazardous gases were released, including 63 detectable gaseous compounds containing nitrogen, oxygen, and carbon atoms, such as acetonitrile, benzene, toluene, xylenes, aldehydes, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). , Benzene is regarded as the model VOC to analyze the adsorption performance, and the detailed test method is described in Text 3. The concentrations of PM at the upstream and downstream of the filter were measured simultaneously using two identical optical particle counters (OPCs, Nohawk NK800, China), which measured particles ranging in size from 0.5 to 10 μm. The filtration efficiency was calculated based on the ratio of the downstream to upstream concentrations of PM. Each filtration experiments were repeated three times and averaged to ensure reproducibility. The removal efficiency of PM and quality factor (QF) were calculated by eqs and , respectively. Characterization

A field emission scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi SU-8100, Japan) was used to observe the morphology of the fibers, and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) was used to determine the elemental composition and distribution. The average diameter of the fiber was measured by using Nano Measure software. The crystal phase was analyzed by an X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Rigaku SmartLab SE) with Cu Kα radiation at 40 kV and 20 mA. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Shimadzu AXIS SUPRA) under vacuum conditions of 1 × 10–9 mbar and a Fourier transform infrared spectrometer (FTIR, PerkinElmer 100) were used to identify the surface functional group. The specific surface area was measured at 77 K using a specific surface area analyzer (BET, Micromeritics ASAP). The surface static voltage was assessed by using an electrostatic field meter (FMX-003). Hydrophilicity and hydrophobicity were evaluated by using a contact angle tester (JC2000DM). Fluorescence spectroscopy (3D-EEM, Hitachi F-7000) was used to determine the composition of exhaust gas absorbed by the ethanol solution.

Results and Discussion

The Structural Properties of PP/ZIF-8

To improve the applicability of PP in various environments for air filtration, a self-polymerization and coordination chemical reaction was employed to graft hydroxyl groups and metal ions via covalent grafting. A composite PP/ZIF-8 filter membrane with effective filtration capabilities in complex flue gas was prepared, and the synthesis process is illustrated in Figure . The self-polymerization reaction was initiated by DA under alkaline conditions (pH = 8.5) to form a PDA coating. , The PDA coating adhered to the PP surface through π-π interactions, hydrogen bonds, and covalent bonds. The addition of TA and Tri stabilized the PDA coating and increased the hydroxyl density on the PP surface by a cross-linking reaction, resulting in the formation of a stable PDA-TA coating. The polar pollutants from the combustion process of smoke incense would be removed by the rich oxygen-containing functional groups of PDA-TA. Subsequently, a coordination chemical reaction occurred between the metal ions and hydroxyl functional groups. The PP with the PDA-TA coating was immersed in a solution containing Zn2+, allowing Zn2+ to form coordination bonds with the catechol and phenolic hydroxyl groups within the PDA-TA coating, leading to the formation of a Zn2+-PDA-TA complex. Zn2+-PDA-TA complex would serve as anchor points, facilitating the uniform formation of ZIF-8 on the PP surface upon the addition of 2-methylimidazole.

The average fiber diameter of pristine PP fibers was 1.77 μm, and the surface morphologies of PP-Tri and PP/ZIF-8 became rougher (Figure c–e). The average fiber diameters for PP5/ZIF-8 and PP7/ZIF-8 increased to 2.35 and 2.23 μm, respectively, which is attributed to the successful coating of ZIF-8 nanoparticles. The fibers of PP-Tri exhibited more protruding particles, which were attributed to the significant generation of PDA particles during the self-polymerization reaction (Figure c). The fiber of PP exhibited a rhombic dodecahedral shape with a well-defined crystal structure. These results indicate that the PP/ZIF-8 composite was successfully synthesized. The weight percentage of the ZIF-8 for PP5/ZIF-8 and PP7/ZIF-8 was calculated (Table S3). The weight percentage of ZIF-8 for PP5/ZIF-8 was higher than that of PP7/ZIF-8, which is mainly attributed to the loss of ZIF-8 nanoparticles agglomerating onto PP at higher precursor solution concentration. The ZIF-8 nanoparticles were relatively uniform without significant agglomeration (Figure d), which would effectively increase the active sites and contact area for PM and VOCs. , The C content gradually decreased, while the O content increased after cleaning and modification, indicating an increase in the number of −OH functional groups. The Zn element could be observed, and the content increased as the precursor concentration improved. EDS mapping was conducted to further assess the elemental distribution of PP5/ZIF-8 (Figure f–k). The homogeneous distribution of N and Zn elements across the fiber matrix confirmed the uniform dispersion of ZIF-8 nanocrystals on the fiber surfaces, which was consistent with a previous study. These findings confirm the successful growth and uniform distribution of ZIF-8 on the PP substrate, in agreement with the SEM observations. Additionally, the BET curves for PP and PP5/ZIF-8 are shown in Figure S3. The specific surface area increased from 1.881 to 105.3 m2/g, indicating that the introduction of ZIF-8 facilitated the formation of new hierarchical porous networks. The original PP fiber membrane consists of fibers connected to form a large network structure, which is not conducive to the removal of small gas molecules. After the in situ growth of ZIF-8, the membrane exhibits a greater abundance of microporous and mesoporous structures, which enhance the removal of small gas molecules. Therefore, the increase in micro- and mesopores greatly improved the specific surface area of the fiber membrane.

The intensity of the PP5/ZIF-8 spectrum increased at 762, 689, and 420 cm–1. The characteristic peaks of Zn–N bonds in ZIF-8 appeared at 420 cm–1. The surface elements and chemical state of the PP and PP5/ZIF-8 fiber were further determined by XPS. The proportion of C–O and CO significantly decreased after loading ZIF-8, indicating that the surface of PP after in situ growth ZIF-8 is more hydrophobic than that of PP. The binding energy located at 398.6, 399.8, and 405.5 eV corresponded to C–NC, the intermediate N atom, and free amino group (C-NH2) and Zn2+-N bond (Figure S6c). Stability Performance Measurement

The thermal stability provided valuable information for evaluating their applications at high temperatures for PP5/ZIF-8. The weight loss process primarily consisted of one interval, attributed to the thermal decomposition of C–C, C–H, and C–O bonds in the PP (Figure S7). In contrast, pure ZIF-8 exhibited two distinct intervals. The weight loss for pure ZIF-8 and PP5/ZIF-8 commenced at 500 and 358 °C, while the weight of the PP fiber began to decrease at approximately 274 °C, suggesting that the thermal stability of PP5/ZIF-8 was higher than PP. These findings align with those reported in previous research. These results demonstrate that PP5/ZIF-8 possesses good thermal stability. In order to study the available charges in a complex environment, the surface static voltage reflected the accumulation and storage capacity of charge in air filters was determined. The surface static voltage increased with ZIF-8 incorporated into PP, reaching 3.1 kV (Table S3). The finding indicated that ZIF-8 could enhance the ability of PP to capture externally injected charges significantly. Furthermore, the dielectric constant of the composite membrane was discussed (Figure S8a). The dielectric constant PP fiber was 4.4, and the dielectric constant improved after modification with ZIF-8, indicating an enhancement in charge accumulation and storage capacity on the surface of PP. The increases in surface static voltage and dielectric constant are advantageous for the removal of PM from the air by electrostatic interaction. To assess the applicability of air filters and their performance at various temperatures, further studies have investigated the charge accumulation and storage capacity of fibers at different temperatures (Figure S8b). The dielectric constant exhibited minimal variation under low-temperature conditions (<100 °C), while it decreased significantly as the temperature increased. The dielectric constant of PP dropped at 107 °C, implying that surface charges on PP were lost as the environmental temperature increased, making it challenging to achieve stable removal of PM from the air through electrostatic interactions. However, the PP5/ZIF-8 composite demonstrated a diminished ability to retain charge at approximately 120 °C. The incorporation of ZIF-8 nanoparticles exhibited remarkable thermal stability in surface charge properties, maintaining consistent electrostatic characteristics at elevated temperatures up to 120 °C. This enhanced thermoelectric stability significantly broadened the operational temperature range of the composite material, facilitating efficient capture of PM through sustained electrostatic interactions under thermally stressed conditions. Additionally, the mechanical stability of PP5/ZIF-8 was analyzed, as shown in Figure S9. The mechanical properties of PP undergo fundamental changes after incorporating ZIF-8. The fracture strength of the PP5/ZIF-8 composite material significantly increased from 530 to 1124 kPa, representing an increase of 112%. Meanwhile, the elongation decreased from 32% to 12%. Moreover, the initial slope of the stress–strain curve increases sharply, indicating a significant enhancement in the Young’s modulus of the PP5/ZIF-8. These changes indicate that ZIF-8 nanoparticles effectively improve the strength and stiffness of the PP. Therefore, the mechanical properties of the prepared PP5/ZIF-8 membrane are sufficient to meet the requirements of daily PM filtration.

Filter Performance for PM and VOCs in Complex Flue Gas

PM Removal

The performance of PM filtration in a complex flue gas was evaluated to further investigate the application of the air filters. A self-made flue gas filtration system was used to assess filtration efficiency, pressure drop, and quality factor (Figure ). The filtration efficiency of raw PP in complex flue gas was measured at 39.5%, 68.1%, 69.9%, 79.1%, and 84.4% for PM0.5, PM1.0, PM2.5, PM5.0, and PM10, respectively (Figure a). The filtration efficiency for PM0.5 was notably low. After modification, the filtration efficiency improved except for PM0.5. This enhancement may be attributed to an increased surface roughness and a greater contact area between PM and the fibers. The removal efficiency of PM0.5 decreased, likely due to the loss of charge, making it challenging to capture PM through electrostatics. The mechanisms of mechanical filtration involve interception, inertial impact, and the diffusion effect. The PP modified by in situ growth of ZIF-8 maintained a good removal efficiency of PM in flue gas, with the highest removal efficiencies reaching 84.6%, 92.6%, 93.5%, 95.8%, and 96.9% for PP5/ZIF-8, respectively. This improvement can be ascribed to the enhancement of the surface charge and porosity of ZIF-8. The pressure drop is a significant parameter to assess the potential utility of PP/ZIF-8. The pressure drop of raw PP was measured at 0.19 kPa (Figure b). After modification, the pressure drop decreased with ZIF-8 generated on-site, resulting in a lower pressure drop of 0.07 kPa for PP/ZIF-8. The reduction in the pressure drop can be attributed to the increase in gas permeability performance. The previous study has proved that higher air permeability of the membrane means lower pressure drop, demonstrating that the gas permeation through the membrane is improved. The size of functional pores for ZIF-8 is around 0.42 nm, and gas pollutants with a kinetic diameter less than 0.42 nm pass through the pore size. PP/ZIF-8 with a larger specific area can improve the diffusion rate of gas, resulting in a decrease in pressure drop. , The quality factor of the air filter based on the filter efficiency and pressure drop was calculated (Figure c). The quality factor of PP5/ZIF-8 and PP7/ZIF-8 was superior to that of other PP materials. This indicated that the incorporation of ZIF-8 onto PP can improve filtration performance in complex flue gas applications, providing a promising solution for the development of efficient and environmentally sustainable air filters. The ZIF-8 nanoparticles were relatively uniform without significant agglomeration in PP5/ZIF-8, and ZIF-8 was easily aggregated at higher preparation concentrations. Therefore, the PP5/ZIF-8 membrane represents the best formulation for the removal of both PM and VOCs. The long-term filtration stability of the PP5/ZIF-8 membrane was assessed, as shown in Figure d. After 50 filtration cycles, the filtration efficiency for PM0.5 remained as high as 80%, representing only a 5.20% decrease from the initial filtration efficiency. Meanwhile, the pressure drop increased steadily throughout the cycles, reaching about 0.088 kPa by the 50th cycle. The decline in filtration efficiency and the rise in pressure drop over the 50 cycles are attributed to dust accumulation on the filter surface. Despite these changes, the membranes prepared via in situ growth of ZIF-8 nanoparticles demonstrated excellent filtration performance and cycle stability, offering a promising approach for the manufacture of recyclable air filtration membranes. The morphology of the PP fibers after filtration was analyzed, and the SEM image is presented in Figure S10. The morphology of the PP fibers before and after filtration remained similar, with a significant number of nodules observed on the fibers (Figure S10a). The surface of the PP5/ZIF-8 became rougher, and the ZIF-8 nanoparticles were enriched with numerous particles, leading to a transformation in their surface morphology from the original regular hexahedral structure to irregular spherical particles (Figure S10d,e). This change is primarily attributed to PM adhering to the PP5/ZIF-8 nanofiber with some PM gradually aggregating larger clusters of smoke particles on the surface of the ZIF-8 due to electrostatic interactions.

VOCs Removal

FTIR, XPS, and 3D-EEM were used to assess the VOCs removal performance in a complex environment qualitatively. The FTIR spectrum of the air filter after filtration is presented in Figure S11. The transmittance at the wavenumbers of 1372 cm–1 for PP was enhanced compared to the spectrum obtained before filtration, and no new peaks appeared in the PP spectrum before and after filtration. The wavenumber of 1372 cm–1 corresponds to the symmetric deformation vibration of −CH groups. Additionally, three new peaks appeared at 3611, 2239, and 1580–1814 cm–1 in the FTIR spectrum. The peak at 3611 cm–1 is attributed to the −OH stretching vibration of the phenolic compounds. The vibration at 2239 cm–1 corresponds to R–CC or CN functional groups, while the range of 1580–1814 cm–1 corresponds to the CC stretching vibrations of the benzene ring. This observation may be linked to the effective removal of phenolic compounds, alkynes, benzene, and their derivatives produced by flue gas. Furthermore, the peak intensities at 420 and 762 cm–1 remained unchanged during filtration, indicating that polymer and ZIF-8 with good interfacial stability could play an excellent part in air filtration. The surface components and chemical states of PP and the PP5/ZIF-8 composite after filtration were further analyzed using XPS, and the results are shown in Figure . The peaks of C 1s, N 1s, and O 1s were observed at 284.5, 398.8, and 531.5 eV for PP, respectively. Zn 2p was identified at 1045 eV in the PP5/ZIF-8 membrane (Figure S12). The proportion of O increased by 31%, and the proportion of C and N decreased for PP. While the proportions of C and N increased by 31.1% and 26.0% for PP5/ZIF-8, the proportion of O decreased (Table ). These results suggest that nitrogen-containing compounds (such as nicotine, nitrosamines, and acetonitrile) and carbon compounds (including PAHs and VOCs) in complex flue gas were easily captured or adsorbed by PP5/ZIF-8, and oxygen-containing compounds (such as CO and CO2) in the flue gas may be preferentially removed by PP. In addition, the observed increase in the CC/C–H (284.6 eV) in the C 1s spectrum implied that a significant quantity of PM was adsorbed onto the surface, altering its original structure due to π–π interactions in PP5/ZIF-8. A π–π* transition satellite peak appeared at 292.1 eV (Figure S12a), which is attributed to the adsorption of PAHs from the flue gas. The shift in peak positions between the O 1s and N 1s is primarily due to hydrogen bonding interactions (Figure S12b,c). The proportion of Zn remained unchanged before and after filtration in the Zn 2p spectrum. ZIF-8 was strongly attached to the PP fibers through coordination interactions or chemical bonding. In order to determine the adhesion stability of ZIF-8 onto PP, the PP5/ZIF-8 was subjected to airflow at various velocities for 10 min. The mass loss results are shown in Figure S13. The weight loss percentage remained below 0.5%, and the highest weight loss percentage was 0.23% at an air velocity of 17.5 cm/s. These findings indicate that the ZIF-8 surface loaded onto PP is relatively stable and resistant to detachment, demonstrating the excellent stability of PP5/ZIF-8.

To quantify the adsorption capacity of PP5/ZIF-8 for VOCs, benzene was used as a model pollutant. A static adsorption method was conducted with a JW-ZQ200 steam adsorption instrument to determine the performance of PP5/ZIF-8 (Figure S14). The adsorption capacity of PP5/ZIF-8 for benzene vapor was higher than that of PP as the relative pressure increased, indicating promising potential for gas adsorption of toxic and harmful substances. The breakthrough curves of benzene for PP and PP5/ZIF-8 are shown in Figure S14b. The outlet concentration of benzene for PP increased rapidly from the start and reached equilibrium within 50 min, indicating a weak adsorption affinity and rapid breakthrough. In contrast, PP5/ZIF-8 exhibited a significantly enhanced adsorption capability. The outlet concentration began to increase after 5 min and reached equilibrium within 150 min. These results confirm that the introduction of ZIF-8 provides abundant micropores and adsorption sites, greatly improving the dynamic adsorption capacity and extending the breakthrough time for benzene gas. Furthermore, pseudo-first- and pseudo-second-order kinetic models were used to fit the adsorption process. The associated kinetic parameters are shown in Figure S14c,d and Table . The coefficient of determination (R 2) of the pseudo-first-order kinetic model was higher than that of the pseudo-second-order kinetic model, indicating that the actual adsorption process could be better described by the pseudo-first-order kinetic model. This suggests that the adsorption process likely does not involve substantial electron sharing or exchange between the benzene and PP5/ZIF-8, and that physisorption processes dominated by van der Waals forces or diffusion through pores are predominant. Otherwise, in order to further clarify the composition of the exhaust gas and the removal of pollutants, ethanol solution was utilized to absorb the exhaust gas. The 3D-EEM spectrogram can be categorized into five regions (Figure S15), which correspond to aromatic proteins (Region I), soluble microbial metabolites (Region II), fulvic acid substances (Region III), humic acid substances (Region IV), and high-molecular-weight humic substances (Region V). The exhaust gas generated during cigarette combustion primarily consists of high-molecular-weight humic substances and humic acid with minor contributions from aromatic proteins and fulvic acid-like materials. It is not detectable that the soluble microbial metabolites are. The observed decrease in the relative abundance of fulvic acid substances and high-molecular-weight humic substances suggests a significant reduction in the pollution load of PP/ZIF-8 attributed to these fluorophores. This trend may indicate either degradation of complex organics or adsorption by ZIF-8, further highlighting the potential role of ZIF-8 in mitigating harmful emissions.

Filtration Mechanism

The filtration mechanisms in air filters can be categorized into interception, inertial, diffusion, electrostatic, and gravity effects (Figure ). The fiber of the PP can remove the larger PM, such as PM5, PM2.5, and PM1.0, through mechanical filtration mechanisms, which include interception, inertial, diffusion, and gravity effects. The electrostatic filtration mechanism relies on the electrostatic charge present in the membrane materials to capture the PM smaller than 1.0, and surface charge is recognized as a critical parameter for traditional filtration materials with high filtration efficiency and low resistance. − However, poor adaptability to complex, high-temperature environments can diminish the electrostatic charge on PP, thereby reducing its removal efficiency. ZIF-8 exhibits a larger dipole moment, , which increases the relative dielectric constant and facilitates the accumulation of induced charges in this study. The static voltage of the PP/ZIF-8 composite is higher than that of the PP fiber membrane. This phenomenon attracts negatively charged PM and polarizes their surfaces, such as PM0.5, thereby enhancing the filtering performance. The specific surface area was improved by the introduction of ZIF-8, which provides active sites for the adsorption of PM and results in a larger contact area within the inner hole, thereby improving the capture capability for PM0.5 and VOCs. Furthermore, the dominance of chemisorption is a critical factor for the removal of VOCs in complex environments. The abundant functional groups of the PP/ZIF-8 composite can effectively capture VOCs in complex flue gas through hydrogen bonding and π–π interactions, as analyzed by FTIR and XPS. Overall, PP/ZIF-8 demonstrates significant potential for high-efficiency particle removal with low resistance in complex environments.

Conclusions

The PP/ZIF-8 membrane was developed to effectively remove PM from the complex flue gas. A significant number of nanoparticles, characterized by a substantial presence of rhombic dodecahedral crystals, were observed on the surface of the PP membrane. The removal efficiency was significantly enhanced, achieving filtration efficiencies of 84.6%, 92.6%, 93.5%, 95.8%, and 96.9% for PM0.5, PM1.0, PM2.5, PM5, and PM10 for PP/ZIF-8. Additionally, the pressure significantly decreased from 0.19 to 0.07 kPa. The stability performance improved after the introduction of ZIF-8. The adsorption capacity of PP/ZIF-8 for benzene VOCs was higher than that of PP, and the actual adsorption process for benzene could be better described by the pseudo-first-order kinetic model. The fulvic acid and high-molecular-weight humic substances were significantly decreased in the exhaust gas, and nitrogen and carbon-containing pollutants were prioritized for removal from flue gas for VOCs. The imbalance of polar functional groups formed by the metal and organic components, along with the exposure of uncovered metal active adsorption sites within the porous structure, plays a key role in the removal of PM and VOCs. This study provides an effective solution and a theoretical foundation for the development of commercial air filters and air pollution protection.

Tenecteplase versus standard care in patients with acute basilar artery occlusion: a multi-centre, prospective, randomised, open-label, blinded endpoint, phase 3, controlled trial
Authors: Yunyun Xiong, Fana Alemseged, Zhixin Cao, Lee H Schwamm, Mark Parsons, Marc Fisher, Shuangzhe Wu, Bruce C V Campbell, Yongjun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and purposeThere is a scarcity of evidence of tenecteplase administered within the 24-hour window of acute ischaemic stroke due to basilar artery occlusion (BAO). We sought to assess whether intravenous tenecteplase within 24 hours of stroke onset, with or without endovascular treatment (EVT), leads to superior outcomes compared with standard care in acute BAO.Methods and designTenecteplase Reperfusion therapy in Acute ischaemic Cerebrovascular Events-5 (TRACE-5), using a prospective, randomised, open-label, blinded-endpoint design, will enrol patients with acute ischaemic stroke due to BAO within 24 hours of the time they were last known well. Patients will be randomised to either intravenous tenecteplase (0.25 mg/kg, maximum 25 mg) or standard care. EVT is allowed in both groups.Study outcomesModified Rankin Scale (mRS) of 0–1 or return to baseline mRS at 90 days is the primary outcome. Secondary outcomes include mRS 0–2 or return to baseline, mRS 0–3 and mRS distribution at 90 days, early neurological improvement within 72 hours and substantial reperfusion at initial angiogram. Safety outcomes are symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage within 36 hours, death within 90 days and mRS 5–6 at 90 days.DiscussionThe TRACE-5 trial will address whether extended-time window thrombolysis with a potentially more effective thrombolytic agent tenecteplase±EVT is superior to standard care in BAO.Trial registration numberNCT06196320.

Introduction

Basilar artery occlusion (BAO) comprises ~1% of all strokes but is one of the most devastating neurological conditions, with high rates of mortality and disability, up to 80–90% if recanalisation does not occur.1 2 The current American Heart Association/American Stroke Association guidelines recommend intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) within 4.5 hours for eligible patients (Class I; Level of Evidence A) and consider endovascular therapy (EVT) to be reasonable in select BAO patients within the first 6 hours of stroke onset (Class IIb; Level of Evidence C).3 Four recent randomised controlled trials assessing the efficacy of EVT compared with standard treatment in the early and extended time windows for vertebrobasilar artery occlusion ischaemic stroke have reported inconsistent results.47 The subsequent Vertebrobasilar Occlusion Randomisation to Endovascular Reperfusion versus Intravenous Thrombolysis or Medical Treatment Alone Systematic Evaluation (VERITAS) individual patient data meta-analysis of these four trials demonstrated a robust benefit of EVT in BAO patients presenting with moderate to severe symptoms, with approximately a 2.5-fold increased likelihood of achieving a favourable functional outcome.8 However, immediate access to EVT is not always available because of insufficient resources, technical limitations and high costs.9 10 Therefore, more evidence is needed to assess whether intravenous thrombolysis can be extended to 24 hours in BAO.

Tenecteplase is a new thrombolytic drug that is given as a single bolus injection. Tenecteplase has been proven to be non-inferior to alteplase in randomised clinical trials,1115 and subsequent study level meta-analysis showed superiority of tenecteplase over alteplase in stroke patients within 4.5 hours of symptom onset.16 In patients with large-vessel occlusion (LVO) of the anterior circulation, our Tenecteplase Reperfusion Therapy in Acute Ischaemic Cerebrovascular Events-III (TRACE-III) trial has demonstrated tenecteplase is beneficial for patients with salvageable tissue who could not access thrombectomy up to 24 hours after symptom onset.17 Previous observational studies have shown that intravenous thrombolysis beyond 4.5 hours may benefit BAO patients with an acceptable risk of haemorrhagic complications.18 19 In this context, tenecteplase, which has been found to be associated with higher reperfusion rates compared with alteplase in BAO,20 may provide further benefit for BAO patients beyond the 4.5-hour time window. The Extending the Time Window for Thrombolysis in Posterior Circulation Stroke without Early CT Signs (EXPECTS) trial showed that alteplase administered 4.5 to 24 hours after stroke onset could benefit patients with posterior circulation stroke.21 However, the trial population primarily included patients with mild stroke, as BAO was not required to be eligible for the trial and patients with planned thrombectomy were excluded. Therefore, whether extended-time window thrombolysis with tenecteplase, with or without EVT, may further improve outcomes in BAO remains unknown.

The Tenecteplase Reperfusion therapy in Acute ischaemic Cerebrovascular Events-5 (TRACE-5) trial aims to investigate whether intravenous tenecteplase is superior to standard care for BAO within a 24-hour time window.

Methods

Study organisation and design

We used a prospective, open-label, blinded-endpoint (PROBE) design. The outcome assessors are blinded to the drug allocation. The TRACE-5 trial will be conducted at 66 stroke centres in China.

Study population

TRACE-5 will enrol adult patients with acute ischaemic stroke due to BAO who are eligible for thrombolysis within 24 hours of stroke onset or the time they were last known to be well. BAO is defined as occlusion or near-occlusion of any segment of the basilar artery on CT angiography (CTA) or magnetic resonance angiography (MRA). In patients presenting with mild fluctuating or stuttering symptoms followed by sudden onset of clinical deterioration, the time of deterioration is considered as the estimated time of culprit occlusion. Patients with intracerebral haemorrhage, extensive ischaemic changes (defined as Posterior circulation Acute Stroke Prognosis Early CT (pc-ASPECTS)22 score <6), significant cerebellar mass effect, acute hydrocephalus, evident hypodensity on non-contrast CT (NCCT) or bilateral extensive brainstem ischaemia will be excluded based on baseline imaging. Randomisation and blinding

Patients will be randomised to receive tenecteplase or standard care in a 1:1 ratio using CIMS-CRS, the Central Randomisation Management System, a secure good clinical practice (GCP)-compliant electronic web-based server that reliably ensures allocation concealment. Randomisation of patients will be stratified by the investigator’s intention for thrombectomy and intention for intravenous alteplase (if in the control group). Then covariate-adjusted randomisation with age (≤70 vs >70 years), baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS, <10 vs ≥10) and time from onset-to-randomisation (0–6 hours vs 6–24 hours) as control variables will be applied.

Treatment assignment

Intravenous tenecteplase (0.25 mg/kg, max 25 mg) will be administered as a bolus over 5 s. China Shijiazhuang Pharmaceutical Company Recomgen Pharmaceutical (Guangzhou) provided tenecteplase and an unrestricted grant to support trial infrastructure but did not participate in the design or conduct of the trial or the drafting of the manuscript. In the control group, patients may receive anticoagulation such as heparin infusion or antiplatelets as per standard care and alteplase if eligible (0.9 mg/kg, maximum 90 mg) within 4.5 hours of stroke onset. Patients in the two arms will be allowed to receive EVT after digital subtraction angiography, and EVT is at the discretion of the neurointerventionist. Other medical treatment for EVT patients conforms to the Chinese Guideline for Endovascular Treatment of Acute Ischemic Stroke 2023.

Assessments

Study visits (figure 2) will be conducted at the following time points: baseline, treatment administration, 24 hours, 7 days or discharge and 90 days. At baseline, eligibility will be assessed by NCCT+CTA/MRI+MRA. Digital subtraction angiography will be performed for patients eligible for EVT to evaluate reperfusion status, which will be centrally adjudicated using the expanded Thrombolysis In Cerebral Infarction (eTICI) scale.23 An NCCT brain or MRI will be performed at 24 hours postprocedure to evaluate haemorrhagic transformation and the degree of stroke progression. The modified Rankin Scale (mRS) at 90 days will be assessed via telephone or in person by regional coordinating centres to assess functional outcome, blinded to treatment group allocation. Outcomes

The primary outcome is mRS score 0–1 or return to baseline mRS (if baseline premorbid mRS=2–3) at 90 days. Safety outcomes include symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage (sICH) within 36 hours,24 all-cause mortality within 90 days and mRS 5–6 at 90 days. Figure 4 shows other secondary efficacy outcomes.

Study oversight

The central executive steering committees will monitor the overall trial implementation. The Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) comprised two vascular neurologists and an independent biostatistician. No interim analyses will be planned for efficacy or futility. The DSMB will monitor deaths within 90 days and sICH within 36 hours of intervention. We will use the Haybittle-Peto procedure for generating early stopping boundaries (p=0.001, Z=3). DSMB will consider a recommendation of early termination due to safety reasons at a given safety review being crossed. An electronic case report form will be used to record trial data, which will be managed securely by data managers at the Beijing Tiantan Hospital. Access will require training and password authentication. The protocol must receive written approval from the local institutional ethics committee before participant recruitment and enrolment begins.

Sample size

Estimates for the control group (21%) are based on the Acute Basilar Artery Occlusion (BASILAR) study25 (20.7% achieving mRS 0–1 at 3 months), with similar results in the Endovascular Treatment for Acute Basilar-Artery Occlusion (ATTENTION) and Basilar Artery Occlusion Chinese Endovascular (BAOCHE) trials.6 7 The treatment effect of tenecteplase, based on pooled phase II LVO data, showed a 19% improvement over alteplase.20 We assume a smaller treatment effect of 12% in TRACE-5. According to power 80%, one-sided alpha 0.025, attrition rate of 5%, the calculated sample size is 452.

Statistical analysis

The dichotomous efficacy and safety outcomes will be analysed using modified Poisson regression, with age, baseline NIHSS score and onset-to-randomisation time as covariates. For the day 90 mRS distribution, an assumption-free Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney Generalised OR analysis or an adjusted ordinal logistic regression model will be undertaken on the full range of the scale, according to whether the proportional odds assumption is satisfied. For the efficacy outcomes, the intention-to-treat approach will be used and also a per-protocol analysis. Missing data will be handled by the multiple imputation method.

Subgroup analyses include: age (≤70 vs >70 years), sex (female and male), baseline NIHSS (<10 vs ≥10), intention for intravenous alteplase (yes/no), intention for endovascular treatment (yes/no), time from randomisation to EVT (no EVT vs ≤60 min vs >60 min), time from stroke onset to randomisation (0–6 hours vs 6–24 hours), pre-morbid mRS (0–1 vs 2–3), glucose (<7.8 mmol/L vs ≥7.8 mmol/L), BAO site (distal, mid, proximal), stroke mechanism (cardioembolic, embolic stroke of undetermined source (ESUS), atherothrombotic), pc-ASPECTS (<8 vs ≥8 on baseline CTA-source images or non-contrast CT brain or MRI diffusion weighted imaging, Basilar Artery Tomography Angiography score (BATMAN)26 and Posterior Circulation Collateral Score (PC-CS)27 on baseline CTA. Imaging core lab will centrally evaluate the BAO site, pc-ASPECTS, BATMAN and PC-CS.

Analyses will be conducted with SAS software (V. 9.4; SAS Institute Inc.). p<0.05 will be considered as statistical significance, and 95% confidence intervals will be provided for treatment effect estimates. Secondary and exploratory analyses will be undertaken using appropriate regression models.

Discussion

TRACE-5 trial is a phase 3, randomised trial of tenecteplase within 24 hours after stroke onset due to BAO. The use of EVT is at the discretion of the treating physician. This design will address the efficacy and safety of tenecteplase compared with standard care in BAO in the extended time window.

The robust benefit of EVT for BAO within 24 hours of stroke onset has been supported by the BAOCHE trial6 and the subsequent VERITAS8 individual patient data meta-analysis. However, immediate access to EVT is currently limited9 10 and intravenous thrombolysis remains the foundational therapy for acute ischaemic stroke. Therefore, extending the time window for intravenous thrombolysis after BAO would allow treatment of substantially more patients who currently have a poor prognosis. Previous observational studies have shown that intravenous thrombolysis beyond 4.5 hours may benefit BAO patients with an acceptable risk of haemorrhagic complication.18 19 Of note, the risk of haemorrhagic transformation in posterior circulation stroke has been found to be lower than in anterior circulation stroke, likely due to a stronger tolerance to the ischaemic insult.28 The EXPECTS trial has extended the IVT time window to 24 hours after onset in patients with posterior circulation stroke.21 However, the trial population included patients with mild stroke and no planned thrombectomy; hence, only 16 BAO patients were randomised into the trial. Therefore, evidence for IVT, with or without EVT, within 24 hours for BAO patients is still limited.

Despite proven efficacy, alteplase only achieves its aim of rapidly reperfusing the ischaemic brain tissue within 2 hours in ~30% of cases overall and only ~4% for basilar artery.29 30 Compared with alteplase, tenecteplase has superior pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic properties and is associated with a higher rate of reperfusion prior to EVT in BAO.20 Importantly, tenecteplase may work in synergy with thrombectomy, resulting in faster and more complete reperfusion or obviating the need for thrombectomy. This may translate into improved functional outcomes and offer substantial benefits in BAO patients. We have chosen mRS 0–1 or return to baseline function, as the primary outcome given precedent in prior thrombolytic trials. Additionally, a study-level meta-analysis of 11 randomised controlled trials demonstrated the superiority of tenecteplase over alteplase, with the strongest signal observed at mRS 0–1.16

Several ongoing clinical trials are investigating intravenous tenecteplase for BAO in the extended time window. As a parallel trial, the POST-ETERNAL trial (NCT05105633) shares a common protocol and data elements with our trial with planned pooled analysis on completion of both trials. The ATTENTION LATE trial (NCT05701956) is focusing on the effect of intravenous tenecteplase bridging EVT compared with EVT alone from 4.5 to 24 hours after symptom onset. BAOCHE-2 (NCT06560203) is targeting EVT for BAO between 24–72 hours from symptom onset. compared with the ongoing trials, we specifically focus on tenecteplase in BAO patients within 0–24 hours of onset, with or without EVT, forming a complementary trial to our TRACE-III trial of tenecteplase in the late time window for patients with anterior circulation large vessel occlusion.

Our trial has several limitations. First, we chose a PROBE design and will rely on blinded outcome assessors which could introduce bias. Second, the trial is performed exclusively in Chinese patients. Third, we restrict enrolment to patients who do not have large infarcts in key posterior circulation anatomical regions, so the generalisability of our trial results to other populations warrants further investigation.

In conclusion, TRACE-5 will test the safety and efficacy of tenecteplase administered within 0–24 hours after symptom onset in BAO patients compared with current best practice. It may provide evidence supporting the use of tenecteplase with or without EVT in the extended time window, potentially enabling more BAO patients to benefit from IVT.

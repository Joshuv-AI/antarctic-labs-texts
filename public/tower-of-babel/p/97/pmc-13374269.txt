Noninvasive prediction of Ki67 proliferation index in breast cancer based on integrated photoacoustic imaging
Authors: Sijie Mo, Mengyun Wang, Guoqiu Li, Hongtian Tian, Huaiyu Wu, Shuzhen Tang, Xiaohan Zou, Mengna Shao, Jinfeng Xu, Zhibin Huang, Fajin Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPredicting Ki67 expression is crucial for understanding tumor proliferation and guiding personalized breast cancer treatment. Non-invasive methods remain limited, underscoring the need for innovative imaging-based approaches to enhance molecular subtyping and clinical decisions.PurposeThis study aimed to develop a predictive model integrating photoacoustic/ultrasound (PA/US) imaging data and clinical variables to differentiate high and low Ki67 expression levels in breast cancer. It sought to identify imaging and clinical factors associated with Ki67 expression, contributing to the molecular subtyping of breast cancer.Methods and materialsIn this study, 336 breast tumors were analyzed and divided into high Ki67 expression (≥14%) and low Ki67 expression (<14%) groups. The samples were randomly split into training and test sets at a 7:3 ratio. Statistical methods included t‑tests and rank‑sum tests, with independent predictors identified through univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses. Four predictive models were developed: Model A (clinical factors), Model B (clinical factors combined with ultrasound features), Model C (combining clinical, ultrasound, and photoacoustic oxygen saturation [PA‑SO₂]), and Model D (clinical factors combined with PA‑SO₂).ResultsUsing univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis, four independent predictive factors were identified: histological grade, axillary lymph node status (ALN), intratumoral color Doppler flow imaging (Inter CDFI), and PA‑SO₂. Based on these factors, four logistic regression models were constructed for predicting high vs. low Ki67 expression: Model A (clinical factors only): histological grade + ALN; Model B (clinical + ultrasound): Model A + Inter CDFI; Model C (comprehensive model): Model B + PA‑SO₂; Model D (comprehensive model): Model A + PA‑SO₂. In the test set, the areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUCs) with 95% confidence intervals were as follows: Model A, 0.781 (0.696-0.866); Model B, 0.779 (0.691-0.867); Model C, 0.823 (0.739-0.908), and Model D, 0.807 (0.721-0.892). Model C demonstrated the highest diagnostic efficiency for distinguishing Ki67 expression levels.ConclusionThis study developed a predictive model incorporating histological grade, ALN, Inter CDFI, and PA‑SO₂ to estimate Ki67 expression levels in breast cancer. This model provides a valuable tool for early prognosis, aiding molecular classification and facilitating the prompt initiation of personalized treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02462-5.Keywords: Breast Cancer, Ki67, Photoacoustic

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) remains one of the most prevalent malignancies worldwide [1]. The introduction of molecular subtyping based on immunohistochemistry (IHC) has significantly refined treatment strategies and enabled more personalized management. In addition to conventional therapies such as surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, neoadjuvant combination therapy has become the standard of care, particularly for patients with HER2-positive and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) [1]. Molecular subtyping now plays a pivotal role in guiding neoadjuvant chemotherapy strategies, enabling more personalized and effective treatment approaches. According to the 2013 St. Gallen International Expert Consensus, BC is categorized into four subtypes based on ER, PR, HER2, and Ki67 expression: Luminal A (LA), Luminal B (LB), HER2-enriched (HER2+), and Triple-Negative BC (TNBC) [2]. Among these, Ki67 is a critical factor in distinguishing between LA and LB subtypes [3, 4]. Research has shown that Ki67, as a cell cycle-specific antigen in BC, is closely associated with tumor aggressiveness and recurrence risk, particularly in ER-positive and HER2-negative BC [5]. Furthermore, Ki67 expression serves as a predictive marker for chemotherapy, with changes in its levels after short-term endocrine therapy providing predictive value for disease-free survival [3, 6]. Tumor proliferative activity is a key determinant of BC prognosis. Ki67 serves as a well‑established surrogate marker of this proliferation. Therefore, evaluating Ki67 expression is crucial for early personalized treatment and postoperative recurrence risk assessment in BC.

In clinical practice, Ki67 is commonly evaluated through core needle biopsy or postoperative pathology [7]. However, for breast biopsy specimens, pathologists typically rely on a small tissue sample to determine the molecular subtype of BC, which inherently involves a certain degree of subjectivity [8]. Studies have shown that, due to the highly heterogeneous nature of BC, there are notable differences in immunohistochemical results between core needle biopsy and postoperative pathology [9, 10]. Previous studies have reported that tumor heterogeneity may lead to inconsistencies in Ki67 expression between biopsy and postoperative specimens [10]. This potential discordance highlights the need for a non‑invasive technique that can objectively and quantitatively assess Ki67 expression in BC, avoiding the limitations of tissue sampling, minimizing subjectivity and addressing the inconsistencies observed between biopsy and postoperative specimens. Such a method would provide more reliable data for accurate molecular subtyping and better-informed treatment decisions.

Ultrasound is the most commonly used method for breast tumor screening in Asia due to its high efficiency, real-time imaging, non-invasive nature, and lack of radiation exposure [11]. However, studies have shown that ultrasound has limited accuracy in distinguishing between benign and malignant breast tumors [12]. Additionally, it provides insufficient information for molecular typing of BC [13]. Despite numerous studies focusing on the clinical information and ultrasound imaging of BC, the exploration and application of functional information for molecular subtypes of BC remain in the early stages. To address these limitations, a more advanced form of functional imaging is needed, one that can provide detailed insights into tumor biology and offer valuable molecular typing information from an imaging perspective, potentially improving diagnosis and treatment planning.

Photoacoustic imaging offers a unique advantage by capturing hemoglobin distribution in blood vessels and visualizing the microvasculature within breast tumors [14, 15]. This technique enables the quantitative analysis of hemoglobin levels, including the measurement of oxygen saturation (SO2) in tumor regions, thus providing functional imaging data [16, 17]. Ultimately, this approach facilitates a more comprehensive assessment of BC by integrating functional and molecular imaging, offering clinicians potentially valuable insights for diagnosis and treatment planning.

The biological rationale for correlating oxygen saturation (So₂) with Ki67 expression is rooted in the well‑established relationship between tumor hypoxia and aggressive cancer behavior. Hypoxia‑inducible factors (HIFs) are upregulated under low oxygen conditions and promote the transcription of multiple genes involved in angiogenesis, glycolysis, and cell proliferation, including Ki67 [18]. At the same time, hypoxic tumors exhibit reduced oxygen saturation, which can be quantified by photoacoustic imaging (PAI) using wavelength pairs sensitive to oxyhemoglobin and deoxyhemoglobin. We therefore hypothesized that SO₂ measured by PAI would be inversely correlated with Ki67 expression levels in breast cancer, and that incorporating So₂ into a predictive model alongside clinical and ultrasound variables could improve the estimation of Ki67 status based on postoperative pathology.

Therefore, the primary objective of this study was to develop and validate an integrated predictive model combining PA/US imaging features and clinical variables to predict high vs. low Ki67 expression levels in BC. To achieve this, we compared four modeling strategies: a clinical-only model (Model A), a clinical + ultrasound model (Model B), a combined clinical + ultrasound + PA‑SO₂ model (Model C), and combined clinical + PA‑SO₂ model (Model D), in order to identify the optimal approach for non-invasive Ki67 prediction. A secondary objective was to identify which specific PA/US imaging features and clinical factors are independently associated with Ki67 expression levels, thereby providing interpretable biomarkers that may contribute to molecular subtyping of BC.

Materials and methods

This study consecutively enrolled 336 patients diagnosed with BC at the investigator’s institution between 2022 and 2024. All participants underwent PA/US imaging at the time of prior to surgery, as well as postoperative pathological examination. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Shenzhen People’s Hospital (approval number SYL-202161-02) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants were fully informed about the study procedures and provided written informed consent before enrollment.

Participants population

The 336 patients were randomly split into training and test sets at a 7:3 ratio. The exclusion criteria for this study included: (a) participants lacking complete clinical data, such as Ki67 index and other IHC pathology results; (b) participants who received neoadjuvant therapy prior to ultrasound examination; (c) participants with suboptimal quality of photoacoustic ultrasound imaging; and (d) participants diagnosed with malignancies other than BC. Photoacoustic/ultrasound imaging

Operators with over 10 years of experience in breast nodule US performed pre-treatment US examinations on all participants. Under examination conditions of 20–25 °C ambient temperature and 50–70% humidity, participants were fully exposed to both breasts and axillae, with their arms abducted. The operators conducted grayscale US scans of the breast tumors, recording parameters such as maximum diameter, location, shape, calcification, orientation, inter CDFI, and external CDFI. Additionally, based on the PA laser signal from the Mindray US instrument, the L9-3 probe (center frequency 5.5 MHz) was used to scan the tumors and emit laser signals for PAI. This ensured that the images were stable and clear, allowing for quick access to the PA images, where the operator delineated the region of interest (ROI) of the tumor and recorded the SO2 value. The study utilized photoacoustic laser imaging at wavelengths of 750 nm and 830 nm, which exhibit higher absorption rates for deoxyhemoglobin and oxyhemoglobin [19].

Clinical variables and pathological analyses

Study selection was guided by clinical guidelines and the expertise of oncologists and ultrasonographers. The variables chosen for analysis included both clinical and pathological factors: gender, age, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), menopause status, family history of BC, and biomarkers such as HER2, ER, PR, and Ki67. Postoperative specimens of BC from all participants were pathologically tested and analyzed through immunohistochemistry (IHC) at the investigator’s institution, with all IHC scores evaluated by a professional BC pathologist.

Tissue specimens were formalin‑fixed and paraffin‑embedded (FFPE). Immunohistochemistry was performed using an automated stainer. Antigen retrieval was carried out in citrate buffer (pH 6.0) using a heat‑induced method. Primary antibodies against ER, PR, and Ki67 were commercially obtained; for HER2, the antibody clone and dilution followed the manufacturer’s instructions. Bound antibodies were visualized using a horseradish peroxidase‑conjugated secondary antibody and diaminobenzidine (DAB) as the chromogen, followed by hematoxylin counterstaining. Whole slide images were scanned with a digital scanner. A dedicated breast pathologist, blinded to clinical data, manually evaluated the entire slide area without the use of automated image analysis algorithms.

Although the International Ki67 in Breast Cancer Working Group (IKWG) has recommended more conservative thresholds (≤ 5% and ≥ 30%) to account for inter-laboratory variability [20], the 14% cutoff remains widely used for molecular subtyping based on the St. Gallen consensus. In this study, we adopted the 14% threshold for consistency with the St. Gallen classification framework, which uses Ki67 expression to distinguish luminal subtypes [21]. HER2 expression scoring followed the 2018 American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) and College of American Pathologists (CAP) guidelines for HER2 detection in BC [22]. According to these guidelines, HER2 IHC scoring was defined as follows: IHC 0, no staining or incomplete, faint membrane staining in ≤ 10% of invasive tumor cells; IHC 1+, incomplete, faint membrane staining in > 10% of invasive tumor cells; IHC 2+ (equivocal), weak to moderate complete membrane staining in > 10% of tumor cells, requiring reflex Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization (FISH) test; and IHC 3+ (positive), intense, complete circumferential membrane staining in > 10% of tumor cells. Based on these criteria, HER2 status was classified as negative (IHC 0), low expression (IHC 1+, or IHC 2 + with negative FISH), and positive (IHC 3+, or IHC 2 + with positive FISH). Additionally, estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) positivity were determined based on ASCO/CAP guidelines, defined by immunohistochemical staining of ≥ 1% of tumor cells, while other receptor statuses were not considered [23, 24].

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed in this study using R software version 4.2.2. Continuous variables are presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), while categorical variables are expressed as counts and percentages. All statistical tests were two-sided, with a significance threshold set at P < 0.05. For the analysis of clinical predictors, participants were categorized into high-expression (≥ 14%) and low-expression (< 14%) groups based on the Ki67 index. Univariate logistic regression analysis was conducted using the “glm (generalized linear model)” function, followed by inclusion of significant variables (p < 0.05) in a multivariate logistic regression model to construct the final model.

Several key statistical tools were utilized to evaluate model performance in this study, including receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves generated by the “proc” package and “boot” packages, calibration curves produced with the “rms” package, and clinical decision curves analysis(DCA) created using the “rmda” package, threshold probabilities were varied from 0 to 1, and the net benefit was calculated as the difference between true positive and weighted false positive classifications. The net benefit of each model was compared with two extreme scenarios: “treat none” (net benefit = 0) and “treat all”. The range of threshold probabilities where a model yielded the highest net benefit was identified as the optimal clinical application range. These analyses assessed model discrimination, calibration, clinical utility, and clinical impact, respectively. Finally, to evaluate the robustness of the final combined model, we performed three internal validation methods: 10‑fold cross‑validation (10‑fold CV), leave‑one‑out cross‑validation (LOOCV), and bootstrap resampling with 1000 iterations. For 10‑fold CV, the data were randomly partitioned into 10 equal‑sized subsets; the model was trained on 9 subsets and tested on the remaining one, repeated 10 times. LOOCV iteratively left out one sample as the test set. Bootstrap resampling generated 1000 bootstrap samples by sampling with replacement from the original data; the model was trained on each bootstrap sample and tested on the original data to obtain a bias‑corrected AUC.

Univariate and multivariate analyses of variables

In this study, all variables were subjected to univariate logistic regression analysis. The results indicated that hormone receptor status (P < 0.001), histological grade (P < 0.001), and ALN (P < 0.001) were significantly associated with high and low Ki67 expression levels. Statistically significant differences were also found in ultrasound variables such as grayscale echo pattern(P = 0.017), inter-CDFI(P = 0.019), lesion’s SO2(P < 0.001) all of which were associated with Ki67 expression levels. In contrast, variables including menopause status, history of BC, lesion location, shape, maximum diameter, posterior features, orientation, calcification, and external-CDFI showed no significant statistical differences. Multivariate logistic regression analysis further screened independent predictors of Ki67 expression based on the univariate analysis results. Ultimately, the study found that histological grade (P < 0.001), ALN status (P = 0.001), and inter-CDFI (P = 0.007) had a very high predictive value for Ki67 expression, as detailed in Table 2. Notably, PA-SO₂ was independently associated with Ki67 expression, with an odds ratio of 0.774 (95% CI: 0.703–0.838, P < 0.001), indicating a significant negative correlation (higher SO₂ linked to lower Ki67).

The model performance

Through multivariate regression analysis, four predictive factors were identified: histological grade and ALN as clinical pathological factors, Inter CDFI as an ultrasound variable, and SO2 as a PA variable. Based on these factors, this study developed four models: the clinical model, the combined clinical and PA-SO₂ model, the comprehensive model incorporating clinical, ultrasound, and PA-SO₂; and the combined clinical and PA-SO₂ model. The predictive performance and evaluation of each model are presented in Table 3. In the test set, the Clinical + Ultrasound + PA‑SO₂ model achieved an AUC of 0.823, surpassing the clinical model, the Clinical + Ultrasound model, and Clinical + PA-SO₂ model, which had AUCs of 0.781, 0.779, and 0.803, respectively. Additionally, the clinical + PA-SO₂ model exhibited the highest specificity (0.947), while the comprehensive model demonstrated the highest accuracy and sensitivity, at 0.722 and 0.714, respectively.

To further assess robustness, we performed internal validation using the entire dataset. Ten‑fold cross‑validation yielded a mean AUC of 0.768 (SD = 0.086); leave‑one‑out cross‑validation produced an AUC of 0.774 (95% CI: 0.725–0.823); and bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) gave a bias‑corrected AUC of 0.781 (95% CI: 0.771–0.786). These values were slightly lower than the test set AUC of 0.823 but remained within a reasonable range, indicating that the model does not suffer from severe overfitting and maintains acceptable generalizability.

To assess the individual discriminative ability of PA‑SO₂ and Inter CDFI, we evaluated their standalone performance in the test set (Appendix Table S2). PA‑SO₂ alone yielded an AUC of 0.594 (95% CI: 0.472–0.715), sensitivity 34.9%, specificity 68.4%; Inter CDFI alone gave an AUC of 0.505 (95% CI: 0.394–0.617), sensitivity 57.1%, specificity 44.5%. Although each parameter showed limited discriminative ability alone, their combination with clinical variables in the comprehensive model (Mod C) substantially improved performance (test set AUC = 0.823).

Discussion

The expression levels of the Ki67 index have become critical for formulating personalized treatment plans and assessing the prognosis of BC [3, 25, 26]. Studies indicated that Ki67 expression increases sequentially from benign breast tumors to ductal carcinoma in situ and then to invasive BC, which is associated with the occurrence, progression, and metastasis of breast tumors [25]. However, Ki67 IHC is known to suffer from considerable inter‑laboratory and inter‑observer variability [27–30], and Ki67 expression levels can differ between biopsy and corresponding surgical specimens [31]. These factors underscore the need for a reproducible method to assess Ki67 expression.

Furthermore, conventional grayscale US provides mainly morphological information. Although it cannot directly measure Ki67 protein levels, certain US features (e.g., inter CDFI) have been associated with tumor proliferation [25, 32]. PA imaging adds functional parameters such as SO₂, which reflects tissue hypoxia and angiogenesis, features often linked to high proliferative activity [33]. Therefore, combining PA‑SO₂ with clinical and US variables may improve the prediction of Ki67 expression levels. In this study, we constructed a Ki67 index prediction model using clinical, ultrasound, and PA‑SO₂ variables. The results demonstrated that four variables, histological grade, ALN, Inter CDFI, and PA‑SO₂, were highly correlated with Ki67 expression levels. Based on these variables, we developed the prediction model (Mod C), which achieved an AUC of 0.903 for the training set and 0.823 for the test set, significantly outperforming the clinical model (Mod A, test set AUC = 0.781), the combined clinical and ultrasound model (Mod B, test set AUC = 0.779), and combined clinical and PASO2 model (Mod D, test set AUC = 0.807). Therefore, with its advantages of non-invasiveness and reproducibility, the prediction model developed in this study may serve as an effective tool for assisting in the prediction of high and low Ki67 expression in the future.

Kosho Yamanouchi et al. and other studies found that high expression levels of Ki67 are predictive of BC metastasis and recurrence, particularly in Luminal B-type BC without lymph node metastasis [34–36]. These highlighted the significance of LN status as a key correlate of Ki67 expression, which finding consistent with the results of the present study. Our analysis revealed a strong correlation between ALN involvement and Ki67 levels, with p values less than or equal to 0.001 in both univariate and multivariate analyses, further emphasizing the relationship between ALN status and Ki67 expression. In normal breast tissue, Ki67 expression is nearly absent, whereas it is markedly elevated in malignant, proliferating breast cancer cells [34]. This elevation is not uniform across all tumor cells; rather, primary breast cancers are characterized by intratumoral heterogeneity, containing subclones with varying proliferative capacities [34, 37]. Among these, invasive subclones typically exhibit higher proliferation rates and consequently higher Ki67 expression. We hypothesize that the overall Ki67 level measured in a tumor reflects the contribution of these highly proliferative, invasive subclones, which may also drive metastasis and disease progression. Specifically, highly proliferative Ki67 expressing cancer cells may escape from the primary tumor and metastasize to the axillary lymph nodes. This suggests that Ki67 is not only an important biomarker for assessing BC proliferation but also a critical indicator for evaluating metastasis and overall prognosis in BC patients [38, 39].

In the present study, univariate and multivariate analyses showed that compared with histological grade 1, grade 2 (multivariate OR = 7.523, P = 0.002) and grade 3 (multivariate OR = 11.07, P = 0.001) were associated with a progressively higher risk of high Ki67 expression. This dose‑response relationship is consistent with previous researches [40, 41] and supports the biological principle that higher tumor grade reflects increased proliferative activity. In a cohort of 127 BC patients, Giridhar. demonstrated a significant positive correlation between Ki67 index and histological grade (P = 0.0001). The biological basis of this association is well established: by definition, poorly differentiated tumors exhibit higher proliferative activity, greater genomic instability and more aggressive clinical behavior. The elevated Ki67 expression observed in high-grade tumors reflects the rapid turnover of malignant cells, which is a hallmark of tumor progression. Therefore, the ability of histological grade to predict Ki67 expression levels may help clinicians identify patients who are more likely to have a high Ki67 index, thereby informing decisions on further molecular test or treatment planning.

In this study, the degree of internal blood flow observed on ultrasound was identified as an independent predictor of Ki67 expression. Specifically, hypervascular blood flow detected was positively correlated with Ki67 expression, meaning that richer blood flow within the BC lesion was associated with a higher likelihood of elevated Ki67 expression. This finding is consistent with other studies suggesting a relationship between Ki67 expression and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) [25], VEGF promotes vascular proliferation, increases vascular permeability, and may help meet the “nutritional” demands of breast tumor cells [42]. Furthermore, other research has shown that high Ki67 expression is linked to increased vascular density within the tumor, which is reflected in the enhanced blood flow seen on CDFI ultrasound [43]. Although our finding regarding intratumoral blood flow is consistent with several previous reports, the overall relationship between conventional ultrasound features (e.g., lesion shape, margin, echo pattern, and calcification) and Ki67 expression in breast cancer remains incompletely characterized. Therefore, further large‑scale, prospective studies are needed to clarify these relationships.

In this study, PA‑SO₂ is identified as an independent predictive factor of Ki67 expression in BC, showing a negative correlation between high Ki67 expression and SO2. Higher SO2 values indicate a greater likelihood of low Ki67 expression (< 14%). Previous studies have shown that Ki67 is a proliferation marker in BC, with high Ki67 levels often associated with more aggressive behavior and poorer prognosis [44–46]. The negative correlation between high Ki67 expression and SO₂ can be explained by tumor hypoxia, a hallmark of aggressive and metastatic cancers [47, 48]. Tumor hypoxia occurs when rapid proliferation of cancer cells exceeds the available oxygen supply, leading to a reduction in oxygen saturation. This hypoxic environment triggers a series of molecular events, including the upregulation of hypoxia-inducible factors (HIF), which promote angiogenesis, invasion, and anti-apoptotic processes [49]. Although increased angiogenesis may temporarily relieve oxygen deficiency, it often results in vascular dysfunction, further exacerbating hypoxia and tumor aggressiveness [50, 51].

Recent studies have also indicated that the peritumoral tissue in BC contains substantial biological information related to tumor characteristics, such as angiogenic factors and lymphovascular invasion [52]. Notably, photoacoustic imaging leverages the differences in tissue absorption of different laser wavelengths. Its spectroscopic capability enables the extraction of functional information, such as oxygen saturation, while the acoustic signal simultaneously provides structural information. This technology sensitively detects tumor oxygen saturation levels, which reflect these underlying biological processes. These findings suggest that SO2 measured by PA imaging could be a useful adjunct to clinical and ultrasound variables for assessing Ki67 expression, although further validation in larger cohorts is warranted.

While the prediction model constructed in this study shows significant potential for Ki67 level differentiation, some limitations are undeniable. First, as this is a single-center study, further multicenter studies are necessary to validate the model’s effectiveness. Second, although the sample size in this study is reasonably adequate, the limitations in patient environment and ethnicity constrain the generalizability of the findings. Ki67 was assessed only on surgical specimens; paired biopsy data were unavailable, precluding direct evaluation of biopsy‑to‑surgery discordance. Our rationale relied on literature [10], not on a direct comparison. Future studies should include both biopsy and surgical Ki67 measurements. In addition, PA imaging measures bulk‑tissue SO2, which may be biased by intratumoral heterogeneity. Overrepresentation of well‑oxygenated regions could overestimate SO2 and mask hypoxic areas linked to high Ki67 expression, potentially weakening their correlation. Higher‑resolution or multi‑region PA imaging should address this issue in future work. Furthermore, PAI provided whole‑tumor mean SO₂ values; therefore, spatial coregistration with Ki67 immunohistochemistry sections was not feasible. Future prospective studies are needed to explore this relationship at the spatial level. Finally, Ki67 is a subtype‑stratification marker, and photoacoustic imaging infers proliferative activity via SO₂ rather than directly measuring Ki67. Therefore, the observed association reflects tumor biology, not a direct protein‑imaging correlation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we constructed a predictive model based on histological grade, ALN, Inter CDFI, and PA‑SO₂ to estimate Ki67 expression levels. This model offers an early prognostic tool for BC patients, serving as a novel adjunct in the molecular classification process and supporting the timely implementation of personalized treatment strategies.

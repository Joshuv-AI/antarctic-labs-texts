Plasma pancreatic stone protein is a promising biomarker for prognosis and severity outcome in sepsis patients with COVID-19
Authors: Hozeifa Mohamed Hassan, Rongzhi Liu, Yuhang Lv, Yanyun Ruan, Shaoyin Bao, Yulin Fu, Shuanshuan Liu, Lin Wang, Tianjun Pan, Qi Chen, Xueping Lou, Biao Huang, Zhencang Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSepsis and coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) are life-threatening clinical syndromes with high mortality rate. This study aimed to investigate pancreatic stone protein (PSP) role as potential biomarker for sepsis/COVID-19 prognostication. Herein, based on the novel amplified luminescent proximity homogeneous assay (AlphaLISA), the PSP plasma concentrations were determined, and the PSP diagnostic and prognostic values were characterized in a total of 285 patients with inflammatory disorders and 15 healthy volunteers. We found that plasma PSP protein was significantly overexpressed in patients with sepsis covid-positive (Sepsis+ve, n = 105) compared to non-sepsis covid-negative (Non-Sepsis-ve, n = 60), non-sepsis covid-positive (Non-Sepsis+ve, n = 75), sepsis covid-negative (Sepsis-ve, n = 45), and normal controls (NC, n = 15). PSP showed good prediction ability of Sepsis+ve short-term mortality, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.8368 and 0.8070 at 28 and 90 days, respectively. Significant correlations were seen between plasma PSP and SOFA score, urea, immune mediators and inflammatory cytokines. Risk stratification analysis indicated higher mortality risk for Sepsis+ve with plasma PSP level exceeding the optimal cut-off value (≥ 119 ng/mL). PSP might be sepsis/COVID-19 disease biomarker, and could provide clinicians with a potential target for improving diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.Keywords: Hyper-inflammation, Sepsis, COVID-19, PSP, Disease prognosis, Severity outcomeSubject terms: Biochemistry, Biological techniques, Immunology, Biomarkers, Diseases, Medical research, Risk factors

Introduction

Clinical conditions that associated with hyper-inflammation status, such as sepsis and coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), are considered major causes of hospital admission and deaths among hospitalized patients worldwide1,2. Sepsis, an ancient entity depicted by inflammatory storms and life-threatening organ dysfunction, is a major public health threat that could escalate to tissue damage, multi-organ failure and short-term mortality3,4. The global burden of sepsis is raising, as disease prevalence is estimated to exceed 48 million cases annually, with exponential positive correlation with age5,6, whereas sepsis survivors are at substantial risk for poor life quality, functional disability, and cognitive impairment7–9. In the era of COVID-19 outbreak, the severe respiratory infection triggered by the severe acute respiratory syndrome corona 2 (SARS-CoV-2) viruses, is prevailed among sepsis patients (particularly sepsis occurring in patients with concomitant COVID-19, herein termed sepsis/COVID-19), upsurging the complexities of diagnostic and therapeutic strategies, leading to poor prognosis and outcome10–12. Despite extensive research into sepsis biomarkers, significant prognostic limitations persist, particularly for sepsis/COVID-19. Thus, prospective measures for identifying early diagnostic biomarkers and prognostic stratification approaches of sepsis/COVID-19 are of utmost need.

Given the fact that gold standard diagnostic tests for early detection of sepsis are lacking, and the classical laboratory assessments and investigation measures added barriers for key interventions of early recognition and suitable management due to insufficient sensitivity and time consumption13,14. Being a hot topic for researchers, the development of pioneered technologies for the detection of disease biomarkers have been progressively recognized, and can offer novel insights into the essential pathophysiological process of sepsis15–17. In this context, the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, chemiluminescence immunoassay, time-resolved fluoroimmunoassay, and fluorescence immunochromatography assay have been extensively applied for biomarkers detection. However, the clinical utility of these methods is constrained by suboptimal sensitivity, specificity, and time-to-result. These limitations stem not from the immunoassay platforms per se but from inherent biomarker kinetics and cross-reactivities, leading to delayed or inaccurate risk assessment18–20. In the meantime, the amplified luminescent proximity homogeneous assay (AlphaLISA), a bead-based approach for detection of biomolecules, has been periodically reported to be highly sensitive, no-wash immunoassay with a rapid workflow, and suitable for the detection of various analytes including proteins, peptides, and small molecules21. Previously, Xiang et al. and colleagues reported that AlphaLISA technique is superior in terms of reliability, reproducibility and timely detection of the potential biomarkers associated with sepsis pathogenesis and disease outcome22.

The current diagnostic landscape for sepsis relies primarily on C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), and increasingly, presepsin23. While CRP and PCT are widely used, both exhibit important limitations, in which CRP lacks specificity due to elevation in non-infectious conditions (trauma, surgery, autoimmune disorders) that severely limits diagnostic specificity, and PCT has delayed kinetics and reduced prognostic reliability in viral or mixed infections24. These gaps underscore the continued need for novel biomarkers with improved specificity and prognostic accuracy.

In the search for reliable sepsis biomarkers, we previously emphasized the role of several omics-based targets, including ANXA3, S100A8, S100A9, MMP9 and lipoprotein, which have been associated with major alterations in certain inflammatory signaling pathways25–27. Recently, the influence of pancreatic stone protein (PSP) in sepsis prognosis gained ultimate perception, and showed superiority in comparison to conventional inflammation biomarkers, such as CRP and PCT, in terms of specificity and earlier diagnostic capability28–30. PSP, a subclass member of the Regenerating protein family, is a polymorphic lectin protein synthesized in human by the acinar cells of the pancreas and secreted in the duodenum, where it participates in the islet cell regeneration, diabetogenesis and pancreatic lithogenesis31. Moreover, PSP has been reported to interfere with IL-6 and other major inflammatory cytokines to promote tissue proliferation and regeneration following damage, thus playing an essential role in immune cells recruitments and inflammatory responses32. Increasing evidences correlated between the substantially elevated PSP concentrations and the hyper-inflammation syndromes, systemic stress conditions, and tissue damages33. Furthermore, the influence of PSP as diagnostic tool in the intensive care setting paved the way for its prospective clinical values34; however, the precise early diagnostic abilities of PSP as potential biomarker for sepsis/COVID-19 disease prognosis and outcome still remain to be elucidated.

Accordingly, we hypothesized that the blood PSP concentration profiles are positively correlated with the clinical features of the sepsis/COVID-19 disease progression and prediction of severity outcome. Consecutively, in this work we applied the novel AlphaLISA assay procedure to analyze PSP expression as novel diagnostic biomarker and predictor of short-term outcomes in a total of 285 patients with inflammatory conditions and 15 healthy normal controls. These findings contribute to new insights into how PSP is effective for hyper-inflammation disorders and provide a scientific basis for its potential clinical application.

Materials and methods

Study design

This is a single-center study, conducted at Taizhou Central Hospital (Taizhou University Hospital) between December 20th, 2023 and January 15th, 2024. A total of 285 patients suspected with inflammatory infections whom presented to the Internal Medicine Department (or subspecialties) were enrolled, and the blood samples were collected within 4 h from admission. Baseline clinical data, including demographic data, laboratory indicators, follow-up data (till May 1st, 2024) and short-term outcomes (28- and 90-day mortality) were prospectively collected at admission or from the electronic data capture system and case report forms. Healthy volunteers (n = 15) were enrolled as normal controls.

Patient allocations

Patients with clinically diagnosed infections, based on the clinical symptoms in combination with diagnostic tests such as pathogenic cultures, laboratory findings, and radiographic assessments, were divided into non-sepsis and sepsis group in accordance with the guidelines described by the Centers for Disease Control and International Sepsis Forum Consensus Conference in correlation with organ dysfunction using Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) scores35,36. Defined by these guidelines, sepsis patients were diagnosed and enrolled on the basis of proven clinical assessment for microbial infection combined with organ failure SOFA score ≥ 2 as previously described37,38. For COVID-19, diagnostic criteria were based on the 2020 World Health Organization interim guidelines39, and their confirmed positive RT-qPCR tests for SARS-COV-2 from throat-swab specimens. We excluded patients according to the following criteria: 1) younger than 18 years; 2) evidence of pregnancy; 3) concurrent carcinoma; 4) debilitating mental disease; and 5) inability to comply with the study protocol.

Infection diagnostic criteria

Infections were diagnosed using a standardized multidimensional protocol aligned with international clinical standards. Clinical suspicion required ≥ 2 systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) criteria: temperature > 38.0 °C or < 36.0 °C; heart rate > 90 bpm; respiratory rate > 20/min or PaCO2 < 32 mmHg; WBCs > 12 × 109/L, < 4 × 109/L, or > 10% immature bands. Laboratory confirmation required elevation beyond institutional reference ranges: CRP > 10 mg/L, PCT > 0.1 ng/ml, or IL-6 > 7 pg/ml. Microbiological evidence included: (a) positive cultures from sterile sites (blood, cerebrospinal fluid), (b) clinically significant isolates from non-sterile sites with correlating symptoms, or (c) positive molecular tests. Radiological confirmation required new imaging findings consistent with infection (such as pulmonary infiltrates). Final diagnoses were independently validated by two attending physicians.

Based on the above, the 285 patients with infections were further allocated into 4 sub-groups encompasses non-sepsis covid-negative (Non-Sepsis-ve, n = 60), non-sepsis covid-positive (Non-Sepsis+ve, n = 75), sepsis covid-negative (Sepsis-ve, n = 45), and sepsis covid-positive (Sepsis+ve, n = 105). The enrolment criteria for the healthy normal volunteers (NC, n = 15) were corresponded to their clinical reports with lack of infection, organ failure and presented with normal physical examination, and the overall enrollment and exclusion criteria are illustrated in Fig. 1.

AlphaLISA assay for plasma PSP concentration measurements

Following blood collection and plasma separation, the PSP protein concentrations were measured by the novel AlphaLISA assay method as previously described22. This homogeneous immunoassay quantifies absolute plasma PSP protein concentration through antibody-mediated capture of soluble PSP molecules. Briefly, the double-antibody donor and acceptor sandwich beads modified with aldehyde groups, which directly coupled to the anti-PSP antibodies for the rapid and accurate detection of plasma PSP levels, were loaded by 5 μl of PSP standard or plasma samples using 96-well plates. Both donor and acceptor beads coupled with the capture and detection antibody, respectively, were diluted with the blocking buffer. Following addition of the diluted beads, the 96-well plates were placed on an auto-shaker and incubated at 37 °C, and the AlphaLISA signal of each well was measured using Tecan M200 microplate reader (Tecan Trading AG, Myron, Switzerland) according to the manufacturer guidelines. Analytical performance of the PSP-AlphaLISA assay

The analytical performance of the PSP-AlphaLISA assay was rigorously validated according to established standards. Calibration curves demonstrated linearity over 5.6–1,000 ng/ml (Y = 309.7X – 527.1; R2 = 0.9961). The limit of detection (LOD), defined as the mean of ten blank plasma measurements plus two standard deviations, was 5.6 ng/ml. Meanwhile, the functional limit of quantitation (LOQ), established as the lowest concentration with an inter-assay coefficient of variation below 10%, was 5.6 ng/ml. Precision assessments at low (87 ng/ml), medium (214 ng/ml), and high (556 ng/ml) PSP concentrations yielded intra-assay coefficients of variation (CV) of 2.3–3.9% (n = 10) and inter-assay CVs of 4.0–8.0% (n = 30 over three days). Accuracy, evaluated through spike-recovery experiments in human plasma matrix, showed recoveries of 97.2–109.1%. Specificity testing against procalcitonin (50 ng/ml), interleukin-6 (1000 pg/ml), heparin-binding protein (500 ng/ml), and C-reactive protein (70 ng/ml) confirmed no cross-reactivity. Interference studies with triglycerides (10 mmol/L), bilirubin (350 μmol/L), and hemoglobin (10 g/L) demonstrated recoveries of 91.6–96.5%, validating robustness in hemolyzed, lipemic, and icteric specimens.

PSP cut‑off calculation and assessment of risk stratification

For clinical application probabilities, the optimal cut-off value of the plasma PSP protein level was identified using X-tile software (Version 3.6.1, Yale University School of Medicine) as previously described40, which performs survival outcome-based optimization by maximizing the χ2 statistic from the Mantel-Cox log-rank test. This method identifies thresholds that best stratify mortality risk, as opposed to ROC curve-based diagnostic classification. PSP concentrations used for this stratification were measured at the time of initial clinical presentation, within 4 h of hospital admission. Accordingly, patients have been stratified into low- and high-risk of death groups, the thresholds were defined as the value that produced the largest v2 value in the Mantel-Cox test. Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan‒Meier method, and the log-rank test was used to examine the significance of differences between the low- and high-risk groups.

Statistical analysis

Unless indicated otherwise, the experimental data, presented as the mean ± SD, median (p25, p75), or number (percentage), were statistically analyzed using Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, Inc., CA, USA) or R/RStudio software (http://www.R-project.org, version 4.0.3). The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) was calculated to assess the accuracy of PSP in predicting short-term mortality. Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r) was used to assess the correlation of plasma PSP compared to that of other major clinical biomarkers, and a correlation plot was generated using the R packages “ggplot2” and “corrplot”. Significance was determined using analysis of variance (ANOVA), and Student’s t test and/or the Mann–Whitney U test were applied for two-group data comparisons. P values ≤ 0.05 were considered indicative of significance.

To support these findings, the accuracy of PSP expression and other sepsis-relevant markers in predicting short-term mortality in Sepsis+ve patients was evaluated by AUROC measurement, in which the calculated AUROC for the predicting 28-day mortality was 0.8368 (95% CI, 0.7266–0.9470), whereas for 90-day mortality, the calculated AUROC of PSP expression was 0.8070 (95% CI, 0.7303–0.8836), as illustrated in Fig. 3C. These values were superior or comparable to those observed for IL-6 (AUROC: 0.7151 and 0.7733), PCT (0.7347 and 0.7562), and SOFA score (0.7500 and 0.6667). These findings indicated that PSP outperforms other routinely used inflammatory markers and scoring systems in predicting short-term mortality, and might be considered as a robust potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for hyper-inflammation disorders as sepsis/COVID-19.

Risk stratification analysis and PSP clinical correlations

Interestingly, our results showed different expression patterns of PSP in Sepsis+ve patients; hence, we further determined the optimal cut-off of plasma PSP using X-tile software for the convenience of clinical translation of our findings. Optimal cutoff value, identified as plasma PSP protein level of < 119 ng/mL for the low-risk group (n = 59) and ≥ 119 ng/mL for the high-risk group (n = 46) through survival-based optimization (X-tile v3.6.1), stratified Sepsis⁺ve patients into two distinct groups with highly different probabilities of survival according to the optimal cut-off value of the plasma PSP level. Patients in the high-risk group showed significantly (P < 0.001) elevated SOFA score, higher levels of creatinine, WBCs, CRP, major inflammatory cytokines (IL-2, IL-6, and IL-10), PCT, as well as a poor prognosis for short-term mortality (28- and 90-day), and the overall clinical characteristics of patients in the low- and high-risk groups are provided in Table 3. Notably, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis showed a significant difference between the low-risk and high-risk groups at both 28-day (P = 0.0054) and 90-day (P = 0.0046) short-term mortality (Fig. 4A).

Next, we investigated the correlations of plasma PSP levels with certain laboratory indicators to evaluate the relationship between PSP expression and Sepsis+ve prognosis and disease outcome. Importantly, Pearson’s correlation analysis showed that plasma PSP exhibited significant positive correlations (P < 0.02) with SOFA score, urea, CRP, PCT, IL-2 and IL-6, whereas hemoglobin showed significant negative correlations (P < 0.001). Surprisingly, no significant correlation existed between plasma PSP and WBCs, and neutrophils (Fig. 4B). Overall, PSP might be considered a promising biomarker for predicting the sepsis/COVID-19 disease severity and short-term outcome, whereas a possible application of plasma PSP analysis could improve diagnostic accuracy and prognostication of hyper-inflammation disorders compared with the existing conventional clinical biomarkers.

Discussion

The modern concept of sepsis was first proposed in 1914 to describe the clinical syndrome of microbial-induced blood stream invasion in patients41. Sepsis, a life-threatening systemic condition, is manifested by dysregulated host response to an infection caused by different pathogens (bacteria, virus, fungi), and considered as a leading cause of death among hospitalized patients and intensive care units42. In China, following a 5 years national mortality surveillance system data analysis, an estimated one million deaths were attributed to sepsis43. Understanding of sepsis pathophysiology has crucial evolutionary impact on the disease definition and clinical management over the past decades, which was certainly attributed to the progression of diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers for early identification of at-risk subjects44.

To improve clinical interventions, the characterization and identification of authentic sepsis diagnostic biomarkers become more perceptive in recent years. Routine procedures for analysis of conventional inflammatory cytokines most widely used clinical tests to diagnose and manage patients with sepsis (namely CRP and PCT), but their speed of detection time and specificity have been frequently disputed45. In this study, the innovative AlphaLISA assay procedure was applied for biomarker detection due to the undoubtedly advantages, as being fast (total assay time: 30 min), highly sensitive (LOD 5.6 ng/ml), more convenient (homogeneous, no-wash format requiring only sample/bead pipetting), and demonstrating extended compatibility with diverse biological analytes, including full-length proteins (5–200 kDa), peptides (0.5–5 kDa), and small molecules (< 500 Da), in complex matrices such as plasma46,47. Numerous studies have reported the beneficial applications of AlphaLISA technique in reliable diagnostic assessment of different pathophysiological conditions, including Parkinson disease, gastric cancer, as well as drug targets discovery and inflammatory mediators48–51. Notably, the PSP-AlphaLISA novel technique exhibited excellent performance in distinguishing common infection from hyper-inflammatory conditions, especially sepsis prognostication, and could facilitated timely clinical and therapeutic interventions22.

In this study, we explored the PSP potentiality as biomarker and predictor of sepsis/COVID-19 pathogenesis, disease severity and outcome, whereas the plasma PSP cut-off value that stratified patients with low- and high-risk might facilitate the clinical transition of PSP as non-invasive tool for sepsis progression. Based on our data, we found that plasma PSP protein expression levels were significantly higher in Sepsis+ve patients compared other disease groups and healthy controls, with higher concentrations in the Sepsis+ve-deceased subjects than Sepsis+ve-survivals, which indicate the promising prognostic value of PSP. The observed distributional overlap between study groups is likely reflects the biological continuum between uncomplicated infection and sepsis, compounded by interindividual variability in host immune responses and our protocol-defined early sampling within four hours of admission. These findings underscore PSP’s primary utility as a prognostic indicator of systemic inflammatory burden in established sepsis with COVID-19, rather than as a binary diagnostic discriminator for infection presence. In this context, inflammatory mediators and cytokine storm are decisive events in the incidence and progression of sepsis, whereas repressions of inflammatory cytokines considered as a corner-stone determinant of improved prognosis52. Meanwhile, sepsis in a well-known trigger of immune paralysis and shifting of the immune cell subsets, a major contributor in sepsis pathophysiology53, our data revealed that Sepsis+ve group T lymphocytes markers, namely CD3, CD4 and CD8 showed the significantly lowest numbers among the study cohort, with median of only 384 (P < 0.001), 195 (P < 0.001) and 118 (P < 0.001) cells/ml, respectively. Interestingly, the markedly decreased numbers of the functional T cells among Sepsis+ve-deceased subjects (with median of 335.5, 182.5 and 113 cells/ml for CD3, CD4 and CD8 respectively) indicated immune dysregulation and dysfunctionality that contributed to the sepsis/COVID-19 severity and worsen diseases outcome.

In the search of reliable inflammatory biomarkers for sepsis to improve clinical practice and provide molecular insights into the disease pathophysiological basis, we observed that plasma PSP protein levels significantly positively correlated with the major inflammatory mediators and cytokines (CRP, PCT and IL-6), especially in sepsis/COVID-19 patients. The observed PCT concentrations in our cohort warrant contextual interpretation. While CRP demonstrated robust elevation across sepsis groups (Table 1), the relatively modest median PCT levels, particularly in COVID-negative sepsis patients (Sepsis⁻ve: 0.11 ng/ml), reflect three key considerations: First, the inclusion of viral-dominated COVID-19 infections attenuated overall PCT responses, as viral triggers elicit weaker PCT elevation than bacterial sepsis24. Second, our protocol-mandated blood sampling within 4 h of admission preceded the characteristic 6–12 h PCT peak in bacterial infections54. Third, the cohort encompassed sepsis patients across the severity spectrum (SOFA ≥ 2), not exclusively septic shock patients who exhibit maximal PCT elevations (> 2 ng/ml). The pronounced CRP elevation aligns with its faster induction kinetics (peaking within 6 h) and responsiveness to broader inflammatory stimuli23.

PSP, a notorious proinflammatory protein, is classically overexpressed in various inflammatory conditions, including severe infections, appendicitis, peritonitis, pneumonia, and sepsis 55. In vitro investigations showed that PSP could facilitated immune cells trafficking and recruitment through activation of polymorphonuclear neutrophil granulocytes, regulating CD62L and CD11b expressions56,57, which explain aggravation of sepsis severity and mortality rates in animals following PSP administration in vivo58. Moreover, different studies highlighted the superior role of PSP in terms of accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and outcomes prediction, compared to the existing inflammatory biomarkers, namely CRP, PCT, IL-6 and other cytokines28,33. Interestingly, our AUROC analysis confirms that PSP exhibits superior prognostic accuracy compared to established inflammatory biomarkers (IL-6, PCT) and clinical scoring system (SOFA) for predicting sepsis⁺ve mortality at both 28 and 90 days. Notably, plasma PSP expression levels considered more reliable, due to their robust steadiness following thermal injury and burn trauma, in contrast to those of CRP, PCT and WBCs, which instantaneously changed in thermal injury regardless of infection59. In the meantime, reduction of plasma PSP levels, especially in intensive care settings, has been considered as positive marker of recovery and linked with high survival proportion60. Overall, in line with our findings, these evidences support the potential role of PSP as stable biomarker for sepsis prognosis and severity outcome. Interestingly, our results also emphasized the robust performance of PSP for predicting sepsis/COVID-19 prognosis, whereas the plasma PSP cut-off value that stratified patients with low- and high-risk might facilitate the clinical transition of PSP as novel tool for sepsis progression. Importantly, PSP levels were measured at admission, supporting its potential use in early risk stratification at the point of clinical presentation.

Nevertheless, our study has few certain limitations and shortcomings, including the study population is based on a single-centered, as additional validations using prospective cohorts and multicenter studies are required to further verify the PSP clinical role in sepsis. Regardless of the reported potent correlations between PSP and disease severity outcome in our study, dynamic longitudinal observations of plasma PSP levels for hospitalized patients and intensive care settings might in clinical decision-making processes and therapeutic intervention strategies. Although the PSP-AlphaLISA assay demonstrated robust analytical performance, potential interferences warrant consideration: heterophilic antibodies or rheumatoid factors can non-specifically bridge assay antibodies, causing false results61, and pre-analytical factors such as hemolysis, lipemia, or improper storage may compromise quantification accuracy62. We mitigated these risks by centrifuging samples within one hour of collection, using single-use aliquots stored at –80 °C, and employing a homogeneous no-wash format. Future validation should include systematic interference testing, heterophile-blocking reagents, and standardized sample-quality metrics to establish clinical robustness thresholds.

Conclusions

In summary, this study provides novel insights into the potential role of plasma PSP as a promising biomarker candidate for the diagnosis, prognosis and severity outcome of hyper-inflammatory disorders, especially sepsis/COVID-19 disease.

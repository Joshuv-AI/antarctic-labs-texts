Associations of arterial stiffness onset age with type 2 diabetes risk: a large-scale Chinese cohort study
Authors: Fei Li, Yalan Li, Xiaoxiao Zhong, Jie Wang, Rujia Miao, Ting Chen, Yuan Hong, Liyong Zhu, Yao Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo explore the associations between arterial stiffness (AS) onset age and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) risk.DesignLarge-scale cohort study.SettingThe largest medical centre in central China.ParticipantsThis study enrolled 22 588 participants free of T2DM at baseline. All participants took an annual physical examination from 2012 to 2016, with fasting blood glucose and brachial–ankle pulse wave velocity measurements.Primary and secondary outcome measuresCox proportional model was used to investigate the association between AS onset age and T2DM risk. Sex-specific analysis was also performed, and the association between early vascular ageing (EVA), normal vascular ageing (NVA), supernormal vascular ageing (SUPERNOVA) onset age and diabetes risk was also examined.ResultsA total of 757 (3.4%) participants were diagnosed with T2DM. Compared with participants free of AS in each specific age group, the earlier AS onset age was more strongly associated with T2DM risk, especially for middle age, with the fully adjusted hazard risk (HR) and 95% CI of 4.63 (2.79–7.67) for AS onset age at 50. Similar results were observed both in males and females, with the fully adjusted HR and 95% CI of 4.54 (2.60 to 7.93) for males and 4.86 (1.48 to 16.01) for females, with AS onset age at 50. Such an association was also observed in the exploration of EVA, NVA and SUPERNOVA onset age and incident T2DM risk.ConclusionThis study revealed that the middle-aged onset of AS was more significantly associated with a higher T2DM risk. Early screening of AS, especially in middle age, may assist in T2DM detection and postpone diabetic vascular complications.

Introduction

The prevalence of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) has been increasing worldwide. According to the 2021 global atlas of diabetes, the total number of people with diabetes has reached 537 million worldwide,1 leading to an increase in vascular complications.2 3 Studies have shown that people with younger-onset age of diabetes were more prone to develop diabetic angiopathy, which is the leading cause of cardiovascular disease and death in patients with diabetes.46 Therefore, early detection of diabetes is of utmost importance, particularly for the prevention of vascular complications.

Multiple studies have shown that pulse wave velocity (PWV) is regarded as the gold standard for arterial stiffness (AS)7 8 and has strong predictive power for vascular complications in patients with T2DM.9 Meanwhile, the abnormal increase in PWV is significantly associated with T2DM onset.10 11 However, limited studies have focused on the association of AS onset age and T2DM risk, and whether certain ages with AS are more exposed to T2DM remains unknown.

This longitudinal cohort study aimed to explore the association between the AS onset age and incident T2DM. Meanwhile, whether this association differed in sex analysis was further examined, as well as early vascular ageing (EVA), normal vascular ageing (NVA) and supernormal vascular ageing (SUPERNOVA) onset age and T2DM risk.

Methods

Study Population

The Health Management Centre of the Third Xiangya Hospital of Central South University (Changsha, China) is the largest medical centre in central China. Hundreds of institutions in Changsha from various socio-economic backgrounds (public services employees, workers, self-employed persons, farmers and others) have consistently chosen the centre to conduct yearly employee medical examinations as previously described.1214 A total of 225 533 participants who attended annual physical examinations from 2012 to 2016 were enrolled. All the participants completed the medical examination, questionnaire survey and blood sample collection. The exclusion criteria were as follows: without brachial–ankle pulse wave velocity (ba-PWV) measurements (n=1 58 197), without follow-up (n=41 776), incomplete information (n=1814) and diagnosis of diabetes at baseline (n=1158). Ultimately, 22 588 participants were included in our study (online supplemental figure S1).

Under the guidance of the Declaration of Helsinki, this study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Third Xiangya Hospital. All participants signed an informed consent form and agreed to share their health information for medical research. The study reporting followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology Statement.

Data collection

Socio-demographic information

Trained investigators used standardised questionnaires to obtain information on demographic variables (age, sex and marital status), lifestyle factors (current smoking, current alcohol consumption and physical activity) and past medical history (T2DM and hypertension) as previously described.1214 Bodyweight and height were measured using a calibrated weighing scale, with participants wearing light clothes and without shoes. Measurements were taken two times, and the mean values were used for analysis.

Measurement of blood pressure

According to the standardisation of blood pressure (BP) measurement in population survey,15 16 BP was monitored on the right upper arm after 10–15 min of rest in the sitting position with a validated digital automatic BP monitor (Omron 9020, Omron, Kyoto, Japan). The systolic and diastolic BP were each measured two times with a 30 s interval, and the mean values were used in the analysis. If two readings of systolic or diastolic BP differed by >5 mmHg, BP was re-measured, and the average of three readings was finally recorded.

Measurement of brachial–ankle pulse wave velocity

The ba-PWV was measured using an automatic waveform analyser (BP-203 RPE III, Omron Health Medical, Dalian, China). Detailed measurement procedures had been described previously.12 Measurements were conducted in the checkroom with a comfortable temperature between 22 and 25 ℃. Smoking and coffee drinking are prohibited before measurements. After 5 min of rest, participants were asked to take off their coats and socks and wear light clothes while lying on the examination couch. The ECG electrodes and heart sound sensor were placed first. Four cuffs were put on the upper arms and ankles on both sides, which are connected to the plethysmographic sensor and oscillometric pressure sensor, for pressure waveforms measurements. The ba-PWV was measured two times for both sides. The mean values were used as the final record.

Laboratory assay

All blood samples were collected from the antecubital vein into a vacuum tube containing sodium K2-ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid from 7:00 to 9:00 AM after at least 10 hours of overnight fasting. Fasting blood glucose (FBG) was measured with the glucose oxidase method (intra-assay coefficient of variation <2.5%). Total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), low-density lipoprotein (LDL), high-density lipoprotein (HDL), serum creatinine and uric acid were measured using enzymatic methods. These were measured in blood samples using an automated analyser (Hitachi 7600–110; Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan) at the central laboratory of the Third Xiangya Hospital.1214

Definition of clinical covariates

A married status was defined as being married or unmarried, and the participants who were widowed or divorced were defined as married. Current smoking was defined as present for consumption of more than one cigarette per day (on average) for >6 months. Current alcohol consumption was defined as consuming alcohol (beer, wine and liquor) at least 2 days per week for >12 months. Physical activity was defined as having a mild or moderate intensity of exercise every day. The body mass index (BMI) was calculated as body weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in metres. Hypertension was defined as systolic BP ≥140 mmHg and/or diastolic BP ≥90 mmHg, current use of antihypertensive drugs or self-reported.

Definition of AS

AS was defined as ba-PWV≥1400 cm/s. SUPERNOVA was defined as a ba-PWV value below the age quintile-specific 10th percentile. EVA was defined as a ba-PWV value over the age quintile-specific 90th percentile, and the remaining participants were considered NVA (online supplemental figure S2).17

Definition of T2DM

T2DM was defined as FBG ≥7.0 mmol/L, current use of antidiabetic drugs or self-reported.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using Stata version 16.0 (Stata Corp., College Station, TX). Significance was determined by two-sided p values of<0.05. The baseline characteristics were presented as median (IQR) for continuous variables and number (percentage) for categorical variables. The differences between groups were calculated using Kruskal–Wallis tests for continuous variables and χ2 tests for categorical variables. The Cox proportional model was used to explore the association between AS onset age and T2DM risk. This association was investigated based on the AS onset age (groups aged 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 and 80 years), with the reference group free of AS at that specific age. The proportional hazards assumption was confirmed by plotting Schoenfeld residuals. The potential covariates were screened using univariate logistic regression analysis. In the end, several adjusted models were analysed as follows: model 1, adjusted for sex and age at baseline; model 2, further adjusted for current smoking, current alcohol consumption and physical activity at baseline; model 3, further adjusted for BMI, systolic BP, TC, TG, LDL and serum creatinine at baseline.

To exclude the potential bias of hypertension, a sensitivity analysis was further conducted with model 3 after excluding participants with hypertension at baseline. In addition, a sex-specific analysis of the association between AS onset age and T2DM risk was conducted. To evaluate whether this association was consistent between EVA, NVA or SUPERNOVA onset age and T2DM risk, subgroup analyses were also performed. In this part, we added a re-estimate that redefined vascular ageing subgroups based on age-specific tertiles of ba-PWV: EVA was defined as ba-PWV≥the upper tertile for the corresponding age group, NVA as ba-PWV between the lower and upper tertiles and SUPERNOVA as ba-PWV≤the lower tertile.

Patient and public Involvement

Patients or the general public were not involved in the design of this study. The results of the research, which were obtained from examination, will not be disseminated to the participants.

Association of AS onset age and T2DM risk

A total of 757 (3.4%) participants were subsequently diagnosed with T2DM after a median follow-up of 3.00 years. Online supplemental table S1 showed the results of the association between AS onset age and T2DM risk. Compared with participants free of AS in each specific age group, earlier age at AS onset was more strongly associated with incident T2DM, especially in middle age. The fully adjusted HRs with 95% CIs of incident T2DM were 1.30 (0.41–4.13) for AS onset age at 30, 3.98 (1.86–8.51) at 40, 4.63 (2.79–7.67) at 50, 3.41 (1.86–6.26) at 60, 2.67 (1.05–6.79) at 70 and 2.68 (0.70–10.17) at 80, respectively (figure 1 and online supplemental table S1).

Models adjusted for sex, age, current smoking, current alcohol consumption, physical activity, body mass index, systolic blood pressure, total cholesterol, triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein and serum creatinine at baseline.

In the sex-specific analysis, the incidence rates of T2DM were 620 (4.2%) for males and 137 (1.7%) for females. Multivariate adjusted HRs for T2DM risk across sex-specific groups of AS onset age are shown in online supplemental table S2. Similarly, participants with AS onset in middle age had the highest risk of T2DM, with the HR of AS onset age at 50 being 4.54 (2.60–7.93) for males and 4.86 (1.48–16.01) for females (figure 2 and online supplemental table S2).

Association between arterial stiffness onset age and type 2 diabetes mellitus risk stratified by sex. In addition, participants with hypertension at baseline were excluded from the sensitivity analysis (n=3695). Such association was still observed in the whole population and males, with the highest T2DM risk in AS onset age at 40 (HR (95% CI) 3.98 (1.86 to 8.51) for the whole population and 3.96 (1.74 to 9.00) for the males), whereas no significant results were observed in females (online supplemental figure S5).

Association of EVA, NVA, SUPERNOVA onset age and T2DM risk

Figure 3 shows the full adjusted results of the association between EVA, NVA and SUPERNOVA onset age and T2DM risk in the whole population. Similar results were observed in that vascular ageing with a middle-age onset is still significantly associated with T2DM risk, both in exploring EVA, NVA and SUPERNOVA onset age (figure 3). Such an association was also observed in males (online supplemental figure S6) and females (online supplemental figure S7). Consistently, the association between redefined vascular ageing subgroups, which were based on age-specific tertiles of ba-PWV, and T2DM risk was consistent with the above primary analysis, further supporting the robustness of our findings (online supplemental table S3).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this study is the first to investigate the association of AS onset age with T2DM risk. The findings revealed that an earlier AS onset age was more strongly associated with a higher T2DM risk, especially for middle-aged participants. This association was observed both in males and females, as well as in the exploration of EVA, NVA and SUPERNOVA onset age with T2DM risk . Our findings indicated that men, particularly those in middle age from around 40 years onward, should pay closer attention to ba-PWV assessment.

AS is an age-related process that reflects the cumulative burden of cardiovascular risk factors on the arterial wall.7 18 AS is an established risk marker for cardiovascular events.71921 The results of the meta-analysis showed that each 1 m/s increase in aortic PWV was associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular events, cardiovascular mortality and all-cause mortality by 14%, 15% and 15%, respectively.19 Meanwhile, AS is a common cause of multiple diseases, such as hypertension, chronic renal disease and diabetes. 22 Elevated AS could result in an excess of pulsatility transmission, promoting the development of microvascular target organ damage, especially for the organs with very low vascular resistance.23 As the pancreas receives the highest blood supply in the body, it is easily influenced by arterial stiffening and induced endocrine dysfunction.23 Therefore, AS must be detected early in the routine measurements to prevent diabetes and thus postpone the onset of vascular disease.

Increasing evidence shows that elevated PWV is an early marker of pre-diabetes, impaired fasting glucose, glucose intolerance and insulin resistance.9 Several advantages of ba-PWV have been recognised in previous research: 1) ba-PWV is an objective, reproducible physiological measurement that does not rely on self-reported information, thereby avoiding recall bias inherent in questionnaires. 2) It captures early, subclinical vascular changes that may precede abnormalities in diabetes, enabling earlier risk identification.10 3) Third, ba-PWV reflects the cumulative effects of multiple cardiovascular risk factors, offering an integrated assessment of vascular health. In addition, several studies have indicated that AS could predict diabetes onset.10 11 24 Zheng et al conducted a cross-lagged path analysis and reported that AS appeared to precede diabetes.10 Muhammad et al reported that increased AS is an early marker for diabetes development.11 Wang et al reported that the AS index is associated with an increased risk of T2DM in a dose–response relationship.24 Although those studies did not focus on the importance of AS onset age in identifying diabetes risk, confirming which individuals of certain AS onset ages are more exposed to T2DM risk could enhance a new understanding of the relationship between AS and T2DM and may assist in the reclassification of T2DM risk.

To date, only two studies have examined AS in various age groups and explored the relationship between AS and T2DM risk.25 26 Soumia Taimour et al investigated the longitudinal association of AS and hyperglycaemia as well as T2DM in two related generations and reported that impaired glucose metabolism and AS were significantly associated only in the parental generation.25 In addition, Poon et al reported a positive association between insulin resistance and aortic stiffness in older adults without T2DM. However, when exploring the association between the annual change of insulin resistance and aortic stiffness from mid-life to late-life in a cross-sectional analysis, no significant result was found.26 The above study showed that older participants with AS are more likely to have T2DM risk or insulin resistance without directly focusing on the association of AS onset age and T2DM risk. Thus, to our knowledge, the present study was the first to investigate the association between arterial stiffness onset age and T2DM incidence, and the results showed that participants with AS onset at middle age are associated with a higher risk of incident T2DM.

Several potential mechanisms could explain the association between AS and T2DM. First, AS and T2DM share part of the same pathogenesis, which induces AS at the same time and will induce T2DM, such as vascular endothelial dysfunction, inflammation and oxidative stress.2729 Second, AS could lead to haemodynamic changes that affect the blood supply to low-resistance organs (especially the pancreas and liver, leading to abnormal glucose metabolism).23 30 Meanwhile, it could lead to decreased perfusion of the vascular bed and sparse blood vessels, which in turn result in impaired glucose use. In addition, the advanced glycation end products and nitric oxide dysregulation play a key role in the pathogenesis of AS.9

This study has several strengths. This population-based prospective study used the largest ba-PWV dataset currently available. Besides, the data accuracy for ba-PWV measurements was high with good in-house repeatability. In addition, the study population was carefully screened to exclude all prevalent cases of T2DM. However, some limitations need to be considered. First, T2DM was diagnosed based on FBG levels rather than on the oral glucose tolerance test or haemoglobin A1c test because of restricted data collection for such a large sample. However, the current use of antidiabetic drugs or self-report was used to define T2DM, which is an acceptable alternative in a large epidemiological study. Second, given the limited data, we did not adjust any inflammatory biomarkers in the model analysis, although AS and T2DM are both linked to inflammation, which warrants more investigation in the future. Third, participants of the Health Management Centre in this longitudinal cohort study tended to be healthier than the general population; in the present study, the prevalence of T2DM (3.4%) was lower than that in the general Chinese population, and the other comorbidity information may be absent. This limitation on participant selection undermines its ability to reflect broader population trends. Fourth, the follow-up time may not be sufficient; younger participants have not yet reached an age when T2DM is more prevalent. The continued follow-up of this longitudinal study will further examine the importance of the age of AS onset for T2DM risk.

In conclusion, this study indicated that participants with AS onset at middle age are more significantly associated with incident T2DM. The findings signal a need for early detection of AS, which may be helpful for the early identification of participants developing T2DM and diabetic vascular complications, especially for middle-aged adults.

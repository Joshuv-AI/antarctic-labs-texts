Transfer learning and knowledge graph enhanced VR animation resource recommendation with creativity prediction
Authors: Cong Yan, Hasnah Binti Mohamed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractVirtual reality technology has transformed animation education by providing immersive learning environments, yet the proliferation of VR-based teaching resources presents significant challenges in resource discovery and personalized learning design. This research proposes an intelligent recommendation system that integrates transfer learning and knowledge graph technologies to address cold-start and data sparsity challenges in VR animation education. The system comprises two core components: a hybrid recommendation engine combining transfer learning algorithms with knowledge graph reasoning to generate context-aware resource suggestions, and a creativity development path prediction model based on LSTM-attention mechanisms that analyzes learning behaviors and forecasts individualized development trajectories. A comprehensive knowledge graph for VR animation teaching was constructed to capture domain concepts, resource attributes, and pedagogical relationships. Experimental results demonstrate substantial improvements over baseline methods across multiple metrics, with the proposed system achieving optimal performance in precision, recall, F1 score, and NDCG. Introduction

Virtual reality (VR) technology has emerged as a transformative tool in animation education, offering immersive learning environments that enable students to interact with three-dimensional models, practice animation techniques in virtual spaces, and experience real-time feedback on their creative work1. The integration of VR into animation teaching has demonstrated potential in enhancing spatial cognition, technical skill acquisition, and creative expression among students2. However, the rapid proliferation of VR-based animation teaching resources has created significant challenges in resource discovery, personalized learning path design, and effective matching between educational content and individual student needs3.

Current teaching resource recommendation systems face several critical limitations that hinder their effectiveness in VR animation education contexts. The cold-start problem remains particularly pronounced when new students enter the system without sufficient historical interaction data, making it difficult to generate accurate initial recommendations4. Data sparsity issues further compound this challenge, as students typically interact with only a small fraction of available resources, resulting in sparse user-item matrices that compromise recommendation quality5. Additionally, existing recommendation approaches often fail to capture the complex relationships between animation concepts, technical skills, and creative development stages, leading to insufficient personalization accuracy and suboptimal learning outcomes.

Transfer learning and knowledge graph technologies offer promising solutions to address these limitations in educational recommendation systems. Transfer learning enables the leveraging of knowledge acquired from related domains or tasks to improve performance in target scenarios with limited data, effectively mitigating cold-start and data sparsity challenges6. Knowledge graphs (KGs) provide structured representations of domain knowledge, capturing semantic relationships between concepts, resources, and learning objectives through interconnected entity-relationship networks7. The synergistic integration of these two technologies presents unprecedented opportunities for building intelligent recommendation systems that can reason about pedagogical relationships, adapt to individual learning patterns, and support personalized creativity development trajectories.

This research proposes a novel intelligent recommendation system for VR animation teaching resources that integrates transfer learning and knowledge graph technologies to address the aforementioned challenges. The core innovation lies in constructing a dual-component framework: first, a recommendation engine that combines transfer learning algorithms with knowledge graph reasoning to generate accurate, context-aware resource suggestions; second, a student creativity development path prediction model that analyzes learning behavior patterns, skill acquisition sequences, and creative output characteristics to forecast individualized development trajectories and provide proactive pedagogical interventions8. The research objectives include: (1) developing effective methods for integrating transfer learning with knowledge graphs in educational recommendation contexts; (2) establishing a comprehensive knowledge graph for VR animation teaching that captures domain concepts, resource attributes, and pedagogical relationships; (3) constructing a creativity development path prediction model based on multi-dimensional student data; (4) validating the proposed system through empirical studies demonstrating improved recommendation accuracy, enhanced learning outcomes, and measurable creativity development.

This research makes four primary contributions to educational technology: (1) Methodological innovation through the first deep integration of transfer learning and KG technologies for VR animation resource recommendation, achieving 18.5% improvement in cold-start recommendation accuracy over baseline collaborative filtering methods; (2) System architecture innovation via a dual-component framework that dynamically adjusts recommendation strategies based on predicted creativity development trajectories, enabling proactive pedagogical interventions; (3) Knowledge representation innovation by constructing a domain-specific knowledge graph for VR animation teaching encompassing 7 entity types and 15 semantic relation types not covered in existing educational knowledge graphs; (4) Evaluation framework innovation establishing a multidimensional creativity assessment system combining automated behavioral analytics, expert evaluation with inter-rater reliability (Krippendorff’s Alpha ≥ 0.80), and peer comparison, providing more comprehensive assessment than single-dimension approaches in prior research.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II reviews related work on recommendation systems, transfer learning, knowledge graphs, and creativity assessment in educational contexts; Section III presents the overall system architecture and methodological framework; Section IV presents experimental design, results, and analysis; Section V concludes the paper with key findings and contributions.

Transfer learning theory and its application in educational recommendation

Transfer learning represents a machine learning paradigm that leverages knowledge acquired from source domains or tasks to improve learning performance in target domains where training data is limited or unavailable9. The fundamental principle underlying transfer learning is the assumption that related domains share common feature representations or model parameters that can be transferred to enhance prediction accuracy in new contexts. Transfer learning approaches are typically categorized into three main types: inductive transfer learning, which transfers knowledge when source and target tasks differ; transductive transfer learning, which addresses scenarios where source and target domains differ but tasks remain consistent; and unsupervised transfer learning, which operates without labeled data in target domains10.

Model transfer approaches involve pre-training deep neural networks on source tasks and fine-tuning specific layers for target applications, preserving general feature extractors while adapting to domain-specific characteristics.

Recent applications of transfer learning in educational recommendation systems have demonstrated promising results in addressing data scarcity challenges12. Studies have applied transfer learning to predict student performance by leveraging knowledge from established courses to new curriculum designs, significantly improving recommendation accuracy for newly enrolled students. The cold-start problem, which severely affects traditional collaborative filtering methods, can be effectively mitigated through transfer learning by utilizing patterns learned from existing user populations or related educational contexts. However, current research limitations include insufficient consideration of pedagogical relationships in knowledge transfer, limited exploration of multi-source domain transfer strategies, and inadequate integration with structured knowledge representations such as knowledge graphs13.

Experimental results and performance evaluation analysis

All experiments were conducted on a workstation equipped with NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24 GB memory) and 64 GB RAM. The system implementation leveraged Python 3.9 and PyTorch 2.0 as the primary deep learning framework, with NetworkX 2.8 handling knowledge graph operations and scikit-learn 1.2 supporting baseline algorithm implementations. This computational setup enabled efficient training of neural architectures while maintaining reasonable inference latency for real-time recommendation generation in production scenarios.

Recommendation system performance comparison experiments

The experimental evaluation utilizes three interconnected datasets collected from authentic VR animation education environments during the 2023–2024 academic year. The recommendation system dataset encompasses 1247 students (734 undergraduates, 428 graduate students, 85 continuing education participants) across skill levels (412 beginners, 589 intermediate, 246 advanced), 87 instructors, and 432 VR animation teaching resources spanning character modeling (28%), motion capture techniques (22%), procedural animation (19%), scene design (17%), special effects (9%), and compositional principles (5%). The dataset contains 89,634 interaction records including resource views (43,251 instances), completion events (28,947 instances), rating submissions (12,108 instances, 5-point scale), and sequential navigation patterns (5328 sequences). The user-item interaction matrix exhibits 94.3% sparsity, calculated as 1—(89,634/(1334 users × 432 resources)), with mean user interactions of 67.2 (SD = 38.6) and mean resource interactions of 207.5 (SD = 142.3). Data collection spanned March 2023 to June 2024 (16 months) across 5 participating institutions.

The source domain dataset for transfer learning comprises 50,000 users, 5000 educational courses, and 2,018,743 interaction records aggregated from publicly available datasets including Coursera course interactions and edX learning analytics covering 12 disciplinary domains: computer science (18%), design (15%), digital arts (12%), engineering (11%), business (9%), humanities (8%), sciences (7%), mathematics (6%), language learning (5%), social sciences (4%), health sciences (3%), and interdisciplinary studies (2%). Source domain data exhibits 96.7% sparsity with broader coverage but lower interaction density compared to target VR animation domain. Key statistics include average course enrollment of 1834 students (SD = 1,247), completion rate of 34.6%, and average engagement duration of 18.3 h per course (SD = 24.7 h). The teaching application experiment deployed the integrated recommendation and creativity prediction system within authentic VR animation education environments throughout full academic term instructional periods47. Comprehensive evaluation spanned multiple assessment dimensions including learning outcome achievement, creativity competency development, user satisfaction perception, and system operational performance. The controlled experimental design allocated student cohorts randomly to experimental conditions receiving intelligent recommendations versus control conditions utilizing conventional resource discovery methods.

Experimental group students accessing the proposed system demonstrated substantial advantages across learning effectiveness indicators compared to control group counterparts receiving traditional recommendation approaches, as illustrated in Fig. 6. Learning outcome improvements manifested through enhanced technical skill acquisition rates, accelerated progression through curricular sequences, and superior performance on summative assessment tasks. Creativity development exhibited pronounced gains in originality and flexibility dimensions, with experimental group students producing work characterized by greater conceptual diversity and innovative problem-solving approaches.

Objective performance metrics collected through pre-post assessment protocols documented measurable gains attributable to system intervention48. Creativity score trajectories showed steeper improvement slopes for experimental group participants across fluency, flexibility, originality, and elaboration dimensions measured through standardized instruments. Work quality evaluations by independent expert raters indicated enhanced aesthetic sophistication and technical proficiency in animation projects produced by students utilizing personalized recommendation pathways. Course achievement grades reflected improved mastery of learning objectives, with experimental group students demonstrating stronger performance on both formative and summative evaluations.

Subjective feedback gathered through structured questionnaires and semi-structured interviews revealed high satisfaction levels among both student and instructor populations. Students appreciated the relevance and timeliness of recommended resources, reporting that suggestions aligned well with their current learning needs and skill development stages. The system’s ability to surface conceptually related materials beyond obvious choices expanded students’ creative horizons and exposed them to diverse animation approaches. Instructors valued the reduction in resource curation burden, noting that automated recommendation enabled them to focus pedagogical effort on higher-level instructional design and individualized student mentoring49.

Learning style analysis revealed differential system acceptance patterns across student typology categories. Visual learners expressed strong preference for resource recommendations emphasizing demonstration videos and visual walkthroughs. Kinesthetic learners favored hands-on practice modules and interactive simulation components. Reflective learners appreciated supplementary theoretical materials contextualizing technical procedures within broader conceptual frameworks. The system’s capacity to accommodate diverse learning preferences through multidimensional resource characterization contributed to broad acceptance across heterogeneous student populations.

Resource utilization metrics indicated high engagement with recommended materials. Students accessed substantial proportions of suggested resources, with completion rates suggesting meaningful interaction rather than superficial browsing. Operational performance evaluation confirmed system scalability and responsiveness suitable for production deployment. Response latency for recommendation generation remained within acceptable thresholds even under concurrent user load. Model inference times for creativity prediction facilitated timely pathway adjustments responsive to evolving student trajectories50.

Implementation challenges identified through deployment experience included initial knowledge graph population requiring substantial domain expert investment, data quality variability across heterogeneous source systems affecting prediction reliability, and occasional recommendation filter bubble tendencies requiring diversity-promoting algorithmic adjustments. Optimization recommendations include automated knowledge extraction pipeline development for reducing manual curation burden, enhanced data validation protocols ensuring behavioral signal reliability, and exploration–exploitation balance tuning maintaining healthy tension between personalization and serendipitous discovery.

Conclusion

This research addresses critical challenges in VR animation education through the development and validation of an intelligent recommendation system integrating transfer learning and knowledge graph technologies with a complementary student creativity cultivation path prediction model. The investigation makes substantive contributions to educational technology research by demonstrating how advanced machine learning paradigms can be effectively adapted to domain-specific pedagogical requirements while addressing persistent limitations in personalized learning support systems.

The primary achievement involves successful construction of the knowledge graph-based VR animation teaching resource representation framework that captures complex semantic relationships among educational content, technical concepts, artistic principles, and pedagogical dependencies. The ontology model encompasses comprehensive entity taxonomies and relation types specifically designed for animation education contexts, enabling sophisticated reasoning about resource relevance and learning sequence coherence. Knowledge representation learning through TransE and TransH algorithms effectively translates discrete graph structures into continuous embedding spaces that facilitate computational similarity measurement and semantic inference operations essential for intelligent recommendation generation.

The transfer learning-integrated hybrid recommendation model represents a significant methodological innovation that synergistically combines collaborative filtering mechanisms with content-based approaches through domain adaptation strategies. Pre-training on large-scale educational datasets from related domains enables the system to leverage generalizable user-resource interaction patterns, while fine-tuning procedures adapt these foundational capabilities to VR animation teaching specifics. The attention-based feature fusion architecture successfully integrates heterogeneous information sources including knowledge graph semantics, user behavioral histories, resource content characteristics, and contextual factors, yielding recommendation quality substantially superior to baseline approaches. Experimental validation demonstrates that the proposed system effectively mitigates data sparsity and cold-start problems that have historically plagued educational recommendation applications, with performance improvements particularly pronounced in scenarios involving new users or emerging content domains.

The student creativity cultivation path prediction model establishes a comprehensive framework for quantifying, tracking, and forecasting creative competency development through multidimensional assessment indicators and temporal sequence modeling. The LSTM-attention architecture with knowledge graph enhancement captures complex temporal dependencies in learning trajectories while incorporating structured domain knowledge about factors influencing creativity growth. Multi-task learning optimization jointly addresses creativity prediction and learning path recommendation through shared representation learning, yielding improved accuracy and pedagogical coherence in generated pathways. Model interpretability analysis reveals actionable insights about resource types and learning patterns most strongly associated with creativity development, providing empirical guidance for instructional design and curriculum optimization.

Comprehensive experimental evaluation across multiple assessment dimensions confirms the system’s practical effectiveness in authentic teaching environments. Comparative experiments demonstrate substantial performance advantages over baseline recommendation methods across precision, recall, F1 score, NDCG, and coverage metrics. Creativity prediction accuracy achieves strong correlation with actual student development trajectories, validating the model’s capacity to support proactive educational interventions. Deployment in semester-long teaching applications yields measurable improvements in student learning outcomes, creativity competency gains, and user satisfaction levels, with both students and instructors reporting enhanced educational experiences attributable to personalized, semantically coherent resource recommendations.

Despite these achievements, several limitations warrant acknowledgment. Dataset scale constraints (n = 1334 users, 432 resources) limit comprehensive evaluation across diverse student populations and animation content domains. Cross-disciplinary transfer capabilities require further development to enable knowledge sharing across broader educational contexts beyond animation-specific scenarios. Knowledge graph construction demands substantial domain expert involvement (estimated 240 h for initial ontology design and 480 h for entity annotation), presenting scalability challenges for rapid deployment in new subject areas. Prediction accuracy for students exhibiting nonlinear creativity development trajectories remains suboptimal (RMSE = 8.12 vs. 6.23 overall), suggesting opportunities for architectural refinements capturing discontinuous growth patterns.

The current creativity assessment framework, while comprehensive across seven dimensions, may not adequately capture domain-specific creative expressions unique to different VR animation specializations. Character animation creativity, for instance, emphasizes timing and acting principles that differ fundamentally from procedural modeling creativity focused on algorithmic thinking and parametric innovation. Our uniform indicator system potentially overlooks such nuanced distinctions, suggesting future work should explore specialized assessment rubrics tailored to animation subdisciplines. Additionally, reliance on interaction logs from a single VR platform introduces potential platform-specific biases that may limit generalizability. Students engaging with our experimental system represent a self-selected population demonstrating higher technological affinity compared to broader animation education cohorts, and usage patterns observed in our controlled VR environment may not fully translate to alternative platforms with different interface paradigms or interaction modalities. Cross-platform validation studies would strengthen confidence in the system’s broader applicability across diverse educational technology ecosystems.

Additional ethical and operational considerations merit attention. Creativity prediction may produce self-fulfilling prophecy effects where low predicted scores negatively influence teacher expectations and student self-efficacy. To mitigate this risk, deployment protocols should: (1) never display raw prediction scores directly to students; (2) frame predictions as pedagogical decision support rather than ability labels; (3) implement regular algorithmic fairness audits assessing differential prediction accuracy across demographic subgroups. Preliminary bias analysis reveals prediction RMSE variance across student demographics: underrepresented minorities (RMSE = 6.89 vs. 6.12 for majority students, p = 0.041), suggesting need for bias mitigation techniques in future iterations.

Computational cost presents deployment constraints. Real-time recommendation generation requires 127 ms average latency under single-user conditions but degrades to 384 ms with concurrent user load exceeding 50 simultaneous requests, potentially impacting user experience during peak usage periods. Model inference for creativity prediction averages 43 ms per student per update cycle. System scalability improvements through model compression (quantization, pruning) and distributed inference architectures warrant investigation for production deployment supporting institutional-scale user populations (> 5000 concurrent users).

Data privacy considerations require rigorous protection mechanisms. The system collects fine-grained behavioral data including VR interaction trajectories, creative process iterations, and assessment outcomes. Current implementation employs data anonymization, access control, and encrypted storage complying with institutional review board protocols. However, re-identification risks persist given behavioral data richness. Future research should explore privacy-preserving techniques including federated learning enabling collaborative model training across institutions without centralizing sensitive student data, and differential privacy mechanisms adding calibrated noise to protect individual trajectories while preserving population-level patterns.

Domain transfer limitations constrain generalization. While transfer learning from general educational MOOCs to VR animation contexts achieved 67.4% feature similarity and 18.5% cold-start accuracy improvement, semantic gaps remain between source and target domains. Exploration of intermediate domain transfer strategies (e.g., sequential transfer through digital arts → 2D animation → 3D animation → VR animation) and multi-source domain ensemble approaches may enhance knowledge transferability and reduce target domain data requirements.

Future research directions encompass multiple promising extensions. Multimodal learning resource integration would enable recommendation systems to reason across diverse media types including text, video, interactive simulations, and immersive VR experiences, providing richer pedagogical support. Federated learning frameworks could address privacy concerns by enabling collaborative model training across institutional boundaries without centralizing sensitive student data. Cross-domain transfer learning exploration may reveal generalizable patterns applicable to broader art and design education contexts, including graphic design, industrial design, and digital media production. Longitudinal studies tracking creativity development over extended periods would validate long-term model predictions and inform iterative refinements. Integration of affective computing technologies capturing emotional engagement and motivational states could enhance personalization through consideration of psychological factors complementing behavioral patterns. These research trajectories promise continued advancement toward truly intelligent, adaptive educational systems supporting creative competency cultivation in technology-enhanced learning environments.

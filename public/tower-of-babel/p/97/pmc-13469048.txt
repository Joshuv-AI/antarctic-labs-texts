Long term survival with trastuzumab deruxtecan for heavily pretreated, HER2 mutated advanced non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Ping Wang, Jing Wang, Qiang Qin, Dongmei Zhou, Dalu Huang, Shuang Chen, Shaorong Zeng, Yaping Hong, Mei Jiang, Yunjian Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHuman epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) mutations occur in approximately 4% of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients and represent targetable gene alteration. Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), an anti-HER2 antibody-drug conjugate, was approved in China, the USA and EU based on the results of DESTINY-Lung02 and DESTINY-Lung05 for the treatment of pretreated unresectable or metastatic HER2-mutated NSCLC patients. Herein, we present five cases of HER2-mutated advanced NSCLC who received T-DXd in second or later line settings.Case presentationThe median age of patients was 67 years (range 48–76 years). Four patients were males, and one was female. All five patients had HER2 exon 20 insertional mutations. They received T-DXd in the 2nd or later line settings. One patient had complete response, two had partial response and two had stable disease. T-DXd led to a progression-free survival of up to 41 months from the start of T-DXd therapy. The median overall survival of the five patients was 15 months post T-DXd treatment (range 9–41 months). Adverse events during T-DXd treatment were expected and manageable. Challenge with T-DXd was successful in a patient with a history of interstitial lung disease.ConclusionIn this small case series, T-DXd demonstrated encouraging antitumor activity and manageable safety in heavily pretreated patients with HER2 mutated, metastatic NSCLC. Although selected patients had durable benefits, the findings are anecdotal and should be interpreted with caution. Background

HER2 exon 20 insertions (20ins), the most frequent HER2 mutations [1], occur in 2.4% of Chinese non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients [2]. Patients carrying HER2 mutations respond poorly to first-line platinum-pemetrexed-based chemotherapy [3, 4] and have limited treatment options in later-line settings. Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), an anti-HER2 antibody-drug conjugate (ADC), improved objective response and survival of patients with pretreated metastatic HER2-mutant NSCLC [5]. In the DESTINY-Lung02 trial, 50% of patients with pretreated metastatic HER2-mutant NSCLC showed an objective response to T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg every three weeks (Q3W), with a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 10.0 months, and a median overall survival (OS) of 19.0 months [6]. In the DESTINY-Lung05 trial conducted among Chinese patients with pretreated metastatic HER2-mutant NSCLC, 58.3% showed an objective response to T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg Q3W and the investigators-assessed median PFS was 10.8 months [5]. Based on DESTINY-Lung02 and DESTINY-Lung05 results, T-DXd was approved in October 2024 in China. T-DXd is currently recommended for treating metastatic NSCLC with HER2 mutations pretreated with ≥ 1 line of systemic therapy in China [7]. Herein, we present the clinical outcomes of 5 patients with HER2-mutated advanced NSCLC who received T-DXd in 2nd or later-line settings.

Case presentation

Case 1

A 75-year-old male was admitted on July 1, 2021, because of metastatic lung cancer for 7 months. His treatments are shown in Fig. 1A. Admission radiological and histochemical studies led to a diagnosis of right lung adenocarcinoma with multiple bone metastases and suspected brain metastasis, stage cT4N2M1 (Fig. 1B). Next generation sequencing (NGS) showed HER2 exon 20ins (tissue abundance 31.7%). T-DXd started at 4 mg/kg (day 1, Q3W) and then increased to 5.4 mg/kg Q3W with improved performance status, for a total of 4 cycles. The patient achieved stable disease (SD) and the brain metastasis became nearly undetectable after two cycles of treatment. T-DXd was adjusted to 5.4 mg/kg monthly after grade 4 thrombocytopenia developed. Chest CT scan (June 2024) showed mild primary tumor enlargement. Anlotinib (8 mg/d for 2 weeks of each 3-week cycle) was added in July 2024. A follow-up CT scan showed SD (Fig. 1C). NGS on December 05, 2024, revealed a tissue abundance of 15.8% for HER2 exon 20ins and no HER2 exon 20ins was detected in plasma circulating DNA. The patient had a progression-free survival (PFS) of 41 months following the start of T-DXd therapy. Treatment with T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg) plus anlotinib (8 mg/d) was ongoing at the date of writing up this report.

Case 2

A 76-year-old male with lung adenocarcinoma was referred to us in September 2019 two months after thoracoscopic wedge tumor resection. His treatments are shown in Fig. 2A. Chest CT and MRI scan at admission showed multiple liver metastases and metastases in the thoracolumbar spine and the right ilium (Fig. 2B). He was diagnosed with lung adenocarcinoma (rT1aN0M1 IV) with lung and bone metastasis. NGS of bone metastatic lesion showed HER2 p.Y772_A775dup exon 20ins (tissue abundance 9.1%), mutated TP53, and a high mutation burden. PD-L1 tumor proportion score (TPS) was 5%. The patient received trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1; 4 mg/kg Q3W for 8 cycles). A CT scan (November 10, 2021) showed a partial response (Fig. 2B).

The patient had postexertional dyspnea. Chest CT scan (November 29, 2021) showed multiple bilateral linear and reticular opacities without associated hilar or mediastinal lymphadenopathy (Fig. 2C), indicating T-DM1 treatment-related interstitial lung disease (ILD). T-DM1 was discontinued. He received 3 additional lines of treatments from December 2021 to July 2023 (Fig. 2A). A follow-up chest enhancement CT scan (January 2022) showed continued presence of ILD (Fig. 2C). A CT scan in June 2023 revealed a new mass in the right chest wall (34 × 18 mm) (Fig. 2E); biopsy revealed metastatic lowly differentiated lung adenocarcinoma. NGS of chest wall tissues revealed HER2 p.Y772_A775dup exon 20dup (tissue abundance 11.7%), and mutated TP53. Immunohistochemistry showed that the tumor was positive for PD-L1 (TPS 15%). The patient received T-DXd 4 mg/kg day 1 Q3W and, after resolution of ILD, 5.4 mg/kg for a total of 3 cycles, and then Q4W from July 2023 to October 2025 for 16 cycles. The patient achieved a complete response during T-DXd therapy, with resolution of tumors in the chest wall, bilateral lungs, the pleura, and the liver (Fig. 2E). The patient had an overall survival (OS) of more than 15 months post T-DXd treatment.

Case 3

A 48-year-old male was admitted in July 2023 because of progressive lung cancer for 1 year. The patient was diagnosed with right lung adenocarcinoma with metastases to the right hilar and mediastinal lymph node (LN), bilateral lungs (cT4N2M1 IV) at a local hospital in July 2022. NGS showed HER2 exon 20ins and EGFR 20ins. Preadmission treatments are shown in Figure S1A.

Admission chest CT scan showed disease progression, carcinomatous lymphangitis, multiple bilateral supraclavicular LN metastases and an increase in the number and size of hypointensities in the liver. The patient received T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg, day 1 Q3W) from July 2023 and achieved a partial response after two cycles. The response was confirmed four weeks later. Chest CT scan in May 2024 showed progressive disease, with a new lesion in the left lung (Figure S1B). Anlotinib (8 mg/d for 2 weeks of 3-week cycles) was added (June 2024). Chest CT scan (August 2024) showed progressive disease, with mild increase in the number and size of bilateral lung nodules and the occurrence of a new lesion (Figure S1C). He had an OS > 12 months from the start of T-DXd monotherapy.

Case 4

A 56-year-old male with a diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma was referred to us on January 10, 2024. Immunohistochemistry showed that the tumor had PD-L1 TPS > 30%. PET CT scan (January 2024) revealed a nodule (1.2 cm × 0.7 cm) in the inferior lobe of the left lung, multiple nodules and effusion in the left pleura and enlarged mediastinal and bilateral hilar LN. NGS of pleural fluid tumor cells showed HER2 exon 20ins. The patient was diagnosed with left lung adenocarcinoma (cT1bN3M1a IVA). Treatment timeline is shown in Figure S2A. The patient received pemetrexed (500 mg/m2, carboplatin (AUC = 5) and sintilimab (4 mg), all given on day 1 of each 3–week cycle for two cycles. A follow-up CT scan (February 2024) showed metastasis to the left pleura, indicating disease progression (Figure S2B). T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg, day 1 Q3W) was given for 13 cycles and was ongoing at the end of 2024. In addition, the patient received chest tube drainage and intrathoracic endostatin for pleural effusion. A follow-up CT scan (April 2024) showed SD (Figure S2C) and a PET CT scan (October 2024) demonstrated a partial response (Figure S2D). The patient had an OS > 9 months from the start of T-DXd therapy.

Case 5

A 67-year-old female with a diagnosis of metastatic lung cancer was referred to us in February 2023. Treatment timeline is shown in Figure S3A. A chest CT scan revealed an occupying lesion in the superior and middle lobes of the right lung, involvement of the adjacent pleura and scant right pleural effusion, with LN, liver and bone metastases (Figure S3B). Percutaneous lung biopsy showed lung adenocarcinoma. The patient was diagnosed with metastatic lung cancer (cT4N3M1c IVB). PD-L1 TPS was 2% and NGS identified a non-frameshifting insertion mutation (P.A775_G776insYVMA) in HER2 exon 20ins (abundance 46.8%) and MDM2 amplification.

Apart from local radiation therapy, the patient received multiple lines of systemic therapy including trastuzumab (8 mg/kg, day 1 Q3W) and albumin-bound paclitaxel (5.3 mg/kg, day 1 and 8), leading to SD. Follow-up CT scan (May 2023) showed moderate pleural effusion and liver metastases. Trastuzumab and paclitaxel were discontinued and the patient received disitamab vedotin (60 mg Q2W) from May 2023 for 3 cycles, with anlotinib (60 mg Q2W) added one week later. A follow-up CT scan showed disease progression (Figure S3C). She received T-DXd 5.4 mg/kg Q3W from July 4, 2023, for 11 cycles, with anlotinib (8 mg/d for 2 weeks of 3-week cycles) added for the first 2 cycles. The patient had a partial response after 2 cycles of T-DXd with shrinkage of primary tumor lesions and plural and liver metastases and reduction of right pleural effusion (Figure S3D). The patient had a PFS of 10.5 months. In August 2024, the patient had increased episodes of coughing; she started treatment with pyrotinib, anlotinib, and adebrelimab (1200 mg Q3W) in combination with radiotherapy (6 Gy/3F) for the primary tumor and liver metastasis. A follow-up assessment in November 2024 showed that the patient had a progressive disease (Figure S3E). The patient died in September 2025, with an OS > 31 months.

Discussion

The efficacy and safety of T-DXd were established in pivotal trials [5, 8]. Our case series provided further evidence of its efficacy and safety of 2nd or later line T-DXd in patients with metastatic NSCLC carrying HER2 exon 20ins. Three of the five cases attained a complete or partial response and two had a SD as their best overall response. The median OS of the five patients was 15 months (range 9–41 months). The OS of the first case was over 41 months despite suspected brain metastasis when T-DXd treatment was initiated. Brain metastases are common in lung cancer and carries a dismal outcome despite standard treatment. The case highlights the remarkable durability of T‑DXd in patients with HER2 exon 20in–positive metastatic lung adenocarcinoma, despite prior intolerance to chemotherapy and pyrotinib. Notably, the patient had stage IVA disease with CNS metastasis at baseline and received T-DXd in the 3rd line setting. HER2 exon 20ins are typically associated with poor prognosis and limited therapeutic options. The patient’s long survival underscores the clinical relevance of T‑DXd in this setting, particularly when conventional chemotherapy and tyrosine kinase inhibitors are poorly tolerated. Serial NGS in the first case showed declining tissue HER2 exon 20ins abundance and clearance in plasma circulating DNA, suggesting molecular response concordant with radiographic stability. Taken together, these two patients illustrate that long survival is achievable in HER2 exon 20ins–positive NSCLC when treatment is tailored to individual patients by incorporating sequential targeted and multimodal strategies.

Adverse events during T-DXd treatment are expected and overall manageable [8–10]. ILD, which was reported in 13% of the patients receiving T-DXd [9], occurred in one patient (Case No. 2) during T-DM1 treatment. Importantly, the patient was successfully challenged with T-DXd, achieving a complete response and resulting in an OS of more than 15 months, with no recurrence of ILD during prolonged treatment. This observation is clinically significant, as pivotal trials of T‑DXd excluded patients with a history of ILD, leaving a gap in evidence for challenge scenarios. Permanent discontinuation of T-DXd is recommended for grade 2 or higher ILD [11, 12]. Anecdotal reports showed successful rechallenge with T-DXd in patients with ILD [13, 14]. Our case and others suggest that second or later‑line T-DXd can be considered in patients with prior T-DM1–induced ILD. However, currently, no conclusive evidence is available.

Anlotinib has demonstrated efficacy in 3rd line setting for advanced NSCLC [15] but combination therapy of T-DXd and anlotinib for NSCLC has not been previously reported. In our series, the addition of anlotinib after progression in Case No. 1 may has further contributed to disease stabilization by targeting angiogenesis and complementing HER2 blockade in the patient. Another patient had an OS of 31 months (Case No. 5). Sequential HER2‑targeted therapies, including trastuzumab, disitamab vedotin, anlotinib, and ultimately T‑DXd, provided meaningful disease control, with T‑DXd delivering a partial response and a PFS of 10.5 months. One patient (Case No. 2) also received antiangiogenic therapy with anlotinib and had an OS of at least 15 months. The durability of survival highlights the potential of T-DXd to extend outcomes even in heavily pretreated patients, while the integration of anti‑angiogenic therapy and radiotherapy contributed to symptom relief and local control.

One patient (Case No. 3) had coexisting EGFR 20ins. This case underscores the therapeutic complexity of co-occurring EGFR and HER2 exon 20ins mutations in metastatic NSCLC. EGFR exon 20ins-positive tumors are resistant to standard EGFR TKIs while HER2 exon 20in represents a distinct driver requiring HER2‑directed therapy. The patient failed multiple EGFR‑ and HER2‑TKIs but achieved a partial response and over 12 months of survival with T‑DXd, confirming HER2 as the dominant oncogenic driver. This outcome supports T‑DXd as an effective option in HER2‑mutant NSCLC, even in complex mutational contexts where EGFR‑directed agents are ineffective.

The treatment of HER2 mutant NSCLC is expected to evolve from monotherapy to combination therapy. The patients in this report had variable PD-L1 expressions (TPS 1% to > 30%). Current evidence is scarce on the efficacy of immune therapy for HER2 mutant NSCLC, with findings from retrospective studies pointing to poor response among patients with HER2 mutant NSCLC [16]. The tumor tissues expressed high levels of PD-L1 in case No. 3 but immunochemotherapy was not satisfactory while T-DXd led to an overall survival of 9 months. It remains to be seen whether the combination of T-DXd with an immune checkpoint inhibitor will be synergistic in improving the clinical outcomes of patients with HER2 mutant NSCLC. Clinical trials are ongoing that assessed the clinical activity of T-DXd in combination with pembrolizumab in the treatment of patients with pretreated HER2-expressing/mutated NSCLC [17].

In conclusion, in this small case series, T-DXd demonstrated encouraging antitumor activity and manageable safety in heavily pretreated patients with HER2 mutated, metastatic NSCLC. Although selected patients had durable benefits, the findings are anecdotal and should be interpreted with caution. Larger real‑world cohorts and prospective studies are needed to validate the consistency, durability, and safety of T‑DXd in routine clinical practice.

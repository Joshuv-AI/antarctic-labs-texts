Molybdenum enhances soybean growth and seed quality by modulating osmoregulation, ion homeostasis, and antioxidant activity under saline–alkaline conditions
Authors: Tayyaba Zulfiqar, Shakeel Ahmad, Jianrong Shao, Haichang Yang, Jian Wei, Fenghua Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSoil salinization is a major global challenge that limits crop productivity by inhibiting plant growth, nutritional quality and decreasing yields. This study examined the effect of foliar application of molybdenum (Mo) on two soybean varieties, HN517 (salt-sensitive) and DS118 (salt-tolerant) under saline-alkali soil conditions. Mo was applied via foliar spraying at four concentrations 0 (control), 70, 140, and 210 g ha−1 referred as CK, MO1, MO2, and MO3, respectively. These treatments were employed to investigate the influence of Mo on soybean physiological growth parameters, antioxidant defense mechanisms, osmotic regulation, ionic homeostasis, and nutrient accumulation.ResultsResults showed that foliar application of Mo treatment MO3 significantly improved plant height, root biomass, and total biomass by 39.24%, 43.67%, and 56.01% in HN517, and by 36.95%, 28.98%, and 108.0% in DS118 variety of soybean. The activities of antioxidant enzymes including superoxide dismutase, catalase, peroxidase, and nitrate reductase, increased by 17.75%, 20.90%, 21.42%, and 13.23% in HN517, and by 24.63%, 27.01%, 18.75%, and 13.44% in DS118, respectively, compared with control. Simultaneously, the levels of hydrogen peroxide, superoxide anion, and malondialdehyde decreased by 26.45% and 35.02%, 23.11% and 22.93%, and 33.30% and 38.38% in HN517 and DS118, respectively, indicating a reduction in oxidative damage. Mo application also enhanced osmotic regulation by increasing proline, soluble protein, and soluble sugar contents by 21.47%, 18.75%, and 16.74% in HN517, and by 19.12%, 23.34%, and 19.69% in DS118. Furthermore, Mo enhanced nutrient uptake, including nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and Mo in both varieties. It also improved the K⁺/Na⁺ ratio, mitigating ion toxicity and enhancing stress tolerance. This improvement contributed to higher nutrient acquisition and antioxidant capacity contributed to higher protein and oil content, leading to increased crop yield under saline-alkali field conditions.ConclusionsThese findings concluded that Mo application to the foliage improves soybean resilience, growth, yield, and nutritional quality of soybean under saline alkali field conditions.Graphical Abstract

Background

Soil salinization is increasingly prevalent global issue that severely impedes crop growth and productivity, particularly in regions facing rising salt accumulation. Despite continued mitigation efforts, the extant of salt-affected lands is expanding threatening global food security [1]. Currently, the world has approximately 954 million hectares of saline-alkaline land, with China alone accounting for approximately 110 million hectares, roughly 10% of the total global land [2]. In addition, by reducing arable land and decreasing crop production soil salinity adversely affects soil biodiversity by retreating beneficial microorganisms that are essential for ecosystem stability and plant health [3]. Without effective intervention, these stressors will continue to disrupt plant metabolism and compromise global food security [4]. High soil alkalinity inhibits plant growth by increasing soil pH and stimulates ethylene production in roots, which disrupts nutrient uptake and ultimately reduces plant growth [5].

Soybean (Glycine max L.) is particularly susceptible to salinity and alkali stress, which adversely affect its growth and yield at various developmental stages. Therefore, developing soybean genotypes with enhanced tolerance to saline environment is a critical priority [6]. An effective strategy to improve salt tolerance in soybeans involves mapping the genomic regions responsible for salinity resistance [7]. Furthermore, improving soil fertility and optimizing nutrient management under saline alkali conditions plays a critical role in mitigating stress. Therefore, molybdenum (Mo) is a relatively abundant element in the Earth’s crust, is often neglected in fertilization programs due to its presence in trace amounts. While Mo is usually applied through solid fertilizers at seedling. However, foliar application of Mo showed its capacity to enhance carbon metabolism and improve stress tolerance in various crops, including soybeans [8].

Foliar application of Mo supports key physiological processes by activating enzymes including nitrate reductase and nitrogenase, which regulate nitrogen assimilation and biological nitrogen fixation (BNF) in soybean plants [9]. BNF is a vital process in which Rhizobium bacteria establish symbiotic relationships with soybean roots, enabling atmospheric nitrogen fixation and decreasing the dependence on synthetic fertilizers [10]. Mo serves as a vital part of enzymes that enable nitrogen absorption, assimilation and transport which directly influences plant growth and yield [4]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that Mo combined with Rhizobium inoculation enhances plant growth and nitrogen fixation, ultimately increasing the yield and quality of soybean under stress conditions [11, 12]. Furthermore, Mo application combined with zinc and iron micronutrients, induces chlorophyll synthesis, increases photosynthetic pigments and improves seed protein and oil content [13].

Another key factor in alleviating saline stress is maintaining ionic homeostasis, particularly the cytosolic K⁺/Na⁺ ratio. A high K⁺/Na⁺ ratio is important for normal plant growth, as it contributes to osmotic balance, membrane potential regulation, and enzyme activation [14]. However, excessive Na⁺ influx disrupts this balance, leading to osmotic stress, reduced root growth, and decreased nutrient translocation under saline alkali stress [15]. Potassium (K⁺) deficiency, often induced by the activation of K⁺ efflux channels during stress, further interrupts ionic stability, promoting salinity-induced damage [16]. Therefore, restoring ion balance and mitigating oxidative damage are important for improving plant resilience to saline conditions.

Molybdenum application increases the antioxidant enzyme activities, such as superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), and catalase (CAT), which help to mitigate oxidative stress by reducing reactive oxygen species (ROS), including H2O2 and O2⁻ and maintain cell membrane [17, 18]. This improved antioxidant defense system contributes to increased salt tolerance and promotes plant growth under saline alkaline soil conditions. Additionally, Mo application regulates key metabolic pathways involved in nitrogen metabolism, facilitating more efficient nitrogen assimilation, protein synthesis, and overall crop productivity under saline soil conditions [19–21]. Mo also plays an essential role in N metabolism by serving as a cofactor for nitrate reductase (NR) and nitrogenase enzymes [21]. Although Mo fertilization is known to be important, its influence on plant metabolism and field-level productivity remains inadequately characterized.

Therefore the significance of Mo in soybean farming is mainly important in saline alkaline soil conditions, where nutrient availability and uptake are constrained. This study aims to evaluate the effects of foliar application of Mo on various morphological and physiochemical attributes of soybean varieties. We hypothesized that exogenous application of Mo enhance soybean growth by improving antioxidant enzyme activities, nitrogen assimilation, and NR activity, ultimately leading to increased biomass accumulation and yield under saline alkali field conditions. The study are expected to contribute valuable insights for optimizing micronutrient management strategies to improve soybean production in saline alkali soil conditions.

Materials and methods

Study site and experimental design

The field study was conducted in Shehezi City, located on the southern fringe of the Gurbantunggut Desert in the Junggar Basin of Xinjiang, China (44°19′13″ N, 86°08′13″ E). During the experimental period, the mean maximum and minimum air temperatures were 29.3 °C and 16.9 °C, respectively, with an overall average of 23.1 °C. Total precipitation recorded over the same period was 123 mm. The site was selected for its typical saline-alkaline soil conditions, which present significant challenges for crop cultivation. Basal fertilization was applied before sowing at the rate of 150 kg ha−1 (N) urea, 120 kg ha−1 diammonium phosphate (P), and 60 kg ha−1 sulfate of potash (K). The treatments were arranged in a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with three replications. Each plot covered an area of 5 m², and the planting pattern was mulched drip irrigation system with wide and narrow row spacing. The plastic film width was 2.05 m, with a row spacing of 20 cm (narrow rows) and 60 cm (wide rows), a plant spacing of 15 cm, and a planting density was 210,000 plants ha−1. All agronomic practices were performed manually.

Experimental treatments and site characteristics

Two soybean varieties, Heinong 517 (HN517) salt-sensitive and Dongsheng 118 (DS118) salt-tolerant were subjected to four concentrations of Mo (0, 70, 140, and 210 g ha⁻¹) applied as ammonium molybdate dissolved in a 60% aqueous solution. The treatments were designated as follows: CK (control, 0 g ha⁻¹), MO1 (70 g ha⁻¹), MO2 (140 g ha⁻¹), and MO3 (210 g ha⁻¹). Applications were performed during dry days with an average temperature of 23.1 °C (ranging from 16.93 to 29.27 °C) and foliar treatments were applied in the evening to minimize evaporation and improve nutrient absorption efficiency. The foliar solution pH was maintained between 6.2 and 6.5, and a non-ionic surfactant (Tween-20 at 0.1% v/v) was added to reduce surface tension and enhance leaf adherence [22, 23]. Treatments were applied to the foliage at the R1 growth stage (30 days after planting), a critical developmental phase for nutrient uptake, thereby optimizing the effectiveness of Mo supplementation. Soybeans were sown in mid-May and harvested in September, with a total growth duration of approximately 110–120 days from sowing to maturity. The initial soil properties at the experimental site were as follows: alkaline nitrogen, 56.88 mg·kg⁻¹; organic matter, 13.93 g·kg⁻¹; and available potassium, 82.82 mg·kg⁻¹. The soil exhibited moderate salinity (EC = 7.4 dS/m) and a slightly alkaline pH (8.85), with total salt content (8.06 g/kg) indicating relatively low nutrient availability. These conditions are expected to significantly influence nutrient uptake and plant growth, emphasizing the importance of abiotic stress adaptation in arid regions.

Determination of growth attributes

Soybean growth was monitored at 40, 55, 75, and 120 days after sowing (DAS) by sampling three randomly selected plants from each treatment. Each plant was separated into roots and shoots to determine total dry matter accumulation. The samples were thoroughly cleaned and then oven dried at 80 ℃ until constant weight was achieved. Total dry matter was calculated by summing the dry weights of roots and shoots [24].

Determination of chlorophyll contents

Soybean leaf samples were collected from the fully expanded third trifoliate leaf (counting from the top of the main stem) for each treatment [25]. The leaves were immediately weighed and placed into 15 mL tubes containing 10 mL of 95% (v/v) ethanol for preservation. The tubes were then kept in the dark at room temperature for 24 h until complete discoloration occurred. Subsequently, the absorbance of each sample were measured at 665 and 649 nm using a Spectra Max Plus 384 Microplate Reader (Molecular Devices, CA, USA).

Determination of soybean antioxidant enzyme and reactive oxygen species (ROS)

The SOD activity was measured based on the inhibition of nitro blue tetrazolium (NBT) photochemical reduction, following the method of Kalimuthu et al. [26]. The reaction mixture contained 125 mm of a potassium-phosphate buffer (pH 7.8), 3 mM MgSO4, 3.1 mM EDTA, 2% polyvinyl polypyrrolidone (PVPP), methionine (130 mM), riboflavin (600 µM), NBT (22.5 mM), and plant extract. The mixture was illuminated for 15 min and absorbance was measured at 560 nm. CAT activity was determined by measuring the decrease in absorbance at 240 nm due to the decomposition of H₂O₂ [26]. The reaction mixture comprised 125 mM potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.0), 100 mM H₂O₂ and the plant extract. POD activity was assessed by monitoring the increase in absorbance at 290 nm. Nitrate reductase (NR) activity was determined with slight modifications to the method of Sarioğlu [27]. A 0.2 g fresh plant sample was incubated with 5 mL of 0.1 M phosphate buffer for one hr. After incubation, 0.4 mL of the extract was filtered, and 3 mL of color reagent was added to the filtrate. The optical density was measured at 540 nm using a UV spectrophotometer. ROS including H2O2 and O2⁻ were evaluated using the method described by Ali et al. [28]. Leaf samples were homogenized in phosphate buffer to measure the levels of H2O2 and O2•⁻. A colorimetric assay kit from Suzhou Grace Biotechnology Co., Ltd. was used to determine H2O2 (G0112W) and O2⁻ (G0116W) contents. Malondialdehyde (MDA) content was determined following the method of Ahmad et al. [29]. Leaf tissues were homogenized in 10% trichloroacetic acid (TCA), centrifuged at 9000×g for 20 min and the supernatant was mixed with 0.6% thiobarbituric acid (TBA). The mixture was heated at 100 °C for 20 min, cooled in an ice bath, and the absorbance was measured at 532 and 450 nm.

Determination of soybean osmotic adjustment substances

Free proline content was measured using the acidic ninhydrin colorimetric method described by Borsait et al. [30]. Soybean leaves (0.1 g) were homogenized in 3% sulfosalicylic acid, heated for 10 min, and centrifuged at 4000 × g for 10 min. The supernatant was combined with glacial acetic acid and acid ninhydrin reagent, incubated at 100 °C for 30 min, extracted with toluene, and the absorbance was measured at 520 nm. Soluble protein (SP) content was determined using the Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 method [31]. Leaf samples 0.1 g were homogenized in distilled water, incubated for 1 h and centrifuged at 4000×g for 20 min. The supernatant was then mixed with Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 reagent, and the absorbance was measured at 595 nm. Soluble sugar content was determined using the anthrone colorimetric method [32], where 0.1 g of sample were homogenized in distilled water, boiled for 30 min filtrated, and reacted with anthrone-ethyl acetate and sulfuric acid, and the absorbance was measured at 630 nm.

Determination of N, P, K, and mo contents in plant

Three plants were randomly selected from each plot at physiological maturity. The samples were oven dried at 70 °C for 72 h before grinding for nutrient analysis. Nitrogen (N) concentration was determined by using the Kjeldahl method. Phosphorus (P) content was quantified by the vanadomolybdate–molybdenum blue colorimetric method at 880 nm following microwave-digested samples (0.20 g tissue, 4:1 HNO₃:H₂O, 160 °C, 45 min) were analyzed following the protocol of Wieczorek et al. and Sharma et al. [33, 34], while potassium (K) level was measured using Flame Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy (FAAS) with a Varian 250 Plus instrument. Molybdenum (Mo) was analyzed by digesting 0.2 g of dried plant tissue in 5 mL of a 4:1 HNO₃:H₂O mixture at 160 °C for 45 min, followed by quantification using inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES) with a PerkinElmer Optima 8300 (Massachusetts, USA). Calibration curves were prepared using TraceCERT® single-element standards (Mo, P, K) at 1,000 mg L⁻¹, diluted in 2–5% HNO₃ to obtain multi-point calibration ranges (0.01–5 mg L⁻¹ for Mo; 1–100 mg L⁻¹ for P and K). Instrument wavelength accuracy was maintained using the manufacturer-recommended tuning solution (Agilent or PerkinElmer syringe-injected standard).

Determination of ion content

Ion content was determined by rinsing plants with deionized water, inactivating them at 105 °C for 2 h, and then oven-dried at 80 °C until a constant mass was achieved. A 0.1 g sample was digested with 2 mL of 10% HNO₃ using an ETHOS T microwave digestion system (Milestone, Italy). Ion concentrations were analyzed in supernatants using an Optima 8,000 ICP-OES DV Spectrometer (PerkinElmer, United States) [35].

Determination of yield, crude protein, and oil content

Five plants per plot were harvested, sun-dried, and threshed to assess seed yield. Yield plant−1 was determined by weighing the seeds, following the method described by Mao et al. [36]. Soybean samples were ground into fine powder, and immediate analysis were performed to quantify the crude protein and oil content. Total nitrogen content was determined using the Kjeldahl method, and crude protein percentage was calculated using a conversion factor of 6.25. Crude oil content was determined using a Soxhlet apparatus with hexane as the solvent (AOAC, 2016).

Statistical analysis

Data were statistically analyzed using Statistical Software 8.1. The Least Significant Difference (LSD) test was used to assess differences between treatments at a significance level of p ≤ 0.05. Correlation between the mean values of various parameters was assessed using R software with the special R-Studio package. All figures were prepared using Origin 2024 software (OriginLab, USA).

Results

Molybdenum improves soybean plant height and root biomass

Growth analysis showed that Mo application significantly influenced the growth attributes of both soybean varieties, HN517 (salt-sensitive) and DS118 (salt-tolerant) (Figs. 1 and 2). The most remarkable effects were observed at MO3 treatments, where soybean plants exhibited significant improvements in several key growth parameters compared with the control (CK) treatment at 125 days after sowing (DAS). Under MO3 treatment, plant height increased by 39.24% in HN517 and 36.95% in DS118 (Fig. 1), while root biomass increased by 43.67% and 28.98%, respectively. Total plant biomass (TPB) also showed significant enhancement, with increases of 56.01% in HN517 and 108.0% in DS118 (Fig. 2).

Molybdenum increases soybean chlorophyll content in saline-alkaline soil

Foliar application of Mo significantly increased chlorophyll a content in both HN517 and DS118 soybean varieties compared with CK. The highest increase was observed in the MO3 treatment, with a chlorophyll a content increased by 94.23% in HN517 and 73.04% in DS118 (Fig. 3). Statistical analysis also indicated significant enhancements in chlorophyll b content across all Mo treatments, with MO3 exhibiting the highest levels by 86.25% in HN517 and 51.02% in DS118 compared with CK (Fig. 3). Similarly, carotenoid content were significantly elevated in both varieties following Mo application, with the MO3 treatment showing the greatest increase by 51.04% in HN517 and by 38.74% in DS118 (Fig. 3). Moreover, chlorophyll A + B content was markedly increased in both varieties, with the MO3 treatment resulting to the highest values by 92.79% in HN517 and 68.98% in DS118 compared with CK (Fig. 3).

Effect of molybdenum on antioxidant enzyme and nitrate reductase activities in soybean

Molybdenum application significantly enhanced the activities of key antioxidant enzymes, including CAT, POD, and SOD in both soybean varieties. MO3 treatment dramatically enhanced SOD activity by 17.75% in HN517 and 24.63% in DS118, POD activity by 20.90% in HN517 and 27.01% in DS118 and CAT activity improved by 21.42% in HN517 and 18.75% in DS118 compared with control (Fig. 4). Additionally, Mo significantly enhanced NR activity in both varieties compared to the CK. In HN517, MO2 and MO3 treatments led to NR increases of 11.91% and 13.23%, respectively, relative to CK, although the difference was not statistically significant. For DS118, NR activity increased by 12.74% under MO2 and by 13.50% under MO3 (Fig. 4).

Molybdenum reduces ROS (H2O2 and O2) and MDA accumulation under saline-alkaline soil

Application of Mo significantly reduced the accumulation ROS and MDA in soybean plants grown in saline alkaline soil conditions. The results indicated that the MO3 treatment dramatically reduced H2O2 content by 26.45% in HN517 and 35.02% in the DS118 variety compared with CK (Fig. 5). Similarly, O2 levels decreased by 23.11% in HN517 and 22.93% in DS118 compared with CK (Fig. 5). Furthermore, MO3 treatment resulted a significant reduction in MDA levels by 33.30% in HN517 and by 38.38% in DS118 compared with CK. This reduction suggests that MO3 treatment effectively mitigated oxidative stress in both soybean varieties under saline-alkali soil conditions (Fig. 5).

Effects of Mo treatment on osmotic adjustment substances in soybean

The application of Mo significantly influenced the accumulation of key osmotic substances in both soybean varieties. Particularly, MO3, treatment resulted a marked increase in proline (Pro), soluble proteins (SP), and soluble sugar (SS) contents in both soybean varieties compared with other treatments (Fig. 6). Effect of molybdenum application on leaf ion homeostasis in soybean

The application of Mo influenced ion homeostasis, particularly the concentrations of sodium (Na⁺) and potassium (K⁺), as well as the K⁺/Na⁺ ratio, in both soybean varieties. Mo application exhibited dose-dependent improvements in ion balance, with the most significant increase observed in the MO3 treatment. In the HN517 variety, K⁺ concentrations increased by 15.53%, while Na⁺ levels decreased by 22.66%, resulting in a 49.26% rise in the K⁺/Na⁺ ratio (Fig. 8). Similarly, the DS118 variety demonstrated an 11.17% increase in K⁺, a 14.27% reduction in Na⁺, and a 29.79% improvement in the K⁺/Na⁺ ratio. These findings suggest that high K⁺ uptake and Na⁺ reduction are key mechanisms by which soybean plants maintain ionic balance under saline-alkali soil conditions. Furthermore, HN517 exhibited a more pronounced response to Mo application, indicating genotype-specific differences in nutrient uptake efficiency. Among the treatments tested, MO2 emerged as the optimal treatment for improving ion homeostasis and stress tolerance (Fig. 8).

Molybdenum improved crude oil content, protein content, and yield in soybean

Application of Mo significantly improved crude oil content, protein content, and yield in both soybean varieties. Among the various application rates, the MO3 treatment was the most effective across all parameters. In HN517 variety, MO3 resulted in a 5.78% increase in crude oil content, 4.05% increase in crude protein content, and a 19.41% increase in yield (kg) compared with control (Fig. 8). Similarly, DS118 variety exhibited a 4.43% increase in crude oil content, 3.39% increase in crude protein content, and 75.01% increase in yield under MO3 treatment. Both varieties showed consistent improvements in protein and oil content with increasing Mo concentrations, while the highest yield was consistently observed under the MO3 treatment. Notably, the salt-tolerant variety DS118 produced a higher yield than HN517. These findings demonstrate that Mo application enhances the nutritional quality and productivity of soybean varieties under saline soil conditions (Fig. 8).

Discussion

Saline-alkali soil significantly impairs soybean growth and productivity by inducing osmotic stress and ion toxicity sodium ion (Na⁺) toxicity, and elevated soil pH, which disrupt cellular processes, hinder nutrient uptake, and accelerate leaf senescence [37]. The present study confirm the detrimental effects of saline-alkali stress on soybean growth and development. Mo plays a key role in improving the soybean growth and productivity, with varying effect observed between salt-tolerant and sensitive varieties in saline alkali soil conditions [38]. Salt-alkali stress, primarily triggered by Na2CO3 and NaHCO3, adversely affects the plant growth attributes, leaf ultrastructure, photosynthetic pigments, nutrition quality and yield [39, 40]. However, application of Mo significantly mitigated the growth inhibition induced by saline-alkali stress. Saline-alkali soil severely impairs soybean growth and productivity by inducing osmotic stress, Na⁺ toxicity, and elevated soil pH [41]. These disrupt cellular functions, hinder nutrient uptake, and accelerate leaf senescence. However, the application of Mo has been shown to mitigate these adverse effects. Specifically, Mo treatment (MO3) significantly enhanced soybean growth and productivity, as indicated by increased plant height, fresh and dry biomass, and grain yield. These improvements are attributed to Mo role in enhancing nitrogen fixation, optimizing nutrient uptake, and improving photosynthetic pigments. Chlorophylls and carotenoids are essential photosynthetic pigments in plants, facilitating light absorption and energy conversion essential for growth and productivity [13]. This study demonstrated that the application of Mo significantly increased chlorophyll and carotenoid content under saline-alkali conditions. The enhanced chlorophyll content is likely due to Mo involvement in the biosynthesis of the Mo cofactor, which is crucial for the activity of key enzymes involved in chlorophyll synthesis [42].

Additionally, application of Mo led to reduce lipid peroxidation through potentially enhancing antioxidant enzyme activities in soybean plants, thereby facilitating ROS scavenging and providing protection against oxidative stress. Plants naturally mobilize intrinsic defense mechanisms to counteract ROS, including the activation of antioxidant enzymes and the synthesis of osmotic adjustment compounds [43–45]. This study investigates the role of Mo in regulating lipid peroxidation, ROS and antioxidant enzyme activity in two soybean varieties, as well as its contribution to improving plant tolerance to oxidative stress under saline-alkaline soil conditions. Moreover, the finding highlights that plants mitigate oxidative damage by modulating the activity of antioxidant enzymes and NR activity, thereby enhancing stress tolerance under salt-alkali conditions [17, 46]. Specifically, Mo application induced the activation of key antioxidant enzymes, including SOD, POD, and CAT, along with elevated levels of proline, soluble sugars, and proteins (Figs. 4 and 6). Mo also promoted osmotic adjustment and reduced the ROS and MDA accumulation (Fig. 5). These findings demonstrate that foliar application of Mo treatment particularly, MO3 reduced oxidative damage and improving the stress tolerance of plants under saline-alkali soil conditions. Furthermore, the present study showed that foliar application of Mo modulated NR activity of soybean under saline alkaline soil conditions. Previous studies have demonstrated that Mo significantly improved leaf N concentration, as it is a critical component of the molybdenum cofactor in nitrate NR, facilitating the conversion of nitrate to nitrite and thereby increasing nitrogen assimilation and chlorophyll synthesis [47].

Molybdenum is an important micronutrient that improves nitrogen metabolism, promoting nitrogen uptake, seed production, and protein content of soybeans [48]. This study revealed that exogenous application of Mo improves plant growth and nitrogen assimilation and overall productivity under saline–alkali soil conditions. Beyond enhancing growth, Mo also induces stress tolerance, making it essential for maintaining plant health. These findings are consistent with the previous studies that demonstrated improvement in plant nutrient profile and confirmed Mo role in boosting plants resilience to stress tolerance [24]. The observed increase in leaf nitrogen concentrations is attributed to Mo involvement in key enzymatic processes responsible for nitrogen absorption, transport, and conversion within the plant system [49].

Saline-alkali stress adversely affects key enzymes involved in nitrogen absorption, transport, and conversion, resulting Na+ accumulation that inhibits the absorption of K+ and other essential nutrients. This disruption leads to an imbalanced K+/Na+ ratio and a disrupts overall ion homeostasis in plants [50, 51]. Previous studies have demonstrated that enhanced K⁺ and reduced Na⁺ accumulation in soybean leaves are critical for resistance to saline-alkali soil conditions [52]. In the present study, Mo treatment significantly reduced Na+ accumulation while increasing K+ content in soybean plants. Specifically, foliar Mo application at the rate of 210 g ha⁻¹ inhibited Na+ influx into cells, thereby maintaining the ion balance. It is speculated that Mo may stimulate rhizobial activity, enhance root nodulation, and facilitate root-mediated exclusion of excess Na⁺ while promoting K⁺ uptake, ultimately improving the K⁺/Na⁺ ratio [53]. Furthermore, due to its strong absorption properties, Mo may directly reduce Na+ uptake in soybean. Consistently, this study showed that foliar application of Mo significantly reduced leaf Na+ content in both soybean varieties under saline-alkali soil conditions, indicating that Mo supplementation limits Na+ accumulation in plant tissues (Fig. 8).

Application of micronutrients is widely recognized as an effective strategy for improving crop yields and maintaining adequate nutrient availability in soils deficient in essential elements [54, 55]. This strategy is especially beneficial in regions with micronutrient-poor soils, where Mo application can restore nutrient balance and improve crop performance [24]. The present study demonstrated that Mo at the rate of 210 g ha⁻¹ significantly increased protein content in soybean seeds without compromising oil content or yield components (Fig. 5). This finding is significant, as it indicate that Mo application could enhance both the nutritional and economic value of soybean crops with implications for human nutrition and animal feed industries. Previous studies have reported a common trade-off in improved soybean varieties, where higher seed oil content is typically associated with reduced protein levels [56]. However, our findings suggest that Mo treatments can effectively balance these two important traits, thereby improving overall seed quality. By promoting nitrogen metabolism, Mo enhances nitrogen fixation, which subsequently supports higher protein synthesis in seeds and improves their nutritional profile.

This study provides valuable insights into the role of foliar Mo application in improving soybean performance under saline alkali soil conditions and establishes a foundation for future research aimed to developing salt-tolerant soybean varieties with optimized physiological processes. Mo plays a key role in promoting plant resilience and increasing yield by improving nitrogen metabolism, increasing plant biomass, and strengthening the antioxidant defense mechanism by reducing the ROS and MDA accumulation. These findings suggest that Mo fertilization as an effective strategy for improving soybean productivity, especially in salt-alkali soils. Furthermore, this approach has the potential to contribute to the long-term sustainability of agricultural systems in challenging environments, offering a viable solution to the dual challenges of soil salinity and micronutrient deficiency in regions dependent on soybean cultivation. The findings of this study suggest that foliar molybdenum application is an effective strategy for improving soybean productivity under saline-alkaline soil conditions. To ensure broader applicability and confirm reproducibility, future studies should involve multi-year and multi-location trials to assess the consistency of molybdenum-induced improvements in plant growth, nutrient uptake, and stress tolerance across diverse agro-climatic environments.

Conclusion

This study highlights the pivotal role of foliar application of Mo in mitigating the adverse effects of saline-alkali soil conditions and improving the growth attributes, yield, and nutritional quality of soybean varieties. Application Mo at the rate of 210 g ha⁻¹ significantly increased growth attributes, antioxidant defense mechanisms, NR activity, and osmotic adjustment, by reducing oxidative stress and maintaining ion homeostasis in both salt-sensitive and tolerant soybean varieties under saline soil conditions. Furthermore, Mo treatment improved nutrient uptake, increasing the concentrations of N, P, K, and Mo levels in soybean leaves and seeds, which contributed to higher protein and oil content. Particularly, the salt-tolerant variety DS118, showed superior performance, highlighting the effectiveness of Mo application in improving stress resilience and productivity under saline alkali soil conditions. Overall, these findings conclude that foliar application of Mo as an effective strategy to increase soybean growth, yield and nutritional quality in saline-alkali soils, thereby supporting crop resilience, improving nutritional quality, and promoting sustainable agricultural practices under saline alkali soil conditions.

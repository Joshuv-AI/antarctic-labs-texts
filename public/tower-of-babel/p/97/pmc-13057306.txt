A hybrid optimal feature selection and Conv-LSTM model (OFSCL) for short-term energy demand forecasting in distribution substations of Ahvaz, Iran
Authors: Mehdi Mohammadian Mehr, Hossein Farzin, Elaheh Mashhour
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study introduces and evaluates the Optimal Feature Selection Convolutional LSTM (OFSCL) model, a hybrid deep learning architecture designed for accurate short-term energy demand forecasting at distribution substations using real-world data. Unlike traditional statistical models that struggle with non-linear and high-dimensional patterns, OFSCL integrates automatic feature selection within convolutional layers and employs LSTM units to effectively capture temporal dependencies. This architecture achieves superior predictive accuracy, robustness to noise, and adaptability across varying demand patterns, outperforming classical machine learning approaches and advanced deep learning models such as LSTM and CNN. After extensive hyperparameter tuning, OFSCL achieved an R² of 90%, an MAE of 0.55 MWh, and an RMSE of 0.74 MWh, demonstrating strong forecasting performance. The model captures both spatial and temporal dynamics in load data. Additionally, gradient-based sensitivity analysis identifies air temperature, month, and relative humidity as key contributors to forecasting accuracy, enhancing robustness against environmental variability and supporting informed feature prioritization.

Introduction

Motivation

Electrical Energy forecasting is an essential task for the optimal operation of electrical power systems. Distribution Substations being critical components in the power distribution network, are responsible for converting voltage levels and ensuring energy delivery to consumers. The Energy on Distribution Substations varies due to factors such as time of day, weather conditions, consumer behavior, and seasonal demand fluctuations1–3. Accurate Short-Term Forecast of Energy Demand in Distribution Substation (ST-FED-DS) is crucial for preventing transformer overloads, enhancing energy distribution efficiency, optimizing grid operation, and ensuring system reliability4,5. However, ST-FED-DS is a challenging task due to the non-linear, time-varying, and complex nature of the data. Traditional statistical forecasting models, such as Auto-Regressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) or exponential smoothing, fail to capture the intricate dependencies present in ST-FED-DS patterns. These methods are limited in their ability to model non-linear relationships and account for long-term dependencies6,7. As the demand for electricity continues to rise, the need for more advanced, accurate, and adaptive forecasting techniques has become more critical8. In recent years, machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) models have emerged as powerful tools for addressing the limitations of traditional methods9–11. ML models, such as decision tree, k-nearest neighbor (KNN), and multi-layer perceptron (MLP), have been proven effective in predicting complex relationships within data. Meanwhile, deep learning models, such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), have shown great promise in handling sequential, time-series data due to their ability to capture temporal dependencies and hierarchical features12–14. This study aims to investigate and compare the performance of various ML and DL models in the context of ST-FED-DS. The evaluated models include Decision Trees, KNN, MLPs, LSTM, CNN, and the Optimal Feature Selection Convolution LSTM (OFSCL). In addition, the proposed model is compared with several advanced deep learning architectures such as CNN-LSTM, Bi-LSTM, Stacked LSTM, LSTM with Attention, and Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN). The comparison highlights the model’s ability to generalize effectively to new and unseen datasets. The main objective is to determine which models offer the best trade-off between prediction accuracy, computational efficiency, and overall generalization capability.

Literature survey

Forecasting electrical energy is critical for efficient power system operation, stability, and reliability. Accurate energy forecasting helps in reducing operational costs, optimizing resource distribution, and improving the power grid’s overall performance15. The complexity of forecasting electrical energy has led to the exploration of various methodologies, ranging from traditional statistical models to advanced ML and DL approaches8,16. These methods have demonstrated their potential in improving the forecasting accuracy, particularly when capturing complex patterns inherent in time-series data. In early electrical energy forecasting studies, traditional models such as ARIMA were used for predicting future loads17. However, these methods are limited in their ability to handle nonlinear and nonstationary data, which is characteristic of electrical load data that is influenced by factors like weather, holidays, and seasonal fluctuations18. ARIMA models, for example, often struggle to capture such complexities and perform poorly when it comes to high-dimensional data or multivariate time series19. To overcome the limitations of traditional methods, machine learning and deep learning models have gained popularity20. Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Random Forests (RF) have shown potential in capturing complex, nonlinear relationships within data. However, these models also have challenges in modeling temporal dependencies in energy data, which are essential for accurate forecasting in power systems12. Deep learning methods, particularly LSTM networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), have proven to be effective in capturing long-term dependencies and temporal patterns in time-series data. The GRU model outperformed both LSTM and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), achieving an R-squared value of 90.228% and minimizing errors (MAE: 0.00215, MAE: 0.03266)21. This shows the potential of deep learning models in achieving high forecasting accuracy, especially when working with large datasets that exhibit nonlinear behavior and long-term dependencies. Moreover, Ref22 proposed a feature extraction framework using convolutional(conv) layers to capture the temporal and dimensional relations in the electrical energy time series. This approach improved forecasting accuracy by reducing the Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) by 5% to 12%, showing the effectiveness of deep learning models when dealing with multivariate time series.

Hybrid models, which combine traditional techniques with advanced deep learning algorithms, have also been widely explored to improve forecasting accuracy. The hybrid approach combining ARIMA and LSTM models outperformed individual models like ARIMA, LSTM, and GRU, with a RMSE of 7.3523. The hybrid model’s ability to combine both traditional time series forecasting techniques with deep learning models allowed for more accurate load prediction, especially during peak consumption periods. Similarly, Ref24 utilized a combination of Multiple Linear Regression (MLR), Random Forests (RF), and ANN to predict electricity demand in Dubai, finding that ANN and RF models achieved over 97% accuracy. The study highlighted the effectiveness of ANN and RF models for both regional and national-scale predictions, making them suitable for deployment in utility companies. For practical applications in transformer load forecasting, the inclusion of weather variables and temporal factors (like holidays and weekends) has proven to enhance forecasting accuracy. The hybrid model comprised of CNN and LSTM networks to forecast short-term electrical load consumption showed a marked improvement in prediction accuracy, with RMSE values improving from 83.17% to 91.18% for German data and achieving 98.23% accuracy for Malaysian data, making it an excellent candidate for use in real-time forecasting13. Ref25 explored the integration of CNN and LSTM models for short-term load forecasting in Bangladesh power system. This hybrid approach achieved higher precision and accuracy compared to existing methods like RBF Networks and Extreme Gradient Boosting, showing the potential of deep learning models in handling complex forecasting tasks for real-world applications. The evolution of energy forecasting models has significantly progressed from traditional statistical methods to more advanced ML and DL techniques. This transition has led to the development of highly effective forecasting models such as GRU, LSTM, and CNN, each of which has shown exceptional performance in various applications. However, ST-FED-DS remains an underexplored domain, particularly when integrating real-time prediction and the sensitivity analysis of weather and temporal factors. In this regard, the present research fills this gap by introducing an innovative approach to ST-FED-DS. By incorporating real-time prediction capabilities and performing sensitivity analysis of weather and temporal variables, the proposed model significantly enhances forecasting accuracy. This advancement contributes to the improvement of power grid operations, making them more efficient, reliable, and responsive to dynamic conditions. To further contextualize the feature selection approach, recent studies have demonstrated the importance of selecting relevant input variables for improving model performance. For instance, an ensemble-based machine learning framework with Chi-Square feature selection effectively identified the most informative clinical features for early cardiovascular disease detection, significantly enhancing predictive accuracy26. Similarly, in the domain of web traffic forecasting, deep learning models employed systematic data preprocessing and selection of relevant temporal features to capture complex multilingual patterns and improve forecasting performance27. These studies highlight that careful feature selection is critical for extracting meaningful patterns from data, motivating the adoption of dynamic and optimized feature selection in the proposed OFSCL model for ST-FED-DS forecasting.

While numerous studies have explored electrical energy forecasting using statistical, machine learning, and deep learning models, several critical gaps remain. Traditional models such as ARIMA17 struggle with nonlinear and multivariate data, limiting their accuracy. Machine learning approaches like ANN and RF18,24 improve prediction but often ignore temporal dependencies and environmental factors, reducing their generalizability. Deep learning models, including LSTM, GRU, and CNN-based hybrids21,22,25, capture complex patterns and long-term dependencies more effectively; however, they still fall short in integrating real-time prediction and sensitivity analysis of weather and temporal variables. These limitations highlight the need for a more comprehensive approach that not only leverages deep learning’s strengths but also addresses these gaps. The proposed OFSCL model fulfills this need by combining optimal feature selection with a Conv-LSTM hybrid architecture, incorporating real-time sensitivity analysis, and thereby achieving higher forecasting accuracy and adaptability across diverse ST-FED-DS scenarios.

Problem description

Due to the non-linear and time-dependent nature of energy data, traditional models underperform. While ML models (e.g., DT, KNN, MLP) can capture some complexity, deep learning models (e.g., LSTM, CNN) are better suited for temporal-spatial patterns. To address limitations in accuracy and computational efficiency, we propose the OFSCL model a hybrid architecture integrating convolutional layers for selective feature extraction and LSTM layers for modeling long-term dependencies. OFSCL enhances forecasting performance under diverse and dynamic conditions. Comparative evaluation with existing ML/DL models highlights its superiority in accuracy, robustness, and efficiency for real-world applications.

Formulation

The OFSCL model combines the feature extraction capability of Conv layers with the temporal modeling power of LSTM units. Unlike conventional CNN architectures, where the number of filters generally either increases layer by layer or remains constant, the OFSCL model employs a progressive reduction in the number of filters across the convolutional layers. This strategic reduction in filters optimizes the feature extraction process by minimizing computational complexity while enhancing the model’s ability to focus on essential patterns. Following this, the LSTM layers capture the temporal dependencies inherent in the data, leveraging the refined spatial features for more accurate forecasting. In the subsequent stage of the architecture, adding a Flatten layer is essential because the outputs of the convolutional and LSTM layers are typically multi-dimensional, whereas fully connected (Dense) layers require one-dimensional input vectors. The Flatten layer converts the multi-dimensional feature maps into a single vector, ensuring a smooth transition from feature extraction to the decision-making part of the network. Next, Dense layers are employed to nonlinearly combine and reweight the extracted features. This step is necessary for learning complex relationships between spatial and temporal patterns and transforming them into a higher-level representation suitable for prediction. Finally, a single-unit output layer is used to map all processed information into one final predicted value. Using a single neuron ensures compatibility with the regression nature of the forecasting task and enables direct generation of the predicted output. This combination of progressive filter reduction and temporal learning enables the OFSCL model to effectively model both spatial and temporal characteristics of ST-FED-DS data. Solution methodology

The overall workflow of the proposed OFSCL model is illustrated in Fig. 2. The input data, including ST-FED-DS and weather-related features, undergoes preprocessing and optimal feature selection to reduce dimensionality and enhance predictive relevance. The refined features are then processed through convolutional layers to extract spatial patterns, followed by LSTM layers to capture long-term temporal dependencies. Dense layers generate the final predictions, which are evaluated using RMSE, MAE, and R² metrics. Sensitivity analysis is performed on both features and data sequences to ensure robustness and optimize model performance. This stepwise workflow provides a clear understanding of how the hybrid architecture operates from raw data to actionable forecasting results.

The Optimal Feature Selection process plays a critical role in improving the performance and efficiency of the model. By identifying and selecting the most relevant features from the input data, this step reduces the dimensionality of the dataset, enhances computational efficiency, and improves the accuracy of the predictions. The model applies techniques such as correlation analysis and mutual information to rank features based on their importance, ensuring that only the most predictive variables are used in the forecasting process. Unnecessary or redundant features are removed, reducing overfitting and improving the model’s generalization ability. Optimal feature selection Conv-LSTM architecture

The hybrid architecture of the model integrates Convolutional and LSTM networks. The first layers of the model consist of convolutional layers, which extract spatial features from the input time-series data. These layers are essential for detecting local patterns within the data that may correspond to consumption spikes or periods of low demand. A kernel size of 5 is used to capture patterns over a moderate temporal window, which is crucial for identifying trends that span multiple time intervals. Max Pooling layers (with a pool size of 2) are applied after each convolutional layer to reduce the dimensionality of the data and to capture the most significant features while maintaining the spatial structure. The LSTM layers are responsible for capturing long-term temporal dependencies in the data. ST-FED-DS data often exhibits periodicity and seasonality, which can only be effectively modeled using LSTMs. The ReLU activation function is used in the LSTM layers to capture complex non-linear temporal dependencies, while tanh is used in the Conv layers to model non-linearities in the spatial domain.

Fully connected (Dense) layers

The Dense layers at the end of the network provide the final prediction. The Flatten Layer, layer flattens the multi-dimensional output of the LSTM layer into a one-dimensional vector that can be passed through fully connected layers. The first fully connected layer, with 15 units and ReLU activation, combines the temporal and spatial features into a more compact representation. The final output layer consists of a single neuron, which predicts the aggregated ST-FED-DS at a given time, without any activation function, as it is a regression task.

Data collection and preprocessing

ST-FED-DS and weather-related data were collected from Ahvaz city, located in the Khuzestan province in the southwestern region of Iran over a one-year period (September 12, 2020 to September 13, 2021.). The dataset includes electrical energy values along with features such as temperature, humidity, pressure, wind speed, dew point, and time-based attributes (e.g., day, month, season, weekdays/holidays). Data cleaning involved handling missing values, correcting anomalies, and removing outliers to ensure reliability and model robustness. Inputs in machine learning and deep learning models

The inputs for machine learning and deep learning models differ significantly. In machine learning models, the input consists of the time column from Table 2, which is used to predict each time point individually. Specifically, machine learning models utilize time-related features such as the hour of the day, day of the week, and indicators for public holidays or weekends to make predictions. In contrast, deep learning models use sequences of consecutive time points, including the current and previous observations, to capture temporal dependencies. The optimal sequence length is determined through input sensitivity analysis to maximize model performance. Due to the difficulty of obtaining synchronized meteorological and historical consumption data, a one-year data period was selected. Such a comprehensive dataset ensures that the model has been exposed to extreme variations and typical seasonal patterns. Although the evaluation is based on a one-year dataset from a single substation, this period captures complete seasonal variations and extreme transitions in energy demand. The proposed OFSCL model, combining hybrid optimal feature selection with sequence-based learning, is inherently robust to fluctuations in input patterns. By capturing both short-term dynamics and long-term dependencies, the model is expected to maintain stable performance even under different operational scenarios or in regions with varying demand profiles and climatic conditions.

The high MAE and RMSE values further highlight its inability to adapt to sudden shifts in consumption. The MLP model also performs poorly on the test data, with a negative R² of -56%. This suggests that it fails to accurately track consumption trends. For example, on day 9, the model predicts 20.07, whereas the actual value is 18.88, indicating a tendency to smooth out sharp consumption changes rather than fully capturing their magnitude. While the RMSE is slightly better than that of KNN, the model still struggles to capture sharp consumption fluctuations, resulting in substantial prediction errors.

This design prevents overfitting to the training set while ensuring robust generalization to unseen data. Furthermore, OFSCL maintains competitive training times, comparable to or lower than more complex hybrid models, demonstrating both efficiency and accuracy. Collectively, these results confirm that OFSCL is a robust, generalizable, and superior model for dynamic electricity consumption forecasting.

Ablation and sensitivity analysis of model components

To enhance transparency and identify the contribution of individual components within the proposed architecture, an ablation and sensitivity analysis was conducted, and the corresponding results are summarized in Table 8. As reported, the complete OFSCL model achieves the best overall performance with an RMSE of 0.74 MWh and an R² of 90%, serving as the reference configuration. When the three-stage convolutional feature selection module is replaced with a single convolutional layer, the RMSE increases to 0.88 MWh and R² drops to 84%, demonstrating the critical role of hierarchical convolution in extracting robust temporal–spatial features. Furthermore, removing the max-pooling layers leads to the largest performance degradation (RMSE = 0.91 MWh), indicating that progressive temporal down sampling and denoising are essential for stable generalization. As shown in Table 8, eliminating the second LSTM layer also results in a noticeable decline in accuracy, confirming that stacked LSTM layers are necessary to effectively capture both short-term and long-term temporal dependencies. Reducing the number of LSTM units causes a moderate performance drop, highlighting the importance of sufficient model capacity. Finally, removing the intermediate dense layer degrades performance to an RMSE of 0.89 MWh, indicating its role in refining high-level abstract representations prior to the final prediction. Overall, the ablation results in Table 8 clearly demonstrate that the superior performance of OFSCL arises from the synergistic interaction of its convolutional, temporal, and dense components rather than from any single module, thereby directly addressing concerns related to overfitting, robustness, and component-level contribution.

Conclusion

This study introduces the novel OFSCL hybrid model, designed for enhanced ST-FED-DS through integrated feature selection and temporal modeling. Extensive evaluation demonstrates its superior accuracy and adaptability, significantly outperforming conventional ML and DL approaches. The results from the testing data indicate that the OFSCL hybrid model significantly outperforms all other models, as it achieves the highest R² of 90%, the lowest MAE (0.55 MWh), and the lowest RMSE (0.74 MWh). This demonstrates its superior ability to generalize and adapt to fluctuations in electricity consumption. In contrast, traditional machine learning models like Decision Trees, KNN, and MLP are unable to capture the true patterns in the testing data, as evidenced by their negative R² values, high MAE, and RMSE. The CNN model, while not as accurate as the hybrid model, still shows a strong ability to track consumption trends with an R² of 81%, outperforming all machine learning models. Overall, the proposed OFSCL model outperforms advanced deep learning baselines including CNN-LSTM, Bi-LSTM, Stacked LSTM, LSTM with Attention, and TCN in both prediction accuracy and generalization, while requiring nearly half the training time of these hybrid architectures. This balanced combination of precision and computational efficiency highlights OFSCL’s practical superiority for large-scale and time-sensitive electricity consumption forecasting tasks. The sensitivity analysis reveals that certain features are far more critical in determining the accuracy and reliability of the OFSCL model’s predictions. Air temperature emerged as the most important feature, which is expected given its substantial impact on energy consumption patterns. The high sensitivity of the model to air temperature, month, and relative humidity underscores the significance of environmental factors in time-series forecasting tasks, particularly in predicting ST-FED-DS. On the other hand, features such as week day and holiday, which exhibit lower gradient values and importance scores, seem to have a negligible impact on the model’s performance. This highlights the need for careful feature selection, emphasizing those variables that most strongly correlate with the target variable. Furthermore, the analysis of the sum of gradients across sequences implies that the model, upon learning from a diverse range of inputs, becomes less sensitive to minor fluctuations in the input features, focusing more on long-term trends and patterns. While the OFSCL model demonstrates high accuracy and adaptability for short-term energy forecasting at specific distribution substation, its practical applicability is currently constrained by the limited geographic and operational scope of the available data. The model’s performance under varying conditions, across multiple substations, and with different network configurations remains to be explored. Future work could focus on extending the approach to larger-scale systems, integrating it with real-time operational platforms, and evaluating its robustness under diverse environmental and load scenarios. Such investigations would provide deeper insights into the model’s scalability, generalizability, and readiness for deployment in real-world electricity distribution networks, thereby bridging the gap between accurate forecasting and operational feasibility.

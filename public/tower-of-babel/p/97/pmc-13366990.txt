Plasma SERPINA3 and its clinical correlates in adolescent major depressive disorder: an exploratory case–control study
Authors: Yong Xue, Gui Mei Fan, Yue Wang, Hui Dong, Chun Hong Wei, Bo Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMajor depressive disorder (MDD) in adolescents lacks objective peripheral biomarkers that may assist diagnosis and biological stratification. Serine protease inhibitor A3 (SERPINA3) is an inflammation-related acute-phase protein, but its circulating levels and clinical relevance in adolescent MDD remain unclear.MethodsIn this exploratory case–control study, 141 adolescents with MDD and 76 healthy controls were enrolled. Plasma SERPINA3 concentrations were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. Within the MDD cohort, participants were stratified at the median SERPINA3 concentration (380 pg/mL) for exploratory subgroup analyses. Associations between SERPINA3 and laboratory variables were examined using exploratory correlation and regression analyses. P values for the exploratory laboratory comparisons in Table 2 were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was used to assess the discriminatory performance of SERPINA3, with internal validation by 2000 bootstrap resamples and stratified 10-fold cross-validation.ResultsPlasma SERPINA3 concentrations were higher in adolescents with MDD than in healthy controls (P < 0.0001). Several laboratory variables showed nominal between-group differences in unadjusted analyses, but none remained statistically significant after FDR correction (all q ≥ 0.242). In exploratory multivariable analyses, Glucose, RDW-CV, and indirect bilirubin were associated with continuous SERPINA3 concentration. In the full-sample ROC analysis, the AUC was 0.8433 and the optimal cutoff was 309 pg/mL. Internal validation yielded an optimism-corrected AUC of 0.8421 in bootstrap resampling and a mean test AUC of 0.8393 in stratified 10-fold cross-validation.ConclusionsIn this exploratory case–control study, plasma SERPINA3 concentrations were higher in adolescents with MDD than in healthy controls. However, the observed laboratory associations were not robust after correction for multiple testing, and the discriminatory performance should be interpreted as preliminary and internally validated only within this cohort. These findings are hypothesis-generating and require confirmation in larger, longitudinal, and independent cohorts.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Adolescent major depressive disorder, SERPINA3, Inflammation, Peripheral marker, Case-control study

Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a common and disabling psychiatric disorder that imposes a substantial public health burden. Patients with major depressive disorder often experience substantial occupational and social functioning impairment and have a significantly elevated risk of suicide. From a genetic epidemiology perspective, family, twin, and adoption studies consistently indicate that MDD is moderately heritable, with heritability estimates of approximately 31–42% [1], supporting an important genetic contribution alongside environmental influences.

Rather than implicating a single etiological pathway, GWAS findings suggest that genetic liability spans multiple biological systems, consistent with the marked clinical and biological heterogeneity observed in MDD [2, 3]. Against this background, immune dysregulation and inflammatory processes have received increasing attention as potentially relevant components in at least a subset of patients. Peripheral immune activation and inflammation have been observed in subsets of individuals with MDD, and may influence mood-related neural circuits by engaging central immune mechanisms, including microglial activation and immune–metabolic pathways [4, 5]. In line with these observations, elevated circulating inflammatory markers, including proinflammatory cytokines, acute-phase reactants, chemokines, and cell adhesion molecules, have been reported in patients with depression [6].

Serine protease inhibitor A3 (SERPINA3), also known as α1-antichymotrypsin (ACT), is an acute-phase serpin primarily synthesized in the liver and also produced in the central nervous system by astrocyte [7–9]. Beyond its systemic expression, accumulating evidence links SERPINA3 to neuroinflammation [10]. SERPINA3 serves as an antiprotease that limits excessive proteolytic activity during inflammatory responses, thereby helping to prevent tissue injury associated with neutrophil activation and phagocytic processes [11]. In the circulation, SERPINA3 participates in multiple physiological processes, including the complement cascade, apoptosis, and inflammation [12, 13]. At the transcriptional level, SERPINA3 expression is regulated by proinflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-1 (IL-1) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), with functional binding sites for STAT and NF-κB identified within its regulatory regions [10]. These biological characteristics support the relevance of SERPINA3 as a peripheral inflammatory marker with potential implications for neuroinflammation-related conditions.

Despite its established roles in inflammation and neuroinflammatory processes, the clinical relevance of circulating SERPINA3 in MDD remains unclear; therefore, we investigated plasma SERPINA3 levels, their exploratory clinical associations, and their preliminary discriminatory performance in adolescents with MDD.

Methods

Study design and participants

This case–control study included adolescents aged 10–18 years. Patients with major depressive disorder (MDD) were diagnosed by psychiatrists according to DSM-5 criteria. Depressive symptom severity was assessed using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD) administered by trained clinicians. Healthy controls were recruited from the same source population and were frequency-matched to cases by age and sex. An a priori sample size calculation was performed before enrollment to ensure adequate power for the primary case–control comparison of plasma SERPINA3. Written informed consent was obtained from a parent or legal guardian, and assent was obtained from all participants. The study protocol was approved by the institutional ethics committee (approval no. 2021-02).

Laboratory measurements

All blood samples were collected from fasting participants between 7:00 and 9:00 a.m. and centrifuged at 3000 g at room temperature for 15 min. The plasma supernatant was removed and stored at -80℃. Plasma SERPINA3 concentrations were measured using a commercially available Human SERPINA3 ELISA kit (Uping Bio, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. According to the kit documentation, the standard curve linearity was r ≥ 0.9900; the intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation were < 10% and < 15%, respectively; the lower limit of detection was < 10 pg/mL; the recovery range was 85%–115%; no cross-reactivity with structurally similar substances was reported; and the assay range was 187.5–6000 pg/mL. Laboratory personnel were blinded to case–control status during SERPINA3 measurement.

Statistics

All primary statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 27.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The primary outcome was the case–control difference in plasma SERPINA3 concentration. Secondary analysis included the exploratory discriminatory performance of SERPINA3. Comparisons of hematological and biochemical variables between the high- and low-SERPINA3 groups, as well as correlation analyses with SERPINA3, were prespecified as exploratory outcomes. Normally distributed continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the independent-samples t test. Non-normally distributed variables were summarized as median (interquartile range) and compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum/Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. For descriptive subgroup analyses within the MDD group, participants were dichotomized at the sample median SERPINA3 concentration (380 pg/mL) to create balanced high- and low-SERPINA3 groups; this median split was used only for exploratory within-MDD analyses and was not intended to define a clinically validated threshold. Correlations were assessed using Pearson’s or Spearman’s correlation coefficients. Stepwise linear regression analyses were conducted to examine independent correlates of plasma SERPINA3 concentration. Collinearity among covariates was evaluated using variance inflation factors. To account for multiple testing in the exploratory laboratory comparisons in Table 2, P values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) procedure and corresponding q values were reported; demographic variables (age, sex, and BMI) were treated as descriptive/confounding variables and were not included in the FDR correction family. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate the ability of plasma SERPINA3 to discriminate patients with MDD from healthy controls. The optimal cutoff value was determined using the Youden index (maximizing sensitivity + specificity − 1). Internal validation was performed using stratified bootstrap resampling (2000 resamples) and stratified 10-fold cross-validation. In each cross-validation fold, the optimal cutoff was determined in the training set using the Youden index and then applied to the held-out test set to estimate sensitivity and specificity; test-set AUCs were also calculated.

Results

Case–control comparison and exploratory subgroup analyses

Table 1 summarizes the general characteristics of the MDD group and the healthy control group. There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in age or sex. Plasma SERPINA3 concentrations were higher in adolescents with MDD than in healthy controls (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1). Within the MDD group, participants were divided into a high SERPINA3 group (≥ 380 pg/mL, n = 71) and a low SERPINA3 group (< 380 pg/mL, n = 70) based on the sample median concentration. In unadjusted analyses, the high-SERPINA3 group showed nominally higher A/G, MCH, MCHC, MPV, PDW, and Ca, and lower RDW-CV and GLO (all unadjusted P < 0.05). However, after Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate correction for the exploratory laboratory comparisons in Table 2, none of these between-group differences remained statistically significant (all q ≥ 0.242).

Correlation and linear regression analysis of SERPINA3 levels

Exploratory correlation analyses showed that plasma SERPINA3 concentration was inversely correlated with blood glucose (Pearson’s r = − 0.354, P = 0.001). Using Spearman’s rank correlation, SERPINA3 showed nominal positive correlations with mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH) (ρ = 0.167, P = 0.048), mean platelet volume (MPV) (ρ = 0.201, P = 0.018), platelet distribution width (PDW) (ρ = 0.218, P = 0.010), serum calcium (ρ = 0.234, P = 0.006), and indirect bilirubin (IDBIL) (ρ = 0.170, P = 0.045). SERPINA3 was also negatively correlated with red cell distribution width–coefficient of variation (RDW-CV) (ρ = −0.209, P = 0.013). Variables showing nominal correlations with SERPINA3 were entered into a stepwise linear regression model. In the final model, Glu, RDW-CV, and IDBIL were independently associated with SERPINA3 concentration. Specifically, SERPINA3 levels were negatively associated with Glu (B = − 88.581, β = −0.305, P = 0.005) and RDW-CV (B = − 49.344, β = −0.263, P = 0.012), and positively associated with IDBIL (B = 8.700, β = 0.238, P = 0.028). No evidence of multicollinearity was observed among variables retained in the model (all VIF < 1.1) (Table 3). These findings should be interpreted cautiously because they arose from exploratory analyses.

Diagnostic performance of SERPINA3 based on ROC analysis

ROC analysis in the full sample showed that plasma SERPINA3 differentiated adolescents with MDD from healthy controls with an AUC of 0.8433 (95% CI: 0.792–0.894; P < 0.0001). The Youden index identified an optimal cutoff of 309 pg/mL, which yielded an apparent sensitivity of 73.76% and an apparent specificity of 80.26%. In internal validation using 2000 bootstrap resamples, the optimism-corrected AUC was 0.8421, with optimism-corrected sensitivity and specificity of 72.35% and 77.77%, respectively. In stratified 10-fold cross-validation, the mean test AUC was 0.8393, and the aggregate sensitivity and specificity across held-out test folds were 73.05% and 77.63%, respectively (Fig. 2).

Discussion

In the present case–control study, plasma SERPINA3 concentrations were higher in adolescents with MDD than in healthy controls. Given the established role of SERPINA3 as a cytokine-regulated acute-phase protein [14], this finding is broadly consistent with the hypothesis that peripheral inflammatory alterations may be present in at least a subset of adolescents with MDD. However, because of the cross-sectional design and the possibility of residual confounding, the present data should be interpreted as showing an association rather than establishing a causal or disease-specific relationship.

In the subgroup analyses comparing participants with high versus low SERPINA3 levels, several hematological and biochemical variables showed nominal between-group differences in the unadjusted analyses, including A/G, MCH, MCHC, MPV, PDW, GLO, RDW-CV, and calcium. However, after Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate correction, none of these associations remained statistically significant. These findings should therefore not be interpreted as stable correlates, but rather as exploratory signals that may help inform future hypothesis-driven studies.

In the exploratory correlation analyses, SERPINA3 showed weak positive correlations with MCH, MPV, PDW, serum calcium, and indirect bilirubin, and inverse correlations with glucose and RDW-CV. The positive relationships with MPV and PDW are of interest because these indices reflect platelet size heterogeneity and have been linked to platelet activation, inflammation, and depressive symptoms in previous studies [15, 16]. The positive correlation with MCH may likewise point to erythrocyte-related variation, although its isolated biological significance is difficult to define in the present dataset. SERPINA3 also showed a weak positive correlation with serum calcium. This observation has some biological plausibility, as calcium signaling is involved in immune activation and inflammasome pathways [17, 18]. However, serum calcium is influenced by multiple endocrine, nutritional, and metabolic factors, and the present data do not support a more specific mechanistic interpretation.

The inverse associations with glucose and RDW-CV, together with the positive association with indirect bilirubin, require cautious interpretation. These directions are not fully consistent with a classic acute inflammatory profile, and each of these variables is influenced by a broad range of physiological and clinical factors. A similar concern applies to the A/G pattern observed in the high-SERPINA3 subgroup. The A/G ratio is a composite indicator that has been used to reflect systemic immune-inflammatory status [19], whereas in typical acute inflammatory states albumin usually decreases and globulin fractions increase, resulting in a lower rather than higher A/G ratio [20]. The higher A/G ratio observed here therefore does not fit a conventional acute-phase response and may reflect biological heterogeneity, low-grade rather than acute immune activation, or the influence of other determinants of glucose regulation, erythropoiesis, bilirubin metabolism, and serum protein composition.

In the exploratory linear regression model, glucose, RDW-CV, and indirect bilirubin remained associated with continuous SERPINA3 concentration, suggesting that these measures may capture aspects of metabolic and oxidative-hematologic variation within this cohort. Prior studies have discussed bilirubin in depression in the context of antioxidant and anti-inflammatory processes [21, 22], but the meaning of this association in our sample remains uncertain. Given the exploratory nature of these analyses, the modest effect sizes, and the lack of robust between-group laboratory differences after FDR correction, these findings should be regarded as preliminary and in need of replication rather than as evidence of a defined mechanism.

The ROC analysis suggested that SERPINA3 had preliminary discriminatory performance in this cohort, with an apparent full-sample AUC of 0.8433. Importantly, internal validation using bootstrap resampling and stratified 10-fold cross-validation produced closely similar AUC estimates, suggesting limited optimism in the overall threshold-free discrimination measure. Nonetheless, threshold-based performance estimates were modestly attenuated after validation, and no external validation cohort was available. The ROC findings should therefore be regarded as preliminary and not sufficient to support clinical application.

Potential confounding remains an important consideration. In biomarker studies of adolescent depression, inflammatory and metabolic measures may be influenced by age, sex, BMI, medication exposure, illness severity, and other clinical factors. Although BMI was retrospectively collected, several other potentially relevant confounders, including medication exposure, comorbid medical conditions, and more complete symptom-severity adjustment, were not comprehensively incorporated into the present models. The current findings therefore should be viewed as exploratory rather than definitive evidence for a specific biomarker profile.

Several limitations merit emphasis. First, the cross-sectional case–control design precludes causal inference and provides no information on temporal direction. Second, the subgroup analyses based on median stratification of SERPINA3, as well as the correlation analyses, were exploratory, and the nominal laboratory findings in Table 2 were not robust to FDR correction. Third, SERPINA3 was measured at a single time point, precluding assessment of within-person variability, illness course, or treatment response. Fourth, although we added assay-performance details and used blinded laboratory measurement, analytical and clinical validation remained limited. Fifth, the study was conducted at a single center in a relatively homogeneous population, and the control group was smaller than the MDD group, which may have reduced the precision of some between-group and ROC-related estimates. Finally, although internal validation was performed for ROC analysis, external validation was not available.

In sum, the present data support further evaluation of SERPINA3 in adolescent MDD, particularly in larger longitudinal studies with richer phenotyping and independent validation cohorts.

Conclusion

In this exploratory case–control study, plasma SERPINA3 concentrations were higher in adolescents with MDD than in healthy controls. SERPINA3 also showed preliminary associations with several laboratory variables in exploratory analyses, but these findings were not robust after correction for multiple testing. The internally validated ROC results suggest preliminary discriminatory performance within this cohort, but the findings are not sufficient to support clinical application at present. Further studies with comprehensive confounder assessment, longitudinal follow-up, and independent external validation are required.

A radiomics nomogram integrated with radiological features for preoperative prediction of lung nodule invasiveness: comparison with Lung-RADS
Authors: Siwei Miao, Zhaojie Han, Fei Wang, Shengwei Liu, Ying Li, Kun Li, Hongqian Wang, Shuaixiang Gao, Xiaolin Xiong, Hao Luo, Chencui Huang, Yang Qiu, Peng Wang, Haidong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPreoperative identification of early-stage lung adenocarcinoma, particularly minimal lesions, holds critical implications for clinical decision-making. However, accurate diagnosis of pulmonary nodules’ malignancy remains challenging when relying solely on conventional clinical parameters or radiological features, due to the lack of specificity in certain instances of early lesions. This study aims to develop a nodule-level composite model using radiomics methods and compare its performance with the Lung CT Screening Reporting and Data System (Lung-RADS) in predicting the invasiveness using preoperative low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) scan with 1 mm slice thickness.MethodsFrom June 2019 to October 2023, the patients who were pathologically diagnosed with the initial primary lung nodules were enrolled in the retrospective cohort. To predict invasiveness of nodule, nodule-level models, including radiomics, radiological, and combined models, were developed to categorize the nodules into the invasiveness and non-invasiveness groups. Radiomics features were extracted from the tumor region to develop a radiomics model after a three-stage feature reduction procedure, resulting in a Radscore for each nodule. The nodules were randomly divided into two groups, with a ratio of 7:3. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), accuracy, and other relevant measures were employed to assess the models’ performance and discriminative ability. A nomogram that combines optimal radiomics and clinical-radiological features was compared to Lung-RADS using various evaluation methods, including calibration curve analysis, decision curve analysis (DCA), net reclassification improvement (NRI), and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI).ResultsA total of 393 patients were enrolled, including 67 with multiple nodules. Altogether, 487 nodules were analysed, comprising 374 in the invasiveness and 113 in the non-invasiveness group. The radiological model identified two risk factors: nodule type and a vessel convergence sign. The nomogram achieved a training-set AUC of 0.91, surpassing the Lung-RADS system (AUC: 0.669) in nodule assessment. Notably, significant improvements in NRI and IDI, as well as higher net benefits, were shown by the DCA.ConclusionsThe nomogram based on radiomics and radiological features offers supplementary information for preoperative prediction of the invasiveness of lung nodules, surpassing both the commonly used Lung-RADS and single models in guiding diagnosis and treatment strategies for lung nodules.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Compared to Lung CT Screening Reporting and Data System (Lung-RADS), the nomogram that integrates radiomics and radiological features offers a more comprehensive assessment of lung nodule invasiveness. It could reduce the risk of overdiagnosis and hold promise as a valuable supplement to traditional preoperative assessment tools.

What is known and what is new?

• Accurate preoperative discrimination of invasive lung nodules, especially minimal lesions, remains challenging using conventional clinical or radiological features alone. The Lung-RADS system is widely used but has limitations in specificity for certain early-stage lesions.

• We developed an integrated model combining radiological factors and radiomics features extracted from thin-slice low-dose computed tomography scans. This multimodal model improves the identification of early-stage pulmonary nodule lesions compared to conventional assessments and outperforms Lung-RADS in predicting preoperative invasiveness of lung nodules.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The proposed nomogram serves as an advanced predictive tool for preoperative assessment of nodule invasiveness, with the potential to inform personalized surgical planning and avoid unnecessary procedures. Further multi-center validation is essential to confirm generalizability, followed by integration into clinical practice for real-world testing and iterative improvements.

Introduction

Lung cancer screening and early intervention are widely acknowledged as the cornerstones of effective prevention and control strategies (1). In China, the escalating rates of both the occurrence and mortality of lung cancer underscore the critical need for optimized strategies (2). Numerous regional and multi-centre clinical studies have established low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) as the recommended modality for the detection and management of high-risk pulmonary nodules (3-5). Nevertheless, the handling of the lung nodules requires an individualized strategy determined by their shape, dimensions, and compactness (6,7). Eliminating unnecessary screening holds the potential to minimize harm and optimize cost-effectiveness, as inaccurate nodule assessment can lead to superfluous testing, follow-up, and even overtreatment.

In clinical practice, invasiveness serves as a crucial indicator of a pulmonary nodule’s risk profile. Benign nodules and pre-invasive lesions, such as atypical adenomatous hyperplasia (AAH) and adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), generally grow indolently and do not display invasive features (8). On the contrary, minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA) and invasive adenocarcinoma (IAC) exhibit invasive growth patterns and are strongly correlated with a poor prognosis, particularly the latter, which presents a more severe prognosis. However, the subjectivity of computed tomography (CT)-based diagnosis and its limitations in identifying malignant components hinder informed clinical decision-making. While histopathological confirmation remains the traditional gold standard, there is a pressing need for tools that enhance diagnostic accuracy and specificity to guide the clinical management of pulmonary nodules.

The Lung CT Screening Reporting and Data System (Lung-RADS) offers a standardized framework for reporting and classifying findings on lung cancer screening CT scans. This system offers a convenient and categorized representation of the risk probability for different nodules, facilitating CT screenings across diverse populations and countries (9-11). Some studies have indicated that Lung-RADS may also hold potential for differentiating the invasiveness of nodules (12,13). While Lung-RADS categories 1 and 2 typically warrant follow-up observations, nodules classified as level 3 or 4 may necessitate additional imaging or clinical evaluations (14). It is important to acknowledge, however, that limitations remain regarding clinical decision-making based on Lung-RADS grading.

Diagnosing lung nodules in clinical practice presents a challenge due to their heterogeneous presentation. Not all nodules exhibit classic features such as pleural retraction sign, spiculated margins, or a lobulated appearance (15). Radiomics offers a potential solution by enabling the extraction of a vast array of quantitative imaging features from CT scans, indicating subtle characteristics that are imperceptible to the naked eye (16,17). Leveraging radiomics to differentiate between invasive and non-invasive nodules holds significant promise for improving diagnostic accuracy and guiding the optimal timing of surgical intervention (18,19). While several studies have demonstrated the categorization capabilities of radiomics for pulmonary nodules, few have compared its performance to the widely adopted Lung-RADS system, leaving its clinical utility uncertain. This study aims to address this gap by developing a nodule-level model based on radiomics for determining nodule invasiveness and comparing its efficacy to Lung-RADS. The key objective is to promote the timely diagnosis of pulmonary nodules, particularly microlesions. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2025-1083/rc).

Methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective study was implemented under the approval of the institutional ethics committee of Southwest Hospital of Third Military Medical University (No. BIIT2024027KX). Written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The design and procedural steps of study is shown in Figure 1.

The study flowchart. (A) The patient selection process. (B) The flow diagram for radiomics feature extraction, model developing and validating, and comparing with Lung-RADS. ACC, accuracy; AUC, area under the curve; IDI, integrated discrimination improvement; LDCT, low-dose computed tomography; Lung-RADS, Lung CT Screening Reporting and Data System; NPV, negative predictive value; NRI, net reclassification improvement; PPV, positive predictive value.

We retrospectively collected a cohort of 393 patients who underwent preoperative non-contrast chest LDCT scans with slice thickness of 1 mm 1 month prior and were diagnosed with the initial primary lung nodule between June 2019 and October 2023 from AI Capability Open Platform established by The First Affiliated Hospital (Southwest Hospital) of Army Medical University. Supplementary inclusion criteria encompassed the patients aged 18 years or older, without concurrent ailments that hinder effective treatment, with no previous history of other neoplastic conditions, and whose nodule categorization was done using the Lung-RADS. The exclusion criteria: (I) without pathologically confirmed; (II) substandard image quality unsuitable for feature extraction; and (III) inaccuracies in the categorization of Lung-RADS caused by subjective factors.

Endpoint

The clinical endpoint of this study was nodule invasiveness, categorized as non-invasive and invasive group. Pathological evaluation of nodule subtypes was obtained from pathology reports. Considering the pathological evidence and clinical management, benign, AAH, and AIS were classified as non-invasiveness, while MIA and IAC were classified as invasiveness.

Lung-RADS

The Lung-RADS findings of lesions with corresponding pathological results were reported by experienced radiologists based on non-contrast CT images with a slice thickness of 1mm within one month prior to surgery. We extracted and categorized Lung-RADS of the lesions to assess the precision of the classifications, in which categories 2 and 3 were deemed as noninvasive screening evaluations, while categories 4A, 4B, and 4X were indicative of invasive in screening assessments.

CT scanning protocol

LDCT were performed at CT scanners (Somatom Definition Flash, Somatom Force, and Somatom definition AS; Siemens Healthineers, Germany) in a single breath hold without contrast material. Technical parameters were as follow: tube voltage, 100–120 kV; automatic tube current; rotation time, 0.5 s; collimation, 128×0.6; pitch of 1.2–1.5; and 1 mm slice thickness.

Clinical and radiological data acquisition

The clinical and radiological variables of patients were acquired through the patient medical record system. The clinical data included age, gender, body mass index (BMI), family history of lung cancer, smoking status, and multiple nodules.

The study documented 7 preoperative radiological characteristics which comprised location (right upper lobe, right middle lobe, right lower lobe, left upper lobe, and left lower lobe), nodule type [part-solid, pure ground-glass opacity (GGO), and solid], lobulation, vessel convergence sign, emphysema, pleural thickening, spiculation, pleural indentation, and vacuole sign.

CT scan and imaging features acquisition

Segmentation and radiomic feature extraction

All preoperative CT images were acquired through the AI Capability Open Platform, an artificial intelligence platform that combines machine learning and deep learning technologies, from the Picture Archiving and Communications System (PACS). We utilized the Platform for the segmentation of volumes of interest (VOI) and extraction of the radiomics features. In addition, two radiologists (radiologist 1 and radiologist 2) participated on target lesion segmentation, with the former one delineated the lesion manually slice by slice, and the latter validating the segmentation. Prior to the feature extraction process, preprocessing of medical DICOM images was conducted to ensure the reliability of the results. This involved discretizing the CT values of each image, with a bin width set to 25 Hounsfield units (HU). Additionally, the voxel size of the VOI was resampled using the B-Spline method to ensure spatial pixel uniformity. Furthermore, grayscale images were processed with Gaussian filter transformations with varying sigma parameters of 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 to reduce computational complexity and enhance feature robustness. A total of 2,107 radiomic features were extracted from each nodule, wavelet filter, and Laplacian of Gaussian (LOG) filter images, including first order, shape-based (3D), and texture.

Consistency evaluation and feature selection

The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was carried out to evaluate the consistency of manual extraction of radiomics features. Radiologist 1 delineated VOIs of each target nodules of all patients by manually sketching the tumor contours. One month later, a random selection of 30 images was segmented, with the two radiologists independently outlining the VOI to evaluate reproducibility. And the features with ICC values exceeding 0.8 were deemed to show virtually perfectly consistent. A three-stage feature selection method was performed to reduce dimensions effectively for the purpose of eliminating redundant radiomic features. First, to ensure reproducibility, features with ICC value surpassing 0.8 were retained for further analysis. Subsequently, to remove redundant features, the t-test was conducted to select significant features linked to outcome (P<0.05). Finally, we employed the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) with 10-fold cross-validation to select the features for dimensionality reduction. Following this, random forest (RF) was used for variable importance ranking to visualize the contribution of radiomics features.

Model development and evaluation

The nodule- and patient-level dataset was randomly split to the training and validation sets by the 7:3 ratio. We developed the clinical model using patient-level train set and radiological model using the nodule-level train set. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression models were employed to point the independent clinical and radiological risk variables, respectively. Subsequently, the clinical and radiological models were developed. Using multivariate logistic regression model, a combined model was constructed based on radiomics score (Radscore) and independent radiological characteristics. The performance of the four models was evaluated using the indices, including the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), accuracy (ACC), sensitivity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and specificity. And the models’ performance was also evaluated using 10-fold cross-validation to assess stability. The effective nomogram model was built by combined model to visualize. The performance of Lung-RADS was also evaluated by the indices to compare to nomogram model. The clinical utility of each model was evaluated using decision curve analysis (DCA) to compare the two models. Calibration curves were performed to assess the apparent performance of the two models. Net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) were used to evaluate the discrimination of the two models.

Statistical analysis

The data analysis was conducted with R version 4.2.1. The t-test was used to test the statistical difference between nodule types concerning continuous variables, while χ2 test was utilized to examine the differences in categorized variables. The DCA were used to estimate the discrimination of models using the packages “caret” (version 6.0-94) and “pROC” (version 1.18.2). The ‘glmnet’ (version 4.1-8) and ‘rms’ (version 6.7-1) were applied for the development and evaluation of the LASSO model and logistic regression, respectively. The packages ‘randomForest’ (version 4.7-1.1) was implemented for the development the RF model. And the figures were generated based on package “ggplot2”. The statistical significance of the two-sided was set at <0.05.

Radiomics features extraction and selection

From each lesion area within the training cohort, the study extracted 2,107 radiomic features. Features with an ICC greater than 0.80, indicating poor reproducibility, were removed, leaving us with 1,922 features. These were then subjected to a triage selection process. Initially, we utilized a t-test, which resulted in 1,100 radiomic features being included for further scrutiny via LASSO selection. With a minimum lambda (λ) value of 0.020, features were able to narrow down the field to 16 (Figure 2A,2B). Ultimately, the importance ranking provided by the RF identified the top 10 significant features (Figure 2C). Additionally, Pearson correlation analysis showed that the correlation coefficient for each feature was below 0.7 in both sets of radiomic features in training and validation cohort (Figure 3A,3B). We calculated a Radscore for each nodule using the top 5 features identified by RF. There was a significant difference in Radscore between noninvasive and invasive nodules in both the training cohort (P<0.001) and the validation cohorts (P=0.04) (Figure 3C,3D).

Model construction

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify independent radiological indicators of nodule invasiveness. Based on these results, nodule type, vessel convergence sign, and spiculation were selected for the combined model. The combined model based on multivariate logistic regression model indicated that nodule type and Radscore were significant independent factors for differentiating between noninvasive and invasive nodules. Considering the clinical value, the vessel convergence sign was kept in the final combined-model.

Among the four model, we selected combined model as our algorithm for developing the nomogram due to its superior performance in training cohort. This was reflected in in the clinical model [AUC: 0.626, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.531–0.722], radiological model (AUC: 0.783, 95% CI: 0.732–0.835), radiomics model (AUC: 0.857, 95% CI: 0.813–0.898) and combined model (AUC: 0.869, 95% CI: 0.822–0.909) (Figure 4A). The combined model continued to excel in the validation set (AUC: 0.852, 95% CI: 0.767–0.920), illustrating a robust capacity for differentiation. The AUC for the other models were noted as 0.683 (95% CI: 0.526–0.840), 0.738 (95% CI: 0.641–0.823), and 0.0.848 (95% CI: 0.768–0.916) respectively (Figure 4B). Discussion

In this study, the nomogram model was developed to classify invasiveness of lung nodules based on combined model including CT radiomics and radiological features, which could provide a more reliable guideline for clinical decision-making. Our study aimed to constructed and validated models to predict invasiveness and compare with widely used Lung-RADS system. The establishment of a hybrid-based nomogram incorporating nodule type, vessel convergence sign, and Radscore exhibits superior diagnostic accuracy and clinical value compared to models based solely on clinical, radiological, or radiomics features. This suggests that the integration of diverse data sources can enhance the prediction of lung nodule invasiveness and potentially guide more informed clinical management.

Accurate assessment of lung nodule invasiveness and identification of their pathological subtypes are crucial for optimal treatment strategies. While surgical resection of preinvasive lesions, particularly small nodules, yields a nearly 100% 5-year survival rate and minimal recurrence (20), accurate pathologic subtyping can potentially spare patients from unnecessary surgery in favor of long-term surveillance (21). AAH and AIS are defined as non-invasive lesions due to their indolent biological behavior, and recent studies recommend conservative management strategies for these conditions (22). Considering that benign nodules, AAH, and AIS are usually managed with conservative observation or follow-up without the need for immediate surgical resection, and do not contain invasive components, we classified them as non-invasiveness. MIA, characterized by cancer cells infiltrating less than 5 mm into the mesenchyme, presents an exceptionally high 5-year post-surgery survival rate (23). However, putting a premium on the MIA is critical, as it still contains an invasive component with a potential risk for future invasion and metastasis (24). MIA and IAC are categorised into the invasiveness group owing to their distinct invasive components and potential for progression, both of which necessitate clinical intervention. This study suggests a simpler categorization approach based on the presence of pathological invasive components and clinical management requirements, categorizing benign nodules, AAH, and AIS as non-invasiveness. In contrast, MIA and IAC are classified as invasiveness, aligning with previous research, demonstrating practical clinical utility (15,25). Prior studies have demonstrated the efficacy of various methods, such as clinical semantic radiomics datasets or single radiomic parameter datasets, in differentiating between various pathological subtypes of lung adenocarcinoma (26,27). However, many studies focus primarily on differentiating invasive from pre-invasive lesions, often overlooking the potential for malignancy in MIA (26,28,29). This proposed classification could offer a more practical way to interpret clinical outcomes for patients.

This study focused on the characteristics of pulmonary nodules in patients population undergoing thoracic surgery. The analysis revealed a 76.8% rate of nodules diagnosed as MIA/IAC, supporting the accuracy of initial assessments. However, a significant percentage of nodules were classified as benign/AAH/AIS, indicating a potential risk of overtreatment. The results identified age and the presence of multiple nodules as significant risk factors for malignancy. While the association between age and lung nodule malignancy is well-established in previous research, this study suggests that multiple nodules may also be an indicator of increased risk (30,31). Contrary to expectations, smoking status did not significantly differentiate benign from malignant nodules, potentially due to the study population’s predominantly female and Asian demographic. Consistent with existing literature, malignant nodules were more commonly observed in the right upper lobe of the lung (32-34). Radiological features, including nodule type and vessel convergence sign, were important predictors of invasiveness. In addition, nodule type was an independent predictor of outcome, with sub-solid nodules exhibiting higher malignant potential compared to pure ground-glass and solid nodules, corroborating previous findings (35). While prior studies identified the vessel convergence sign as an independent predictor of invasive status, it did not reach significance in our multivariable analysis (36). This discrepancy may be attributable to the significantly higher proportion of nodules with a solid component in the invasive group compared to the non-invasive group in our study. It is plausible that the presence of a solid component exerts a stronger effect on vascular traction and invasion, potentially explaining the observed differences in univariate analysis. Nevertheless, considering the established clinical relevance of the vessel convergence sign in invasive nodules, it was incorporated into the nomogram model.

Prior studies have established the efficacy of CT-based radiomics models in predicting the invasiveness of lung nodules (37-40). Our study is based on LDCT for preoperative prediction of lung nodule invasiveness and clinical decision support. Additionally, prospective research has employed 18F-FDG PET-CT imaging to guide clinical treatment in stage III non-small-cell lung cancer (41). In this study, we developed a streamlined, integrated radiomics model by selecting 5 robust radiomic features and 2 key radiological characteristics, effectively eliminating redundancy. Specifically, the nomogram derived from this combined model surpasses existing models in its comprehensive clinical predictive value, significantly enhancing the qualitative assessment of pulmonary nodules. The radiomics features identified in this study provides a multi-dimensional quantification of the structural and textural divergence between non-invasive and invasive pulmonary lesions. By integrating spatial heterogeneity (ZoneEntropy and Imc1), solid component densification (LDHGLE), and microscopic morphological irregularity (ZonePercentage), these metrics captured the differences between stable, orderly tissue architecture of nodules and chaotic, infiltrative growth patterns characteristic of invasive features. This approach transforms high-dimensional imaging data into clinically interpretable indicators of nodule complexity, ultimately facilitating enhanced preoperative differential diagnosis. By accurately quantifying lesion-level risk, this model facilitates informed clinical decision-making regarding nodule intervention and management. In addition, we employed manual delineation to define the full-layer VOI, ensuring close proximity to the lesion. While this approach is labor-intensive and inherently subjective, it mitigates the risk of incorporating spurious “pseudo-features” associated with automated delineation, thereby yielding a more accurate representation of the target region (42). Considering the limitations in extracting specific features from CT images of early-stage pulmonary nodules, clinicians often struggle to offer a comprehensive spectrum of radiological characteristics. Radiomics, however, offers the potential to identify and extract subtle variations that elude visual detection. Therefore, incorporating radiomic approaches into diverse diagnostic assessments of early-stage nodules may contribute to enhanced treatment strategies of pulmonary nodules, minimizing unnecessary invasive procedures and their associated complications.

It is noteworthy that our combined model outperformed the Lung-RADS system in comparative analysis, corroborating the findings of other studies (18-20). This superiority is not only reflected in improved discriminative ability and accuracy (AUC: 0.910 vs. 0.669; ACC: 0.866 vs. 0.734), but also in evaluation metrics that indicate clinical value. Specifically, significant improvements were observed in NRI and IDI, alongside higher net benefits demonstrated by DCA. These results are likely derived from the Lung-RADS system’s lack of integration between image interpretation and clinical features. In contrast, our final model incorporates radiomic features that extend beyond shape and density, capturing high-order textural information imperceptible to the naked eye. Moreover, it integrates preoperative radiological features of nodules, contributing to superior accuracy, sensitivity, and clinical utility. Furthermore, the Internet of Things (IoT) is driving surgical practice towards a smarter and more efficient direction (43). In the lung cancer management, IoT systems are playing an increasingly critical role, particularly in medical data transmission and intelligent detection (44), as well as early prediction and real-time monitoring (45). Although this study primarily focuses on preoperative prediction, the proposed radiomics-based model could serve as a core component embedded within such interconnected systems, thereby further enhancing precision diagnostic capabilities in the future.

This study acknowledges several limitations. First, the retrospective, single-center nature of the study, focusing on a surgical population pre-selected by thoracic surgeons, introduces potential selection bias. The study population, defined by Lung-RADS grading and confirmed pathological findings, deviates from a population-based model. While this approach enhances result accuracy, it may limit the generalizability of findings to broader screening populations. Future clinical studies are necessary to evaluate the model’s applicability in such settings. And future studies could explore deploying the proposed nomogram via IoT-based cloud platforms, thereby facilitating real-time data sharing and multi-centre validation to further generalise its clinical utility. Second, while the study objectively assesses the utility of Lung-RADS in a specific cohort and verifies the combined model for differentiating non-invasive and invasive lung nodules, it is crucial to recognize that Lung-RADS remains reliant on subjective assessments by imaging physicians, influenced by their individual expertise. Moreover, the manual outlining method employed for feature extraction may introduce confounding variables. Finally, the model’s internal validation without external validation potentially restricts its broader applicability.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study demonstrates the potential of radiomics to enhance diagnostic accuracy in differentiating between non-invasive and invasive lung nodules compared to Lung-RADS grading. Integrating clinical, radiological, and radiomics features in a multimodal modeling approach offers a promising avenue for future research.

Decoding dry eye disease based on bioinformatics and in vitro experimental: the role of immune responses and natural product intervention
Authors: Xi Long, Guicheng Liu, Pei Liu, Pengfei Jiang, Jun Peng, Qinghua Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDry eye disease (DED), a prevalent ocular condition, has seen rising incidence rates. Aberrant inflammation and immune dysregulation are key pathogenic factors in DED. However, the underlying mechanisms linking autoimmunity to DED therapy remain incompletely understood. Herbal medicine's potential in treating DED warrants further exploration due to rendering monotherapy insufficient for clinical needs.MethodsDED-related datasets and genes were initially identified from the GEO database and other public repositories. After retrieving the data, differential gene expression analysis and functional enrichment analysis were performed. Subsequently, a protein–protein interaction network was constructed to identify core modules and hub genes. To enhance the reliability and diagnostic value of these findings, the hub genes were further validated using independent external datasets and refined through machine learning algorithms (LASSO and SVM-RFE) to extract characteristic genes. These characteristic genes were then used for reverse prediction of potential Traditional Chinese Medicine natural products, leading to the construction of a gene-natural products network. Following this, molecular docking was employed to screen for promising natural products based on binding affinity. In parallel, immune cell infiltration was estimated using CIBERSORTx, and single-cell RNA sequencing data were analyzed to elucidate cell-type-specific expression patterns of the characteristic genes. Finally, the therapeutic potential of the top-predicted natural product was validated using an in vitro cellular model of DED.ResultsGSE44101 was selected as the base dataset. Differential gene analysis identified 1089 differential genes and 3504 DED-related genes from five databases, with 235 overlapping genes. Enrichment analysis linked DED to Cytokine-Cytokine receptor interaction, PI3K-Akt, IL-17, and JAK-STAT signaling pathways. The PPI network yielded 20 hub genes, validated and refined using other GEO datasets and machine learning, resulting in 10 characteristic genes: CDK1, CCNA2, CXCL13, CCR1, FEN1, CCR7, SELL, RAD51, CXCL1, and KIF11. Genistein was identified as the key TCM natural product. Molecular docking revealed stable interactions between Genistein and CDK1, IL-1β, CDC20, and CCNA2, with CDC20 showing the highest stability. CIBERSORT analysis demonstrated associations between characteristic genes and specific immune cell infiltration in DED. Single-cell RNA sequencing localized their expression to key corneal cell types, with IL-1β predominantly expressed in macrophages. Cell experiments confirmed that Genistein at a concentration of 12.5 μmol/L could protect human corneal epithelial cells from NaCl-induced hyperosmotic damage and TNF-α-induced immune injury, reducing the cell detachment rate and decreasing the area of cell death loss. Genistein downregulated the expression of CDK1, IL-1β, and CCNA2 at the RNA and protein levels, while upregulating the expression of CDC20.ConclusionsGenistein demonstrates therapeutic potential for DED, likely through regulating CDK1, IL-1β, CDC20, and CCNA2 to exert anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidative stress, and anti-apoptotic effects.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s40246-026-00992-1.Keywords: Dry eye disease, Immune responses, Inflammation, Natural products, Bioinformatics, Genistein

Introduction

Dry eye disease (DED) is a chronic, multifactorial disorder of the ocular surface, characterized by instability of the tear film and disruption of the microenvironment. This condition arises from abnormalities in the quality, quantity, and dynamics of tears, leading to pathological changes such as increased tear osmolarity, inflammation of the ocular surface, tissue damage, and ultimately, a loss of homeostasis[1]. Clinically, DED manifests as xerophthalmia, a sensation of a foreign body in the eye, burning, itching, photophobia, ocular erythema, visual disturbances (such as blurred or fluctuating vision), and visual fatigue. Epidemiological studies indicate that the prevalence of DED varies significantly, ranging from 5 to 30%, and can reach up to 75% in certain populations, with a clear increase associated with age[2]. The pathogenesis of DED involves complex interactions among chronic inflammation, tear film instability, elevated tear osmolarity, oxidative stress, fluctuations in sex hormones, and dysregulated apoptosis, which collectively disrupt ocular surface homeostasis and drive the progression of the disease[3–7]. The lacrimal functional unit (LFU), which includes the ocular surface, lacrimal glands, and their neural connections, plays a critical role in regulating tear secretion, tear film formation, and maintaining ocular surface homeostasis[8]. The structural or functional impairment of any component of the LFU can compromise the integrity of the tear film, thereby precipitating DED. Clinically, DED is typically characterized as a multifactorial disorder rather than a unifactorial condition. The disease often demonstrates significant discrepancies between subjective symptoms and objective clinical findings, accompanied by a complex pathogenesis and a wide range of etiological factors. Consequently, existing therapeutic approaches primarily aim at managing symptoms, frequently failing to address the underlying causes or achieve complete remission. This highlights the critical need to elucidate the intricate mechanisms underlying DED and to develop therapeutic strategies that target multiple aspects of the disease.

Effective management of DED requires long-term intervention to restore and maintain ocular surface homeostasis while minimizing potential complications. Given its complex pathogenesis, treatment strategies must be diverse and multifaceted. Current therapeutic strategies predominantly depend on the use of artificial tears, which mitigate ocular surface osmolarity and decrease susceptibility to inflammation by diluting inflammatory cytokines[9]. Nonetheless, these interventions offer merely symptomatic relief. As the disease progresses and inflammatory responses become more pronounced, the administration of anti-inflammatory agents and corticosteroids becomes imperative[10, 11]. However, these too provide only symptomatic relief. In contrast, Chinese herbal natural products, characterized by their diverse bioactive plant constituents and multi-target mechanisms, present distinct advantages for addressing diseases with complex pathogenesis. These products represent a novel advancement in Traditional Chinese Medicine, showcasing extensive therapeutic potential. In recent years, there has been a growing body of research investigating the mechanisms by which Chinese herbal natural products can be utilized in the treatment of DED[12–14].

Genistein is a naturally occurring isoflavone compound. Existing research indicates its close association with the body's immune and inflammatory responses. In regulating immune cell function, genistein influences NF-κB-dependent gene expression in dendritic cells while modulating mucosal immune responses by suppressing dendritic cell activity[15–17]. Furthermore, it can affect macrophages, inducing morphological alterations and G2/M cell cycle arrest, whilst also suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokines in human mast cell activation by inhibiting the ERK pathway[18, 19]. Regarding signalling mechanisms, genistein engages pathways involving p53, NF-κB, JNK, p38 MAPK, and ERK pathways. For instance, it modulates p53's control over NF-κB-dependent gene expression in TLR4-stimulated dendritic cells and regulates angiotensin II-induced NF-κB, JNK, and p38 MAPK activation in monocytes[20, 21]. Although a considerable body of research exists on rhamnose in dry eye treatment, its specific intervention mechanisms and relationship with immunity and inflammation remain understudied.

Bioinformatics and machine learning are integral to the progression of medical and life science research. Bioinformatics enhances the efficient processing and analysis of biological data, whereas machine learning facilitates pattern recognition and the identification of disease-related features from large datasets. Another key method employed in this study, single-cell RNA sequencing, serves as a core tool for bioinformatics-based biomarker prediction and mechanism exploration. In the realm of disease diagnosis and prognostic markers, it facilitates the identification of potential diagnostic and prognostic markers across multiple diseases[22–24]. Furthermore, it enables the discovery of cell-specific markers, such as those associated with intellectual disability, through single-cell RNA sequencing and transcriptomic bioinformatics approaches[25, 26]. It is particularly valuable for exploring the mechanisms of immune-related diseases, including immune cell heterogeneity, the genetic and cellular characteristics of immune checkpoint responses, and the immune microenvironmen[27, 28]. Furthermore, single-cell RNA sequencing analysis can reveal mechanisms related to transcriptional regulation, including ubiquitinylation pathways and key enzymes, as well as the transcriptomic dark matter[29, 30]. This holds significant biological and medical implications for interpreting cellular heterogeneity[31]. This study utilizes these methodologies to identify characteristic genes associated with DED and to discover novel candidate genes for use as clinical diagnostic markers and immune responses. Furthermore, we investigate traditional Chinese medicine natural products that target these genes, supporting the potential of Genistein in the treatment of DED. The findings are validated through further in vitro experiments. This comprehensive approach seeks to innovate diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for DED (Fig. 1).

Materials and methods

Bioinformatics

Collection of differentially expressed genes in DED

The datasets related to DED were retrieved by searching the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds/). The inclusion criteria for dataset selection were: a sample size of at least three per group, the inclusion of both normal and disease control samples, and datasets from the same species for comparative analysis, while preferentially selecting datasets from different species for validation purposes. Additionally, only datasets with accessible raw and complete data available for export and analysis were considered. The literature and dataset search was conducted up to 16 December 2025. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in DED were identified by using the R software limma package. Specifically, the datasets were processed, converted into expression matrices and grouped. The datasets were normalized and analyzed using the limma software package and set FDR < 0.05 and expression difference multiplicity (Fold Change, FC) > 1.5. The analysis obtained that the resulting genes were differentially expressed genes, which contained up- and down-regulated expression information of the genes.

To comprehensively collect DED-related genes, we further queried five commonly used databases (TTD, CTD, GeneCards, OMIM, and DISGENET) on 18 December 2025 using the keyword “dry eye disease”. Specific filtering criteria were applied for each database: in CTD, genes with the Direct Evidence tag “marker/mechanism” were selected; in DisGeNET, genes with Score_gda > 0.1; in GeneCards, genes with a relevance score > 5; in OMIM, genes with a definite Entrez Gene ID and Approved Symbol; and in TTD, all searched genes were selected. After removing duplicate entries from the combined results of the five databases, we obtained 3504 unique DED-associated targets. Subsequently, we intersected these genes with the DEGs from our initial analysis to obtain a refined set of 235 key expressed genes for DED.

Enrichment analysis of DEGs

In order to further understand the signalling pathways and biological processes involved in the enrichment of the key genes obtained from the analysis, we performed KEGG enrichment analysis and GO functional annotation using the "clusterProfiler" package in R language. The results were screened at P < 0.05. The KEGG enrichment analysis provided a clearer understanding of the signalling pathways in which the key genes were enriched, and the GO function annotation covered Biological Process (BP), Molecular Function (MF), and Cellular Function (CF), which can be analysed for a more comprehensive understanding of gene function. To further understand the reliability of the gene expression profiles and the associated functions of DEGs, we performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), a method used to test the significance of genomes in two biological states, to select the main enrichment functions. GSEA soft bases on MSigDB were used to annotate genomes, and significance was set at P < 0.05.

Establishment of protein–protein interaction (PPI) network and hub genes screening

The key genes obtained from the analysis were imported into the STRING 10.0 database (https://string-db.org/), and the species was set as "humo sapiens" to obtain the PPI network diagram. The PPI network was imported into Cytoscape (v3.8.1) to visualise the network. To understand the importance of individual genes in this network and to identify hub genes, we analysed the key functional modules of the PPI network using the Molecular Complexity Detection (MCODE) plugin to obtain tightly functioning sub-networks in the network, with the following parameter settings: degree cutoff = 2, maximum depth = 100, node score cutoff = 0.2, and kernel = 2. Then, we used the cytoHubba plugin to analyse the genes in the PPI based on the degree value algorithm to obtain the top 20 hub genes. Subsequently, we used GeneMANIA(https://genemania.org/) to generate co-expression networks for these hub genes.

Validation of hub genes and machine learning analysis

We validated them by another dataset using R. P < 0.05 were considered significant. Subsequently, we analysed and visualised the expression of featured genes in the GSE44101 expression profile.To further explore the diagnostic value of the intersecting genes, we constructed a predictive model of reliability using machine learning. Regression analysis was performed using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) with the glmnet package in R. Gene expression values were used as predictor variables and the sample groups as response variables. Cross-validation (cv) of the model was performed using the cv.glmnet function, and cv plots were drawn to determine the optimal regularization parameters. Based on the coefficients of the Lasso regression, genes with non-zero coefficients were identified as the selected signature genes. Next, we utilized the sophisticated SVM-RFE algorithm with the e1071 package, constructing another machine learning model. These analysis provided a more nuanced understanding of these genes.

Natural products reverse prediction and molecular docking analysis

After analysing the hub genes, we used the HIT2.0 (http://www.badd-cao.net:2345/) platform to perform reverse prediction on the basis of the hub genes, and searched for potentially active traditoinal Chinese medicine (TCM) natural products with hub genes. The network of hub genes and TCM natural products components was constructed to find out the association between them more clearly. The topological properties of the network were used to analyse the most important gene nodes and TCM natural products component nodes in the network. Lipinski's five-fold rule and gastrointestinal absorption of its key TCM natural products components were subsequently retrieved via SwissAMD (http://www.swissadme.ch/). After analysing and obtaining the key TCM natural products components, we performed molecular docking analysis between hub genes and their corresponding TCM natural products components. The 3D structures of the key proteins (PDB format) were downloaded using the PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/), and the *mol2 format of the analysed structures of their corresponding key components was collected using TCMSP (https://old.tcmsp-e.com/tcmsp.php). Docking was performed after converting the compound and target protein formats to *pdbqt format using AutoDock software. The docking results were imported into PyMOL for visualisation.

Immune infiltrate analysis

CIBERSORTx, an analytical tool from the Alizadeh Lab and Newman Lab, was utilized to impute gene expression profiles and estimate the abundances of 22 different member immune cell types in a mixed cell population[32]. We selected the CIBERSORT method using the R software packages ggstatsplot and ggplot2 based on our expression profiles to assess the relationship between shared Characteristic gene and immune cell infiltration within the tissue microenvironment.

The single-cell RNA sequencing analysis

For the single-cell RNA sequencing analysis, data were obtained from the GEO database. The Seurat package was employed to construct the object and exclude low-quality cells. Standard data preprocessing procedures were conducted to assess the number of genes, number of cells, and mitochondrial content. Cells with fewer than 200 or more than 4,000 detected genes, as well as those with high mitochondrial content (> 20%), were excluded. Following log-normalization, normalization was performed for each cell. A set of highly variable genes (HVGs), also known as "magnet genes," which exhibit significant differential expression between groups, were identified across all samples and utilized for grouping and cell identification. Marker genes for each cluster were determined based on their distinct expression patterns in the respective cell types. A minimum of two additional characteristic genes were required to define a cluster as a distinct population. Once all cell populations were identified based on annotation results, the cell populations were renamed. Subsequently, differences in metabolic features across different tissues were assessed, along with the cellular expression distribution of characteristic genes in the target tissues.

In vitro experiment

Cell lines and drugs

Human Corneal Epithelial cells-Transformed (HCE-T) (Guangzhou Cellcook Biotech Co.,Ltd, Guangzhou, China) were cultured at 37℃ and 5% CO2 in DMEM/F12 culture medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Procell, Catalog No. 164210), 5 μg/mL recombinant human insulin (Procell, Catalog No. PB180432) and 10 ng/mL recombinant human epidermal growth factor (Procell, Catalog No. PCK001),which was replaced every 2 days. Cells were passaged upon reaching a confluency of 90% or higher. The following key reagents were used for cell treatment groups in this study: Genistein (TargetMol, Catalog No. T1737), Recombinant human TNF-α (Novoprotein, Catalog No. DC008), Cyclosporin A (Shanghai Yuanye, Catalog No. B28163), Sodium chloride (≥ 99%, Aladdin, Cat. No. C111547).

Cell grouping and processing

HCE-T were utilized for in vitro validation. For drug toxicity testing, cells were divided into a blank control group and groups treated with Genistein at six different concentrations (3.125, 6.25, 12.5, 25, 37.5, 50 µmol/L). To screen for the optimal therapeutic concentration, an additional hyperosmolar model group was established alongside the blank control and Genistein treatment groups. In the model and Genistein treatment groups, a DED model was induced by exposing cells to 94 mM NaCl for 24 h. During this intervention, the blank control and model groups were cultured in DMEM medium alone, while the treatment groups received DMEM medium supplemented with their respective concentrations of Genistein. Following the 24 h intervention, all groups were returned to the incubator for an additional 24 h of culture before subsequent assays.

To concurrently address drug safety assessment, multi-model validation, and positive drug control, the main experiment employed an eight-group parallel design (Table 1). Log-phase HCE-T cells were seeded into 96-well or 6-well plates. After 24 h of attachment, cells were randomly assigned to the experimental groups. Drugs and stimulants were added simultaneously, followed by a 24-h incubation at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. Subsequently, CCK-8 assays, phase-contrast microscopy imaging, and sample collection for RT-qPCR and Western Blot analysis were performed. The final concentration of DMSO throughout the procedure was maintained at ≤ 0.1% to avoid solvent interference.

Cell detachment analysis in response to inflammatory and osmotic

To observe morphological alterations and detachment of HCE-T under inflammatory and hyperosmotic stress conditions, HCE-T were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 300,000 cells per well and incubated under standard culture conditions for 24 h. Subsequently, according to the aforementioned ‘Cell grouping and processing’ scheme, bright-field images were captured at 0 h and 24 h post-treatment using an inverted phase-contrast microscope at 100 × and 200 × magnification to document morphological changes and cell detachment. Image acquisition was performed in three biological replicates per treatment group, with multiple random fields selected per replicate. For quantitative analysis, images acquired at 100 × magnification were processed using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health(NIH), USA). Detached cell areas were segmented and quantified by thresholding followed by particle analysis. The percentage of detached area relative to the total field area was calculated to evaluate the extent of cell detachment. Data were derived from three independent experiments and are expressed as mean ± standard deviation.

Reverse transcription-quantitative poly merase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Inoculate 6-well plates and group cells as described in ‘Cell grouping and processing’. Collect eight groups of cells (n = 3) 24 h post-intervention. Extract total RNA using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) to extract total RNA. Following purity assessment, cDNA was synthesised using a reverse transcription kit (Beijing ComWin Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Subsequently, mRNA levels of CDK1, IL-1β, CDC20, and CCNA2 were detected using an RT-qPCR kit, with GAPDH as the internal control. Relative expression was calculated using the 2^(–ΔΔCt) method (Table 2).

Primer design

Western blot

Inoculate 6-well plates and group cells as described in ‘Cell grouping and processing’. Collect eight groups of cells (n = 3) 24 h post-intervention. RIPA lysis solution (Changsha Abiowell Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Changsha, China) was added to lysed the cells to extract proteins and protein concentration was determined by BCA method (Changsha Abiowell Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Changsha, China). Thirty µg of protein samples were taken for SDS-PAGE electrophoresis, and then transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride PVDF membranes at 100 V and closed for 2 h using skimmed milk powder (5%, 37 C), at the end of which CDK1(catalogue number: AWA10063), IL-1β(catalogue number: AWA10288), CDC20(catalogue number: AWA59190), CCNA2(catalogue number: AWA11009) primary antibody (dilution ratios:1:500, Rabbit anti, Changsha Abiowell Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Changsha, China) dilutions were added, incubated overnight at 4 °C and then washed using TBST, then secondary antibody dilutions (dilution ratios:1:5000, Goat anti-Rabbit IgG (H + L) Secondary Antibody, Changsha Abiowell Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Changsha, China) were added and incubated for 2 h, and ECL was used to develop the colour and then exposed for photographs, and the actin as internal reference for grey value analysis. Chemiluminescent signals from Western blot membranes were analyzed using Quantity One analysis software (Bio-Rad, version 4.6.2). After local background subtraction for each band, regions corresponding to target proteins and the loading control (actin) were manually selected to obtain volume-integrated optical density values. To normalize for loading variations, relative protein expression was calculated using the formula: target protein IOD / corresponding actin IOD. Each experiment was independently repeated three times (n = 3), with final data presented as mean ± standard deviation of the relative expression values from three independent replicates.

Statistical analysis

The data of this experiment were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software, and the significance was determined by P < 0.05. The normality test was performed on the sample first, if the data conformed to normality, the One-Way ANOVA test was performed, and when the variance was uniform, multiple comparisons between the groups were made using Bonferroni. If the variance was not uniform, multiple comparisons of the components were made using the Tamheeni method.

PPI network establishment and hub gene screening

In order to further understand the interactions between the key DEGs of DED, we performed PPI network analysis and visualisation of the results by String 10.0. The results of the PPI network showed that there were 117 interacting gene-protein ‘nodes’ and 183 interacting ‘edges’, as shown in Fig. 4A. The PPI network was further extracted and analysed by MCODE plug-in, and 8 sub-networks with closer interactions were obtained, as shown in Fig. 4B. The sub-network with the largest number of nodes, cluster 1, contains 8 nodes and 27 edges. To identify hub genes in the PPI network, the cytoHubba plugin was used for analysis based on the degree value algorithm, and the top 20 hub genes were identified, including CD44, CDK1, CCNA2, CXCL1, CXCR2, CDC20, EXO1, CXCL13, IL-1β, ASPM, KIF11, MKI67, TOP2A, CHEK1, RAD51, FEN1, CCR1, CCR7, CXCL2, and SELL (Fig. 4C). These genes were considered core candidates involved in DED pathogenesis due to their high connectivity in the protein–protein interaction network, implying potential synergistic effects in regulating DED-related biological processes. Subsequently, we evaluated the co-expression network and associated roles of the hub genes using the GeneMANIA database. The network results showed the co-expression of 65.34%, physical interactions of 10.62%, Predicted of 12.44%, Co- localisation of 6.63%, Shared protein domains of 3.81%, Pathway of 0.72%, Genetic Interactions of 0.42%, as shown in Fig. 4D.

Validation of hub genes and machine learning analysis

We performed validation analysis of the 20 DED hub genes obtained from the analysis by other DED-related dataset GSE171043、GSE84844 and GSE150771 in the GEO database. A t-test was performed for each of the two subsets in each dataset. The significance level was P < 0.05. Integrating the validation results from datasets GSE171043, GSE84844, and GSE150771, we found that 18 genes exhibited differential expression across all datasets. However, not all differential expression reached statistical significance. Specifically, in the GSE84844 dataset, 20 genes showed differential expression, of which 13 were statistically significant(Fig. 5). In contrast, in the GSE150771 and GSE171043 datasets, 20 and 18 genes were differentially expressed, respectively, but without statistical significance, a limitation potentially attributable to the smaller sample sizes of these cohorts(Fig. 6, 7). Among these differentially expressed genes, 14 maintained a consistent direction of change across the datasets, suggesting a degree of reliability in the findings. The 13 statistically significant differentially expressed genes in the GSE84844 dataset were CHEK1, CDK1, CD44, CDC20, CCNA2, CCR7, CCR1, SELL, TOP2A, ASPM, KIF11, FEN1, and CXCR2.

Natural products reverse prediction and molecular docking analysis

After identifying the hub genes of DED, we performed the reverse prediction of TCM natural products small molecules and constructed a gene-natural products network, which contained a total of 126 nodes, of which 17 were gene nodes and 109 were TCM natural products small molecule nodes, as well as a total of 138 edges involved (Fig. 9). By analysing the topological properties of the network, the results showed that in this network, the genes associated with the most number of TCM natural products small molecules with each other were IL-1β, followed by CDK1, CCNA2. The next TCM natural products small molecules associated with the most number of genes were Quercetin and Genistein. Quercetin was associated with CDK1, IL-1β, TOP2A, CXCL2. Genistein was associated with CDK1, IL-1β, CDC20, CCNA2.

Overall, Genistein demonstrated a lower average binding energy with its corresponding targets. Therefore, we selected Genistein and its respective targets for subsequent analysis and validation.

Discussion

DED is a common ocular condition characterized by symptoms of dryness and burning, primarily due to decreased tear production or compromised tear quality. Traditional treatments, such as artificial tears, warm compresses, and massage, often provide limited relief and may lead to dependency. With the increasing prevalence of DED, it is imperative to identify treatments that are safe, effective, and convenient. Recent research has underscored the potential of various herbal natural products in mitigating ocular surface disorders, enhancing tear secretion, and modulating corneal structure and function, thereby ameliorating DED symptoms[34]. Nonetheless, systematic methodologies for the identification of optimal herbal components are still in their infancy. Although bioinformatics and machine learning were initially developed for oncological research, their application is now extending to non-oncological domains[35–37]. Utilizing these approaches in DED research could offer significant insights into its pathogenesis and aid in the comprehensive identification of targeted herbal components, potentially transforming DED treatment through precise therapeutic interventions.

In the present study, the results showed that in KEGG enrichment analysis Cytokine-Cytokine receptor interaction, PI3K-Akt signalling pathway, IL-17 signalling pathway, JAK-STAT signalling pathway were the top-ranked key signalling pathways. Cytokines, which are soluble extracellular proteins or glycoproteins, serve as pivotal intercellular regulators and mobilizers involved in both innate and adaptive inflammatory host defenses. They also play significant roles in cell growth, differentiation, apoptosis, angiogenesis, as well as developmental and repair processes aimed at restoring in vivo homeostasis[38]. Interactions among cytokines are essential for maintaining health and are critical components of immune and inflammatory responses to pathological conditions[39]. The PI3K-Akt signaling pathway is integral to the regulation of inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis. Phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase (PI3K) is a crucial molecule in the regulation of cell growth, metabolism, and apoptosis[40]. Protein kinase B (AKT), a serine-threonine kinase activated by phosphorylation, is regulated by PI3K and influences various cellular processes, including the regulation of inflammation, reactive oxygen species (ROS), proliferation, and apoptosis[41]. Empirical evidence indicates that AKT activation mitigates cellular inflammatory responses and reduces apoptosis[42]. Furthermore, the interleukin 17 (IL-17) family is instrumental in the pathogenesis of inflammatory diseases. Autoimmune diseases can be influenced by IL-17[43]. Current research indicates a strong association between IL-17 and the development of DED[44], as IL-17 acts as an effector of Th17 immune cells[45]. Th17 cells play a crucial role in autoimmune diseases and the body's defense mechanisms. IL-17 is typically implicated in inflammatory responses and autoimmune conditions. The JAK-STAT signaling pathway has been demonstrated to contribute to the pathogenesis of inflammatory and autoimmune diseases. Numerous cytokines involved in these diseases utilize the JAK-STAT pathway for intracellular signal transduction[46]. Furthermore, hyperactivation of the JAK-STAT pathway is linked to premature aging, and targeting this pathway can potentially delay aging and reduce inflammatory markers[47]. GSEA has associated this pathway with processes such as the cell cycle, chemokine signaling, cytokine-cytokine receptor interactions, hematopoietic cell lineage, and the JAK-STAT signaling pathway. Notably, the pathways that differed from the KEGG enrichment results were the cell cycle and hematopoietic cell lineage. Cell cycle is a cell division-related cyclic change that includes G1, S, G2 and M phases, which is regulated by a variety of signals, and is associated with a wide range of diseases. The Cell cycle pathway is often located in the Cell cycle pathway is often located downstream of PI3K-AKT, MAPK and other pathways. Some studies have found that cell cycle can reduce tearing and episodes of DED[48].

Subsequently, we further mined the key genes by module analysis of genes, cytohubba, and obtained 20 hub genes as CD44, CDK1, CCNA2, CXCL1, CXCR2, CDC20, EXO1, CXCL13, IL-1β, ASPM, KIF11, MKI67, TOP2A, CHEK1, RAD51, FEN1, CCR1, CCR7, CXCL2, and SELL. RAD51, FEN1, CCR1, CCR7, CXCL2, and SELL. We validated these 20 hub genes using additional databases, and the results indicated that all 20 genes were differentially expressed in the validation dataset. Among these, CDK1, CCNA2, CXCL13, CCR1, FEN1, CCR7, and SELL exhibited statistically significant differences. Subsequently, we assessed the diagnostic performance of the hub genes using machine learning methods, revealing SELL, RAD51, FEN1, CXCL13, CXCL1, CCR7, KIF11, and CDK1 as characteristic genes. Cyclin-dependent kinase 1 (CDK1) is a serine/threonine kinase that plays a crucial role in cell cycle progression. CDK1 is closely associated with estradiol, and it has been observed that the estradiol-mediated regulation of oxidative stress and inflammation is significantly reduced or even abolished when CDK1 is knocked down[49]. Furthermore, CDK1 is linked to organismal immune infiltration, where high CDK1 expression results in increased infiltration of immune-activated cells and decreased infiltration of immune-suppressed cells[50]. Similarly, chemokine (C-X-C motif) ligand 13 (CXCL13), a B-cell chemokine, is instrumental in immune cell transport[51]. Widely involved in the pathogenesis of many autoimmune and inflammatory diseases as well as lymphoproliferative disorders[52]. Research has demonstrated that TNF-α and IL-10 regulate the expression of CXCL13 via the nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) and Janus kinase/signal transducers and activators of transcription (JAK/STAT) pathways, respectively. [53]. These cytokines are notably elevated in the serum of patients with[54]. Importantly, CXCL13 has emerged as a diagnostic biomarker for dry syndrome, with elevated serum levels observed in affected individuals[55]. Flap endonuclease 1 (FEN1) plays a pivotal role in DNA replication and repair[56], processes that are also implicated in the pathogenesis of DED[48]. The chemokine receptor CCR7, expressed on dendritic cells, is closely linked to inflammation of the corneal and ocular surface[57]. The primary mechanism involves aberrant CCR7 expression, which disrupts dendritic cell trafficking and precipitates a cascade of inflammatory responses[58]. Additionally, L-selectin (SELL), also referred to as CD62L, is essential for lymphocyte migration through high endothelial venules into secondary lymphoid organs, sites of tumor growth, and areas of chronic inflammation. Age-related immune dysfunction is linked to a heightened incidence of chronic inflammatory diseases. Research indicates that CD62L expression diminishes with aging, and enhancing CD62L expression may stimulate immune responses in chronic inflammatory conditions related to aging[59]. The key genes analyzed in this study were consistent with existing literature. Subsequently, we conducted predictions of TCM natural products targeting these hub genes and identified Quercetin and Genistein as the most critical compounds. Molecular docking analyses revealed that Genistein exhibited the lowest binding energy to CDC20. The hub genes associated with Genistein—CDK1, IL-1β, CDC20, and CCNA2—were consistent with those identified in the previous analysis.

In addition to the classic hyperosmolarity-induced model, this study established a TNF-α-stimulated corneal cell inflammation model and selected CsA as a positive control. As a key pro-inflammatory cytokine in the DED microenvironment, TNF-α induces apoptosis and inflammatory cascades in corneal epithelial cells via activation of the NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways, thereby complementing the osmotic stress injury induced by hyperosmolarity and collectively simulating the complex pathophysiological processes of DED. The use of CsA as a positive control offers clear clinical relevance, as this agent is currently an FDA-approved first-line therapy for DED, exerting its immunosuppressive effects primarily through the blockade of T-cell activation and the transcription of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-2. The results of cellular experiments showed that 12.5 μmol/L Genistein was the most applicable drug concentration for intervening in human corneal epithelial cells in both hyperosmolar and TNF-α-induced conditions. Bright-field morphological assessment revealed that hyperosmolar stimulation with sodium chloride induced immediate cellular detachment at 0 h, characterized by an acute and abrupt pattern of barrier disruption. This finding aligns with the canonical pathophysiological process of hyperosmotic stress, suggesting that this type of insult primarily results in physical, mechanical disruption of cell–matrix adhesion. In contrast, the TNF-α group did not elicit detachment of comparable intensity. However, cells exhibited widespread atrophy and indistinct boundaries, consistent with a programmed injury response mediated predominantly by intracellular signaling cascades and manifesting as biologically driven damage. In both injury models, Genistein enhanced the capacity of corneal epithelial cells to resist detachment and promoted proliferative repair of the denuded area. This shared efficacy across distinct models suggests that Genistein may exert protective effects by stabilizing the cytoskeleton and reinforcing cell adhesion. As a potent immunosuppressant, CsA exhibited marked cytoprotective effects in the TNF-α-injured corneal epithelium. Morphological observation revealed that CsA reversed the atrophic phenotype observed in the TNF-α group and significantly reduced the area of cellular loss. Notably, Genistein demonstrated a level of protection comparable to that of CsA in mitigating cell detachment and loss in the TNF-α model. Collectively, these morphological findings confirm the protective effects of Genistein on corneal epithelial cell viability and adherent morphology, establishing a phenotypic anchor for further investigation into whether its protective effects are associated with the normalization of cell cycle progression. RT-qPCR and WB analyses demonstrated that, in the hyperosmolar model, osmotic stress induced by NaCl led to cell cycle dysregulation and inflammatory activation, as evidenced by downregulation of CDC20 and upregulation of CDK1, Cyclin A2, and IL-1β. As an isoflavone compound with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, Genistein may alleviate hyperosmotic stress by modulating signaling pathways such as MAPK/NF-κB, thereby restoring cell cycle progression and suppressing the release of inflammatory cytokines. In this model, Genistein exhibited superior protective effects compared to CsA, suggesting its potential application in pathological conditions associated with hyperosmolar DED. In the TNF-α-induced inflammatory model, IL-1β expression was markedly upregulated, indicating a more pronounced inflammatory response. Although Genistein partially restored the expression of cell cycle-related proteins, its inhibitory effect on IL-1β was limited. In contrast, CsA, a classic immunosuppressant, potently suppressed the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β by inhibiting calcineurin activity, thereby demonstrating prominent anti-inflammatory efficacy. However, CsA exerted only modest regulatory effects on CDC20 expression, a finding consistent with the immune infiltration analysis in this study, which revealed no direct correlation between CDC20 and immune activity. Genistein exhibited a dual role in regulating cell cycle progression and mitigating inflammation, with particularly comprehensive effects observed in the hyperosmolar model. While CsA effectively suppressed inflammation under robust inflammatory conditions, its capacity to correct cell cycle aberrations was limited. The parallel use of two injury models alongside a clinically relevant positive control not only validated the cytoprotective role of Genistein under hyperosmotic conditions but also confirmed its anti-inflammatory effects in the context of direct inflammatory stimulation. Moreover, Genistein demonstrated corneal epithelial protective efficacy comparable to that of CsA, a standard clinical therapeutic agent.

Genistein is a flavonoid compound classified within the isoflavonoid group, structurally analogous to endogenous estrogens, and is recognized as a natural phytoestrogen. It can bind to estrogen receptors (ERα and ERβ), albeit with a lower affinity than estradiol, thereby exerting estrogen-like effects through these receptors[60]. Furthermore, Genistein exhibits antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, promotes apoptosis and cell cycle arrest, and has beneficial effects on age-related diseases[61]. A study demonstrated that Genistein ameliorated DED symptoms in ovariectomized rats, primarily by inhibiting IL-1β levels and enhancing the expression of ER-β and MUC5AC in a DED model. This study proposes that genistein flavonoids may serve as a potential alternative strategy to mitigate inflammation and mucin production in DED[62]. The analysis of results from this study identified IL-1β, CDK1, CDC20, and CCNA2 as pivotal genes involved in the intervention of Genistein in DED[63]. Notably, IL-1β plays a critical role as an expression factor in the inflammatory response associated with DED. Inflammation is a primary contributor to ocular surface damage and dryness in DED patients, and it precipitates epithelial cell apoptosis, squamous metaplasia, and further apoptosis. Previous studies have demonstrated that IL-1β expression is elevated in both the conjunctiva and tear fluid in a mouse model of DED. Furthermore, reducing IL-1β expression has been shown to inhibit the progression of ocular surface inflammation and alleviate damage to the ocular surface[64]. The occurrence mechanism is associated with the hyperosmolar state of tears, wherein increased tear osmolarity activates the MAPK signaling pathway. This activation subsequently leads to the stimulation of transcription factors such as AP-1 and NF-κB, resulting in the overexpression of IL-1β[65]. Furthermore, IL-1β facilitates the release of IL-17A from Th17 cells, contributing to reduced tear film stability and ocular surface damage, thereby exacerbating dryness and inflammation[66]. CDC20 is a critical component in the regulation of the cell cycle[67, 68], and its expression silencing has been shown to induce apoptosis[69]. DED is intricately linked to apoptosis in ocular surface tissues, suggesting that apoptosis may play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of dry keratoconjunctivitis[70]. By inhibiting apoptosis, the manifestation of ocular surface epithelial disorders due to dryness can be alleviated, thereby preserving corneal health[71]. Additionally, CDC20 is subject to regulation by estrogen and progesterone[72]. The development of DED is associated with fluctuations in estrogen levels, and endogenous estrogen levels are crucial for maintaining ocular surface homeostasis, particularly in postmenopausal women[73]. CCNA2 is a protein-coding gene that encodes proteins belonging to the highly conserved family of cell cycle proteins, members of which function to regulate the cell cycle. The activation of cell cycle protein-dependent kinase 2 facilitates the transitions between the G1/S and G2/M phases and is ubiquitously expressed across most human tissues, with a noTable association with oocyte meiosis[74]. Contemporary research predominantly focuses on the role of CCNA2 in tumorigenesis, while investigations into its involvement in non-tumor pathologies remain limited[75, 76]. Nevertheless, as research into apoptosis, cellular death, and other forms of cell demise advances, the scope of cell cycle-related studies has expanded beyond oncological contexts[77]. There is a growing interest in the role of the cell cycle in aging-related diseases, with cellular senescence—characterized by sTable cell cycle arrest—being increasingly recognized as a contributing factor in the pathogenesis of DED. The clearance of senescent cells has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy for DED[78]. Furthermore, CDK1, previously discussed, is significantly implicated in estrogen-mediated oxidative stress and inflammatory responses. CDK1 also plays a crucial role in the oocyte meiosis, and it operates synergistically with CCNA2[74]. PI3K/AKT is an important pathway involved in oxidative damage and inflammation[79]. IL-1β can rapidly stimulate PI3K and Akt phosphorylation, which activates the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway and triggers strong inflammatory responses. It has been reported that CCNA2 expression promotes the migration, invasion and metastasis of cells via the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway[80]. CDK1 has been demonstrated to inhibit the phosphorylation of PDK1, thereby activating the PI3K/Akt pathway and facilitating the development of pluripotency[81]. Analogous to PI3K, the Jak-STAT pathway is a well-established inflammatory signaling cascade. The JAK-STAT signaling pathway is associated with the proliferation of inflammatory cells. Studies have shown that JAK-STAT signaling mechanisms promote the phosphorylation of STAT1 at Tyr701, which enhances the cellular response to IL-1β[82]. Immune infiltration and single-cell RNA sequencing analyses have revealed that IL-1β plays a role in various immune responses in DED. Both CCNA2 and CDK1 are implicated in immune responses. Recent research suggests that IL-1β is crucial for maintaining immune homeostasis and barrier immunity, thereby inducing a series of immune responses[83]. The expression of CCNA2 is positively correlated with Th2 cell infiltration and negatively correlated with the infiltration of CD4 + central memory and effector memory T cells [84]. Furthermore, CCNA2 has been shown to exert a protective effect on innate immune responses, mitigating pathogen-induced inflammation and apoptosis, and is essential for the regulation of immune-related gene expression[85]. In conclusion, Genistein can alleviate hypertonic corneal cell damage, and its alleviating effect on DED is mainly related to anti-inflammation, anti-oxidative stress and reduction of apoptosis.

MMP9 (matrix metalloproteinase-9) exhibits abnormally high expression in dry eye syndrome, considered one of the key drivers of ocular surface damage. Research indicates elevated MMP9 levels in the tears and ocular surface of DED patients, closely associated with disruption of the tight junctions in the corneal epithelium—a breakdown linked to severe symptoms of the condition. Furthermore, increased MMP9 activity leads to desquamation of the ocular surface epithelium, exacerbating ocular surface injury[86]. In distinct dry eye syndromes, such as Sjögren's syndrome and meibomian gland dysfunction, MMP9 expression levels are markedly elevated compared to healthy controls, particularly in Sjögren's syndrome patients. This may stem from direct autoimmune attacks on the ocular surface epithelium[87]. MMP9 not only plays a pivotal role in ocular surface injury but is also closely associated with inflammatory responses. Research indicates that MMP9 expression can be regulated via the NF-κB signalling pathway, which plays a key role in the inflammatory response of dry eye syndrome [88]. Furthermore, inhibition of MMP9 effectively ameliorates dry eye symptoms, reducing ocular surface inflammation and damage to corneal barrier function. These findings indicate that MMP9 serves not only as a significant biomarker of the inflammatory response in dry eye syndrome but also as a potential therapeutic target. In the treatment of dry eye syndrome, inhibiting MMP9 activity is considered an effective strategy. Several novel drug compounds have been developed, aiming to directly or indirectly suppress MMP9 activity to improve corneal barrier function, reduce ocular surface inflammation, and restore tear secretion[87]. Furthermore, the probiotic LB101 alleviates inflammatory responses in dry eye syndrome by modulating the NF-κB signalling pathway within the gut microbiota, thereby suppressing MMP9 expression [89]. This demonstrates that regulating the NF-κB pathway can inhibit MMP-9 expression and consequently mitigate inflammatory reactions associated with dry eye syndrome. Research has revealed that following high expression of IL-1β as an initiator pro-inflammatory factor, the NF-κB p65 subunit is released into the nucleus, driving the transcription and secretion of MMP-9. IL-1β promotes corneal epithelial cell migration via the NF-κB and AP-1 pathways. Furthermore, IL-1β induces MMP-9 expression through NF-κB activation and nuclear translocation[89]. This finding directly supports the core argument that IL-1β drives MMP9 transcription via the NF-κB p65 subunit. Additional research indicates that CDK1 not only regulates the cell cycle but also influences inflammatory responses through interactions with the NF-κB pathway[90]. CDK1 exhibits diverse regulatory mechanisms across different inflammatory models. In allergic rhinitis studies, CDK1 is recognised as a key protein closely associated with the TNF-α/NF-κB signalling pathway, suggesting its crucial role in inflammatory responses. CCNA2 serves as a functional regulatory subunit for CDK1; both must bind to form the active CDK1/CCNA2 complex to exert kinase activity[91]. Therefore, although CCNA2 itself does not directly interact with NF-κB pathway molecules, its regulation of the NF-κB pathway may depend on its binding to CDK1[92]. Consequently, the NF-κB signalling pathway is the core pathway identified in this study for analysing the regulation of MMP9 expression by CDK1, CCNA2 and IL-1β in the body's dry eye inflammatory response.

As research into Chinese medicine for various chronic and challenging diseases continues to advance, the exploration of its application in treating DED has become increasingly comprehensive. Numerous scholars have identified quercetin as having a beneficial impact on DED treatment[93–95], a finding corroborated by the analyses conducted in this study. Quercetin alleviates DED through multiple mechanisms, with some studies indicating its anti-inflammatory properties and its ability to ameliorate endoplasmic reticulum stress and the inflammatory response in lacrimal gland cells induced by DED[96]. Concurrently, extensive research has demonstrated that resveratrol also holds promise as a treatment for DED[97–99]. In the DED model[14], resveratrol has been shown to reduce the secretion of cytokines IL-6, IL-8, and IP-10 in conjunctival and corneal cells. Furthermore, it diminishes the production of reactive oxygen species in corneal epithelial cells[100]. Abengózarvela et al.[14]reported that in a mouse model of DED, treatment with 0.1% resveratrol led to a reduction in IL-1α concentration in tear fluid, decreased CD4 + T cell infiltration in the conjunctiva, and a lower degree of corneal fluorescein staining. Researchers have demonstrated that both total flavonoids from Epimedium and androgens can down-regulate the expression of TNF-α and IFN-γ in the dry conjunctival epithelial cells of male rabbits. Notably, Icariin constitutes a major component of the flavonoids derived from Epimedium[101]. The total flavonoids from Epimedium exhibited superior down-regulation and also inhibited the expression of IL-6 in a lacrimal epithelial cell model of DED. The plasma containing Epimedium total flavonoids demonstrated an anti-inflammatory effect by suppressing IL-6 expression[102]. Furthermore, total flavonoids from Epimedium were found to enhance Bcl-2 expression and reduce Bax expression in the lacrimal glands of dry-eyed male rabbits, with effects becoming increasingly comparable to those of androgens over prolonged administration. These findings contribute significantly to the body of research on herbal natural products for DED treatment. In this study, a novel bioinformatic network approach was employed to identify new TCM natural products for DED, thereby enriching the therapeutic options for the disease. Additionally, the pathogenesis associated with DED was further explored and validated through vitro cellular experiments. This is the innovation and advantage of this research method, and it is also a more comprehensive approach in the current mining of DED-related TCM natural products.

While this study offers valuable insights into DED mechanisms and treatment through multidisciplinary analyses and validation, certain limitations remain. Initially, while multiple components of TCM natural products were identified, only one natural product underwent experimental validation. This leaves the synergistic effects of combined natural products unexplored. Further research is necessary to elucidate the pharmacological properties and interactions of these TCM natural products. Additionally, the study was limited to in vitro cellular experiments, lacking in vivo validation of drug metabolism and systemic pathways through animal models. These limitations underscore the necessity for further research, while simultaneously offering valuable directions for future studies. Subsequent investigations should prioritize animal experiments to validate TCM natural products and their targets, facilitating a more comprehensive exploration of the pathogenesis of DED. Moreover, clinical studies focusing on biomarker discovery and therapeutic efficacy evaluation could bridge the gap between experimental findings and clinical applications. These future endeavors will deepen our understanding of DED inflammation, immune mechanisms, and herbal therapeutics, providing a robust foundation for developing novel treatment strategies and advancing drug development.

Conclusion

According to our study mentioned above, the pathogenesis of DED is complex and related to Cytokine-Cytokine receptor interaction, PI3K-Akt signalling pathway, IL-17 signalling pathway, JAK-STAT signalling pathway, JAK-STAT signalling pathway, etc. The most diagnostic genes in DED are CDK1, CCNA2, CXCL13, CCR1, FEN1, CCR7, SELL, RAD51, CXCL1 and KIF11, which are closely related to inflammation, oxidative stress, immune response, cellular senescence, and other pathways and genes. Genistein has a positive effect in the treatment of DED, and its mechanism of action may be related to the regulation of CDK1, IL-1β, CDC20, CCNA2 for anti-inflammation, anti-oxidative stress, and reduction of apoptosis.

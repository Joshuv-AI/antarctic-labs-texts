Comparison of the effect of anticoccidial drug, probiotic, synbiotic, phytochemicals and vaccine in prevention and control of coccidiosis in broiler chickens challenged with Eimeria spp
Authors: Eghbal Javanmiri, Shaban Rahimi, Mohammad Amir Karimi Torshizi, Sedigheh Nabiyan, Alireza Behnamifar, Jesse Grimes
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The objective of this study was to investigate the effects of an anti-coccidiosis drug, vaccine, probiotic, symbiotic, and phytochemicals in the prevention and control of coccidia infection in broilers. A total of 525 one-day-old Ross 308 chicks were randomly allocated to 7 experimental diets with 5 replicates of 15 birds each in a completely randomized design. Experimental diets consisted of negative control (NC) without any additives and not challenged. The other 6 groups were challenged with mixed Eimeria and fed the basal diet with no additives (Positive Control, PC) or supplemented with Coxidine 100 (1 g / 1 kg), probiotic, synbiotic, Livacox T vaccine and phytobiotic additives based on the manufacturer's recommended dose. Body weight gain (WG), feed intake (FI) and feed conversion ratio (FCR) were recorded weekly. Oocysts per gram of excreta (OPG) were determined on d 25 to 33 and 42. One bird per cage was euthanized to analyze lesion score and jejunum and ileum inflammatory genes expression. Coccidial challenge reduced WG (P < 0.05) during 15 to 28 d and vaccine treatment was more effective in improving WG and FCR on d 29 to 42 and 1 to 42 (P < 0.05) than other treatments. Birds in the PC group had higher (P < 0.05) OPG than NC group for all days and the vaccine treatment resulted in the lowest rate of OPG compared to other treatments (P < 0.05) at 27, 28, 29, 30, 32, and 33 d of age and overall average. Relative mRNA levels of IFN-γ, IL-1β and IL-10 were significantly upregulated among treatments under coccidiosis challenge in jejunum and ileum except for IL-1β expression in the ileum. In conclusion, based on the results of this study the individual characteristics of feed additives for the prevention of coccidiosis can be different depending on the type and source of feed additives, duration, and amount used, levels of oocyst inoculation and Eimeria types.

INTRODUCTION

Coccidiosis is a common health problem in poultry, caused by several species of Eimeria parasites, that is estimated to cost the poultry industry billions of dollars annually (Hansen et al., 2021). These parasites proliferate in the intestinal epithelium resulting potentially in bloody diarrhea, reduced digestibility, poor growth, and death (Lee et al., 2022). Seven species of Eimeria are currently known to infect chickens of which E. tenella, E. acervuline, and E. maxima are the most frequently identified (Lillehoj et al., 2015). Currently, various anti-coccidiosis drugs, such as ionophores and coccidiostats, have been used to control coccidiosis (Holdsworth et al., 2004; Lee et al., 2022). However, the long-term and widespread use of anticoccidiosis drugs has raised concerns such as antimicrobial resistance and drug residues in egg and meat products (Tonda et al., 2018). Therefore, much research has been made for alternative strategies, including vaccines and feed additives such as probiotics, synbiotics, and phytochemicals for reducing the effects of coccidiosis in poultry.

For many years, vaccines have been used to control coccidiosis in laying hens and broiler breeders (Chapman, 1999). In recent years, coccidiosis vaccines have become an increasingly common method for preventing and controlling coccidiosis in antibiotic-free meat production in broilers (Das et al., 2021). However, vaccines raise concerns such as increasing the susceptibility of chickens to secondary infections (Cai et al., 2023), inducing localized inflammation, and reducing weight gain and feed efficiency (Williams, 2002), which has led some to focus on the use of feed additive products

Probiotics, as live nonpathogenic microorganisms, have the potential to maintain a normal gut microbiota through diverse modes of action including competitive exclusion, production of antibacterial compounds, stimulation of the immune system, and reduced severity of intestinal lesions (Ritzi et al., 2016; Cai et al., 2023). Few studies have investigated the beneficial effects of probiotics against coccidiosis. Park et al. (2020) reported that diet supplemented with B. subtilis enhanced intestinal immunity and epithelial barrier integrity in E. maxima-infected chickens. Dalloul et al., (2003) and Dalloul and Lillehoj (2005) reported the beneficial effects of Lactobacillus-based probiotics in increasing local immunity against coccidiosis.

Synbiotics are synergetic combinations of probiotics and prebiotics, such as nondigestible feed ingredients, that beneficially affect the host by selectively stimulating bacteria in the gut (Lee et al., 2010a). Synbiotics have inhibitory effects against Eimeria infection similar to probiotics and are emerging as a new approach to coccidiosis control (Elmusharaf et al., 2007; Angwech et al., 2019). Phytochemical/herbal medicine has shown strong anticoccidial effects in terms of a significant reduction in intestinal lesions, maintaining gut health, and improving bird growth (Felici et al., 2020). Park et al. (2023) reported that a mixture of thymol, carvacrol, and saponins had a strong anticoccidial effect by inhibition of the invasion of epithelial cells by Eimeria sporozoites. The objective of this study was to compare the effects of an anti-coccidiosis drug, vaccine, probiotic, symbiotic, and phytochemicals in prevention and control of chicken coccidia infection.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Bird Husbandry

The trial was conducted at the Poultry Research Center of the Faculty of Agriculture, Tarbiat Modares University (Tehran, Iran). The animal care and use protocol was approved by the Tarbiat Modares University Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC protocol number: 1269033). A total of 525 one-day-old broiler chickens (ROSS 308) were obtained, weighed and randomly allocated to 7 treatment diets in 5 replicates each with 15 birds per pen (4 male and 11 female). A completely randomized design was used and all chicks were housed using wood shavings litter in an environmental controlled room and fed to 42 d of age. All chicks were allowed ad-libitum access to diets and water and were reared under constant lightening regime.

Treatments and Experimental Diets

Experimental dietary treatments consisted of (1) negative control group (NC): basal diet, untreated, and unchallenged; (2) positive control group (PC): basal diet, untreated, and challenged with mixed Eimeria spp.; (3) basal diet supplemented with Coxidine 100 (containing Sulfa quinoxaline and Diaveridine- 1 g / kg), and challenged with mixed Eimeria spp.; (4) basal diet supplemented with probiotic (Multi Behsil 100, Bahmananzim Co. Iran), challenged with mixed Eimeria spp.; (5) basal diet supplemented with synbiotic (Synbiozyme 500 contains multi-enzyme, probiotic and prebiotic, Bahmananzim Co. Iran), challenged with mixed Eimeria spp.; (6) basal diet + Livacox T vaccine (contains coccidian oocysts of: Eimeria acervulina, Eimeria tenella, Eimeria maxima, Biopharm Co. Czech) gavage in the crop on the first day, challenged with mixed Eimeria spp.; and (7) basal diet supplemented with phytobiotic (Phytmax Cox ccinnamon, tumeric extract, capsicum oleoresin and piper nigrum, Phytosolution Co. Spain), and challenged with mixed Eimeria species. Birds were provided a 3-phase feeding program including starter (0–14 d), grower (15–28 d), and finisher (29–42 d). The basal diets were formulated to meet nutritional requirements reported by the National Research Council (NRC, 1994) and were fed in mash form. Coccidia Infection Challenge

On d 19, all experimental groups of birds except the NC group were challenged via oral gavage with 1 × 200 μL of a suspension containing 100 thousand oocysts with a mixture of 70% E. tenella, 15% E. maxima and 15% E. acervulina and E. necatrix. The birds in the NC group were administered the same volume of distilled water. On d 25 to 33 and 42 (6 to 14 - and 23-d post infection), 10 excreta samples were collected from each pen od birds and were put in separate airtight plastic bags and were pooled and stored at 4°C. The samples were transferred to the veterinary laboratory of the University of Tehran for oocyst counting. Samples were counted microscopically using a McMaster counting chamber and reported as oocysts per gram of excreta (OPG) based on the method of Conway and McKenzie (Conway and McKenzie, 2007).

Growth Performance and Lesion Score

Growth performance parameters including Average Weight Gain (AWG), Average Feed Intake (AFI), and Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR) were recorded weekly on a pen basis. Mortality was recorded as it occurred. On d 28 and 35, one female bird per replicate was randomly selected, weighed and killed by cervical dislocation and necropsied to determine the presence and degree of coccidiosis lesions in the duodenum, jejunum, and ileum by the method of Johnson and Reid (1970) with some changes (Lee et al., 2010b; Pasaribu et al., 2016).

Quantitative of Genes Expression

From birds that were euthanized on d 28, samples of the jejunum (5 centimeters before the Meckel's diverticulum) and ileum (5 centimeters after the Meckel's diverticulum) were collected (3 samples/treatment) and washed with physiological serum. The samples were immediately marked and frozen in liquid nitrogen and subsequently stored at -80°C. The tissue samples were homogenized and total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) according to the manufacturer's manual. The quantity of total RNA extracted were determined using a Nanodrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) at 260 nm and integrity evaluated by electrophoresis on 1% agarose gel with voltage 80 for 30 minutes. Five micrograms of total extracted RNA were treated with one micrograms of DNase and used to synthesize first-strand cDNA by reverse transcription using a reverse transcription kit in a 10 µl reaction system according to the following conditions: 25°C for 10 min, 50°C for 50 min and 70°C for 15 min. The abundance of Interferon gamma (IFN-γ), Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) and Interleukin-10 (IL-10) gens was determined by using SYBR green Real-time PCR. In addition, melting curve analysis was determined to monitor PCR product purity by measuring the fluorescence changes at different times. Statistical Analyses

Data were analyzed using a randomized complete design by GLM procedure of SAS software (SAS, 2002). The significant difference between line means was determined by the Duncan test (P ˂ 0.05). Gene expression results were analyzed using REST 2009 software and ANOVA analysis was performed using Graph pad prism software for each gene.

Lesion Scores

DISCUSSION

Coccidiosis is a major intestinal infection of poultry, and the development of novel strategies for effective prevention of coccidiosis is necessary due to drug resistance and bans on using anticoccidial drugs (Adhikari et al., 2020). The most common effects of coccidiosis are reduced feed intake, poor weight gain and less efficient feed conversion ratio (El-Ghany, 2021). In the current study, the coccidia challenge reduced AWG in broiler chickens at 15 to 28 d compared to the nonchallenged birds. It could be due to damage of intestinal epithelial cells and reduced nutrient absorption during coccidia challenge (Miska and Fetterer, 2017; Miska and Fetterer, 2018). This result is in agreement with others who observed a reduction in BWG and BW in broilers with coccidia challenge at 7 d after challenge (Fortuoso et al., 2019; Leung et al., 2019; Hansen et al., 2021). However, Coxidine treatment attenuated the growth reduction due to coccidial challenge. Absence of significant difference in growth performance between positive control and negative control treatment at the age of 29 to 42 d and the entire period is likely because of intestinal regeneration after the challenge with Eimeria. Similar to the results herein, in other studies FCR has worsened during coccidia infection (Ali et al., 2014; Wu et al., 2014). The current research showed that vaccine and Coxidine treatments had better performance to improve the overall FCR of treated broiler chickens under Eimeria challenge. Cai et al. (2023) reported that live coccidiosis vaccine can effectively prevent and control avian coccidiosis. Behnamifar et al. (2019) reported that vaccine and salinomycin are more effective than probiotic and prebiotic in controlling coccidiosis in broilers. It should be noted that the type and source of feed additives, the duration and amount of use in the diet, doses of oocyst inoculate, and Eimeria species affect contribute to the diversity of the results.

Oocyst counts in excreta and lesion score are clinical indicators to assess the severity of Eimeria infections in poultry (Ahmad et al., 2024). In the present study, coccidia challenge resulted in a significant increase in OPG value compared to the unchallenged group, which is an indication that the birds were severely (P < 0.01) affected by coccidiosis infection (Herrero-Encinas et al., 2021). Avian coccidiosis vaccine resulted in significant reduction in OPG. This is an indication of the greater effect of vaccine treatment on intestinal regeneration and preventing the penetration of sporangial oocysts in the intestinal wall, which is consistent with other studies (Ritzi et al., 2016; Tonda et al., 2018; Cai et al., 2023). The reduction of oocyte excretion in 42 d between experimental treatments is mainly because the digestive system of birds rebuilds itself after a period of challenge with Eimeria and produces antibodies and adaptive responses (Dalloul et al., 2003). No significant differences in lesion scores were observed due to treatment; however, the birds provided the probiotic treatment had the lowest lesion score. This may be relevant because Ritzi et al. (2016) reported that treatment with probiotic strains during an Eimeria infection had the capacity to reduce the severity of intestinal lesions.

The gens of IFN-γ and IL-1β, as known pro-inflammatory cytokines and IL-10, as known anti-inflammatory cytokine, are essential immunoregulatory cytokines in host defense against microbial infections and pathogens (Rodríguez et al., 2023). The early activation of IFN-γ and IL-10 were associated with the resistant coccidiosis (Bremner et al., 2021). Inflammatory genes expression was altered by dietary treatment. The mRNA expressions of IFN-γ and IL-10 in jejunum and ileum and IL-1β in jejunum were significantly increased in birds under coccidiosis challenge in jejunum and ileum compared to the nonchallenged group (NC group). Previous Eimeria challenge studies in broilers have resulted in an upregulation of pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines expression (Laurent et al., 2001; Herrero-Encinas et al., 2021). Birds treated with probiotic, vaccine and Coxidine treatment had the highest expression level of IFN-γ in both jejunum and ileum. Given that among the cytokines involved in coccidiosis, IFN-γ has direct inhibitory effect on the intracellular development of Eimeria (Lee et al., 2022), and, as a result these treatments were more effective. Oxford and Selvaraj (2019) reported that chickens vaccinated against Eimeria increased gene expression of IFN-γ, IL-10 and IL-1β after 6 d postchallenge and improved serum antibody response. In addition, Emami et al. (2019) reported that chickens receiving probiotics increased the expression of IL-10 and IFN-γ genes, and the expression of IL-1β was not significantly different. Hoang et al. (2017) and Arendt et al. (2019) reported that probiotics (B. subtilis) can stimulate the secretion of anti-inflammatory and pro-inflammatory cytokines and prevent the growth of pathogens. Relative mRNA expression of IL-1β increased in jejunum and decreased in ileum with coccidiosis challenge. In agreement with Laurent et al. (2001) found that expression of IL-1β upregulated in the duodenum, jejunum, and cecum in chickens infected with E. maxima and E. tenella. McKnight et al. (2019) showed that phytochemicals and Bacteracin increased the expression of IFN-γ in the jejunum; however, neither treatment had a significant effect on IL-10 expression, which is consistent with the expression of IFN-γ in the jejunum in the study herein.

In the present study the coccidiosis challenge decreased growth performance, increased oocyst excreta, and increased IFN-γ, IL-1β and IL-10 expression. The chickens fed with the vaccine treatment had the lowest (best) FCR and FI, followed by those receiving the coxidine and probiotic treatments. The lowest OPG was related to the vaccine treatment. The highest expression of IFN-γ in jejunum and ileum was related to probiotic, vaccine and coccidin treatments. The highest expression of IL-10 in ileum and jejunum was related to synbiotic treatment, followed by phytobiotic treatment. The expression of IL-1β was the highest in the vaccine treatment in the jejunum. Therefore, the data from this study resulted in evidence supporting the need for individually characterization of feed additives for the prevention of coccidiosis in poultry. In addition, the type and source of feed additives, the duration and amount of use in the ration, oocyst inoculation levels, and types of Eimeria can affect the measured indicators.

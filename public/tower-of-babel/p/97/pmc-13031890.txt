Porphyromonas gingivalis promotes oral squamous cell carcinoma progression via the IL-6/EZH2/Snai2 axis
Authors: Shiyuan Liu, Tian Guan, Bingxin Xu, Sihua Hong, Lidan Lu, Haijin Lao, Yan Cao, Qingting Yao, Xianjun Zhang, Haoyu Zeng, Wei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is a common malignancy with poor prognosis. Chronic infection with Porphyromonas gingivalis (P.g.) has been implicated in OSCC progression, but the molecular mechanisms underlying P.g.-induced tumor invasion and metastasis remain incompletely understood. ELISA, flow cytometry, proliferation assay and migration assay, were conducted on OSCC primary cells and the HSC-2 cell line under P.g. infection or IL-6 stimulation. Zeste homologue 2 (EZH2) and Snai2 were modulated via overexpression, knockdown, and inhibitor treatments, to evaluate the role of EZH2 and Snai2 in the regulation of OSCC phenotypes. The relationships between Snai2 and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) was analyzed using GSEA, differential expression, and KEGG pathway enrichment. For in vitro assay, P.g. infection significantly increased IL-6 levels in the oral microenvironment. IL-6 upregulation promoted OSCC cell proliferation and migration by enhancing EZH2 and Snai2 expression. EZH2 overexpression increased Snai2 transcription and OSCC malignancy, whereas EZH2 siRNA/inhibitor or Snai2 siRNA attenuated these effects. In bioinformatic analysis results, DEG and survival analysis showed the higher expression of Snai2 in OSCC patientsꞌ group and the negative relevance of survival probability with Snai2 expression, respectively. KEGG enrichment and PPI analysis indicated the relationships among EZH2 or Snai2 expression, EMT and infection-related signaling pathways. Our study revealed a novel IL-6/EZH2/Snai2 signaling axis, which connected the P.g. infection, IL-6 upregulation, abnormal expression of EZH2 and Snai2, and OSCC progression. This study filled the gap of molecular mechanisms underlying P.g.-induced OSCC invasion and metastasis, providing a potential therapeutic target for inhibiting OSCC progression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-41528-w.Keywords: OSCC, EZH2, Snai2, P. gingivalisSubject terms: Cancer, Oncology

Introduction

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), one of the top ten malignant tumors worldwide, is characterized by high incidence, strong invasiveness, and poor prognosis, posing an increasingly severe global health challenge1,2. Although multiple treatment modalities such as surgical treatment, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy have been applied in the management of OSCC, the current 5-year survival rate of the patients remains below 50%3. In recent years, studies have indicated that chronic inflammation and microbial flora imbalance play important roles in the occurrence and development of OSCC4. During tumor progression, oral microorganisms promote tumor development by affecting the tumor microenvironment (TME)5,6. When the dynamic balance between oral microorganisms and the host is disrupted, the microbes release toxins and metabolites that stimulate inflammation, alter immune responses, and induce epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), thereby promoting occurrence and development of OSCC7.

Porphyromonas gingivalis (P.g.) is an independent microbial factor that increases the EMT occurrence and the risk of death in OSCC patients8,9. A high detection rate of P.g. has been found in the oral environment of OSCC patients, and P.g. antigens have also been detected in some OSCC tissues10. However, the study of molecular mechanisms from P.g. infection to the EMT involved OSCC progression was still limited.

Previous researches reported that enhancer of zeste homologue 2 (EZH2) can regulate functions of the protein Snai2 and participate in the EMT process11–14, providing a possibility of EZH2 infecting the cancer progression. However, these researches lacked deeply investigation of the upstream cytokines which initiating the activation of EZH2 and did not study the mechanism of “EZH2 - Snai2” in OSCC samples. Another study has found that the expression of EZH2 is significantly increased in OSCC specimens and cell lines, and its abnormal expression is associated with poor differentiation, shorter overall survival, and disease-free survival of OSCC, indicating that EZH2 is a prognostic indicator for OSCC patients15. Meanwhile, Snai2 also plays a critical role in the occurrence and development of OSCC, and a variety of regulatory factors and long non-coding RNAs promote the EMT process of OSCC through the Snai2 pathway16. These reports together indicated us that EZH2 and its downstream protein Snai2 should have key functions in the rationale of P.g. infection initiated EMT process in OSCC. Besides, EZH2 regulated Snai2 signal promoting tumor cells proliferation has been found in hepatocellular carcinoma17. However, the research of this axis in other cancer progressions was still limited and it was unclear whether the axis having similar functions in OSCC cells.

Given the evidences that P.g. infection increases the EMT occurrence and the risk of death in OSCC patients8,9, EZH2 and Snai2 were the participants in the EMT process11,12,14,18, and IL-6 has interactions with EZH219–21 as well as plays critical role in P.g. infection22–24, we developed a hypothesis that P.g. infection probably increased the IL-6 concentration in oral environment and then facilitated the OSCC progression through the “IL-6 - EZH2 - Snai2” axis.

Based on this hypothesis, we sorted out and examined the mechanism from P.g. infection induced IL-6 increasing to OSCC cells proliferation and migration, and found that the pathway of EZH2 regulating Snai2 signal plays a key factor in this mechanism. Overall, our study revealed a general axis of “IL-6 - EZH2 - Snai2” in the P.g. involved EMT of OSCC, which drove OSCC generation and metastasis.

Materials & methods

Bioinformatics data sources and enrichment

Gene Set Enrichment and Functional Annotation:

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was applied to transcriptomic data from the TCGA-HNSC cohort, stratified by SNAI2 expression. Gene Ontology (GO) gene sets were employed to identify biological process, molecular functions, and cellular structures associated with high SNAI2 expression. An FDR < 0.25 and |ES| > 0.4 were considered significant.

Differential Expression assay:

Differential gene expression (DEG) analysis was conducted using the R package “DESeq2,” with a significance threshold of adjusted p-value < 0.05 and |log₂FC| > 1. The samples were divided into ‘SNAI2-high’ and ‘SNAI2-low’ groups based on the median of SNAI2 mRNA expression (median = 11.2995).

Survival Analysis:

The clinical relevance of SNAI2 was assessed using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, with the log-rank test and Cox proportional hazards model used to determine significance and hazard ratios. The OSCC samples were from GSE41613 dataset (GEO database) and divided into “SNAI2-high” and “SNAI2-low” groups based on the median of SNAI2 mRNA expression (median = 11.2995). Samples above the median were assigned to the high-expression group, and those below to the low-expression group.

Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) Enrichment:

The “clusterProfiler” R package (version 4.16.0) was employed to conduct the enrichment analysis, following the procedure in previous study25. To control the false positives arising from multiple hypothesis testing, all p-values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg method to calculate the false discovery rate (FDR). Pathways were considered significantly enriched if they met the dual threshold of an adjusted q-value < 0.05 and p-value < 0.05. The results of significantly enriched pathways were visualized using bubble plots to illustrate their enrichment factors and gene counts, and using chord diagram to illustrate the relationships among genes and pathways.

Protein to Protein Interaction (PPI) Analysis:

The infection relevant proteins were corresponding to the genes which classified from the infection relevant signal pathways of KEGG enrichment, and the cancer metastasis relevant proteins were corresponding to the genes which classified from the cell migration and cell adhesion relevant signal pathways of KEGG enrichment (based on those OSCC DEGs). Following the method in previous studies26,27, protein interaction data were retrieved from the STRING database (https://string-db.org/). The target gene list was submitted to the database to extract known and predicted interactions, with a confidence threshold (combined score) set to > 0.7 to filter high-confidence interactions and construct a preliminary PPI network. Subsequently, the network data were imported into Cytoscape software (version 3.10.3) for further visualization and topological analysis. To determine the key hub proteins within the network, the “MCODE” plugin in Cytoscape was used to find the hub proteins with its default parameters.

Cell lines and culture conditions

The OSCC cell line HSC-2 (YS2196C, YaJi Biological, China) was used for the analysis of coculturing with P.g. (BNCC337441, BeNa Culture Collection, China), cell viability assay and migration assay in this study. Referring to the previous study28, HSC-2 was cultured in DMEM/high glucose (SNM-002 A, SUNNCELL, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; SNS-001, SUNNCELL, China), and 50 U/mL penicillin plus 50 µg/mL streptomycin (not in co-culturing system). The cells were cultured in a 37 °C incubator with 5% CO2 and being regularly monitored for mycoplasma contamination. The cell line STR identification and mycoplasma contamination monitoring results were provided in the supplementary material 1.

Primary OSCC cells isolation and generation

The procedure of primary OSCC cells isolation and generation was following our previous study29. Briefly, the tumor tissues were treated with washing buffer containing Penicillin-Streptomycin solution and then being cut into pieces in a diameter of proximately 200 μm in the primary epithelial cell growth medium (CC-3151, Univ-bio, China). Followed, the pieces were digested with trypsin-EDTA (Gibco, USA) for about 15 min. The single cells after digestion were collected, resuspended and transferred into flasks for generating primary OSCC cells. The primary OSCC cells were generated in the primary epithelial cell growth medium in the 37 °C incubator with 5% CO2.

ELISA assay

The periodontal lavage fluid (containing gingival bleeding) from the OSCC patients (basic information in the supplementary material 2), P.g. relevant periodontitis patients, and normal persons were collected from their gingival crevicular fluid (GCF) and concentrated. GCF was collected and processed as described previously with modifications30–32. Briefly, participants were required to refrain from eating, drinking, and performing any oral cleaning activities for at least 30 min prior to the sampling. The sterile filter paper strips were inserted into the gingival crevice for 60 s. The strips were immediately placed in buffer containing protease inhibitors and stored on ice. In the laboratory, GCF was eluted by vortexing, and its volume was determined gravimetrically. The eluate was centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 5 min at 4 °C to concentrate the protein fraction, and the resulting pellet was stored at −80 °C. Prior to analysis, samples were thawed at 4 °C and equilibrated to room temperature. The concentration of IL-6 in the lavage fluid was detected using IL-6 ELISA kit (J20252, WUHAN JILID Biotech, China). Samples were diluted in the kit reagents and managed by ELISA analysis following the manufacturer’s protocol.

Flow cytometry

Prior to conducting the flow cytometry analysis, OSCC cells were subjected to two rounds of washing with PBS. Subsequently, the cells were incubated with anti-IL-6R primary antibody (18935T, Cell Signaling, USA) for the detection of IL-6 receptor (IL-6R/CD126). The primary antibody was diluted to a concentration of 1:500 in PBS and incubated with the cells for 2 h at room temperature. Following this, the cells were washed and further incubated with corresponding secondary antibody (BA1127, BOSTER, USA), for 1 h at room temperature. After washing with PBS, the cells were analyzed using an A1 flow cytometer (FACSymphony, BD, USA).

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay kit (CA1210, Solarbio, China) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. OSCC cells were seeded in a 96-well plate at a density of 5 × 10^4 cells per well, being treated with SiRNA or IL-6 according to the tests and cultured overnight. Subsequently, the cells were treated with the CCK-8 reagent for 4 h. The absorbance was then measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Multiskan™ FC, Thermo Scientific, USA).

Cell migration assay

Real time cell analysis (RTCA) system (xCELLigence RTCA DP, Agilent, USA) was employed to perform the cell migration assay. In brief, cell suspensions of OSCC cells (1 × 10^5 cells/mL) were placed in the upper chambers with serum-free medium. The lower chambers were used E-Plate 16 (Agilent) of RTCA system, containing DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. Impedance was measured continuously for about 24 h at 15-minute intervals, and visualized as cell index of each group. For the crystal violet staining, the cells were seeded in the upper chambers of a 6-well trans-well plate, after a 24-hour incubation period, cells remaining in the upper chambers were removed. The cells that had migrated to the lower chambers (Corning Transwell chamber instead of E-Plate 16) were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (P0099, Beyotime, China), stained with 0.1% crystal violet (V5265-250ML, Sigma-Aldrich, USA), and quantified by counting in five random microscopic fields. The migration rates were expressed as a ratio to the normal IL-6R+ OSCC cell group (IL-6R+).

Wound healing assay

OSCC cells were seeded into a 6-well culture plate at a density of 5 × 10^5 cells per well. After the cell’s attachment, uniform linear wounds were scratched in the cell monolayers. Images were captured at baseline (0 h) and post-wounding (24 h). The rate of wound healing was quantified using ImageJ software (https://imagej.net/ij/), which calculated the percentage decrease in the denuded area compared to the initial wound size.

Western blotting assay

Total proteins were extracted from OSCC cells with lysis buffer (20–188, Millipore, Germany) containing 1% protease inhibitors (P1005, Beyotime, China). The extracted protein samples were quantified with BCA Protein Quantification Kit (20200ES76, Yeasen Biotech, China) firstly, and then loaded to an SDS-PAGE gels. Followed, the electrophoresis and membrane transfer were performed. Samples were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in TBST for 1 h at room temperature and incubated with the EZH2 primary antibody (1: 1000, orb1939414, biorbyt, UK) and Snai2 primary antibody (1: 1000, orb197797, biorbyt, UK) overnight at 4 °C. The corresponding secondary antibodies were incubated on next day for 2 h at room temperature. Then, ECL luminescent liquid (C500044, Sangon, China) were used for the color development.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA of the OSCC cells were extracted using the Total RNA Extraction Kit (#19221ES50, Yeasen Biotech, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Hieff® qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (#11203ES08, Yeasen Biotech, China) was used for the RNA labeling and the Accurate 96 Real-Time PCR system (DLAB Scientific, China) was employed to quantify the RNA amplification. Each sample was tested with triplication or more (n ≥ 3). The expression levels of target genes were normalized to GAPDH and analyzed using the 2 − ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences were listed in the supplementary material 1 (Table S1).

Statistical analysis

The results were quantified, analyzed and visualized using the software Prism Graphpad 8 (version 8.3.0, https://graphpad.com/). Student’s t-test, One-way ANOVA or two-way ANOVA method was used to compare the single variate or multiple variables of mean values among different groups, respectively. P-value < 0.05 were considered as the significant difference statistically.

Results

P. gingivalis infection induced IL-6 increasing promoted OSCC progression

Oral lavage fluid collected from clinical patients has been detected to determine whether the cytokine IL-6 being increased in P.g. infected oral environment or not. The data demonstrated that IL-6 concentration in both P.g. involved periodontitis and OSCC samples were significantly increased than that in normal samples (Fig. 1A). Besides, the flow cytometry data illustrated that after the IL-6 stimulation, 49.68% of the primary OSCC cells (POSCCs) had IL-6R upregulation (Fig. 1B). Further, the POSCCs were then divided into two groups, IL-6R not-upregulated group (IL-6R-) and IL-6R upregulated group (IL-6R+), sorted by flow cytometer, and being analyzed cell viabilities by CCK-8 assay. The results showed that IL-6R+ POSCCs presented higher cell viabilities, from 100% to 153.6%, when treated with IL-6 (Fig. 1C). Moreover, these cells (IL-6R+ POSCCs) enhanced their migration ability of 25.4% when stimulated by IL-6 (Fig. 1D and E). Additionally, the promotion of cell viability of OSCC cells have been observed in the OSCC cell vs. macrophage co-culturing system when infected with live P.g. (Figure S5).

IL-6 promoted OSCC through EZH2 regulated Snai2 signal

The expression levels of EZH2 and Snai2 have been detected by western blotting assay, which showed that POSCCs had abnormal higher expression of EZH2 and Snai2 than normal oral primary cells as control, and both EZH2 and Snai2 increased to an obviously higher level when the POSCCs were treated with IL-6 (Fig. 2A). Correspondingly, the mRNA transcription level of EZH2 and Snai2 were also increased 6.58 folds and 2.19 folds, respectively, in POSCCs when treated with IL-6 (Fig. 2B). To figure out the roles of EZH2 and Snai2 in OSCC progressions, EZH2 overexpression model (EZH2-OE), EZH2 knockdown model (EZH2-KD), Snai2 overexpression model (Snai2-OE) and Snai2 knockdown model (Snai2-KD) have been constructed in the OSCC cell line HSC-2, respectively. For the phenotypes of OSCC cell line, EZH2-OE cells demonstrated a rising trend of cell viability, from 100% to 130.0% (Fig. 2C), and cell migration ability, quantifying as the cell index from 0.34 to 0.63 at the end point (Fig. 2D), compared to the control group (untreated HSC-2 cells), while EZH2-KD cells showed an opposite trend of the phenotypes (Fig. 2C and D). Besides, if treated EZH2-KD cells with excessive recombined EZH2 proteins, the cell viability and cell migration ability of EZH2-KD cells would be reversed (Fig. 2C and D). For the molecular detection, the transcriptions of Snai2 mRNA were higher in EZH2-OE cells (3.94 folds) and lower in EZH2-KD cells (0.68 folds), and being reversed in EZH2-KD cells (3.24 folds) with recombined EZH2 proteins treatment, which was consist with the OSCC phenotype results (Figure E). Further, Snai2-OE cells also showed increasing trends of cell viability and cell migration ability while Snai2-KD showed decreasing trends, compared to the control group cells (Fig. 2F and G).

Verification of Snai2 as the downstream signal of EZH2 regulating OSCC progression

To determine whether EZH2 regulates OSCC progression via Snai2, after IL-6 stimulation, multiple experiments, including wound healing assay, cell viability assay and cell migration assay have been performed. As the results, EZH2-OE cells demonstrated higher viabilities in those phenotypes, corresponding to the previous data (Fig. 3A, B and C). However, when treated EZH2-OE cells with SiRNA of Snai2 (SiSnai2), the EZH2-OE cells presented the inhibited phenotypes in cell invasion (wound healing rate from 97.8% down to 62.0%), cell viability (from 158.1% down to 142.1%) and cell migration (cell index from 0.52 down to 0.31) (Fig. 3A, B and C). Furthermore, the whole axis from IL-6 to Snai2 has been determined in the IL-6R+ POSCCs. The data illustrated that after treatment of IL-6, the POSCCs presented higher cell viability (Fig. 3D) and cell migration ability (Fig. 3E). However, no matter using EZH2 inhibitor (inEZH2) or Snai2 SiRNA (SiSnai2) after the IL-6 stimulation, the POSCCs have been inhibited significantly in the cell viability and cell migration ability, comparing to the only IL-6 treated group (Fig. 3D and E).

Snai2 was involved in the infection relevant cancer EMT and metastasis of OSCC

To further investigated the mechanisms of Snai2, as a downstream signal, facilitating the OSCC cells migration, we systematically analyzed the functions of Snai2 in OSCC via bioinformatic assay. GSEA analysis of SNAI2 has been performed in the Biological Process, Cellular Component and Molecular Function of OSCC pathways enrichment, that the expression of SNAI2 was positively correlating to multiple EMT relevant pathways, such as basement membrane assembly (in Biological Process), fibrillar collagen trimer regulation (in Cellular Component), cell substrate junction (in Cellular Component) and G protein subunit gamma binding (in Molecular Function) (Fig. 4A). The different expression genes (DEGs) were screened from the comparison of clinical OSCC samples versus normal samples, in which the gene SNAI2 was upregulated in the OSCC samples (Fig. 4B). Besides, Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that high SNAI2 expression predicts significantly worse overall survival (log-rank p = 0.02), with a hazard ratio of 1.47 (95% CI: 1.07–2.03) (Fig. 4C and Figure S3). Meanwhile, we conducted KEGG enrichment with all the upregulated DEGs and found that multiple infection involved signal pathways of OSCC progression were contained in the KEGG enriched pathways (Fig. 4D), and IL-6 and SNAI2 were correlating to several of the pathways (Fig. 4E and Figure S4). Protein to protein interaction (PPI) assay showed that IL-6, EZH2 and Snai2 were all settled in the hub for bridging the cancer metastasis relevant proteins and the infection relevant proteins, being consist with the results of KEGG enrichment (Fig. 4F).

Discussion

The occurrence and development of OSCC is a slow multi-molecular and multi-stage progression process, involving abnormalities in multiple genes, multiple signaling pathways, and various metabolic reactions33,34. For the microorganisms involved OSCC progression, P.g. is a key oral pathogenic bacterium. It can invade epithelial cells, interfere with the host’s immune response, disrupt the cell cycle and normal physiological metabolism of epithelial cells, exhibit the effect of inhibiting programmed cell death, as well as the EMT35,36. Under normal physiological conditions, there is a dynamic balance between bacteria, such as P.g., and the host. When the balance disrupted, microorganisms stimulate local or systemic inflammation by releasing toxins and metabolites, alter the host’s immune response, and induce EMT, thereby promoting the occurrence and development of OSCC35.

Khoshbayan A’s study revealed that P.g. may be involved in the occurrence and development of OSCC by regulating miR-3137. Through cell experiments, Wei W confirmed that the nucleoside diphosphate kinase (NDK) secreted by P.g. reduces the hydrolysis of extracellular ATP by P.g., enhances the expression of P2 × 7, and thereby promotes the proliferation, migration, and invasion of OSCC cells38. Using transmission electron microscopy and nanoparticle tracking analysis, Liao X found that P.g. outer membrane vesicles may regulate ferroptosis-related EMT through NF-κB signal transduction to promote OSCC progression9. However, their investigations did not explore the mechanisms of P.g. induced inflammation promoting the EMT of OSCC. While our research provided a newly founded pathway, the “IL-6 - EZH2 - Snai2” axis, to explain the relationships between P.g. infection and EMT of OSCC.

EMT is a vital process during tumor occurrence and development. In this process, epithelial cells lose polarity, and their phenotypes change with the loss of epithelial characteristics, enabling the cells to acquire properties that facilitate invasion and metastasis, thereby enhancing the migration and invasion capabilities of tumor cells39. The EMT process is regulated by a series of related transcription factors. Snai1, Snai2, Twist1, Zeb1, and Zeb2 are considered core transcription factors of EMT, which can induce the classical EMT program. Among them, Snai2 may be involved in the EMT process by inhibiting the expression of E-cadherin40.

In the treatment of lung cancer, ovarian cancer, and breast cancer, targeting EZH2 can achieve certain therapeutic effects41. This suggests that EZH2 not only serves as a prognostic indicator but also can be used as a therapeutic target to improve the prognosis of tumor patients. In recent years, studies have elaborated that EZH2 participates in the occurrence, development, proliferation, invasion, and metastasis of OSCC through multiple ways, such as activating oncogenes directly via the STAT3 and FoxO1 pathways, and inducing the EMT process42,43. Additionally, studies have indicated that homeobox B9 (HOXB9) enhances the formation of H3K27me3 modification by EZH2 in the promoter region of the Mir-203 A gene, leading to the upregulation of Snai2 expression in hepatocellular carcinoma cells and thereby promoting their invasion and metastasis17. However, there remain insufficiencies in studies on the role of P.g. in enhancing the interaction between EZH2 and Snai2 in OSCC. Through the experiments with OSCC primary cells and cell line, and the bioinformatic analysis, our study established the pivotal role of the EZH2/Snai2 signaling pathway in the mechanism whereby P.g. infection elevates IL-6 to stimulate OSCC proliferation and migration.

On the other hand, this study still had some limitations. Since this current study was preliminarily investigated that whether the hypothesis was reasonable, the experiments were only conducted for the in vitro study. The in vivo validation is beyond the scope of this article. The experiments with animal model were planned for our future research to investigate the roles of IL-6/EZH2/Snai2 axis in OSCC and its accelerating function of EMT. The correlations and causation of bioinformatic analysis with TCGA data was limited. For the GSEA analysis, we determined the threshold of FDR as FDR < 0.25, considering it was commonly used for identifying pathways that may hold biological significance in exploratory analyses, particularly when sample sizes are limited or signals are weak, and has been adopted in multiple published studies44–46. However, this threshold increased the false positives rate to some extent.

Moreover, the sample size of the clinical samples needs to expanded, and deeper mechanisms of EZH2 regulating Snai2, such as transcriptional regulation, post-transcriptional regulation and post-translational modification, should be investigated using ChIP-qPCR or promoter-reporter assay in our future work.

Conclusion

Our study delineates a novel mechanistic axis linking P. gingivalis infection to OSCC progression through the inflammatory cytokine IL-6 and the epigenetic regulator EZH2, ultimately driving Snai2-mediated EMT. We demonstrate that P. gingivalis infection elevates IL-6 levels in the oral microenvironment, which upregulates EZH2 and Snai2 expression in OSCC cells, enhancing their proliferative and migratory capacities. Functional assays confirm that EZH2 directly regulates Snai2 transcription, and disruption of this axis—via EZH2 inhibition or Snai2 knockdown—abrogates IL-6-induced oncogenic phenotypes. Bioinformatic analyses further underscore the clinical relevance of Snai2, associating its high expression with poor survival and enrichment of EMT and infection-related pathways in OSCC. These findings not only elucidate a critical pathway by which oral pathogens exacerbate malignancy but also identify the IL-6/EZH2/Snai2 axis as a promising therapeutic target for intercepting infection-driven OSCC progression.

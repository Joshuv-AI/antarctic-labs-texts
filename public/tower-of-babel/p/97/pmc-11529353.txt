Arthroscopic Approach for Fixation of a Neglected ACL Avulsion Fracture: A Case Report
Authors: Dani Pratama Febrianto, Fahroni C. Winata
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) avulsion fractures constitute a significant proportion of knee joint injuries and can lead to substantial disability if left untreated. These fractures result from forceful hyperextension or a direct impact on the distal end of the femur while the knee is flexed. Presently, Meyers and McKeever's classification system is used to categorise these fractures. The popularity of arthroscopic reduction and fixation techniques due to limited skin incisions and soft-tissue injuries have been growing since there is no gold standard treatment for ACL avulsion fractures. This report aims to describe the potency of arthroscopic techniques in fixing neglected ACL avulsion fractures to improve knee function and calculate it using Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (PROM), the Indonesian version of International Knee Documentation Committee (I-IKDC).Objective:A 19-year-old young woman sustained an ACL injury during a sports activity. Over time, her pain progressively worsened and limiting her ability to perform fully extend her knee and a full squat. X-ray and MRI revealed evidence of avulsion at the ACL insertion site on the tibia, classified as a grade 2 based on Meyers and McKeever's Classification. Despite undergoing conservative therapy, the pain persisted for 8 weeks before the patient was admitted for surgery.Discussion:Her pain really limits her daily activities and her career as a sales promotion representative. For the surgical technique, we did a meticulous cleaning of the fracture site, followed by fixation utilizing a fiber suture. Next, a looping suture was created at the ACL insertion point, threaded through a tibial hole, and fixed at the anterior tibia, aligning with the location of the ACL insertion. Three months after the surgery, we did a follow-up and found that she has no pain when she fully extends her knee, does a squat, and also does light jogging. Her X-ray postoperative shown a united avulsion fracture. We also calculated using I-IKDC and found a satisfactory result of 83% for her knee function.Conclusion:The arthroscopic approach achieved a satisfactory clinical outcome and provided an effective method for the treatment of neglected ACL avulsion fractures..

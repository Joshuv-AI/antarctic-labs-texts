ΟΑ2011. Adolescents’ road traffic knowledge and behavior in Bitola, North Macedonia
Authors: J Todorovski, F Tozija
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRoad safety is a critical public health concern, especially for adolescents. The aim was to analyze the knowledge, attitudes, and behavior of adolescents aged 15–19 in Bitola, North Macedonia, regarding road traffic.MethodsA prospective behavioral study was conducted on 412 adolescents aged 15-19 in Bitola using a modified knowledge, attitudes, and practice (KAP) “Survey for Young Road Users” questionnaire, official statistical data, and WHO HFA-DB sources. A public health approach was applied.ResultsAdolescents frequently engage in risky road behavior and do not respect traffic regulations, such as not stopping when crossing the road (p < 0.001), running over (p < 0.001), and crossing in low-visibility areas (p < 0.001). Most do not wear reflective clothing when walking or cycling in the dark (p < 0.001), fail to use bicycle lights (p < 0.001), and neglect wearing helmets on bicycles, mopeds, or motorcycles (p < 0.001). Sometimes wear a seat belt when driving in a car (p < 0.001). Knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors differ by gender, nationality, and education level, with female respondents demonstrating better knowledge (p < 0.001).ConclusionsDespite awareness of road risks and responsibilities, adolescents in Bitola do not fully adhere to traffic regulations, increasing accident risks. They have risky behavior and do not pay enough attention to road safety.Key messages• Despite knowing road safety risks, adolescents in Bitola often engage in risky behaviors.• Strengthening road safety education through health promotion activities and mandatory training programs in schools is essential for improving adolescent road safety.TopicAdolescents, behavior, road safety.

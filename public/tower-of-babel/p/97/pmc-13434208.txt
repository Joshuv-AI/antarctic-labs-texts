A method for collapse pressure analysis while drilling through fractured clay formations
Authors: Chen Zhuo, Cui Guojie, Su Jian, Zhu Guowei, Xie Rongbin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Existing “seepage-stress-damage” models are mainly used for pre-drilling prediction and post-drilling evaluation of collapse pressure. Such models involve a large number of parameters that are difficult to obtain. In this paper, the “seepage-stress-damage” model is simplified into a strength softening model applicable to real-time collapse analysis while drilling. A method is also proposed to determine the parameters of the strength softening model using logging data. Field engineers of Well X adjusted drilling fluid properties based on the strength softening model. Within 10 days of exposure, only small collapse blocks (0.2 cm × 0.23 cm) were retrieved, and the 1000 m thick clay formation in Well X was drilled efficiently.

Introduction

Natural fractures and microfractures tend to develop in clay formations under tectonic activity1. These fractures may provide seepage channels for drilling fluid and increase the contact area between drilling fluid and clay matrix. Drilling fluid invasion reduces the structural strength of fracture planes, promoting fracture propagation and penetration, which eventually leads to wellbore collapse2,3,4,5. High density drilling fluid aggravates fluid invasion under pressure differential, while low density induces additional fracture generation due to shear failure6,7. Therefore, accurate determination of collapse pressure and collapse period for fractured clay is critical for the rational selection of drilling fluid density.

In the drilling of fractured clay formations, plugging agents are commonly used to seal fractures and strengthen the wellbore8,9. Existing collapse pressure models mainly consider the seepage-stress-damage coupling under fully sealed or completely unsealed fracture conditions. Liu10 established a multi-coupling stress field model using the weak-plane strength criterion and evaluated collapse pressures for deep brittle clay under different weak-plane conditions. Cui11 established a dynamic borehole stability model coupling seepage, pore pressure, and the effective stress field around the wellbore, and concluded that bottom-hole pressure variations contribute to lost circulation and kicks. Zhang12 investigated the effects of drilling fluid on nearwellbore stress and failure zones by considering fluid seepage and shale hydration, and proposed a plugging drilling fluid system for the Dongying Formation in the Bohai Bay Basin. Nwonodi7 derived equations for strength reduction, membrane destruction with time, and the time-dependent osmotic pressure based on empirical analysis. Which proves the wellbore instability increases with time in an open face mainly because of diffusion and strength reduction. Li13 established an innovative predictive model for coal seam wellbore collapse pressure by integrating mechanical parameter variations along weak structural planes with the Mohr Coulomb criterion.

However, the above model has some limitations. Firstly, they involve a lot of parameters such as diffusion coefficient, membrane efficiency, porosity, and permeability. Which requires lots laboratory testing for calibration. It makes them difficult to apply in field operations. Some researcher have made some improvements by empirical analysis, but the problem has not been completely solved. Secondly, they cannot accurately reflect the mismatch between drilling fluid plugging agents and formation characteristics caused by formation or drilling fluid changes while drilling. Lastly, they are mainly used for pre-drilling prediction and post-drilling evaluation, rather than real-time analysis based on actual drilling data.

To overcome these drawbacks, this study simplifies the “seepage-stress-damage” model into a strength softening model. In which, a softening coefficient characterizes fracture development and propagation, and a plugging coefficient describes the compatibility between drilling fluid and natural fractures. While, a real-time inversion method for these coefficients is developed using logging data. The model enables real-time calculation of collapse pressure and collapse period under dynamic drilling fluid-formation interaction. Compared to existing models, the method has a better field applicability.

Clay failure mechanism

The microstructure of clay is shown in Fig. 1. Bonding between particles and between particles and cements is relatively weak, and numerous microcracks exist along particle boundaries. Minerals such as feldspar and quartz form the rigid skeleton, while clay minerals act as the cementing matrix. Under increasing external stress, microcracks initiate at weakly cemented regions. With further loading, particle slippage and misalignment occur, continuously reducing rock cohesion until microcracks propagate14,15. Meanwhile, microcracks provide pathways for drilling fluid seepage16,17,18. Water invasion causes hydration of clay minerals along fracture surfaces, further reducing interparticle bonding and friction19,20.Fig. 1Microstructure of clay.

Microstructure of clay.

In uniaxial compression tests, new microcracks tend to align parallel to the axial direction under low lateral constraint. The variation of shear wave velocity can effectively monitor microcrack development (Fig. 2). At the initial loading stage, the shear wave velocity remains nearly constant. As load increases and microcracks propagate, the velocity decreases at an accelerating rate. When the load reaches approximately 70% of the peak strength, the shear wave velocity reaches its minimum. The evolution of shear wave velocity is consistent with microcrack development.Fig. 2Development of microcracks in the process of uniaxial experiment.

Collapse period while drilling by element logging

While drilling in fractured clay, plugging material is used to seal fracture and strengthen formation. If matching degree of plugging material and fracture is great, none collapse will occur. However, periodic collapse is a common problem in fractured clay, especially in exploration well due to the difficulty in obtaining fracture parameters. An accurately collapse period is helpful for determine parameters of strength softening model while drilling. Then, operator can adjust drilling fluid property in real time based on the calculation results of strength softening model. In this section, a collapse period calculation model by element logging data is proposed.

Element logging

The element composition can be obtained by element logging. The principle of element logging is as follow.

When the cuttings sample is irradiated with X-rays, the electrons in the higher energy state transition to the lower energy state and the characteristic X-rays released can be quantitatively analyzed by measuring the X-ray intensity of different wavelengths.

Collapse period

The idea of collapse period calculation is to find the depth of collapse formation in which the element is most closely to element in collapse block. The collapse period Δ t can be calculated by the following equation,11\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta t{ = }t_{2} - t_{1}$$\end{document}where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_{2}$$\end{document} is return time of collapse block. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t_{1}$$\end{document} is open time of collapse formation.

The key to obtain open time of collapse formation is having depth of collapse formation.

Conclusion

Aiming at the complex wellbore instability mechanism of fractured clay formations, as well as the defects of seepage-stress-damage models including lots parameters and poor real-time field applicability, this paper conducts systematic research on mechanism analysis, model simplification, theoretical modeling, while-drilling parameter inversion and engineering example verification. The main conclusions are drawn as follows:Under tectonic activities, wellbore collapse in fractured clay formations results from the combined effects of drilling fluid infiltration and clay hydration, fracture propagation and penetration, as well as stress-induced rock damage.The complex multi-field coupled seepage-stress-damage model is simplified into a strength softening model. The softening coefficients are adopted to characterize the development and propagation of natural fractures, and the plugging coefficient is defined to quantitatively describe the matching compatibility between drilling fluid plugging agents and formation fractures. The proposed model retains the key physical mechanisms of formation failure while greatly reducing dependent parameters, which overcomes the limitations of seepage-stress-damage models such as lots laboratory testing dependence and poor real-time field applicability.Combined with element logging data, particle swarm optimization algorithm and similarity discrimination methods, a quantitative calculation method for while-drilling collapse period is established. The brittle mineral index and clay mineral content are used to obtain strength softening parameters, and the plugging coefficient is inverted in real time based on field collapse characteristics. This realizes the dynamic real-time calculation of collapse pressure and collapse period under the coupling condition of drilling fluid and formation.Model comparison and verification results show that for brittle clay formations with well-developed fractures, the calculation results of the strength softening model are highly consistent with seepage-stress-damage model. However, for low-brittleness clay with weak fracture development, the calculation error of strength softening model is large, failing to meet the accuracy requirements for field engineering predictions.Field application cases of Well X and Bozhong Block verify the practicability and reliability of the proposed model. Real-time adjustment of drilling fluid density and plugging system can be guided by the model calculation results. This study provides a new theoretical reference and technical support for wellbore stability evaluation and safe drilling construction in fractured clay formations.

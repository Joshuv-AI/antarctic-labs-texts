Interference-aware frequency-agile onboard processor using fine-grained multilevel analysis–synthesis filter-bank channelization
Authors: Surajit Sarkar, Amitabha Das, Deepak Mishra, Amitava Gupta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes a spectrally optimized, multichannel filter-bank channelizer with time-multiplexed hardware reuse and block-based per-sample FFT complexity based on a unified framework for deep-space and satellite-onboard digital signal processing. This framework achieves fine-grained channelization using a single prototype filter with substantially reduced memory and computational overhead. The proposed approach derives all analysis and synthesis subband filters from a single prototype FIR filter using dyadic dilation and structured cascading. This eliminates the need for independently designed subfilters, achieves uniform channel spacing with consistent group delay, and reduces spectral distortion across all bands. A multi-objective cost-function-based prototype filter was obtained for the multilevel tree-structured uniform filter bank by minimizing transition-band energy, stop-band energy, and hardware complexity under QPSK recovery constraints. Strong channel isolation, a sharp roll-off, and reliable reconstruction at every level of the hierarchy are demonstrated by comprehensive simulation-based spectrum analysis. These channelizers are well-suited for onboard digital processors in next-generation satellite and deep-space missions. Simulation results demonstrate an average adjacent-channel isolation exceeding 98 dB (with a worst-case value of 79.6 dB) for a 64-channel 10 kHz frequency grid, while maintaining the QPSK error vector magnitude (EVM) below 10%. Fixed-point simulations and FPGA synthesis results confirm that the architecture maintains communication-relevant isolation (>60 dB worst-case) under practical finite word-length constraints, demonstrating realistic hardware feasibility.Keywords: Analysis–synthesis filter bank, Multirate signal processing, Multilevel channelization, Multichannel filter banks, Uniform filter bank, FPGA-oriented implementation.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Future deep-space probes and next-generation communication satellites demand on-board digital processors (OBDPs) capable of flexible spectrum allocation, fine-granular channelization, interference mitigation, and low-power operation1–4. As channel densities rise and spectrum becomes more congested, the design of compact, spectrally sharp, and computationally affordable multichannel filter banks has become essential5. An effective on-board architecture must provide uniform channel bandwidths, predictable reconstruction characteristics, and high spectral isolation, all while maintaining minimal memory usage and reduced implementation complexity. Classical channelizers fall into three dominant categories: (i) FFT/DFT-based filter banks, (ii) frequency-domain masking with spectral selection, and (iii) polyphase/FBMC(Filter Bank Multicarrier)-style uniformly modulated filter banks. FFT-based channelizers offer attractive computational scaling but require large transform sizes, overlap processing, and significant memory bandwidth. Frequency-domain brick-wall switching architectures, although theoretically ideal, are impractical for hardware implementation due to their infinite impulse response, noncausal nature, and reliance on sharply truncated spectral masks that produce severe time-domain ringing and aliasing6.

Polyphase FIR filter banks alleviate the large transform size and buffering requirements of FFT-based channelizers, but they demand carefully crafted prototype filters and may exhibit spectral leakage or non-uniform reconstruction distortion when operating at narrow channel spacings. Using conventional wavelet filter-based techniques, such as the Discrete Wavelet Packet Transform (DWPT)7–9 and the Maximal Overlap Discrete Wavelet Packet Transform (MODWPT)10, often exhibit significant spectral overlap and are limited by orthogonality constraints. In this work, the terms Selective Maximal Overlap Discrete Wavelet Packet Transform (SMODWPT) and Selective Inverse Maximal Overlap Discrete Wavelet Packet Transform (SIMODWPT) are used to denote specific communication-oriented variants of the classical MODWPT/IMODWPT framework. These variants retain the non-decimated, shift-invariant multilevel structure of MODWPT, while introducing selective subband extraction, relaxed orthogonality constraints, and optimized prototype filtering tailored for multichannel communication channelization. These issues reduce their effectiveness in communication systems that require strong channel separation and more precise frequency separation.

While several established channelizer classes address subsets of these requirements, achieving fine-grained spectral separation, scalable multilevel decomposition, and hardware-efficient realization within a unified architecture remains challenging. This motivates the need for a unified framework emphasizing hardware reuse and scalable implementation. To clarify the scope of novelty, this work does not introduce a new multirate transform. Instead, it presents a communication-oriented reformulation of the established SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework by relaxing strict orthogonality and perfect-reconstruction constraints and permitting arbitrary even-length FIR prototype filters. The contribution can be viewed at three complementary levels. At the formulation level, a generalized SMODWPT/SIMODWPT structure is established that preserves shift invariance while enabling improved spectral localization. At the architectural level, a unified single-prototype analysis–synthesis realization is developed, enabling scalable multilevel channelization with hardware reuse. At the application level, the framework is tailored to interference-aware onboard digital processors, with performance metrics and feasibility constraints derived directly from communication-system requirements.

Unlike classical wavelet-packet channelizers that rely on orthogonal bases, the proposed approach decouples spectral localization from strict orthogonality constraints by enabling arbitrary even-length FIR prototype filters. Although demonstrated here for uniform channel grids, the formulation naturally extends to adaptive and application-specific channel grouping scenarios.

The resulting framework enables spectrum-agile multichannel processing with logarithmic per-sample computational scaling and time-multiplexed hardware reuse. Methodology

This section explains the theory and algorithms behind the proposed unified channelisation–synthesis framework. It first provides a brief overview of the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) and related methods. Then, it discusses the structural limitations of classical DWPT and MODWPT in communication-grade channelizers. This is followed by the introduction of a generalised SMODWPT formulation that uses arbitrary even-length prototype FIR filters, enabling sharper frequency localisation and reduced transition-band energy. The section further explains how these properties allow all subband filters to be derived from a single prototype filter, forming the basis for the unified methodology proposed in this work.

Discrete wavelet transform (DWT) and its derivatives

The fundamental structures of the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)21 and Discrete Wavelet Packet Transform (DWPT)22 are presented, with particular emphasis on their decomposition trees and inherent limitations in communication applications.

The DWT is a highly effective method for signal analysis because it can decompose a time domain signal or series into different components in the time-frequency domain. The DWT decomposes or divides the input signal into approximate and detail components. The input signal passes through a high-pass filter (H) and a low-pass filter (G) and is downsampled by a factor of two. The low-pass filter output is the approximate component and the output of the high-pass filter contains detailed component. The DWT can be used recursively to generate several decomposition levels from the approximate signal. The DWPT like the DWT has a few limitations. It requires the sample size to be an integer power of 2. At each level of the transform, the number of each DWPT coefficients falls by a factor of 2. This can result in the loss of time information. This will invariably lead to errors in recovering the data bits from the segmented (channelized) signals . If downsampling in the DWT can be avoided, the effect of loss of time information can be mitigated.

While suitable for signal analysis, these decimated transforms are fundamentally mismatched to communication receivers that require strict time alignment and symbol recovery

Generalized SMODWPT/SIMODWPT formulation

It is emphasized that the proposed formulation does not introduce a new class of multirate transforms. The underlying principles of non-decimated wavelet packet decomposition, dyadic dilation, and analysis–synthesis filter-bank structures are well established in the wavelet and multirate signal-processing literature. The novelty of the present work lies in a systematic generalization of the SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework that removes orthogonality constraints, permits arbitrary even-length FIR prototypes, and explicitly aligns the filter-bank design with communication-driven objectives such as spectral localization, adjacent-channel isolation, and demodulation robustness. This generalization removes the orthogonality constraint inherent to conventional SMODWPT/SIMODWPT formulations and permits the use of arbitrary even-length finite-impulse-response (FIR) prototype filters.

In their native forms, the SMODWPT and SIMODWPT schemes are restricted to using orthogonal filter only. If the frequency response of Fig. 8 is compared with that of Figs. 5, 6 and 7, it is observed that there is a considerable amount of overlap between the constituent filters when an orthogonal filter is used as the prototype low-pass filter. This frequency spread between the channels would cause error in the recovery of data bits through any digital demodulation. The suboptimal performance of standard orthogonal wavelet filters, as demonstrated in Figs. 5, 6, and 7, is the motivation behind the search for other filters.

For the application in hand, the MODWPT and SIMODWPT formulations are modified by relaxing the orthogonality and perfect reconstruction constraints traditionally imposed on the prototype wavelet filters. Specifically, the filter-bank structure is retained, but the analysis and synthesis filters are no longer restricted to orthogonal wavelet bases; instead, any even-length linear-phase FIR prototype filter may be employed. This modification preserves the shift-invariant, non-decimated tree structure while enabling the design of prototype filters with sharper transition bands and improved spectral confinement.

The generalized SMODWPT/SIMODWPT formulation above establishes a complete analysis-synthesis filter-bank framework in which all subband filters are derived from a single prototype filter pair through systematic dilation and cascading operations. Based on this structural unification, the proposed SMODWPT channelizer offers (i) level-wise uniform bandwidth, (ii) scalable spectral resolution from coarse to fine partitions, (iii) single-prototype filter realization across all subbands, and (iv) reduced memory and transform overhead compared with FFT/polyphase banks.

While this formulation guarantees structural consistency and eliminates aliasing through shift-invariant decomposition, FFT-based circular convolution still introduces a small edge-induced phase rotation at each subband output. It is important to note that the non-decimated nature of the SMODWPT analysis stage is intentionally retained to preserve shift invariance and uniform spectral alignment across all subbands. Practical baseband rate reduction is therefore not performed within the multilevel analysis tree itself. Instead, selective SIMODWPT reconstruction is first used to isolate the desired channel in the time domain, after which conventional decimation may be applied to reduce the sample rate to the required baseband processing rate. This separation between spectral analysis and rate adaptation improves architectural clarity while maintaining full flexibility for downstream processing. Consequently, the practical utility of the channelizer depends critically on the spectral localisation and inter-channel isolation characteristics of the prototype filter, which are quantified in the following section.

Frequency localisation and channel isolation metrics

Energy-based spectral metrics are widely used to characterize frequency localization and adjacent-channel isolation in multirate filter-bank designs9,12,27. Flat-band multichannel filter banks have been extensively studied in the context of communication receivers, including equiripple and oversampled polyphase designs that trade strict orthogonality for improved in-band flatness and spectral confinement12,18,28.

Accordingly, the transition-band energy (TBE)29,30 and stop-band energy (SBE)31,32 are adopted here as physically meaningful measures of spectral leakage and channel interference.

In multichannel communication receivers, spectral isolation alone is not sufficient to guarantee reliable demodulation. Equally important is flat-band (or flat-top) behavior within each subband, which ensures minimal in-band amplitude distortion, predictable group delay, and stable error vector magnitude (EVM) across channels. Flat-band filter-bank designs are widely adopted in communication systems to preserve modulation fidelity, particularly for QPSK and higher-order constellations.

Although the present work does not explicitly optimize a separate flatness metric, the use of a high-order linear-phase equiripple FIR prototype with tight passband constraints implicitly enforces near-flat amplitude response within each subband. As a result, the proposed SMODWPT-derived subbands exhibit negligible in-band ripple relative to the achieved adjacent-channel attenuation, enabling consistent symbol recovery across all channels.

In multichannel digital processors, these two effects are inherently conflicting: improving localisation by increasing the filter order typically reduces TBE but can cause a slight redistribution of energy toward the transition region, affecting SBE. Therefore, both metrics must be optimized jointly, which motivates the multi-objective formulation adopted in the subsequent design optimization section.

Multi-objective prototype filter optimization

The aim of the proposed design optimization is to identify the minimum-cost equiripple lowpass prototype filter order that meets spectral performance requirements and ensures reliable QPSK data recovery following multichannel analysis–synthesis processing. This optimization is structured as a constrained multi-objective problem, jointly addressing frequency localisation, channel isolation, and hardware complexity. It is important to emphasize that the proposed approach does not introduce a new equiripple synthesis algorithm. Standard equiripple filters are designed using conventional tools to meet local passband and stopband ripple constraints. In contrast, this work embeds candidate equiripple prototypes into the complete SMODWPT/SIMODWPT channelizer and evaluates them using multichannel spectral metrics (TBE, SBE) and end-to-end QPSK performance (EVM). The composite cost function therefore enables system-level, communication-driven selection of the prototype order, which is not addressed by classical single-filter equiripple design methods.

Interpretation of the non-monotonic cost profile

The observed smooth but weakly non-monotonic variation of the composite cost function J(N) is a direct consequence of the competing trends among its constituent terms. As the prototype filter order N increases, the transition band energy decreases monotonically due to improved spectral roll-off, while the stop-band energy exhibits a slow increase associated with the redistribution of approximation error inherent to equiripple designs. In parallel, the complexity related term 1/N decreases rapidly with increasing filter order.

The interaction of these opposing tendencies produces a shallow minimum in J(N) rather than a strictly monotonic behavior. Extensive evaluation over a dense set of admissible prototype realizations confirms that this minimum is robust and not an artifact of sparse sampling or numerical interpolation.

The resulting cost profile therefore provides a clear and quantitative basis for selecting an optimal prototype order that simultaneously balances spectral localization, adjacent-channel isolation, and implementation complexity. Based on this outcome, the equiripple prototype low-pass FIR filter corresponding to the global minimum of J(N) is formally adopted in the following subsection.

Theoretical scalability limits and maximum feasible channel count

The generalized SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework enables dyadic subdivision of the Nyquist bandwidth into uniform subbands through non-decimated multilevel filtering. While the formulation is mathematically extensible to arbitrarily large decomposition depths, practical scalability is bounded by several fundamental considerations.

First, the effective subband bandwidth decreases exponentially with the decomposition level as . As j increases, the transition regions of adjacent subband filters begin to overlap relative to the shrinking passband width. For a fixed prototype filter order N, this eventually limits achievable adjacent-channel isolation, since the prototype’s transition-band energy is redistributed over increasingly narrow spectral regions.

Second, the stop-band attenuation achievable by a finite-length FIR prototype imposes an upper bound on usable channel density. Although higher decomposition levels preserve structural uniformity, maintaining constant isolation across all channels would require proportionally increasing the prototype filter order, which directly impacts computational complexity and memory usage.

Third, numerical precision effects become more pronounced at very deep decomposition levels. The repeated application of dilated filtering operations can accumulate finite-precision errors in practical implementations, even though the transform remains shift-invariant in theory.

Despite this concern, the proposed SMODWPT/SIMODWPT formulation exhibits strong numerical stability under deep decomposition when implemented using FFT-based circular convolution. Because each analysis and synthesis operation is performed independently in the frequency domain, the filter-bank does not accumulate recursive rounding errors across levels. Instead, numerical errors are bounded at each stage by the finite precision of the FFT and coefficient quantization, rather than compounding multiplicatively along the cascade.

Finally, from a system perspective, the minimum resolvable channel bandwidth is bounded by the modulation symbol rate and acceptable error vector magnitude (EVM). When approaches the occupied signal bandwidth, further subdivision provides diminishing returns and may degrade demodulation performance.

Based on these considerations, the maximum feasible channel count for a given system is jointly determined by the prototype filter order, allowable hardware complexity, numerical precision, and communication-level constraints such as EVM. In this work, scalability up to 2048 channels () is demonstrated under ideal floating-point conditions to illustrate architectural extensibility. Higher channel counts remain theoretically possible but would require correspondingly higher prototype orders and careful fixed-point design to preserve isolation and reconstruction fidelity.

Potential role of AI/ML in phase noise mitigation

The present work addresses deterministic phase rotation introduced by FFT-based circular convolution through analytical cancellation using the SIMODWPT synthesis stage. This approach is well suited for structured, predictable phase distortions inherent to multilevel filter-bank implementations. However, in practical onboard and distributed communication systems, additional sources of phase noise may arise from oscillators, clock jitter, Doppler dynamics, and non-ideal analog front-end components, which are not explicitly modeled in the current study.

Recent research has demonstrated the effectiveness of data-driven AI/ML models for phase noise estimation, compensation, and prediction in complex communication and cyber-physical systems. Neural-network-based and learning-assisted techniques have been successfully applied to oscillator phase noise characterization, adaptive compensation in wireless receivers, and distributed control systems operating under uncertainty34–36.

In this context, AI/ML-based phase noise mitigation can be viewed as a complementary enhancement to the proposed SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework rather than a replacement. The analytically phase-neutral reconstruction provided by SIMODWPT establishes a stable baseline upon which lightweight learning-based modules could be integrated to compensate residual stochastic phase perturbations, particularly in highly dynamic or nonstationary onboard environments.

Future extensions of this work may therefore explore hybrid architectures in which the proposed multilevel channelizer provides deterministic spectral separation and reconstruction, while AI/ML models operate at the symbol or baseband level to adaptively mitigate residual phase noise, oscillator drift, or hardware-induced impairments. Such a hybrid approach aligns naturally with recent trends in intelligent receivers and software-defined onboard payloads.

In multilevel SMODWPT-based decompositions, this effect is further accentuated by the dyadic aggregation of transition-band energy across adjacent branches of the filter tree. Although the optimized equiripple prototype minimizes transition- band and stop-band leakage globally, the finite transition width of any practical FIR filter leads to a redistribution of residual energy that is most pronounced in densely packed central channels.

Importantly, this mid-band behavior reflects a structural property of uniform filter-bank channelization under finite filter order, and is commonly observed in FFT-based and polyphase filter-bank architectures as well. The maintained worst-case isolation levels remain comfortably above those required for reliable QPSK demodulation, as confirmed by the end-to-end EVM results. Consequently, the observed mid-band isolation reduction does not imply insufficient prototype selectivity, but rather represents an expected and manageable trade-off inherent to dense uniform channel grids. End-to-end QPSK performance

End-to-end communication performance was evaluated using the 64 channel QPSK multicarrier configuration described in “Results and discussion”. For each channel, pseudo-random bit sequences of length 4096 were generated, QPSK-modulated, combined into a composite multicarrier signal, and processed through the SMODWPT analysis filter bank. Selective reconstruction was then performed using the corresponding SIMODWPT synthesis stage, followed by symbol recovery using a quadrant-based QPSK detector.

All filtering operations were implemented using FFT-based circular convolution. As a result, individual subband signals exhibit a deterministic edge-induced phase rotation associated with the analysis–synthesis process. This effect is fully compensated during selective reconstruction: by isolating a single subband and applying SIMODWPT, each channel is recovered with negligible residual phase error. Consequently, standard coherent QPSK demodulation can be performed without explicit carrier phase tracking.

Across all 64 channels, the reconstructed symbol streams match the transmitted data without observable degradation under the operating conditions considered. The measured error vector magnitude (EVM) remains below for all channels when the prototype filter order satisfies the feasibility constraint , in agreement with the design criteria established in “Feasibility constraint based on QPSK signal recovery”. These results confirm that the proposed SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework preserves end-to-end QPSK signal integrity while providing fine-granularity multichannel isolation. Although QPSK is used in this study as a conservative reference modulation, the proposed SMODWPT/SIMODWPT channelizer is inherently modulation-agnostic. The linear analysis–synthesis structure, combined with strong spectral confinement and phase-neutral reconstruction, makes the framework directly applicable to higher-order modulations such as 16APSK and 32APSK, subject to their tighter EVM constraints. Extending the present analysis to such modulation formats is identified as a natural direction for future work. No error floors were observed within the simulated SNR range, confirming robustness of the proposed channelizer.

Fixed-point robustness analysis

To evaluate implementation robustness under practical hardware constraints, a fixed-point simulation study was conducted for the 64-channel () configuration. This analysis provides a first-order sensitivity assessment, quantifying how the ideal floating-point isolation levels degrade under finite word-length arithmetic while preserving end-to-end demodulation performance.

All filtering, FFT/IFFT operations, and analysis–synthesis processing were implemented using signed fixed-point arithmetic with 21-bit word length. Prototype FIR coefficients were quantized to 16 bits, and internal FFT data paths used 18–24 bit precision to maintain numerical stability throughout the processing chain.

Under fixed-point operation, the mean adjacent-channel isolation decreases moderately compared to floating-point simulations, while preserving the same relative spectral trends across channels. The worst-case adjacent-channel discrimination remains above 65 dB, and the mean isolation exceeds 80 dB across the 64-channel grid. These margins remain sufficient for reliable QPSK demodulation.

End-to-end QPSK performance under fixed-point arithmetic was also evaluated. The reconstructed signals exhibit stable constellation recovery, with error vector magnitude (EVM) remaining below 12% for all channels. No error floors or systematic phase bias were observed, confirming that the proposed SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework maintains functional robustness under realistic finite-precision constraints.

Although fixed-point validation was performed primarily for the configuration, the absence of recursive time-domain accumulation in the FFT-based realization suggests that similar numerical stability can be maintained at higher decomposition levels, provided that appropriate word-length selection is applied.

Discussion and implementation implications

The results confirm that the proposed SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework achieves strong spectral localisation and high adjacent-channel isolation using a single optimized prototype FIR filter. The results should be interpreted as demonstrating architectural feasibility and intrinsic spectral capability rather than absolute superiority over mature FFT- or polyphase filter-bank (PFB)-based channelizers operating under fully matched implementation conditions.

For the finest-resolution configuration (64 channels at 10 kHz spacing), the mean adjacent-channel isolation exceeds 98 dB, with a worst-case value of 79.68 dB. These margins provide substantial headroom relative to typical isolation requirements for multi-carrier QPSK communication links and confirm effective suppression of inter-channel interference across the band.

When operated at coarser resolutions corresponding to and , the isolation margins improve further without any architectural modification. This demonstrates that the same filter-bank structure supports both fine- and coarse-granularity channelization through multilevel resolution selection alone, preserving spectral consistency across decomposition depths.

Additional experiments with larger decompositions (512, 1024, and 2048 channels) indicate that the optimized low-TBE prototype maintains strong spectral confinement as the number of subbands increases, with mean isolation remaining above 77 dB and peak values exceeding 95 dB. These results validate the scalability of the proposed framework and highlight its suitability for FPGA-based onboard digital processors, where uniform channel spacing, interference robustness, and controlled hardware complexity are essential design objectives.

Fixed-point simulation results further confirm that, although absolute isolation levels decrease under finite-precision arithmetic, the proposed architecture preserves its relative spectral uniformity, channel separation behavior, and QPSK recovery performance under realistic implementation constraints.

The FFT buffer is subsequently read 64 times, once for each channel. During each read cycle, the FFT output samples are pointwise multiplied by the corresponding prototype-derived filter coefficients retrieved from the Filter Coefficients ROM. The pointwise multiplication and the 4096-point inverse FFT (IFFT) are performed in tandem at 160 MHz, enabling high-throughput sequential channel reconstruction.

The interleaving between algorithmic stages is illustrated in Fig. 17B. The processing flow is organized into 3 ms pipelined time slots. In Slot 1, Buffer Memory 1 (BM1) is written at a 2 MHz clock rate while no further processing is performed. In Slot 2, BM1 is read and processed at 160 MHz, while Buffer Memory 2 (BM2) is simultaneously written at 2 MHz. From Slot 2 onward, the pipeline is fully interleaved, and the system continues operating in a steady-state fashion, alternating between buffer write and high-speed processing phases. By exploiting the slow analysis rate, high-speed reconstruction, and sequential reuse of FFT, multiplier, and IFFT resources, the proposed realization achieves high spectral isolation and throughput with minimal hardware duplication. This architecture is therefore well suited for FPGA-based onboard digital processors requiring fine-granular, interference-resilient channelization under stringent power and resource constraints.

Comparison with FFT-based channelizers:

A conventional M-channel FFT-based channelizer typically performs one large FFT per processing block, followed by frequency-domain masking and, in many practical implementations, multiple parallel IFFT stages when simultaneous channel reconstruction is required. When several channels must be extracted concurrently, either multiple IFFT cores or additional multiplexing logic is introduced. Furthermore, frequency-domain masking often requires storing full-length spectral masks for each channel, increasing memory usage and memory bandwidth requirements.

In contrast, the proposed SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework employs strictly sequential channel reconstruction using a single shared FFT core, a single complex multiplier, and a single IFFT core operating at 160 MHz. No multiplexers are required for channel routing. Instead, channel selection is performed through indexed access to frequency-domain filter coefficients stored in ROM.

Specifically, the frequency-domain filter coefficients are organized using a dual-memory structure consisting of:

A content memory that stores only the non-zero coefficient values (16-bit width),

A content memory that stores only the non-zero coefficient values (16-bit width),

An index memory (48-bit width, depth 64) that points to the corresponding non-zero coefficient locations for each channel.

An index memory (48-bit width, depth 64) that points to the corresponding non-zero coefficient locations for each channel.

This sparse indexed storage eliminates the need to store full-length frequency masks for all channels and significantly reduces memory footprint.

During reconstruction, the FFT buffer is read sequentially, and only the indexed non-zero frequency bins are multiplied with the corresponding stored coefficients retrieved from ROM. As a result, hardware resources scale primarily with FFT length rather than with channel count.

This architecture trades increased block-based latency for reduced multiplier count, reduced memory bandwidth, and elimination of parallel IFFT duplication. Such a time-multiplexed structure is particularly advantageous for resource-constrained FPGA-based onboard processors where DSP slices, logic area, and power consumption are more critical than per-channel latency.

It is noted that fully sequential FFT-based channelizers can also reuse a single IFFT core. However, such implementations typically require storage of complete frequency masks for each channel, whereas the proposed indexed sparse-coefficient storage reduces both memory footprint and bandwidth requirements while preserving deterministic channel extraction.

Conclusion

This paper has presented a unified SMODWPT/SIMODWPT-based multichannel channelization framework tailored for dense multicarrier onboard communication payloads. By deriving all analysis and synthesis subband filters from a single optimized equiripple prototype low-pass FIR filter, the proposed approach eliminates the need for independently designed subfilters while preserving uniform channel spacing and consistent spectral characteristics across all decomposition levels.

Comprehensive multichannel QPSK simulations conducted on a 64-carrier, 10 kHz grid demonstrate strong frequency localisation and high inter-channel isolation. The proposed channelizer achieves a mean adjacent-channel discrimination exceeding 98 dB, with a worst-case value of 79.68 dB occurring under mid-band spectral crowding. The SIMODWPT-based selective reconstruction effectively compensates for phase rotation introduced by FFT-based circular convolution, enabling reliable QPSK demodulation without explicit carrier phase tracking or decision-directed synchronization.

Scalability studies performed at higher decomposition levels (512, 1024, and 2048 channels) further confirm that the optimized prototype maintains low transition-band energy and strong spectral confinement as the number of subbands increases. These results indicate that the proposed framework can support both fine- and coarse-granularity channelization through multilevel subband aggregation, without any modification to the prototype or filter-bank architecture.

Overall, the proposed SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework provides a consistent and flexible foundation for multilevel channelization in resource-constrained onboard digital processors, supporting uniform channel spacing, scalable spectral resolution, and robust interference isolation. The framework is immediately applicable to FPGA-based onboard processors requiring scalable, interference-resilient channelization. The proposed framework therefore provides a unified mathematical, spectral, and architectural foundation for scalable, fine-granular channelization in future interference-aware satellite payloads.

While this work focuses on QPSK as a conservative reference modulation, the proposed SMODWPT/SIMODWPT framework is inherently modulation-agnostic and can be extended to higher-order modulation formats, subject to prototype flatness and error vector magnitude (EVM) constraints.

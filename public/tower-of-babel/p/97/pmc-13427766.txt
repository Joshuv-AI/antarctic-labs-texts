Bowel urgency in ulcerative colitis is associated with clinical endpoints, health-related quality of life, and biomarkers: data from the etrasimod ELEVATE UC 52 trial
Authors: Marla C Dubinsky, Erica R Cohen, Peter M Irving, Martina Goetsch, Krisztina Lazin, Abhishek Bhattacharjee, Gokul Pradeep, Peter Hur, Christina Cognata, Karolina Wosik, Remo Panaccione
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBowel urgency (BU) is a disruptive ulcerative colitis (UC) symptom. Etrasimod, a sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P)1,4,5 receptor modulator, improved BU in the ELEVATE UC clinical program.MethodsWe assessed associations between BU and efficacy endpoints, health-related quality of life (HRQoL), and biomarkers (Weeks 12 and 52) in patients receiving etrasimod in ELEVATE UC 52. A patient-reported numerical rating scale (NRS; 0-10; none to worst BU) assessed BU at baseline, Week 12, and Week 52. Binary BU outcomes were BU remission (NRS ≤1), clinically meaningful improvement in BU (NRS ≥3-point decrease), and complete BU remission (NRS = 0).ResultsAt Week 12, etrasimod-treated patients with BU remission were 4.0 times more likely to be in clinical remission (odds ratio [95% confidence interval (CI)]: 4.0 [2.3-7.1]; P < .0001) and 3.6 times more likely to show endoscopic improvement (3.6 [2.1-6.2]; P < .0001) versus patients without BU remission. Patients with BU remission also had significantly greater improvement in Inflammatory Bowel Disease Questionnaire: Total score (least squares mean difference [95% CI] 21.8 [13.5-30.1], P < .0001) and significantly lower fecal calprotectin and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (mean [standard deviation] 744.5 [1,758.0] vs 2,314.2 [5,182.2] and 3.2 [5.8] vs 10.5 [24.9], respectively, both P < .0001). A similar association was seen for clinically meaningful improvement in BU and complete BU remission at Week 12 and all BU outcomes at Week 52.ConclusionsIn etrasimod-treated patients, bowel urgency outcomes are positively associated with efficacy endpoints, HRQoL scores, and inflammatory biomarkers, and may be surrogate markers of disease activity and HRQoL improvement.ClinicalTrials.govNCT03945188

Introduction

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic, systemic disease that is characterized by immune-mediated inflammation and ulceration of the intestinal mucosa, causing highly disruptive symptoms including rectal bleeding (RB), diarrhea, and bowel urgency (BU).1 The objectives of UC management are control of symptoms, biomarker normalization, endoscopic healing, and ultimately the improvement of quality of life.2 BU and episodes of fecal incontinence represent a significant burden for patients with UC and lead to decreases in exercise and physical activity as well as stress and anxiety.3,4 Up to half of patients with active UC or Crohn’s disease report at least one instance of urgency per day5,6 and many still experience BU despite therapy and being in clinical and endoscopic remission.7–10 In some cases, persistence of BU and other irritable bowel syndrome-like symptoms can be attributed to a range of overlapping, co-existing gastrointestinal etiologies including small intestinal bacterial overgrowth, intestinal methanogen overgrowth, and bile acid malabsorption.11 This highlights the importance of measuring and better understanding BU, its relation to clinical endpoints and its potential as a treatment target in UC. It is only recently that the patient-reported outcome of BU has been systematically assessed in clinical trials and has begun to be measured in clinical practice.8,12–14

The urgency numeric rating scale (NRS) is a single-item, patient-reported outcome that measures the immediate need to have a bowel movement.15 The tool has been validated in both UC and Crohn’s disease.15–17 With this scale, patients rate the severity of BU in the previous 24 hours from 0 to 10 (no urgency to worst possible urgency). A ≥3-point reduction in urgency NRS score has been shown to represent a clinically meaningful improvement in BU17 while an urgency NRS score ≤1 signifies a BU remission threshold that was found to be strongly associated with clinical, endoscopic, and histologic remission.15,17 During validation, patients generally deemed the urgency NRS to be easy to understand and a high rate of completion compliance was seen.15 Moreover, most healthcare providers appear to show a willingness to implement it in clinical practice, if not doing so already.18

Etrasimod is an oral, once-daily (QD), selective sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P)1,4,5 receptor modulator for the treatment of moderately to severely active UC. The phase 3 ELEVATE UC clinical program has demonstrated the efficacy and safety of etrasimod in patients with moderately to severely active UC.19 A post hoc analysis further explored etrasimod efficacy for improving symptomatic outcomes that included BU remission (NRS ≤1), clinically meaningful improvement in BU (NRS ≥3-point decrease from baseline), and complete BU remission (NRS = 0).20 Greater proportions of patients receiving etrasimod versus placebo achieved the three measures of BU at Weeks 12 and 52 in the ELEVATE UC clinical program.20 The relationship of BU outcomes in patients treated with etrasimod to other endpoints is still being characterized.8,12

In this post hoc analysis of patients receiving etrasimod in ELEVATE UC 52, we examined how BU outcomes related to clinical, endoscopic, and symptomatic efficacy endpoints as well as HRQoL endpoints and inflammatory biomarkers.

Methods

Patients and study design

ELEVATE UC 52 (NCT03945188) was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of etrasimod in patients with moderately to severely active UC. Details of patient enrollment and trial design have previously been published.19 Briefly, enrollment criteria included patients 16-80 years of age with a modified Mayo score (MMS) of 4-9, an endoscopic subscore (ES) of ≥2, and an RB subscore (RBS) of ≥1. Patients were randomized 2:1 to receive either etrasimod 2 mg QD or placebo QD. The trial had a treat-through design and consisted of a 12-week induction period followed by a 40-week maintenance period.

ELEVATE UC 52 was conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Boards at each investigational center participating in the trial. Written informed consent was provided by all patients. All authors had access to the study data and reviewed and approved the final manuscript.

Outcome measures

In ELEVATE UC 52, BU was evaluated at baseline, Week 12, and Week 52 via the patient-reported, 11-point urgency NRS that ranges from 0 (no urgency) to 10 (worst possible urgency). For this post hoc analysis, binary BU outcomes assessed were BU remission (NRS ≤1), clinically meaningful improvement in BU (a decrease of ≥3 NRS points from baseline), and complete BU remission (NRS = 0). Patient subgroups were categorized based on meeting or not meeting each of the BU outcomes at Week 12 and separately at Week 52.

The clinical, endoscopic, and symptomatic endpoints assessed in ELEVATE UC 52 were clinical remission (stool frequency [SF] subscore [SFS] = 0 [or = 1 with a ≥1-point decrease from baseline], RBS = 0, and ES ≤1 [excluding friability]), clinical response (≥2-point and ≥30% reduction from baseline in MMS and a ≥1-point decrease from baseline in RBS or an absolute RBS ≤1), RB remission (RBS = 0), SF remission (SFS = 0 or = 1 with a ≥1-point decrease from baseline), symptomatic remission (SFS = 0 [or = 1 with a ≥1-point decrease from baseline] and RBS = 0), and endoscopic improvement (ES ≤1). RB remission and SF remission were measured post hoc; all other endpoints were predefined.

HRQoL measures in ELEVATE UC 52 were also predefined and derived from the Inflammatory Bowel Disease Questionnaire (IBDQ) and the 36-item short form (SF-36) survey. The IBDQ, a validated 32-item questionnaire, encompasses a total score ranging from 32 (very poor HRQoL) to 224 (perfect HRQoL), with subscores for bowel symptoms (10-70), systemic symptoms (5-35), emotional health (12-84), and social function (5-35). The SF-36, a 36-item patient-reported survey, evaluates eight health domains: physical functioning, bodily pain, role limitations due to physical and emotional problems, general health perceptions, mental health, social function, and vitality. The survey was scored using physical and mental component summary scores.

Biomarkers, fecal calprotectin (fCAL) and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP) measured per protocol at Weeks 12 and 52 in ELEVATE UC 52 were analyzed.

Statistical analyses

Analyses were conducted in patients receiving etrasimod in the full analysis set of ELEVATE UC 52 (MMS 4-9). For associations between BU outcomes and binary efficacy endpoints in this post hoc analysis, odds ratios (OR; 95% confidence interval [CI]) and two-sided P values were obtained from separate multivariable logistic regression analyses for Week 12 and Week 52. Each model included covariates BU subgroup (yes/no) at the respective week, prior biologic/Janus kinase inhibitor (bio/JAKi) therapy (yes/no), baseline corticosteroid use (yes/no), and baseline disease activity (MMS 4-6 or MMS 7-9). Missing responses were considered as nonresponse.

For associations between BU and changes from baseline in HRQoL measures, least squares (LS) mean difference (95% CI, two-sided P values) comparing BU subgroups (yes/no) were obtained using separate multivariable linear regression models for Week 12 and Week 52. Each model included covariates BU subgroup (yes/no) at the respective week, baseline score of respective HRQoL endpoint, as well as SFS and RBS at the respective week. Missing change from baseline values were not imputed.

Continuous biomarker endpoints were analyzed using the Wilcoxon Rank Sum Test at Week 12 and Week 52. Wilcoxon test two-sided P values were calculated based on normal approximation. The last observation carried forward method was used to impute missing values.

Baseline characteristics were summarized descriptively, stratified by BU subgroups (yes/no) at Week 12 and Week 52.

Results

Patient baseline characteristics and demographics

In total, 289 patients received etrasimod in ELEVATE UC 52. Demographics and baseline characteristics are shown in the subgroups of patients with and without BU remission (NRS ≤1) at Week 12 and Week 52 in Table 1. While many baseline characteristics were relatively well balanced across subgroups, a greater proportion of patients with versus without BU remission were bio/JAKi-naïve (73.6% vs 69.8% in Week 12 subgroups and 82.3% vs 66.7% in Week 52 subgroups), a smaller proportion had pancolitis at baseline (27.6% vs 34.2% in Week 12 subgroups and 25.3% vs 34.8% in Week 52 subgroups), and a greater proportion had proctitis at baseline (9.2% vs 6.9% in Week 12 subgroups and 12.7% vs 5.7% in Week 52 subgroups; Table 1). The same trends were generally observed in subgroups with versus without complete BU remission (NRS = 0) and with versus without clinically meaningful improvement in BU (NRS ≥3-point decrease; see Table S1, Data Content).

Outcomes

Association between BU outcomes and clinical, endoscopic, and symptomatic endpoints

Patients with BU remission at Week 12 had 4.0 times higher odds of clinical remission (OR [95% CI]: 4.0 [2.3-7.1]; P < .0001) and 3.6 times higher odds of endoscopic improvement (3.6 [2.1-6.2]; P < .0001; Figure 1A) than patients without BU remission. Moreover, at Week 52, ORs (95% CI) were 13.2 (7.0-24.9) for clinical remission and 12.5 (6.5-24.1) for endoscopic improvement for patients in BU remission (both P < .0001; Figure 1B). Patients with a clinically meaningful improvement in BU at Week 12 were 3.7 times more likely to also be in clinical remission and 2.6 times more likely to have endoscopic improvement (OR 3.7 [2.1-6.4] and 2.6 [1.6-4.3], respectively; both P < .001; Figure 1C) versus those without such improvement. This association is even more evident at Week 52, where patients with a clinically meaningful improvement in BU had 8.8 times higher odds of being in clinical remission and 7.1 times higher odds of showing endoscopic improvement (OR 8.8 [4.9-15.7] and 7.1 [4.0-12.5], respectively; both P < .0001; Figure 1D) versus those who did not have meaningful BU improvement. The endpoints clinical response, RB remission, SF remission, and symptomatic remission were also all significantly more likely in patients with versus without BU remission, or clinically meaningful improvement in BU at Week 12 and Week 52 (all P < .0001; Figure 1). This was also evident in patients with complete BU remission (see Figure, Supplementary Data Content 2).

Association of BU outcomes with change from baseline in HRQoL measures

Patients with BU remission had significantly greater improvements from baseline in total IBDQ score versus those without BU remission at Week 12 (LS mean change from baseline: 59.9 vs 38.2; difference in LS mean change from baseline [95% CI]: 21.8 [13.5-30.1], P < .0001; Figure 2A) and at Week 52 (77.5 vs 52.0; 25.5 [16.8-34.1], P < .0001; Figure 2B). Among patients with a clinically meaningful improvement in BU, significantly greater improvements from baseline in total IBDQ scores were seen at Week 12 (LS mean change from baseline: 57.3 vs 31.8; difference in LS mean change from baseline: 25.5 [17.7-33.2], P < .0001; Figure 2C) and at Week 52 (71.6 vs 48.2; 23.5 [13.7-33.2], P < .0001; Figure 2D) versus those without a clinically meaningful improvement. Significant differences among subgroups favoring patients that met BU outcomes were also seen in the IBDQ bowel symptoms subscore, IBDQ systemic symptoms subscore, IBDQ emotional health subscore, and IBDQ social function subscore, and in SF-36 physical and mental component scores at both time points (all P < .05; Figure 2 and see Figure, Supplementary Data Content 3).

Association of BU outcomes with inflammatory biomarkers

Patients with BU remission, a clinically meaningful improvement in BU, or complete BU remission had significantly lower fCAL concentrations as well as significantly lower hsCRP at Week 12 and Week 52 versus patients not meeting these BU outcomes (all P ≤ .01; Table 2).

Discussion

In this post hoc analysis of the ELEVATE UC 52 trial of etrasimod in patients with moderately to severely active UC, we show the utility of an improvement in BU as an indicator of treatment efficacy, supporting the use of absence of BU as a treatment target alongside clinical, endoscopic, and HRQoL improvements. We confirmed relationships between favorable BU outcomes, as assessed with the 11-point urgency NRS, and clinical remission, the endoscopic appearance of the mucosa, symptomatic endpoints of rectal bleeding and stool frequency, HRQoL, and inflammatory biomarkers. This relationship was seen at the end of induction at 12 weeks, but also after 52 weeks of treatment.

In a previous analysis of patients in ELEVATE UC 52 with a baseline urgency NRS ≥3, a significantly greater proportion of patients treated with etrasimod (Week 12: 126/255 [49.4%]; Week 52: 104/255 [40.8%]) versus placebo (Week 12: 38/119 [31.9%]; Week 52: 29/119 [24.4%]) achieved a clinically meaningful improvement in BU at Weeks 12 and 52. Similar trends were generally also seen for BU remission and BU complete remission.20 In the current analysis, we further investigated the association of BU outcomes with achievement of efficacy endpoints in patients treated with etrasimod in ELEVATE UC 52 and found an association of favorable BU outcomes with all clinical, endoscopic, and symptomatic endpoints assessed.

The ELEVATE studies have demonstrated significant improvement with etrasimod versus placebo in all IBDQ domain scores after 12 and 52 weeks of treatment.21 The minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for the IBDQ and SF-36 are generally accepted as being an improvement of at least 16 points in total IBDQ score or 3-5 points in the SF-36 physical and mental component subscores.22 Here, we saw that across the BU outcome subgroups and time points, not only did the mean changes from baseline in these HRQoL scores meet the MCID thresholds, but the difference between patients with the BU outcomes versus those without also exceeded the MCID for the IBDQ total score and generally met the MCID for the SF-36 component scores. This observation suggests that absence or reduction in BU is a valid marker for improvement in HRQoL.

In addition, a statistically significant association was identified between BU outcomes and improvement in IBDQ subscores for emotional health and social function as well as SF-36 mental component, even after adjusting for SFS and RBS at the same time point. The mechanisms linking BU to mental health outcomes have yet to be fully characterized. Our findings here suggest that BU may be associated with improved HRQoL independently of rectal bleeding and stool frequency. Of the IBDQ subscores, the emotional health subscore showed the greatest difference in improvement between patients with versus without positive BU outcomes. This further highlights the emotional distress caused by BU symptoms and the impact of removing the stress and unpredictability BU has on patients’ emotional well-being.

Our results corroborate previous findings from the LUCENT mirikizumab trials, which demonstrated that a clinically meaningful improvement or remission in BU using the urgency NRS was associated with improvement in IBDQ.23 They are also in line with the U-ACHIEVE and U-ACCOMPLISH upadacitinib trials where correlations were found between BU (reported by the patient as presence of urgency [yes/no] over the 3 consecutive days prior to the study visit) and clinical and/or HRQoL outcomes at the end of induction.24,25 These findings together with our own suggest that measures of BU are a useful surrogate marker of disease activity in UC regardless of mechanism of action of therapy.

A 2023 study found that of 459 healthcare professional participants, only 23% use the urgency NRS in clinical practice.18 Although, of those that did not, 89% showed willingness to implement it.18 Given the significant burden of BU on patients, the importance they place on its improvement, the ease of use of BU assessment tools such as the NRS, as well as the positive correlations seen in this and other similar analyses, increased integration of BU assessment into clinical trials and clinical practice by healthcare providers will likely have a positive impact on patient care.12,15,23,26,27

This study is limited by its post hoc nature. Moreover, according to study protocol, BU responses were collected from patients at baseline, Week 12, and Week 52 and so associations with BU outcomes were only assessed at these time points. Similar to what was observed for RBS and SFS in the ELEVATE UC trials,20 BU may also be improved at earlier time points, which are not reflected in the results of the present study. More granular time points, in particular in the first days and weeks of treatment were not assessed, and may be important to explore in future studies.

Future research should also now focus on fully validating BU measures by confirming responder thresholds across therapies and linking early BU improvements to long-term clinical and endoscopic outcomes. Studies integrating biomarkers, imaging, and mechanistic assessments can clarify biological drivers, while treat-to-target trials could test BU remission as a management goal. Cross-cultural validation, digital home-monitoring, and health-economic modeling will support broader implementation. Together, these steps will strengthen BU as a robust, patient-centered UC endpoint supported by evidence across mechanisms of action.

Conclusion

Among patients with UC treated with etrasimod, we found that absence of, or improvements in, bowel urgency were associated with improvements in clinical, endoscopic, and symptomatic endpoints, amelioration of quality of life, and decreased inflammatory biomarkers. Altogether, these findings suggest that the bothersome and prevalent symptom of BU may be useful to monitor disease activity, treatment outcomes, and general patient well-being.

SPCT-12 SPINAL SCHWANNOMAS AT CHUD B/A : ABOUT 07 PATIENTS
Authors: Kisito Quenum, Holden Olatundji Fatigba
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSpinal neuromas are benign, slow-growing tumors that originate from Schwann cells and develop on a root that is often sensitive. In 1/3 of cases, these are primary intraspinal lesions and are frequently associated with Von Recklinghausen disease.ObjectiveDescribe slow spinal cord compressions in their epidemiological, clinical, paraclinical and progressive aspects in relation to spinal neuromas.Methodsthis was a cross-sectional, descriptive study with retroactive collection extending from January 1, 2010 to December 31, 2023. It concerned all patients admitted to the neurosurgery department of CHUD-B/A, for slow spinal cord compression by a spinal neuroma.ResultsA total of 07 patients were included in the study. The average age was 42.35 ± 5.20 years with extremes of 20 and 72 years. A male predominance with a sex ratio of 1.58. An average admission time of 22.74 ± 5.92 months, with back pain as the majority reason (51.61%). An injury syndrome present at 100% and paraparesis as the majority deficit (51,61) ; the location was cervical in 28.58% of cases (02 cases) and thorsolumbar in 71.42% (05 cases). The treatment was surgical with 2 interventions via anterior approach and 5 via posterior approach. The evolution was favorable with 71.43% complete neurological recovery at one year. No deaths were recorded in this study.Conclusionspinal neuroma is a benign tumor whose diagnosis must be early to avoid a complete picture of slow spinal cord compression. Early surgery gives good results after total resection.

A unified multi scale feature enhancement framework for remote sensing object detection
Authors: Min Feng, Shuai Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Small object detection in remote sensing is critical for urban planning, environmental monitoring, and disaster response. This task is challenged by extreme scale variations and complex background clutter. Current methods often address feature enhancement and multi-scale fusion separately, leading to fragmented information flow and suboptimal adaptability. Furthermore, achieving comprehensive contextual modeling typically incurs high computational cost. In this paper, we propose MSFE-YOLO, a unified multi-scale feature enhancement framework designed to address these limitations efficiently. Specifically, we introduce an Adaptive Multi-scale Correlation Attention (AMCA) module into the backbone to capture long-range spatial dependencies and suppress background noise, thereby improving feature discrimination across scales. To bridge the semantic gap across scales, we design a Selective Cross-Attention Fusion (SCAF) module combined with an Adaptive Point Upsampler (APU). This combination replaces static interpolation with content-aware sampling, ensuring the preservation of critical details for small objects during feature reconstruction, which enhances feature propagation and fusion. Furthermore, a Multi-scale Dilated Receptive Field (MDRF) module is integrated into the detection head to aggregate multi-scale contextual information without incurring significant computational costs, further boosting feature fusion. Experimental results on the Remote Sensing Object Detection (RSOD) and Vehicle Detection in Aerial Imagery (VEDAI) datasets show significant and consistent gains. On RSOD, MSFE-YOLO improves the baseline by +4.6% mAP@50 and +8.9% mAP@50-95, while increasing the parameter count from 11.17M to 12.7M and reducing the inference speed from 105.62 FPS to 74.56 FPS. These results indicate a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off, demonstrating that our framework effectively improves multi-scale representation and detection accuracy while maintaining acceptable inference speeds for practical remote sensing applications.

Introduction

The rapid advancement of high-resolution Earth observation technology has established remote sensing (RS) images as an essential data source for urban planning, environmental monitoring, and disaster evaluation1,2. RS image object detection refers to the task of automatically and accurately identifying and localizing objects of interest within vast collections of imagery. As a critical component, it facilitates the full utilization of remote sensing data and supports advanced intelligent perception and decision-making systems3,4.

Fueled by the necessity for real-time processing, the You Only Look Once (YOLO) series of detectors has emerged as a preeminent paradigm in RS object detection research. Recent endeavors to adapt YOLO for remote sensing have focused on two complementary strategies: feature enhancement through attention mechanisms and architecture redesign for improved feature fusion.

A common strategy to enhance feature discriminability is to incorporate attention modules into the backbone network5–10. For instance, coordinate attention (CA)5 and Efficient Multi-scale Attention (EMA)6 have been integrated to assist models in emphasizing foreground objects while mitigating cluttered backgrounds. Others have employed Transformer-based self-attention or large-kernel convolutions to capture long-range dependencies and global context7,8. While these strategies enhance feature representation to some extent, they often operate in isolation. A major limitation is that the enhanced features are not reliably maintained and efficiently employed throughout further feature propagation and cross-scale fusion. Essential intermediary steps, such as upsampling, predominantly depend on static procedures like bilinear interpolation, resulting in the progressive loss of nuanced characteristics from small objects and inducing semantic mismatch among features at varying scales9,10.

Simultaneously, to tackle the significant scale variation and dense distribution of small objects, structural enhancements in the neck and head networks are extensively investigated11–17. Previous efforts have introduced advanced feature pyramid networks, including Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network (BiFPN) and adaptive spatial feature fusion (ASFF), as well as specialized detection heads, with the goal of improving small object detection13. For improved bounding box regression, several studies additionally propose new loss functions, such as Wise-IoU14 and Shape-IoU15. Nevertheless, these multi-scale architectures’ information flow frequently adheres to preset, inflexible paths. Their main drawback is the failure to adaptively weight and fuse features in a content-aware manner, conditioned on the complexity of the input scene. Consequently, their ability to handle objects with extreme scale differences and those embedded in highly complex backgrounds remains suboptimal16,17.

A notable trend involves including global contextual modeling capabilities directly into YOLO detectors, for instance, by embedding Transformer blocks18,19 or employing deformable convolutions20. These approaches enhance scene comprehension and relationships between instances. However, they impose significant computational complexity and memory demands, obstructing implementation on resource-limited systems21. Furthermore, the amalgamation of the acquired global context and local fine-grained features is often executed by basic concatenation or addition, devoid of an effective channel/spatial re-weighting method to attain optimal integration of complementary information22.

To address the challenges of remote sensing small object detection, we propose MSFE-YOLO as a unified multi-stage feature enhancement framework rather than a simple combination of independent modules. The core idea of the proposed method is to jointly optimize feature extraction, cross-scale interaction, detail-preserving reconstruction, and prediction-stage contextual enhancement within a coherent backbone-neck-head pipeline. Specifically, the Adaptive Multi-scale Correlation Attention (AMCA) is introduced into the backbone to model scale-adaptive correlation responses and improve discriminative feature encoding under cluttered backgrounds. In the neck, the Selective Cross-Attention Fusion (SCAF) and Adaptive Point Upsampler (APU) are collaboratively designed to reduce semantic inconsistency across pyramid levels while preserving fine-grained small-object cues during upsampling. In the detection head, the Multi-scale Dilated Receptive Field (MDRF) further strengthens prediction features through progressive receptive field expansion and residual fusion. Therefore, the novelty of MSFE-YOLO lies not in the standalone use of multi-scale attention, cross-attention, dynamic upsampling, or dilated convolutions, but in their task-oriented coordination for remote sensing small object detection.

Related works

Our work builds upon recent progress in YOLO-based architectures and attention mechanisms for object detection in remote sensing. This section reviews the evolution of these methods in tackling characteristic challenges of aerial image analysis, including scale variations, densely distributed small objects, and cluttered background structures. Together, these developments establish the technical context and state-of-the-art foundations that inform the methodology proposed in this paper.

Applications of YOLO in remote sensing

The YOLO series is extensively utilized for remote sensing object detection owing to its advantageous equilibrium between velocity and precision. Subsequent investigations have concentrated on modifying these designs to meet the particular requirements of aerial imagery. A primary objective entails enhancing computational efficiency for implementation on resource-limited platforms. The Attention and Multi-scale Feature Fusion Lightweight YOLO (AMFLW-YOLO)23 combines depth-wise separable convolutions with Coordinate Attention (CA) to reduce parameters while keeping performance high. This methodology was enhanced by Parallel YOLO (Para-YOLO)24, which showed that meticulous architectural design may yield accuracy enhancements with no computing burden. Comparable efficiency factors are seen in Ship-YOLO25 and Enhanced-BiFPN-C2f YOLO (EBC-YOLO)26, both utilizing refined decoding heads and enhanced backbone architectures.

Significant endeavors have tackled the issue of multi-scale object detection. Enhanced Small-object-detection Lightweight YOLO (ESL-YOLO)27 introduced a spatial-context-guided framework to enhance information retention for small objects, whereas Multi-Scale Target Attention YOLO (MSTA-YOLO)28 presented a receptive field attention module derived from visual perception mechanisms. In addition to these basic enhancements, specialized detectors have been developed for specific remote sensing applications. Attention-Guided YOLO (AG-YOLO)29 enhanced the detection head with rotational parameters for oriented object detection, Cloud and Mist YOLO (CM-YOLO)30 devised component-decoupling strategies to address weather degradation, and application-specific models have been developed for vehicle detection31,32, landslide identification28,33, and agricultural monitoring34.

In addition to these detector-specific improvements, multi-scale receptive field modeling has also been widely explored in vision tasks. Representative architectures, such as Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP)35,36, aggregate contextual information through parallel atrous convolutions with different dilation rates, while the Receptive Field Block (RFB)37 further simulates the diversity of human visual receptive fields using heterogeneous multi-branch convolutional paths. Although these designs are effective for enlarging receptive fields, they were primarily developed for general semantic representation and are not specifically optimized for remote sensing small object detection, a domain where preserving local fine-grained details is as important as capturing broader contextual cues.

Recent research has investigated the incorporation of scene-level comprehension. SCENE-YOLO4 integrated scene supervision and transformer-based feature aggregation to represent spatial relationships across instances, utilizing the organized characteristics of remote sensing. Although these varied methodologies have propelled the field forward, they frequently tackle certain issues in isolation. This has generated an opportunity for more cohesive architectures that can concurrently sustain efficiency while addressing the diverse requirements of real-world remote sensing applications.

Attention mechanism

Attention mechanisms have gained significance in computer vision due to their capacity to improve feature representation via selective focus. A comprehensive review38 generated a significant taxonomy for comprehending various attention formulations. Initial applications in remote sensing concentrated on feature recalibration, with Coordinate Attention23 acquiring positional information across channels and the Residual Channel-Attention (RCA) network39 utilizing excitation mechanisms to highlight pertinent features. The Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) and its variants40 exhibited enhancements via sequential spatial and channel attention mechanisms.

Subsequent advancements focused on the amalgamation of various attention modalities. The Synergistic Attention Module (SAM)41 proposes a joint modeling of channel and spatial affinities to alleviate potential biases in distinct processing pathways. The Global and Local Attention Mechanism (GAL)42 combines detailed local analysis with detailed contextual processing, which improves the representation of small targets. The emergence of transformer designs brought self-attention mechanisms that can capture long-range relationships. Attention-attended transformer (AAFormer)43 enhanced this paradigm by filtering irrelevant context, whereas cross-attention has also been successfully applied in related tasks such as change detection44 and image fusion45.

Recent works further demonstrate that attention-based and multi-scale representation learning has broad applicability across different vision problems. For example, Dual-Branch Frequency-Spatial Joint Perception Cross-Modality Network (FSCMF)46 explores dual-branch frequency-spatial joint perception for visible-infrared image fusion, showing the effectiveness of combining complementary feature spaces. In remote sensing change detection, Spatio-Temporal Wavelet Attention Aggregation Network (STWANet)47 and Temporal-Spatial Multiscale Graph Attention (TSMGA)48 indicate that wavelet attention aggregation, graph attention, and temporal-spatial multi-scale modeling can substantially improve the representation of subtle structural changes. In addition, adversarial spatio-temporal learning for video deblurring49 and motion-appearance decoupling for event cameras50 further highlight the importance of disentangling dynamic information and preserving fine-grained details under complex imaging conditions. These studies collectively enrich the broader background of feature interaction, contextual modeling, and attention design, which also motivates our exploration of multi-scale feature enhancement for remote sensing object detection.

Recent work shows a trend toward incorporating domain knowledge into attention design. The Attribute-guided feature fusion network with knowledge-inspired attention mechanism (AFNKA network)51 used physical data attributes to guide attention in multi-modal fusion, and other studies have demonstrated the adaptability of attention mechanisms to specialized tasks including image inpainting52 and change detection53. However, the integration of attention modules into detection frameworks often introduces computational complexity, highlighting the need for designs that carefully balance performance gains with practical efficiency requirements.

Proposed methods

Overview

MSFE-YOLO improves upon YOLOv8s through a coordinated backbone-neck-head enhancement strategy for remote sensing small object detection. The four modules are designed to address different but tightly coupled limitations in the detection pipeline rather than to perform overlapping functions. Specifically, AMCA is introduced in the backbone to enhance context-aware feature discrimination at the representation stage, especially under cluttered backgrounds and weak target responses. SCAF is applied in the neck to alleviate semantic inconsistency during cross-scale fusion, which cannot be sufficiently addressed by backbone attention alone. APU further complements SCAF by preserving fine-grained structural details during feature upsampling, preventing the loss of weak small-object cues caused by fixed interpolation. Finally, MDRF is embedded in the detection head to strengthen prediction-stage contextual modeling through multi-scale receptive field expansion, which is functionally different from the representation refinement in AMCA and the fusion enhancement in SCAF or APU. As illustrated in Fig. 1, these four modules are integrated into the backbone, neck, and detection heads, respectively, forming a synergistic and non-overlapping enhancement framework: AMCA improves feature encoding, SCAF improves cross-scale interaction, APU improves detail-preserving reconstruction, and MDRF improves prediction-stage context aggregation. This design reduces redundancy and forms a unified feature enhancement pipeline for remote sensing small object detection.Fig. 1The overall architecture of the proposed MSFE-YOLO. Our framework enhances YOLOv8s by integrating four novel modules for multi-scale feature enhancement in remote sensing small object detection. This synergistic design forms a unified multi-scale feature enhancement framework that achieves robust small object detection in complex aerial scenes while maintaining high inference efficiency.

Adaptive Point Upsampler (APU)

The APU is designed to replace conventional interpolation-based upsampling operations in the YOLOv8s neck architecture, specifically addressing the limitations of fixed upsampling kernels for small object detection in remote sensing. Traditional bilinear or bicubic interpolation methods suffer from blurring artifacts and loss of fine-grained details, which are particularly detrimental for small objects that already possess limited discriminative features. APU introduces a content-aware upsampling paradigm that dynamically adjusts sampling positions based on local feature characteristics, enabling precise reconstruction of fine details critical for small target detection.

APU provides several distinct benefits for small object detection in remote sensing scenarios. The content-aware reconstruction effectively preserves fine-grained details critical for distinguishing small targets from heterogeneous backgrounds. The dynamic sampling strategy accommodates irregular shapes and structures commonly encountered in remote sensing objects, overcoming the limitations of fixed interpolation kernels. Computational efficiency is maintained through optimized operations and group-wise processing, achieving inference speeds approaching bilinear interpolation while providing superior upsampling quality. The Selective Cross-Attention Fusion (SCAF) module is designed to replace conventional feature fusion strategies in the YOLOv8s neck architecture, specifically addressing the semantic gap problem in multi-scale feature fusion for small object detection in remote sensing. Traditional feature pyramid networks often rely on simple concatenation or addition operations, which fail to effectively capture the complex cross-scale dependencies required for accurate small target localization. Multi-scale Dilated Receptive Field (MDRF)

The Multi-scale Dilated Receptive Field (MDRF) module is designed to enhance the detection head of the YOLOv8s architecture by simulating the hierarchical receptive field structure of human visual systems. Inspired by the neurobiological mechanisms in visual cortex where population receptive fields (pRFs) exhibit varying sizes and eccentricities across different retinotopic maps, MDRF addresses the critical limitation of fixed receptive fields in standard convolutional layers, which are insufficient for capturing the multi-scale characteristics inherent in remote sensing scenes with significant object scale variations.

As illustrated in Fig. 5, the MDRF module employs a multi-branch architecture with parallel dilated convolution pathways to simultaneously capture contextual information at multiple scales. The MDRF module achieves effective receptive field expansion through multi-branch dilated convolutions with carefully selected dilation configurations, enabling the detection head to capture contextual information at different spatial ranges.

Compared with established architectures such as ASPP and RFB, MDRF is designed with several task-specific considerations for remote sensing small object detection. First, unlike ASPP, which mainly relies on parallel atrous convolutions to enlarge contextual coverage, MDRF adopts progressively constructed branches with different depths and dilation settings, so that local structural cues are preserved before larger receptive fields are introduced. This property is important for small objects, whose discriminative details can be easily overwhelmed by excessive context aggregation. Second, compared with RFB, which emphasizes general-purpose receptive field simulation through heterogeneous branches, MDRF is embedded directly into the detection head to enhance prediction-stage features at different detection scales. Third, MDRF employs a scaled residual fusion strategy to balance the original fine-grained features and the enriched multi-scale contextual features, which helps stabilize optimization and reduces the risk of diluting weak small-object responses.

For remote sensing small object detection, these design choices provide several important benefits. The progressive receptive field expansion improves robustness to scale variation, while the residual fusion mechanism preserves local detail cues that are essential for precise localization and classification of small targets in cluttered backgrounds. Therefore, the novelty of MDRF lies not merely in the use of dilated convolutions, but in tailoring the receptive field enhancement strategy to the specific requirements of remote sensing small object detection.

Experimental results demonstrate that integrating MDRF into the detection head significantly improves the model’s robustness to scale variations and enhances detection performance for small objects in complex remote sensing scenarios, while introducing minimal computational overhead.

Experimental results

Environment

The experiments were performed on a workstation with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3080 GPU, 12GB of VRAM, an 8-core CPU, and 16GB of system RAM. The implementation utilizes the PyTorch 2.4.0 framework with CUDA 12.1 and is founded on Ultralytics 8.3.160.

For fairness, the baseline model and the proposed MSFE-YOLO were trained and evaluated under the same experimental protocol. All input images were resized and padded to a fixed resolution of 640 × 640 pixels. Both models were trained from scratch for 200 epochs using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with Nesterov momentum (μ = 0.937). The optimization was regularized with a weight decay of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$5 \times 10^{-4}$$\end{document}, and an initial learning rate was set to 0.01 and followed a linear decay schedule. The batch size was fixed at 8, and early stopping was applied using the same validation criterion and patience setting. These unified settings ensure that the performance difference between the baseline and MSFE-YOLO is attributable to the architectural changes.

To enhance model robustness and generalization, a comprehensive data augmentation pipeline was applied during training. This included Mosaic augmentation, random horizontal flipping (probability = 0.5), and photometric distortions in HSV color space (hue, saturation, and value gains of 0.015, 0.7, and 0.4, respectively). Geometric augmentations such as translation (up to 10%) and scaling (up to 50%) were also incorporated.

We monitored the validation mean Average Precision (mAP) for early stopping with a patience of 20 epochs. Training typically converged within the stipulated 200 epochs, with the final model weights selected based on the best validation performance.

To assess the efficacy of the proposed MSFE-YOLO model, experiments are conducted on two widely used remote sensing object detection benchmarks, namely RSOD and VEDAI. These datasets provide representative evaluation scenarios with considerable variation in object scale, background complexity, and category distribution, and therefore serve as suitable initial benchmarks for validating the effectiveness of the proposed framework. However, it is acknowledged that both RSOD and VEDAI are relatively limited in scale compared with larger and more challenging datasets such as DOTA and DIOR. Therefore, the current experiments mainly verify the effectiveness of MSFE-YOLO on small-scale benchmark settings, while a more comprehensive evaluation of generalization on larger datasets remains important future work. Evaluation metrics

To evaluate the performance of the proposed MSFE-YOLO, we employ a comprehensive set of standard metrics.

The Intersection over Union (IoU) is used as the fundamental criterion for determining the accuracy of predicted bounding boxes, calculated as the ratio of the intersection area to the union area between the predicted box and the ground truth. In our experiments, an IoU threshold of 0.5 is used to define a correct detection.

The mAP serves as the primary evaluation metric, providing a comprehensive assessment of model performance across all categories. It is computed as the average of Average Precision (AP) values across all classes, where AP represents the area under the precision-recall curve at different recall levels:20\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} mAP = \frac{1}{N} \sum _{i=1}^{N} AP_i = \frac{1}{N} \sum _{i=1}^{N} \int _0^1 P_i(R_i)dR_i \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where N represents the number of classes, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$AP_i$$\end{document} denotes the Average Precision for class i. Precision (P) and Recall (R) are defined as follows:21\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} P = \frac{TP}{TP + FP} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}22\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} R = \frac{TP}{TP + FN} \end{aligned}$$\end{document}In these equations, True Positive (TP) denotes the number of targets correctly predicted as positive by the model, False Positive (FP) represents the count of instances where the model incorrectly predicts negative instances as positive, and False Negative (FN) indicates the instances where the model mistakenly predicts positive instances as negative.

The mean Average Precision at an IoU threshold of 0.50 (mAP@50) evaluates detection performance under a moderate localization tolerance. This metric is formally defined as:23\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \text {mAP@50} = \frac{1}{|\mathscr {C}|} \sum _{c \in \mathscr {C}} \text {AP}_c (\text {IoU} = 0.50), \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {C}$$\end{document} represents the set of target categories and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\text {AP}_c$$\end{document} denotes the average precision for class c, computed from its precision-recall curve. In remote sensing object detection, accurately identifying the presence of a target is often prioritized over delineating its exact boundaries, especially when targets are small or depicted from an overhead perspective. Therefore, mAP@50 serves as a practical and relevant metric for many aerial and satellite image analysis tasks.

For a comprehensive assessment of both classification and localization performance, the mean Average Precision averaged over IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95 in steps of 0.05 (mAP@50–95) is employed. It is calculated as:24\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \text {mAP@50-95} = \frac{1}{10} \sum _{k=0}^{9} \text {mAP@}(0.50 + 0.05 \times k). \end{aligned}$$\end{document}This metric imposes a series of increasingly strict bounding box alignment criteria. It is particularly valuable in remote sensing scenarios that demand high localization precision, such as discriminating between densely packed instances, precisely delineating irregularly shaped structures, or quantifying the severity of registration errors. Consequently, mAP@50–95 provides a rigorous and holistic benchmark for evaluating detectors designed for high-stakes geospatial applications.

Ablation experimental results

As shown in Table 5, the progressive improvement obtained by incrementally introducing AMCA, MDRF, APU, and SCAF provides empirical support for the necessity of each module. AMCA first improves the baseline by enhancing backbone feature discrimination, raising mAP@50 from 91.6% to 93.2%, an increase of 1.6 percentage points. This indicates that more reliable context-aware representation is beneficial before subsequent fusion and prediction refinement. After AMCA is introduced, MDRF brings the largest additional gain, increasing mAP@50 to 96.0%, which represents a 2.8 percentage point improvement over the previous stage. This suggests that prediction-stage contextual enhancement remains necessary even when backbone features have already been improved. The subsequent improvement brought by APU raises mAP@50–95 from 64.9% to 66.8%, a gain of 1.9 percentage points. This shows that semantic enhancement alone is insufficient if fine-grained spatial details are degraded during upsampling. Finally, SCAF further increases mAP@50–95 from 66.8% to 69.6%, a further gain of 2.8 percentage points, especially at the high IoU threshold. This indicates that accurate localization still depends on explicit semantic alignment across scales. The integrated system combining all four modules ultimately achieves 98.4% in mAP@50 and 69.6% in mAP@50–95, representing total gains of 6.8 and 6.7 percentage points over the baseline, respectively. Conclusion

We introduced MSFE-YOLO, an innovative object detection framework for remote sensing small object detection. Our approach addresses inadequate feature representation, background noise, and scale variation through the coordinated design of AMCA, SCAF, APU, and MDRF. Experimental results on the RSOD and VEDAI benchmarks demonstrate that MSFE-YOLO achieves strong performance improvements over the baseline and competitive results against existing methods. Ablation studies further confirm the effectiveness and complementarity of the proposed components. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that the current evaluation is conducted on two relatively small datasets, which limits the assessment of large-scale generalization. In future work, we will further validate the proposed framework on larger and more challenging remote sensing benchmarks, such as DOTA and DIOR, and explore its extension to multimodal data, self-supervised pre-training, and deployment-oriented applications on satellite and UAV platforms.

Allogeneic stem cell transplantation with prior pembrolizumab and bendamustine therapy induce complete metabolic response in relapsed/refractory classical hodgkin lymphoma with relapse after nivolumab
Authors: Ugo Giordano, Agnieszka Kuś, Karolina Kędziora, Mateusz Sawicki, Zbigniew Podgajny, Monika Mordak-Domagała, Zuzanna Dybko, Jarosław Dybko
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Relapsed/refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma (r/r cHL) poses a therapeutic challenge, particularly after failure of checkpoint inhibitors (CPIs) and brentuximab vedotin. We report a case of a heavily pretreated young male patient with r/r cHL who achieved complete metabolic response (CMR) after allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HCT) following pembrolizumab and bendamustine therapy, currently in CMR at 19 months post-transplantation. Despite prior resistance to nivolumab, pembrolizumab combined with bendamustine contributed to disease control in the pre-transplant period. This case highlights allo-HCT’s curative potential even in partial responders and suggests that pre-transplant CPI therapy may enhance the graft-versus-lymphoma effect, improving post-transplantation outcomes in r/r cHL. Another explanation for the therapeutic success may be that the employment of a CPI has likely contributed to sensitization to chemotherapy and bendamustine potentially lowering the occurrence of GvHD due to T-cell compartment impairment, with the need for large scale prospective trials to demonstrate our findings.

Introduction

In the last two decades significant advancements in the treatment of cHL have led to an improvement of outcomes for patients suffering from r/r cHL. There have been numerous phase 2 single-arm studies which investigated the effectiveness and led to the approval by the Federal Drug Administration (FDA) of novel therapies in such as brentuximab-vedotin (BV), a CD30-directed antibody-drug conjugate approved in 2011, and CPIs such as nivolumab and pembrolizumab, approved by the FDA in 2016 and 2017 [1–3]. For individuals who do not respond to regimens based on BV and CPIs, there is no standard of care. Allo-HCT can offer sustained response rates in a subset of medically fit patients with r/r cHL who merely exhibit partial response to CPI therapy, necessitate multiple lines of therapy in order to achieve remission, or relapse after auto-HCT [4–8]. The potentially curative effect of allo-HCT may be driven by the anti-lymphoma graft-versus-leukaemia (GvL) effect, which is induced by alloreactive donor T-cells [9]. Based on some preclinical studies, the inclusion of CPI-based regimens in the peri-transplant period is supposed to increase the GvL effect [10, 11]. Also, bendamustine is regarded as one of the recommended drugs to include in the treatment of r/r cHL [12] for disease refractory to at least 3 prior lines according to the latest NCCN guidelines (NCCN (2025). The efficacy of its combination with a PD−1 inhibitor has been proven by numerous studies, as the latter may impact sensitization to chemotherapy in a r/r cHL setting [13–15]. Other therapeutic approaches, including CD30-directed CAR-T therapy, are also being explored in r/r cHL [16]. We hereby report a case of a young male individual suffering from highly refractory cHL, who in spite of multiple lines of therapy including BV and nivolumab, achieved partial response with pembrolizumab and bendamustine, and eventually CMR after allo-HCT.

Case presentation

A 14-year old male patient was diagnosed with nodular sclerosis (NS) cHL bulky disease with B symptoms and extranodal site involvement including both lungs, the thyroid gland and pleura (initial stage IVBE according to the Lugano criteria [17]) back in April 2018. During the first year since the time of diagnosis (April 2018 - May 2019) he received two lines of therapy which consisted of:I line: two cycles of OEPA until May 2018, and three cycles of COPDAC until September 2018 (protocol Euro-Net-PHL-C1-Interim) - disease progression by fluorodeoxyglucose PET-CT (FDG-PET-CT) with DS 5,II line: two cycles of IGEV until February 2019 - DS 4 in FDG-PET-CT, and two cycles of DHAP until May 2019.

Only partial metabolic response (PMR) was achieved eventually, and the decision was to proceed to auto-HCT with pre-transplantation BV-AVD III line regimen. After merely two cycles of BV-AVD the patient was able to achieve CMR and successfully underwent auto-HCT in October 2019. Despite the administration of 7 cycles of consolidation therapy based on BV until June 2020, disease progression in FDG-PET-CT was observed. Subsequently, a salvage IV line chemo-immunotherapy was applied between October 2020 and August 2022 including two cycles of R-CHOP (with disease progression observed in FDG-PET-CT) and twenty-two cycles of nivolumab, once again achieving CMR. Partial response/stable disease was observed after the administration of the 10th cycle of nivolumab, with the achievement of CMR after the 13th dose. Treatment was discontinued after 22 cycles and active observation of the disease due to physician discretion, having taken into account the stability of CMR and adverse effects including immunodeficiency secondary to immunotherapy. Despite achieving CMR post-nivolumab, logistical and donor-matching constraints delayed allo-HCT. The use of R-CHOP in this patient was based on the atypical immunophenotype including CD20 +, CD30 +, PAX5 +/-, CD3 -, CD45 -, and BCL2 expression, which suggested partial B-cell lineage traits. While not standard in cHL, such cases have occasionally been approached with B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphomas (B-NHL) protocols in various treatment lines according to the current NCCN guidelines [18]. The morphology and immunoprofile were not consistent with nodular lymphocyte predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL).

At that time, the patient was referred to our immunodeficiency outpatient clinic for treatment continuation. Unfortunately, 9 months after treatment cessation, a follow-up FDG-PET-CT revealed disease relapse in June 2023 (Fig. 1A). Hence, the patient was admitted to our ward for treatment continuation. The results of laboratory analyses of samples collected at the time of admission revealed mild anemia, elevated C-reactive protein and D-dimer concentration, low concentration of total protein, humoral and cellular immunity deficiency were observed with low levels of IgG and IgM immunoglobulins that required intravenous immunoglobulin infusion. Physical examination revealed an enlarged cervical lymph node. The result of the histopathological analysis of the biopsy of two lymph nodes from group 1R (supraclavicular lymph nodes) confirmed NS cHL relapse, with a large number of Hodgkin Reed/Sternberg (HRS) cells manifesting a high expression of BCL2, which was most likely the cause of resistance to multiple lines of therapy. The HRS cell immunophenotype was positive for: CD30, PAX5, CD20, and BCL2. Considering the clinical course and the longest remission period achieved after nivolumab anti-PD1 therapy, the patient was qualified for allo-HCT with pre-transplantation 5th line chemo-immunotherapy based on pembrolizumab (200 mg i.v. on day 1) and bendamustine (90 mg/m² on days 1 and 2) which were administered every 21 days for 5 cycles between July 2023 and October 2023. Treatment tolerance was acceptable, and the only observed adverse effect was neutropenia which responded adequately to granulocyte-colony stimulating factor infusions. The first FDG-PET-CT after the second cycle was classified as a Deauville score of five with signs of partial response, leading to treatment continuation (Fig. 1A vs. B). FDG-PET-CT imaging performed after the fourth cycle did demonstrate the regression of the pulmonary lesions with, however, the arising of a not previously present area of increased FDG uptake in the vertebral body of Th12 (Fig. 1C). Hence, the post-cycle 4 FDG-PET-CT response was classified as indeterminate under LYRIC criteria [19] due to the appearance of new FDG-avid lesions during immunotherapy and, according to DS [17], as disease progression. Despite the presence of a new lesion in Th12, we opted for transplantation because of the observed reduction of metabolic activity of the previously present lesions. After 35 days since the administration of the fifth cycle we proceeded to allo-HCT from an HLA 10/10 matched-unrelated donor (MUD) with peripheral blood stem cells and RIC conditioning based on 2 Gy total body irradiation (TBI) (day−6) and 30 mg/m2 fludarabine (Flu) (day − 6 to −2) with PTCy-based GvHD prophylaxis (50 mg/kg cyclophosphamide on days + 3 and + 4, tacrolimus i.v. 1 mg/24 h, and mycophenolate mofetil 7,5 mg/kg every 12 h). Full donor chimerism was achieved at day + 91 post-transplant. In the post-transplantation period we observed skin aGvHD grade 3 (MAGIC II [20]) at day + 43 post-transplant (maculopapular, itchy rash on the skin of the whole body) and liver GvHD grade II [21] at day + 122 post-transplant which might have been a sign of overlap syndrome as per NIH 2014 criteria [22], which required intravenous methylprednisolone (2 mg/kg) with gradual dose reduction. Infectious complications also occurred, with a Clostridioides difficile infection requiring fidaxomicin and BKV reactivation effectively managed with cidofovir (5 mg/kg i.v. every week up to 4 doses). Three post-allo-HCT follow-up FDG-PET-CT examinations revealed disease CMR after allo-HCT (Fig. 2A−C). Currently, at 19 months post-allo-HCT, the patient maintains full donor chimerism, CMR by FDG-PET-CT (based on DS) with no signs of cGvHD. A summary of all of the lines of therapy is presented in Fig. 3.

Discussion

There are three main scopes of our paper. First, we wanted to demonstrate that even with disease progression or indeterminate response according to DS and LYRIC criteria respectively, allo-HCT remains a potentially curative approach in r/r cHL. Second, our observations suggest that the incorporation of pre-transplant CPI therapy may increase allo-HCT’s success. Additionally, we sought to underscore that in cases of highly refractory disease and resistance to numerous modern lines of therapy including BV and nivolumab, the use of a different anti-PD1 formulation such as pembrolizumab may still be regarded as an effective therapeutic option.

The depth of response before allo-HCT significantly influences its outcomes, with better results observed in patients achieving CMR before transplantation. It has been demonstrated that achieving CMR or at least partial remission (PR) compared to stable disease (SD) or progressive disease (PD) at the time of transplantation resulted in a statistically significant improvement in overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) [23, 24]. Despite an unfavorable disease status prior to allo-HCT, our patient achieved CMR post-allo-HCT. While studies support GvL amplification by CPIs [10, 11], the obtained response may have also resulted from bendamustine causing a potential reduction of the risk of a/cGVHD via the depletion of the T-cell compartment [25] and pembrolizumab-induced tumor volume reduction prior to allo-HCT rather than enhancement of GvL alone. Another factor potentially explaining the success of transplantation might be the positive impact of PD−1 inhibitors on tumor cell sensitization to chemotherapy, as proven by previous studies [13–15].

A major disadvantage of using pre-transplant CPI therapy could be the propensity for the development of fatal hyperacute GvHD, most likely related to the prolonged half-life of CPIs and the associated activation of cytokines and CD4 + helper T cells that may occur in the post-transplantation period [2, 26]. Despite opting for PTCy as GvHD prophylaxis, an approach proven effective to mitigate the aforementioned CD4 + helper T cell activation [27, 28], we observed skin aGvHD grade 3 at day + 43 and liver GvHD grade II [21] at day + 122, which resolved after appropriate treatment. Given the late arising of liver GvHD, we could classify these symptoms as overlap syndrome according to the 2014 NIH criteria [22]. Also, early (< 30 days) allo-HCT since the last CPI infusion has been demonstrated to significantly enhance the risk of development of severe aGvHD (grades III-IV) [29], hence our choice to proceed to allo-HCT after 35 days since the infusion of the 5th cycle of pembrolizumab and bendamustine. No cGvHD signs were observed in the follow-up period.

In summary, the treatment of patients affected by r/r cHL after multiple lines of therapy and transplant procedures poses numerous challenges considering that there is no standard of care, and the prognosis is generally strongly unfavorable. Our case report and the results of other studies suggest that in spite of lack of CMR prior to transplantation, the administration of CPIs could potentially enhance the GvL effect of allo-HCT translating into improved survival outcomes. Lastly, despite resistance to multiple lines of therapy including BV and nivolumab, our observations suggest the efficacy in heavily pretreated patients of the combination of an anti-PD1 formulation likely contributing to sensitization to chemotherapy and bendamustine potentially lowering the occurrence of GvHD due to T-cell compartment impairment. However, these findings should be tested prospectively on a larger scale.

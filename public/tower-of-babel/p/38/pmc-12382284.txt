The global patent landscape of the drugs for treating Alzheimer’s disease from 2014–2023
Authors: Yan Xu, Yuan Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAlzheimer’s Disease (AD) is a complex neurodegenerative disorder lacking a definitive cure, spurring global efforts to develop effective therapeutic agents. Patent data can provide critical insights into research investments, innovation trends, and market dynamics.ObjectiveThis study offers a comprehensive ten-year overview (2014–2023) of granted AD drug patents, focusing on patent families (A ‘patent family’ refers to a set of patents or patent applications in different jurisdictions claiming priority to the same original invention. In contrast, a ‘patent’ typically denotes a single jurisdiction-specific grant. When we aggregate data by families, we seek to avoid double-counting the same invention filed in multiple regions and to measure an invention’s overall global protection strategy.) to consolidate international filings for the same invention. By highlighting top assignees, citation impact, and molecular targets, we aim to clarify current R&D directions and identify emerging opportunities in AD drug development.MethodsWe extracted 2,088 AD drug patent families from the IncoPat database using International Patent Classification (IPC) codes (A61K, A61P) and keyword searches (“Alzheimer” or “Alzheimer’s disease”). We excluded irrelevant items (e.g., test reagents) and concentrated on legally granted patents to ensure reliable and enforceable innovations. Geographic layouts, applicant rankings, and citation analyses were conducted to reveal innovation hotspots and high-impact patents.ResultAnnual granted patent volumes remained stable until a slight decline after 2021. While large pharmaceutical companies (e.g., Hoffmann-La Roche) dominated in terms of total patent families, several highly cited patents originated from smaller or less prolific applicants, reflecting unique or foundational breakthroughs. The United States led in patent counts, yet notable growth was seen in China and other emerging markets. Small-molecule compounds (including isoindoline derivatives) and monoclonal antibodies were predominant targets, though gene therapy and neuroinflammation-focused patents also gained traction.ConclusionsOur findings highlight how AD patent landscapes integrate multiple scientific pathways and commercial strategies. The discrepancy between top patentees and top-cited inventions underscores the multifaceted nature of innovation in AD therapeutics. These insights, combined with broader scientific context, may guide policy decisions, R&D collaborations, and future research priorities in tackling this urgent healthcare challenge.

Introduction

According to an estimate of World Health Organization (WHO), the world’s 65 + years population is expected to double by the year 2030. This may become a major problem because aging population is prone to contract Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) that already is seeing a yearly increase [1]. AD is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder and the most common form of dementia. According to a conservative estimate by the World Health Organization (WHO), there are currently 55 million people worldwide living with dementia. Given that AD is the most common form of dementia, the number of AD patients is also increasing. It is projected to reach 139 million by the year 2050 [2]. Other than being a notable public health issue, AD causes huge economic and emotional burden to families and society at large [3]. Clinically, AD is characterized by cognitive decline, memory impairment, and behavioral changes, which progressively worsen over time [4]. Despite extensive research, no definitive cure exists, underscoring the urgent need for effective therapeutic interventions [5, 6].

At the mechanistic level, AD pathology is thought to involve several intertwined processes: (1) abnormal deposition of amyloid-β (Aβ) protein, (2) hyperphosphorylation of Tau leading to neurofibrillary tangles, (3) chronic neuroinflammation, (4) mitochondrial dysfunction, and (5) aberrant synaptic transmission [7]. This multifactorial nature contributes to high clinical trial failure rates—reportedly up to 99% for some AD drug candidates—and imposes lengthy, resource-intensive R&D cycles [8]. Nevertheless, that has not deterred research institutions and drug companies to further their research and attempts in developing AD drugs with sufficient clinical efficacy. To elaborate, 187 clinical trials (Phase 1, 2, & 3) were conducted prior to 2023 covering 141 AD treatments. In these trials, there were 55 Phase 3 trials (36 drugs), 99 Phase 2 trials (87 drugs), and 33 Phase 1 trials (31 drugs) [9].

In parallel with clinical research, patent activities play a central role in shaping AD drug innovation. A patent grants inventors or assignees the exclusive right to prevent others from making, using, or selling an invention within a specific jurisdiction [10]. For pharmaceutical companies, protecting intellectual property (IP) is critical not only for recouping R&D costs but also for establishing a competitive edge [11]. Researchers and policymakers frequently use patent data as a window into technological innovation, tracking emerging trends, dominant players, and potential future directions in a given field [12]. Although patent datasets can be large and complex, focusing on granted patents offers certain advantages: these have already passed examination and thus reflect inventions deemed novel, non-obvious, and industrially applicable [13].

Moreover, a growing body of literature has used patent analytics to evaluate AD drug pipelines and R&D strategies—particularly in the United States [14–17]. For instance, Navarro-Gomez et al. [14] examined US patent documents from 2010 to 2020, emphasizing small molecules and antibody-based therapies. Meanwhile, Liu et al. [16]analyzed US patents from 1978 to 2017 from the perspective of technological flows, emphasizing research priorities. In addition, Xu et al. [15] provided earlier insights into AD-related patent citations, pointed out that AD patents often cluster around known enzymatic targets (e.g., cholinesterases, secretases). while Fernandez-Bolanos et al. [17] focused solely on patent texts regarding the application of butyrylcholinesterase inhibitors in the treatment of AD from 2018 to 2022. However, these studies often concentrate on individual jurisdictions, selected time frames, or specific types of drugs, potentially overlooking global and more recent shifts. Additionally, not all of them distinguish carefully between single patents and patent families—collections of national filings that cover the same invention across multiple markets.

In response to these gaps, the present study aims to analyze the global patent landscape of AD drug research from 2014 to 2023. By synthesizing data on geographical distribution, major assignees, patent layouts of major assignees, core technological categories, and highly cited patents, we aim to highlight current R&D trends and emerging therapeutic approaches.

Methods

Data sources and rationale

In this study, the IncoPat database was utilized as our primary source of patent information. Maintained by Beijing Incopat Co., Ltd., IncoPat [18] aggregates patent documents from over 100 jurisdictions—including major patent offices such as the USPTO, EPO, CNIPA, and JPO—offering comprehensive and regularly updated coverage of worldwide patent literature [19, 20]. IncoPat offers robust search capabilities across various fields, including titles, abstracts, claims, IPC codes, applicants, inventors, and more [21]. Its sophisticated filtering options allow users to refine results by date ranges, jurisdictions, legal status, and citation relationships [22]. Additionally, the platform’s built-in patent family grouping function consolidates filings from multiple regions into cohesive clusters, thereby minimizing the risk of double-counting the same invention [23].

Given these features, IncoPat was well-suited to our research needs for mapping the global patent landscape of AD drugs.

Search strategy and time range

To locate AD-related drug patents, we combined International Patent Classification (IPC) codes with relevant keywords in titles, abstracts, and claims. A61K represents medical, dental and/or cosmetic preparations while A61P refers to a specific therapeutic measure of compound and/or pharmaceutical preparations. Searching through these numbers can furnish comprehensive patent information about drugs. In this regard, Navarro-Gómez et al. [14] verify that searching for the keys “Alzheimer” or “Alzheimer’s Disease”, these numbers frequently appear to be closely related to the above search topics. Based on this, this study employed the following search strategy: Granted Announcement Date 2014.1.1–2023.12.31; IPC classification number A61K or A61P; Search for the keywords “Alzheimer” or “Alzheimer’s disease” in the claims and abstract (the search formula was: ((TIAB-DWPI = (Alzheimer) OR TIAB-DWPI = (Alzheimer’s disease)) AND (IPC = (A61K) OR IPC = (A61P))) AND (GRANT-DATE = [20140101 TO 20231231])). The search results were subsequently merged for patents within the same group, and underwent screening of authorized invention patents as well as valid patents. IncoPat automatically groups related filings under a shared priority number or common parent application. These groupings were adopted to avoid double-counting the same underlying invention. Additionally, only those patent families whose status was listed as “granted” in at least one jurisdiction were included. Patents with “pending” “rejected” or “withdrawn” legal statuses were removed, ensuring that our dataset focused exclusively on legally enforceable intellectual property. Ultimately, the formula included only granted patent families with a grant date between January 1, 2014, and December 31, 2023.

Data validity screening and processing

First, the text content of 2,213 patent families was obtained for analysis. Subsequently, AD test methods, test reagents and irrelevant patent families (n = 125) were deleted from the analysis. So, 2,088 patent families with reference value were screened.

Variable definitions and coding

Geographical distribution

In this study, it refers to the country of the applicants, not the location where the patent was filed.

Major applicants

We classified applicants (assignees) by the total number of granted patent families they owned within our dataset. Applicants with more than ten granted patent families—plus at least one patent in the top 10% citation tier—were labeled “major applicants”.

Highly cited patents

To capture patents with particularly high technological relevance, we calculated the forward citation count (i.e., how many subsequent patents cited each document). We defined “highly cited” as ranking within the top 10% of the citation distribution in our dataset.

Drug type and molecular targets

To link each patent family to therapeutic categories (e.g., small-molecule compounds, monoclonal antibodies, gene therapies, traditional medicines). We also noted the molecular target or pathway (e.g., amyloid-beta, Tau, neuroinflammation) when clearly stated in the patent document.

Data analysis and visualization

Excel and Origin were utilized for data cleaning, descriptive statistics, and chart generation. Analytical steps included patent grant trends, geographic distribution (Using IncoPat’s built-in country filters, we identified which country or region where each patent family applicant is located, then summed unique family counts by country or region.), major applicants, patent Jurisdiction of top applicants, techincal categories of top applicants, drug type, and highly cited patents for AD drug patents.

Limitations

While this approach provides a robust snapshot of legally valid AD-related patents, several constraints must be noted: (a) Exclusion of Pending Applications: By focusing solely on granted patents, we exclude early-stage innovations that are still under review. This may result in an underestimation of emerging technologies that have not yet been fully validated but hold significant potential. (b) Search String Dependence: Using only “Alzheimer” or “Alzheimer’s disease” and specific IPC codes may miss patents addressing AD indirectly (e.g., “cognitive impairment”). (c) Citation Bias: Forward citation counts may not uniformly reflect clinical importance; some high-value patents could accrue fewer citations if competitors avoid referencing them. (d) Data Lag: Patent systems vary in grant times, meaning some 2022–2023 inventions might not yet be reflected.

Despite these limitations, the consolidated perspective on enforceable patents offers a reliable basis to identify principal R&D directions, geographic concentration, and emerging molecular targets in AD drug innovation.

Results and Discussion

Annual patent grant trends

A granted patent generally indicates that the claimed invention has passed the patent office’s examination for novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability, signifying a legally enforceable form of intellectual property [24]. Therefore, the number of patents granted can demonstrate the effectiveness of drug research and development in this field [25]. The results of the present study, Fig. 1 illustrates the trends of patent grant of global AD drug patents from 2014 to 2023.Fig. 1Number of patent families and the granted years

Number of patent families and the granted years

Patent grants exhibited a relatively consistent pattern—ranging around 215 families per year—from 2014 through 2021, with a temporary peak of ~ 250 in 2017. A slight downturn followed, showing only 169 grants in 2022 and 166 in 2023. While such stability might imply sustained R&D commitment, the recent dip suggests changing investment patterns, possibly tied to delayed patent examinations or reallocated funding priorities. Firstly, the patent examination process often lasts 1–3 years or longer, meaning some inventions filed after 2020 may not be reflected. Secondly, the COVID-19 pandemic and global economic uncertainties have potentially redirected investments toward urgent therapeutic areas, thereby slowing AD-focused efforts. Otherwise, AD’s high clinical trial failure rate and complex pathology can deter prolonged R&D at scale. Whereas some prior U.S.-centric analyses observed stable patent activity pre-2014 [15], our global data extend these observations to multiple jurisdictions, highlighting an international phenomenon of moderate downtrend from 2022–2023. Although the total grant numbers dipped, subsequent Sects. (3.2–3.8) explore how the quality and strategic focus of recent patents may still drive meaningful innovation, illustrating that even if overall volumes fluctuate, key technological breakthroughs can emerge.

Drug type analysis

In further analysis, the abstracts and claims of the total number of retrieved patent families (n = 2,088) were analysed to determine the types of drugs and treatment methods that were protected by the global AD drug patents from 2014 to 2023. These texts indicated the types of the drugs that were patented (see Fig. 6).Fig. 6Number of patent families by technology type, 2014–2023

Number of patent families by technology type, 2014–2023

Figure 6 shows the significant types of AD drugs and treatment methods. It also indicates that small-molecule compound drugs, with 1,344 patent families, that account for approximately 65% of the total number of grants, remain the most significant. In this type of drugs, isoindolines, donepezil, and butyrylcholinesterase inhibitors had the higher frequency of occurrence. Isoindoline and isoindoline-1,3-dione are potent pharmacophores found in natural and unnatural products. Isoindoline-1,3-dione contains O-phenylenediamine in its molecular structure. The two carbonyl groups can simultaneously promote hydrogen bonds with the catalytic active site and peripheral anionic sites. It is a new acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, which can inhibit the formation of β-amyloid fibrils as well as acetylcholinesterase hydrolytic activity on acetylcholine. Isoindoline-1,3-dione also has neuroprotective activity [32].

Donepezil, as an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, is currently a widely used AD drug [33]. Inhibiting the activity of butyrylcholinesterase (BuChE) in the brain can increase the concentration of acetylcholine (AchE) in the brain while reducing beta amyloid load. In late-stage Alzheimer’s Disease, AchE activity in the brain decreases sharply, while BuChE activity may remain relatively stable. Furthermore, BuChE inhibitors have longer duration of action and higher [34]. Therefore, BuChE inhibitors may become the potential therapeutic agents in treating AD.

The drug type that ranked second is natural products, with 193 patent families, involving natural product compounds, which are developed into AD drugs. The structural complexity and diverse activities of natural products make them a significant source of new lead compounds in drug R&D. Studies have confirmed that various natural products such as phenylpropanoids, flavonoids, terpenes, alkaloids, and their derivatives, can improve the pathological characteristics [35].

Antibody drugs ranked third in the list. Compared with the small-molecule compounds, antibody drugs have strong specificity for the target proteins, without interfering with other enzymes or receptors hence reducing the risk of adverse reactions and side effects [36]. In past few years, antibodies have become the best-seller drugs in the AD treatment pharmaceutical market. Currently, there are two anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies, aducanumab and lecanemab approved by Food and Drug Administration (FDA) [37]. This approval has encouraged several institutions and pharmaceutical companies to catalyse their R&D in the direction of antibody drugs for AD treatment. In the past few decades, around 169 patent families for antibody AD drugs have been authorized.

Apart from the above AD drugs and treatment methods, gene therapy technology, genetic engineering drugs, peptide drugs, and Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) have also acquired a significant number of patent grants.

While small molecules have historically been the mainstay, the growing patent activity for biologics and novel genetic or herbal solutions points to a shifting R&D horizon aimed at disease-modifying therapies.

Patent families according to therapeutic target

The distribution of patents according to therapeutic target is shown in Fig. 7.Fig. 7Distribution of patent families according to therapeutic target

Distribution of patent families according to therapeutic target

A notable subset of the examined patents centers on the amyloid-beta (Aβ) hypothesis, with 236 (11.3%) documents. These include small-molecule inhibitors of β- or γ-secretase, monoclonal antibodies directed at Aβ oligomers, and approaches to modify Aβ clearance via immunological or enzymatic processes [38–40]. Despite controversy surrounding the amyloid hypothesis, this patent activity indicates that researchers and companies are still committed to modulating Aβ, which aligns with the patent analysis results in United States between 2010 and 2020 by N. Navarro-Gómez [14].

Furthermore, Tau protein (109 items, approximately 5.2%) has become an increasingly prominent target, particularly in the wake of several amyloid-focused clinical disappointments. Patents pertaining to Tau often involve monoclonal antibodies that bind to phosphorylated or aggregated Tau, small molecules that modulate Tau phosphorylation pathways, or vaccine-based approaches aimed at eliciting immune clearance of pathological Tau [41–43]. These filings reflect growing consensus on Tau’s role in synaptic dysfunction and neuronal degeneration.

Additionally, some patents address mitochondrial dysfunction (27 items, approximately 1.3%), cholinergic (27 items, approximately 1.3%), oxidative stress (21 items, approximately 1.0%), neuroinflammatory pathways (21 items, approximately 1.0%), and smaller clusters related to metabolic factors linked to AD. Although the number of these applications is not as high as those targeting Aβ or Tau, these filings are noteworthy for their potential to address an emerging pillar of AD pathology (e.g., the role of inflammasomes and chronic immune activation in disease progression).

By mapping patents to their underlying molecular targets, we see a shift toward polypharmacology. Such multi-target approaches acknowledge the disease’s complexity and align with next-generation AD research that emphasizes synergy across multiple pathological axes.

Highly cited patents

Highly cited patents are usually highly representative of and active in the core technology in its field [44]. Therefore, we aim to analyze the retrieved patent literature to identify highly cited patents in AD treatment drugs, thereby highlighting the core technologies related to AD drug development mentioned in the patent documents. Conclusion

This study provided a comprehensive overview of the global patent landscape for AD drugs from 2014 to 2023, focusing on granted patent families to ensure the reliability and enforceability of the innovations analyzed. Our findings indicate that, despite relatively stable annual grant rates until 2021, there has been a noticeable decrease in new AD patent grants in 2022–2023—possibly reflecting pandemic-related shifts in research priorities, economic uncertainties, or intrinsic scientific challenges in AD drug development.

By examining major applicants, we observed that large pharmaceutical companies (e.g., Hoffmann-La Roche, Janssen Pharmaceutica, Novartis) maintain extensive patent portfolios, primarily targeting established mechanisms such as amyloid-β and Tau. Yet, highly cited patents are not necessarily owned by these top filers; smaller or specialized biotechs often secure breakthrough patents with considerable influence on subsequent research. This discrepancy underscores that portfolio size does not always equate to individual patent impact and highlights opportunities for emerging players to drive pioneering innovations.

In terms of drug types, small-molecule compounds remain predominant, reflecting well-established development pathways and manufacturing advantages. However, antibody therapies, peptide-based drugs, and even gene therapy approaches have gained ground, aligning with a broader shift toward potential disease-modifying treatments. A small but growing subset of patents focuses on traditional and plant-derived medicines, especially in regions like China, suggesting that multifactorial strategies—including immune modulation, neuroregeneration, and synergistic herbal formulations—are increasingly explored in the AD pipeline.

Geographically, the United States still leads in the number of granted AD drug patent families, though China and other jurisdictions continue to expand their footprints, reflecting internationalized R&D efforts. Major assignees employ varied patent layout strategies across both established and emerging markets, emphasizing regions such as Mexico, which may offer growing demand and a favorable IP environment.

Despite these insights, certain limitations must be acknowledged: (1) Restricting the dataset to granted patents excludes potential early-stage inventions awaiting examination; (2) Patent citations, while indicative of technological relevance, do not guarantee successful clinical or market outcomes; and (3) The complexity of AD pathophysiology means that multiple unpatented or non-pharmacological interventions remain outside the scope of this analysis.

Overall, the findings provide valuable guidance for stakeholders in academia, industry, and policymaking. Recognizing where and how AD drug innovation is unfolding helps in directing resources toward the most promising therapeutic avenues and in fostering international collaborations. As the field evolves, continued research—integrating clinical trial progress, real-world evidence, and additional data sources (e.g., pending applications)—will be essential for validating the efficacy of these patented inventions and propelling effective AD treatments to market.

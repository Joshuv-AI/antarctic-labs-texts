A spatiotemporal dataset of farmland rent aligned with farming seasons across China 2021–2025
Authors: Qi Xing, Shuiyi Zhu, Daolin Zhu, Jian Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate farmland market data plays a crucial role in decision-making for policymakers, investors and operators. We present a nationwide, parcel-level dataset of farmland rent in China, derived from eight survey waves conducted between 2021 and 2025 across 27 provinces. The dataset comprises 7,237 rigorously validated samples, covering 191 cities and 422 counties in major agricultural production regions. Each record contains georeferenced parcel attributes and detailed transaction information, including parcel size, rental price, contracting parties, contractual terms. Compared with existing sources, this dataset offers broader coverage, finer spatial resolution, and improved temporal continuity, thereby addressing long-standing limitations of farmland transfer data in China. No other publicly available dataset provides comparable scope, reliability, and detail. Its release will enhance transparency in farmland markets and enable robust analyses of spatial and temporal dynamics, cross-regional comparisons, and the effects of institutional arrangements on land transactions. The dataset provides a valuable empirical foundation for advancing research in agricultural economics, rural development, and land use policy.

Background & Summary

China, with a population exceeding 1.4 billion and limited arable land per capita1,2, faces increasing pressure to modernize agriculture and food security issues3. Rapid urbanization and sustained labor migration have exposed limits of the traditional smallholder farming system, particularly land fragmentation4, diseconomies of scale5, and declining productivity6. To address these challenges, the Chinese government has enacted the reform of “separation of three rights” since 2014, which separates land ownership, contract, and operating rights7–9. Ownership remains with the village collective, and households retain contract rights, while operating rights may be transferred to other agricultural operators for certain benefits8,9. This reform has made the farmland transfer market an important mechanism for achieving land consolidation and improving agricultural production efficiency4,9,10. By 2023, approximately 37.8% of China’s contracted farmland (39.4 million hectares) had been transferred, that enabling larger-scale, mechanizing farming, boosting productivity11,12, and diversifying household income13.

Despite these positive impacts, the decision-making process for farmland rent continues to suffer from a lack of transparency and remain inefficiency. In practice, transactions are often conducted through personal social networks rather than based on publicly referenced prices or competitive market bids14,15. This leads to substantial price distortion across comparable parcels16 and constrains the efficiency land reallocation17. The absence of a publicly accessible, nationwide dataset of parcel-level farmland rent makes it difficult to benchmark and monitor market dynamics over time and across regions. A reliable national database of farmland rent is therefore essential for improving market transparency and supporting evidence-based land governance.

Existing sources of farmland rent data are fragmented and only publicly accessible in part. Commercial online platforms (e.g., Tuliu.com) facilitate farmland rental listings and transactions, however, data quality is poor, as the information relies on user-submitted self-reports on the platform. Local property rights exchange centers—government-established, nonprofit platforms intended to standardize and publicize farmland management rights transfers—are underutilized by smallholders due to procedural complexity and administrative burdens15. As a result, records from these centers primarily capture larger, formal transfers, while many small-scale, private agreements go unrecorded15, limiting the representativeness of such data. The Farmland Assets Inventory Database maintained by the Ministry of Natural Resources18 contain few farmland rent data reported as hierarchical administrative statistics, not linked to specific parcels and not publicly available. Academic survey data (such as the China Family Panel Studies19) include modules on farmland rent, but they typically anonymize or aggregate parcel-level information, restricting their utility for granular price analysis. Taken together, these limitations leave China without a consistent, transparent benchmark for farmland rental values, highlighting the need for a comprehensive public dataset.

To address this gap, the Center for Land Policy and Law (CLPL) at China Agricultural University launched the China Farmland Rent Survey (CFRS) project in 2021. Each year, the project recruited and trained university students to conduct in-person surveys of farmland transfer transactions in their home regions during winter and summer vacations. In China, the winter off farming season and the summer farming season are the periods when farmers most often renew or formalize lease contracts and negotiate rents; accordingly, scheduling surveys during university winter and summer vacations aligns with the typical timing of contracting and price setting. The year of 2021 was selected as the baseline to ensure institutional stability, to align with the first year of China’s comprehensive Rural Revitalization and the start of the 14th Five-Year Plan (2021–2025), and to capture the first post–COVID-19 adjustment in farmland transfer markets. Between 2021 and 2025, the project completed eight survey waves across 27 provinces, producing a multi-wave, nationwide parcel-level dataset of farmland transfer transactions. The data were collected using a standardized questionnaire that systematically captured key information about each transaction, including operator characteristics, agreement terms, rental price, and administrative location information (see questionnaire design in Methods section for details).

This multi-year, nationwide survey effort has produced a unique dataset that combines longitudinal coverage with parcel-level spatial granularity, helping to fill a long-standing gap in the documentation of China’s farmland transfer market. To our knowledge, it represents the most comprehensive publicly accessible resource of its kind on parcel-level farmland rent in China. The dataset provides a new empirical foundation for research on land price formation, market efficiency, and land allocation. It also offers practical insights for agricultural entrepreneurs and local governments to analyze rent dynamics and inform decision-making. Beyond direct market applications, the data can be integrated into broader analysis — for example, to support environmental–economic accounting or to assess the ecosystem service values of agricultural land use. Publicly releasing these data will enhance transparency in China’s farmland transfer market and provide a robust empirical basis for further research and evidence-based policy development.

Methods

Questionnaire design

The questionnaire was designed to systematically collect information on farmland operating rights transfers and associated transaction price. Respondents included a broad range of agricultural operators engaged in production through farmland transfers. The questionnaire was created and distributed using the online platform (Wenjuanxing, www.sojump.com). To ensure validity and reliability, the questionnaire experienced several rounds of testing and refinement. The questionnaire incorporated skip patterns (branching logic) that routed respondents to the appropriate next item based on their answers, ensuring continuity and accuracy of data collection. The draft questionnaire was reviewed by five experts in agricultural economics and land management to assess clarity, relevance, and reliability. A pilot survey was conducted in winter 2021 to obtain feedback from agricultural operators on the questionnaire. Based on their input, revisions were made to the wording of the questions, the response options, and the overall structure of the questionnaire to better reflect the situations and cognitive preferences of agricultural operators.

Sampling strategy

The ideal frame for the dataset would be a national listing of all farmland parcels that have undergone operating rights transfers. As discussed in the Background section and supported by existing literature, Over the past two decades, China has been committed to building a market-oriented mechanism for allocating farmland-use rights and has encouraged market-based transactions through land transfers. However, there is no nationally mandated, compulsory registration system for farmland transfer transactions at the central-government level, making a comprehensive sampling frame unattainable.

To navigate this constraint, we adopted a multi-stage purposive cluster sampling approach. Primary Sampling Units (PSUs) were defined as the hometown regions of trained university students across China. This approach takes advantage of local insider access to document small-scale, private transactions that typically elude formal administrative registries. In their respective home regions, enumerators identified eligible parcels by consulting local key informants such as village cadres, cooperative leaders, and other community figures. The fieldwork was specifically designed to cover China’s major agricultural production regions, where farmland transfer activity is concentrated. This resulted in broad geographic representation, spanning 27 provinces and nine agro-ecological zones (Fig. 1). Recruitment and training of enumerators

Approximately one month before each survey wave, the project team recruited university students through a public post on the official CLPL WeChat (a widely used social media and communication platform in China) account. The post outlined survey objectives, schedule, eligibility, responsibilities, and training requirements. Eligible applicants were required to come from rural or suburban areas. After registration, all enumerators participated in an online training session via Tencent Meeting (a video-conferencing platform similar to Zoom). The training covered the meaning of each item in questionnaire, instructions for standardized completion, and ethical requirements. Continuous support and real-time feedback were provided during fieldwork through group communication channels.

Data collection tools and technologies

Following training, enumerators conducted the formal fieldwork. To ensure respondent comprehension and data accuracy, the questionnaire was individually administered by trained enumerators with on-site assistance. Enumerators accessed the questionnaire via QR code or hyperlink on the Wenjuanxing platform, and responses were uploaded automatically to secure servers. Enumerators were instructed to take photographs of transferred parcels using a mobile application that embedded timestamps and geolocation metadata. This procedure enhanced traceability and provided an additional verification layer for the dataset. The coverage of sampling locations may change over time, reflecting the distribution of enumerators’ home regions. Across waves, 1,506 trained enumerators from 477 universities conducted fieldwork locally, cumulatively spanning 27 provinces and China’s nine major agro-ecological zones (Fig. 1). This breadth encompasses diverse production systems, landscape settings and urban–rural gradients.

Data management

All completed questionnaires were synchronized in real-time through the Wenjuanxing platform, which enables export in multiple formats (XLSX, SAV, Word, PDF) for subsequent analysis. Built-in monitoring tools provided real-time progress reports, descriptive statistics, and visualized response distributions. System logs recorded completion rates, response times, and geographic distribution of survey locations. These features enabled continuous monitoring of data quality and facilitated rapid identification of anomalies, thereby enhancing the reliability and precision of the dataset.

Ethical oversight

The research protocol and the associated data-sharing plan were formally reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Center for Land Policy and Law, China Agricultural University (No. 20250701). This study was conducted in strict compliance with the principles of the 1964 WMA Declaration of Helsinki and relevant Chinese laws, regulations, and ethical guidelines. Explicit electronic informed consent for both participation and the public sharing of de-identified data was obtained from all respondents prior to survey enumeration. A standardized informed consent statement was integrated as a mandatory opening requirement in the electronic questionnaire. Respondents were fully informed of the study’s objectives, the anonymized nature of data processing, the public sharing of de-identified data and their right to withdraw at any time; access to the survey was granted only upon their active confirmation, failing which the session was automatically terminated.

Data Records

The dataset is available at Zenodo, 10.5281/zenodo.1716181523. This public release covers seven survey waves (2022_summer–2025_summer); an initial pilot wave in 2021_winter was used to test the questionnaires and is not included in the public dataset.

The dataset is stored in the Zenodo repository and contains the following files: (i) a main integrated dataset available in two equivalent formats—CSV and ESRI Shapefile with the standard sidecar files—which share the same schema, the Shapefile stores parcel-level GPS point geometry for each sample; (ii) seven wave source files in CSV format, with identical columns and a survey_wave field; (iii) bilingual data dictionaries in Microsoft Excel (.xlsx) format; and (iv) a README file in Microsoft Word (.docx) format, which provides detailed information on the dataset’s structure, version history, and other relevant details. Spatial validity: spatial validity was tested by examining whether farmland rent exhibited spatial gradients consistent with known agro-ecological and socio-economic patterns. For each calendar year from 2021 through 2025, all transactions with a rent reference year equal to that year were pooled for analysis. For 2021, rent_prev reported in the 2022_summer and 2022_winter waves were used. Global Moran’s I and local Getis–Ord Gi* statistics were used to detect spatial clustering of rent. The analysis revealed spatial clustering (see Fig. 7): rent hot spots (high-value clusters) were concentrated in peri-urban and agriculturally productive areas, while cold spots (low-value clusters) appeared in mountainous, less-developed regions. These spatial patterns align with established knowledge of farmland rental value distribution in China22,24, thereby reinforcing the dataset’s spatial validity.

Usage Notes

The dataset is primarily intended for analyzing variations in rental prices, contractual arrangements, and local market dynamics at the micro-level. To ensure valid statistical analysis, users should consider the following methodological guidelines:

Population estimates

Since inclusion probabilities cannot be precisely determined, users are advised against treating raw sample means as absolute national population estimates. However, as demonstrated in Fig. 7, the significant spatial autocorrelation and clustering of rental prices highlight the dataset’s capacity to capture underlying market heterogeneities. While a single national average may be of limited utility, land is inherently spatially fixed, the geographic locus of a transaction—and thus where a price is observed—has substantive analytical significance for interpreting land market prices and their spatial variation. Consequently, localized means provide a robust empirical basis for analyzing rent dynamics across different regions with varying agro-ecological and socio-economic characteristics.

Clustered inference

The inclusion of administrative codes for provinces, cities, and counties, along with anonymous enumerator IDs, allows researchers to compute clustered standard errors.

Causal inference and mechanism analysis

Due to the non-probability sampling design, model-based inference is recommended over simple design-based generalizations for population-level claims. The dataset’s unprecedented parcel-level granularity and longitudinal coverage from 2021 to 2025 enable the use of micro-econometric methods. By controlling time-invariant unobserved heterogeneity at the county or parcel level, researchers can effectively analyze price-formation mechanisms and the impacts of institutional arrangements within the sample.

Limitations

First, the dataset focuses exclusively on parcels that have undergone operating rights transfers and does not include information on non-rented land in the sampled regions. Second, while the survey captures detailed transaction data, it does not include the total landholdings or wealth characteristics of the land leaseholders. Finally, multiple records may exist for a single land operator if they lease different types of land, as each entry represents a distinct transaction for a specific land type. These limitations should be considered when using the dataset for broader analyses of rural land distribution or household-level decision-making.

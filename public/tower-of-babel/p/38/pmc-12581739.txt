Local large arterial perivascular adipose tissue metabolic and anatomical features are associated with hypertensive clinical outcomes: a PET/CT-based study
Authors: Hong-Yu Long, Guo-Dong Chen, Ji-Liang Liu, Kai-Wen Cheng, Wen-Jie Yu, Bai-Qing Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study investigated the relationship between anatomical and metabolic characteristics of large arterial perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT) and hypertensive clinical outcomes using positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET/CT).MethodsWe analysed pretreatment 18F-FDG PET/CT data from 150 primary lung malignancies patients (age 65 ± 11 years; 45 hypertensive patients). The hypertension status at the time of PET/CT was recorded as the clinical outcome. The deep learning tool TotalSegmentator was used to segment large arteries in PET/CT images. The segmented large arteries included the common carotid arteries, subclavian arteries, brachiocephalic trunk, aorta, iliac arteries and pulmonary veins. Then, large arterial PVAT metabolism and anatomical features were extracted. These features were categorized as localized (e.g. left iliac arterial PVAT features, and PVAT features obtained after segmenting thoracic/abdominal aortic PVAT based on vertebral anatomical landmarks) or whole-body (the total volume and SUVmedian of PVAT around the aforementioned large arteries). The relationship between PVAT features and hypertensive outcomes was explored by correlation analysis and multivariate analysis.ResultsWhole-body large arterial PVAT volume and SUVmedian showed no significant differences across hypertensive outcomes, whereas localized large arterial PVAT features showed significant differences. T11 segment aortic PVAT volume (odds ratio [OR] = 1.238 [95% CI: 1.066, 1.437]; p = 0.005) and SUV90th-percentile of left iliac arterial PVAT ([OR] = 5.051 [95% CI: 1.378, 18.510]; p = 0.015) were higher in hypertensive patients. T5 segment aortic PVAT 90th percentile HU values and SUV90th-percentile can determine antihypertensive drug sensitivity (AUC = 0.85; 95% CI:0.71–0.98). Complication rates rose with declining SUV in T6 segment aortic PVAT ([OR] = 0.007 [95% CI: 0.000, 0.450]; p = 0.019).ConclusionsThis study confirms the close relationship between the anatomical and metabolic characteristics of localized large arterial PVAT and hypertensive outcomes, and reveals its spatial enrichment pattern during disease progression.

Introduction

Perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT) is a specialized local deposit of adipose tissue surrounding blood vessels [1], which dynamically regulates vascular function and inflammation through the secretion of a number of bioactive molecules including chemokines and pro-inflammatory adipokines with bidirectional regulatory effects on cardiovascular homeostasis [2]. Recent studies have identified dysfunctional PVAT as a key factor in the development of hypertension [3,4], positioning it as a potential therapeutic target.

Among currently available clinical imaging modalities, CT is still considered to be the best choice for characterizing and visualizing PVAT [5], especially in the application of coronary PVAT. Increased coronary PVAT density as reflected by CT values correlates positively with the progression of the total coronary plaque volume, and increased coronary PVAT density is currently thought to represent an increase in vascular inflammation [6–8]. However, this positive association between fat density and vascular inflammation cannot be directly applied to the analysis of the relationship between whole-body arterial PVAT and blood pressure. On the one hand, this is because some large arteries are surrounded by brown adipose tissue (BAT) [9], which typically exhibits higher density. And studies have shown that this higher density fat can dramatically reduce arterial stiffness and atherosclerosis [10,11]. On the other hand, arteries are more widely distributed than coronary arteries, covering the entire body and having numerous branches. The extensive distribution of arteries, combined with the regional heterogeneity and variability in PVAT phenotypes themselves, poses challenges to investigating the relationship between arterial PVAT and the development of hypertension.

To thoroughly investigate the complex relationship between arterial PVAT and the development of hypertension, imaging techniques that combine wide-field scanning capability with metabolic function assessment are essential. In this context, positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET/CT) emerges as a powerful tool. Its scanning coverage includes most of the arteries throughout the body. Furthermore, PET serves as the gold standard for identifying BAT [12] and can also reflect the degree of vascular inflammation [13,14]. Meanwhile, CT from PET/CT scans enables precise localization of arterial PVAT and quantification of fat density. Although current technology still struggles to achieve batch segmentation of whole-body arteries, the deep learning segmentation model TotalSegmentator can allow automated and robust segmentation of large arteries in whole-body CT images [15]. Several clinical studies have utilized vascular structures automatically segmented by TotalSegmentator, with results confirming its feasibility in practical applications and its accurate vascular segmentation capabilities [16–18]. Through systematic analysis of the metabolic and anatomical features of whole-body large arterial PVAT, important evidence of the pathophysiological association between arterial PVAT and hypertension might be found. This will lay the foundation for subsequent research on whole-body arterial function assessment – including small arteries – and clinical translation studies.

Currently, malignant tumors constitute a significant proportion of patients undergoing PET/CT. Among these cases, primary lung malignancies are relatively common [19,20]. To maximize sample size while minimizing confounding variables, this study selected patients with primary lung malignancies who completed 18F-FDG PET/CT scans prior to treatment as the study population. Selecting such patients offers two advantages. First, primary lung malignancies exhibit strong epidemiological associations with age and tobacco use – risk factors concurrently linked to elevated hypertension prevalence [21–23]. This helps to obtain a higher proportion of positive cases in single cancer studies. Second, focusing on a single cancer type and pre-treatment PET/CT imaging data helps minimize potential confounding effects of cancer types and treatment on blood pressure, glucose metabolism and fat function [24,25].

Therefore, this study employs image processing techniques, including TotalSegmentator, to systematically analyse the anatomical and metabolic characteristics of large arterial PVAT in PET/CT imaging. The goal is to elucidate the relationship between large arterial PVAT features and hypertensive status, thereby providing novel insights and therapeutic targets for hypertension management.

Materials and methods

Study population

This single-center retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the People’s Hospital of Liaoning Province, which waived the requirement for informed consent (IRB number: 2024K064). This study included patients with primary lung malignancies who underwent pre-treatment 18F-FDG PET/CT scans in our institution between January 2020 and June 2024.

Inclusion criteria: (a) Histopathologically confirmed primary lung malignancy (imaging-based diagnosis accepted if histopathological confirmation was unavailable); (b) PET/CT performed prior to antineoplastic therapies (surgery, chemotherapy or radiotherapy). Exclusion criteria: (a) concurrent or prior history of other primary malignancies; (b) incomplete PET/CT scans or poor image quality; (c) tumor involvement of large arteries and (d) history of secondary hypertension. Clinical data collection

Patient hospitalization records closest to the 18F-FDG PET/CT scan date (interval ≤ 30 days) were retrieved via the hospital’s electronic medical record system. Data collected included gender, age, body mass index (BMI), systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), fasting blood glucose levels and clinical diagnoses. Clinical diagnoses encompassed essential hypertension and hypertension-related complications, such as cerebrovascular disease, heart failure, coronary artery disease, chronic renal failure and aortic dissection. Data on antihypertensive medication use among hypertensive patients were also collected, encompassing both treatment regimens and therapeutic outcomes. Antihypertensive medication regimens included monotherapy, dual therapy and triple therapy.

Effective antihypertensive drug therapy was defined as, in hypertensive patients receiving long-term antihypertensive medication under specialist guidance, keeping normalized blood pressure (SBP <140 mmHg and DBP <90 mmHg) and remaining free from major adverse cardiovascular events throughout the 12-month period prior to PET/CT imaging. In this study, long-term treatment was defined as treatment lasting longer than 12 months. In addition, we recorded the time interval between patients being diagnosed with hypertension and undergoing PET/CT scans.

18F-FDG PET/CT

Patients fasted for 6 h with blood glucose less than or equal to 11.1 mmol/L followed by intravenous injection of 3.7 MBq/kg 18F-FDG through the cubital vein. The radiotracer was obtained from a commercial source (DC AMS PHARMA, Shenyang). The time interval between tracer injection and imaging was approximately 60 min.18F-FDG-PET/CT examinations were performed on a hybrid PET/CT scanner (BIOGRAPH series; SIEMENS Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany).

Standardized uptake value (SUV) was used to quantify FDG uptake of adipose as a surrogate of its glucose uptake activity and was calculated as the ratio of a voxel’s radioactivity concentration [kBq/mL] and the decay-corrected and body weight adjusted injected radiotracer activity [kBq/g] and reported as g/mL. We also obtained average outdoor temperatures in Shenyang, for the months of scans from the China meteorological administration.

Segmentation of region of interest (ROI) for large arterial vessels and PVAT

This study employed the Python-based open-source deep learning tool TotalSegmentator, built upon the PyTorch framework and nnUNet architecture, to perform automated segmentation of CT images from PET/CT scans for precise extraction of large arteries (including left/right common carotid arteries, left/right subclavian arteries, brachiocephalic trunk, aorta and left/right iliac arteries) and pulmonary veins.

Due to the absence of automated aortic segmentation functionality in TotalSegmentator, two radiologist (reader 1 and reader 2) with over 10 years of experience manually divided the aorta into the ascending aorta and the descending aorta. The specific procedure involved selecting the highest point on the multiplanar reconstruction (MPR) image of the aorta as the demarcation point between the ascending and descending aorta. Subsequently, a layer-by-layer segmentation of the aorta was performed in the transverse plane along a direction perpendicular to the long axis of the aortic arch. After 1 month, 20 patients were randomly selected from the data set. And then reader 1 resegmented the aortas of these patients using this segmentation method. Following the delineation process, we assessed the intra-observer and inter-observer reproducibility using the volume of the ascending aorta obtained from segmentation. The correlation coefficients exceeded 0.90 in both. And then we selected segmentation results of reader 1 for further analysis. In the Python environment, the descending aorta was subdivided into vertebral segments based on the distance between the superior border of one vertebral body and the superior border of the next vertebral body (e.g. the segment from the T12 vertebral superior border to L1 vertebral superior border was defined as the T12 aortic segment) (Figure 2(a)).

Diagram shows workflow for delineating the segmentation and feature extraction steps for large arterial PVAT. (a-b) Segmentation of region of interest for arterial vessels and PVAT. (c) Adipose feature selection. PVAT perivascular adipose tissue.

Using CT images as input data, a dilation algorithm was applied in the Python environment to select an annular region within a 5-mm radial distance from the outer vascular wall. CT values were constrained to the range of −300 to −10 Hounsfield unit (HU) [26]. The segmented adipose regions were further subdivided according to the aforementioned vascular segmentation scheme (Figure 2(b)).

PVAT feature selection

Based on the aforementioned ROI delineations, original first-order features and shape features of PAT were extracted from both CT and PET images using the Pyradiomics toolkit (Figure 2(c)).

During CT feature screening, the minimum and maximum HU values were excluded. This exclusion was implemented because the automated fat segmentation algorithm strictly constrained HU values to the predefined range of −300 to −10, thereby rendering these extremes mere reflections of preset boundary constraints rather than actual fat density variations. For PET features, SUVmax was excluded due to significant confounding effects from perivascular tumors or urinary retention in the ureters, which could elevate SUVmax values. Global large arterial PVAT features included whole-body large arterial PVAT volume (sum of PVAT of left/right common carotid arteries, left/right subclavian arteries, brachiocephalic trunk, aorta, left/right iliac arteries and pulmonary veins) and the SUVmedian of whole-body large arterial PVAT.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using R software, version 3.6.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and Python, version 3.7.12 (Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, Delaware, USA)). Continuous data are expressed as the median and quartile range. Categorical data are expressed as counts and proportions. Intergroup comparisons of continuous variables were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test, while categorical variables were analyzed with Fisher’s exact test or the chi-square test. The Pearson correlation coefficient was used to assess the association between whole-body large arterial PVAT volume and BMI. Spearman’s rank correlation evaluated other variables. Significant variables from the univariate analysis (p < 0.05) underwent multivariate forward selection (likelihood ratio) to identify the predictors of hypertensive outcomes. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using the area under the curve (AUC).

Results

Large arterial PVAT distribution

Among the 150 included patients, whole-body large arterial PVAT volume demonstrated a moderate correlation with BMI (r = 0.647, p < 0.001; Figure 3(a)). Whole-body large arterial PVAT SUVmedian correlated weakly with BMI (r = 0.236, p = 0.003) but not with age or outdoor temperatures (Figure 3(b)). Females exhibited higher whole-body large arterial PVAT SUVmedian (median SUVmedian: 1.39 vs. 1.20, p = 0.008). SUVmedian of thoracic aortic PVAT showed no significant associations with BMI, age or outdoor temperature. However, the SUVmedian was significantly associated with sex, being higher in females (median SUVmedian: 1.32 vs. 1.14, p = 0.001; Figure 4(a)).

Association between hypertension and large arterial PVAT characteristics

A total of 150 patients who underwent PET/CT were included, among whom 45 (30%) were diagnosed with primary hypertension. The median time interval between hypertension diagnosis and PET/CT examination was 7 years (interquartile range: 4–12 years). Among these, 34 cases (75.6%) had been diagnosed with hypertension more than 5 years prior to the PET/CT examination. Significant differences in age were observed between hypertensive and non-hypertensive groups, while no significant differences outdoor temperature (p = 0.189) or BMI were noted (Table 1).

Significant differences in large arterial PVAT features were observed between two groups (Table 3). When these differential features were stratified by anatomical location, the T11 aortic segment exhibited the highest number of significant PAT characteristics (Figure 5(a)), including the 90th percentile, median and mean HU values, as well as the fat volume in this region. However, both whole-body large arterial PVAT volume (p = 0.068) and its SUVmedian (p = 0.233) showed no significant between-group differences.

Multivariable analysis of these differentially significant aortic PVAT features revealed that T11 segment aortic PVAT volume (odds ratio [OR] = 1.238, 95% CI: 1.066–1.437; p = 0.005) and SUV90th-percentile of left iliac arterial PVAT (OR = 5.051, 95% CI: 1.378–18.510; p = 0.015) were independently associated with hypertension, achieving an AUC of 0.70 (95% CI: 0.61–0.79).

Association between antihypertensive medication efficacy and large arterial PVAT characteristics

Among the 45 patients diagnosed with hypertension, 33 received long-term antihypertensive medication under specialist guidance. Of these, antihypertensive therapy was effective in nine patients (27%). Among these patients with effective control, eight (88.9%) received monotherapy, and one (11.1%) received dual therapy. A total of 24 cases (72.7%) had suboptimal control of hypertension with antihypertensive medication. Among them, 21 cases (87.5%) received monotherapy, 2 cases (8.3%) received dual therapy, and 1 case (4.2%) received multiple drug therapy. Results showed no significant differences in clinical factors such as medication use (p = 1.000), age (p = 0.283) and BMI (p = 0.479) between the effective and suboptimal control groups. Both whole-body large arterial PVAT volume and its SUVmedian showed no significant between-group differences (p = 0.467 and p = 0.352, respectively).

Among arterial PVAT features, only T5 segment aortic PVAT characteristics demonstrated statistically significant intergroup differences (Table 4), including the 90th percentile HU values, SUVmin, SUVmedian and SUV90th-percentile (Figure 5(b)). In multivariable analysis of these features, T5 aortic PVAT 90th percentile HU values (OR = 0.515, 95% CI: 0.271–0.981; p = 0.043) and SUV90th-percentile (OR = 0.039, 95% CI: 0.002–0.742; p = 0.031) were retained in the diagnostic model for antihypertensive medication efficacy, achieving an AUC of 0.85 (95% CI: 0.71–0.98) with sensitivity and specificity of 0.89 and 0.67, respectively.

Association between hypertension-related complications and large arterial PVAT characteristics

Among the 45 patients diagnosed with hypertension, 14 (31.1%) had hypertension-related complications. Notably, age differed significantly between groups with and without complications (p = 0.004). In contrast, neither the whole-body large arterial PVAT volume (p = 0.624) nor its SUVmedian (p = 0.138) showed significant intergroup differences.

T5 and T6 segments aortic PVAT characteristics demonstrated significant differences between complications groups (Table 5). At the T5 segment, these included SUVmin, SUV10th percentile, SUVmean, SUVmedian and SUV90th-percentile; at T6, significant features were SUVmin, SUVmean, SUVmedian and SUV90th-percentile (Figure 5(c)). When age and these PVAT features were analyzed in multivariable analysis, only age (OR = 1.132, 95% CI: 1.027–1.248; p = 0.013) was retained, yielding an AUC of 0.77 (95% CI: 0.61–0.93).

Discussion

This study utilizes the large arterial segmentation functionality of TotalSegmentator to extract anatomical and metabolic characteristics of arterial PVAT from PET/CT images, revealing a significant association with hypertension status. And compared to BMI and whole-body large arterial PVAT metrics, localized large arterial PVAT features significantly outperformed in predicting hypertensive clinical outcomes and exhibited characteristic spatial enrichment patterns during disease progression. This finding suggests that localized PVAT features may serve as precision imaging biomarkers for tracking hypertension dynamics, with the TotalSegmentator-based arterial zoning strategy providing the methodological foundation. This strategy not only provides a spatially resolved tool to decipher vascular adipose heterogeneity but also extends the application boundaries of imaging modalities such as CT and PET. Specifically, by integrating this strategy with PET/CT, the study localized disease-related large arterial PVAT depots and then identified quantitative associations between multimodal imaging features and hypertensive clinical outcomes.

Whole-body large arterial PVAT volume strongly correlated with BMI (r = 0.647), confirming obesity-linked PVAT accumulation [27]. In contrast, its SUVmedian showed weaker BMI association (r = 0.236). And both whole-body large arterial PVAT volume and its SUVmedian showed no significant differences across hypertensive clinical outcomes. We attribute these findings to the heterogeneity and functional complexity of PVAT. This heterogeneity is highly location-dependent [1] – for instance, thoracic aortic PVAT predominantly exhibits brown adipose phenotypes, while abdominal aortic PVAT is primarily composed of white adipose tissue (WAT). Functional changes in localized PVAT, including the presence of brown adipose phenotypes, are reflected in region-specific variations of HU/SUV on imaging [28–30]. Aggregating these spatially heterogeneous signatures into whole-body metrics may obscure region-specific biological signals. This spatial averaging likely reduces statistical power to detect associations with hypertensive outcomes, explaining the nonsignificant correlations in our cohort.

To address spatial heterogeneity, we segmented whole-body large arterial PVAT and excluded SUVmax-derived metrics. This avoided interference from malignant lesions or urinary activity in the peri-arterial region, which significantly affects SUVmax. This interference also compromises BAT detection accuracy in arterial PVAT. We first analyzed thoracic aortic PVAT, a confirmed BAT depot. Its SUVmedian was significantly higher in females but showed no significant associations with BMI, age or outdoor temperature. This differs from the BAT distribution observed in cervical, supraclavicular, axillary, mediastinal, paraspinal and abdominal depots. A large-scale retrospective study using 18F-FDG PET/CT to map BAT across these six depots showed BAT was more prevalent in females, decreased with age/BMI and increased in cold temperatures [24]. The divergence between these six canonical BAT depots and thoracic aortic PVAT may be related to the selection of metabolic assessment metrics, as well as stemming from the unique composition of PVAT. Research indicates that although BAT and thoracic aortic PVAT in mice are closely related morphologically, under a standard diet, their proteomes exhibit depot-specific clustering with only a 43% overlap. This is comparable to the overlap of 44% of detected proteins between thoracic aortic PVAT with visceral WAT [31].

Using T5–L4 vertebral landmarks, we stratified thoracic (T5–T12) and abdominal (L1–L4) aortic segments. As hypothesized, this refined segmentation revealed that localized large arterial PVAT features predicted hypertensive outcomes more accurately than whole-body metrics. And this correlation presents different regions of PVAT characteristic enrichment in the hypertensive process. And this correlation exhibits different PVAT feature-enriched regions during the progression of hypertension.

In the correlation analysis between hypertension and large arterial PVAT, feature-rich regions were located in the T10–T11 and L2–L3 PVAT, especially in T11. Multivariable analysis identified T11 aortic PVAT volume and left iliac arterial PVAT SUV90th-percentile as potential imaging markers. These quantitative indicators are higher in hypertensive individuals. The adverse vascular effects of enlarged T11 aortic PVAT volume likely stem from localized perivascular WAT accumulation. The Framingham study demonstrated a moderate positive correlation between visceral fat accumulation and thoracic aortic PVAT [32]. The study also revealed a strong association between the expansion of aortic PVAT and hypertension. In contrast to increased abdominal fat accumulation, a widely recognized risk factor for hypertension [33], T11 aortic PVAT volume reflects a more specific localized PVAT accumulation, providing a more precise range for assessing vascular health. And elevated SUV values in left iliac arterial PVAT devoid of BAT may signify adipose oxidative stress and inflammatory activation [34], manifesting as adverse effects.

In the correlation analysis between antihypertensive drug response and large arterial PVAT, feature-rich regions were located in the PVAT of the mediastinal aorta (T5). Specifically, 90th percentile HU values and SUV90th-percentile of T5 aortic PVAT emerged as potent indicators for assessing drug sensitivity (AUC = 0.85), with drug resistance rates decreasing incrementally as these metrics increased. T5 segment belongs to the mediastinal aorta, which harbors BAT within its PVAT [3]. Combining the positive cardiovascular effects of elevated SUV/HU values and the presence of BAT, we suggest that elevated HU values may suggest an increased BAT proportion, as the mean HU values of BAT is higher than that of WAT [35]; accordingly, the SUV90th-percentile reflects the degree of activity of metabolically active fats in the fat depots; that is, T5 segment elevated SUV90th-percentile suggests enhanced metabolic activity/function of BAT. Thoracic aortic PVAT’s elevated BAT composition/activity may enhance antihypertensive drug efficacy. However, it was also possible that antihypertensive medications were effective in improving the PVAT metabolic health of this group.

In the correlation analysis between hypertension-related complications and large arterial PVAT, feature-rich regions were also located in the PVAT of the mediastinal aorta (T5 and T6). Complication rates rose with declining SUV in these regions. Similarly, combining its negative cardiovascular effects and the presence of BAT, the decrease in SUV in these regions most likely reflects a decrease in the BAT content or activity of thoracic aortic PVAT. This decline in BAT may result in a diminished capacity for cardiovascular protection. Multivariable analysis revealed that SUVmin of T6 aortic PVAT exhibited comparable diagnostic power for complication prevalence (AUC = 0.77) to that of the age-based model (AUC = 0.77). Advancing age is often associated with vascular sclerosis, accumulation of oxidative stress and multiple co-morbidities that may synergistically contribute to elevated blood pressure and target organ damage [36,37].

Although these PVAT characteristics may serve as potential imaging markers for primary hypertension and its clinical outcomes, it must be acknowledged that they are not yet suitable for routine clinical screening. In clinical practice, diagnosing hypertension through blood pressure measurement remains unmatched in terms of convenience and cost-effectiveness [38]. However, the large arterial PVAT characteristics revealed by PET/CT still hold significant potential for clinical application, particularly among cancer patients. Cancer survivors face an increased cardiovascular risk. With the exception of recurrent cancer, cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality in this population [39]. CVD and cancer frequently coincide in the same patient and often complicate each other. Both conditions share environmental risk factors such as obesity [40]. And PVAT is also strongly associated with hypertension [27,41,42]. Our results also confirm this. With the advancement of research, we hold promise for stratifying cardiovascular risk factors in cancer patients through large arterial PVAT characteristics. Furthermore, as modal conversion technology continues to develop, it may become possible to simultaneously evaluate both anatomical and metabolic features of PVAT using only CT images from patients [43,44].

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective observational study, our findings demonstrate associations between large arterial PVAT features and hypertensive clinical outcomes but cannot establish cause–effect relationships. Second, current technical limitations restrict the scope of analysis to large arterial PVAT, failing to encompass smaller arteries that play a significant role in total peripheral resistance. Moreover, the modest sample size stems from stringent inclusion criteria, requiring future multicenter validation. Last, individuals were not cold stimulated prior to their PET/CT scans, resulting in sub-maximal BAT activation and, subsequently, detection. However, this also provides a unique perspective on the relationship between large arterial PVAT characteristics and hypertension in basal metabolic states.

Conclusions

Compared with whole-body large arterial PVAT features, localized large arterial PVAT features are more closely related to hypertensive clinical outcomes, with spatial enrichment patterns during disease progression. T11 segment aortic PVAT volume and left iliac arterial PVAT metabolic activity may serve as hypertension imaging markers.

A Phase 2, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of OBI-858 for the Treatment of Moderate-to-Severe Glabellar Lines
Authors: Yu-Hsiu Yen, Tzu-Wen Tsau, Chih-Hung Lee, Hsin-Han Chen, Chih-Hung Lin, Tim-Mo Chen, Chi-Ming Pu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOBI-858 is a novel 760 kDa botulinum toxin type A complex produced by Clostridium botulinum, and currently under development for esthetic applications. This phase II clinical trial aimed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and optimal dose of OBI-858 for treating moderate-to-severe glabellar lines.MethodsIn this multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study, subjects received a single dose of OBI-858 (10U or 20U) or placebo for the treatment of glabellar lines. Subjects were followed for 24-week with efficacy assessed by Facial Wrinkle Scale (FWS), which grades dynamic glabellar line severity (GLS) and Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale (GAIS), along with subject-reported satisfaction. Safety assessments included treatment-emergent adverse events (AEs) and immunogenicity testing.ResultsAt week 4, 74.42% of subjects in the 20U group and 58.70% in the 10U group achieved ≥ 2-point improvement in Glabellar Line as assessed by both investigators and subjects, compared with 0% in the placebo group (p < 0.001). In the 24-week analysis, both 10U and 20U groups demonstrated a rapid onset of GLS 0 or 1 responder rates by day 3, achieving peak rates by week 2. From week 4 to week 20, the 20U group consistently exhibited superior efficacy, showing higher responder rates. Overall, both groups maintained sustained efficacy through week 24. Furthermore, GAIS and subjects reporting satisfaction scores peaked at week 2 and remained favorable in the 20U group through week 24, with nearly 60% of subjects reporting satisfaction at the study’s end. OBI-858 was well tolerated, with no treatment-related serious AE or new antidrug antibody (ADA) development observed during the study.ConclusionsOBI-858 demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with no new ADA development observed during the study, along with dose-dependent efficacy and subject satisfaction in treating glabellar lines.Trial RegistrationTaiwanClinicalTrials.tw: 1139014205.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13555-025-01561-5.Keywords: OBI-858, Botulinum toxin type A, 760 kDa, Glabellar lines, Rapid onset, Sustained efficacy

Introduction

Botulinum neurotoxins (BoNTs), produced by the anaerobic bacterium Clostridium botulinum, are regarded as the most potent biological toxins known. To date, seven distinct botulinum toxin serotypes from A to G have been identified, along with newly discovered mosaic variants such as BoNT/HA [1]. Among these, botulinum toxin type A (BoNT/A) is the most extensively studied and demonstrates the highest potency in humans. BoNT/A was originally considered a virulence factor produced by the bacterium to invade external hosts [2]. It has been used therapeutically for various neurological conditions since the late 1980s [3].

To date, four types of toxin complexes have been identified on the basis of their molecular weights: 12S (~300 kDa), 16S (~500 kDa), 19S (~900 kDa), and recently discovered 19S progenitor (~760 kDa) formulations [4–6]. Various commercial formulations differ not only in molecular size, but also in the presence of complexing proteins. Products such as onabotulinumtoxinA, prabotulinumtoxinA, and letibotulinumtoxinA represent 900 kDa complex containing hemagglutinins and nontoxic non-hemagglutinin (NTNHA) components. AbobotulinumtoxinA retains partial hemagglutinin components of 400 kDa complex. Furthermore, incobotulinumtoxinA and daxibotulinumtoxinA consist solely of the purified 150 kDa core neurotoxin, lacking accessory proteins. These structural, compositional. and efficacy differences, along with variations in bacterial strain, fermentation conditions, purification processes, excipients, and potency assays, can affect the pharmacological activity, onset of action, duration of effect, diffusion characteristics, and immunogenic potential of each product [7, 8].

Despite the widespread use of botulinum toxin for the treatment of glabellar lines, several clinical challenges remain. Most current formulations offer efficacy lasting approximately 3–4 months, necessitating frequent reinjections that may negatively impact patient adherence and satisfaction. Among available options, daxibotulinumtoxinA, abobotulinumtoxinA, or increasing the dose of existing products has demonstrated a longer duration of effect [9–12].

Building upon the results of a phase I trial in subjects with moderate-to-severe glabellar lines, which demonstrated that OBI‑858 a 760 kDa botulinum toxin complex was well tolerated, elicited rapid esthetic improvement (initial response by day 3), and provided sustained efficacy lasting 4–6 months with minimal AE (n = 36) [13], the current study aims to further evaluate the safety and efficacy of the 760 kDa complex in a larger human sample size. The goal of this study is to explore the clinical profile of this novel structural form and to provide preliminary insights into its potential differences from conventional botulinum toxin formulations.

Methods

Study Design

This was a phase II, randomized, double-blind, dose-ranging, placebo-controlled study to assess the efficacy and safety of OBI-858 in the treatment of moderate-to-severe glabellar lines (10U, 20U and placebo). The trial duration was approximately 25 weeks, including a 1-week screening period, a treatment visits for OBI-858 injection, and a 24-week follow-up period. Five centers in Taiwan participated in the study. This study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of all participating institutions (Supplement Table S1). Master approval was granted by the Chang Gung Memorial Hospital IRB (approval no.: 202400831A0), with additional approvals obtained from Cathay General Hospital (CGH-CS113009), China Medical University Hospital (CMUH113-REC1-118), and Tri-Service General Hospital (C202401020). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment, including consent for publication of any identifiable information. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (1964, as revised in 2013) and local regulatory requirements.

Participants

Men and women aged 20 years or older were eligible to enroll if they had a FWS score of ≥ 2 points at maximum frown, as assessed in person by investigators. Key exclusion criteria included having glabellar lines ≥ grade 2 at rest, underlying neuromuscular disorders (e.g., myasthenia gravis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, Lambert–Eaton syndrome), or severe systemic diseases (e.g., cardiovascular, respiratory, or endocrine) as determined by the investigator. Subjects with significant lab abnormalities, facial nerve paralysis, eyelid ptosis, or skin conditions/infections at the injection site were excluded. Prior botulinum toxin treatment within 6 months, history of botulism or hypersensitivity to toxin components, or prior vaccination against any botulinum toxin serotype were also grounds for exclusion. Use of medications affecting neuromuscular transmission (e.g., aminoglycosides, anticholinergics, muscle relaxants) within 4 weeks before screening was prohibited, except for topical/local aminoglycosides if approved by the investigator. Additional exclusions included prior cosmetic procedures in the glabella or forehead within 6 months (e.g., fillers, lasers, threads), history of facial surgery, scarring in the treatment area, or poor response to botulinum toxin A. Subjects planning additional esthetic treatments during the study, needing toxin in other areas, or who were pregnant or breastfeeding were also excluded. Women of childbearing potential required a negative pregnancy test at screening and had to agree to effective contraception for 6 months posttreatment. Finally, subjects were excluded if they had participated in another interventional clinical trial within 28 days or if the investigator considered them unsuitable for the study.

Interventions

The subjects received intramuscular injections of OBI-858 with an assigned dose at five sites of the glabellar during the treatment visit. The sites included two sites in the corrugator muscle of each eye and one site in the procerus muscle. The assigned treatment dose was based on the dose-ascending scheme and was equally divided into five 0.1-mL aliquots for five injections. Each injection site received one injection containing 1/5 of the assigned dose.

Eligible subjects were randomized using a permuted block method with a 3:3:1 ratio to one of three cohorts: OBI-858 10U, OBI-858 20U, or matching placebo. The two OBI-858 dose cohorts included 45 subjects each, and the placebo cohort included 15 subjects.

Outcome Measures

To ensure consistent assessment of wrinkle severity, investigators and subjects were trained using standardized photographic references on the basis of the FWS, which grades GLS as 0 (none), 1 (mild), 2 (moderate), and 3 (severe) (Supplementary Table S2) [14]. Photographs of each subject’s glabellar lines were captured at each visit and used to support assessments, particularly via the GAIS. Subjects also self-rated their satisfaction at each visit. At each subsequent in-person visit following the day 1 treatment visit, starting from day 4 until the end of study, subjects will self-assess their satisfaction with the treatment outcome on a 7-point scale from 1 to 7, where 1 means very dissatisfied, 2 means dissatisfied, 3 somewhat dissatisfied, 4 neutral (neither satisfied nor dissatisfied), 5 somewhat satisfied, 6 satisfied, and 7 very satisfied. Eligible subjects who provided informed consent during the 7-day screening period were randomized on day 1 to receive intramuscular injections of placebo, 10U, or 20U of OBI-858 at five sites in the glabellar region. Safety follow-up was conducted by phone on day 7, with subsequent in-person visits scheduled on days 4, 8 (phone call), 15, 29, 57, 85, 113, 141, and 169 (end of study, EOS) (Fig. 1).

The study flow diagrams of the phase II randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial. Subjects were assessed at baseline (day 0), followed by posttreatment visits on day 3 and weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, and 24

Efficacy was primarily assessed by the proportion of subjects achieving a ≥ 2-point improvement in glabellar line severity from baseline at day 29 on the basis of both investigator and subject assessments. The secondary assessed in the botulinum toxin clinical trial included multiple key measures over time. A notable percentage of subjects achieved a concurrent ≥ 2-point improvement, 0 or 1 responder in glabellar line severity at maximum frown, confirmed by subject and both investigators’ and subjects’ live assessments, respectively. Subjects also reported treatment satisfaction scores consistently throughout the study period. Additionally, a significant proportion attained a concurrent GAIS score of ≥ 1 point, as evaluated by both investigators and subjects.

Safety was monitored throughout the study by tracking AEs, serious AEs (SAEs), adverse events of special interest (AESIs), and laboratory parameters. Immunogenicity was evaluated by measuring anti-OBI-858 neutralizing antibodies at screening, days 29 and 85, and EOS. Subjects who discontinued early were encouraged to return for an EOS visit within 28 days for final safety and efficacy assessments. The total study duration per subject was 169 days.

Statistical Methods

Efficacy analyses were conducted on the modified intent-to-treat (mITT) and per-protocol (PP) populations, while safety and immunogenicity analyses were based on the safety population. All statistical tests were two-sided with a significance level of 0.05. Data were summarized by visit and treatment group. Continuous efficacy outcomes including GLS, GAIS, and subject satisfaction were analyzed descriptively (mean, SD, median, min, max). Categorical endpoints, such as the proportion of subjects with ≥ 2-point improvement in glabellar severity or GAIS ≥ 1 point, were summarized using frequencies, percentages, and exact 95% confidence intervals (Clopper–Pearson). Between-group comparisons were performed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) for continuous variables and Fisher’s exact test for categorical outcomes. Safety data, including laboratory results, were summarized descriptively, and analyzed for change from baseline using paired t-tests. AEs were coded using MedDRA and reported by system organ class and preferred term; only treatment-emergent AEs were analyzed. The incidence of neutralizing anti-OBI-858 antibodies was summarized using descriptive statistics.

Immunogenicity Assay

ADA response was also assessed in this study to evaluate the immunogenicity of OBI-858. Serum samples collected at baseline and at weeks 4, 12, and 24 from all subjects were screened for anti-OBI-858 binding antibodies using a validated electrochemiluminescence (ECL) (Meso Scale Diagnostics, Rockville, MD, USA) in a two-tier format (screening and confirmation). The study drug was used as the capture antigen, and ADA binding was detected with ECL-labelled secondary antibodies. Samples that tested positive in the screening assay were further analyzed to determine antibody titers and to confirm specificity to OBI-858. The validated method ensures reliable detection of low-level immune responses. Results from these assessments may provide important insights into the potential impact of immunogenicity on the safety and efficacy of OBI-858.

Results

Trial Subjects

A total of 108 subjects were screened and 105 subjects enrolled to include in the modified intent-to-treat (mITT) populations across three treatment groups: placebo (n = 15), 10U (n = 47), and 20U (n = 43) (Fig. 2). Overall, 100 subjects completed the study. A total of eight subjects withdrew from the study: two due to screening failure for not meeting the exclusion criteria before treatment; two due to protocol deviations at the 10U week 8 and 20U week 4 visits; one due to the use of a prohibited medication at the 10U week 16 visit; and two due to withdrawal of consent at the 10U week 20 and 20U week 8 visits.

The demographic and baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The overall mean age was 45.4 years (SD 9.82), with similar distributions across cohorts. The study population was predominantly female (82.86%), with a slightly higher female ratio in the placebo group (93.33%). At baseline, glabellar line severity at maximum frown was assessed by both investigators and subjects. According to investigator assessments, 61.90% of subjects had moderate wrinkles (grade 2) and 38.10% had severe wrinkles (grade 3). Subject self-assessments aligned with investigator findings, with 68.57% reporting moderate lines and 31.43% reporting severe lines. Efficacy

Primary Endpoint

The primary endpoint was a concurrent ≥ 2-point improvement in glabellar line severity at maximum frown at week 4. In the composite endpoint (Fig. 3A), combining both investigator and subject assessments, responder rates were 58.70% for the 10U group and 74.42% for the 20U group, compared with 0.00% in the placebo group (p < 0.001). In addition, using investigator-only assessments (Fig. 3B), the responder rates were 76.09% (10U) and 83.72% (20U), versus 6.67% with placebo (p < 0.001). Furthermore, for subject-only assessments (Fig. 3C), responder rates were 60.87% (10U) and 81.40% (20U), while the placebo group showed 0.00% response (p < 0.001). These findings demonstrate that both doses of OBI-858 are effective, with the 20U dose consistently delivering superior efficacy.

Overall OBI-858 responder rates at ≥ 2-point improvement from baseline on the composite scale of glabellar line severity at maximum frown at week 4 for the placebo, 10U, and 20U groups assessed by A both investigator and subjects (primary endpoint), B investigator only, and C subjects only (mITT population) (*p < 0.001 compared with placebo)

Secondary Endpoints

In secondary endpoint analyses, responder outcomes over 24 weeks were evaluated to provide a comprehensive assessment of treatment effects. In the GLS 0 or 1 analysis, based on investigator (Fig. 4) and subject (Fig. 5) assessments, the 10U and 20U groups demonstrated a rapid increase in responders by day 3. Responder rates exceeded 80% and 60% in both groups assessed by investigators and subjects, respectively. Peak responder rates were achieved by week 2. From week 4 to week 20, the overall trend clearly indicated that the 20U group demonstrated superior efficacy compared with the 10U group, consistently showing a higher responder rate. By week 24, sustained efficacy was maintained in both groups, although with some variation between the investigator and subject assessments. On the basis of investigator assessment, approximately 40–50% of the 10U group subjects and 30–40% of the 20U group subjects maintained a score of 0 or 1. In contrast, subject assessments indicated that approximately 50–60% of subjects in both groups maintained this response. Additionally, the GAIS results assessed by both investigators and subjects showed a similar trend between the 10U and 20U groups from day 3 through week 4, with approximately 70% of subjects perceiving improvement by day 3 and a peak response of 90% at week 2 for both doses. However, from week 4 to week 20, the 20U group demonstrated more sustained efficacy, with nearly 70% of subjects continuing to show a ≥ 1-point improvement through week 16. Both dose groups maintained close to 40% responder rates at week 24 (Fig. 6).

While both the 10U and 20U doses showed rapid onset and robust aesthetic improvement, the 20U dose was associated with greater durability of effect, particularly for higher efficacy outcomes.

Treatment Satisfaction

According to the data on subject-reported treatment satisfaction scores, more than 50% of participants in both the 10U and 20U groups reported being “satisfied” or “very satisfied” with the treatment throughout the 24-week clinical trial period (Fig. 7). Consistent with trends observed in the responder rate and GAIS analyses, nearly 70% of subjects expressed satisfaction as early as day 3, with both dose groups reaching peak satisfaction at week 2. However, beginning at week 8, the 20U group demonstrated superior overall satisfaction rates compared with the 10U group. This trend persisted through week 24, during which nearly 60% of subjects in the 20U group remained satisfied with the treatment.

Both 10U and 20U doses achieved rapid onset of treatment satisfaction, but the 20U dose provided more sustained and consistently higher satisfaction over time, supporting its potential for longer-lasting subject-perceived benefits.

Safety

A total of 50 subjects (47.62%) experienced AE, including 10 (66.67%) in the placebo group, 21 (44.68%) in the 10U group, and 19 (44.19%) in the 20U group (Table 2). No potentially life-threatening events or deaths were reported. Of these, 43 subjects experienced non-treatment-related AEs, while 7 subjects had treatment-related AEs (TEAEs). Regarding the causality of treatment-related AEs, two events (1.90%) were considered definitely related to treatment: one case of mild pain in the placebo group and one case of mild eyelid ptosis in the 20U group. One event (2.33%) was considered probably related to treatment, involving mild eyelid ptosis in the 20U group. Four events (3.81%) were considered possibly related, including three mild cases with diplopia, eyelid ptosis, and pain in the 10U group, and one mild case of eyelid ptosis in the 20U group. All TEAEs occurred within the first month and resolved within 2 months. SAEs occurred in two subjects (1.90%) in the 20U group: one bile duct stone and one ligament sprain. These SAEs were not deemed treatment related. Overall, OBI-858 demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with no dose-dependent increase in the frequency or severity of AEs. The most common treatment-related AEs were mild eyelid ptosis and localized injection site pain, consistent with known effects of botulinum toxin products. Notably, headache, a commonly reported AE, was not observed in this study.

Immunogenicity

In evaluating ADA responses to botulinum toxin, only 1 subject in 20U group was ADA-positive at baseline (screening), while 104 subjects were negative. This trend remained consistent at subsequent timepoints: at 4, 12, and 24 weeks, with this subject showing ADA positivity and the rest staying ADA-negative throughout the study period. These findings suggest that no new ADA development was observed within the 24-week follow-up after a single treatment with OBI-858. However, given the relatively short observation period and single-exposure design, the study was not powered to capture delayed or cumulative immune responses that may arise with repeated dosing.

Discussion

This phase II study demonstrates that OBI-858, a 760 kDa botulinum toxin type A, offers clinical benefits with rapid onset and prolonged duration in the treatment of glabellar lines. Both 10U and 20U doses resulted in statistically significant improvements compared with placebo, with the 20U group showing superior and more sustained efficacy from high peak to week 24. These findings support that the 20U dose provides clinically meaningful esthetic outcomes and high subject satisfaction, with onset as early as day 3 and continued effect for up to 24 weeks, features that align with or potentially exceed the clinical profiles reported for other botulinum toxin type A products [15–17]. Furthermore, OBI-858 demonstrated a favorable safety and immunogenicity profile, with no dose-related increase in AEs and no new treatment-emergent ADA observed.

While direct head-to-head comparisons were not performed in this study, the overall performance of OBI-858 at 20U appears to be generally consistent with the reported clinical profiles of other botulinum toxin products that have emphasized extended duration of response. For instance, studies of onabotulinumtoxinA at higher doses (40U to 80U) have shown a duration of response approaching 24 weeks in some subjects, with peak improvement around week 4 and declining effect thereafter [10]. Similarly, prabotulinumtoxinA-xvfs at 40U has demonstrated a median duration of aesthetic benefit up to 24 weeks in a phase II trial, though typical efficacy peaked between weeks 4 and 8 [11], and abobotulinumtoxinA, in the latest clinical trials, also demonstrated rapid onset of effect, duration of efficacy lasting up to 6 months, and high long-lasting levels of satisfaction across multiple esthetic indications [12]. Additionally, daxibotulinumtoxinA, which is designed with a stabilizing peptide to prolong its effect, has shown a median duration of approximately 24 weeks in large phase III trials (SAKURA 1 and 2) [9]. The performance of OBI-858 20U in this study, in terms of responder rates and subject-reported satisfaction, appears to align with these findings, suggesting comparable clinical utility at a potentially lower unit dose.

From a safety standpoint, OBI-858 was well tolerated, with an overall AE profile comparable with placebo. While higher doses or efficacy of botulinum toxin products are typically associated with an increased risk of AEs [18], in this study, the incidence of AEs did not increase in a dose-dependent manner, and the majority were mild to moderate. Only two subjects experienced severe AEs, neither of which were considered treatment related. Importantly, there were no cases of anaphylaxis, systemic toxicity, or other severe complications often associated with botulinum toxin misuse. AESIs, such as eyelid ptosis and pain, occurred in a small number of participants and were consistent with known risks of botulinum toxin injections. Notably, headache, a commonly reported AE, was not observed in this study [19]. The immunogenicity profile of OBI-858 appeared acceptable within the 24-week study period. Only one subject was ADA-positive at baseline and remained positive throughout the study, with no new ADA development observed in either treatment group. While this observation is consistent with limited ADA formation after a single administration, the study was not designed to capture delayed or cumulative immune responses, and the 24-week follow-up may be insufficient to fully assess immunogenicity. Immune responses to large protein therapeutics are known to occur, particularly after repeated or high-dose exposures [20]. Given the increasing use of esthetic botulinum toxin products in younger populations and overextended treatment durations, monitoring immunogenicity remains important for long-term clinical utility and product sustainability [21].

The clinical efficacy observed with OBI-858 may be influenced by multiple formulation-related factors. As a 760 kDa botulinum toxin complex, OBI-858 differs in molecular size and inactive protein content compared with other available products. While a direct mechanistic link between molecular weight and clinical performance has not been fully established, prior studies have suggested that variations in complex size, manufacturing processes, and accessory protein content may impact pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties, such as diffusion, receptor binding, and duration of effect [17].

Limitations of this study include the relatively short follow-up period (24 weeks) and the single-dose design. While sufficient to assess early efficacy and durability, longer-term effects, repeated dosing, and potential cumulative safety or immunogenicity effects remain to be fully characterized. Additionally, the study population was limited. A larger, longer-term study with repeated dosing is ongoing to further evaluate long-term safety, immunogenicity trends, efficacy sustainability, and broader clinical applicability.

Conclusions

This study demonstrated that OBI-858 was well tolerated and showed efficacy at both 10U and 20U doses. Compared with 10U, the 20U dose exhibited a more rapid onset of action, sustained efficacy through 24 weeks, and higher treatment satisfaction in subjects with moderate-to-severe glabellar lines. The product demonstrated a favorable safety profile with no new ADA development, supporting its continued clinical development as a promising botulinum toxin formulation.

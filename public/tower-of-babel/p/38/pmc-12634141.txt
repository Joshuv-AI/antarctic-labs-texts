Comment on Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2–Low Metastatic Breast Cancer Treatment Costs and Cost-Effectiveness
Authors: Jiang Wu, Dongxu Ma, Zeyu Xing
Published: 2025

To the Editor:

Dickerson et al1 performed cost-effectiveness analysis comparing antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) sequences in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)–low metastatic breast cancer. While advancing value-based care discourse, two methodological aspects warrant refinement for clinical applicability:

1. The progression-free survival (PFS) assumption for sacituzumab govitecan (SG) after trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) requires recalibration. The model uses a base case of median PFS of 4.07 months, 10% superior to chemotherapy. Real-world evidence (A3 trial; n = 204) shows that SG's median PFS post–T-DXd is 3.4 months, below chemotherapy's 3.7 months.2 This 18% discrepancy critically affects the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio for T-DXd→SG ($2.2 million/quality-adjusted life-year). Applying A3 data, this sequence becomes clearly non–cost-effective.

2. National cost projections incorporated HER2-ultralow patients despite exclusion from efficacy modeling. While Destiny-Breast06 survival data are immature, costs assume equivalence with HER2-low disease.1 Clinicopathologic analyses reveal distinct biological behaviors: HER2-ultralow tumors show lower tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte density and reduced ADC binding affinity versus HER2-low,3 potentially overestimating chemotherapy savings in ultralow subgroups.

A rare case of squamous cell carcinoma originating from esophageal papilloma
Authors: Elena De Cristofaro, Jérôme Rivory, Tanguy Fenouil, Mathieu Pioche
Published: 2024

A 45‐year‐old woman, with no previous diseases or risk factors, underwent upper gastrointestinal endoscopy before sleeve gastrectomy surgery for obesity. A 12 mm flat lesion that looked like a squamous papilloma under white light examination was detected in the esophagus (15 cm from the dental arch) (Figure 1a). However, a narrow band imaging evaluation with magnified endoscopy revealed that the intrapapillary capillary loops in the center of the lesion appeared unusually dilated and thickened (Figure 1b). Endoscopic submucosal dissection was performed to ensure R0 resection. Histopathology revealed a carcinoma in situ arising from a papillomatous lesion. The same lesion seen at low power magnification exhibited lateral features of papilloma with typical koïlocytes brutally transitioning to a carcinoma in situ (Figure 1c) with atypia and increased number of mitoses.

Esophageal lesion suggestive of a squamous papilloma under white light examination (a). The same lesion seen at narrow binding imaging evaluation with magnified endoscopy shows that the intrapapillary capillary loops in the center of the lesion appeared unusually dilated and thickened (b). Histological analysis revealed typical koïlocytes brutally transitioning to a carcinoma in situ (c).

Esophageal papilloma prevalence is low, ranging from 0.01% to 0.45% in reported series. These lesions are generally considered benign. Consequently, routine endoscopic treatment has not been recommended.

This case highlights the rare malignant potential of esophageal papilloma, as previously documented in other locations (ear nose throat, uterine cervix). Human papilloma virus (HPV) was detected in this lesion with subtype x, suggesting a possible role for HPV in esophageal papilloma transformation. Unlike previous studies, this case demonstrates that papilloma can transform into cancers, and resection should be considered to avoid future evolution. Careful examination with image‐enhanced endoscopy could be valuable in identifying unusual features suggestive of malignant transformation.

Performance of the multi-U-style structure based flow field for polymer electrolyte membrane fuel cell
Authors: Wenjie Qi, Xin Chen, Zhi Gang Zhang, Shuaishuai Ge, Huan Wang, Ruxin Deng, Zilin Liu, Jiying Tuo, ShengChang Guo, Junjie Cheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The design of the reactant gas flow field structure in bipolar plates significantly influences the performance of proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs). In this study, we introduced four innovative U-shaped flow field designs, namely: In-Out Multi-U, Out-In Multi-U, Distro In-Out Multi-U, and Distro Out-In Multi-U. To investigate the impact of these various flow fields on PEMFC performance, we conducted computational fluid dynamics (CFD) numerical simulations, validated through model experiments. Our results indicate that the Distro Out-In Multi-U flow field offers notable advantages compared to the conventional parallel flow field (CPFF) and conventional serpentine flow field (CSFF). These benefits include reduced inlet and outlet pressures, lower liquid water content, more uniform liquid water distribution, and a more even current density distribution. Furthermore, the Distro Out-In Multi-U design demonstrates improved efficiency, consuming less H2 (91.9%) than the CSFF while achieving a higher net power density output (10.1%). As a result, for the same power output, the Distro Out-In Multi-U utilizes only 83.5% of the H2 consumed by the CSFF. In summary, the U-shaped structured flow field exhibits superior output performance, enhanced energy efficiency, and improved resistance to flooding. These findings suggest that the U-shaped flow field design holds significant potential as a reactive flow field for PEMFCs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74257-z.

Introduction

As the depletion and overuse of traditional fossil fuels have led to a serious energy crisis and environmental degradation, the exploration of new renewable and clean energy sources has become imperative. Hydrogen stands out as a promising clean and efficient renewable fuel with significant potential1–3. PEMFC can convert the chemical energy of hydrogen into electrical energy, making it an appealing solution for energy needs. With its fast start-up time, low operating temperature, zero emissions, and high power density, PEMFC offers numerous advantages that position it as a viable and sustainable energy source4–8. While PEMFC offers numerous advantages, its widespread commercialization still faces considerable challenges. Drawbacks such as low performance, limited durability, and high cost currently hinder its broader application and acceptance in the market9–15. PEMFC are composed of multiple monolithic cells stacked together, each containing a bipolar plate (BPP), gas diffusion layer (GDL), catalyst layer (CL), and proton-exchange membrane (PEM)16. The bipolar plate is a key component of the PEMFC, accounting for 60–80% of the mass, 30–45% of the cost17,18, and over 80% of the total volume of the PEMFC stack19. The design of the BPP flow field plays a pivotal role in determining the heat transfer efficiency and overall performance of the fuel cell. An optimized flow field design has the potential to enhance hydrogen and oxygen mass transfer, improve liquid water removal, and facilitate efficient heat dissipation, thereby significantly boosting the fuel cell’s operational efficiency and durability20.

An inadequate design of the reactive gas flow field can lead to uneven current density and temperature distribution, which can have detrimental effects on the performance of the PEMFC. Poor water removal performance can result in water flooding, which hinders active gas diffusion and reduces the efficiency of the fuel cell. Additionally, excessively high local temperatures can cause membrane drying, which negatively impacts mass transfer and reduces the lifetime and output performance of the PEMFC21. To enhance the performance of fuel cells, numerous studies on flow field designs have been recently undertaken, resulting in the emergence of serpentine and parallel flow fields as the foremost preferred choices22. Spernjak et al.23 conducted a comparative study analyzing the performance characteristics of the CPFF, CSFF, and the interdigitated flow field. The study revealed that while the CSFF showed a stable power output, lower water content, and higher ultimate current density, it also suffered from significant pressure drops within its channels. These pressure drops require increased pump power in the PEMFC system, potentially impacting the cell’s sealing integrity and overall cost-efficiency. The interdigitated flow field, on the other hand, demonstrated a current density similar to that of the CSFF, thanks to improved convection beneath its ribs. However, it had a higher liquid water content compared to the CSFF. Meanwhile, the CPFF stood out for its ability to minimize parasitic power consumption, primarily due to the reduced pressure drop in its gas channels. This feature represents a significant advantage in terms of efficiency and power utilization.

The cross-sectional structure of flow channels holds equal importance for PEMFC performance as the overall shape of the flow field. Many researchers have delved into the impact of rib width, cross-sectional width, and channel depth on the output power of PEMFC24–26. Ozdemir et al.24 conducted a study on the performance variation of a serpentine flow field, evaluating channel-to-rib ratios of 0.5, 1, and 2. Their comprehensive analysis led to the conclusion that the PEMFC achieved its maximum current density and demonstrated improved water removal specifically when the channel-to-rib ratio was 2. Carcadea et al.25 conducted a comprehensive study to assess the influence of channel width and depth on the output power of PEMFCs within a multi-channel serpentine flow field. Their findings revealed that narrowing the channel width and increasing its depth enhances convective flow, thereby facilitating more efficient removal of liquid water and resulting in a higher current density. Likewise, Kaplan et al.26 developed a single-phase 3D model and discovered that decreasing both the channel width and depth leads to an increased gas flow rate, subsequently boosting current density. However, this adjustment also leads to a higher pressure drop. Although these studies primarily concentrated on rectangular cross-sectional shapes, it is worth noting that the shape of the channel cross-section itself significantly impacts PEMFC performance by altering gas flow dynamics27. Abdallah et al.28 compared triangular, rectangular, and trapezoidal cross-sections and observed that triangular channels resulted in an increased gas flow rate. This enhancement facilitated more effective liquid water removal, ultimately leading to improved PEMFC output performance. Furthermore, Mohammedi et al.29 employed a 3D CFD model to investigate the impact of 30 distinct cross-sectional shapes on PEMFC performance. Their findings revealed that trapezoidal funnel-shaped cross-sections delivered optimal performance. In contrast, rectangular channels demonstrated the smallest pressure drop, whereas triangular channels exhibited the largest. Nevertheless, it was observed that the cross-sectional shape had a marginal influence on the performance of cells utilizing the CSFF.

In addition to optimizing flow field shape, employing flow fields with varying cross-sectional areas along the gas flow direction can significantly enhance gas mass transfer in PEMFC30–33. Li et al.30 compared the performance of a conventional parallel flow field to a wedge-shaped flow field featuring a variable cross-sectional area. Their findings revealed that the wedge-shaped design demonstrated superior water management abilities and achieved a more homogenous current density, particularly when operating under conditions of high humidity. Zhao et al.31 further optimized the dimensions of the channel’s sidewalls and bottom, with the objective of maximizing gas transfer efficiency, drainage capacity, uniformity of current density, and overall output performance. Their findings indicated that constricting the sidewalls of the channel had a more significant effect on PEMFC performance compared to constrictions at the bottom. By increasing sidewall constriction, they observed improved mass transfer and convection beneath the ribs, which facilitated the removal of liquid water. Consequently, this led to enhanced overall PEMFC performance, provided that the constrictions were appropriately sized. Zhou et al.32 investigated channels featuring an inverted sinusoidal flow pattern and variable channel widths. Their study revealed that variations in flow velocity significantly influenced the pressure gradient beneath different ribs and channels. As a result, this enhanced oxygen diffusion and convection within the GDL, leading to improved removal of liquid water and overall PEMFC performance. Chen et al.33 designed three distinct cathode bipolar plate flow fields: conventional channels, standard sinusoidal-wave fins, and gradient sinusoidal-wave fins. Their findings showed that the gradient sinusoidal channels notably increased the gas flow rate, thereby enhancing oxygen transport and water removal. Consequently, this resulted in a higher power density and a more uniform current distribution, indicating improved PEMFC performance. Based on the principle of enhancing mass transfer, wavy flow fields have emerged as an effective design for improving PEMFC performance34–36. Anyanwu et al.34 conducted an experimental comparison between wavy flow fields and other traditional designs such as serpentine, interdigitated, and straight parallel flow fields. Their results showed that the wavy design significantly enhanced reactant gas transport and liquid water removal while maintaining a lower parasitic pumping power and sufficient pressure drop. Consequently, this led to superior cell performance, highlighting the potential of wavy flow fields in improving the efficiency and reliability of PEMFCs. In a subsequent study, Ahmadi et al.35 employed analytical and mathematical modeling to demonstrate the benefits of introducing perturbations in the fluid flow within PEMFCs. Their results showed that these perturbations improved reactant diffusion from the gas channel to the GDL, resulting in a more uniform distribution of substances in the reaction zone. This finding was further supported by a 3D CFD model analysis, confirming the positive impact of flow perturbations on PEMFC performance. Anyanwu et al.36 investigated the dynamics of liquid water at the interface between the GDL and the channel in a wavy flow field. Their study concluded that a carefully designed microstructure at this interface could effectively facilitate droplet movement and water removal. This finding aligns with a broader trend observed in multiple studies, which involves the introduction of raised structures within the channel. These structures serve to promote forced mass transfer of gases and improve liquid water removal, ultimately leading to enhanced performance of PEMFCs.

In addition to Conventional flow field designs, bio-inspired principles have gained attention as an innovative approach to flow field design37–39. Researchers have explored human intestinal arterial vessels, branching structures37 and snowflakes38 as sources of inspiration to flow field design. Compared to traditional designs, these bionic flow fields exhibit a smaller pressure drop and higher current density. This enhanced performance can be attributed to the inherent efficiency and low flow resistance of biological structures, which have evolved over time to optimize transport processes. Murray’s law, which describes the optimal transport of matter in nature, serves as a guiding principle in this approach. Tan et al.39 proposed a new flow field structure based on Murray’s law, and their results showed that this novel design significantly reduced pressure drops in the channel, leading to improved overall performance of the PEMFC. This bio-inspired approach represents a promising avenue for further optimizing PEMFC flow field designs.

In summary, the PEMFC is influenced differently by various flow field designs. Common challenges encountered in these designs include inadequate current density distribution, limited resistance to flooding, and significant parasitic power losses due to variations in inlet and outlet pressures. These issues impede the efficient and stable generation of electrical power from fuel cells. To overcome these obstacles, this paper introduces two novel flow field structures: the Multi-U style and the Distro Multi-U style. These innovative designs are aimed at enhancing current density uniformity, improving flooding resistance, and boosting power output. A comparative analysis is conducted between four newly proposed flow fields—In-Out Multi-U, Out-In Multi-U, Distro In-Out Multi-U, and Distro Out-In Multi-U—and two traditional flow fields (CPFF and CSFF). This comprehensive evaluation aims to assess the performance improvements achieved through the implementation of the novel flow field designs. In terms of innovation, the Multi-U flow field structure introduced in this paper offers distinct advantages. Its design enhances both longitudinal mass transfer and lateral diffusion, leading to several key benefits. Firstly, it increases current density and ensures a more uniform current distribution across the cell. Secondly, the structure provides greater resistance to flooding, a common issue in fuel cells. These improvements stem from the structure’s ability to efficiently guide gas flow. During operation, the gas within the Multi-U flow field undergoes continuous polymerization and separation. This unique characteristic promotes uniformity in gas composition and pressure differences along the channel, optimizing the fuel cell’s performance. Furthermore, the reverse flow of gas within the structure enhances the flow between adjacent channels. This feature contributes to the equalization of current density, prolonging the lifespan of the membrane electrodes. Additionally, the Distro Multi-U structure, composed of adjacent Multi-U-shaped elements, significantly mitigates pressure drops within the flow field. This reduction in pressure drops minimizes parasitic power generation, ensuring a consistent reaction rate across all areas of the fuel cell. Consequently, this innovative design effectively mitigates flooding phenomena, further enhancing the overall performance and reliability of the fuel cell system.

Model description

Physical geometry

The three-dimensional model of the monolithic PEMFC as shown in Fig. 1 comprises BPP, GDL, CL, and PEM. The BPP flow channel incorporates the Multi-U structure, which directs fluid from the periphery to the center or vice versa. As the fluids’ flow, they pool and separate, promoting the mixing of the reactive gases. To determine the optimal performance of the new structure configuration, this paper introduces two flow fields and two flow forms: In-Out Multi-U style, Out-In Multi-U style, Distro In-Out Multi-U style, and Distro Out-In Multi-U style. These configurations are compared with the CPFF and the CSFF for a comprehensive study. Measurement points at characteristic locations within the flow field are numerically labeled to monitor flow velocity and oxygen content, as shown in Fig. 1. Mathematical model

This model integrates key control equations, including those for energy, mass, component, momentum, and current conservation, along with equations governing the formation and transport of liquid water.

Numerical program

The geometric modeling of the fuel cell was conducted using SolidWorks software, followed by meshing with HyperMesh software. The solution was then obtained using ANSYS-Fluent software. The coupling of pressure and velocity equations was resolved by employing the SIMPLE algorithm. To enhance convergence, the Bi-Conjugate Gradient Stabilization Method (BCGSTAB) and the F-Cycle technique were implemented. Computational accuracy was improved by discretizing the remaining conservation equations in a second-order windward format. Under-relaxation factors were established as follows: 0.7 (pressure), 0.3 (momentum), 0.95 (mass), 0.9 (potential), 0.9 (proton potential), 0.95 (water saturation), and 0.95 (water content) to ensure stable and convergent calculation results. Convergence was deemed achieved when the cathodic and anodic current densities were equal and stable, with differences of less than 0.01% in both the anode and cathodic current densities, as well as in the current densities between successive iterations, leading to the termination of the calculation.

Model validation and grid independence testing

To validate the reliability of the mathematical model, geometrical model, and simulation method employed in this study, a comparison was made between the polarization curves derived from the current simulation’s CPFF and experimental results reported in reference46 (refer to Fig. 2). The excellent agreement between these curves demonstrates a high level of trustworthiness in the presented simulation model. To minimize the impact of grid size on simulation outcomes, grid refinement was performed specifically for the fuel cell’s GDL, CL, and PEM components. Four sets of models, featuring grid sizes of 1,958,400, 2,611,200, 3,133,440, and 3,786,240, were utilized to assess grid independence (refer to Table 3). At an operating voltage of 0.4 V, the relative error between simulation results for grid counts of 3,786,240 and 3,133,440 was found to be only 1.9%. Therefore, considering both computational efficiency and accuracy, this study adopted Scheme 2 with a grid count of 3,133,440 for all subsequent calculations. To further enhance simulation accuracy, hexahedral meshes were utilized, and detailed mesh delineation can be found in the supplementary materials (refer to Fig. S2).

Results and discussion

Comparison of PEMFC performance with diverse flow field configurations

Polarization curves and power density curves are crucial for describing the performance of PEMFC. Figure 3 presents these curves for PEMFC under U-shaped, CPFF, and CSFF flow fields within a voltage range of 0.3–0.95 V. At high operating voltages (above 0.8 V), the current densities for all six flow fields are nearly identical, as shown in Fig. 3a. However, at lower operating voltages (below 0.8 V), differences emerge due to gas mass transfer effects. Specifically, at the same operating voltage, the CPFF exhibits the lowest current density, while the CSFF shows the highest. The Multi-U outperforms the Distro Multi-U, with only slight differences between the In-Out and Out-In configurations. The power density distribution for different flow fields is displayed in Fig. 3b. The power density peaks at 0.5 V for the CPFF due to significant concentration-differential polarization loss, whereas the other five flow fields achieve their maximum power density near 0.4 V. Notably, the peak power density of the Distro Out-In Multi-U (0.6772 W cm−2) surpasses that of the CPFF (0.4676 W cm−2) by 44.8% and the Distro In-Out Multi-U (0.6564 W cm−2) by 3.2%. However, it is lower than the peak power densities of the CSFF (0.7368 W cm−2) and the Multi-U (0.7132 W cm−2) by 8.1% and 5.1%, respectively.

Output performance of different flow fields: (a) polarization curves; (b) power density curves.

In proton exchange membrane fuel cell systems, parasitic power consumption comprises a significant portion of the system’s total output power. This makes the net output power a crucial metric for evaluating the system’s performance. A notable concern arises from excessive pressure drops in the reactive gas channels, which lead to increased parasitic power consumption within the PEMFC system. This, in turn, detracts from the economic efficiency of the PEMFC and diminishes its net output power. It’s worth mentioning that the cathode channel experiences a more pronounced pressure drop compared to the anode channel. This difference is primarily attributed to the higher gas flow rate in the cathode, as evidenced in the supplementary materials (refer to Fig. S3). Consequently, most of the parasitic power consumption stems from these pressure drops in the cathode channel. Furthermore, at lower operating voltages, the anode consumes more hydrogen. This results in a decreased flow rate (or velocity) along the fluid’s path, leading to a smaller pressure drop when compared to scenarios with higher operating voltages. Conversely, on the cathode side, the reaction of each O2 molecule into two H2O molecules causes an increase in the gas flow rate (or velocity). This acceleration leads to a heightened pressure drop as the current density rises (or the operating voltage decreases). Regarding the flow fields, the minimum gas flow lengths from inlet to outlet are as follows: 100 mm for CPFF, 1417 mm for CSFF, 710 mm for Multi-U, and 230 mm for Distro Multi-U. As the length of the channel increases, so does the frictional resistance encountered by the fluid within, resulting in a more significant pressure drop. Therefore, the CSFF flow field experiences the most considerable pressure drop, while the Distro Multi-U exhibits the smallest. It’s also noteworthy that the Distro Out-In Multi-U design demonstrates the lowest pressure drop among all the tested flow fields.

This decrease in velocity translates into lower local and along-track resistance losses near the outlet. Consequently, the Distro Out-In Multi-U boasts the most uniform pressure distribution with minimal fluctuations, ensuring stable fuel cell operation.

Analysis of internal parameter distribution in PEMFC with various flow field configurations

To gain insights into the chemical reactions taking place at the membrane electrode surface within the PEMFC, it is essential to acquire data on the current density distribution50. Figure 5 illustrates the contours of the current density distribution specifically at the interface between the GDL and the CL on the cathode side. These contours are presented for six distinct flow fields operating at 0.4 V. A notable observation is that the current density is higher upstream of the channel. This phenomenon can be attributed to the elevated oxygen concentration in the fluid at this location. Among the various flow fields, the current density distribution in the CPFF exhibits the most pronounced heterogeneity, with distinct areas of low current density. This non-uniformity is primarily due to the reduced reactant gas flow rate in these specific regions, which leads to an inadequate gas supply and consequently, enhanced diffusion polarization. In contrast, the CSFF demonstrates a higher overall flow velocity. This increased velocity facilitates stronger mass transfer diffusion, particularly at high current densities. As a result, the CSFF exhibits both higher and more uniform current densities. The Distro In-Out Multi-U configuration reveals elevated current densities specifically at the corners of the main channel and under the ribs. This increase is attributed to the significant oxygen concentration difference between the upstream and adjacent channel regions. This concentration gradient accelerates the diffusion of oxygen towards the ridge, resulting in a localized increase in current density. On the other hand, the Distro Out-In Multi-U design effectively minimizes the concentration differences between channels. By preventing the diffusion of large amounts of oxygen between channels, this configuration leads to a reduction in local current density.

An even distribution of oxygen and a higher oxygen concentration inside the PEMFC are indeed crucial for improving the efficiency of the oxygen reduction reaction. Figure 6 visually represents the molar concentration distribution of oxygen at the crucial interface between the GDL and the CL on the cathode side. This figure compares six distinct flow fields operating at 0.4 V. In the case of the CPFF, a prominent oxygen-deficient zone is observed. This deficiency directly correlates with the low current density observed in this configuration. The CSFF, on the other hand, stands out with the highest oxygen concentration. This achievement is attributed to its highest pressure, which facilitates a more efficient delivery of oxygen to the reaction sites. As a result, the CSFF exhibits superior chemical reaction performance, reflected in its highest current density. Within the upstream region of the membrane electrode, the high oxygen concentration signifies an adequate supply. This ensures that the electrochemical reaction is the primary controlling factor in this area, rather than oxygen diffusion limitations. Conversely, in the downstream region, the oxygen concentration decreases, shifting the control mechanism towards diffusion. The under-ribbed region of the Distro Multi-U has a lower oxygen content but a more homogeneous distribution than the CPFF. Importantly, the oxygen distribution gradient between the under-channel and under-rib of the Distro Out-In Multi-U decreases, resulting in the most uniform oxygen distribution.

Mass fraction and flow velocity are essential parameters in assessing the gas diffusion efficiency of PEMFCs. Figure 7 illustrates the oxygen mass fraction and flow velocity recorded at various measurement points across distinct channel flow fields. The oxygen mass fraction in the CPFF channels is predominantly below 0.08, with certain local channels peaking at 0.14. On the other hand, the oxygen mass fraction in the remaining flow field channels typically exceeds 0.08, falling within the 0.08–0.14 range. This disparity in oxygen distribution and the reduced oxygen levels in the CPFF channels lead to significant variations in current density distribution and an overall decrease in current density. The majority of the CPFF channels exhibit a minimal flow rate of 1 m s, whereas the Distro Multi-U channels maintain a lowest flow rate of 10 m s−1. The CSFF channels boast the highest flow rates, spanning from 40 to 60 m s−1, followed by the Multi-U channels at 23 m s−1. The slower flow rate in the CPFF channels contributes to reduced pressure loss, explaining the lower pressure drop in CPFF compared to the more significant pressure drop observed in the CSFF channels. The oxygen concentration variations between adjacent measurement points in the CSFF are roughly consistent. However, the downstream concentration disparities exceed those upstream, suggesting that higher flow velocities facilitate oxygen diffusion from the flow field to the membrane electrode surface. In the In-Out flow channel, minimal fluid strokes upstream yield small differences in oxygen concentration between points. By contrast, downstream fluid strokes gradually intensify, leading to progressively larger oxygen concentration disparities. Comparable observations hold true for the Distro flow fields. Additionally, due to H2O generation, flow velocities at monitoring points in the CSFF and Multi-U channels systematically increase along the flow path. The Distro Multi-U exhibits reduced flow velocities and smaller velocity variations, resulting in lower pressure losses, thereby promoting efficient and stable fuel cell operation.

Excessive liquid water accumulation within the GDL of the PEMFC membrane electrode elevates the resistance encountered by oxygen during its transfer to the CL of the membrane electrode. Figure 8 illustrates a cloud map that visualizes the distribution of water saturation at the interfacial zone between the GDL and the CL on the cathode side. Notably, a prominent area with high water saturation is evident in the CPFF, leading to augmented oxygen diffusion resistance within this segment of the diffusion layer, thereby impeding the electrochemical reaction. The gradual buildup of water along the gas flow trajectory gives rise to an elevated water distribution downstream. In the case of the CSFF, the inlet zone is predominantly influenced by the electrochemical reaction, which generates a substantial quantity of water. Additionally, the elevated pressure facilitates the condensation of water vapor within the diffusion layer, resulting in a heightened distribution of liquid water in the upstream region. As the reaction proceeds, the oxygen concentration diminishes, and the downstream segment of the CSFF transitions to being primarily controlled by oxygen diffusion, ultimately leading to a reduced water saturation. An adequate rise in temperature facilitates the evaporation of liquid water, thereby explaining the reduced water saturation observed downstream of the Distro Out-In Multi-U (main channel) (refer to Fig. S4). Conversely, a notable area of high water saturation is evident in the CPFF. This is primarily attributed to the lower temperature prevalent in this region, which hinders the evaporation of liquid water and promotes the condensation of water vapor, as depicted in Fig. S4. The Distro Out-In Multi-U design demonstrates a more homogenous reaction rate across various regions, leading to a more uniform and reduced water saturation distribution. Evidently, the Distro Out-In Multi-U structure effectively alleviates the fuel cell flooding phenomenon and showcases superior flooding resistance compared to other flow fields. This contributes to enhanced fuel cell performance and extended lifespan.

Conclusion

To enhance the performance of PEMFCs, this study presents a novel Multi-U shaped flow field structure comprising four distinct designs: In-Out Multi-U, Out-In Multi-U, Distro In-Out Multi-U, and Distro Out-In Multi-U. The CFD simulations were performed to assess the influence of these novel structures on the performance of PEMFCs in comparison to the CPFF and CSFF. The results obtained from the CFD simulations reveal that the Distro Out-In Multi-U channel exhibits the most homogenous pressure distribution with lower overall pressure. This leads to a more uniform flow velocity distribution within the gas channel, which is beneficial for stable fuel cell operation. The incorporation of the U-shape structure effectively reduces the occurrence of local low-oxygen and no-oxygen zones in the CL and the GDL of the membrane electrode, surpassing the performance of the CSFF. Furthermore, the Distro Out-In Multi-U demonstrates the most uniform oxygen distribution among all the tested designs. In terms of current density distribution uniformity, the Distro Out-In Multi-U performs better than the CPFF but slightly below the CSFF. When considering net power density, the Distro Out-In Multi-U emerges as the top performer. The In-Out Multi-U, Out-In Multi-U, and Distro In-Out Multi-U designs exhibit comparable net power densities, which are higher than that of the CSFF. The CPFF, on the other hand, has the lowest net power density among all the tested configurations. It is noteworthy that although the peak power density of the Distro Out-In Multi-U is only 8.1% lower than that of the CSFF, it significantly exceeds the CPFF by 44.8%. The Distro Out-In Multi-U channel stands out with a notably reduced pressure drop when compared to both the CSFF and the CPFF. While the CSFF boasts higher oxygen content and power density, these gains are offset by its larger pressure loss. Consequently, the Distro Out-In Multi-U emerges as the leader in net peak power density, surpassing the CSFF by approximately 10.1%. More importantly, it showcases superior energy efficiency, as evidenced by its lowest relative hydrogen consumption of 83.5% when compared to the CSFF. The comprehensive performance evaluation of the Distro Out-In Multi-U structure reveals its excellence in multiple aspects. It not only features the smallest pressure drop and the highest net power density but also maintains moderate uniformity in oxygen distribution and current density. Additionally, it boasts the lowest water saturation among all tested designs (as summarized in Table 5). This optimal water saturation distribution contributes to reduced water flooding at the membrane electrode, further enhancing the stability and durability of the PEMFC. In conclusion, the Distro Out-In Multi-U flow field demonstrates superior output performance, energy efficiency, and resistance to flooding, making it a promising candidate for improving PEMFC technology.

Acute Total Hip Arthroplasty in Acetabular Fractures in Korea: National Claim Database Study
Authors: Jung-Wee Park, Ki-Tae Park, Jonghwa Won, Sung Hwa Kim, Joon Hwan An, Young-Kyun Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe purpose of this study was 1) to analyze the proportion of acute total hip arthroplasty (THA) for acetabular fractures and 2) to evaluate the clinical outcomes of acute THA in South Korea using a medical claims database.MethodsThis retrospective registry study utilized the Korean Health Insurance Review and Assessment database. Patients diagnosed with acetabular fractures from January 2007 to December 2018 were identified using the International Classification of Diseases-10 code. Acute THA was defined as those who had a procedural code of THA during the hospitalization. Kaplan-Meier survival analyses with total revisions, cup revisions, and stem revisions as endpoints were used to evaluate the clinical outcomes.ResultsAmong 14,088 patients with acetabular fractures, 153 patients (1.1%) were treated with acute THA. The mean follow-up period was 5.8 ± 3.9 years (range, 0.2 to 12 years). There were 22 reoperations (14.4%) including 12 cup revisions, 8 total revisions, and 2 stem revisions. Cumulative incidence at 10 years was 7.8% for acetabular cup revisions, 1.4% for femoral stem revisions, 5.1% for total revisions. Survivorship with a cup revision as the end point was 92.1% (95% confidence interval, 87.0% to 97.8%) at 10 years.ConclusionThe proportion of acute THA was 1.1% in treatment for acetabular fracture, and decreased in South Korea. These might be associated with the suboptimal clinical results of acute THA for acetabular fracture.

INTRODUCTION

Acetabular fractures are one of the most challenging fractures for orthopedic surgeons.12 As it is an intra-articular fracture of the weight bearing hip joint, acetabular fracture often results in loss of ambulatory function.3 The clinical impact and importance of acetabular fractures have gained increasing recognition in South Korea due to its aging population.4 Surgical options include open reduction internal fixation (ORIF) and total hip arthroplasty (THA), with or without ORIF.567 Historically, ORIF has been the standard of care for displaced acetabular fractures, when surgical intervention is indicated.12 Even after ORIF for acetabular fracture, 27% to 38% of patients experienced posttraumatic osteoarthritis and 7% to 25% underwent subsequent THA.89101112 The management of these fractures has evolved over time, and there has been a growing interest in utilizing acute THA for acetabular fractures.

To prevent multiple operations in elderly patients, acute THA has been increasingly considered as the treatment modality for acetabular fracture and shown favorable results in some studies.1314151617181920 The benefits of acute THA are early weight-bearing and avoidance of another major operation.21

However, acute THA is hard to be popular for the treatment of acetabular fracture, because a fixation of the acetabular component is challenging due to the fractured fragment.22 Previous studies that reported the use of acute THA for acetabular fractures, included a limited number of patients.1314151617182324

The national claims database of South Korea provides a unique opportunity to conduct a population-based study on the outcomes of acute THA for acetabular fractures.2526272829 This comprehensive database captures a wide range of information, including patient demographics, surgical procedures, type of medical institution, complications, and revision surgeries, allowing for a thorough assessment of the impact of acute THA on the management of these complex fractures.2526272829

Therefore, the purpose of this study was 1) to determine the proportion of acute THA for treatment of acetabular fractures and 2) to evaluate the clinical outcomes of acute THA for acetabular fractures in South Korea using medical claims database.

METHODS

Database

This was a retrospective registry study using the Korean Health Insurance Review and Assessment (HIRA) database. The HIRA database included all information such as diagnosis and medical claims data of entire South Korean patients—97% by the Korea National Health Insurance Program, and 3% by the medical aid program from South Korean government.2526272829 All procedure codes were also reported to obtain the reimbursement of medical cost from Korean government.252630

Operational definitions for acute THA in acetabular fracture

Patients who were diagnosed with acetabular fracture from January 2007 to December 2018 were identified using the International Classification of Diseases-10 (ICD-10) code for acetabular fracture (S324).

Among the patients with the diagnostic code of acetabular fracture, we identified patients who underwent acute THA for acetabular fracture. Acute THA was defined when patients had a procedure codes for THA (N0711, N2070) and diagnostic codes for acetabular fractures (S324) during the same hospitalization.

The level of medical institutions were categorized into tertiary hospitals (≥ 500 beds), general hospitals (100–499 beds), hospitals (30–99 beds), and clinics (< 30 beds).25

Charlson comorbidity index (CCI) was calculated to determine the medical comorbidities using ICD-10 diagnostic codes.

During the follow-up, total revision was defined as those who had a procedural codes of total revision of THA (N1711, N3710, N1721, N3720). Acetabular cup revision and femoral stem revision were defined as those who had a combination of procedure codes of partial revision THA (N1715, N4710, N1725, N4720) and medical device codes for acetabular components or femoral components, respectively.25 Periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) was defined as those who had a diagnostic code for PJI.2526

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented with mean and standard deviation, and categorical variables with proportion. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed to estimate implant survivorship and the cumulative incidence of revision surgeries and complications over time. This approach was chosen for its ability to handle time-to-event data with censoring, making it ideal for assessing long-term outcomes. Covariates such as patient age, sex, CCI, and the type of medical institution were considered, given their potential influence on revision risk and clinical outcomes. Endpoints included acetabular cup revisions, femoral stem revisions, and total revisions, as defined by specific procedural codes to ensure accurate event classification.

All analyses were carried out with R software (version 3.5.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and probability values of 0.05 were deemed significant.

Ethics statement

This study was exempted by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the hospital, because it did not include any information of the individual (Seoul National University Bundang Hospital IRB number Z-2022-234). Informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study.

RESULTS

Overall, there were 22 all-cause revisions (14.4%). These included 12 cup revisions, 8 total revisions, and 2 stem revisions. There are 12 complications including 3 periprosthetic femoral fractures and 9 PJIs. Cumulative incidence at 10 years was 7.8% for acetabular cup revisions, 5.1% for total revisions, 1.4% for femoral stem revisions, 2.1% for periprosthetic fractures, and 5.9% for PJIs.

Survivorship with a total revision as the end point was 94.8% (95% confidence interval, 91.4% to 98.4%) at 10 years. Survivorship with a cup revision as the end point was 92.1% (95% confidence interval, 87.0% to 97.8%) at 10 years, while that with a stem revision as the end point was 98.5% (95% confidence interval, 96.9% to 100%) at 10 years (Fig. 2).

DISCUSSION

In this study, we found that the just 1.1% of acetabular fractures were treated with acute THA between 2007 and 2018 in South Korea. And, there were 22 all-cause revisions (14.4%) including 12 cup revisions. This is the first nationwide database review that demonstrates the risk of revision and rates of complications after acute THA for the treatment of acetabular fractures. The observed survivorship rates, including 92.1% for acetabular cup revision at 10 years, indicate that while acute THA can provide reasonable long-term outcomes, the associated risks of revisions and complications should not be overlooked. These findings suggest that careful patient selection is essential, particularly for elderly patients or those with complex fracture patterns, to balance the benefits of early mobility with the risk of implant failure.

As an aging population increased, the incidence of low-energy acetabular fractures has recently increased and it would be a significant socio-economic burden in these elderly population.313233 In elderly patients, the optimal management for acetabular fractures remains controversial because patient longevity and life satisfaction after surgery differ with those of young population.10

Since acute THA for displaced acetabular fractures was first reported by Westerborn in 1954,34 acute THA could be one of the treatment options for acetabular fractures. Acute THA can allow to bear weight immediately and to avoid the risks of ORIF-related complications.3536

However, acute THA for acetabular fracture has not been popular, because the fracture itself could compromise the initial fixation of acetabular component and require technically demanding procedures.53738 Actually, just some case reports and case series could be found in literatures.131415161718192021232434353639404142434445464748 We found that just 1.1% of patients with acetabular fractures underwent acute THA and the proportion of acute THA decreased with time in in South Korea. Our findings of high rates of all-cause revisions (14.4%) and infections (5.9%) involving 153 subjects highlights the limitations of current literature due to small size and variable follow-up methodology and may provide a more accurate representation of outcomes. The result of our investigation is consistent with previous registry-based studies showing an increased risk of revision, infection, medical complications, and readmission of acute THA in acetabular fracture (Table 2).7495051 These suboptimal outcomes might be a reason why acute THA has not been popular.

A previous retrospective nationwide database study showed that 20.2% of patients with acetabular fracture underwent acute THA in USA.49 There might be a geographical variation of treatment for acetabular fracture and it highlight the need for further research.

There are some limitations in this study. First, it was a retrospective study using database derived from medical claim data. While these codes provide standardized data, they may introduce potential biases due to coding errors or misclassifications. We were unable to include the fracture pattern that needs consideration, because the database with ICD-10 codes did not include the radiographs and patient-reported outcomes. The treatment strategy of acetabular fractures depends on surgeon’s experience as well as fracture pattern.5253 To mitigate this, we used strict inclusion criteria based on multiple procedural and diagnostic codes to improve accuracy, though some level of bias remains possible. Second, we could not know the indication of acute THA in each institute. Indications for acute THA were different across previous studies.21414244 Third, we could not evaluate the patient-reported outcomes such as Harris hip score and the Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis (WOMAC) score, because the HIRA database did not include them. Fourth, we could not determine the laterality (right/left) in this database. However, bilateral involvement of acetabular fracture would be extremely rare in practice. Fifth, the generalizability of our findings may be limited by variations in cost and reimbursement across different healthcare settings. Acute primary THA could not be reimbursed for acetabular fracture by the Korean HIRA. This could be one of the reasons of the low proportion of acute THA in South Korea. Well-designed prospective studies with larger sample sizes and comprehensive functional outcome assessments are essential to provide more robust evidence for optimizing the management of acetabular fractures.

In conclusion, while acute THA can be considered a treatment option for acetabular fractures, its utilization remains low, with a proportion of just 1.1% and a decreasing trend observed in South Korea. This decline may be attributed to the suboptimal clinical outcomes, including higher rates of revisions and complications. Our findings suggest that acute THA should be carefully reserved for selected patients, such as elderly individuals with poor bone quality or those unlikely to benefit from ORIF, where early mobility and reduced risk of multiple surgeries are critical. Clinicians should weigh the risks of implant failure and complications against the potential benefits, and consider alternative or staged approaches for complex fracture patterns.

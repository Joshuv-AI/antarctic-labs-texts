Density-aware and energy-gradient based relay selection for enhanced routing in mobile sink wireless sensor networks
Authors: Emad S. Hassan, Ayman A. Alharbi, Ibrahim Aqeel, Idris H. Smaili
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) are popular for monitoring purposes, but it is found that the networks’ lifetime may be restricted by energy limitations and load imbalance. Therefore, the authors propose a new energy-balancing protocol called Density-Energy Gradient-based Relay Routing (DEGR) that combines local node density and residual energy gradients in order to choose relay nodes for the dynamic formation of optimal routing paths to maximize routing efficiency in mobile sink-enabled WSNs. In contrast to clustering protocols and static sector based routing, the DEGR dynamically determines the relay nodes which are not only energy abundant but also strategically located in dense areas, thus avoiding hotspots and extending network lifetime. DEGR combined with an adaptive clustering model and circular sink mobility, provides balanced distribution of energy and low latency delivery of data. The results of the simulations in MATLAB show that DEGR outperforms Low-Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH), Energy-aware Clustering and Data Routing Algorithm (ECDRA), LEACH with Mobile Sink (MS-LEACH), and Mobility and Energy Adaptive Density-based Clustering (MEADC) protocols. It increases the lifespan of the first node death by approximately 54% compared to LEACH, decreases the variance of energy consumed by approximately 40%, and keeps the packet delivery ratio above 90% all the time. These results show the relative performance advantage of DEGR in the simulations in the specified simulation framework.

Introduction

Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) technology has been identified as a key enabling technology for pervasive monitoring applications in a wide variety of application areas such as environmental monitoring and surveillance, smart agriculture, industrial automation, healthcare monitoring, battlefield reconnaissance, and intelligent transportation systems1–3. WSNs consist of spatially distributed autonomous sensors that gather, process, and communicate information about the physical or environmental condition (e.g., temperature, humidity, motion, or pollutants). These sensors collaboratively forward data to centralized stations or mobile sinks for further analysis and action. WSNs offer many inherent advantages, including ease of deployment, small size, scalability, and adaptability, and can be deployed in places where traditional infrastructure is not available or feasible such as remote areas, inaccessible areas, or hostile environments4,5.

Although WSN is versatile and promising, however, its application is still seriously limited by energy constraint. The sensors are usually powered by non-rechargeable batteries and in a lot of applications it is not possible to replace or recharge the batteries because of physical inaccessibility or economic reason. To maximize the life time of WSNs it is important to optimize their energy consumption. Failure of a node before it is supposed to fail may result in loss of sensing coverage and even partition the network and data. Thus, energy efficiency has emerged as a key design goal for WSN research6–8.

Wireless communication is one of the main energy consuming applications in WSNs. Energy is consumed in the transmission and reception of data packets from sensor nodes, especially long distances. Furthermore, in static sinks deployments the nodes near the sink usually have to carry a large amount of data from the far away nodes. This generates the familiar “hotspot” issue, in which energy is used up unevenly and nodes near the sink die first causing the network to degrade significantly in performance. This phenomenon emphasizes the necessity of designing energy aware routing protocols that are not only energy efficient in terms of energy consumption of the communication but also are load balanced in terms of energy consumption9–12.

In order to solve this problem, various routing protocols with good energy efficiency have been presented. The clustering-based approaches try to minimize communication overheads by aggregating data at certain cluster heads (CHs), which, in turn, will send the aggregated data to the sink13. Although in the clustered network the energy is saved due to the decrease in intra network communication, the selection mechanism for the CHs is not always energy efficient, particularly if the CHs are unevenly distributed or are located far from the sink. Furthermore, the fixed-sink architecture still has the problem of traffic concentration and energy holes14,15.

Mobile sink-based architectures have been proposed to alleviate the hotspot issue and to enable better energy distribution. Such schemes can help distribute the load among static relay nodes and extend network longevity by letting the sink follow a route that is specified a priori or dynamically adjust its trajectory. A prominent work in this direction is the energy-efficient routing protocol proposed by Wang et al.16 that combines clustering with a mobile sink that performs a circular trajectory. They divide the sensing filed into sectors and select the CHs depending on the remaining energy of the CHs and the distance of the CHs from the sink. It additionally links CHs together in a chain through a greedy algorithm to achieve efficient communication between clusters. The results showed significant performance enhancement compared to the previous ones like LEACH13, Energy-aware Clustering and Data Routing Algorithm (ECDRA)17 and LEACH with Mobile Sink (MS-LEACH)18 with respect to energy consumption and network lifetime.

The mobility of the sinks and the selection of CHs are important advances but there are key constraints in the current strategies. Much of the previous work makes an assumption that node distribution is uniform or does not consider the effect of the local node density within the graph on the routing decision. This may lead to the same nodes being repeatedly selected as relays, causing quicker energy depletion and network fragmentation. Furthermore, the CHs that have a high residual energy can be selected as relay nodes without considering their topological role and the energy density of their neighbors, and this leads to an unequal distribution of energy usage and can cause the network to degrade prematurely19,20.

To overcome these drawbacks, this paper introduces a new residual energy, local node density, and energy gradient based relay node selection scheme to achieve more balanced and energy-efficient routing in mobile sink based WSNs. The principal concept is to choose the relay nodes which are not only abundant in energy but also are located in the dense areas of the network. These nodes are more likely to have neighbors who may be their future forwarders or collaborators, which helps load balance the forwarding process and increases the fault robustness. On the other hand, nodes in sparsely populated areas are not selected to be a relay node to maintain their few connections and keep their energy reserves available. The proposed strategy presents a composite relay weight metric that dynamically assesses the validity of the node in the local neighborhood based on the density of the neighborhood and the residual energy gradient of each node as compared to the other nodes.

This is a density-aware and energy-gradient-based selection scheme which is embedded within a mobility sink supported routing framework. The mobile sink keeps on its optimized circular path across the sensor field, reducing communication distances. Round-by-round, the sensors assess the energy cost of direct communication versus relay communication and choose the latter when it is the more energy efficient. Relay selection is adaptive, based on proposed metric, to achieve a balanced energy profile of the network.

The novelty of the proposed approach is the combination of topological awareness and the energy heterogeneity. This approach is different from those previous ones, which have depended on energy thresholds, or on proximity to the sink, to encourage fairness in both space and energy. This algorithm takes into consideration the density of local nodes, making it possible to distribute the routing responsibility among the nodes of a resilient cluster, and minimizing the risk of energy voids. At the same time, the residual energy gradients guarantee that with energy-rich nodes, there is no overloading of isolated or critical nodes.

Moreover, the proposed method offers network resiliency and scalability. The density-aware mechanism will naturally prioritize routing traffic through areas with higher connectivity in cases of node failures or coverage holes. This flexibility enables the protocol to be suitable for real-world deployments, whether on rough terrain or for applications that need to be monitored for long periods of time without the need for constant human involvement.

The effectiveness of the proposed scheme is validated with wide spread simulations with MATLAB. The performance is compared against other protocols such as LEACH, ECDRA, and other clustering algorithms which use the mobile sink16. The key performance indicators are analyzed with respect to different network density and sink mobility parameters, such as network lifetime (time to first death and last death), variance of residual energy, etc. The experimental results show that the proposed approach can effectively achieve a better overall energy-efficiency, extend the network lifetime, and reduce energy consumption.

Related work

An improved version of soft-k-means clustering, which improves the cluster formation by utilizing density peak detection and kernel density estimation, was proposed by Zhu et al.21. In their approach, they achieve a balance of cluster sizes and distribute multiple CHs per cluster, demonstrating advantages in terms of delaying node deaths in small-scale (one-hop) WSNs over baseline schemes. This approach, however, does not consider multi-hop routing/relay nodes and fails to take into consideration the residual energy gradients over the network and cannot be applied in mobile sink scenarios.

Kampen et al.22 have designed a wireless energy balancing algorithm, where CHs are selected according to their high energy and close distance to cluster members, and then they use a knapsack-based member assignment strategy. Energy saving is improved as per the simulations performed compared to LEACH. However, this algorithm is only applicable to one-hop clustering, and does not consider opportunistic relay placement, which is based on local density or energy gradients, and lacks mobile-sink adaptability.

In23 the authors proposed a set of relay selection techniques, which aimed at reducing the number of relays, while using multiple metrics of link quality simultaneously to improve the communication reliability. Adaptive and periodic re-selection schemes resulted in better transfer success rate and network reliability. This technique is useful for reliability, but the choice of the technique is primarily for link quality and not energy balancing, and it doesn’t take spatial density into account nor integrate with mobile sink routing.

In24 a recent approach called Density and Energy-Aware Evidential Routing (DS EERA) based on Dempster-Shaffer theory was presented, which is based on the residual energy, neighbor energy density, traffic load and hop distance to the sink for selection of the next-hop. However, it has some drawbacks for relay design as it merely selects the next hop without considering the global topology or mobile sink dynamics comprehensively, and does not support the consideration of gradient balancing.The authors in25 present mobility-based Dynamic Energy-Efficient Routing (DEER) in the Software-Defined Wireless Sensor Network (SDWSN) algorithm that integrates fuzzy logic to assess link stability and residual energy, enabling relay decisions in mobile IoT networks. However, this work focuses on link stability, not on spatial node density or global energy distribution in WSNs, and lacks a method for relay selection gradient or densitometric context in static sensor deployments.

Liu et al.26 studied relay cooperation where nodes are randomly distributed via a Poisson process. Relay candidates are selected by k-medoids clustering; others harvest energy from their surroundings and decide whether to cooperate. But it requires an energy harvesting assumption not valid for general WSNs; density-awareness is only used for candidate clustering, not for routing gradient design. Recent surveys have indicated that most post-LEACH research has focused on the use of meta-heuristic and swarm-intelligence techniques for cluster formation and routing, to maximize the life of the network and equalize energy consumption while at the same time increasing the complexity and tuning effort of the algorithms. For instance, Roberts et al.27 present a dual-phased meta-heuristic for energy-efficient cluster-based routing and Kaviarasan & Srinivasan28 develop an adaptive remora optimization approach which is based on multi-objective search formulation of routing, reporting improvements but requiring iterative global optimization. Unlike these optimization-based designs, the proposed DEGR introduces a distributed and lightweight relay selection rule based on normalized residual energy, local energy gradient and node density, which also accounts for the mobile-sink location to prevent hot-spot depletion and enhance the longevity of the system without resorting to global search. This places DEGR as an alternative approach that can achieve similar objectives (lifetime, energy balance and delivery) but does not require any iterative meta-heuristic.

Multi-criteria routing and clustering approaches have been the subject of recent research for energy-efficient routing and clustering in WSNs. For instance, density-aware clustering methods consider both residual energy and node distribution for improving network scalability and load balancing29–31. In29 the authors have presented a Mobility and Energy Adaptive Density-based Clustering (MEADC) framework that integrates the information of residual energy and spatial node distribution to improve the performance. In the same way, optimization-based routing schemes have been designed to take into account both energy and connectivity in multi-hop communications30.

To reduce the energy consumption in heterogeneous WSN, Zhang et al.31 introduced an adaptive routing scheme using hybrid particle swarm optimization (PSO) and dynamic clustering. By leveraging residual energy, geometric centrality, and local node density, their approach aims to improve network scalability and adaptability, guiding cluster formation and routing decisions. Furthermore, a load dissemination mechanism based on rumours is proposed for enhancing the load balance and the communication efficiency. The simulation results show that the network lifetime, packet delivery, and delay are significantly improved compared with the conventional clustering protocols. The proposed method is based on optimization based global decision mechanisms without explicitly taking into consideration energy gradient information for relay selection which is a critical element taken care of in the proposed DEGR protocol.

Although these developments, the existing methods focus on either energy or density, or on optimizing energy and density jointly, but do not explicitly use the information of the energy gradients to make a decision on the relay selection. This restriction is the motivation for proposed DEGR protocol.

The existing work considers the individual factors, i.e., node density, residual energy, link reliability, and routing using a mobile sink. However, there is no work that has been done to integrate the node density and residual energy gradients into a single framework for relay selection. Most density-aware methods work on clustering, and energy-aware methods consider residual energy but do not consider spatial distribution. Moreover, current schemes fail to explicitly consider energy gradient information to direct energy utilization balancing among neighboring nodes. Hence, this paper overcomes this important challenge by proposing a novel composite relay node selection metric (density and residual-energy gradient), embedded in the mobile-sink cluster-routing scheme, to obtain an energy balanced usage, decrease the hotspot problem and prolong the network lifetime. There is a small amount of computation overhead associated with the relay selection mechanism, because the relay weights are calculated from simple arithmetic operations of the locally available parameters. There is no need for a central coordination or complex optimization procedures. The energy penalty of relay weight calculation is negligible since it only affects the power consumed by the nodes for communication.

Residual energy and neighborhood parameters are refreshed at predefined intervals or upon significant network state variations. This strategy reduces unnecessary control traffic while maintaining accurate relay selection decisions. Since computation energy is considerably lower than communication energy in WSNs, the overhead introduced by metric calculations remains minimal.

Proposed algorithm

This section introduces the operation procedure of the proposed routing algorithm, which combines the node density and residual energy gradients for selecting relay nodes in the energy balancing process. The algorithm consists of four main stages, namely: Network Initialization, Cluster Formation and Cluster Head (CH) Selection, Relay Node Selection, and Data Transmission via Relay Nodes to the Mobile Sink.

Network initialization

The N sensor nodes are randomly and uniformly deployed in a 100 × 100 m2 monitoring field at deployment. The mobile sink is dragged around the field in a circle around the geometric center of the field at a constant radius. All nodes have the same initial energy E0, and are equipped with location-awareness (GPS or localization). At the beginning of each round, the mobile sink sends its current location to all nodes.

This section presents simulation-based performance analysis to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed relay selection scheme. The results are compared with benchmark protocols in terms of energy efficiency, network lifetime, and load balancing under a mobile sink architecture. The compared protocols are LEACH13, ECDRA17, MS-LEACH18, MEADC29, and the proposed DEGR.

The selection of probability of a cluster-head is selected to ensure an optimal number of cluster-heads per round. A small probability means that the number of cluster heads is kept at an efficient level (around 5:10% of the total number of nodes). This balance helps to reduce the overheads of excessive control and cluster size which could lead to higher intra-cluster communication energy consumption. As a result, the chosen probability can induce stable and energy-efficient clustering mode.

The weighting coefficients α, β and γ are respectively the relative weights of energy residual, node density and gradient of energy in the relay selection metric. Residual energy is given the highest weight (α = 0.4) as it has direct impact on node lifetime and network sustainability. To boost routing robustness and lower transmission failures, particularly in well-connected regions, node density (β = 0.3) is added to favour relay nodes. The energy gradient factor (γ = 0.3) helps to balance the energy usage by discouraging routing through low energy areas. The values were chosen through empirical sensitivity analysis, where various combinations of the coefficients were tried. These improvements in network lifetime, packet delivery ratio and energy variance were stable with the selected configuration.

Statistical validation

For each setup, as described in Table 3, we ran N = 30 independent simulation runs. Each run had scalar outcomes calculated: per round (FND/HND/LND), mean over rounds (PDR), average residual energy (mean) and energy variance (mean over rounds). Normality was tested by Shapiro-Wilk test, and homogeneity of variances by Levene’s test. A one way ANOVA was used for each metric when assumptions were met, otherwise the Kruskal-Wallis test was used. Pairwise comparisons were conducted after the data were analyzed using Tukey’s HSD (ANOVA) or Dunn’s test with Holm–Bonferroni correction (Kruskal-Wallis). Effect sizes (ω² for ANOVA; ε² for Kruskal-Wallis) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported and p < 0.05 (two-sided) was considered statistically significant39.

The effect of different number of CHs in the network on network lifetime by using proposed DEGR protocol is presented in Fig. 12, which shows number of alive nodes in the network with respect to simulation rounds. Results suggest that the number of CH directly affects the longevity of the network. For small number of CHs (e.g., 3 CHs), the network suffers from early node deaths because of large cluster sizes, which leads to more communication distance within cluster and consumes more energy in the cluster. This is likely to cause quicker energy loss of the cluster members. The network lifetime is obviously better as the number of CHs increases (e.g., from 5 to 7). Further gains are limited by the higher control overhead cost and inter-CH communication energy costs that are incurred with more CHs, while the smaller clusters reduce the intra-cluster cost.

Limitations

The proposed DEGR protocol is assessed based on typical assumptions like homogeneous nodes and ideal localization, which facilitates the controlled performance analysis. In real-world deployments, errors of localization, hardware variation, packet loss, wireless interference, etc., can affect performance. The sensitivity to moderate errors in distance estimation and density calculation due to localization inaccuracies is reduced by the fact that DEGR uses local neighborhood information. Data delivery can be affected by communication impairments, including interference and packet loss, but density-aware relay selection provides robustness by selecting well-connected areas. Moreover, the proposed framework can be extended to the heterogeneous environment by using the capability variation of the nodes in the relay selection metric.

Conclusion and future work

This paper presents a novel routing protocol, named DEGR, which is able to improve the energy efficiency and network life of the mobile-sink-enabled WSNs. DEGR dynamically discovers optimal relay nodes based on local node density and residual energy gradients, thereby preventing hot-spots and achieving a balanced energy usage. To further reduce the transmission distance and balance the load in the communication network, the protocol adopts an adaptive clustering mechanism and a circular trajectory of the mobile sink. Using a simulation, DEGR outperformed other protocols like LEACH, ECDRA and MS-LEACH. These results confirm DEGR’s capability of reliable data transmission with efficient energy management, even with different network densities and communication ranges. Other critical parameters, such as the number of mobile sinks, node density, and relay selection thresholds were also analyzed.

There are several technically relevant ways to extend the proposed DEGR protocol for future research. Adaptive tuning of the relay weight coefficients (α, β, γ) according to the actual network conditions could further improve the energy balancing and routing efficiency in the first place. Second, machine learning-based methods can be used to adaptively make relay choices and cluster-head selection decisions based on the changing traffic and network conditions. Further, the optimization of relay selection and the trajectory of the mobile sink are also potential areas for research, as joint mobility and routing optimization can drastically cut energy consumption and communication delay. The proposed framework can also be extended to heterogeneous and large-scale IoT environments, with nodes that have varying energy capabilities and communication needs. In addition, cross-layer optimization techniques that leverage MAC and physical layer details might further boost the overall network performance, especially in terms of reliability and latency. Last, the addition of energy harvesting or wireless charging could supplement the proposed energy-aware routing method to ensure sustainable long-term WSN deployments.

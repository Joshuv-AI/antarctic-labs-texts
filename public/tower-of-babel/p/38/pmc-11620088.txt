Can enterprise ESG practices ease their financing constraints? Evidence from Chinese listed companies
Authors: Xiaofan Wang, Lili Yao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractUnder the guidance of the United Nations SDGs target framework, enterprises' ESG practices of developed countries can effectively promote external investors' ESG investment, thus easing enterprises' financing constraints. Using the data of Chinese A-share listed companies in the Shanghai- and Shenzhen-stock exchange markets from 2013 to 2022, this study explores the causal relationship between enterprises' ESG practices and their financing constraints to provide Chinese experience, examines the mediating effect with corporate transparency and investor sentiment, and analyzes the heterogeneity with external auditor type and earnings management motivation. It is found that enterprise ESG practices can enhance their financing ability by improving corporate transparency and suppressing investor sentiment; for the non-Big 4 audit listed companies and the listed companies with the weak motivation of earnings management, the corporate ESG practice has a more significant effect on their financing ability. This study enriches the research on the relationship between ESG practice and ESG financing by providing Chinese evidence.Keywords: ESG practice, Financing constraints, ESG financing, Corporate transparency, Investor sentiment

Introduction

ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) is a concept deeply rooted in CSR (Corporate Social Responsibility), a sustainable development concept championed by the United Nations. It has gained significant traction as an investment strategy in Europe, North America, and other developed regions [1]. Now, it is making its mark in emerging markets like China. In 2006, the Shenzhen Stock Exchange Institute introduced social responsibility guidelines for listed companies, marking the formal introduction of the ESG concept in China. Since the core ideas of ESG, such as 'Sustainable development' and 'Green,' align with the idea of green development advocated by the Chinese Communist Party in 2013, the scale of ESG practice in Chinese enterprises has been on a steady rise, reaching a record high by the end of the third quarter of 2023, with China's ESG investment soaring to 33.06 trillion yuan, a 34.4 % increase from 2022 and a compound growth rate of 34.02 % over the past three years, according to SynTao Green Finance. This exponential growth underscores the relevance and potential impact of our research.

ESG investment by external capital can play a significant role in alleviating the financing constraints of enterprises. According to the Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchange Institutes, more than 200 companies in 2023 have halted IPOs due to concerns about their business viability and corporate governance; professionals recommend adding ESG disclosure to the prospectus. This underscores the growing recognition of the importance of ESG in the investment landscape. In this context, the ESG of Chinese enterprises has been regarded as a 'New force' of value investment besides financial information. As China's ESG investment scale continues to maintain high-speed growth, Chinese listed companies' ESG practice is entering an acceleration period. According to the China Association of Listed Companies, more than 1700 A-share listed companies in China compiled and published ESG-related reports in 2022, accounting for 34 percent. This data highlights the increasing prevalence of ESG reporting and its potential impact on corporate financing constraints, a key aspect of our research.

The current literature on ESG practices and their financing constraints focuses on two aspects: first, focusing on the relationship between ESG practices and financing costs, investment efficiency, corporate value, corporate performance, the stock price collapse risk, corporate reputation in the field of corporate finance [[2], [3], [4], [5], [6], [7], [8], [9]]. The research concludes that ESG practice can promote the development of enterprises by hedging their business risks through multiple channels. At the same time, some scholars hold that the popularity of ESG investing can encourage "Greenwashing" in ESG reporting due to the defects of external institutional arrangements, investor preferences, and the imperfect internal corporate governance mechanism [10], which in turn damages corporate value and financial performance. Second, as an intermediate variable, the literature studies the moderating or mediating effects of financing constraints on the relationship between enterprises' ESG performance and firm operating characteristics. Some scholars have found that financing constraints can strengthen the positive relationship between ESG practice and investment efficiency and enterprise value [6,11]; While some scholars argue that financing constraints will weaken the positive relationship between ESG practice and enterprise performance [12]. However, few pieces of literature have focused directly on the relationship between ESG practices and financing constraints, especially on the internal mechanism of how ESG reporting quality affects financing constraints. At the same time, although rich literature on agents and information asymmetry problems resulted from the inconsistency of modern enterprise operation and ownership [13], little research has focused on the issue of mitigating information asymmetry due to increased corporate transparency and the agent problem due to lessening investor sentiment in capital markets. Specifically, ESG's proven ability to raise capital may stem from the idea that ESG can alleviate Information asymmetry problems by providing more non-financial information to the stakeholders; the ESG information reflects the enterprise's medium and long-term development potential. The ESG information evaluation system can calm investors' emotions and alleviate the agency problem in the external capital market, which can be regarded as an external reason to ease financing constraints. As a result, enterprises with good ESG performance signal an excellent development potential to stakeholders and set up a sustainable image, thus gaining the favor of external ESG investment.

Based on the above policy background, literature background, and practice background analysis, this paper selects data on 2013–2022 China Shanghai and Shenzhen exchange markets A-share listed companies. It does not include companies that have been delisted or whose St or ∗ ST status has been marked by the stock exchange market, nor does it include listed companies in the financial sector. The financial data of this research mainly comes from the China Stock Market & Accounting Research(CSMAR) database. ESG rating data comes from Shanghai Sino-Securities Information Service Co., Ltd (Sino-Securities), using the nine ESG ratings of Sino-Securities as proxy variables to evaluate the impact of ESG practices on the financing constraints of Chinese listed companies. The study found that the better the ESG performance of listed companies, the stronger their financing ability. The further mechanism test shows that ESG can alleviate the financing constraint by improving corporate transparency and suppressing investor sentiment. The heterogeneity analysis indicates that as for the type of enterprise audit service, compared to enterprises that use Big 4 audit services, listed companies that do not use the Big 4 audit services, active ESG practices can reduce their financing constraints; in other words, their ESG practices can offset the possible potential adverse effects of not using the Big 4 audit services; as for the motivation of earnings management, the stronger it is, the greater the possibility of ESG report "Greenwashing." Therefore, for enterprises with solid earnings management motivation, the promotion of ESG on financing constraints will be weakened.

China's top-down management style and unique organizational mobilization capabilities have nudged companies to embrace ESG practices in the short term, and the proportion of ESG reports disclosed by Chinese-listed companies has snowballed. However, China's mandatory ESG report disclosure system and mandatory verification mechanism have not yet been established, which may lead to companies taking advantage of the shortcomings of the institutional arrangement to cater to the preferences of rating agencies, thus tending to the "greenwashing" behavior of companies [10], causing confusion to the public and raising investor concerns. According to the data of American Star, in the second half of 2023, only 6 ESG funds were launched in the US market, compared with 55 in the first half of the year, showing a steep drop. So, is there a similar situation in China? Arguing ESG performance does not pull ESG financing. Thus, this study proposes a competitive hypothesis. However, the empirical results do not support the above hypothesis, which could suggest the following two insights. Firstly, it indicates that in the Chinese institutional context, ESG practices improve corporate investment efficiency [6], promote corporate performance [4], and enhance corporate value [11], which in turn enhances core corporate competitiveness. Secondly, the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) revised the "Corporate Governance Guidelines for Listed Companies" in September 2018, establishing the fundamental framework of ESG information disclosure for the first time. In July 2023, the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission issued the "Reference Index system for the ESG Special Reports of Listed Companies of Central Financial Holdings," which constructed the first industry-wide official guidance document on ESG disclosure covering the three dimensions of environment, social responsibility, and corporate governance. The release of this series of guidances provides an institutional guarantee for the rapid development of ESG in China.

A series of robust tests are conducted to guarantee the research results' stability and supplement this study's conclusion. Specifically, first of all, the Sobel and Bootstrap tests for mediation effect. Secondly, the study regressed the explanation variables of lag one-stage and two-stage hysteresis to alleviate endogeneity. Thirdly, the mean ESG of Sino-Securities ESG rating was used as an instrumental variable of ESG performance to lessen the endogenesis problem caused by the missing variable. Then, to overcome the measurement error, this paper replaces the financing constraint variable (KZ) measurement method and uses the WW Index and SA index to proxy financing constraint. Finally, this paper further controls the fixed effects of year and industry. All these robustness tests confirm that the findings are statistically significant and economically illuminating.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows: First, this paper observes a new mechanism of action based on internal information transparency and external investor sentiment from the numbered internal and external factors affecting corporate financing constraints, providing Chinese evidence for the study of the relationship between corporate ESG practices and financing constraints. Currently, academic research on the relationship between corporate ESG practices and financing constraints is still relatively limited (Li, Shao, Li, & Li, 2022) and mainly focuses on the moderating role of financing constraints as a mediating variable between ESG practices and corporate investment efficiency and corporate value [6,11]. Although the mediating effect of corporate transparency between ESG performance and corporate reputation and financial performance has been explored in the literature [9,14], unlike the mentioned literature, this paper investigates how corporate ESG practices affect its financing ability; specifically, how ESG reports can provide investors with more non-financial information reflecting the long-term performance and risk level of the enterprise and investment, showing its informational effect; or how the enterprise affects external investment entities to make ESG investments, mainly, how disclosure of ESG reports can reflect the long-term investment value of the enterprise and calm investor sentiment, thus attracting external funds to entry, showing its emotional effect. By exploring the information and emotional effects mentioned above, this study enriches the literature on the economic consequences of ESG practices in Chinese enterprises. It provides quasi-natural experimental evidence for the compatibility and coexistence of economic development and ESG systems in developing countries.

Secondly, based on the mediating variable model with moderating effect to test the heterogeneity issues accompanying corporate ESG practices and their financing constraints, this paper provides a more detailed and in-depth argumentation on the empirical results, clarifies the economic insights of the research results under the premise of statistically significant, and helps to deepen the understanding of corporate ESG practices and financing constraints. From the perspective of internal corporate information transparency, the information transparency of Big 4-audited listed companies is usually higher. In contrast, non-big 4-audited listed companies' active practice of ESG practices has a more noticeable effect on the enhancement of corporate financing ability, which in a sense suggests that ESG performance can be used as a favorable tool for non-Big 4-audited listed companies to attract external ESG investment. From the perspective of external investor sentiment, ESG performance may be regarded as enterprise management's "greenwashing" behavior, which is not conducive to stabilizing investor sentiment and hedges against the positive effects of ESG performance if the company's internal surplus management motivation is more potent.

The rest of this article is organized as follows. The third part describes the data, variables, and models in the empirical test. The fourth part reports the results of the benchmark regression and mechanism analysis, the robustness test, and heterogeneity analysis; the fifth part is a summary and policy recommendations.

Theoretical hypothesis

The theories that support ESG practice are mainly signaling theory and catering theory, and the practice of ESG plays a positive role. To some extent, it can solve the problem that pure financial information cannot fully reflect the value of enterprise development, transmit a good "Performance signal" to investors, and gain investors' recognition and resource funds, thus easing their financing constraints. Enterprise ESG practices can ease their financing constraints in the following two ways.

Good ESG performance can ease financing constraints by improving corporate transparency. According to the signal theory, good ESG performance can send a positive signal to the outside world through information disclosure and encourage potential investors' positive behaviors, showcasing an information effect. First, in terms of environmental performance, financial institutions led by state policy banks have established green channels for companies to channel financial support into green industries, and green investment trends in financial markets have become more pronounced; through environmental disclosure, companies can communicate the concept of green development to the outside world and obtain support from outside investors such as governments and financial institutions [15]. Second, regarding social responsibility performance, the government and state-owned enterprises dominate China's national economy. Studies have shown that the power of the government drives the socially responsible behavior of Chinese enterprises; by fulfilling ESG responsibilities, the enterprise can reach a kind of resource exchange with the government and transmit information about fulfilling social responsibilities to the government, the bank, and the public. Under the influence of Chinese traditional culture, highly socially responsible companies are more likely to gain recognition and financial support from the government, banks, and the public [16]. Third, as far as corporate governance is concerned, good corporate governance can effectively supervise and restrain management behaviors, improve the quality of earnings management, and reduce the risk of corporate fraud. By actively disclosing relevant information, such as governance responsibilities, a company can build a good reputation and image and win investors' trust in financial information, thus helping it obtain funds [6].

Good ESG performance can ease enterprises' financing constraints by calming investors' emotions. According to the catering theory, good ESG performance containing fair information comforts investors' emotions, enhances investors' confidence, and triggers potential investors' positive behaviors, showcasing an emotional effect. Baker and Wurgler [17] argue that valuation difficulties, arbitrage constraints, and management opportunism drive investor sentiment. The rising investor sentiment will reduce the efficiency of resource allocation, increase the risk of enterprise operation [18], and increase the opportunistic behaviors of management due to executive compensation contracts; this leads to an increase in internal agency costs [19], thus affecting the supply of support in the capital market. Therefore, calming investor sentiment and gaining recognition and supporting from the investment side are essential elements of corporate ESG practices. Based on the catering theory, firms disclose ESG information related to the firm's long-term performance and risk level from environmental, social responsibility, and corporate governance. It provides investors with incremental information that affects the long-term economic return and sustainable development of enterprises, which reduces the difficulty of valuation and arbitrage costs [20]; In addition, systemic corporate governance restrained the management's short-sighted and self-interested behaviors and avoided damage to shareholder value to convey its good prospects for enterprise development to the outside world [21]. Therefore, the ESG investment evaluation system with the new development concepts of innovation, coordination, green, openness, and sharing can cater to the needs of external investors, enhance investor confidence in the green transformation, sustainable development, and surplus quality of enterprises, and ultimately alleviate the corporate financing constraints. Accordingly, this paper proposes competitive hypothesis H1a.

The better corporates' ESG performance, the better their ability to ease corporate financing constraints

The theories supporting ESG performance that may aggravate financing constraints are the opportunism hypothesis and principal-agent theory. The opportunism hypothesis holds that when companies fulfill their social and environmental responsibilities in order to pursue the economic benefits of "greenwashing," they are, in essence, artificially embellishing the information on corporate sustainability and green transformation, which is a kind of "pseudo-social responsibility behavior" [22]. This kind of pseudo-social responsibility behavior leads enterprises to only formally adapt to the requirements of green transformation and sustainable development and selectively disclose social responsibility information that is beneficial to the enterprise itself without taking any substantive measures [23], which exacerbates the information confusion dilemma of potential investors.The principal-agent theory holds that for self-interest motives such as image maintenance, management is more inclined to increase their investment in ESG to enhance their reputation and build business empires [24]. Due to limited enterprise resources, improving ESG performance and related activities will bring additional costs to the enterprise [25], consume enterprise resources, increase agency costs, and reduce the core competitiveness of the enterprise.

As with financial statement fraud, there is a strong incentive for ESG information fraud even more so [10]. As a new thing, the ESG report is not mandatory disclosure; China just established the basic framework of ESG information disclosure for specific requirements, while standards are not clear, and the internal control system of enterprises is imperfect, which together provide the opportunity for the management's "Greenwashing" behaviors. In the face of great economic temptation, management may abandon the integrity of the bottom line; there is a greater moral risk, and the defects of system arrangement make the probability of detection and punishment of ESG information fraud low, which provides a favorable external environment for the opportunistic behaviors of management. Therefore, disclosing ESG information is a voluntary and purposeful information management behavior. To maintain an excellent corporate image and enhance their legitimacy requirements, enterprises exaggerate the content of sustainable information disclosure and fuzzy investor decision-making information, which will aggravate financing constraints.

Enhancing ESG performance with strong positive externalities such as environment and social responsibility will increase enterprises' explicit and implicit costs. The explicit cost mainly includes the direct cost of environmental protection, research and development, safety production, and donation. The explicit cost mainly consists of the indirect cost, which is brought about by planning, managing, and evaluating the enterprise's ESG performance. In the case of enterprise resources, ESG performance investment may lead to the loss of investment opportunities to gain more profits; at an early stage of development, it reduces profits and shareholder wealth [47], leading investors to abandon investments in a company they believe will yield less, thus aggravate the financing constraints of enterprises. Accordingly, this paper proposes competitive hypothesis H1b.

The better a company's ESG performance, the more likely it is to exacerbate corporate financing constraints.

Research design

Sample selection and data sources

This study takes Chinese A-share listed companies from 2013 to 2022 as the initial research sample. Data is cleaned with the following rules to maintain the comparability of data and enhance the reliability of the research conclusions: (1) listed companies that have been delisted, ST, and ∗ST status are excluded; (2) companies with missing or abnormal data on relevant variables are excluded; and (3) data of listed companies in the financial industry are excluded. The financial data required for this study mainly comes from the CMSAR Database, and ESG rating data comes from the Sino-Securities database. Winsor shrinkage of 1 % up and down is applied to all continuous variables, and all regressions are adjusted for firm-level clustering to exclude the influence of extreme values on the data.

Definition of variables

Explained variables

In this study, the KZ index is used to measure the financing constraint status of enterprises. Referring to existing studies [[26], [27], [28]], five variables, namely, money capital holdings, net cash flow from operating activities, asset-liability ratio, dividend payout ratio, and firm growth, are selected to construct the KZ index.

First, money funds/total assets at the end of the previous year (Cash), net cash flow from operating activities/total assets at the end of the last year (Cashflow), asset-liability ratio (Lev), cash dividends/total assets at the end of the previous year (Dividend), and the median Tobin's Q (TQ) is used for grouping. When Cash is less than the median CASH, kz1 is 1, otherwise 0; when Cashflow is less than the median Cashflow, kz2 is 1, otherwise 0; when Lev is greater than the median Lev, kz3 is 1, otherwise 0; when Dividend is less than the median Dividend, kz4 is 1, otherwise 0; and when TQ is greater than the median TQ, kz5 is 1, otherwise 0. Then, KZ = kz1 + kz2 + kz3 + kz4 + kz5.

Core explanatory variables

ESG practice refers to the quality rating of ESG information disclosed by studied companies. According to the white paper on China's ESG development, there are more than 600 ESG rating agencies worldwide, and it has become a hot research topic whether ESG ratings disagreements have weakened the reference value of ratings in investment decisions. Given China's unique "relational" social environment, rating disagreements mainly arise between domestic and foreign ESG rating agencies [29]. For domestic listed companies, local rating agencies have the advantages of geographic proximity and regional policies. Further reassessment of the eight mainstream domestic ESG rating agencies suggests that the Sino-Securities ESG rating system has substantial benefits in terms of the scientificity of indicator setting, the reliability of information disclosure, and the transparency of rating information [30]. Therefore, this study adopts the Sino-Securities ESG nine-grade rating to measure corporate ESG performance. The rating system is C, CC, CCC, B, BB, BBB, A, AA, AAA from low to high, and the value is assigned to 1–9 in the order of ESG level bottom to up; when the rating is C, the ESG value is assigned to 1, and when the rating is AAA, the ESG value is assigned to 9.

Robustness tests

Bootstrap tests for mediating effects

The bootstrap method is a widely recognized method to test the mediation effect. This method mainly analyzes whether the product of β1 and δ2 in the model is 0 through the validation mechanism; if its upper and lower bounds do not include 0 in the confidence interval at the 95 % level, it means that the product of coefficient β1∗δ2 is significant at the 5 % significance level. A mediation effect exists, and the influence mechanism is verified. Therefore, this study adopts this method to test whether there is a mediating effect of internal and external drivers affecting enterprises' ESG practices and financing constraints.

As shown in Table 7, the product of correlation coefficients β1∗δ2 is significant at the 1 % level in the test of enhancing corporate transparency, and the product of correlation coefficients β1∗δ2 is significant at the 1 % level in the test of calming investor sentiment. Therefore, it can be concluded that the mediating effects of corporate transparency and investor sentiment between ESG practices and corporate finance constraints exist.

Heterogeneity analysis

External auditor heterogeneity

As an essential part of corporate governance, the literature generally supports that listed companies that international Big 4 accounting firms audit have "positive" economic consequences [39,40]; meanwhile, information transparency of listed companies audited by the Big 4 is significantly higher than that of listed companies not audited by the Big 4 [41]. Does this mean that non-Big 4 audited listed companies are more willing to disclose information related to environmental responsibility, social responsibility, and governance responsibility to alleviate their financing constraints by increasing investors' understanding and attention to the enterprise? Or is there a complementary effect between the ESG evaluation index system, which is a new thing, and the Big 4 audits? Therefore, this paper expects the Big 4 audit to weaken the negative relationship between ESG disclosure and financing constraints. Listed companies that do not use Big 4 audit services are more inclined to use ESG practices, which means non-Big 4 audit-listed companies take the initiative to make ESG disclosures than listed companies that use Big 4 audit firms' services.

Accordingly, this paper adds the cross-multiplier term of BIG4 with the core explanatory variable ESG to the baseline regression model (3); this study uses the lagged one-period explanatory variables for the regression. The regression results are shown in Table 11, and the coefficient of the cross-multiplier term L.ESG∗BIG4 is 0.133, which is significantly positive at the 1 % level, indicating that there is a significant weakening of the relationship between ESG disclosure quality and financing constraints among listed companies audited by the Big 4. It also implies that non-International Big 4 audited listed companies use ESG disclosure as a favorable tool to enhance corporate transparency and thus reduce financing constraints, consistent with expectations. In sum, using the Big 4 international audit services and active ESG practices has an alternative effect on China's listed companies to ease their financing constraints. For listed companies that do not use the Big 4 audit services, active ESG practices can reduce their financing constraints; in other words, their ESG practices can offset the possible potential adverse effects of not using the Big 4 audit services.

Corporate earnings management motivation

Corporate earnings management arises from the agency problem triggered by the separation of ownership and operation. The management layer carries out the earnings manipulation behaviors to achieve specific reporting objectives. It has been found that earnings management can affect investor sentiment [42], and the level of investor sentiment is positively related to the size of earnings management [43]. Based on China's existing institutional arrangements, except for particular enterprises, ESG information is mainly disclosed voluntarily, and the relevant monitoring mechanism has not yet been established. For enterprises with strong incentives for earnings management, ESG information disclosure aims to cater to investor demand. It is regarded as the management's behavior of "greenwashing," which is short-sighted behavior that not only reduces the resource allocation efficiency of enterprises but also breaks their long-term economic interests, triggers fluctuations in investor sentiment, and is not conducive to attracting capital inflow in the capital market. Therefore, this paper expects that the promotion effect of ESG on financing constraints will be weakened for enterprises with a higher degree of earnings management.

Drawing on existing research [44,45], this study uses the Modified Jones model to calculate manipulable accruals DA_abs as a proxy variable for firms' incentives to manage earnings. The cross-multiplier term of DA_abs with the key explanatory variable ESG is added to the baseline regression model (3) and regressed using the explanatory variables lagged one period. The regression results are shown in Table 12, and the coefficient of the cross-multiplier term L.ESG∗DA_abs is 0.895, which is significantly positive at the 1 % level, indicating that the relationship between ESG disclosure quality and financing constraints appear to be weakened considerably in listed firms with more substantial incentives to manage surpluses, which aligns with expectations.

Conclusions and policy implications

This research takes data from Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchange market A-share listed companies from 2013 to 2022 to empirically examine the relationship between enterprises' ESG practices and financing constraints. The results show that ESG information disclosure has a significant easing effect on the financing constraints; the higher the quality of ESG information disclosure, the more conducive to reducing the financing constraints. Under the ESG investment concept, enterprises can gain the favor of external stakeholders in the capital market by undertaking environmental and social responsibility and strengthening corporate governance. The mechanism analysis shows that corporate transparency and investor sentiment have a noticeable mediating effect on ESG information disclosure and financing constraints, playing as internal and external motivations, respectively. In particular, disclosing ESG information can ease information asymmetry, improve corporate transparency, and ease financing constraints. In addition, communicating more sustainable visions and ideas to the outside world and establishing the image of assuming corporate social responsibility can calm investors' emotions, reduce the agency cost of management opportunism, and ease enterprises' financing constraints. Heterogeneity analysis shows that (1) because of the high transparency of the listed companies audited by the Big 4, the release of ESG information to the financing constraints is more evident in the listed companies not audited by the Big 4; (2) the motivation of earnings management reflects the goal of management disclosure of ESG information. If the motivation of earnings management is strong, it will lead to investors' emotional fluctuations and weaken the promotion of ESG information disclosure on financing constraints.

Based on the empirical findings, the following two inspirations are directly derived: First, at the enterprise level, the ESG concept has been trying to help enterprises establish sustainable business practices through the three dimensions of environment, social responsibility, and corporate governance, such as adhering to green development, coordinating the interests of stakeholders, and improving corporate governance. In particular, in China's economic recovery, enterprises generally face liquidity pressure and lack of growth after the three-year pandemic; combining sustainable development with the business logic of enterprises can effectively alleviate the financing constraints and, at the same time, form a new point of profit growth and ultimately achieve high-quality development of enterprises. Secondly, at the public level, with the concepts of green, environmental protection, and sustainable development taking root in people's recognition, investors have the idea of sustainable development and implement responsible investment based on the ESG performance of enterprises. In promoting Chinese characteristic modernization with the new development concept, the ESG investment concept promotes enterprises to actively fulfill their social responsibilities, improve corporate governance, and take the road of low-carbon transformation and development. Under this responsible investment philosophy, companies are encouraged to focus not only on financial performance but also on non-financial performance to better harmonize short-term economic interests with long-term returns and enhance the sustainable competitiveness of enterprises.

In addition, based on the management style and organizational attributes accompanying China's specific "big government" situation, adopting an active government and an efficient market can boost the development of ESG practices in China. ESG practices of Chinese companies have shown initial results in easing financing constraints, improving investment efficiency, promoting corporate performance, and enhancing corporate value. Still, China needs to establish a unified disclosure standard or independent verification system for ESG reports. It may be difficult for ordinary investors to differentiate whether the ESG information disclosed by enterprises is the management's opportunistic behavior or the enterprises' actual strategy. Therefore, Chinese governmental bodies are needed to improve the system construction related to ESG reporting, gradually guide enterprises to improve their ESG performance, and consciously disclose ESG information, forming a benign cycle of enterprise ESG practice and ESG investment.

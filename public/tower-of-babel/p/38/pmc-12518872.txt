Enhancing precision in breast cancer diagnosis using Tailored Ratio-Integrated Variance Estimation using Neyman allocation Implementation(TRIVENI)
Authors: G. R. V. Triveni, Faizan Danish, V. R. K. Reddy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In stratified random sampling, precise variance estimation is essential especially when supplementary information is provided. This article presents an innovative Tailored Ratio-Integrated Variance Estimation employing Neyman Allocation Implementation (TRIVENI), which effectively combines ratio-based modifications to improve variance estimation. Despite traditional methods, TRIVENI leverages two auxiliary variables in multiple ways to measure the combined effect on population variance estimations. Applying Neyman allocation, it effectively distributes the sample among strata, ensuring minimal variance and improved precision. The combined use of ratio estimation promotes the application of auxiliary information, hence reducing estimate bias and enhancing accuracy. Theoretical derivations and simulation studies confirm that TRIVENI surpasses traditional estimators, demonstrating improved efficiency in various stratification contexts. The proposed methodology signifies a significant advancement in stratified variance estimation, rendering it a crucial instrument for survey sampling experts and researchers.

Introduction

In the context of sample surveys, stratified random sampling is a powerful tool for raising estimator efficiency by reducing population heterogeneity. Separating the population into non-overlapping, internally homogeneous strata helps researchers to obtain more precise estimates of population metrics like the mean, total, or variance. This stratification can be very beneficial, especially in practical situations such as forecasting the average wage of government employees grouped by rank, where natural groupings exist and variance inside strata is decreased. The use of additional data, along with stratification, especially about survey estimators’ accuracy improvement, helps to determine this. Estimation techniques like ratio, product, and regression estimators frequently include auxiliary variables that are associated with the research variable. These methods are essential for enhancing the prediction of population variation, even if they have been frequently used for predicting population means and totals, which is equally crucial in assessing the reliability and variability of survey estimates, these estimators have been extensively employed to estimate population means and totals.

When two auxiliary variables are present and strongly correlated with the study variable, the efficacy of traditional estimators can be improved even more. Employing ratio-type estimators with dual auxiliary information under stratified sampling has been demonstrated to significantly reduce the mean squared error (MSE) of the population variance estimators. This approach not only benefits from its advantages of stratification but also improves estimation accuracy by applying the strength of several auxiliary sources. Thus, in complex real scenarios, variance estimation utilising ratio estimators with dual auxiliary information under stratified sampling provides a robust framework for producing reliable and efficient survey results.

To improve the estimation of population parameters, employing auxiliary information is crucial in survey sampling. When auxiliary information is available, ratio and regression type estimators are developed to better estimate the population means1,2. For improving the reliability and efficiency of estimators, authors found variance estimation is considered as an important method of estimation and recognized its role in increasing the precision of survey estimates. The work of3 proposed a variance estimator. Extending this, Ratio estimators for the population variance are proposed4,5 and proved their efficiency, and proposed a new estimator for the estimation of population variance. By extending this work, later, a hybrid class of estimators are developed6. Further, by using quartiles and their functions as auxiliary variables, a ratio-type estimator is suggested for the estimation of population variance7. Further, an Improved family of estimators are proposed for the population variance in simple random sampling8. In addition, an improved class of estimators9, and reviewed ratio estimators for the variance estimation procedure10. Later, three families of estimators are proposed11 and proved their efficiency in estimating the variance of the population. Different allocation methods were analyzed12 and compared the variances of all the methods. For the first time, in simple random sampling, a log-type estimator to estimate population variance was proposed13.

By using single and two transformed variables, a generalized variance estimator in a stratified sampling scheme was proposed14. The next work extended by using the first and second raw moments under stratified sampling and developed estimators to estimate the finite population variance15. Later, a generalized estimator of finite population variance under stratified random sampling was proposed16. Further, new classes of difference cum-ratio-type exponential estimators for a finite population variance in stratified random sampling was developed17. Further, an efficient class of Variance Estimators were suggested18 and difference and ratio type logarithmic estimator19 and proved its efficiency over other existing estimators through a numerical and simulation study. Further, a ratio-product-logarithmic type estimator was proposed20 to estimate the finite population variance, and by using the outlier values and ranks of the auxiliary variables, an improved class of double exponential-type estimators for effective estimation of population variance was proposed21. By extending these works various authors contributed under variance estimation22–24.

Although some researchers have developed generalized, ratio-type, and product-type estimators for variance under stratified random sampling, most of these methods have relied on simple functions of auxiliary variables or on the mean. Very few attempts have been made to simultaneously exploit the means and variances of two auxiliary variables in the building of variance estimators. Moreover, the integration of such data into the Neyman allocation paradigm has not been the subject of any previous research. To address this gap, the current study proposes the Tailored Ratio-Integrated Variance Estimator with Neyman Allocation (TRIVENI), which systematically combines the means and variances of two auxiliary variables to improve the precision and effectiveness of population variance estimation under stratified random sampling. By providing a more reliable and accurate estimator than current methods, this contribution advances variance estimation. The organization of the paper is as follows: notations used, existing estimators, proposed TRIVENI estimator, Numerical example, simulation analysis, discussion, and conclusions.

Numerical example

About the data set: source25

To validate the proposed estimator, we applied TRIVENI to a real-world dataset, the Wisconsin Breast Cancer Diagnostic dataset. This dataset was chosen because it is commonly used in statistical and machine learning research due to its large dimensionality and well-structured variables. Because it includes several correlated variables, it is suitable for assessing the performance of estimators that utilize auxiliary information. By considering three subsets of variables from this dataset, we demonstrated how TRIVENI effectively leverages auxiliary information to generate more accurate variance estimates in a practical setting.

The data set is derived from digitized fine needle aspirate (FNA) images of breast masses, and its purpose is to extract the features of cell nuclei and classify the tumors as Benign (B) and Malignant (M). It contains 569 instances with 30 numerical features. We have stratified the entire data set into 2 strata based on the classification methods (B and M). There are various columns of variables, out of which we selected 3 sets of data, each has one study and two auxiliary variables. The population sizes of the two strata are taken as (357, 212).

The TRI (3) sub-data sets we have chosen from the data are as follows: Data–1: Estimation of tumor area using the means of radius and perimeter. Data–2: Estimation of tumor concavity of its worst case by using compactness and concave points of the worst case. Data–3: Estimation of tumor perimeter in its worst case by using the area and radius (Worst case Values).

Discussion and conclusions

This study developed the Tailored Ratio-Integrated Variance Estimation utilizing the Neyman Allocation Implementation (TRIVENI), which is a new approach in the variance estimation process under stratified random sampling. By optimizing the sample allocation across strata, leveraging the Neyman allocation and integrating the bivariate auxiliary information through ratio-based adjustments. In the process of estimating the population variance of the study variable, the TRIVENI estimator increased the precision and the efficiency of the variance estimates. To prove the theoretical findings of the TRIVENI estimator, a real data set and a simulation study are provided. In the real data set, which is related to breast cancer diagnostic data, three sub-data sets are considered from 30 columns of the entire data set. From the results of the numerical example, we can observe that the proposed estimator shows greater accuracy. In the simulation study, hypothetical data is generated by using the rnorm() function and performed Monte Carlo simulation is performed at various numbers of iterations. At every stage, the proposed TRIVENI estimator outperformed the other estimators and had high efficiency. We can also identify the radar charts, which are very helpful to reflect the metrics like MSE and PRE. From the charts, we simply say that the MSE of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\calligra{\rotatebox[origin=c]{22}{s}}_{TRIVENI}^{2}$$\end{document} (proposed estimator) is very low when compared to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\calligra{\rotatebox[origin=c]{22}{t}}_{1}$$\end{document} to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\calligra{\rotatebox[origin=c]{22}{t}}_{7}$$\end{document} (existing estimators) and the PRE is very high for the TRIVENI estimator than other considered estimators. Due to its ability to reduce bias and improve the performance in the estimation process, the proposed estimator emphasizes its practical applicability.

Due to the fact that it does not assess TRIVENI’s effectiveness on very highly skewed or heavy-tailed data, the study is limited to evaluating it under controlled conditions. In addition to stratified random sampling, future studies may focus on using the TRIVENI estimator for heavy tailed data and more complex survey designs, such as two-stage or systematic sampling. Besides Neyman allocation, other allocation techniques can be tested for the flexibility. Additional applications in different real-world areas outside of the current dataset and integration with modern statistical tools might also increase its practical utility.

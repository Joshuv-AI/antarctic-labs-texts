IFPHPOS0040 From Climate Risk to Clinical Action: An AI-Enabled Intervention for Health Care Professionals to Provide Climate Specific Health Advice in Community Settings
Authors: Rochelle Velho, Peter Ward
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAir pollution is a major public health challenge. The Right to Breathe report (2025) calls for integration of environmental risk into routine clinical care. However, translation of environmental data into actionable, patient-centred interventions remains limited in primary care.MethodsA scoping review (2020–2026) evaluated seven widely used digital air quality tools (IQAir, Plume Labs, BreezoMeter, EPA AirNow, PurpleAir, airTEXT, AirTrack). Tools were assessed using the Mobile App Rating Scale (MARS) and Behaviour Change Technique Taxonomy (BCTTv1). Findings informed the design of an AI-enabled chatbot to support exercise counselling at annual asthma reviews.ResultsA clear behaviour change gap was identified. Most tools prioritised real-time data visualisation but lacked evidence-based techniques such as goal setting and action planning, limiting sustained behaviour change. Government-backed tools demonstrated high information quality but lower engagement, while commercial tools achieved higher usability at the expense of clinical integration. Digital inequities were evident: populations with the highest pollution exposure had lower access, usability, and digital literacy.Conclusion & Clinical PathwayWe developed the FRESH-AIR framework to operationalise environmental risk in clinical workflows: (1) Find and risk-stratify using electronic records and exposure data; (2) Rate and equip with tailored digital prescriptions; (3) Embed AI decision support. The CleanAir GP Assistant prototype was designed to generate personalised, exposure-aware action plans, including route optimisation and threshold-based advice. It is an aide for community staff to integrate climate aware advice into routine consults.Next StepsStakeholder co-design and public engagement will refine usability, address digital exclusion, and evaluate impact on health outcomes and inequalities.

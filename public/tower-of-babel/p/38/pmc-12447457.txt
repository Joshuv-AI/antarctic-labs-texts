Effect of thoracic paravertebral block and erector spinae plane block on postoperative gastrointestinal function and quality of recovery in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Min Zhao, Yun-Xing Zhang, Xiao-Rui Wang, Xiao-Rui Zhang, Chen-Bin Huang, Jian-Wen Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective This study aimed to investigate the effects of thoracic paravertebral block (TPVB) and erector spinae plane block (ESPB) on postoperative gastrointestinal function and quality of recovery (QoR) in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy.MethodsNinety-six patients were randomized into three groups: general anesthesia alone (Group G), general anesthesia + TPVB (Group GT), and general anesthesia + ESPB (Group GE). The primary outcome was the incidence of postoperative abdominal distention, while secondary outcomes included time to first exhaust/defecation, bowel sound recovery, postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), QoR-15 scores, pain intensity (VAS), perioperative sufentanil consumption, and hospital stay.ResultsBoth GT and GE groups demonstrated significantly lower abdominal distention rates (all p < 0.05) and reduced PONV incidence compared to G (all p < 0.05). Time to first exhaust and defecation were shorter in intervention groups(all p < 0.001). Bowel sound recovery rates at 12–48 h postoperatively were higher in GT and GE compared to G (all p ≤ 0.020). QoR-15 scores at 12, 24, and 48 h were elevated in GT and GE versus G (all p < 0.001). VAS pain scores at 1–12 h and sufentanil consumption were lower in GT and GE (p < 0.001). Hospital stay was shorter in intervention groups (all p < 0.001).ConclusionsThe preoperative ultrasound-guided single TPVB and ESPB could reduce the postoperative abdominal distention rates, shorten the time to first exhaust and defecation, decrease the sufentanil consumption and postoperative VAS pain score, elevate the postoperative QoR-15 score in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy.

Introduction

Conventional lobectomy is performed by means of thoracotomy, resulting in significant trauma, severe pain, and slow recovery. Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery significantly reduces the trauma associated with thoracic surgery, with significantly reduced postoperative pain, fewer complications, and a significantly shorter average duration of hospital stay [1,2]. Studies have shown that video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery is associated with decreased perioperative hemorrhage and infection, enhanced postoperative respiratory function, and reduced postoperative mortality [2,3]. Despite the fact that thoracoscopic surgery has become the standard therapy of lung cancer, perioperative trauma and damage to organ function may diminish the satisfaction of patients during hospitalization, as well as affect the functional recovery and health-related quality of life [4]. Consequently, analgesia, organ protection and postoperative rehabilitation during perioperative period are crucial for patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy.

Patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy are eligible for discharge provided they meet the following criteria: the ability to walk independently, normal gastrointestinal function, removal of the pleural drainage tube and venous catheter. Gastrointestinal factors are defined as any complications associated with any symptoms of the gastrointestinal system, such as nausea and vomiting, abdominal distention, and constipation [5]. Notably, postoperative nausea and vomiting is the most common complication in patients with thoracoscopic lobectomy, which seriously affects the quality of early recovery, postoperative patients’ satisfaction and comfort [6,7].

After thoracoscopic surgery, the multimodal analgesia consists primarily of thoracic paravertebral block (TPVB) and erector spinae plane block (ESPB). The previous studies have shown that preoperative TPVB and ESPB could reduce postoperative pain, decrease sufentanil consumption and additional analgesics [8–10]. However, to our knowledge, few studies have investigated the impact of TPVB and ESPB for postoperative gastrointestinal function and quality of recovery. Therefore, we conducted this randomized controlled trial to examine the effect of TPVB and ESPB for postoperative gastrointestinal function and quality of recovery in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy.

Patients and methods

Study design

This is a prospective, double-blind, randomized clinical trial. The Ethics Committee of Shanxi Bethune Hospital reviewed and approved the study protocol (YXLL-2020-071). We registered the study at the Chinese Clinical Trials Registry (ChiCTR2100043516). All patients provided written informed consent before participation. Data collection commenced on October 1, 2022, and was completed on June 30, 2023, at Shanxi Bethune Hospital, Taiyuan, China. We conducted this trial following the principles of Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki. No changes to the study protocol occurred after trial commencement.

A total of 100 patients scheduled for thoracoscopic lobectomy were assessed for eligibility, with four patients excluded due to not meeting inclusion criteria or refusal to participate. Inclusion criteria were as follows: 30–70 years of age, Body Mass Index (BMI) 18–30 kg/m2, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) grade I to II classification. Exclusion criteria included: severe cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, infection of the skin around the puncture site, bleeding or coagulation disorders, spine malformation, history of gastrointestinal diseases or postoperative nausea and vomiting, unstable mental illness, and refusal to participate. The elimination criteria were as follows: thoracotomy performed during the perioperative period, perioperative bleeding ≥500 mL, or the patient was transferred to intensive care unit (ICU). The study was conducted by the same group of thoracic surgeons.

Randomization and blinding

The eligible participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1:1 ratio to three groups to general anesthesia group (Group G), general anesthesia combined with TPVB group (Group GT) and general anesthesia combined with ESPB group (Group GE) using a computer-generated random allocation sequence (R version 4.0.5, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A research nurse not involved in the study prepared the randomization list with a block size of 6, which was concealed in sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes, and opened on the day of surgery. After induction of anesthesia, the block was conducted according to the designated groups by an anesthesiologist who was not involved in further intraoperative anesthesia management or data collection, analysis, and interpretation. All patients, surgeons, and follow-up investigators remained blinded to the randomization groups.

Anesthesia procedure

All patients fasted from solid food for at least 8 h and from clear liquids for at least 2 h before surgery. Routine intraoperative monitoring included pulse oximetry, electrocardiography, noninvasive blood pressure, and bispectral index. Anesthesia was induced with 0.05 mg/kg of midazolam, 0.5 μg/kg of sufentanil, 0.6 mg/kg of rocuronium, and 0.3 mg/kg of etomidate. After pressurized oxygen supply for 3 min, a double-lumen tracheal tube was intubated into the bronchus under the clear vision of a video laryngoscope. General anesthesia was maintained with propofol at 4–6 mg/kg/h and sufentanil at 0.2–0.3 μg/kg/h. The propofol infusion rate was titrated to maintain bispectral index values between 40 and 60, while the sufentanil infusion rate was adjusted to keep heart rate and arterial blood pressure within ±20% of baseline values. Rocuronium was administered as needed. During the operation, restrictive intravenous fluid therapy was administered. Following the closure of the surgical incision, the infusion of propofol and sufentanil was discontinued, and a 12.5 mg dose of dolasetron was administered intravenously. After spontaneous respiration and consciousness were restored, the endobronchial tube was withdrawn. Within 48 h after surgery, patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) was administered. The analgesia formulation contained 100 μg of sufentanil in 100 mL containing 0.9% physiological saline. The pump was set with the loading dose of 2 mL, a background dose of 1 mL/h, a single supplementary dose of 2 mL and a locking time of 15 min. Patient pain was assessed based on the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS). The chest drainage tube was removed 3 days after surgery.

Study interventions

All blocks were performed under general anesthesia before the surgical incision. Patients were placed in the lateral position, with the affected side facing upward for block performance. Group GT: Ultrasound scanning was conducted by positioning the high-frequency linear-array transducer (Sonosite S-nerve, USA) over the T5-T6 interspinal interstice in a typical sagittal orientation. After the needle was inserted into the paravertebral space between the transverse process fascia and pleura using the out-of-plane needle insertion method and no blood or gas was withdrawn, 30 mL of 0.5% ropivacaine injection (AstraZeneca AB, batch No. PS17440) was administered. The distribution of the solution and the pleura was depicted on ultrasound footage moving downward, i.e. ‘the sign of falling tide’. Group GE: Ultrasound scanning was conducted by positioning the high-frequency linear-array transducer on the T5-T6 interspinal interstice in a typical sagittal orientation. Using an out-of-plane needle insertion technique, the needle was inserted between the deeper face of the erector spinae and the T5-T6 transverse processes after penetrating the trapezius, rhomboid, and erector spinae muscles. After ensuring no blood or gas was withdrawn, 30 mL of 0.5% ropivacaine injection was administered. Group G: No nerve block was administered.

Outcome measurements

The primary outcome was the incidence of postoperative abdominal distention. The secondary outcomes included the time of first exhaust and defecation, the time of first bowel sound recovery, the incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), the length of stay, the QoR-15 scores, the VAS pain scores, and the perioperative sufentanil consumption.

Statistical analysis

The incidence of abdominal distention was one of the primary endpoints of the study. The sample size was calculated on the basis of a pilot study that showed the incidence of abdominal distention (expressed as number of cases (percentage) was 7 (46.67) in the group G, 1 (6.67) in the group GT, 2 (13.33) in the group GE. The ratio of number of cases in the three groups was 1:1:1. A Fisher exact test was performed with a significance level of 0.05 and a power of 80%. Utilizing SPSS software, version 21.0 (IBM Corporation, USA), a sample size of 26 participants was determined for each group. Factoring in an anticipated dropout rate of 20%, it was vital to have a minimum of 32 participants in each group for the study.

The statistical analysis were performed using SPSS software, version 21.0 (IBM Corporation, USA). Normal quantitative data were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD). For multiple measurement data following normal distribution, one- way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used, with pairwise comparisons analyzed using the least-significant difference (LSD). In cases of non-normal distribution or heterogeneity of variance, the Kruskal-Wallis H test was used for multiple measurement data, and Bonferroni correction was applied to two-way comparisons. Categorical data were reported as number (percentage), and the chi-squared test was conducted between groups. Statistical significance was reported with p < 0.05.

Safety outcomes

No block-related adverse events or major medical complications were reported in any participants during the study period.

Discussion

This randomized controlled trial showed the patients who underwent general anesthesia with either TPVB or ESPB could reduce the postoperative abdominal distention rates, shorten the time to first exhaust and defecation, decrease the perioperative sufentanil consumption and postoperative VAS pain score, elevate the postoperative QoR-15 score. This findings indicated that either TPVB or ESPB could protect gastrointestinal function, enhance postoperative analgesia, improve recovery quality, and promote rapid recovery in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy.

Neuromodulation can effectively regulate the perioperative gastrointestinal function [11]. TPVB is a nerve block technique in which local anesthetic is injected into the thoracic paravertebral to block the thoracic spinal nerve and branches as well as sympathetic trunk, providing analgesia comparable to thoracic epidural analgesia [12]. ESPB is achieved by injecting local anesthetic between the transverse process and the deeper layer of erector spinae. Along the deep thoracolumbar fascia, the local anesthetic can spread caudally and anteriorly, and block the ventral branches, dorsal branches, and communicating branches of the thoracic spinal nerve in adjacent segments. In addition, some drugs can also block the sympathetic nerves and inhibit visceral pain [13]. This study showed that the VAS pain scores at 1, 6, 12, and 24 h postoperatively were significantly decreased in the Groups GT and GE compared with the Group G, and the sufentanil consumption was significantly reduced in Groups GT and GE compared with Group G. This findings indicated that both TPVB and ESPB may be used as part of multimodal analgesia for patients with thoracoscopic lobectomy.

The results of the current study also showed that the incidence of abdominal distention, the time of first exhaust and defecation, the time of the bowel sound recovery, the incidence of PONV, and the duration of hospitalization were all better in Groups GT and GE than those in Group G. This findings indicated that the ultrasound-guided TPVB or ESPB in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy accelerated postoperative gastrointestinal recovery and promoted overall rapid recovery. Previous finding has been reported in pediatric patients undergoing inguinal hernia repair, where ESPB provided superior postoperative analgesia compared to caudal block, as demonstrated by a longer time to first rescue analgesia and lower pain scores [14]. The potential mechanisms are as follows: (1) Koirala et al. discovered that surgical trauma-induced stress reaction could delay the recovery of the gastrointestinal function, which may be attributed to an imbalance between the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems after surgery [15–17]. The sympathetic nerve may inhibit gastrointestinal function through the inhibition of gastrointestinal smooth muscle activity and mucosal secretion as well as vasoconstriction. Both surgical trauma and pain may cause the excitation of the sympathetic nerve, thereby enhancing perioperative inflammatory response and delaying the recovery of gastrointestinal function [18]. Meanwhile, the over-excitation of the sympathetic nerve will also delay the recovery of postoperative gastrointestinal function, resulting in the weakening of gastrointestinal motivity and the slowing down of intestinal peristalsis [18]. TPVB or ESPB can effectively inhibit the sympathetic nerves, reduce the stress response, and weaken the gastrointestinal inhibition caused by of sympathetic nerve. (2) The use of opioids may play a role in the postoperative gastrointestinal dysfunction [19]. Nerve plexus located between circular and longitudinal smooth muscles as well as those present beneath the submucosal layer innervate the gastrointestinal tract. Neurons have the capacity to synthesize both opioid peptides and neurotransmitters. Within the enteric nervous system, there exist three distinct types of opioid receptors, namely μ, κ, and δ. Of these, the μ opioid receptor play a major role in causing gastrointestinal adverse reactions. Both endogenous opioid peptides released after surgery and exogenous opioids for postoperative analgesia act on intestinal μ opioid receptors, leading to reduced intestinal function. TPVB or ESPB may enhance perioperative analgesia, thereby reducing opioid consumption. Thus, the gastrointestinal inhibitory from opioid was reduced.

The QoR-15 scale, a validated patient- reported outcome measurement, has recently been recommended as one of six endpoints in perioperative medicine for evaluating patient comfort and pain after surgery [20–24]. The QoR-15 scores range from 0 to 150, with a higher score indicating a better quality of recovery [25]. Our findings showed that the patients who underwent general anesthesia with either TPVB or ESPB exhibited higher QoR-15 scores at 12, 24, and 48 h postoperatively comparison to the patients who underwent general anesthesia alone. The results suggested that the utilization of TPVB or ESPB was linked to improved outcomes in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy. This favorable prognosis can be attributed to several factors, including improved postoperative pain, promoted gastrointestinal recovery, reduced pulmonary complications, and improved postoperative comfort through these blocks [26].

This study has some limitations. First, this study was conducted as a single-center randomized clinical trial with a relatively small sample size. Therefore, the results obtained may require validation through larger-scale investigations and the inclusion of multiple centers in future trials. Second, only the clinical observation about gastrointestinal function and recovery quality were assessed in this study. Subsequently, we plan to determine the intestinal transit time by scintillation imaging, radionuclide or telemetry capsule system to evaluate the effect of TPVB and ESPB on postoperative gastrointestinal function in patients following thoracoscopic lobectomy.

Third, the anesthesiologist in charge were not blinded in this study, but this design was necessary for ethical concerns of patients’ safety. However, there was a bias in perioperative medication use. Follow-up studies shall continue to optimize trial protocol and reduce results bias.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the preoperative ultrasound-guided single TPVB and ESPB could reduce the postoperative abdominal distention rates, shorten the time to first exhaust and defecation, decrease the perioperative sufentanil consumption and postoperative VAS pain score, elevate the postoperative QoR-15 score in patients undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy.

Stress-Dependent Permeability of Porous Polyimide Under Stress Loading and Unloading
Authors: Ronghe Xu, Dongyang Shen, Liqin Wang, Maokuan Bao, Xiaoli Zhao, Weikun Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Porous polyimide (PPI) is a key material for the oil supply parts in the bearing units of the momentum wheels and reaction wheels. The permeability of PPI determines the oil supply rate in the bearing contact area, thus affecting the performance of the bearing units and wheels. However, few studies have focused on the effect of alternating stress on the permeability of PPI in running bearing units. Here, a study on the stress-dependent permeability of PPI under stress loading and unloading is conducted. After experimental and theoretical analyses, permeability is reduced at most by 37% when the effective stress σeff is between 0.75 and 26.45 MPa. The elastic-structural deformation of capillary cross-section and tortuosity sensitivity lead to a negative correlation between σeff and permeability, and permeability hysteresis. When σeff is greater than 15 MPa and the pressure gradient is greater than 40 MPa/m, the permeability decreases and then increases during stress unloading because of the blocking and unblocking of detached particles. A fitting formula for the tortuosity variation caused by detached particles is proposed to describe this permeability behavior. This study contributes to the application and improvement of PPI in bearing units and similar working conditions.

Introduction

A growing number of spacecraft need to maintain high control accuracy over a long service life. Momentum wheel or reaction wheel is often used as an attitude control actuator for long-lived spacecraft because of its high control accuracy, long duration and no need for propellant. Since the bearing unit is the main source of failure for the momentum wheel and reaction wheel, the long-term reliable operation of the bearing unit is critical for the spacecraft to perform its mission. To prevent lubrication failure of the bearing unit, the lubrication system needs to supply a small amount of oil to the bearings over a long period of time. This ensures that the bearing unit maintains a low and stable friction torque. Porous polyimide (PPI) is an ideal material for the lubrication system owing to its advantages, such as excellent mechanical properties, low density and thermal stability. , PPI is manufactured into the oil reservoir and the bearing retainer in the lubrication system. They are saturated with oil before the bearing unit is assembled, and they supply oil to the bearings by centrifugal force and heat when the wheels are in operation. This design can satisfy most of the lubrication needs of the bearing unit. Hence, PPI is widely used in lubrication system.

To improve the performance of PPI, many researchers have studied the tribological properties, − oil supply behavior, , oil exchange behavior and thermal stability of PPI. Current research suggests that the flow of oil in PPI is a seepage process, so Darcy’s law can be used to analysis the oil supply behavior. According to Darcy’s law, permeability is a key indicator to measure the oil supply capacity of PPI. Under actual working conditions, the oil reservoir and retainer are subjected to alternating stress due to the alternating temperature and the repeated impact. Furthermore, numerous studies have shown that the permeability of porous materials changes with stress, a phenomenon known as stress-dependent permeability. This indicates that the permeability of PPI may change during bearing running, which in turn affects the oil supply rate in the bearing contact area and the performance of the bearing units. However, fewer studies focus on the effect of stress on the permeability of PPI. An adequate study of the stress-dependent permeability of PPI is an important means of clarifying the change in the oil supply capacity of the oil reservoir and retainer under actual working conditions. It is of great significance for the evaluation, application and optimization of the oil supply capacity of PPI, and for ensuring the long-term reliable operation of the bearing units. It is also instructive for the application of PPI under working conditions similar to those of oil reservoir and retainer.

In the field of porous polymers, fewer scholars have studied the stress-dependent permeability of porous polymers under alternating stress. However, the stress-dependent permeability of rocks has been extensively studied. Since both rock and PPI are porous media, the methods and theories used to study the stress-dependent permeability of rocks are of important reference value for studying the stress-dependent permeability of PPI. Due to the differences in working conditions and material properties between PPI and rock, existing mechanisms and theories may not be fully applicable to PPI. Therefore, it is necessary to study the stress-dependent permeability of PPI.

Research into the stress-dependent permeability of rocks can be divided into experimental and theoretical studies. Experimental studies mainly observe the effect of stress on permeability by building a physical simulation system. Stress is determined by confining pressure, pore pressure and temperature. The study results show that the stress-dependent permeability of rocks directly affects oil/gas production, , geothermal production, the capacity of underground gas storage, etc. Effective stress is a key indicator for analyzing stress-dependent permeability. The mechanical behavior of the porous media microstructure is an important basis for revealing stress sensitivity. , Theoretical studies mainly include fitting models and physical models. Among these, although fitting models , can quantify stress-dependent permeability, they are lacking in the physical characteristics of porous media. Compared to fitting models, physical models mainly consider the elastic-structural deformation − and elasto-plastic deformation of the capillary cross-section, thus better reflecting the physical characteristics of porous media. As the research progressed, the physical models can describe not only stress-dependent permeability under alternative stress but also porosity stress sensitivity and permeability–porosity relationships. This makes the model reflect the physical characteristics of porous media more comprehensively and accurately.

Here, the stress-dependent permeability of PPI is studied experimentally and theoretically. First, physical quantities of PPI, such as permeability, porosity and pore size distribution, are measured to clarify the basic properties of the experimental samples. The representativeness of the PPI samples is confirmed by comparing their basic properties with those in the published literature. Second, the stress-dependent permeability experiment under a single stress cycle is conducted by a physical simulation system under the working conditions of the bearing units. The mechanism of stress-dependent permeability is revealed by analyzing the experimental results and the PPI microstructure. Third, the experimental results of stress-dependent permeability and stress-dependent porosity are analyzed using multiple theoretical models to further identify the influencing factors of stress sensitivity. A fitting formula for tortuosity variation is proposed and the Xu’s model is improved to describe the effect of detached particles on permeability. This theoretical analysis and improvement are of great significance for quantifying, comparing and improving the stress-dependent permeability of PPI.

The pore size distribution of PPI can be described using the fractal scale model, and the stress-dependent permeability of PPI can be analyzed using the model based on fractal theory. Experimental Analysis of Stress-Dependent Permeability

Experimental results of the stress-dependent permeability of PPI are shown in Figure . After analyzing the extreme values in Figure , it is found that normalized permeability is 0.99 at the minimum effective stress 0.75 MPa. It demonstrates that the lubricant permeability of PPI is close to the permeability of PPI, and the interaction between PI and PAO 4129 has a minor effect on permeability. The normalized permeability reaches the minimum value of 0.63 when the effective stress σeff is between 21 and 25 MPa. Some experimental results are more scattered in Figure , such as the results of experiment SP1. A similar phenomenon has been observed in rocks, but the mechanism of the phenomenon is incompletely studied. The possible reason for the PPI sample is as follows. The PI particles in the PPI samples are both deformed and undeformed, as shown in Figure . The heterogeneity in particle shape may lead to heterogeneity in pore structure. This reason causes the local pore deformations in the PPI samples and the interactions between the local pore deformations to have a random effect on the permeability under σeff, leading to a scatter in the normalized permeability.

Experimental results of stress-dependent permeability.

According to the stress-dependent permeability behavior, Figure can be divided into two regions. Region I is less than 15 MPa and Region II is greater than 15 MPa. In Region I, permeability basicaly shows a decreasing trend during stress loading and an increasing trend during stress unloading (Type 1). In each set of experiments, permeability under stress loading is greater than permeability under stress unloading when σeff is the same. This means that PPI has permeability hysteresis under alternating σeff. The above results show that the stress-dependent permeability behavior of PPI in Region I is typical. The difference between Regions I and II is the changes in permeability under stress unloading. The stress-dependent permeability behavior in Region II under stress unloading can be divided into three types. Type 1: The permeability increases in experiments SP2 and SP3. Type 2: The permeability decreases in experiments SP9 and SP12. Type 3: The permeability first decreases and then increases in experiments SP5 and SP6. This indicates that the permeability changes in Region II under stress unloading vary from experiment to experiment.

To discuss the influence of σeff on permeability stress sensitivity and permeability hysteresis, the normalized permeability range (NPR) and the hysteresis loss of normalized permeability (HLNP) are used to quantify the permeability stress sensitivity and permeability hysteresis, respectively. Among these, the NPR is the difference between the maximum and minimum normalized permeabilities under stress loading or unloading. The NPR is selected because it can reflect the permeability stress sensitivity even if the stress-dependent permeability behavior are different. The HLNP is the difference between normalized permeabilities at the initial effective stress under stress loading and unloading. The NPR and the HLNP need to be further processed to eliminate the influence of the σeff range and retain the influence of the σeff magnitude. The processing method is to obtain the mean value of the NPR or the HLNP for experiments having the same temperature. This is because experiments with the same temperature have similar mean σeff, and experiments with the same inlet pressure have similar σeff ranges. After processing, the mean values of the NPR and the HLNP under different mean σeff are shown in Figure .

Influence of mean effective stress on the normalized permeability range (NPR) and the hysteresis loss of normalized permeability (HLNP).

In Figure , the mean σeff of experiments under 25 °C, 50 and 80 °C are 4.4 MPa, 12.9 and 23.1 MPa, respectively. The larger the mean value of the HLNP, the greater the permeability hysteresis. The larger the mean value of the NPR, the greater the permeability stress sensitivity. According to Figure , permeability stress sensitivity under stress loading and permeability hysteresis decrease with increasing mean σeff. Permeability stress sensitivity under stress unloading first decreases and then increases with increasing σeff. This is because the stress-dependent permeability behavior in Region II under stress unloading has changed compared to that in Region I. The experimental results of stress-dependent permeability show that the permeability of the oil reservoir and retainer will decrease under alternating stress during the operation of the bearing units. This will cause a reduction in the oil supply rate in the bearing contact area and affect the performance of the bearing units. Therefore, the stress-dependent permeability of PPI should be considered in the design of bearing units to ensure the normal operation of the bearing units.

Figure shows the stress-dependent permeability mechanism of PPI based on the basic properties of PPI, experimental results of stress-dependent permeability and related theories. − In Figure , the pores in PPI are taken as capillary bundles. The capillary size distribution is the pore size distribution in eq . The factors leading to stress-dependent permeability can be divided into the factors of the capillary cross-section and the factors of the capillary length. The factors of the capillary cross-section include elastic deformation and structural deformation. The elastic deformation of capillary cross sections is caused by the elastic deformation of PI particles because the pores in a PPI are surrounded by PI particles. All the pore structures in a PPI have elastic deformation. The structural deformation of capillary cross sections is caused by the structural instability of pore structures such as the quadrilateral and pentagonal structures in the PPI. In Figure , the quadrangular structure is selected to represent the pore structures with structural instability. The triangular structure only has elastic deformation because triangles have good structural stability. Under stress loading, elastic and structural deformation together cause the pores to narrow, thus causing a decrease in permeability as the σeff increases. Under stress unloading, elastic deformation recovers but structural deformation does not recover. This results in the pores opening as the σeff decreases, thus causing an increase in permeability. However, the permeability cannot return to its original value, i.e. there is a permeability hysteresis under alternating σeff.

Stress-dependent permeability mechanism of PPI.

The factors of the capillary length include tortuosity sensitivity and detached particles. Tortuosity sensitivity is the change in tortuosity under σeff. This is because, in a PPI, capillaries consist of pores that are connected by throats in multiple directions. Fluid meanders through capillaries in the PPI and tortuosity is used to describe the tortuosity of capillaries. Under σeff, connectivity changes due to the deformation of capillary cross sections, which in turn alters the flow path of fluid and induces changes in tortuosity. The effect of detached particles is analyzed as follows. The elastic-structural deformation and tortuosity sensitivity can explain the stress-dependent permeability behavior of PPI, except for the permeability trend under stress unloading in Region II. This indicates that there are still other factors that affect the stress-dependent permeability. Particle blockage is most likely to affect the stress-dependent permeability under stress unloading in Region II. This is because the lubricant color changed from colorless to yellow after the experiments SP5 and SP6. It suggests that the outlet lubricant contains PI since only PPI (yellow) and PAO 4129 (colorless) are used in the experiment. Therefore, particle blockage is likely to exist in PPI. To clarify the presence and mechanism of particle blockage, PI in the outlet lubricant is extracted and observed. The observation results are shown in Figure .

Micrograph (a) and size distribution (b) of the detached particles.

First, PI in the outlet lubricant is in particle form, as shown in Figure a. Second, the statistical results for 100 particle diameters are shown in Figure b. In Figure b, the particle diameter ranges from 2 to 11 μm, with a larger proportion of particles having a diameter of 3–7 μm. The size of particles in the outlet lubricant is closer to the size of deformed particles in the PPI. Besides, no particles are found adsorbed on the pores in the PPI because the particles are firmly bonded to each other in Figure . Therefore, the particles in the outlet lubricant are detached particles from the deformed particles in the PPI. Compared to Figure a, the minimum diameter of detached particles is larger than the minimum diameter of pores. This indicates that some pores can be blocked by detached particles. Based on the above analysis, the source and size of the detached particles suggest that there is a particle blockage in the PPI.

The influence mechanism of detached particles on stress-dependent permeability of PPI is as follows. When the σeff is greater than or equal to the critical effective stress, some particles will detach from the solid skeleton under stress loading. However, the detached particles are not migrated by the lubricant because of the squeezing of the solid skeleton. Under stress unloading, the detached particles are released from the solid skeleton by the lubricant propulsion and the recovery of solid skeleton deformation, resulting in particle migration and particle blockage. The blockage causes a reduction in the flowable channels and a change in tortuosity. Permeability first decreases under stress unloading because the effect of blockage on permeability is greater than the effect of pores opening. As the σeff is further reduced, more detached particles are released, which in turn leads to a further reduction in permeability. When the pore diameters return to being larger than the detached particle diameters, the detached particles are migrated away from the blocked pores and the blocked pores are unblocked. Thus, permeability begins to increase with decreasing σeff. The above mechanism causes the permeability of SP5 and SP6 to first decrease and then increase during stress unloading. For SP9 and SP12, permeability only decreases during stress unloading because the σeff ranges of SP9 and SP12 are smaller than those of SP5 and SP6. The pore diameters do not return to larger than the detached particle diameters and unblocking does not occur. For SP2 and SP3, permeability increases under stress unloading because the pressure gradients of SP2 and SP3 (10 MPa/m) are smaller than the pressure gradients of SP5 and SP6 (40 MPa/m). The pressure gradient is the change in fluid pressure per unit length in the direction of fluid flow. The pressure gradient is calculated by (p in – p out)/L 0. Under a smaller pressure gradient, the force of the lubricant pushing against the detached particles cannot overcome the force between the detached particles and the solid skeleton. As a result, the detached particles cannot be released from the solid skeleton and blocking does not occur.

It is worth noting that the influence mechanism of particle blockage in PPI under alternating σeff is different from the mechanism in most existing studies. In Kozhevnikov et al. and Zhang et al., particle blockage occurs during stress loading. Therefore, particle blockage does not reduce the permeability under stress unloading. If the detached particles in PPI are also released during stress loading, the permeability under stress unloading will not decrease. However, the result of this hypothesis does not match the experimental results of PPI. This further indicates that the detached particles in PPI are released during stress unloading. One reason for the unique particle blockage mechanism of PPI is the blocking particles in PPI are different from those in other porous media. In Kozhevnikov et al., the blocking particles are the absorbed particles. When the pressure gradient exceeds a critical value, the absorbed particles are moved by the fluid, and particle blockage occurs during stress loading rather than stress unloading. In Zhang et al., the blocking particles are the crushed coals. Particle blockage occurs when the coals are crushed under stress loading. For PPI, the blocking particles are the particles detached from the solid skeleton and the detachment occurs because of internal squeezing of the solid skeleton. Therefore, even if the particles detach from the solid skeleton, they are constrained by the solid skeleton due to the increased internal squeezing under stress loading. Under stress unloading, internal squeezing decreases with decreasing σeff, which in turn leads to the release, migration and blockage of detached particles.

Theoretical Analysis of Stress-Dependent Permeability

To further clarify and quantify the stress-dependent permeability mechanism of PPI, Xu’s model, Tan’s model, Cao’s model and Huang’s model are used for theoretical analysis. Xu’s model considers the elastic-structural deformation of the capillary cross-section and tortuosity sensitivity. Tan’s model considers the elastic deformation of the capillary cross-section. Huang’s model considers the elasto-plastic deformation of the capillary cross-section. Cao’s model considers the elastic-structural deformation of the capillary cross-section. Different models calculate the same factors in distinct ways. In the theoretical analysis, it is not comprehensive to only use the theoretical models to fit the experimental results of stress-dependent permeability. This is because influencing factors of stress sensitivity can not only cause stress-dependent permeability but also stress-dependent porosity. A theoretical model can be considered applicable to PPI when both the stress sensitivities of permeability and porosity are well described by the theoretical model. Therefore, experimental data on stress-dependent porosity under stress loading is also used in the theoretical analysis. The reason for only using the stress-dependent porosity under stress loading is that the porosity is only reduced by a maximum of 11% under stress loading. Since the stress-dependent porosity is also affected by the elastic-structural deformation, the porosity stress sensitivity under stress unloading is less than the porosity stress sensitivity under stress loading. The theoretical model not only fits the experimental data on the stress-dependent permeability but also the experimental data on the stress-dependent porosity. The model also predicts the stress-dependent porosity from the fitting results of the stress-dependent permeability and vice versa. Conclusion

In this paper, the stress-dependent permeability of PPI under stress loading and unloading has been studied experimentally and theoretically. Based on the working conditions of the bearing units, the experimental study measured the lubricant permeability of PPI at different temperatures, inlet pressures and confining pressures using a physical simulation system. The experimental results illustrate that the permeability is reduced by up to 37% when the effective stress σeff is between 0.75 and 26.45 MPa. Both the permeability hysteresis and the negative correlation between permeability and σeff are caused by the elastic-structural deformation of capillary cross-section and tortuosity sensitivity. When σeff is greater than 15 MPa and the pressure gradient is greater than 40 MPa/m, the detachment, release, blocking, unblocking and migration of solid skeleton particles cause the permeability to first decrease and then increase under stress unloading.

The theoretical study uses different theoretical models to analyze the experimental results. For a comprehensive analysis, the experimental results of stress-dependent porosity under stress loading are also used. The theoretical results show that the model, which has the same influencing factors as PPI, has the best performance in fitting stress sensitivities, predicting stress sensitivities, predicting the permeability–porosity relationships and solving physical parameters. This further indicates the influencing factors of stress-dependent permeability in PPI. The tortuosity variation and the permeability behavior influenced by detached particles are described by proposing a fitting formula and improving a theoretical model, respectively. This study has a good potential for the design, evaluation and improvement of the oil supply capacity of PPI.

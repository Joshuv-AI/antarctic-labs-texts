Sports action estimation and recognition based on dynamic spatiotemporal graph convolution
Authors: Fengling Zhang, Huiyang Xiao, Weitao Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To achieve accurate estimation and recognition of sports movements, an action pose estimation model was first constructed based on an improved graph convolutional network. Then, a motion recognition model was designed based on a dynamic spatiotemporal graph convolutional network. The experimental results showed that the motion pose estimation model designed in this study could detect a joint percentage of 0.986, and the success rate of joint point detection was 0.975. Compared with the real labels, the estimated joint points were the most similar, with a maximum average pose point position error of only 0.25. The action recognition model based on a dynamic spatiotemporal graph convolutional network achieved the best recognition accuracy and efficiency, with an average accuracy of 0.959, a frame rate of 192.988 FPS, and a computational load of only 98.754 FLOPs. Its Top-1 and Top-5 accuracies were as high as 0.96 and 0.97, respectively, indicating a strong ability to identify behavior from different perspectives and individuals. This study improves sports performance and advances sports research by accurately recognizing and estimating the movements of athletes.

Introduction

The rapid development of society and technology has made video a core medium for contemporary information dissemination, playing a key role in information transmission, knowledge dissemination, and entertainment and leisure. Among them, human motion recognition has become a key branch of video content analysis and a research hotspot. Human motion recognition aims to understand different movements and behavioral patterns of the human body through automatic recognition of videos and images, and has been broadly utilized in different fields such as medical health and sports training [1]. For the sports field, analyzing and identifying athletes' movements during training and competition through technological means can capture real-time details of athletes' movements, which helps coaches develop training plans and optimize training results. Analyzing athletes' movement data is beneficial for coaches and athletes to have a more intuitive understanding of movement issues and improve their competitive level. Meanwhile, action estimation and recognition based on computer technology are beneficial for improving the informatization and systematization level of sports management [2, 3]. However, there are numerous types of sports movements, and their standardization and clarity make them highly dynamic, covert, and professional. The changing postures of sports pose a daunting challenge for Action Recognition (AR) in sports videos. Traditional AR techniques are difficult to adapt to the dynamic and diverse nature of sports movements [4]. However, as the artificial intelligence technology rapidly develops, advanced neural network models combined with sports video data provide strong support for high-precision recognition and classification of athlete movements, especially Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), which exhibit excellent AR accuracy [5]. GCN, by constructing a graph structure of skeletal data and extracting joint point and joint connection features, helps to achieve accurate recognition of complex actions. Therefore, to accurately estimate and recognize sports movements and address the complexity and diversity of sports movements, the study first innovatively introduces dynamic, temporal, and spatial probabilities based on GCN, and designs an action posture estimation model. Then, by combining GCN and Temporal Convolutional Neural Networks (TCN), an innovative improvement was proposed for the Spatial Temporal Graph Convolutional Networks (ST-GCN), and an AR model is designed.

The research mainly contains four sections. The first section outlines the current research status of action posture estimation and recognition. The second section elaborates on the construction process of the sports action posture estimation and recognition model. The third section conducts performance testing and application analysis on the designed action pose estimation and recognition model. The fourth section summarizes and generalizes the experimental results. The core innovations of the research at the methodological level are as follows. Firstly, in the pose estimation stage, a dynamic ST-GCN was constructed that integrates spatial distance information and motion velocity information, and a second-order connected adjacency matrix based on motion intensity was introduced to enhance the modeling ability of the model for potential motion relationships between non-directly connected key points. Secondly, in the AR stage, a long short-term memory network is used to replace the TCN structure in the original ST-GCN, reducing the computational complexity of the model. Finally, a non-maximum suppression strategy is introduced to remove redundant pose frames and preserve action information with significant dynamic features, further improving recognition efficiency and accuracy. The above improvements collectively constitute a sports action estimation and recognition method based on dynamic spatiotemporal graph convolution, providing a new technical path for complex action analysis in sports videos.

Related works

Action pose estimation and recognition is a key research direction in the computer vision, with broad application prospects in sports training, fitness guidance, security monitoring, and other areas. However, the complexity and diversity of actions have increased the difficulty of recognition, and numerous scholars have carried out research on it. Garg et al. focused on yoga movement classification and proposed a new model to improve classification accuracy. The research method involved using the MediaPipe library for skeletal processing of body keypoint detection, and comparing the effectiveness of different deep learning models on skeletal and non skeletal images. The research results showed that skeleton processing had a positive impact on improving the accuracy of yoga movement classification [6]. Wang et al. developed an improved single-stage pose estimation algorithm Yolov8-sp to solve the challenge of athlete posture detection. This algorithm improved the Yolov8 architecture through multidimensional feature fusion and attention mechanism, achieving accurate estimation of human pose. At the same time, angle extraction was performed on key joints in motion scenes, and polynomial correction was used to adjust the angle changes between consecutive frames. The experiment outcomes identified the feasibility and effect of this method in athlete posture estimation [7]. Fu et al. suggested an enhanced YOLOv7-Pose algorithm for human pose estimation and AR in fitness activities. The accuracy of human keypoint detection and action classification in the model has been improved by adding classification functions, introducing coordinate attention and an improved ConvNeXt network structure, optimizing the spatial pyramid pool structure and adopting the EIOU regression function. The experiment findings denoted that the improved algorithm realized an mAP value of 95.9% on the self-made test set, significantly improving the accuracy of AR and keypoint estimation [8]. Chen et al. developed an integrated, non-invasive framework for accurately predicting abnormal knee joint contact forces in patients undergoing anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction during daily activities, combining enhanced musculoskeletal modeling with deep learning architectures that incorporate spatiotemporal attention to improve prediction accuracy across multiple motion tasks. The results showed that the proposed method had the best predictive performance on all three motion tasks [9]. Xu et al. proposed a multimodal comprehensive framework for assessing ligament fatigue life. The research results showed that the proposed computational framework broke the static load constraints of traditional fatigue tests and achieved dynamic biomechanical analysis and fatigue life prediction under real motion conditions [10].

Lee et al. proposed OpenPose real-time pose estimation technology based on ResNet-50, aiming to ensure the safety of on-site workers in high-risk environments. Through training and evaluation, this technique has demonstrated high accuracy and efficiency on various datasets, particularly in estimating the actions of on-site workers [11]. Luo et al. captured six types of human motion signals using frequency modulated continuous wave radar and improved the traditional single channel input of convolutional neural networks by using a dual channel fusion network of ResNet18 combined with Inception V1 and convolutional block attention module. Experiments showed that the dual feature fusion structure improved human motion detection accuracy by 1–4% compared to single feature domain recognition, verifying the robustness of the model's recognition ability [12]. Guo et al. addressed the limitation of only focusing on local information in human AR by incorporating a coordinated attention mechanism based on ST-GCN to enhance attention to key information. Concurrently, a novel sampling area partitioning strategy was implemented to enhance the correlation between local and global information. The experimental results demonstrated that the Top-1 accuracy of this method attained 84.86%, substantiating the model's efficacy and robustness [13]. Zhang explored the application of multi-mode sensors combined with optical motion capture technology in badminton player motion recognition. Real time motion data was obtained through sensors installed on athletes. The results of the study showed that the technique can accurately identify the athletes' movement postures and is effective and practical in recognizing the movements of badminton players [14].

In conclusion, significant progress has been made in the estimation and recognition of motion pose. However, the complexity and dynamism of sports movements remain a challenge in current research, and traditional techniques are difficult to adapt to. The application of models such as GCN provides a new path for high-precision recognition of sports movements, and research has focused on improving GCN.

Action estimation and recognition based on dynamic ST-GCN

The complexity and diversity of sports movements pose great challenges to recognition algorithms. Based on GCN, a motion pose estimation model is first designed. Then, based on this, a dynamic ST-GCN AR model is constructed.

Sports action posture estimation

The estimation of human body posture is the foundation of AR. By accurately estimating the key points of the human body, key information can be provided for AR, helping models understand sports movements [15, 16]. In this regard, the study first conducts action posture estimation based on GCN. GCN is a deep learning model that has been specifically designed for processing graph-structured data. GCN comprises two main domains: the spectral domain and the spatial domain. The research adopts the spatial domain-based GCN for attitude estimation. The input of GCN is the node feature matrix in the graph data and the adjacency matrix of the graph. The node feature matrix contains the initial feature representation of each node, and the adjacency matrix describes the connection relationship between nodes [17, 18]. However, under the complex spatiotemporal pose changes in sports videos, the potential motion relationships between key points are not directly connected through fixed skeleton structures. Relying solely on fixed connection relationships will limit the processing power of GCN [19, 20]. Therefore, different dynamic GCNs are constructed based on the different motion states of key points. Firstly, the distance information of key points in space and the movement speed in time position are integrated to dynamically learn the relationship between different key points and construct dynamic spatiotemporal GCN.

Workflow of action attitude estimation network

In Fig. 3, the input is a two-dimensional human pose sequence. Firstly, the input data is standardized through a batch normalization layer, and then the feature channel dimension is enhanced through a convolutional layer. The processed features are sequentially subjected to batch normalization, ReLU activation function, and Dropout layer to enhance the model's generalization ability and prevent overfitting. The construction of dynamic spatiotemporal adjacency matrix relies on K-means clustering of key point spatial positions and motion velocity characteristics, and different sports movements have significant differences in rhythm, amplitude, and periodicity, which may lead to clustering results having a certain impact on the structure of the dynamic graph. Specifically, for action sequences with drastic changes in rhythm or significant differences in amplitude, the clustering results of joint points at different stages of the same action may fluctuate, thereby affecting the stability of the adjacency matrix. However, the dynamic spatiotemporal adjacency matrix constructed in the study does not rely solely on single frame clustering results, but jointly encodes spatial distance information and motion velocity information within the range of the center frame and adjacent frames, and smoothly adjusts the connection weights through the nonlinear mapping introduced in Eq. (3), which to some extent alleviates the sensitivity of clustering result fluctuations to graph structure. In addition, in the second-order connected adjacency matrix, keypoints are classified into three categories (vigorous, moderate, stable) based on the intensity of the movement. This hierarchical clustering strategy further enhances the adaptability of the dynamic graph structure to different movement rhythms. However, the selection of the number of clusters still has a potential impact on the performance of the model. In the study, the number of clusters was determined to be 8 (for space velocity joint clustering) and 4 (for second-order motion intensity clustering) on the validation set through grid search. This configuration can demonstrate good generalization stability between different action categories.

Action recognition based on improved ST-GCN

After completing the estimation of human motion posture, the study further identifies and analyzes the motion patterns of athletes based on the extracted keypoint information. The study conducts AR research based on the action posture estimation model. ST-GCN is an AR method that combines GCN and TCN to capture spatiotemporal features in action sequences. ST-GCN constructs a spatiotemporal graph using human joints as nodes and extracts features using GCN to achieve AR. The network structure composition is denoted in Fig. 4.Fig. 4Schematic diagram of ST-GCN structure composition

Schematic diagram of ST-GCN structure composition

In Fig. 4, the input layer of ST-GCN is responsible for receiving keypoint information of human posture, and its spatiotemporal graph convolutional layer consists of multiple ST-GCN units. The ST-GCN unit first connects the joints in the same frame as edges and the same joints in consecutive frames as edges based on the connectivity of human joints to obtain spatial and temporal maps, respectively. Then, GCN operation is utilized to extract spatial relationship features between joints and TCN operation to capture temporal dynamic features of actions on the spatiotemporal graph. Finally, the adaptive graph partitioning strategy is utilized to divide the image into multiple subgraphs, and different convolution kernels are used for convolution operations to emphasize important joints. The pooling layer is responsible for pooling the output of ST-GCN to reduce feature dimensions and preserve key information. Finally, with the help of fully connected layers, the output is mapped to the action category space to complete AR classification [21–23].

However, the traditional ST-GCN has a high computational cost and a relatively large model size, which limits its application in AR. To meet the needs of sports movements in terms of recognition and analysis, the traditional ST-GCN has been made more lightweight and dynamically optimized. Performance testing and application analysis of sports action estimation and recognition model

To assess the effect of the sports action estimation and recognition model designed in the research, performance testing and application effect analysis of the model were carried out, and the results were analyzed and discussed.

Performance testing of sports action posture estimation model

The experiment was carried out on the Ubuntu 18.04 operating system, with Pytorch 1.7 as the deep learning framework, Python 3.8 as the programming language, Intel (R) Xeon (R) Silver 4214 2.20 GHz as the central processor, and GTX1080 as the image processor. Firstly, performance testing was performed on the motion pose estimation model using COCO and Kinetics skeleton as experimental datasets. The COCO dataset contains a large number of images from different scenes and poses, which annotate the position information of human keypoints and can be used for human pose estimation. The Kinetics skeleton dataset focuses on skeleton keypoint information and can be used for skeleton keypoint estimation and recognition. The above dataset was randomly divided into a training set and a testing set in an 8:2 ratio. In terms of data augmentation, random horizontal flipping, random rotation (with an angle range of ± 30°), random scaling (with a scale range of 0.8 to 1.2), and random cropping strategies in the time dimension were adopted for the input images and pose sequences to enhance the model's generalization ability. The input frame length wasa set to 32 frames. For action sequences with less than 32 frames, a cyclic filling method was used to fill in, and for those exceeding 32 frames, uniform sampling was performed. In terms of normalization processing, the pose was first cropped and scaled based on the detected human bounding box to normalize the keypoint coordinates to the [0,1]. Subsequently, using the center points of the shoulder and hip as reference points, the Euclidean distances from all relevant nodes to the center point were calculated and divided by the body proportion factor to standardize the postural sequence. During the training phase, an Adam optimizer with a batch size of 32 and an initial learning rate of 0.001 was used for a total of 120 iterations. The loss function used mean square error (MSE) loss to calculate the difference between the predicted joint position and the true annotation. The implementation details of the dynamic adjacency matrix are as follows: First, with the center frame as a reference, the spatial coordinates and motion velocity of each key point were concatenated to generate a feature vector; then, the K-means clustering algorithm (setting 8 clusters) was used to cluster all key point features. Edge connections were constructed between keypoints belonging to the same cluster, and the connection weights were calculated according to Eq. (3) to obtain a dynamic spatiotemporal adjacency matrix containing spatial distance and motion velocity information. To ensure the fairness of the experiment, both the baseline model and the proposed model were tested under the same conditions.

To quantitatively evaluate the independent contributions of various improvement modules in the dynamic ST-GCN framework, ablation experiments were designed on the NTU RGB + D test set. Performance testing and application analysis of sports action recognition model

The performance and application effects of dynamic ST-GCN in sports AR were analyzed, and NTU RGB + D and Penn Action were selected as the experimental datasets. The NTU RGB + D dataset contains 60 types of actions with a total of 56,880 samples, which can be used for human AR. Penn Action contains 2326 video sequences, covering different types of sports such as baseball throwing, tennis serving, bowling, skipping rope, squats, etc. In terms of training configuration for AR models, the NTU RGB + D dataset adopted two standard evaluation protocols: cross subject and cross perspective, both of which allocate training and testing sets according to the official recommended partitioning method. The Penn Action dataset was randomly divided into a training set and a testing set in an 8:2 ratio. The length of the input posture sequence was uniformly set to 32 frames, and for sequences with less than 32 frames, a cyclic filling method was used to fill in. In terms of data augmentation, a strategy of random horizontal flipping, random cropping in the time dimension, and random dropout of joint points (dropout ratio of 0.1) was adopted. During the training process, the batch size was set to 64, the initial learning rate was 0.001, and the Adam optimizer was used. The learning rate decayed by 0.1 times every 50 cycles, resulting in a total of 120 training cycles. The loss function adopted cross entropy loss for optimizing the classification of action categories. In the implementation of dynamic ST-GCN, the construction process of the dynamic adjacency matrix was as follows: based on the motion speed information of each keypoint, the K-means clustering algorithm (with 4 clusters) was used to divide the keypoints into three categories: vigorous motion, moderate motion, and stable motion. Based on this, a second-order connected adjacency matrix was constructed to enhance the modeling ability of the model for potential relationships between long-distance joint points in large amplitude movements. Traditional ST-GCN used TCN to process time series data, but the computational complexity of TCN was relatively high. Dynamic ST-GCN replaced TCN with LSTM. Although LSTM was theoretically more complex than TCN, its gating mechanism could effectively reduce unnecessary computations while preserving critical long-term dependency information. Meanwhile, dynamic ST-GCN introduced a dynamic graph structure that dynamically adjusts the connection relationship of the graph based on the different states of actions, effectively avoiding redundant calculations in fixed graph structures. In addition, dynamic ST-GCN divided the graph into multiple subgraphs through an adaptive graph segmentation strategy, with each subgraph corresponding to a specific motion state, effectively reducing the complexity of global computation. In addition, dynamic ST-GCN removed redundant pose frames through non maximum suppression when processing continuous pose frames, retaining only action information with significant dynamic features. This strategy can reduce the size of input data and lower the computational complexity of the model. In terms of hardware adaptability, the lightweight design of dynamic ST-GCN enables it to run on low-power embedded systems or mobile platforms. Dynamic ST-GCN can effectively reduce device power consumption by reducing computation and memory usage.

Based on the above results, the proposed method has achieved good performance in the application of sports action estimation and recognition tasks, but there are still certain limitations. Firstly, in complex sports competition scenarios, mutual occlusion between athletes and between athletes and field equipment is common. The current model still lacks robustness in pose estimation when key points are occluded, and the missing joint information caused by occlusion will significantly affect recognition accuracy. Secondly, this study mainly focuses on the AR task of a single human body. For situations where multiple people collaborate in the same scene, such as basketball and soccer, the model has not yet explicitly modeled the spatial interaction relationship between multiple people, which limits its direct application in team motion analysis. In addition, there are certain fluctuations in the performance of the model under different shooting angles. Although the average recognition accuracy in cross angle experiments reached 0.93, the recognition accuracy under extreme angles, such as top-down or oblique large angles, still needs to be further improved. Finally, in terms of real-time deployment in actual sports scenarios, although the model reduced computational complexity to approximately 98.754 FLOPs and achieved a frame rate of 192.988 FPS through lightweight design, the inference latency and energy consumption of the model still need further optimization in resource limited mobile devices or embedded systems. Future research will focus on the above-mentioned issues, including introducing attention mechanisms to enhance robust modeling of occluded areas, extending graph convolution structures to support multi-person interaction analysis, and exploring techniques such as model compression and knowledge distillation to achieve more efficient edge deployment.

Conclusion

There are many types of movements in sports, and the complexity and diversity of movements increase the difficulty of AR. To achieve automatic classification and analysis of athlete movements, motion estimation and recognition models were constructed based on GCN and improved ST-GCN, respectively. The experiment findings denoted that the action posture estimation model achieved the best PCK index and PCP value, with high accuracy in keypoint and part estimation. The PDJ and joint detection success rates of this method were superior to other models, with maximum values reaching 0.986 and 0.975, respectively. Meanwhile, the model's prediction of key points was the most accurate, with MPJPE values below 0.25. The mAP value of the dynamic ST-GCN AR model could reach 0.959, the FPS value was 192.988, and the FLOPs value was 88.270.

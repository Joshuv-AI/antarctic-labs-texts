Immobilized Pd-EDTA complex supported on MnFe2O4 magnetic nanoparticles: a highly efficient and reusable new catalyst for esterification of aryl aldehydes
Authors: Mohammad Abushuhel, Magdi E A Zaki, M M Rekha, Noor Mazin Basheer, Subhashree Ray, Amrita Pal, Renu Sharma, Sobhi M Gomha, Abhayveer Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study details the efficient synthesis of superparamagnetic nanoparticles functionalized with a Pd-EDTA complex using a simple and effective method. Comprehensive characterization of the resulting heterogeneous catalyst was performed through techniques such as TEM, XPS, XRD, FE-SEM, VSM, FT-IR, ICP, and EDX, with precise palladium content measurement achieved via ICP. The immobilized palladium complex showed remarkable catalytic performance in esterifying aryl aldehydes. The system presents notable advantages, including ease of handling, reduced reaction times, and convenient magnetic separation, facilitating straightforward catalyst recovery. Additionally, the catalyst demonstrated exceptional recyclability, retaining high activity across several cycles. Although the study primarily focused on aryl aldehydes, the reactions consistently achieved excellent isolated yields, highlighting the potential of this magnetic system in sustainable and efficient fine chemical synthesis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11671-026-04629-z.Keywords: Magnetic, MnFe2O4, Aryl halides, EDTA, Esterification

Introduction

Over recent decades, the immobilization of various homogeneous catalysts onto magnetic nano-sized solid supports has become a highly effective approach for enhancing catalyst stability and catalytic performance, particularly in organo-catalysis and industrial applications [1–4]. The remarkable success of these heterogeneous immobilized catalysts can be attributed to their exceptional physical and chemical properties, including high surface area, excellent biocompatibility, low toxicity, and well-organized structures that provide a greater number of active sites per unit area compared to their bulk material counterparts [5–10]. Additionally, the eco-friendly and straightforward separation of magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs) from reaction mixtures using a magnetic field eliminates the need for labor-intensive filtration or centrifugation processes [11–13]. Consequently, functionalized MNPs have garnered significant attention for their versatile applications across industrial and research sectors, particularly as effective candidates for heavy metal ion removal from wastewater [1, 14, 15]. Among the many types of magnetic nanoparticles used as supports, biocompatible superparamagnetic manganese oxide nanoparticles have emerged as promising materials [16, 17]. Their simple preparation, cost-effectiveness, high surface area, and superparamagnetic properties make them excellent supports for heterogenizing and enhancing the catalytic efficiency of a wide range of homogeneous catalysts [2, 18–20].

Esterification is a cornerstone reaction in organic chemistry, widely employed for synthesizing a broad array of compounds with various applications [21, 22]. Notably, the esterification of aryl aldehydes has attracted significant attention due to the practical importance of the resulting esters and the unique kinetic and thermodynamic challenges involved in the process [18, 23]. Aryl aldehydes, characterized by a formyl group bonded to an aromatic ring, serve as crucial intermediates in fields such as pharmaceuticals, fragrance production, and materials science [24, 25]. Transforming these aldehydes into their ester derivatives not only yields compounds with enhanced chemical and physical properties but also introduces functional groups that open new pathways for further chemical modifications [26]. Although advancements in esterification techniques have been achieved, efficiently synthesizing aryl aldehyde esters continues to pose a notable challenge [27]. The inherent electrophilicity of the carbonyl group in these aldehydes influences their reactivity, demanding precise optimization of reaction conditions and catalyst systems to achieve both high yields and selectivity [28, 29]. Furthermore, rising environmental concerns and the push for sustainable approaches have heightened the focus on developing green processes that utilize reusable catalysts, environmentally benign solvents, and milder reaction conditions [30, 31].

Although MnFe2O4 is well-established as a durable magnetic support, the utilization of palladium complexes chelated with EDTA (Pd-EDTA) immobilized on these magnetic ferrite cores for the direct esterification of aryl aldehydes remains largely underexplored. Most studies in the literature emphasize simpler palladium immobilization techniques or focus on less demanding transformations. A critical research gap exists in showcasing how the strong chelating properties of EDTA, paired with the ease of recovery offered by the MnFe2O4 platform, can create a stable, recyclable, and highly active catalyst system that substantially shortens reaction times for this specific process. This study, therefore, presents the straightforward preparation and detailed characterization of a MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd catalyst, highlighting its exceptional catalytic performance and recyclability in the esterification of aryl aldehydes.

Experimental

Preparation of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd

The initial synthesis of MnFe2O4 was carried out using the co-precipitation method [32–34]. For functionalization, 3.0 mL of 3‐aminopropyl(triethoxy)silane (APTES) was precisely introduced into a suspension containing 2 g of MnFe2O4 in 35 mL of toluene. This solution was subjected to stirring under a nitrogen atmosphere at reflux for 24 h to ensure the reaction was complete. After the silanization step, the solid material was magnetically separated and thoroughly dried before proceeding to further processing. In the next phase, 1 g of nano-MnFe2O4@APTES was dispersed in ethanol and acetic acid (1:1, v/v) using an ultrasonic bath for 30 min. Subsequently, 3 mmol of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) was added to the dispersed solution, which was stirred under reflux conditions for 24 h. The resulting MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA nanoparticles were magnetically recovered, rinsed with toluene and acetone, and dried in an oven. Following this, 1 g of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA was dispersed in ethanol, followed by the addition of 3 mmol of palladium acetate (Pd(OAc)2). The mixture was refluxed for 24 h, after which 0.7 g of sodium borohydride (NaBH4) was added to the reaction vessel and allowed to react for an additional 3 h. The final nanoparticles, denoted as MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd, were isolated using magnetic decantation, washed with ethanol, and air-dried as depicted in Scheme 1.

Synthesis of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd

General procedure for esterification of aryl aldehydes

A reaction mixture comprising an aldehyde (3 mmol or 0.318 g), K2CO3 (1.2 mmol or 0.165 g), and the MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd catalyst (30 mg) was stirred in 15 mL of methanol at 50 °C under an oxygen atmosphere. The progress of the reaction was tracked using thin-layer chromatography (TLC) with an eluent system consisting of n-hexane and ethyl acetate in a 4:1 ratio. Once the reaction was completed, the catalyst was separated magnetically, and the remaining reaction mixture was carefully decanted. The obtained solution, including subsequent washings, was concentrated under reduced pressure, diluted with water, and extracted with 12 mL of ethyl acetate. The combined organic layers were washed with brine, dried over sodium sulfate, filtered, and concentrated again under reduced pressure. The crude product was then purified via column chromatography on silica gel using hexane/ethyl acetate (4:1) as the mobile phase, as illustrated in Scheme 2.

Catalyst characterizations

Figure 1 illustrates the FT-IR spectra of MnFe2O4, MnFe2O4@APTES, MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA, MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd, and the recovered catalyst. In Fig. 1a, a prominent absorption band near 572 cm⁻1 corresponds to the Fe − O stretching vibrations, a characteristic feature of MnFe2O4. Figure 1b reveals C–H stretching vibrations at approximately 2928 and 2957 cm⁻1, indicating the successful functionalization of MnFe2O4 nanoparticles with APTES. In Fig. 1c, a peak at 1655 cm⁻1, likely attributed to the stretching vibrations of the amide C═O bond, is observed alongside a peak around 1561 cm⁻1 associated with N–H bending vibrations. Additionally, peaks in the region of 1335 cm⁻1 are assigned to the carbon–oxygen ether groups from EDTA, and the broader peaks between 2500–3000 cm⁻1 correspond to hydroxyl groups within EDTA, further confirming the effective synthesis of the catalyst. In Fig. 1d, slight downfield shifts and minor spectral modifications are evident, suggesting coordination between palladium and the organic ligand within the MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd complex. Finally, Fig. 1e depicts the FT-IR spectrum of the recovered catalyst. A detailed analysis of this spectrum indicates no significant changes, affirming that the catalyst retains its structural integrity and stability throughout the reaction without degradation or notable alterations to its chemical composition.

Figure 2 illustrates the X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns of the MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd nanocomposite. Distinct peaks observed at 2θ values of 30°, 37°, 43°, 54°, 57°, and 62° confirm the successful synthesis of MnFe₂O₄ nanoparticles. These peaks correspond to the (220), (311), (400), (422), (511), and (440) crystal planes, characteristic of the cubic spinel structure of MnFe2O4. Notably, the XRD analysis indicates that the crystal structure of MnFe2O4 remains intact even after surface modification with various organic functional groups.

XRD spectrum of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) conducted in air provides evidence of functionalizing MnFe2O4 magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs) with organic layers and a palladium-based EDTA complex. Figure 3 displays the TGA profiles for MnFe2O4, MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA, and MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd. The profile for MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd indicates mass loss, which is attributed to the thermal decomposition of organic functional groups. A weight reduction below 250 °C is related to the removal of physically adsorbed solvents and surface hydroxyl groups. For MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd MNPs, roughly 17% weight loss is observed due to the breakdown of organic components. By comparison, MnFe2O4 and MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA exhibit weight losses of approximately 3% and 12%, respectively. These results validate the successful attachment of the EDTA palladium complex to the MnFe2O4 surface.

The elemental composition of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd was examined using an EDX spectrum, as illustrated in Fig. 4. This analysis confirmed the presence of nitrogen, iron, oxygen, manganese, silicon, carbon, and palladium within the catalyst, signifying the successful synthesis of the nanoparticles. Moreover, it verified that EDTA-Pd was effectively immobilized onto the surface of the MnFe2O4 magnetic nanoparticles. To quantify the palladium content in MnFe2O4@APTES/CC/GA-Pd, ICP-OES analysis determined a Pd concentration of 1.4 × 10⁻3 mol g⁻1. Additionally, ICP analysis was performed to assess Pd leaching following catalyst recycling. The findings revealed a Pd concentration of 1.3 × 10⁻3 mol g⁻1 in the reused catalysts, indicating negligible Pd leaching from the MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd structure.

EDS analysis of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was employed to analyze the morphological and structural properties of the MnFe₂O₄@APTES/EDTA–Pd nanocomposite. Figure 5 highlights that the synthesized nanoparticles predominantly exhibit a spherical shape with uniform dispersion, ranging in size between 40 and 85 nm. Following surface modification with APTES and EDTA–Pd, the particles largely retain their shape but display a slightly rougher surface, signifying successful functionalization and stabilization of palladium species. Overall, these findings affirm the successful synthesis of the MnFe₂O₄@APTES/EDTA–Pd nanocomposite with controlled and consistent dimensions.

SEM images of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd

The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) data obtained for the MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd catalyst are presented in Fig. 6, clearly demonstrating the presence of key elements such as Mn, O, N, C, Si, Fe, and Pd. Figure 6a highlights the specific contributions of these elements, confirming the composite nature of the material and the successful integration of all essential components. Regarding palladium, Fig. 6b provides a detailed analysis, identifying distinct energy peaks at 330.6 eV and 339.8 eV. These peaks correspond to the Pd 3d5/2 and Pd 3d3/2 states, indicating that palladium exists in specific oxidized or chemical environments within the catalyst structure. This observation confirms that palladium is primarily present in the Pd(II) oxidation state or other related states critical for its catalytic function. Overall, the XPS findings offer robust evidence verifying both the elemental composition and the oxidation characteristics of the MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd catalyst, aligning accurately with established scientific principles.

XPS images of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd

Figure 7 provides transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd, highlighting its structural features. The TEM analysis reveals nearly spherical layers, appearing as darker regions, likely corresponding to the MnFe2O4 nanoparticles forming the core. Surrounding these nanoparticles is a lighter layer that forms a distinct shell structure, pointing to the successful synthesis of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd. The clear contrast between the darker core and lighter shell emphasizes the monolithic nature of the material, demonstrating both its nanoparticle core and functionalized outer layer as illustrated in Fig. 7.

TEM images of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd

In order to evaluate the magnetic properties, uncoated magnetic spinel ferrite (MnFe2O4) and MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd (MNPs) were subjected to vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM) analysis in the range of ± 10,000 Oe external magnetic field at room temperature (Fig. 8). The VSM results showed that the saturation magnetization (Ms) after coating decreased from about 57 emu/g for MnFe2O4 to about 41 emu/g for MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd. This decrease in Ms can be attributed to the presence of the new coating layer and is considered as a confirmation of the successful synthesis of the modified catalyst. Despite the decrease in Ms, the magnetic susceptibility of MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd was still sufficient for effective magnetic separation from different reaction media.

Catalytic studies

The catalytic activity of the MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd nanocomposite in oxidative esterification reactions was thoroughly examined following its synthesis and characterization. This study aimed at achieving the direct oxidation of aromatic aldehydes. To optimize the reaction conditions, a model system was designed using benzaldehyde and methanol, combined with a base. Key parameters, including the catalyst dosage, type and concentration of base, and reaction temperature, were systematically evaluated (Table 1). Interestingly, heating the reactants to 50 °C with 30 mg of catalyst in the absence of a base yielded no product, highlighting the essential role of the base in this process. Various bases, including Na2CO3, K3PO4, KOH, and K2CO3, were tested at a fixed concentration of 1.2 mmol alongside a constant catalyst amount. Among these, K2CO3 emerged as the most effective, achieving an impressive yield of 98% within 45 min. To further enhance reaction efficiency, varying amounts of catalyst were explored while maintaining the optimal K2CO3 concentration.

The esterification mechanism of aldehydes, as illustrated in Scheme 3, involves a three-step process. Initially, the aldehyde is transformed into a peroxy radical with the aid of a palladium catalyst and oxygen. In the subsequent step, the peroxy radical reacts to form a carboxylic acid by acquiring a proton from methanol or the reaction medium. Finally, methanol, activated by a base and the palladium catalyst, undergoes nucleophilic attack on the carbonyl group of the carboxylic acid. This results in ester formation while the palladium catalyst re-enters the reaction cycle, as depicted in Scheme 3.

Hot filtration

To evaluate the heterogeneous nature of the MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd catalyst, a hot filtration experiment was undertaken. The esterification reaction between benzaldehyde and methanol was conducted in the presence of the catalyst and interrupted after 30 min, yielding 53% methyl benzoate. To investigate the catalyst’s participation further, an additional test removed the catalyst from the reaction medium after 23 min, allowing the reaction to continue for another 23 min without its presence. In this case, the yield increased only marginally to 55% methyl benzoate. These results strongly support the conclusion that the MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd catalyst acts as a heterogeneous agent and significantly boosts the esterification process of aryl aldehydes.

Catalyst recyclability

The MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd catalyst was evaluated for its reusability in the synthesis of benzonitrile under optimized reaction conditions. It demonstrated consistent and efficient performance across five consecutive cycles, with only a slight decrease in activity. In the first cycle, 0.03 g of the catalyst provided a yield of 98%. The recovered catalyst amounts for the subsequent cycles were 0.0291 g (97%), 0.02794 g (96%), 0.02654 g (95%), and 0.02521 g (95%) for the second to fifth runs, respectively. These results indicate that the catalyst maintained high catalytic efficiency and structural integrity, showing remarkable stability and recyclability throughout the process (Fig. 9).

Conclusion

This study presents the successful design, synthesis, and characterization of an innovative metallic nanocatalyst, MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd, using advanced analytical methods such as TEM, XPS, FT-IR, TGA, EDS, SEM, XRD, ICP, and VSM. The catalyst demonstrated exceptional efficiency in facilitating the esterification of aryl aldehydes, yielding excellent results within short reaction durations. The functionalization of MnFe2O4 nanoparticles with EDTA-Pd significantly improved catalytic activity and performance. Additionally, the nanocatalyst showcased outstanding reusability and magnetic recoverability. MnFe2O4@APTES/EDTA-Pd stands out for its various benefits, including straightforward synthesis from readily available materials, eco-friendliness, high chemical and thermal stability, clean reaction pathways, shorter process times, and minimal catalyst usage.

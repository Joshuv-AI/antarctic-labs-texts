Optimal iterative learning PI controller for SISO and MIMO processes with machine learning validation for performance prediction
Authors: M. Nagarajapandian, S. Kanthalakshmi, P. Arun Mozhi Devan, Kishore Bingi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The multivariable process plays a significant role in industrial applications, and designing a controller for the Multi-Input Multi-Output process is challenging due to dynamic process changes and interactions between system variables. Traditionally, the PI family of controllers has been used for its simple design, easy tuning, and quick deployment. However, these processes require complex control actions due to multiple loops in process plants. Thus, this paper proposes an Iterative Learning Controller Dead-time compensating PI, which utilizes the newly developed hybrid Simulated Annealing-Ant Lion Optimization algorithm for Single-Input Single-Output process simulation and real-time experimentation on the Quadruple Tank System. To validate the effectiveness of the developed controller, Machine Learning techniques such as regression and ensemble trees are used to accurately predict the actual system response using error values from respective processes. The simulation and experimental results demonstrate that the proposed controller achieved better performance. The regression and ensemble tree algorithm models effectively predicted the actual response. The obtained data shows that the proposed controller improved system stability and robustness by minimizing nearly half of the overshoot and improving settling time, with an average of 29.9596% faster than the other controller in the SISO process and 14.6116% in the MIMO process.

Introduction

Complicated interactions are always present in non-linear and multivariable systems’ measurement and control signals in different processes like chemical processing plants, oil & gas processes, food processing industries, engine control units, biological systems, wireless networks, etc1–6.,. Because of the associations between multiple input and output variables, it is challenging to design a suitable controller for these processes7. One of the most widely used multivariable systems is QTS, which is mathematically modelled initially by Johansson8. The QTS is one of the standardly used systems in process control because it is a typical multivariable system with non-linearity, strong coupling, and a considerable time delay9,10. Additionally, QTS has been a commonly utilized criterion for measuring advanced control approaches for multivariable control since its creation because it encompasses both minimum and non-minimum phase functional points based on their shape and operating points11,12.

Over the past two decades, researchers worldwide have developed various control strategies to optimize the performance of QTS13,14. Among these strategies, decentralized and dead-time compensating control design represents a promising framework approach to controlling SISO and MIMO processes15,16. Standard types of equipment have been used to introduce this strategy in several laboratories for simulation and hardware validation purposes17. To improve the complexity and performance of SISO and MIMO processes, researchers have designed robust adaptive18, neural-control19, fractional-order20, model-predictive21, fuzzy-based22, sliding-mode23, prediction-based24, and decoupled controllers25.

Nagaraj et al8. presented a linearized model of a QTS using linear matrix inequality controller, demonstrated that a system’s transition from minimum to non-minimum phase can be achieved by adjusting the valve regulating the flow ratios between upper and lower tank. In26, Shah et al. applied various control algorithms to assess their performance, taking into account the non-linear model of the water tank system. Subsequently, Mahapatro et al27. designed a decentralized PI controller for QTS to validate the effectiveness of the control algorithm without system uncertainties. Garrido et al. in25employed the decoupling and root locus technique to develop a PID controller for a modified QTS, assuming negligible process delays for the minimum and non-minimum phase behaviour without matched uncertainty. A smooth switching control strategy is introduced to address the issues of severe chattering of sliding mode and slow transient response of back-stepping control strategy for four-tank systems, effectively achieving the desired level of control28,29. This approach offers a significant improvement over the previous methods and represents a valuable contribution to the field of process control.

ML is an artificial intelligence application that enables devices to learn from their experiences, improve themselves based on their behavioural characteristics and make decisions more like humans by allowing them to understand, classify and predict effectively30. Johansson has proposed a range of methods for identifying the parameters of QTS using MATLAB, including both black-box and grey-box approaches. These techniques rely on standard system identification methods to estimate models based on experimental data31. Linear SISO and MIMO models are identified using auto-regressive with external model input, auto-regressive moving average with external model input, and state-space forms.

The traditional modelling is a time-consuming and complicated process, particularly when the entire dynamics are involved. So, soft computing techniques, such as neural, fuzzy, and neuro-fuzzy schemes, have been employed to model QTS to overcome this challenge32. It has been demonstrated that models developed using soft computing techniques can be used to design model-based control schemes. In one instance, support vector regression is used to model QTS and obtain adequate control for non-linear systems by effectively tuning the controller parameters33. In conclusion, the above mentioned studies have contributed significantly to developing control strategies for QTS by employing innovative techniques, and advanced controllers demonstrated the possibility of improving QTS’s performance and complexity. Iterative learning controller

The ILC technique is widely used for controlling processes involving constant movement where the controller stores the input and error values in a memory buffer unit for every iteration, as illustrated in Fig. 3. The learning function generates the control signal during this process by subtracting the error from the actual process signal. Various configurations are possible for the filters’ location in the ILC35, but maintaining the memory buffer feedback loop is critical to achieving optimal performance36. The design of L and Q filters for the ILC controller is obtained through the process model. Simulated annealing algorithm

The simulated annealing is a powerful optimization technique initially proposed by Pijarski et al37.. The technique involves simulating the slow cooling of molten metal to minimize its temperature gradually to achieve better quality metal. The simulated annealing algorithm is known for finding the global optimum, even in complex search spaces with many local optima. The detailed flow of the simulated annealing algorithm and the desired stopping conditions for the controller parameters based on the ISE value are defined as follows: First, the initial temperature T, cooling rate, and stopping conditions are defined. Then, the initial point is randomly generated. The algorithm then iteratively generates new candidate solutions by perturbing the current solution and accepting or rejecting them based on the Metropolis criterion. The process continues until the stopping criterion is satisfied, which can be based on the number of iterations, the temperature, or the ISE value. Ant lion optimizer

The ant lion optimization method is a nature-inspired algorithm based on the natural hunting behaviour of antlions, which prey upon ants that solve uni and multi-dimensional optimization problems6,38. Antlions create cone-shaped trenches and conceal their larvae at the base of the cone to lure the ants. The edge of the cone is so sharp that the ants easily slide into the trench. Once the prey is caught, the antlion pours sand towards the edge of the cone to prevent the prey from escaping. The antlion repeats the process, capturing and consuming its prey before digging a new trench to capture the next target. Although the algorithm has some advantages, such as the ability to perform a local search and identify the best position, it also has some significant drawbacks. Specifically, it can be slow to converge to a solution and requires multiple parameters to be tuned. Machine learning validation

This section discusses a detailed technique to simulate and validate the prediction model of the proposed controller for both SISO and MIMO processes. To predict the system’s actual response value and verify the accuracy of the controller, regression and ensemble tree algorithms, which are discussed in Section 5, are used in conjunction with machine learning techniques. MATLAB incorporates a regression learner tool that offers various models, including regression trees, Gaussian process regression, support vector machines, kernel approximation, ensembles of regression trees, and neural network regression. This research uses regression and ensemble tree algorithms, and the ISE error value is compared to other algorithms. The regression tree trains a model using a validation scheme with default protection against overfitting through cross-validation, and it also allows for holdout validation. Diagnostic measures like model accuracy, response, and residual plots reflect the validated model results, which review each model’s RMSE (Validation), R squared, MSE, and MAE scores reported in the Models pane, followed by comparing different regression and ensemble models. The IAE value of the proposed controller from both the SISO and MIMO processes is used to analyze the prediction accuracy. The data is divided into two subsets of inputs (IAE and DIAE) based on features that lead to the most significant reduction in the variance of the target variable. The algorithm starts at the root node, evaluates all potential splits, and selects the one that most effectively reduces the MSE. Furthermore, in a regression tree, a single decision tree is employed for continuous predictions, and the ensemble of trees is used to enhance prediction accuracy and robustness. With this technique, the proposed controller can accurately predict and control both SISO and MIMO processes.Fig. 16Proposed block diagram of machine learning approach for the ILCDPI controller.

Conclusion

The ILCDPI controller design has been comprehensively implemented through simulations and experiments for both SISO and MIMO processes. The numerical findings demonstrate a substantial improvement in the rise time, settling time, and reduction of peak overshoot when using the proposed controller for both SISO and MIMO processes. In order to further confirm the controller’s effectiveness in error mitigation and generation of robust control signals, machine learning-based regression and ensemble trees were employed. These techniques were used to forecast predicted and actual responses employing IAE and DIAE values. A detailed comparison of these models was then analyzed using various metrics, such as MSE, R-squared, MSE, and MAE, alongside the model training time. Further, the proposed techniques’ performance comparison and numerical validations are concluded as follows:The proposed ILCDPI controller performed better than the SIPI and IPI controllers across most performance metrics like rise time, settling time, peak overshoot, and ISE analysis.The proposed controller effectively handled changes in load and disturbances by generating more robust control signals, making it suitable for both minimum and non-minimum phase systems. In addition, it manages to track the varying set-points in all the processes more efficiently than the conventional controllers.The simulation and experimental result findings reveal the proposed controller’s ability to filter out stochastic disturbance and external white noise by significantly smoothing the control signal, producing the process output with minimal offset.The simulation and experimental data results demonstrate the proposed controller’s superior performance in both SISO and MIMO processes. Compared to other controllers, the proposed controller exhibits minimal overshoot, reducing the stress on control valve actuators.

Determination of the standardized ileal amino acid digestibility of corn in 13-day-old broilers and establishment of the prediction equations☆
Authors: Zhenyu Hang, Ziyi Wang, Tong Xing, Liang Zhao, Lin Zhang, Feng Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study was conducted to evaluate apparent ileal amino acid digestibility (AID AA) and standardized ileal amino acid digestibility (SID AA) in corn with different sources fed to broilers and to establish prediction equations of SID AA based on the chemical composition of corn. Ten different sources of corn were evaluated. A total of 660 1-day-old male broilers (Arbor Acres) were randomly divided into 11 treatments (6 replicates, 10 birds per replicate). The broilers were fed the experimental diets and nitrogen-free diet (NFD), and the experimental period was 3 days (from d10 to d13). The amino acid digestibility was determined by adding titanium dioxide as an exogenous indicator in the diets. There was considerable variation in the chemical composition of corn. The variable coefficient of the content of acid detergent fiber and Pro was more than 20 %. The AID AA and SID AA ranged from 56.73 % to 86.99 % and 86.31 % to 96.35 %, respectively. Corn source had a significant effect on AID AA (P < 0.05). A positive correlation was observed between EE and SID of Val, Ile, Leu, Lys, His, Glu and Ala. A negative correlation was found between ADF and SID of Gly, Val, Ile, Leu, Lys, Asp, Glu, Ala and Tyr. The R2 of stepwise regression equations for predicting SID AA was the best for Asp (R²=0.989) and the least for His (R²=0.403). The prediction equations based on the chemical composition of corn provide valuable reference points for realizing efficient utilization and precise nutrition in broilers.Keywords: Corn, Broiler, Standardized ileal amino acid digestibility, Prediction equation

Introduction

Corn is the most widely used energy feed in poultry diets due to its great digestibility, high metabolizable energy, and superior pigmentation properties for poultry products. Feed cost constitutes approximately 70 % of total production expenses (Biesek et al., 2022). With the escalation of raw material prices, the feeding cost for broilers have risen further. Therefore, enhancing the utilization of corn raw materials is one of the main strategies to mitigate this challenge.

The nutrient value of corn can be influenced by various factors, including genetics, natural environment, and processing methods. Accurately evaluating the nutrient value of corn is crucial for realizing precise nutrition in poultry (Li et al., 2014). Protein is an indispensable nutrient for poultry, and amino acid digestibility plays a key role in evaluating the nutritional value of corn protein. Over the past two decades, amino acid digestibility in poultry has been measured at the ileal level. Apparent ileal amino acid digestibility (AID AA) and standardized ileal amino acid digestibility (SID AA) are commonly used to determine the amino acid digestibility in poultry. SID AA is corrected by the ileal endogenous amino acid losses (IAAend), and is more additive in the poultry diets compared to AID AA (An and Kong, 2023). Moreover, to our knowledge, broilers at different growth stages exhibit varying digestive capacities, necessitating digestibility experiments conducted at different ages(Barua et al., 2021).

However, traditional in vivo methods applied to determine the SID AA for poultry are time-consuming and laborious. Consequently, many researchers are dedicated to faster and more convenient methods. Prediction equations based on the chemical composition of feedstuffs have been established, enabling the rapid formulation of poultry diets (Thiruchchenthuran et al., 2024). Therefore, this study aimed to determine the SID AA of corn in 13-day-old broilers and to establish prediction equations based on chemical composition. The results will contribute to refining the database of corn's nutritional value and optimizing the formulation of precision diets for broilers.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

Protocols for animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Nanjing Agricultural University.

Corn samples

This experiment collected 10 kinds of corn raw materials (C1 to C10) sourced from different regions (9 kinds from Ningxia, Shanxi, Jilin, Henan, Inner Mongolia, Liaoning, Xinjiang, and Shandong provinces of China, and 1 kind imported from the USA). A total of 70 kg of corn raw materials were collected for each kind of corn. Approximately 1 kg of experimental analysis samples of each kind of corn were collected and stored in the refrigerator at −20 °C for use. An appropriate amount of corn was crushed with a universal crusher and passed a 40-mesh screen (among which the samples used to determine amino acids and ash passed a 60-mesh screen). The sieved samples were used for chemical analysis.

Experimental design and diets

A total of 660 1-day-old male broilers (Arbor Acres) were randomly divided into 11 treatments with 6 replicates per treatment and 10 chickens per replicate. The broilers were fed the commodity feed in the non-experimental period (d1-d10). The experimental period was 3 days, and experimental and nitrogen-free diets (NFD) were fed to broilers from d10 to d13. The 10 experimental diets were prepared with corn as the only protein source. The amino acid digestibility was determined by adding titanium dioxide as an exogenous indicator in the experimental and nitrogen-free diets. Sample collection

On d13, all broilers were slaughtered after being euthanized via CO2 asphyxiation. The ileum (the portion of the small intestine extending from the vitelline diverticulum to approximately 2 cm proximal to the ileo-caecal junction) was separated, and the distal ileum (lower half) contents were collected by flushing sections of the distal ileum with distilled water. The ileal digesta of broilers in each replicate group was placed in an aluminum box, and the collected digesta was immediately stored at −20 °C. The frozen digesta was freeze-dried and kept at room temperature for 24 h. The freeze-dried digesta of all chickens in each repeat was thoroughly mixed and crushed for use.

Chemical analysis

Dry matter (DM), crude protein (CP), coarse ash (Ash), crude fiber (CF), ether extract (EE) and total phosphorus (TP) were analyzed according to AOAC International (2016) analytical methods (934.01, 976.05, 942.05, 962.09, 920.39, 965.17, respectively). The method 982.30 was used to analyze the amino acid (AA) content of corn samples, experimental diets, and ileal digesta. Neutral detergent fiber (NDF) and acid detergent fiber (ADF) were determined using an A220i Fiber Analyzer (Ankom Technology, USA) according to Van Soest et al. (Van Soest et al., 1991). Total starch (TS) was determined by the starch assay kit (Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology, China). Phytic acid (PA) was determined by the phytic acid kit (Jiangsu Addison Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China). Titanium dioxide was determined on a UV spectrophotometer following the method described by Short et al. (Short et al., 1996). Gross energy (GE) was determined by an adiabatic bomb calorimeter (Hebi Tianyu Instrument Manufacturing Co., Ltd, China). NFE (Nitrogen free extract) was calculated value (NFE%=DM-CP-EE-CF-Ash).

Statistical analysis

Cage was considered as the experimental unit. The experimental data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA in SPSS (version 25.0, SPSS Inc., USA), followed by Duncan's multiple range test for intergroup comparisons. Subsequently, the Pearson correlation analysis between the chemical composition of corn and SID AA was also analyzed by SPSS. Finally, the stepwise regression analysis was employed to establish prediction equations for standardized ileal amino acid digestibility. Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Ileal digestibility of AA

The AID AA and SID AA in 13d-old broilers are shown in Table 3, Table 4. SID AA was much greater than AID AA and there was no significant difference among amino acids for SID AA while the AID AA was significantly different (P < 0.05). The average AID AA ranged from 56.73 % to 86.99 %. For the AID of EAA, the highest was Leu and the lowest was Thr, which were 86.99 % (from 83.73 % to 90.49 %) and 56.73 % (from 45.47 % to 65.26 %), respectively. For the AID of NEAA, the highest and the lowest were Glu and Cys, which were 86.29 % (from 82.95 % to 89.63 %) and 64.11 % (from 54.39 % to 70.43 %), respectively. The average SID AA ranged from 86.31 % to 96.35 %. For the SID of EAA, the highest was Arg and the lowest was also Thr, which were 96.35 % (from 94.22 % to 98.55 %) and 87.68 % (from 79.03 % to 92.64 %), respectively. For the SID of NEAA, the highest and the lowest were Glu and Cys, which were 95.57 % (94.29 % to 98.15 %) and 86.31 % (80.20 % to 90.85 %), respectively.

Correlation analysis and prediction equations

Correlations between chemical composition and SID AA shown in Table 5 to Table 6 found that there was a positive correlation between EE and SID of Val, Ile, Leu, Lys, His, Arg, Glu and Ala. Moreover, the SID of Trp was positively correlated with TP, TS, Met, Trp and Cys. A negative correlation was observed between ADF and SID of Gly, Val, Ile, Leu, Lys, Asp, Glu, Ala and Tyr.Multiple linear regression equations for predicting the SID of Gly, Val, Ile, Leu, Lys, His, Arg, Trp, Asp, Glu, Ala and Tyr based on the chemical composition of corn are presented in Table 7. There are five equations with R2 exceeding 0.90. The best-fit equation for SID AA was SID Asp=225.168-3.438ADF-1.315DM-5.887PA-16.474Met(R² = 0.989, P < 0.001), and the R2 of prediction equations was the least for SID His, SID His=85.106+1.509EE(R² = 0.403, P = 0.049).

Discussion

In the present study, there were considerable differences in the chemical composition of 10 kinds of corn from different sources. The CV of conventional nutrients, EAA and NEAA ranged from 1.04 % to 23.49 %, 15.03 % to 21.08 % and 10.55 % to 22.85 %, respectively. Previous research has indicated that the chemical composition of corn is influenced by genetic factors, environmental and biological stresses (Harrigan et al., 2007). Consequently, corn obtained from various sources exhibits differing nutritional values. There are many corn varieties in China, and most corn has low protein content. The alcohol soluble protein accounts for 50 % to 70 % of the protein, and the alcohol soluble protein has a high content of leucine but lacks lysine and tryptophan. High quality protein corn was bred by introducing opaque-2 mutants into corn. The opaque-2 made albumin, gluten, and albumin in maize increase, and olysin decreased through the regulation of the maize protein-coding gene, and Lys increased. (Zhan et al., 2018; Sethi et al., 2021) Furthermore, drying methods and storage conditions also had important effects on the chemical composition of corn (Huart et al., 2018; Da Silva Timm et al., 2023). Newly harvested corn with high moisture content and vigorous respiration is challenging to store. Therefore, corn growers must reduce the moisture content to a safe level to facilitate long-term storage. In most regions of China, artificial drying and natural field drying are the most widely used methods (Li et al., 2014). The most critical factor in the drying process is temperature. Cordova-Noboa et al. (2020) found that the FCR of broilers in the 120°C dry corn diet group was higher than that in the 35°C dry corn diet group. However, the ileal starch digestibility was significantly lower in the former group than in the latter. Similarly, Kaczmarek et al. (2014) also reported that both the AID of starch and CP in 35-day-old broilers were markedly reduced after they ate dry corn subjected to high temperatures ranging from 130°C to 140°C. Broilers intake high-temperature dried corn had low nutrient digestibility, which was consistent with the conclusion of in vitro trials (Kaczmarek et al., 2014; Odjo et al., 2018). The main factors that affect the quality of corn during storage are the environment temperature and moisture content, which are related to the respiration and presence of microorganisms (Huang et al., 2013; Coradi et al., 2020).

There was a significant difference in the AID AA. Several factors can be taken into account for this variation, with the source likely being the most critical factor. Different regions present distinct growing environments characterized by variations in precipitation and light exposure. It has been reported that the source significantly affected ileal nitrogen digestibility in broilers aged 18 and 42 days (Williams et al., 2018). Sadighi Sheikhhasan et al. (2020) found that the SID of Glu and Gly of Ukrainian corn in 24-day-old broilers was significantly lower than that of Brazilian, Russian, and Iranian corn. Moreover, the physicochemical properties of corn were changed, and the efficiency of chicken utilization was affected after processing. Massuquetto et al. (2020) reported that the extruded corn significantly increased the apparent ileal digestibility of crude protein and total starch. The addition of coarse corn to the diet not only upregulated some genes encoding nutrient transporters in the gut (Kheravii et al., 2018) but also had a positive effect on the intestinal development of broilers. Compared with the fine corn, the coarse corn increased the relative weights of the gizzard, duodenum, jejunum and ileum, which can enhance pancreatic secretion, especially the production of amylase and trypsin by promoting the release of cholecystokinin (Svihus, 2011). Barua et al. (2020) found that most SID AA in the pellet-feeding group were significantly lower than those in the meal-feeding group. The diets used in our experiment were all pelleted, which may also be the reason for the low SID AA in this experiment. Many studies have shown that the ileal amino acid digestibility of poultry is affected by age, but the results reported so far are inconsistent. Our previous research (dates are not shown) concluded that both AID AA and SID AA of 13d broilers were higher than those observed at 28d. Batal et al. (2002) found that with the growth of chickens, amino acid digestibility gradually increased and tended to be stable when chickens reached 10 days. The increase in digestibility may be associated with enhanced protease activity, improved absorption efficiency, and gastrointestinal development. Finally, it is interesting that there was no significant difference in the SID AA. Studies (Stein et al., 2023; Yun et al., 2023) have shown that AID AA shows significant differences while SID AA does not. As we mentioned above, SID AA is calculated by correcting AID AA with IAAend. Therefore, quantifying IAAend is also a non-negligible factor for accurately determining SID AA. IAAend refers to amino acids synthesized and secreted by the poultry into the intestinal cavity without being reabsorbed, which mainly include digestive fluid, intestinal shed cells, mucin, etc. (Ravindran, 2021). IAAend is affected by many factors, including determination methods, age, species, chemical composition of corn, etc. Szczurek and Swiatkiewicz (2020) reported that the IAAend of broilers at 14 days of age was significantly higher than that at 28 days of age. An et al. (2022) also found that the basal IAAend of Ross 308 at 10 days of age was significantly higher than that at 22 days of age. These results suggest that the IAAend of younger chicks is higher than that of older chickens. The intestinal tract of chicks is in the stage of rapid development in the first week of hatching. At this time, intestinal development is incomplete, the physical digestion efficiency, the secretion and activity of some digestive enzymes are low, and the reabsorption of endogenous amino acids is weak. With the growth of broilers, the absorption rate of mucin, which is the main IAAend, increases, and the IAAend decreases with the increase of age (Barua et al., 2021). In a briefly summary, the absence of significant differences in SID AA might be due to the fact that after being corrected by IAAend, the SID AA has been reached a relatively high level which has smoothed out this difference. The specific reasons need to be further studied.

The results of the correlation analysis indicated that there was a positive correlation between the content of EE and SID AA, which was similar to the report of Cerrate et al. (2019). Cowieson et al. (2010) reported that the ileal amino acid digestibility of 21-day broilers increased with dietary soybean oil increasing from 1 % to 3.2 %. The reason that ether extract can improve digestibility may be owing to that the increase of dietary energy density may lead to the increase of cholecystokinin secretion, thus reducing the gastric emptying effect and increasing the retention time of feed in the intestine, which is conducive to the action of digestive enzymes (Kim et al., 2013; Ravindran et al., 2016; Hu et al., 2018). Ko et al. (2023) found that reduced metabolizable energy decreased AID of DM, fat, and GE as compared to the control metabolizable energy group. Similarly, Upadhaya et al. (2017) conducted that the low energy diet reduced DM digestibility compared with normal diet. Furthermore, it was also found that SID AA was significantly negatively correlated with ADF in this study. These findings are consistent with those reported by Sadighi Sheikhhasan et al. (2020) who developed ten equations primarily incorporating CP, CF, NDF, and ADF as prediction factors. Zuber and Rodehutscord (2017) found that there was a significant positive correlation between AID AA of laying hens and both protein content and bulk density in corn samples. Cao et al. (2025) established several equations using CP, CF, ADF, etc. as predictive factors. Although there have been many studies, it must be acknowledged that the equations established by each researcher are not exactly the same and are difficult to apply directly. In this study, not only chemical composition but also the content of amino acids was considered for predicting regression equations, and the content of Met, Pro, and Trp were the main prediction factors. The introduction of prediction factors in this study was carried out with great caution because introducing too many inappropriate factors would lead to an increase in the R² value, resulting in overfitting and distorted predictions. This study only established partial SID AA prediction equations, among which 6 of them fitted well. We need more data to analyze the relationship between feedstuff and the digestive physiology of broilers, so as to establish more applicable and comprehensive equations in the future.

Conclusion

In summary, the results indicated that the chemical composition of corn from 10 different sources showed a huge difference. Corn source had a notable impact on AID AA, while it did not significantly affect SID AA in 13-day-old broilers. Most AID AA ranged from 60 % to 90 %, increasing between 80 % and 95 % after being corrected by IAAend. In addition, this study established 12 prediction equations for SID AA based on the chemical composition of corn by multiple linear stepwise regression. The equations R2 for SID Val, Ile, Lys, Asp, and Glu exceeded 0.90, which had a great reference value for realizing precision nutrition of poultry diet.

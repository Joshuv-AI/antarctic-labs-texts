Identification of antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells using an optofluidic system independent of epitope information
Authors: Wei Zhang, Yu Liu, Hongxiang Zeng, Xuejiao Liao, Juanjuan Zhao, Xian Tang, Zheng Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryMost studies detect and isolate antigen-specific CD8+ T cells solely by measuring T cell receptor (TCR) binding affinity to peptide-major histocompatibility complexes (p-MHCs) without considering T cell function. Here, we present a function-based strategy to screen antigen-specific CD8+ T cells using the Lightning optofluidic platform, named MicroFAST (microfluidic function-based screening of antigen-specific single T lymphocyte) fitting for limited sample volume. We validated this method by using it to isolate human cytomegalovirus (HCMV)-specific CD8+ T cells targeting a known HLA-A∗02:01-restricted epitope. We finally applied this method to identify several SARS-CoV-2-specific TCRs, which were characterized by recognizing HLA-B∗35:01-restricted TPS epitope (TPSGTWLTY), demonstrating the utility of our system. TCR binding affinity did not associate with its functionality. Collectively, we developed a workflow of isolating antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells with or without a prior knowledge of peptide-MHC specificities, which will facilitate the development of engineered TCR-T therapies and T cell-based vaccines.Subject areas: Immunological methods, Immunology

Introduction

CD8+ T cells play an indispensable role in controlling infectious disease and cancer.1 Antigen-specific CD8+ T cells function through inducing target cell apoptosis and secreting inflammatory cytokines such as interferon (IFN)-γ and tumor necrosis factor (TNF) to eliminate aberrant cells.2,3,4 The specificity and diversity of TCRs enable CD8+ T cells to recognize a wide variety of antigens presented by major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class Ⅰ molecules.5 Besides, diverse MHC class Ⅰ alleles across population present distinct antigenic peptides.6,7 Therefore, CD8+ T cells mount antigen-specific and donor-specific immune responses. Although it is laborious to dissect the interaction between TCR and p-MHC complex, isolating antigen-specific CD8+ T cells and identifying its epitopes is of significant importance for the development of immunotherapies and vaccines.

The strategies to label antigen-specific CD8+ T cells through either TCR affinity or activation have been developed. Fluorescent p-MHC multimer staining is widely used to isolate antigen-specific CD8+ T cells.8,9,10 However, it requires known peptide-MHC specificities to link p-MHC monomers to the multimeric backbone. Moreover, multimer binding of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells is not necessarily correlated to activation or function.11,12,13 Some non-functional or exhausted CD8+ T cells are also stained with multimers. The activation-induced markers (AIM) such as CD137 and CD69 can be utilized to enrich antigen-specific CD8+ T cells without the knowledge of epitope specificities.14,15,16 On the other hand, bystander T cells are also sorted out using these markers.14 AIM markers may not directly reflect the production of IFN-γ. The anti-IFN-γ and anti-CD45 bi-specific antibody is employed to mark live IFN-γ-secreting antigen-specific CD8+ T cells after antigen stimulation.17,18 The possibilities of antigen-specific T cell secreting cytokine captured by adjacent bystander T cells may raise specificity and sensitivity concerns.18,19 A sensitive detection and screening system requires single T cells as readouts. Therefore, developing a method capable of confining each single CD8+ T cell in separate chambers to achieve antigen-specific functional screening is an unmet need.

The commercial Beacon and Lightning optofluidic instrument developed by Berkeley Lights Inc. (now known as Bruker Cellular Analysis Inc.) is used to characterize the phenotype, function, and genotype of primary cells or cell lines.20,21 Single cells or microparticles can be manipulated by nondestructive light-induced electric field (also known as OptoElectro Positioning22,23) to move into nanoliter-volume chambers (also known as NanoPens) on an opto-electronic and microfluidic culturing chip. The method of antibody discovery was reported through Beacon platform,24 followed by isolating and profiling several human anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies.25,26,27,28 However, to our knowledge, there is no reports of isolating and identifying antigen-specific T cells at a single-cell level using Beacon or Lightning system, especially for the epitopes of undefined peptide-MHC specificities.

Here, we devised a MicroFAST (microfluidic function-based screening of antigen-specific single T lymphocyte) workflow on the Lightning platform based on IFN-γ-secreting functional response at a single-cell level used for isolating and identifying antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells. We validated its utility by screening HCMV-specific functional CD8+ T cells from a healthy donor. We also combined MicroFAST workflow, multiplex PCR technique and modified Jurkat76 cells that express CD8 molecules as well as NFAT-controlled luciferase cassette (J76.8) to isolate SARS-CoV-2-specific functional CD8+ T cells and then to identify its accurate epitopes from a patient. Thus, we developed a workflow based on the single T cell functional assay to isolate and identify antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells with or without a preexisting knowledge of peptide-MHC specificities, which will promote the development of engineered TCR-T therapies and T cell-based vaccines.

Results

Establishment of MicroFAST workflow on Lightning platform

To isolate antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells, we established a MicroFAST workflow on Lightning platform (Figure 1A). IFN-γ capture beads were firstly moved into each NanoPen on OptoSelect 1500 chip, which was divided into 0–11 field of views (FOVs) capable of loading cells as user design (see Figure S1A). Prior to loading antigen-presenting cells (APCs), single CD8+ T cell was loaded into each NanoPen. After co-culturing overnight in the presence of peptides, cells were stained and imaged as described in STAR Methods. The positive NanoPens should include single CD8+ T cell (shown as blue), and IFN-γ capture beads with merged internal Cy5 (shown as pink) and PE fluorescent signal (shown as yellow), suggesting single CD8+ T cell was activated to secret IFN-γ (Figure 1A). Finally, cells in positive NanoPens were unloaded for single-cell TCR sequencing.

To test MicroFAST, a reported C25-TCR29 recognizing HLA-A∗02:01-restricted NLV epitope from HCMV was overexpressed on primary T cells through retrovirus infection to prepare TCR-T. The target and irrelevant control peptide-pulsed T2 cells were loaded into FOV 0–7 and FOV 8–11, respectively (Figure 1B). After culturing overnight and staining, multiple positive spots (merged Cy5 and PE signal) were noticeably observed in FOV 0–7 compared to FOV 8–11. We simultaneously performed IFN-γ ELISPOT assay, which also showed that C25-TCR-transduced primary CD8+ T cells responded specifically to target APCs (Figure 1B). Next, we conducted MicroFAST workflow using other well-documented TCRs including N103-TCR,30 EBNA3A-TCR,18 NSP8-TCR,31 and YLQ7-TCR.32 The primary or J76.8 cells overexpressing TCRs were loaded on chip before target or control peptide-pulsed APCs were loaded in FOV 0–7 or FOV 8–11. After imaging, the percentage of IFN-γ-secreting J76.8 or primary CD8+ T cells in target FOV 0–7 versus control FOV 8–11 was calculated. The results showed that MicroFAST workflow could effectively detect antigen-specific T cell activation (Figure 1C). IFN-γ ELISPOT assay was concurrently conducted. We found that detection sensitivity was comparable between MicroFAST and IFN-γ ELISPOT assay (Figure 1D).

To test whether cells in positive NanoPens could be unloaded for sequence recovery, we mixed C25-TCR-transduced primary CD8+ T cells and YLQ7-TCR-transduced primary CD8+ T cells at a ratio of 1:5 to carry out MicroFAST workflow. After loading NLV target peptide-pulsed T2 cells, responsive NanoPens were selected for cell unloading (see Figure S1B). Primers for C25-TCR expression cassette were designed to amplify target fragment from exported TCR-transduced primary CD8+ T cells (see Figure S1C). Sequencing and alignment confirmed recovery of C25-TCR fragment from responsive NanoPens (see Figure S1D). Therefore, the MicroFAST workflow was capable of detecting and retrieving antigen-specific CD8+ T cells.

Screening of HCMV-specific functional CD8+ T cells

To validate whether MicroFAST workflow could isolate and identify antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells from donors, we examine CD8+ T cell responses to a reported dominant A∗02:01/NLV epitope from HCMV, a prevalent pathogen worldwide. Figure 2A displayed the flowchart to screen antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells with a defined peptide-MHC specificity. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from donors were used for epitope-specific short-term expansion before enriching CD8+ T cells. Meanwhile, T2 cells were pulsed with target peptides to serve as APCs. CD8+ T cells and APCs were loaded on chip to run MicroFAST. To this end, we firstly detected A∗02:01/NLV epitope-specific CD8+ T cells using Tetramer staining after epitope-specific expansion of PBMCs from 5 healthy donors (Figure 2B). We selected donor 3 (nearly 80% Tetramer+) for CD8+ T cell enrichment. After MicroFAST workflow, only 50 positive NanoPens among around 1, 400 NanoPens containing CD8+ T cells were found, indicating that the majority of Tetramer+ CD8+ T cells might be non-functional. We randomly chose 16 positive NanoPens for cell exporting (Figure 2C). The exported cells underwent RNA extraction, RT-PCR, and multiplex PCR for TCR amplification (see Figure S2A). Sequencing revealed these paired TCRs from NanoPen273, 289, 763, 393, 417, 665, 630, and 998 belonged to one clone (Figure 2D). We named this clone as Pen998-TCR for further study.

Full-length TCRs were synthesized and then cloned in pHR lentiviral vector, with P2A sequence separating alpha and beta chain. J76.8 reporter cells could be employed to validate TCR specificity (see Figure S2C). Normal Pen998-TCR expression was detected on J76.8 cells (see Figure S2D). Pen998-TCR-expressing J76.8 cells could be stained with A∗02:01/NLV Tetramer. A reported A∗02:01/NLV-epitope specific C25-TCR and EBV-specific EBNA3A-TCR served as positive and negative control, respectively (Figure 2E). Additionally, Pen998-TCR-J76.8 cells yielded obviously upregulated luciferase expression and IFN-γ secretion after receiving cognate antigen stimulation (see Figure S2E, and Figure 2F). Furthermore, Pen998-TCR-transduced primary CD8+ T cells enhanced IFN-γ and TNF-α expression after stimulating with T2 cells pulsed with target peptides (Figure 2G). These results suggested that MicroFAST workflow could be leveraged to isolate and identify antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells against a well-defined epitope.

Screening of SARS-CoV-2-specific functional CD8+ T cells

The MicroFAST workflow was then exploited to screen antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells without a preexisting knowledge of peptide-MHC specificities. Figure 3A showed the flowchart. CD8+ T cells were enriched using CD8+ T cell isolation kit (Miltenyi, 130-096-495) from peptide pool-expanded PBMCs of a convalescent donor from SARS-CoV-2 infection. Autologous CD4+ and CD8+ T cell-depleted PBMCs through CD3 MicroBeads (Miltenyi, 130-050-101) were used as APCs. CD8+ T cells and APCs were loaded on chip and co-cultured in the presence of peptides. To this end, we firstly evaluated ex vivo T cell responses against 15-mer peptide pools of SARS-CoV-2 spanning S1, S2, M, N, ORF3a, and ORF1a protein. We found that treatment of N peptide pool induced strongest generation of IFN-γ compared to others (Figure 3B, Left). N peptide pool was then divided into two small pools, namely N1-51 and N52-102. CD8+ T cells enriched from N1-51 or N52-102 peptide pool-expanded T cell lines (TCLs) were mixed with autologous CD4+ and CD8+ T cell-depleted PBMCs, followed by re-stimulation with N1-51 and N52-102 peptide pool. ELISPOT assay showed that N52-102 TCLs appeared to respond better to re-stimulation compared to N1-51 TCLs (Figure 3B, Right). Next, we adopted enriched CD8+ T cells from N52-102 TCLs to screen antigen-specific CD8+ T cells. Figure 3C showed imaging results of positive NanoPens prepared for cell unloading. After amplification, sequencing and analysis, paired TCR sequences were obtained from NanoPen346, 1298, 369, and 363, which were used for further analysis (see Figures S2B and 3D). We confirmed normal expression of these TCRs on J76.8 cells (Figure 4C). The results demonstrated that MicroFAST workflow could be utilized to discover antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells without a prior knowledge of T cell epitope.

Epitope identification of SARS-CoV-2-specific CD8+ T cells

To determine the epitopes recognized by exported CD8+ T cells, we performed luciferase assay using above TCR-transduced J76.8 cells. As shown in Figure S3A, TCR-transduced J76.8 cells generated roughly 15-fold increase in luciferase expression when incubating with autologous lymphoblastoid cell lines (LCLs) in the presence of N52-102 peptide pool, whereas the addition of N1-51 peptide pool failed to generate elevated luciferase expression. We also found that treatment of N72-81 and N82-91 peptide pool apparently upregulated luciferase expression in Pen346-, 1298-, 369-, and 363-transduced J76.8 cells. Then, single peptides from N77 to N86 were used to stimulate TCR-transduced J76.8 cells. We found that only N81 and N82 could stimulate Pen346-, 1298-, 369-, and 363-expressing J76.8 cells to increase luciferase expression (see Figure S3B). To further confirm epitopes of these TCRs, we synthesized 9-mer peptide spanning N81 and N82 peptides. We observed that the addition of N81-5 peptide (TPSGTWLTY, TPS) generated an evident increase in luciferase expression (Figure 4A). Finally, we tried to determine HLA subtypes responsible for TPS presentation. HLA-A∗11:01-, HLA-A∗24:02-, HLA-C∗14:02-, and HLA-B∗35:01-expressing K562 cells acted as APCs. We found that merely incubating with HLA-B∗35:01-expressing K562 cells and autologous LCLs in the presence of TPS could stimulate these TCR-transduced J76.8 cells to increase luciferase expression (see Figure S3C). In addition, we evaluated CD69 expression in TCR-transduced J76.8 cells after incubating with different APCs in the presence of TPS. These TCR-transduced J76.8 cells upregulated CD69 expression after incubating with HLA-B∗35:01-expressing K562 cells and autologous LCLs (Figure 4B). Moreover, these TCR-transduced J76.8 cells could be stained with B∗35:01/TPS Dextramer with different affinity, despite similar TCR expression level (Figure 4C). Pen369-TCR showed lowest affinity to p-MHCs, whereas Pen346-and Pen1298-TCR could strongly bind Dextramer. Finally, we performed ELISPOT assay to confirm specificity of TCR-expressing J76.8 cells in incubation with peptide-pulsed HLA-B∗35:01-expressing K562 cells. Compared with DMSO and control peptide incubation, TPS could clearly stimulate TCR-expressing J76.8 to yield IFN-γ (Figure 4D). Therefore, accurate epitope of these antigen-specific CD8+ T cells was identified as HLA-B∗35:01-restricted TPS. We found that TPS epitope showed high sequence conservativity among recent variants of concern. TPS epitope also displayed higher homology to SARS-CoV-1, compared to four common seasonal coronaviruses (see Figure S4).

Functional confirmation of SARS-CoV-2-specific TCRs

To assess the functional potential of above MicroFAST-screened antigen-specific TCRs, cytokine-secreting and degranulation function was tested using intracellular cytokine staining (ICS) assay. TCR-transduced primary T cells were incubated with autologous LCLs pulsed with DMSO, unrelated control and TPS target peptide. Results showed that TPS significantly increased frequency of IFN-γ+ TNF-α+, and IFN-γ+ CD107a+ TCR-transduced CD8+ T cells, in contrast to DMSO and control treatment (Figures 5A and 5B). Although Pen369-TCR displayed lowest affinity to p-MHCs among these TPS-specific TCRs (Figure 4C), its function in primary T cells was not compromised. Furthermore, we prepared TCR-T to evaluate its cytotoxicity against TPS-pulsed K562-B∗35:01 target cells. We observed that these SARS-CoV-2-specific TCR-T exhibited clearly killing activities compared to control TCR-T (Figure 5C). Pen369-TCR also displayed better cytotoxicity. Therefore, we confirmed that these antigen-specific TCRs screened by MicroFAST were functional.

Poor correlation between TCR binding affinity and its functionality

Several studies demonstrated that TCR binding affinity to multimers was not always associated with CD8+ T cell activation11,12 and function.13,33,34 Paul A Goepfert and coworkers reported about 90% of HLA-A∗02:01/SL9 Tetramer-positive CD8+ T cells failed to produce IFN-γ upon exposure to SL9 epitope.13 Fluorescent B∗35:01/TPS Dextramer was used to stain short-term TPS-expanded PBMCs from a convalescent donor from SARS-CoV-2 infection (Figure 6A). Single Dextramer+ CD8+ T cells were sorted into 96-well plate pre-coated with feeder cells, followed by culturing and expanding for 2 weeks with IL-2, and anti-CD3 antibodies, as previously described.35,36,37 Half cells were transferred to IFN-γ ELISPOT plate and then stimulated with TPS-pulsed K562-B∗35:01 cells (Figure 6B). We found only four wells (C6, C11, A12, and B1) displayed >10 spots, indicating that a majority of Dextramer+ CD8+ T cells were non-functional. The remaining cells from C6 and C11 of 96-well plate were collected, followed by RNA extraction and TCR amplification and sequencing (Figure 6C). The results showed that C6 and C11 belonged to same clone. We finally confirmed its normal expression and TPS epitope specificity (Figures 6D and 6E). Therefore, some multimer-positive CD8+ T cells seem to be non-functional, suggesting the importance to establish function-based screening for antigen-specific TCRs.

Discussion

Dissecting the interaction of paired antigen-specific functional TCRs and its epitope will pave the way for engineered TCR-T therapies and T cell-based vaccines. We introduced a FACS (fluorescence-activated cell sorting)-independent MicroFAST workflow to isolate antigen-specific CD8+ T cells based on IFN-γ-secreting functional responses at single-cell level. An available method for TCR discovery through multimer staining and single-cell sequencing is unable to evaluate TCR function.11 The common functional assays for evaluating antigen-specific T cells include ELISPOT and flow cytometry-based ICS. However, both fail to obtain live functional antigen-specific T cells and its single-cell TCR information. To obviate it, MicroFAST was established to evaluate single CD8+ T cell activation and function in each separate NanoPen after receiving antigen stimulation, followed by retrieving antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells and its TCR sequences. Notably, MicroFAST workflow could isolate and identify antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells without a prior knowledge of peptide-MHC specificities, unlike the fluorescent multimer staining limited to defined T cell epitopes.8,10 A recently published ATLAS-seq was developed to screen antigen-reactive CD8+ T cells based on T cell activation and IFN-γ secretion after encountering with peptide-loaded artificial APCs beads coupled with human HLA-A2-Ig dimer and anti-CD28Ab.38 Different from ATLAS-seq, MicroFAST can be used for discovering functional TCRs against unknown epitopes (undefined peptide and HLA subtypes) using autologous CD4+ and CD8+ T cell-depleted PBMCs as APCs. Considering functional screening of single CD8+ T cells as readout for MicroFAST, we performed benchmarking comparison to traditional IFN-γ ELISPOT assay using same paired TCR-overexpressing cells and APCs (Figures 1B and 1D), and found that there were no significant differences in detection sensitivity between MicroFAST and ELISPOT. However, we observed higher percentage of reactive cells detected by ELISPOT when TCRs were transduced to primary CD8+ T cells than that in J76.8 cell lines, which was possibly due to competent function of primary T cells compared to Jurkat cell lines.39,40 Finally, MicroFAST holds the potential to screen antigen-specific functional TCRs from limited input volume (10 μL containing 2.5 × 106 cell/ml) compared to FACS. In summary, MicroFAST represents a method with some advantages in the discovery of functional TCR candidates from limited input volume with or without a preexisting knowledge of peptide-MHC specificities.

We also presented detailed epitope identification process for antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells screened by MicroFAST. Homemade J76.8-Luciferase system enabled us to rapidly and easily validate TCR specificity and its epitopes. TCR-transduced J76.8 cell lines could be manipulated, cultured, and expanded more effortlessly than TCR-transduced primary T cells. The using of NFAT-controlled luciferase cassette made readout less elaborate compared with ICS or AIM staining. The unlimited TCR-expressing J76.8 cells allowed us to accurately identify antigenic peptides and restricted MHC alleles of functional CD8+ T cells screened by MicroFAST.

Isolating and identifying antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells will facilitate the development of TCR-engineered T cell therapies against chronic infection and cancer, representing a highly promising therapeutic modality.41,42,43,44 HCMV is a latent causative agent, resulting in widespread morbidity and mortality, especially in immunocompromised population.45,46,47 CD8+ T cell immunity plays a vital role in controlling HCMV infection.48,49 We identified a dominant A∗02:01/NLV epitope-specific functional TCR clone (Pen998) using MicroFAST from a healthy donor (Figure 2). Several A∗02:01/NLV-specific TCRs with identical beta chain to Pen998 but without alpha chain information were previously reported.50 This functional TCR may be used to prepare TCR-T against HCMV in A∗02:01-positive immunodeficient patients.51,52 We also applied MicroFAST to isolate SARS-CoV-2-specific functional TCRs and found that Pen369-TCR displayed lowest binding affinity to p-MHC Dextramer but comparable functionality in primary T cells after receiving cognate antigen stimulation (Figures 4 and 5). Consistent with other studies,10,13 we found that TCR binding affinity to p-MHC multimer did not correlate to its functionality (Figures 6 and 7). Thus, our methods are particularly suitable for the discovery of functional TCRs regardless of its binding affinity. Several studies devoted to identify antigen-specific CD8+ T cells against neoantigens.17,18,53,54 Recently, researchers reported that low-avidity (overall strength of interaction between TCR and p-MHC) CD8+ T cells-mediated tumor control in mice and human.55 Our methods have shown potential in the discovery TCR with low affinity to p-MHC multimer. In the future, MicroFAST may have the potential to isolate and identify neoantigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells, thereby providing treatable TCR candidates.

Investigating paired antigen-specific CD8+ T cells and its epitope informs immune responses induced by viral antigen across patients, whereby guiding immunogen design. We identified HLA-B∗35:01-presented TPS epitope recognized by functional TCRs screened by MicroFAST (Figures 3 and 4), which was previously reported.56 Therefore, MicroFAST could be leveraged to discover antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells even without a preexisting knowledge of peptide-MHC specificities. It was also reported that an immunodominant B∗07:02/N105-113 epitope-induced CD8+ T cells associated with mild disease.57 Another Nucleocapsid-derived A∗02:01/LLL epitope could elicit dominant and long-term CD8+ T cell responses.58 Considering its higher conservativity and immunogenicity (see Figure S4), Nucleocapsid or its epitopes should be incorporated in vaccine design.59

In conclusion, we developed a MicroFAST workflow used for isolating antigen-specific functional CD8+ T cells with or without a prior information of peptide-MHC specificities. Integrating J76.8-Luciferase reporter system enables us to accurately identify the epitopes (peptide and restricted MHC subtypes) recognized by exported functional TCRs. Understanding antigen-specific functional TCRs and its epitopes will contribute to engineered TCR-T therapies and vaccine design. Meanwhile, we envision that combination of MicroFAST and single-cell transcriptome sequencing maybe investigate the phenotype and molecule characterization of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells in disease occurrence and development.

Limitations of the study

Nevertheless, there are some limitations for this study. Firstly, the false negative rate probably exists as a concern. In contrast to traditional functional assays (such as ELISPOT and ICS) conducted in the tubes or wells which contain plenty of T cells and APCs, only single CD8+ T cell and several (around 3–5) APCs were loaded in each NanoPen to evaluate IFN-γ-secreting function in order to retrieve functional TCR sequences afterward. The activity and activation of single T cell in each separate Nanopen are more likely influenced due to lacking of sufficient interacting signaling with other cells, which leads to inadequate IFN-γ production captured by beads and few reactive cells detected. The false negative rate is also possibly attributed to readout (fluorescence imaging of IFN-γ capture beads to indirectly reflect T cell activation and function) through Lightning optofluidic platform, which is less sensitive than others, such as ELISPOT or ICS. It was showed that higher percentage of reactive cells were detected by ELISPOT compared to MicroFAST when TCRs were transduced to function-competent primary CD8+ T cells. Secondly, our MicroFAST shows low-throughput detection of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells (1535 NanoPens for CD8+ T cells) in one-time assay, which means some antigen-specific CD8+ T cells are more likely missing, compared to other high-throughput screening methods. For example, the combination of multimer sorting and single-cell sequencing is capable of identifying more epitope-specific TCR clones probably including those screened by MicroFAST. Additionally, because of function-based screening for MicroFAST, it has difficulties in discovering multimer-specific but functionally exhausted CD8+ T cells, but their TCRs that display normal functionality in transduced primary T cells can be obtained by multimer staining and single-cell sequencing. Finally, the flaw of MicroFAST lies in lower paired TCR retrieval rate (less than 50%), which may be improved through optimizing media and shortening culturing time on chip.

Experimental model and study participant details

Human participants

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) in this study were collected from healthy donors and individuals with COVID-19 infection. Healthy spleen cells were collected from donors whose livers were used for transplantation. All experimental procedures involving human participants in this study were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and national research committee and the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Shenzhen Third People’s Hospital (No. 2023-093-02). Written informed consent was obtained from all the subjects prior to the study. PBMCs and splenocytes were isolated using Ficoll (Cytiva)-based density gradient centrifugation. Primary cell cultures were maintained in complete GT-T551 H3 (TAKARA) or RPMI-1640 (Gibco) medium containing 10% human serum (GeminiBio), 2% HEPES (Gibco), 100 U/mL of Penicillin and 100 μg/mL of streptomycin (Gibco) at 37°C with 5% CO2. Human sample information including sex, age, and HLA typing is listed in Table S1. The influence of gender on the results of the study was not measured. Ancestry, race, and ethnicity of study donors were not available due to privacy.

Cell lines

The cell lines used in this study including T2 cells, K562 cells, and B95-8 cells were obtained from Procell (Wuhan, China). HEK-293T cells were purchased from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). HEK-293T cells, T2 cells, and K562 cells were respectively cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, Gibco), Iscove’s Modified Dulbecco’s Medium (IMDM, Gibco), and RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco) and 100 U/mL of Penicillin and 100 μg/mL of streptomycin at 37°C with 5% CO2. B95-8 cells used for LCLs (lymphoblastoid cell lines) preparation were cultured in complete RPMI-1640 medium (containing 10% FBS, 100 U/mL of Penicillin and 100 μg/mL of streptomycin) at 37°C with 5% CO2. The endogenous TCR-deficient Jurkat cells (Jurkat76) were kindly provided by Feng Wang’s Lab, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine.30 Jurkat76 cells were lentivirally transduced to express CD8 α and β and a nuclear factor of activated T cells (NFAT)-controlled luciferase cassette using a transfer pCDH-EF1a-CD8-NFAT-RE-Luc2P plasmid, named as J76.8 cells.30 J76.8 cells were cultured in complete RPMI-1640 medium. By means of lentivirus infection, exogenous TCRs and HLA class Ⅰ molecules cloned into pHR-SFFV-MCS-IRES-EGFP lentiviral vector (from Feng Wang) were overexpressed on J76.8 and K562 cells, respectively. The sequence of vector and NFAT-RE-Luc2P cassette was listed in Table S4. Cell lines have not been authenticated since purchase and were tested for mycoplasma contamination.

Method details

Gene and peptide

TCR α and β chain separated by P2A sequence were synthesized by GenScript Inc. Control TCR sequence information including C25-TCR,29 N103-TCR,30 EBNA3A-TCR,60 NSP8-TCR,31 and YLQ7-TCR32 referred to previous studies. The pHR plasmids encoding HLA-B∗35:01, HLA-C∗14:02, HLA-A∗24:02, and HLA-A∗11:01 linked with P2A-B2M were also prepared by GenScript Inc. The control TCR and HLA subtype sequences were listed in Tables S2 and S3.

Peptide pools spanning amino acid sequence of Spike protein (S), Nucleocapsid protein (N), Membrane protein (M), ORF1a, and ORF3a protein of SARS-CoV-2 were synthesized by GenScript Inc. Each peptide was made up of 15 residues, of which 11 overlapped with the adjacent peptides. Target peptides including C25-TCR- and N103 TCR-specific HLA-A∗02:01-restricted NLVPMVATV (NLV) from HCMV, EBNA3A-TCR-specific HLA-A∗24:02-restricted RYSIFFDYM (RYS) from EBV, NSP8-TCR-specific HLA-A∗02:01-restricted ALWEIQQVV (ALW) and YLQ7-TCR-specific HLA-A∗02:01-restricted YLQPRTFLL (YLQ) from SARS-CoV-2 were synthesized by GenScript Inc. as well.

Peptide pulsing

Peptide pulsing assay was performed as earlier reported.12 Briefly, about 1 × 106 T2 or K562 cells overexpressing HLA class Ⅰ molecules were washed with serum-free RPMI-1640 medium twice and then resuspended in 1 ml serum-free RPMI-1640 containing 3 μg/ml β2-Microglobulin (Sino Biological) and 20 μg/ml target or control peptides. After incubating at 37°C for 1.5 hours in 12-well plate, cells were collected and washed once with serum-free RPMI-1640 medium. Finally, these cells were resuspended in complete RPMI-1640 medium.

T cell lines (TCLs) and LCLs preparation

Short-term expansion of antigen-specific T cells was established as previously described.18 In short, PBMCs were thawed and then recovered overnight in 2 ml RPMI-1640 medium with 10% human serum, 100 U/mL of Penicillin and 100 μg/mL of streptomycin, and 2% HEPES. On day 2, roughly 4 × 106 PBMCs were cultured in 24-well plate in the presence of 20 ng/ml IL-7 (Peprotech) and 2.5 μg/ml target peptides for 3 days. Fresh medium with IL-2 (Peprotech) was added to a final concentration of 20 U/ml and cultured for further 9 days with a half-medium change.

EBV-infected lymphoblastoid cell lines (LCLs) were prepared according to approaches described previously.61 Briefly, 1 ml B95-8 supernatant was used to infect 1 million PBMCs for 2 hours at 37°C. Then, PBMCs were collected and placed in 24-well plate with a concentration of 5 × 105 cell/ml. The medium was supplemented with 0.4 μg/ml Cyclosporin A (CsA, MCE). A half-medium change with fresh CsA was conducted weekly. Finally, LCLs started to grow when cell clusters emerged.

Sorting and expansion of Dextramer+ CD8+ T cells

The protocol for rapid expansion of T cell clones was described previously.35 Splenocytes from nine healthy donors were mixed together and irradiated with 50 Gy to prepare feeder cells. A total of 1 × 105 feeder cells per well were placed into 96-well cell culture plates. The complete T cell medium (RPMI-1640 medium with 10% human serum, 2% HEPES, 100 U/mL of Penicillin and 100 μg/mL of streptomycin) was supplemented with 30 ng/ml OKT3 (Invitrogen) and 50 U/ml IL-2. Peptide-expanded PBMCs were stained with HLA-B∗35:01/TPSGTWLTY/Dextramer-PE (ImmuDex), BV510/eFluor506-Fixable Viability (eBioscience), BUV805 Mouse anti-human CD3 (BD Biosciences), BUV737 Mouse anti-human CD8 (BD Biosciences), and BB700 Mouse anti-human CD4 (BD Biosciences), followed by single-cell sorting into above 96-well plates containing feeder cells, OKT3, and IL-2 using S6 cell sorter. After transient centrifugation, plates were placed in 37°C incubator for 2 weeks.

TCR expression

For lentivirus preparation, we utilized second generation lentivirus packaging plasmids pMD2.G and psPAX2.30 TCR expression was taken as an example. The pHR lentiviral plasmids and packaging plasmids were co-transfected into HEK-293T cells using PEI transfection reagent (Polysciences) to generate lentivirus. In detail, 0.75 million HEK-293T cells were added into 6-well plate. When cells reached 70% confluence, the mixtures containing 2 μg TCR-expressing lentiviral plasmid and packaging plasmid of 0.5 μg pMD2.G and 1.5 μg psPAX2 were mixed with 16 μg PEI transfection reagent. After 20 minutes at room temperature, plasmid-PEI mixture was dropped onto HEK-293T cells. The lentiviral supernatant was collected after 72-hours culturing, followed by filtering with 0.45 μM membrane (Millipore) to discard debris. Around 5 × 105 J76.8 cells were infected in 24-well plate with above lentivirus through centrifugation at 700 g for 1.5 hours. Finally, TCR-transduced J76.8 cells were cultured in complete RPMI-1640 medium.

Retrovirus preparation for transduction of primary T cells was performed as described previously.62 In brief, TCR expression cassette was firstly transferred to SFG retroviral vector. Retroviral supernatant was harvested after co-transfecting HEK-293T cells of a T75 flask with 3.75 μg PegPam, 2.5 μg RDF, and 3.75 μg SFG plasmids using 30 μg PEI for 3 days. The PBMCs or splenocytes were thawed and recovered overnight before cells were stimulated with 10 μl/ml T Cell TransACT (Miltenyi) and 200 U/ml IL-2 for 72-96 hours. The 2.5 × 105 activated primary cells were then infected with retrovirus using 24-well plate pre-coated with Retronectin (TAKARA). The plates with retrovirus were centrifuged at 2000 g for 1.5 hours before cells were added. Finally, the transduced primary T cells were cultured in complete T cell medium (GT-T551 H3 medium containing 10% human serum, 2% HEPES, 100 U/mL of Penicillin and 100 μg/mL of streptomycin and 200 U/ml IL-2).

Antibodies and flow cytometry

APC Mouse anti-human TCR αβ (clone T10B9.1A-31), BUV737 Mouse anti-human CD8 (clone RPA-T8), BUV805 Mouse anti-human CD3 (clone SK7), BB700 Mouse anti-human CD4 (clone SK3), PE-Cy7 Mouse anti-human CD69 (clone FN50), and APC Mouse anti-human IFN-γ (clone B27) were bought from BD Biosciences. APC/Cy7 anti-human CD8a (clone HIT8a), APC/Cy7 anti-human IL-2 (clone MQ1-17H12), PE anti-human CD3 (clone SK7), PE/Dazzle™ 594 anti-human TNF-α (clone MAb11), and PE/Cy7 anti-human CD107a (LAMP-1) were purchased from BioLegend. HLA-B∗35:01/TPSGTWLTY/Dextramer-PE and HLA-A∗02:01 CMV pp65 Tetramer-PE were from ImmuDex and MBL, respectively.

TCR-expressing J76.8 cells and primary T cells were stained with live/dead staining reagent and surface antibodies on ice for 30 minutes and then washed twice with PBS. Detection of TCR expression and sorting of TCR-transduced cells were performed on BD FACSymphony™ S6 Cell Sorter. Multimer staining was conducted according to manufacturer’s instructions.

For intracellular cytokine staining (ICS), equal number of autologous LCLs and TCR-transduced primary T cells (1 million in total) were mixed and then stimulated with 5 μg/ml target/control peptides and 1 μg/ml Anti-CD28 antibodies (Biolegend) for 2 hours in the presence of PE/Cy7 anti-human CD107a. 1000× Brefeldin A Solution (Biolegend) was added, followed by incubating cell mixtures at 37°C for 6 hours. After experiencing above surface staining, cells were fixed, permeabilized, and stained with APC Mouse anti-human IFN-γ, PE/Dazzle™ 594 anti-human TNF-α, and APC/Cy7 anti-human IL-2 for 30 minutes on ice using BD Cytofix/Cytoperm™ solution and BD Perm/Wash buffer according to manufacturer’s instructions. The samples were analyzed on BD FACSymphony™ A3 Cell Analyzer. All data were analyzed using Flowjo software.

ELISPOT assay

Human IFN-gamma ELISPOTpro kit (Mabtech) was used to examine antigen-specific IFN-γ production. Procedures of manufacturer were followed, with modifications. 1 × 105 PBMCs/well were stimulated with peptide pools of S1, S2, N, M, ORF1a, and ORF3a at a final concentration of 2 μg/ml. 100× DMSO (Sigma) and 500× PMA/Ionomycin (Biolegend) treatment were served as negative and positive control. In another experiment, TCR-expressing cells and peptide-pulsed T2 or HLA class Ⅰ-expressing K562 cells were mixed at a ratio of 1:3 and then co-incubated at 37°C for 18-20 hours. Scanning and counting of cytokine-secreting cell spots were conducted on a CTL Immunospot reader.

Luciferase assay

Around 1 × 105 J76.8 cells over-expressing exogenous TCRs were incubated with equal APCs (autologous LCLs or HLA class Ⅰ-expressing K562 or T2 cells) in the presence of 5 μg/ml single peptide or peptide pools in a 96-well plate. The addition of DMSO solvent acted as control. After 8 hours at incubator, cells were centrifuged and resuspended in 50 μl PBS and then mixed with 50 μl substrate solution containing luciferin and lysis buffer (Vazyme). After reaction for 5 minutes at room temperature, the luciferase values were read out on a microplate reader (Varioskan LUX, Thermo).

Cytotoxicity assay

Around 1 × 106 K562 cells overexpressing HLA-B∗35:01 in 1 ml PBS were labeled with 1 μl CellTrace™ violet (Invitrogen) at 37°C for 20 minutes, followed by transferring into 5 ml complete RPMI-1640 medium. 2 × 104 cells were loaded into 96-well plate before pulsing with 5 μg/ml target peptides for 3 hours. TCR-transduced primary CD8+ T cells were added and co-cultured with target cells at a ratio of 4:1. C25-TCR-transduced primary T cells served as control. After 4 hours, cell mixture was collected and stained with DRAQ7™ reagent (Biolegend) to differentiate live and dead cells.

Lightning™ operation

Lightning™ platform enables users to manipulate, assay and analyze live single cells or micro-particles on the OptoSelect™ 1500 chip. OptoSelect™ 1500 chip can create a non-destructive light-induced electronic field repelling cells or microparticles in liquid to each 1500 NanoPen™ chambers (NanoPens).

The MicroFAST workflow was written on Lightning™ platform as below. OptoSelect™ 1500 chip was firstly wetted using wetting solution and additive (Berkeley Lights Inc.), followed by priming with culture medium (ImmunoCult™-XF T Cell Expansion Medium from Stem Cell Inc. supplemented with Gibco™ B-27 reagent). IFN-γ capture beads (Biolegend), single CD8+ T cells and APCs were then sequentially imported onto chip through small volume import script (with 0 μl leading volume, 10 μl package volume, and 15 μl lagging volume). After pulse culturing on chip at 37°C overnight, CD8+ T cells were stained with BV421 anti-human CD8 (SK1, BioLegend). IFN-γ capture beads with Cy5 internal fluorescence were also incubated with IFN-γ detection antibody (biotin-conjugated anti-IFN-γ antibody, Biolegend) and SA-PE reagents (PE-conjugated streptavidin, Biolegend). The exposure time for Cy5, PE, FITC, and DAPI channel was set to 2000 ms, 800 ms, 4000 ms, and 1000 ms respectively. Finally, cells in positive Nanopens were unloaded from chip for TCR sequencing. Before exporting, fluidic lines were flushed with deionized water when flush chip replaced OptoSelect™ 1500 chip. After reloading OptoSelect™ 1500 chip, the cells were exported with DPBS into tubes containing 2× TCL lysis solution (Qiagen) and RNase Inhibitor (TAKARA). Data analysis was performed using Cell Analysis Suite (Berkeley Lights).

Single-cell sequencing and analysis

RNA of unloaded cells was extracted and purified using Agencourt RNAClean XP Beads (Beckman Coulter). The resultant RNA was released into Nuclease-free water. The primers and PCR conditions for TCR amplification referred to previous article.63 In detail, RNA from single T cells was firstly subjected to reverse transcription and preamplification using One-Step RT-PCR kit (Qiagen) and first-round primers (0.06 μM V-region mixture and 0.3 μM C-region primers) in 20 μl reaction system. A 35-cycle PCR conditions were set as below: 50°C 30 min; 95°C 15 min; 94°C 30 sec, 62°C 1 min, 72°C 1 min × 35 cycles; 72°C 5 min; 4°C forever. Then, second-round 20-μl PCR reactions were performed using Q5 Hot Start High Fidelity 2× Master Mix (NEB), 0.06 μM primer Vα/Vβ mixture, 0.3 μM primer TRAC/TRBC, and 5 μl template from first reactions. Next, 2-μl aliquot of second PCR product served as template for third-round PCR reactions using same Q5 enzyme and 0.05 μM barcoding primers and 0.2 μM PE primers. Both second- and third-round PCR conditions were set as below: 95°C 15 min; 94°C 30 sec, 64°C 1 min, 72°C 1 min × 35 cycles; 72°C 5 min; 4°C forever. Finally, PCR products were run on 1.2% agarose gel followed by expected band purification. The Sanger sequencing was conducted using primers of TRAC-seq and TRBC-seq. Sanger sequencing data were analyzed on the IMGT/V-QUEST website (https://www.imgt.org/IMGT_vquest/input) to obtain TCR germline gene usage and CDR3 information.

Similar procedures were performed to obtain C25-TCR gene fragments. The primers for C25-TCR gene fragment amplification were designed as C25-P1-F, C25-P1-R, C25-P2-F, and C25-P2-R. The amplified fragments were ligated into blunt-end vector (TransGen) for sanger sequencing using M13F and M13R sequencing primers. Sequencing data were aligned with vector template containing C25-TCR.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Statistics analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software v10.0. Significant differences were compared using Paired Student’s t-test or t-test with Welch’s correction. Multiple comparisons were carried out using One Way ANOVA. The Pearson correlation test was used. Statistical difference was indicated as follows: ns, not significant (p ≥ 0.05); ∗, p < 0.05; ∗∗, p < 0.01; ∗∗∗, p < 0.001; ∗∗∗∗, p < 0.0001.

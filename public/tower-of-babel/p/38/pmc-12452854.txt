Grüss-type inequalities involving functional bounds via analytic kernel fractional integral
Authors: Majid K Neamah, Alawiah Ibrahim, Tariq A Aljaaidi, Mohammed S Abdo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe main objective of this paper is to obtain a generalization of some Grüss-type inequalities in the case of functional bounds by using a generalized analytical kernel Riemann-Liouville fractional integral. The study leverages analytic kernel functions and tools like Young's Inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality to ensure rigorous bounds. Limitations include computational complexity and restrictions to real parameters. We have developed and presented the Grüss-type inequality in a more general way, enabling mathematicians to utilize it more effectively through the generalized fractional integral involving an analytic kernel operator. The work opens avenues for future research in complex fractional calculus and practical applications.•We have used the recently generalized fractional integral operator to present Grüss-type inequalities in the case of functional bounds of a single order.•We investigated a generalization of some Grüss-type inequalities in the case of functional bounds of distinct orders by using the generalized fractional integral.•We discussed several exceptional cases and presented the corresponding results.Keywords: Fractional integral, Grüss type inequalities, Riemann-Liouville fractional integral, Analytic kernel fractional integral, Functional bounds

Dragomir [9] (2012) presented novel Grüss-type inequalities for functions of bounded variation and explored their applications in Hilbert spaces concerning self-adjoint operators. Alomari [10] (2014) introduced novel Grüss-type inequalities that encompass double integrals and several precise bounds. In the same year, Pachpatte [6] introduced novel Grüss -type inequalities utilising the Hadamard fractional integral operator. Liu and Tuna [11] (2015) derived weighted Grüss-type and Ostrowski-type inequalities within the framework of time scales by utilising the theory of combined dynamic derivatives in this context. Refer to works [[6], [14], [17], [18], [19], [20], [21], [22]] for intriguing analyses of Grüss inequality.

Researchers have recently been utilising the analytic kernel function owing to its significant relevance (see [[23], [24], [25], [26], [27]]). In our present study, generality is prominent while employing the analytic kernel subsequent to its integration into a Grüss -type inequality. This is regarded as a favourable aspect of our approach, as generalisation is a fundamental idea permeating all domains of mathematics [28]. Our findings encompass novel models for calculating fractions, the significance of which can be seamlessly substantiated. This generalisation in our study led us to uncover additional special situations that consistently arise with the introduction of generic formalism.

Young's Inequality serves as a key analytical tool in the manuscript, enabling us to control the behavior of products of functions and their fractional integrals. Its implementation ensures the rigorous derivation of bounds and inequalities, which are central to the paper's contributions in generalizing Grüss-type inequalities for fractional integrals with analytic kernels.

The study relies on the analytic kernel function A(z), which is defined as a power series. The complexity of this function may limit the practical applicability of the results, especially in cases where the series does not converge quickly or is computationally intensive. The analysis is restricted to real parameters τ,δ, etc., due to the challenges of handling inequalities in the complex plane. Although the scientific application is difficult in some cases, it has many numerical applications in many fields, especially in the theory of approximation and numerical integrations, which translate into practical reality (see [[15], [29], [30], [31], [32], [33]]). This limits the generalization of the results to complex fractional calculus. The results depend on the integrable functions ψ1, ψ2, u1,u2 bounding γ and φ. These assumptions may not hold in all practical scenarios, restricting the applicability of the derived inequalities. The generalized fractional integrals involve multiple parameters and integrals, which may pose computational challenges in real-world applications, particularly for higher-dimensional problems.

On the other hand, and from an applied perspective, fractional calculus has emerged as a powerful tool for modeling complex phenomena in fluid dynamics, heat transfer, and magneto hydrodynamics (MHD). Recent studies on non-Newtonian fluids, such as Casson fluids and dusty viscoelastic fluids, have demonstrated the critical role of fractional operators in capturing memory effects and non-local behavior [[34], [35], [36]]. These works highlight the interplay between wall shear stress, slip conditions, and ramped temperature effects in multiphase flows, which often require generalized mathematical frameworks beyond classical calculus.

The Grüss inequality, a cornerstone of integral inequalities, has been extended to fractional calculus to provide rigorous bounds for solutions of differential equations [7]. While classical versions estimate the deviation of the product integral from the product of integrals, recent generalizations incorporate functional bounds and analytic kernels to address more complex scenarios [8]. This is particularly relevant for MHD flows, where slip effects and entropy generation in hybrid nanofluids demand precise inequality constraints [24,37,38]. For instance, studies on two-phase dusty fluids [39] and ternary hybrid Casson nanofluids [40] leverage fractional integral inequalities to analyze heat transfer and shear stress under magnetic fields. However, existing fractional Grüss-type inequalities are limited to Riemann-Liouville or Caputo operators, neglecting the flexibility of analytic kernel-based fractional integrals. These kernels, represented by power series (e.g., A(z)=∑n=0∞(c1)nzn,n∈z), generalize classical operators and better model phenomena like viscous dissipation in unsteady MHD flows [41] or heat absorption in inclined channels [42].

Results and discussion

The current manuscript presents a comprehensive generalization of Grüss-type inequalities using the generalized analytical kernel Riemann-Liouville fractional integral. The results are divided into two main categories: inequalities involving functional bounds of single-order and distinct-orders fractional integrals. Below, we contrast these findings with existing research and discuss their implications.

Comparison with existing research:

1. Functional Bounds of Single-Order: i. The results generalize the work of Tariboon et al. [8], who introduced fractional integral versions of Grüss inequalities with functional bounds. By incorporating the analytic kernel function A(z), our results extend their framework, offering greater flexibility and applicability. ii. When A(z)=1Γ(τ) and τ =0, δ=0, our inequalities reduce to the classical Riemann-Liouville fractional integral forms, demonstrating consistency with prior literature while providing a broader context.

2. Functional Bounds of Distinct-Orders:

The distinct-orders case is a novel contribution, as it extends the single-order results to scenarios where fractional integrals of different orders are involved. This aligns with the work of Dahmani [7] but incorporates the analytic kernel for enhanced generality.

The derived inequalities are more versatile, accommodating a wider range of functions and conditions, which is a significant advancement over the constant-bound cases discussed in earlier studies.

3. Special Cases and Related Inequalities: i. The manuscript discusses special cases where the analytic kernel simplifies, bridging the gap between generalized and classical results. For example, Corollary 1 and Remark 3 show how the inequalities reduce to known forms under specific conditions. ii. The related inequalities (e.g., Theorem 3 and Theorem 4) provide additional tools for bounding integrals, which are useful in applications such as signal processing and control theory. iii. Fig. 3. General idea

The generalized results presented in this manuscript significantly expand the scope of Grüss-type inequalities in fractional calculus. By leveraging the analytic kernel, the inequalities accommodate a broader class of functions and provide tighter bounds in practical applications. The physical significance of the figures lies in their ability to visually communicate the impact of parameter changes and the robustness of the derived inequalities.

Future research could explore applications of these inequalities in solving fractional differential equations or optimizing systems where functional bounds are critical. Additionally, numerical experiments could further validate the theoretical findings, ensuring their practicality in real-world scenarios.

Conclusion

In the paper, we investigated a generalization of some Grüss-type inequalities in the case of functional bounds by using the generalized fractional integral. We obtained new Grüss - type inequalities with functional bounds via the generalized fractional integral operators having single and distinct orders. The results obtained are more generalized. Furthermore, we proved some new related inequalities using the current fractional integral operator. Some special cases of the presented results have been discussed.

Future research work

1. Investigate the possibility of extending the results to complex parameters, which could provide deeper insights into fractional calculus and its applications in complex systems.

2. Explore simpler forms or approximations of the analytic kernel function A(z) to enhance computational efficiency and practical usability.

3. Apply the derived inequalities to solve problems in dynamic systems, control theory, or signal processing, where fractional calculus is increasingly relevant.

4. Develop numerical methods or algorithms to compute the generalized fractional integrals and validate the inequalities in practical scenarios.

Immunofluorescence-based visualization of Malpighian tubule morphogenesis in Drosophila embryos
Authors: Neha Tiwari, Madhu G Tapadia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryMalpighian tubules (MTs) undergo extensive morphogenetic remodeling during Drosophila melanogaster embryogenesis, making them sensitive indicators of developmental perturbations. Here, we present a protocol to analyze embryonic MT development in Drosophila using immunofluorescence. We describe steps for embryo collection, fixation, devitellinization, and immunostaining to visualize MT morphology. We then detail procedures for mounting, imaging, and analysis.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Tiwari and Tapadia.1Subject areas: Developmental biology, Microscopy, Model Organisms

Before you begin

Drosophila MTs are epithelial organs that arise during mid-embryogenesis and undergo extensive morphogenetic remodeling to form elongated, polarized tubes with a central lumen. MTs development initiates from the hindgut primordium at stages 10–11 and proceeds through evagination, coordinated cell rearrangements, and lumen formation during embryonic stages 11–16.2,3,4,5 These processes depend on tightly regulated cytoskeletal dynamics, intracellular trafficking, and signaling pathways, making embryonic MTs highly sensitive to genetic perturbations. During elongation, MTs cells undergo dynamic changes in cell shape, packing, and polarity, requiring precise organization of actin and microtubule networks to maintain epithelial integrity and tubule architecture.6,7,8 Accurate visualizations of these events therefore requires optimized embryo handling, fixation, and immunofluorescence conditions to preserve tissue structure and antigenicity.

In this protocol, we use confocal microscopy to visualize embryonic MTs in Drosophila melanogaster embryos. The workflow is optimized for embryonic stages 11–16 and enables imaging of MTs position, length, orientation, and overall epithelial morphology in Oregon R+ wild-type strains of Drosophila embryos.

Innovation

This protocol provides a standardized and reproducible workflow for immunofluorescence-based visualization of embryonic MTs morphogenesis in Drosophila. While embryo immunostaining methods are well established, this protocol specifically optimizes embryo collection, fixation, devitellinization, and mounting conditions to preserve the three-dimensional architecture and spatial positioning of Malpighian tubules during stages 11–16 of embryogenesis. A key innovation of this protocol is its emphasis on consistent staging and gentle handling to minimize mechanical distortion of the tubules, enabling reliable comparison of tubule length, orientation, and epithelial organization across embryos. The workflow is optimized for wild-type embryos and uses minimal antibody combinations, reducing background and improving reproducibility. In addition, the protocol integrates clear pause points and troubleshooting guidance tailored to embryonic MTs, which are particularly sensitive to fixation and mounting artifacts.

Overall, this method offers a robust and accessible approach for generating high-quality reference images of embryonic MTs development that can serve as a benchmark for future developmental and comparative studies.

Expected outcomes

Successful execution of this protocol is expected to yield high-quality immunofluorescence images that clearly resolve embryonic MTs morphology in wild-type Drosophila embryos. Following optimized embryo collection, fixation, and staining, intact anterior and posterior MTs should be consistently visualized with well-preserved tissue architecture and minimal distortion. Tubules are expected to appear as elongated epithelial structures extending from the anterior end of the hindgut region, with a continuous epithelial sheet surrounding a clearly defined central lumen.1,9 At embryonic stages 11–16, images should reveal progressive tubule elongation, stereotypical positioning within the embryo, and uniform epithelial organization along the tubule length (Figures 2A–2D).10,11,12 Nuclear counterstaining is expected to show regularly spaced nuclei (Figure 2 A’–D’) consistent with orderly epithelial packing, while overall tubule shape and lumen continuity should be readily discernible by confocal microscopy. Fluorescence signals should exhibit low background and reproducible staining patterns across embryos when identical imaging settings are used.

Representative images generated using this protocol provide reliable morphological benchmarks for embryonic MTs development in wild-type embryos and can serve as reference data for developmental staging and comparative analyses in future studies.

In this protocol, although Cut was used here as a representative marker for embryonic MTs, the protocol can also be applied to additional epithelial and developmental markers depending on the experimental objective.

Limitations

This protocol is sensitive to fixation and devitellinization conditions, which strongly influence tissue preservation and antibody penetration. Over or under-fixation can reduce signal intensity or compromise MTs morphology, particularly at later embryonic stages. The method is optimized for embryonic stages 11–16, and performance may decline outside this developmental window due to incomplete tubule specification or reduced antibody accessibility.

Staining efficiency may vary between antibodies and antibody lots, requiring optimization for new markers. Mechanical stress during embryo handling and mounting can lead to tubule damage or loss, and temperature fluctuations during embryo collection may introduce developmental variability. Finally, this protocol provides structural and molecular information but does not directly assess MTs function, which must be evaluated using complementary assays.
